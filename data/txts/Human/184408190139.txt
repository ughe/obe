 2051. THOMAS HESTER was indicted for stealing 2 towels, value 8 d  .; and 2 1/4 lbs. weight of sugar, 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Richard Rouse , his master. RICHARD ROUSE. I keep the White Conduit tavern, at Islington. On the 31st of July I had a dinner, and the prisoner was employed to assist—I missed two towels and some sugar—these are the towels—they are marked in two separate places—I do not pretend to swear to the sugar.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  If a person were regularly en gaged, there would be no harm in his taking these towels, and returning them? A.  They might be returned—there has been instances of it—the prisoner is a most respectable man—he was found drunk, and I think he might have taken these things accidentally. NOT GUILTY.
 2052. WILLIAM ADCOCK was indicted for stealing 6 1/2 oz. weight of mutton, value 2 d  .; and 12oz. weight of beef, 3 d  .; the goods of Leaming ton Ward , his master. LEAMINGTON WARD. I am a butcher, and live in St. Pancras. The pri soner was a journeyman in my employ—I keep a lad beside—on the 31st of July I got up to watch—the prisoner was in the shop, and about half-past six o'clock he was cutting up the meat—about seven I went out to Seymour-street, and came back about eight o'clock—I then looked at the prisoner's coat, which was hanging up in the kitchen, and found in it two pieces of mut ton and one piece of beef—it was my meat, and was wrapped up in paper—I called a constable, and directed his attention to it—the prisoner was sent to Seymour-street on an errand—he changed the meat from his great coat to the coat he has on now, and his great coat was taken up stairs to the bed-room—he went to Seymour-street—I went and apprehended him there—the po liceman said he wanted him—he said, "What for?"—I said, "For robbing me"—he pulled me on one side, and said he wanted to speak to me—I said I had nothing to do with him—the meat was found on him—he said he had paid for it.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Was any one present when he said he had paid for it? A.  Yes, the policeman.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .
 2053. MARY ANN HARDIMAN was indicted for stealing 1 pelisse, value 10 s  .; 6 night-gowns, 16 s  . 6 d  .; 2 pinafores, 3 s  .; 1 shift, 2 s  .; 1 apron, 6 d  .; and 1 pair of sheets, 8 s  .; the goods of John Charles Batterbee . CHARLOTTE BATTERBEE. I am the wife of John Charles Batterbee. The prisoner was my servant—I was confined, and soon afterwards I missed the pelisse, the night-gowns, and other things stated—I have only found the shift and the apron—the shift she had on on the 6th of June, when she was taken—this is my shift and apron.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What do you know the shift by? A.  By the name marked on it—the prisoner was with me nearly two months—she never slept in my house. MARY ANN REDMAN. I searched the prisoner, and found this shift on her. JAMES LAWS( police-constable G  120.) I found this apron in a box at the prisoner's lodging—she said Mrs. Batterbee lent it to her. GUILTY. Aged 29.— Confined Three Months .
