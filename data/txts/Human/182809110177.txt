 RICHARD WEBB. I am a  sailor and live at Blackwall. On the 17th of August, at night, I went to the Black Boy public-house with Stewart, between nine and ten o'clock- I went home with her and went to bed; I had two sovereigns, and 19s. in silver, in a purse, in my trousers pocket, and five 5l. notes in my fob; I did not take my trousers off at all, and am sure I had all safe when I went to bed - when I awoke, I got up and left Stewart in bed; I felt my pockets and found only two 5l. notes - the purse sovereigns, 19s. and three 5l. notes were gone; I asked her where it was - she said she had put it all into my pocket again; I had given her a sovereign the night before, and took out my purse to get it.
   SARAH ANN SMITH. I live in Black Boy-lane. I was at the Harrow public-house that night and saw the prosecutor and Stewart come in, both very much intoxicated - they went out together; Stewart returned with some papers in her hand which looked like Bank notes: a person asked her if they were songs - she said No, they would like to have such songs in their pockets; I afterwards heard somebody say they were 5l. notes - I did not see Piddington.
   JAMES BEACHY. I am a Thames-police surveyor. On the morning after the robbery, Webb came to me; I went to the Black Horse public-house, at Poplar - I heard some of the girls say "Here is Beachy coming;" I then heard a rush towards the back-door - I ran up then, and somebody passed me at the front; I went on to the back-door, and took Stewart there - I told her I wanted her for felony, and asked where the notes were which she had taken from the sailor; she said she had not got them, but would give me two sovereigns if I would go into the long-room and see blackguard Nance (meaning Piddington), who had got the money - I went with her to the long-room, but Piddington was not there: I then took Stewart, and she said she had taken three 5l. notes and two sovereigns from the prosecutor, but had left him two 5l. notes, and he ought to be satisfied with that, and she was sorry now that she did not take the whole - I asked her what she had done with the rest of the money: she said that I had got to find out - she said Webb had given her a sovereign, and showed me the bed they had been on; she said he had his jacket off, but not his trousers; that she took them for fear anybody else should, as there was another bed in the room.
   JEREMIAH BULKLEY. I am a constable. On the 18th of August, I was going up East Smithfield, and saw two sailors who had hold of Piddington; they said she had robbed a shipmate - I took her in charge, and found this purse in her bosom; I asked her where she got it - she said Stewart had given it to her, and four sovereigns; that she had returned two, given one to a coachman, and one is in it now.
   RICHARD WEBB. This purse is mine.
 STEWART's Defence. The prosecutor was very tipsy.
 STEWART -    GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 PIDDINGTON -    NOT GUILTY.
 1936.  JOSEPH SIBLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 1 pair of shoes, value 3s., the goods of James Wilson.
 JAMES WILSON. I go to a charity school, and live with my parents. I lost a pair of shoes, on the 17th of July, from behind a row of houses in  Church-street; I gave them to John Fox, a little boy, to take care of for me - I did not see who took them, and have not got them again.
 Q. You have been examined before, and if you do not speak the truth it will be worse for you - you were behind some houses, where were they? A. Near New-cut-street, Lisson-grove; Wear was there - I gave my shoes to Fox to hold.
   WILLIAM WEAR. I saw Wilson by Lisson-grove, and saw him give his shoes to Fox; I saw the prisoner go up to Fox, snatch the shoes out of his hand, put them under his coat, and run away - I told Wilson of it.
 Q. Why did you not run after him? A. The other boys said they would jump on me if I did: Fox is about seven years old.
   PHILIP WEBSTER. I took the prisoner into custody.
 Prisoner's Defence. Wilson was fighting with another boy and Ware, and presently he lost his shoes; I went to get my father's sapper and was taken.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1937.  EDWARD JONES and SARAH JONES were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , 2 sheets, value 2s., 2 table-cloths, value 9s., and a shift, value 2s., the goods of William Laurence.
   MARY LAURENCE. I am the wife of William Laurence. The prisoners lodged with us, at Hampton-wick, from the 15th to the 25th of July; the woman came as a traveller - I missed a table-cloth and a shift on the 22d; on the 24th I missed a blanket and gave them in charge - I do not think the man knew anything about it.
   WILLIAM HARRISON. I am a pawnbroker. I have two sheets, one table-cloth, and a shift, pawned by Sarah Jones, on the 19th of July, and another table-cloth on the 22d.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 SARAH JONES -    GUILTY. Aged 38, Confined Three Months.
 EDWARD JONES -    NOT GUILTY.
 1883.  EDWARD HILL was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 hat, value 8s., the goods of William Simmons.
 WILLIAM SIMMONS. I am a  servant. On the 7th of of September I was in St. James-street. near the door of No. 50; my hat had been in the inner hall; I saw the prisoner come with it down the steps; he ran up the street; I pursued and took him with it in his hand - he said "Is this it."
   JAMES ADAMS. I am an officer - I took the prisoner and bring the hat.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. A person ran by me and dropped the hat, and when the prosecutor came up to me I told him so; on the way to the watch-house; he said, "I think I have made a mistake, and I wish to let him go," but I said"I will not be let go, I am given in charge, and let the officer do his duty." At the watch-house he had his doubts, but he was persuaded to press the charge.
