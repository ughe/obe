883. JULES BARNARD (32), FRANCIS PAUL LAUNET (29), and
CHARLES LINDEN (32), Obtaining rom Louis Sindoux Dutap 10 francs
by false pretences, with intent to defraud, and other sums from other
persons with a like intent. Other Counts for conspiracy. (GIOVANNI
DOMPÉ was also charged in the indictment together with the prisoners, but the
COMMON SERJEANT quashed the indictment as against him.)
MESSRS. GILL and BODKIN Prosecutod.
Louis ARISTIDES SInvoux DOTAP (Interpreted). I am a journalist, for
merly of Natur, now living at 32), Greek Street-I saw an advertise-
ment to the effect, but not in the same words, as this one. (This stated
that servants of both 86.68 toere wanted for England, that English was not
necessary, and that expenses would be refunded; application to be made to
Barnard, 214, Noel Street, Berwick Street, London; 30 centimes to be
enclosed for an answer.) I wrote to Barnard and received this letter in
reply. (This had a printed heading, French Registry Office, dc., J. Bar
nard, director, 21, Noel Street, and stuted that they had several situations
as professors in schools, with salaries from 100 to 200 fruncs per month,
with board and lodging, or as French correspondent with 75 to 300 franos a
month, or as correspondence clerk and bookkeeper at a form of diamond
merchants at 400 a month, and requesting him to send references and
10 francs for inquiry fee, and adding they would charge 5 per cent on the
first year's salary as commission.) I sent references and 10 francs to
Barnard, and received this letter from him dated 10th July. (Stating
that in eight days they would be able to put him into communication with
the diamond merchants who required a clerle and bookkeeper, and who they
hoped would accept his services.) In consequence of that I came over to
England on 25th July, and went to 21, Noel Street two or three days
after my arrival, but saw none of the prisoners there-I was told to call
again, and I went again next day, when I saw the three prisoners,
I asked for Mr. Barnard; Launet came forward as if he were he-he
said the place for which I came had been taken two or three days before,
but that they had a place at that moment which would suit me, and to
which I should be introduced; that was to be the director of Mr. Dompé's
club at 40, Charlotte Street, near Fitzroy Square-I went there with
Linden, who called himself Durant, and after having a glass to drink he
introduced me to Dompé-after doing so he said, "Now I have intro-
duced you to Dompé, and if you suit him we shall speak of the matter
to-night"-we did not speak of business that day, but next day I went
again with Linden to the place, and then Linden spoke to Dompé by
himself, after which he informed me that Mr. Dompé expressed now
some objection to my getting the place, and that he asked a deposit of
