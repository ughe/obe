Blackfriars—I produce an agreement for the hire of some household furniture—the prisoner signed it; it contains a list of the articles. (  This agreement was dated 27th February , 1877, and was for the hire of some furniture by On prisoner from the prosecutor, at the rate of  15 s. a week, payable quarterly the prisoner to have the option of purchasing within  12 months, the prosecutor in default of payment to take possession of the goods ). I have since seen the goods at different pawnbrokers, and identified them—there are seven or eight articles such as looking glasses, and lamps, and other things—I know nothing about a bill of sale on them.
   Cross examined.  I first knew the prisoner in December, 1876—I did not know her during her husband's life; I believe she has a good deal of furniture in her rooms—she lived in Trigon Road, Clapham—she bought a looking glass of me in December, 1876, and paid me 2 l . 10 s . for it; she did not bur a dining table, she had that on hire—previous to the agreement she paid me between 12 l . and 13 l . for goods—in respect to the agreement she paid me 50 s . on 28th February, 20 s . on 5th March, 40 s . on 12th March, 20 s . on 17th April, 10 s . on 13th April; I gave her a receipt—the value of the goods was about 75 l .—she did not pay the weekly payments regularly; the payments were to go towards the purchase—I was sent for to the police court; I have no wish to prosecute—I did not give her in custody—I saw the prisoner's sister, and an arrangement was entered into with the Magistrate's sanction with the pawnbrokers to redeem the goods—the prisoner's sister handed me some pawn tickets to get the goods—I find that the prisoner has got into the hands of loan offices—I know she took in dress making, she seemed to be always at work—she had assistants; she has complained of being robbed of material, which she was at great trouble and expense to replace.
   By THE COURT . The value of the things pawned was about 20 l ., 10 l . had been advanced upon them. ALEXANDER HENRY DAVIS. I live at 20, Penton Place, Kennington, and am managing clerk to Mr. Carpenter, of 106, Stand; he lends money—I produce two bills of sale, one for 13 l . 10 s ., and the other for 20 l ., executed by the prisoner—she received 10 l . on the 13 l . 10 s . bill, and 15 l . on the 20 l ., bill, to be repaid by monthly instalments.
   Cross-examined.  She was to pay 3 l . 5 s . a month on the two bills—the schedule is in the second bill—we do not as a rule take a second inventory when there are two bills of sale, the schedule answers to both—the schedule is placed in the second bill of sale by mistake.
