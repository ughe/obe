I went with Roberts to the Prince of Wales, and saw the prisoner in the

row-he let the parlour-1 followed him, and said, "What will you fetch a
gang for?" and caught him by the collar, and pushed him down in the
passage-about twenty minutes afterwards, Roberts and I left the house,
and saw the prisoner over at the brick-stack gathering something up-be
came straight up to the path we were on, six or seven yards from us, and
threw a brick, which struck Roberts- I turned round to look, and he
heaved another, which caught me and knocked my hat off-be ran away-
I went after him, and met bim coming up with a crowd, and gave him in
custody-he said he would have his revenge if he lived, to rip my guts
open, mentioning my name.
JAMES WALEY ( policeman, N 206). I took the prisoner--the two men
were having their heads dressed at a druggist's-he repeated several times
that he would have his revenge, if he ripped their guts open.
(The prisoner produced a letter to the Court, in which he stated that
Robert knocked him down in the passage, saying he would kill him; that
when Roberts came out, being a bigger man than him, he threw part of a
brick at him to frighten him, not intending to hurt him.) Guilty. Aged 22.
Recommended to mercy by the Jury.-Confined Three Months.
554. WILLIAM LEIGHTON WOOD, forging and uttering a bill of
exchange for 431.; with intent to defraud Stephen Rogers.
MR. PARNELL conducted the Prosecution.
STEPHEN ROGERS. I am a timber-merchant, of Cleveland-street, Fitz.
roy-square. The prisoner is a pianoforte-manufacturer-he owed me
331. 178. 6d. for timber which he had last Feb.-on 22nd Feb., in con-
sequence of a letter I had written to him, the prisoner paid me this bill of
exchange-(this was daied Sept. 4, 1850, drawn by W. L. Wood on J.P.
Vinnicomb, of Exeter, for 431.) —upon receiving that, I supplied him with
161. worth more goods-bis bill is now 501. 53. - I paid this bill into my
banker's in Jan. - it was returned to me.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. I believe you have known the
prisoner some time? 4. About ten years; he was in a good way of
business- I have attended a meeting of his creditors-my solicitor did not
apply for payment after I knew it was alleged to be a forgery.
JOHN PEWTNER VINNICOMB. I am a musical instrument-maker, of
Northern Hay, Exeter. I know the prisoner-I owed him a balance of
431.-on 16th Sept. he asked me for payment-I accepted a bill for him ;
it was ante-dated-he said he should not be able to use it at that time,
and it would be more convenient if I would allow him to ante-date it-
