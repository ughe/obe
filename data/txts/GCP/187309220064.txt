612. WILLIAM NEWMAN (21), Stealing a pair of boots, the property
of Thomas Lilley.
Mr. Buck conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD Peroy. I am manager to Mr. Lilley, who keeps a boot shop-
on 28th August I saw the prisoner rise up from the doorway-I got up off
my stool and missed a pair of boots worth 88. 6.- I went outside and saw
the prisoner with a bag under his left arm--I asked him what he had got-
he dropped the boots and ran away, and I pursued him, brought him back
and gave him in custody.
Prisoner. I looked at the boots and felt them, but I never moved from
the door.
John LORD (City Policeman 874). I took the prisoner and found 83d. on
him-Wheeler gave me the boots-the prisoner said at the station that he
took them up and intended to buy them by paying 6d. a week.
Prisoner's Defence. I did not intend to steal the boots. I should have
paid him 6d. down. I wanted them and was looking at them, but I never
had them in my hand.
Guilty.**-'He had been nine times convicted. ---Five Years' Penal Servitude,
613. JOHN DUNN (23), Stealing a pocket-book, twelve pieces of paper,
and two photographs, of William Boardman, from his person.
MR. CHARLES MATTHEWS conducted the Prosecution, and Mr. Mead the Defence.
GEORGE WESTWOOD (City Policeman 707). On 9th August, about 9.30,
I was on London Bridge, and saw the prisoner-he was afterwards taken to
the station, but not by me- I searched him there and found in his pocket
a quantity of papers, and these photographs (produced).
WILLIAM BOARDMAN. I am a preacher living at Clapham-on 9th
August, about 9 p.m., I was crossing London Bridge, and missed my
pocket-book containing several receipts, and these photographs of my wife
and myself, also a letter which I want, but which has evidently been thrown
away with the pocket-book.
. Cross-examined. I have lost the pocket-book.
Nor GUILTY.
614. JOHN DUNN was again indicted for stealing a handkerchief from
the person of a man unknown.
MR. CHARLES MATTHEWS conducted the Prosecution; and MR. MEAD the
Defence.
GEORGE WESTWELD. On the same evening, about 9.30, I saw the prisoner
on London Bridge, following a gentleman-I watched him, and in King
William Street saw him take this handkerchief out of the gentleman's
pocket and put it into his own-I caught hold of him-he struggled for
some minutes, and said "I have got nothing, only what belongs to me"-I
lost sight of the gentleman in the struggle.
Cross-examined. I was in plain clothes-he went about three yards
before I caught him-no other handkerchief was found upon him-I never
lost sight of him.
The Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate. “The handkerchief belongs
to me.”
GUILTY.
He was further charged with a previous conviction at Newington, in January,
1866, when he was sentenced to Seven Years' Penal Servitude; to this he
PLEADED GUILTY.-Fourteen Years' Penal Servitrede.
