253. JOHN BRIAN, stealing 7lbs. weight of lead, value 15d.; the goods
of Thomas Henry Maudsley and others, his masters; and WILLIAM
TAYLOR, feloniously receiving the same; to which
BRIAN pleaded GUILTY.
JAMES SHERIFY. I am foreman to Thomas Henry Maudsley and others,
engineers-Brian was in their employ, In consequence of suspicion I watched
him on Thursday, at dinner time, to Taylor's, a marine-store dealer's shop in the
Upper Marsh, Lambeth-I saw him take something from his person, which
was put into a scale, weighed, and then thrown under the counter by Mrs.
Taylor-next day, at twelve o'clock, I watched him there again, and between
twelve and half-past five, I got a constable to go with me--he went into the
shop, and took possession of something-I immediately followed, and when
I got in, Taylor said to the constable, "Do you call that lead ?"-he said, "I
do"— Taylor said, "It is zine" -- he was asked if he had bought any other
articles of the man before-he said, no; he did not know that he had ever
seen him in his shop before-Brian had taken the lead from his person-
the policeman asked him where he got it-he made no reply-I believe it
to be Mr. Thomas Henry Maudsley's and Joseph Field's.
JAMES CUDDY (policeman.) On Thursday evening, 28th Nov., I received
information from Sheriff, watched Brian into Taylor's shop, and saw him take
a piece of lead from under his jacket-Taylor's wife was inside the counter,
and put the lead into the scale-he was passing in and out, and might not
wave seen it-he only said afterwards, “You don't call that lead; it is zinc."
