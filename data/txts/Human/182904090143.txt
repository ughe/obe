 OLD COURT.
 EIGHTH DAY. SATURDAY, APRIL 18.
 Third Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Recorder.
 996.  JOHN PETTET, JAMES WHITE, JAMES MARTIN, and SAMUEL HILLICK were indicted forfeloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Sarah Howorth , on the 3d of April , at St. Luke, and stealing therein 2 tables, value 10s.; 4 chairs, value 10s., and 2 candlesticks, value 3s., the goods of Sarah Howorth; I trunk, value 10s., and 10 sovereigns, the property of Joseph Bennet ; 2 chests of drawers, value 16s.; 2 coats, value 7s.; one pair of trousers, value 4s., and 40 fishing-lines, value 10s., the goods of William Gardner and another.
 MESSERS. BOLLAND & CRESWELL conducted the prosecution.
   SARAH HOWORTH. I am single, and live at No. 42, Goswell-street, in the parish of St. Luke; I have lived there thirty-five years - Mr. Robert Ray is the landlord, and has been so about twenty-one years. On the 3d of April I was at home, and in consequence of information I saw Pettet about half-past one o'clock in the passage of My house; he demanded 120l. for six years' back rent - I told him I did not owe any such money; I asked him to walk up stairs - he followed me up, accompanied by two more men, who I cannot identify; Pettet pulled out a paper, and read it - I do not know what it was; he then demanded the rent in the name of William Chapman- I should not know the paper again, for I did not have it; I never heard of William Chapman in my life - Pettet said his own name was John Smith; he asked me to open my lodger's door, whose name is Bennett - I told him I could not, for my lodger was out; his door was fastened with a padlock outside - I was very much alarmed; he told me not to flurry myself, for I should have five days to replevie if he was in the wrong - when I said I could not open Bennett's door, he said he could not wait a minute; I said, "I have another way backwards, if you will wait while I go round and see if that is open;" he said he should not wait a minute - he rushed by me, and wrenched the padlock off before my face; he went into the room, and the two men with him - I did not go into the room; I went down-stairs, and went out to look for my brother - I was absent nearly a quarter of an hour; when I came back there was a van at the door loaded with goods - I thought I saw a table on it which was mine; I did not notice any thing else - when I got into the house it was nearly empty, and the house was so full of people in the passage I could hardly get in; I found Mr. Butler there, who is the rent-gatherer for Mr. Ray - he has been so about ten or eleven years; I never knew any other rent - gather during that time - I had some conversation with Butler, and then fetched Hawthorne, the officer: he came back with me - Pettet was then standing at the post of the parloar-door, and I gave a charge of him - the constable asked him by what authority he took the goods; he refused to show his authority; he said he was not going; to have it turn up - two tables were taken out of the house, four chair, two brass candlesticks, one pair of cotton stockings, and some crockery; the house was nearly empty - I had never received any notice not to pay the rent to Butler.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Since you have been tenant of these premises has any person demanded rent from you, except Mr. Ray and Butler? A. Not since Mr. Ray has had the house; before that Mr. Barker did - I have not paid any rent for four years, because I have a little property which I cannot get at, and Mr. Ray is so kind as to wait for it; I am confident Pettet did not say he had come as an agent of Smith or by his authority - he said his own name was Smith; I was very much alarmed, but I am certain of that; he demanded the rent in the name of Chapman, and said his own name was Smith: he did not represent that he was acting for Smith; I first saw him in the passage; I think the door was shut when I saw him; the passage was full of people, when he refused to show his authority for fear it should be torn up - he did not say I should have a list of the articles taken away; he was asked for the inventory, and said he had got it, but would not show it.
 MR. CRESWELL. Q. Who demanded to see the distresswarrant? A. Butler and the constable.
