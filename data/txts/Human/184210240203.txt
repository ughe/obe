   Cross-examined by MR. WILDE . Q.  You asked Gosbell what he was? A.  Yes; he said he was a man employed by Mr. Beaumont—I asked if he was the managing man—he said there was a Mr. Smith there, bat he was out, or had left for London, or something to that effect—Mr. Beau mont's name was over the shop-door—I had heard, before I went down, that there was such a shop at Maidstone, and I wrote there to ascertain the fact—when I returned I went to Bachelard's—what Gosbell had stated was carried out by what Bachelard stated, and I sent more goods—I had a conversation with one creditor, who said that the parties were respectable.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Who was that creditor? A.  Mr. Minton—I sent more goods to Beaumont, after what Gosbell had told me about his marrying this lady.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  Did you not know that Beaumont was only commencing business? A.  Yes; but I knew what he repre sented, and how many goods he had bought, and I knew that goods ought to be in the shop if he intended to open business—I first went to Maid stone by Mr. In gram's direction; and again, in a fortnight, of my own ac cord—Gosbell appeared to be shopman—I do not know that Beaumont has married a person of property—I did not know Bachelard till I called on him—I called on him several times, and I met him in the street—Gosbell answered me candidly and fairly, as far as I could judge, some questions, and some he did not—be told me that the person in the shop, who I knew to be old Mr. Beaumont, was another name—I only knew about Mr. Beaumont from what I heard.
