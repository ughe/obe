 1618.  THOMAS BARNET was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 2 shirts, value 7s., and 1 shift, value 3s., the goods of William Griffith.
 MR. QUIN conducted the prosecution.
   MARY FOSTER. I was in the service of William Griffith. On the 17th of July I hung out two lines of linen about two o'clock; at a quarter before three Groves brought two shirts and a shift to me - they are part of what I had hung out.
   JOHN GROVES. I live in Burton-crescent, Islington, and have a view of Mr. Griffith's premises from my back window. I saw a person get over the railing from the drying-ground, with a parcel - he went under a shed, and then into a brick-field - I followed; I touched him on the arm, and said, "What have you got here?" he dropped the bundle and ran off; I cried, Stop thief! I cannot swear that the prisoner is the man; he took a kind of circuit round the field; I took up the articles, and took them to Mr. Griffith's.
   EDWARD SHIRLEY. I am a brick-maker, and work in Mr. James Rhodes' field, at Islington. I saw the prisoner, on the 17th of July, (I believe it was him) in the brickfield - he was running - somebody called Stop thief! but I do not know who it was; I do not know where the prisoner had been employed before that day.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far were you from the person you think was the prisoner? A. Fifty or sixty, or a hundred yards; his back was towards me as he ran past me - I had never seen him before that I know of - I had an opportunity of seeing him for about half a minutes; I did not see him drop any thing.
 COURT. Q. Do you know the person of the man? A. I believe it was the prisoner.
 Q. Have you any doubt about it? A. No.
 Q. Upon your oath, was it, or was it not, the prisoner? A. Yes, that is the man who ran by me - no one was running after him - I did not see Groves that day - not to be confident it was him.
 Q. Upon your oath, did you see John Groves that day? A. No, Sir, not to be positive it was him; I saw nobody pursuing the prisoner; I do not know who cried Stop thief!
   JAMES FOSTER. On the 18th of July I was at Mr. Griffith's, waiting for my wife, and the groom came in and said, there was a person waiting about the back-ground - I went and took the prisoner; I asked how he came to take these shirts and the shift; he said he did not - but when I got him up to Mr. Griffith's stable, he owned it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was there any thing said before he owned it? A. I said it would go hard with him if he did not tell the truth.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1619.  CHARLES JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 30th June , 1 half-sovereign, 3 half-crowns, and 2 shillings, the monies of James Webb , from his person.
 JAMES WEBB. I  keep the  Cock and Lion, public-house, in Wigmore-street. On the 26th of June, the prisoner came to live with me as pot-boy- on the 30th I sent him with beer to Mrs. Robinson's - he came back, and said, "Master, does not Mrs. Robinson owe you for three pints of beer?" I said, "Yes;" he said, "If you will have the kindness to give her change for a sovereign, she will pay you;" I counted him out half-a-sovereign, three half-crowns, and 2s. 6d. in silver; he took it, but did not come back; I never saw him again till he was in the hands of the officer, on the 5th of July.
   CATHERINE ROBINSON. I live in Wigmore-street, and am a customer of Mr. Webb's. I told the prisoner on the 30th of June, that I would pay the bill, but he brought me no money.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am an officer, and apprehended the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 15. Of stealing only. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1620.  ELIZABETH DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of June , 2 sheets, value 5s.; 1 blanket, value 4s.; 1 bolster, value 3s., and 2 pillows, value 4s., the goods of Samuel Mann , in a lodging-room.
   ANN MARIA MANN. I am the wife of Samuel Mann - we live in Chapel-street, Somer's-town. The prisoner has lodged with me about one year and a half, in the front-room, on the second-floor, and these articles were part of the furniture let with the room. On the 21st of June I missed these articles; there was an execution against her - and I went up stairs, and found the bed was stripped - before I got down she had run away.
   JOSEPH PEWTNER. I am a pawnbroker, and have a bolster and pillow, which have been in pawn twenty months - I do not know who brought them.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did it through complete starvation - and when I had got a little work, I would have got them home; she said she never objected to lodgers pawning, if they made all good before they left.
 MRS. MANN. I deny that. GUILTY. Aged 32.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
