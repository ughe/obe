   RALPH HEARN. I am a brass-turner, and live in Grub-street. On the 19th of October, about half-past one, o'clock, I was sitting up for my lodger; I heard a rattle spring, and went to the door - the watchman was bringing a man along; I heard a scuffle among the Irish people at the corner of Broad Arrow-court - I went over; I saw the prosecutor, and observed them give him a violent blow - I saw him fall; another one put his foot across him, and began kicking him - I immediately went for assistance; I cannot swear to any particular man. for I ran to fetch a watchman - I met Sackerson, and sent him into Grub-street; he ran - I ran on to the watch-house, to fetch more watchmen, and as I came back they had the prisoner.
   JOHN HOGWOOD. I am a watchman. A man was given into my charge, about one o'clock, on the 19th of October -I was going to the watch-house with him; Hearn came to the watch-house, and fetched me - I went down to Grub-street, and saw Perrott lying on the pavement; I assisted him up - he was quite unable to tell me the cause of it; I left him leaning against the public-house, and went in pursuit of the prisoner, whom I was given to understand had gone down the street, with the hat; I followed a short distance down Grub-street, and met Sackerson about fifteen yards from the spot, coming back with the prisoner and the hat.
   WILLIAM SACKERSON. I took the prisoner; I met him coming out of Silk-street, about one hundred yards from where this happened - he had the hat in his hand, and as soon as he saw me, he put it behind him; the handkerchief was in it - I rather think he had not found his way out of the street, and was returning; I collared him, and took it from him.
   JOHN COMPTON. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house between one and two o'clock; he said he picked the hat up in the kennel.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. This is not the handkerchief that was in the hat when I picked it up - it was an old one, not worth a penny; it was a small bit of a handkerchief; I was at work till twelve o'clock that night - I went and had a pint of beer, and as I came along Grub-street there was not a soul in the street; I saw the hat in the gutter, and took it up - I went about five yards, and stood at the corner of Silk-street, with the hat in my hand; the watchman asked what I had - I said, "A hat," and gave it him.
   WILLIAM PERROTT. This is the very handkerchief I had in my hat that night.

 216.  DANIEL CARTER was indicted for feloniously assaulting Amelia Woodroffe , with intent to rob her, and her goods and monies from her person violently and feloniously to steal.
 AMELIA WOODROFFE. I live at No. 17, Denmark-court, Strand. On the 26th of November, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, I had been to the English Opera-house; I went, also, to Drury-lane Theatre - I was coming back to the English Opera-house again, and, as I came out of Drury-lane Theatre, I saw the prisoner, in  Bridges-street, talking to a woman who had lived servant with me; I crossed over from the woman, and was going into the oyster-rooms - a man came up, in a white fastian jacket, he took me round the waist, and asked if I wanted a coach; I said No, and with that the prisoner at the bar came up, and said, "What is it o'clock?" That was the first time I saw him; I said, "I don't know," and then the one in the white fustian jacket said to the prisoner, "It is of no use, it is a duffer's watch" - (I had had no conversation with the woman and the man; she had left me on the Friday, and this was Monday) the prisoner said, "Never mind, we will have it;" I then crossed over Russell-street, towards Bow-street, to get from them - the prisoner then came up and said, "What is it o'clock?" I made him no answer - this was in Bow-street; both of them had followed me - I did not see my servant again; I went into Bow-street - the prisoner came up to me again, pulled my cloak aside, and took the watch in his hand, but it was supported by two watch-books and a tape; it hung it my belt - he turned it round; the other man was ten or fifteen yards from him at that time - that man gave a whistle, I think with his fingers in his mouth, by the sound, and the prisoner caught hold of the watch; I called Watch! the watchman came up and took the watch out of his hand; I still retained possession of it - the books still held it to my belt; he said to the watchman, "It is of no use taking me - it is only a duffer's watch;" the watchman laid hold of it at that time - he asked the watch-house-keeper to allow him to speak to me; he then gave me a desperate blow, and knocked me down in the watch-house - I had a desperate black eye; the Magistrate would not believe he had knocked me down, but he said himself that he had; he said, "Will you appear against me to-morrow?" I said Yes, and he gave me a desperate blow; I never saw him before, till I saw him talking to my servant, nor had I seen the man in the fustian jacket before.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. How many names have you? A. Clara Fisher is the name I go by, because I do not wish to disgrace my parents, by going by my right name; I was living with Mrs. Belasco - she keeps a brothel in Denmark-court; my servant's name is  Louisa Ward- she was living with the prisoner at that time - she is no rival of mine, for I had never seen the prisoner before, she was servant to Belasco; one or two ladies live there - I occupy the first-floor, and another lady the other; there are dress lodgers and lodgers in ordinary - I was a lodger in ordinary; the watch was my own - I gave twenty guineas for it, and that woman knew it; I had had it between twelve and eighteen months - I have been at Belasco's nearly two years; Mrs. Belasco was not with me when this happened - one of the books which fastened my watch is lost, and the other broken; the prisoner followed me for about a quarter of an hour - the watch was not to be seen until the man came up, and took me round the waist; I crossed over to Bow-street to find somebody to get hold of him.
 Q. Was there any difficulty in getting the watch if he was disposed? A.Decidedly; because I held the seals, and pulled the watch, or he could have got it - I pulled the stone out of one of the seals with holding it; I will take my oath it was in his hand when the watchman came up; Ward told the Magistrate that it belonged to Belasco.
