CHARLES GREAVES, assistant to Mr. Dixon, linendraper, Blackfriars-road.  On the 22nd of May, Cannon came for change for a sovereign—Gar rard stood just outside the door—I gave Cannon four half-crowns, the rest in shillings and sixpences—he said he would rather have a half-sovereign—I gave him one, and took away the four half-crowns—I took the half-sovereign from the desk—there was no other in the place where I took it from—I had weighed it, and it was full weight—I had examined it to see whether it was a good one—after I had handed the half-sovereign to Cannon, he called in Garrard, and said he believed he owed him a half-sovereign—Garrard said he would rather have silver, and Garrard handed the half-sovereign to Cannon—he passed it on to the desk again, and said he would rather have silver—I took the half-sovereign from him, and gave him silver—I believed it to be the same I gave him—I put it back to the desk without examining it—it was done in an instant—it was put back into the bowl where the gold was—there was no other half-sovereign there—they then left the shop—soon after the policeman came—I gave the half-sovereign to Morris—he marked it, and handed it to Goff.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you a distinct recollection of weighing this half-sovereign? A.  I received it from Morris, and weighed it—it was the only one I had received that morning—I weighed it by a pair of scales and a stamped weight—I did not examine the bowl at the time I put it in, but I did a few minutes before, when I turned over the gold, there were but two or three sovereigns in the bowl.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Do you know when this half-sovereign was taken? A.  About twelve o'clock—there were no others put in the bowl. WILLIAM MORRIS. I am assistant to Mr. Dixon, in Blackfriars-road. I received a half-sovereign from Mr. Greaves, and marked it—I handed the same to Gough.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What mark did you put on it? A.  A cross on the neck, with a pair of scissors. BENJAMIN LOCK WOOD. I am assistant to Mr. Bayfield, a pawnbroker, in the New-cut. In coming from Kennington-cross to the Blackfriars-road you can come through the New-cut—on the 22nd of May, Garrard came to me for a sovereign for a pound's worth of silver—I did not see any on else with him. GEORGE LANGRIDGE, assistant to a pawnbroker, Waterloo-road.  On the 22nd of May, Cannon came and asked for a sovereign for a pound's worth of silver—I gave him a new sovereign—he asked me to give him one of that reign. ESTHER CLARK. On the 22nd of May, I was in the service of a stay maker, in Westminster-road. Cannon came and asked for change for a sove reign, and handed me a new sovereign—I had not got it, and I sent Sarah Jenkins out—she returned with four half-crowns and a half-sovereign change, and handed it to Cannon—he said, "Thank you," and went to the door, and said, "Here, John, is the half-sovereign I owe yon;" and then I saw Garrard—he said, "I should like silver;" and Cannon said, "Perhaps this young person will give you change for the half-sovereign"—I looked for some silver, and gave it to Cannon—he passed me over a half-sovereign—I put it into the till—there was no other half-sovereign there—I am sure of that—they thanked me, and went out—the policeman came in immediately after—I then gave him the half-sovereign I had received from Cannon.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you ever seen either of the prisoners before? A.  No—I had examined the till before I put the half-sovereign in—Mr. Cox took the cash away the night before, and we had not received a half-sovereign in the course of that day.
