Cross-examined. Q. If a letter was put in at Lambeth between five
and six o'clock, what time would it find its way to the General Post-office.
London? A. Between six and seven o'clock, or probably before six-
Peek, the officer, found these other purses- I was not present when one
was found, but was when this other was found by Peek, with his own hand
-I saw him find this one in the back room on the first floor-I was not
present when this other was found-Mr. Peacock, Peek, and the women
were present at the house, nobody else in that room-I do not know whe-
ther there is a family living there besides Mrs. Weatherhead's—I did not
see any other person-I did not see the paper box till Peek produced it
the prisoner was not present, but the sister was- I think the question was
asked as to whose box it was, and either the sister or mother answered,
"It belongs to us all”--the prisoner was then absent-I cannot recollect
that the sister said it was her box-I will not say she did not-I do not
remember hearing her say so-I understood it to be common property
among the whole of them.
ROBERT WILLIAM Peacock. I am assistant to my brother, the solicitor
to the Post-office. I was present on this occasion-inquiry was made of
the prisoner whose purse that was-she answered, "It is mine"-I asked
how long she had had it-she said, “About three months"-I said,
“Where did you get it?"-she at first said, "I bought it"-I said,
“Where ?"-she answered, "It was given to me by a friend, who pur-
chased it some where in the Strand" - I said, "At what place, what is the
name of your friend ?"-she turned round to her sister who was sitting by
the side of her, and said, “Why, it is yours" -- the sister replied, " It is
not mine, I know nothing about it"- then asked the prisoner again,
“Who gave it you?"-she made no reply.
HERBERT PRITCHARD. I am a letter-carrier, in the Twopenny Post-
office, and collect the letters from Mrs. Weatherhead's office twice a day-the
prisoner generally made up the collection of letters for me-she has been
in the habit of doing so, I should say for the last twelve months-she or
casionally took the letters out, she stamped them, and tied them up-I
should say I have seen her receive letters in the office, and receive the
postage.
Mrs. LOUISA PEPPERCORN (looking at one of the purses produced.). I
know this purse--I made it myself--I put the tassels on myself, and the
slides—this slide is one that was on it-the other which was on it was like
it-(the other slide had been mislaid in Court.)
Q. Look at this other purse, do the slides on that belong to your purse!
A. Never, as far as I know-I am quite certain the first purse I saw
mine-I inclosed it in an envelope, and took it to Mrs. Weatherhead's
post-office, East-place.
Cross-examined. Q. Is there any thing about the purse you identify,
except its general appearance, by which you swear to it? 4. Only being
exactly the same in every respect as I made one-it was quite new when
I parted with it-it does not appear now to have been washed it has been
worn-pot very much it is stained-it is of the commonest pattern.
