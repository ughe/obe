TO
MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS Proseoutod; MR. HOFFMEISTER Defended.
ANNE POOLE. I am the daughter of Thomas Poole, and live with my
father at Park Street, Liverpool-I have a sister, Sapphire Healey-in
1876 I was living with my father at 2, Charrington Square, Barracks
Town, Cork-my sister was then a widow named Sykes; she had been a
widow 10 years—I knew Sykes perfectly; he went to Chicago-my sister
received two letters, the first said he was doing well; the next was of
his death-we always burn letters-my sister went into the service of
Mr. Stewart, the Magistrate of the village; that was in 1869—my sister
did not go into mourning for him-we have not heard of Sykes from that
day-I was present on 23rd June when my sister was married to the
prisoner - this is the certificate-they lived together four months, when
the prisoner was ordered to India—I saw my sister Saphire four days
ago; she was in perfect health.
Cross-examined. I think my sister was married to Sykes in 1865-he
was an engineer; he worked at Oork—they had one child, which was
born about 10 years ago; it is now in heaven-we got one letter from
Sykes; my sister never answered him; I do not know why the child
lived with my sister, she was called Matilda Sykes—my sister wrote to
him for money; sha told him he was only a husband in name, and she
could have nothing more to do with him; we never heard since I saw
three letters from the prisoner--my sister went to service at several
places; she was never out of the country; she lived in Cork—I went to
Liverpool nearly six years ago I have seen all the letters.
Re-examined. We received the letter announcing Sykes's death in 1870
---the first letter came from Chicago; the second from Tasmania, from the
person with whom he was living.
