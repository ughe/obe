 909.  HENRY HARVEY was indicted for wilful and corrupt perjury on the trial of Peter Newberry and William Iverson for a highway robbery, at the Sessions house in the Old Bailey in February Sessions, 1785*.
 * See No. III, Part I. of this Mayoralty.
 (The indictment opened by Mr. Garrow.)
 Mr. Fielding, of counsel for the defendant. My Lord, I am sure you will not be surprized, in a case of this kind, that I should wish all the witnesses to retire before Mr. Morgan opens.
 Court. Let lists of witnesses, except to character, or those that are to prove what passed in Court, be given to each party, and let them all go out of Court; there can hardly be a subject that requires a more serious and solemn investigation for the security of the subject on the one hand, and the safety of the defendant on the other.
 Mr. Morgan, of counsel for the Prosecution, then opened the case as follows:
 May it please you, Mr. Recorder, and gentlemen of the jury, to favour me in support of this prosecution; you are already informed this indictment is for perjury, committed on the trial of  Peter Newberry and William Iverson; upon that trial, which was for a highway robbery, there was not any witness to the fact of the robbery but the prosecutor himself; there was no other material evidence obtained on the trial but that which was given by Harvey, and that that was material, you will in a few moments, I think, be properly convinced: fear and slight are in general pretty certain signs of guilt; and supposing what the defendant swore on that trial is false, he knew the consequences of his evidence by charging Newberry with having exhibited those two signs of guilt; after stating that he had been to the place, and came back, being asked if he had found either of the prisoners, he answered thus;
