 773.  GEORGE RIGGS was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of February , 2 shillings, the monies of Thomas Groves.
 MR. CHURCHILL conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY DEWDNEY. I am clerk to Thomas Groves. The prisoner was in his service; on the 18th of February I gave him 10s., with a written order to get some nails - I told him to bring a bill of them; he returned with this bill, which amounts to 8s. 8d. - he brought me 1s. 4d; I told him one item was much overcharged; and on Wednesday morning, as he was going by the shop, I desired him to call and get it corrected - Mr. Adams sent the bill back.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where had you sent him? A. To Messrs. Adams and Morris; I think I mentioned the mistake to him on the 18th - he staid with us till Thursday morning, when he was taken.
   WILLIAM SALKELD ADAMS. I am shopman to Morris and Adams. On the 18th of February the prisoner came with an order; I sent the goods with this bill - it amounted to 6s. 8d., but since it left our shop a figure of 2s. has been added to an item of 11d., and the 6s. at the bottom is altered to 8s.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you enter it in your book? A. Yes, it is 6s. 8d. there - I looked at the entry yesterday; I am certain of it without referring to the book.
 COURT. Q. Is the bill now in the state the prisoner brought it to you? A. It is.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When he returned did he not tell you he had made a mistake? A. He gave the bill to Mr. Adams, and said it was a mistake; Mr. Adams called me and said it was a mistake of mine - I said it was not; the prisoner wished me to write a note, and give him the bill back, but I would not and kept it, saying there was a gross imposition some where.
 Q. Was not your deposition copied three time? A. The clerk made so many mistakes, the Magistrate said he would have them copied again, or they could not be read.
 COURT. Q. Are you quite sure the alterations in the bill are not your making? A. I am.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, declaring his innocence.
   HENRY SPICER. I took the prisoner in charge - I remember the mistakes being made in Adam's deposition; it was taken by a new clerk - the Magistrate had them copied again.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 22. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 774.  ELIZABETH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of January , 1 bolster, value 3s.; 1 pillow, value 2s.; 1 sheet value 4s., and 1 flat-iron, value 1s. the goods of Sarah Conlan.
 SARAH CONLAN. I am a  widow. The prisoner lodged at my house with a man; I missed these things.
   STEPHEN STEVENS. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned a pillow, sheet and flat-iron with me at different times.
   RICHARD BARBER. I have a bolster, pawned by the prisoner, on the 15th of December.
   CHARLES GEORGE VINCENT. I am an officer, and took the prisoner - she said she did it through distress; I found the duplicate on her.
 The prisoner pleaded poverty, and received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 49. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Two Months.

 775.  JAMES CARROLL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of January , 1 boot, value 4s., the goods of Lucy Pendrill.
 LUCY PENDRILL. I am a  widow, and live in Angel-street, St. Martin's-le-grand. On the 31st of January my two boys were playing in my shop - one of them called Stop thief! and the prisoner was brought back with this boot.
   GEORGE PENDRILL. I was in my mother's shop, and saw the prisoner come in and take a boot off the shelf - I called Stop thief! he dropped it, and ran away; I am sure he is the man.
   HEZEKIAH WILMER. About seven o'clock on the 31st of January, I saw the prisoner at the corner of this window, and soon after heard a cry of Stop thief! I ran back, and took the prisoner, who was running from the shop; the boy said he had taken the boot.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Whipped and Discharged.
