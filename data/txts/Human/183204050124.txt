 1122.  SARAH PERROTT was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 1 watch, value 30s., and 1 watch-key, value 3s., the goods of William Miller.
   WILLIAM MILLER. I am employed on board a ship. I met the prisoner on London-bridge on the night of the 25th of February, a little before nine o'clock; we went into the Redcross, and sat some time - we came out towards the bridge again; we then went under one of the arches of the bridge - we staid there some time, and then she said she rather expected there was a Policeman at the corner; she went to see - I then missed my watch out of my pocket: I went out, but did not see her - I went on till I saw a watchman, and told him the case - he told me to go the Policeman; I am sure my watch had not fallen out.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe you had not much money? A. I had 25s. or 26s.; I had not paid her any thing - I was to have paid her, and should if she had not gone away - I did not give her my watch; it had no case to it - I was to have given her 1s. and something to drink; I might be a little the worse for drink - I cannot say how much I drank: I might be two hours in the public-house - I had pawned the case of my watch.
   JAMES LAWSON. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner brought this watch on Monday morning, and wanted to pawn it - she said she had found it on London-bridge; I asked where the case was - she said her husband had broken it, and it was gone to be repaired; my lad then put a paper into my hand, that such a watch had been stolen - the prisoner said she would fetch her husband, and ran out; I pursued and took her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1123.  MARY ANN PHILLIPS was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of February 1 shawl, value 5s., the goods of Harriet Handerside.
   HARRIET HANDERSIDE. On the 28th of February I was unfortunately in the station-house; I had a shawl on; the prisoner, who was a stranger to me, was there - I was in there for some hours, and while there I lost my shawl; I told the officer of it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you walk to the station? A. I dare say I was taken there for protection, because I would not give my address; the prisoner was tipsy - I do not know which of us was the worst; I was sitting sleeping when I was robbed.
   JAMES TITT(Police-contable G 7.) I took the prisoner down to Worship-street; I then heard of this robbery, and saw the corner of a shawl hanging below the prisoner's gown - I took her back to the prosecutrix, who was at the station; I asked her to describe the shawl, which she did, and when I showed it her, she claimed it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were not the prosecutrix and the prisoner both drunk? A. The prosecutrix was very drunk; the prisoner was not very drunk - she might have been drinking.
 Prisoner's Defence. I certainly had the shawl somehow; but I was not in the least conscious of it till I was told of it.
     NOT GUILTY.
