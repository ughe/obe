and some one, I cannot say who it was, pulled Peters off me—I cannot say how long they were beating me about; it seemed a long time to me—they went off, and I followed them—they said they would serve me a great deal worse—I saw the policeman on the oppo site side—I went and spoke to him, and he came across the road with me—the prisoners were going with the crowd; I should think there were 300 persons—I gave charge of the prisoners—I was bleeding very much from the nose, and some one snatched my handkerchief out of my hand—when I gave charge of Peters, just at the time the policeman collared him, Peters knocked me down, and he began kicking the policeman—Quaid said that he should not take his partner to the station—Quaid was holding the police man by the collar, and Peters was kicking him—they did that for a long time—I do not know whether they got him down—he got his staff out, and Peters tried to take his staff from him—the policeman was very much hurt—I did not see the prisoners taken to the station, I was obliged to leave; I was so hurt and kicked about that I could not get along so fast as they did—I was taken to the station, and from there to the hospital—I was an out patient for three weeks—I made a charge against the prisoners, and they were looked up, and I was taken to the hospital—I had a black eye—the bridge of my nose was very much bruised, and my face all over blood—before I was struck I had not said a word to the prisoners—I was standing on the pavement, filling my pipe—I was perfectly sober—I had just left work.
   Cross-examined by MR. PLATT . Q.  They were quite strangers to yon? A.  Yes—some person pulled Peters off me—Quaid had got hold of ins then—Peters came up, put his hand round my neck, and slapped me on the face, ai a drunken man would—it appeared to me that this was done by way of a joke—on that I called him a nasty, a—humbug—he did not say, "I could not help it"—they were coming straight along; they were not stag-gering—I was filling my pipe—the tobacco box was not knocked out of my hand—when I had called Peters what I did, Quaid knocked me down—I did not go after them before the blows were struck—Quaid came back, and struck me on the nose—I collared Quaid after that—no nght took place—nothing but the blow on the nose was given me in answer to she language used by me—all the ill usage I got was before I went to the policeman, except one kick on the leg—I do not know where Ginn was when the blows were given to me—the blows were all over before I went to the policeman—I went to him, and said, "Here are two chaps  have been insulting me, and kicking me about"—he came across the road, and asked me which they were—I could not get at the prisoners, because the crowd was so great—I pointed to them, and said, "Those are the persons"—he did not refuse to take them because he had not seen the assault—I did not look at Peters' shoes that night, I saw them afterwards at the station: they appear to lace up in front, such as costennongers wear, they were tipped, or hob nailed.
