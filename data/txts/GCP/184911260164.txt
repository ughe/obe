JONATHAN RUSSELL. I keep the Fisherman's Arms, in Cold Bath-street,
Greenwich, at the corner of Morden-street-on 9th Nov., a boy, who I should
not know again, came between ten and twelve o'clock-Mrs. Russell sent a
note of a declaration up-stairs- I came down and found the boy waiting-
this is the note (looking at it) that was sent up-stairs- came down and tore
the corner off a blank, and gave it to Mrs. Russell--the blanks are kept in a
corner.
Prisoner. Q. Do not you tear them all off in the same way? A. Yes:
I do not swear to this-I gave four out that week, and this is one of them
I cannot speak to this being the same I gave Mrs. Russell-I have every
reason to believe it is-she passed it over to the boy in my presence.
MR. PARRY, Q. When did you see the boy again? A. On the Wed.
nesday following--the paper was brought to me the same day, about twelve
o'clock, by John Flaxty, wrapped in a bit of paper-William Cornwell was
present at the time-be is one of the visitors- I gave him the paper directly,
and he said it was a counterfeit-David Anderson is a member of the com-
pany, and there are about 400 others-in consequence of what Mr. Corn.
well said, Flaxtey was taken that evening-I did not see the prisoner till be
was brought into Court before the Magistrate-the paper was brought to me
folded up as this is now.
JOHN FLAXTEY. I live in King's-street, Lewisham-road, and am a smith.
I have known the prisoner for some years-on 9th Nov. I saw him at the
Coach and Horses public-house, about ten minutes before twelve o'clock-
Mallett was there-the prisoner came in--we were saying we had had no
breakfast, we should like to have a bit of bread and cheese--the prisoner said,
"I have just turned a shilling job away; a woman has asked me to take a
club paper to the Fisherman's Arms, but as I am a member I do not like to
take it myself"-we spoke out directly and said, “I will go ; I will go " -
the prisoner went out of the house and came back directly with a note, and
gave it to me-it was wrapped up in a piece of newspaper like this one-I
took it, and gave the paper he gave me to Mr. Russell over the bar--Mr.
Cornwell was there at tbe time--Mr. Russell opened it, looked at it, and gave
it to Mr. Cornwell, and he said it was counterfeit-I said I knew nothing
about it, but William Stokes, a member of the club, gave it me, and he was
up at the Coach and Horses, he had better see him I went back to the Coach
and Horses—I saw the prisoner there, and told him it was a counterfeit-ho
ran out of the house directly, after this woman, as he said-he did not find
her-I was taken into custody-I have always given this account of it, as
near as I can remember.
