 SEVENTH DAY. THURSDAY, APRIL 16.
 Second Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 968.  SARAH EVANS was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Charles Rushbrook , on the 14th of March , and stealing 4 coats, value 10l.; 5 boys' dresses, value 8l.; 2 waistcoats, value 20s.; 2 pairs of trousers, value 2l.; 3 yards of cloth, value 50s., and 1 pair of drawers, value 16s., his property.
   CHARLES RUSHBROOK. I live in Leather-lane, at the corner of Baldwin's-gardens, and am a tailor. I went to bed about eleven o'clock at night on the 13th of March, and got up about a quarter-past six in the morning; I had left my house quite secure, but found a piece cut out of one of the shop shutters, round the corner in Baldwin's-gardens - it had been cut by an orger, and I found the piece inside that had been cut out; they then broke one of the squares of glass, and brought the property through the hole - they must have put a hand in to have got the property; I missed property worth 25l. or 30l. - I know nothing of the prisoner.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I am a Bow-street officer. On the 14th of March, about half past twelve, or one o'clock in the day, I saw the prisoner going down Sun-street, with this bag in a bundle-handkerchief; she was threequarters of a mile from the prosecutor's; two men were ten or fourteen yards behind her, and then went in front of her; I cannot say they were in her company - she went in a direction for Petticoat-lane - the two men then got in front of her; I knew one of them, which excited my suspicion, and in a street leading to Petticoat-lane I went up to the prisoner, and asked what she had got there; she said she did not know - I asked her where she had brought it from; she said she had met a man in Smithfield, who asked her to carry the bundle, and he would give her a shilling - I asked her where she was to carry it to; she said she did not know, but the man told her to follow him - the two men had then got in front of her, as far as they were before from her; I asked her if either of the men walking in front was the man who gave it her - after some hesitation she said it was the stoutest, and at that time one of the men looked round, and both walked fast; they turned the corner, and were out of sight in a minute - I took her to the office, with the bag, which contained this property.
     NOT GUILTY

PATRICK O'DONNELL and PETER DALEY were indicted for feloniously assaulting Elizabeth Coombs , on the King's highway, on the 30th of March , at St. Luke, putting her in fear, and taking from her person, and against her will, 1 bonnet, value 3s.; 1 shawl value 1s.; 1 handkerchief, value 3d.; 1 pair of patterns, value 6d., and one sixpence, her property.
   ELIZABETH COOMBS. I am single, and live in May-place, Stamford-street, Blackfrairs-road. On Sunday night, or Monday morning, the 30th of March, between one and two o'clock, I was going home, and O'Donnell spoke to me, at the end of Fleet-street; he asked me where I was going - I never saw him before; I am certain he is the man; I told him I was going home; he said he would give me half-a-crown if I would take a walk along with him - I said I knew no place on this side the water, and he said he did; I turned, and went with him; he took me to Lion and Lamb-court, Golden-lane- I was never there before; we did not go into a house; the patrol came up, and asked if I lived there; and he asked O'Donnell if he lived there, and he said Yes; the patrol told me I had better go home, it was quite time I was at home, and the man likewise - I turned from the prisoner, and asked the patrol to direct me to Blackfriars-bridge - he took me a little way below the brewhouse, and told me to go across Smithfield, and that was the way to the bridge; I had scarcely left the patrol a minute before O'Donnell came after me, tapped me on the shoulder, and told me he would give me another shilling if I would go back with him - I refused, and said I was going home;
