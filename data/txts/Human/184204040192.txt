   COURT . Q.  Was the empty sack put with the full sacks in the stable? A.  Yes—the sacks were all shot when I got into the stable, but he had only delivered six full ones to Hall—I found all the seven empty sacks together—after taking Starr to the station, I went back to the stable, and found Hall there—I asked him how many sacks of corn he had received of Starr on account of Mr. Mountain—he said seven—I asked where he shot them—he said, "Two and a half in here below," meaning the stable, "and four and a half in the loft"—I took him into custody—we went to Starr's house, at Enfield, searched, and found some corn there—his house is two miles on the London side of the road to Waltham-cross—we brought away between two and three bushels of corn from there, and found the papers, which were recognized by Mr. Mountain, amongst the corn found in the bin in Starr's stable.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did Mr. Mountain know the corn at once? A.  His foreman did.
   Starr's Defence.  I never had a grain of Mr. Mountain's corn in my life.
 (Starr received a good character.)
   STARR— GUILTY. Confined One Year.
   HALL— NOT GUILTY.
