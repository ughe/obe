 1713. CATHERINE DONOVAN was indicted for feloniously as saulting Catherine Mahoney , on the   26th of April , and cutting and wound ing her in and upon the left side of the head, with intent to do her some grievous bodily harm. CATHERINE MAHONEY. I am single, and lodge at No. 6, Redcross-court, Borough—the prisoner's husband rents the house, and is my land lord. On the night of the 26th of April he applied to my mother for a week's rent—she could not pay it—he went down to his room, and I heard the prisoner using very low language, and calling the old woman hard names—I said my mother did not look more like the names she called her than her mother did, and then she called me the names myself—I had a bit of potato in my hand eating, and I flung it at her—then she took the chopper from the side of her kitchen fire-place in her kitchen (I must go through the kitchen to get to my room) she said, the first one that came down the stairs that night she would chop their b——head off—my mother came and locked the door—about half-an-hour after I went down for some water, and saw her with the chopper in her hand—I said, "I think you are going to do what you said"—she said, "Yes, I intend to do it"—I said, "I will see whether my head will be chopped off or not," and then she gave me a violent blow on the forehead—I flinched back from it, and received it on the top of my head, and did not receive the violent blow she was going to give me, or it would have been straight down my face—it came lighter on me through my falling back—I fell to avoid the blow—I called my mother down, and said, "I am murdered by the chopper"—she came to my assistance, and I was taken to the hospital.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You were quite yourself? A.  Yes—I did not use any bad language to her, nor give her any provocation—my mother had some words with her, but I did not interfere—I said, "Mrs. Donovan, you called my mother an old b—-, but she is no more one than you are"—I did not say, "There you are sitting there, a w—to your own brother"—on my oath she did not say, if I called her a w—to her own brother again, she would chop my head—I did not say I would prove what I had said—that I swear—she had a child on her lap when I threw the hot potato—I do not know where it hit her—I will swear it did not hit her in the face—I threw it into her lap—I threw it as hard as I could—it did not hit the child—it came into her lap—I was four stairs off when I threw it—I do not know that she had her cap torn off, and her hair pulled about—I left her and my mother together—I swear the child was not knocked down before I went to the hospital—three years ago last August I had a few words with a neighbour, and was sent to prison because I could not pay 40 s .—I did not nearly knock a young woman's eye out with a brick-bat—I was sent to Kingston gaol for it, and was there twenty-one days—I was never in prison in my life before—I was never in Brixton
