 2193. ELIZABETH ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of August , 1 1/2 lbs. weight of pork, value 8 d  ., the goods of Zachariah Rich ard Catchpole . PHILIP STREBER. I am shopman to Zachariah Richard Catchpole, of Lisson-grove. On the evening of the 16th of August I was at the door—I saw the prisoner come to the shop, take a ticket off a piece of pork, and put it on another piece—she then took the pork and walked off with it—I took her with it about three yards off.
   Prisoner. I  did not intend to steal it; I had money in my hand. GUILTY. Aged 60.— Confined Three Months.
 2194. JOHN CARR , JAMES MERCER, and JAMES CLARK, were indicted for stealing, on the   16th of August , 1 cap, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of William Saunders . WILLIAM SAUNDERS. I keep a shop in High-street, Shadwell. On the evening of the 16th of August, between eight and nine o'clock, I missed a cap from my door—it had been tied to a string—the policeman came—I went to the station-house and found the cap—this now produced is it. JOHN RANSOM( police-constable K  328.) On the 16th of August I saw Carr coming along with two others—Carr had something tied in a handkerchief—I asked what he had got—he said a cap, which was his own, and he had bought it of Mercer, who was with him—Mercer said, "What a story, I am sure you did not"—I took them to the station—Carr then said, "Now I will tell the truth about howl came by this cap; James Clark and Mercer brought it down Twine-court and offered it to me and my uncle for sale for 2 s ., and I went to get money on it to pay for it"—I went and took Clark—at the Thames-police Mercer said he did it for hunger—Clark said he had got a bad leg, and his father said if it did not get better he must go and do as Mercer did for his living.
   Carr's Defence.  I was at work all day in the docks; in the evening Mercer and Clark came and offered the cap to me and my uncle for sale; I was going to get money on it. NOT GUILTY.
 2195. JANE FREEMAN was indicted for stealing, on the   14th of August , 1 pair of spectacles, value 14 s  .; and 1 spectacle-case, value 1 s  ., the goods of William Midgley , from his person. WILLIAM MIDGLEY. I am a house-agent, and live in Rawstorne-street, On the 14th of August, between eleven and twelve o'clock, I was return ing home through St. Lukes, and was pinioned by three or four women—I tried to make my way out, but in doing so I lost my spectacles out of my waistcoat-pocket—these are them—I was not drunk—I had had a pint of half-and-half and two glasses of rum—I was sober enough to walk home.
   Prisoner.  He said at the Magistrate's that he had beeji accosted by five or six women in Old-street, and treated them with gin. Witness.  No, the spectacles were taken from my pocket; I did not give drink to any  women.
