GEORGE GODFREY. I received him in charge at the watch-house, and found some sham gold seals on him - he is a duffer, and sells these articles - he seemed a little drunk when he was taken, but, before we searched him, he got excessively drunk without more drink.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Publicly Whipped and Discharged.
 263.  JOHN GOODMAN and ANTHONY FORMAN were indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 1 handkerchief, value 6d., the goods of Henry Frazier , from his person.
   FRANCIS KEYS. I am an officer. On the 9th of November I was at the end of Blackfriars-bridge, at the time the Lord Mayor was on the water; I saw the prisoners together, following a lady and gentleman; Forman took the handkerchief from a gentleman's pocket, and gave it to Goodman; I crossed over, and told the gentleman - he caught Goodman, and snatched the handkerchief out of his hand; he said his name was Frazier, but did not appear at the office; he gave me no Christian name, but it was marked H.F.
     NOT GUILTY.
 264.  JOHN TOWNS and WILLIAM HUNT were indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November , 90 lbs. of lead, value 22s., belonging to David Evans , and fixed to his dwelling-house.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to be fixed to a building.
   WILLIAM THRESH. On the 24th of November, about twenty minutes to six o'clock, I was just by Mr. Evans' premises, at the corner of John-street, Crutched-friars, and met Towns with this piece of lead on his shoulder - he was carrying it openly, and I asked where he was going - he said to take it home to his master; I said it was useless to say that; but to take it back to where he got it from; I had seen him ten minutes or a quarter of an hour before, and asked who he was waiting for; he said for some persons who were at work there; I have since compared the lead with what remains on the premises - it corresponds exactly.
   JOHN THOMPSON. I am a constable. I met Hunt on the 24th of November, a little before six o'clock in the evening, about a quarter of a mile from Mr. Evans' premises, with this piece of lead in this bag; I asked what he had got - he said, "Some wet things;" I felt it was a substance, and found this lead in it; I have compared it with what remains - it corresponds exactly.
 DAVID EVANS. This lead was taken from my manufactory, which adjoins my dwelling-house - it belongs to me; I have seen the lead compared with what remains - they correspond exactly.
   JOSEPH HUNT. I am an officer. I took Towns to the Compter; I asked if he had a knife about him; he said No, but I was untying his hands in the Compter, and this knife was in his hands - it appears to have lately cut lead.
 TOWN's Defence. I was hired to carry the lead.
 TOWNS -    GUILTY. Aged 21.
 HUNT -    GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
 265.  JAMES RAVEN was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of September , 2 sovereigns, 6 half-sovereigns, one 10l., and five 1l. promissory notes, the property of James Wesson.
 JAMES WESSON. I am a  tailor, and live at Saffron-Walden, Essex. On the 26th of September I packed up a parcel, containing a 10l. and 5l. Bank note of Saffron-Walden bank, with six half-sovereigns, and two sovereigns- it was all mine; I packed it in brown paper, and directed it to Mr. William Aldington Seal, Minories - it was to go to the Blue Boar public-house, Whitechapel - this is the 10l. note. - I gave the parcel to Spinks to deliver to the Stortford coach; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   JOHN SPINKS. I drive the coach as far as Stortford. I received this brown paper parcel from Wesson on the 26th of September; I locked it in the hind boot - we change coaches at Bishop-Stortford; I took all the parcels out of my coach, and put them into Firman's.
   GEORGE FIRMAN. I drive the Bishop-Stortford coach to town; I did not see this parcel.
   JOHN PORTER. I am porter at the Blue Boar, Whitechapel. The Bishop-Stortford coach arrived there, about twelve o'clock in the morning, on the 26th of September - the prisoner was horse-keeper there, and helped to unload the coach; I did not help to unload it that day; I know no more of it.
   JOHN FORRESTER. About three weeks after this parcel was taken, I heard the Regulator coach had been robbed of a parcel containing 20l., and, on inquiry, I found the prisoner had left the Blue Boar, and gone to Mrs. Nelson's, at the Bull inn; I went to him, and said, "Young man, I hear you have found a little money;" he said, "Yes, I have;" I said, "How much might it be, pray?" he said a 10l. note; I said, "Where did you find it?" he said, "In Cheapside;" I said, "Was it loose in the street?" he said,"No - it was wrapped in brown paper;" I asked if there was any more; he said, "No - only a 10l. note;" I asked if it was a Bank note - he said, "No - a country note;" I asked where he changed it; he said at Mr. Meason's, the hatter, in Aldgate, and that he gave his address, James Thompson, Leadenhall-street, on the back of it; I went to Mr. Meason, and found this note with that written on it, and here is the hat which he bought; I found thirteen sovereigns in his box.
   JOHN STAPLES. I am in the employ of Mr. Meason, hatter, of Aldgate. I remember the prisoner coming to our house once, previous to buying this hat, which was on the 13th or 14th of October - he paid me a 10l. Saffron-Walden note; I rather doubted its being good, and sent to the Saracen's Head inn, where the Saffron-Walden coach comes, to know if it was good, or if it had been stopped - they did not doubt it - I asked him his address - he said James Thompson, Leadenhall-street, and said he worked at Messrs. Cross and Lewis, Fenchurch-street; I paid the note away in the course of the week, and it got down to Saffron-Walden.
 JAMES WESSON. This is my note - it came from the Saffron-Walden bank to me; I know it by the number, which I took when I packed it up.
 WILLIAM ALDINGTON SEAL. I live at Sparrow-corner Minories. I expected this parcel - it never arrived, and I wrote in consequence of it.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
