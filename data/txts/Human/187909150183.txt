I shall be 19 in December, and my brother William is 24—my father was always kind to my mother and to us—my brother William is very spiteful—I have had to go to the police-station three or four times through his knocking me about—he has stabbed my father—I was present and saw him do it—he was locked up for it, and my father was bail for him to keep the peace—I was at the police-court—I did not go to 2, Windmill Street to get my brother's clothes—that is next door to the Windmill public-house—I was not in the Windmill that night—it is kept by a person named Louisa, not by Cole—I was the first to find out that my brother's clothes were there—he showed them to me after we had been to the police-station—I then went back to get my change and hare my coffee—he had on a butcher's linen smock, which reached "pretty nearly down to his ancles—it was covered with blood—his trousers were saturated with blood, and there was blood inside the flap where the buttons are—these are them—when he showed me the trousers I saw the blood—I had seen the blood on the smock, and saw that it was blood; it was still wet—I had not said anything to him before he showed them to me; he did so of his own accord.
