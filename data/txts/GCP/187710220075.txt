801. HENRY KIRKE SWANN (38), Unlawfully obtaining by false
pretences from Timothy Arthur Symonds, an order for the payment of
100l., with intent to cheat and defraud.
MR. D. METCALFE conducted the Prosecution; and MR. M. WILLIAMS
the Defence.
TIMOTHY ARTHUR SYMONDS. I am a booksellers' assistant-I saw an
advertisement in the Daily Telegraph on September 12th, and I wrote to
"C. A., Post Office, West Bromwich”-I saw the prisoner at my lodgings
in Stanley Street, Pimlico, on Saturday, 15th September-he said he was
the gentleman who had advertised (Read : “Cashier and clerk wanted
in the office of an established country newspaper: salary 308. to commence,
security 1001., apply O. L., West Bromwich, near Birmingham ")he
also said he was the proprietor and editor of the Lichfield Chroniole, and
that it was being carried on in partnership with Mr. Brown, who did
the printing and publishing; that a disagreement had arisen, and a dis-
solution of partnership had been drawn up, and he was going to carry on
the paper alone-he said he should want some one to do general office
work, and to act as cashier, and he would give me 28. 6d. more than the
salary advertised-ultimately I accepted the terms, and I was to com-
mence at once, but I was to draw up an agreement on the Saturday,
and forward it to him, and he would let me know on the Monday, which
I did I did not hear from him that week, but on the following Friday
I received a post-card from Mr. Swann, and on Saturday the 22nd I had
this telegram “Meet me at Charing Cross Hotel at 2.30, if not there,
shall drive to Pimlico”-I did not go to Charing Cross it was 2.30
when I got to Pimlico I waited for the prisoner till four o'еlock, but
did not see him I then started to Pulbourne, near Cambridge I re-
ceived a letter from Mr. Swann the following morning-(This letter was
dated 22nd September, 1877, Charing Cross Hotel, and stated that it was
absolutely necessary to arrange with the prisoner or someone else at onco, as
the arrangements had been made for publishing)-in answer to that letter
I sent this telegram - "Will come to you on Tuesday next to settle
agreement on Wednesday”-the prisoner came to me at Pulbourne on
Monday the 24th, and said the matter must be settled at once, and he
was considerably put about-we came back to London, and at the Charing
Cross Hotel he told me that all was ready; that I had good security;
that his wife's relations were worth 30,0001.; that he was well acquainted
with Messrs. Swindle and Collis, great coal-mine proprietors; that he
would settle at once, and I could have a week's holiday, and commence
on the next Monday-I believed, his representations, and handed him the
cheque-he gave me this receipt "24th September, 1877, received the sum
of 1001, deposited with me this day by Timothy Arthur Symonds, to be held
as security during his engagement with me, and at the termination of
the said engagement to be returned to him. Notice of the termination
of the said engagement to be given in writing one month previously:
