ALEXANDER KINNELL, instruetor at the Mansions Motor Garage
Company, 28, Victoria Street. In October, 1904, I was in want
of employment, and wanted to learn to drive a motor-car. I
had had education as a mechanical engineer and in using elec-
trical tools. I joined an institution called the Motor Drivers'
Institute, 199, Piccadilly, of which Medawar was the proprietor.
The garage was at 14, Coach and Horses Yard, which was after.
wards the garage of the Motor Drivers Union. I went through
under 8 Mr Furlong He treated me all right and I got a cer
course of private instruction there, for which I paid E15,
tifieate. I had five or six driving lessons, going from the
garage to Richmond and back. I was allowed to drive the car
myself. I became instructor at the Motor Drivers' Union in
June, 1905, and continued to the beginning of August. I was
seeretary at the Motor Drivers' Union and Kerr was secretary
at the Institute. I suoceeded Kerr at the Union. I instructed
for both. I had about 30s. a week. There WaS a body of a
M.M.C. ear smashed, and we showed the pupils what was in
the chassis. That was sent out to be repaired. There was also
the body of the Gobron-Brillie ear, and we instructed on that.
Medawar told me not to let the pupils touch the machinery and
not to take the bonnets off. There were no repairs done for
the sake of teaching pupils.
