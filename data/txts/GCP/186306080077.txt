WALIAM MILLER (Policeman, G 148). In consequence of information
I received, I went, on 22d April, to Mr. Johnson's, Basinghouse-yard, I saw
the prisoner, and told him I should apprehend him on suspicion of being
concerned in stealing a horse and cart, and eleven sacks of flour, belonging
to Mr. Hunt, New Kent-road--- he said, “I know nothing about it"-on the
following morning, when I was bringing him from Spittal-aquare station to
Worship-street, he said, " I forgot; the day the flour was stolen I was at
bome in bed ill”--the horse and cart were brought to the station, and iden-
tified by the prosecutor.

RODERT CASSER. I am manager to Mr. Johnson-the prisoner is one of
our carmen-I have the books here on 10th February the prisoner went to
Hackney, to the top of Lea-bridge-road, and Upper Clapton- I cannot speak

that all the goods he took out on that day were properly delivered these
(produced) are the tickets which would be delivered with the goods; they
are duly signed-amongst the people to whom he had to deliver there was
a Mr. Rowe, and a Mr. Robinson, a butcher-We had four carmen at that
time--all the other three were employed upon different districts upon that
day; he was the only person who had that district.
Cross-examined. . I see on one ticket, " Received from William Johnson.
Signed J. Neal, carman;" that is not the prisoner's name A. No; the

tickets are only intended to get the signatures of the parties to whom the
goods are delivered—I did not see the prisoner lerve the yard myself.
