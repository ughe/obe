I live in Park-place, No. 5 is at the back of me—about three minutes before 6 o'clock that morning, I was in my garden and heard screams—I went to No. 5 and saw Mrs. Gay, she was at the end of the passage on the edge of the kerb throwing her hands about and seemingly in great pain—she had at that time a razor in her hand—I took the razor from her and gave it to the policeman—the razor was bloody—I saw the prisoner about five minutes afterwards, I saw him wash his hands in a pond—I walked by his side till I met a policeman, and he gave himself up—after I gave up the razor at the station he gave up the sheath and said it belonged to it. THOMAS NEWMAN( Police-sergeant, V  25). About ten minutes after 6 o'clock that morning, I was on duty at Clapham—I saw the prisoner followed by the last witness and several others; they said, "Stop that man, he has cut his wife's throat"—the prisoner said, "I don't want to be stopped, I have done it, I was coming to the station to give myself up"—after I took him to the station, I went to the house and saw Mrs. Gay on the floor, the doctor was attending her—the place was bloody all over; the passage, the floor, and the walls—this is the razor I received from the last witness—the prisoner took this sheath of it out of his own pocket at the station—I think he said, "This is the sheath." SARAH WILLIAMS. I am bar-maid at the Oxford-arms, Clapham—about 5 o'clock on the morning of the 6th April the prisoner came there—he had some brandy and ale—he remained till about a quarter before 6 o'clock; he then left and was away about ten minutes, he came back and asked for more brandy—he said, "I am going to pay you for it, this is the last I shall ever drink; I have out my wife's throat, I have come here to wait for the policeman to take me"—I begged him not to stop—his hands were covered with blood—he said, "Look here"—he just went in the parlour and then went away. ELIZA DRUMMOND. I live at 39, Park-place, opposite to where Mrs. Gay lived—on 6th April, about 6 o'clock, I heard several screams—I looked out of window and saw the prisoner and his wife struggling in the passage—his wife was upon her knees, and the prisoner seemed as if he was getting up from his wife—he was at her back—he rushed into the road and then ran up the street.
