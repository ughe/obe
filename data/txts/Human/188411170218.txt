WILLIAM EASTER. I am carman to Mr. Condy—I delivered these goods at 5, Melbourne Grove.
   Cross-examined by Young.  I believe A. Young signed for them—I can not remember you—I only saw you for two minutes. GEORGE ALDRIDGE. I am manager to Howard and Co., drysalters, 143, New Kent Road—about four months ago we supplied goods to Frederick Young, of Melbourne Grove, to the amount of 3 l . 6 s . 6 d .—Mr. Welsford is our traveller—our terms are cash on delivery—cash not being paid, on the following Saturday I called at the oil shop in Melbourne Grove—I saw some one; neither of the prisoners—that person made an appointment for the following week—I then saw the prisoner Young—I told him I came after the account, 3 l . 6 s . 6 d .—he said Mr. Young was not in; he would be in next Saturday—I called then and saw the other person—he said Young was not in—I used some strong language, and the prisoner Young then came forward—he said "I am Mr. Young, what have you got to say?"—I said "You might have told me that in the first instance," and I believe I called him a swindler—he did not pay me—I left, and never got any money—while this transaction was going on Randall called at our place, gave me this card, ( As before ), and ordered some goods amounting to 10 l . or 12 l .—I asked for a reference, and he gave Mr. Young, of Champion Hill—I did not go there; I told Eandall that Young's reference was hardly good enough, and we did not deliver the goods—while I was at Champion Hill I saw Randall there—he was taking goods away in a small trap—I saw him there four or five times; he was taking away goods more than once.
   Cross-examined by Randall.  You told me you were manager to Mr. Smart—you did not say anything about terms; you simply gave me the order—I saw you taking away several descriptions of goods; several cwt. of soap for one thing, and a bag of dog biscuits—the soap was not wrapped up—I saw you loading other goods on other occasions, but I am not able to specify what.
   Cross-examined by Young.  Your order was given through our traveller—he is not here—I did not see the goods delivered. SAMUEL QUINT. I live at 116, Manor Road, Brockley—I am owner of the shop, 3, Doveridge Terrace, Melbourne Grove, Champion Hill—I let that shop to the prisoner Young from 14th January—he said that he and his brother wished to go into business, that they had got very little money to start with, something about 30 l .—I said I would do all I possibly could to assist them—I let them the shop with fixtures and stock—he signed this agreement "Frederick Young," at a rent of 50 l . a year—he gave me 5 l . in part payment and a promissory note for 25 l . signed by the prisoner and Albert Stanley—Stanley came to my place as a reference, and gave his address as 408, Albert Road, Battersea Park Road—he said he knew both the Youngs and their parents, and he thought they were very honest, straightforward young chaps  —he called with one of the Youngs—the quarter's rent became due in March—I got 10 l . the day after by putting in the brokers, from what the police told me—I went and found they had gone and the door left open—they took away some of the fixtures worth 4 l . or 5 l ., and the stock was all gone.
