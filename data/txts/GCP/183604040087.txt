Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How far are the cottages from the
Geld? A. About a mile and a half-the footmarks went about ten yards
before I lost sight of them, then over a bank, and I saw them again, and
they were traced down to the cottage where the prisoner lives. donkey's
and a man's footmarks were traced—I can swear I traced footmarks from the
field to the cottage - I will not swear there were not more than one man's
foot-marks, but I saw no more--I live about a mile and a half from the
field, I know the lane, and know about a camp of gipsies being there,
but it had been removed a week before-they did not go away in the
middle of that night, but a week before I saw the prisoner taken
into custody-he did not attempt to run away-Halton-bridge is about
200 or 300 yards from the station-the mark on the skin was a round
fing and two J's-I have no doubt about its being the skin.
CHARLES EDMONDs. I am a farmer of Arlington. I assisted in trading
the footmarks--they appeared to me to correspond exactly they went
directly to the prisoner's cottage, and there were traces of a donkey's feet
well here are the prisoner's shoes—they tallied and corresponded in
all respects with the footmarks-here are two peculiar nails out of the
usual place I saw the prisoner's wife with a basket - it contained seven
Joints of mutton fresh killed, and not in a butcher-like way I heard bim
deny living in the cottage I do not think I saw any part of the mutton
fitted into the skin-I went into the prisoner's house afterwards, and found
keep's head boiling in the pot-there was no head in the basket--there
was a sack of fresh turnips, beans, and brocoli.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean to say that you yourself took either of
these shoes, and examined them with the marks ? A. The constable did, I
pointed out the marks to him I did not myself fit the shoe, but I saw it
done there are hundreds of such shoes in the country the constable
pressed down one by the side of the footmarks, to see if the nails
would make the same impression---there had been a good deal of rain that
night.
JOSEPH TAPlin. I am a farmer, and live at Drayton. I traced some

prisoner, “Peggy," that is the name he goes by, " Peggy, I thought you
had known better than to do such a thing as this" he said, 4 Oh, I did
