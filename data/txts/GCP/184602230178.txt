807. JOSEPH GIBSON was indicted for bigamy.
MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM DAVISON DAY (police-constable K 74.) I apprebended the pri
soner at Rotherhithe, on the 12th of Feb.--I told him he was charged with
bigamy-he said it would now be settled, and that it was his wish that it should
be so-be asked me on our road to the Court what his imprisonment would
be-I said I could not tell-be said his second wife knew of his first wife
being living at the time, and that she was married again I went to the church
of the Holy Trinity, at Kingston-upon-Hull, on the 19th of Feb.--the mi-
nister of the parish showed me the register, and I examined this copy with
the orginal-it is correct—this is it-(read-certifying that Joseph Gibson
and Mary Walton were married on the 18th of Dec., 1824.)
MARY ANN JORDESON. I am the wife of a person named Jordebon, living
at Hull. I know the prisoner-I was present at his marriage with Mary
Walton in 1824-she was alive last week, when I left Hull-she has never
been from home-I do not think the prisoner and her lived together above
three or four years.
Cross-examined by MR. WILDE. Q. The prisoner is a seafaring man!
A. Yes--they lived together three or four years, and had a child-he was
away at sea a year and ten months when he returned his wife was far gone
in the family way--that was the reason they parted—they divided the furni-
ture and separated-I believe he has been in Hull, but I did not see bim-
I do not know that he has been there for seventeen years-his wife married
again about two years ago.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Was there any difficulty in his finding where bis
first wife was ? A. No.
ELIZA MARY MILES. I am a widow. I married the prisoner on the Ist of
July, eight years ago, at St. Saviour's, Southwark- I was in no business-
was protected by my brothers, who are since dead.
Cross-examined 'Q. The prisoner had been living in the house where you
were residing? 4. Yes, he lodged there-since our marriage my mother and
my son have lived in the house the prisoner has not supported us all-my
son has always been employed- I have some property of my own-I married
the prisoner in 1838, and have lived with him ever since my son was mar
ried in the summer- I gave him a bed and bedstead on his marriage.
COURT. Q. Did that lead to a family dispute ? A. Yes I understood
the prisoner to have been married, and to have been away from home for
years, under the circumstances that have been detailed I expected him to
deal fairly by me.
