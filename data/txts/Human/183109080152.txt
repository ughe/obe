 1905.  JOHN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 shilling and 1 sixpence, the monies of John Cane , from his person.
   JOHN CANE. I am master of a poor-house. I was opposite the Horse Guards, standing on the pavement, at the time of the Coronation; the Policeman came to me, and said, "Do you know you are robbed?" I said No - he said, "Look at your pocket;" I looked - my pocket was turned inside out, and one shilling and one sixpence, which was all I had in it, was gone - the prisoner was close to me; the officer had hold of him.
 Cross-examined by MR. BALL. Q. Was there not a great crowd? A. Very little - it was just as the King was returning; I thought if I was robbed, I would not lose all my money, so I divided it.
   ROBERT ROBERTSON. I am a Policeman. I was on duty about a quarter before four o'clock, as the King was returning - I saw the prisoner busy behind the prosecutor's back; I watched him, and saw the four finger and thumb of his right hand take the 1s. 6d. out of the pocket, which was turned inside out - he put the money into his left hand; I seized his left hand with the money in it - he struggled to get away, and in the struggle tore his coat: I did not lose hold of him - he threw the 1s. 6d. on the pavement; I took it up, and this is it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am positive the officer must be mistaken - I heard some money fall, and he accused me of stealing it.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1906.  SUSANNA WATSON was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of July , 1 coat, value 10s.; 2 pairs of trousers, value 20s., and 2 handkerchiefs, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Shaw.
   THOMAS SHAW. I live at the Lord John Russel, in the City-road. I lost two pairs of trousers from the back kitchen; I had seen them safe about a fortnight before the 13th of July - I know nothing of the prisoner.
   THOMAS WILLIAM BLEWITT. I live with Mr. Matthews, a pawnbroker. I have a coat and waistcoat, pawned by the prisoner, on the 13th of July.
   PATRICK DAILEY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner about a fortnight after the robbery.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she was an unfortunate girl, and the coat was given her to pledge by a man who had been in her company.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1907.  JUSTINA SUMNER WILKINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 3 blankets, value 10s., and 1 flat-iron, value 6d., the goods of John Griffin.
   JOHN GRIFFIN. I live at the George and Dragon, in Leather-lane. The prisoner and her husband had a furnished lodging at my house for eighteen months, and she was entrusted to clean the room and make the beds for the other lodgers. On the 27th of July I missed these articles.
   JOHN PEARCE. I am a pawnbroker, in Baldwin's-gardens. I have three blankets and a flat-iron, pawned by the prisoner, in April, May, and July.
   WILLIAM BENTON. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - she very readily gave me the duplicates from her bosom.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received an excellent character. GUILTY. Aged 21.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor. Fined One Shilling, and Discharged.
 1908.  CHARLES WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 4 shillings, the monies of John Hooper.
   JOHN HOOPER. I am an agent for the sale of gastubes. On the 8th of September I was between the end of the Strand and the Admiralty; I found the prisoner's hand in my trousers pocket - I took hold of the hand, held it fast in my pocket, and called for the Police, but none appeared; Mr. Smart came up, and assisted me in taking his hand out of my pocket, which had the four shillings in it; I had one sovereign and 9s. in my pocket - four shillings came out in his hand, and he dropped them on the ground.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.Nothing was taken out of your pocket by the prisoner? A.His hand was in my pocket, and when we pulled it out the four shillings came out in it.
   ADAM SMART. I heard Mr. Hooper call for the Police- there was a very great crowd - I went up, and saw the prisoner's hand in his pocket; I took him by the collar, and said, "Let go your hold;" he would not, and I pulled his hand out of the pocket, and four shillings fell on the ground.
 Cross-examined. Q. Then you made him take the money, whether he would or not? A. I made him take his hand out; I would have forgiven him, but a soldier took him to the station. GUILTY. Aged 58.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Transported for Seven Years.
