495. WILLIAM HARPER (26), Feloniously killing and slaying Thomas
Faye.
MR. SHARPE conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE STOWER. I am a cab driver of 4, Parliament-street, St. George's
Market, Southwark-I knew the deceased Thomas Faye, a coal-porter, he was
about forty years of age-on Friday, 16th March, I saw him at the Elephant
and Castle public-house, near Vauxhall Railway station, about half past 9
or twenty minutes to 10 o'clock—the prisoner was also there—they both
appeared as if they had been drinking, they were rather the worse for
liquor—I heard them quarrelling, but did not know what it was about one
of them proposed to go out and fight; I cannot say which, but they were
both willing, and I tried to prevent them-I held the deceased and pre-
vented him from going out : after a little struggle I said, “Well; if you will
go, you must go and take your chance "-they both left and went under the
arches, somewhere : into the grounds of the station. I remained some few
minutes, and then went towards the arches, where my cab was standing for
hire when the trains came in I heard a noise like a fall, and went and saw
Thomas Faye fall, just as I got to the spot-he laid on his back very quiet
-I did not lift him up--he did not speak or make any movement-the
prisoner was about three yards in front of his feet-he was lifted up-I did
not see him taken away-I went to the Elephant and Castle, remained
there perhaps two minutes, and then went away to my cab.
Cross-examined by Mr. DICKIE. Q. Had both the men had a good deal of
drink! 4. Yes ; but they could walk-Faye was a very stout healthy
man--I have heard that he was in the habit of sleeping in the gas factory-
he was very dirty in his habits I have never seen the waiters at the tap-
room carry water for him to wash himself-he was often at the publio-
house.
DENNIS HARRINGTON. I am a labourer of James'-place, Fore-street,
Lambeth-I was at the Elephant and Castle on Friday, 16th March, and
heard the prisoner and deceased quarrelling about work-I could not under-
stand exactly what was said, but after some time they agreed to go out and
fight-I do not know which proposed it, one was as'willing as the other-they
were both under the influence of liquor—they left the Elephant and Castle
together to fight-I remained behind some little time I then went to see
what was taking place (The witness was cautioned that he need not say any-
thing tending to criminate himself, I do not want to say anything—I will
speak the truth I took no part whatever in encouraging them to fight-I
saw them sparring together, and after that I saw the prisoner lay hold of
Faye by the legs aud chuck him on his head-he fell on the back of his head,
as far as I could see I said to the prisoner that it was a shame, and he
struck me I struck him back again I did not see Faye afterwards till
I saw him on Monday night-he was helped up and led away-I cannot
