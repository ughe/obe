SIR FITZROY KELLY. Q. Do you know any business that Richard Brewer
could have had within these gates after business hours, in the way of his
duty. A. I don't know that he had any—if he wanted to go to the water-
closet, or the blacksmith's shop, he was obliged to go this way if he wanted
to go to the north door, he must pass through the boiler-house, and through
these gates-George Brown's duty kept him there as late as 5 or 6 on
Saturday.
MR. SERJBANT LALLANTINE. Q. From the direction in which Brewer
seemed to be going when you saw him pass, did be appear to be going
towards the water closet, or the blacksmith's shop, or towards the north
door A. Sometimes towards the blacksmith's shop, sometimes towards
the north door, and sometimes towards the water-closet (referring to the
model) - this is the north door.
SER FITZROY KELLY. O Did he always go in the same direction! 4.
After be passed the window I could not see which way he went_if he wished
to go in at the north door, I should not have been able to see that he went
there if he went towards the water-closet, he could cross over and go in at
the north door.
Court., Q. Could you see enough of the direction in which he was
going at the time he passed you to know whether he was tending to the
right or left . Yes; to the best of my knowledge he was going towards
the water-closet; that is to the left-my impression was that he was always
going to the left, to the water-closet.
MR. SERJRANT BALLANTINE to MR. SHENTON DUBATOY, Q. Did you
know, as a matter of fact, that Brewer was in the habit of going to the
grindstone after hours ? A. I did.
Str FitzROY KELLY. Q. Where is that grindstone A Down in the
corner, against the carpenter's shop-in order to get there he would have to
go in at the first gate, and through the second gate whether he would have
Decasion to go there after the carpenter had left, would depend upon his own
work at home I suppose he had George Brown's key then be had no key
of his own he would have to borrow Brown's key, or get in by some other
means--if it was after half-past 5, and George Brown had done work, the
gate, and the little door in the gate, would be both locked.
COURT. 6. You say you know he was there; how do you know it 4.
Because he had asked liberty of me to go down to grind tools I should
my that was in the early part of 1862, when he had got work to do at
home--he appeared to be turning tools for the purpose of turning metal.
FRANORS MURRELL I am the wife of George Murrell, a finisher, at the
mill-I know Richard Brewer in the spring and summer of 1862, I have
Been him go through the gates I can't say exactly the time it might be
from 6 to 8, or from 7 to 8, and at different times I have not noticed the
time he was there-it might be a quarter of an hour I might have soen
him there as late as half-past 8, but I did not observe at all, not thinking
anything about it I have not seen him often; I can't say how often-it
might be half-a-dozen times, or a dozen; I don't know.
