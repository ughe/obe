Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 921.  JOHN EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of February , 1 pair of shoes, value 5s., the goods of Galen Bamfield.
   CHRISTOPHER BAMFIELD. I am the son of Galen Bamfield- he is a boot and shoe-maker, and lives in the Strand. On the 23rd of February, about five o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came in, and asked for a pair of boots I went outside the window to get him a boot; I saw him take these shoes, and put them into his pocket - I came in, and he said the boots I showed him were too light, he wanted strong ones; he went out, and I called Stop thief! he was taken without my losing sight of him, and the shoes found in his coat pocket.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the shoes found on him, he had just taken out of pledge.
 C. BAMFIELD. It is not true - I saw him take them off the shop-board.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Confined Three Months.
 922.  JOSEPH DANIELS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of March , 1 apron, value 1s. 3d., the goods of Peter Puttock.
   GEORGE BRODIE. I live in Chapel-street, just opposite Mr. Puttock's. On the 22nd of March, about five o'clock, I saw the prisoner looking in at the window - I saw him snatch an apron off the eggs in the shop, and run away; I saw him running, but could not catch him - I had seen him several times before, and knew him perfectly well; I saw him in custody the next day.
   CHARLES CLARK. I apprehended the prisoner in a yard, among a parcel of boys - I knew him well by the description given of him; when he saw me he ran into a house, and tried to hide himself - I took him, and he said,"What do you want?" I said, "You will soon know," and as we went along he said, "I know nothing about the apron;" I had not mentioned an apron.
 PETER PUTTOCK. I was at home, and missed the apron, but did not see it taken.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
 923.  SARAH DRISCOLL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of March , 3 drinking-glasses, value 1s. 6d., the goods of John Gurney.
 JOHN GURNEY. I  keep  the Rum Puncheon, in White-cross-street. On the 31st of March, about six o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came in, and had half a pint of porter in a glass - she stopped about an hour and a half, then went out, and we missed three glasses; I followed her to the Asylum for the Houseless, in Playhouse-yard, and found three glasses on her, which are mine - one has my name on it.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. - Transported for Seven Years.
 924.  ELIZABETH CRESWICK was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of March , 1 coat, value 10s.; 1 pair of breeches, value 2s.; 3 shirts, value 6s.; 1 pair of stockings, value 1s. 6d.; 2 waistcoats, value 2s., and 1 umbrella, value 1s., the goods of James Goodhall.
 JAMES GOODHALL. I live in  Wilson-street, Somers'-town. On the 22nd of March the prisoner was cleaning out a room at my house, and at night, when she was gone, I missed a sheet off the bed, a coat out of a box, and the breeches off the table; she was my char-woman - I have known her two years: I never allowed her to pawn my property.
   JAMES LIMBIRD. I apprehended the prisoner - she gave me the duplicates of the property.
   GEORGE WADE. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Charlton-street, Somers'-town. I have all the articles stated in the indictment, which were pawned by the prisoner, at different times.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 42. - Confined Three Months.
 925.  CHARLES BRINKLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of March , 1 jewel-case, value 40s., and 1 flute, value 10s., the goods of Samuel Starkey.
 SAMUEL STARKEY. I live in  Queen-square, Aldersgate-street. On the 24th of March, between three and four o'clock, a person whom I employ out of doors, sent his son with some work; he rang the bell - the boy answered it; he said he would wait five minutes for an answer - during that time somebody entered the parlour, and took this case and flute; my boy went down, found the parlour door open, and gave an alarm - I went out, and caught the prisoner within five minutes, in Peter's-lane, St. John-street, with the jewel-case under his arm, covered with a handkerchief; I found my flute in his trousers pocket - he was quite a stranger.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN ABRAHALL. I am the prosecutor's errand boy - the bell rang; I came down to a boy, and left the door open, but I did not see the prisoner - I knew the flute was in the parlour.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming across Smithfield - a gentleman came up, and asked me to carry these things to the Angel, at Islington - I thought he was behind me at the time.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Transported for Seven Years.
