129. PATRIOK WILLIAMS aud CHARLES MCARTHY, feloni-
ously placing certain bricks upon the North London Railway, with intent
to obstruct certain engines using that railway.
Mr. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
ARTHUR LONG. I am a watchman, in the employ of the North London
Railway. On Monday, 8th Dec., a communication was made to me between
12 and 1 Oelockin consequence of that I went to a bridge that crosses the
railway, and saw the prisoners there, and five more boys-] watched them for
some time; saw Williams place 2 bricks, and MCarthy three bricks, on two
of the rails, one of the up rails, and one of the down rails, in a place where
there is a very sharp curve in the line-this brick (produced) is what is
called a clinkerI went down to the tail and took the prisoners into custody
-M'Carthy said, 44 It was not me it was the other boy, he put more on
than I dhuWilliams said, No, it was you; you know you put nine on,
and I only put seven"what I saw them put on was five pieces train
had passed by shortly beforetrains run along that line sometimes every
quarter of an hour: some trains more frequently- gave the prisoners into
custody.
JAMEs SMTHERS. E am a guard in the employ of the North London
Railway Company. On that Monday I was acting as guard to a train-I
passed that spot about 12-33-we struek against a lot of brickbats on the
line-we threw them about in different directionsthere is a life guard in
front of the engine, which struck the bricks-they flew into pieces--I do
not know whether they were bricks of this kind-I did not see them when
they were on the line-they were broken in all direetions, some pieces flew
into the train, and some into the engine--we had passengers in the train-
it was between Highbury and Kingsland, at a very sharp curve of the line
-that was a very dangerous place to put them.
DANIEL STOREs (policeman, N 133). The two prisoners were given into
my custody-William charged M'Carthy with having laid nine on the line,
and said he had only laid two on himself-M'arthy denied it.
COURT to ARTHUR LoNg. Q. You saw several boys there; what size
were they A4. About the size of the prisoners; there were no large boys
-I asked the ages of the prisoners; one said he was between twelve and
