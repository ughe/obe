Mr. Justice Lawrence. How do you explain that when the affidavit
was sworn neither you nor your clerk was present?
Witness. He gave instructions, and asked to have the affidavit,
and said he would get it sworn. I do not think it is unusual for
solicitor to let his olient take a document to swear before & Commis-
sioner without either himself or clerk attending with him. I did not
know where he lived; his place of residence was, left open. I do not
think it is unusual for a solicitor to act for a gentleman in an action
whom he has never seen at all. If the manager comes I do not
always see the proprietor of the business.
Mr. Justice Lawrence. If that is the usual practice I advise you
not to follow it in the future. If you do you will certainly get your
self into trouble.
Further examined. I filed the affidavit (produced in the setion
of Lever v. the "Financial Guardian." My client in that onse was
George Harris. I understood that George Martin was at that time
proprietor of the "Financial Guardian." I never saw George Martin
for the purposes of business. The instructions for that affidavit came
from George Harris. The affidavit purports to be sworn by George
Martin. I have not had the opportunity of comparing the signature
to see whether "George Martin" is in the same handwriting as that
of "H. K. Ablett" in the other affidavits. There is a resemblance
certainly, though no two letters are alike, but I cannot say they were
written by the same person. I have known Perryman about 10 years,
and at the time of these actions was conducting business for him. I
did not introduce Harris to the Middlesex Bank when he opened an
account in the name of George Martin. I gave the name of George
Martin as the name of a client. I had not seen George Martin, but
I had seen his representative. I did not know at the time that
George Martin was George Harris.
