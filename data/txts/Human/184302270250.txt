There were more than twenty—I could not say whether they had anything or no—persons might go in to light their pipes only. SARAH BASS. I am housekeeper to the prosecutor. On the Monday night, I cleared the money from the till after the business of the day was closed—I then put some money in to begin business on the following morn ing—I put into the packet or paper 1 l . worth of silver, which had been all marked by my master—I put in 5 s . in shillings and sixpences loose, and 5 s . in copper—the business begins about five in the morning, and the prisoner has the management of the bar from that time till breakfast—at half-past nine on the Tuesday morning I went to the till—I found a half-sovereign in the gold box which had not been there the night before, and found 10 s . in the paper where 1 l . of silver had been the night before—the half-sovereign was separate from the silver—the gold box is kept locked—it is in the same drawer, but in a separate division—there was very little increase above the 30 s ., that I had left in the till—there was not above 32 s . or 33 s .
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you take the money out and count it? A.  I did not take it in my hand, but I could see what there was—I had not my spectacles—I could see that without them—I swear that at half-past nine o'clock, there was not 5 s . in addition to the money I had left—there might have been 4 s .
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Did you examine the till to look? A.  Yes—the till is large, you can see what is in it without taking it out—I am quite sure it did not exoeed what I have stated—about 8 s . or 9 s . are usually taken at the counter till half-past nine—I was not there that morning till half-past nine.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Do you remember the Monday or Tuesday morning previous? A.  No, I do not remember any particular morning—some morn ings there were 9 s . or 10 s .—Mondays and Tuesdays there is not quite so good as others. NOT GUILTY.
