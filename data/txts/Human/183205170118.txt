 ROBERT BOONE. I am warehouseman at Bird's auction-room. I received the goods from these witnesses - I kept them till the day of condemnation; I was present when they were condemned by Mr. Wilmot, a broker - I saw an inventory in their hands; there were several people present, but I cannot say who - it took place in the second room; I looked out the lots, and Mr. Wilmot went round and put his price upon them; they then said they would go and look for Mr. Parker - I think this was on a Monday or Tuesday early in April; I saw Parker at the room the next day, with the prisoner.
 COURT. Q.Upon your oath, on whose account were the goods sold? A. That I cannot tell - I attended the sale, but did not look at the book; I do not know who had the money - I swear I never heard from the prisoner; there was a constable, but he did not come into the room - Mr. Wilmot's goods have been sold, and I believe he is gone to America.
 Prisoner. I had no other appraiser but Mr. Wilmot - we went to some constable in Mile-end; I do not know his name.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1380.  WILLIAM BUTTERWORTH was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Henry Taylor , from his person.
 MARGARET TAYLOR. I am the wife of  Henry Taylor, a cheesemonger, who lives in Union-street, Bishopsgate. I was returning home with him on the 13th of May - I felt something; I turned, and saw the prisoner, who had nearly got my husband's handkerchief out of his pocket; I seized him - he threw it behind him; I took it up, and gave him in charge.
 EDWARD BILSON (Police-constable H 102). I took the prisoner, and have the handkerchief.
 Prisoner. I was pushed up against the lady by two boys - they took the handkerchief, and threw it down.
 The prisoner received a good character from his master, who engaged to employ him.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Whipped and Discharged.
 1381.  JAVIS COULTON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of May , 1 sack, value 4s., and 3 bushels of bones, value 4s., the goods of Joseph Goodwin.
   MOSES BELLINGHAM. I have known the prisoner some time - he worked in a dust-yard next door to where I live. On a Saturday in May I saw him go into the yard - he said to me, "Are they all gone;" he went in, and I did not see him again; the other witness is not here.

 1383.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of May , 1 brass gas bracket, value 10s., the goods of Charles John Hunt , and fixed to a certain building of his; against the Statute.
 WILLIAM RICE. I was a Police-constable. On the afternoon of the 6th of May I saw the prisoner and two others, under the Quadrant, in Regent-street; I suspected them, and told the officer - we watched them for about twenty minutes, and then saw the prisoner coming out of a house with a brass bracket, which he dropped at the door - I secured him.
 JOHN BROWN. I am servant to  Charles John Hunt, who keeps a billiard-room, in the Quadrant. I was on the staircase at one o'clock on the 5th of May - I heard a noise, and saw the prisoner in the passage; he opened the door, and ran out; I saw this bracket at the door, on the ground - Rice brought him back.
 JAMES KENNERLEY (Police-constable C 30). I was in Regent-street, and watched the prisoner and two others; I saw the prisoner enter the house, and I saw him brought back by Rice - I found this nut of the brass pipe in his pocket; he seemed in great distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined One Month.
 1384.  JAMES KENT was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of April , 1 coat, value 10s.; 1 waistcoat, value 2s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 10s., and 1 sheet, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Kent.
   THOMAS KENT. I live in Satchwell's-rents, Bethnal-green- the prisoner is my son. On Easter Monday I went to work early in the morning; the prisoner came to me afterwards; I gave him leave to go home, and do some work - he took the key of my apartments; I returned a little after eleven o'clock in the forenoon; I found the key in the door - I went in, and missed a suit of clothes, which had been in a box in my room; I saw him again on the Thursday afterwards; I got an officer, and he was taken - I found a duplicate on the mantelpiece in the room.
   ELIZABETH AGAR. I am the wife of Thomas Agar - we live in Cottage-court; I can see the steps of the prosecutor's door from our house. On Easter Monday I was standing on the steps of our door, and saw the prisoner come down the steps of his father's door with a bundle; he turned to the right, towards Church-street.
 PHILIP COOK. I was in Church-street, Bethnal-green, on Easter Monday. I met the prisoner (who lives within three doors of me) going with a bundle towards Mr. Cassles, the pawnbroker.
   HENRY HUDDY(Police-constable H 72). I took the prisoner on his father's premises.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Seven Years.
