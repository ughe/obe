Cross-examined by Mr. HEATON. Q. How long have you known the
prisoner? A. Since the 3rd of Sept.-I have paid my rent twice to him-I
did not see Mr. Judd daily-sometimes he was not there for two or three
days together-sometimes not from one week to another-he has another
shop at some distance-I have lived in the house with my husband since the
6th of May-We were on friendly terms with the prisoner-I did not ask
him to get me this stand at the cost price-my son-in-law sent me to buy a
double washhand-stand-I do not know that the prisoner ordered it of the
maker for me, nor that 4s. is the maker's price for it, and the ordinary charge
68.-the prisoner did not say he had got this from one of the persons who
made for them, on purpose for me when I asked him he said he had not got
one at that price in the house-he did not say he would get one--it might be a
week or a fortnight before I got it I did not tell him that 43. was as much
as I could afford-I do not recollect that he said he would get me one-it
was not for myself, and of course I took no more trouble about it-I did not
ask him to get one made for me-he never said he would get the person who
made for Mr. Judd to make one for me, or anything of the kind there was no
other person present when I paid for it I did not see the stand in the shop,
nor see it brought up to my room-it was painted, and looked quite new and
clean-I do not know whether it looked as if it had come from the maket-1
know that a stand would acquire a darker hue by remaining in the shop-it
did not look as if it had been in a broker's shop.
SOPHIA MARIA UNDERWOOD. I live in Fore-street, Cripplegate. On the
5th of Dec. I bought at Mr. Judd's shop, twelve yards of carpeting at 28, 2d.
a yard, and a hearth-rug for 12s. 6d. --my daughter was with me to the
best of my belief the prisoner is the man I paid he gave me this card (pro-
ducing it) as a bill-the things came to 11. 18s. 6d.
Cross-examined. Q. Was not Mr. Juda present? A. The prisoner
showed us a hearth-rug, which was 128. 60.--we said we would rather have a
darker, and he brought us one which he said was 148. 60.-I said I would
give him 12s. 6d. for that-Mr. Judd came into the shop, and the prisoner
showed him the mark on the back, and he then left the shop-I said, "If
they won't take 12s. 6d. for it, I won't have it," and the prisoner rolled it
up, and we had it-after the 148. 6d. rug had been shown to us, the prisoner
and Mr. Judd spoke together we had agreed for the carpet before I did
not hear the prisoner ask Mr. Judd whether he would take 128. 6d., nor tell
Mr. Judd the quantity of carpet we had bought-it was then rolled up-it
was a Kidderminster carpet- I did not hear the prisoner say there were twelve
yards of it at 23. 2d. a yard, or ask whether he might let the rug go at
128. 6.-he rolled it up, and then Mr. Judd left the shop.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Had you been long in the shop? A. No, I was only
there about a quarter of an hour-Mr. Judd came in at a side door.
Eliza ANN UNDERWOOD. I went with my mother to Mr. Judd's shop, on
the 5th of Dec.--I saw her pay for the carpet and rug-I think the prisoner
is the man she paid 11. 188. 6d. to-Mr. Judd came in while the bargain
was going on-he staid a very short time he left us together.
