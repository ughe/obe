JOSEPH Cook. I saw the prisoner with a pair of shoes on Sunday,

he had them on the last witness is my sister.
MR. PAYNE. Q. What are you? 4. A shoemaker-I was not before
the Magistrate-I was first applied to to speak about this matter the week
before last, by Mr. Powell - I saw the prisoner on this Sunday standing
outside Mr. Bolter's, the Waterloo Arms-be put up his feet to show
Powell that he wanted his shoes mended-Powell said if he would let him
have them on Monday he would do them—this was about half-past
four o'clock in the afternoon- I had called to see my sister and brother-in-
law, and we went out for a walk in the afternoon, and there saw the pri-
soner-I have known him about six months-I knew he had had a pair
mended the week before I was at Powell's, and saw them before and
after they were mended - they were only tipped and heeled, and he had
worn the soles out and cut the front against a stone-they were the same
shoes- I live in Boss-street, James-street, Horsleydowa-I work on my
own account-I used to work for a shop in the City-I have not had
much work lately-I am very seldom with the prisoner-I saw him with
these shoes on twice-Powell had shown me what he had to do to the
shoes, and when I saw the prisoner with them on, after they had been
mended he showed me what had been done to them, and said he had made
a good job of them.
MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you ever see him wear a cap at all like
that? A. No-he wore a blue cap.
