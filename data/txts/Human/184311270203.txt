Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 282. JOHN WESTWOOD and EDWARD ELLIS were indicted for stealing 2 saddles, value 2 l  . 10 s  .; 1 pair of reins, 4 s  .; 2 pair of trace, 1 l  .; 1 pair of harness, 10 s  .; and 1 breeching, 10 s  .; the goods of Joseph Cheek their master. JOSEPH CHEEK, bricklayer, Commercial-road, Lambeth.  I rent No. 11 arch of Waterloo-bridge, in which I keep horses and harness—the prisoners were in my employ—on Friday, the 10th of Nov., Westwood was employed at the arch all day to clean the harness—he had no business to remove any without orders—he left me on the 13th—I missed the things stated on the 12th—I saw the prisoners again in custody on the 18th, charged with taking the harness—they denied it—I have never seen it since.
   Cross-examined by MR. NEALE . Q.  Have you always given the same account of this? A.  Yes; I swear I have never said that the prisoners left without giving me notice—I asked them on the Saturday when I paid them if they were satisfied—they said, "Yes"—I did not tell them I did not want them any more—I may have said that I missed the things on the 13th—I missed them on the 12th and 13th too, and on the 13th I sent to make inquiries about it—they were taken on the Friday—the arch is locked every night at eight o'clock, and another gate outside is locked—there is a regular watchman on the premises—I leave about eight o'clock—there is only one entrance—the other end is bricked up—I had six or seven men at that time—I have all their names in my book—they had been working with me about a month—the prisoners, I believe, worked the shortest—eight o'clock is the time for closing the vault—I was there about five o'clock on Friday evening, and told the prisoners to do them up—I came back and found Jeffreys and two others at work, but the prisoners were gone—I remained there about half an hour, seeing the work done—the harness that was taken hung up on the right hand as you go in—I keep horses and carriages there as well—when I left it at half-past six o'clock I left the door open, and left the men that were there and my son—he always stops to close the door—beyond the door are high outer gates, which are kept locked—a man named Griffin was in my employ, and worked for me a week after the prisoners left—he was at work on Friday the 10th—he comes at half-past seven o'dock, and stops till eight o'clock—I or my son are there to receive him, and take away the key—I always look round the place, lock it up, and take the key—it is my son's duty to look round as well as mine, but I do not look round on a Monday—most likely my son took the key home on the Friday—I saw it next morning at seven o'clock hanging on a nail by the fire-place in my front parlour—no one could get at it—I give it to the first that comes in the morning—I pay my men at my house every Saturday night—they do not come into the room where the key hangs, and I do not think they knew where it was kept—they were paid at the door—I never told Griffin I would give him two guineas if he would reveal the real thieves, nor was any sum mentioned by me or my wife in my presence—I never asked him to try and find the thieves—I dis charged him last Saturday week, as he said he was going to drive for another person—I did not see him last Friday—I did not say any thing to Mr. Wise about the real thieves—I have not treated her—we had our dinner together—a man named Brown, who has worked for me on
