   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Who was the first that spoke to the prisoner? A.  Mr. Bone—he was speaking to me—I then called the prisoner in, and Mr. Bone said to him he had lost some catalogues, and he mentioned the number—I heard him say it would be better for him to confess. GEORGE PALMER. I am a chandler, and live in Bedfordbury. The prisoner has been to my shop during the last three or four months—he has brought paper similar to this now produced—the paper I received from the prisoner I giye up to Mr. Bone and the officer—I cannot say how often I have pur chased paper of the prisoner—I paid him 2 d . a pound for the greater part; but he brought so much that I told him I was almost stocked, and for the latter put of it 1 only gave him 1 1/2 d .—it did not occur to me that these were cata logues, and might be of value to the party—I considered them as waste paper, which I purchase a great quantity of—I did not know the prisoner, but he told me he was a waste-paper dealer—I had about lcwt.—they were not perfect catalogues, not the whole book—they were just as they are here apparently—I pointed out to him, on one occasion, that they seemed to have a great deal of these catalogues—he said, "We are regular waste-paper dealers; we bought as much as 5 cwt. yesterday"—I bought some music-paper of him—the largest quantity he brought at one time was from 17lbs. to 19lbs.—from the begin ning of this year to the latter end of April he used to come sometimes once or twice a week, sometimes once a fortnight.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You buy a good deal of paper of this sort? A.  I do—it is the custom for persons who stitch to have the outside sheets. JAMES SOUTHWELL. I am a grocer, and live in Moor-street, Soho. I bought 71bs. weight of paper of the prisoner twice—I gave him 1 1/2 d . a pound for it—I gave 91bs. of it up to the policeman. LUKE MARROW re-examined.  These came to me in 5000 sheets in one bundle—I can identify these as being the sort of catalogues that I was em ployed to fold for Mr. Bone—these are folded, but not stitched.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You know that Mr. Norman is the printer, by his name being at the bottom of these catalogues? A.  Yes; be is an extensive printer, and Mr. Bone is an extensive publisher—I always bind for Mr. Norman and Mr. Bone—they never print on outside paper—very often the first im pression is not correct—there are very often spoiled sheets—they send me waste sheets to keep the others clean—I collect that waste at times, and sell it—I have sold some of it, but not any quantity—I do not recollect that I have employed the prisoner to sell any paper for me—he has been to Mr. Bone's and Mr. Norman's for me.
