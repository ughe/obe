 735.  ANN BRODERICK was indicted for uttering a counterfeit sixpence to one Sarah Palmer, spinster, on the 10th of September, and in a second Count, within ten days after, to wit, on the 17th, for uttering another sixpence to the said Sarah, knowing them to be counterfeit.(The case was opened by Mr. Knowlys.)
   SARAH PALMER sworn. - Examined by Mr. Knowlys. I keep a chandler's shop, in Little Bell-alley; I think it was on the 10th of this month, but I cannot be certain, the prisoner came to my shop; the second time she came was the 17th, it was on a Wednesday, both times; she first asked for some bacon, as much as came to five-pence, and then some butter, which came to two-pence halfpenny, making together seven-pence halfpenny; she gave me six-pence to pay for the bacon, and I gave her a penny out of it; she then gave me another six-pence to pay for the butter, I gave her change, and she left the shop; in less than five minutes, she came again, and said, I think I am like a child, I have forgot half my message, I want a small quantity of tea and sugar, she had half an ounce of six shilling green tea, and one pennyworth of moist sugar, and she gave me another six-pence, for which I gave her change; her paying me three sixpences, rather raised my suspicion, I looked at the sixpence, and said, before the woman's face, that I did not think it was a good one; my sister was in the shop, I gave it to her, she differed from me in opinion, and I took it; after she was gone, I went to the door and found it to be a very bad one, the the shop is very dark; I then took the other two to the door, and found them both had likewise; I then put them aside from the rest of my money, and I saw no more of her till the next Wednesday, about half past time nine in the morning, she asked for a threepenny loaf, and half an ounce of tea, which came to five-pence farthing; she then offered me another six-pence, which I took, and sent for Mr. Powell, the constable, and she was taken into custody; that sixpence was also a bad one.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Alley. Q. You take a great many sixpences in the course of a day? - A. No, not many, it is a small shop.
 Q. Had you known the prisoner before? - A. No.
 Q. There were a great many bad shillings and sixpences found upon her, I suppose? - A. No, neither.
 HENRY POWELL sworn. - Examined by Mr. Knowlys. I am a constable; on the 17th of September, I was sent for to take charge of the prisoner, she behaved very well, she desired ever having been in the shop before, (produces the sixpences) I received them from Mrs. Palmer.
 Mr.  William Parker proved the money to be counterfeit, and Mr. Alley addressed the Jury in behalf of the defendant.
     GUILTY on the Second Count. Confined six months in Newgate, and find sureties for six months more.
 Tried by the London Jury, before Mr. RECORDER.
