   Q.  When they saw each other, did they ask each other how they did, or what was said? A.  No, it appeared they had been together before—Wright might have said, "Halloo"—I do not know that he did—I cannot recol lect any thing that was said—it might have been half-past eight or nine o'clock—I should say it was half-past eight o'clock, as near as I can recol lect, because I was out with my porter—I go out with it at eight o'clock, and I was just going home—I might have some porter left—I generally have—I cannot say whether I had or not.
   Q.  Did you toss for any porter? A.  We might have tossed, I do not recollect tossing—we might have been a pint each, or stood  a pint, either one of us—I have no recollection whether we tossed, or whether we were a pint each, or whether one of us was a pint—I cannot say we did not toss—we might have tossed, and I forget it—I immediately went in, and was waiting on the customers after that—I came out again that night—I might come out with beer after that—that would be at nine o'clock, if it was half-past eight before—if it was after nine o'clock I did not come out—I do not sleep in the house—I sleep with my wife, in St. George's-court, Dover-road—it might be twelve o'clock when I went home—I cannot recollect when I went home that night—I cannot recollect who I saw when I was going home.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You were taking out beer for your mistress—what is the method? do you account for the number of pots you take out? A.  I have to pay for all the porter I take out every day—I never return any—I pay for all every night—the person who came to me this morning did not produce any thing, but had a cab  for me to ride over—I live 200 or 300 yards from the public-house I work at—I cannot swear whether I left that night before or after the house was closed. CHARLES ALLEN. I live at Wilson's Cottage, Pocock's-fields: I am a boot and shoemaker—I have seen Mr. Templeman's house since his death, but not before—I know it now—I should say it is between 300 and 500 yards from where I live—I have known the prisoner, as near as I can tell, about twelve months—he has lodged at my house several times—I cannot possibly state when he commenced lodging with me the last time—he lodged with me at least seven nights before this occurred—I know he had very little money, if any, before the 16th of March; and I was confident, from circumstances which occurred, that he had no money—when he has come home of an evening he has said he had no money; and I considered by his getting up of a morning, and not having breakfast before he went out, that he had none, for few people like to go out without food, if they have money to purchase it—on the Saturday night before this occurred he had none, because he made an observation when he came into the house that he had no money, but he should have some in the course of to-morrow, which was Sunday—on the Monday morning following that Saturday, I think he went out between eight and nine o'clock—he had no breakfast with us before he went out that morning—I believe he was dressed some thing similar to what he is now, but I cannot positively swear whether he had that coat or a jacket on—as far as I can tell, I believe he was dressed much as he is now—he had on a pair of shoes, which I made—I did not make them for him—they were misfits, but I sold them to him—they were ancle-shoes, similar to what I have on now, shoes the lea ther of which comes above the ancle—they had nails—I saw Gould
