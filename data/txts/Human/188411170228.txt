MATTHEW CHARLES BENNETT. I am a labourer of 111, Livingstone Road, near Clapham Junction, Battersea—on 24th October, about 6 o'clock, I was at 19, Britannia Place, where I borrowed 4 d . of the prisoner to pay my fare to Lambeth—I am Mrs. Bennett's son. JOHN BUDD. I am the prisoner's son—on 24th October I came home with my money at 5 o'clock from Price's candle factory, where I work—the prisoner was at home then, and I saw her there nearly all the evening till I went out at 5.55 or 5.50. ANNIE CHAPPLE. My husband is an engineer—we live at 7, Luscombe Street, Wandsworth Road—on Wednesday, 22nd October, I was at my sister's, the prisoner's, house, from 11 o'clock till my husband came home to tea about 5.30 or 6—I go by the tram to my sister's; it is a twopenny fare—it might have been a little before 11 when I went; I generally go about that time.
   Cross-examined.  I go there once or twice a week—I wash and take my little girl to school, and then go—I left home about 9.30, and called on a friend, and got to her about 11—I did not notice the time I got there—I do a little needlework for my sister—she was at home, and made me a cup of tea—she very rarely comes down to me—she has a large family of children—I have lived in the Wandsworth Road for two years, near this butcher's—I have known Langley eight or 10 years—I was in the same shop as he was.
   Witness recalled in Reply:  — ALFRED VAUGHAN. On 24th October the prisoner came to my stall about 5.30—I fix the time because the men were just coming out of the works opposite my stall—on 25th October I saw her in Luscombe Street, and communicated with the constable—I had never seen the prisoner before—I don't know Mrs. Chapple.
   The prisoner in her defence contended that it was a case of mistaken identity and that she knew nothing about it. NOT GUILTY.
