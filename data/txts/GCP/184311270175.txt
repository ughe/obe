John Newman. I am apprentice to the prosecutor. On the 21st of March
last, by direction of my employer, I called on the prisoner, and said I had
come from Mr. Dunkin for the amount of his bill-I produced a bill from my
pocket, which I showed him-his words were to this effect, “I cannot settle
it now, but I will when I pay my Christmas bills"-he said nothing about a
receipt, or having paid it.

MR. LONGER examined by Mr. CLARKSON. I am a Commissioner of the
Court of Requests. When this case was decided, I applied to Mr. Parket,
our legal adviser, as Mr. Dunkin wanted to impound the receipt, whether we
had authority to do so, and after taking advice, I said I did not think I could
-I insisted on its being given up to the defendant--one Commissioner
said if he was an honest man, he would leave it in the hands of the chairman,
and then he handed it to me-I requested the clerk to seal it upon the 6th
of Oct. it was said that the debt was debarred by the statute of limitation
the prisoner said he did not wish to take the benefit of that, and would pro
duce the receipt--we examined the prosecutor's books very minutely before we
gave our decision-I believe nine Commissioners were present when the case
was decided.

WILLIAM SIMPSON. I am clerk to George Eagles Marsden, and two others,
brewers, at Lewisham. On the 2nd of Oct., between nine and ten o'clock at
night, I left the Horse and Groom, Blackheath-hill, somewhat the worse for
liquor-I had two 501. notes, six or seven sovereigns, and some silver, all in
my left hand waistcoat pocket, in a bag I got home about ten- I went down
Blackheath-hill, and up Lewisham-lane-when I got home. I missed my
money-I was accosted on my way by a female-I cannot exactly recollect
what took place I was not accosted by more than one female, to my know-
ledge-I had taken the money on my masters' account.
