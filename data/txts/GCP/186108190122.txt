715. FREDERICK BOSTON (24, a soldier), stealing 1 watch, value
51., the property of George Barrell, from his dwelling-house.
MR. BEST conducted the Prosecutim.
GEORGE BARRELL. I am a watch-maker, of 23, Thomas-street. Woolwich
-I remember the prisoner coming into my shop on 29th July, and
inquiring for a ring he had left to be repaired-he said a person of the native
of Mary Ann Peel was to call for it, and I was to give it to her he asked me
about a watch, as he wished to buy one and pay for it by instalments-
took one out of the window and that did not suit him-I then took another
out of the drawer, and said, I would be ready for him as soon as he w
ready for me-he then said, “There is one in the window-I am expecting
& remittance from my friends to-morrow"-I took the watch out of the
window and was in the act of opening it when he gave a whistle, snatched
the watch out of my hand, and rushed out of the shop-I am sure he w
the man, because of leaving the ring to be repaired- I did not see the watch
again-I next saw the prisoner on Wednesday, when he was in custody-I
inentioned this to the police, and described the person.
JOHN SERGEANT. I am a police-constable of the R division, stationed &
Woolwich--from information I received, I took the prisoner, he was then in
military custody for absence without leave, for nine days—that was two day
after the robbery-I asked him if his name was Boston-he said, “Thats
my name here"-I then told him he mnst consider himself in my custody for
having stolen & watch from the shop of Mr. Barrell on the 29th-he said, "l
is all right, you will find more to-morrow"-he stated that the detective
was after him, and he thought he might as well give himself up as he w
put back for court martial.
Prisoner's Defence. I was never in that shop after the 22d.
The sergeant stated that he had been four years in the service, and
character was bad.
GUILTY.-Confined Twelve Months.
