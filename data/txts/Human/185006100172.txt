   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.  A potter; I had been to the theatre, and was too late to get in at home—I went to the coffee-house next door to the theatre—I had a cup of coffee—I went to sleep; I cannot say for how long—I went in about one o'clock—perhaps it was about two when I went to sleep—I slept till about four—White was not talking to the prose cutrix; he sat by her—that was about a quarter before four—I was sitting right opposite—she was asleep—I did not say anything.  1215. ELLEN GRANT , stealing 1 half-crown; he moneys of Hugh Rowen , from his person: having been before convicted. HUGH ROWEN. I am a mason's labourer. On 12th May, between five and six o'clock in the morning I went into a whiskey-shop in St. Jame's-place, Graham-lane—the prisoner was there—she asked me if I was not going to give her a glass—I said, "No"—she put her hand in my waistcoat-pocket, and took out a half-crown and two penny-pieces—I shook her hand; the half-crown fell, and another female picked it up—I gave the prisoner in charge. CHARLES JACKSON LEWIS( policeman, M  76). I was sent for, and took the prisoner—I told her the charge—she said she knew nothing about it, and it was a b—y shame she should be taken when he was talking to another girl.
