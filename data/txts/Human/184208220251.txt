   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not lose sight of me? A.  No, never—when I came back the box was unlocked—you broke it open with a chisel. ELLEN BARRETT. The prosecutrix is my niece—I live next door to her—I was looking out at my window on the 21st of July, and saw her go out—I did not see her come back, but I heard her scream—I went down stairs, and she told me to take care of her house—I went and found one of her boxes drawn half-way from under the bed—it was unlocked. WILLIAM SOUTHWICK. I am a boiler-maker, and live in the next room to the prosecutrix—I heard her cry, "Stop thief" on the 21st of July, and saw the prisoner running down Colchester-street—I laid hold of him, and kept him till the officer came. JAMES SIMEON. I am a boiler-maker. I was with Southwick when he took the prisoner—I saw the prisoner throw three keys into the gutter and put his foot on them—the prosecutrix picked them up. JOHN COX. I am a policeman. I was fetched to Colchester-street about a quarter before four o'clock that evening—I saw the prosecutrix, the pri soner, and several persons round him—the prosecutrix charged him with being in her house, but she did not know whether he had taken anything—somebody called out about the prisoner's left hand pocket—I searched it, and found in it two keys and a chisel, and in his other pocket this empty bag—I have these other keys, which were given me by the prose cutrix—the prisoner tried to get away, and the mob was against his being taken to the station—I tried those keys to the prosecutrix's room, and one of them opened the door—I fitted this chisel to the marks on the box, which had been broken open—it corresponds exactly.
   Prisoner's Defence . I saw people running, I was behind them; this man put out his foot, and I fell down. GUILTY. Aged 28.— Confined Four Months.
