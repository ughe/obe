 1324.  GEORGE SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of William Thompson , from his person.
 WILLIAM THOMPSON. I live in Gloucester-buildings, Walworth. On the 25th of May, at a quarter-past four o'clock, I was in  Watling-street, and thought I felt something at my pocket - I turned round, and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand, concealing it under his jacket - I took it from him, and gave him in charge - I saw nobody with him.
   JOHN JACKSON. I am a constable, and received him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)

     GUILTY. Aged 12. Transported for Seven Years.

 1326.  GEORGE HARDING was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of April , 1 trunk, value 10s.; 1 coat, value 30s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 10s.; 12 printed bound-books, value 10s.; 80 sheets of printed paper, value 10s., and 2 pocket-books, value 4s., the goods of George Leonard Swile.
 GEORGE LEONARD SWILE. I am a  baker, and lodge in a second floor, in Water-lane, Fleet-street. On the 28th of April my trunk, containing this property, was taken from my room - I left it safe at half-past two o'clock - a person was cleaning the room - I returned about twelve at night, and it was gone - I have found none of the things - I had not seen the prisoner there that day - he used to bring coals to my former lodging, in Crown-court, Whitefriars - it was a hair-trunk.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Had you left the servant to clean the room? A. She comes at times to do it.
   SARAH SWILE. I am the prosecutor's mother - we keep a little shop at this house. On the 8th of April, about five o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came to my room; he looked much confused - he came in at the side door, not at the shop door - he asked the price of a penny pie, then turned round, shoved the door too, and stood with his back to it - he went out at the side door, and seemed alarmed - I do not know whether he went up-stairs; he took a two-penny pie, and went away.
 Cross-examined. Q. There was a way to the shop, through the side door? A. Yes, he had no coals with him; I never knew him bring coals to that house.
   MARY MERCHANT. I live in Water-lane. I have known the prisoner some time by sight. On the 28th of April, about five o'clock, I was standing at my street door, which is opposite to Swile's - I saw a very respectable man, dressed in blue, walking to and from by the door - the prisoner was inside the passage, and a hair trunk laid in the passage by him, near the door - the man in blue stepped up, took she trunk on his left shoulder, and walked down the lane as fast as he could; the prisoner came out in about two minutes, with two pies in his hand - he was by the trunk when the man took it. I did not say what I had seen till next morning - I had heard of the robbery at twelve o'clock at night; I did not see them speak to each other; it was done momentarily.
   PHOEBE PULSFORD. I live with this witness, and keep a school. I know the prisoner, and saw him go into Swile's passage that afternoon - I saw nothing more.
   ALEXANDER JOHNSON. I apprehended the prisoner - he denied the charge. I saw nothing more.
 MRS. SWILE. I never knew him to bring coals to this house; I have been there six months; he said nothing to me about a trunk being in the passage.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1327.  WILLIAM TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of April , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of a person unknown, from his person.
   JOHN GIRTON. I am a constable of Broad-street. On the 23d of April, about half-past seven o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner and another in Cornhill, as the mail coaches were starting - I saw the prisoner go behind a gentleman, and take a yellow handkerchief out of his pocket; I touched the gentleman on his back, and told him what I had seen - he turned to come across the street with me, but my eye was upon the prisoner, and I lost the gentleman; I followed the prisoner and his companion into the Old Jewry - they went down Princes-street, and were coming into Cheapside again; I took hold of the prisoner, and companion ran away; I took the prisoner up Meeting-house-court, and searched him - I found the handkerchief in his hat; he said it was his own - he had no other. I do not know the gentleman's name.
 JURY. Q. Was he out of your sight? A. No.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had bought the handkerchief on Monday, and gave 2s. 6d. for it - I put it into my hat, and was going down the Old Jewry; this man came and took me - the handkerchief has a blue stain on it; I got into conversation with the other lad, and he went to the pump.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Life.
