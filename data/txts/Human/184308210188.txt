 2484. WILLIAM BRIDGES was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of August , 1 tame rabbit, value 5 s  .; the goods of Thomas Manders . HARRIET MANDERS. I am the wife of Thomas Manders, of William-street, Hoxton. We keep rabbits—I had this one ( looking at one ) safe in the shed on the 9th of August, and missed it on the 10th. ALLAN CAMERON( police-constable 91 M. ) On the 10th of August I met the prisoner carrying this rabbit, at a quarter past five in the morning.
   Prisoner.  I bought it as I was going to work. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Four Months .
 2485. THOMAS MASON was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of August , 2 sixpences; the monies of Matthias Graves . MATTHIAS GRAVES. I live in Bishop's-court, Clerkenwell. The prisoner? was lodging with me—I marked some shillings and two sixpences—when I was in bed on the 12th of August I heard a chink—next morning I called in the policeman, who found these two sixpences on the prisoner, which I had marked with a centre-bit—they had been in my left hand waistcoat pocket.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  When you heard the noise did you affect to be asleep? A.  No, I was talking to the prisoner at the same time—he was talking about his honesty, and while he was pretending to pull off his clothes he robbed me—he said he had no money of his own—my clothes were on the box—he hunghisclothes on the door—there was about two feet between his clothes and mine—we had slept in the same room for seven or eight days—he owes me 1 l . 8 s . 4 1/2 d . for rent and money lent—I said I did not want to prosecute, all I wanted was my money, and I would recommend him to mercy if the money was paid—I heard the chink—he came to bed, and I went to sleep—next morning I got a policeman—I had turned waistcoat inside out, and laid it on the box, and when I came to it in the morning it was all in a rumple. THOMAS CLARK( police-sergeant 13 G. ) I took the prisoner. I took up a waistcoat and said, "Is this your's?"—he said, "Yes"—I felt in the pocket of it, and found these two sixpences—he said it was his first offence, and he hoped the prosecutor would forgive him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he not say he found them on the floor and put them into his pocket? A.  Not when I took him—he said so at the magistrate's office.
   COURT . Q.  Did the prosecutor describe the marks on the sixpences before you found them? A.  Yes, he shewed me a shilling, which had the same mark on them as the sixpences, and here is the punch which he said it marked them with.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months

HENRY JOSEPH KING( Thames police-constable , 30.)
