   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did you see the schooner ashore at the Lower Tunnel-pier? A.  No. WILLIAM JEFFORD. I am a waterman. On Friday, the 25th of Oct., I was on the Lower Tunnel-pier, and saw the Waterman , No. 6, coming up the river—she was going at the rate of eight or nine miles an hour—I do not know whether that is her full speed or not—there was a schooner moored to the Lower Tunnel-pier—I had moored her there some time before—I saw a skiff and a man rowing—the steam-boat struck it—she was not above her own length from the skiff when I first saw her—she was just on the point of striking—I did not see whether it had at all altered its direc tion before it struck the boat—the skiff was twenty-one feet long—the speed of the steamer was not diminished to my sight—I had seen the steamer before—at the time I saw the skiff the steamer was going in the same direction as she had been before—she had not altered her course from the time I first saw her—when I first saw her she was about 150 feet from where I stood—it was not half a minute between my first seeing her and her striking the skiff—I have been acquainted with the river for some time.
   Q.  What is, in your judgment, the proper rate for going in the Pool? A.  Four miles against the tide, I think it is; and six miles with it—I dare say the tide itself would drive a barge along at the rate of four miles an hour, with a person just now and then to straighten her or turn her round—in my judgment there ought to be an increase of only two miles an hour put on by the steamers—the schooner that I had moored caused some eddy; but at the time the skiff was overturned she had not come within the influence of that eddy.
   Cross-examined by SIR JOHN BAYLET . Q.  Are you employed at the Lower Tunnel-pier? A.  Yes—the "Waterman" boats do not run to that pier—I did not see the steamer starboard her helm—she might have done so, in the course of the way she was going—I think she ought to have starboarded her helm, to have cleared the boat—I saw the schooner aground—she caused a little eddy above her—she caused more eddy when she was aground than when she was lifted—a good many steamers come to the Lower Tunnel-pier, as many as to the other—they travel at the same speed as the others—there is very little difference in any of them—one is as good as the other—there is never above a minute difference in speed in any of them—sometimes one is first, and sometimes the other is first.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  I suppose you are only employed as a servant at this pier? A.  That is all—I have no share or interest in the boats—I think if the helm had been starboarded, it would have enabled the steamer to avoid the boat. JOHN CHAMBERS.
