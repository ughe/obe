667. WILLIAM HILL THOMPSON (46), Feloniously marrying Ann
Steward Wooding, his wife Amy being alive.
ANN THOMPSON. My name was Ann Steward Wooding-I was married
to the prisoner on the 17th last May, at St. John's, Limehouse - I believed
him to be a single man (certificate read).
ELIZABETH SMITH. I am a widow, living at Manchester-terrace, Millwall
-I was present on 31st August, 1852, when the prisoner was married to
Amy Patrick at Mr. Colton's, the registrar-office-I put my mark as a
witness—I know Amy Patrick-I saw her just this minute in Court-she
lived with the prisoner twelve years—he left her the day before Good-
Friday this year—they lived together up to that time (certificate read).
Prisoner's Defence. On 31st August, 1852, we went to Mr. Colton's office
- he said we should have to wait two or three days, I said I was going up
to London, and it must be done then, and then he said he supposed he must
do it as well as he could, and he read a part of the service-there were only
two lads there-he never put the ring on at all-I had it in my pocket, and
I put it on when we came out in the street-I thought it was a very peculiar
sort of marriage-she went back the next day, and asked him if it was right,
and he said "Yes," and wrote out that certificate.
ELIZABETH SMITH (re-examined). Mr. John Martin married the prisoner
-he is the registrar.
Guilty.-Confined Six Months.
