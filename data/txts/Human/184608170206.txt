   Q.  Suppose the prisoner, right or wrong, took coals for sale to his friends at the same price as Moore, would these entries be right? A.  No, the pool price on the 13th of Jan. was 18 s . 6 d . a ton—the price charged in the book is 4 s . 6 d . extra, 23 s ., but the price charged in the bill it 25 s .—that would make a difference of 6 s . 6 d .—the entry at the trade price would be right, all except 6 d . t if he was allowing 4 s . 6 d . it would be right—he does not account for the last entry in the cash-book—the pool price here was 16 s . then—4 s . 6 d . added to that, would make 20 s . 6 d .—three tons would be 3 l . 6 d .—I had not quarrelled with him before he left—Mr. Moore was constantly seeing the books—I have the ledger here—the entries are Moore's writing—after the prisoner left went to Mr. Nash's public-house—I do not know that the prisoner lodged there—there was a removal of some of his goods—my wife had given his wife some things—she was anxious that they should not appear in a public-bome among his wardrobe, and I went to Mr. Nash's—the prisoner called afterwards, and asked what I meant by overhauling his things—I parted with him under the best of feelings—this might be a fortnight or ten days after he left—he had improved my business—I considered him an excellent servant-after he left some of my customers told me he had solicited them for a new master—he went into the service of Mr. Wright, who took a wharf 500 or 600 yards from mine.
   Q.  Did Wright commence business there the very day you took the pri soner up? A.  I cannot recollect, but as soon as I became acquainted with those facts I went to my solicitor—a great many of my customers have gone to Wright—some of them are now returning—they were hucksters who came to the wharf for their coals—it was his usual business to treat with thent, and receive their money—I know nothing of Watts—he had not been a customer of mine—I very likely saw Mr. Wright at the Coal exchange on the 21ft of May—I do not recollect saying to him, "I see you are going to be a neigh bour of mine, and take my late clerk into your service"—I will not swear did not—he did not say, "Yes, is there anything against his honesty?"made no reference to character, nor did I say, "Not the slightest"—he mip have said, "Have you anything to say against him?" and I studiously avoided giving any answer.
   Q.  Did not you say "Not the slightest?" A.  Certainly not—it is im possible for a man to recollect everything he says—I cannot recollect whether I did or not—it was after that I discovered this—the prisoner's brother called on me on a Saturday.
