Taylor spoke to me, in consequence of which I overtook the prisoner on Kennington-green, and asked Taylor, in his presence, if he was the man who took the watch from the man who was knocked down in Wallcot-square—he said, "Yes"—I put my hand on the prisoner, and asked him for the watch, or to tell me what he had done with it—he said, "It is all right"—I said, "All right will not do for me, and said that he must tell me where it was, or give it to me"—he put his hand in his waistcoat-pocket, and gave me this watch—he was charged at the station, and said that he never intended to keep it, but the man asked him to take care of it, and he could not get it off, so was obliged to break the chain—I asked him if be knew Mr. Todd; he said, "No"—I asked him if he knew where he lived; he said, "At 3, Albert-place, Hercules-buildings, and that he was going to return the watch to him to-morrow."
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you go to my employer? A.  I did, and found that you bore a very good character, but you had only been employed there a week; I also found that you had been employed by Mr. Oartwright, in the New Cut, who also gave you a good character for four or five months.
   The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate:  —"I did not strike him at all, he was drunk and incapable; when I first saw him he was lying in the centre of the road, near Brixton Church; I saw him as far as the "Horns, "he said I was a good sort of fellow, and he would reward me with a glass of ale; I said I did not want any; he was reeling along the road. When we got to Kennington-road he wanted me to leave him, but I said I would see him further; he said, "Take care of my watch, I live at 3, Hercules-buildings;" he tried to give me the watch, but we both fell together, and then it was broken from the chain; I put it into my pocket, intending to return it the next day. GUILTY.— Confined Nine Months . Before Mr. Recorder.
 490. THOMAS STANTON  (17) , Stealing a purse and 10 s  ., the property of Mary Westbrook , from her person.
   MR. WOOD conducted the Prosecution, and MR. TAYLOR the Defence. MARY WESTBROOK. I am a widow, living at No. 20, Vine-terrace, Lam beth—on Saturday night, 1st April, about half-past 9, I was in the Waterloo-road with a basket full of potatoes, and some grocery, on my arm—the prisoner came to my side, and made an attempt to snatch a purse out of my left hand which was underneath the basket—he gave a second pull, and I saw his face distinctly—there was no lamp-post there; there was light enough to enable me to see his features—he then ran up Granville-street—there was a 5 s . piece and 3 s . in my purse—he took the purse and the money—I took my things home, and then went down Tower-street, and gave information to the police—I described the man—I saw him again on Monday morning, at Tower-street station, amongst several more, and I picked him out—I said, "I feel quite sure he is the man, if there were 100 people there"—I feel quite sure of him now.
