   MR. SHEPHERD . Q.  You receive the letters and money through the tunnel and them to their proper places, and account to the person you take them to, how you obtain them? A.  Yes—the runnel is worked on my side—they can only work it on the foreign side by pulling a cord, which is not the usual way—we have a bell on each side—if they want to send any thing to me they ring the bell, and if I want to send to them I do the same. CHARLES BULLBECK. I am a messenger in the Post Office. It is my duty at attend at the tunnel on alternate days—I was there on Thursday, the 3rd of March—I went at six o'clock in the morning—there was no letters or postage in the box that morning, I am quite certain.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  How long do you remain on duty? A.  Till about nine o'clock in the morning on the average—it depends on when the duty ceases—i am there about hours—when we leave the duty is over till evening entirely. GEORGE LEADBITTER.
