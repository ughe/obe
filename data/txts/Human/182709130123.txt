 1852.  ANN CONNOR was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 1 shawl, value 15s., and 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Richard Elliott.
   SARAH ELLIOTT. I am the wife of Richard Elliott - we live at Windsor. On the 17th of July, about eleven o'clock, I went into the Ship public-house, James-street, Covent-garden; I had this handkerchief and shawl on my neck; there were four females there - one of them took up my glass of porter, and drank it; they then surrounded me, and insisted on my giving them liquor, but I had only three halfpence, which I paid for the beer - I went into the bar, to the landlord, and asked him if he would take either of my silk handkerchiefs for half-a-pint of gin, which they pressed me to give them. The prisoner, who was one of them, then spoke up violently, and said she would take my pockets off; she reached over the bar, took my handkerchief and shawl off, and ran away with them.
   JOHN KNOWLES. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a shawl, which was pawned on the 17th of July, by a woman, in the name of Ann Connor - I cannot swear to the prisoner.
   FRANCIS FAGAN. I am an officer. I received information, and took the prisoner on the 17th of July. I found no duplicate on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw the prosecutrix in liquor - several men were with her; I was afterwards in the Ship: she came in, and had some liquor; some time after she came with a beadle, and took me, but he let me go twice - he took me a third time; the prosecutrix is an unfortunate woman, and is not married.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 1853.  DANIEL HAGAN was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 1 saw, value 3s. 6d., the goods of William Nicks.
   THOMAS ARMSTRONG. I am a pawnbroker. I have a saw, which the prisoner pawned on the 10th of September, in the name of Jones.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you know him before? A. No; he was taken in a quarter of an hour.
 WILLIAM NICKS. I lost this saw from  St. Katherine's Dock.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you not on intimate terms with the prisoner? A. No - I knew him. I had pawned his saw some time before, as it could not be used. I have sometimes gone by the name of Jones.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1854.  FREDERICK MOODY and THOMAS GALLIGHAN were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 17 handkerchiefs, value 24s., the goods of William Jones.
   ROBERT MARKS. I live with Wm. Jones, a pawnbroker. These handkerchiefs were safe in his shop on the afternoon of the 20th of August - I did not miss them till they were brought to me next morning - I know nothing of the prisoners.
   BENJAMIN TIMBRELL. I am an officer. I received information, and took the prisoners, in company together, between nine and ten o'clock in the morning of the 21st of August; I found on Gallighan these six handkerchiefs, between his shirt and waistcoat.
   WILLIAM SCHOFIELD. I was with Timbrell. I found these eleven handkerchiefs on Moody.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 MOODY's Defence. I was near the Horse Guards, and met a man running very fast with two or three bundles - he dropped one, which I took up; it contained this property.
 GALLIGHAN's Defence. I met my fellow prisoner - a man in a blue cloak ran by us; he dropped a bundle - we took it up, and shared the handkerchiefs.
 MOODY -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 GALLIGHAN -    GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1855.  THOMAS SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of August , 1 pair of shoes, value 6s.; 1 Guernseyfrock, value 2s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 5s., and 1 knife, value 6d., the goods of James Kenney.
 JAMES KENNEY. I live at  Limehouse, and keep a shop. I was in the back room - the prisoner came and said he wanted to fit out, to sail; I brought out some shoes, handkerchiefs, and other things, which he looked at; he wished a better assortment, and showed me an order to the amount of 2l. I went out for half an hour, to get some of the things, leaving him with my wife; when I returned he was gone with these things.
   SUSAN KENNEY. I am the prosecutor's wife. My husband went out to get some things, to show the prisoner - he sat in a chair, close to the door in the parlour; he tried on a frock and shoes - I said, "Your are not going to put them on?" he said, "No, I am only going to try them;" I wrapped up two silk handkerchiefs for him: the bell rang - I went into the shop, and on my return he had gone out at the passage door. I missed this property - he left his own shoes behind.
   JAMES DRYDEN. I am an officer. I have a pair of shoes and a Guernsey-frock, which I took off the prisoner's person, about six days after the robbery - he was intoxicated.
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor agreed to let me have the things, and he was to be paid for them when I got to Gravesend. I merely went out for some tobacco, and seeing the ship moving, I went to assist, which was my duty- I went to join the ship, and found another man was engaged in my room.
     GUILTY. Aged 39. Confined Three Months.
