186. JOHN HARVEY was indicted for stealing, on the   14th of Nov ., 6lbs. weight of ham, value 3 s  ., the goods of Henry Garrett ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. WILLIAM HELLEWELL. I am in the service of Mr. Henry Garrett, a cheesemonger—he lives in St. George the Martyr, Southwark. On the 14th of Nov. I placed two half hams inside the window at seven o'clock—I do not remember seeing them after that—I saw the prisoner at ten minutes before ten o'clock, about twenty yards from our window, with one of the half hams in his possession—I walked towards him—he saw me, and placed the half ham down by the side of a gateway—he walked on a short distance—I took up the ham, and was returning with it in my apron—the prisoner passed me, and I gave him into custody. MARY WILKINSON. I am the wife of Joseph Wilkinson. On that evening I saw the prisoner take the ham out of the window which was open—he laid it down by the gateway, then came a few yards towards me, and turned back, and the young man directly gave him into custody. JAMBS PERRY( police-constable M  60.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's conviction, which I got from the clerk of the Peace of Surrey—( read )—"Convicted on the 16th of March, 3rd Vict.; and transported for seven years"—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Transported for Seven Years.
 187. FREDERICK CHILD , WILLIAM ROGERS, and HENRY ROGERS , were indicted for stealing, on the   20th of Nov ., 1 trowel, value 3 s  ., the goods of Peter Dunn . PETER DUNN. I am a plasterer, and live at Kennington. I was at work outside a building at Stock well on the 20th of Nov.—I had a trowel inside the room—I saw it safe at half-past twelve, when I returned from dinner, and missed it at three o'clock—I saw Child and another lad with him, in the road that day, and soon afterwards I saw William Rogers in the room where the trowel was—this is my trowel that I missed.
   JURY . Q.  How do you know it? A.  When I bought it I took the handle off, and put this handle on, and filed it off myself—I can swear to it. EDWARD WHITE. I am a labourer. I went to look after the prisoners—I saw Child with the trowel under his arm, and the other two prisoners were with him—I followed them, and Child went and pawned it at a pawnbroker's—I went to him when he came out, and asked him if he had seen the Rogers—he told me no, he had just come from Vauxhall-bridge—I told the pawnbroker to keep the trowel. GEORGE MARK LINTHORNE. I am a pawnbroker. I produce this trowel, which was pawned by a boy, who I think was Child, but I cannot swear to him—White came and told me something in about a quarter of an hour.
   Child . I met these two boys; they told me to go there and I could get a job; I went in, and was coming out, when I met William Rogers with the trowel, and he asked me to pawn it, which I did; I was not aware it was stolen.
   William Rogers'Defence . He said he had no need of his trowel; he had no work, and would I go and get it for him, it laid on the floor; I went and got, it and he went and pawned it.
