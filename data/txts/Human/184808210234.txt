On 15th Aug., Saunders came and asked for half an ounce of bear's greases—he gave me a Victoria shilling—I gave him 10 1/2  d . change, and he left an officer, came in directly, and I gave him the shilling—I had but one other which was Georce the Third's—the officer followed the prisoner—I followed the officer—I fell in with the two prisoners down a turning—they were greasing their hair and laughing—I told Saunders he came to my house just now to buy some bear's grease—he said, "Yes"—I told him he gave me a bad shilling—he said, "Did I"—I said, "You know you did"—he was going to put his hand into his left hand pocket to give me some money, and Jones said, "No, not that pocket"—he put his hand then into his right pocket, and gave me a good shilling—I then asked him for the 10 1/2 d ., and he gave it me from the same pocket lie took the good shilling from—I collared him, and called the officer. JOHN WHITLAMB( policeman, M  89). On 15th Aug. I went to Mrs. Symm's after Saunders came out—she gave me this shilling—I had seen the prisoners before together, and afterwards I followed them—Saunders went into Mr. Anderson's—Jones was about 100 yards off, apparently watching the door—Saunders came out, and I followed the two prisoners to Hughes' Fields—I saw them rubbing their hands on their heads—I took Saunders. THOMAS WEST( policeman, M  249). I went into Mr. Anderson's shop—he gave me this shilling—I followed the prisoners—I afterwards saw Mr. Anderson with them—he called me—I took Jones, and found this tobacco in his pocket. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—these shillings are both counterfeit, and cast in the same mould.
   SAUNDERS  — GUILTY. Aged 22.
   JONES  — GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Four Months

   MESSRS. SCRIVEN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. ARTHUR BLUNT. I live in Regent-street, Lambeth. On 4th Aug. the prisoner came to my shop, and purchased a tea-pot for 6 d .—she gave me half-a-crown—she was on my right—I gave her the change, and she moved to my left side—she said, "This is not right, I purchased the tea-pot for 3 d .—I said it was right, she had purchased it for 6 d . she said she would not have it, she would give me my money again—she gave me two shillings, one was bad—I said I did not give her that bad shilling—she said I did—the shillings I gave her were what I had taken of a customer not five minutes before—they were white—the one she returned was black—several people assembled—a man came from the other side of the way, I had seen him and another man with the prisoner before—he came close to her, and looked over her shoulder, and asked what was the matter—I looked at him, and he went away directly—I believe the white shilling has not been found—I said to the prisoner, "This is bad money, I shall keep it and give you in charge"—she walked into my parlour, and begged very much to be dis charged, and said if I would give her the tea-pot she would go about her business—I called a policeman, and gave her in charge. MARY SHERIDAN. I searched the prisoner at the station—I asked what it was for—she said, "For passing a bad shilling"—I asked if she had got—she said, "No"—I found 1 s . in her bosom, which I marked, and gave to the inspector—she called me very bad names—I said it would be the worse for her, as I should tell the Magistrate—she said, "You have got me now can't be any worse."
