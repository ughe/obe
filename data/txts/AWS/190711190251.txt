ELEANOR Hoop, 43. May Road, Plaistow. I lived at 111, St.
Mary's Road, Plaistow, on October 1 last. Hill was my lodger,
He rented a back room at 2s. a week. Hayes came with Hill. They
were joint tenants. They were not at home from 11 to 11.45 on
pension night. I had been to sleep and woke up when they came
home. I looked at my husband's watch, and it was 2 o'eloek; that
was half-an-hour fast I heard footsteps but no voices. At 10 in
the morning Stowe same down to wash. I asked him if he had any
beer. He said, Yes, send up a jug, and Miss Franklin took it up.
He paid me up on pension day. The three prisoners went out
together on the morning of October 2.
Cross-examined by Stowe. I saw you in the morning between 9
and 10, when you came down to wash.
Cross-examined by Hill. There are other people on that landing,
but they were in before you came home.

Cross-examined. It was 8 Tuesday. At the police court I did not

HENRY STowe (prisoner, on oath). On Oetober 1 I left home about
three o'eloek. I arrived at the Queen publie-house in Green
Street about 5.30, when I met Hill and Hayes. I stopped in their
company till about nine and had several drinks, which we generally
do on pension day. I left them about nine or 9.30 and got home
a little after 10, when my wife and mother-in-law were sitting there.
They passed a remark to me and said I looked a little bit drunk. 1
said "Yes, I had several, I think I will go to bed," and I did. I
went out the next morning and made my way to Hill's house. I
had arranged with him to have the rent off me, which he was owing
at the time of his arrest. That is all I say. I can safely say when
this was supposed to have happened, between one and two on the
morning of the 2nd, I was in bed with my wife.
Cross-examined. I have heard what Mr. Thomas said about what
oceurred at the railwiay station. It is not true. I heard the coffee
stall keeper swear he SaW me outside the station. It is not true.
ERNERT HArEs (prisoner, not on oath). At 10.30 that night I went
