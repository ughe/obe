JAMES JEPPERYS. I keep the Good Intent, East-street, Walworth. On
Sunday evening, 25th Nov., the prisoner came with a 101.-note in his hand
- he asked me to change it, and said he never saw such a d-d poor place as
this, that he had been to a dozen houses and could not get change-I would
not give it him, and he said he would give anybody a shilling to get him
change-Fellows went for it-I saw him return and give the change to the
prisoner--next morning the prisoner came into my back yard and asked me
what change that old gentleman gave him I said, "A 51.-note, four sove-
reigns, and small change" he said, "I have lost the 51.-note" I said.
"You cannot have lost it; you are too artful and too cunning to do that"
(I had seen him twice before, when he came in for two half-pints of beer,
and once he came to ask me to have some coals)--he said, "Never mind;
light come, light go."
Cross-examined. Q. Did you give evidence before the Magistrate ? A. No;
this is the first time I have mentioned the conversation in public-another
man was with him and his wife-it was between ten and twelve o'clock-he
came into the back yard by himself—the other mau did not hear the con-
versation.

worth," and gave him a 51.-note, four sovereigns, and 1l. worth of silver-
next day I paid it to Mr. Gurney, of Islington--I received it back from

JOHNSTON EDDINGTON. I keep the Artichoke at Camberwell. A twelve-
month or two years previous to Nov. last, I had seen the prisoner at my
house, but I did not know him when he came in-I bad a regular customer
named William Thomas, who I have known about twenty years-between six
and seven o'clock on the evening of 25th Nov., the prisoner came and asked
for Mr. Thomas, his uncle; I said he had not come yet, but I expected him
- he said he wanted particularly to see him, for he wanted to leave him 21,
-I said if be liked to stop a short time his uncle would be there be said he
was very much pushed for time-I offered to take the 21.- he said he could
not do that unless I changed him a 101. note, and he put the note into my hand
-I did not like the feel of the paper, and handed it to my wife, as I cannot
see well without my glasses-- she said she thought it was good, and I gave
the prisoner 81. for it, and was to give 21. to his uncle-when I went up for
the change, I left the note with the prisoner, and when I came down I saw
his name on it-this is it produced here is “Caleb Thomas" on the back
of it-I locked it up I was to tell his uncle that 30s. of the 21. was to be
sent to his daughter in the country to bring her home-I paid the note away
on Wednesday the 28th, and on the Saturday following, Dec. Ist, it was
brought back by Mr. Potter as a forgery.
