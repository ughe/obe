827. WILLIAM LAMBDEN was indieted for stealing, on the 23rd
of December, at Ashford, in Middlesex, a gelding, value 51., the property of
James Grieves and another.--2nd Count, stating it to be the property of
George Sherborn.
GEORGE SHERBORN. I am a farmer, and live at Ashford, in Middlesex,
On the morning of the 24th of December I missed a cream-coloured, long-
stailed gelding from my straw-yard there--I afterwards saw the same geld-
ing at the stable of the Griffin Inu, at Kingston, in Surrey-Ashford is
about fourteen miles from Hyde-park corner-1 afterwards attended before
the magistrate, and saw the horse the magistrate's clerk, Mr. Horne, took
down the evidence-but he is not here.
RICHARD HANKS. I am a constable of Kingston, which is about eight
op nine miles from Ashford. On the night of the 23rd of December, about
ten minutes before twelve o'clock, I met the prisoner at Kingston, leading a
cream-coloured horse I stopped him, and asked where he was going
with it-he said, "To London market" -I asked him, "What for b e
said, “ To sell it"--I asked him where he brought it from the said at first,
"From Staines"—then he told me he brought it from Weyhill, in Hamp-
shire-1 told him I should detain him and the horse, as his story did
not satisfy me I should take him before the Mayor, and if they were
satisfied, I should be- I put him into the watch-house, and locked the horse
up at the Griffin Inn; and on Thursday, the 24th, Mr. Sherborn came and
owned it-I went before the Mayor that day at Sunbury, which is five
miles from Kingston.
