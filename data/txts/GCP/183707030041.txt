THOMAS SMITH. I was in the house in Buckeridge-street, and saw
Goulding and Welch there-I went out to fetch some things in for tea,
leaving Goulding and the prisoner together in the kitchen when I came
back I saw the prisoner and the deceased at the fire quarrelling- I do not
exactly know what they were saying-they said they had been larking with
the deceased- I was not there at the commencement of it-the first thing I
saw was the deceased had got hold of the tea-pot, that was by the fire-place,
and said he would chuck it at the prisoner-he attempted to do so, but I
took it out of his hand-he then held up his fist towards the prisoner's face
--the prisoner said, "Do you mean to strike me!"-directly afterwards
the prisoner took him by the coat and threw him down-that was the first
blow I saw pass between them the prisoner appeared to be intoxicated
knew him before, and have seen him intoxicated several times--the
deceased's head came in contact with the form that was in the room-he
appeared to be burt-he struck the back of his head against the form as he
fell he was not sensible after that he never spoke nor moved, but lay on
his back about a minute or two-some one in the room said a doctor
should be sent for, that the man appeared to be dying the prisoner and
another man assisted him up in a sitting position--he appeared to me to
be dead at that time,
Prisoner. Q. Did not the deceased strike me before I shoved him at
all ? A. He held his fist up, but I did not see him strike you—if he had
done so, I think I should have seen it.

JOSEPH BURGESS. I am a surgeon and apothecary, and have been sur-
geon to the parish of St. Giles' thirty-four years. The deceased was
brought to me by a policeman he was dead then, and appeared to have
been so for ten minutes at least I live about three stones throw from
where this took place, but he was taken to Oxford-street before he was
brought to me--there were no marks of violence about his body-there
was a post mortem examination on the 4th of October—there were no
marks of violence about the body, but in cutting away the hair and dividing
the scalp, I found an immense bruise over the left ear, and a prodigious
quantity of coagulated blood between the scalp and skull—there was no
fracture of the bone-I opened the head and found a quantity of extrava-
sated blood pressing on the brain-the whole of the brain presented one
general state of inflammation the blood-vessels of the brain were exceed-
ingly turgid-some were ruptured, and there was a pressure of blood on
the brain, which occasioned death-I apprehend the man died of apoplexy,
occasioned by the fall, and I think the vessels were ruptured in conse-
quence of the violent fall he had-his being in liquor would make the
vessels turgid and predisposed to apoplexy, but if he had not fallen I do
not think he would have had the apoplexy.
GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined Fourteen Days. ro
