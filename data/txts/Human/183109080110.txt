   WILLIAM FLETCHER. I am a baker.  Barnett's Defence. A boy asked me to go bird's nesting, and these things laid on the common - he took them up, and as we came along Fletcher met us; I had told him to take them to his mother and have them cried.

 1771.  JOHN BLACKERBY was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of March , 1 weather-glass, value 20s.; 2 brushes, value 1s.; 2 gowns, value 1s.; 1 piece of carpeting, value 1s.; 1 Bible, value 10s.; 1 coat, value 3s.; 1 chest of drawers, value 1l.; 3 pairs of sheets, value 10s.; 3 spoons, value 6s.; 1 bed, value 3l.; 1 counterpane, value 6s.; 10 lbs. of starch, value 13s.; 40 gallons of varnish, value 22s.; 1 writing-desk, value 10s., and a variety of other goods, the goods of William Dalton Gibbs , in his dwelling-house.
   WILLIAM DALTON GIBBS. In March last I lived in Kendal-mews. I took a shop and parlour in Duke-street, Lisson-grove, and sent my furniture there on the 23rd of March, but did not sleep there till the night after the robbery, nor did any of my family; there were lodgers in other parts of the house. Plummer, who lived next door to me, volunteered to go with the van which Summerfield drove; I was not present when it was loaded - I do not know where Plummer is - I was in a situation at that time; I went home on the morning of the 24th, in consequence of information - my wife having gone with the rest of the things, I went to Plummer's to breakfast, and then went to Duke-street -I then went with my wife and Warren, the officer, to the prisoner's house in Middlesex-mews; I there found a weather-glass behind a table, two brushes in a box, and a coat which I have now on - I did not know the prisoner.
   JANE GIBBS. I was present, between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, on the 23rd of March, when the van was loaded to take some things to Duke-street - I saw a bed and mattress, a chest of drawers full of linen, two wooden chests, a quantity of varnishes in carboys, and three bundles of clothes - my husband's coat was in one chest; Summerfield drove the van - Plummer went with him; he offered to go of his own accord - he kept a beer-shop next door to us: I do not know where he is now - I went myself about ten minutes after the van left - I got to Duke-street, and found the house-door fastened; the van was not there - I looked through the key-hole, and saw a carboy there; I came home, and got the key of the parlour from Plummer; I went again between eight and nine o'clock the next morning, with another load of goods; another man drove that van - Plummer went with me; he took the key from me, and when I went in there was nothing left but an empty carboy and can; I received information, and went to find out the prisoner's place; as I stood in Mitcham-street I saw some of my things coming out of a stable in Middlesex mews; a woman and a boy were bringing them out -I saw a man in a cart which had some of my goods in it; I cannot say whether it was the prisoner - there was a mattress in the cart, a table, a chest, and a fender - they were part of the things which had been in the van; I have heard the stable belonged to the prisoner - the things were in the cart when I got there; I left it there, and went for assistance - I returned with Lock, the Policeman, and the cart was gone; we went in search of it - I missed Lock, and then met Harrison; we came down to the stable, and knocked at the door; some children looked out of window, and refused to open it - we got in, and found a bundle inside, tied in a shawl; it contained linen, mere rags, pieces of my own, which I can swear to - I found some of my children's clothes up stairs in a chest - here they are, and this weather-glass is ours; all these things were in the van which I sent on the 23rd of March.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q.What was Plummer? A. He kept a beer shop - he is not related to us, but was rather intimate with my husband; he appeared respectable - we had only lived there three months; he has absconded.
   WILLIAM SUMMERFIELD. I drove the van on the 23rd of March, and deposited all the goods in a shop and parlour in Duke-street - three men went with me, and while I was doing up my cords the door was shut, and we all came away together.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it not clear day-light? A. It was.
   THOMAS HARRISON. I am a Policeman. I went with Mrs. Gibbs to Middlesex-mews - two children answered us from the window; I got in, and in the stable found a shawl, with things tied in it - I went up stairs, and in a room, under the bed, found a carpet, and in a box some children's clothes and two brushes; in a basket under the bed eight books - in the next room I found the weatherglass behind the table, and two gowns; Mr. and Mrs. Gibbs claimed the things - I do not know whose house it was; nobody was there but the children - I do not know the prisoner.
