THOMAS HENRY GURRING. I live at 59, Holborn Viaduct-I have
made handwriting a study-I have compared tbe letter and envelope
addressed to Captain Erskine with the one to Colonel Champion, and I
believe them to have been decidedly written by the same person-the
initials "J. U. 8.” on both envelopes appear to me identical-the "J" is
of a very peculiar formation; the "C" in “Club " is something of the
shape of an imperfect "O"-the “J” is very much the shape of the figure
7, and the "y" is similar, and the “f” is strikingly similar, I could
point out other similarities.
Cross-examined. The letter to Captain Erskine is in a slightly dis-
guised hand in my opinion-I have only received these two documents
for consideration I have studied handwriting for about six or seven
years—this is not my first appearance as a witness, by a great many-I
have been a clerk in the Board of Trade-I have not studied under
Chabot, Netherclift, or Inglis, and have not been discredited yet.
Re-examined. I gave evidence at the police-court, no one on the
defendant's behalf submitted any specimens of his handwriting to me.
MR. WILLIS submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury, the letter

ho not done so the matter would have dropped ; and although it might give riso
to a civil action, it was not the subject of a criminal prosecution. After hearing
Mr. Avory, the RECORDER would not stop the case, but, if necessary, would
reserve the point.
