   Cross-examined. Q.  Did Edwards afterwards agree to pay you 2 s . 6 d . a week for the goods? A.  Yes, and he left one half-crown—I had known Edwards before for two or three years—he is a watch-colourer and glider—I have an I O U of his, by which he agreed to pay half-a-crown a week for the amount of the chain.
   Madell.  I returned the two best chains to you. Witness.  Yes—the one not returned was worth 3 l . 17 s . FREDERICK CLEMENT ELLETT. I am the son of Samuel Ellett—I first saw the prisoners on the 17th of April—Madell came to my father's shop—he wanted some dressing-cases—I went with him to the public-house—he said his customer, Mr. Whiffin, was at the public-house over the way, and I went to see him—Edwards was the only person he showed them to in the public-house—I was introduced to Edwards—I supposed him to be Whiffin—when Madell go in he began to talk to Edwards, and showed him the dressing-cases—they talked together some time, and they went out together—Madell came back and brought this paper—he said, "I want five dressing cases; I will go to your father and ask what time he can have them ready"—we made up this order—I went at nine o'clock, and when I got there Madell was not there—Edwards was there alone—I said, "Is Mr. Madell here?"—he said, "No, he is gone down to your house; he left word that you were to go back directly—you will find him at your house"—I went back, and he had not been—I then went out with my father, and met Edwards is✗ Aldersgate-street—Madell said Mr. Whiffin was brother to Miss Whiffin, and that she was principal in the business.
