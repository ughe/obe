1888. JOHN TERRELL was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of
July, 1 pair of boots, value 6s., and I waistcoat, value 48.; the goods of
Michael Cartwright.
MICHAEL CARTWRIGHT. I live in Great Titchfield-street. I had a
pair of boots and a waistcoat in my room up stairs, on the 2nd of July-1
saw them safe about nine o'clock in the morning, and missed them about
nine in the evening--these now produced are them the prisoner lived in
the next room to me-he is a tailor--the things were not locked up.
ROBERT JOSEPH. I am a pawnbroker. I produce the waistcoat and
boots--the prisoner pawned the boots-I took them in of him on Friday,
the 2nd of June.
Prisoner. I did not pawn them.
GUILTY. Aged 32.-Confined Three Months.
1889. WILLIAM RUDKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th
of June, 1 pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of James Reeves, his master.
MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES REEVES. I keep the Orchard public-house at Black wall; the
prisoner was my pot-man. On the 26th of June, between one and two
o'clock in the day, I wanted the prisoner, and went to the pot-house where
the pots were cleaned, he was not there-- I then went to the cellar--(the
prisoner had no business there at that time I heard a chopping, and saw
him on his knees, behind a pile of wood in the corner of the cellar--he
was partly concealed—I asked what he was doing-he said chopping
wood, or putting the cellar to rights, or something to that effect-I told
him to go to the parlour, which was where it was his duty to be at that
time-when he was gone I went to where I had seen him, and found a
quantity of metal stowed away in a spittoon-it had been cut up and
flattened-it was such as the pots are made of-I locked up the cellar,
and placed the key in the bar in about a quarter of an hour afterwards,
I found the prisoner again in the same situation, where the wood was-1
asked him what he was doing-he said, "Chopping wood "-I told him
to lock the cellar-door, and take the key to the bar-he came out, and
locked it-in the evening I sent for a policeman-I went to the cellar, and
pointed out the place—the metal was still there-this is it-amongst it is
some which has my name on it--the metal had no right there.
JOSEPH BEALE (police-constable K 252.) I took the prisoner, and was
directed to where this spittoon was, behind some wood in the cellar-I
found this metal in it-I found on the prisoner 1s. 110.--this metal has
been cut with a chopper.
John BURROUGHS (police-constable K 283.) I was at the station when
the prisoner was there—this chopper was produced--the prisoner asked
what the officer was tying up, I said, "A chopper "-he said, " the
b chopper, it will sell me, I am sorry it is found."
