Certainly—the prisoner behaved in the kindest possible manner during the man's illness—he sent him wine from his house, which was necessary—he told me to do all I could, and he would pay every expense. MARGARET DENMAN. I attended the deceased during his illness—the ery sipelas commenced by the side of his head, near his ear—I had seen him on the 5th, and he seemed quite well then—I believe he was thirty-two years old.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long was it before he took to his bed? A.  On the 17th—I think he was at home, sitting by the fire-side during the previous week—I am not aware that he went to a public-house—he might have done so without my knowledge—I did not see him on the 7th—I cannot say whether I saw him on the 18th—he was walking about—he frequently called on me in the morning, saying he was going to the hos pital—I live next door to him.
   MR. PHILLIPS called THOMAS RAWLINS. I am ostler at the Castle and Falcon. I was in the public-house on the night in question, and saw the deceased there—he said he would have another pot, or else he would have a lark  to night—the landlord brought in a pot, and then attempted to shut up the bar—he then went into the room again to gather up the pots—the deceased sat on the table on my right hand, and said he would have a lark  —he jumped on the table, put the gas out, and ran away, and the landlord followed him—I saw them together next morning, and heard the deceased say be was more in fault himself than the landlord was the night before. HENRY COX. I am porter at the Castle and Falcon. On the day after the occurrence, I was in the tap of Philpot's house—the deceased told me he had made it all up, and he was as much to blame as the landlord was—I afterwards went into the tap, and saw him there for three days after-wards, and every time he was taking refreshment, smoking and drinking—the deceased told me the prisoner gave him 5 s . for the loss of his two days' work.
   JURY to MR. STEPHENS . Q.  Are you able to say whether the erysi pelas came on in consequence of the blow, or might it have come on in consequence of drink? A.  I think it would not have come on except from the blow, but it became more fatal from drink—I think the blow was quite sufficient cause for erysipelas, but if the man had paid more attention to himself it might not have been fatal.
