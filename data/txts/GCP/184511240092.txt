JURY. Q. Was there any quarrel between you and your husband ? A. We
had parted, but my things were to be forwarded to me, and were left with Mrs.
Bark for me-my husband put no more confidence in the prisoner than in a
common servant.
Elizabeth Bark re-examined. Q. You have seen these things that are laid
as your husband's property-were they handed over to you and sold to you
by Mr. Morris? A. They were, I am quite sure-I was with the prisoner in
removing the parcels off the shelves--they were all in little bundles, and
when my back was turned she took some, and put in her own room-I saw
her do it-I saw her rob me, but I did not know this business, and the pri-
soner was to assist and give me a knowledge of it.
Guilty. Aged 42.- Transported for Seven Years.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner, and she had been
before transported.)
89. GEORGE HOUGHTON was indicted for stealing 1 pair of shoes,
value 58., the goods of Alexander Cowan.
ALEXANDER Cowan. I am a general dealer, and live in Aldersgate-street.
I had a pair of shoes in my window on the morning of the 14th of Nov., tied
round a nail with a string-a person brought the prisoner back with the shoes
in his hand they were mine, and had been in my shop.
WILLIAM HAXLEY. I am in the employ of Mr. Bailey, of Aldersgate-
street. I saw the prisoner take this pair of shoes and put them on the win-
dow, and then put them into a basket which he had-he came across-I
tapped him on the shoulder and said, “You are my prisoner-take out those
sboes"-he took them out, went back, and put them down on the window.
JOHN WELSH. I saw him take the shoes off the nail, put them into his
basket, and go across the road.
Prisoner. I saw them marked 58. 3d.; I took them down and put them
into my basket; I was going into the shop to pay for them; I had got the
money.
Guilty. Aged 17.-Confined Three Months.
