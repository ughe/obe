THOMAS BRIDGEWATER. I am house-surgeon of King's College Hospital.
I made a post mortem examination of the deceased—I found some slight
bruises about his body, one on his right eye on the upper lid, a slight bruise
on the white of the same eye, and a little fluid came out of his mouth, slight
bruises on the upper gums, a slight bruise on the right breast, a slight bruise
on the outside of his left elbow, and on his left knee—on examining the brain,
I found blood effused on the surface, and a small quantity at the base, and in
the ventricles ; and the lungs were congested—I attribute his death to concus-
sion of the brain—that might have been produced by a fall such as described
—it might have been produced by blows.
Cross-examined. Q. Was there a Vessel on the brain broken? A. Several
vessels; I attribute that to concussion—it would be very likely to occur from
a tumble down stairs by a drunken man ; drunkenness is a very predisposing
cause, it renders vessels much more likely to rupture—there was not suff-
cient external injury to account for the concussion which caused his death.
MR. HUDDLESTONE. Q. Might a blow cause concussion of the brain
without leaving any external mark? A. It might; it is possible that a blow
may cause concussion of the brain, leaving only such appearances as I saw.
