   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Is this a copy of the letter you sent to the pro prietors? A.  Yes; it was addressed to all the proprietors—I do not know whether Mr. Bird attended the meeting or no—these are the printed regu lations—they were calculated, in my judgment, to benefit the public, and the proprietors also—these regulations were mine; whethere they have acted upon them all, I cannot say.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  After the agreements were made, I think some per sons were summoned before the Police-committee? A.  Yes; for obstruc tions—the regulations were never binding upon the Police-committee, but the persons were summoned for attempting to break the regulations which the Paddington proprietors had formed—they were convicted in 5 s . or 10 s . each, or imprisoned—the Magistrates of London have convicted persons for causing obstructions, which would not have been caused if the regu lations had been attended to.
