JOSEPH CHILD and THOMAS ROACH were indicted for stealing, on the   20th of February , 15lbs. weight of copper, value 7 s  .; 5 copper bolts, value 5 s  .; 2 staples, value 1 s  .; 161bs. weight of iron, value 1 s  .; and 1 iron wedge, value 6 d  .; the goods of Thomas Beech and another, in a certain vessel on the   navigable river Thames . HENRY PEACHY. I am foreman to Thomas Beech and Son. We had a ship being broken up at Mr. Sedger Wade's, at Rotherhitbe—there was some copper and bolts there, about the 20th of February—we have lost such things as these produced—I believe these to be the property of Beech and Son. HENRY HARDING MIDDLETON( police-constable M  163.) About nine o'clock on Monday morning, the 20th of February, I went to Sedger Wade's—I law the two prisoners putting property from a tug-boat into their boat—I went into their boat—Roach pushed me back into the other boat, and got away—Child and I were shoved  into the river—Child said he would go quietly if I would allow him to take the oars, which I did.
   Child.  He took them out of the tug-boat, and swore he took them out of my boat. Witness.  I threw them on the tug-boat first, because I could not get up the wharf—I picked up this wedge on the wharf—these three pieces were all I found in their boat, and these others.
   COURT . Q.  How far from Sedger Wade's was their boat? A.  Fifteen or sixteen feet—to get property on their boat it was necessary to get first on the lug-boat. GEORGE THOMAS MYERS. I found this copper in the bottom of the boat—I know this piece is Mr. Beech's.
   Child.  You did not know it at the police court. Witness.  I swore to this one at the police court, and to this iron bolt also. WILLIAM NOAKES( police-constable M  104.) I went in search of Roach, and found him secreted in a hen-coop on the adjoining premises. NOT GUILTY.
