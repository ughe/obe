716. CHARLES FLATMAN and JOHN JONES, were indicted for a
like offence.
WO
Messrs. Botkin and ELLIS, Jun., conducted the Prosecution.
SARAH Swan. I am the wife of Joseph Swan, a grocer and postmaster
at East Acton. On 11th June, the prisoner Flatman came about a quarter
before 8 o'clock in the morning, he bought half an ounce of tobacco which
came to 1 d.; he gave me a shilling, I gave him change and he left the shop
-before he had quite left, I told him it was a bad shilling, and called him
back-he said there was no harm done, and he gave me back the tobacco and
the change, and I gave him the shilling-he went away and joined the other
prisoner outside the shop; they went together towards Acton-I gave infor-
mation to the police.
Jones. Q. How can you swear to me? 4. By your features and dress,
and general appearance.
EMILY CLIFTON. My husband keeps a baker's and corn chandler's shop.
On 11th June, Jones came to my shop about a quarter before 9 o'clock in
the morning-he asked for a half quartern loaf, which came to 24d.-he ten-
dered me a half crown-I rang it on the counter; I said, “This is a bad
one"- the policeman came in, he rang it a second time, and said, “This is
a bad one"-the policeman took it with him-I gave Jones into custody.
HENRY BAYLEY ( policeman, T 250). Mrs. Swan gave me information on
11th June, and I pursued the prisoners—I saw them entering the village of
Acton together, I watched them on their getting near Mrs. Clifton's shop,
I saw Flatman pass something to Jones-Jones entered the shop, and Flat-
man walked on-on looking through the window I saw Jones served with a
half quartern loaf, and a half crown put on the counter-I entered the shop;
Mrs. Clifton was looking at the half crown, and said it was bad-I took it,
and took Jones into custody- I called Mr. Clifton to take charge of him
while I went after Flatman, whom I found in a public-house-I told him
what he was charged with he drew his hand closed from his pocket-I
called for assistance, and took him with his band closed to the station-I
found in his hand this shilling-I went and took Jones.
JOHN TICKNER. I went to assist the last witness-I took Flatman, and
kept his hand closed to the station, and took this shilling from his hand.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These are both bad.
Jones. I earned two half crowns; I did not know they were bad.
FLATMAN-Guilty. Aged 38. Confined Twelve Months.

Acre. On Monday night, 28th June, a few minutes past 9 o'clock, the

to the foreman, and Fleming went out of the shop-I saw the same pieces of
meat that I had served her with at Bow-street, about an hour and a half
afterwards---they were the same that I served Fleming with.
Flering. Q: How much meat did you give me? 4. I did not weigh it ;
