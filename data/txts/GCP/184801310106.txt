HOOPER, Mayor.
Wardour-street, Soho. On Thursday morning, 13th Jan., about eight o'clock
Charles Everard came to our shop and purchased a loaf, and some tea and
sugar-he tied them in a bundle which he had with him, and he asked in
would allow him to leave it for a few minutes, which I did-he did not
come for it, and I called in an officer-there were three new coats in it
besides the tea, sugar, and bread-Charles came about ten o'clock at uigh
Wells took him and the coats.
THOMAS WELLS (police-sergeant, C 1.) On the evening of 13th Jan. I
was called to Garrati's-the bundle containing the coats, tea, sugar, and
bread, was shown me-part of the binding in the coats had been cut away
I went home, put on a plain coat, went to the shop, and waited-Charles
Everard came about ten o'clock-I heard bim ask for his bundle-he Wit
told to come into the parlour for it-I shut the parlour door, and put my
back against it-I told him I was an officer, and asked what the bundie
contained-- he said, “Coats"-I asked what he was-he said, "A tailor"_
said, "I suppose you made these coats?"-he said no, he bought them some
time since of a man he did not know-I asked him where he lived-he said
with his father, but his father knew nothing of the coats--I took him to the
station-in going along he said it was no use concealing it any longer, he
was in the employ of Mr. Bax, and took the coats from there some money
was found on him, which has been given up-I went to 62, George-street,
Hampstead-road, and found two more coats there one of them bad MR
Bax's label on it-on 17th Jan. I went to the Yorkshire Grey in Piccadilly,
and saw Aon Pollard-she gave me this coat the label appears to bave been
recently taken off it-I went to Thomas Everard's house, beckoned him out
and said he must consider himself my prisoner-I asked him if he had been
to Mr. Dawson's, in Piccadilly, and said, “You know the suit I am on"
he said, “I do, the suit of my brother Charles"-I went to his house again
on the 14th, and found a coat there without a label.
ANN POLLARD. I live at the Yorkshire Grey. On the morning of 14th
Jan. Thomas Everard came about eight o'clock, and asked me to take
charge of a coat, which I did-he came again on the 17th, and asked
the landlord for the coat-he said he could not give it up; he had heard of
robbery, and wished to inquire into it-it was given to the officer,
DANIEL Bax. I live at 46 and 48, Regent-street. I missed some coats
-Charles Everard was in my employ previous to 13th Jan.-he was 3
confidential man, and was trusted with the keys of the shop-he went there
in the morning before other persons-these coats are all mine.
(Charles Everard received a good character.)
CHARLES EVERARD-GUILTY. Aged 80.-Recommended to mercy by
the Prosecutor.-Confined Four Months.
THOMAS EVERARD-Not Guilty.
