Certainly not—I went to fetch some beer, therefore I could not—I went to fetch the beer and bacon after he came home—it was not his bacon I cut when I disco vered his shoes, it was my own bacon—I did that while the prisoner was sitting at the table, before I went to fetch his bacon—I believe I ate my own supper before I fetched his.
   Q.  At that time had there been any conversation about the murder? A.  The conversation might have been about the murder, but I took very little notice of any of the conversation, therefore am not bound to answer the question.
   Q.  Now I ask you to answer the question, had any conversation taken place about the murder before, as you say, you cut the bacon, and saw his new shoes? A.  I have already answered that the conversation when I entered the house was on that subject, but I paid so little attention to the conversation between my wife and the prisoner, that probably I could not utter fifty words of the conversation the whole evening, unless the prisoner spoke to me—I mean, I could not utter fifty words of the conversation between the prisoner and my wife, except I had the leading point to lead to it, named—there was a good deal of conversation between him and my wife—I had some suspicion at the time I discovered the new shoes—I cannot say whether it was after that that I went and fetched the bacon for the prisoner—I believe I had eaten my own supper before I fetched it, but I will not swear it—I am not confident whether the bacon was cooked before or after.
   Q.  At the time you went out to fetch the bacon, had you any suspicion as you have stated? A.  Certainly I had at the time, and before—I had before he came into the house—I fetched it from a person of the name of——I cannot recollect—it was about a hundred yards off—I was absent about ten minutes or a quarter of an hour—on my return I found the prisoner sitting at the table in conversation with my wife—as soon as his bacon was cooked he had his supper, smoked his pipe, and went to bed—when I bolted him in his bed-room I said, "He is in, he shall never come out any snore by me"—if my face had been towards him I think he may have heard that, but my back being to wards him, he might or might not—it is a question I cannot answer—I sever bolted him in before—I have merely put the bolt to to keep the children in of a morning, but not bolted him  in—the window of his room looks into the front garden—it is about two feet and a half or three feet from the ground—I cannot tell whether he paid me for the old shoes which I made him—what I have no dealings with I never answer, I left it to my wife—he paid her in part, whether she had the whole I cannot tell, I know nothing of it—I have been ill this twelvemonth, and have had very little dealings with any person whatever—I have done very little shoe-making within the last twelve months—I was once in the police—I left it, I think, eight years ago—I went after the first division that went to Whitechapel—I was in it, as near as I can tell, a year and four or five months—I left it entirely of my own accord, without any complaint being made.
   Q.  Now, do I understand you distinctly that there was a great deal of conversation that you cannot recollect? A.
