SARAH GAD. I am the wife of Henry Gad; I keep a shop at Tottenham.
I remember the day on which the prisoner came to the shop-in consequence
of something that was told me, I looked at the till between 5 and 6 o'clock
in the evening; I found one 5s.-piece in it, and only one-I put it in the
detector, and bent it-I found an officer, and gave it him.
Arnsy Frank Benwell. I am shopboy to Edmund Reynolds, a baker,
at Tottenham. On 29th Aug. the prisoner came, about half past 5 o'clock
in the evening, for two penny buns--I served her-she gave me in payment
a half crown--my master came, and took the half crown out of my hand-I
am sure that the one he took was the one the prisoner gave me.
Prisoner. You did not touch it; it was a strange man took it up. Witness.
There was a gentleman there, he took it up, but it was not out of my sight-
he gave it to my master.
Court. Q. How did the gentleman come by it? 4. He was in the
shop- the prisoner put it on the counter, and I looked at it-the gentleman
took it, and gave it my master.
EDMUND REYNOLDS. I got a half crown on the 29th of Aug., from Ben-
well—there was a strange gentleman sitting there, but the lad gave me the
half crown; and he told me, in the prisoner's hearing, that he got it from the
prisoner, and he said it was bad—the prisoner said she was not aware of it,
ind it was given her the evening before by a gentleman-I kept her for half
an hour, and no one came just as she was going out the policeman came,
and I gave the half crown to him.
Prisoner. It was the gentleman who gave it you; the boy never touched
it at all. Witness. No, the boy gave it to me.
