NEW COURT.-Wednesday, April 15th, 1840.
zone
First Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
1286. CHARLES DUNECLIFT was indicted for a misdemeanor.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM PERDER. I keep a book-stall, in Carnaby-street, Golden-
square. Some time ago I was indebted 12. 138. to a person named Scott,
for which I received a summons-I attended the Court of Requests, at
Westminster, and was ordered to pay the sum in four weeks, with 38. costs-I
went to the Court, I think, on the 28th of November, to pay the money,
and saw the prisoner in the Court, opposite the place where the money
ought to be paid in, where they call people's names over-I was looking
about, and the prisoner said to me, “ What is your business ?" I said I
had come to pay in some money which I was sued for by Mr. Scott--I
had the order with me, and showed it him he said, “ It is all right, I can
take your money; it will be perfeetly right" - I had not known him be-
fore, nor did I employ him-I understood him that he could take my
money for the Court-I firmly believe he said he was an officer of the
Court-he said he could take my money, and it would be perfectly right
--he said he had not got a pen and ink there, but if I would walk with
him over the way, he would take my money and give me a receipt for it-
went over the way with him, and paid him the money, because I believed
him, from his own words, to be a lawful officer of the Court to receive the
money-I paid it under no other impression-I paid bim 1. 138. for the
debt, and 8s. for the costs-he gave me this receipt on a card (read)
-“Received the debt and costs due to Mr. Scott, of the Gas Company,
11. 16s. Charles Duneclift. Nov. 28" when he gave me this card, with
this receipt on it into my hand, I said, “Now, sir, is this perfectly right?"
- he said it was perfectly right, and I should never hear any more of it
on the 9th of March a distress was put into my house for the same debt,
and 88. more costs, and I had to pay it twice over-I never saw nor heard
any more of the prisoner. se
Cross-examined by Ms. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you go to the Court after-
wards? 4. No, he did not point out at all the place where the money
was to be paid I was never summoned in my life before.
CHRISTOPHER Curr. I am the chief clerk of the Westminster Court of
Requests. I am the proper person to receive money paid into Court there
- there is no other person authorised to receive money--the prisoner had
no authority to receive money, or to act in any way an order was issued
for William Pedder to pay the debt and costs, and it was not paid in
there are two orders up in the Court against agents receiving money,
GUILTY. Aged 38.-Confined One Year.ro
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
