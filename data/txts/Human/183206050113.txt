 1704.  LOUISA WHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of June , 2 half-crowns, and 3 halfpence, the monies of James Beckett , from his person.
   JAMES BECKETT. I am a shoemaker. I met the prisoner in Brick-lane at half-past ten o'clock at night, on the 24th of June; I was sober; I went with her, and gave her a quartern of gin - I took out 6d., and paid for it; I had then two half-crowns, and three halfpence - we went out together, and I felt her draw her hand from my left-hand waistcoat pocket; I accused her of stealing my money: she said I was a liar; I called the Policeman - she then denied it again; she was searched, and the money found on her.
   JOHN GREEN. (Police-constable H 91). The prosecutor accused the prisoner of robbing him; she denied it till she got to the door of the station - she then said she had robbed him, and would return the money; I found one half-crown, and three halfpence in her bosom - the other half-crown she gave from her pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met him; he asked me to have something to drink - we then came out, and he wanted to know if he could go home with me; I said No, not to where I lived - he then asked me to go some where else; I said I did not mind - we went; he told me to go into the house first, and gave me this money.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1705.  DANIEL BRYANT was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of Robert Passmore , from his person.
   ROBERT PASSMORE. I live in Orange-street, Bloomsbury, and am a book binder. I was returning home on the 17th of June, and was in Whitechapel- I had passed the church, and Savage gave me information; I felt and missed my handkerchief - I left a lady who was with me, went after the officer, and met him with the prisoner; this handkerchief is mine - it was produced to me; it had had J. E. on it, but part of it had been taken out - it had been given to me by an acquaintance.
   WILLIAM SAVAGE(Police-constable H 50). On the 17th of June I was in Whitechapel, about half-past three o'clock - I saw the prisoner and four others - he was close to the prosecutor; I saw him quit him, and run off - I spoke to the prosecutor, and then ran after the prisoner down Essex-street; I saw him put this handkerchief into his pocket - I pursued him into Wentworth-street, and from there to George-street; I observed the corner of this handkerchief out of his pocket - I stopped him in George-street, and took it from him; he said he had picked it up - I then met the prosecutor, and he claimed it; the prisoner sells wood in Whitechapel; I never knew any harm of him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1706.  ANN ROGERS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June , 2 silver spoons, value 5s.; 1 napkin, value 1s.; 3 dusters, value 1s.; 4 handkerchiefs, value 4s.; 1 basin, value 2d.; 1 glass, value 1s.; 3lbs. of tea, value 12s.; 8lbs. of sugar, value 6s., and 6lbs. of soap, value 3s., the goods of James Edward Mivart , her master.
 MR. CHURCHILL conducted the prosecution.
   JAMES EDWARD MIVART. I keep an hotel in Brook-street, Grosvenor-square; the prisoner was in my service. On the 6th of June I missed some articles - the officer was sent for; her box was searched, in my presence - a quantity of tea and sugar, some dusters, napkins, two silver spoons, some bars of soap, and a cut glass were found, some of which I can swear to; there were a great many more things, which I could not swear to.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. Can you swear to the tea? A. No; I can swear to the spoons - this basin matches the rest I have; it is full of sugar, and just as it was taken: the pattern is peculiar - these cloths have my name on them; she said they were master's property - she came into my service about a year and a half ago; I had no quarrel with her - I have found some fault with her about the business of the house; I never put my first in her face - I have a person named Frogget in my service; she has not been dismissed - she went away for a short time, on account of ill-health; I certainly never told the prisoner not to mention any thing that passed between me and Frogget; nothing improper ever took take place - the prisoner would not give up her key till the officer came, and told her to consider herself in custody.
   BENJAMIN SCHOFIELD. I am an officer. The prosecutor sent for me; the prisoner went up stairs and consented to have her boxes searched - she then made some objection, but when she was given into my custody she opened a cupboard, and gave the key of her box, in which were these articles, which have been in my care ever since- she said these dusters and the napkin were her master's, but the tea, sugar, and other things were not.
 Cross-examined. Q. She acknowledge these dusters and napkin belonged to her master? A. Yes, and the glass and basin, but not the other things, nor the spoons; she said she could produce others like them.
