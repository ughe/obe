GEORGE HENRY TIPPER. I manage the Criterion-on Saturday, 11th

Cross-examined. I told the policeman I only wanted you sent to the
station to have your name and address taken, and I did not want to
charge you.
JOSEPH COLLINS (Policeman CR 6). I took the prisoner, and received
these coins-he said that he was not guilty, and did not know it was bad-
I told him I should search him, and he pulled out a good florin and a bad
one-nothing was found on him.
Cross-examined. You walked by my side from the Criterion to Vine
Street Station.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This shilling is bad- these two florins taken from
the till are bad, and from the same mould—this florin found on the prisoner
is bad, but from a different mould.
The Prisoner produced a written defence, stating that he did not know ho
had any bad money.
GUILTY.-Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
885. MICHAEL JONES (17), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
MERS. LLOYD and A. METCALFE Prosecuted.
MARY ELIZABETH REES. I serve in the shop of Mrs. Bute, & confectioner,
of Great Turnstile-on 7th October I served a man, not the prisoner, with a
sponge cake-he gave me a half-crown: I told him it was bad, and gave it
back to him-he said, “Oh, is it?" and gave me a penny and left-Walter
Bute followed him.
WALTER BUTE. I was in my mother's shop and saw a man tender a bad
half-crown to the girl - I knew it was bail by the sound of it on the counter
- he paid with a penny and left-I followed him to a public-house about five
minutes' walk off, and joined the prisoner and a third man--they passed
something between them, and I followed them-they walked together on
the other side of the road--they saw me speak to a constable-we followed
them to Bow Street-two of them ran up Oxford Street and the prisoner
went down a court-I went round the other way and met the constable with
him--the other two got away.
