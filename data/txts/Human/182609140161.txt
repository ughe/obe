1740. THOMAS SLARK, JOSEPH CANE, and DANIEL LYNCH, were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Benjamin Busby , on the 27th of June , at St. Mary-le-bone , ( Edwin Busby and others therein being), and stealing 12 pairs of stockings, value 3l., the goods of the said Benjamin Busby.
 EDWIN BUSBY. I am shopman to Benjamin Busby - he is a  hosier and glover, and lives in Oxford-street, at the corner of John-street. On the 27th of June there were twelve pairs of silk stockings missing, which I had seen safe about half an hour before the officers came, and told me of the robbery; I had placed them near the window. They are worth 3l. I found the window had been cut.
   ANGELIOUS BERTRAUN. I saw the prisoners Slark and Cane in Oxford-street on the 27th of June; they were joined by another, who, I believe, was Lynch; they went up to the prosecutor's shop, and stood there two or three minutes; I then saw Slark run away, as fast as he could - I told Roberts to follow him; Cane came into the middle of the road, and I took him back to the shop, and kept him till Roberts brought back Slark. I saw the window was cut, and something taken out.
   THOMAS ROBERTS. I was with Bertraun, and saw the three prisoners at the prosecutor's window; I followed Slark into Cavendish-square; he there threw down these stockings, and struck me a blow - I then secured him. I had known Lynch before, and had passed them twice.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Was Lynch doing any thing? A. No - I did not see him do any thing.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I took Lynch on the 30th of July, from the information I received from Bertraun.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 CANE's Defence (written.) I was returning home through Oxford-street, a person laid hold of me, and asked what I had been doing; I did not know what he meant; he then took me to a haberdasher's shop, and accused me of cutting this window. He went out, and brought in another boy, whom I had never seen in my life time. I solemnly declare I knew nothing, either of the boys, or the robbery, and cutting the window - I was peaceably going along on my father's errand.
 SLARK -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 18.
 CANE -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 14.
 LYNCH -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 18.
 Third Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1741.  EDWARD ELLIOTT was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Bricks , about seven o'clock in the forenoon of the 14th of September , at St. Pancras , (the said John Bricks and other persons therein being) and stealing 1 box, value 1s.; 1 watch, value 2l.; 1 seal, value 10s.; 2 coats, value 3l. 10s., and 1 pair of trousers, value 1l., the goods of John Walker.
 JOHN BRICKS. I am a  journeyman tailor; I live at No. 14, Steven's-street, Tottenham-court-road, in the parish of St. Pancras. On the 14th of September, about half-past six o'clock in the morning, I was getting up to go to work; there were other persons in the house; the first thing that attracted my attention was the screaming of women, upon which I opened the parlour door where I slept; I looked up the stairs and saw the prisoner, who was a stranger, coming down; I looked at him with astonishment, and held up my hand to strike at him; I was not near enough to hit him, and he darted on me - I caught him by the collar; he was quite a stranger, and had no business in my house; I had fastened my house up, and I do not know at all how he got in; I found no part of it broken; he was not there when I went to bed; he tried to get away, but I held him fast - John Walker's property had been removed.
 JOHN WALKER. I have lodged in Mr. Brick's house five months. On the night of the 13th of September I fastened up the house and went to bed about ten o'clock: on the 14th I got up and went to work, about twenty-five minutes past six o'clock - I am sure I locked my room door; (it is the back-room on the second floor,) and hung the key up above John Brick's room-door where he sleeps; I had left my box in my room - I was fetched from work by Bricks; and when I got home my key was gone from the place where I had left it, and was in my room-door, and my box moved to the middle of the room - I had left the box securely locked, and in it a watch worth 2l. 10s.; a seal, and two coats not finished, worth 3l. 10s.; and a pair of of trousers, worth 1l.; they were my property - when I went out, I fastened the street-door with the latch - I put my shoulder to see if it was fast; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   ANN SULLIVAN. I live in this house, and have the front room, second floor - Mr. Walker lives in the back-room. On the morning of the 14th of September I heard the bolt of his door slip back very softly; I was in bed at the time, but I got up immediately just as I was, and threw open my door - I saw the prisoner with the chest on his back, ready to carry it away - I said nothing but Oh; and then I shut my door - I heard him throw down the box and go down stairs - I opened my door again, and screamed out Thieves! my landlord caught him at the bottom of the stairs; I know it was Mr. Walker's box.
   MARIAH NEALE. I live in this house; about half-past six o'clock I heard a scream at the top of the stairs; I got up and ran from the kitchen up stairs; I saw a stout tall man, not the prisoner, in his shirt sleeves going out; I ran and caught hold of him; he stamped upon my toes, and got away - I followed him into the street, and the door was shut against me; when I got in again I found my landlord scuffling with this prisoner.
   WILLIAM SHEPPARD. I am the street-keeper; the prisoner was brought to the watch-house.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. These are two persons who, I believe, are tailors, whom I have not been long acquainted with; one is a cutter-out, and lives in this house up stairs; he came to me on the 13th, and asked if I could carry a box for him? I said, I did not mind, and he sent for some liquor to my house - I did not go with him for five or six hours; when we got to the door of that house, he said,"Oh! I forgot myself, it does not matter to-night; if you come to-morrow morning it will do" - his brother-in-law Andrew, came the next morning; I went with him to the house, and he was standing at the door in his stockings; I went up stairs with him to bring the box away; he lifted up the box and put it on the bed, and said to me,"Do you think you can carry it?"
