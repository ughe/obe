1271. JOHN ARTHUR RAWSON, stealing 5 shirts, and other
articles, value 238.; the goods of Harriet Bryan, and another,
HARRIET BRYAN. I keep a shop, for the sale of ready-made linen, in
High-street, Camden-town-I have one partner. On 6th December the
prisoner came and asked to look at some shirts-I showed him some ; and be
selected some, which were to be sent on approbation to see whether they
fitted-be said if be approved of them be would give me an order for
half a dozen-he also asked for some boys shirts for his brother, and if
they fitted he would have half a dozen of them
they fitted he would have
they were all ready-made,
they would not want anything doing to them-be asked me to send them,
and to allow them to be left till Tuesday evening or Wednesday morning,
which I agreed to do I sent three or four gentlemen's shirts, two boy's
shirts and some collars, by my servant, Ann Humphreys on the Wednesday
morning, before I had sent for them back, the landlady of the house came
and told me that they had left her apartments--I saw no more of the prisoner
till he was in custody- the shirts were to be returned, and if they suited I
was to make him half a dozen-I am quite sure that these were only sent to
enable him to judge of the size and make--they were of different sizes.
Prisoner. I said I would keep them, and give an order for half a dozen.
Witness. No; these were to be returned.

ANN CUNNINGHAM. I am the wife of Michael Cunningham, a tailor, of
No. 32, Arlington-street-at the latter end of November, or beginning of

evening without giving any notice, or paying for any thing I did not see any
shirts on the premises, but I saw some collars- they took them away with
them.

sent-he never complained that they had not reached him-he never came
back, and never gave any other order.
