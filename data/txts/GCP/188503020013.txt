822. LEON TISSIER (43), Unlawfully obtaining 300 lb. of butter
by false pretences from Ulysse Poulet, with intent to defraud. 200
MR. GRAIN Prosecuted; ME. HALL Defended.
ULYSSE POULET (Interpreted). I am a butter merchant, in the province
of Jura, in France - I received this letter (produced). (A translation was
read. It was dated Jan. 7th, 1885, from 28, Colebrook Row, Duncan Terrace,
London, signed Leon Tissier, stating that he had obtained Mr. Poulet's address
from an article in a newspaper describing his butter as of a superior quality, and
if he was disposed to try the English market he could easily supply from 2,000
to 3,000 kilos a week, at a commission of 8 per cont.) In consequence of that
letter I sent 121 kilos of butter, worth 171. 168., addressed as stated, and
in course of post I recrived this letter, dated 12th January, enclosing a
cheque for 151. 158. 3d., on the Royal Exchange Bank, 75, Cornhill,
signed Leon Tissier-I sent the cheque to my bankers, and it was paid
I afterwards received this letter of 16th January-before receiving it I
had sent off a second consigoment of hutter of 227 kilos--I sent that
before the cheque was paid- I received another letter of 20th January,
enclosing a cheque for 291. 108. 2d.-I endorsed it away; it was returned
to my bankers unpaid, and they handed it to me.I have never been paid
that amount- when I received the cheque I believed it to be a good one
-in consequence of the first cheque being paid I sent 650 kilos more
butter, value about 2,400 francs-on 29th January I wrote to the
prisoner, the consignment had already been sent; I stopped it on the
night of 30th January-I received the telegram, "Through error,
banker's cheque returned ; sending bank-notes by nail"_I have never
received any bank-notes-I received this other letter. (This stated that is
consequence of the dishonesty of a clerk, the cheque, with others, had been
returned, from want of funds, but would do what was necessary to avoid a liko
docurrence.)
