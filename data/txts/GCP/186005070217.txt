SARAH CURTAIN. I am the wife of John Curtain, of Pomeroy-street,
Hatcham-on the night of 12th April I was standing at my door at half-past
11 o'clock, waiting for my husband-a policeman spoke to me as he passed
I have not seen him here to-day that I know of_I saw another policeman;
& short one they crossed over to the Victoria tavern, which is right opposite
and the short policeman stayed a little time, till the tall policeman came and
went into the Victoria, and brought out a pint pot those are the two
policemen, (Lnuscombe and Cox) they partook of it, the tall one then took
it back-Orutcher, the milkman, came up, and a joke passed between them
Crutcher was very tipsy-Mr. Greedy was turning them all out of his
house, as it was 12 o'clock, and the prisoner came out and crossed to
the kerb I knew him before going backwards and forwards they were
on the edge of the pavement, against the sign-post, drinking the beer, and
Crutcher in front of them-I did not observe how the prisoner came out;
it was very dark; they had douted the lamps- I went in half an hour
before to ask for my husband, and the prisoner was very tipsy in front
of the bar, but I did not notice what state he came out in, whether
steadily or not my husband came out after the prisoner, and persuaded
him to go quietly up the street-I remained at my door the whole
time till Vowles went away-my husband pursued him, but he said he
would not go home, because he knew his mates were not gone home when
the policeman went away my husband took him up the street, and laid hold of
his arm-Vowles needed support; he was very tipsy-when he was gone the
policeman knocked at the publie-house door-Mr. Greedy said, "Who is
there!” and while they were talking, the prisoner came down again, and
