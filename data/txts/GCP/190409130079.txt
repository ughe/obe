Ernest APPLETON (39 K.) About 5.30 on Tuesday, August 16th, I
was called to 47, Tanner's Hill-after the fire was over I entered-I saw
paraffin in the first floor front room on the bed, on the floor, on the
picture cords, and on the mantel shelf-I cautioned the prisoner; he
said, “I had a row with my wife, and poured paraffin over the shop and
bed room and set light to them "-I told him I should take him into
custody-as he was going down the stairs, he said, "I did not tell the
children or the servant I was going to do it; they went out at the back
door "-when charged he made no reply-he appeared very much upset.

BENJAMIN ROBERT. I am agent for the Norwich Union Fire Insurance
Co. at 6, High Street, Deptford-the prisoner took out a polioy in July
last for £100 on 47, Tanners' Hill-he last paid the renewal premium on
July 11th--there was £40 on the shop and fixtures, &c., and £60 on the
household goods.
The prisoner, in his defence, said that he did not recollect what occurred,
but that his wife had had a row with him which grieved him; that he had
had a drop of beer, and that he was sorry.
Evidence for the Defence.
Luke Henry MOORE. The prisoner is a hard working man; I have
known him eight years, he was always good to his wife and children;
and I have never seen him in drink or in trouble-his wife had a very
sulky temper but he never struck her.
LENA CORBETT. The prisoner and his wife have good characters, but
sie is a bad tempered girl; the prisoner is a good husband, and has lived
with her seven years, not having raised his hand to her; she owns it is
her own fault and that before the fire she had upset her husband.
Not Guilty.
