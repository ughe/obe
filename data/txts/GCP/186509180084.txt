intent to defraud.
Thomas FRANCIS Shaw. I am the sub-manager of the Birmingham
Banking Company at Birmingbam-at the latter end of March the prisoner
Taylor came to the bank, and had a conversation with me about opening an
account-he wanted to know our rates of commission-I asked him the
nature of his account, and asked him whether he should want to pay in bills
-he stated he should want to pay in trade bills, and should require to draw
against them-I then asked him how many would be current at one time-
he said, "Some 4,0001. or 5,0001." -I then referred him to the manager-be
subsequently opened an account at the bank-we were in the habit of
furnishing our customers with credit papers--this is one of them (produced)
I believe it is Mr. Taylor's handwriting-there are five entries on the
credit paper-among others this bill of 2791. 88. 9d.-I believe the endorse-
ment to this bill of exchange to be in Mr. Taylor's handwriting-the
endorsement to this other bill of 1341. is also in Taylor's handwriting-1
know nothing whatever of the other part of the bills - both these bills were
paid into the bank by Taylor (The first bill was for 2791. 88. 9d., dated April
i0th, 1865, drawn by J. Mardlin, accepted by H. W. Ward, 36, Seething
lane, endorsed J. Mardlin, Benjn. Taylor. The other was dated April 10th,
