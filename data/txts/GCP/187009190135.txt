JOSEPH FLEXMAN (Detective Officer). On the night of 8th August I took
the prisoner at 2, Wilson Place-I told her it was for having set fire to her
house on the 5th-she said " Oh, my God! I am innocent of that charge"
-on the way to the station she said "Well, I know the house has been set
on fire, but I do not know who by. I heard three knocks at my front door,
I went and opened the door, but I could not see anybody; I turned round
and saw a blue light from the kitchen, and heard something like an explo-
sion, but I do not know what it was."

THOMAS DENNYS. I live at 3, Providence Place, Vassal Road, and was the
prisoner's errand boy for about a month before the fire she had no lodgers
during that time- I left the house on the night of the fire at about 9.45
there was then no one in the house but Miss Gent, as far as I know I had
not been in the house all day-I saw nobody there but her when I left-I
had not been in the house during the day, only till about 9 o'clock--there
vas a lady in the front parlour, but Miss Gent let her out-I Baw no one
elke there-Miss Gent let me out, and I heard her shut the door-I did not
hear how she did it up, I was in the back parlour before I left—there was
no fire in the grate-one gas was alight-I did not go into the front parlour
or kitchen—there was a paper ornament in the stove in the back parlour
I did not notice the carpet or curtains.
Cross-examined. Q. Used your mother to do the charing for Miss Gent?
4. Yes, and my sister used to sleep with her, I do not know how long-

had the upper rooms whitewashed and papered ready for them-she is a

things I used to work in a little shop opposite them.
MR. COOPER. Q. Was your sister at home that night when you went
