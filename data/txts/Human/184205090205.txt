ANN MART RUDGE. I am the niece of Richard Carpenter, a licensed victualler, of Church-street, Greenwich. On the 6th of April the prisoner came to the tap-room of the house—I heard her go up-stairs—I had made the bed where the sheets were about one o'clock, and at eight I went up to show a person to bed and the sheets were gone.
   Prisoner.  A girl gave, them to me and told me to pawn them, which I did. JOHN MORRIS. I am apprentice to a pawnbroker in Greenwich. I pro duce a sheet pawned by the prisoner on the 6th of April—she said she pawned it for a person named Smith, living in Bridge-street, Greenwich—I found the name of M. R. Carpenter upon it.
   Prisoner.  The female who was with me told me to pawn it in that name; I was very much in liquor. JOSEPH BUCKM ASTER( police-constable  42 R. ) I apprehended a person named Daley who was in company with the prisoner—I apprehended the prisoner on the 7th of April—she was sober. RICHARD CARPENTER. I keep the public-house. I know this sheet to be mine—the "M" is my wife's initial—the prisoner was in my house on the evening of the 6th of April, and was heard to go up stairs—the sheets were safe on the bed at six o'clock, and were missed at eight—the prisoner had been drinking in the tap-room that day and the day before, and so had Daley.
   Prisoner.  Daley was up stairs with the soldiers there; I was in liquor and hardly knew what I did. Witness.  I had not seen the prisoner above four times before—there is a beck entrance to my house, so that they might go up stairs without being seen. GUILTY. Aged 33.— Confined Three Months.

JOHN BOYLE was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of April , 1 tea-caddy, value 2 s  .; 2 spoons, value 2 s  .; and 1 pair of sugar-tongs, value 1 s  .; the goods of Andrew Haynes ; and that he had been previously con victed of felony. ELIZABETH HAYNES. I am the wife of Andrew Haynes, and live at No. 3, Church-street, Deptford. My husband is a tract distributor to the Christian Friend Society—I lost a tea-caddy from the front room of my house, between eleven and twelve o'clock on Saturday, the 16th of April, with a silver spoon, a caddy-spoon, and plated sugar-tongs—I keep a school myself, and leave the street-door ajar—this is my caddy, there is tea and sugar in it. JOHN GITTINS. I am an out-pensioner of Greenwich Hospital. About eleven o'clock on the 16th of April the prisoner, in company with two more, came towards me—one of them asked me if I would have some tea, and gave me some in my hand—I thanked him—it was loose in his hand—he said, "Down that gate there is a tea-caddy, go and take it"—I stood and looked at it—they said "That is it, it is all right"—I went down the court, put the tea into it, and called to Mrs. Stacey, "Have you lost anything, this is stolen?"—they left me—I left the caddy with Mrs. Stacey, while I went after them—I met the prisoner and two more in the same spot where they gave me the tea—I said, "Did that caddy belong to you?"—"Yes," said they—I said, "Come with me"—they came with me down the alley, and one of them told Mrs. Stacey to give it me, for it was their property—the prisoner had told me that it was his property—Stacey gave
