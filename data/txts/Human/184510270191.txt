JOHN SHARPE. I am servant to Mr. Hay—on the 4th of Aug. he sent me with a check—I do not know whether this is the check—it was wrapped in a bit of paper—what Mr. Hay gave me I took to Mr. Bennett's—I gave the check to the prisoner—I did not tell him anything—he got change and gave me the change and this bill—I took it back to my master—there were five sovereigns, and some silver—I did not count the silver.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Had you any conversation with the prisoner about the amount? A.  No, I merely gave him the check in a piece of paper—I counted the sovereigns I received back, but not the silver—I just took what he gave me—I was in Mr. Bennett's shop when I gave the prisoner the check—there were one or two people in the shop—I do not know where he got change for the check—he went out to get it, and came the way of the London-road.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Whatever money the prisoner gave you, you car ried to your master? A.  Yes—I carried it all the way in my hand. DEBORAH BENNETT. I am the wife of Solomon Bennett—I recollect, on the 4th of Aug., the prisoner gave me 4 l . 7 s ., and said Mr. Hay bad paid his bill—it might have been about eleven or twelve o'clock in the day—the prisoner then went to the book, and I saw him writedown 4 l . 7 s .—this is the book, and this is his handwriting, "Hay paid bill, 4 l . 7 s ."
   Cross-examined. Q.  In whose handwriting is this bill and receipt? A.  The items I cannot say—my husband makes out the bills sometimes, and sometimes Harris does—I think this is my husband's writing—my sight is very bad—Mr. Hay owed 5 l . 7 s ., and the prisoner gave me 4 l . 7 s .—I did not know the amount Mr. Hay owed—I am not always in the shop—I know the prisoner brought an action against us. NATHAN HARRIS. I have been book-keeper to Mr. Bennett for many years—this bill is my writing—I make out all the bills—Mrs. Bennett knows nothing about them—this receipt to it is the prisoner's writing—on Sunday, the 10th of Aug., I went to the prosecutor's books, as I do every Sunday—no one writes in the ledger but myself—here is the com mon waste book, and everything that occurs is entered in this—here is another book, in which the entry is made from that common waste-book, and in this it is entered 5 l ., in the prisoner's writing—this is for me to enter into the ledger—this is 5 l . only, but no shillings; that led to tne finding it out—here is, "Hay paid bill, 5 l ."
   JURY . Q.  If you had got the 5 l ., you would have been 7 s . short? A.  Yes—that caused the finding it out—I said it must have been 5 l . 7 s ., and then it was only 4 l . 7 s .
