1706. THOMAS WALL was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , 1 tea-kettle, value 2s. 6d., the goods of George Hoffman.
   SAMUEL RODWAY. On the 15th of September I saw the prisoner lurking about Hoffman's shop, at the corner of Wilmington-place, Spa-fields; he went in, and came out, in five minutes, with this tea-kettle in his hand; I I pursued, and took him near the toll-gate.
   ANTHONY LAWRENCE. I heard the cry of Stop thief! and caught him in my arms.
 GEORGE HOFFMAN. This kettle is mine - it stood about three yards within my shop.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Whipped and Discharged.
 1707.  WILLIAM BATES was indicted for stealing, on 15th of September , 1 piece of linen, containing 5 yards in length, value 2s., the goods of Samuel Cowley and Henry Hewetson.
   SAMUEL BEAMAN. I am carter to Samuel Cowley and Henry Hewetson. On the 15th of September I was bringing five bales of sheeting from Hoare's wharf to our warehouse, in Cateaton-street; I met the prisoner on the wharf, whom I had known some years - he had been a carter to Mr. Evans - he is now patrol of St. Botolph, Aldgate; we went to the Coopers' Arms, public-house, to have some beer and my dinner - it was then between one and two o'clock; while I was at the house he went out and got my dinner - and as he went out, I saw through the window, that he put his hand on the rail of the waggon, and his foot on the wheel - but I did not see that he got into it, though he raised himself up; he returned in about five minutes, and after I had done my dinner, I drove away; when I got on to the Minories I missed a piece of sheeting-wrapper from my waggon; I think it might be five yards, or more; I had put it in, and the lock-chain over it. I had never said that previous to my going to the Cooper's Arms, he had seen me double it up, and put the lock-chain over it; he must have seen it when he raised himself up - he was near the cloth, and could get it; I went to his house that night - I found his wife at the gin-shop; I went with the officer and found the prisoner on his beat - he denied it to the officer - but when I went up to him, he said, if I went home with him, he would give it to me; I found it there.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did not the officer tell him he had stolen it? A. No; he said, "There has been a waggon robbed - do you know any thing about it?" I did not say. I would give him something to make an apron; I did not think any thing about his putting his hand and foot on the waggon. We drank the beer, and came up as far as Butcher-row, and had some rum; I did not ask the prisoner to hold the whip while I went up Morgan's-rents; I left the whip inside, and he brought it out, but I did not desire him.
   JAMES WITNEY. I am a headborough. I received information from the witness, at a quarter before ten o'clock, and went to the prisoner on his beat, in Nightingale-lane, with Beaman - I went up to the prisoner, and said, "Bates - you are charged with stealing a piece of linen out of a waggon to day;" he said he had not; I then stepped five or six yards back, and Beaman went up, and said."Bates"- I did not hear more, but when they had been in conversation about a minute Bates said, in a louder voice, "Yes, I did take it out of the waggon - if you come home with me you shall have it;" we all three went to his house, and, after searching a few minutes, we found this linen on the floor, at the foot of the stairs, in the lower room - the prisoner took it up. I took him to the watch-house.
 Cross-examined. Q. When you went up did not you charge him with stealing it? A. Yes - the carman knew what he was about - I think he was sober.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 50.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined One Month.
 1708.  MARY MAHONEY and MARY MANNING were indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , 1 watch, value 50s.; 1 hat, value 10s.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s., and 1 crown piece, the property of James Henderson , from his person.
 JAMES HENDERSON. I am a  sailor, and belong to the Nicholas, a collier, which laid off the Tower. On Saturday night, the 5th of August, I left the vessel at half-past eight o'clock, and met the prisoners at a public-house in Ratcliff-highway; they were there when I went in; they came to me - Manning asked me to give her a glass of gin; I gave her 1s. to get half-a-pint for the prisoners and two old women, who were there - they had a glass a piece - I had nothing but a single pint of porter; I left the house with the prisoner about eleven o'clock; I went with them to a house I never was in before, in Blue Anchor-court; I then went up stairs with Mahoney; I undressed myself, and laid my clothes on the table; I went to bed, and she laid on the bed with me - she then said she wanted to go down stairs for a minute, and she left me in bed - she did not come up again to my knowledge; I fell asleep, and slept till two o'clock as near as I can guess - there was no light in the room; I then went to my clothes, and missed my watch, hat, and handkerchief; I am quite certain I had put my watch on the table when I went to bed, and the crown-piece was in my pocket; I listened for awhile, and then an old woman came up, and said, "There are no girls here belonging to you;" Manning then came up, and said she knew nothing about it, but she would fetch the other to give me my things; I came down, and found Mahoney outside the door, who said she would give me a glass of something, and give me my things; I said I should like to know the number of the house, but I could not find it; we then went to a public-house to get something - there were a great many people - the prisoners got through them, and got out of the back door; I told the landlord, who said he would search the house, but they were not to be found - he then told me to go out, and see for the watchman, which I did, and the prisoners were taken; I have never seen any of the property since.
