   Prisoner. Q . You mentioned at Bow-street that you found these articles in a room over the washhouse? A.  Yes—I was never in the washhouse, I supposed it to be one—I am not a mechanic—I believe I called this instru ment a dagger, when I was first examined—I do not know whether I called it a pike—I have seen a burnisher—this is not like any burnisher I have ever seen—I should not imagine it to be one.
 (  Mr. Thomas Cope read the paragraph from "  The Times," as in page  670; the extracts from the record of the conviction of John Mitchell were also read. ) Prisoner's Defence.  It is not my intention to follow the learned gentleman through all the mazes of the law—first, because I conceive that the late stringent enactments that have been passed for the purpose of crushing those who have embraced the same political opinions which I this day stand here to advocate, are no doubt sufficiently explicit in themselves, to give him all the advantage which a prosecutor requires to enable him to procure a conviction; and secondly, it would be utterly out of my power to do so—being immured in a gaol, I was of course obliged to submit to the discipline of the place; and although I was supplied with materials for writing, and such purposes, yet I was denied the use of those books "which I considered were essential for my defence; this may seem rather strange, nevertheles it is to fact, and this restriction was carried to such an extent, that not only were political pamphlets refused me, but even a sight of the Penny Cyclopœdia , containing a definition of the word "Sedition," had the chaplain's veto affixed to it; and further, I could obtain no knowledge of the specific nature of the charge against me until Saturday, when I heard it very imperfectly in this Court, when I was called up to plead; but whether this is an arrangement for the distinction of political and criminal offenders, or whether it is an established rule of all the prisons in the kingdom, I am not sufficiently con versant with criminal jurisprudence and prison discipline to determine; but I trust you will at once perceive, that labouring under such disadvantages, it places me in a position not at all calculated to meet the charge, particularly when that charge has been preferred by a gentleman who stands at the top of his profession.
