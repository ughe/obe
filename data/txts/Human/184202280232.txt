   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you not tell Cannon or Lee that you did not believe the prisoner was the man? A.  No, I never said any such thing—he was not pointed out to me on Mr. Rolls' premises—I saw him on Wednesday or Thursday—I went down by the wish of Mr. Humble—he did not mention the prisoner's name to me—I first heard his name at Union-hall—Mr. Humble took me to the premises to know whether I could show him the man—I saw fifteen or sixteen persons there—Day was not there—Mr. Humble asked me the description of the man, and I said I believed the prisoner was the man—I saw him three or four minutes on the morning he was in the cart—I was close to him—he did not leave the cart—I saw down as far as his knees—I have talked to the two men that were with me about it—I have not got a little more certain since that conversation.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How long have you worked at the mills? A.  Twenty-two years—I never saw the prisoner before to my knowledge. WILLIAM CANNON. I work at Mr. Davis's emery-mills. I was going to breakfast with Ford and Lee at half-past eight—when we got to Weston-street I saw Mr. Rolls' cart—the head of his horse was standing towards Snow's-fields, and there was another cart at the tail, with the horse's head towards Long-lane—they were standing close to one another—I saw two pieces of oil-cloth in the little cart, and they were in the act of getting a third piece out of Mr. Rolls' cart—I cannot swear to the prisoner as being one of the men, because he was at work in the cart—I believe, by his dress, he is the man—I could not see his face—he was holding out a piece to Mendes, the other man—I knew Mendes by sight—Mr. Rolls' cart turned into Snow's-fields—the other turned to go up Maze-pond.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were not before the Magistrate? A.  Yes—I have had a subpoena since—I did not stand and look at the cart—the only opportunity I had of seeing the men was while I passed.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was Rolls' a tilted cart? A.  Yes—I went to Mr. Rolls to see the cart—it was the same. DANIEL LEE. I am a fellow-workman of Cannon's—I was going to breakfast with Ford and Cannon—we went through Weston-street—I saw Mr. Rolls' tilted cart and a small cart—I did not see any man in the titled cart—I have seen the man with the little cart before—the carts were stand ing tail to tail—there were two pieces of oil-cloth in the little cart as we passed I saw them put in a third piece in—Mr. Rolls' cart then turned up towards King-street, and the other cart went down Old Weston-street—Mendes went to the corner of the Rose public-house and turned back again.
