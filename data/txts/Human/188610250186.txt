At 20 minutes to 12 on the night of 2nd October I was in the Clapham Road, and saw a tram coming along from Clapham Common with a number of men singing on the top—when the tram was about to stop I heard some one on the top halloa out "I am stabbed!"—I ran up to the top of the tram, and saw the prisoner being held down and the prosecutor bleeding from his head, hand, and breast—I apprehended the prisoner, and handed him over to another constable—he had been drinking, but knew what he was doing—he said "I don't care for my head; I would as soon have it off as on"—Harris gave me this knife covered with blood. WIILLIAM HARRIS( Policeman W R  19). I was with Rivenhall on 2nd October—I picked up this knife from the ground all over fresh blood on the left side of a tramcar, which was the side on which the prisoner was sitting. CHALES DOWNING. I am a surgeon of Clapham—at 20 minutes to 12 on 2nd October the prosecutor was brought to me—he was bleeding very profusely from the left side of his head, and had an incised and lacerated wound on the middle finger of his left hand, which had cut away the tendons of the finger, and an artery was bleeding very freely; I secured this—evidently he had lost a considerable quantity of blood—on his index finger was a clean incised wound—on the right side of his chest, a little way from the breast bone, between his fourth and fifth ribs, there was a wound about three-quarters of an inch deep, obliquely, not penetrating the chest cavity—he had another incised cut behind his right ear, and a scratch on the ear itself—the wound on the finger, if not attended to, might have bled to a dangerous extent—the wounds might have been caused by this knife—the wounds on the head and chest fitted exactly with the point of it—he will probably lose the use of his finger; it will be perfectly stiff.
