Mary Moran. I am the wife of Timothy Moran, of Star-court, St.
George the Martyr, Southwark. The prisoner was tipsy- I took her into
my house as she said her husband was angry with her - I left her in the
house on the 20th of Jan. when I went out I returned between seven and
eight o'clock in the evening-she was gone, I found my husband's box
broken open, and missed the articles now produced.
John RUSSELL (policeman.) I apprehended the prisoner in George-alley
on Sunday morning, the 21st of Jan., and found a shawl, shirt, and handker-
chief, in the room she was in.
William MARTIN (policeman.) I found two shawls, two petticoats, &
gown, sheet, and other articles-some in the room the prisoner was in, and
some in the next room, in George-alley--the prisoner was intoxicated, and
said nothing

789. PATRICK MURPHY was indicted for burglariously breaking and
entering the dwelling-house of Walter Cutchy, in the night of the 8th of
Jan., at St. George the Martyr, Southwark, with intent to steal, and stealing
therein, 3 coats, value 12.; and 1 waistcoat, value 5s. ; his property
WALTER CUTCHY. I ar a hosier and clothier, and live at 67, Blackman-
street, Borough. On the night of the 8th of Jan. my house was closed, and
all safe-I went to bed at eleven o'clock-I was awoke about two by the
policeman I went with him into the shop, and found one of the fan-lights
over the street-door broken-it was safe when I went to bed-I missed three
coats, and one waistcoat, which 1 have not recovered found a stick and
two waistcoats behind the door--they bad been removed from the place they
occupied over-night.
Richard JAMES ( policeman.) On Tuesday morning, the 9th, I was on
duty, and saw the prisoner standing on another one's shoulders, with his arm
through the prosecutor's fan-light-1 ran across the road, and as soon as he
dropped off the other one's shoulders, I took him into custody, and conveyed
him to the station-I then returned to the shop, called Mr. Cutchy up, and
on going into the shop he missed three coats and a waistcoat-I saw some
broken glass on the table, and a stick and two waistcoats, which had been
knocked down--the goods were banging upon brass rods, inside the fan-light
- I dare say this stick would bring the goods off the rod to the window
there is no hook to it, or anything peculiar about it.
