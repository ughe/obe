 Cross Examination.
 What are you? - A lighterman, and live in Artichoke-lane at the bottom of Virginia street.
 Did you see Sadler there? - Yes.
 Was Wilkinson there? - Yes.
 Did they all come in together? - No; Downs came in first, I believe; Sadler and Wilkinson came in together.
 What liquor did the prisoners, Sadler and Wilkinson, drink? - Beer, I believe; I did not see any thing else.
 Did you see the fire at Lebarty's? - I did not see it; I d id not hear of it till the next morning, if I had gone out perhaps I might have seen it, but I did not take any notice about it.
 Were there a good many people in the Ship? - I cannot say how many.
 Twenty or thirty? - I cannot say.
 Did no-body say any thing to you about Lebarty's house being destroyed that night in the Ship? - No, no-body said any thing to me.
   SARAH HART sworn.
 I was at Mr. M'Kenzie's, the Ship, the night Mr. Lebarty's house was destroyed; the prisoner and I went home together at about half after twelve o'clock.
 What became of him then? - He went to bed and did not get up till between seven and eight next morning.
 Cross Examination.
 You live with the prisoner? - Yes; I have lived with him nine years.
 Are you married to him? - No.
 - CLARKE sworn.
 I am servant to Mrs. Hart.
 Do you recollect what time your master and mistress came home the night Lebarty's house was destroyed? - Yes; they came home, as near as I can guess, at half after twelve o'clock, and went to bed; I locked the door; the prisoner never went out again till seven or eight o'clock in the morning, for I had the key in my pocket.
 Cross Examination.
 How came you to be so exact to the time? - The watch hung up, and I saw what time it was when he went to-bed.
   HANNAH SMYTH sworn.
 I work at my needle, and lodge at Mrs. Hart's.
 Do you recollect at what o'clock Sarah Hart and Downs came home on the night Lebarty's house was destroyed? - Between twelve and one, I was up at the time.
 Who was the first who got up in the morning? - I was; Mr. Downs got up about eight or rather sooner.
 Cross Examination.
 Do you always take notice what time Downs goes to bed and gets up? - Being always in the house I do.
 What time did Mrs. Hart go to bed last night? - I believe about the same hour.
 What time did she get up this morning? - About eight o'clock.
 What are you? - I work at my needle, or any thing else I can get to do among the neighbours.
 - NEWPORT sworn.
 I am keeper of New-Prison; the prisoner has been a servant of mine three or four months; he has behaved with the strictest honesty, according to the best of my knowledge; he was very attentive to my business; he was at Justice Clarke's office when he came to me.
 How long have you been keeper of New Prison? - Six or eight months.
