 1339.  WILLIAM ALABASTER was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of April , 1 handkerchief, value 1d., the goods of a man unknown , from his person.
   GEORGE FREDERICK BOOTH. I am inspector of St. Brides' watch; I was on duty on Ludgate-hill about nine o'clock in the evening, on the 23d of April; I saw the prisoner go into a crowd; I watched him, and saw him take a handkerchief from a person unknown, and put it into his right hand pocket; I seized him, and just at that time there came a sudden rush in the crowd, which took the gentleman away - I took the prisoner to the watch-house; I am a chandler by trade, and have been an officer about twelve months - this is the handkerchief.
 Prisoner's Defence. This man takes a false oath: I was going up Ludgate-hill, and saw the handkerchief on the ground - I took it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1340.  THOMAS ROBERTS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of May , 13 pairs of unmade trousers, value 3l.6s., and 1 pair of spectacles, value 1s., the goods of John Pearson.
 JOHN PEARSON. I am a  tailor, and live in Bell-alley, near the Bank; I had thirteen pairs of trousers in my apartment - on the 8th of May at half-past five o'clock in the morning, I went out, and left the prisoner there; he went away, and returned at seven o'clock; he staid there with me until eight o'clock - I then went out, and returned in twenty minutes; the prisoner was gone, and the property likewise; I had left my spectacles on the bed - they were gone, as well as the trousers; I have never got the trousers since - the prisoner was taken on the Friday evening; I charged him with it - he said he knew nothing about it; he is a shoe-maker, and had been at work for my master the day before.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Was any other person there on the day of the robbery? A. No - a person worked there, but he was not there then; I do not know that he had not been there while I was out; I saw the trousers at five o'clock when I made my bed - the other man should have come to work at six; I had seen the prisoner working with spectacles the day before, but he did not borrow mine - I had had no quarrel with him; I was not drunk the day before - the prisoner and the other man were; I took up the other man.
 COURT. Q. Who is the other man? A. A man named Joiner; I went to his lodging - the prisoner and he worked together; the prisoner did not return to me.
   MARY ANN JAMES. I live where Pearson does. On the morning of the 8th of May, at twenty minutes past eight o'clock, I was at my own door - the prisoner passed by me; he stumbled, and his hat fell off - he took it up and put it on; I saw it was full of something - he also seemed bulky round the waist.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is it anything unusual for a tailor to take home work? A. His hat was full - he could hardly put it on.
   HENRY AMBROSE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on Friday evening, and found these spectacles on him - he said he knew nothing of the prosecutor or the property, and was never at the place.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you find any more than one pair of spectacles on him? A. Yes, a pair which he said was his own; he afterwards acknowledged that these belonged to Mr. Pearson, though at first he said he did not know him, and his name was Wilkinson.(Spectacles produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. He allowed me to take the spectacles to work with - I put them into my pocket going out.
     GUILTY. Aged 46. Confined Six Months.
 1341.  THOMAS JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of May , 18 yards of ribbon, value 27s., and 14 yards of other ribbon, value 18s., the goods of James Harris.
   CHARLES HENRY MARSHALL. I am in the employ of James Harris, haberdasher, of Fore-street, City. On the 28th of May, at half-past eight o'clock in the evening, the prisoner and a boy came into the shop together; I saw the boy draw his hand from a drawer of ribbons on the counter, and give something to the prisoner - I taxed them with it, and before I could get round the counter they ran off, and the prisoner dropped these ribbons against the door: they are my master's property; I followed, and never lost sight of him till the officer took him.
   JAMES BUTLER RIDGWAY. I saw the prisoner run out and drop something at the door; I ran on to London-wall, and the officer had got him - Marshall was on before me.
   ABRAHAM HAM. I am an officer. I stopped the prisoner - Marshall was pursuing him; these are the ribbons.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went for three-quarters of a yard of shoe ribbon - the boy came in after me; the witness said, "Have you taken any ribbon" - he said, No; he ran out and dropped the ribbon at my feet.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
