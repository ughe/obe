Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You found them in a desti tute state? A.  Yes, in a room with a great number of old beef bones, and other things. JAMES PARKER. I am brought from White Cross-street prison. I did live at No. 41, Ely-place—I called at the request of Mr. Burton, on the prisoner Hobbs, at New Manor-street, Chelsea—(I cannot recollect when it was)—I found Hobbs at a beer-shop—I told him, that Mrs. Streeton, of the New Forest, Southampton, had given a reference to him as to her respectability—he said he did not like to give characters in general, but the was a very respectable woman—I asked him if she had rented a house of his brother for seven or eight years—he said she had, and had always paid her rent regularly—I asked him what establishment she kept—he said, a phaeton, a pair of ponies, and I am not sure whether he said three or four servants—I told him she was about taking a house of Mr. Burton, at 40 l . a-year rent—he said she had rented houses of double that rent, and he should have no objection to let her one, only his own were not in a sufficient state of forwardness—he told me I should find her a close-fisted woman, but I should not have to call a second time for the rent.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did you make any memorandum of the conversation? A.  I did not—I afterwards inquired, and found that Mr. Hobbs passed himself off as a builder, but he was not known as such—I inquired of Sergeant Tedman, but did not make inquiries in the neigh bourhood—I am quite sure that Mr. Hobbs did not say that Mrs. Streeton had told him she kept a phaeton—he did not say that he had seen it—I am not in Whitecross-street for debt, but through a mistake in a legal process—I have made application to a Judge to get out, and shall be out in a few days.
   GOODMAN . ( Looking at an agreement )—This is my name, as a witness to this agreement—I saw Mrs. Norman sign it, in the name of Streeton, on the day it bears date.
 (  The agreement was here read for letting the house for three years certain ,
   and was dated 27th October , 1838.) WILLIAM SMALLPIECE WHITTINGTON. I am clerk to Messrs. Gill, wine-merchants, of Mark-lane. The prisoner Norman called at my em ployers' counting-house on the 29th of October—she said she had taken a cottage of Mr. Burton, at Finchley, that he had named us as his wine-mer chants, and she wanted some wine—I asked what wine she required, and she gave an order for three dozen of sherry, three dozen of port, and two gallons of brandy; and here is a subsequent order, in her writing, on the 1st of November, for one dozen of madeira, and two gallons of ram—the amount of the whole was 24 l . 3 s . 6 d .—she said I was to put down the prices as low as possible, as she always paid cash for the goods—the goods were sent by Drewett, the carrier—I went down to the house on a Monday, I think, the 5th or 6th of November—I found the house in custody of an officer, but I could not find the bottles or wine, or any part of the spirits-from information I afterwards received, I went to a house in Bridge-water-square, where I found some wine, and two gallons of rum in a jar—I could identify the jar of spirits as one we had forwarded to Finchley, and our cellarman could identify the wine—the bottles had been sealed when they left our counting-house, but the seals had been cut off close to the bottles.
   COURT . Q.  What house did you go to in Bridgewater-square? A.  No. 15—the landlord came for me, and I went with him.
