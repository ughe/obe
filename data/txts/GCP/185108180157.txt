1742. ROBERT GOODRIGHT, stealing 1 sovereigo, and 1 51. bank-
note; the moneys of Jobn Stevens. MR. HORRY conducted the Prosecution.
DAVID LAWSON. I am a quartermaster-sergeant of Royal Artillery, at
Woolwich. On 1st July, I received from Cox's agent, eight 51. Bank of
England notes, Nos. 44,439 to 44,446, all dated 12th June, 1851-I gave
three of them to John Stevens on the same day.
John STEVENS. I am a sergeant of Royal Artillery, at Woolwich. On
1st July, I received three 51. notes, dated 12th June, 1851, from Lawson-
I had no other notes- I changed two at the canteen the same day, and locked
up the third with a sovereign in my desk, in the barrack-room--I saw them
safe on Sunday the 6th-on the Monday, I and all the men belonging to that
room were out on drill-the prisoner was left in, and had access to that room,
15 well as the others on the Tuesday evening, I missed the note and sove-
reign--the desk was still locked, and I had kept the key in my pocket; it is
a common lock.
THOMAS MICKLEFORD. I am assistant to Messrs. Davis, pawnbrokers, of
Woolwich. On Tuesday, 8th July, the prisoner came and redeemed a watch,
which had been pledged on 30th June for 11.-he gave me this 5l. note in
payment, which I changed for him (produced), it is No. 44,446, dated 12th
June, 1851- it was a similar silver hunting watch to this one produced by
Newell.
JOHN NEWELL (policeman, R 340.) I took the prisoner on 12th July,
and told him he was charged on suspicion of stealing a 5l.-note, and asked
him where he got the 51.-note from he had changed at Messrs. Davis's he
said he had had no 51.-note, and had not been there--I asked if he bad taken
a watch out of pledge-he said, “That you must find out"-I took him to
Davis's, and he was identified as the man who took the wateh out I searched
bim, and found this silver watch and chain (produced), 11s. 6d. in silver, two
knives, and a memorandum-book; he said his sister gave him the guard-in
his box I found one sovereign and three half-sovereigns.
Guilty. Aged 28.-Confined Nine Months.
