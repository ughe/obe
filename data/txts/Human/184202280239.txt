1124. ROBERT TINDALL was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of February , 2 jackets, value 2 l  .; 1 pair of trowsers, value 1 l  .; 1 waistcoat, value 10 s  .; 3 handkerchiefs, value 8 s  .; 1 shirt, value 2 s  .; 1 half-crown, 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence; the property of John Lind : and 1 jacket, value 5 s  . the goods of John Crown , in a certain vessel on the   navigable river Thames. JOHN LIND. I am apprentice on board a brig lying at Rotherhithe—the prisoner came up from Sunderland with me. About half-past ten o'clock at night, on the 5th of February, he was gone from the vessel—he ought to have been on board—the next morning I opened my chest, and missed my two pairs of trowsers and the other things stated—in conse quence of information I went to a brothel with the officer—we found him in bed with a girl, and found these clothes there. JOSHUA JUDGE. I am a Thames-police inspector. I found the prisoner in bed in a brothel in Twine-court—I found these things in a bundle in the room—I said, "What did you do with the money?"—he said, "I took it out of the pockets, and gave it to the girl."
   Prisoner.  The girl said I took the money and gave it her; I did not say so. Witness.  Yes you did. GUILTY. Aged 24,— Confined Four Months.

 1127. ANN HAYES was indicted for stealing, on the   27th of February , 1 bonnet, value 6 d  .; and 2 shillings; the property of Richard M'Carthy : and 1 shawl, value 2 s  ., the goods of Hannah Crowley . JANE M'CARTHY. I am the wife of Richard McCarthy, and live in Neate-street, Camberwell. The prisoner came to my house last week—she had no place to go to, and I gave her a lodging—she staid from Tuesday till Sun day, the 27th of February, when she left my house at seven o'clock in the morning—after she was gone I missed my bonnet and two shillings—the bonnet hung up close to my door—the two shillings were at the bottom of a pot on my mantel-piece—I saw her on the following Tuesday at Mr. Day ling's house at Rotherhithe—I gave her in custody—I found my bonnet at the station.
   Prisoner.  She gave me the two shillings at twelve o'clock on Saturday night, and I said, "Call me at five in the morning to go to market"—I took the bonnet, took the ribbon off my bonnet, and put it on that bonnet, and said it was better than mine. Witness.  I said I would give her the two shillings if she was good, but I never did—I never gave her or lent her the bonnet. JOHN WALLAS( police-constable M  224.) I took the prisoner on the 28th of February, in King-street, with this bonnet on her head. NOT GUILTY.
   Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
