Cross-examined. I did not know Mr. Hill was the receiver till they
told me-I looked upon this as Jarvis's debt to me-he was Mr. Swaine's
acting managing clerk: I only knew him in this matter this was to
restrain the execution creditors from selling Fanny's furniture; she is
my cousin, and was just confined-I heard from Olney at the police-court
that he received 11. of this money himself—Jarvis told me that he went
to Maidstone to get the furniture freed; it was not sold-Olney is in
possession now-I only know from what Ohney and Jarvis said that the
injunction was expiring about that time; Olney said, “It is the last day
for restraining creditors, to-morrow will be too late”-Jarvis said that
they must have some money, or the Sheriff's officer would go into
possession; he said, "Mr. Hill has sold the stoek, and has money in
hand; they gave us 5l. out of the money he had in hand belonging to
Olney's estate'-I cannot say whether Mx. Hill returned the next day;
I did not see him till I went to Mr. Swaine's office with an officer on the
day Mr. Jarvis was arrested I am not the prosecutor---Mr. Swaine took
the IV U from my hand and said, “This is the third fraud which has
cropped up by that fellow this morning; you will have to come with me
to the Old Jewry."
