I received a note from Brown—I afterwards found my phaeton at the police-office, and the britska at the Red Lion, Maidenhead.
 (  The note being ready stated that Brown had been disappointed and post  poned payment for a week.)
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did Smith at all appear till after Brown took the phaeton? A.  Not in the hiring—I will not swear as to the first time Smith came—they came at different times—Smith was present when the britska went away—he was not in the shop when Brown and I agreed for 95 l .—after that, Smith generally came and stood in the street, or at the door—I and Brown were in the shop together—Smith came sometimes alone to see how the alterations were going on—I had written out a bill of parcels and given it to Brown—I think it was on the 17th that Smith came and wanted some money, and I think Brown came the day after, and bought the phaeton at the sum we agreed, and said he should pay for the britska on the 21st, and the phaeton on the 28th—the cheque that Smith brought was to be paid on the 21st, and on the 21st Smith came to me with the note—I believe it was previous to that, that he had called and asked me for something for the servant, but I will not swear it.
   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  Was Brown a perfect stranger to you when he called, a few days previous to the 2nd of September? A.  He was—he said he had not a card in his pocket, but he gave me his address, which I wrote down," No. 13, East India-chambers, Leadenhall-street"—I did not make any inquiries about him—I went there, and saw the name on the side of the passage, "Mr. William Brown," and I thought that had the appearance of respectability—I made inquiries afterwards, and found he never lived at Blackheath, but I did not inquire between his first and second call—the britska was to be paid for by ready money, before it went out of the shop—I made no other inquiry before I let him have the phaeton on hire, or sold him the britska. thinking to have the money for the britska I thought it was no use to inquire—Brown's papers have been taken from him by the policeman, and I believe the bill of parcels was amongst them—I sold the phaeton for 30 l . or 35 l ., I cannot swear which.
