EDWARD AGER, builder, High Street, Plaistow. On Saturday, August 31, about 11 p.m., I went to see a neighbour a few doors away, leaving my door ajar. After a short time I returned and met charles Smith and a policeman; on going into my house I found that two vases, which were there when I left, had disappeared; their value was 5s. 6d. I have known prisoner several years and have employed him. At the police court he declared that he took those vases for wages due to him; I saw there were no wages due at all; I do not owe him a penny. I never authorised him to take the vases.
 To Prisoner. You saw me during the week. I paid Ellis, the fishmonger, 3s. out of wages due to you; this was by your instruc  tions. CHARLES SMITH, labourer, 6, Albert Road, Plaistow. On August 31, about 11.20 p.m., I was sitting in my front garden when prisoner came and asked me if I wanted to buy a pair of vases; I said., "No"; he said, "come on, you can have them for a shilling." I asked him where he had pinched them; he said he got them from the fair at Wantead. He showed them to me; they ratiled, and I told him he would break them; so he turned them upside down. Three or four cards fell out; one had on the name and address of Mr. Ager, so I took the card to him. I did not take the vases; prisoner took them away.
 Detective-sergeant FREDERICK STEVENS, Plaistow. On Septem ber 1 I went to prisoner in a public-house and told him that I was a police officer and should arrest him for stealing a pair of went from Ager's house the previous night. He said, "All right." I said, "I shall send you to the station while I go to your house." He said, "It's no use going round there, they're smashed; I would have smashed his b——head if I had met him." At the station he made no reply to the charge. I have not been able to find the vases.
   PRISONER . What I said was that if I had met him I should have hit him on the head to the vases.
 Prisoner's statement before the magistrate: "I took them for wages."
   PRISONER (not on oath). I met Ager several times during the week and asked him for my wages; he said, "All right, old boy." On the Saturday I went to his house 20 times, and he was always out. I met Ellis, the fishmonger, who told me, "I have seen Ager, end he has given me 2s. out of your money, and he is going to give me the other 1s. by and bye"; I said, "What, give you 3s. out of 3s. 4d.; how am I going on about dinner to-morrow if I've only get 4d. to take?" I subpoeaned Ellis for to-day, but he refused to take the subpoens.
 Verdict, Guilty. Prisoner confessed to a previous conviction, and his record was a very bad one. Sentence, 12 months' hard labour.
