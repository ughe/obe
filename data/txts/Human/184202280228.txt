Q.  In what capacity was Mr. Stanhope received at your house? A.  He was paying his addresses to me—he represented himself to be a gen tleman of 300 l . a-year, with 9000 l . in the funds—I saw him again on Sunday, the 17th of October, at our house, and afterwards we went to take tea at his house in Newton-terrace—when I was at his apartment I perceived him drawing the ring off my finger—I said to him, "what are you doing?"—he said, "Oh, I wish to get you another ring made, a diamond and ruby ring"—he said, "Don't be afraid, you shall have this again; I only wish to take it to the jeweller's to get you a better; you shall have this returned with the diamond and ruby ring"—I allowed him to take it on that representation—I never saw this ring again till the day he was at Union-hall—it was produced there—he never gave me the dia mond and ruby ring, nor this one back.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You are a governess, are you? A.  Yes—I was governess to Mrs. Gowland's children, and I have had other pupils—I am twenty-one years of age—I am sorry to say I met with the prisoner, and formed an acquaintance with him in an omnibus—I did not ask him to my house, upon my oath—I have no one who pays his addresses to me now—I know a Mr. Logan, as a friend—I knew him before I knew the prisoner—I, and my mamma, and my sister were out—there was a crowd, Mr. Logan very kindly offered to take us through the crowd, and this we became acquainted with him—he made no pretensions, but he called at our house occasionally.
   Q.  The prisoner called on you on the Sunday, and you went to his house, did you not go rather before your mamma? A.  Yes, my mamma and papa were not quite ready, and the prisoner said we would walk on first—my papa came to the house afterwards, but he was not there when I went—I went with the prisoner, and went into the parlour—I swear I was never in his bed-room—I did not take tea in his bed-room.
   Q.  Did not you and mamma take tea in the prisoner's bed-room, and has he any other room in the house but that bed-room? A.  Yes, he had—people do not generally take tea in the bed-room—my mamma had not come to the house when the prisoner took the ring off—upon my oath the ring was taken off in the parlour—I am quite sure of that—no one was present but the prisoner and me—the people of the house were Dot pre sent—that parlour is a room of which the prisoner has the exclusive use—he and I were there perhaps half-an-hour before mamma came—I did not consider there was any impropriety in being alone with him, when I was to be united to him at Christmas—he got the ring away on that oc casion—I am sure he has never given me the ruby ring since—I have never had it to this day, nor any other ring from him—he brought one to try on, but took it away again.
