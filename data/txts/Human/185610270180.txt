1070. HENRY JACKSON , feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling house of Simon Cassimir , and stealing 11 coats; his goods. ( See page  750).
   MR. RIBTON . conducted the Prosecution. SIMON CASSIMIR. ( by an interpreter ). I live at No. 24, Devonshire-place, Kennington-lane—I have lived there for the last fifteen years—on a day in Aug. I left my house in the morning—I had fastened the doors and left the house in good order—I returned about 1 o'clock, I found the house all in disorder—I had a deal of trouble to open the house door—two men were convicted here last sessions for this same offence. ELIZABETH REASON. I am single. I live at No. 11, Devonshire-place, Kennington—the prosecutor lives at No. 24, on the same side of the way—on 11th Aug., between 9 and 10 o'clock, I saw the prisoner pass the house—I know he is the same man by the mark on his lip—some time after I saw three men standing by the prosecutor's door—that was a little after 11 o'clock—I saw two men enter the house, and the third man went to the bottom of the street—I am not able to say whether the prisoner was the third man—I went and gave information to the policeman that two men had gone into the house.
   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH . Q.  This was on 11th Aug.? A.  Yes—I did not see the prisoner till Sept.—it is five weeks to day since I last saw him—I had not seen the man, whoever he was, before—I am able to swear that he is the man that passed my house, but I could not swear that he was one of the three—when I looked out again I saw three men loitering about the street, and I saw three men go to the door—I cannot tell what clothes the man had on that passed—I could not tell whether they were light or dark—I only know that he had a cap on his head—he had whiskers when he passed the house, and when he was at the police court he had not the whiskers—he had a scar on his right lip.
   COURT . Q.  When you next saw him he was in custody? A.  Yes, he was shown to me with three other men, I picked him out. ANN ROOKE. I am the wife of Michael Rooke. In Aug. I lived it No. 22, next door but one to the prosecutor—in the beginning of Aug. I was at my front window, and I saw three men loitering about—the first time I saw them was about 10 o'clock—I saw them again a little after 11 at the same place—the prisoner is one of the three men—I gave evidence last Aug. against two of the men, who were convicted—I am sure the prisoner was one of the three, I saw him, he went towards the street door—he went to the key hole and looked, and said, "All right, mate"—I was at that time at my window, which was open—I went down stairs—I had something in my hand—I went to the yard and then to the front door, and when I came to the front door I found the two men were in custody—that was about a quarter of an hour afterwards—they were the two men that I had seen with the prisoner—the prisoner was not there—I am quite sure the prisoner is the man—he had whiskers on that day, and there are no whiskers on him now—I saw him at the police court afterwards, he was with one other man, I picked him out of the two.
