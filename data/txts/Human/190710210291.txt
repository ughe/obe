(Monday, November 11.) JAMES WILKES HAWKES. I was subpœnaed by the prosecution and have been in attendance every day for a fortnight. My age is 80. I have known Perryman about 18 years and worked for him when he was on the "Financial Observer"; Harris was also working with him at that time. I remember taking some premises from a man named Dove; 2, Tokenhouse Buildings; that was for the purpose of carry ing on the business of Chalmers and Cox. I was Chalmers and Cox, stock and share dealers—outside brokers. I composed the circulars. I have not in the ordinary composition of a circular had to ask the assistance of Perryman. I gave the orders for the printing to Bleach (the witness) and also to his father. I think Bleach arranged all the Argus work; I paid Bleach. Perryman never paid money to the Argus, as far as I know. I had control of the banking account and drew cheques. I consulted Perryman very frequently in connec tion with the business, in reference to the stocks and market, and generally. A Mr. Watson was associated with me as partner subse quently, when I removed to 49, Moorgate Street; that was in 1904. I gave notice to leave Tokenhouse Buildings. Mr. Dove and I did not come in contact; I always saw the managing clerk. I told the latter that Harris was going to take over the office. I could not give the exact date of that, because Harris did not take over the office at the time he negotiated with me to do so. He was to do it at Christmas, whereas it was about March when he did. I have seen Perryman occasionally at 43, Moorgate Street. I have shown him some of my circulars and he has made suggestions and alterations upon them, but I cannot say I have always acted on them; they did not always fit in with my view. I remember being ill; about which time I wrote to Perryman, seeking his advice on various stocks that I had opened, and difficulties I was in at the time. I used to buy shares from Perryman; Bonanzas, Chartered, and United Collieries, I think. I have also opened up stock with him—to protect myself. Perryman was a very large dealer I always understood, and on one or two occasions ho sold me the stock, not necessarily for delivery. I could not tell you whether I have opened up stock with anyone else, but I have an idea that I did with Harris. Perryman never came to my office. He did not have any share in the profits of Chalmers and Cox; I carried the business on myself; but, of course, he assisted me a good deal. The business is not in existence now, due to a variety of causes, want of capital and badness of business generally; the expense was too great. I have had interviews with the police on this matter. I have seen Mr. Ottaway several times, but have not had much to say to him. I really could not tell you the meaning of the phrases in some of these letters, "The Complete Angler," for instance, or "Hairbrush"; the latter appears to be the nickname of some client.
