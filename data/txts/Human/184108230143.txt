ELIZABETH GLAZIER was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of August , 3 printed books, value 3 s  ., the goods of James Stephens : 2 printed books, value 2 s  ., the goods of Ann Fletcher : and 1 printed book, value 1 s  ., the goods of Matilda Morgan . JOHN DAVIES CAUSTON. I am clerk at Eaton Chapel. On the 11th of Au gust, during the evening service, I saw the prisoner in the chapel—she went into a pew, and I missed a hymn-book from there—I told the beadle, on my making a signal he was to stop her, if she attempted to go out; but she stopped during the service—I then took her myself into the vestry, and these books were found on her. CHARLOTTE SARAH GREATOREX. I saw the prisoner come in at the door, and saw her give up the books in the vestry. JAMES STEPHENS. Three of these books are mine.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I met some friends, who gave me some ale and spirits, and it got into my head. GUILTY. Aged 34.— Confined One Month.
 2178. PETER GREEN was indicted for embezzlement. WILLIAM WALKER GILBERTSON. I am a baker, and live in Judd-street The prisoner was in my service two or three years—he was entrusted to receive money from my customers on my account.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDEROAST . Q.  He has a family? A.  Yes, two children, I believe—he had not a great Quantity of business—he was out a few hours in the day with a barrow—it was his duty to settle with me every evening—he has not accounted to me for either of these sums. MARY ANN BEESTON. I live with Mr. Burrell, in Burton-crescent., I take bread of the prosecutor—the prisoner used to deliver it—on the 24th of January I paid him 1 s . 9 d .; on the 27th of February, 1 s . 9 d .; and on the 19th of June, 1 s . 9 d .—here are the receipts he gave me.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined Four Months.

 2179. JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of August , 1 hat, value 20 s  ., the goods of Abraham Harris . ABRAHAM HARRIS. I am a clothier. I lost my hat on Sunday night week, the 15th of August, at Mr. Garrett's coffee-room, which is opposite my house—it was a pretty good hat—I laid it on the table, and when I looked for it to gap away, it was gone—the prisoner was stopped with it on his head—he had left a very shabby one instead of it, which is two sizes too small for me.
