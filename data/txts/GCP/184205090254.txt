WILLIAM JONES. I am assistant to Mr. Smith, a pawnbroker, in South
street, Manchester-square. I know Daniel Good-he was in the habit of
being at our shop and pledging-I have known him between two and
three years--I saw him last at the shop on Monday the 4th of April-I am
sure it was that day-it was between three and four o'clock as near as I
can recollect-near three o'clock that afternoon he brought a gown, petti-
coat, and two shawls--I lent him ten shillings on them he had an um-
brella in pawn with us at the time-(looking at two umbrellas)--this silk
one with a brown handle is it-he redeemed it on Monday the 4th of
April, and pawned these articles-he said the reason he was pawning them
was because he was going out of town for a few days and was pledging them
for safety.
LYDIA SUSANNAH BUTCHER. I live at Woolwich with my father and mo-
ther. I have known the prisoner for some time-he has been to see me
at my father's house several times I have been to see him at his master's
in Putney-lane three or four times I have staid there all night-I do not
know when that was—I remember his coming to me on a Wednesday-it
was a week or a fortnight before that, as near as I can judge--I slept in the
harness-room-the prisoner let me in, and I went away in the morning-I
did not see him from the time I slept at the stable until Wednesday the 6th
of April, when he came to my father's house in a pony-chaise with a little
boy, between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, as near as I can tell
- I had not seen the little boy before, that I know of-I heard bim speak to
the prisoner, he called him “father"-the prisoner brought me that day this
fur tippet and gown, and the blue bonnet produced, also some boots, and
here is a bandkerchief which he brought me-these are the boots, they lace
on one side, and this is the fur tippet, it has no collar to it-the articles were
in an old handkerchief, and the bonnet in an old hat-box without any lid-
he told me he had brought me those things if I would accept of them--he
did not say anything particular about them then he said they had been his
wife's, he said he had brought these things for me, and he had told me previ-
ously that his wife had been dead these five years.
Q. Did you expect him to give you any thing-had he said anything
about giving you anything? A. Yes, several weeks before he did not
say what they were to be, but said he had some things be would give me
if I would have them-he said he had got some things of his wife's, and he
would bring them for me some day, but did not say when he would
bring them--he told me this some weeks before he brought them, and
when he brought them on Wednesday he said he had brought them-
he asked me if I would wear the bonnet-he did not say anything
more-he brought two squares of silk at the same time, but took them
away-it was shot silk trimmed with lace-these things remained in my
possession until the next evening, Thursday, when Sergeant Parry came to
my father's house--the prisoner went away on Wednesday evening,
think between five and six-he took tea with me-the little boy and me
and my younger sister went with him to New Cross, but my sister
got out at half-way to Greenwich.
