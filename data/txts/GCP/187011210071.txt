Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Bag.
42. JAMES ATKINS (27), Feloniously forging and altering an order for
the payment of 481. 76. 6d., with intent to defraud. Second Count--for
uttering the same.
M RS. POLAND and STRAIGHT conducted the Prosecution ; and MR.
BROMBT the Defence
BIOHARD HEYDAY. I am a flour factor, and live at 22, Milk Street,
Cheapeide--in consequence of receiving this letter produced), in driving
my tvunds I called at the address named in it (Read: "6, Hyde
Road, Hoxton, June 12, 1870. Dear Sir-Will you please call on me
at your earliest convenience. Please drop me a line, and let me know what
day you will call.--Yours, J. W. Hamilton")-When I called I saw the
prisoner on the subject of that letter-I had a conversation with him, but
did no business-it was a baker's shop — I could not swear that he has been
at my office; he might have been-I think I have a recollection of him,
but I would not swear it-on 29th Maroh my place was broken into, and
several ready-drawn cheques, and some that were cancelled, were swept
away, as well as three or four cancelled cheque-books, and my watch and
seals among the cheques were these two (produced); one is dated London,
12th September, 1870, on the Metropolitan Bank, for 88L. 7., payable to
Messrs. Surr & Gribble, or bearer, and signed "Richard Heyday that
was a grossed cheque, and when I lost it it was dated MarchSurr & Gribble
are solicitors the second cheque is dated 12th September, 1870, for 76,
payable to Mr. Goodman; that was also dated in March when I lost it ; it
was not crossed I keep an account at the Metropolitan Bank-I gave them
information, and ordered them not to pay the cheques,
Oros-examined. I did not mention this letter before the Magistrate,
had not found it then I lost more than twenty cheques, of different dates
-I stopped all cheques, and hereafter they were to pay none but those
signed in red ink; but they paid the one for 75L signed in black ink a
WILLIAN TATLOR.On 20th September, I was salesman to Messrs.
Salmon & Grant, of Tooley Street, the prisoner came there on that day he
said he was about to set up a little general shop, and asked me what
articles he should buy, leaving it to my judgment-I said he would
want so-and-so he gave the name of Gribble I put a porter on to select
goods for him that I thought suitable for his trade, and made out this
invoice, it amounted to 281. 186. 4d. nett cash, lese 38. 6d. discount--the
prisoner gave me this cheque for 482 78. 60.--I had my suspicions about
it I said "How will you have these things sent we shall be sending
down there, very likely, to-morrow"-ho said "I will get a neighbour of
mine, who is coming up on Friday, to bring them down for me, have you
got change for this cheque !"--I said “No'--I said to Clark, one of the
porters, "Go with this gentleman to the bank, take this cheque, don't part
with it; if you get the money, pay him the difference, and receipt this
invoice"- the prisoner gave his address, 21, Tunner's Hill, Deptford ; I
wrote that on the invoice--the prisoner and the porter left together-I
never saw the prisoner again till he was in custody-nobody came for
the goods.
