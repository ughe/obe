We then decided to stay the night at the hotel. Brown telephoned
to the Duke of Norfolk. It was about 8.30 p.m., and after ringing
The Duke of Norfolk up he said, "I am Pleydell Bouverie. Please
explain to the Duke that I am unavoidably detained here on im-
portant business at Herne Bay. I have already wired postponing,
but now I am quite unable to attend." Bouverie then said, “By
the way, I will finish adding up that score; we will settle that," and
he sat at the table and said that I owed each of them something,
smounting altogether to £201. Quill owed to Brown and Meggs,
and Brown included his loss to Meggs in a cheque to Meggs, then
Quill gave Brown a cheque for £12, being what he owed him and
the other together. I said I had no cheque there, the matter would
have to stand over. They agreed. I'did not see the score. Meggs
and I went into the town and talked about the site. We called at &
place where a man was extending his hotel. Moggs said, "That poor
fellow has probably only got about a paltry £10,000. I have got
£20,000 lying idle at the bank." I then spoke of the cards, and said,
I do not understand about these losses at cards, and I do not like it.
I shall not be able to pay you. You owe me money now, and I shall
want some on account. You had better let me have some, and
probably Mr. Kinder will be wanting some on account, too." He
said that would be quite agreeable to him for both of us. He said
I must not speak to Bouverie as I had spoken to him about not
being ablo to pay, because it would appear to Bouverie that I was
not a substantial man, that it would never do, that I must remember
his position with regard to the Earl of Radnor, that he objected to
my discussing any financial matters with Bouverie, and that he did
not like business and sport being mixed up together. He said, "If
you settle with Bouverie within the next few days, you shall have
what you like from me within a week." We went back to the hotel,
and it was too late for dinner and we went to bed. Meggs came to
my bedroom and started discussing the site again. Bouverie (Brown)
came in, and after he left I said, "You do not want to discuss this
before Bouverie, but I must tell you I do not understand about
these cards and I do not like it. I should like to know some
thing about it. We must talk it over." The next morning we went
to the station, and on the platform I said to Meggs and Brown to
fether, "Now, what about these card losses! You want some money,
Mr. Bouverie, and you look remarkably like getting it: between our
selves I cannot do it.” Meggs pulled me away from Brown and said,
"I told you not to mention such things to Bouverie, you must not do
it-if you want to say anything, speak to me." I said, "I do. I want
to tell you that I cannot do this £201-I cannot understand it. You
owe me now a fair sum, and if you like to pay me, this £201 shall stand
out of it." He said he could not mix sport with business, that Bouverie
must be paid, and it would very unplensant for him if Bouverie was
not paid." I suggested, “Suppose I pay Bouverie, then vou and Quill
can wait." He said, "No, if you settle this matter within a day or
