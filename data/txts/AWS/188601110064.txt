BENJAMIN WILLIAM LBADER (Re-azamined). I have paid attention to
the evidence in this case-I have never before heard of having a copy of
an artist's picture, and afterwards having his signature put on it--I have
seen seven or eight protures with my name on them-I never knew
a
respectable dealer sell a copy with the name of the real artist, and when
a picture is sold with another artist's name on it it is always pronounced a
forgery-I should no more think of putting another man's name on a
picture than I should of putting another man's name to a cheque-thena
on the picture indicates the actual paintera copy of a picture means a copy
of the style as well as the subjeet, and sometimes the style is the important
thing-I do not see any resemblance in this to the style of my pictures;
it is similar in subjeet but not in execution-it could not be called a copy
of a Leader unless the signature was there--it is not a copy of any work
of art of mine.
Cross-eramined. The execution is very much below mine-I do not
always paint my signature in quite the same oolour; if it is a light picture
I use a light colour, and if it is dark, dark colour--in a grey landsoape
I should not use a tint which would warm the picture-I generally use
the left-hand corner-the practice of most artists is to write their name in
dark brown or black, and that will harmonise with all colours; a briek red
was used some years ago-some of the old masters, such as Durer, worked
their signatures or initials into the landscape, and it was part of the
picture therefore you could not copy Durer outpainting the signature
but I know of no modern instance-I have never painted copies since I
was a student, and then I never put the signatures.
Cross-aramined. With regard to living artists, I should say that the
name is never put on a copy, except for the purpose of frad-Albert
Durer painted 400 years ago.
By the COURr. I said that I never knew of copies being made and the

stretoh of the imagination to say that it was after me-it would be noticed
