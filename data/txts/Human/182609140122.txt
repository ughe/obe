 1591.  WILLIAM MARLIN was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of September , 5 trusses of clover hay, value 15s., the goods of James Ball.
 JAMES BALL. I live at  Irongate Wharf, Paddington, and am a hay salesman. On the night of the 4th of September, I was going home at a quarter past nine o'clock, and looking round my premises I saw the large shed-door moving; I cried out, "Who is there?" I got no answer - I called to my daughter, and she brought a light; we saw the prisoner standing at the door, and pulling this truss out of the shed; he had come up with the boats of hay that day, but he was not in my employ, and had no right to take my hay; we found five trusses of hay ready for taking away - he said he was going to stop there, because his boat leaked.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you never given leave to any persons to sleep there? A. Yes, at haymaking time, but not lately - there was no young woman with him upon the hay; and I had given no one leave to sleep there; I fastened the shed myself, bolted, and pinned it.
   ELIZA BALL. My father called for a light; I saw the prisoner, and knew him directly; I saw by his hat that he had been carrying hay, and told him so; he said, "No, I have done no such thing" - we found one truss inside the shed, and five trusses outside the door.
 Cross-examined. Q. Then this young man spoke to you, and you to him? A. Yes, my father took hold of him; he could not run.
   FRANCIS BRADBURY. I am the officer. I took the prisoner; I found nothing on him but some money; he said, that Mr. Ball could not swear he took them out.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1592.  WINIFRED HAYLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of June , 1 pair of boots, value 6s., the goods of Henry Reeve.
 HENRY REEVE. I am a boot and  shoe-maker, and live in Fetter-lane. On the 20th of June I was entering my shop, and saw the prisoner there; the shopman was serving her with a pair of shoes; I saw her look at him with a watchful eye, and take a pair of lace boots from a rail at the end of the counter; she wrapped them up very carefully, and put them under her shawl; I seized her directly - walking towards the shopman.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took them up, and was going to put them down again on the counter - he took hold of me, and said all my country people were going to steal things; I said, I was not going to steal them, I was going to speak to the shopman. GUILTY. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy by Prosecutor, believing her in distress. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1593.  MARY GRIFFITHS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 cap, value 5s., the goods of Joseph Goodwin.
   MARTHA GOODWIN. I am the wife of Joseph Goodwin- we live in Cow-cross-street, Smithfield, and keep a grocer's shop. On the 8th of September this cap was safe when the prisoner came into my shop to offer me a large baking dish to sell; I told her I did not want it; she pressed hard for me to buy it, and put it on the counter; I laid the cap down, she took it up, and said "Very well, it is of no use to buy if you don't want it;" she went out; I ran after her - she was taken, but had not the cap then.
   BENJAMIN PHILLIPS. I am a constable. I saw the prisoner come out of the shop with the dish in her hand; she walked at a quick pace; the prosecutor came out, and called "Stop that woman" - she turned into a lane; I pursued and took her - she had several persons round her, who said "Go the watch-house" - no cap was found on her.
 JURY. Q. Were you the first person who took her? A. Yes; she said I might search her, but I should find no cap on her, but she was in a crowd, and, to the best of my belief, it had been given to another person.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1594.  WILLIAM GRAHAM and WILLIAM SCOWN were indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 1 coat, value 2l., and 1 waistcoat, value 10s., the goods of George Richards , and 1 waistcoat, value 10s.; 1 pair of breeches, value 1l., and 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of John Percy.
   ANN RICHARDS. I am the wife of George Richards - he lives at Chiswick. Scown was our apprentice; I left home on the 24th of August, leaving him at home with my child; I desired him to look after the house; I returned about half-past eight o'clock; I found the house all safe; I went up stairs, and found an earthen plate on the bed- four boxes had been broken open, and a coat and waistcoat missing from them, which were the property of my husband, and from the box of John Percy, a lodger, a pair of small clothes, a silk handkerchief, and a waistcoat were taken; Graham had worked on the premises before, but he had nothing to do there then.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Did not Scown work for a bricklayer? A. Yes; he did at first - my husband was in distress; Scown did not wish to go home; I kept him with some money I received, which my husband had worked for; my husband is now in Whitecross-street- Scown might have had work if he would.(Property produced and sworn to).
