ROBERT EMMERSON. I am sergeant of the same division of police as the prisoner and Halter. I went to the prisoner's lodging on Monday morning with Salter, and in searching the bed-room I found a sack containing four large pieces of bacon under the bed-clothes, weighing 76lbs., which when put together formed a flitch—I also found a parcel of sugar tied in a handkerchief, and a brown paper bag—the prisoner's beat on Saturday night would be in Larkhall lane till four o'clock in the morning, and after four it would take in the prosecutrix's house—his duty would cease at six o'clock in the morning, when he should report himself at the station-house on Clapham-common, which he did on Sunday and Monday—his lodging is three or four hundred yards from the prosecutrix's—I was present when he was examined before the Magistrate, and heard him make statements which were taken down in writing in my presence—he was told it would be taken down and read against him, which Mr. Clive the Magistrate signed—I did not see the prisoner sign it.
