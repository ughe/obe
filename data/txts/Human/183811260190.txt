Certainly not, of any kind whatever—nor did I authorise any body to do it for me. ANDREW RAMSAY. I am a wine-merchant, and live in George-street, Minories. About the 13th or 14th of September, I was requested by Mr. Ramsbotham to take charge of his business during his absence for a few weeks—I did so, and saw the prisoner at Mr. Ramsbotham's counting-house—I told him that in undertaking the management of Mr. Rams botham's business during his absence, and he telling me he had transactions with him for four butts of wine then on the quay, I wished to know his account of it—he mentioned something about giving bills, and some were afterwards sent to the counting-house—I had received no authority from the prosecutor to take bills in payment—I never received them in pay ment, only as a deposit.
   Cross-examined. Q.  A bill is always a deposit—I suppose you took them, and if they were paid it was all right, if not, yon held the man re sponsible who had the goods? A.  The bills had four months to run—I could not have taken them in that sense—the prisoner was to pay the amount by the 9th of September, but in the mean time he deposited the bills as security—the prisoner told me that he had given Mr. Ramsbothan security for 500 l .—the prisoner brought one bill to the Dock in the first instance—that was on Bragg and Co. of Bristol—I took that bill, but not in payment—I told him at the time I took it, that I wanted the money—I cannot tell on what day it was—it was between the 14th and 29th of September—he again repeated that on the 29th of September, he would bring the money—he had the warrants in his possession at the time—I saw him about the Docks everyday, and was every day urging him for the money-very likely eight days might elapse between his giving me the first bill and my receiving the others—he gave me another on Mr. Bragg, which I discovered was an altered bill—I recommended him to draw it afresh for his own safety—he said he had no change to purchase a stamp, and Mr. Ramsbotham's clerk gave him the money for it—he afterwards brought the bill properly drawn and left it with me—I have the bills here—they are drawn at four months.
   Q.  Is not a bill at four months in the wine trade reckoned cash? A.  Certainly not—nor at three months-not even bankers' bills, if they have only one month to run—I gave the bills to Mr. Ramsbotham's clerk to lock up till his return, and I was urging in the mean time payment of the money from the prisoner.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did you tell the prisoner you would not consent to receive them as payment for the wines? A.  Undoubtedly, in the most pointed manner—I explained to him that I merely received them as a de posit for the money, he assuring me that the 29th of September would be the latest period of his being in ample funds to pay the money.
