I went to Jerrard's shop; the place appeared in a general state of confusion; I saw no flour there; there were a few twopenny loaves there—I have traced fifty of my sacks of flour to Nicholls's shop, and the corn and oats to a man named Brown, who is here—when I heard of Jerrard being in gaol, I asked Keene how he came to say he had seen three 50 l . notes in his hand—he said it was a mistake, that they were three fives—I was somewhere in the neighbour hood of Bayswater, and saw Jerrard through a public house window, as I was sitting in my gig; I went into the bar, and he was gone; I went into the back premises; he came out from an outhouse, and I gave him into custody—Keene was taken some time afterwards at Gloucester.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  You say you took down the name from Keene as Jerrard; allow me to look at your memorandum? A.
