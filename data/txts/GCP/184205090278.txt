--on Sunday, the 27th, about ten o'clock at night, she came and knocked
at the side shutters- I was then getting my supper-I went to the front
door and saw my wife-I am sure it was her-she asked me for the money
which she did not receive on the Saturday night I gave it to her, and a
she parted away from the door, she said, "I would have you be careful,
or I will have my revenge yet"-I made no reply--she went away-1
went in and got my supper, after which, I changed my coat, as it was
raining, and went to the Bull to get half a pint of ale- I think it must
have been half past ten or a quarter to eleven-I met her about twenty
yards from my door-on seeing me, she turned, and walked in an oppo-
site direction, crossed the road, and went on the opposite side of the way
towards Eaton-place, which is opposite to the Bull-she was walking
backwards and forwards there I went into the Bull, at the private er-
trance, the passage being rather dark, I thought she would not see me
enter that door-I got half a pint of ale there--I remained in the Bull, I
think not more than two minutes--as I came out she met me at the door
and asked me if I was going to stand any gin-I made no reply, and
while I was going away, homewards, I saw her draw her hand from under
her cloak, she threw a liquid in my face, and exclaimed, “That is what I
have long wished for, and now have got my revenge”- the liquid gave me
great pain, almost as if I had been scalded with hot water, a burning pain,
it went into both eyes- I was obliged to shut my eyes instantly, since
that I have lost the sight of one, and partly of the other-one is getting
better- I was taken to Guy's Hospital - I did not see what the liquid was
in.
Prisoner. Q. Did not you run from your promise in not paying me my
money on Friday precisely? A. When I went to Union-hall it was on a
Friday, and I paid her the 10s. in advance, and 1s. 60. to pay her cab-hire,
and it was understood I was to pay every Friday.
Prisoner. He did not fulfil his promise ; I went to Union-hall, the
Magistrate told me to take witnesses with me and ask him civilly for the
money; he said he had got no money; I said, “What am I to do, and
my poor baby is ill;" he said, “ You may work for it as I do ;" I said I
would work, for the baby was not too bad to be left; he said, “I will
send it;" and I walked away directly : I went and asked him civilly for it
on Saturday night; I said, “Would you not like to see the poor child;"
he said, “I neither want to see you or it;" he said he would send it, but
he never did.
PETER MONTAGUE POPE. I am a surgeon of Guy's Hospital --the pro-
secutor was brought there-his face was very much inflamed, and both
eyes injured-it was produced by some acid--if a liquid thrown in his face
produced it, I should say it was some corrosive acid cannot say what
it was, probably nitric acid-I am certain it was some corrosive fluid-he
has entirely lost the sight of one eye-the other is likely to recover, but
he will never have the perfect use of it-it will be partially restored.
RICHARD Wood. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner di-
rectly opposite the Bull-I told her she must go with me-she expressed
her readiness to go-she said she had been in hell the last two years, and I
could not take her to a worse hell, and as we went along she said she had
plenty of cause to do what she had done.
