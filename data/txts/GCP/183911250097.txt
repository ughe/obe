124. JOSEPH QUAINTON was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of
November, 8 tame fowls, price 128., the property of John Elmore; 2nd
Count, for stealing eight dead fowls.
WILLIAM GLASSCOCK (police-constable T 61.) Between seven and eight
o'clock in the morning on the 11th of November, I saw the prisoner in
Star-street, Paddington, five or six miles from Willesden-I stopped him
and asked what he had got in the rush basket which he had he said,
“Poultry"-I asked him where he was going to take it he said to Smith
field market, and that he had brought it from Mr. Tanner's, at Harlesden-
green--I found in the basket five fowls, some of them were warm- in one
of his pockets I found two more fowls, and one fowl in another pocket-1
found on him some lucifer matcheshe said then that he met a man on the
road, who asked him to bring them to London. xic ot
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Are you quite sure he mentioned
the name of Tanner and Harlesden-green? 4. Yes that is to the left of
Willesden-green-you go up the Harrow-road to both of them the fowle
were all dead those in the basket were warm that might have been
from their being closely shut up, or being but a short time killed.
CHARLES WETRE. I am a servant to Mr. John Elmore, who lives
in the parish of Willesden. The prisoner came to me on the 10th, in the
farm-yard, and asked if I could give him a job of work I said I should
want two men on the Monday, that I should see Mr. Elmore in the course
of the day, and if he came on the Monday morning I would set him to work
I saw the hen-house all safe that night, and on the Monday morning
opened the door, and sent a man in to let the fowls out-they all flew out so
fast I could not count them, but I knew I had lost some I saw eight
fowls at the station-house on the Tuesday, and they were Mr. Elmore's.
