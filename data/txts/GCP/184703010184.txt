855. WILLIAM KING was indicted for stealing 12 pieces of wood,
value 18., the goods of Benjamin Bobbins, his master.
WILLIAM LEE (police-constable V 38.) I was on duty at East-bill,
Wandsworth, at a quarter-past five o'clock in the afternoon of the 4th of
Feb., and met the prisoner carrying some wood in a basket-I stopped him and
asked him where he brought it from-he said from where he lived, at an
unoccupied house, opposite the Swan-I stopped him about 100 yards from
Mr. Bobbin's--this is the wood.
CHRISTOPHER CASTLE (police-constable V 123.) I saw the prisoner
stopped-I examined the wood-it had marks on it, which I had put on it
the night before, when it was in a basket, in a stable at Mr. Bobbin's.
Prisoner. Q. You did not mark it before you saw it in the basket ? A. No.
BENJAMIN FOULGER. I am gardener to Mr. Benjamin Bobbins, of East-
hill-he employed the prisoner as a gardener for some days-he was employed
on Thursday, the 4th of Feb.--this wood is Mr. Bobbin's--they are pieces
for fire-wood, and were cut on Mr. Bobbin's premises for the use of his house
-the prisoner had no permission to take any-he had been employed to lop
trees-it is not his perquisite to have any of the lopping.
Prisoner. It is a usual custom to have a basket of wood when lopping or
topping trees; I put the wood into a basket two days before I took it home,
and hung it over a cinder-box, where the servants always go; the stable-
door stood open for two days, and the night before I went, I put it in the stable
and locked it up; somebody opened it again; I told them where I brought
it from.
Not GUILTY.
