 Cross-examined. It was owing to the action of Luck's son that I was able to arrest Friend. Friend came to Luck's house, the seperin  tendent of police was sent for, and he was arrested at the house. At the station Luck identified Friend as the man Smith he had been speaking of. Friend admitted he was Smith, and that Luck's state ments were true.
 Detective-sergeant ALBERT HAWKINS, Stoke Newington. I was present when the prisoner Friend and two other men were discharged by the Justices at Dorking Police Court on August 9. I told Friend I was going to arrest him and take him back to London for stealing two horses from Chingford on July 12. He said, "I never stole from Chingford, I was at my house getting a cup of tea for my missing at eight o'clock on the morning of July 12. She was expecting to go to bed (to be confined). I live at 542, Old Ford Road, and went out to get some buter, and when I came back with it I not a man named Wado. He had two horses with him, and I asked him if he was going to the Barbican. He asked me if I knew anyone who would buy them, and I said I knew several people. I asked him how much he wanted for them, and he said £30 for the two. I told him if he liked he could go with me and put them on the rail at London Bridge. I knew a man named Luck, who would buy them. I took the buter home and then went with Wado. I sent a wire to Luck to meet me at Dorking Station, and Mr. Luck was there and met us. Wado led the horses to Mr. Luck's place, and I jumped up with Mr. Luck in his trap. The horses were put in the van and tried, and Mr. Luck gave me £8 10s. in gold and two ponies, which were put on the rail for London Bridge, and Wado took them away. I got a sovereign out of it for my trouble; Wado gave it to me." From Old Ford Road to London Bridge Station in about four miles. I should think it would be possible to get from Old Ford to Lon don Bridge by the time the telegram was despatched (8.50 a.m.). FRANK HOLLAMBY, of the General Post Office, produced the original of the telegram dispatched from London Bridge by Friend. CHARLES STEVENS, booking clerk, Dorking, proved the arrival of the two horses by the 11.53 train, and that they were signed for by prisoner Luck, who paid the charges, 13s.
