DIANA LEVY. I live at Hackney, and am a furrier. -The prosecutor employed me - on the day he charged the prisoner with this I was there, and she desired I would search her; I found three duplicates in her pocket - I read them to him; one was for a shirt, one for a cloak, and one for these two shifts - she told me that she had her victuals and drink, and 1s. a week - she then said he had given her a bed, which was worth 3s., and the whole of the time she was with him was but three weeks, and she had drawn another 1s. - when I found this duplicate she said that one of the shifts was the prosecutor's, and one was her own; she did not tell him, in my presence, that if she had not money she would pawn something - I saw 10s. taken from her; I do not know Mrs. Michael; I had been there but a fortnight.
 WILLIAM HENRY KING. I am a constable. I received the prisoner in charge, and got the duplicates from Levy.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1282.  CHARLES BRIGHTLEY, JOSEPH JACKSON, and WILLIAM SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of George Mence Boyes , from his person.
   GEORGE MENCE BOYES. I am a merchant's clerk. On the 12th of May, in the afternoon, I was passing under an arch-way, in Upper Thames-street, and felt a twitch at my pocket; I turned, and missed my handkerchief - Brightley and Smith were close to me; I seized Brightley, and my handkerchief fell from under his coat - I pulled him a short distance; the officer ran up, caught Smith, and told a person to take Jackson, as they had been together - Jackson was walking off, but was brought back.
   JAMES BATES. I am a draper. I was speaking to the officer, and he drew my attention to the three prisoners; I saw them go to a stall, buy an orange, and then walk on towards Billingsgate; they met the prosecutor -I saw Brightley touch his pocket; they all turned, and followed him, and two or three doors before they came to Fish-street-hill, they made another attempt; he then went on under the arch, and Brightley there took the handkerchief - the others were close to him at the time; he dropped the handkerchief - I took Jackson, who was walking off, and the officer took Smith.
   PHILIP HENRY PARISH. I am an officer. I saw the three prisoners, and pointed them out to Bates; I followed with him about half way towards Billingsgate, where they met the prosecutor, and returned after him -I did not see the attempt made, but I saw he had hold of Brightley; I went up, and the prisoners were secured.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Brightley's Defence. I was waiting at the London-docks - a gentleman asked me to carry a box to the steam-pocket, which I did, and he gave me 6d. for it - as I came back this gentleman took me.
 Jackson's Defence. I was going on an errand, when the prosecutor laid hold of Brightley, and then the other gentleman said I was one of them.
 Smith's Defence. My father sent me to my aunt's, in the Strand, and told me to call on a man at Billingsgate; I forgot it till I got to the bridge - I turned back; I saw the man, and was then going on, and they took me behind a cart.
   JAMES BATES. I am sure he was with the others when the attempts were made, and the handkerchief drawn.
 BRIGHTEY -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 JACKSON -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months.
 1283.  JOHN TUDOR was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of John Robert Witherden , from his person.
 RICHARD BOTHEROYD KAY. I keep the King's Arms, in Beech-street, Barbican. On the night of the 13th of April, I saw Mr. Witherden pass my door, with a handkerchief hanging nearly out of his pocket - I saw the prisoner and another, rather bigger, following the prosecutor; I followed, till they came to Messrs. Hoare's, the bankers, in  Fleet-street- I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief from the prosecutor; I collared and took him to the watch-house.
   JOHN ROBERT WITHERDEN. I live in Bacon-street, Bethnal-green, and was in Fleet-street when the witness spoke to me; I had gone through Beech-street - the witness produced to me the handkerchief, which I had had in my pocket - the prisoner was then in custody.
   JOHN READ. I am an officer, and took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1284.  CHARLES KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of December , 600 sets of printed books, value 100l., the goods of Henry Colburn.
 THREE OTHER COUNTS, varying the description of the property.
 WILLIAM MOSS. I am a printer; and in December, 1830, I was in the employ of Mr. Baylis and his partner; I missed 600 sets of printed books from their premises; they were tied up, and ready to go to the publishers.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. When had you seen them safe? A. On the 9th of July, 1830.
   JOHN CHIDLEY. I live in Goswell-street, and am a bookseller. In January, 1831, I bought a quantity of printed paper of the prisoner - he said he had bought it at Mr. Colburn's, and there was about half a ton of it - I asked why he did not bring the whole, or let one of my men go and get it - he said the men were busy, and he could but bring it by 1 cwt. at a time; I agreed to have it so - I did not see him again till last April, when I saw him in Barbican; I thought he was the man, and as soon as he saw me, he ran off, but was bindered - I got up to him, took him, and said "You must go with me;" he said, "I will see you d-d first;" he gave a cast with his arm, and got from me - I seized him again, and kept him down till the officer took him - this is the paper - I had no other like it.
   WILLIAM MOSS. I cannot swear that this had not been sent to Mr. Colburn, and he is not here - I had not seen it from July, 1830.
     NOT GUILTY.
