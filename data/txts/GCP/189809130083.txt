SAMO EL DESHAM (Inspector M). On July 17th, after midnight, the
prisoner came to the police station-he said, “I want to say something. I
am living with a woman, I am not married to her, we have had a quarrel,
I pushed her over a table and she is dead. Dr. Burt told me to come to
you and make a statement”-I told him it might be given in evidence
agaittst him-he said, “All right" he then made a further statement,
which I took down, and he signed itthis is it.
Cross-examined. He was not exactly in tears, he cried afterwards a
good deal he was very cool and sober, but he was distressed-he had to
stop two or three times while he was making his statement-I should say
he was generally distressed and upset by what had happened.
ERNEST FREEMAN. (Inspector M). At 2 p.m. on the morning of July
17th, I charged the prisoner at the station first with manslaughter-he
made no reply-I found in his pocket a woman's leather belt-be said, "I
took it off her when she was lying down faint"-after some evidence at the
Police-court, I formally charged him with murder on July 26th-he
made no reply-I have here the bar of iron-it was pointed out to me by
Mrs. Bailey about 3 o'clock on the morning of the 17th-I took it from
the corner of the fireplace.
