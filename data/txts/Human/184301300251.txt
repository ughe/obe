   Q.  Mr. Boys has said that your attention was called to the amount due, namely, 1,109 l . therein stated? A.  I beg Mr. Boys's pardon—I never had my attention called to any such thing—this case was never shown or read to me by Mr. Boys—I never had any case shown to me there—he never called my attention to his having taken counsel's opinion respecting the new deed—I dare say I should not have understood it if he had.
   Q.  Did the prisoner ever state in your presence and that of Mr. Boys, that the deed which is now called forged, was executed on the 30th of Dec.? A.  I believe I can truly swear, that I never heard the prisoner mention my father's name in any way in his life—I never gave Mr. Boys to understand that the first deed was executed on the 30th of Dec.—I could not have told him so, nor should I have suffered such a statement to have passed unnoticed.
   Q.  This confirmation deed which you executed, conveys to the executor and executrix of Stanley, all the property, to pay the supposed creditors of your father, and states that his estate is indebted 1109 l . odd to Stanley's executors; was that explained to you by Mr. Boys? A.  No—I had no explanation of the kind—I never entered into it—it was never suggested to me, or ever sus pected—it was given to the prisoner under a very different impression—I agreed to make it over to him under a very different impression.
   Q.  What was the expectation held out to you to make over the pro perty to him? A.  That he would give me a deed of annuity of 30 l . a year, that he would clear the estate of the incumbrances bearing on it of 2,300 l ., I think, and Mr. Parker's bill of costs.
