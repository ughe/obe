1053. JANE DIXON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of April , 1 shawl, value 2s. 6d., the goods of James Martin Bass , from the person of Ruth Bass.
 RUTH BASS. I am the  wife of James Martin Bass. On the 11th of April, a little after eleven o'clock in the evening, I was with my husband going along Ratcliff-highway, my foot slipped, and I fell; a strange woman came up and offered to assist me, but she took my shawl off and ran away; I cannot say that it was the prisoner.
 JAMES MARTIN BASS. I was behind my wife with the umbrella; she fell down, and I saw a woman come up to her - I cannot say it was the prisoner.
   THOMAS CUNDALL. I am a watchman. I was coming along Shadwell; I saw several persons in the middle of the road; the prosecutor said "My wife has lost her shawl;" and some person said "There runs the woman" I took hold of the prisoner who was running with the shawl; I said "Give me that shawl;" I took her to the watch-house - she said "There is the shawl;" that was all that passed.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was not running; I was standing at the corner of Bluegate-field - a man came up and gave me the shawl, and asked me to take a walk with him - the watchman came up and I gave it to him.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1054.  SAMUEL VICKERY was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 1 picture, value 40s. the goods of Thomas Serle.
 THOMAS SERLE. I am a  picture-dealer and live in Frith-street, Soho. I lost this picture on the 14th of April.
   CHARLES HOPE. I am a straw hat-maker. The prosecutor has part of my house: between eight and nine o'clock in the morning of the 14th of April, the shop bell rang; the little boy who generally attends the door, went to it, and soon after called out "shop;" I went into the shop and missed a picture; I looked into the street and saw the prisoner with it, partly concealed under his arm, and just turning into a court; I pursued him, and called Stop thief! he ran pretty sharp, but there were three person on horseback who pursued him, and he was taken; I kept him in view all the time, except when he turned a corner; when I came up to him I asked where the picture was; he said, "I have no picture, this is what I have taken," and he gave me this card - I took him back the same way he had run, and the picture which he had dropped was given to me by of the neighbours. I knew the prisoner before.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. When I came back, the card was given to the gentleman by another person, not by me; the picture was given me by a man to hold, and when I had it he ran away.    GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined for Three Months.
 1055.  JOHN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of April , 40 lbs. weight of horse-hair, value 30s. the goods of Thomas Bird.
   THOMAS FRANCIS. I am hackney coachman to Thomas Bird; he keeps his coaches in his yard, in Rathbone-place; on the 12th of April I was in Oxford-street, about a quarter before six o'clock in the morning; I saw the prisoner carrying this horse-hair, tied up with string; I called the watchman and he asked him where he got it, he said, "I got it out of that yard," alluding to my masters; I went to the yard and found the inside of two chariots and a coach all cut to pieces; he was quite a stranger.
 THOMAS BIRD. I am a  coach-master- my yard is in Rathbone-place; I was at home in bed. I had left my carriage all secure; when I came down that morning I found the linings cut, and the horse hair taken out, to the damage of more than 20l. - my yard is not locked at night, but the watchman minds it; I believe this to be the lining of the carriages.
   JOHN FREEMAN. I am a watchman. I was called, and took the prisoner with this horse-hair.
 Prisoner's Defence. When the watchman stopped me I told him that was the man who sent me for it, he was then about two yards before me; he took to his heels and ran off - several ran after him, but could not take him.
   JOHN FREEMAN. I saw no other man.
 THOMAS FRANCIS. There was a man several yards before him, but he did not run.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Transported for Seven Years.
 1056.  JAMES CUNNINGHAM& JOHN WYGOLD were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of April , 3 live tame fowls, price 6s., and 7 chickens, price 1s., the property of John Dixon.
 JOHN DIXON. I live in  Richardson-street, Stepney. On the 10th of April I got up between four and five o'clock in the morning; I saw my hen-roost open, which had been shut too - I went in, and missed a cock, two hens, and seven small chickens; I made enquiries, and found them all at Mr. Barber's, about a mile from my house - I had seen them all safe on the night before; the prisoner lived but a little way from me, and knew my place very well.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was Cunningham ever in your place in his life? A. Not that I know of.
   JOHN BARBER. I live at Bethnal-green. These two prisoners came to my house on Friday morning, about nine o'clock. Wygold had these fowls and chickens in a baker's basket; he said he had some fowls to sell; I asked what he wanted for them - he said 6s. 6d. or 7s. 6d; I said"I will give you 5s. 6d. for them," which I paid Wygold- they were very common fowls, middling size, and the chickens were but two or three days old.
   JOHN DIXON. I think these fowls are mine, but fowls are very much alike; I should have sold them for 6s. or 7s.
 CUNNINGHAM -    NOT GUILTY.
 WYGOLD - GUILTY. Aged 18. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
