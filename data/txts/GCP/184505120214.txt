Witnesses for the Defence.
WILLIAM TROUGHT. I live in East-lane, Walworth, and am a green
grocer-I have known the prisoner for years.-he has been a baker-I mu
a baker in 1843 and 1844-1 supplied him with bread from the 11th all
Sept. till the 23rd of April, 1844-I allowed him a halfpenny a loud
profit- I have supplied him, according to my recollection, with a couple
of French loaves and a quartern of flour a week, which I left at his home
-it was on such terms as would enable him to sell it-it is a genen
custom of bakers to give their men bread-the prisoner has children that
are bakers.
Court. Q. Do you know Mrs. Hunt? A. Now I have heard the
lives in George-street-she did not live nearer to me than to Mr. Bush,
but it would be handier for her to get a supply from me I served two
customers in the same street.
JOAN STANFIELD. I live in Earl-street, London-road, and am a bake
--I have supplied the prisoner as a baker during 1843 and 1844-I have
frequently supplied him with bread in my rounds, sometimes four times
week.
COURT. Q. What do you mean by your rounds ? A. I mean my best
where I go round to my customers-he was employed within the limits
of my rounds-when he was first in the employ, he met me and asked
me several times whether I could let him have a few loaves, and I let bin
have it at a price to sell it again-he might get a penny a loaf, but always
a halfpenny-a halfpenny is what we allow to persons we know nothing
of to sell again, but, knowing him, I would willingly allow him a penny
à loaf.
MR. CHARNOCK. Q. When did this supply take place ? A. It began
about this time two years as near as I can guess I have no books here-
I wanted no books, it was ready money-we never book it-I supply
customers near to the prosecutor four times a week, on Monday,
Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday-I met the prisoner several times, and
he took a few loaves- I should say he had about two loaves a week.
Not GUILTY.
