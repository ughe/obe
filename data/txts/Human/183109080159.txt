EMMA BROOKS. I am servant to Henry Dyner, of Great-Earl-street. I was in his shop on the 18th of August, in the afternoon; he sells ready-made clothes - the prisoner came in with another woman - they asked for two aprons; they tied them on; I said the price was sixpence each; they said they were going for some money, and wished me to trust them till they returned - I refused; they took them off, and returned them; this gown laid on a table, about a yard from the counter: they left the shop; the other woman took the gown and gave it to the prisoner, saying, "Take this, we will have something;" the prisoner walked out with it; they had not asked the price; I did not leave the shop, as there was nobody there - Mr. Dyner came in in about half an hour; I described them to him; he went to the top of the street - I saw the other woman in custody that afternoon, and the prisoner was taken next day - the other woman was discharged, and the prisoner would not have been committed, but she put up her fist, and cut my mouth open.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not a woman come to the door, and give you half a crown for a gown? A. Yes - that was a gown outside the door; the other woman stopped behind, and abused me in the shop.
   WILLIAM NORMAN. I was a Policeman. I heard of this on the 19th of August, and took the prisoner that day in Seven-dials; I told her it was for stealing a gown - she said, "The gown be d-d, it was ginned," and she scratched my face very violently - when before the Magistrate she knocked Brooks down.
 Prisoner's Defence. This girl lived with my sister four years ago, and robbed her; I met her one day, and gave her in charge: on the day in question she called me in, and asked me to buy an apron - we had some words, and I left; it is an old spite which she has against me.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1927.  JOHN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , 28 yards of cotton, value 14s., the goods of Samuel Smyth Forsaith.
   SAMUEL SMYTH FORSAITH. I am a linen-draper, and live in Whitechapel-road. On the 14th of September this cotton hung outside the door, on a rod, and was pinned - an alarm was given; I saw it was gone, and in about a quarter of an hour the prisoner was brought to the shop, and a person brought in the print.
   GEORGE MOIR. I am a shopman to Mr. Forsaith. I heard the alarm, went out, and saw the prisoner about twenty yards off with the print under his arm - two or three persons raised a cry of Stop thief! he saw me pursuing, then dropped the print, and ran; a young man stopped him - I am certain he is the person.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going home, and never saw the print till I was taken to the shop.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Fined One Shilling and Discharged.

 1929.  HENRY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 2 handkerchiefs, value 8s., the goods of Isaac Welchman.
 JAMES VALLOTON. I am shopman to Isaac Welchman, a  linen-draper, of Holborn-hill. On the afternoon of the 29th of August the prisoner came and asked to look at some black silk handkerchiefs, which were shown to him; he spread them on the counter, took from under them two handkerchiefs, and put them into his left-hand pocket - I seized him; he pulled them out of his pocket, and threw them behind him - he begged we would do nothing to him, and said these were hard times.
   BENJAMIN CATMULL. I am a constable, and took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Two Months.
 1930.  WILLIAM BARROWS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 1 handkerchiefs, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Worters.
   THOMAS WORTERS. I am a pawnbroker, in Phoenix-street, Somers'-town. On the afternoon of the 12th of September a young woman gave me information; my shopman ran out, and brought back the prisoner - I asked him what property he had about him; he said none - I sent for an officer, and he was searched; I saw him shuffling about, and this handkerchief was found at his feet; it had been hanging inside of the door, fastened with a string - the prisoner had been there to redeem a waistcoat.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q.Had you known him before? A. I had seen him once or twice - one part of it was across his feet, about three yards from the door, where it had been; there were three persons in the shop - he had been in the shop an hour and a half before, and I consider he loosened the string, so that a slight pull would get it down; he ran about fifty yards.
   WILLIAM RICHES. I am an officer. I searched the prisoner - I did not see any thing on the floor when I began to search, but I soon saw this handkerchief on the floor; I took him to the station - he said he wanted a black silk handkerchief, and he had just taken hold of this and it fell into his hat.
 Prisoner's Defence. I wanted to buy a handkerchief; I looked at this, and it fell down - I hung it over the door, and went away; when I came back it was found behind the door.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Fourteen Days.
