JAMES HIBBALD. I am an oilman, and live at No. 119, Bishopsgate-street. I do not know the prisoner—he was not my traveller, or in my employ—this bill was not written by my authority—my bills are printed—the heading is not exactly our form—we have the number, and name of the street—it is merely "London" here, and my bill heads are made out "James Hibbald," in full length—I sell such things as are men tioned in this paper—neither the bill, nor what purports to be the acquit tance, was written by any authority from me.
   COURT to JOSEPH RULE . Q.  Did your faith in the genuineness of this receipt lead to a further transaction by which you were a sufferer? A.  It did, to the supply of unreal goods—I believed the receipt to be the genuine authority of Mr. Hibbald, whose initials it bore, to be the receipt of his servant, in discharge of a supposed debt contracted with him—be lieving the genuineness of that receipt, I was led to deal with him thence-forth as the real servant of Mr. Hibbald—when I was an apprentice, I always signed a receipt "Settled same time," and my master's name, the same as this—I believed it was genuine—I gave faith to it as genuine, and acted on the footing of its being genuine. GUILTY  . Aged 30.— Judgment Respited.

WILLIAM KNOTT. I keep the Stamford Arms, in Stamford-street, Blackfriars-joad. On the 4th of April the prisoner came to my house and produced this paper—I knew Mr. Godfrey, of No. 86, Bankside—I deal with him—the prisoner said he was sent by Mr. Godfrey for a gallon of gin, and half a gallon of brandy—he had a leather apron on, such as Mr. Godfrey's men wear—I supposed him his servant, and I delivered to him a gallon of gin and half a gallon of brandy, which he took away—I have known the prisoner for a long time, which removed my suspicion.
