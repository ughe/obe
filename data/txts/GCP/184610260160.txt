JOHNSON, Mayor.
WILLIAM PALMER. I paid the prisoner half-a-crown on or about the 20th
of Sept. for bis master. I employed the prisoner every Sunday for months to
take me to church or chapel-he always charged half-a-crown.
Prisoner. Q. You never paid me but once, and that was 18. 6d. Witness.
Yes, I paid a great many more times--when my wife had not money I paid
-it was half-a-crown.
JOHN NEAL (police-constable P 30.) I took the prisoner-he told his
master he was very sorry for what he had done, and he would make it up
to him if he would allow him time.
Guilty.-Confined One Year.
2197. WILLIAM MARLER was indicted for stealing 5lbs. 2oz. of
bristles, value 10s. ; 30 brushes, 10s.; 2 wool rugs, 10s.; i tray, 6d.; and
2 yards of webbing, 4d.; the goods of Thomas Dutton Templar Sparrow,
his master; and MARIA MARLER was indicted for feloniously receiving
30 brushes, part of the same, knowing them to have been stolen ; and that
William Marler had been before convicted of felony.
Thomas DUTTON TEMPLAR SPArrow. I am a brush-maker, and live in
Stamford-street, Blackfriars-road. William Marler was in my service-in
consequence of suspicion, one evening, as he was going out, I said to him,
"John, (which was the name I called him by,) you have been robbing me"-he
said I had been a kind master, he should not requite me so-1 searched him
- I found some bristles in his pocket, and some in his bat-I sent for an
officer, and gave him into custody- I have lost a great many bristles-I
found these brushes at a pawnbroker's-1 can identify some of them, and
believe they are all mine--I had such, and lost such-I have heard nothing
against Maria Marler-every one speaks favourably of her.
William Marler. He said at the Magistrate's office that he missed two
bundles of these bristles, and these only amount to one; there was a man at
work there who brought three bundles down, and dressed them bimself, and
he brought another and gave them to me to work.
ROBERT Davis (police-constable L 120.) I went to Maria Marler's, and
asked her for some duplicates-she went to a watch-stand on the mantel-piece
-she took some papers, and selected two or three, and dropped them into
the fire-I jumped by her, and picked some duplicates for brushes out of the
fire-they led me to Mr. Mackie's, and there I found these brushes.
ALFRED MACKIE. I am a pawnbroker. Maria Marler pawned these
brushes with me last Saturday fortnight.
William Marler's Defence. I made the brushes, and took them to my
mother's ; she said if they belonged to me she would take them to pawn;
I told her they did, and she took them; the bristles I bought at different
times.
Maria Marler's Defence. I am innocent of knowing they came from his
master's.
John CRAWFORD (police-constable K 101.) I produce a certificate of
William Marler's former conviction at this Court-(read-Convicted 24th
Nov., 1845, and confined one month)-he is the person.
WILLIAM MARLER-Guilty. Aged 22.-Confined Nine Months.
MARIA MARLER-Nor GUILTY.
