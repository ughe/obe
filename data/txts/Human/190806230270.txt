 (  DEFENCE OF TROTT .) GEO. ALLEN, 150, Skidmore Street, Mile End, master plasterer. I have known Trott about ten years as a very honourable, hardworking, upright man. I know Calcutt. I went to Southend on one occasion with Trott and our wives. We met Miss Calcutt on the front with another young woman. We lunched at Mrs. Calcutt's. We met Mr. Calcutt coming out of the station about two o'clock and we went into the "Middleton" and had a drink. Calcutt took us out in the back and showed us a cart, and said, "What do you think about that?" I said, "It is a nice thing." He told the man to fetch out a horse, and said, "You had better come for a drive." We were rather surprised, but said, "All right." Some thing was said about there not being room for us, and Calcutt got a landau and we went, I think, to Hockley. Something was said about payment and Calcutt said, "That is all right, everything is settled." I was there on another occasion when Trott spent the day with me and I met Calcutt in the evening accidentally. WALTER TROTT(prisoner, on oath). I have lived in Mile End all my life and have known Calcutt from a lad. He was a customer of mine. I became a Guardian in 1904. After Calcutt became a Guardian his custom increased and became very considerable. Sometimes the account would go on two or three weeks. Mrs. Calcutt would pay. I have done carting for Calcutt—sand, carrots and coal—about £3, which is not yet paid. I did not tell Calcutt I was about to become a Guardian and I never said to him, "When I get on the Board I shall hold out my hand." I knew Calcutt was working for Crowe in 1904. Calcutt never gave me a penny except for goods supplied. I have voted for and against him. Draper took exception to Calcutt's figure for the indestructible paint, I think. I don't remember Knight ever complaining of him. I have never received anything on the Board since I have been there. What Calcutt has said is a parcel of lies. In regard to the indestructible paint, my attention was called to the schedule of prices and I was under the impression there were to be two coats of varnish at 10d. a yard and that would make 1s. 8d. I thought it must be better if Calcutt charged 1s. 6d., and that is one of the reasons I voted for it. We were all members of the Scattered Homes Committee.
