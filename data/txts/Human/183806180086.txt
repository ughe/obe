1523. JAMES WILLIAM SMITH and FREDERICK BARROW we again  indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 3 pairs of boots, value 18 s  ., the goods of Lucy Hale , the mistress of the said James Wm. Smith LUCY HALE. I keep a shoe shop. Smith was in my service—the boot now produced are mine, and were taken out of the cases in the shop. WILLIAM CHAPMAN. I am shopman to a pawnbroker, in Long-acre, I took in a pair of ladies' boots on the 6th of June from Barrow, and one pair on the 7th from Smith—he said they were for his father. HENRY NASH. I am shopman to a pawnbroker, in Ryder's-court. I took in a pair of boots in the name of Spence, they were pledged, I believe, by Barrow.
   SMITH— GUILTY. Aged 14.— Whipped, and Confined Six Days BARROW— GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Six Months.
 1524. JAMES THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of May , 1 sovereign and 1 half-sovereign, the goods of James Dawson . JAMBS DAWSON. I live with my father, in Everett-street, Russell-square—he keeps an oil and Italian warehouse. The prisoner was his errand-boy for about a fortnight—on the 17th of May I had some money in a little tin box, in a roll of pictures, in a drawer at the top of the house—the drawer had no handle to it—I saw it secure about seven o'clock in the morning, and missed it about half past six o'clock in the evening, when I came home—I have never found the money since—my father accused the prisoner of it in the evening, in my presence—he would hardly sayy any thing, whether he had taken it or not—my father sent me round to the prisoner's mother—I lost one sovereign and a half out of 3 l . 5 s . which was in the box. LYDIA DAWSON. I am the mother of James Dawson—he kept his money in the attic—on the 17th of May, my little son, nine years old, and the prisoner, were at home—my husband was out, and all my other sons—I have seven—nobody could get at the money but the prisoner—I keen no maid-servant—the prisoner was seat to the room where the money was, to clean the windows.
   Prisoner.  There were lodgers in the house. Witness. There  was a lady and gentleman, but they went out that morning, and did not return till after the prisoner had left. FREDERICK WILLIAMS( police-constable S  144.) I received information from the prosecutor's father, who went with me to the house where the pri soner lived—the prisoner's mother said she had not seen him—on the Wed nesday following I saw the prisoner standing near the house—I went to him, and asked him his name—he said his name was Simmons—I took him, and said, "Your name is Thompson, is it not?"—he said, "Yes"—I said, "You know what I want you for"—he said, "Yes, for that money from my master." JAMES DAWSON re-examined.  I lost this money on the 17th, and the prisoner left that night, and did not return.
   JURY . Q.  Had you told him of it before he went? A.  Yes. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Three Months.
