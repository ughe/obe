JAMES BUCHANNAN (police-sergeant, B 8). I received five sixpences
from Mr. Davey and five from Marshall, these are them.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This sixpence which was uttered is counterfeit;
and seven of these others are counterfeit, from the same mould-two of
the others are from one mould of William IV.
Prisoner. The landlord followed me and knocked me down in the
street; as to bad money, I had none about me.
Guilty. Aged 30.- Confined Twelve Months.

694. MARY BEASLEY, stealing 1 lamp, value 78.; the goods of
Samuel Ridenton; having been before convicted.
ELIZABETH RIDENTON. I am the wife of Samuel Ridenton, we keep
a broker's shop. On 14th Feb., at half-past nine o'clock in the morning,
I came in from the next door, and found the prisoner in my shop-I
had not known her before-she had a lamp-glass in her hand, and asked
me if I would purchase it-I saw she had got something under her cloak
-I opened her cloak, and found this lamp, it is mine- I had seen it safe
a few minutes before, on a turning-lathe-the prisoner said, "Don't give
me in charge "-and kept soliciting me to let her go, but I gave her in
charge to the policeman-I wish you to be kind with her-I do not think
she is too witty.
GEORGE Smith (policeman, G 213). I took the prisoner in custody;
and have the lamp.
Prisoner. I am very sorry for what I have done.
JOHN CLOVER (policeman, G 156). I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction at Clerkenwell-(read-Convicted Oct. 1849,
and confined One Month)-the prisoner is the person.
Guilty. Aged 17.-Confined Four Months.
