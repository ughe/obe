 1292.  WILLIAM INGRAM was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 1 pair of stockings, value 4d.; 1 shirt, value 6d., and 1 razor-strop, value 6d., the goods of Richard French.
 RICHARD FRENCH. I live in  Brick-lane, Spitalfields. These things were in my bed-room - the prisoner lodged in the same room. On the 14th of April I got up at six o'clock in the morning, and went out, leaving him in bed; another person slept in the same room, who got up, and went out when I did - I returned about twelve o'clock, and the landlady mentioned to me about a new sheet being taken off the bed; I went up, and missed these articles - I met the prisoner on the 18th of May, and asked him if he knew me - he said he did not, and I gave him in charge.
 JOHN BOURNE. I am a pawnbroker. On the 13th of April, this razor and strop were pawned in the name of Wright, for 9d.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to my lodging: the landlady told me the prosecutor wanted me at a public-house- I went there, and asked what he wanted; he said, "D-n you, it is all right, drink a glass of ale;" I did, and then he said, "Have you any objection to a young man sleeping with us?" I said if they had no objection I had not - he said, "D-n me, that is all right," and he gave me another glass of ale: at half-past eleven o'clock the landlord came to clear the house; the prosecutor said he would have some more, and he had some rum - he said to the other man,"You must stay till they are gone to bed;" he then came up, and we went to bed; I heard them talking in the night - when I awoke they were gone.
   RICHARD FRENCH re-examined. Q. Who was that young man? A. A butcher, named Lawson, who lived just by; I am certain he left the room with me; the prisoner was searched at the watch-house, but we could not find the duplicate - I asked where it was: he took me to two places, but the things were not there - he then took me to the right place.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1293.  SARAH SPALDING was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of March , 1 cloak, value 15s.; 1 veil, value 12s.; 1 bonnet, value 12s., and 1 pair of stockings, value 1s., the goods of Mary Ann Bird ; and 1 shift, value 3s.; 1 scarf, value 3s.; 1 petticoat, value 2s., and 1 pair of shoes, value 1s., the goods of Harriet Bird.
 MARY  ANN BIRD. I live at No. 3, Winchester-street, Pentonville, and am a milliner and dress-maker. The prisoner came to me, and said she had been turned out of a situation for being out an hour too late; I took her in, and gave her a lodging from Friday till Monday, the 30th of March, when she said she should get a situation; she said on Monday that she was afraid she should not get a situation till the Saturday - I said she might stay, but she left me that day without notice; I missed this property from my bed-room up one pair of stairs - I had seen them about an hour before they were taken; I saw them again on the 16th of April, when the prisoner was before the Magistrate - my sister's things were found on her person.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you lend me this property to go to my sister's, to borrow a sovereign to pay your rent? A. No; you said you had no sister in town; you did not lend me 5s. on the Saturday night before; I was not taken to the watch-house.
   HARRIET BIRD. I am the prosecutrix's sister. The prisoner occupied part of a room; I lost a shift, a scarf, a petticoat, and a pair of shoes, on the 30th of March - they had been safe in my room an hour before; the prisoner gave me no notice of going: I did not see her again till I was before the Magistrate - she had my petticoat on; my shift and scarf were found at her lodging by the officer - my shoes have not been found.
 COURT. Q. What are you? A. An upholdstress; I had known the prisoner at Ipswich; she was a servant - she was not an unfortunate woman, to the best of my knowledge; I met her in the street at Pentonville - she said she had lost her situation, and she came to tea; the next day, she said she was an hour too late, and lost her situation; I did not lend her these things; there was nothing particular happened at Clerkenwell the other day.
 Prisoner. Q.Did not you lend me the body linen and the shoes, to go out walking with you? A. No, I never did.
   SAMUEL MOUNT STEPHENS. I am a constable of Clerkenwell. I was in this box on the 15th of April last, and the prisoner came into this Court; I took her into custody on the charge of robbing Mary Ann Bird - she had this veil on her bonnet; this cloak I found at No. 20, Speldhurst-street, where she told me she lived - the stockings she had on the prosecutrix claimed, but they were not taken off; this shift was on her - this scarf was at Speldhurst-street; the bonnet I found at Bell-street, Paddington, where she had exchanged it for another.
 Prisoner. Q. Are you not in the habit of visiting these young woman? A. No; I have seen them repeatedly - I did call there once while you were there, to inquire for a person who was charged with taking a watch; I did not stop five minutes - I saw you there, and from that I knew you; you told me you came here to give yourself up to me.
 Q. Did not  Harriet Bird go for a quartern of gin, and you drink it with them? A. No; I never drank with them- I did not drink tea there; there were two brokers in the house, West and Evans, who I believe had sold her a bedstead - I do not recollect Mary Ann Bird saying if she were ruled by the officer, she would send you out of the country for life.
 JOSEPH OADBY. I am a constable. I was with the witness Stephens when the prisoner was taken; she was very candid in telling where the things were - we took her the next morning to the place.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not I say they lent me the property as soon as I saw you? A. Yes, but the prosecutrix contradicted it.
