BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT.
(Wednesday, October 23.)
VAUGHAN, Reuben (28, fitter), pleaded guilty to stealing a
gramophone and 26 records, the goods of John Edward Redon; forg-
ing and uttering a certain acquittance or receipt for money, to wit
for the sum of £10 10s., with intent to defraud; obtaining by false
pretences from Thomas Causer the sum of £10 10s., with intent to
defraud. Prisoner confessed to a previous conviction. Several other
convictions were also proved.
Sentence, Six years' penal servitude,
STRATFORD, Ernest (19, barman), pleaded guilty. of carnally
knowing Daisy Florence Gudlestone, a girl above the age of 13 years
and under the age of 16 years. It appeared that the girl had not
been very unwilling. Sentence, Six weeks' bard labour.
SWETTENHAM, Kate, pleaded guilty of feloniously marrying
John De Fraine, her husband being then alive.
It was stated that the first husband had received 10 years' penal
servitude. Upon his coming out prisoner provided him with money
to enter into a business, but he soon- got tired of that. Then he re-
ceived from prisoner more money, in order to go to Canada, which
he did not do; finally because prisoner would not give him more
money he informed the police of the bigamy.
Prisoner was bound over on her own recognisances in £10 to come
up for judgment if called upon,
SAUNDERS, Henry (21, engineer), pleaded guilty of stealing one
brooch and one ning, the goods of Solomon Bolsom.
Prisoner had previously been convicted of fraud on a Milway, for
which he was fined.
Sentence, 12 months' hard labour. An order for restitution was
made.
COWDERAY, Joseph, pleaded guilty to assaulting Joseph Day,
'thereby occasioning him actual bodily harm.
It appeared that prisoner was under thº influence of drink at the
time, and was behaving in a disorderly manner in a shop, and inter
fering with goods for sale. He was very violent, and struck the
officer Day who arrested him a very severe blow, from which he was
still suffering. He also threatened in a most dangerous way another
officer who came to assist
Bentence, Six months' hard labour.

Mr. Arthur Gill prosecuted; Mr. G. L. Hardy defended.
