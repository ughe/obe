274. JOSEPH POULTON was indicted for stealing 1 purse, value 4 d  .; and 1 half-sovereign; the property of Henry James Shrapnell . HENRY JAMES SHRAPNELL. On the 25th of Oct. I put a half-sovereign in my purse in my jacket pocket, and put it on a chair in the kitchen—there was no person present but the prisoner—I missed the purse and half-sovereign about a quarter before eight—I asked the prisoner if he knew anything about it—he said, "No, he did not"—no one else had been in the room, I am sure, during the whole time—he was not searched; but on Sunday, the 29th, he came to me in the tap-room for 6 d . worth of halfpence, and pulled out of his pocket a half-sovereign—I asked him where he got it—he said his master gave it him for wages—that was the money I had lost on the 25th. JAMES CODRINGTON. I am the prisoner's master. I did not pay the pri soner any half-sovereign; I paid him 10 s . in silver. JAMES CUNNINGHAM( police-constable V  195.) I was on duty in Wands worth-road on the 29th of Oct., and received the prisoner in charge—he wanted me to take him to his master—I said I could no—he then pulled out the half-sovereign and said, "Harry, here it the half-sovereign, take it." GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Months .
 275. MARY ANN CARTWRIGHT was indicted for stealing 3 yards of jean, value 1 s  . and 1 cloak, 3 s  .; the goods of Richard Young , her master. ELIZA YOUNG. I am the daughter of Richard Young. I missed a woollen cloth boy's cloak and some jean in August—these are them—the prisoner was in our service. THOMAS ELWOOD, shopman to Mr. Chapman, pawnbroker. I produce this cloak and a piece Of jean—the prisoner pawned the cloak—I cannot say who pawned the jean. THOMAS DALY( policeman. ) I produce a duplicate of the jean, which I got from the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 44.— Confined Three Months .
 276. THOMAS ROSS was indicted for embezzling 8 s  . 2 d  .; and that be he had been before convicted of felony. HENNRY JOHN LEGG. I am a shoemaker; the prisoner was in my service. On the 24th of Oct. I sent him with fourteen pairs of shoes to Mr. Thoma's, in Cheapside—he was to receive 8 s . 2 d ., which he ought to have brought back, and other materials—if he received that money, he did not return.
   Prisoner.  I never saw you before in my life. Witness.  Yes, you did—they were fourteen pair of back-strap shoes, six by nine—the prisoner had been with me a few days over a fortnight—I am positive he is the person. JOHN STANCOMB. I am foreman to Mr. Thomas. The prisoner brought fourteen pairs of shoes on the 24th of last month—I gave him 8 s . 2 d ., and a dozen pairs of work for his master to manufacture.
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you know what they were? A.  Some were grained patent, and some grey—you gave in fourteen pairs of shoes, and I recollect they were all back-straps.
   Prisoner.  All I have to say is, that the prosecutor is as false a swearing man as ever swore in his life, and it is a pity such a man should be allowed to take an oath. THOMAS BALDWIN( police-sergeant L  13.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from the Clerk of the Peace for Surrey—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 23.— Transported for Seven Years .
