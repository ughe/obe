Mr. Courtney would generally open the letters in the morning; they would afterwards be put away in the back office, not in Cleobury's room—I daresay I have seen 60 or 80 letters of complaint from persons who have had subscriptions demanded from them, from after Chinnery left until I left—I had nothing to do with the collecting of debts—I kept no book showing that the money received from the Court was sent out of the coffers of Smith. JOHN THOMAS DAY. I am manager to a shoe and leather company, and in 1894 I was with two others, the proprietors of the Drapers' World,  and had the lease of the second floor at 19, Cheapside—in 1897 I was interested in the lease, and granted the sub-lease to Smith on December 20th, 1897—it is granted in the name of A. J. H. Smith—after this prosecution had commenced the prisoner Smith called on me and asked me to release him from the lease—I did not know him—I said, "Are you my tenant, or in your son my tenant, because I notice the lease is in the name of A. J. H. Smith, which is the name of your son"—he said, "Yes, there was a mistake made by the solicitor in drawing the lease; I adopted the name to save trouble"—he has always paid his rent. CHRISTOPHER MAUGHAN. I am manager to W. Cave & Co., printers, of Brixton—we have done printing for Smith for many years—the printed documents which have been put in in this case have been printed by us—we printed them from time to time, from 1,000 to 10,000 at a time—we also printed letter-paper on the instructions of Smith, headed with the name of Cleobury—we were paid for all the printing by Smith, never by Cleobury.
