ROBERT BOULTBEE (Policeman G 164). On 11th September about a quarter
to four o'clock I was called to the prisoner's room, and found it all over
blood-the prosecutrix was in the next room-I took the prisoner to the
station, and on the way he said, "I have made a pretty job of it now, but
she aggravated me to it. I came home at 11 o'clock, and she did not get
home till 2; the child was very cross, and I could not not keep it quiet;
she would not get into bed, and this is what it has come to "-he also said
that he supposed she had taken a little too much whisky-the girl had
nothing on, and was covered with blood, and he only had on a bit of his
shirt sleeve-he put his wife's shift on to go to the station--the child was
in bed in Mrs. Cooper's room -I found these tongs by the fireplace
ouvered with fresh blood, and on some rags in a wooden chest under the
bed this table-knife with blood running from it—the lid had been lifted
up and the knife dropped under.
ELIZA TRIPP. Last September I was living with the prisoner as his
Wife on 11th September I came home between 1.30 and 2 in the morn-
ing, and found the prisoner going to bed-I had had a drop too much
drink, but he had had nothing at all-be asked where I had been-I
told him I would not tell him, he must find out-he went to bed- I did
not undress myself-he said that if I told him where I had been he
would not touch me I told him he could do what he liked, I should not
tell him-I then went to bed too, and he asked me again, but I would
not tell him, so he got out of bed, and hit me on the head with the
tongs- I was not in bed then; I jumped out at the same time that he did,
and after he hit me I got a knife-I always run to the knife when I am
in a temper-I got out of bed because I knew he was going to hit me I
tried to stab him for hitting me I never cried out, I only said, “Oh,
Jack, don't hit me”-I never used the word "murder" all the time we
were quarrelling, but I believe people in the next room called out
"murder” when they heard I was being knocked about-the prisoner
tried to pull the knife out of my hand, and my thumb got cut in the
struggle-I tried to stab him with it on the back of his neck, when he
put his head down and I bit him, and we fell on the floor together, and
he got the knife from me, and cut my thumb in getting it—he had struck
me two blows on the head with the tongs, but not heavy blows—I did
not feel them much at the time I believe he got the knife from me,
but I do not know anything more till the policeman came in-I was not
lying on any chairs, there was not room for them where we were he
has only hit me once before, but I have stabbed him twioe -I ran out
of the room with my petticoat round me I do not remember how my
throat was cut-I was not taken to Mrs. Cooper's room; I went there.
By the Court. I said before the magistrate that I thought he tried to
out my throat-it was done,I remember the surgeon coming and ex-
amining my wounds.
