   New Court, Monday, March  13 th , 1905.
   Frederick Battlebury, in his defence on oath, said he engaged Lamplough because he found he was a successful traveller; that he was about to advertise for persons who had witnessed the accident on March  8 th or  9 th; that Lamp  lough had said he was well known in Hackney, and that he accepted his offer to make inquiries, but that he had been bothered for money by Mason, and doubted him; that Haysman never called on him about the accident, but only about a grandfather's clock he had for sale, and as he found the evidence was not sufficient he abandoned the action, paying the costs and thought the matter was ended, but there was no conspiracy to defraud or to suborn evidence.
   Evidence for Frederick Battlebury. HARRY CLIFFORD TURNER.
