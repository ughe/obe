JOHN BRANN. I am a policeman of Maidstone. The prisoner, whom I had seen once before, came to me on the 22nd of July, and said he wished to give himself up for horse stealing, he and another had stolen a horse.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was he drunk? A.  No, sober—he said, "I want to give myself into your custody"—I said, "For what?"—he said, "Horse steling"—I said, "From whom did you steal the horse?"—he said, "From Mr. Holmes, at Walworth"—he said there was a previous charge, and I should find that on him—I took him to the station, and found a bill on him that he was out on bail—he did not mention the name of the other man—I cau tioned him, and asked him what reason he had for giving himself up—he said through the men that were bail for him.
   MR. CHAKNOCK called HENRY NEVILL. I am a coal-porter. The prisoner used to work as a coal-heaver—on Thursday, the 18th of July, I was with him at the Waterman's Arms, Belvidere-road—my brother was there, but he is now gone into the country—I was with the prisoner there from one o'clock till nearly four—I and he were working that day for Messrs. Cole and Child—I know what day it was, because it was the first day I had worked for them for these two years—I know I was with him till four o'clock, because I had then to go to work—there was a clock in the yard—I cannot tell how far that is from Silver-street, Clerkenwell—I should think it is a mile and a half or two miles—I did not leave the prisoner at the public-house—he went away, and told me he was going home—I am certain I was with him from one o'clock till four.
   COURT . Q.  What work had you to do that day? A.  To load the wagon—we do it at so much per ton—I began work that day at six o'clock in the morning, and worked till six at night, but from one till four there were no wagons to load—my brother was not at work with me, but he could prove the prisoner was there from one till four o'clock that day.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined One Year .
