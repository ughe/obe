 1940. MARIA ELIZABETH LANGLEY was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of March , 2 pillows, value 4 s  . 6 d  .; 1 candlestick, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 pillow-case, value 6 d  .; 1 bolster, value 5 s  .; 2 curtains, value 3 s  .; 1 quilt, value 2 s  .; and 1 dressing-glass, value 8 s  .; the goods of Sophia Ledbrook . SOPHIA LEDBROOK. The prisoner lodged with me, in Dorset-crescent, nearly six months—she sometimes went out nursing—I missed this property when I went into the room with the officer, who came to me with the duplicates—I never permitted her to pawn them—she was in distress at times, and neglected by her husband. GEORGE HANSON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a blanket, a swing-glass, a counterpane and curtain—the counterpane and curtain were pawned by the prisoner—the others I did not take in. GEORGE THOMSON. I am a journeyman pawnbroker—I produce a pillow—I do not know whether the prisoner pawned it, but I know her. JOHN LOVICK. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pillow pawned in March, in the name of Ann Adams—I do not know who by. CHARLES PALMER( police-sergeant G  19.) I took the prisoner on ano ther charge—I went to the prosecutrix's house, and she missed this pro perty—I found the duplicates of all the property on the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  I was in distress through my husband's leaving me destitute at home—it was my full intention to have restored every thing. SOPHIA LEDBROOK. I was in the country for a month, and when I came home I missed these things—her husband did not live there. GUILTY.
