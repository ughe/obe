This is my watch—my son goes up with the barge—the prisoner generally takes our boats at Reading or Newberry—I met him in Castle-street, Reading, on the 31st of May—I said, "Well, Isaac"—he said, "Well, master, what stir have you been making about me?"—I said, "About that watch"—he directly said he took the watch out of the cupboard, and put it into his pocket, kept it a day or two, he was ashamed to give it up, and had pawned it at Wandsworth—I asked him for the duplicate—he said he had left it at Paddington—when I came to Paddington, he got it of a person, and gave it me.

I employed the prisoner to tow a barge—I agreed to give him 1 s ., and when the work was done I gave him 1 s .—he returned it to me, and I went on shore—he afterwards asked me if I would give him what I promised him," and I said, "I have given you one"—on the Tuesday after I went to pay Mr. Chandler for the barge, and the prisoner came up, with a weapon in his hand, and said, would I pay, and he up with a cart prop, and knocked me down directly—he hit me right over the head—it bled for five or six days after.
   Prisoner.  If you had been injured you could not have been walking about and drinking. Witness.  I have never been drunk since I have been in London. WILLIAM REEVES.
