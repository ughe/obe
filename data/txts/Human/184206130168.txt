   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  I take it for granted, yon are in the habit of buying books? A.  I am—I bought the Ritson, I believe at Gladdin's—it is worth a guinea and a half—I think I got it for 16 s .—it might be 14 s .—my books have been sold by auction—the value of the books I sent was 1000 l .—I sent these books to Evans, the first week in March—I left the hospital the night before the books were sent away-I went to the hospital two or three times after I left—I had dealt with the prisoner perhaps about ten times—I found him fair and honest—they were cheap—they were books of trifling value—I got them as cheap as I could—the most of them were second-hand school books—the Camp bell has not been found—the Sharp's edition of the poems, were duodecimo size—I did not leave the stall before I gave the prisoner into custody—I sent for the policeman.
   MR. CLARKSON . A..  What was the value of the Campbell? A.  Not much—it was bound in Russia, and my name being in it, I must have lent it some one to read—I always put my name in it, that they may know where to return it—I have very frequently found a book, and not known who it belonged to. MICHAEL GORMAN. I am porter to Mr. Evans, in Pall-mall. I was employed to remove the prosecutor's books from the London Hospital to my master's premises—the prosecutor unlocked the bookcase, and pointed out these books to me—there were several bundles tied up, before I got there—I put them in bags, and the bags into the cart, and took them to Mr. Evans—it was stated ion the newspaper after that one of these bags was stolen from the cart, but I am positive it was not from the time they were put into the cart, till I got to the end of Aldgate, as I walked on one side, or behind the cart, and from there, to the best of my recollection, I got into the cart—I should think there was no opportunity for any one to steal them while I was in the library, unless they were stolen after I bagged them, and went out to get some refreshment—to the best of my belief there were none untied from the time I went out—I deposited the books in my master's premises, locked the door, and gave the key to Mr. Evans.
   Cross-examined. Q.  If I understand, you are ready to swear that none of these books were stolen? A.  Yes—not between the London Hos pital and Mr. Evans's—we had a pot of beer on the road—I left the cart outside for about two minutes—the carman went in first—I followed him—I am in the h bit of attending Evan's auction rooms—they sell books cheap there sometimes.
