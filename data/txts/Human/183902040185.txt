JOHN WARREN. I live with Mr. James Ashman and Son, pawnbrokers, in Long-acre. On the 29th of January, the prisoner Inglis came and asked for Mercer-street—he had not been gone two minutes before I saw him and Jewell looking in at the window close to the goods—Inglis came in again—he saw the shopman, and ran out, and in about half an hour I muted a pair of trowsers—these are them. WILLIAM HOWARD. I am a policeman. About two o'clock, on the 29th of January, Warren described the prisoners to me—I saw them looking into another shop, and took them—Jewell said, "You can search me, I have nothing about me"—on Inglis I found 7 s ., and on Jewell a large knife, and 3 d .—they were remanded till the following day, and then Inglis told me where I could find the trowsers and he took me to No. 88, Monmouth-street, to a cellar—I found a girl—she called her mother, who said she had bought them for 2t., that they had been seated, and she had sold them for 3 s ., but she would get them if I would let her—she then went out with the girl, who came back with the trowsers, and said her mother was ill, but she had sent her on, not to keep me waiting—the woman did not appear before the magistrate and the girl said her mother was taken ill with the fright of my going, and was compelled to go to the Lying-in Hospital. MARY DEVANNY. I live with my mother at No. 88, Monmouth-street. She buys and sells second-hand clothes—I was in her place when Inglis brought these trowsers, and asked half-a-crown for them—Jewell was waiting for him close by—my mother gave Inglis 2 s . for them.
 (The prisoners received good characters.)
   INGLIS  — GUILTY. Aged 9.
   JEWELL  — GUILTY. Aged 11
   Recommended to mercy. Whipped and discharged.
