600. EDWARD WILSON (36), Unlawfully conspiring with James
Wilding and others to obtain by false pretences from John Langford
Hughes 301. with intent to defraud. (See Vol. 80, page 189).
MR. DALTON conducted the Prosecution, and Mr. Sims the Defence.
JOAN LANGPORD HUGHES. I keep the post-office at 42, Curzon Street,
Mayfair-on 30th April or 1st May, 1874, a man named Wilding came to
my shop and asked my clerk in my hearing for change for a cheque--
this is the cheque ( produced), it is for 301.—the clerk declined to cash it
and I stepped forward and said I had not got sufficient to cash it for him
-Wilding said “You will very much oblige Mr. Warren if you will let
him have the change "--Mr. Warren is a publican round the corner-I
have cashed cheques for him before-I said I have not got sufficient "--
the prisoner came in at that time and said "I want a 201. order and then
you can oblige your friend”--the clerk gave two orders of 101. each and I
gave the 201. and 101. more for the 301. cheque to Wilding and let him go-I
afterwards saw Wilding at Mr. Lewis's office in Marlborough Street.
JANE TEORLEY. I am single and reside at 40, Hertford Street, Mayfair
-I have known a man named Wilding for fourteen years I have met the
prisoner at Mr. Wilding's once, that was on the 28th or 29th April-
Wilding lived in Davies Street-I saw the prisoner there with a cheque-
book and I saw him write in it, but I don't know what he was writing
he asked me if could change a cheque for 181.-I said I could not, but I
would go and ask one of the tradespeople-I took it to Mr. Devereux and
asked him to change it-he said "I have changed two before this, I can't
change any more," and the prisoner came up and said it was
good for 1001.--I said “You may be honest and upright and the
cheqne might be bad " I gave the prisoner the cheque in Mr.
Devereur's presence and said would have nothing to do with it-he
followed me to several places and asked them, but they wonld not have
anything to do with it-Wilding was not there then-I afterwards saw
some money pass between Wilding and Wilson-Wilson came and demanded
some money from Wilding, I don't know what it was-Wilding gave him
money, it was over 121., but I could not say bow much for I did not take
any notice not knowing there was anything wrong going on--I was sober-
-Mrs. Wilding and Mr. Wilding's sister were present and also the house,
keeper who used to live with Mr. Wilding and the children.
