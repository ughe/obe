The RECORDER, in leaving this case to the Jury, stated that simple as the
facts were, they involved a question of law of some importance-viz., whether
they established a larceny or not; there was no doubt that if a person entrusted
his servant with a sovereign to change, and the servant appropriated it to his
oton use, that would be a larceny, because the owner had not parted with the
possession of the sovereign, his servant's possession being, in fact, his own; it
had, however, been held that if a person parted with the possession of his pro-
perty to another, not his servant, and that other disposed of it for his own pur-
poses, that would not amount to a larceny; but if property be obtained from the
owner upon some mere pretence, with the intention of converting it to the use of
the person obtaining it, that would be a larceny; and it could only be upon that
ground that the Jury in this case could find a verdict of guilty.
