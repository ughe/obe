ELIZABETH SALISBURY. My father keeps a butcher's-shop, in Leader-street, Chelsea. On the 13th of April, the prisoner came about half-past seven o'clock in the evening for a saveloy, which came to 1 d .—he gave me a bad sixpence in payment—I gave it to my brother. THOMAS SALISBURY. I am the brother of Elizabeth Salisbury. I re ceived the sixpence from her on the 13th of April—I took it to my father, and wished him to give change. JOHN SALISBURY. I am the father of Thomas Salisbury. I received a bad sixpence from him—I kept it till I gave it up at the police-station. JOHN LONG. I am a policeman. I received a bad sixpence from Mr. Salisbury, and two pieces from Mr. Howell—I produce them. MR. JOHN FIELD. The sixpence and the two pieces of a sixpence are all counterfeit.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The last piece was passed from one to another, and was out of their sight. THOMAS SALISBURY re-examined.  It was passed round the shop to different people, but was not out of my sight.
   Prisoner. Q.  How do I know you might not have changed it? A.  I am sure I did not. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Six Months.
 1529. MARY ANN SAWYER was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. ELLIS and ESPINASSE conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE WILLIAMS. I am a milkman, and live in Cleveland-street, Fitzroy-square. On the 13th of April, the prisoner came to my shop for some eggs—I served her with sixpenny worth—she gave me a bad crown-piece in payment—I gave her into custody, and gave the crown to Harris after marking it. WILLIAM HARRIS. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into cus tody, and received this crown from Mr. Williams—the prisoner was dis charged on the next day. FREDERICK HUMPHREYS. I am servant to Mr. Tuck, a nurseryman, in Sloane-street. On the 13th of April, the prisoner came and bought sixpennyworth of mignionette-seed—she gave me a bad five-shilling-piece—I gave it to Dunbar, the foreman, for change—I afterwards asked the prisoner where she got it—she said from a gentleman in Oxford-street—she did not know who he was—I then gave her into custody. WILLIAM THOMAS DUNBAR. I am the foreman. I received a crown from Humphreys—I saw it was bad—I kept it in my hand, and gave it to the policeman—I asked the prisoner where she came from—she said, "From Westminster"—I asked her how she came so far for the seed—she said she had an aunt at Knightsbridge, and she had received the money from her to get the seed—I said I would send for a policeman to go with her—she begged I would not. THOMAS WORFELL. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into cus tody at Mr. Tuck's—I received from Dunbar this crown-piece—the pri soner said she got it from a gentleman in Oxford-street, but she did not know who he was. MR. JOHN FIELD. These crowns are both counterfeit. GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined Six Months.
