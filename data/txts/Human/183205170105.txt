ELIZA GRIGGS. I am in the service of Mr. Henry Vickress- he keeps the  Pied Bull, in the Lower-road, Islington- the prisoner is his porter. In consequence of something that occurred. I placed 20s. in silver in the till, which had been marked by Mr. Vickress, and given to me- a little after six o'clock in the morning on the 6th of May, the prisoner had been cleaning the bar, and I missed two shillings - when Mr. Vickress came down I told her of it; the officer was called, and these two shillings were found on him; I know they are two of the shillings which had been in the till - they have the marks on them.
 GEORGE COLLIER (Police-constable N 94). I was called in, and took the prisoner - I found the two marked shillings in his waistcoat pocket.
 HENRY VICKRESS. The prisoner was in my employ. -I marked these two shillings, and know them again.
 The prisoner received a good character, and his master engaged to employ him again.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Confined Seven Days.
 1345.  SARAH GOLDING was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of May , 2 pairs of gloves, value 1s.; 12 yards of quilling, value 7d.; 1 yard of ribbon, value 4d., and 3 yards of lace, value 2s. 9d., the goods of Richard Hall and another.
 CHARLES WARMALL. I am shopman to Richard Hall and another,  linen-draper s, St. John-street. On the 9th of May the prisoner came and bought some quilling net, gloves, and stockings, which came to 1s. 10d. - I saw her conceal these articles, which were worth 4s. 9d.; she handed them to her little boy, who is about seven years old, and he put them into her basket; she took them out and put them into her pocket - I let her go out, and as she passed the window I took her back; my master told her to give up what she had - she took them out and put them down.
 CHARLES WRIGHT (Police-constable G 105). I received charge of the prisoner, and produce the articles, which were given to me by Mr. Freeman - the prisoner said her little boy took them, and she was not conscious of it.
 Prisoner's Defence. The little boy put them in unknown to me.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1346.  JOSEPH HENLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of April , 2 pairs of callipers, value 3s.; 1 hammer, value 3s.; 1 fuller, value 1s. 6d., and 1 chisel, value 1s., the goods of James Wilmott.
 JAMES WILMOTT. I live in  Hind-street, Hatton-wall. I deal in tools, and am a smith- the prisoner was in my father's employ, and afterwards in mine for two years; he had my tools to work with, but was not to take them away- I missed two pairs of brass callipers, and some other tools; I spoke to the prisoner about them - he said another person, named Hudson, had taken them, whom I had employed two days in the week; I afterwards went to a house in St. Luke's, with an officer - I found the prisoner there, and these callipers in a tea-caddy, and under the bed I found this hammer, and this fuller - he said they were his; I can speak to these two pairs of callipers with certainty, as being mine; one of them is broken at one end, and the other overlaps.
 Prisoner. I brought them up to town with me when his father sent for me - I worked for him seven years, and then for his widow, when this prosecutor turned her out of the house; and now he wants to get me out - he has reduced me to four days' work in a week; I used to live in the house. Witness. I had a spare room, and I let him have it - he said, when those things were found, that he brought them from the country; I cannot say any thing about the hammer and fuller, but I lost such things.
 HENRY HUDSON. I work for the prosecutor. There were two pairs of callipers there, and this pair I can be certain of; I have known this these twelve years - this hammer I am certain of, the prisoner made it himself.
   JOHN LEAVER. I work for the prosecutor. I have known this hammer for eighteen months, and this fuller I can swear to by some marks on the neck of it - it is the prosecutor's; the prisoner knows that he made another a size larger, because this was too small for some work.
 Prisoner. About four years ago this man was discharged for stealing a hammer, and I got him in. Witness. I was discharged, but I never knew what for.
 SAMUEL FREEMAN. I have worked for the prosecutor, and have known the prisoner twelve months. A few weeks ago he came to do some work at my place; he brought some tools, and said they were for his use; he threw them down, and said he would b-y soon alter them; this gave me suspicion, and I told the prosecutor; the prisoner came one morning just as I opened my shop, and took the tools.
   THOMAS MORRIS(Police-constable G 27). I took the prisoner at a house in Little Mitchel-street - I found these articles there; he said they were his own.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the tools were his own - and that he had gone into business on his own account, which had caused this prosecution against him.
   JAMES WILMOTT. I believe he had gone into business.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1347.  MARY JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of May , 3 lbs. weight of pork, value 2s., the goods of James Pallet.
 EDWARD KING. I am servant to Mr.  James Pallet, a cheesemonger, of Oxendon-street, Somers'-town. On the 5th of May a customer told me this pork was taken; I pursued, and took the prisoner - she dropped it by her side.
 SAMUEL HEYWOOD (Police-constable S 144). I took the prisoner - she asked me to forgive her.
     GUILTY. Aged 66. - Confined Seven Days.
 1348.  JAMES THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of May , 4 loaves of bread, value 1s. 6d., the goods of William Martin.
   ROBERT HIGGINS. I am in the service of Mr. William Martin, a baker, in Chancery-lane. I left my basket in Cursitor-street; I was not absent two minutes - I turned, and saw the prisoner take four loaves out of it, and run away; I ran, and called Stop thief! he threw them down- I never lost sight of him till the officer took him.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Six Weeks.
