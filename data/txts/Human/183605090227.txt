THOMAS HADDEN. I am in the employ of Mr. Strang. I noticed, on two days in April, two children, daughters of the prisoner, who are ten and twelve years old, were loitering about—my master had missed things before, and desired me to watch them—we found a mug on one off them. JOSEPH BUTTERFILL. I am a constable. Between eight and mine o'clock on the evening of the 28th of April, I went to Mr. Strang, find ing he had two children in custody who had robbed him of different articles—he was not at home—I waited while Mrs. Strangg sent for him—he came with two other persons, and we thought proper to go to the residence of the prisoner, who occupies a room as a weekly tenant—there is a garden, but I do not know whether she claims it—it belongs to that house—I believe she has access to it, and goes through it—I told her I had her two children in custody, respecting the things that they had taken from Mr. Strang's—I named the candlestick and some cups and saucers—finding the children were detained, she stated that she had placed the things in the gar den—she went with me into the garden, and pointed out the place, and there we found the things—she begged that I would have mercy on her—I told her it was out of my power to do any thing but take her to the station-house till the following day. WILLIAM CAMPION. I am a constable. I went to the prisoner's house where she lodged, and told her we should be under the necessity of taking her into custody—she said she would take us to where the things were—she took us into the garden, and pointed out one spot where we found these things—I saw the ground had been dug in another part, and there found the other things—she is in distress, I believe she has not a thing to lie upon. NOT GUILTY.

 1344. WILLIAM BOWDELL was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of August , 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  .; the goods of a person unknown, from his person. RICHARD TOZER( police-constable A  120) On the 1st of August I was in front of Greenwich Colleg—there was a mob in consequence of their Majesties coming to visit the college—I saw the prisoner with another man—the prisoner gave his name Thompson—I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief from a man, and give it to his companion, named Dobsen—I took them both with assistance to the station-house—the prisoner was remanded—he broke out of the cage, and got away—I do not know what became of the gentleman—he did not appear—the officer went to give something to a person in the cage, and this prisoner and the others who were there knocked the man down and ran away—he was taken a fort night ago—when the prisoner gave the other man the handkerchief he put it into his hat, and put it on his head—in going to the station, Dubson took his hat off and gave me this handkerchief—Dobson was committed for a month.
   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  Had you seen the prisoner before? A.  Not to my knowledge—I had known Dobson—this was about three o'clock in the afternoon—I did not see the gentleman afterwards—I do not know that the gave any name to me—I did not hear him—I took the prisoner about half a mile to the station-house—I have been to where he lived—his neighbours were surprised to hear of it, and said he was an honest, hard-working man.
