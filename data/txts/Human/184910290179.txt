I did not take notice—the prisoner served the first and second-class—there may be another clerk who served part of them—I have the book here, to show how many tickets are acknowledged to be issued that night; "on 1st Sept., for the second-class, thirty-eight"—I have not the first-class book here—this book would show all the second-class tickets that were issued that night, if it were correctly kept, and none but the proper number from the funnel had been issued—the prisoner kept this book that night—here is the commencing number, 45, and the closing number is 83, which makes thirty-eight—he charges himself with thirty-eight tickets that night—I have a book which tells the amount of money I received from him that night, but it is not here—the money embezzled for these tickets would not be accounted for—the money was correct to a farthing according to the tickets he accounted for—he accounted to me about a quarter before nine o'clock that night—I cannot tell how much money he paid me.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q. IS  it his duty to account at night for the money received in the day? A.  Yes; I did not check it with the number of tickets that night, but afterwards—the money he accounted for was correct with the number of tickets that appeared to have been issued—the money is accounted for to me every night—I cannot then refer to the tickets—the money for the thirty-eight second-class tickets in this book, was accounted for to me by the prisoner that night—I should not have found out afterwards that there had been more tickets issued than he accounted for—the tickets were in the Audit-office—if tickets had been taken out of their order, issued, and money received for them, and the tickets put into the drawer again, I could not have detected them that night; but afterwards, when the account went through the Audit-office, I could.
   Q.
