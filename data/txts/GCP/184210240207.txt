the 11th of June-there were two Beaumonts, the father and son-I saw
them both, and Mr. Gosbell and a Mr. Smith, but not this prisoner--they all
appeared to act as shopmen and masters-it was some time before I knew
who was master-Gosbell was assisting in the shop as shopman-the
stock of the shop appeared to consist but of few goods-I remained there
till the shop was closed, which was some time about the middle of August
I only know of two parcels coming down during that time I did not
see any silks or velvets in the shop-there were four shawls hanging up,
I did not see any more-customers came and asked for articles they could
not be supplied with, and went away without being served-Beaumont and
Smith were there till the Wednesday in the first week-they then left, and
came back again on Saturday evening-they went away again, I suppose
1 on the Monday, and returned on the following Saturday, about six
o'clock in the evening, with two ladies--Shackleford came with them
I shut up the shop that night between eleven and twelve o'clock,
and I went home-I asked Gosbell what time I should come on
Monday morning to open shop-he went into the room behind the shop,
where Smith, the two Beaumonts, and Shackleford were, to inquire-he
came back, and said I was not wanted 'till eight o'clock-I went on the
Monday morning about eight-the shop was open-Gosbell was there, and
the elder Beaumont-I did not see any others-Beaumont told me to dust
the window, and then he said I was not wanted, I might look in about
noon+I left the shop-I returned between twelve and one o'clock, and
found the elder Beaumont there on the following Saturday young Beau-
mont and Smith came down in the last buss, about hall-past nine in the evening
--they staid about half-an-hour, and then wept away-I have not seen them
since-Gosbell and old Beaumont continued at the shop-old Beaumont re-
mained there till the shop was closed, which was in the middle of August, on a
Thursday-I closed the shop on the Wednesday night-Beaumont desired
me to get a cart to remove the goods, and said he should not open the shop
any more--I got a cart the following morning to remove the remainder of
the stock, and what furniture there was, to a house which his wife had taken
opposite the barracks- I went there with a cart and donkey at five o'clock
-I found old Beaumont there-he directed me, and I took the things to
this house opposite the barracks--there was some hosiery and other linen-
drapery goods, there were six boxes in all-there was very little furniture,
I suppose altogether it was not worth 501.-after Smith and Beaumont had
left the last time, I saw a decrease in the quantity of stock, from the
shelves-some pieces that hung about the shop for making dresses were
gone--young Beaumont's wife was there for three or four days-no goods
were unpacked in the cellar while I was there--- I went into the cellar several
times, I never saw any goods there I had to go there every evening, to
turn the gas on and off.
Cross-examined by Mr. Price. Q. Have there not been some injurious
reports about the Beaumonts ? A. There had been ; two gentleman came
down one morning, and were talking there for three quarters of an hour
Beaumont left Maidstone before I heard any rumours- the goods were
removed openly in an uncovered cart, and we were there till ten o'clock.
Cross-examined by MR. WILDE. Q. What was your occupation when you
first went there? A. The first thing I did was to clean the windows-1
attended there every day, and went round with some bills all round the
