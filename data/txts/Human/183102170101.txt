 585.  WILLIAM WALTON was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of February , 1 door-mat, value 5s., the goods of Abraham Lamert.
   JOSEPH LAMERT. I am the son of Abraham Lamert- he is a patent medicine-vender. On the 9th of February I was sitting in our parlour, and was alarmed by my dog, who heard the prisoner in the passage - I got up, opened the door, and the mat was gone; I looked out, and saw the prisoner about four doors off with the mat on his back - I pursued him, brought him back, and sent for a Police-constable - I have seen the prisoner before, but did not know him; our front door is always open, and this mat was between that and the inner door.
 GUILTY. Aged 55. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Three Weeks.
 586.  MARY WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , 1 bonnet, value 1s.; 1 bag, value 1s., and 1 book, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Stillman , from the person of Hannah Amelia Stillman. (See page 262.)
   SUSAN STILLMAN. I am the wife of Thomas Stillman; we live at No. 15, Sherrard-street, Golden-square - my daughter Hannah Amelia is five years old. On the 17th of January she had been at school the whole of the day, and ought to have returned at a quarter-past four o'clock, but she did not come till a quarter-past five, when she came without her bonnet, her bag, her book, and two or three other things - she was in a very fatigued state, and had been crying.
   ANN HOLE. I keep the school to which this child comes - I tied on her bonnet at four o'clock, on the 17th of January, and sent her home with her bag and her book; my house is not a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's.
   ANN LOMAX. The prisoner came to my shop, No. 78, Monmouth-street, which is about fifty yards from the prosecutor's, on the 17th of January; she brought an old bonnet in her hand for sale; I asked what she wanted for it - she said 6d.; I said I could only sell it for that, and I offered her 4d. - I asked if it was her own; she said Yes, that her sister lived in a gentleman's family, and she should have many of these little things; this is the bonnet.
   JOHN JAMES GANNELL. I am a Police-constable. I saw the prisoner on my beat on the 17th of January; I watched her for some time - she went into a marine-store shop, and offered a small jug for sale; the woman would not buy it, as she said she had offered a bonnet for sale - the prisoner said she had sold the bonnet for 4d.; I went and inquired about it, then ran after the prisoner, and took her; she threw the jug away, and broke it - I said I wanted her for a bonnet; she denied it, but I took her to the station, and there she was recognized.
   SUSAN STILLMAN. This is my child's bonnet.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. - Transported for Life.
 587.  THOMAS BACCHUS was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of January , 1 pair of boots, value 5s., the goods of George Pygall.
   SARAH PYGALL. I am the wife of George Pygall- he is a shoemaker. On the 27th of January I was in our parlour, at nine o'clock in the morning; I saw the prisoner leaning over the counter; he appeared to be reaching for something - I went up to him, and asked what he wanted; he made no answer - I said he should not go out till he told me what he had in his apron - he then said it was a pair of boots which he was going to mend; he opened his apron - I said he had taken them from the counter; he said I was wrong, he had bought them in coming down the street for 1s. 6d.; I detained him till my husband came, and then he said he had bought them in Holborn - my cousin came out of the parlour, and stood against the door till my husband came in.
   WILLIAM MASTERS. I am the cousin of this witness - she took the boots from his apron; I came out of the parlour, shut the shop door, and detained the prisoner - he said he had them to mend.
   JOHN GOODWIN. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I leave it to the mercy of the Court
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined Three Weeks.
