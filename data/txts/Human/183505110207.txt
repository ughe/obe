DANIEL REARDON. I am a policeman. I was at the fair—I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief from the prosecutor's pocket—he was alone—I had seen him in the fair before, and watched him.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you see me knock his hat off? A.  No; I took the handkerchief from his hand, and secured him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I and another young man went to the fair to sell four hundred oranges—I took a walk through the fair to have a look at it—I saw two Young lads, one of them had a handkerchief in hit hand; the other said, "D—n that old thing, go and fetch us a tanner"  —he threw it down; I took it up—the policeman said, "Give us that handkerchief"—I said, "I shall not"—I did not know he was a policeman; he was in plain clothes—if I had given it to him, he would have said nothing about it. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Six Months.

I was at Greenwich fair on Easter Monday, between two and three o'clock—I did not feel my handkerchief taken—the constable tapped me on the shoulder and showed me my handkerchief—I am sure it is mine—I had been in the fair about three quarters of an hour. WILLIAM DYKE. I am a policeman. I was in the fair about a quar ter-past two o'clock, and saw the prisoner following the prosecutor, who had a friend with him—the prisoner was alone—I saw him poll the hand kerchief about three inches from the prosecutor's pocket at first, and when he got about twelve or fourteen yards further, I saw him take it out entirely—I caught hold of him directly, and he dropped it on the ground—I detained him—there wat not much crowd then—he denied the charge. WILLIAM HOGG re-examined.  I know the handkerchief to be mine, by a little hole in the middle, and the letters "W. H." in the corner.
