   JOSEPH MYERS. I live in Princes-street, Soho. On the 16th of June, between eleven and twelve o'clock at night, I was standing at my door to tell the Policeman to call me up in the morning, and saw the two prisoners coming from Leicester-square towards my shop; Nunn had a bundle, with a white cloth covered over it; he said to the other, "You go and beef, and I will go and lumber them" - he gave to him what appeared to me to be a waistcoat; Pridham then crossed the road, and Nunn went on to a pawnbroker's - I, knowing they were bad characters, went to lay hold of Pridham, but he ran down a street, and I thought if I followed him I should lose the other - I then went back to the pawnbroker; I just heard the pawnbroker say, "I can't take them in, it is too late," meaning a pair of trousers - I laid hold of Nunn, and said I should detain him, for having stolen these trousers - he said, "Where did I steal them?" I said, "No matter;" I took him out - I cannot be mistaken in Pridham's person, because I was in Clerkenwell prison at the time he was.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You never saw a pair of trousers with Pridham? A. No - I think I was eight days in Clerkenwell prison; I have not been in any other prison - I am a pencil-maker by trade; I was in the Police for thirteen months, and had several prisoners here - I never had any charge against me; I was reported for being too late, and was fined 1s. - I was dismissed for taking a placard from a person with the word "Reform" on it, near the House of Lords; the Commissioners thought I ought not to have interfered with it; that was the only reason why I was dismissed - I was fined twice; I was in prison for having some drugs without the stamp on them.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. How often have you given evidence in this Court? A. Six or seven times.
   ELIZABETH STROUD. I am Nunn's sister. He brought me, on the 16th of June, a pair of trousers and silk waistcoat, which he had bought, and wanted me to lend a sovereign on them.
 THOMAS HOBBS (Police-constable C 85). I went to Stroud's, and got these trousers.
   THOMAS FARRANT(Police-constable C 42). I was in the office when the trousers were first brought in -Stroud came, and said they were her husband's; Nunn was liberated - I afterwards received information; I went, and took Nunn again - I asked where the woman was; he said he did not know - I locked him up, and went with Hobbs to Stroud's, and got the trousers again; I afterwards got information, and took Pridham.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Nunn's Defence. I was sitting in the Globe public-house, and a man called me out at a quarter-past eight o'clock - he said he had a pair of trousers for sale; he owed me 6s., and I gave him 16s. more for them - I took them to Stroud's, and wanted her to lend me a sovereign; she said she could not, but she would pawn the trousers for me and a black silk waistcoat of my own; she took them, but did not get enough - I told her if she could lend me the rest of the money I wanted, I would pay her, but she could not; I then went, and got them out again - I went to the other pawnbroker's, where I was taken; the man I had them of is a tailor.
 Pridham's Defence. I know nothing at all about it.
 NUNN -    GUILTY. Aged 19.
 PRIDHAM -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Fourteen Years.

 1697.  MARY NEALE was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of June , 1 watch, value 1l.; 1 watch-chain, value 1s.; 2 watch-keys, value 4d.; 1 seal, value 6d.; 1 box, value 4d.; 1 sovereign, 2 half-sovereigns, and 5 shillings, the property of Thomas Pearce , from his person.
   THOMAS PEARCE. I am a labourer, and live in Oxfordshire, but was in town on business. On the 23rd of June, about ten o'clock at night, I met the prisoner - I do not know in what street it was; she asked me to go home with her - I agreed to go; we went to a public-house, and had a quartern of gin, and then went to a room; I gave her 1s. 6d. for the room, but had no concern with her - I was tired, and fell asleep; when I awoke the prisoner was gone, and my money, watch, and box; the officer took the prisoner in a few minutes - I know she is the person.
   THOMAS HAINES. I am an officer. On Sunday morning, at half-past two o'clock, the prosecutor said he had been robbed - he was then getting nearly sober; I went in search of the prisoner, and found her; I had seen her with him at half-past ten o'clock - she denied all knowledge of the property, and said it was not her, but another girl.
   THOMAS ARNOLD(Police-constable D 32.) I assisted to take the prisoner - she objected to be searched by a man, and I was going to send for a woman; at last she said a married man should search her - I then untied the string of her apron, and this box fell on the ground; I took it up, and said, "What is in this?" she said, "Only a shilling;" I opened it, and found two half-sovereigns: she said, "Take one of them, and say no more;" I said,"We don't do business in that way;" I then untied the string of her flannel petticoat, and this watch fell down.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the prosecutor at half-past nine o'clock, and he gave me the watch; he was very drunk - I never looked into the box at all; I only went to the next door, and he called the officer.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
