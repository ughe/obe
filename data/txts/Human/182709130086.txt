 1712.  ANN MOSELEY was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , 2 sheets, value 6s.; 1 frock, value 2s.; 2 blankets, value 2s.; 1 cloth, value 1s.; 4 napkins, value 2s.; 2 pockets, value 6d.; 2 pillow-cases, value 1s.; 2 remnants of lace, value 2s.; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 bottle, value 1s.; 1 pair of gloves, value 3d., and 1 cap, value 3d., the goods of Daniel Gardiner; and MARY SPAREY was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, knowing them to have been stolen, against the statute.
   DANIEL GARDINER. I am a brush-maker, and live in Spitalfields- Mosely was nurse in my family, from Easter to July, when she left to go to see her friends - I missed the table-cloth from my bed-room drawer the night she left - a dirty one had been put in its place; next day I found the drawers were stripped of my wife's wearing apparel - the articles stated were gone among others. I got a search-warrant; we found Mosely at work, at a house in Rosemary-lane, where she had gone from my house - the officer searched her, and found some duplicates on her - we went in a coach to her lodgings; we found Sparey sitting there; she said something to her - the officer found a quantity of things there; they have my wife's name on them; my wife died a few days after this, it gave her such a turn when she heard of it - she had been ill for some time. Sparey had called to see Moseley when she was at my house.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did not you at first find Sparey in the room, and make her tell you where Moseley was? A. Yes; the officer said he had a search-warrant; they told us where most of the things were. I have been robbed by my nephew.
   THOMAS VANN. I am an officer, and took Moseley - as we were in the coach, I saw her put her hand into her pocket; I said, "I must search first." I found some duplicates on her - she then begged Mr. Gardiner's pardon; we found Sparey at the lodgings. I said I had a warrant, and they had better give up the things, which she did - Sparey said, "Moseley had brought them, and told her the sheets would do to shift her bed;" she seemed near her confinement - Moseley said Sparey knew nothing of it.
   EDWARD RUBY. I am a pawnbroker, of Whitechapel: I have a sheet, shirt, and handkerchief, pawned by Moseley for 7s., and a tea-spoon, for 1s. 6d., in the name of Mary Gorby.
   JOHN YOWART. I am a pawnbroker, and have a tablecloth, pawned by Moseley.
 MOSELEY's Defence. The things I took to Sparey's I had bought for her. I had pawned the other articles for Mrs. Gardiner, who was a very drunken woman, and lived on brandy, rum and gin, all the time I was there.
   DANIEL GARDINER. My wife was deranged for twelve months; the prisoner had the care of her.
 MOSELEY -    GUILTY. Aged 57. Confined One Month.
 SPAREY -    NOT GUILTY.
 1713.  WILLIAM TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 1 rug, value 7s., the goods of William Cowen.
   SOPHIA COWEN. I am the wife of William Cowen, a broker- we live in Clerkenwell; this rug was stolen from a chair in our shop, about half-past eleven o'clock in the morning.
   GEORGE WADDINGTON. I was in Compton-street; the prisoner passed me with the rug: I followed him, and he dropped it - I laid hold of him; he said it was his first offence, and if I would forgive him, he would not do so again; I did not lose sight of him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up.
 GUILTY. Aged 13. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1714.  ESTHER BRANSOM was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , 1 shawl, value 4s.; 1 clock, value 6s., and 1 frock, value 2s., the goods of Abraham Cooper, her master.
   MARIA COOPER. I am the wife of Abraham Cooper; the prisoner was in our service on the 9th of August, and on the 10th I gave her in charge; she said nothing to me - she said she had lent the articles to her brother, to keep him dry, as it rained hard, but that was not stated to me - she was a year and eight months with us.
   WALTER LEY. I am an officer. I produce a cloak and shawl, which I got from the prisoner's brother - I found the prisoner in the watch-house; she said she lent the cloak and shawl to her brother, and he had pawned the other articles, which she had given him.
   JOHN BRANSOM. I am the prisoner's brother; she gave me the shawl and cloak - I had been out of work, and asked her for 4s. - she said she had no money, but would give me a cloak and whittle of her own, which I pawned for 4s.
   WILLIAM ROBERTS. I am a pawnbroker, and have a frock and some patchwork, pawned in the name of Bransom - I do not know who by.
   WILLIAM CLUNES. I am a pawnbroker; the prisoner's brother pawned this cloak and shawl with me - they were redeemed again before I heard of this.
   JOHN HUTT. I took the prisoner into custody; she said she lent her brother the cloak and shawl one wet night; her brother brought them to the watch-house.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I lent him them for a week.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. Strongly recommended to Mercy. - Confined Two Months.
