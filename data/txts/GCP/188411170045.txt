JOSEPH REED. I am manager to a tobacconist at 11, New Guard
· Street, Peckham-on 14th June I saw this advertisement in the Daily
Telegraph. (This was for a secretary in an office, with a progressive salary,
commencing at 1001., security 50% cash)— I replied, and got this letter.
(From Morley Jervis, secretary to the Defence Society, offering the witness
21. a week, to be increased to 31., and requesting him to call. I called on, I.
think, June 14th, and saw Jervis-he said the business was to assist
him and keep accounts, and take minutes of the meetings-he gave me
this prospectus. This was marked "Printer's proof," giving the names of
the President and Vice-Presidents of the Defence Society, and 50 or 60
names on the committee, with the names of the managing committee in
writing, among which was E. 0. Kent, Esq.) - Jervis said that one of the
officials of the society had absconded with 1007., and that was why he
wanted the 501. security-he afterwards sent me this agreement. (Dated
17th June, 1884, for 21. a week salary, and 607. deposit:)-I then called
and saw Tervis and handed him the money, and the agreement was
signed-he gave me a promissory note for the 501. at three months that
has never been paid I was to commence my duties in a fortnight from
the signing of the agreement-1 afterwards received this letter. (Dated
Jody Toth, signed by Jervis, and stating that in consequence of a dispute
which he was having with the committee, the witness could not commence his
duties for a fortnight, but his salary would commence at once.)-I afterwards
received this letter: “July 26th. Dear Sir, I will not trouble you to
come on Monday, but please call and see me on Wednesday, at 2 o'clock.
J. M. Jervis." I called and saw him-he told me he was having dis-
agreements with the committee, but he thought they would come to his
terms, but if they did not he should resign, and he would then let me
know, that I might come and receive my money-he said that Lord
Randolph Churchill and Lord Shaftesbury would be asked to become
Presidents-on 4th August I received this letter: “Dear Sir, I much
regret to inform you I am unable to arrange with the committee; on the
25th instant the cheque shall be sent for your deposit, with five weeks'
salary added, and I regret you have been put to inconvenience. M. Jervis."
I called at the office, but did not see either of the defendants– I then
received this letter: "8th August. Dear Sir, I shall be happy to see
you at 2 o'clock to-morrow. Yours truly, M. Jervis, per V. Garland."
