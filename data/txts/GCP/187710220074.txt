799. HENRY INGRAM (44), and ELIZA JOHNSON (34), Stealing
fifty-seven yards of silk-bordered regina, of William Bond Skerton.
Second count-receiving the same.
MR. GOODMAN conducted the Prosecution; MR. AUSTIN METCALFE appeared
for Ingram, and MR. PURCELL for Johnson.
WILLIAM BOND SKERTON. I am a commission agent, and reside at 6, Russia
Court, Milk Street-I employed a licensed messenger to take a number
of parcels to Monkwell Street-he left my premises shortly after nine
a.m.-among the parcels was fifty-seven yards of silk-bordered
regina, that is umbrella silk, worth 7d. & yard-this produced) is it-I
followed with the invoice, and arrived there after he had taken a certain
number of bundles upstairs—I saw some lying in the passage, and this
bundle was among them.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE. I identify this bundle by the ticket
and the quantity-it is one of my tickets from which I enter the goods.
STEPHEN CLARK. I am a licensed messenger-I was employed to take
twenty-three parcels in a hand-barrow to Monkwell Street - I took some
upstairs, leaving the others in the passage—when I came down one was
missing-this was a little after nine.
STEPHEN MERINAY (Policeman G). On 11th September, about 12.15 in
the day, I followed the two prisoners—they were walking together—the
female had something bulky under her shawl-I stopped them, told
them I belonged to the police, and asked what she had got there she
said “It don't belong to me, it belongs to " and then she stopped-
Ingram turned round and looked at her at the time she then threw the
bundle down and said “Oh, I found it”-on the way to the station she
said "I will tell you the truth; I found this this morning about half-past
eight in Old Street”-Ingram said at the time I stopped them. "I don't
know anything of this woman"-he gave his address—I knew it-John-
son gave no address.
WM. OSBORN (City Policeman). I have seen the prisoners together before
11th September.
Cross-examined by MR. PURCELL. I have known Johnson for years as 8
prostitute-she bears an honest character for anything I know-there are
rumours.
HERBERT BUSWELL (Polioman T R 13). I saw the prisoners together
on 10th September in Bunhill Row, near Ingram's house.
The prisoners received good characters.
GUILTY.
Ingram also PLEADED GUILTY to a previous conviction in March, 1871.
