   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Before that Pateman had said he had gone round by Hextable, and had not seen Reynolds, who was then, or shortly afterwards, in custody—I don't know that he made any statement to Terry—I recognise the main road, "The Bull," and the station on plan, which are correctly described— here  is the "Lullington Castle," and the road leading down to Mills' house—Pateman's father lives here , where the vans are—in going round by Hextable Pateman would go this  road round that  turning is what we call "Five Wents" ( describing on the plan )—if he went this  way he would say "I went round by the "Five Wents." GEORGE TERRY(257 Kentish Constabulary ). On Monday, the 16th July last, I apprehended Reynolds at "The Bull," Birchwood—I told him I should charge him with highway robbery with violence on an engine-driver, between Birchwood Corner and Swanley Junction, last Saturday night, about nine o'clock, in company with Walter Pateman—he said "I know nothing about it, I was not with Walter Pateman last Saturday night, and I didn't see him till Sunday morning"—I afterwards heard Sergeant Fisher tell him the charge when they were both together, and they made no reply.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Swanley Bottom is about a mile from Swanley Junction—I recognise the main road and the road leading down to Mills' shown on the plan—Pateman's father's house is where the carts are—he would not go that way by Swanley Bottom—the nearest way would be from Swanley Junction straight up the turnpike road—I appre hended one of the Reynoldses, Thomas—the other Reynolds was taken into custody by Sergeant Fisher, and was in custody till Tuesday morning, when he was discharged—I do not know why.
   E. L. FISHEE ( re-examined ). I took George Reynolds and Terry took Thomas—George was locked up till Tuesday morning, when Mr. Miller, the Magistrate, discharged him—the three prisoners were shown to the prosecutor, when he picked out one of the Reynoldses and the other prisoner. HENRY TURNER. My father keeps "The Woodman" beerhouse, near Swanley—the two prisoners were at our house on Saturday evening, the  14th July, and tow  others were with them, one of whom I know as George Reynolds—they left, as nigh as I can say, at about twenty minutes to nine.
