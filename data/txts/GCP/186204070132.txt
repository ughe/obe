499. ALFRED REPATH (28), Burglariously breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of Joseph Bradbeer, and stealing therein l shawl, 1 000
1 curtain, 12 knives, 12 forks, and 11 spoons, his property
in a MR PALMER conducted the Prosecution. de
RUTH BRADBEER. I am the wife of Joseph Bradbeer- our house is in
the parish of St. Mary, Lambeth-on 11th March last I was awoke by
policeman at a quarter to in the morning-on coming downstairs I found
the parlour window broken-some person had entered, taken a cont, shawl
ten terspoons, a dozen knives and forks, or it might have been more, and a
eurtain, which was attached to the window I used some of the knives and
forks at 10 o'clock the night before for supper—they were placed on the
window-sill in a basket - the shawl and coat were hanging up behind the
door on a peg- I have seen half a dozen teaspoons and three forks sino
these are them ( produced)-I bought these two forks the day before, from
a person next door--this one I have had ten years this spoon I baye had
quite as long as the fork--those I bought of a man about three weeks before
the robbery, and used them daily-I saw these things at a wardrobe shop
facing the Victoria Theatre, where they buy second-hand clothes and different
things the forks were at a marine-store dealer's--I saw those on the same
day as I saw the others; the Friday after the robbery—the curtain was with
the spoons- this is my shawl (produced) -I know it by having it dyed, and
by the plaid I see in it-this is my curtain-I saw it the evening before the
robbery I fastened the window myself at a quarter past 12, and bolted all
the doors--no glass was broken at that time I know the prisoner by his
coming to my place and sleeping with his father-his father was a lodger for
sixteen weeks the prisoner was often to and fro during that time-the
father often brought him to sleep there I saw him frequently he came to
me last Friday, the 4th, and mid that he had heard that I had lost these
things; that he had bought them of a man aud woman, and he felt very
unhappy after he had heard they belonged to me, as I had been so kind to him
and his father; and he came to give himself up, and hoped I would forgive
him-I said I could not think of doing that, I wanted my shawl, which
was a present, and I set great value on it-he said he would give me te
shillings to buy a new shawl.
Prisoner. 8. The first words that you said were, “Hallo, what do you
do here?" A. Yes; I did not tell you what I had lost--you said you were
very unhappy-you must baye known this shawl belonged to me.
