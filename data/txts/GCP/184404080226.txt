Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Had you looked at the gun on the
30th of March ? A. No, I saw the case there-- I cannot rightly say when
I had seen the gun in the case-perhaps ten days before.
WILLIAM JOHN BRp. I am a broker, and live in Exmouth-street, Spa-
fields. On the night of the 2nd of April, I saw all the prisoners-Burgess
came first to my shop about half-past eight o'clock-I knew him before-be
said, there was a double gun, and part of another gun to be sold, I asked
whose they were, and where they were to be seen-he said, "Never mind
that; if you will come with me I will shew you them "-I said I had not
had my tea, I could not go then, and said, "I don't like to go out after
these things ; can't you bring them to my house?"-he said, No, the land-
lord of the place where they were, bad advanced 21. on them, and therefore he
could not bring them to my house-I agreed to meet him opposite Day and
Martin's, in Holborn, and I met him about nine o'clock-he told me to follow
him-he took me to the Archers public-house, in Newton-street, Holborn,
which, I believe, is kept by Oldham-I saw him behind the bar, serving-I
saw Conner there, and two other persons, who have escaped-Burgess intro-
duced me, and said I was, the person about the guns-after waiting some time,
I went to the front of the bar, and stood there about two minutes-the other
two men spoke to Oldham about showing me the gun-be said, “Presently,
presently"-I waited about, and at last Burgess and the two men not in cus-
tody asked me to go into the tap-room-I sat there some time-at last one of
the other two men told Connor to get a light-Connor got a light, pulled the
tap-room door to after him, and shut me in the tap-room with Burgess and
the other two men-I felt rather uneasy, and I opened the tap-room door, as
if I was going out-I then saw a gun-case wrapped in a bit of serge come
up through a trap-door in the passage-Conner took it up stairs-Burgess and
I and the other two went up stairs to the back-room on the first floor-the
gas had just been lighted-Conner brought the gun, and put it on the table--
he then went and waited outside the door-I remained in the room with
Burgess and the other two men-I examined the gun, and said that would do
- this is it-(looking at it)-it was then wrapped up in the same piece of
serge, and given to Conner-I and Burgess and the other two men walked
downstairs-Burgess said, “Come on "-he and I and the other two men
went to Parker-street, Drury-lane-one of the other two men tapped at the
window of a room there-he said his mother was out, but he would go and get
the key-he went to another house, next door but one, and brought down a
key-bugle--we went into the room, and there they showed me part of a double
gun in a mahogany gun-case, a gun-stock, and part of a lock-we then all
went back again-Burgess wished me to purchase the things that night-I said
I could not that night, I was to give twelve guineas for the key bugle and
the two guns--next morning, at nine o'clock Burgess came to me and
I appointed to meet him again at Day and Martin's, which I did-he asked
me what was the most I would give for them-I said I should not give more
than 101. or ten guineas-he said he would go and see-he then went
to the Archers-the other two men came out, and were walking up and down
for some time-Burgess then came and told me it was finally agreed; they
were all ready, and I was to give these other two men 91.- I said, "What about
the 21. that Oldham has lent on the guns? I am not going to pay that "-he
said, "You must, or you won't have them"-I then agreed to give the other
two men 91., and Burgess was to ride home in a cab with me, and take the
difference of the ten guineas- I was to give him 58. for his trouble--he then
went to the house, and came and told me every thing was ready-I said,
