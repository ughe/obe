1283. ELLEN MUNICAN and ELLEN SULLIVAN were indicted for
stealing 6 blankets, value 31. ; 8 sheets, 1l.; rug, 12s. ; and 1 pair of
drawers, 68.; the goods of William Brown, their master; and that Sullivan had
been before convicted of felony.
WILLIAM Brown. I live in Stratton-terrace, Shadwell. The two, pri-
soners were in my service-Manican left me on Saturday, the 10th April,
and Sullivan left on Monday the 12th-when she had left, I missed the
articles stated—I had Munican taken up--she said she took two blankets and
pawned them in one shop, and one blanket at another shop, and had shared
the money with Sullivan.
Mary Ann SYRELL. I live servant with Mr. Brown. On Saturday, the
10th April, Sullivan took a blanket off the bed-she took it into the back
room, folded it up, and gave it to Munican, at the bottom of the stairs--I saw
her do it, and Munican directly afterwards took it out.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE, Q. Where were you? A. Up stairs, in
the same room—it was taken before me—it is a lodging-house for sailors.
there are not many persons coming in and out,
SAMUEL SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. I have two duplicates of a blanket
pawned for balf-a-crown, in the name of Ann Brown-the person who pawned
it had one duplicate and I had the other-it was pawned on Saturday the 10th
of April, and taken out on the Monday-I cannot say it was by either of the
prisoners.
Ebenezer CLARK. I am assistant to Mr. Hall, a pawnbroker, in High-
street--I have the duplicates of a pair of blankets pawned by a female, but
redeemed again.
William CHARLES POTTER police-constable K 212.) I went to Wapping
workhouse, to apprehend Munican-I told her I wanted her, for robbing her
master of some blankets and sheets—she said, “I am very sorry I did take
the blankets and sbeets"-I then went to Sullivan, I told her I wanted her
for taking a sheet and blanket-she said she did not, but she had received
13. 3d., part of half-a-crown which Munican pawned a blanket for. 980
