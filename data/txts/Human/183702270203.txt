I heard this statement of the prisoner read over by the clerk, at the police-office, and the prisoners signed their names to it—( read )—"Stedman says Gellart gave me the waistcoat to pledge—I gave him the ticket and the money."
 (The prisoners received a good character.)
   GELLART— GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined One Month; One Week Solitary.
   PHILLIPS— GUILTY. Aged 15.
   STEDMAN— NOT GUILTY.
 905. ROBERT PHILLIPS was again  indicted for stealing, on the   26th of January , 1 waistcoat, value 6 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Richard Scott , his master; and ISAAC WETHERALL for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. RICHARD SCOTT. While Phillips was in my employ I lost this waistcoat—it is mine—I lost it from the 23rd to the 28th of January—I found it on the following Monday at the pawnbroker's—I swear I did not lose it previous to the 23rd of January. JOSEPH NUGENT. I am shopman to a pawnbroker—this waistcoat was pawned by Tarrant.
   Cross-examined. Q.  In what name? A. "Wetherall"—I know Mrs. Wetherall. JOHN TARRANT. I live in Whitby-place. I pledged this for mrs. Wetherall, for 2 s . 6 d .—she gave me nothing. MARY WETHERALL. I am the prisoner's mother. I gave this waistcoat to Tarrant, to pledge—my son bought it of Phillips—I was not present.
   Cross-examined. Q.  you had this from your son? A.  Yes; he had brought it home some days before, telling me he had bought it—I wanted money, and I asked his leave to let me raise money on it—I did not send him, as he was employed at Mr. Brown's. RICHARD SCOTT re-examined.  This is Mr. Wedgewood's, the Magistrate's, handwriting, and Phillips signed it before him—(read)—"The prisoner phillips says, I sold the waistcoat to Wetherlal. The prisoner Wetherall says, I bought the waistcoat of a person, and left it at my mother's."
   PHILLIPS— GUILTY. Aged 15.
   WETHERALL— NOT GUILTY.
