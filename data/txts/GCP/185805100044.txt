545. CHARLES PULLUM (15) and EDWARD WILLIS (58), Steal.
ing 500 yards of cotton cloth, value 241., and other cloth, value 181. 108.;
the goods of Frederick Albert Jordan, the master of Pullum; to which
PULLUM PLEADED GUILTY, and received a good character.-Confined
One Month.
GEORGE RUSSELL. I am a detective city officer. On 23rd April, I went
to Mr. Jordan's warehouse, in Noble Street; Pullum was employed there I
called in Willis, and asked him if Pullum was the boy from whom he had
received the property-he said that he was I told him I must take him into
custody for stealing the goods which I named. De t
- EDWARD LOWTHER BENTON. I am assistant to Messrs. Pickford and
Roberts, pawnbrokers. I produce three pieces of challis, pawned at our shop
on 3rd April, for 11, by Willis, in the name of William Jones-I asked him
who they belonged to, he said, a person named Edward Willis, in Monkwell
Street, and his name was William Jones here are three other pieces pawned
by him on 10th April; he gave the same account then-here are some more
pieces, taken on 17th April, but I was not there.
GEORGE WHITLEY. I am assistant to Mr. Sowerby, & pawnbroker, in
Chiswell Street. I have two pieces of goods, pawned on 23rd Jan., by
Willis-he said he brought them from a person in Monkwell Street-here
are some other pieces which were not pawned with me.
ALFRED PEFFER. I am assistant to Mr. Reid, pawnbroker. I produce
one piece of goods, pawned on 18th Feb., by Willis, for 6s., in the name of
John Willis, he said he lived at No. 25, Monkwell Street there are two
other pieces pawned by him on 26th Feb., he gave the same name and
address there are three other pieces pawned by him on the same day.
WILLIAM BRENNING. I am assistant to Mr. Allen, a pawnbroker, in the
City Road. I have two pieces of goods, pawned by Willis on 6th March
I asked him his name, and he gave the name of Edward Willis- there are
four other pieces, all pawned by him.
JAMES HAINES. I am assistant to a pawnbroker. I have a piece of
cotton, pawned on 30th Nov. by Willis; and another, pawned on 18th Dec.
by him; and two gown pieces, pawned on 20th March by him-I asked him
who they belonged to, he said, to a gentleman in Monkwell Street.
CHARLES MARSH. I am traveller to Mr. Frederick Albert Jordan, im-
porter of foreign goods. Pullum has been his errand boy about eighteen
months-Willis was employed as packing porter, and has been in the habit
of coming there occasionally-since Jan., when we took stock, we have missed
about forty-six pieces of goods—these goods produced are part of the missing
goods-both the prisoners had access to the warehouse, Pullum more particularly,
Willis' Defence. I have nothing to say, further than that I pawned the
goods; I had no idea they were stolen, I gave him the tickets and the money,
WILLIS-GUILTY.-Confined Twelve Months.
Before Mr. Justice Crowder and the First Jury. .
