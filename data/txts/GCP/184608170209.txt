1716. JOHN WICKS was again indicted for stealing 2 sovereigns and 2
half-sovereigns, the monies of Nathaniel Pegg.
JOHN WARE. I am a wheelwright, living in Glasshouse-yard, Aldersgate-
street. In April last I ordered fourteen tons of coals of the prisoner the
price was to be 22s. 60.--I am not at all acquainted with the prisoner-they
were to be sent on the following Thursday or Friday-they came with these
vender's tickets, which are dated 17th April-I gave the order direct to the
prisoner at the prosecutor's wharf-the check produced was given for the
fourteen tons of coals-it is drawn by my father, and it is made out to Messrs.
Pegg and Co.-it was given to Alfred James, who is in Mr. Pegg's service-
this bill was delivered previously; I do not know who by-I think the boy
brought it the day before, and said if the money was paid the following day
five per cent would be allowed the amount is 15l. 158.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. What is the date of the check ?
1. The 16th of May-before the transaction we had a van for sale--we had
had no dealings with Mr. Pegg at that time, and knew nothing about him-
the prisoner came with a man named Wickens, who purchased the van-I
should think that was a year and a half before I bought these coals-I cannot
recollect whether I asked 50.. for the van.
Q. Did you afterwards agree to take 43l. or 421., and to give Wickens an
order for ten tons of coals? A. I gave him an order for ten tons, which I
received from Mr. Pegg-Wickens was the retail dealer, I believe--from that
time I ordered coals at the same place the bill for those coals was delivered
to Wickens, not to us-I ordered the coals in question at Mr. Pegg's
counting-house-I never had any dealings with Pegg till the dealing about
the van.
Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. With whom did you deal for these coals? A.
Mr. Pegg--I knew nobody else.
ALPRED JAMES. I am clerk to Mr. Pegg. I called on Mr. Ware with a
bill, 15l. 158., for these coals, by the prisoner's desire-he told me if Mr.
Ware would give me a check I was to take off five per cent. discount-
left the bill on the Thurday, and got the check on Saturday-I changed it,
and received 2 5l. notes, and the rest in silver-I gave the whole to the pri-
soner-in the course of the day he gave me 5s. for my trouble in getting the
money-I have collected for him on several occasions-he once before paid
me for collecting from the same party-that was the only transaction he paid
me for, to the best of my belief-I showed the check to the prisoner, and got
the money for it, by his desire.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Had Moore the check in his hand
some time before you got the money? A. Not that I am aware of he was
in the office-I do not believe he had it-he could see the transaction-he was
not present when the prisoner sent me for the account, but he was when I
showed the prisoner the check, not at the time he told me to get it changed
-that was five or six minutes after-I think I can swear he was not present
-he saw the check-I cannot swear he did not have it for an hour-he knew
I was going first, and knew I had been the day before the bill was made on

in this account I never heard anything about the van-I collected one or two
bills for clover for the prisoner received nothing for that the 58. was for
the whole-Moore knew I had the 5s.
