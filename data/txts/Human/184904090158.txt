   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Are you going to sail with him again? A.  I cannot say; the Indian  has sailed—Abrams knew nothing about the duties of a ship—this Was my first voyage—I was whipped when I deserved it, with a rope's-end, but never with a marline—it blew very hard off the "Horn," and there was a little snow—it was not six inches deep on the poop, not more than an inch—I did not see the boy in the tub off the "Horn"—I never heard him scream out—I have heard him say he would not do it again, and that he could not help it—he used to wash in the same tab that the men did—he never used to wash himself at all without he was told to do so. JOHN SHEDDON. I was carpenter on board the Indian  —I remember Abra hams coming on board at Auckland—I noticed that his habits were dirty about a fortnight or three weeks afterwards, and he was put out of the fore castle on the complaint of the men—I made him a bunk in the top-gallant forecastle, it was protected from the weather—when he came on board he looked healthy and clean—after he was put into the galley I saw large black dry sores breaking out upon him—he was only in the galley two nights—he then went in among the stock for his own pleasure, no one forced him to go—he was a dirty, filthy boy—he then went into the lower forecastle, which was a comfortable plate to sleep in—there were sails there, and worn to hang up a hammock; I cannot say whether he had one—the burden of the vessel was 560 tons—half of the seamen had not beds, only bunks—the boy was better off in the lower forecastle than the men, and more clear of the weather—there was no hardship in placing him there—the first time I noticed these boils upon him was sixteen or seventeen days before his last illness—Mr. Leith paid every attention to him for eighteen or twenty days before he died—he was not whipped to my knowledge during that time—he would go to him, and order him clean things, he gave him two cotton shirts of his own—I never saw a cat-o'-ninetails on board—I saw the boy corrected three times for his filth and dirt, and that was all—I noticed a dry sore on the boy's finger on his left hand about three weeks before he died; the flesh was eaten right away—he has spoken to me about his habits at New Zealand, which might relate to that disease—the animals on board had no distase, nor any vermin that I know of—he slept on a rack that was made for the sheep, and was about four feet from the turkeys—he would never change his clothes except Mr. Leith would go and stand over him, and as soon as his back was turned he would take them off, and put on his dirty ones again—I have seen that more than once, twice, or thrice—his uncleanly habits lid not commence with his going to live with the stock—I should say, from the state of his head, he must have had lice months before he came on board—the skin was half off his head—he was turned out of the galley for being dirty in the saucepan in which the cook was cooking daily—his bowels were not loose at that time—I saw him in the tub for about five minutes, and every man was in it, more or less—I was in it—he did not stand in it for two hours, or an hour and a half, off the Horn—I never saw him in it but once, and that was for about five minutes—the weather was then quite mild.
