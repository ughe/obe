Before Russell Gurney, Esq., and the Seventh Jury.
703. ANN HOFFLICK, stealing 98. 6d. in money, of Andrew Lund,
from his person.. MR. LILLEY conducted the Prosecution.
ANDREW LUND. I am a seaman, and about a month ago was discharged
from the ship City of Manchester. On that day I went to live at the
prisoner's house, No. 1, Shorter's-street, Wellclose-square-on 11th June I
pawned my watch for 11. 108., and paid away all but 98.6d, in the course
of the same day-I had been drinking, and about 2 o'clock in the day I
went up to my room to go to sleep-the 98. 6d. was then in my right waist-
coat pocket-there were two half crowns, two shillings, and some sixpences
- I did not lock my room door-I laid down and went to sleep, and when
I got up again I felt for my money, and it was all gone-I went down, and
asked the servant about it—the prisoner was not there, she was down in
the kitchen; and in consequence of what the servant had said, I went
down and asked the prisoner if she would give me my money back again,
she said, “I do not know anything about your money; what do you come
to me for ?"-I said, “I know you took the money, there is the servant,
she tells me she saw you take it; you wanted the servant to go and take it,
but she would not "-she then struck the servant hard on the face, it bled,
and I went out to the police court directly.

-I did not give her any of the money, nor did she ask me for any-we
