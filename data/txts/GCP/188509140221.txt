died at 3 o'clock on Sunday morning-I brought home my wages, &

RICHARD LAMBERT LAMPREY. I am a registered medical practitioner
at Wandsworth-in August I was acting as substitute for Dr. Nicholson,
the parish medical officer-if an order had been given by the relieving
officer it would have been my duty to attend to this child—I had attended
it in September, 1884, for a short time-on 2nd August I saw its dead
body-I made a post-mortem examination--there was no mark of violence,
and no particular disease--the organs were healthy, except that they
were anæmio-the cause of death was exhaustion from diarrhe- & child
of that age suffering from diarrhea ought to be medically attended at
once-if this child had been seen during Saturday it might have had a
fair chance of living.

Mr. Fulton submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury; the only
offence, if any, which the prisoner had committed was an error of judgment;
that the evidence left it in doubt what the actual duty of the prisoner was, or
sohether in such a case as this he was not allowed a certain amount of dis.
cretion. Me. POLAND referred to the case of Clark v. Jocelyn, 27th Nero
Series, p. 762, and contended that there was a sufficient evidence of neglect
by the prisoner for the Jury to act upon. MR. JUSTION HAWKINS left the
case to the Jury.
