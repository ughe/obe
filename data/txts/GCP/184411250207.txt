RALPH Scott. I am a waterman's apprentice. On the morning of the
25th of Oct. I was on the Upper Tunnel-pier, and saw the accident-1
did not see anything done by the steamer in order to avoid it-the accident
would not have occurred if the schooner had not been where she was-she
created an eddy-in my judgment the skiff could not have been seen by
the steamer before she rounded the schooner.
COURT. Q. The captain of the steamer would see the schooner there,
would be not? A. Yes-the eddy drew the boat's head down, and threw it
out into the tide again-if the captain had seen the boat a bundred yards
before he came to her, and had stopped the engine, or turned the helm
starboard, that would have prevented the accident.
SAMUEL Bristow. I am mate on board the Waterman steamer, No. 6.
I was on board on the 25th of Oct.-I saw the skiff when I was abreast
of the schooner's stern-the first thing I did was to hail the skiff.
COURT. Q. Whereabouts were you? A. I was on the fore starboard
deck--the prisoner was on the paddle-box-it was his business to look
out-he was the person who was directing the motions of the vessel.
