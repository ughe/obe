MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  You did not sign it at Moseley's, and if you did not sign it in the private room you did not sign it at all? A.  That is correct—I believe Mr. Humphreys was in the private room, but I am not certain—I do not recollect seeing any counsel for the prosecution there that I know. Mr. Clarkson examined me at the police-office—I am not certain that he was in the private room—I am not aware that any copies were made of my statement—how many Mr. Abrahams may have made I do not know—when I came before the Magistrate in the public room, I was asked questions about this transaction—they were to exactly the same matters as I had stated in the private room, and which were con tained in that paper—it had been read over in the private room.
   Q.  And after you had rehearsed in the private room, and given evi dence in the public room, still there was a statement which was not cor rect, and which the clerk did not correct? A.  There was—I signed it as it was—I think I was about a quarter of an hour in the private room—I was not there three hours—I had been in the public office before that, about half an hour—I should suppose it was about an hour altogether, from the time the Magistrates went out till they came into Court again.
   COURT . Q.  You went into the private room to make the statement; at that time had you been sworn? A.  I had not been sworn—I went back again into the public room to be sworn.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  There the questions were asked you again? A.  There were no questions in the private room at all—questions were asked me in the office, to which I returned the same answers as I had given before—it was taken down as my statement.
   COURT . Q.  When you went back into the public room to be sworn, were questions put to you? A.  Then questions were put to me to which I gave answers.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  And did the questions so put to you produce answers conformable to what you had stated before? A.  They did; the questions, and the answers I gave, were the same in substance as what had been read over to me in the private room, and something in the same order—I was sworn before the clerk took down what I said—I was sworn to state the whole truth—I did not know what they were going to ask me—I had no business to guess—I could not tell what they were going to ask me—I was there to give an account of the business—I never went before the Justice before—I think it was on the 7th of May that I was first examined before the Magistrate—it was in the early part of May—I was examined again about eight or ten days after to the best of my recollection—I do not recollect whether I said any thing before the Justice in the pri vate room about Selim Solomon going to Mrs. Abrahams in consequence of the message delivered to me—it was on Sunday night, the 14th of April, that she called—on the 7th of May, when I was examined, I did not say a word about that—I had told Mr. Abrahams of it before—I went a second time to the Justice—I was ordered to attend there again, and went—Mr. Abrahams did not tell me of it—I think Selim and I went together the day I was re examined, to the best of my recollection—I went to state that I had omit ted a fact concerning Alice Abrahams—I do not know what he went to do—I did not know what would be wanted of me till I was there.
