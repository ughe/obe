 1022.  WILLIAM ROGERS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of April , 2 shirts, value 6s., the goods of George Goodwin.
   GEORGE GOODWIN. I am conductor of an omnibus. I went to an eating-house in Bell-street, Marylebone, and came out, leaving two shirts in a bundle behind me; I returned for it, and it was gone - I did not see the prisoner there.
   CHARLES CLARK. I am a Policeman. I met the prisoner about four o'clock in the morning of the 1st of April, in Bury-place, carrying three shirts - I said, "You are the person I have been looking for, for taking these shirts out of a cook-shop, about half-past twelve tonight" - he said, "Here they are;" I secured him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not intend to steal them, but to take them home to the prosecutor.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1023.  THOMAS REECE and JOHN BAILEY were indicted for stealing, on the 6th of March , 1 cruet top, value 5s., and 1 spoon, value 5s., the goods of the Rev . Hugh Hodgson ; and that the said Thomas Reece had been before convicted of felony.
   RICHARD BRITTING. I live at the Rev. Hugh Hodgson, in Dean-street, Westminster. On the 6th of March, between two and three o'clock, I was in the kitchen - something drew my attention to the window, and on going towards the passage, I saw the door open, which I had shut not ten minutes before; I went by the pantry, and missed the top of a mustard-pot and a spoon - I ran up the area, and saw the prisoner walking on the terrace, within twenty yards of the area - I walked past them; I collared Bailey - Reece immediately ran away; I called Stop thief! and he was stopped by Lloyd - he threw the spoon over into the garden, and the mustard top was found in his pocket - nothing was found on Bailey; they were within a yard of each other, and appeared in company.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Had you seen a person loitering about for two or three days before? A. No, I never said so.
   EDWARD LLOYD. I heard a cry of Stop thief! saw Reece running, and took him in custody - he threw the spoon out of his hand, and the mustard-top was found in his pocket.
   DAVID PHILLIPS. I received the prisoners in charge, and found the mustard top in Reece's waistcoat pocket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   EDMUND CHICHELEY, I am a Policeman. I have a certificate of the former conviction of Reece; I was a witness on his trial, and know him to be the man - (read).
 REECE -    GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 BAILEY -    NOT GUILTY.
 1024.  MARY RODENWINCH was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March , 4 sovereigns, 1 half-sovereign, and five 5l. Bank notes, the property of William Fenwick , from his person; against the Statute, &c.
 WILLIAM FENWICK. I belong to the Juno. On the 13th of March I found the prisoner at the sign of the Crown, where I went to have some refreshment - she asked me to treat her, which I did; I went to the  Match-walk with her, to a house - I agreed to stay all night with her; I had changed one 5l. note, and put the change into my purse - I had five other 5l. notes there; I am sure it was all safe when I got to the room - I put my purse in my trousers, and put them on a chair; we went to bed at near twelve o'clock - I awoke about seven, and she was gone; I looked into my trousers, and the money and purse were gone - I gave information; I am sure she is the girl.
 Prisoner. Q. You know you went with two girls, and bade me good night? A. No - I am sure you are the girl.
   HENRY LENNON(Police-constable, K 98.) The captain told me he had been robbed, and gave a description of the girl - I went and searched the house, and found this purse between the sheet and bolster, in the bed down stairs; the prosecutor said it was the bed the girl took him to.
   MARY DAVIS. I am bar-maid at the Crown. I saw the prisoner and the prosecutor there; I changed a 5l. note for him, and he put the money safe before he left the bar; the prisoner was not at the bar a when he took his change, but they went out together - they were both intoxicated.
   GEORGE PHILLIPS. About half-past twelve o'clock on the 13th of March I met the prisoner and the captain in the highway; he said to me, "This girl is following me, and I can't get rid of her;" I turned her away, and he said he was going to Lower Shadwell - in ten minutes I saw them together again, going towards the Match-walk.(Purse produced and sworn to)
