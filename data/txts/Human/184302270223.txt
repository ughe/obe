 1061. WILLIAM HURRY was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of Feb ., 1 coat, value 30 s  .; and 1 handkerchief, 2 s  .; the goods of John William Car rington . JOHN WILLIAM CARRINGTON. I am a dentist, and live in High-street, St. Giles's. On the 21st of Feb. the prisoner and two other men came into my shop—one of the other men requested to have a tooth extracted—I examined his mouth, and saw no tooth that wanted extracting; but he fixed on one in the lower jaw, and said he would have it out—I said it was quite sound, but he insisted on having it out—I desired him to walk into the sur gery—they all three walked into the surgery—I then set the man in a chair to take his tooth out, and on the back of another chair was my coat—I pro ceeded to extract the tooth, but the man slipped out of the chair, and pre vented me—I made another attempt, and he served me the same again—I then made a third attempt, and succeeded—I got the tooth out, but I am sure he did not expect it—while I was doing so the prisoner left the surgery, and in a few minutes the other man left, and left me with the man whose tooth I had taken out—he then washed his mouth—he asked what it was; I said one shilling—he said he could afford no more than sixpence, and I took it—I went back to the surgery to put away the things, and missed the coat. ANN KEALL. I am the wife of John Keall; we live in Charles-street, City-road. I saw the prisoner and two others go into the prosecutor's shop—I can swear the prisoner is one—I saw him come out with a coat on his right arm. GUILTY. * Aged 19.— Transported for Seven Years .
 1062. RICHARD PECK was indicted for obtaining a loaf of bread by false pretences. JOHN BRANCH. I am a baker, and live in St. James's-street, Islington. I do business with Mr. Ramsay. On the 3rd of Feb. the prisoner came and asked for a quartern loaf for Mr. Ramsay—my wife asked which Mr. Ramsay—he pointed to the house of Mr. Ramsay, which is at the corner, and said, "That"—I let him have the loaf on the faith of his coming from Mr. Ram say—I watched him as far as the corner to see that it was correct, and as he turned, the corner I judged it was—he was soon after brought back by the officer. JOHN RAMSAY. My house is near Mr. Branch's shop—I did not autho rize the prisoner to fetch a loaf for me on the 3rd of Feb. THOMAS WITHERS( police-constable N  211.) I had seen the prisoner loitering about—I stopped him with the loaf—I asked where he got it—he said he bought it for 7 d .—I took him to the prosecutor's shop—he then said a boy sent him for it.
   Prisoner.  I was passing by, and a young man at Mr. Ramsay's asked me to go for a quartern loaf for Mr. Ramsay; I could not see the man again; I was going towards Mr. Ramsay's house with it. Witness.  He had passed the house nearly ten yards. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Months .
