1311. WILLIAM READ was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of
September, 12lbs. weight of pork, value 78., the goods of William Burls,
in a vessel on the navigable river Thames.
123
JULIUS BUTCHER. I was cabin-boy on board the brig Harriet, in 1837
we had half a side of bacon on board-I had seen it safe one morning in
September—the vessel was just coming up the river--three men came on
board, who I should know again-1 am quite sure the prisoner was one of
them-I have never seen him since--I saw them take the pork, put it into
their boat, cover it with a handkerchief, and row away—the Custom-house
officer was near, and saw them—the captain was not on board, and the
sailors were busy at the time I was on deck minding the ship the
steward was down stairs—I had all the things in the ship in my charge
the pork belonged to the captain, William Burls-I am almost sure the
prisoner is the man-I am quite sure he is.
Sto se to
John WHITE (police-sergeant K 2.) I was before the Magistrate when
the prisoner was examined, in April last-Beal, who apprehended him, is
not here I know Mr. Broderip's hand-writing (looking at the examina-
tion)—this is it-I heard the prisoner make this statement, it was read
over to him before he signed it-read-The prisoner says, “I was there
certainly."
a sd
Prisoner's Defence. I went on board the ship with the other two men
to get tide-work; I asked the pilot if he wanted any hands to get the ship
into dock; he said, “No," and I got into the boat again-daring this time
the waterman rowed out into the stream; he had the meat in the boat,
and he put me on shore-I did not know it was taken till I got into the
boat.
JULIUS BUTCHER re-examined. This was about two years and a half
ago-I did not see the prisoner take the pork-he was in the boat while
the pork was handed down-we hallooed after the men, and they towed
off as fast as they could I saw the pork taken-I was by the galley at
the time---the men had been on board about a quarter of an hour they
had no business there. 1917
NOT GUILTY.
