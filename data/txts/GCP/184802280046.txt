815. SAMUEL BROWN was charged by the two first Counts, with
unlawfully making divers false statements (setting them out) to the Rer.
William Frederick Hamilton, intending the same to be inserted in a register
of marriages; and, by the 3rd and 4th Counts, with making certain fale
statements touching the particulars required by the Act to be registered, with
a like intent.
THE ATTORNEY-GENERAL, MR. BODKIN, and Sir John BAYLEY, conducted
the Prosecution.
SIR FREDERICK THESIGER (with MESSRS. CLARKSON and BALLANTINE)
applied to the Court that the Defendant might be allowed to withdraw his plus
of Not Guilty to the 3rd and 4th Counts, and to demur to then, as not
tainable in point of law, he having pleaded in the absence of his legal adoiuer,
not being bound then to plead, but having a right at that time to denur ; this
was a matter in the discretion of the Court, if a case of substantial injustice
was made out See The Queen v. Purchase, 1 Car. & Marshman, 617,
The Queen v. Odgers, 2 Moody f Rob., 479) and could not be made a ground
of motion in arrest of judgment, under the provisions of 7 Geo. IV., c. 64. The
ATTORNEY-GENERAL resisted the application. The COURT was of opinion the
the Defendant ought not to be allowed to demur, the Counts, (stating the offend
in the general terms of the Act.) being good with a plea of Not Guilty to then;
and as the Defendant did not allege that he was unaware of the general scope
of the charge, no case of substantial injustice was made out.
