315. JOHN SMITH (41), Stealing a purse and eight pawnbroker's
duplicates, from the person of Mary Johnstone.
Mr. FARRANT Prosecuted.
MARY JOHNSTONE. I am widow, at one of the Royal Customs Houses-
on 16th February I was in Cheapside with my two daughters, one on each
arm, on the outside of a crowd, looking into a photographer's window-
I saw the prisoner, and moved over to the other side of the window-
then I saw his face over my shoulder and felt his hand in my dress pocket
-I caught hold of his arm, and said, What have you got your hand in
my poeket for ?"-he pulled his hand out and ran round the corner-I
called out, 44 Stop thief T stop that man "-I had had this purse in my
poeket with pawntickets in it-the prisoner ran down Bow Church Yard
and under some horses' heads, and a gentleman caught him-he threw
the purse across the road.
Cross-eramined by the Prisoner. I was standing outside the window for
five or ten minutes-only my daughter was on my right hand, no other
person-nobody but you was at my back-ten or more people stood by
the shop in front of me; I was outside the erowd.
AGNE8 JOHNSTONE. I am the daughter of the last witness; I am
thirteen years old-on 16th January I was with my mother looking into
this photographer's shop in Cheapside-.the prisoner put his hand into
my mother's pocket and took out the purse-mother held his hand, and
then he slipped his hand away and ran down the street, and a gentleman
caught him, and he threw the purse over a horse's head-I picked up
the purse and gave it to my mother.
Cross-eramined. We stood there from five to ten minutes; we moved
to different parts of the window-w were outside the erowd of about
ten or twelve gentlemen who were in front of the window-I don't think
any women were on our right hand I had hold of my mother's right
hand.
CHARLES HERBERT Rees. I am a fireman, of 11, Maria Street,
Kingsland Road-on 16th February I saw the prisoner in Bow Lane,
running from Cheapside-Mrs. Johnstone was running after him; she
halloaed, 44 Stop thief ef!"-he tried to dodge away in the road, and put his
hand in his right pocket and I clarmed him-he had this purse in his
hand-I said, 44 What is that you have taken from that woman?",
he said, 44 Nothing, I have not got anything it is another man "-I
said, It's a lie; it's a purse; there it is over the road "-I held him til
a constable was fetched; he was given into custody.
Cross-eramined. I saw the purse in your hand-you threw something
over my shoulder, and when I took you you had nothing-the little girl
picked the purse up.
