MARY ANN LILLEY. I am the wife of the last witness- I know the pris

half crown, I examined it; it was bad- I told her so-she said she would go
and change it at the baker's-she went, and did not return-on 23rd Aue.
my husband brought me a half crown-I took it down and saw the prisoner
in the shop-I said to her, “You are the lady that was here before; I shall
not let you go away"-she said a gentleman gave her the balf crown in the
Strand, and she did not know it was bad.
· Prisoner. I had not been in your shop for several years; I gave your

JACOB THATCHER ( policeman, F 155). The prisoner was given into my
custody on 23rd Aug., by the last witness-the prisoner told me she did not
know it was a bad half crown, that she had taken it of a gentleman who
gave her three balf crowns to go in a brothel in Exeter-street-I went with
her to No. 34 there, the house she pointed out, and they told me there in her
presence that she had not been there at any time-the prisoner made several
excuses, and said she had been there-I then took her to the station-Ipro-
duce two half crowns, one I got from the last witness-the female searcher
found the other on the prisoner,
Prisoner, Q. Who gave you the half crown, when you came to Mr.
Lilley's shop? A. Mr. Lilley gave it me; I went into a public house with
it, because Mr. Lilley did not know it was bad himself-I gave it to the land-
lord, and he gave it me again he did not turn round and reach to a shelf
behind bim-the half crown was not out of my sight at all.
HANNAH GREEN. I searched the prisoner on 23rd Aug.; I found in ber
basket a half crown-there was a cucumber and some other things in the
basket, and at the bottom was this half crown, wrapped in paper-I found on
her 58. 10d. in good money.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These half crowns are both counterfeit, and from
the same mould.
