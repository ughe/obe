Lucy WHITBREAD. I am head nurse at the infirmary at Kingston Union
-I know the prisoner by the name of Alice Ward; she was admitted into
the infirmary by that name on 3rd June-she was in labour when she came
and on the 4th I delivered her of a male child; it was born with a double
caul over its face-on 2nd July she was discharged with her baby-during
the time she was in the workhouse she treated the baby kindly; it
WA & very nice child she nursed and suckled it and dressed it after the
fortnight, and behaved to it as a good mother when she left with it on
2nd July it was to all appearance in a healthy state-she told me she was
going to Weybridge to her mother's—the child was vaccinated before it left
the Union; it was done twice; the first time the arm did not take, and she
had o stay another week on account of the baby's arm-when it left it had
on a little nightgown, a petticoat, a flannel belly-band, and a diaper--the
band was hemmed instead of herring-boned; I called her attention to that
I have since seen a belly-band and diaper produced by the police ; they
were the same that the child had on when it left the workhouse-I have
not seen the nightgown or petticoat; there was what is called a back flannel
all to pieces, I could not identify that--this (produced) is the diaper ; it
is an old calico one with a hole in the corner-I identify that and this
belly-band by being hemmed instead of herring-boned ; I never saw one
hemmed before."
Cross-examined. The prisoner paid every attention to the child while she
was at the Union—the fact of the diaper being made of calico is not ex.
traordinary-I can't say that the hole in the corner is uncommon, it may
not be--it is the diaper the child had on-I have been four years a nurse at
Isleworth-I never saw a diaper like this, I have certainly seen diapers
with holes where the safety pin goes; it is not usual to pin them in the
corner like this- I was examined twice before the Magistrate ; I don't
recollect saying that I only saw the articles once-I don't think I did-my
deposition was read over, and I signed it-I am quite sure the child was a
healthy child-it did not refuse the breast she was not nursing other
children to my knowledge-I never saw her do it I could not always be
in one ward; there are a hundred patients to see to-I am the only person
here from the infirmary-she might have nursed other children without my
knowing it.
