THOMAS PUOKNELE I live next door to the “Butcher's Arms"-on 2nd
Noveraber I saw the prisoners about 100 yards from there, talking together
--Tibble then crossed over to the side the "Butcher's Arms" is on --Mrs.
Featherstone came outside her door-I nodded to her, and she went in,
and fetched a balf-crowa, which was bad- I went down Chigwell Road,
but missed the prisoners--I afterwards found them together on the green,
three quarters of a mile off-I showed the half-crown to Tibble, and said
"Do you know that balf-crown, it is a bad one, and you have just passed
it at the 'Butcher's Arms"-she said that she was very sorry; she had no
change, and what was she to do Taylor said "I will lend you 2 6d."
I took the 2.. 6d., and gave Taylor the half-crown-I had just bent it with
my teeth.
EMMA MARIS. I am a dressmaker, of 1, Queen's Place, Backhurst Hill
-on 2nd November Tibble orme in for some Berlin wool, which we did
not keepshe then asked for & roll of crochet cotton, and a book, which
came to 2d., and gave me a half-crown-I gave her 2s. 4d. change-after
she left I bit the half-crown, and went to Buckhurst Hill Railway Station
to ask if it was bad I saw both the prisoners on the platform, and said
to Tibble " This is a bad half-crown you gave me"-I gave them in custody
with the half crown.
Elza KING. I manage the Prince of Wales," Buokhurst Hill-on 2nd
November, about 6.30, I served Tibble with a glass of ale, she gave me a
florin, which I put in the till, where there was only a crown and a
half-crown, and gave her the change—Aldis spoke to me, and I gave him
the florin-Tibble came in with a person about a quarter of an hour after-
wards, I knew her again.

HENRY ALDIS. I am signalman at Buckhurst Hill Station--on 2nd
November, I saw the prisoners there together, they had taken tickets for
London-I went to the Prince of Wales," at a few minutes past 6 o'clock,
and saw the last witness there ; she gave me a florin, which I gave to Hicks.
John Hicks (Policeman N 259) I saw the prisoners at Buckhurst Hill
Station, and told Tibbles she was charged with passing bad half-crown-
she said, "Anybody might make a mistake, you might have done the same
yourself." -I told Taylor, I took her on suspicion of being concerned with
Tibbles, she said that she accidentally fell in with Tibbles at the top
of Queen's Road, but knew nothing of her only by living in the same neigh-
bourhood-I found on Tibbles, & purse containing 20. 6d. in good silver, and
tvo railway tickets; and on Taylor, 198. 8d. in silver, 31. 2d. in copper, and
23 skeins of Berlin wool, a quantity of cotton, a crochet book, and
other articels--I got this bad half-crown from Emma Maris, and this bad
florin from Henry Aldis-nine more florins were found next day, close to
where the prisoners passed.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This half-crown and florin are bad.
Taylor received a good character.
TAYLOR-GUILTY-Eighteen Months Imprisonment.
TIBBLES—Guilty-Eighteen Months Imprisonment
