MR. JUSTICE HAWKINS. In my opinion Mr. Gorst is entitled to a general
Reply. The right of Reply is in my judgmont vested in the Attorney-General
by reason of his reprosenting the Crown in all oases which are really prosecuted
at the suit of the Crown, by which I mean, not ordinary proseoutions which are
not proseouted at the suit of the Crown, although the Queen'a namo appears nomi-
nally as the prosecutor, but prosecutions on behalf of the country at the instigation
and direction of the law officers of the Crown, and which may for this purpose
be termed State prosecutions. Wherever a case is really proscouted in that
son186, I think the Crown has the prerogative right of Reply, and if the Attorney-
General, whose duty it is to conduct such a prossoution appears in person to
conduct it, he has that prerogative right, not because of a mere personal pripi-
lege attached to himself, but because he is the representative of the Crown,
azercising the prerogative of the Crown. For this reason it is that the Solicitor.
General, in the absence of the Attorney-General, and representing him, has always
been allowed the same right, and not because of a personal privilege attached to
his office, and for the same reason I am of opinion that any Quson's Counsel or
ordinary barrister appearing as the representative of the Attorney-General in
such proseoutions as I have referred to, is entitled, on behalf of the Crown, to
the same privilege of Reply.

JAMES ABRAHAM FOOTE. I am a barrister-I have known Mr. Thompson
about 25 or 26 years—in the early part of that period I knew him more
intimately than the latter part-I have not seen much of him lately,
formerly I saw a good deal of him-he has always borne a high character
for honesty and integrity; perhaps I might add that I became acquainted
with him as the managing director of the Merchant's and Tradesman's
Life Assurance Company, and he always maintained that a charge of 50
per cent. upon the first year's premiums was a sound and healthy busi-
ness, with 5 per cent. on renewals.
By the COURT. That was 25 years ago I certainly did not agree with
him; we paid 25 per cent. on the first year's premiums, and 5 per cent.
on renewals-Mr. Thompson advocated that it would extend business to
give 50 per cent., but the directors, I believe, objected to it.
