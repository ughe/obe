On the 23d of January I met the two prisoners about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutrix's house - I had seen them both before, and knew Phillips very well: I saw the spout of a tea-pot, which I knew was Warren's, hanging out of Tubbs' apron - I stopped them, and took them back to Mrs. Warren's.

   JAMES HANLEY. I am an officer, and took the prisoners the same night.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoners put in a written defence, stating that they had called on the prosecutrix, and finding her from the room, had taken the property in a joke, to alarm her, and that she had since taken 10s. to forego the prosecution.
 COURT to  ELIZABETH WARREN. Q. Is there any pretence for charging you with taking any money? A. No - I never permitted them to take my property. I never knew they were near the place.

 656.  JOSIAH JAMES HOSIER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 2 bushels of coals, value 2s., the goods of William Yool , in a certain barge upon the Navigable River Thames.
   JOSEPH WILSON. I know Mr. William Yool - he is a coal-merchant. On the 16th of February, a little before two o'clock in the afternoon, I was going down in the craft on the river, and saw the Edward barge laying outside the dock of Brooks and Penny's wharf; I am their clerk. I saw the prisoner's head just over the barge, which was loaded with coals; he was taking coals out of the barge, into his boat; I came up to him, and he said, "If you will let me go, I will put them back," and he did throw two or three pieces back; I took hold of the chain of his boat, and held him till an officer came - I suppose there were nearly two bushels of coals in the boat- they appeared the same sort as those in the barge.
   ALEXANDER MITCHELL. I am an officer. I was sent for, to take the prisoner; Wilson was standing on the gunwale of the barge Edward, and had hold of the chain of the boat, in which there was about a bushel of small coals and two bushels of large coals; they appeared to me to be the same sort as those in the barge.
   CHRISTOPHER BEARPARK. I am wharf-clerk to Mr. William Yool. I went to the boat, and found the coals in it; they appeared to me to be part of the bulk of the barge Edward.
 COURT to  ALEXANDER MITCHELL. Q. Did you hear the prisoner examined? A. Yes - this deposition was read over to him; this is Mr. Broderip's signature to it; there was no promise or threat made to the prisoner - (read).
 "I have been led into it, by keeping company with a man whom the officers know to be a noted thief."
 The prisoner put in a written defence, standing that the coals were only the sweepings of the barge. He received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 19. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 657.  JANE ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of February , 2 pots, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Joseph Bennett; and 1 pot, value 1s. 6d., the goods of George Hamble.
   WILLIAM HAYES. I am a tailor. On the 9th of February I saw the prisoner come and take two pots from off the rails, next door to me; I detained her, and sent for Mr. Bennett.
   JOSEPH BENNETT. I am a publican. Mr. Hayes sent for me; I found these two pots, which are my property, in a bag on the prisoner.
 GEORGE HAMBLE. I  keep the  Northumberland Arms public-house, Tottenham-court-road. This quart pot, which was found on the prisoner, is mine.
     GUILTY. Aged 54. Confined Six Months.
 658.  JOHN ATKINSON and JAMES MASON were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of December , 5lbs. weight of pork, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Moorman.
 THOMAS MOORMAN. I  keep a cheesemonger's-shop, in Golden-lane. On the 29th of December, about a quarter before nine o'clock in the evening, I saw three men pass my shop, turn the corner, and make a stop; I told my boy to look out, and in about three minutes the boy said, "Halloo, master - what have you got at?" a piece of pork was then gone; I saw Mason running away: the boy pursued him, and cried Stop thief! I saw two men run from the place where they had all stopped; I did not see Atkinson when Mason ran away; but I had seen them together about three minutes before, and am quite certain of their persons; neither of them were taken that night.
   WILLIAM MOORMAN. I was at the window, and saw both the prisoners pass, in company together; in about three minutes afterwards I saw Mason take a piece of pork from the window; I gave the alarm, but they ran away - I had not seen them before, but am quite certain of their persons - I knew them again the moment they were taken up, and am quite sure of them.
   JOHN TWEEDY. I am a constable. I took the prisoners on the night of the 16th of January, from the description of the prosecutor; the lad Moorman came up in a moment, and said, "These are the men that took the pork away."
   JAMES BUCKLE. I was with Tweedy; the lad pointed out the prisoners - I had heard of the robbery on the night it happened, and the prisoners' persons were described to me, but I had to go into the country on account of the death of my father, and therefore they were not taken.
 ATKINSON's Defence. The boy said he believed I was one of them, and he goes by what his father said; the Magistrate said he would have the father locked up for telling the boy what to say.
 MASON's Defence. I have lived in the neighbourhood; the prosecutor knew me very well, and saw me several times after it was done.
