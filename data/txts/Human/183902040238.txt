FRANCIS DUBOIS. I keep the Fox and Hounds beer-shop at Lewisham, about two hundred yards from Mr. Shove's shop. The prisoners came there, on the 26th of December, between six and seven o'clock—I cannot say whether they came together, but they were in the room together—they had some ale—they paid for part, and other people in the room paid for part—Morris paid for his in halfpence and pence, and Dyson paid in far things—Beviostock fetched one pot of ale from the bar, and paid me twenty-four farthings for it—Morris was in the tap-room at the time, but not within hearing.
   Dyson. Q.  I came into your shop about seven o'clock? A.  Yes; and you were there till I shut up between ten and eleven—there were from eighteen to twenty persons in the room that night. GEORGE BARHAM. I am a constable of Lewisham. In consequence of information I apprehended Dyson, and found on him 3 s . in silver and 4 d . in halfpence—I received a quantity of farthings from Dubois.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What day did you take Dyson? A.  I believe it was the 1st of January—there was one examination when he only was in custody—Morris was taken the following day—I have known Morris six years, and always knew him to be an honest industrious young man. GEORGE ABBOTT. I am a constable of Lewisham. On the 26th of December, about six o'clock in the evening or a little after, I was passing Mr. Shove's shop, and saw Dyson standing against the window, and an-other, who I cannot swear to, stood on the other side of him—there was nobody in the shop.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe you knew Morris before this transaction? A.  Yes—I thought it was not Morris I saw, till I heard Bevinstock's evi dence—I have known him from a child—he always bore a respectable cha racter—I took another person before the Magistrate, who I thought it was. NOT GUILTY.
 830. THOMAS DYSON was again  indicted for stealing, on the   26th of December , 22 cigars, value 3 s  . 8 d  ., the goods of William John Clements . JOHN COWAN. I am apprentice to William John Clements, a hair dresser, at Lewisham, about 150 yards from Mr. Dubois. On the 26th of December, about half-past eight or near nine o'clock at night, I came down stairs, and caught the prisoner at the sash of my master's window with one hand up to his right hand pocket, and the other hand in the window up to the elbow—I asked him what he wanted—he said, nothing, but held his head down, walked to the door, and ran—I ran after him, calling "Stop thief"—he got away—I missed about half an ounce of tobacco out of a jar, and some cigars from the window, also a pair of scissors and some razors—I found some cigars on the floor—I had seen the scissors at two o'clock that afternoon—the prisoner was in a situation to have taken the things—he did not ask to buy any thing, but ran out.
