(William Carroll, a painter and glazier, of Kentish-town; Benjamin Swain.

GUILTY. Aged 21.-Transported for Seven Years.
567. HENRY SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of
January, 1 glazier's diamond, value 168., the goods of John Owen
Smith; and that he had been before convicted of felony
JOHN OWEN SMITH. I am a painter, and live in Ironmonger-street, St.
Luke's. The prisoner is my son-I missed a glazier's diamond from my
house, on the 18th of January-I charged him with taking it, I did not
threaten him--I have tried every way I possibly could to make him better
-I told him he would have to go back from whence he came-he is my
only child---my wife has been dead six years-he told me that I could find
the diamond at Mr. Matthews', in Whitecross-street.
WILLIAM HENRY Royes. I am shopman to Mr. Matthews, a paw-
broker, in Whitecross-street. I produce the diamond-I took it in of
a lad about the prisoner's size and appearance-I have no doubt he is
the person it is worth seven or eight shillings.
WILLIAM GARROD. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into
custody.
MATTHEW Peak. I am a policeman. I got this certificate of the
prisoner's conviction from Hick's Hall (read) the prisoner is the same
boy that was tried.
GUILTY. Aged 14.-Transported for Seven Years.
568. WILLIAM BLANDFORD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th
of January, 1 book-stand, value 8s.; 6 pieces of velvet, containing 4
yards, value 38.; part of a book-stand, value 18d. ; 1 pen-rack, value 25.;
4 looking-glasses and frames, value 88.; 1 watch-stand, value 48.; 1 box,
value Is. ; 2 dressing-cases, value 11. 12s. ; 2 glass bottles, value Is. 6d.;
3 boxes, value 12s. ; and 1 inkstand, value 2s.; the goods of Charles
Thomas Pratt, his master.
CHARLES THOMAS PRATT. I am a cabinet-maker, and live in Vineyard-
walk, Coppice-row, Clerkenwell. The prisoner was my journeyman for
fourteen months-I missed some things and gave him into custody-he
said he lived at No. 10, Bowling-green-lane I went there and found part
of the property and some duplicates, on the 15th, I found this velvet.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Is there any mark on that velvet?
4. I have a piece here which this was torn from-this is my box-
I cannot swear to this in particular-I had a dozen of them made, and
filled them up as dressing-cases—the duplicate of it was found at the
prisoner's lodging-he was constantly in my employ for fourteen months
and had 258. a week-I believe he has a wife and two children-he has
borrowed money of me-I lent him 21. when he first came into my employ,
and he repaid me a few shillings at a time-he did not pay me 21. 106. for
it-I cannot exactly say how much it was-he asked me some time ago to
lend him 21., for which he accounted to me, I think, 21. 48., or 58.the
loan was for a month or two-I did not charge him 2s. for the loan of 10
to my recollection-I will not swear it-you will understand I never
charged him any thing-it was his own offer-he often stopped away from
work longer than he should, and I considered he was only paying back
my own money.
