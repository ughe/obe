Mr. Muir: Did not you realise at some period of this business that
it was a swindle ?
Witness. Well, no. I did not like the business; it was too risky;
there was too much anxiety for a person of my age. I did not tell
Perryman it was a swindle. I did not think it was; not even now,
Perryman said he thought I was a very poor outside broker. I was
very good as a writer; or something to that effect. He said some-
thing like, "You will never make a bucket shop keeper." I do not
know why he should use that term, because there is no such thing
now as a bucket shop in England; it is an American institution.
You are not allowed to deal on the tape now in England. Bucket
shop is rather an offensive term. I do not keep books at all; I made
up my accounts on pay-sheets every fortnight. Betson kept them, as
a matter of fact. They were not shown to anyone that I am aware
of, Perryman did not see them; they never left Tokenhouse Build-
ings and he never came there. I cannot say whether Harris saw them;
very likely he did. The only book that I know Perryman kept was &
Stock Exchange bargain book. I have seen that at Moorgate Street.
I could not tell you what transactions were recorded in it; those I
had with him were, certainly. I should imagine Herbert Smith's
were ; but I know nothing of that. The contract notes, I should say,
were usually made out at the end of the day; it depended on circum-
stances. I made them out myself; but I have had assistance from
Harris; I do not recollect having had from Perryman. In what I
have told you I have come as near the truth as I could ; the entire
truth I have told you, as far as I can recollect. I have made and
signed a statement about this business, one on August 17, witnessed
by Mr. Stanier, the City Solicitor's managing clerk, and another on
July 4. witnessed by Mr. Towell, another clerk. I believe I told
them the truth.
