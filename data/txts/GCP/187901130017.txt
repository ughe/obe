JOSEPH SOPER (Policeman K 383). About 10 o'clock on the evening of
22nd December I saw the younger prisoner at 6, Joseph Street, St. George's
in-the-East, in a ground-floor back-room which is occupied by the other
prisoner the younger prisoner was wearing a black moustache I was look-
ing for some stolen property, and did not take him, as I did not know a
second person was ranted.
Cross-examined. No. 6 is on the door–I did not say that I saw you
afterwards with your moustache dyed.
David TURNER (Policeman K 659). I have seen the younger prisoner
several times he has been wearing a black moustache since 22nd December.
Cross-examined. I did not see you in Turner Street snowballing on the
Sunday that this happened, but I saw others.
RICHARD WILDEY (Police Inspector K). I took the younger prisoner at
Ilford-he was under remand there on another charge; that was a fortnight
after the elder prisoner was taken-I said that he would be charged with a
burglary at 204, Cable Street-he said “You have got me right this time."
Cross-examined. There was only one other prisoner with you when you
were identified, but there were six persons altogether—Mrs. Colombo looked
at you for about two seconds, and then said "I think that is the man "LE
said " Touch him”-she had not seen you go to the train, we kept her from
doing so.
The younger prisoner in his defence stated that when he was remanded
on a charge of drunkenneas Mrs. Colombo saw him get out of the train
without a cap or a jacket, and therefore easily identified him, and said
that he had a black moustache, rohich was not so, and that he knew
nothing of the case, and wished to call his fellow-prisoner.
WILLIAM JENKINS the elder. I have pleaded guilty to these two indiet-
ments. I do not know this prisoner; I never saw him in my life, and was
never in his company.
