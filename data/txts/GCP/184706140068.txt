HENRY FRANCIS HEALEY. I am a stonemason, and live in Upper Grove,
Islington. My workshop adjoins the dwelling-house of the prisoner Wil.
liams on the 17th of May, about seven o'clock, a van was drawn up in front
of my shop-Stephen Eaton, Mary Smith, and another person were in it
great quantity of iron and rags were brought out of Williams's house, and
put into it-Mary Smith assisted in carrying them out-I saw Stephen Eaton

looked at it, and told the carman it belonged to me--I said, " If you detain
it in your van I shall give you in custody"-he said, " If it belongs to you
take it out"-Williams came home afterwards-he gave it to the policemas,
said it was his, and that he had purchased it—this is it-it is mine-I had
seen it safe about three weeks before.
CORNELIUS MURPHY (police-constable N 127.) I was called by the pro-
secutor-be told me his property was in the cart-I went with him-I asked
Williams if he had got the iron; he said he had, and it belonged to him, be
had bought it-he gave it up to me.
HENRY FRANCIS HEALEY re-examined. He told me it was not my iros,
it was Mr. Starkey's-we are both tenants under Mr. Starkey-he handed the
