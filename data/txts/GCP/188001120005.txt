had a communication from a gentleman who had applied at Mitchell's for
tickets, and they said they had not got any--it is not a fact that Mitchell's
returned the tickets and refused to sell them, giving as a reason the charac-
ter of the women who applied for them--that comes to my ears for the first
time to-day-it is a fact that they returned them--they said they had rather
not sell them--they did not give me any reason - I allowed 10 per cent to the
booksellers on the sale of the tickets - I think Hayes had 16 per cent. I
did not ask Mitchell's the reason why they refused to sell them, because I
went in in a rage. I had a letter from a gentleman livirg in Queen Anne's
Mansions - I have not got the letter with me-he is not here he was
subpænaed on the last oeension - he had applied for a ticket, and the clerk
told him I had put Mr. Mitchell's name down without asking his permission
I prid 14 guineas for the room at the Cannon Street Hotel--the supper
was dr or to 6d., wine and spirits were extra---I made rather a good thing
of it-I don't know that it was in the interests of the profession; it was
partly in my own interest, not entirely, but substantially, yes the profita
all went into my own pocket-there was no contribution to any dramatic
fund for the relief of poor and distressed players--I sold the bulk of the
tickets to gentlemen I am acquainted with I sent them in envelopes
to gentlemen I am acquainted with; having been acting manager, at
three places in London, I know & great many people who are under obliga
tions to me, that I have given orders to; and when I have a benefit, or
anything of that sort, I should send them tiekets; I looked on this as a
benefit--there were a few tickets on sale at the Naval and Military Club in
Piccadilly, Lord Palmerston's old house-the hall-porter there had charge of
them-I was to allow him 10 per cent. commission, and I did so I have
not yet been to see how many tickets he sold—I have not got a running account
with him--one of the waiters at the London Pavilion sold some-- that is a place
frequented both by ladies and gentlemen--I did not sell any at Evans's--I did
not put any of my bills on the tables at Evans's—I swear that I did not-
I caused them to be put there I left some of these circulars with Newby,
the head-waiter, to place on the tables for me I should not think of being
my own bill-distributor, that would be decidedly unbecoming the dignity of
the profession-he had no commission-I asked him to olilige me as a
customer there-ladies do go there, or did go there; not for some consider
able time though, I believe---at this ball thore were no members of the demis
monde that I am aware of, not that I personally knew my knowledge in
that direction of society is not extensive, not more so than most people's, I
should think-I don't think there were many of that clase there, or I should
have had my attention called; there might be many--the waiters at Evans's
had no tickets to sell, so I could not have paid them for selling any--they
have not said to me“ We have sold a ticket to Mrs. So-and-So," nor have 1
paid them two shillings for doing so, that has happened at a former ball,
not at this ball-I have not sold tickets at Evans's through the instrumen-
tality of Toome and Slack and paid them for selling them; that I svenr, in
no case-in my former ball Newby sold them-he was the only one I paid.
