I was on duty in Wilderness Row about 1 o'clock on the morning of the 14th September, and saw the deceased in the road with the witness Ball—I spoke to the prisoner—he gave me the knife ( produced )—he said, "I have done it, and I will swing for it"—I assisted to take the woman to the station.
   Cross-examined.  I do not know the meaning of the term "I'll do a drag  for you"—I have never heard the phrase. HENRY FEEK( Policeman G  235). I was on duty in "Wilderness Row at 1 o'clock on the morning of 14th September—I heard a scream, and saw two women struggling in the middle of the road—I saw the prisoner cross over towards Goswell Street, and arrested him; he said, "I am the one who done it; I am sorry for it, she drove me to it; I worked hard for that girl till I could not get any more work, and I went thieving for her: I was three months in the House of Correc tion for her, it is done, and it cannot be helped, I must suffer for it"—he said he loved her as he loved his own life—I told him he would be charged with stabbing her, and he was taken to the station.
   Cross-examined. "Drag" is a low word meaning "hanging."
   Witnesses for the Defence. THOMAS WILLIAM SCARFE. I am superintendent of the Shoeblacks' Home, 241, Marylebone Lane, formerly master of the Union Jack brigade, Limehouse—about nine years ago prisoner was under my charge in the shoeblack brigade for four years—at first he was a great trouble, of a morose, sullen disposition, and sometimes seemed unable to learn his lessons, while at others he could do them very well—we taught him the big drum music, and he would sometimes play the time and tune well, while sometimes he could not keep them at all, and we had to tap on his shoulders the time—sometimes he understood punishment, and at others would appear quite unconcerned.
   The Court considered the evidence too remote to affect the case, unless it could he proved that the prisoner was net responsible for his acts. THOMAS PRATT, the prisoner's father, being totally deaf, was sworn by the oath' being written in pencil on a slate. MR. KELLY then wrote the ques  tions he desired to put to the witness also on the slate, and handed them to the Court, who ruled that the proposed evidence was too remote to affect the case. HENRY PRATT. I am no relative of the prisoner—on the 13th, from 6 a.m. till 4 p.m., I was at work with him on board ship by the side of Millwall Docks—we were working amongst strong turpentine down the hold, where if you remained a quarter of an hour you were just as if you were drunk—we were obliged frequently to come up from the hold, and were not allowed to remain down more than a quarter of an hour—when I got home my wife thought I was drunk—I could not walk straight.
