EDWARD GrRaRD. I am relieving officer for their district of St. Paneras
-on 27th November the male prisoner came to me in great distress, and
said his wife had left him and there was nothing for the children, and
asked me for reliefI then visited him and he said she had been seen with
another man in Fleet Road, and that the baby was in great want beeause
it was at the breast, and his wife had left it-I visited the house, and
gave him some temporary relief in the shape of meat and bread, and
milk for the baby, and told him the relief committee would meet on the
following Tuesday, when he would apply-he came on the Tuesday, and
said that his wife had returned to him, and he had made up, and he
did not require anything more-in spite of that I applied to the relief
committee, and an order was made for their admission to the workhouse,
and it was ready for them if he or she had applied-on 6th
December, the following Monday, a police-sergeant brought the prisoner
to me and told me his attention had been drawn to this case, and he
wished me to charge him-I said there had been already an order for
him to go into the workhouse, and I sent for him from the waiting
room, and gave him the order, and he promised to avail himself of it
I did all I could to persuade him to do it, because I knew it would be for
the benefit of the children-I called next day and BaW the female
prisoner--I asked her how the children were-she said they were all
right, and that she could get on very well without my interference,
without reliefon the 20th December, in consequence of a message from
the police I visited the house again about half past 8 in the evening-the
prisoners were not then there; the child was in bed asleep with the other
two children-when he heard my voice he woke up and began to ery
very mueh-I sent a boy for some milk, and he brought a large jugful
-after some time I found the feeding bottle, in a most filthy eondition-
I cleaned it, and left enough milk to last him next day-I wrote an order
for the medical officer to come and see the child-I took the order myself
and accompanied Mr. Warne, the medical officer, on the 20th-I went
next morning about half past eleven and saw the children-I saw there
was some food for the elder children, but I BaW nothing for the younger
one-I made inquiry, and went next day, the 22nd, about the same time;
I didn't see the parents then-I didn't go on the 23rd, but on the 24th
I went-I found a boy there, and gave him a message; neither of the
prisoners were present-the relief committee met on the 28th-l brought
the case before them, and obtained another order-on the 3rd January E
heard of the death of the child, and the mother applied to me for a burial
order, producing the certificate of death-she was in a state of intoxica-
tion, or a semi-state of intoxication-that was at half past 10 in the
morning-I reported the matter to the Coroner.
