COURT. Q. Do you mean, on your oath, to swear that when you put
your name to that paper, the signature, "Elizabeth Hollis Constable," was on
it? A. Elizabeth Hollis Constable's name, signed, "Elizabeth Hollis Con-
stable," was on that paper when I put my name as a witness-the words be.
ginning, “I have carefully compared," were certainly on that paper when I
put my name to it-I believe that I read them over-I will swear when she
signed it they were there-I believe I can swear positively they were there
that day-- to the best of my belief, I will swear that those words were on the
paper when I put my name to it-I recollect the words distinctly being an-
nounced to Miss Constable that night, upon which she stated, that if I re-
quired it, she could produce further receipts from her father's cash-book.
Q. What did you want further receipts for ? A. To fill up the deed--
this was after the deed had been taken out of Mr. Birch's hands, and was in
blank, but which Miss Constable stated ought to be filled up, for 8001. odd
she thought was the sum which her father was indebted to Joseph Stanley-I
am not certain whether Mrs. Blackwell was present when this was read over
I will not swear that she did not read it over-I will not swear that she
did not write this in my presence--she might have written it, but I did not
notice it.
Q. Will you swear that Mrs. Blackwell did not write the whole of that "Eli-
zabeth Hollis Constable," and all ? A. If she did I did not see it-I believe
that part of the name, when I was called to witness it, either " Elizabeth,"
or something, was written-I then distinctly said, "I will not put my name
to that till I see Miss Constable take a pen, and write Elizabeth Hollis Con:
stable."
Q. When did this deed, the genuineness of which is now questioned, first
come into your possession ? A. After the confirmation deed had been exe-
cuted, I think, about January or February, 1842, as near as I can remember
--my solicitor placed it in my possession-I rather think it was sent down by
the clerk to me, at No. 13, Wood-street-I believe that was the way it was
left at the warehouse for me the first time I ever saw the deed was in the
handing it over by Sutcliffe and Birch's clerk to a clerk of Hale, Boys, and
Austen-I believe the prisoner was present then I got possession of it from
two to three months after that the prisoner wished to borrow some money
to pay off Sutcliffe and Birch-I said, "I will not advance you more money
without you will give me security of some kind."
Q. Never mind that, but tell us what the arrangement was by which you
got that deed ? A. That Miss Phæbe Blackwell and the prisoner should
allow me to hold the deeds, as a collateral security for money which I bad
advanced to pay off the lien-both Phoebe Blackwell and the prisoner said
that-the prisoner said that he would allow me to hold the deeds belonging
to his estate, as a collateral security for the money I had advanced-I entered
up a warrant of attorney on the premises in Crawford-street-the agreement
was, that I was to pay Sutcliffe and Birch's bill, and to hold this deed as 8
collateral security for my debt-I paid Sutcliffe and Birch's bill, I think, to
the best of my recollection on the 1st or 2nd of Dec., 1841, late that evening
--the deeds were handed to Hale, Boys and Austen's clerk, by their clerk-I
had possession of these deeds, to the best of my recollection, in Feb., 1842-
they were handed to me by my solicitor.
