1976. MARY ANN BRYANT and CLARA ALDERSON were indicted
for stealing 2 shifts, value 6s. ; 3 scarfs, 108.; 1 pair of bracelets, 38.; and
1 smelling-bottle, 18.; the goods of Sophia Yates: to which
BRYANT pleaded Guilty.- Recommended to mercy.-- Judgment Respited.
Sophia YATES. I am single, and live in Bartlett-street, Lambeth. Some
months ago I left a box containing wearing-apparel and other things, at my
uncle Bryant's, the prisoner's father-I went into the country for four months
on my return I missed from my box two shifts and some other things--the
two prisoners slept together when I was in the house, and I left my box in
the room they used-I did not charge Alderson with breaking my box open,
but with pawning the things- I learnt from her that I might expect to find
them at Mr. Matthews', the pawnbroker-three scarfs, a smelling-bottle, and
a great many little things are still missing—the things that are here are mine
-I know this night-gown by the mark, and I know these others by the make
of them I found them in consequence of what Alderson told me.
WILLIAM Bryant. I am the prisoner Bryant's father-I live at Lambeth
my niece, Sophia Yates, left a box with me-my daughter and Alderson
slept in the room where the box was about the 10th of Aug. my daughter
left the house, and I found my niece's box had been broken open-my
daughter was brought home, and I accused her and Alderson with breaking
the box open-my daughter acknowledged to breaking it open-Alderson
acknowledged to pawning the things, but denied breaking the box-I found
some duplicates--I redeemed all the things that I knew of, but the things
that are here I was not aware of.
HANNAH MALLETT. My husband is a tailor-we live in Fair-street, Lam-
betb-Alderson came to tea with me one evening in August-she left a black
handkerchief on my table, and a smelling-bottle in a china case-I returned
them to her on the Tuesday morning afterwards.

CHARLES WILLIAMS. I am in the employ of Mr. Matthews, a pawn-
broker. These two shifts and a bed-gown were pawned by Alderson.
EDWARD Dixon (police-constable L 85.) I took Alderson into custody-
I told her it was for a robbery at Mr. Bryant's she said, "Is it not settled
yet? he gave me in charge once"-I said, "No, there are more things miss-
ing”-she said, "I know nothing about it."
Alderson's Defence. I went out one day, and when I returned a lodger in
the house told me that Mary Ann had broken open her cousin's box ; I went
up stairs and found it was so; I asked the reason she had done so, and
scolded her; she made up a bundle the next morning, and asked me to take
it to pawn; I hesitated some time; I then took it ; I brought her back the
money and the duplicates; I had not the least idea I was doing wrong.
ALDERSON-Not Guilty.
