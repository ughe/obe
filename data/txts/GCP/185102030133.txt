610. WILLIAM WICK, stealing 2 bird-nets, 1 gown, and other
articles, value 31. 28.; the goods of Thomas Wick..!
THOMAS Wick. The prisoner is my son; I live at 3, Manor-way, New
Charlton. On 28th Dec. I went to London about five o'clock in the
evening, leaving only my little boy, eleven years old, at home-I returned
about eight, and in consequence of what my son said to me I looked at
some boxes up-stairs, found them broken open, and some bird-nets, a
gown, a scarf, five shawls, and other articles, worth about 31., gone—these
things (produced) are mine-I had put them apart in a box for my married
daughter-I did tell the prisoner he might have the nets.
WILLIAM SALMON. I am in the employ of Mr. Barnett, pawnbroker,
in the Dover-road. This satin gown and silk scarf were pledged by a
female on 28th Dec.-on 7th Jan. Mary Keefe came with the ticket, paid
the interest, looked at the articles, and had a ticket made out in her
own name,
HOLL
Mary Kaars. I bought two tickets of the prisoner on 7th Jan., for
38.—I went with him to Mr. Barnett's with one of them, and had it trans-
ferred-he stood outside-I got this black gown, shawl, and two petticoats
(produced) with the other ticket.
LARGESON HAMMOND. I live at 36, Kent-street, Borough. On 8th
Jan, the prisoner brought these nets for sale-I told him a gentleman of
the name of Wick had been and ordered me to stop them, I believed these
to be the same nets, and him to be Mr. Wick's son--he owned to being
his son, but said the nets were his own.
THOMAS SWAINE (policeman, M 106). I took the prisoner on 8th
Jan., and asked him where he got the nets-he said, “From a friend in
Union-street, to sell."
Prisoner's Defence, My father gave me the nets, and my brother the
pawn-tickets.
Not GUILTY,
611. SARAH WILLIAMS, stealing 4 lbs. weight of mutton, value
28. 4d. ; the goods of George Peppercorn and another: having been before
convicted.
JAMES WILLIAM CROUCH (policeman, R 118). On 27th Jan. I was on
duty in the Broadway, Deptford, and saw the prisoner go up and down
six or seven times by the prosecutor's shop-she then went to the right
band, and put her shawl over part of a leg of mutton on the shop-board-
she then went into the shop, did not stop a second, and came out-
stopped her, and asked her what she had got-she said, “Nothing"-I
took part of a leg of mutton, 4lbs. weight, from under her shawl.
WILLIAM BAKER. I am in the employ of George and Alfred Pepper-
corn, butchers, of the Broadway, Deptford, Crouch brought this leg of
mutton; it was safe before-it is their property.
Prisoner's Defence. I was very much in liquor.
JAMES WILLIAM CROUCH re-examined. She was quite sober-she had
walked up and down several times.
