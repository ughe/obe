FREDERIO KING. I am a cheesemonger, of 20, Little Cross Street-I
was passing down St. Martin's Lane about 12.30 on 28th November; I
saw the last witness and the prisoner struggling; the prisoner threw the
prosecutor on the ground; a crowd gathered.
EDWARD MITCHELL (Policeman E 237). About half-past 12 on the
night of 28th November I was in St. Martin's Lane and heard cries
I went to the spot; I saw the prisoner holding the prosecutor by the
throat; he was bleeding from a wound in the face ; he said "I charge
this man with stealing my watch the prisoner made no remark-
took him to the station and searched him; I did not find the watch the
prosecutor said he had been drinking, but he knew what he was doing.
GUILTY. He also PLEADED GUILTY** to a conpiction in this court in April
last in the name of Thomas Murphy-Five Years' Ponal Servitudo.

Robert Mills, with intent to steal.
Mr. GEOHEGAN Proseouted; MR. RAYEN Defended. lt
JOSEPH SOPER (Policeman F 383). On Saturday, the 18th December,
about 7.30 p.m., I was with Police-constable Enright in Broad Street,
Ratcliff-I BAW Mills very drunk standing up against a wall; he stag
gered; the prisoner came up and struck him on the eye, knocked him
down, and put his hand into his coat pocket-I went towards them; some
one cried out to Orawford “Leave him alone, you will get yourself into
trouble"_I took the prisoner into custody; I charged him with assault-
ing a drunken man--the prisoner said "He challenged to fight me first
-I took him to the station; I found on him l. 117d. in his trousers
pocket-I did not see any sailors about.
Cross-examined. I was in a doorway watching; I said go to the Magis-
trate-I did not hear any challenging I was on the same side of the
road the prosecutor was knocked down when I first saw them I was on
the other side-I saw no row; they crossed the road and came to the side
I was; I was about forty yards away when I first saw them, and about
five yards when they crossed the road.
Ro-examined. The prisoner followed the drunken man across the road
-I was in plain clothes.
JAMES ENRIGHT (Policeman H 293). I was on duty with the last wit-

Mills-I found 18. 64d. on Mills.
Cross-examined. I was concealed in a doorway with the other constable
- they were from fifteen to twenty yards off-I heard no squabble I
did not see the prosecutor kick the prisoner people gathered round; I
could not swear how many; about two.
