Cross-examined by Eden. I paid my traveller at the close of the trans-
action-you paid him your first instalment I think; I cannot tell if he
gave you a receipt; you gave him 158. and he handed me 108.he kept the
other 58. to pay next week as he was doubtful if you would pay then the
only other amount I had was & post-office order for 108. on the second
watch-I don't know if my traveller is to be called.
Cross-examined by Johnson. It was about the middle or end of June
you called on me and introduced yourself by card as a tradesman desirous
of buying good watches, which you understood I sold by instalments-
you arranged to pay 21. down and 21. a month for a ten-guinea one-on
the first hearing before the Magistrate I said I wished every one had
served me as you had, and that I had received a letter from your wife
desiring me not to send the watch-on the second occasion you sent &
boy with a note for the watch; I don't know if you sent the money by
him; I didn't ask him as a rule I have a promissory note when selling
in this way, I would not let the boy have the watch-you owe met
nothing. du troca
Cross-examined by Wolls. Robinson spoke about you before you called
-your first visit was within a week of your last-I received this letter
from Robinson in the interim. (This stated that all he knew of Wells was
having seen him in business, and judging from that he should think ho would
pay all right.) I would not let you have a watch.
Cross-examined by Mr. HOPKINS. White, my traveller, is not the
prisoner, whom I never saw till he was at Worship Street.
HENRY THOMAS HOLLAND. I was formerly order clerk to Morrison and
Wood, confectioners-on 3rd April I received an order from R. May,
5, Ridley Road, and goods amounting to 6l. 198. 11d. were delivered,
but were returned because cash was not paid on delivery-- I wrote for a
reference and received this reply. This referred to B. L. Vandam, 31,
Bishopsgate Street, and to his bankers, the Oripplegats Bank, Whiteoro88
Street.) I applied to Mr. Vandam, but he did not sufficiently know him
-I got a satisfactroy reply from the Cripplegate Bank, and on 13th
April sent the goods, and on 17th April more to the amount of 61. 178. 6d.
-I have never been paid the 131. 17s. 5d. which the two lots amount to
-in March, April, May, and June, 1883, I received orders from Browne,
in the name of H. Hart, 1 and 2, Haydon Road, South Wimbledon,
amounting altogether to 297. 28. 1d. he has returned 78. Bd. worth, so
that the total is 281. 146. 10d. still owing-on 29th October I received an
order from Waters, of 57, Kender Street, New Cross, of 101. 2s. 3d.
those goods were never paid for-I issued a County Court summons, but
never obtained any money on the judgment– I supplied goods to Owen's
first order, for which we had a cheque; and then, on 21st May, he had
more goods to the amount of 37. 28. 5d., of which 58. 5d. was returned,
leaving 21. 178., which he owes now.
