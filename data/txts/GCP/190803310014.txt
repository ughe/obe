WILLIAX BOWES (prisoner, not on oath). When I had money I
assisted my nephow to the extent of £2,000, and I also assisted his
mother. I have now no money. I applied several times to get a
hotel license in various parts of Scotland, and my nephew, being •
wholesale wine merohant, was in the habit of assisting people, but be
never would assist me. I came to London with £150 in my pocket,
which was soon swallowed up by the expenses of taking my furniture
and books to London and fitting up the shop, etc. The business
turned out a complete failure, the books I had brought being of too
high a class for Holloway Road. Many a day I have stood in the
shop and never drawn a single shilling. One Saturday I stood for
13 hours in the shop and never took a single penny. When I wrote
the letter to my nephew I had 3s. left. I could do nothing. I had
a wife and four children in Glasgow. I had to sell my furniture bit
by bit. I lived at the extravagant rate of 1s. a day, 3fd. for my
breakfast, 6d. for my dinner, and 2d. for my tea. I am now 63 and
was never in trouble before, and I think Mr. Duncan has shown him.
self one of the basest specimens of humanity I ever came across.
