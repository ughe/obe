   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE Q.  When did you first hear that you would be wanted as a witness against these parties? A.  I heard nothing about it till a man came after roe at the Great Western Railway—I do not at all recollect when it was, but he said I must come to the Man sion-house, at eleven o'clock next morning, to give evidence—it was more than a month after I left the Bull and Mouth—I should not think it was two months—the man brought me a paper—when the Lord Mayor asked me if I knew the prisoner, I said yes—no one had spoken to me before that—I cannot tell how long after I had been at the Bull and Mouth I first saw either of the three prisoners, or which I first saw—I did not know there were two Mr. Smiths—there was Mr. Beaumont, a stout gen tleman—I cannot say how many times I have seen Mr. Smith bring paper parcels there, but very frequently—I have seen him bring them in and take them away under his arm—I cannot say when I first saw him bring any parcels—I saw two others, besides Mr. Smith, bring parcels—one was a man with a wen on his cheek—I do not recollect a man named Ketcher—I cannot tell when it was that I carried the parcel for Mr. Smith, for which he gave me 6 d .—I cannot say how long it was before I left—I do not know that I should know the handkerchiefs again which Mr. Smith showed me—some were bright yellow ones—( looking at some produced by Mr. Ballantine )—I could not swear to these—I will not say either way——( looking at some others )—I cannot swear to these—they may be brought out of the shop like them, and then I should not know—( looking at others )—these are like the handkerchiefs I saw, but they may not be them—Mr. Smith opened the yellow ones to show me—I took particu lar notice of them—I do not know a person named Bates, who was in the habit of coming to the house—I cannot say whether any parcels were brought on the 9th of July, or on the 11th, 12th, or 13th—I took no notice of the time, nor can I say when these handkerchiefs were shown to me—my mistress might have been very good friends with Mr. Smith—I saw none of it—he was a very good customer, for aught I know, he always paid for what he had—she did not like any of them, and did not study their custom—I cannot recollect that she mentioned any names when she said she did not like any of them.
