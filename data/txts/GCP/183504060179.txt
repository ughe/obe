MARY MASSAM. I keep a shop in the Harrow-road. On the 25th of
March the prisoner came to my shop for half-an-ounce of tobacco, which
came to three-halfpence--he gave me a shilling, and I gave him sixpence
and fourpence-halfpenny-I put the shilling in the drawer, where I had no
more silver-as he turned to go out, something struck me that it was not
right-I took the shilling to my sister, who said it was bad- I followed
the prisoner-I found him in the road-he said he did not know it was
bad, and he had no right to know it-I brought him back, and told him to
give me the tobacco and the change ---he would not-I told him I would
call in the policeman-he still stood there, and said his time was very pre-
cious, but he would not give it up-- I called in the policeman, and he gave
back the tobacco and the change - I gave the shilling to the officer.
SAMUEL WANE. I am a policeman. I was called in by this witness,
and received the shilling from her.

ANN TAYLOR. I keep a general shop, in Earl-street, Kennington, On
the 11th of March the prisoner came there for half-an-ounce of tobacco
- he laid down a shilling--I saw it was bad, and refused it-he took it up
again, and said he knew where he took it-I am sure he is the boy-he
had a white great-coat on.
_TEMPERANCE STEEL. My husband keeps the Hand and Flower, at
Hammersmith. On the 11th of March, between three and four o'clock, a
boy came in, in a light coat-he asked me for change for a shilling-I gave
it him he went out, and I saw the shilling was bad I sent my niece
after him--she brought back the change, and gave the boy the shilling
the prisoner is like the boy.
EMMA WORDLEY. I am niece to Mrs. Steel. She desired me to go
after a boy who had given her a bad shilling - I believe the prisoner is the
boy-I got the change from him, he took the shilling, and said he knew
where he had taken it he had got three doors off, and I met him coming
out of a pastrycook's with some buns-he went and got the penny back
which he had given for the buns, and gave the change to me. Bongo
