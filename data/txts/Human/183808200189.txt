I am a Thames police-constable. I was called on board the Adelaide, on Friday, the 6th of July, at half-past eight at night—these articles were delivered to me, and I took the three prisoners—I searched Smith on the deck, and found on him two ounces of tobacco—I asked M'Cormack where he got the spoons—he said he saw a piece of paper on one of the cases, and when he came to overhaul it, he found it to contain spoons, and put them into his pocket-Sloane said he picked up the sheet lying by the water-closet—he thought it was washed linen, and intended to give it to the steward. THOMAS COYLE. I am shipping clerk to the agent of the Dublin and London Steam-packet Company. I was on board the Adelaide on the 6th of July—I received the chest—there was a ticket on it marked "No. 14," and in the evening it was discovered to be broken—the name of the cap tain is John Soy. CHARLES HENRY FALCONER. I am a Thames police-surveyor. Bart lett reported to me that he had these men, and I sent for them—I told them I understood they were charged with breaking open a plate chest, on boarcl the Adelaide, but to be careful what they said-Sloane said, "I was going to the water-closet, and found the sheet, I kept it as was my duty, till I saw some of the crew, and when I came on deck I gave it to Lawson"-M'Cormack said, "I was going for a south-wester in another part of the hold, and on my way I found a parcel of spoons, I had them in my hand, but never put them into my pocket"-Smith gave an account of something found on him, but not of these things.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe that was a bit of tobacco? A.  Yes. SARAH CRAWLEY. I live in Bridge-street, Greenwich. Mrs. Coats-worth was my mistress—she lived at Mrs. Smallwood's, at Greenwich—she left for Falmouth on the 7th—I was with her till the Friday morn ing preceding—I corded up all her boxes but one large chest, which was too heavy for me—I saw it at her house before it went away—there was too direction on it to go to Mr. Coyle, to go on board the steam-packet—this sheet—( looking at it )-is hers—this tea-pot and spoons are hers—they were to go by the Royal Adelaide.
