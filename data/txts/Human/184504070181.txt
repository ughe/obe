CHARLES TAYLOR. I was in the employ of Mr. Larman. On the 20th of March I was at Whitechapel-market, and Mr. Larman gave me a mes sage to take to Mr. Dixon's—I went there, and Mr. Basham gave me a ticket like this, but I cannot read—I gave it to my master. JOHN BASHAM. I am clerk to Mr. Dixon. I remember Charles Tay lor coming that morning with a message from Mr. Larman—I think it was between ten and eleven o'clock—I gave him this ticket, which I got from Angle, who was in the yard, taking in some straw—the last two carts were just about going out of the yard—I asked Angle what he had done with the ticket he had out of the counting-house—he said, "Here it is"—this refers to the clover-hay and cinqfoil—he took it out of his pocket—the carts which had left the meadow-hay were gone, the other carts were not—Angle did not give any reason why he had not given this ticket to the carman. WILLIAM LARMAN re-examined.  I got to Banks's that morning about ten o'clock—his house is in Brick-lane, nearly a quarter of a mile beyond the turning that should be taken to go to Mr. Dixon's—I did not see any of the carts but Morrell's—I sent Charles Taylor to Mr. Dixon's for the ticket, because I wanted to know whether my carts had left Mr. Dixon's, and if he met them he was to ask my men for the ticket—it was about eleven o'clock when I sent Charles Taylor—it was after Banks had been taken into custody, and after I had told Morrell that I would take him into custody—when I sent Charles Taylor, Morrell bad been gone from Banks's door about an hour—there was plenty of time for him to have got to Dixon's, and have told anybody there that I had found him at Banks's—I did not see George Taylor, the prisoner, again till he got home at night. SAMUEL TAYLOR. I am constable of Whitechapel-market. I accom panied Mr. Larman to Banks's shop thai morning—I had seen these carts come into the market that morning, but I did not see them go out—I went to Banks's, and found three trusses of clover and one of cinqfoil—I took Banks into custody—I took George Taylor, Morrell, and Shepherd the same evening at a beer-shop at Enfield—they were not sober. WILLIAM LARMAN re-examined.  Morrell's cart was loaded with cinq foil—he could not have got clover except from the other men—the clover I found in Banks's shop is what I sent by Taylor, Shepherd, and Ovenall—I could swear to it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How do you know it? A.  By the growing of it, and the tying of it—there was not much of that clover grown, for the first cut was almost all stubble—I cannot tell how many acres it grew upon, or how much there was of it—I had three loads of clover that day in the market, and they were all sold to Mr. Dixon—I had none then left at home—that was the last of the stack of clover-hay—there had been fourteen loads and six trusses in the stack, and that was the last of it.
 (Morrell and Banks received good characters.)
   MORRELL  — GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Nine Months.
   BANKS  — GUILTY. Aged 28.— Confined Six Months .
   TAYLOR , SHEPHERD , and ANGLE  — NOT GUILTY.
