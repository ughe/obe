WILLIAM ALLERTON Frost. I live at 11, Le Bridge Gardens, Leyton
--on November 26th, about 2 o'clock, I was in Commercial Street, and
saw the two prisoners--Walsh was driving a pony--I drove after them
and stopped them, and detained them till * policeman came up.
OLIVER THOMPSON (Policeman, G) On November 26th Frost gave
the prisoners into my custody; chey said that the pony had been wolen
Walsh said, "I will go with you, but I bought the pony. Villiams

GEORGE KING (Police-sergeant). On November 26th I was on
duty at Lea Bridge Station about 3 p.m., when the prisoners were
brought in- I told them that the pony had been stolen, and asked where
they got it-Walsh produced * receipt, and said, "I bought it of MF.
Brown for £9 10s.; it was one out of five" I asked if he knew Mr. Brown
-he said that he nuver saw him before, and did not know where he
lived.
JAMES BALLARD (Detective Sergeant, N). On December 12th I saw
Walsh at the Police-court--he said, “I bought the horse at Stapleton's,
of a man named Brown, on November 20th ; I gave him £9 10s. for the
pony and harness, and the receipt produced is the one he gave me he
did not mention any hour.
WILLIAM BRADFORD. I am a carman, of 3, Moss Street, Bethnal Green
--on November 20th I was stationed on Southwark Bridge, with a horse,
and saw Walsh pass about 7.40 a.m., and again between 9 and 10, and
again about 11.10. going towards Tooley Streets in charge of a horse and
cart, carting bricks--I knew him as a carman.
GEORGE HAWKFIELD. I am a carman working for Fortescue and Son,
of Manor Street, Hackney-on November 20th I met Walsh carting
bricks for Fortescue on Southwark Bridge, on the south side; the last
time I saw him was 9.45,
JAMES BALLARD (Re-examined). He said, “I bought it at Stapleton's.
at 10.30 on November 20th "-(Reading his note).
HENRY ALWORTHY (Re-examined). The pony would have been three years
old on January 24th, it was not quite broken in, but it would go in
barness—I value it at £20, but it would not fetch so much at Stapleton's
-I was offered £14 for it when it was only 14 months old,
The prisoners produced written defences, in which Walsh stated that he
bought the pony for £9 108. of a man outside Stapleton's, who was too late
to put it into the sale, and that if he had been allowed bail he could have
found the man. Williams stated that he met Walsh, who took him to Staple-
ton's, where he bought the pony for £9 10s. of a man who gave him a
receipt, and that Walsh then asked him to take the pony home, as he wanted
to finish his work.
WALSH-GUILTY. He then Pleaded Guilty to a conviotion at
Clerkenwell on February 11th, 1899.-- Twelve Months' Hard Labour.
WILLIAMS—Not Guilty.
