   BEFORE JUDGE LUMLEY SMITH
 (Monday, November 25.) CHASEY  , John (23, fireman) ; robbery with violence on Henry Edward Brown and stealing from him the sum of £2 5s.
 Mr. Metcalf prosecuted; Mr. Coumbe defended.
 (In opening the case for the prosecution Mr. Metcalf stated that the reason why the prisoner had not been charged before was that he could not be found.) HENRY EDWARD BROWN. I am a seaman. On November 15, 1905, I was lodging at No. 39, Milford Road, Plaistow, which was occupied by a Mr. and Mrs. Read. On that day I had a seaman's advance note, which I cashed for £3 10s. with a man named Smith. Having received the money I went with Mr. and Mrs. Read and Mrs. Smith to a public-house called the "Peacock," near the Custom House. We had a drink there. While we were there two men came into the "Peacock." and got into conservation with me. One of them was the prisoner. The other man has already been convicted. We were there about half-an-hour. I treated them, and gave them is each. After I did that Mr. and Mrs. Read and myself left the public house, and we all got on a tramcar going to Barking Road. When I was on the tramcar I found these two men were on the tramcar at the back of me. I spoke to them and paid their fares—1/2 d. each. Mr. and Mrs. Read and myself got down at Barking Road and went into the "Swan" public-house. We remained there about 20 minutes to half-an-hour. The two men followed me in there. I was talking to them part of the time and also to my friends. I again treated these man to drinks, and gave them another 1s. each. I then left the house with Mr. and Mrs. Read, and we walked towards 39, Milford Road. When we were near the house—it was rather dark—we heard footsteps behind us, and I stopped to see if it was really the same two man that were following me—I had my suspicions that what they had said, namely, that they were shipmates of mine was not quite true. I turned round and saw the other man (Marwell) and the prisoner, and asked them if they were following us. They said no, but they lived close round there, and I said, "Well, that is how you come to know me then, but you have never been shipmates with me." With the permission of my landlord I invited the men into stopper, which we finished shout 10 o'clock. The prisoner then said, "Who is going to start the game?"
