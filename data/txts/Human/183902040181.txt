 738. JOHN FLETCHER was indicted for stealing, on the   30th of Janu ary , 1 pair of boots, value 6 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of George Savage , his master. GEORGE SAVAGE. I live in Tottenham Court-road, and am a shoe maker—the prisoner was my errand-boy. On the 30th of January I re marked, when he went out to breakfast, that one of his pockets appeared bulky—he had his great coat on—when he came back he put his great coat on a shelf, and about an hour before dinner-time I felt his great coat, and found in it something like boots or shoes—I then went and spoke to the policeman, the prisoner went out, and the policeman brought him back with these boots. JOHN FORD( police-sergeant F  20.) I waited outside, and took the pri soner as he was going to dinner—he was putting on his great coat as he came out—he walked from the door buttoning it up—I went to him and asked what he had got in his pocket—he said a pair of boots which he was going to take to Rathbone-place—I asked what house—he said, "No. 18"—I asked him the name of the person—he said he did not know—I asked if they were paid for—he said "Yes"—I asked how much—be said, "6 s . 6 d ."—I asked if he had paid his master the money—he said "Yes"—I took" them from him, took him back to the shop, and asked Mr. Savage if he had given him a pair of boots to take to Rathbone-place, the prisoner then said, "No, I did not say so, I was going to take them to my mother." GEORGE SAVAGE re-examined.  His mother lives in Well-street, which is rather nearer to Rathbone-place than where I reside—he bore a good character before—he had only entered my service on the Saturday before.
   Prisoner.  What I had in my pocket at breakfast time was my breakfast. NOT GUILTY.
   Sixth Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 739. JAMES EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of January , 1 cloak, value 3 l  . 3 s  ., the goods of Henry Girdlestone . WILLIAM BRIMBLECOMBE. I am a policeman. On the 23rd of January I met the prisoner in the Edgeware-road with a bundle—I asked what he bad got—he said his coat which he had taken out of pawn—he afterwards said a man gave it him to carry—there were two more men with him, who ran tway—I took him to the station-house, and found this cloak in the bundle HENRY GIRDLESTONE. I am a medical student. left this cloak at Mr. Howlett's—it is mine. HENRY HOWLBTT. I am a surgeon, and live in Cambridge-terrace, On the 23rd of January I went out, about one o'clock, and took this cloak—when I came home, about two o'clock, I left it in a small room adjoining my surgery, and told the boy to hang it up—I went in again at four o'clock, and saw it still on a chair—I went there again at six o'clock, and my impression was, that it was there then, but I will not be certain.
   Prisoner.  I met two young men, who said they would give me 6 d . to carry the bundle to Oxford-street.
 , (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Three Months.
