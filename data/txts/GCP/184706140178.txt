Q. Was there then a ratification of his appointment sent by the Poor-lay
Commissioners, prescribing what his duties were ? 1. No, merely approvine
his appointment-his duties are prescribed in the order-the Guardians are
bound by that prescription of the duties-the order directing the election, also
prescribes the duties--the prisoner was appointed by the Guardians-be hus
since been dismissed by the Poor-law Commissioners, not by the Guardians
they have no power of dismissal—the prisoner gave a bond to the Guardians
on being appointed--I have got that bond-every Wednesday morning he
was expected to show to the Guardians how the rates stood-he did not go
before the Guardians, he rendered an account every Wednesday morning to
me, as clerk to the Guardians, of the money he had received on the different
rates that were then due-it can hardly be called a weekly account--it was the
result of the monies collected-the Guardians are the Guardians of the Union
--they direct a certain sum of money to be paid by the Overseers to them, u
their share of the expenses of the Union-the money is collected for the
Overseers, and paid to the account of the Overseers, at the banker's--they
have to pay a great many small local expenses, Police-rates, and so on, and
then they pay the amount ordered by the Guardians over to the Guardians.
COURT. Q. Are the sums he is charged with embezzling, included in the
sum of which you have been speaking ? A. No.
MR. ROBINSON. Q. Is Mr. Mitchener the auditor of the district, or of the
whole Union ? A. Of the district-be is the auditor for that district, as well
as for other places--the prisoner is paid his commission by the Overseers.
COURT. Q. Do you mean that he is paid out of the rate of the parish!
A. No, paid by the Overseers, by the hand of the Overseers- there is no
deduction.
MR. ROBINSON. Q. Look at that receipt, is that the prisoner's hand-
writing? A. It is, (reading it,)“ Received of the Overseers of the Liberty
of Saffron-hill, the sum of 421. 2s. 9d., for poundage to Michaelmas last.
Thomas Truman"-he is paid by a poundage-the money collected is paid in
to the bankers, to the account of the Overseers, the Overseers have to pay
the Police-rate, the County-rate, &c., that is without any connection with the
Guardians, quite independent of them--the Guardians know nothing of it, in
fact all that they know is with regard to their own receipt for the main-
tainance and support of the poor-the Overseers pay a certain amount to the
treasurer of the Union, for the Guardians for the relief of the poor, and the
surplus, after that amount is so paid, is retained by the Overseers for the
next year.
Q. There is no accounting, as I understand, by the Overseers to the
Guardians at all ? A. There is the result of every Wednesday--the collector
is expected to show the balance of each rate as it stands at that time.

there is any laxity on the part of the collector, the Guardians might come
municate with the Overseers.
