328. IIENRY SCOWEN (24), Unlawfully obtaining 11. 145, from
Elizabeth Wragg, 11, 108. from Henry Silis, 17 from John Blake, 11. 148.
from Mary Stock, and 31. 98. from Charles Wilkinson, by means of false
pretences.
MR. LAWRENCE conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH WRAGG. I am the wife of James Wragg, of 7, Essex-street,
Bethnal-green-road-about the beginning of September last, the prisoner
came to me-he said he was a ship's carpenter, and that he had come
from Australia from my son-in-law's, and he wanted lodgings-be said he
had valuable things aboard : monkeys, parrots, crape shawls, and other
valuables, and he wished to place them under my care, as my son-in-law
said they would be safe—he asked me to recommend him a carman; I did so,
a man who used to bring my son-in-law's things home, and he said, “I can-
not fetch them, but I will recommend him to another carman, as my borse
is ill," and he did so—just before he went away the prisoner said, “Oh!
by-the-bye, can't you give me some money to clear the ship?”-I said, "I
have only 148., and that is my rent-money”-I gave him that, he said,
"Can't you get some more ?"-I sid, "I will try "-he said, "I will be
back in one hour, and give you double, when I am paid at 3 o'clock"
- I went to a neighbour, and got 11., and lent him that-I let him bave
11. 148. altogether I lent him the money from what he told me--I should
not have lent him it if he had not told me what he did I believed be was
a ship's carpenter-he saw a model of a ship in the house, and told me
John had not made it cleverly.
