   Cross-examined by MR. DUNCAN . Q.  Did you take the silk from Tilley in the evening? A.  Yes, four hanks; It was arranged between the police man and me that they should be returned to Tilley for him to dispose of in the same way as he had told us he had previously done; it was for the pur pose of laying a sort of trap for the other person—that was done by In spector Newnham, of Bow-lane station; I went there after I came from Mr. Griffiths—I was ordered to go to the station and see the inspector—it was me that took the four hanks of silk from Tilley; I took them to Mr. Griffiths, and he saw them—it was the officer who gave them back to Tilley; I had given them to Inspector Newnham by Mr. Griffiths's orders—I was present when Turner, the officer, gave the silk to Tilley; it was done with my concurrence—that was not at the station, but at the warehouse.
   MR. PARRY . Q.  Did you communicate to Newnham the statement that the boy had made to you? A.  Yes; it was after that that the inspector did what he did; and it was under his advice that I acted in everything that I did. WILLIAM TURNER( City policeman , 449).
