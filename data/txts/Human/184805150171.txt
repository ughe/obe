THOMAS WEST( policeman, M  249.) About nine o'clock last Saturday I saw the prisoners in company in Old Kent-road—I followed them to Mr. Francis's in Alfred-place—Waller passed and repassed the window—I went on, came back, and saw Burdett leave the shop-door, and place these numbers in Waller's apron—I stopped them—Ellis said, "They are ours"—Waller threw them from his apron, and Ellis ran off with them—I pursued and took her—Burdett was brought back—he said he would pay for them.
   Burdett's Defence.  I went in, and asked the young man if he had got any old numbers; he said, "Yes," I could have them for a shilling. WILLIAM JENNINGS( policeman, M  273.) I produce a certificate of Waller's former conviction—( read—Convicted Dec. , 1846, and confined two Months )—he is the man.

 1450. THOMAS CRAWLEY , JOHN WILLSHER, and WILLIAM RUTT , stealing 1 handkerchief, value 3 s  .; the goods of William Johns , from his person. WILLIAM JOHNS. I live at Kennington. On 28th April, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, I was in Walworth-road—I felt my handkerchief taken out of my pocket—I turned, and the prisoners were behind me—I charged Rutt with having taken it—while I was speaking, West came and took him.
   Rutt. Q.  Did I go away? A.  No. THOMAS WEST( policeman, M  249.) I was in Westminster-road between seven and eight o'clock that evening—I saw the prisoner Crawley try a lady's pocket, Willsher was covering him—they went on and attempted another pocket, but did not succeed—they went on to the Walworth-road—Crawley put his hand into a gentleman's pocket, and took out something white—I asked if he had lost anything—he said, "A white handkerchief," but he should not know it—in about ten minutes Mr. Johns passed—the pri soners followed him about 200 yards—Willsher stepped into the road, just by a stall—this handkerchief, which Mr. Johns claims, was found there—I went up, and found Rutt in conversation with Johns, who said, "They; have just picked my pocket"—I took Rutt and Willsher—Rutt said, "I know nothing of that man," meaning Willsher. THOMAS TOURESEY( policeman, M  237.) I was watching the prisoners—I saw what West has stated—they were all trying pockets. MARTHA EASTER. I wa sitting at my stall about half-past eight o'clock that evening—I saw a handkerchief lying—I picked it up and gave it to West.
