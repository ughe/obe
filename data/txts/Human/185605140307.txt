 547. HENRY CHARNELL , WILLIAM MARTIN , JOHN SALE , CHARLES PESHELL, and JOHN TILLETT , stealing, in the dwelling house of Richard Trott Fisher , the master or Charnell, 1 silver tea kettle, and a variety of articles of plate, value 500 l  .; his property.
   MR. GIFFARD . conducted We Prosecution. RICHARD TROTT FISHER. I live at No. 30, Eaton-place, in the parish of St. George's, Hanover-square. Charnell was my butler—on the night of the 4th April I went to bed about half past 12 o'clock—I went by accident into my study, got hold of a book, and sat there for half or three quarters of an hour—the house was perfectly quiet when I went to bed—I supposed that every body was in bed—my dressing room is in a situation that I can hear the least talking or noise (I do not make a practice of going to bed after the servants, I generally go at about 11 o'clock)—about 7 clock in the morning in consequence of something a female servant said to me, I went down and saw Charnell—I should have been up soon after 8 o'clock, in the ordinary course—Charnell met me on the stairs, and began in rather an extravagant way to ask that I would forgive him—there were two maid servants stand ing there, and I told him to go into the drawing room and say what he had to say—he said, "I hope you will forgive me, I am sure you never will forgive me"—I said, "Just go into the room, and I will oome and speak to you"—I went in and shut the door, and he said that he had been called out at night soon after he got into bed, a relation came or had sent for him, that a cousin of his was dangerously ill, that he got up, went away at 12 o'clock, was absent two hours, and when he came back he found that the plate was taken—I said, "Why, I was up myself at 12"—he said that it might been twenty minutes or half past 12, that the husband of his cousin had come and called him up—I went and dressed, saw him afterwards, and asked if he had gone for the police—he said that he had not—he told me after wards that his bed was put down, he sleeps in the pantry, and he told me that his watch was lying on the bed—the plate was kept in a very strong plate closet, built on purpose, fire proof, and opening out Of his pantry, and there are two strong chests to keep what is not used in, and which I believe are in Court ( produced )—there is a fire proof door which opens out of this butler's pantry, where he sleeps, and the two chests are in the closet; in which to send the plate backwards and forwards—they are fitted up for each parti cular article—the keys of the chests, as well as of the closet, were in the possession of the butler—I gave him the keys and the entire control of all the plate—I have no reason to believe that there were marks on the cheats before—the last time I saw them was when I
