"Oh, if that is the case, here is the man I bought it of," pointing
to Gregson, who was standing against a potato-bin, in the shop-that was
said loud enough for Gregson to hear-I said to Gregson, "How do you
account for it?"-Gregson said, “I know nothing at all about it"-
Christall and Gregson were then taken into custody- I took Christall, and
desired Hopkins to bring the butter away in the truck-Hopkins was
quite near enough to hear the conversation I have spoken of-I went to
Lambeth-street, locked up the butter, and sent Taylor with Gregson to
the station-house, and took Hopkins and Christall myself-I asked
Hopkins where he brought the butter from-he said from Whitechapel-
I said I was aware of that--he said he would show me where he brought it
from, I went with him and Christall, to the Angel and Crown, Toy's
house-he took me there-Toy was in the bar-it was about half-past
six o'clock-on going in, Christall said to Toy, “You have brought me
into a nice mess with your butter"-I told Christall to hold his tongue,
and then told Toy that I had stopped these two men with a truck, and a
cask of butter, which I suspected to be one of 20, obtained by a forged
order from Brewer's Quay, and that from what information I had received,
I expected there was some more of it there, or that he knew of it—he said
he knew nothing about the butter, he had seen no butter, he knew nothing
about it, it had never been there I then called Hopkins forward, and
asked him in Toy's presence, where he took the butter from-he imme-
diately turned and shoved the tap-room door open, pointed to the table
in the right-hand corner, and said, "There is where I took it from"-I
said to Toy, "What do you think of this?”—Toy told Hopkins it was a lie,
it had never been there-Taylor then returned-I left Hopkins and
Christall in his custody, and took Toy into the passage--he went with me
into the passage--I told him that he being a publican, I knew him to be a
Tespectable man, and it was much more to his credit, if he knew any thing of
the butters to tell me, than otherwise--he said he knew nothing of them
they had never been there, and after a minute or so he turned round, and
said directly, "I have heard of some butters in the neighbourhood, but
from what I can understand they have been removed about three quarters
of an hour-I think they were taken away in a cart like a gravel cart, but
if you will come down to me to-morrow morning, about half-past nine
o'clock, there is no doubt yon may hear something about them, and I will
put you on the men and the butters too"-I did not take him into custody
that night-on that representation I left the bouse-on the Sunday after
this I accompanied Childs and Taylor to Cumberland-place, Waterloo-
town, and took Northcote into custody there--we stopped at a public-
house, coming from there, with Northcote in custody-I saw Childs take
a pocket-book from Northcote in that public-house, which he has-I
looked at it, and saw some writing in it.
