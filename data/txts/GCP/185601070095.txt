JOSEPH BENTON (policeman, K 381). On the evening of 26th Dee. I was
on duty, about 7 o'clock, in High-street, Stratford, with Collins, a police-
man, and saw the prisoners together-I watched them from the dark side
of the way, and they were on the other we then removed our capes,
crossed the road, and followed the prisoners to Shea's marine store shop
they had some conversation outside the shop-it was shut up, it being boxing
day-Collins then left Kelly, and went on the other side of the way
Kelly pulled the shop bell—the door was opened, he went in, and I fol-
lowed I saw him hand three brass taps, with some leaden pipe attached to
them, to Mr. Shea I asked Kelly how he came by them-he said, "I
shall not (or will not) tell you "-I was asking Mr. Shea some questions,
and Kelly escaped—I directly afterwards apprehended Collins at the Bird
in Hand public house, near Mr. Shea's I told him the charge, and he
said that he had not seen Kelly, and had not been with him that evening
the prisoners were remanded next day, and I made inquiries, and found
that the taps came from Nos. 2, 3, and 4, Waddington-place, Windmill-
lane, Stratford-I fitted them to the water butts there, and found that
they corresponded at each house-I saw footmarks in the gardens of No. 2
and No. 4, and very slight ones in No. 3—the impressions were of two.
