Wu. OSBORNE (City Detective). About three o'clock on the 11th October
I went with detectives Maroni and Westwood to the warehouse of
Messrs. Coulden and Son, 44, Gresham Street, where we saw the prisoner
France-I took him into custody, and he was conveyed with Cain to
the police station-from information we received from France and Cain,
Westwood, Maroni, and myself went to Shenstone's shop in the
Goswell Road on the same day, the 11th-she expressed a wish to go
to the back yard, and I went with her she went for a certain pur-
pose, and then washed her hands-she said "I am sorry I bought them,
but you know what it is when things like that are offered by a boy;
it is a great temptation, and you want to get them as cheap as you
can, I suppose it is only a leaden penalty,” which expression was
accompanied by her putting her hand under her apron and rattling
something like money-at that time one of the G Division Metro-
politan Police came into the shop-we had sent for him--we generally
make it a practice of doing so-Shenstone said “What does that police-
man want"-I said “You have denied buying any more skins-I
believe you have two dozen and nine more, and we are going to
search the place for them "-her son then came to her, and she pointed
to a shelf and said “You will find them there”-and there was &
nuntber produced from the shelf-Mrs. Shenstone and her son selected
two dozen and nine-she said she thought they were them, and pointed
to the lad Cain, and said she gave & shilling each for them, the same
as she did for the others she was then taken to the station and the
charge read over to her she said "I admit buying the five dozen
and nine of the boy, but those are mine," pointing to a bundle of ten
dozen.
Cross-examined. That bundle is here—what I have related was her
own statement without any questions from me there was a remand
in the case I was examined on the first occasion, and gave part of
my evidence, not the whole, because it was taken by the Court to
affect each prisoner-I gave evidence, and Mr. Vesey informed the
Court that he should have to recall me I gave evidence against Mrs.
Shenstone on the second occasion-she was in the dock on the first
occasion, and my giving evidence against her was, with Mr. Wontner's
concurrence, left till the next remand- I do not mean that I suppressed
or kept back evidence with Mr. Wontner's concurrence-I did not say
on the first occasion anything about Shenstone going into the back
premises, but I was prepared to say it-it was a very busy day at
Guildhall-Mr. Pratt opened the case, and I gave evidence, and left
the box without saying a word about this supposed conversation—I
said I had other evidence to give personally-I spoke of the appre-
hension of Cain, and what he said in answer to the charge; and I.
was then about to commence about Mrs. Shenstone, when Mr. Pratt
said to Mr. Martin there was evidence, but proposed to take the next
witness, and Mr. Martin, the Chief Clerk, concurred she was taken
into custody by Westwood.
Re-examined. The first evidence I gave was only in reference to
Cain and France the other two officers, Westwood and Maroni, gave
their evidence upon the first occasion-Mr. Pratt was the solicitor for the
prosecution at that time-he said in open Court before the Alderman that
Osborne would give further evidence with respect to Mrs. Shenstone, and
