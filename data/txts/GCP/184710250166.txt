Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. The John does not belong to you?
A. No, to Bell and Co.-a lighterman is frequently absent from the barre
and he would be absent on this occasion, because he could not get the craft
out--there would be no necessity to stop in the lighter when it was empty-
they have to wait for the tide to get out.
COURT. Q. Do they go on board each other's lighters ? A. Yes, talkine.
when they are waiting for the tide.
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. If Wells had gone to take
his tea or coffee, that would have been no neglect of his duty ? A. Nowe
leave barges perfectly safe in the dock.
Isaac Bradford. I gauged 100 hogsheads of rum on the day previous,
and on the day of delivery-amongst them were Nos. 9125, 9127, and
9157-I gauged them again on the 20th, and found from No. 9125, there was
about three pints, from No. 9127 a gallon and a half, and from 9157 about
a gallon and a half gone-there was a deficiency of three gallons and one
quart.
COURT. Q. Are you a judge of the strength ? A. I am-I tested the
strength both before and since-I measured the quantity in the bladders-in
one of them was one pint of spirit, which was 15 per cent, and 6-10ths under
proof-the bulk in the hogshead 9157, which I think that was taken out of
was 10 per cent. under proof-that in the bladder had lost 5 per cent.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Was this foreign rum? A. Yesit
was not arrack-arrack is an East India spirit- this was from Havannah and
Cuba.
WILLIAM LONG. I am a constable of the docks-Bradford pointed out
three hogsheads to me, Nos. 9125, 9127, and 9157-I noticed fresh spiles
had been put in them-I took Wells into custody, and told him to be careful
what he said, that I took him on suspicion of stealing two gallons of rum in
company with Gingle-he said, “I was not with him in the dock, nor was be
in my barge."
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Was not Wells examined
first as a witness against Gingle? A. Yes-other circumstances occurred,
and he was afterwards taken into custody-he said he knew nothing about it,
and was getting his tea.
(Wells received a good character.)
GINGLE-Guilty. Aged 55.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury
Confined Twelve Months.
WELLS-GUILTY. Aged 31.- Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor
and Jury.- Confined Three Months without hard Labour.
2463. THOMAS WHEATLEY was indicted for stealing 1 pair of steps,
value 108.; the goods of John Williams.
PATRICK JORDEN. I am in the service of Mr. John Williams, of Hamp
stead-road. I was employed in a building in New Oxford-street-on tae
4th of Oct. I was coming down the ladder, and saw the prisoner coming
out of the building with a pair of steps on his shoulder-1 followed him
to Museum-street-he put the steps by a public-house door, and went in--
came out again, and went round a fence and went into the public-house
second time I went in and gave him in charge.
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. He left the steps in
street ? A. Yes--I knew they were my master's-he went away from them,
came back, and went into the public-house-he was a little intoxicated.
