   BENJAMIN SCHOFIELD. On the morning of Tuesday, the 15th of September, I went to Pichott's house on a complaint of a trunk and box having been broken open - I saw Pichott who showed me a box at the entrance of the area, which he said had been left there full of articles on the Sunday, when he went out with his wife to spend the day, leaving the house quite safe - he said he likewise left a trunk full of articles belonging to Mr. Bellingham; I asked how he supposed they got in - he said by the area door he supposed, which had been left on the single lock; he showed me a shutter put too so as not to be seen, and likewise a key-hole, with some paper put in, in a door which he said he supposed they entered; it was not broken at all - he then took me up stairs to the parlour floor, and showed me a trunk, which he said he supposed had been taken up stairs, that it might not be so public as from the window below - both the boxes had been broken open as it appeared with an instrument with two prongs; I asked if any body had been to see him lately - he said no one, but there was a person living over the stable who might have access to the premises, and there was a door leading out of the stable - I went out, saw the coachman, and then went into the mews, and from what I heard there I returned, and wished Mrs. Bellingham to give charge of Pichott; she said she had known him so long she could not suspect him, and he went on appealing to God that he was no thief - I said, when he had done, "I must do my duty:" I then asked where he had been that day - he said to dine with a friend, and had returned in the evening; that he had left no one in the house, and had no strangers to visit him; on the following Thursday I got further information from the same parties, in consequence of which I suspected Butler - I got Mrs. Bellingham to the office, but in consequence of her good opinion of Pichott she would not take the oath; we went to Butler's, and saw the necklace round Mrs. Butler's neck - Mrs. Bellingham said it was hers; I told her to be very cautions, as I had seen a similar one round her neck before, and she would not give charge of her- Mrs. Butler said she would let Mrs. Bellingham go about the house with her, but not me, as I had no warrant; some words took place, and Mrs. Bellingham ran out of the house I staid some time, and then I went out; Mrs. Bellingham then said she would go to Pichott's; we went there, and found him and his wife - I told them about the necklace; Pichott said it was all false, it was not her necklace, he was sure -I then said I could do no more; I thought they were all bad characters together, and I left the house.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did not Pichott offer you every assistance throughout the business? A. Yes, he did, and the prosecutrix refused to give him in charge; I did not take him - he had time to run away if he chose; I did not see Butler on any of these occasions, but the prosecutrix would not take out a warrant - Mrs. Butler showed her her drawers.
 COURT. Q. Did you hear on the Tuesday that Mrs. Murray had said some person was about the house? A. She gave me information at first that a person had been lurking about there, but she only described his person; she afterwards told me she heard his name was Butler - when I went to Pichott's on the Friday, I said, "You know you sent your wife last night to Butler, to warn them of my coming" - he said he had, and he had a right to do it; he was a very respectable man.
   HENRY WILLIAM MORRISON. I am a serjeant of the Police. On Sunday, the 6th of December, Butler was taken on another charge; I searched his house, No. 80, Drummond-street - I found in a chest of drawers, in the back parlour, these five table napkins, a white lace veil, these pieces of lace, a smelling-bottle and work-box, and in the kitchen I found this large table-cloth; on Tuesday, the 8th, I went to No. 25, Chester-terrace, and saw Pichott moving his goods in a four-wheeled van - the man belonging to the van was coming up with a table on his shoulder; I asked Pichott if Butler was there on the Thursday- he said No, but he was there on the Sunday: I said I was an officer, and wanted to search the house - he said I was welcome to do so; the van was full of boxes, which were taken to the watch-house, and Pichott also - he said all the boxes were his property; when we got to the watch-house, I got a light, and said, "I am going to search the boxes, which are yours?" he said, "The whole of them"- Mrs. Pichott was present at the time; I found this sheet and these table-napkins in the boxes - this centre-bit, these picklock-keys, and these tools, I found at Butler's.
 JURY. Q. Was Butler in custody on the Sunday? A. Yes, on another charge - I found this key in one of Pichott's boxes, which has been altered, - I call it a skeleton-key; it did not open the prosecutor's box.
