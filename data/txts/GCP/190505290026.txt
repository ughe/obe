The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that on April 18t he got home

of ale and took it back to the Watsons' in a bottle, arriving about 8 p.m.; that
Mr. Watson and his wife, Mr. and Mrs. Curl, and himself all had some of
the beer, that he and Watson and Curl then went to the Pear Tree and had a
drink ; that they returned to Watsons' about 8.45 with some more beer; that
he went to sleep and did not remember any more till he had some supper
given to him by Mrs. Watson ; that the others left the room and that he did
no remember any more till he woke up in the Watsons' room about 3.30 a.m.;
that he did not go outside the house from the time he returned from the Pear
Tree till Mrs. Watson gave him some supper, then she told him that a man
had been killed in Garvan Road named Tomlin and that she had heard tha!
he had done it; that he said, "I have been with Bill all night,” and offered to
go to the station, but that she said, “Don't go, as they will look you up for being
drunk"; that when he had had supper he must have gone to sleep again, that
when he woke up a: 3 a.m. he walked home and was then arrested.
The Court considered that the evidence was not sufficiently dear, and the
Jury returned a verdict of Not Guilty.
