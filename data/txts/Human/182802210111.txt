Cross-examined. Q. Did he not look round as if to look for the other man? A. He did not say any thing to me - he might look round.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 669.  MARY ELLCOCK was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of February , 1 sheet, value 3s., and 1 blanket, value 4s, the goods of Maria Davenport.
 MARIA DAVENPORT. I am  single, and live in Turnmill-street. On the 2nd of February, I let a ready-furnished lodging to the prisoner - she ran away at eight o'clock in the morning on the 5th of February; the articles stated were let to her with the lodgings - she passed as a married woman, but at the watch-house she said she was not married - she gave me no notice: her husband came down the morning she left, and told me his wife had robbed me of a blanket - I went and looked, and missed the sheet and blanket likewise; he went after her; she came to me on the Friday evening following, and said she had pawned them, and would redeem them, and bring them back - I said, I did not wish to hurt her; I believed then that she was married, but when at the watch-house she said she was not married, and could not take the man's name - she put down Mary Ann Smith there.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am an officer. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house - I found the duplicates on her, and she told me where they were pawned.
   THOMAS REDPATH. I am a pawnbroker. I have a blanket pawned by the prisoner.
   HENRY PARKER. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet pawned by the prisoner on the 8th of February.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. My husband and I both took the room on the Friday; I had 18s. to pay, and I pawned those things, meaning to redeem them; I had no intention of stealing them - my husband is, I believe, living there still; I did not return for fear of being ill used.
     NOT GUILTY.
 670.  MARY ELLCOCK was again indicted for stealing on the 9th of February , 1 tea-pot, value 3s., the goods of Mary Ann Humbley.
 MARY ANN HUMBLEY. I lodge in  Turnmill-street. I lost my tea-pot, on the 9th of February, out of the back kitchen - the prisoner lodged in the house with her husband; I believe I missed my tea-pot on the Sunday after the prisoner was taken into custody - I saw it at Hatton-garden.
   THOMAS REDPATH. I am a pawnbroker. I have a tea-pot, pawned on the 7th of February by the prisoner for 2s. 6d.
 Prisoner's Defence. The tea-pot was on the kitchen chimney - I took it with an intention of getting the sheet out, but I was afraid to go home, and was obliged to break into the money.
     NOT GUILTY.

 671.  HENRY GIBBS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of February , 8 lbs, weight of tea, value 2l.; 3 lbs. weight of coffee, value 5s., and 9 lbs. weight of sugar, value 7s., the goods of Edward Davis.
   JAMES JONES. I am a porter in the employ of Edward Davis, a grocer, of Philpot-lane. On the 8th of February, I was sent out, about three o'clock, with the horse and cart, and some tea and coffee in it - about half past four o'clock, I was in Verulam-buildings, Gray's-inn-lane; I left my horse and cart at the door of No. 4, while I went in to take some parcels - I went over to the Talbot public-house to get change; when I came back, the prisoner was coming up to the cart, with a man on each side of him, with some parcels of tea, sugar, and coffee - there were names on the parcels; I believe one was Stuckey, and one Bean - I am sure they were safe in my cart when I left it.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long were you away? A. Five or six minutes. I did not stay at the Talbot - I had not seen the prisoner near the cart.
   THOMAS DAVIES. I am in the employ of the prosecutor. I sent out the porter with these parcels; I did not write on them, but I saw that they were written on.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know the writing on them? A. No. I have compared these parcels with the book, and the bills of parcels, but they are not here.
 COURT. Q. Were they parcels like these? A. Yes; I see no difference.
   JOHN JONES. I am a painter. I was at work in Verulam-buildings; I saw the porter come there, and leave the cart - I directed him to go to the Talbot for change; I saw the prisoner close to the shaft of the cart, and another man on the shaft; be took the parcels out and gave them to the prisoner, who ran away with them; I pursued and stopped him with them, in the gateway, leading to Gray's Inn - I had not lost sight of him: I took him back to the cart, and gave the parcels to the carman.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it a covered cart? A. Yes; the man on the shaft delivered the parcels to the prisoner. I cannot say whether they came together; the prisoner went away at a swift walk, at a brisk pace; he said he was employed by the man who belonged to the cart - he came back without resistance: he carried them in his arms uncovered.
 COURT. Q. Did he say where he was directed to carry them? A. No. I took him within ten minutes.
   WILLIAM WEALAND. I am a painter. I was with Jones and went in pursuit of the prisoner; we took him with three or four parcels on him, and brought him back to Verulam-buildings.
   WILLIAM BROWN EDWARDS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and bring the parcels.
 Prisoner's Defence. The witness states that I was at the side of the cart, which I was not - the man in the cart dropped one parcel, and I took it up; he said "Will you have the goodness to take these parcels to the lodge in Gray's Inn." I took them, and he went on before me; he ran on, and then I went quicker, till the witnesses came; I said "If you ran through the lodge, you will see the man;" and they took me back to the cart.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 39. Confined Six Months.
