him--this is the transcript that I made-there was another examination on
14th October, and another on 13th May-I have compared the transcript
with the shorthand notes, and it is correct-(The examination of 29th Sep-

Cross-examined. Q. Were there three separate examinations . The

WOLTAM STROUD, In August, 1869, in consequence of something I
beard from a man named Warlow, I went to Pearso when I saw him he
said "I want you to go with me to see Mr. Levi about the furnishing of a
pair of villas at Twickenham," and we went together--the prisoner said
* The contract must be in your name, as I am a bankrupt "-I was to
moeive my wages daily, as a working man-I am & carpenter I was not
present at the discussion between Levi and Pearse—Mr. Pearse went in and
saw Levi, and left me in the hall-I signed the contract, and I worked
there several months--I had worked for Pearse before he was a bankrupt-
I was paid while I was at work—the money was brought to me by Pearse
---501. was given to me by Levi to pay wages, and I paid the men with it
I handed it to Pearse; he took it from me--I signed my name at the back
of that cheque--I handed it to Pearse when I got it.
Cross-examined. l. Was that contract finished when you left 4. I
don't think it was-Mr. Pearse had the 501., but he brought me the money
the next day to pay the wages-Pearse did not get the money himself-I
paid the wages most times I think by the contract Levi was to pay 400
odd pounds--I did not see the contract after I signed it I can't say how
much I paid in wages-I paid every week—there were sometimes eight men,
sometimes twelve at work-it would be 141. or 15l. a week-since Pearse
has been bankrupt he has been working as foreman on the works, not as
master-I believe he has been making his living like that ever since he has
been a bankrupt.
st
MR. GIFFARD. Q. Do you mean that you remember paying 501 to work-
men after changing the cheque? A. Not the whole- I might have paid
201.--I paid a portion of my own wages out of the money I received-I have
no account of what I paid.

JOSEPH Frost. (Not examined in chief.)
Cross-examined. Q. Were you employed as the defendant's carter A.
Yes-I know Warlow, his foremen--they were generally together I used
to get orders from one or the other, I went to Mr. Pearson's the iron-
monger's—I could not say who told me to go there; they were both together,
one of them told me to go--I put the things in the stable according to
their orders-Mr. Pearse said “When you come back put the things in the
stable, you will find the key in the house, and lock the door, and put the
key back again"-Warlow and Pearse were together when he told me
that I did according to his orders—I locked the door and gave the key to
the servant--she gave it to me, and I gave it to her again.
MR. GIFFARD. Q. Whose servant ! A. Mr. Pearse's, at his house in Lan-
caster Road.
