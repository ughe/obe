PAILIP GEORGE. I keep an eating-house in Praed-street. On the 7th of
Ang. Barker came to me with a crown-I changed it for her-I put it in my
pocket-I ultimately gave it to Mrs. Griffith-after Barker had come and got
the change, the prisoner and another man came the prisoner had a 13.
worth of potatoes and paid for them with a 12. the other man gave me a
55. piece in payment for a mutton-bone-it was perhaps half an hour after
Barker had been gone-I sent the crown piece that Barker gave me by a
girl to get change, the girl brought it back I gave the crown-piece the other
man gave me back again to him.
ELLEN HENRY, I received a crown from Mr. George to get change-I
went to the baker, he rang it, and said it was bad--I went to the beer-shop
and they would not change it-I took it back to Mr. George.
GEORGE RANDALL. I keep a shop in Great Carlile-street. On the 11th
of Aug. the prisoner came to my house and asked if I sold cigars 2–1 said,
yes-1 opened the box, he took a penny cigar out, and gave me a shilling -
gave him change, he went away-I put the shilling in a cup in the till where
there was no other money-in about ten minutes I looked at it again1
found it in the cup as I left it, and I found it was bad-I marked it another
man had been in the interval between my taking this shilling of the prisoner
and my finding it was bad, and I had taken another bad shilling I kept
them apart from other money-I gave them both to the officer.
THOMAS HARDWICK (police-sergeant D 7 I took the prisoner at a beer-
shop in Lisson-grove-I told him the charge, he said he was willing to go
with me--I received this shilling from Mr. Randall, and this half-crown from
Mr. Garratt.

JOSEPH NIFTON (police-sergeant D. 17.) I saw the prisoner in company
with another man on the 7th of Aug. crossing Praed-street-they went in
the direction of Mr. Griffith's-I produce a crown-piece which I got from Mr.
Griffith about a quarter before two o'clock that day.
Mr. John FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint-this crown,
half-crown, and shilling are all counterfeit.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Confined One Year. CONTES

Mary Lawson. I am the wife of John Lawson, of Earl-street, Mary-
lebone. On Monday the 10th of Aug., about nine o'clock at night, the prisoner
came for half an ounce of tobacco, which came to 14d.-be paid me with a
shilling-I gave him 103d, change-I put the shilling in the till, there was no
other shilling there I noticed that it was a lion shilling he went away,
and in ten minutes another person came and had Id. worth of tobacco-he
gave me a shilling, I put that in the same till-in a quarter of an hour two
persons came in for a 1d. worth of cheese, they paid me with another shilling
put that into the till soon afterwards I looked in the till I found all the
three shillings were bad- the first was a lion shilling, the other two were
Victoria's I gave them to the officer.
