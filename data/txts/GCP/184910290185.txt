WILLIAM Hunt. I reside on Dulwich-common, about fifty yards from
Dulwich-wood. On Wednesday night, 19th Sept., about a quarter to ten,
I heard a noise in the road-my wife opened the door I went and saw a
female there, who turned out to be the prosecutrix-her face and hands wera
completely covered with blood, and her bair hanging all down the sides of
her face and neck-my children cried with alarm at the spectacle she pre-
sented, and, from some impression I had at the time, I put her outside-
I afterwards went out with a light, and found her in the road-she had her
dress clenched up in ber hand, and tucked in her throat--in consequence of a
complaint she made to me, I took her to Mr. Ray, a surgeon in the neigh-
bourhood-after I had done that, in consequence of what she said, I went
into the wood with Wood the constable-he walked before me and picked up
a bonnet, a fur Victorine, a fur cape, a black shawl, two white pocket-
handkerchiefs, the white bandle of a razor, and a bottle of blue ink--they
were in the wood, about 360 yards from the stile where you enter the wood
-I observed finger-marks of blood on the stile.
John Wood (policeman P 131). On the night of 19th Sept., I recom-
panied Hunt to Dulwich-wood-I went over the stile, and at some distance
from it I found a fur Victorine, a pocket-handkerchief, a small bottle of blue ink,
a green drawn silk bonnet, a black erape shawl, and a razor bandle the blade
was picked up by Jefferys about three feet off, among some leaves and grass
near the footpath-I saw him pick it up-it was about three feet from where
we found the other things—they were all together saturated with blood, and
there was a great quantity of blood in the footpath-it was not above a foot
wide—there was a great quantity of blood on the bashes and oak leaves.
JOHN JEFFERYS (policeman, P 303). I accompanied last witness and Hant
w' Dulwich-wood, on 19th Sept., between twelve and one o'clock-I found
the razor produced.
