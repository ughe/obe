1893. SARAH HANCOCK was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , 1 gown, value 14s., the goods of Elizabeth Williams.
 ELIZABETH WILLIAMS. I am  single. I lost my gown, on the 12th of July, from a back parlour, in Bowling-street, Clerkenwell; it was taken out at the back window, which I had left a little way open - I had seen it at five o'clock that evening, when I went out: I returned in half an hour- the prisoner lived next door.
   RICHARD MOATE. I am a pawnbroker. I have a gown pawned with me on the 12th of July; I am not quite certain of the prisoner's person, but I believe it was by her - I gave this duplicate for it.
   MARY LEWIS. I keep a little clothes-shop. On the 12th of July, the prisoner brought this duplicate to my house, and asked me to take care of it, as she was going to meet a gentleman; I said I would - there weeks after, the officer came to me, and I gave it to him.
   JAMES TERRY. I am an officer. I produce the duplicate which I got from this witness.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she had taken the gown and pawned it to pay her rent, but intended to redeem it; that the prosecutrix was living in a state of adultery, and had perjured herself by giving the name of Clark at the office.
     GUILTY. Aged 44. Transported for Seven Years.
 1894.  HANNAH HAGGERTY and MARY ANN SHAY, were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of August , 4 handkerchiefs, value 4s. the goods of Joseph Crane.
   HENRY CARPENTER. I am assistant to Mr. Joseph Crane, draper, of Ratcliff. The two prisoners came to the shop together on the evening of the 22d of August - Haggerty bought half a silk handkerchief for 7d.; they took the handkerchiefs off the line at the door, and brought them in to me; there were three parcels - I missed one from off the counter; I looked over the counter and they were on the floor - Shay took them up; I cut off the handkerchief for Haggerty, and then I missed some more - Shay was then gone; I asked Haggerty where she was gone - she said she did not know; I said I would detain her, but finding she had a bundle of more value than the handkerchiefs, I said if she would leave that, and bring Shay back, I would let her have it; she agreed to do so, and she went to the door, went across the road and joined the other - I followed and brought them both back - I found on Shay a bonnet in a little blue hat; I took that out, and in the crown of the bonnet were the handkerchiefs I had lost; I had asked Shay before if she had seen the handkerchiefs, and she said with an oath, she had not - nothing was found on Haggerty.
   JAMES YOUNG. I was sent for, and took the two prisoners - Shay said it was the first time she had done anything of the kind.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 SHAY -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Confined One Month.
 HAGGERTY -    NOT GUILTY.
 1895.  EDWARD GEAVES was indicted for stealing, on the 30th July , 1 1/2 bushel of oats, beans and chaff, mixed, value 6s. the goods of John Kent.
   JOHN KENT. I am the son of John Kent; he keeps a livery-stable, in Southampton-street; the prisoner used to supply us with food for horses; on the 30th of July he came with some tares about one o'clock, to another yard we have in Gloucester-street; I saw him going away with a quantity of corn in a sack; he did not deal in corn, but I believe carried it for his horse; I went to call a man, and when I returned, he was out of sight - the sack of corn was then missing; I afterwards saw a sack with a mixture of chaff, corn and beans, it corresponded with a mixture we had at Gloucester-street yard, but I could not positively swear to it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Is there not a way throught your premises? A. Yes; a person may go through - the prisoner has occasionally taken tares through there; I did not make any observations on the sack - I think it was his own; I believe he buys a quanlity of tares and sells them.
   WILLIAM KIRK. I am a coachman. I was in Mr. Kent's employ; he called to me and I went after the prisoner, but did not see him; I went into Southampton-street, and there his cart stood and his mate with it; I looked, and saw the prisoner at the corner of King-street beckoning his mate to come to him; I called him - he came and asked what I wanted - I said master wanted to speak to him; he said "I am going to Hatton-garden, and can't come, what can be want with me;" Mr. Kent then came running up the road and accused him of taking the corn - he said he had not and knew nothing about it.
   RICHARD PURSSORD. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Theobald's-road. On the 31st of July, I was in the shop, some persons came and asked me to let them leave a sack under my window, and they would call in ten minutes - I cannot say who it was.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1896.  JOHN GIRDLER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 1 carpet, value 40s., and 1 chest of drawers, value 40s., the goods of James Strachan.
 JAMES STRACHAN. I am a  cabinet-maker, and live in Gerrard-street, Soho. I employed the prisoner, on the 1st of August, to take a pair of drawers to Mr. Jackson, in Bedford-street, and he never took them: I never saw him again till I caught him about a fortnight afterwards asleep on the flap of a wine cellar in Hart-street, Covent-garden; he said he had put them down at a public-house door, and went to have a pint of beer, and when he came out they were gone - Mr. Jackson is not here, nor any one from him, but I know they were not delivered, because they sent for them.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
