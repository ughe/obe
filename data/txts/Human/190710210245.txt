One of them was rather a rude young lady—the one exa  mined here the other day. It was early in the morning I paid my visit. I went back to Witham, and next morning received letter 91 signed "George Martin" dated April 19, 1906. Contract 92 was en closed, showing that the "Financial Guardian" Share Department bought from me under-noted shares to close Canadian Pacific £1,000 stock 177 7/8 cum. bonus, I having bought at 175 1/2. I got no cheque or remittance. I then wrote 93 and got answer 94. Letter 93 pointed out that the papers said 183 1/2. I got my price from the "Daily Telegraph." On April 21 I got a circular from the "Finan cial Guardian" about Rio Tintos. I sent cheque No. 96 for £10 for 10 Rio Tintos, which was paid by my bank. I got an acknowledg ment and a contract for the shares at 65 7/8. I had had nothing on ac count of Canadian Pacific's and wrote letter 99, and got letter 100. The next pay day would be somewhere about May 12. I went up to 2, Tokenhouse Buildings and asked for Martin, and Harris said he was not in town. I told him I wanted a settlement of my account. Harris said I might expect a settlement for certain the next pay day. He said they had been hit very hard and had lost about £2,000 and same of their clients were pressing them hardly. I waited till July 6, when I wrote letter 101, to which I got no reply. I waited again a long time and then called at the office, about three times altogether, and saw Harris. He said something to the same effect as before. Then I wrote letter 102 on October 27, and, not getting any reply; I gave my solicitor instructions in the matter. I never had any part of the money. My solicitor took proceedings on my behalf. When I parted with my money, having read she circular, I thought the "Financial Guardian" was a paper; I did not know they were bucket-shop people. From first to last I was not able to see Mr. George Martin.

I did not expect to get the scrip, but I would much rather have taken up the shares than lose too money. I was prepared to find the value of the shares. I dis  puted the price at the time of closing the shares, but I was claiming the difference shown upon the contracts. The amount of the profit was put down at £118 15s. That was done by my solicitors. I was not angry when I did not get any satisfactory reply in April, but I could not think they were treating me fairly. I was a double fool to invest with them again. I did not know whether Rio Tintos have gone steadily up or not. It was a bad investment for me. I have not got the money. If they had given me £40 I would have been de lighted, but they would not pay anything, and they never have paid anything. FREDERICK LEWIS, managing clerk to W. B.
