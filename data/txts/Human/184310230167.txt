MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD MOORE. I am beadle of Covent-garden market. On the after noon of the 2nd of Oct. I was employed watching Green, who was servant to the prosecutor, who keeps a shop in the market—I saw Green empty some damsons out of a bushel basket, at the prosecutor's stand, into a smaller sieve which laid on the ground—Weaver was close by—Green left the basket for some minutes, between Weaver's brother's stand and the prosecutor's—Weaver was near him at the time, and could see what was done—I made some inquiries of the prosecutor's daughter, then went back to the stall, and the basket was gone.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILKINS . Q.  Weaver is a porter employed in the market? A.  Yes.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Weaver's brother has a stand next to the prosecutor's? A.  Yes—he was between the two stands—there were no damsons on Weaver's stand—I have not told any one that they must not come up to give the prisoners a character, nor said, that if any money was sub scribed towards the prisoner's defence, those persons should never be em ployed in the market again—Green lives in Duke's-court, Bow-street. JOSEPH BAXTER. I am a porter in Covent-garden market, and live in Great Wild-street. On the afternoon of the 2nd of Oct. I watched Green by desire of Moore—he was standing at his master's stand—he turned, out, some damsons, and put them down, partly in his master's stand and partly in Wea ver's brother's stand—he then spoke to Weaver—they talked together two or three minutes—after that Green went back to his work—Weaver followed him, and took the same damsons on his head to Duke's-court—he took the basket off his head, and put it into the passage, inside the door of No. 14—he came out with the empty basket—I saw Green taken, and heard Johnson ask him if he had authorized Weaver to take any damsons—he said no—he asked if he had authorized any one else—he made no reply—Johnson asked Weaver what became of those damsons he had taken up Bow-street—he said he had taken them to Green's house, by Green's direction—(Green lives at No. 16, and Wearer lives at No. 14)—I then went to No. 16, Duke's court, with Weaver—I heard him ask Green's wife where those damsons were that he took there a little while ago for John—she denied all knowledge of them—I am positive Weaver took them to No. 14—I stood in the court till he came out with the empty basket.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILKINS . Q.  How long was Weaver in the house before he came out? A.  About half an hour—it was a foreign basket—there is an enormous quantity of foreign fruit sold in the market—I was walking up and down, while I was waiting there—Weaver took the basket a little before three o'clock in the afternoon—there were a great many people about—Weaver's brother has a stand outside his shop, and the pro secutor has the same—Green was at his own work at his master's stand, not inside the shop—Green gave no directions to Weaver, to my knowledge—Weaver was at the end of Russel-street when Green went to speak to him in the first instance.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Do not you know that Weaver keeps a greengrocer's shop? A.  Yes—I was not above three doors from him when he went into the house—when Green took the basket, Weaver's stood was shut—there were other baskets about there.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When you were in this court, were you in a situa tion to see whether Weaver came out of No. 14 to go to No. 16? A.  Yes, I can positively say he never came out of No. 14 till he came out with the empty basket, and he then came out of the court—I have no doubt it was the same basket.
