grey whiskers, it appeared as if he had been a traveller, that he had a
weather-beaten countenance--if I recollect right, he said he appeared to be
about sixty years of age, and had grey hair-- he said he did not know him
-he said he supposed, from what he stated, that he had come from Mr.
March, and he did not know whether it was Mr. March or not-about
eight o'clock that night I was at the Swan-with-two-Necks, making in-
quiry about the Falmouth coach, and while there, the prisoner and his
father came in-he inquired about the Falmouth mail, and said he was
going by the Falmouth mail-I made inquiry, and found the mail had been
gone about a quarter of an hour before they came in told the prisoner I
thought if he had a wish to go, he might overtake the mail at the South-
ampton rail-road I went with them in a coach, but the mail had been
gone about ten minutes I think--the mail goes by the rail-road I do not
think he had stated to me before, any intention of going to Palmouth-
have no recollection of it-he called at two or three coach-offiees coming
back, and said he should go next morning as we returned, Ellis Caspar
left the coach--I am not quite positive where he left it I thought it was
at Charing-cross, but it was between Charing-cross and St. Paul's - the
prisoner said there would be hand-bills out next day, and 500L reward
offered-he said Mr. Allen, and Mr. De Mole were to have them printed
--I expressed a wish to see Mr. De Mole before they were printed, and
the prisoner requested his father to go immediately to prevent their being
printed-I had been to the St. Paul's Coffee-house in the morning, and
found three persons were putting up there from the Palmouth mail that day,
and I went to see them I called there again in the evening with the
prisoner, after his father left-I gave a description of the person as the
prisoner had given me, grey hair, and whiskers, a person shorter than
him, and about sixty years of age-he never contradicted me, or said that
was not the person-there was a description given then, that such a per
son had been there-one of the three who had come by the mail
think the waiter gave me that description, and the prisoner said that
appeared the sort of description of the person that person was described
as about sixty years of age, with grey hair and whiskers-the prisoner
also said he thought I could nat do better than to pursue the inquiry
there, and stick to the person at that house he also told mo the same
thing, two days afterwards.

Q. How long afterwards did you trace the cab to New-street? 14. The
first cab I traced, when the prisoner was with me, in the Minories
afterwards traced a cab to the neighbourhood of New-street, and went to a
house in New-street, which had no tenant, and where & plate had been
taken off the door-I have since ascertained that to be Moss's house-
found a quantity of nails in the grate, and some under the grate, and some
in the dust-hole.
