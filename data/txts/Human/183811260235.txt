I am clerk to Biddulph and Co., of Carmarthen. I received this bill, to the best of my recollection, from the managing partner—I afterwards saw the prisoner about it—perhaps the day or day after it was due and dishonoured—I showed him the bill, and told him it was dis honoured—I do not recollect what he said to it—it is a single bill, and among the many thousands passing through my hands, it is not likely I should re collect—I do not know that he said any thing when I told him it was dis honoured—I had known him eight or ten years—the bill was due on the 17th of September, 1837—it bears his indorsement—to the best of my belief it is his own hand-writing—I have seen him write frequently, and believe it to be his—he has indorsed it to Messrs. Biddulph—I have heard it pronounced a forgery—I made a demand, both on the drawer and ac ceptor, when it became due—we gave notice of the bill being dishonoured, which the prosecutor never attended to—he called some few months after wards, and requested I would show him the bill, which I did—I spoke to the prisoner about it, and he told me it was not a forgery—I told him the prosecutor had said the bill was a forgery—he replied, there was a con spiracy, he believed, got up against him, but it was no forgery—that there was an attempt to transport him, or something of that sort—I did not call his attention to the signature of the acceptor, and he said nothing about the acceptance—I do not know who brought the bill to us—it was passed to the credit of the prisoner's account—the prisoner, I believe, did not bring it to me. MORGAN RICE JAMES. I am an attorney, living at Haverford-west. Last September twelvemonth I went to Carmarthen prison, to see the pri soner—I asked him if there was not a bill at Biddulph's bank, purporting to be signed by Mr. James—he said there was—I asked him whether there were any other bills—he said there were—he told me there was a bill of 200 l . at Messrs. Morris, the banker's, and one of 100 l . at Mr. William Phillips's, and another, I think, for 100 l . at Mr. Posthumous Phillips's—I asked him whether he had any authority from Mr. James to sign those bills'—he said no, he had not—I saw him twice or three times the same day—I recom mended him to make out his accounts, and get the business settled, as it was a serious case, and he promised me he would—I told him I was sent to him from Haverford-west, to inquire what bills he had forged, and he then told me the bills he had forged, as I have already mentioned—I do not know whether that was the way it occurred—I was with him some time, and on friendly terms with him—I did not know I should have to state what transpired—I have related the substance of what passed—I do not know whose writing the acceptance to this bill is—I have seen the prose cutor write repeatedly—I do not believe it to be his writing—I should say it is but a humble imitation of it.
