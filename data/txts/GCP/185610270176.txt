SALOMONS, Mayor.
BENNETT LEWTY. I am the son of the last witness. On Sunday, 19th
Oct., in consequence of the note being returned, I went to the address on
the note, to No. 11, Acorn-street, between 7 and 8 o'clock in the evening
the prisoner was in bed-I stood outside for a minute or two, and the pri
soner came out I asked him to walk to our house with ine-he had
no jacket or boots on-he went back and put them on, and came out with
them-he came to our bar parlour—the note laid on the table in such
position that he could see it-I told him that the note that my sister had
changed for him had come back from the bankers, and they believed
it was forged-he said, "Is it? I was not aware of it ; I suppose I must
get you the money "-I asked him where he got it from-he said, “I don't
think I am bound to tell you"- I told him he had better, or it would be
a very bad job for us both, and cause a great deal of trouble-he said, “I
will tell you then ; I found two of them in a brass-bound porte monnaie, in
the Borough, and I gave the other to my landlord "-I said, "Did yout_I
then said I would go to the Bank in the morning, and if it was a forgery I
would come down to his house-he said, “Very well "-I went to his house
the next day, about half-past 2 o'clock-I had then been to the Bank, and
found the note was forged-when I got to the house the door was shut-1
did not see the prisoner-I saw the lodger-I believe the prisoner has s
wife; I did not see her-I went to the Peckham police station, and gave
information to sergeant Bond-on Thursday, 16th Oct., I went with ser-
geant Bond to No. 9, York-terrace, Peckham, and found the prisoner there
with two children.
Cross-examined. Q. In what part of Camberwell is Acorn-street A.
About a quarter of a mile from the Green-the prisoner did not tell me
that he picked them up on 19th Sept.-he said that he waited for some time
to see if they would be advertised for, and that he took them to some
tradesman, whose name he mentioned, to see if they were good- I cannot
recollect that he told me that there was a shilling and a sixpence in the
porte monnaie besides the notes—when I went to his house, I did not tell
him what I wanted him for, till he came to our bar parlour, and he walked
in with me, and looked at the note—he did not say that he must go in the
country to get the money-he said he would try and get it-he said after
wards that he had been to a friend of his in the country to try to get it.
. Did he and his wife both tell you that they had been trying to raise
the 5l., and if you had not given them into custody at that time, they should
have raised the money? A. I have no recollection of his saying that-he
told me that he had been to a friend, who had promised to advance it for
him, and he should have raised it if I had not taken him he said that he
had been in the country to his father.
John Bond (police sergeant, S 26). I went with the last witness to Acorn.
street, on Monday, 13th Oct., I went there on several subsequent com
sions-I did not find the prisoner on any occasion on 16th, I went
with Mr. Lewty to No. 9, York-terrace, and took the prisoner- I searched
him at the station, but I found nothing on him from Acorn-street to No
9, York-terrace, is about a mile.
JOSEPH BUMSTEAD. I am an inspector of notes to the Bank of England.

Cross-examined. Q. Are they very good imitations! A. Yes, they are
