Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
1550. HENRY SMYRKE was indicted for feloniously breaking and
entering the dwelling-house of Charles Clarke and another, at St. Mary,
Newington, and stealing therein 2 shawls, value 31. 138. ; 1 half-shawl, 17.;
24 handkerchiefs, 31. ; 8 neckkerchiefs, 1l. 108.; 3 scarfs, 18s.; and 637
yards of silk, 602. 78.; their property.
JAMES WICKENS. I am carman to Chaplin and Horne, and live in Corn-
bury-street, Old Kent-road. On the 3rd of May, about a quarter past six
o'clock in the morning, I saw two men walking backwards and forwards in
the Dover-road, close to Mr. Clarke's shop-one of them took off his top coat,
hung it before his legs, and close by the area grating-the other one came
behind him, lifted up the grating, and somebody from below put out a large
sack--the one who lifted it up put it on his shoulder, and walked towards
Napier-street--the prisoner was not one of those men-he was about fifteen
yards from the place with a cab—he saw the men coming with the bag, opened
the cab-door, and the person who had it put it into the cab-I informed the
police-I had seen the cab standing there for about five minutes-the prisoner
could see what they were doing, if he was looking
Prisoner. You stated before the Magistrate that I was half-way down
Napier-street. 4. I said your cab was a little way down the street-I do
not know the length of Napier-street- I believe you were fifteen yards off
Napier-street-I was about 11 yards from the shop, and you were about two
yards down the street.
WILLIAM RANDALL policeman.) On the morning of the 15th of April I
received information, went towards Napier-street, and saw a cab which the
