I do not call her by that name—her name is Elizabeth—at the office she booked her name as "Betsy"—that is what I call her—I do not deny but I have made her one of the family, and she had a good situation—I certainly have kissed her when the boy has asked me—the boy is only two years old—I went to the prisoner's father while she was locked up—I did not tell him that as soon as she got out of this I would take her back again—I swear that—never any thing of the sort—I did not tell him I had been kissing her—there never was any unpleasant feeling at home about this—the pri  soner never complained of my conduct to her—she always behaved as a good girl till this happened—I never endeavoured to thrust my hand up her petticoats—what I have done I am not ashamed to do before any body, I have done nothing to the girl though I am accused of it, and my character injured—it is merely the town talk since she has been in custody—I never heard it before.
   Q.  Did you ever ask her for a lock of her hair? A.  Never, no more than a lock of my wife's hair given me before I was married, was set in a brooch—it came home from being repaired—I asked Betsy to look at it—she said, "Dear me, it looks a great deal better than it did, is that your mistress's hair?"—I said, "Yes, Betsy, you have got a good looking head of hair; if I was a young man, not married, I should like a lock of it in a brooch"—she turned round, and said, "Master, I should not like to give my hair away without it was to somebody I respected"—I said, "Oh, Betsy, I am a married man."
   Q.  Did she call out, and tell your wife of it? A.  Oh no, it was merely a joke—she never gave notice to leave—I never heard that she had done so—I never heard it was said so—she did not bring a sovereign to me which she had found—it was laying on the table about half-past seven one morning, I was serving customers—my wife was in bed—we take it by turns to get up in the morning—my man and me are always up first—I did not know the sovereign was there—she did not bring it to me—she said, "Master, here's a sovereign"—I said, "Oh, I suppose my wife left it there last night?"—I have a tailors' club held there, and I suppose she gave change late at night—this was about three or four, two months ago.
   Q.  Did you ever tell her not to go to the house of your wife's mother? A.  She would not allow her to come—I told her she was not allowed to go—I never told her not to go—I had quarrelled with my mother-in-law—my mistress said to my mother-in-law, who went to live opposite us, "I will send Elizabeth over for the boy," and my mother-in-law said, "If Elizabeth comes, I will slap the door in her face"—I told her my mo ther-in-law would not allow her to come over to fetch the boy, and would slap the door in her face—I did not tell her they were jealous of her—it was about three weeks or a month ago that I talked to her about being bar-maid—I gave her into custody yesterday fortnight.
   Q.  Did she refuse to take the bar-maid's place? A.  I did not put the question to her—all I said, was, "I must have a bar-maid," and said to my wife, "We may as well learn Elizabeth the business, and have some body to do her work"—I said to her, "If you like to take this place, I shall advance your wages"—she did not refuse it—she said she should not dislike it.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Had you any idea that she was robbing you at that time? A.  None at all.
