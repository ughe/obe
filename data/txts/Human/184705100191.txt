1291. JOSEPH BARTON and THOMAS GARLICK were indicted for stealing 7lbs. weight of mutton, value 6 s  .; the goods of Frederick Reeve . JOHN SAFFREY. I am in the service of Frederick Reeve, a butcher, at Greenwich—on the evening of the 23rd of April, about nine o'clock—I concealed myself in a garden opposite the shop, and saw the two prisoners walk past the fence on the opposite side to our shop—they walked up the hill and down again—then separated, and Garlick went down a turning—Barton went across to our shop, unhooked a leg of mutton, put it under his coat, and walked away—I walked out of the gate and took him—he denied that he had it—I took it from him—I do not know where Garlick was then.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  How long had you been watch ing? A.  An hour and a-half—it was quite dark.
   JURY . Q.  There was a light in the shop? A.  Yes, that enabled me to see what took place. JOHN SWAINE( police-constable R  325.) I was on duty, and saw the two prisoner together at the top of Royal-hill—I went down my beat, and in about a quarter of an hour I was called to take Barton, who was in custody of the witness, and the meat.
   BARTON  — GUILTY. Aged 17.— Recommended to mercy. Confined Three Days .
   GARLICK  — NOT GUILTY.
 1292. ELIZABETH EADE was indicted for stealing 4 shirts, value 12 s  .; the goods of James Mottram . ELIZABETH MOTTRAM. I am the wife of James Mottram—we live at Woolwich—I take in washing—on the evening of the 13th of April some Shirts and other things hung out to dry, and I missed four shirts, which are now here. WILLIAM CANT. I am shopman to a pawnbroker—the prisoner offered one of these shirts to me, and I stopped her with it. GEORGE BARTON. I am pawnbroker—I produce a shirt, which was pawned by the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  I know nothing about them—they were given me to pawn. GUILTY.* Aged 52.— Confined One Year .
