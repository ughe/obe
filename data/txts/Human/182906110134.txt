1307. ANN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of January , 2 sheets, value 8s.; 2 pillows, value 8s.; 1 blanket, value 4s.; 1 window-curtain, value 3s., and 1 carpet, value 10s., the goods of Christopher Green.
   WILLIAM ROWE. I am an assistant to Christopher Green, a baker, who lives in Gray's-inn-lane. The prisoner took a ready furnished lodging in Mr. Green's house in December last; she staid ten weeks, and went away without giving me notice - these articles were missed from her room.
   HANNAH LAW. I went to Mr. Nicholls, a pawnbroker, in Gray's-inn-lane, and pawned a blanket for the prisoner on the 12th of January.
 SAMUEL HAMPSTEAD. I am in the employ of Mr. Nicholls. I have a blanket pawned in the name of  Ann Swaine, No. 6, Tash-street.
   WILLIAM KING. I am a pawnbroker. I have a carpet, two sheets, a curtain, and two pillow-cases, pawned with me, in the name of Swaine, at different times - I cannot tell by whom.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went out, and this woman said there was a lock on the door - they said they would send the duplicates and the key in a letter.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined One Month.
 1308.  JOHN MALCOLM was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of June , 1 bag, value 3d., and 40 lbs. weight of rice, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Plant.
 DAVID PLANT. I am related to Mr. Thomas Plant: a  grocer, of Upper Seymour-street. A bag of rice was taken from his premises last Thursday, but I did not miss it till the officer came and told me of it - it had been on the threshold of the door.
   ROBERT TEASDALE. I met the prisoner going down Seymour-street: I asked what he had got - he said a bag of rice, which he got from Mr. Roberts', at the left-hand corner; I said he had better take it back, and he took it to where it came from.
 Prisoner's Defence. A gentleman asked me to carry it to Chapel-street - I have only been in London six weeks.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. Confined Six Weeks.
 1309.  JAMES MASON& WILLIAM HOWARD were indicted for stealing, on the 30th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of a certain man unknown, from his person.
 THOMAS GOOK. I am an officer. I was on duty near the palace on the day of the drawing-room, the 30th of April - I saw the two prisoners and another person in the crowd; they went close to a gentleman, and Howard put his hand into his pocket - they went on, and Howard put his hand in again, took out a handkerchief, and gave it to Mason, who I thought put it into his trousers, but it was under his jacket - I followed them, and pushed them into an open place; I touched the gentleman, and said, "You have lost your handkerchief;" he said No - I said, "Feel, sir;" he then said he had, and owned this as his: I begged him to come with me, but he said he would not have any trouble with it - I begged him to give me his card, but unfortunately it has no address on it, only the name; I took the prisoners, and the gentleman appeared to follow me - I lost him; the prisoners were remanded twice, but I could not find him.
   BENJAMIN MARSHALL. I am a constable of St. James'. I was on duty with Gook - I did not see the prisoners till Gook said, "Hold these two fellows;" he touched the gentleman on his shoulder, and said, "You have lost your silk handkerchief;" he felt, and said, "I have" -Gook said, "I can show you where it is," and pointed to Mason, and said, "It is in his trousers, I believe," but he found it in his waistcoat - the gentleman said it was his, but refused to prosecute.
 MASON'S Defence. I received this handkerchief on the pavement, and had it a quarter of an hour before the officer had any suspicion of me - he then took hold of me, and let me go, and took Howard; the gentleman said it was not his; the constable asked him if he would go to St. James' watch-house - he said, "I can give you no satisfaction, the property is not mine;" Gook said, "It is not likely you will get much hurt, as there is no smell to appear against you;" I have since found he meant by smell a gentleman; I said "I am outward bound, and have no money to give you."
 HOWARD'S Defence. I am innocent, and have been in the infirmary.
   BENJAMIN MARSHALL re-examined. The gentleman said at first he had not been robbed, and then said he had- Gook showed him the handkerchief, and he owned it on the spot.
 MASON -    GUILTY. Aged 24.
 HOWARD -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Life.

   FRANCIS ROZARE. I am second under-steward, on board the Mount Stewart Elphinston, which was in the St. Katharine's Docks. The prisoner came on board - he said he was distressed, and asked me to let him say on board a few days, as his master owed him some money, and when it was paid he would go; he staid one week, and went on shore on the Monday; I missed four spoons - they had been in the caddy; he had the key of it; I came on shore the next day, and saw him in Shadwell; I told him I had lost some silver spoons - he said he knew nothing about it; a friend said he had some money in his possession; I asked where he got it - he said he had taken his money of his master; I sent my friend to know if it was true, and he gave him in charge; the spoons are lost, but I have seen the top of the mustard-pot - it is the property of Alexander Hanning.
