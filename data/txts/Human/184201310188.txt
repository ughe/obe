I know the prisoner, he has come to our firm three or four times for steel—on the 12th of January I saw him there—it is my duty to make out the invoices, which I did for these goods—I do not know that the prisoner spoke to me—he presented me with a written order for the goods now produced—he said nothing—be  lieving it to be genuine, I made out a bill for two bundles of spring-steel, 3 l . 12 s .—I do not remember his saying any thing—he took the steel away—he had had goods before—not on his own account.
   Prisoner.  I did not have the steel, and never made out the order—I was on board a steam-boat at the time. Witness.  I am sure he is the man, I know he went away with the steel—I cannot recollect whether he took it on his shoulder or in a truck—he must have had a truck, it weighed 2cwt. lqr.—I am certain he is the man. JEMIMA DRY. I am a widow, and carry on the business of a wheel wright and smith, in Horse and Groom-yard, Westminster. The prisoner was in my employ five years ago, as hammerman to his father, who worked for me—I have not seen him since—he was discharged five years ago—I dealt with Messrs. Moser then, and do so now—this order is not my writing—I never authorised any body to write it for me, and none of the goods ever came to my premises. WILLIAM MORRISS. I am porter to Moser and Son. I know the pri soner, and have seen him at our premises more than three times—I can not state the date, but I have seen him twice since Christmas—I delivered some steel to him on a Tuesday, the last time he came, but I know no thing about the order now produced—it was another order—I cannot recollect the transaction of the 12th of January—I know nothing of the delivery of these goods myself—we have no other customer named Dry—( order read )—"12th January, 1842. Please let the bearer hare two bundles of spring steel.—J. DRY ." GUILTY. Aged 22.

JOHN BENNETT was again  indicted for feloniously and know ingly uttering, on the   27th of January , a forged order for the delivery of goods, with intent to defraud Richard Moser the elder , and another. WILLIAM MORRISS. I am a yard porter to Messrs. Moser. I know the prisoner by sight—I am positive of him—he brought me this order on the 27th of January, and asked me to look out the steel for him—I said, "Your people are using a great deal of steel, and you tell young master Dry to be more careful in his writing; he is getting a worse scholar now than before he went to school"—the prisoner said young Dry was a bad scholar—I selected the steel in consequence of the order, weighed it, and our yard clerk had it to enter, and gave him the invoice—I am posi tive he is the man—I delivered the steel to him, helped him tie it up, and he took it away on his shoulder—it was under 56lbs. JOHN BILL. I am clerk to the prosecutors. On the 27th of January the prisoner came, I ticked this order, and gave him the invoice—it came to 2 l . 11 s . 6 d ., weighing 2qrs. 91bs.—be took it away on his shoulder—I noticed the order at the time—it was signed "J. Dry," which Mrs. Dry generally signs. JEMIMA DRY. This paper is not my writing, nor my son's—I never authorised anybody to write it—my son is not in the habit of drawing these orders, and if he did, he always signed my name as well as his own—I always sign Jemima in full.
