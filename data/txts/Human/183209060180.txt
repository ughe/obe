 2202.  JAMES BURNS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of September , 1 waistcoat, value 3s, the goods of Anselom Jewell.
 ANSELOM JEWELL. I live in  High-street, Shadwell, and am a slop-seller. On the 20th of September, 1831, the prisoner came to my house and asked me to cash an advance-note for the ship Trusty; I agreed to do so - he looked out some shirts and other things; I gave him a sovereign, and some silver out of the note - I took the goods he bought on board the Trusty; I then missed a waistcoat - I never saw him again till the 11th of September last, when he had the waistcoat on; I said, "You are a pretty sort of fellow to come and get my things from me; and leave me a cash-note of which I could make no use, as you left the ship directly" - he said he had come home from Antigua, and if I would go with him I should find where they were to be paid; he walked me round the dock twice, but I refused to go any further, and took him into custody - I am quite certain he is the man. I went back to the ship - he had taken these things he bought of me, and was gone; I cut out vthis waistcoat, and know it well - this is the note he gave me.
   GEORGE DIX. I am a Thames Police-constable. I took the prisoner with this waistcoat on.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw this man in the Londondocks; he asked why I did not go in the ship - I said,"What ship?" as I did not know him again, as he had an old frock-coat on; I said, "You are not the man that cashed my note:" he said, "Yes, I am;" I said, "If you are, I will make it up with you" - I did not tell him I came home in a ship from Antigua; I came in the Cato, from Grenada.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Confined Three Months.

 2204.  MARY REYNOLDS was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , 3 yards of printed cotton, value 3s., the goods of John Brown.
   JOHN COLLINS. On the afternoon of the 11th of September I was in Pickett-street, holding a horse for a gentleman; I saw the prisoner and another woman at Mr. Brown's shop window - I think the prisoner called her Ann-Crawley; they looked in at the window, found that nobody perceived them, and the other woman put her hand over the rail and untwisted a piece of cotton some persons came by and she could not succeed, and then the prisoner tried - the other woman then took it, and gave it to the prisoner, who did it under her shawl, and walked towards Fleet-street; I went after the prisoner, and sent a person to tell Mr. Brown.
   GEORGE WHIPONS. I am shopman to John Brown of Picket-street, a linen draper. I saw the print safe on the afternoon of the 11th of September; it was twisted round the legs of some wooden steps - in consequence of some information I missed it; I went out and followed a woman, was who pointed out to me - I came up with the prisoner near Temple-bar; she was carrying the print under her arm - I believe she was drunk.
   JAMES ADAMS(Police-constable F 62). I took the prisoner - I went to the address she gave to a person I know very well, but she was not at home.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had not concealed it - I had it in my hand; my husband is ill in St. Giles' with the cholera, and I have buried two children with it - the woman said she bought it, and told me to pawn it for 2s.
 GUILTY. Aged 40.
     Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.

 MR. BARRY conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY BROUGHTON PARRY. I am proprietor of a tobacconist-shop on the north side of the Piazza, Covent-garden; I had one or two hundred pounds worth of cigars. On the night of the 29th of May I secured my premises myself; I had a shop, but no dwelling-house there - I left the premises secure about three o'clock in the morning of the 30th - and the same morning my servant came to my dwelling-house, in Villiers-street, and informed me of the robbery; I went to the premises, found the place had been broken open, and the property stated was gone - I saw it again at Bow-street the same day; I have every reason to believe the cigars are mine - they were contained in two sacks, and an apron belonging to my shopman.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Three in the morning is rather a late hour? A. Yes, rather later than usual; these cigars have been kept by the Excise since the Magistrate delivered them up - I am certain they were not removed at three o'clock in the morning for fear of the Excise; I was detained there by two gentlemen from the Piazza-hotel, sitting smoaking there, and it would not do for me to turn them out - I am an Englishman; I have adopted the name of Henriquez, which is over my window, owing to my connexion with the Havannah and Spanish America - I have given receipts in that name.
