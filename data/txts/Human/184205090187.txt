   Prisoner. Q.  It was not at my entreaty, then? A.  You came to me and asked whether I was agreeable to let my son go—I did not earnestly solicit you to take him—I have been a prosecutor several times before—I was indicted about eighteen months or two years ago for suffering card playing in my house, not for keeping a disorderly house, or allowing bad company to come there—I had a very respectable card party up-stairs—it was a charge made by a policeman.
   COURT . Q.  What did you let him have besides the 7 l .? A.  Nine teen yards of broad cloth, two watches, and a time-piece—those were goods paid for part of my son's passage—I gave part of them into the prisoner's hands and part I sent to his lodgings by my son—I let him have them entirely for him to pay my son's passage to Port Phillip, upon hit promise of taking him there and getting him a situation—he stated several times that he would be a father and a friend to him—he said he had paid a deposit to the owners to secure the passage for seven persons, and my son was included in the seven—his representing that he had come over to receive 700 l . or 800 l . operated on my mind in letting him have the rest—he gave me a card stating that he was the sole proprietor of the Yarrow Steam Packet Hotel—I asked him how he and his wife could leave the bu siness—he said he had left some one to. take care of it while he was away.
   Prisoner. Q.  Have you any witness that heard me say to you that I had come to receive 700 l . or 800 l .? A.  There was no one present, but I could bring half a dozen persons to whom you stated the same thing.
   COURT . Q.  Was it partly owing to that, that you trusted him? A.  It was—I thought he was going to take a good deal of property with him to do work.
   JURY . Q.  Did you send out your son on the faith of that representa tion? A.  I did—he was a stranger to me but I knew his relations—his wife's relations are in very humble circumstances—some of his relations are in very good circumstances—his brother-in-law was in a situation in Throgmorton-street—his mother is a widow, and gets her living by a mangle. EDWARD MARKLEW, JUN. I am the prosecutor's son. I was intro duced to the prisoner by Williams. I was present at conversations between the prisoner and my father—I heard him say, in my father's presence, that he was the proprietor of the hotel, and he had come here to receive money—I do not remember what sum he mentioned—I heard him say he had paid a deposit for the passage of seven persons, of whom I was one—I was not present when my father paid him the money—I went on board the Pathfinder  at Gravesend on the Tuesday afternoon with the prisoner and his wife, and Williams and his wife—Williams's wife is the prisoner's sis ter, and the prisoner's wife is Williams's sister—we sailed about three o'clock on Wednesday morning—we started from the Downs, I think, about the 20th or 22nd of October, and meeting with an unfair wind when we got to Dungeness we were obliged to return to the Downs—the priso ner and his wife left the ship on Sunday afternoon, and said he should be on board in four days—he asked the captain whether he would allow him four days—he promised to meet us at Plymouth in four days—two boxes and one large box were left on board belonging to him—the prisoner did
