BENJAMIN HENRY WALPOLE WAY. I have an estate at Denham, nes

his hands, leaving him to effect sales, and then deliver according to sample-
prior to 20 November I had placed samples of corn in his hands for the
purpose of effecting sales for me—that was done through my steward on
November-I gave my steward directions about the price-on 30 November
I received this letter from the defendant, dated the 20-(Read: “Uxbridge,
November 20, 1860. Sir, I have sold your wheat at 191. 108. the best, and
181. the other; I have sent to your steward about delivering it, as he wished
me. D. Basset.”)- In consequence of receiving that letter I gave instructions
to my steward to deliver the bulk according to the samples-I should not
have parted with the bulk unless I had believed the prisoner had sold it for
me—the value of it was about 2911. I think I have not received any portion
of that, except a dividend of 38. 6d.-part of the corn went to Mr. Kelsey's
mill, I believe, after the declaration of insolvency, and was ultimately
recovered, but not from the prisoner.

-I never named to him a price for the wheat except once, and that was in
consequence of his having sold it at 21. less than it was worth the time before
- he did not pay me the difference I had named the price on 9d No
vember-it was 191. 108. and 181., and the letter states he sold it at that
