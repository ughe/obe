   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Had this decanter been found by you before this occurrence took place? A.  No, by some one else—it was shown to me secreted under the stairs—it had been evidently removed by some one intending to do something improper with it—it was not off the premises—it was between two and three o'clock when I first saw it—to the best of my belief, I said before the Magistrate that I asked her where she got it, and she said, "My brother gave it me"—I said, "What for?" and she said she did not know—I think those were the words I used before the Magistrate—if it was not taken down it is the fault of the clerk—my deposition was read over to me before I signed it, and I was asked if I had any thing to add or amend—I do not know whether I did say so.

 1690. GEORGE MARROTT and JOHN FINCH were indicted for stealing, on the   16th of April , 80lbs. weight of lead, value 30 s  ., the goods of Thomas Salmon , and fixed to a certain building. WILLIAM MARTIN. I live with my uncle, Thomas Salmon, who is a builder. He has built some houses in Tidman's-fields—I was on the gutters on the 15th, and they were all safe—on the 18th I missed some lead which had been fixed to the building.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Where is your uncle? A.  Gone to Smithfield—these houses were built about three or four months ago—I was present at the building—I know they are my uncle's houses—he did not put the lead on them—the plumbers did—he superintended it, and paid for it—he was here about five minutes ago, and said he would come back—he was before the Magistrate, but was not examined—my uncle took the land of Mr. Wood, Lord Holland's steward—my uncle is a poor man, and lives in lodgings—he has no builder's yard or shop—he keeps the bricks on the premises when he has got any, when he is build ing—I do not know any thing about my uncle's entering into a written agreement with Mr. Wood—I cannot tell how long these houses have been building—I have not been with my uncle above six months—they have been left to go to rack and ruin—the floors are not in, nor the doors and windows—when I first came to my uncle they were going on—I have seen Mr. Wood on the premises now and then.
   COURT . Q.  Have you seen your uncle directing the men when they were building? A.  Yes—I have been there every day for three months to look after the men for my uncle—there were about twenty men working there at first—I saw my uncle pay them, at Mr. Parker's—none of the houses are let—my uncle is employed by persons to build for them—the lead was put on the premises about four months ago—I saw it put on by Mr. Jones, the plumber's man—I have not seen Mr. Wood on the pre mises since that—I saw my uncle pay Mr. Jones upwards of 20 l ., at the beer-shop, between two and three months ago—he received that money
