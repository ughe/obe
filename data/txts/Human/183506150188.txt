   COURT . Q.  Do you mean you kept him three weeks after you found out the loss of this 4 s . 4 d .? A.  It might be a fortnight—I never asked him for the money.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Do you mean to swear you did not speak to him about the leg of mutton? A.  I told him the day I was at Union Hall that I understood he left a leg of mutton, and he had received the money—he said he had a hole in his pocket, and it got out—he said he had lost the money—I did not say I should deduct it from his wages—I do not think I spoke to him again about it—I did not to my knowledge—I discovered about the veal some days after—I have laid that on the 4th of April—I cannot tell when I discovered about it, or how long I kept him in my service after it. NOT GUILTY.
 1581. GEORGE PURCELL was again  indicted for embezzle ment. WILLIAM THOMAS ROPER. The prisoner was in my service—on the 18th of April—he took a leg of mutton to Mr. Adams, which came to 4 s . 10 d ., and I believe on the Saturday week or Sunday week fol lowing, Mr. Adams had some more meat—I sent it by the prisoner, and sent an account of that and the previous leg of mutton—the prisoner came back and said Mr. Adams had paid for the leg of mutton—I said, "No," I had not received it—he said, "Yes, you did, Mr. Adams saw you"—I said to Mrs. Roper, "Scratch off this, and I will see further into it"—I am quite sure the 4 s . 10 d . was not paid to me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was this the first thing you discovered? A.  No—Mr. Adams lived two doors from me—I desired this to be taken off the bill, because the prisoner told me he had received it, and paid it to me—I did not go to Mr. Adams for three or four days to have it explained—it was on Sunday morning when the prisoner told me—I do not know why I did not speak to Mr. Adams on the Monday or the Tuesday—I might have forgotten it. NOT GUILTY.
 1582. MARY FITZCUMMINS was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of March , 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of George Fisher , her master. GEORGE FISHER. I live in Upper Stamford-street. The prisoner was in my service at the letter end of March—after she was gone I missed this handkerchief and some other things—I can swear this handkerchief is mine.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  What mark have you on it? A.  My initials were on it, but they have been picked out—here is the mark where the letter "F" has been—the policeman brought us some duplicates in about two months—I described to him the handkerchief and other things—the prisoner called on us, I believe, the day after she left the service, and it appears to have been pledged in that day.
