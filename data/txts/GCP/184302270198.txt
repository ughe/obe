monies of William Noonan.
WILLIAM Noonan. I live in Plough-court, near Field-lane. I have
known the prisoner three weeks-about one o'clock in the morning of the
28th of Feb., she knocked at my room door, and said she had been turned
out of doors by the party she lodged with-my wife got up, and let her in--
I had two shillings, three sixpences, and the other money stated in my pocke
-I let her bide in the room, and went to sleep-I saw her go out about
seven o'clock, and soon after I perceived my trowsers, which I left on the
back of the chair, had been removed, and my money was gone-I afterwards
met her in Whitechapel-I asked her if she was not ashamed, for robbing me
-she began to cry, and offered me part of the money-I would not acceps
