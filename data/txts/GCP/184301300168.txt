749. JOSEPH SWAINE and WILLIAM SADD were indicted for steal-
ing, on the 19th of Jan., 1 horse-collar, value 58. ; 1 bridle, 88. ; 1 pair of
harness, 18. ; 1 pair of traces, 2s. ; 1 crupper, 18.; and 1 curry-comb, 1s.;
the goods of Philemon Harvey.
Francis Ruwald. I am in the service of Mr. Philemon Harvey, a fancy
trimming maker, Osborn-place, Whitechapel. This harness is bis--I saw
them safe in his stable on Thursday morning, the 19th of Jan.-I saw them
again on the Saturday following.
GEORGE TEAKLE (police-sergeant H 8.) On Thursday night, the 19th of
Jan., I saw both the prisoners together at the door of No. 23, St. John-street
-I think they saw me-they both rushed into the house, and up stairs--the
street-door was pushed to-I ran to the door, and heard more than one person
running up stairs- I got a light, went up, and found both the prisoners stand-
ing together on the top of the second stairs, and behind them was a black
bag containing this barness-I said, “Halloo, who does this belong to?"-
Swaine said, “It belongs to a person I know"--I said, “Who is he, and
where does he live?"-he said, " I tell you it belongs to a person I know,
what do you want more?"- I took them and the bag.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. The bag was lying on the floor? A.
Yes.
Not Guilty.
750. JOHN YOUNG was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of Jan., 1
a watch, value 101. ; 1 watch-key, 3d.; and 1 watch-ribbon, 3d. ; the goods of
Frederick Henry Ridley, in his dwelling-house : and WILLIAM SCOTT,
for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen;
against the Statute, &c.; and that Scott had been before convicted of felony.
LOUISA Howes. I am servant to Mr. Frederick Henry Ridley, of Broom-
field-row, Maida-hill, Paddington. I saw Young about a quarter past twelve
o'clock on the morning of the 18th of Jan., in my master's scullery-I did
not know him before-he was not dressed as a butcher, but he had a page's
jacket on, and a piece of beef in a butcher's tray, which had not been ordered
- he left the beef in the scallery-I said, "This is not for here"-he said, " It
is"-I asked where he brought it from, and he mentioned a name which I
cannot recollect-I left him in the scullery, and went to the dining-roon to
ask my mistress about it-I came back in about two minutes, and found him
where I had left him-I again said, "I don't think it is right"-he said it
was, and went out-I followed him, and locked the door-I returned to the
kitchen in about three minutes, and missed my mistress's gold watch off the
shelf-it had a key attached to it by a ribbon-I know it was safe about ten
minutes before it was gold, and was worth more than 101.-l am quite sure
it was there--I made inquiries, and my mistress had not ordered the beef-I
am quite sure Young is the lad--the watch has not been found.
Young. I did not go out till a quarter to twelve o'clock, and was back by
a quarter past ; it was not me; you said it was ten minutes after, I was gone
you missed it. Witness. No, I said in three minutes after I looked to see
what time it was, and the watch was gone-there was no one there but you
and I.
