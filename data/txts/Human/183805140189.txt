   Q.  Tell us any thing you may have heard from them? A.  I object to such a question as that, because I do not understand it—to the best of my recollection, I never heard the poor voters refuse to vote for him, for that reason—I have heard as many as a hundred say, if he came twenty times they would always vote for him-to the best of. my recollection, I never heard them refuse to vote for him—they said they would vote for him, because they liked the man, or gentleman, but man was the common phrase I heard—I have been a year and nine months in Mr. Miller's ser vice—I have been at three elections—I have gone about a dozen yards with Mr. Mason, perhaps—I never saw the money going about—I took money of my own down to the election.
   Q.  Did you have a portmanteau of sovereigns that went to Liverpool instead of Newcastle? A.  No—we have had no such thing—we have had boxes which I travel with—I never knew there were any sovereigns in them—I never knew or heard of a box of sovereigns going to Liverpool instead of Newcastle. THOMAS BASTERFIELD. I am servant to Mr. De Horsey, M. P. for Newcastle. I was down there with him, at the last election—I remember the day of the nomination—I assisted in waiting at dinner, at the Castle hotel that day—my master dined there—the dinner was served up about five o'clock, in a large room, at the back of the house, and was over about half-past six o'clock—Mr. Miller and Mr. De Horsey, and most of the gentlemen left the hotel at that time—I remained in the hotel after that—I know the room in front of the house from which the speeches are made—I went into that room—it is on the first floor—I remained there from seven o'clock until ten o'clock—I did not see Mr. Miller at the hotel after half-past six o'clock—I was there all the time—there were no speeches made from the window that day.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Were you in the room all the time? A.  I never left it—Mr. De Horsey is quite well, and in London. THOMAS BADDELEY. I am in the employ of Mr. Mason, in his hat manufactory. I was at Newcastle at the time of the last election, in July—I remember being at the Castle on the Saturday, the day of the nomina tion—the gentlemen had begun dinner when I went, but I was there before they had done—I got there between five and six o'clock at night—after dinner, I remember Mr. Miller and the rest leaving the Castle—that was between six and seven o'clock at night—I remained at the Castle till eleven o'clock—Mr. Miller never returned to the Castle at all that night—there were no speeches made from the room that evening.
