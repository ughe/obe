230. ELIZABETH LENNARD was indicted for stealing on the 20th of February , one watch value 40 s. a seal value 2 s. and one pair of silver buckles, value 10 s. the goods of Richard Hind.
     ACQUITTED.
 231.  EDWARD JONES was indicted for stealing on the 18th of January last, one muslin handkerchief edged with lace, value 2 s. the goods of Hester Delamonte, widow.
 The house was on fire and the handkerchief found upon the prisoner. - Produced in court and deposed to by the prosecutrix.
     GUILTY. To be transported for seven years to the East Indies.
 Tried by the London Jury, before Mr. Justice BULLER.
 232.  MARTIN TAYLOR was indicted for stealing on the 18th day of February , one wooden box value 12 d. and two hundred and twenty-five plates of tin, value 47 s. the goods of John Brown.
   John Brown deposed, he sells tin plates by commission, that he had lost several boxes of tin plates, that he set a watch at his warehouse door, that about eight o'clock he saw a person stand on the threshold at the door, that he went and laid hold of him, and caught the prisoner in the fact, with a sack on his shoulders, which he then threw off, and in the sack he found the plates which he deposed were his property.
 The box of plates was produced in court.
 The prisoner said he never went into the warehouse.
     GUILTY. To be publickly whipped and confined to hard labour twelve months in the house of correction.
 Tried by the London Jury, before Mr. RECORDER.
 233.  HENRY COHEN was indicted for stealing on the 22d. of January , a cotton handkerchief, value 12 d. the Goods of John Pope.
   John Pope deposed, the prisoner went round him in Long-Lane, on the 22d of of January, and stole his handkerchief, that he cried stop thief, and he was taken and the handkerchief found upon him.
     GUILTY. To be publickly whipped and imprisoned six months in the House of Correction.
 Tried by the London Jury, before Mr. RECORDER.
 234.  HENRY LEVI was indicted for stealing on the 22d of December , one pair of coach bridles, value 5 s. and part of a leather leading coach harness, value 13 s. the property of James Sharp, Esq.
   John Jones deposed he was coachman to Mr. Sharp, that he lost the things mentioned in the indictment, and heard that Moses Lealter had got them.
   Moses Lealter, a coach master, deposed, that the prisoner used to look after a horse and cart in the yard where his coach stood, that he sold him these things mentioned in the indictment, that he gave him 14 s. for them, he had no suspicion they were stole, it is usual for gentlemen to give old harness to their coachmen.
