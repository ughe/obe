2240. DANIEL RICKETTS, THOMAS SKIGGS, and WILLIAM
BENNETT were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July, 3 trusses of
hay, value 10s., the goods of Michael Woodhouse.--2nd Count, stating
them to be the goods of James Gingell.
MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution,
Michael WOODHOUSE. I gave directions to send two loads of hay to
Whitechapel-market-Gingell was my salesman - I have a little boy
named Ebenezer Woodhouse-Ricketts was my carter-it was his duty to
carry the hay to Whitechapel.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHAMBERS. Q. You are a farmer, and grow hay
and clover ? A. Yes--my hay-binder is not here—I know nothing about
two loads being bound up-Gingell's business is to sell the hay-he pays
me the money when I like to go after it-sometimes a month or two passes
-he always passes me the bills of parcels back from the man that has it-
I do not recollect the price at all - I expect the salesman to do the best he
can for me-Ricketts has been in my service sixteen years-he was always
bonest for any thing I know-he lives in my house as in-door servant.
Mr. Doane. Q. Have you been paid for this hay? A. Yes, by
Gingell.
WILLIAM BYER. I am in the service of Mr. Gingell, a salesman in
the haymarket, Whitechapel. On the 10th of July, Mr. Banks, the ma-
naging-man for Mr. Rhodes, came to purchase two loads of hay-there
was a wagon of Mr. Woodhouse's, which had two loads on it-I sold it for
two loads, seventy-two trusses-Ricketts and a little boy came with it
I promised to send this hay to Banks's, for Mr. Rhodes—I did not expect
to be paid till he received it-Ricketts and the boy went with it--they were
to go direct to Mr. Rhodes at Stoke Newington--they had no right to go
any where else.
Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS. Q. You know the mode of this
business? A. Yes-the hay-carts are driven into High-street, White-
chapel, when a purchaser comes, and we make the bargain-I agreed to
sell this for six guineas a load-I told Mr. Banks I could not take less---
Rhodes had dealt with me before they used to pay once in six weeks, or
when we liked to take a bill-I cannot tell when they paid for it-it was
a goodish bit after it was sold-perhaps a month.
WILLIAM BANKS.--I am foreman to Messrs. Rhodes. I purchased two
loads of hay-they were to be delivered before I paid for them they were
to be sent to Stoke Newington. On Saturday morning, the 10th of July, I
went and saw some clover-I asked Byers the price, he said, “Six gui-
neas "-I desired him to send two loads to Stoke Newington---there were
seventy-two trusses in two loads--I presumed he sent it home I got
home twenty minutes before twelve o'clock-when I got home, I found the
Wagon and hay had arrived-I saw Searing and Carswell, and in conse-
quence of what Carswell said to me, I went to the station-I got Mr.
