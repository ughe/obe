 1752. WILLIAM PRYOR was indicted for stealing 1 goose, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Thomas Deane.
 The prisoner did not plead to the indictment; and on the evidence of Mr. Holding, the assistant surgeon of the gaol, the Jury found him INSANE.
   Before Edward Bullock , Esq.
 1753. BENJAMIN RUNXTMANN, MOSES LEBRECHT CARL LOENTHALL, and ISIDOR GUTTSCHALK were indicted for stealing 66 yards of silk, value 12 l  . 7 s  ., the goods of James M'Gill and another) in the dwelling-house of Thomas Nutting; to which
   RUNXTMANN  pleaded GUILTY.— Confined Six Months .
 (The prisoners being foreigners, had the evidence communicated to them by an interpreter.) RICHARD THOMAS TOMS. I am a commission agent, in partnership with James M'Gill, at No. 1, Bread-street, Cheapside, in the dwelling-house of Mr. Thomas Nutting. On the 10th of June, about half-past one o'clock, I saw Runxtmann and Loenthall at our warehouse, which is on the first floor—they inquired the price of various goods there, silk goods chiefly—Loenthall spoke to me as well as he could in English, and said it was difficult to buy a large parcel of goods, not understanding the English—he said he had got a carpenter at home who spoke English and German well, but he was sick—he laid his head on his hand and made all manner of motions—I showed him the commoner sort of goods—he objected to them as not being good, and required them to be weighed, and the boy was sent down into the shop to weigh them—he then inquired the length and breadth of the goods—he gave me his address, and I wrote it down, and required him to write his name himself—he told me his name, but I could not understand what he meant—he agreed about the discount, and kept me in conversation along time, during which Runxtmann went down stairs—when Loenthall went, I put on my hat and followed him—when I got out I saw Loenthall going up Bread-street to Cheapside—I then saw him cross and join Guttschalk, about seventy feet from the warehouse—they walked towards Lawrence-lane—Runxtmann crossed over and joined the other two—they had some conversation at the corner of Trump-street—by the time I got to the corner of Trump-street, they had got down King-street, and were on the cab-stand—Runxtmann got into a cab , and Loenthall went up King-street towards Cheapside—at that
