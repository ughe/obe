JAMES SMITH. I am an engine fitter, in the employ of Sir Samuel
Morton Peto. On Saturday, 5th Sept., about half past 12 o'clock, I went
into my employer's engine shed; there were four engines there, two of
them in pieces, undergoing repair-the things were all right then, and in
their proper place I saw these pieces of brass there on the Saturday
I went to the shed on Monday at half past 1 o'clock, and found several
parts of the engines lying on the floor, all deranged—they had been moved,
and the brass taken out from them-even the duplicate parts of an engine
will not fit with the engine itself-this is a piece belonging to one of the
engines where the brass was taken off-it is marked with a cross, and so is
the brass ; so that any individual who is a mechanical man would know
how to fit this engine so as to be ready for it to work-if this bad
been lost, it would have cost 501. or 602. to fit up, perhaps more-I do not
know the prisoner; I might have seen him there, but I do not remember
having done 80-I recognise this brass as being the property of Sir Samuel
Morton Peto.
