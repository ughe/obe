   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I can swear I saw you come out of that room, no one but you was there—the bedroom and parlour are separate rooms, leading into one another—there were three of us talking to gether, McCarthy was the third—we were all treated—you did not speak as you came out—I said "Are you going to treat us out of that shilling the governor gave you for washing his trap?"—you said we could come over and have some of the beer—the George is nearer than the Windmill—you did not give Boo twopence because he said he had no money.
   Re-examined.  He changed no shilling at the Windmill. LOUIS LOCKWOOD. I am one of the prosecutor's carmen, and live next door—on Tuesday, 5th of August, I went to the prisoner at the police-station and said "Walliker, tell me where the missus's  watch and chain is?"—he turned round and said to me "I know nothing about it, they cannot get nothing out of me; let them try what they like"—I asked him the same question again and he made the same reply—I did not ask him why he had not been to work on Tuesday morning; the policeman took me away then. HENRY JUPE( Detective Sergeant L ). On the night of the 5th of August, at 10.30, I took the prisoner into custody at the Equestrian Tavern, Blackfriars Road—I said "You will be charged with stealing a watch and chain and 10 l . in money from your master"—he made no reply, but said "I will go"—I searched him at once and found 3 s . 6 d . in silver on him, and 7 1/2 d . in bronze—I should say he had been drinking heavily—he was three-parts drunk; he was smoking a cigar and had a woman with him—on the way to the station he said "I know nothing about the b——things"—the bedroom of this house is partitioned off from the parlour—the door could be easily burst open, there is only a small bolt—my recollection is that it was a half-glass door; the glass was whole—the wash-house door was left open for the men to get water—he would go through there to burst open the door.
   The prisoner in his defence stated that on this day he came out of his own bedroom, and seeing the men in the yard treated them at their request out of the shilling his master had given him, and that on leaving them he met a suc-ession of friends, who continued treating him until he was given into custody. GUILTY  .— Judgment respited to give the prisoner an opportunity of giving some information as to the watch and chain.
