MR. WILKINSON Prosecuted ; MR. PURCELL Defended.
MICHEL DINERSTEIN. I carry on business as a money exchanger at
13, Brick Lane, Spitalfields—on 'March 22nd I was with my father in the
offce when at 8.45 p.m. the prisoner came in and put these five roubles
(Produced) on the table and asked my father to exchange them for 28. ach
—the price is Is. lld., and we offered him Is. IOd.—we looked at the
and on examination we found them to be lighter than they ought to have
been, and that they were bad—we sent for a policeman and when he came

three bad coins—we told Fm that the roubles were bad after the
policeman had come, and he said that it was good money—we asked
him where he had got them from, and he said they were given to
him by a man who had left London—we wanted to lock him up and
he said to my father, You have not lost hundreds of pounds ; you have
only lost 68. I will give you back the 6s. and you let me go ' '—he showed
us 68. , but my father would not take it—the policeman took the roubles
from his hands—when he offered us the 6s. we had not said anything
about the amount of the coins that had been exchanged on the Saturday
before.
Cross-examined. My father and I were standing together, and he put

in the office when the two mens came in the Saturday before, and my

not at the Police Court—it was after we told him about that that he
said. " You have not lost hundreds of pounds ' '—one rouble was lying
on the table, and the other four the policeman took from his hands.
