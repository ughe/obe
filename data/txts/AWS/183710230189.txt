DAVIS, were indicted for a misdemeanor.
MEssrs. ADOLPHUS and CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
JOBN VICKERS HARRISON. I carry on the business of a glue-maker
in Grange-road, Bermondsey, in partnership with Mr. Raymont. On
the 28th of October, Bonnell came to my house and gave an order
for 12ewt. of glue, to be sent to No. 21, New Church-street, Lisson-
grove-I did not know him before-he said he should pay 101. in
cash and the remainder within the month-he referred me to Mr. Good,
of Banner-street, St. Luke's-my partner Mr. Raymont went to make
inquiry, and in consequence of that the goods were supplied-he said he
should want more glue-the value of the 12ewt. was I believe 361. 28.-
on the following day a person of the name of Clark came to the factory,
he brought 101. from Bonnell, and said he was his traveller-he said
be travelled for other people, as one commission alone would not pay him
in London-the goods were sent in pursuance of the order-on the 12th
of November, Clark came to the factory again and produced a letter-he
never left it at all-it was addressed to Clark from Bonnell-I did not
read the whole of it-Clark pointed out what referred to the glue-I read
what he pointed out-as far as I recollect, the contents were, that Bonnell
had sold 10cwt. more glue in the country, and that he wished to have
the dark glue-he said it would suit the country market best-Clark
asked me to have it ready for him that afternoon, and said he would
come for it himself at the time-I said it should be ready-he went
away and came about an hour before the time mentioned, and brought
Isaac Anns, and a horse and cart, on which was the name of Sarah
Martin, Wheat Sheaf-yard, Farringdon-street--the glue was packed in
bags and delivered to him-he and Anns went away with it-there was
10ewt,-on the 9th of November Clark came again and produced this
letter (No. 1,)-(See the letter No. 1, page 1000) -and told me that the
glue was to be sent to Lisson-grove immediately-I did not refuse the
order that time-after he left I turned to Bonnell's account, and in conse-
quence of the state of that account I determined not to execute the whole
of the e order-we sent 5ewt. to Lisson-grove, with a view of getting the 204.
-after that we received a caution from a gentleman named Sewell-in
con sequence of which I went to the house of the defendant Davis, in
Brick-lane, the next day, after I had sent the 5ewt. of glueI believe

was sent the same day the order was given-thi is the entry in our day-

Clark's direction-(I took no further steps until the credit I usually gave

that paid from Bonnell-I never could find him)- waited a considerable
time in Davis's shop-he was engaged with a person-it was a chandler's
shop-I asked Davis if he was a buyer of glue-he said he was not-he
said he had bought some glue, or I made the observation that he had some
glue there-he said he had bought it-I said, Yes, you have bought it
either from Clark or Bonnell"-h said he had bought it from neither-I do
