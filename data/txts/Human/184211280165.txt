 240. JOHN WAKELING was indicted for embezzlement. EMMA SMITH. I am a widow, and live in Stratford-grove, Essex. My husband died four months ago—the prisoner was in his employment, and con tinued in mine afterwards—he received money on my account—at my hus band's death, I sent him to a Mr. Flax ton, who was indebted to my husband—he said he had seen Mr. Flaxton, and that he would call on me in a few days—the amount of his bill was 1 l . 3 s . 4 d .—I never received any part of it—the prisoner absconded as soon as it was found out—I had another cus tomer named Coates, part of his bill was due before my husband's death—I gave the prisoner in charge.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q,  He came to yon again afterwards? A,  Yes, and said he was very sorry for it—I am left sole executrix of my hus band's affairs—I carry on the business of a furrier—part of Mr. Coatts's debt, to the amount of 1 l ., occurred since my. husband's death—the prisoner worked for me twelve months at 22 s . a week. HENRY COATES. I had an account with Mrs. Smith, after her husband's death—I paid the prisoner money on her account several times—a good deal more than 1 l ., but only 12. on the account since her husband's death—it was on the 6th of Sept.—I have known him to conduct himself very properly. GEORGE SMITH. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner—I told him I supposed he knew what it was for—he said he did perfectly well, and he was sorry of it.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months .
