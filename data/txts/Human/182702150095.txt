These boots lay on a box by the fire-place - I met the prisoner (who is a stranger) in Jews'-row, between eleven and twelve o'clock, on the 15th - we got into conversation; she said she could help me to two or three days' work every week, and she would go home with me, that she might know where to send for me; we went into my room; and as I turned my back to bid a neighbour good bye, she must have concealed the boots; she went away - I went out, and returned at half-past seven o'clock; I then missed them - I am sure they were there when she was in the room - nobody else was there - I locked my door when I went out, and found it still locked - I never allowed her to take them.
   JOHN KILLINGSWORTH. I am servant to Mr. Perkins, pawnbroker. On the 15th of February, between twelve and four o'clock, the prisoner pawned these boots for 2s. - I knew her before - Mrs. Loft claimed them that night.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM ALLEN. I took her in charge.
 Prisoner. I leave it to your mercy.
 GUILTY. Aged 42. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 748.  JANE HOWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 sheet, value 3s.; 1 blanket, value 4s., and 1 saucepan, value 3s., the goods of Charlotte Ann Ward , widow , in a lodging-room.
 CHARLOTTE ANN WARD. I live in St. Ann-street, Westminster. On the 10th of February the prisoner took a lodging at my house, which is in  St. John the Evangelist, Westminster; she had the first floor front room, furnished; she represented herself as a married woman - I did not see her husband till next day - I have since heard she is not married; she was to pay 4s. 3d. a week; she staid till the Tuesday night, when she was apprehended on another charge, and I missed this property, which was let with the room - I never gave her leave to pawn them.
   DELMOUR WADE. My mother keeps a general dealer's shop in St. Andrew-street. On the 12th of February, about nine o'clock in the morning, the prisoner sold us a large iron saucepan for 14d., and we have sold it again - I am certain she is the woman; Harris, the pawnbroker, of Tothill-street, has the property, but will not attend.
   BENJAMIN TIMBRELL. I am an officer. I have the property which Harris delivered to me.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   LOUISA STEPHENSON. I heard the prisoner acknowledge that this property was the prosecutrix's.
 Prisoner's Defence. I beg for mercy - my friends are too respectable to let them know of my situation.
     GUILTY Aged 40. Confined Two Months.

 750.  PAGE DONALD was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , at the precinet of St. Katherine, 12 sovereigns, the monies of David Livingstone , in the dwelling-house of Joseph Wilson.
   ELIZABETH WILSON. I am wife of Joseph Wilson, who is a watchman. We rent a house, No. 9, Phoenix-court, Butcher-row, in the precint of St. Katherine's. David Livingstone lodged in our house. On the 13th of February he complained to me of having lost some money; he slept in our top room with the prisoner. Livingstone is agent to his son, who is a manufacturer at Dundee.
 DAVID LIVINGSTONE. I lodge at Mr. Wilson's. The prisoner slept in the same room; I have been there thirteen weeks - he was there before me; I had my money in my chest, which was locked, and stood in that room - I put twelve sovereigns in there on Saturday, the 10th of February, in the foot of a stocking, and missed them on Monday, the 12th - all the twelve sovereigns were gone; another man, named  Joseph Harrison, lodged in the room- he still remains there; the prisoner and him slept in one bed; I slept in another in the same room - I am sure I locked my chest on Saturday when I put my money in, and on Sunday I took out a shirt, and locked the chest again - I found the lock on Monday as I left it - it had not been opened by any violence, but must have been opened by a false key; I am sure I had not left my own key in the room; I was fearful of bringing reproach on the house, and did not fetch a constable for a day or two, but the prisoner kept spending money - he was taken up on the Friday or Saturday, and was then quite drunk - he remained in the house till then.
 E. WILSON re-examined. Livingstone did not mention his loss to me till the 13th - I told the prisoner, if he had the money he had better say so, and give it up - this was on the morning of the 13th.
   WILLIAM STOWELL. I am a publican. On Thursday last, 15th of Feb., between 6 and 7 o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my house with two other men - they had some beer - one of the men went out with the prisoner, and then they came in again - the prisoner had used the house before, and I knew him; he came to the bar, and said to me, "I have saved up a little money - will you take care of it for me?" and gave me nine sovereigns - they were not in any thing; he came on Friday morning, and had one back - he came again on Saturday, and had one from my wife - I produced the other seven.
   THOMAS OBORNE. I am night-beadle of Aldgate. I took the prisoner into custody on Saturday, and asked how he came by the nine sovereigns - I held out no promise or threat - his answer was, he supposed he had stolen them - I might take him to the Police-office, or do what I pleased with him - I heard him say at the office that he found them in the room where he lodged.
 Prisoner to LIVINGSTONE. Q. Was there any silver with your sovereigns? A. I do not know, but think there was twelve sovereigns and 20s. together, wrapped in paper, and put into a stocking; here is the paper they were in - I know it by some figures, and it was given me by Mrs. Wilson, who found it in the room.
 Prisoner. I own I picked up the money under my my feet in the room - there were ten sovereigns, two half-sovereigns, and 19s.
