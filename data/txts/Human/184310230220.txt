   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Who it your attorney? A.  Iam my own—in the first instance I employed Mr. Cripps, an attorney——he said, "I will help you get the bill"—I cannot exactly say when I first missed the rule from the premises—it was about the latter end of May I saw the mark on it—it was on the premises while the prisoners were working for me-—Haines had worked for me twenty-five years, and they had not left off finally after the rule was gone—I think it might be about two months afterwards that I ceased to employ them, because they had got this piece-work, and they defied me to keep them out of the premises—I had no quarrel with them about leaving my work to go on with other, no further than telling them I should set on other hands—I am sorry to say By field lived rather too near roe—I should not think of prosecuting for this rule, only knowing I was repeatedly robbed before—Byfield lived not above 200 or 300 yards from this building, in Mill-lane, I believe—the house has no number—Haines has lived about Brixton twenty-fire years—Haines called upon me on the 7th of Aug.—I had repeatedly claimed the rule of them before that—I sent three distinct messages that they were not to deceive themselves, provided they did not bring that rule back to the place, I should have them locked up—when I first saw the name on it I claimed it—I did not claim it twenty times—I saw By field, and said to him, "Do not deceive yourself, I shall certainly have you locked up"—he did not say I was a liar—he said he bad nothing to do with it, Haines had got it—I went before a Magistrate, and he said, "I should like to do away with regard to the felony, this might be a mistake of these men"—I cannot say whether any de position was taken, I think there was—they only heard one witness that I had—the prisoners called two witnesses to prove the rule was not mine—I had three witnesses in Court, bot they only heard one—I did not wish to drive the case for the Magistrate to send it to trial—I wished him to give them repri mand—he did not discharge them—he laid, "I do not discharge them, I leave it to you to indict them at the Sessions."
   Q.  Did they immediately afterwards bring an action against you for false imprisonment? A.  Yes, but that was only to bounce it over me, that I should not go to the Sessions—the Magistrate said, "You can take the rule, and do as you like"—I said, "No, I won't, they may contend that I altered the rule," and the rule has been in the hands of the policeman ever since—the pri soners served me with a process—I dare say that was dose to get rid of me—the solicitor came and tried to laugh me out of it.
