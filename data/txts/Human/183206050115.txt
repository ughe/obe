CAROLINE ADAMS. I am the wife of John Adams- we live at Ratcliff; I let a ready furnished room to the prisoner, containing the articles stated - I never allowed them to take any of them away.
   WILLIAM LANCE. I am a pawnbroker. I have a bolster, two pillows, a looking-glass, a blanket, two sheets, a flat iron, and a set of fire irons pawned at different times, from November to June, chiefly by the female prisoner - she has two daughters who have pawned them; they were most of them pawned weekly, and taken out again on a Saturday night - I can speak positively to one thing the female prisoner pawned, and that is the looking-glass; it was pawned the last time on the 15th of June, and some days before she had pawned it for 5s., and then she had something more on it - these are the duplicates of the things.
   JOSEPH HAWES. I am a pawnbroker. I have a bed, pawned by a woman, in the name of Mary Franklin - I do not know who by.
   JAMES HENRY ANDREWS(Police-constable K 104). I took the female prisoner, and found the duplicate of the bed on her.
   EDWARD CALVER. I am a pawnbroker. I have a quilt, but I do not know who pawned it.
   WILLIAM GRIFFIN(Police-constable K 65.) I took the male prisoner, and found five duplicates on him, which relate to articles at the different pawnbrokers.
   Samuel Franklin Q. Where did I get them? A. I saw your wife give them to you in going to the watch-house.
 CAROLINE ADAMS. These are my property - they continually kept the door locked, whether they were out or at home.
   Simon Franklin Q. Is the shovel yours? A. No, the tongs and poker are, and I have the shovel at home, which corresponds with them.
   Simon Franklin's Defence. I am innocent of all these pawnings - I did not know of the bed being pawned until after I was in custody; we had bed and bedding of our own besides - I had a daughter going to service, and to get her some things, I said I would pawn our bed, but my wife took this bed - I did not know the difference - I could not miss the other things, as we had things of every description; I belonged to the Police, in which I remained till 1831 - I then got a situation as a flax-dresser, and in October last I went to the prosecutor's.
   CAROLINE ADAMS. The quilt was the quilt off the bed, and the bolster and the pillows belonged to it; the prisoners had a bed, but it was a flock bed; they had no looking-glass.
   Mary Ann Franklin. I pawned the things unknown to my husband.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1710.  JOHN BAILEY was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of December , 1 coat, value 10s., the goods of William Gardiner.
   WILLIAM GARDINER. I am a milkman and a shoemaker; I live at Isleworth. In the end of December I lost a coat from my cow-house, which was broken open - I did not see the coat again till the 21st of June - the prisoner was in our neighbourhood at the time I lost it - I had occasionally employed him.
   JOHN GREGORY JACKSON. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Hounslow. I have a blue great-coat, which I took in from Smith, on the 7th of January.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. What is Smith? A. A labouring woman - I asked who she brought it for; she said John Brown.
   ELIZABETH SMITH. I am a labouring woman. I pawned the coat - I was going up the town, and met the prisoner with Thomas Flicker- he was a soldier; he deserted, and was taken again, and is now in Ireland; the prisoner asked me to pawn the coat; I told him I never did such a thing, but as I knew Flicker I would - the prisoner had been carrying the coat - he gave it to Flicker, who gave it to me- he told me to ask 8s. for it, and I got 6s. - I pawned it in the name of John Brown, as I was told.
 Cross-examined. Q. Who first spoke to you? A. Flicker did - the prisoner asked him to ask me - I gave the money to the prisoner, who put it into his right hand pocket, and the duplicate - he thanked me, and they said good day- I had not seen the prisoner before; but I knew Flicker.
   ROBERT PARNHAM. On the 4th of June, at half-past ten o'clock, the prosecutor gave Smith in charge; she told me where she got the coat, and I took the prisoner from her description.
 Prisoner. She said she gave the money to Flicker, and he gave it to me, because he had holes in his pocket.
 SMITH. No; he never touched it.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.

 1712.  JOHN HAWKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 2 planes, value 3s., and 6 gross of screws, value 6s., the goods of John Witney.
   WILLIAM WITNEY. I am a carpenter. On the 5th of July, at eleven o'clock at night, I locked these things up in a counting-house in the yard, in White's-alley, Chancery-lane, and at six in the morning I found the counting-house broken open, and property gone - the prisoner lives in a house adjoining the premises.
   HENRY GAST. I am a carpenter. I was with Witney, and saw the prisoner and his wife together; his wife had a bundle; they parted - the prisoner took the bundle from her, and offered the tools for sale at a shop in Wild-street; we then took him.
   MICHAEL RUSSELL. I am an officer. I took the prisoner with these things - I found a hammer in his room, which fits the marks on the counting-house door.
