Eliza Hopson. I live with my husband, George Hodson, at 10, Augusta-
aquare, Regent's-park. On Saturday evening, the 25th of Sept., about
twenty minutes past nine o'clock, I left the house by the front door, which
I pulled to after me--it is a corner house—there is a window on the Square
side in front-I left that quite safe-I returned in about an hour, the front door
was as I had left it-I missed some things-some one must have entered by
the little window, which was shut when I went out-1 went to the station,
my things were shown me there-the property here is mine.
Cross-examined by Mr. Horry. Q. It was a wash-house where they got
in ? A. Yes-in front of the house-I saw it safe just before I went out.
Jown BATSFORD. I live at 1, Augusta-square. On the evening of the
25th of Sept. I was in my garden, and saw Mrs. Hodson leave her house,
and almost immediately I saw four men walk up to the house, examine the
front of it, and look at the area window-one of them went into a public-
house, and two of them went in front of the house they went away, and
commenced drinking beer and smoking cigars, and then two went in front of
the bouse again, and I heard a breaking of glass--I directly missed one of
the men-I afterwards saw him come out of the front door, go down the
steps, and beckon to the others-one went to him directly, and went into the
house by the street door; the other two stood by the rails, drinking porter
and smoking--the prisoner is one of the men who went in at the front door-
I sent for Mr. Bott, a neighbour, from a public-house opposite—he came in
two or three minutes, and while I was speaking to him the prisoner came out
with a bundle on his head,
Cross-examined. Q. How far off were you in your garden? 4. Fifteen
or twenty yardsthere were no houses between me and Mr. Hodson's-I
could see four persons-it was a moonlight night-it was the prisoner who
gave the beer and cigars to the men—the steps of the square were fifteen or
twenty yards from me I saw the prisoner as he came down the steps,
followed him to his house, and found the bundle there-- it was from Mr. Bott's
garden, three doors off, that I saw the prisoner come down the steps-he came
towards me, but on the opposite side of the way.
CHARLES Cock (policeman.) I took the prisoner-took him to Mr.
Prince's-received a bundle, and took it to the station-the prosecutor's house
is in the parish of St. Pancras.
