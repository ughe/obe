983. WILLIAM WARD  (18) , Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. GOODRICH Prosecuted. FRANCES COWEN. I am shop woman to Mr. John Bennett, a baker, of 172 Tooley Street—at a quarter to nine on 29th July the prisoner came in and asked for a penny cake and tendered this half-crown in payment—I gave him 2 s . 5 d . change—I put the halt-crown in the till where there was one other which I am quite sure was good—it had been given me during the day—I put this in the tester, it broke—the week after I was fetched to Kennington Road Police-station and I picked out the prisoner from several others as the man that tendered the half-crown.
   Cross-examined.  I had not seen you before that night—I knew you again by your height. GEORGE POCOCK. I am landlord of the Windsor Castle, West Street, Southwark—about 9.30 on 29th July my barmaid made a communication to me—I went to the bar and saw the prisoner there—I said "This is a bad half-crown," showing him the one—he said he got it from his master where he worked, and he produced a brass ticket with "E. D. Bennett & Co., Swan Lane Iron works, 58 morning," on it—he asked for the halt-crown back—I refused to give it—I asked him for the money for the ale which he had drunk—he walked outside the house—I followed and gave him into custody by the Spa Tavern, which is just by Spa Road, at the corner of which is the wall of some chocolate works, but some houses separate Spa Road from the wall—it was a dark night.
   Cross-examined.  You said you were very sorry, you did not know it was bad, and pulled out a ticket which I understood you got from your master.
   Re-examined.  I walked by his side the whole way from my house—I did not observe anything. MARIAN SPENCER. "I am barmaid at the Windsor Castle—on 29th July the prisoner came and asked for a pint of ale, tendering a half-crown in payment—I cannot say if this is the one—I rang it on the counter, thought it was bad, and took it to Mr. Pocock and made a communication to him.
   Cross-examined.  I put it in the till, gave it to another lady, and she took it away and gave it to the governor—I did not hear you say you were very sorry. GEORGE GRIFFEN( Policeman M  36). I was called to the Windsor Castle and Mr. Pocock gave the prisoner into my custody for attempting to pass a bad half-crown—he said "I am the one"—I said "You will have to go to the station with me"—I took him to the station—he admitted passing it but did not know it was bad—he made two statements—one that it was given to him at the Borough Market and the other that he got it from his employer—he said he got it for pulling a barrow from Borough Market to some green for a man who gave him a half-crown and left him—on 2nd August I received these five half-crowns from Wm. Skinner—he did not keep the paper in which they were wrapped.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  You said you did not get it at your work but for pulling a barrow from Borough Market to Camberwell Green—but you made another statement as well, that you got it from your employer—they were two distinct statements.
