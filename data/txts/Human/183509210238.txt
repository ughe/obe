   Cross-examined by MR. BALL . Q.  The "Emperor" and "Albion" were with him? A.  Yes; they were summoned at the same time, on the 25th of June, and fined. RICHARD NATHANIEL COLSON. I have had some conversation with Aaron Bray respecting his running his omnibus—he said that unless he could receive 100 l . from the proprietors, he would indict them all for a con spiracy, and ruin them—I remember two of Trevett's men being in custody—Bray said he had been as good as his word—It was not the men he wished to hurt, but their masters, as he could get nothing out of the men.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  You are one of the time-keep ers? A.  Yes; I am at all the parts in the course of the day—I pay the other time keepers—I go from one place to another through the day—I receive the money to pay, from the conductors of the respective omnibuses—I know Mr. New ell—I have not rendered accounts to him latterly—I did some time since—I think the last was six or seven months since—It was the account of what money I received, and what I paid at the time-keepers—I did not account with him as the secretary—I did not know he was secretary—I accounted to him for all the money I received from the conductors, and for all I paid—I never received money from him to make up any deficiency—I have paid trifling sums to him.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Were you subpœnaed as a witness by the prosecu tor? A.  Yes; I was—I was not called.
   MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  I suppose you are subpœnaed on the other side too? A.  I am subpœnaed by the attorney for some of the defendants—I have paid occasional surpluses to Mr. Newell, and I have done so to Mr. Bol ton since, from the time I ceased to account with Newell—I received two shillings and sixpence from the conductors for each omnibus—that is supposing they all pay regularly on Saturday evening—sometimes they do not pay regularly—then it is the middle of the next week before I re ceive their money—I received half-a-crown from each—It amounts to about 6 l . 15 s . a week, and if this has not been all paid to the time-keep ers, I pay the overplus to Mr. Bolton—I pay them 6 l . 10 s . a week—I re collect Mr. Bray's omnibus coming on—Johnson has put on an additional omnibus since then, and so has Pearce—I do not recollect that Newell has—Mrs. Watson has—Bolton has put on two omnibuses since, and Trevett, and Cowderoy, and Pearce—the first remark that Bray made, in reference to his omnibuses, was very soon after they started, in Holborn, I think within a week, it was near his own premises, where his omnibuses were in the habit of stopping to change horses—I think there were several people there—there was generally a crowd round his omnibus—the second conversation was on the morning that two of the defendants were taken into custody on this indictment—It was on a Wednesday, the 22nd of June or July—I have a memorandum of the date—Mr. Bray came up to me when the officer signified to the driver and conductor of the omnibus that he wished to take them into custody, that they were his prisoners—he said, "I am very sorry for you, young men; I have no enmity against you, it is not the men, it is the masters I want, I can make nothing of them; the law must take its course;" he concluded by saying," If they will give me 100 l .
