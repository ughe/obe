pepper, and 3lbs. soda, value 5s. 2d. ; the goods of John Higgins, his master;
and MARY MILNER, feloniously receiving the same; to which
ROCK pleaded Guilty. Aged 16.-Confied Two Months
John HIGGINS. I am an oilman at High-street, Shoreditch. Rock w
in my service about eighteen months. On 23rd March I sent him out with
goods I went to his cart at the door, and found a bottle of turpentine in
bag-he had no order for that I sent my son to watch him-he never came
back-I found my cart at Mr. Gibbs', in Kingsland, next morning-I went to
a house in New North-street, and saw Rock, and Milner's, husband, and after
wards Milner-her husband said, “Tell the truth."
MR. PARRY contended that what the husband said could not be evidence
against the wife; the Court, having consulted Mr. Justice Cresswell, decided
that the statement could be heard.
Witness continued. I said, "You are a wicked woman," and said I should
certainly punish ber-her husband said he was very sorry to have to inform
me that his wife bad been receiving goods of the boy, and he felt it his duty to
let me deal with the two as I liked, for he was in an awkward situation, in
consequence of the communication he had received from his wife; that after
the boy absconded she was very uneasy, and a man came and asked her to let
him have 108., which she owed the boy for the goods; that she gave him
108.; that next day the man came again, and told her he must have 11., and
a coat and waistcoat to cover Rock's retreat out of London; and that, to
avoid his getting into any difficulty, she gave him them- the wife implored
me to forgive her, after which her husband said, "Tell Mr. Higgins the
truth"--I said, "Do not say anything to me that will criminate yourself"
did not say, "Mind what you say, for it will be better or worse for you"
she said, I am very sorry I have done it"-her husband said, “What have you
done ?"-she said, " That I received the goods"-we asked, "What goods !
-she said, she had received two bars of soap, three pounds of soda, one
pound of pepper, and three pounds of candles, from the boy-1 said I should
surely punish her, that she was a wicked woman, and knew how my previous
shopman had robbed me I took Rock to the station-Milner had dealt with
me weekly, between three or four years.
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Have you any vindictive feeling
against her? A. No-she always paid me regularly-I saw her busband
before she came down-he expressed no irritation towards ber-be did not
charge her with having left him-I think he said, I might take her and do
what I liked with her-he asked me to overlook the case.
