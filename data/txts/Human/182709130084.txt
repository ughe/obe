 1701.  ELIZABETH BOYLE was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 1 pelisse, value 20s., the goods of Edward Baylis.
   GEORGE MELLISH GRAHAM. I am servant to Edward Baylis, pawnbroker, of Hampstead-road. The prisoner came in to pawn an article on the 7th of August, but refused to take what was offered her; I was folding up some goods, and had a pelisse laying there - I was called away for a moment, and on returning, she was gone and the pelisse also; I sent a lad after her, but he could not find her; she came again in the afternoon, with the article, and said she would take the money we had offered her: I said we had lost a pelisse: she said if I thought she had it, I had better take her; in the evening Stack brought the pelisse to pawn, and said where she got it: I went with her, and found the prisoner in bed - she was taken.
   MARGARET STACK. The prisoner gave me the pelisse to pawn.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence (written.) I went to the shop to pawn a coat - I was in liquor, and when I got home with it, I found this pelisse in it, which I was ignorant of.
   GEORGE M. GRAHAM. She did not appear in liquor - she frequented our shop; she said it had been folded up with the coat.
     GUILTY. Aged 40. Confined Two Months.
 1702.  DANIEL CORCORAN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , 1 shawl, value 1s., and 1 pair of boots, value 5s., the goods of Levy Hart.
   LEVY HART. I am a salesman, and live in Monmouth-street. On the 9th of August, about nine o'clock at night, the prisoner, three other men, and a woman, came into my shop, and looked at some hats; while I was looking for one, they pushed down a horse with clothes on it, and while I was picking it up, the prisoner went out with the shawl; I followed, and collared him three yards off, and took it from him - the boots were wrapped up in it - he had taken them off the counter.
   TIMOTHY RYAN. I took him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went with two soldiers to buy a hat; one of them was tipsy, and went against the horse, which fell down; I declare I never saw the things till I got to the office.
     GUILTY. Aged 51. Confined Two Months.
 1703.  JOHN EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 1 ham, value 3s., the goods of Charles Docker.
   HENRY COOKE. I am shopman to Charles Docker, cheesemonger, of Great Chapel-street, Westminster. I was at tea on the 20th of July; Norris came in, and said a man had got a ham; I went and saw the prisoner at the counter, with something in his apron; I asked what he had there: he said, nothing: I put my hand under his apron, and this ham fell on the floor.
   JOHN NORRIS. I am shopman to Mr. Docker; the prisoner came in, and asked for a rasher of bacon, and while I turned he took the ham.
   JOHN SIMPSON. I was sent for, and took him; he had no money whatever.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he was intoxicated, and did not know that he had the ham, and pleading distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 38. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1704.  THOMAS FRANCIS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 5 live tame fowls, price 9s., the property of Edward Thomas Rogers.
   EDWARD THOMAS ROGERS. These fowls are not mine, but my brother's. NOT GUILTY.
 1705.  RICHARD HOWARD was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , 1 cheese, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Henry Bonsor.
   HENRY BONSOR. I am a cheesemonger, and live at Shadwell. I was sitting in my back room, and saw three men pass my window twice, and look in: I heard a noise the second time, like something being drawn off the board; I ran out and missed the cheese; I went into the street, and saw the prisoner running down the turning with something under his arm; I called Stop thief! and he dropped the cheese - he was secured, without my losing sight of him.
   ROBERT JEFFREY. I received him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Two Months.
 1706.  GEORGE HARRIS was indicted for stealing on the 7th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of John Manley Gerrish.
   ELIZA GERRISH. I am the wife of John Manley Gerrish. On the 7th of August I had this handkerchief to wash for a gentleman; I missed it about two o'clock in the afternoon, from the end of a table in my room: I had seen it a few minutes before - the prisoner had brought some bread to my house that day; I went up stairs, and when I returned he stood with his back to the table.
   JOHN MANLEY GERRISH. I went next morning with an officer to a house, and found the prisoner in bed with a young woman; I told him about the handkerchief; he asked if I had any children to take it away; I told him No, and he had better give it up, as my wife would lose the family washing: I then gave charge of him, and, as we went along, he said it was pawned, and he had thrown away the duplicate, but the officer found it on the witness.
   EDMUND GROVER. On the morning the prisoner of him; I taken, I bought the duplicate of a handkerchief of him; I sent my wife to the pawnbroker's, and she brought back this handkerchief; I put it on my neck; the officer and Mr. Gerrish came and claimed it.
   RICHARD CONSTANTINE. I am an officer, and apprehended the prisoner; he at first said he had not taken it; we then went to a public-house; some people there said to him, "Why don't you tell the truth? the poor woman will lose her washing:" he then took me to the pawnbroker, who said he was the man who pawned it; he then told me who he had sold the duplicate to.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
