184. ALFRED PRATT was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd
Oct., 1 bag, value 1d.; and 5 sovereigns; the property of Samuel Grora
from his person.
SAMUEL Groves. On the 22nd of Oct., I had five sovereigns in mi
bag, in my right-hand pocket, at twelve o'clock in the day--it was a life
annuity I had received-I was then in a lane near the Two Brewers, at Clar-
ham-I got to that public-house just before twelve-I staid there some
time, and then went to a beer-shop-I came out about nine in the ever-
ing: and when I got half-way home, in Manor-lane, the prisoner came
rustling up against me, and put his band up my thigh-he put his hand
in my pocket, which had no button, and thrust me down-he got my
bag--I saw him with it he put the money into his pocket-he shook
the bag in my face, and wanted me to take it-I took it, and found there
were only two sovereigns out of five-I said I would be one with him. I
would have him taken up- I gave the bag up to the policeman, and the
two sovereigns--I can swear I had five sovereigns in that bag when he
came up to me-I had not taken them out, or changed one of them-
had twelve or fourteen in my pocket beside.
Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK. Q. What may you be? 4. A
bricklayer's labourer-I know the prisoner--we had been drinking togt-
ther at the Two Brewers--I had received the five sovereigns on the 14th-1
never saw them after twelve o'clock on the 22nd--the first public-house !
went into was the Two Brewers, and the prisoner met me there, as I was
going in--we staid there an hour, or it might be longer--we had two or
three pots, perhaps-we then went to a beer-shop, about three o'clock,
and remained till nine-we were there six hours--I paid for seven pots of
ale, but several more came in-one had a glass, and another had a glass-
we did not drink it ourselves-we smoked our pipes--I left first, and the
prisoner came running after me--I could walk as well as I can now-the
prisoner did not lead me I did not want leading- I did not rest my arm
on his-I never asked him to see me home, and say, after some time, "Lat
me try if I can't go alone," or put my hands into my pocket, and then
reel dowo-be thrust me down--we only had one glass of gin apiece at the
Two Brewers--I said, before the Magistrate, that the prisoner shook the
bag in my face, and said, “Here is your money, Sam"-when I had fallen
down, the prisoner endeavoured to help me up-after this occurred, the
prisoner walked away from me-after I said he had robbed me, we walked
about twenty yards together-Mrs. Edwards, the witness, knows me per-
fectly well-I went home I did not call a policeman-I could not find
one-this was in Manor-lane, not 100 yards from the beer-shop, about
400 yards from Nelson-row, where I live-I slept that night where I
lodge, which is at a beer-shop.
SARAH EDWARDS. I am the wife of William Edwards, a coal-dealer, in
Little Manor-lane-at balf-past nine o'clock in the evening, on the 22nd
of October, I heard a person fall under my window-I looked out and saw
Groves on the ground, and the prisoner's hand in his pocket--Groves
said, "You have robbed me of my money"-the prisoner said he had not
- I think the prisoner staid with him five or ten minutes, and then he
dragged him across the road-I did not see any more of him.
Cross-examined. Q. Groves was very far gone, was he not? A. Not
80 far but he knew what he was about-he did not wish to be dragged, but
he could not get away-
