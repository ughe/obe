   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Now, upon the subject of this private examina tion, was the clerk to the Magistrate present who read over the paper which you had signed? A.  Yes—it was purely a voluntary statement made by me to my attorney to be forwarded to the Magistrate.
   Q.  Did the Magistrate give you any caution, or make any observation to you on the subject of your statement before it was read? A.  To the best of my recollection he said it might be used against me, or it might be destroyed—he said if the statement proved untrue I should be put in the bar, and it would be used against me—at that time no promise was made me that I should be a witness—I did not know I was to be received and examined for the Crown till long after. ISAAC SOLOMONS. I am the son of Henry Solomons. My father had a shop in the Strand, and another shop at No. 9, Coventry-street—I assisted him in the business at the shop in the Strand—I was at that shop on Easter Tuesday last—the prisoners, Mrs. Abrahams and Mr. Moses came there—Moses came first—I was in the front shop next the street when he came—my father was there—Moses spoke to him—he merely said he wished to speak to him, and they went to the passage, and went up stairs—they re mained up stairs about ten minutes—Moses then left—about three-quarters of an hour after he was gone Mrs. Abrahams came—she went into the back shop—my father was there with her—there are furnaces in the back shop—I do not know whether there was any fire in them at that time, I believe there was—I did not remain at home all the time Mrs. Abrahams was there—my father directed me to go out—in consequence of directions from him I went to Ransom's, his banker—I returned and told ray father what had happened there—I went out again, and took a cheque for 80 l . with me, drawn by my father on Ransom's—I took the change I got for it to my father—I know Woolf, a tailor in Regent-street—in consequence of my father's directions I went to Woolf, and received a cheque of 100 l . from him on Ransom's—I had also received three£5 notes and five sovereigns from Woolf—I went to Ransom's with the 100 l . cheque of Woolf's, and received the amount of it—I do not remember what I received from Ransom's in payment of that cheque—I delivered it to my father in the same state I received it—I took a bar of gold to Cock and Johnson's the same day, and they gave me a cheque for 400 l . on Williams and Labouchere's bank on account of that bar—I presented it for payment, and received a£300 note and a£100 note—this is the£300 note I received ( looking at the one marked No.  15)—I took it to the Bank of England and got for it twenty £10 notes, and twenty£5 notes—I changed the£100 note for a hundred sovereigns—I carried them back to ray father—I found Mrs. Abrahams
