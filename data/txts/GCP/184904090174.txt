Thomas JACKMAN. I am house-surgeon to St. Bartholomew's Hospital.
The child was brought there on 27th Jan.-I made a post mortem examination
-the cause of death was a burn--the body was in a very emaciated state ; the
lungs were affected with inflammation of the lower lobes to a considerable
extent, otherwise the child was healthy; there were some recent bruises on
the head and legs-there was a bruise on both eyebrows, which could not be
produced by a child falling on the floor there were about three bruises on
each leg, corresponding with each other, as if the two legs were struck at the
same time-there was no disease about the child to account for the emaciated
state in which I found it.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you been admitted as a surgeon
1. About twelve months—I pay for my place at the hospital, I have no
salary-I cannot always tell by inspection what disease has or has not existed.
MR. PRENDERGAST called
Mrs. Parker. I am the female prisoner's aunt, and have known the child
from its birth—it has always been a very ailing child, and was in the country
with its grandmother more than a year-it returned to town, and I saw it
almost every day till its death-I lived right opposite, and have done so
twenty-one years--I never saw the father or mother illtreat it in my life-I
never saw any marks on his person-the parents have been in a great state of
want, but they treated him as well as their circumstances would admit they
have been in a state of starvation-I have known them to breakfast on 2d. a
morning for the last two months : half an ounce of coffee, half an ounce
of sugar, and a pennyworth of bread, and I have given them firing to do it
with I have known them to be from Monday to Wednesday without fire or
food, she and her two children, when he has been out butchering with Mr.
Bamford, but he has had nothing to do since Feb. or March last-I recol-
lect the child being taken to the bospital; the mother carried it-it never
complained of its mother's treatment; it wanted to go home to her at the
hospital-I once threw a bit of bread in at the window to it, and many times
I have thrown to it a bit of sweet-stuff, or gingerbread, or an apple when I
have seen it at the window, and he bas come down to the door and I have
given him something for himself, and his sister too; both when the mother
