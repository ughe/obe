Cross-examined. Q. How long did you see them before they got into the
coach ? A. A few seconds, a very short time-neither of them made any
complaint to me that either had received an injury of any kind—I got
down to let them in- I do not remember their faces- I did not see any-
thing that denoted that either of them had been subjected to any violence-1
saw no blood, or anything of the sort.
JOHN GEORGE Rogers. I was a City policeman in November, 1844_n
the night this occurred I was on duty in the Minories, and recollect seeing
some persons there who afterwards got into a backney-coach-I cannot say
who stopped the coach-I was close to them when it stopped-I cannot say
who opened the coach-door-I had my light with me it was a very dark
wet night--both the men were the worse for liquor I did not assist either
of them in- I was standing by the door at the time they were getting in, on
the right side-my light was so directed that I could see them both-they
got in by the assistance of the coachman-one got in with as great facility as
the other-neither of them made any complaint of having received any injury
--there was nothing that attracted my attention, more than that they were in
liquor saw a third person cross the road, about two or three minutes before
they got into the coach--I had not been walking up the Minories with these
two persons - I was coming down my beat there were three persons cross-
ing the road as I was going down my beat, and they came up to me by the
railway bridge-the coach came up at the time, and it was hailed, by
who, I do not know -- the two persons got into the coach--I cannot say what
became of the third person--they had the appearance of being all three 10-
gether, but one was separate-I cannot say how far he was from the two.
Cross-examined. Q. How near were you to the two that got into the
coach ? A. Within two or three yards, close to the door-I did not take
particular notice of their faces--I noticed nothing about the person of either
to denote any act of violence.
GEORGE MARRIOTT (policeman G 31.) I was on duty near Constitution-
row, Gray's Inn-road, and came up shortly after the coach came up the
persons were crossing the pavement from the carriage to the house wheu I
came up-one appeared to be very much in liquor I cannot say positively
about the other had a light and saw an umbrella in the coach, leaning
against the front seat, with the ferrule end upwards-there was blood on the
ferrule end, and also on the covering of the umbrella-there was also some
at the bottom of the carriage-I did not notice blood on one of the cushions.
