ROBERT RICHARDS, Esq. I am a barrister on the 19th of July I paid the
prisoner a 101. note, ten sovereigns, and a half-sovereign-I should say I paid
it to the credit of both the parties-Mr. Clark had not authorized me to pay
any-I was aware of the purport of the letter which Mr. King had written-
I went in about three o'clock, and found Mr. King writing the letter-I ada
vised him to finish his letter, and let it go forward-I paid the money-I was
sure that Mr. Clark would recognize it the next day-I was aware of the
letter- I would have acted upon it.
COURT to MR. CLARK. 'Q. Whose check was this? A. I am the drawer
of the bill-I presume the check was my owo-the prisoner told me he had
got the check-I told him to go and take up the bill—the 201. 10s. I pre
sume was also paid to me-I sent the prisoner with 401. 10s. to take up the
bill with the check, and 201. 108. which I had got of Mr. Richards-he told
me he had paid it for me.
MR. METCAL. Q. What were the duties of the prisoner to you? A.
To make up medicines and any drugs that might be made up for sale
to go out for me, in fact to do anything and everything I ordered him if
he had refused to take up this bill, or to do any other menial work for me, I
should have compelled him.
Court, Q. If he had not done this, should you have discharged him? A.
Yes--he had done it for me often before.
MR. METCALF. Q. He was taken by you to assist in your professional
duties? A. Yes I should have discharged him if he had not done a duty
of this sort, because I considered it was what he ought to have done for me
-I should not have conceived he was bound to go and take up a bill-he
might have said, “That is not physic"-but no master I think would retain
his servant an hour if he bad refused.
Court. Q. Did you employ him as your servant, and did be take the
bill as such ? A. Yes.
MR. METCALY. Q. Did you employ him as a servant, or as an assistant
in your medical profession ? A. As my servant-I think had he in any way
refused to do this, and I had dismissed him, I should have had to pay his
month's salary--this was one of those sort of things that no medical man
could have kept an assistant half an hour after, if he had not done it.
Guilty of larceny.- Confined Six Months.
1956. MARTHA SEERS was indicted for stealing 8 cigars, valae 9d.;
the goods of Rachel Dallen, her mistress.
WILLIAM DALLEN. I am a licensed victualler. I act for my mother
Rachel Dallen, who lives at Chelsea—the prisoner was her servant-on the
12th of Aug., I missed some cigars from the cigar-box-I found them
amongst the ginger-beer bottles, at nine o'clock at night-I afterwards gave
the prisoner into custody.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'BRIEN. Q. How long has the prisoner lived with
your mother, is it fifteen or sixteen years ? A. I do not know-I believe my
sister is here to give her a character-my sister is married, she has not been
living under the same roof with me, and I should be sorry for her to do so.
GEORGK LINDON. I am pot-boy to Mrs. Dallen. On the 12th of Aug.,
the prisoner came to me, and said she had got eight cigars concealed
amongst the ginger-beer bottles, and if I would accept of them she would be
much obliged to me- I told my master-she said she could not get out at
the back door, as it was locked-she often goes through the stable.
