   ANGELIOUS BETRAUN. I was with Roberts, and saw the prisoner with another lad; the prisoner left him about two yards behind, and went up to the prosecutor - he suddenly turned away from him, and ran; I pursued, and caught him - he threw the handkerchief down; I took it up, and Roberts told the prosecutor, who claimed it; I did not say."We must not go to Bow-street," nor did Roberts - I took him first to the watch-house, and they would not take him in, because it was not eight o'clock. I took him to Bow-street - it was then just eight, and the door was shut; I took him to the watch-house again, and next morning having to go to Marlborough-street on another charge, I took him there, as I could not be at two places.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw Betraun running after another boy; he turned, and took me.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
 961.  JOHN BRISTOW was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of March , 1 piece of wood, value 9s., the goods of Ann Aldridge,
   ALFRED WESTON. I am in the employ of Ann Aldridge, timber-merchant, of Bunhill-row. On the 7th of March I was in the yard between twelve and one o'clock; I saw the prisoner loitering about the gate for half an hour; I watched him, and saw him take this plank of Spanish mahogany; he took it outside, set it down, and ran down Bunhill-row; our porter followed him, and I fetched the plank in.
   STEPHEN BOWER. I am the porter. I saw the prisoner lurking about the door; he came and took the wood out; I ran after him, and fetched him back.
   JOHN BROWN. I am an officer, and saw the prisoner running.
 Prisoner's Defence. I could not lift that wood off the ground; I was running to see Punch and Judy when I was taken.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped, and Discharged.
 962.  WILLIAM DRAPER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of March , 1 watch, value 2l.; 1 seal, value 7s.; 1 key, value 7s., and 1 watch-chain, value 6d, the goods of Charles Hopewell , from his person.
 CHARLES HOPEWELL. On the 5th of March, I was in  White Horse-street, Stepney, at near eleven o'clock that night; when I was opposite the watch-house, I met the prisoner coming towards me, and made way for him to pass on the left-hand side against the wall; he took hold of my watch-chain; I collared him - a scuffle ensued, and we both fell; he was uppermost, and when we were down he said, "Let me go," or something to that effect: I said,"No; if you go, you shall take me with you" - I called, Watch! the two headboroughs came, and took us both into the watch-house - I gave charge of him; they searched him, and could not find the watch; there was no one else near me - I am sure he was the person.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Were you quite sober? A. I had been drinking, but had not lost my senses - I had two glasses of gin and water - my watch was brought into the watch-house without the case; I had not seen him throw it away; I saw nobody else near me.
   JOHN GROOM. I am a headborough. I saw the prisoner on the ground, on the top of the prosecutor; when they got up, the prosecutor said, "I charge this man with taking my watch; take him into custody:" the prisoner said nothing - I took him to the watch-house. Clark found the watch about six yards from where I took the prisoner.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he sober? A. He was neither drunk nor sober - he gave a full description of the watch.
   ABRAHAM CLARK. I was with Groom; we heard a noise in the street; ran out of the watch-house, and saw the prosecutor flat on his back, and the prisoner on him - I took hold of the prisoner - Groom helped the prosecutor up; he was without his hat - I went out with a candle, and found the watch in the same line as I had brought the prisoner - the prosecutor described it - the case has not been found.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not the prisoner say he knew nothing about the watch? A. No, he never said a word.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined One Year.
 953.  WILLIAM BASS and HANNAH BASS were indicted for stealing, on the 3d of March , 2 sacks, value 1s. 6d., and 15 lbs. weight of potatoes, value 6d., the goods of William Collins.
 WILLIAM COLLINS. I live in  Baltic-street. The prisoners, who are husband and wife, lodged with me - I deal in coals and potatoes. On the 3d of March I was going into my cellar, I told my boy to light a candle, and go down first; he did so, and found Hannah Bass in the cellar; he told me, and I took hold of her as she came up the stairs, and said, "This is the way you do it." She said, "What?" She then went into their room, which is on the ground floor; I went in with her, and saw a sack of mine on the floor; I took it up, and saw the boards were broken, and there was a hole down into the cellar; I then looked under their bed, and saw two sacks belonging to a man whom I deal with, and a quantity of potatoes, some kidney and some other - another lodged of mine and William Bass were sitting in the room, and I said, "This is a pretty start:" the other woman said, "What?" I said, "Mrs. Bass was in my cellar" - she clapped her hands together, and said, "I dare say she went there to hang herself" - the man said he knew nothing about it - I sent them all to the watch-house.
   HENRY WIDDERSON. I live with the prosecutor; I went into the cellar about three o'clock that afternoon; I saw Mrs. Bass in the corner of the cellar - I came up and said, "Uncle there is Mrs. Bass in the cellar;" she came up - he laid hold of her - there was a board wrenched up in their room, and a hole down into the cellar.
   ROBERT LOCK. I took the prisoner - there had been a square hole cut in the floor of the room, but the boards had been fixed down again, and required force to get them up.
 Hannah Bass put in a written defence, stating, that being in great distress, and her family in a state of starvation, she had taken a few potatoes, but that the sacks were only used as a covering instead of blankets.
