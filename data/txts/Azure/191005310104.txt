BEFORE MR. JUSTICE GRANTHAM.
(Friday, June 10.)
FAKE, Thomas (38, scaffolder), feloniously stabbing Charlotte Fake
with intent to kill and murder her or with intent to do her grievous
bodily harm.
CHARLOTTE FAKE. Prisoner is husband; we have five children ;
we lived at 153, Darwin Road, Soumt\ Ealing. On May 2 1 went to the
police court and took out a summons against prisoner for a—ault. I
was afraid to sleep in the house after that, so I only went there in
the daytime to look after the children. On May 7, while I vas there,
prisoner came into the kitchen; he said, "Why did ou summons
meV' I said, "I must defend myself"; he said, "1Yhave come to
do you in and your sister afterwards." I said, " Don't be silly, don't
do it. " He had a knife in his hand ; he made a dash at me and we
both fell to the door ; he stabbed me four times. I wu taken to the
hospital and kept there ten days.
Cross-examined. We have been married 14 years. Prisoner was in
the Army. He went out to India and came back on furlough to fetch
me and the children ; he altered his mind and, instead of
there, he left the Army. I deny that it was on my account he
did this ; I was quite ready to go to India with him. We did not live
happily together ; he was a good father to the children, but he und to
ill-use me he would come home boozed about every other week and
give me a black eye now and then.
CHRISTOPHER FITTS, prisoner's young stepbrother, who was in the
kitchen when the trouble commenced, corroborated prosecutrix's story.
JOHN MARTLOVE, house surgeon at West London Hospital, said that
on grosecutrix being brought to the hospital she was suffering from four
Sta wounds, two of which had .pentrated the lung substance and
might have given rise to complications resulting in death. Some force
must have been exerted to inflict the wounds with the knife produced.
Prosecutrix has now quite recovered.
