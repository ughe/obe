CHARLES CLARKE (Policeman M 250). On 7th July I was in Grange
Road, saw a crowd, and met Hansen, whom I stopped-immediately
afterwards Dillon came up and said "That is one of the men"
directly after I saw Mrs. Smith, who said “That is the third man"
-Hansen said "I will go quiet; I have done nothing"-he went
very quietly-he said not a word at the station.
WALTER HOPKINS (Policeman M 265). Shortly after 3 o'clock on 7th
July I was in Bermondsey Street - I Paw Leary being carried face down.
wards to the station-he was very violent; I assisted in carrying him-
I think six had hold of him--at the station he was very violent; I
assisted in taking off his shoes--I saw Wiltshire was very uneasy at
the station--I sat on the table; I was in plain clothes-1 saw him put
his left hand into his left-hund pocket, and take something out-I could
see something in his hand, but could not discern what it was-he rubbed
bis left leg, as if he had been kicked, and passed his left hand down to
about his shoe, and then I saw his hand disappear behind his leg-
the inspector then said "Bring them into the charge-room”- they were
brought in and placed in the dock-I walked straight to the seat where
Wiltshire was sitting in the corner of the room, and picked up this purse
(produced)-it contained 14 sovereigns, two rings, four foreign coins, and
& farthing--the prosecutor identified it--when charged Wiltshire said
"You did not see me do anything "-I said “Yes I did "- I was about
three yards from him.
Cross-examined by Wiltshire. The room was full of witnesses-the
word was given to bring you in at the same time-& constable was
standing in front of you, but he had not got hold of you-I was looking
after you.
RICHARD LEONARD TUNGAY. I am a furniture dealer of 39, Hill Street,
Peckham-on 7th July, about 3.30, I saw a crowd and went to see the
matter, and saw Harding with Leary-Harding was bleeding, his helmet
was at the back of his head and he seemed as if he could not hold
the prisoner-I said: "If you are innocent why don't you go?"-he hit
me in the eye-he was very violent, I assisted to take him to the station
-he kicked me several times in the private parts.
Cross-examined by Leary. I said at the station you kicked me eighteen
or nineteen times in the testicles with your knee- I did not say I had a
black eye, my face does not bruise.
Leary in his defence said they were all the worse for liquor, that he was
innocent of the charge, but that he was a bit violent. Wiltshire in his defence
said they were all perfectly drunk. Hansen said the prosecutor was very
drunk, und behaved so badly in the cab that they wanted to leave him, and
that he (the prisoner) did not know that the prosecutor had lost his money
GUILTY. Leary then PLEADED GUILTY** to a conviotion of felony at this
Court in January, 1884, and Wiltshire to one in Otober, 1887, in the name
of George Smith. There were other indictments against Leary for assaults
on the police, and on Mr. Tungay. LEARY and WILTSHIRE-Pong
Years' Penal Servituule each. HANSEN-Eighteen Months' Hard Labour.
The Grand Jury commended the conduct of Harding, Tungay, and
Patchell, in which the Court and Jury concurred, and also commended Mrs.
Smith and Dillon. The Court awarded 21 to Mrs. Smith, and to Dillno,
Bigrave, Tungay and Patchell, ll. each.
