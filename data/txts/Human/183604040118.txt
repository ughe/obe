 969. PHILIP PARR and JOSEPH SNELL were indicted for a robbery on William Clerk , on the   4th of March , putting him in fear and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 watch, value 2 l  .; 4 seals, value 2 l  .; and 1 watch-chain, value 6 d  .; his property. WILLIAM CLARK. I am a cooper's labourer, and live in Easton-street. On Friday evening, the 4th of March, about half-past eight o'clock, I was coming from home, and going to Laystall-street—coming up Mount Plea sant, I saw two young men scrummaging or fighting together; I could not well tell which—I went on one side—I passed them—Parr after me, and caught me round the waist, and threw me into the kennel—I did not know him before—he thew me down and doubled his knee into my belly—I lost my senses then for a bit, and laid flat on my back, with my heard be ing bruised against the paved stones—when I recovered, I got on my hands and knees—I found my watch lying on the pavement by the side of me—the buttons were all torn off my waistcoat, which was torn open; and I found my watch-fob pulled right out of my trowsers—I got up, and put my watch into my side-pocket, and buttoned my coat—I followed the pri soner Parr into Pool's-buildings—he returned from Pool's-buildings into Laystall-street, when I met a policeman, and gave him in charge—the policeman took him into a wine-vault to search him, and asked me for the watch—I delivered it to him—I lost the seals and things, besides the watch—they were all lying by my side—I picked them all off the pavement.
