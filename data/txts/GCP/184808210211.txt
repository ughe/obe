Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Which way, do you turn to go into
the parlour 4. You must go into the passage, and pass the stairs leading
to the bed-room-the parlour is beyond the stairs, and the bar is on this side
of them—there is a back door that leads into the garden--there is no com-
munication with any other premises --the back door is shut, it was not
fastened, we always keep it shut-the bar commands a view of the passage
-I think a person could not slip in at the back door wbile my back was
torned-I should hear a step coming in that way-I might not hear them if
they came in stealthily.
Cross-examined by Mr. Robinson. Q. Would you not be just as likely
to hear a step on the stairs ? A. It would depend on how a person went up
-it is almost an impossibility for anybody to come in the back way-I
was not in the parlour when Chappel left-he could bave gone out at the
back door-he did not come out the front way-all four went away together
after he had returned he was only out a short time there is a watering
place at the back door-I last saw the money safe at eight o'clock-the pri-
soners were there half an hour-it is impossible to say how many people
were there at the same time, thete were several coming for supper beer-I do
not swear distinctly to Chappel-I can to Lawrence.

Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How is the garden surrounded ? A. By a pailing-
it is a row of houses-there are houses and gardens both sides of us--there is
no opening into a street or lane-there are not houses at the back-the
pailings are half as high as your head-if a person got over our pailing he
Would get into the adjoining garden, and that leads to another house.be
back door was fastened with two bolts and a latch-if the latch had been
lifted I do not know that I should have heard it in the bar there is a sum-
mer-bouse at the bottom of the garden, and a hedge behind that if a person
got over there he would come into other people's gardens, not into a lane.
