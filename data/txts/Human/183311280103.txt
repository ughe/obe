Newbry;" I always copy the address of money-letters, into this book; and the name, and street, with great correctness and care - I generally copy the Christian name when I copy it at length, and the surname I always copy at length - according to the best of my belief, the entry is a fair copy of the letter - it is Grafton-street, "Grafton" is spelt with one "f" and an "a" - I recollect the letter being franked - my attention was called to the entry, about a month after the 10th of October - I supposed that letter was franked, because it corresponded with the one stated to be lost - (I never could have written "bry" for "berry,") that is the only reason I suppose the letter to have been franked - we always lay the money letters in a small box, made for that purpose; the box was not locked; it was in the centre of the office - master has nine children and a wife in the house, who have access to it; it is a shop, but the office is parted off with a book-shop; there is an entrance from the book-shop into the office - the shop is much frequented - we should see a person passing from the shop to the office; there is no door, but there is an open place to the shop - the postmaster takes the letters out of the box; if he is unwell, his wife, Mrs. Deck, does it, and if she was unwell, I should take them - about four persons in general have access to the box - the post goes out at ten o'clock, the shop is closed at nine o'clock, and then the office duty is done, till the mail stops; the letter arrived on the 10th of October, and was sent to London the same night.
   JOHN DECK. I am postmaster, of Bury, St. Edmunds. On the evening of the 10th of October, I did not open the bag which came from Lord Bristol's; a letter bill is forwarded from the office every night, to town, with the letters (looking at a bill) - this signature, John Deck, is mine - I forwarded that bill on the 10th of October, with the letters, as it is now (reads) "10th of October, unpaid letters, for London, 3l. 17s. 6d. unpaid letters passing through London, 16s.; paid letters 1l. 4s.; money-letters, one name Ascroft, anothernamed Newberry"- here is at the bottom of the bill, a direction, that letters containing cash, should be entered, and wrapped up in the bill; and I wrapped up myself, that night, the two letters in the name of Ascroft and Newberry, and forwarded them that night.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you see this book? A. Every night; as the letters are sent off I enter in the book the amount of each bill, the amount of postage from each town, the memorandums are inserted previous to my inserting the amount of postage - on the 10th of October, when I wrote the amount of postage, the memorandums were there - the business of my post-office ought to be conducted with care, and I believe it is; I generally look at the memorandums, containing the name and addresses of the money-letters - I saw that the addresses of the money-letters corresponded with the book, and the letter bill, and I did so on the 10th of October; I discovered no mistake - my clerk opened Lord Bristol's bag - he generally opens it - I have no recollection of his doing so, on the 10th of October - if he is unwell I open it myself - and sometimes part of my family - my family have not assisted me in the business, for months - as regards the bag, I am confident they have not opened it; having examined the bill with the book, I despatch the bill with the moneyletters in it to London - the other money-letter is entered in the bill by the name of Uscott.
   CHARLES WILLIAM BARNARD. I am a clerk in the General post-office. On the 11th of October, I was clerk of the money-book - it is my duty to enter in the book such money-letters, as arrived at the post-office, and to receive the letters - a receipt is made out for each letter, which is handed to the letter-carrier, and is brought back to me, and delivered to them - on the 11th of October, I find a money-letter entered "Newberry, Grafton-street, Manchester-square, from Bury, in Suffolk," signed for by J. Borrett, in the hand-writing of the prisoner - (looking at the bill) I received this bill, that morning; here are my initials to it as having received two money-letters - I have another entry, of one in the name of Ascroft, Great Leonard-street, next to it - I have not a doubt that entry was taken from this bill - I delivered the letter addressed to Newberry, to the prisoner.
 Cross-examined. Q. How often have you seen the prisoner write? A. Perhaps fifty or one hundred times; I have been in the Post-office for more than four years - I swear that is his signature; I have not the least doubt of it - we are as particular as we can be in making entries in the book; considering the time we have to do it in - we make mistakes sometimes - I have spelled the name "Newbury;" I copied it from the letter, not from the bill - I cannot swear that it was "bury" on the letter; it is "berry" on the bill apparently - I should not think of contracting to "bry" purposely - with the greatest care mistakes will happen; I do not call it a great mistake - we write as carefully as possible.
   DENNIS NEWBERRY. My wife is in the service of Lord Jermyn, and was so in October. I live at No. 6, Grafton-court, Paradise-street, Manchester-square. I expected to receive a letter from my wife with money in it, and I never got it - I do not know when it was to come - I wrote to her, when she wrote to me to know why I did not answer this letter, which I had never received - I was sent for to the Post-office afterwards - I have never received a letter from her with a sovereign in it.
