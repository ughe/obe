   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  Whether Loup had advanced money on them or not, you did not know, but he was interested in their dis position? A.  Yes—I have known him about twenty-seven years—he was formerly principal clerk to Tail and Co., Manchester warehousemen, of 8, Old Jewry, for two, three, or four years, till Mr. Tail died—he was then in busi ness for two or three years, in Cateaton-street—the firm was Loup, Godby, and Co.—they became bankrupts, or something of the sort, in 1840—he was afterwards in the house of Pearman and Co., of Glasgow, and managed a house of theirs at 8, Old Jewry, for about eighteen months, up to just before last March—I have known him down to the time of this change—I never knew or heard of his being charged with dishonesty—as far as I knew he bore a respectable, upright character—he has latterly been a commission-agent, selling for any persons who employed him—he was so up to the time of this charge. FREDERICK TUCKER. I went to Loup and told him I had heard that he had some tools for sale—he went with me to Johnson and Day's, and I saw some tools there—I asked where he had got them from—he did not say, but said he merely had them for sale—I asked him once or twice where they came from, and he evaded giving an answer every time.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  I suppose by your knowing where to find him, you knew where he lived and carried on business? Q.  Not be fore that day—I was referred to him by a Mr. Buck, over the water—I am a tool-maker—Loup came and bought a few tools of me a week or so after this—he paid for them—it was a trifling transaction—I knew these tools to be my father's make directly I saw them, and they had my father's name on them—my father is a plane-maker—I went to look at them, understanding they were for sale—they were readily sold to me.
   MR. BODKIN. Q.  Where was it you had the conversation with him? A.  Going from his house to Johnson and Day's—I did not tell him who I was—I did not ask him who was the maker of the tools. CHARLES PROCTOR. I have been in Mr. Loup's employ as porter, on and off, for four or five months, for four or five days a week—he told me in Aug. he had some tools to sell, and said they were at Johnson and Day's—he wanted to know if I knew anybody who would purchase them—I was living in the Waterloo-road—I said there was a person named Buck living close by, and I would call and leave the invoice with him as I went home—he had given me an invoice, which I left at Mr. Bucks, at the corner of Oakley-street, Waterloo-road—I saw big son, and told him the tools were for sale, that was in pursuance of directions given me by Loup—I said I hoped he would come to look at them as soon as possible, as there was a Spanish gentleman about to look at them next day—next day I went to Aldermanbury, to Loup's office, saw him, and told him what had passed—I used to go there every day—I never saw the other defendants, except at the Mansion-house—Seager I never saw till yesterday.
