1653. MARGARET WILTSHIRE and MARY SHEA were indicted
for stealing, on the 6th of May, 1 purse, value 1s. ; 1 seal, 3s. ; and 4 half-
crowns: the goods of William Kay, from his person.
WILLIAM KAY. I am a private in the first batallion of Grenadier Guards.
I was at the Forresters' Arms on Saturday the 6th of May, between three and
four o'clock in the afternoon-Wiltshire came in and asked me if her hus-
'band was in the hospital-I had seen her before, and knew the person whom
she described - I said I believed he was-Shea then came in-Wiltshire
spoke to her, and I gave her some beer-Wiltshire then went away, and came
back-I staid there till six-I then went away with the two prisoners and
another woman while I was going down Gloucester-place, talking to Wilt-
shire, I felt my right hand coat-skirt fall-Shea was walking close behind me
-I put my hand into my pocket, and missed my purse and four hall-crowns,
and a small metal seal-Wiltshire was at that time close to me, and bad hold
of my arm-Shea was about a pace in the rear of me when I turned 1 ac-
cused Shea of taking my purse-she said she had not, and she ran away--I
caught her, and took her to the Sovereign, in Park-row-I found Wiltshire
there-I saw Wiltshire throw my purse on the ground it had my seal still
in it, but no money.

Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. The prisoners are sisters, are they
not? 4. I do not know-I do not know that Wiltshire is married to a com-
rade of mine--the one she describes as her husband is in the hospital-
Margaret Burke was in the public-house with me I am in confinement in
the barracks for being absent-I was a lance-corporal, and am now reduced
to a private- I was at the beer-shop with Wiltshire and Burke about half-past
three, and Shea came in a little after-Shea said she had come to fetch her sister
-Wiltshire asked her to stop, and we continued drinking--about four o'clock
Shea's mother came --I did not ask her to drink-she went away, leaving the
prisoners there when I went out I did not ask the prisoners to go with me
--when I went into the first public house, I had about 178., ard when I left
it I had about 98., besides the four half-crowns--the silver was in my left-
hand coat pocket--I had not taken my purse out at all I did not say when
the purse was thrown down, that it was of no use without the money - I was
quite sober-I am quite certain that the purse was not in my hands, and the
hands of the prisoners while I was in the public-house-Wiltshire did not
appear to be tipsy-I did not make her drunk-we did not drink three pints.
altogether-she might have been the worse for liquor-she did not say in the
public-house that she would take care of the money, and pay for any more
liquor that came in- she never saw the money I had- I did not say I would
spend it in drink.
