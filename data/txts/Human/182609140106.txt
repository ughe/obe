 1537.  JAMES HEMMINGS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July, 1 cage, value 1s.; and 1 singing bird, value 1s., the goods of George Hirdsfield.
 GEORGE HIRDSFIELD. I live at No. 4,  City-gardens. - I had a thrush in a cage which I missed on the 16th of July, I received information; I went out, and saw the prisoner at the bottom of my brother Thomas's garden, at No. 1 - he took off his hat, went into the privy, and threw something down - I found the cage against the back gate - this is the bird and the cage; the bird is very remarkable, never having had any tail.
   JOHN EDWARDS. I live in City-gardens; I am a silversmith. On the morning of the 16th of July, about five o'clock, I opened my back window, and saw the prisoner; I thought he made a mistake - considering it might be a thoroughfare; he then went away - I came to my front window, where I saw him returning; I dressed, came down, and saw him in Hirdsfield-garden - I asked what he did there? he said, he was taken ill, and had been to the privy; he ran away, and I called, "Stop thief! and ran after him - Hirdsfield hearing the noise, jumped out of the window, and he was stopped.
   THOMAS HIRDSFIELD. I heard the cry of Stop thief! I dropped from the window; I took hold of the prisoner, but being naked, I left him a few minutes to put on my clothes; I saw him go and throw something down the privy; I went and found it was the bird in this handkerchief.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Whipped and Discharged.
 1538.  HENRY MACK was indicted for feloniously assaulting Peter Barry , on the 30th of July , with intent his goods from his person, feloniously to steal.
 PETER BARRY. I am a  seaman, and lodge at the Black-horse, Dowgate-hill. On the night of the 30th of July, about half-past twelve o'clock, I was going across Tower-hill; the prisoner came up and struck me on the right cheek, and with that he caught hold of the chain of my watch - I closed my arms, and he could not get it out; another then came up and struck me with a stick - I sung out Murder! and the other ran away - the watchman came up, and the prisoner got away, but he took him.
   JOHN KING. I am the watchman; I went up and saw the prisoner and another running - I pursued and took the prisoner; he asked "What was the matter?" - Barry came up and said, "I will swear that is the man who struck me, and tried to take my watch."
 Prisoner. Q. Was not I walking about? A. Yes, when I took you - but you was running when I first saw you.
   THOMAS OBORNE. I am watch-house keeper. I asked the prisoner how he came to assault the man in that way? he said, he knew nothing about it.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1539.  THOMAS GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , 1 pair of shoes, value 7s., the goods of James Darvill.
 JAMES DARVILL. I live at  Uxbridge; I am a shoemaker. On Saturday, the 5th of August, I missed a pair of shoes about five o'clock, which I had given to the prisoner on the Friday morning, to make for me - he worked in my house; he came to work about seven o'clock on Saturday morning; I told him they were missing; he said, he had left them under his seat the night before; I said, I was determined to find out who stole them, as I had lost a watch and some other articles - he said, he he knew nothing about the shoes; and I might as well say, he stole the watch - I wished him to stop while I went to get somebody.
   JOHN FARRANT. I went to the shop of John King, where I got the shoes.
   JOHN KING. I live at Uxbridge, one mile from Hillingdon; the prisoner brought me these shoes on the 4th of August - he said, he had made them for a customer, and they were too large - I bade him three shillings; he said, I should have them, as they were made on his own account.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Twelve Months.
