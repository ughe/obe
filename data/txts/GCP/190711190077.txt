To prisoner Campbell. I do not know you at all.
Susan SHAPCOTT, Ashley Villa, Lancaster Road, New Barnet. I
know Preston as Redgrave. Ho came to lodge with me on July 8
He left on the day of Childs's arrest (August 28). I have seen Childs
and Redgrave sitting together outside my house. That would be
two or three weeks before Redgrave left. Young MeQueen called
upon me on August 28 with a letter from Redgrave, and I gave him
Redgrave's clothes. My sitting-room is in the front of the house
and my kitchen at the back. The front sitting room was occupied
by Redgrave. I can seen anybody coming to the front door from
the kitchen. Childs came one Sunday to see Redgrave.
Cross examined by Mr. Salter. Redgrave did not appear to be in
want of money at the time. He was well-dressed. I did not take
notice of what money he had. He did not appear to me to be shont.
I would not wish for a better lodger.
Eliza Cooksey. I live with my btother-in-law at 10, Duke Street,
Manchester Square. His name is Saunders. We let an office. On
July 23 I saw an advertisement in the Daily Telegraph," to which I
replied. Prisoner Redgrave called in reply to that letter, I think,
on July 25. My brother-in-law was not in, and I told Redgrave to
call again on Saturday (July 27). The rent was two guineas. He
gave the name of Davenport as reference. My brother-in-law wrote
to Davenport asking if he considered Redgrave satisfactory in every
way and received the following reply: "We are in receipt of your
letter of 27th inst. In reply thereto we beg to inform you that
we have known Mr. Redgrave for many years and you need have
no hesitation in accepting him as tenant, as he will in all ways prove
satisfactory. We regret the delay in replying to yours owing to our
Mr. Davenport being out of town. We delayed writing you until
his return.We are, yours faithfully, Davenport and Co."
The Common Serjeant. Of course, this may be from Redgrave!
himself. Davenport says he wrote no reference.
