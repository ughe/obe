 Witnesses for the Defence.
   ANN KELLY. On the 8th of August I saw the prosecutor going down the court - he said, "Mrs. Lane, you are very well paid - there was a sovereign in the pocket;" when he came from the country I heard him say, "I am going to take them up for the half-sovereign;" I said,"You ought to stick to the same story;" the Magistrate let the woman out, and he wanted to bring a man to say he had the half-sovereign, but they would not let him - he said, "I have said it was a sovereign, but now I can tell what I like."
   MARY EARLY. I live in the same court as the prisoners do.  1724.  ADAM MARSHALL and JOHN MARSHALL were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 6 loaves of bread, value 2s., the goods of William Fittock.
   MARY FITTOCK. My husband's name is William - he is master of a Hambro' trader. On the 20th of July I caught John Marshall, who is a soldier, going out of my house in Grafton-street, Soho, at six o'clock in the morning, with something in a bag - I did not see what was in it, but I gave him in charge of the Policeman; the other prisoner is my journeyman - I keep a baker's-shop: I suspected he had given him the things in the bag.
   JOSEPH TILLEY. I am a tailor, and live in Grafton-street, opposite the prosecutrix's; I saw John Marshall coming out of her house five minutes after six o'clock, with a bundle across his shoulder - she immediately followed him, tapped him on the shoulder, and said, "Now I have caught you in your villiany," and she gave him in charge.
   JOHN JULIAN. I am a Police-officer. I found six 2lbs. loaves in the bag; I showed them to the prosecutrix at the watch-house, and she said they were hers - I went to her house, and she gave me charge of Adam Marshall; he went on his knees, and begged forgiveness - he said he had committed an error, and robbed a good mistress.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did you state this before the Magistrate? A. Yes; Adam handed me 2s.; I did not go into the shop; I went into the bake-house - the prosecutrix said, "John Marshall has taken out six loaves, and this man gave them to him;" I said he had better put on his things and come with me - he said,"I am very sorry, I have taken 2s. of the other man for them; here is the 2s. which the soldier has given me - I know I have committed myself;" he did not say he took the 2s. for the bread.
 MRS. FITTOCK. I saw some loaves at the watch-house, but I could not swear they were mine; the price of them was 2s. 0 1/2d., and Adam Marshall laid that money down to pay for them - the Policeman took up the 2s., and said, "Never mind the 1/2d.;" he said he was very sorry he had committed himself that morning; he said he had served the soldier with six 2lbs. loaves, and he had paid him for them - he begged me to forgive him.
 Cross-examined. Q. Then the value of them was 2s. 1/2d.? A. Yes; it was taken from the dough-trough, and the soldier was a customer.
 COURT. Q. Did the soldier tell you what he had in the bag? A. Yes; he said bread, but he had paid for it.
 Adam Marshall's Defence. The soldier was taken in charge before I knew any thing about it; if the woman had come down I should have given her the money - he used to come and have the bread out of the bake-house; I said, "Here is the money for the bread."
   John Marshall's Defence. I am allowed to sell bread, coffee, and other things; I went to the prosecutrix's for bread, and said I would pay for it once a week, but the men did not pay me as I wished, and I went to her, and said I could not pay the bill, but I paid part of it to Adam Marshall, and the prosecutrix said she had received it.
   MARY FITTOCK. He always was a customer, and paid very honestly; I had told Adam Marshall to let him have bread - I did not wish to go before the Magistrate, but I was forced to go.   NOT GUILTY.

 1726.  TIMOTHY REEDY was indicted for stealing, 74lbs. of lead, value 12s., the goods of Edward Jones.
   JAMES PERRY. Mr. Edward Jones is contractor for iron pipes to the New River company. I missed a pig of lead from the works on the day in question; I had seen it safe between ten and half-past ten o'clock, at the end of our trench - I have seen it since at Marylebone-office - it belongs either to the New River company or to Mr. Jones; it has N. R. C. on it - they are cast in a mould; it was lead of this description - I have known the prisoner five or six years; I never saw the lead in his possession, or spoke to him about it.
