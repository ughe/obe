 1113.  THOMAS HALLAM was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of July , 3 silver spoons, value 7s.; the goods of Thomas Ayres.
 THOMAS AYRES. I am a  cabinet-maker, and live in Bath-street, City-road. The prisoner was my errand boy for about a fortnight - we missed three spoons, one at a time - part of one was found at a refiner's, and one on the prisoner.
   EDWARD ALLISON. I am apprentice to the prosecutor. He missed three spoons - I accused the prisoner of having one - he denied it; but he pulled this one from his breeches pocket.
   JOHN JEFFRYS. I am a refiner. A boy (who I think was the prisoner) brought a broken spoon to our house, which was placed among other silver - I asked him his mother's name, and he told me a name which corresponded with the name on the spoon.(The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 14. - Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury. - Whipped and Discharged.
 1114.  MARY ASHTON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 2 gowns, value 5s.: 2 pinafores, value 3s.; 1 pelerine, value 1s.; 6 shells, value 1s.; 1 cap, value 2s.; 1 half-sovereign; and 40 shillings; the goods and monies of Elizabeth Gull , her mistress.
   JOSEPH WINNEY GULL. The prisoner was servant to my mother, Elizabeth Gull - she keeps the  Duke of Marlborough public-house- she was with her three months, and my mother gave her warning to leave - on the day she was going, she brought her box (which was locked) down to the ground floor - my mother said it was her custom to search the boxes of her servants, and the prisoner gave her the key - she opened it, and the property was found - the prisoner said she was very sorry, and thought there was no harm in taking these things - the money was not in the box - I told the officer we had better search her person - she resisted very much; but we searched, and found on her 2l. 16s. - she said, "You cannot prove that is yours" - she was then taken to the station-house - she asked me if I would forgive her - I said it was not in my power.
   ELIZA SARAH GULL. I am the prosecutrix's daughter. I was present when the prisoner's trunk was opened, and the property found - we had not missed it, nor the money - she said several times that she did not think there was any harm in taking the things; they are but of small value - my mother never gave them to her.
   ELLEN EARLS. I lived at the prosecutrix's. I saw the prisoner at the closet where the cash box was kept, and heard the money rattle; but I cannot say that she took any - when she went to bed, she told me there were six cakes in her mistress's closet.
 (  William Hitchin, a silk and trimming-manufacturer; Sarah Smith; and Mrs. Howard, gave the prisoner a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 18. - Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutrix and Jury. - Confined Three Months.
 1115.  JOHN SIMMS and THOMAS WOOLBARD WHITTON were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 1 bracelet, value 2s., the goods of Robert Parker , from the person of Frederick Parker.
   MARY BILLINGSLEY. I am nursemaid in the family of Mr. Robert Parker. On the 1st of July, I had the care of his child, Frederick Parker - I was in Kingsland-road, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon - he had a bracelet on - it was snatched off suddenly - I turned and saw the two prisoners - I called, "Stop thief;" and they ran - I pursued, and the policeman took them just as I came up to them.
   GEORGE BONNEY. I was there, and saw Billingsley with the child - I saw the two prisoners - one of them made a snatch at the bracelet - they ran away, and I pursued them - I did not see the bracelet found.
   WILLIAM NORTON. I was going along the road, at a quarter before five o'clock, and picked up this bracelet in the road the prisoners ran.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN GREEN(police-constable N 214). I heard the alarm, saw the prisoners, and took them.
 SIMMS -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 WHITTON -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped and Discharged.
