JAMES MAYLOR. I am a seaman. I was on board this skiff on the
25th of Oct.-I saw the steamer coming up-at that time there was
nothing between us and the steamer-she was coming at nine or ten knots
an hour I saw Shadrack get up and make a sign-there was a man on the
paddle-box at that time-if he had put his belm a-starboard the least bit
it would have cleared us-it came up to us there was no lessening of
greed that I noticed the boatman pulled his right-hand oar-that was
the proper course to adopt, or else the steamer would have cut the boat
right in the middle-the stem of the paddle-box struck the boat, and it
was overturned-I can swim-I attempted at first to swim to the steamer,
but did not do so, because I saw that she was not stopping-ber paddles
were still in motion, but they might have been eased a little I did not
notice the schooner moored to the Lower Tunnel-pier, and have not seen it
since our skiff was not drawn by any current caused by anything near the
shore-it was in no current at all, except the tide. low toob
Cross-examined by Sir John BAYLEY. Q. You say Shadrack hailed the
steamer? A. He waved his hand-I did not hear the steamer hail us
till she was close on us, and then they sang out, “ Hoy"- that was just
as the collision happened-it was too late then we were about two or
three barges' length from Wapping Dock-stairs, as near as I could
judge.

THOMAS PALMER. I am a blacksmith. On Friday, the 25th of Oct., I was
one of the four persons who were going over to Wapping Dock-stairs, in the
boat rowed by the deceased--we saw the steam-boat coming up about 150
feet from us--a sailor, who was in the boat, stood up, and halloed out
several times, and waved his hand several times--no notice was taken the
course of the steamer was not at all altered, nor the speed slackened she
struck our skiff, and we were all upset--the wheels of the steamer were in
motion at the time the collision took place.
Jury. Q. Are you sure they were turning the usual way, and not tarned
the other way? A. No, they were turning towards our boat-I am quite
sure of that.
Cross-examined by Sir John BAYLEY, Q Did you see the steamer give
a sudden careen? Å. Yes-that did not alter her course-I saw her coming
up as fast when she struck our boat, as when I first saw her--my jaw was
broken in three places on this occasion, and it hurts me very much now-the
steamer went in towards the shore-she did not hail us- I did not see any
one on board hail us-I heard somebody call out,“ Stop her;" but whe-
ther that came from the steamer or any small boat I cannot say I heard
that when she was about twenty or thirty feet from us-I was before the
Coroner-there were some small boats on the river, and the cry of “Stop
her" might have proceeded from then-I am sure it did not come from
