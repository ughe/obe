CATHERINE KIDD. I am the wife of John Kidd. On Ash-Wednesday I lost these articles from my children's bed-room - I had seen them on the Tuesday; a lad came and told me a person was up stairs - I went, and found the prisoner with them; I asked how she came by them- she said the door was open, and any one might have taken them as well as her; I had never seen her before.
   WILLIAM IFGRAVE. I live in the same house. I saw the prisoner there with some things under her cloak - she was coming from the room; I asked where she got them - she said she had been to bring them down for a friend of hers to take care of, and asked if a person named Turner lived there; there was no such person there - she said she knew she was right, because she saw the corpse lying in the house; I called the prosecutrix, who said she was a very bad woman for taking her things; the prisoner said that the door was open, she came up by mistake, and was going to get a coffin of the parish, but that was for a person at another house, not at the prosecutor's - it did appear to be a mistake.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did not she knock at the door on the first floor? A. Not to my knowledge - I met her there; she did not say she came to see Mrs. Turner, whose husband was lately dead, and she found those things about, and knocked at the door to mention it; a woman, named Turner, lived very near, whose husband lay dead; I have heard so since - I do not know whether the room on the second floor was left open.
   DANIEL PAYNTER. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - she said she mistook the house, but Mrs. Turner's husband lay dead about fifty yards from there - she said her sister-in-law lodged on the first floor.
   ELIZABETH WYERS. I was going to get a coffin for my husband, who was dead - I met the prisoner, who asked me to have a glass of something to drink; she asked me to take her to see my husband, which I did - it was dark, and she was rather in liquor; she said she would call again, and see if she could relieve me with something - my house is twenty yards from the prosecutor's.
     NOT GUILTY.

 880.  JAMES SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of April , 1 silk handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of William Stone , from his person.
   WILLIAM STONE. I am an officer in the army. On the 17th of April I was going from Wimpole-street to Oxford-street, about half-past nine o'clock in the morning; I missed my pocket-handkerchief from my pocket when I got to Oxford-street - it was a new one; I had not seen the prisoner.
   WILLIAM IRELAND. I was in Wimpole-street on duty; a gentleman's servant called to me, and said, "These two men have taken a handkerchief from a gentleman's pocket"- I went and took the prisoner, who Killick said had received it from the other; the prisoner took the handkerchief from his pocket, and attempted to throw it down an area; I caught it, and took him - this is it.
   HENRY KILLICK. I saw the prisoner and another in Wimpole-street; I saw the other take the handkerchief from the prosecutor's pocket - the prisoner received it; they were both close to him.
   WILLIAM STONE. This is my handkerchief; it was safe when I left my house in Wimpole-street.
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
 881.  ELLEN CRAWLEY and ELIZABETH TURPIN were indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of April , 1 basket, value 3d., and 90 oranges, value 18d., the goods of John Baines.
   JOHN BAINES. I am a fruit-salesman, in Covent-garden-market. I lost this basket and oranges from my stand on the 3rd of April, about four o'clock in the afternoon; I had seen Turpin near the stand.
   WILLIAM HERBERT. I am a fruiterer. I was near the prosecutor's stand; I saw Turpin take this basket of oranges, and give it to Crawley; I followed her round a corner, and took her with it - I sent to the prosecutor, who had them both taken.
   WILLIAM MORGAN. I am constable of Covent-gardenmarket, and took the prisoners; in going along Turpin asked if I would let the young one go, as she knew nothing about it.
 Turpin's Defence. I was going down the market, and met a friend; we went and had something to drink - she then met her husband, and they had some words; she said she had bought some oranges, and asked me to go and get them; she had left her boy with them - I went, but did not see the boy; I took up this basket, thinking it was hers - I said to this girl, "If you will take this up to the top shop, I will go and look for the boy;" that is all I know.
 TURPIN -    GUILTY. Aged 50. Transported for Seven Years.
 CRAWLEY -    NOT GUILTY.
