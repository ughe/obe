MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
John Elliott. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Kingsland-road, St.
Leonard, Shoreditch. On Monday evening, the 18th of July, I went to
bed about eleven o'clock or half-past, having gone over my premises, and
seen the shutters, doors, and all the fastenings correct- about one o'clock
in the morning, my bell rang- I looked out of the window, and saw some-
body standing in the street, in consequence of whose information I dressed
myself, got a lantern and examined a window which is at the extent of
my premises in Union-walk, and found the shutters forced open-I do
not sleep in that part--my servants do-there is a covered com-
munication between my dwelling-house and the warehouse I have
three houses made into warehouses, and they communicate internally
--part of the brick work of the area grating had been disturbed, and
the window at the extremity of the house was broken-if they got into
the area grating, they could go up some stairs into the room where they
forced open the shutters—the shutters had been fastened-I imagine they
were opened by the force of the hand, or some small instrument the win-
dow was up, so that a person might get in or out--nothing was missed at
that time I have since missed the leg of a table--two servants sleep in
No. 1 warehouse, which communicates with Nos. 2 and 3. do
Cross-examined by Mr. DOANE. Q. Do any of your servants sleep in
the room where the window was broken? A. No, in the adjoining ware-
house, which communicates with the other without going out of doors
part of the yard is covered with tile and wood to keep the wet out-
I must go through my dwelling-house, and through the covered way to get
into the warehouse.

MARY ANN CARR. I am the wife of William Carr, and live at No. 2,
Union-walk, Kingsland-road. I do not live with my husband a win-
dow of Mr. Elliott's warehouse faces my house-on Monday night, the
18th of July, I was sitting up to wait for a lodger between eleven and
twelve o'clock-I opened the door to look out, and saw three men kneel,
ing down on an iron railing of Mr. Elliott's warehouse-- I went in again, and
in a quarter of an hour, or twenty minutes afterwards, I heard as though
they had a piece of iron breaking the brick work, and knocking against
the iron- I opened the door, and the men ran round the corner--they
were in the act of raising the grating I waited a few moments, and saw a
policeman coming-I saw another man, or he might be one of the three,
run round the building, and walk bebind the policeman, and then before
him-he was dodging him like, till he came before him at my door-I did!
not speak to the policeman then I was afraid to do so as I thought the man
was listening-I shut my door again after waiting some time, I beard as
though the bursting open of the shutters--I opened my door again, and heard
the policeman coming up the walk-I told him wbat I had seen, and as I
was speaking to him the shutters of Mr. Elliott's warehouse burst open,
the window was smashed, and I saw one or two men, I cannot say which,
jump out-the policeman sprung his rattle, and they ran round Union
street-he cried, “Stop thief," and ran after them-I should not know
either of the men, if I were to see them-the prisoner Gates resembles
the height of one of them.
