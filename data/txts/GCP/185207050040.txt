735. JAMES HAYES was indicted for embezzlement.
MR. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution.
John Woolmer. I am a warehouseman, in Aldersgate-street. The
prisoner was my traveller and collector-it was his duty to receive money on
my account from customers—there was a book at the counting-house, in
which he was to enter all the sums he received-he was to pay the sums he
received to me every night-this is the book I have looked through it-
there is no entry of 101. received from Mrs. White-here is an entry on
10th June of 91. from Mrs. White-the other entries are from Mrs. Benson,
on various dates-bere is no entry of 121. 158. 8}d.
ANN THOMPSON. I am a widow, and carry on the business of a haber-
dasher in Camden-town. I know the prisoner-on 12th April I paid him
21. on account of his master-he gave me this receipt-I saw him sign it.
Cross-examined by Mr. BookIN. Q. You have seen the prisoner several
times ? A. Yes; there was a dispute as to whether he received this al. or
not-Mr. Woolmer was a party to some inquiries about it-I paid two
separate sums of 21. on the 12th and on the 19th-Mr. Hayes said I had
pot paid that 21. on the 12th, and I had paid it-he said that meant the 21.
paid on the 19th.
HARRIET Emily Benson. My husband is a haberdasher, in Earl-
street, Lisson-grove. I am a customer of Mr. Woolmer - I know the
prisoner--the last payment I made to him was 178. 6 d., on 14th June-I
paid him several sums, and he gave me a receipt on each occasion-I paid him
as much as 121. 153. 8 d. at different times, but not at one time.
Harriet White. I am the wife of James White; I am a milliner, in
Somers'-town. I deal with Mr. Woolmer, and know the prisoner-I paid
him 101. on 10th June, and he gave me this receipt-it was a payment on
account--the bill amounted to 261. odd.
Cross-examined. Q. Where was this paid ? A. In the parlour at my
house-I do not recollect that I gave him an order.
