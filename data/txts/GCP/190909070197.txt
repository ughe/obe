GEORGE HENRY REDFERN, chartered accountant. In September,
1907, I was introduced to prisoner by Blagden, and asked to prepare
for Feltham's Bank a statement of liabilities and assets to be ex-
bited in the bank under the Companies Act, 1862, Beotion 44. I
prepared statement produced to August, 1907, showing liabilities
1111,814, including a loss on trading during the year 1907 of £2,578;
assets £109,235, including " Hockley property £95,000." On April 7,
1908, I saw prisoner with reference to the Hookley property, for
which a prospectus had been issued arranging to sell it to a com-
pany for £20,000. I asked prisoner how he would account for the
salue of £95,000 which the property stood at in the books of the
bank, it having been sold for 95,000 shares of the bank. He said
be proposed to put in a Jewish School at Ramsgate. I told him
thal in my opinion the only way to properly get over the difficulty
was to cancel the allotment of shares and have a proper number
issued in respect of the property; that that would be a reduction of
capital requiring an application to the Court. Monk, representing
Cannon and Sons, solicitors, was present. Prisoner said he would
consult Monk. I intimated that I wished to resign my position as
auditor; that I could accept no responsibility for the figures in the
balance sheet, especially with regard to the Hockley property.
Statement produced contains “Hockley property and other contracts
and assets £95,000," the words "other contracts and assets" being
sdded without my authority; I have seen that statement exhibited in
Blagden's office at Feltham's Bank. I finally resigned my position
w auditor on April 8. I applied for my account, £140, and on
May 6 received letter produced, saying, "Our managing director has
repeatedly asked you for a balance-sheet, but can never get the same
from you, only your repeated grumblings and trying to find flaws in
our business re brickworks, etc." In my report, dated April 29,
1900, I stated, “Hockley property, £95,000. With regard to the
valuation put upon this property I do not know upon what basis it
has been calculated, as I understand a competent firm of valuers
Meently valued the property at between £16,000 and £17,000.". I
prepared profit and loss account to December 31, 1907, showing a
loss on trading of £2,578 11s. 8d.
Cross-examined. You told me if a certificate should be necessary
I might qualify it how I liked. You said you would get advioe as
to the way to deal with the brickfields, that you would see Mr. Monk,
and that you wished to do what was right. You stated that the bank
Tas a private company, that they were not selling or offering shares
to the public, and had no intention to do so. You never asked me
to do anything wrong. The bank was summoned' for not exhibiting
statement under the Act, for 42 days. I believe a small fine only
Fas inflicted and I wrote congratulating you upon it. Most banks
in London mention on their prospectus their authorised capital.
