561. MARY MILTON was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of De-
cember, 3 sheets, value 6s. ; 1 table-cloth, value 38.; 1 handkerchief,
value 1s. 6d. ; 7 gowns, value 48. ; 2 petticoats, value 28. ; 2 pillow-cases,
value 2s. ; 1 cloak, value 28.; 1 shawl, value 2s. 6d. ; 1 pair of trowsers,
value 4s. ; and 1 waistcoat, value 28. 6d.; the goods of Joel Wright, her
master.
JOEL WRIGHT. I live in Clerkenwell, and am a bricklayer. The pri-
soner was in my service-it was her business to look after my children,
my wife having died on the 23rd of November-she absconded on the 20th
of December, while I was gone out to work, leaving my child who was
lying bad with the small-pox-I gave the prisoner 53. a week, and her
victuals-I left her 18. a day, to get what she wanted, and she had the
book to get shop things—when she was gone I missed three sheets, and all
the things stated—these are them.
Jonn Moss. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Goswell-road. I have a
shawl, and a waistcoat which were pawned by the prisoner.
WILLIAM BRITTAIN. I produce a pair of trowsers, a waistcoat, and a
number of other articles pawned by the prisoner.
WILLIAM ALLEN (police-constable G. 38.) I went to King's Head-court,
Westminster, and took the prisoner-I said I must search the room for the
duplicates--she said there was no occasion for that, she would give them to
me, which she did she said, did Mr. Wright want to prosecute her, if
he got his things again-I said that I could say nothing about her hus-
band went with me to take her--he was taken first, and said if he was
liberated she would give information where the things were.
