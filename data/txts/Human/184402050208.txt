   MISS WILLIAMS re-examined.  This is the money I gave the policeman—I am quite sure there was only two shillings besides what I received from the prisoner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How late do you keep your shop open? A.  Till twelve o'clock—I never left it till it was closed—I went to the drawer, about eight next morning, when the policeman came, and saw the shillings there—there was only my father and mother in the house—they had not been in the shop—my brother never goes behind the counter.
   COURT . Q.  Was there more than three shillings there on Sunday? A.  No—anybody could tell the money was bad. NOT GUILTY.
 785. CHARLES ELLIOTT was again  indicted for stealing 10 cheroots, value 1 s  .; the goods of Mary Ann Horsley. MARY ANN HORSLBY. I keep a shop in Little Charlotte-street, Black-friars-road. On the 6th of Jan., about a quarter past seven o'clock, the pri soner came in and asked how many Bengal cheroots there was for Is.—I said ten—he said he would take a shilling's-worth—I wrapped them up in paper—he took them up, threw down a bad shilling, and ran out—I ran to the door, but he was gone—I have destroyed the shilling. NOT GUILTY.
 786. CHARLES ELLIOTT was again  indicted for stealing 2 newspapers, value 1 s  ., the goods of James Richards . ELIZABETH RICHARDS. I am the wife of James Richards, of Union-street. On Saturday the 6th of Jan., before eight o'clock at night, the pri soner came and asked for the "Illustrated London News," "Bell's Life," and "Parley's Penny Library"—I said, "Bell's Life" was 6 d ., and he said be would only take the two newspapers, which came to 1 s .—I gave him the two papers—he laid down a counterfeit shilling—I said that would not do, and he ran away—he had not gone out of the shop when I said it was bad—I spoke to a man at the door, who brought him back—I marked the shilling, and gave it to the policeman—I had showed it to my husband, but do not think I lost sight of it.
   Cross-examined by MR. CROUCH . Q.  He did not turn back when you objected to the shilling? A.  No, he ran out—he must have heard me say it was bad, unless he was deaf—I do not know whether I spoke as low as I do now—I held the shilling in my hand till my husband came in, which was in about two minutes—the prisoner had the newspapers when he was brought back. PETER O'NEIL( policeman. ) I was on duty in Union-street, on the 6th of Jan., and saw a crowd at Richards's shop—I went in, and Mrs. Richards gave me 1 s . MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of counterfeit coin to the Mint. This is a counterfeit shilling.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is it a good imitation? A.  I should say it is rather inferior—it might impose on a person not well acquainted with money. NOT GUILTY.
