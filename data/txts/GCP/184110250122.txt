2712. JOHN SHAYLER was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of
October, 1 sixpence, 12 pence, and 32 halfpence; the moneys of John
Allen Butler, his master.
John ALLEN BUTLER. I am a baker, living in the Hackney-road-
the prisoner was in my service for fourteen months. In consequence of
circumstances I marked 58. worth of coppers, and put them into the till
-about seven o'clock in the morning on the 23rd of October I came down
-the till was locked as I had left it-I opened it, and missed 2s. 4d. in half-
pence and a silver sixpence-I sent for the policeman-he desired the pri-
soner to turn out his pockets, which he did, and there was one silver six-
pence and 3 d. in copper, all marked.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. You marked some shillings that
you found all safe? A. Yes-the prisoner has four children-- I do not
wish to press this against him.
GUILTY. Aged 45.- Recommended to mercy.--Confined Three Months.

2713. EMMA WATTS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of Oc-
tober, 1 frock, value 58. ; 1 pair of boots, value 38.; and 1 petticoat,
value 28.; the goods of Elizabeth Cox.
ELIZABETH Cox. I live in Charles-street, Drury-lane-I have known
the prisoner about a fortnight. On Thursday night, the 21st of October,
I was with her in her room, and another young woman who slept with me
- I took my clothes off, and put my frock and petticoat on the top of the
bed-in the morning I missed them and my boots-these are my boots.
Prisoner. The person who lived with us sent me to pawn the frock
and petticoat. Witness. I did not know the other girl.
William Crush. I am a pawnbroker, in Museum-street. I took the
boots in pledge of the prisoner, to the best of my belief-my duplicate
corresponds with this one.
John TALBOTT. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner, and received
these duplicates from her-one of them is for the boots.
NOT GUILTY.
