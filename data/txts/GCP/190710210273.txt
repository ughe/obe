1905. For years past I have advised people in respect of their circu-
lars. I used to advise George Gregory, the largest outside broker
we have ever had. I used to write for him, and I wed to advise
other people. I was supposed to have some knowledge; having had.
20 years' experience of newspaper work. There is a very large honest!
business being done by outside brokers at the present time.
Mr. Justice Lawrence. What does that mean "Honest business" |
is such a comprehensive term.
Witness. Outside brokers have been dubbed "bucket shops,
which is presumably an offensive term for the purpose of assuming
that they are fraudulent institutions, but there is a great number of
sound, honest, outside brokers who conduct legitimate businesses now
and have done for the last 20 years to my knowledge
Mr. Justice Lawrence. Does that mean places where they speeu-
late in differences, but speculate in differences Honestly
Witness. No, large transactions in genuine shares and the trans
ference both by sales and purchases of genuine shares. An outside!
broker's is not only a place where people speculate in differences, but
thousands and thousands of pounds, hundreds of thousands of pounds,
pass in the legitimate transfer of shares. I think every outside
broker does both businesses. Naturally no outside broker confines
himself to any particular class of business. As a rule he takes any
business that comes to him. If a man wants to buy shares he buys
them and delivers them to him; if he wants to speculate in dif-
ferences, he speculates for him. A shareholder will, I presume, sell
to anybody if he is paid. I have received money from Hawkes, but
after this lapse of time it is rather difficult to give particulars
Generally speaking, if Hawkes has paid me money it has been small
amounts advanced on loan; other amounts have been for shares sold,
and there would be cover on stocks that might be open. The offices
at Tokenhouse Buildings consisted of two rooms and a lobby. From
February, 1905, to Christmas, 1906, I used to give assistance occa-
sionally at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings. I knew Harris was carrying
on an outside broker's business. The name of the Financial
Guardian” was on the door, and I saw a man there named Ablett.
The name of the "Financial Guardian" was not up in Hawkes's time.
The person I saw as Ableu was of different identity from Harris
should think I saw Ablett half a dozen times; it did not run long
after I first saw him; I should think about two months. In 1905 I
used to see people at Tokenhouse Buildings by appointment. So far
as I knew the business of the "Financial Guardian" was an honest
business. I knew nothing of the existence of a man named Martin
in connection with it. I cannot say when I first knew that Harris
and Martin were identical. I eventually moved somo papers and
things there from 41, Charing Cross Road. I think that was in Sep.
tember, 1906, shortly before I took a room for myself, on which were
the letters "O. W. P." Those papers, weighing about 2 cwt., and in
cluding some old "Financial Observers, some cheque books, and
two tin boxes of private papers are now in the hands of the police.
