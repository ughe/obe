GEORGE HARDING (prisoner, on oath). A man named Charles
Pred Russell was indebted to me for £1 8s, 6d., money lent; he
had promised to pay me on February 22 if I met him at 6.15. I
met him at a billiard hall adjoining Moorgate Street Station. He
then said, “Well, I have got the money, but I have only got a cheque,
and I cannot pay you till I get it cashed. It is a crossed cheque.
and therefore will have to go through a bank." He said it was
from a customer of his. Russell is a commercial traveller with Long
and Co., in Leadenhall Street. His brother is the manager there.
I pointed out to Russell that the cheque was not payable to him. He
led me to believe Carter, the payee, was the customer from whom he
received it. I said I thought anyone would change it with such a
well known name on it. He said if I could change it I might do so,
and keep what he owed me. I was to meet him again at the same
place that evening at half-past nine to give him the change. He
told me I should not be able to change the cheque without buying
something. I said, “What shall I buy ?” He replied, "I have been
going to buy myself a belcher ring; you can give 25. or 30s. for it;
I don't want to pay more." When he left me it was about quarter
