MUSGROVE, Mayor.
EMMA GREENING. I live with my mother, Maria Elizabeth Greenine
who is a widow, and is a baker. The prisoner was in her employ, it
was part of his duty to receive money for her, and pay it to me the same
night-I keep my mother's accounts-I believe the prisoner keeps a
memorandum-book which he takes out in the cart, but he does not show
that to me--he tells me what he receives, and hands it over to me-he has
pot paid me 11. 1s. 11d., on Mr. Ponsford's account; or 11. 12. 2d. on Mr.
Pember's; or 9s. from a Mrs. Holford-he never told me anything
about them--the bread is counted out to the prisoner when he goes out.
and we count it when he comes back--this is Ponsford's account, the
amount he owes us is 11. ls. 11d.; the last week's bread was 38. 3d. : Mrs.
Holford owes 11. 58. 2d., and Mr. Pember 11. 128. 2d. - I give the
prisoner the bills to deliver every Monday- I did not ask him for these
acconnts, because I did not suppose they were paid-he has never said
anything about them.
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. Is this book all in your writ-
ing? A. Yes; I make the entries from the statement the prisoner gives
me-he received a guinea a-week, and his bread and flour-my mother
has received money from him-she is not here, she is very ill, she was
unable to attend before the Magistrate-I enter in this book what the
prisoner pays me; be has paid my mother money, and she has handed it
over to me, and I entered it in this book-my mother was not anxious to
prosecute, and her son was not particularly anxious to prosecute-the
prisoner has brought me large sums of money-if I am not at bome at
night bis bread is hooked next morning ; but that is very rare-be had
been in our service nearly two years, and four or five with my brother,
it may have been six; I cannot say whether it was eight-I believe he
has lost his wife ten months ago-about three months ago he broke his
collar-bone, and was taken to the hospital - I believe his sister is now in
the hospital-my brother has nothing to do with our business, he comes
in and out; he has quarrelled with the prisoner-I have heard that the
prisoner went to work drunk, and ran after my brother with a knife-1
do not know that he brought an action against my brother, and I do not
believe he recovered damages, or that my brother paid him any money in
consequence of the violence he received from my brother-he was laid
up some time.
MARY PONSFORD. I deal with Mrs. Greening--sometimes the prisoner
used to bring the bread, and sometimes another person-I used to pay
the prisoner every week-on 1st Feb. I paid bim 38. 3d.
ELIZABETH HOLFORD. I deal with Mrs. Greening—the prisoner used
to bring the bread-I used to pay him every fortnight-on 28th Jan. I
paid bim 98., which was all I owed.
ELIZABETH DANIels. I am the wife of Charles Daniels, and have
been cook to Mr. Pember-about four days before the prisoner was
taken I paid him 11. 12. 2d. for Mrs. Greening--this is the receipt
(produced).
JAMES PLUMB ( policeman, P 89). I took the prisoner, and told him
the charge-he said he had received the money, and could not account
for it. Guilty. Aged 29.- Recommended to mercy by the Jury.
Confined Three Months.
