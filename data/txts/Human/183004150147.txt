   WILLIAM CROOK. I was in the service of Mr. Blades; I know the prisoner perfectly well - I certainly never received any money from him, or any thing stated by him to contain money from the Ironmongers' Company; he was not at all employed by Mr. Blades in his general service - I never had any conversation with him respecting any money received from the Ironmongers' Company till before his examination had taken place; he came down to ask if I did not recollect receiving the money - I said I did not; there was a Mr. Jones in Mr. Blades' service - I think the prisoner said he paid it about July; I can say with perfect recollection, and with perfect truth, that he never paid any money over to me - I was in the habit of opening all notes at that time, both private and public; I had a special power of attorney to transact any business on Mr. Blades' account, and in his name; if any money had come I ought to have known it, but Mr. Jones sometimes opened letters; I know Mr. Blades' gave the prisoner authority to go and receive the money at the proper time.
 Cross-examined. Q. Can you say of your own knowledge that Mr. Jones opened all notes and letters which you did not? A. That I cannot say; Mr. Blades was in such a state that no notes relative to business certainly, and I believe no private notes were delivered to him; when Mr. Blades received money it first passed through the cashier's books, but this money never did in the business accounts - I had a good deal to do with his private accounts while he was indisposed; if I had received this money I should have entered it in the public books; the year before they did not usually come into my hands - the prisoner was not in the habit of calling on Mr. Blades; I should think he did not call for six month's together - he never left letters to my knowledge since I had authority to open them, and I should think not previous to that, because Mr. Blades was much out of town; I had many letters that he never saw.
 COURT. Q. Was Mr. Blades in a state in which letters would not have been received by him at this time? A. Yes, certainly.
   FRANCIS JONES. I was in the employ of the late Mr. Blades - I occasionally opened letters that came to him; I certainly did not receive any letter from the prisoner containing notes or money at that time, or any account that he had received money; he came to me and asked whether I recollected his bringing a note to Mr. Blades, directed " I.  Blades, Esq., Treasurer of St. Brides' School" - I told him certainly not; I perfectly recollect that be never had - he had no general employ from Mr. Blades.
 Cross-examined. Q. You did not open all letters? A. I did not; but, generally speaking, I knew the contents of all letters, at least ninety-nine out of a hundred. I am in the shop from eight o'clock till eight - there are eight or ten persons in the shop, and sixteen or eighteen persons altogether; the prisoner might have come in during my absence, and have left a letter, without its coming to my hands.
 COURT. Q. Had any other person authority to open letters except Mr. Crook and you? A. There was occasionally, but very rarely; they principally came to him or me.
   JOHN SILLINGHART. I am porter to the Company of Ironmongers. I produce the book to certify that the prisoner's signature is in it, and that he received the cheque; this is it.
   WILLIAM HENRY WRIGHT. I am clerk to Messrs. Hankeys. I know nothing of the prisoner, but I paid a cheque for 15l. drawn by Thomas Pellatt, who was clerk to the Ironmongers' Company at that time: (read) "July 1, 1829, pay St. Bride's school, or bearer, Fifteen Pounds, Thomas Pellatt;" I paid it in three 5l. notes, Nos. 6116, June 4, and 2728, June 15; there is an error in the number of this note - I omitted a one; the real number is 27,281- the other note is No. 24,961, June 6.
 COURT to MR. CROOK. Q. Did you ever receive any 5l. notes of these numbers on account of Mr. Blades? A. That is impossible to say, but if I did they most likely have my signature upon them.
   JOHN HUCKLE. I am inspector of the watch in St. Bride's parish. I took the prisoner into custody; I was desired by Mr. Devey to request him to come to town, and to be as delicate as I could, on account of his wife and family- he at first refused to come, and said he would come on Monday; I then requested to speak with him, and told him my business - he then said, "I will go with you," but he made no acknowledgement to me at all.
 Cross-examined. Q. I presume he knew you? A. No, he did not.
   JOHN REAHDEN. I made the prisoner a pair of trousers; he gave me a 5l. note, and I charged it in the course of a few days, at a public-house; I wrote the prisoner's name on it - I have seen it since, and am quite sure it was the note I received of him some time in July last, I think.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you every make any clothes for him before? A. No, but I have made him a coat since - he found his own cloth.
   GEORGE DYER. This note was paid into the Bank on the 3rd of August; it is No. 27,281, 15th of June, 1829.
   JOHN REAHDEN. This is the note I received from the prisoner; I had no other customer of the name of Nettleton, and had no other note at all.
   RICHARD WOOD. I am a publican. I changed a 5l. note for the prisoner; I did not observe whether there was any name on it, but I wrote on it, "Little gentleman, in the morning;" this is it - it is No. 6116; I cannot say when it was.
   GEORGE DYER. This note was paid into the Bank, and is dated the 4th of June.
