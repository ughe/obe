28. JOSEPH HIGHLY, for a like offence.
Messrs Ellis and Bookin conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES FREDERICK HEAP. My mother keeps the Prince of Orange, in
Philip-street, St. George's. On 6th Nov. the prisoner came to my mother's
bar, called for a penny worth of gin, and gave me what resembled a shilling
I gave him 11d. change-I then took up the shilling, tried it by a test which
we have in the til, and found it was bad--I gave it to my mother-I tried to
get the prisoner but he was gone-Mr. Solomon came in-I showed him the
shilling, but he did not have it in his hand-I cut it, and put it on a recess-on
the following Monday, the next day but one, the prisoner came again, about
seven o'clock in the evening-I knew him directly-he asked for a penny-
worth of gin-I went and told my mother, then came back to the prisoner, and
served him a pennyworth of gin-he laid down a counterfeit shilling- I took
it up, applied the test to it, and it was bad-Mr. Solomon was there-be de-
tained the prisoner, and we got an officer-I told the prisoner he bad tried it
on before with me-I marked the shillings, and gave them to the policeman.
Prisoner. I was at your house on the Monday, but not on the Saturday,
Witness. I am quite sure he is the man who was there on the Saturday,
CHARLOTTE HEAP. I keep the house. On 6th Nov, my son showed mo
a bad shilling-he marked it, and I loeked it up in my own drawer, apart from
other money-my son gave it to the officer on Monday,
SAMUEL ELMORE (policeman, H 68.) I produce the two shillings I received
from Mr. Heap-I saw the prisoner at the station --- he said he had changed a
shilling on Saturday night-he did not say where.
CALEB EDWARD POWELL, These are both counterfeit, and east in the same
mould.
GUILTY. Aged 26.--Confined Six Months.
