JOHN DAVIES (City Detective). On 12th September I went with Mitchell
to Victor's house - I went from there to Mrs. Overton's, about 4 p.m., and
said to her “I am a detective of the City Police; Mr. Victor is in custody
on a charge of receiving a quantity of brushes, and says he bought them
from you, and you will have to go with me to his house she made no
reply-she went with me, and outside the house she said voluntarily “I will
tell you the truth, I do sell brushes, but I bought them at the Cattle Market
from time to time”-I said “Did Victor know where you got them from ?"
-she said “Yes; he knew as much about it as I do "-I then took her
down to Vietor's house to Sergeant Mitchell-I heard the conversation
between Mitchell and the prisoners, but did not hear Mrs. Overton say
that Victor did not know where the brushes came from-I was close by her,
and if she had said that I must have heard it.
Cross-examined by Victor. I asked her if you knew anything about
them, and she said Yes, you did I thought she was telling the truth
I heard Mitchell ssy at the police-court "I will not swear that Overton did

JOSEPH NOWLAND. I am messenger to Bordier and Co., merehants,
44, Coleman Street I was in Mr. Rooney's employment and knew Bowman
as a fellow-servant there I was at Victor's shop in Widegate Street in
November last, buying some tools, and Bowman came in and said to Victor
"I see you are engaged now, I will call in again;" I recognised Bowman's
Voice and turned round and saw him, and he went away--the shop con-
fained a general stock of everything; some horse brushes ; some hair brushes
which appeared new, but to have been in stock some time; also stock, dis-
temper, scrubs, and other kinds.
Cross-examined by Victor. I was subpoenaed last Thursday-I knew
Bowman and recollect his calling at your shop on the day mentioned-I
made no remark to you.
