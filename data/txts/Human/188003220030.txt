ALICE BROOKSON. I live with my father, a baker, of 184, Long Lane, Bermondsey Square—on Saturday, 6th March, between 8 and 9 p.m., I served the prisoner with a half-quartern loaf, which came to 3 1/4 d .—he gave me a bad half-crown; I gave it to my mother, who gave it to my father when he came home—I did not give the prisoner the change, and he left the loaf on the counter—I saw him a few days afterwards before the Magistrate and recognised him—I have no doubt he is the man. THOMAS BROOKSON. I keep this baker's shop—my wife gave me two bad half-crowns—I took them to Southwark Station and gave them to the inspector—this is the one uttered on March 6th—I know it because the other has a thinner edge—the inspector marked it. WILLIAM JAMES HUNTLEY( Police Inspector ). On the evening of March 6th Brookson brought two half-crowns to the station—I marked them both, and handed the one produced to-day to Butler. HENRY FULLER. I keep the William the Fourth—on March 7th I was standing by my wife when she served the prisoner with 1 d . worth of tobacco and a glass of ale, which came to 2 1/2 d .—he put down a half-crown—she tested it, broke it in two pieces, and handed it to me—I jumped over the counter and detained him, and gave him into custody, with the broken half-crown—he said that he was a hardworking man, and had taken it. MABUN BURGIN( Policeman ). On March 7th I was called to the William the Fourth, and the prisoner was given into my custody—he said nothing—I found 3 d . on him—he gave his name John Bracher—Fuller gave me these two pieces of a half-crown. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. These are pieces of a bad half-crown—the other half-crown is bad also.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I am innocent of the last two cases. GUILTY.— Nine Months' Imprisonment.
