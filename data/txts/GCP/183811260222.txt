235. WILLIAM BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 26th
October, 1 pair of shoes, value 78., the goods of George Carpenter and
another; and HENRY BURT, for feloniously receiving the same, well
knowing it to have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.
GEORGE CARPENTER. I am in partnership with Samuel Carpenter, a
boot and shoemaker, in London-street, Greenwich. I was in the parlour
on the 26th of October-I turned and saw Brown in the shop-I asked
him what he wanted-he said he had done mischief, he had strewed some
papers on the floor-he asked if I wanted any pattens or clogs-I said,
no-as soon as he had left, I missed a pair of shoes-I went to the
end of the street, and brought him back-I asked him if he knew any
thing of the shoes-he said no, but that he saw a lad leave the shop with a
bag on his back-I asked if he should know the lad-be said, yes--I took
him to several places about the town, but could not find the lad-I let
him go, but still followed him, and saw him go to the other prisoner-they
went across the Creek bridge-each of them was carrying a bag- they
saw me, and dropped their bags—I caught Brown and took him to Dept-
ford-Burt's bag contained three pairs of shoes, one of which belonged to
me--Burt was taken afterwards.
Brown. I had nothing at all in my bag. Witness. Burt had not been
in my shop that day-he could not have taken them.
Brown. I saw this boy-we went on towards London, and a man came
and gave him the bag.
