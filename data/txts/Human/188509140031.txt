EDWIN ACKLAND. I live at Uxbridge—on Thursday night, 13th August, I heard screams in the Chequers Yard—I went to the spot and found the prisoner with his arms round Davis's neck—Mrs. Plumridge came up to see what was the matter—I then heard more screams, and saw Mrs. Plumridge leaning against the railing; I saw her reel and fall—I did not see the prisoner touch her. JOHN HOPPER. I live at Uxbridge—I saw the prisoner holding Mrs. Plumridge by the neck—she reeled round and fell on her back—I looked at her, and she was dead—I gave the prisoner into custody. AMBROSE CHARPENTIER, B.M. I am assistant to Mr. Roberts, surgeon, of Uxbridge—I was called to the deceased about 20 minutes past 10 on the 13th—she was then dead—I made a post-mortem—the cause of death was fainting from shock—there were no marks of violence—all the organs were in a considerable state of disease—a mere shock would account for death. NOT GUILTY.
