 Cross-examined. Q. How long was it before you apprehended them? A. I suppose three quarters of an hour - we went to the Commercial-road, and they went, away; I merely went to the public-house for information not for the sake of the rum - I would drink vinegar or turpentine for information; they were not long in my company - there was no attempt at concealment; I had not known them before - the toll-house is not on my beat; Eves was much the worse for liquor.
   CHARLES LOXTON. My snuff-box was a dark brown, something of a box-wood colour - it contained two pins, a brooch, and two keys; the brooch was an old fashioned one, with three letters on it - this is very much like one of the keys; I had kept it in the box because it had lost its ring.
 Cross-examined. Q. I believe you were on the most friendly terms with Eves? A. Yes; his brother is one of the superintendents of the trust, and it would have been his duty to be there at half-past five or six o'clock the next morning - I went to sleep about eleven, and was awoke about ten minutes before twelve; this robbery must have been between eleven and twelve - I have seen many other keys like this, and I will not swear to this; I have seen no other property - it was Sunday night, and the shops were not open.
 JURY. Q. Have you seen many such keys without a ring? A. No.
 Eves' Defence. I know nothing of the business; the Policeman is quite mistaken in saying it was a wooden box- it was a tin tobacco-box, and the articles in it were the brooch out of my shirt, the ring off my finger, and the key, which I put into the box for safety.
   ROBERT HENRY CLEET. I am a master mariner, and live at No. 5, Johnson-street, Old Gravel-lane. On Sunday night, the 5th of December, I saw the two prisoners going into Mr. Ireland's, at the Crown and Dolphin, at the corner of Cannon-street-road. soon after ten o'clock - they sat drinking in the tap-room, and I went into the bar, which commands a view of the two entrances; I was there till near twelve o'clock, and during that time I never saw Eves go out till he went out for good, which was between a quarter to twelve and twelve - I saw Gough go out twice during the time; he was absent but a short time - I saw him come in again each time; perhaps he was out about five minutes - the reason they left was, Mr. Ireland was desirous of getting rid of them. as Eves was very much in liquor - he could scarcely stand; he stood twenty minutes or half an hour at the bar - I am quite positive Eves did not go out.
     NOT GUILTY.
 256.  ROBERT FITKIN, WILLIAM TUSTON, and JOHN GRIMES were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November , 3 books, value 9s., the goods of Lorenzo Lacklan.
   JOSEPH WILKINS. I am an officer. On Saturday evening, the 27th of November, I saw the prisoners standing near the White Horse, where they generally assemble together: they had some books, and knowing there were some stolen, I watched them, and saw them taking them about for sale; I got a man to assist me, and I went and took two books from Fitkin, and while I was taking a third book from Tuston's hat. Fitkin ran away - I pursued and took him in the top room of a house - I then went to Tuston's house, who came with me very willingly; Grimes was the first who had offered the books for sale - I afterwards found the prosecutor in Scotland-yard.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. What did Grimes and Tuston do? A. Both ran away - Tuston said, he received them of a man in Orchard-street, in a long white coat.
 LORENZO LACKLAN. I live in  Scotland-yard. These books are mine - I missed them on the Monday morning, but they were taken on the Saturday.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you keep them? A. Outside the window; I am sure they were there between three and four on Saturday.
   ANN SUMMERS. On that Saturday Grimes brought me three books, and asked me to buy them - I said I would not; the other two prisoners were with him.
 Grime's Defence. I was going through Orchard-street- I met these two young men, who said, "We have been and bought some books - I wish you would ask Mrs. Minchington, where Summers lives, and what is the value of them;" I went to ask, and she called out that she did not deal in books; we went out, and Summers came after us, and said her mistress would buy them - we showed them to her, but she said she would not.
 Fitkin's Defence. I was offered the two English books for 2s. 6d., and I gave 2s. and some halfpence for them.
 The prisoners received good characters.
 FITKIN -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 TUSTON -    GUILTY. Aged 19.
 GRIMES -    GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined One Month in the House of Correction.
