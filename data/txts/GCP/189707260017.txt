WILLIAM JOHN HILLYER. I am a furrier, of 91, Sewardstone Road-
on July 7th, a little after two o'clock, I was in a tram-car, going towards
Shoreditch--I heard a cry of “Stop thief !” and saw the prisoner run
down a little alleyI jumped off the car, and kept him in sight; I never
lost sight of him-he was the foremost man running; he ran into a public-
house, and came out into the crowd-the prosecutor followed at the
beginning, but he had to stop and walk.
Cross-exantined. I saw his face; he was about the length of this Court
from me, and he was running-I noticed his face; he had no beard then
-it was after two o'clock.
WILLIAM SMITH (577 M). I was on a tram-car in Commercial Street,
and heard a cry of "Stop thief!" and saw the prisoner coming from Red
Lion Court into Spitalfields Market, and into a public-house-I got off
the car, and ran beside him for half a minute, and took him; he was ex.
hausted and sweating-he said, “You have made a mistake”-I said, "I
have not”; he was running for half a minute in front of my face-ho took
his hat off and wiped it, and his forehead-I found nothing on him-he
said he had just come from his lodging, and gave me the address, but
they said that he was not living there then; he said that he had been in
London two years, earning a respectable living.
The Prisoner, in his statement before the Magistrate, said that he saw a
crowd in front of the King's Head, asked what was the matter, and was
taken in charge.
The Prisoner received a good character.

and a gentleman said, “You are going past him"-a policeman took him,
and. I charged him--they did not strike me, but my nose was bleeding."
