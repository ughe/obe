CHARLOTTE SOPHIA CLIFTON. I live at No. 4, Berkeley-place, Connaught-square—the prisoner was in my service for one month, and was my only servant—she was to leave on Saturday night, the 2nd of Aug.—having some suspicion of her, I insisted on searching her box that morning, which was out-side the kitchen door—there were three or four witnesses present at the time it was searched—the prisoner gave me the key out of her pocket, and I unlocked the box—it was a little key with a chain to it—I found in the box a pair of French stays, a pair of new stockings, two pocket-handkerchiefs, two books, and a scent-bottle, which I said were mine—I did not find a needle-case there, that was found at her sister's lodgings, which the officer searched—her sister never visited her at my house—I had a lady visiting me at the time.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALYF. Q.  What were the prisoner's wages to be? A.  Eight pounds a year—she was to have nothing besides wages—I never gave her things, she was too short a time in my service for that—I was constantly missing things—I am a single woman—there are no other persons living in my house, only a professional lady—she is no relation of mine, but a friend—I received a character with the prisoner—there stays cost me two guineas, they are quite new, and were going to be cleaned—I am quite independent—I was induced to look into her box, because I had missed a sovereign in a very singular manner—I changed a 5 l . note, and had it all in gold, I never went out after receiving it, and I dropped a sovereign, and asked the prisoner if she had seen a sovereign about, she said, "No"—I searched thoroughly that night, but could not find it; and the next day I said I was very uneasy about it, I must have it found, we came into the parlour together, I would not allow anybody else to sweep the room, and underneath the sofa, where we had looked the night before, I found the sovereign placed—I had been on the sofa the night before, I had dropped it in the parlour—it could not have rolled where I found it—the lady that was with me is here—the prisoner is not charged with stealing the sovereign, but I suspected her of many things—I told her to leave, but said I must search her box before she went out—I had a great many ladies in the room, and we all looked underneath the sofa for the sovereign—there were none but ladies there that night—there were no gentlemen—one lady was my sister—perhaps the others might not like their names mentioned—Mrs. Hawkes, my niece, my sister, and myself, were all in the room the night before, there was not a nook or corner in which we did not search—I had given the prisoner a month's warning—she was not servant enough, and I suspected her—I was constantly losing things out of my dressing-room, ever since she has been with me, and when I found this property in her box, I asked the lady to go up stair and see if she missed anything, she came down, and said she had lost three large sheets—the prisoner did not give me warning, warning was given before the sovereign was lost—her duties were merely to keep the house clean, the kitchen, and the upper part of the house—she did not have to sit up late—I always made her go to bed—if I was out late, I always let myself in, she was not to sit up—I never keep company—she was not required to sit up late the night before.
