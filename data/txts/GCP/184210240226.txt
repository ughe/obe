MARY MUSGRAVE. I live in Pepper-street. I was employed to nurse
Mrs. Bowler-on the Saturday on which her husband died I was in the
house at one o'clock-I was not there at all on the Wednesday, Thursday,
or Friday-he appeared very ill-he was in great pain at the pit of his
stomach, and was sick-the prisoner was there-I fetched some castor oil
with a little peppermint water, and gave it to Mrs. Bowler, and saw her
go into the bed-room-I did not go in myself at that time I saw him again
about half-past four o'clock the same day-he was very much convulsed
then-the prisoner was there he never spoke at all at that time, only
shook his head-I staid till eight-I did not see him after-I was clean
ing and doing my business in the house I was not in his room--Mrs.
Bowler was in the parlour-nobody was with him then-I asked her when
I went away, between seven and eight, how he was-she said he wa
dosing-I went up at eleven, and found him dead-I saw the prisoner
then she was very much affected at that time.
Q. On what terms did they appear to live ? A. There was never hardly
any words between them- more of silent contempt, sulky like, to one
another-they had no words before anybody.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you attend her in both confinements? 4.

husband's room both times when I went in I had my business to do-
she went for the castor oil, and took it into his room-they lived come
fortably together, as far as I observed I was there for a month-during
the lying-in he came home at night regularly.
