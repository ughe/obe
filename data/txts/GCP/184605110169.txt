1202. HENRY HILL was indicted for stealing 1 half-crown and 1 six
pence, the monies of Edward Kent, his master.
MARY KENT. I am the wife of Edward Kent, wine-merchant, Bedford.
place, Old Kent-road; the prisoner was in his service. I sent him, on the
2nd of April, for a shin of beef-he went, and brought the young man with
him, with the shin of beef, and a note saying it was 38. 60.-I said I under-
stood it was only 38.--the prisoner went down, and returned and said that
would do I gave him a half-crown and a sixpence, to give to Mr. Neal's
servant, whose name was Paynter.
Prisoner. When I left she stopped the money for it. Witness. That was
a different concern-he absconded on the 15th of April, before I had found
this out-his mother came two days afterwards for his wages--Mr. Kent said
he would not pay any body but Henry-the prisoner came on the Saturday,
and we deducted a half-crown that he had absconded with before.
GEORGE JOHN PAYNTER. I live with Mr. Neal. I brought the shin of
beef on the 2nd of April-it came to 38. 60.--the prisoner came down and
asked me if 38. would not do--I said I could not take any less than 38. 6d., as
I gave 38. 6d. for it-- he went up, and came down again and said, “Very well,
Mrs. Kent will call and pay"-he did not give me any money.
Guilty. Aged 16.-Confined Six Months.

1203. GEORGE COOPER was indicted for stealing 80 cheroots, value
68.; the goods of Frances Strode.
FRANCES STRODE. I am single-I live in the Triangle at Kennington-
cross-a lad about the prisoner's size came to my shop last Saturday-he took
a bundle of about half a pound of cheroots out with him I have no recoller
tion of his features.
CHARLES Rowe. I am apprentice to a shoemaker in Walworth-road-
about ten o'clock, last Saturday, I was going by the Triangle at Kennington
-I saw a woman on the pathway calling out Stop thief!" and a boy run
ning from where the cry was-he ran across the road and down Queen's-rov
- I ran after him till he was nearly at the top-he was stopped by the police-
man, and said that he was running after another person-I am of opinion be
ran from the place where the woman stood-I only saw one person running
-I never lost sight of him—there was a woman running, but nobody else-
I did not see anything in his hand.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You did not see anything throw
away? A. No-I saw him before Mr. Smith commenced running.
CHARLES JAMES SMITH. I live in Queen's-row, Kennington, about four
teen yards from Kennington-road-I was in my shop-I heard a cry of " Stop
thief"-I ran out and saw two persons running-I tried to stop the first-he
made a leap past me-I cannot swear that it was the prisoner-he dropped a
bundle of cigars on the ground-I distinctly saw it fall-Rowe was running
after him and crying “Stop thief!"-I kept my eye on the person till he got
into the arms of the policeman-though I do not recollect his person, he wa
the person that Rowe was following.
Cross-examined. Q. You stooped down to pick up the bundle and then
ran again? A. Yes I did not see the prisoner's face till I got to the station
- the inspector asked if I knew the lad I said, "No"-I asked the consta
ble if the prisoner was the person he stopped, and then I said it was him
that dropped the bundle--there was nobody else in the street.
