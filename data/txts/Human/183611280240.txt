 224. EDWARD CRISP was indicted for a misdemeanor. THOMAS LIFELY. I am a horse-keeper, and live at Battersea, Surrey. I be long to a club, of which John Mayhew is a member—we have a fund among ourselves to give relief to the members in sickness—I act as treasurer to pay the money—on the 6th of November the prisoner came to me on the subject of a person of the name of John Mayhew, to whom there was a sum of 5 s . due considering him as a member of the club—when the prisoner came he said that John Mayhew was very bad, his wife could not leave him to come, and had asked him (the prisoner) to come—he said he came for John Mayhew's sick money—I paid him 5 s . ELLEN MAYHEW. I live in the parish of Wandsworth, Surrey—my hus band was sick—I gave no direction to the prisoner to go Lifely on the 6th of November—I had on a former occasion, on the 23rd of October, to get the money due to my husband—he got 6 s . on the 26th, and brought it me home—he told me he had put it on the chimney-piece—I was not at home at the time—he told me of it, and found we had got 6 s  —I did not send him the second time. THOMAS LIFELY re-examined.  On the 26th of October I paid the pri soner 10 s ., and 5 s . on the 6th of November. ELLEN MAYHEW re-examined.  The prisoner did not bring any money to me or my husband on the 6th of November, nor had I sent him for it.
   Prisoner.  The first time you sent me was on Sunday morning—Tho mas Lifely said he must see his master, and I went on Monday—he did not pay—on Tuesday he gave me 10 s .—I brought you 6 s ., and you asked me to go on the 6th of November. Witness.  No, I did not. JOSEPH SMITH. I saw Lifely pay the prisoner 10 s . on the 26th of Oc tober, in Wandsworth parish, on the Tooting road. TERENCE CLARK( police-constable V  107.)
