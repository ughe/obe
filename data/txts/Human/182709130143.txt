1938. WILLIAM WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 4 half-crowns, 8 shillings, and 4 sixpences, the monies of John Gurney.
   EDMUND CRISP. I am in the employ of John Gurney, of Salisbury-court, Fleet-street. I laid four half-crowns, some shillings and sixpences on the counter, for a gentleman- the prisoner came in, and stood close to the gentleman - he was having some rum and water. I went to another part of the counter, to give another person change, and the gentleman asked for his change, which I thought he had taken up, and immediately I said some one had taken it, he went out, leaving half his liquor - I stopped Proudfoot, who was taken to the watch-house; the prisoner was brought there afterwards - it was about a quarter-past ten o'clock; he confessed it to the Alderman.
   GEORGE PROUDFOOT. I was at Gurney's wine-vaults; the money laid on the counter, near where the prisoner and I stood; I went out; Crisp came after me, and charged me with taking it, and took me to the watch-house; I had some money of my own, but not theirs.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I not go voluntarily with you to the watch-house, to see you righted? A. Yes; I accused him of it - he denied it, but went to the watch-house quietly - there were eight or ten other people there.
   WILLIAM ELMES. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house by Proudfoot, about a quarter to eleven o'clock- I found on him three half-crowns, 41/2d., and a gold ring; I heard him confess it before the Alderman, and say he did it through distress. He was rather in liquor.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in liquor - I had been in Proudfoot's company from seven o'clock till ten.
   GEORGE PROUDFOOT. I had been in his company.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1939.  RICHARD GREENFIELD was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 1 memorandum-book, value 9d., the goods of a person unknown, from his person.
   JOHN ROBINSON. I am an officer. On the 31st of July I was at a fire near London-bridge; I saw a gentleman on Fish-street-hill- the prisoner and a number of others were about him; I saw the prisoner lift up the flap of his coat over his shoulder, and take this book out of his pocket; I went up; the prisoner dropped the book on the ground, and I secured him; a girl took it up - she refused to give it to a friend of mine, and I took her to the watch-house, where she gave it up - the gentleman got away.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is the girl here? A. No; she said she found it in the street, and a young man dropped it; I was close to the gentleman, but there came such a rush I could not get to him.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am an officer. I was with Robinson, and saw the prisoner attempting to get his hand into the slip pocket of the gentleman, but he could not; I saw him turn the flap of the coat up, and get the book out - it dropped, and the girl took it up.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long did this take? A. It might be a minute or two - I was two or three yards from the gentleman, but I did not give an alarm, because there were so many of them - I had a neighbour with me.
   WILLIAM TARRANT. I was going down Fish-streethill, and saw the prisoner with another person - they tried every one of the gentleman's pockets, and then turned up the flap of his coat, and the book dropped out; a girl picked it up - she would not give it up at first.
 Cross-examined. Q. How near were you to the gentleman? A. As near as I could be, next to the officers, not more than two yards from him; the prisoner was two or three minutes putting his hands into his pockets; the moment the prisoner was caught hold of, there was a mighty rush, and the gentleman was carried away with the stream.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1940.  ROBERT THOMAS CAPPS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 1 table-spoon, value 10s., the goods of William Gibson.
   DANIEL SARGENT. I am a waiter at Baker's coffee-house, Cornhill, which is kept by William Gibson. On Friday, the 17th of August, the prisoner came there about half-past one o'clock; he went up stairs into the coffee-room; I asked if he was going to dine; he said No, he was going to wait a little time; he asked for a paper, but there was none; he asked for a glass of sherry, which I went down and got him; I thought he was looking at the tables, and before I went for it I counted the spoons - one table had four on it, another three, and another two; when I returned with the sherry I counted them again, and they were right; I went down and told Mr. Gibson; I went up again, and then one of them was gone - the prisoner was then coming out of the room; I said, "I must detain you;" he said, "For what?" I said, "For a spoon;" and he produced it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. What part of his person did he take it from? A. I could not see, as he turned round; he gave Mr. Gibson his name and address when he came up.
   JOHN COLLING. I was dining at this coffee-house; Sargent went down; he came up again, and accused the prisoner, who seemed in great trepidation, and took up the newspaper, and, after perusing it for a moment, he was endeavouring to take something from his pocket, and then on a table, on which I had seen but three spoons, I saw four; one was a different sort to the others; I will not swear he placed it there.
   JOHN DIXON SMITH. I am an officer. I went to the coffee-house, and took the prisoner; he was accused of taking one spoon from the George and Vulture, and one from Mr. Gibson.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I put it to the Jury to say whether I could commit the offence, as I am not in want of character, money, or business.
 The prisoner received an excellent character
     GUILTY. Aged 30.
