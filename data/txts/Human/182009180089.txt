SAMUEL COHEN. On Friday, while the prisoner was in the house, my wife informed me of this. I told her to keep her till I was at leisure. I then told her that two bundles in the shop had been misplaced and rumpled in the night, and some cambric taken away. The handkerchiefs were mine. She said it was the first thing she ever took. I was called down to the shop. I said she must not go away without being searched. I saw her go out while I was busy. I sent for Levy on Saturday; as my father knew the girl to be a thief, and I missed other things. We found her at her sweetheart's apartments. She was searched and the coins found on her, which I swear to be mine. They were taken from my looking-glass drawer, they were marked in a particular manner by a stamp. It was done some years back. She was then taken into custody, but let go again, as her father said he knew where all the property was. She was apprehended again on Sunday.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long were the handkerchiefs missed before you turned her out of doors - A. She was not turned out exactly. My wife said she should not sleep in the house. I had missed nothing but the handkerchiefs when she left. They were worth 3 s. each. I missed no more that day.
 Q. Pray, how soon after did you write to Mrs. Gasheer to make you amends for the false character she gave her - A. About three-quarters of an hour, and before I missed any thing else. I said 200 l. or 300 l. would not compensate for what I had lost. I demanded no money of Mrs. Gasheer.
 Q. When the coins were found on her, they were in your possession a day and a half afterwards - A. No. I kept them a few hours after, they were found on her on Sunday. I pointed the marks out when they were found, and gave them to the Magistrate on Monday, as the officer would not take them of me.
 Q. Did she say your niece took them - A. She said so on Sunday.
 MR. ANDREWS. Q. When you missed more property and told her of it, what did she say - A. She mentioned the person who was the instigator of it. Mrs. Gasheer is her aunt. The marks were on the coins before they were lost. Mr. Isaacs, the prisoner's attorney, attended before the Magistrate in my name without my authority.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. At the time Mr. Isaacs attended, was not the prisoner examined as a witness - A. I charged her with the robbery. One Jacobs was charged as the receiver.
 COURT. Q. How long before had you marked the money - A. Some years. They were all marked with the same punch. I cannot find the punch now. This is the letter I wrote to Gasheer - (looking at it.)
   SAMUEL LEVY. I am a constable. I apprehended the prisoner on Sunday the 16th in Roper's-buildings. I had searched her on Saturday at her father's. Her father and Cohen searched her, two shillings, a sixpence, and two halfpence were found on her. I have had them ever since two hours after she was in custody, when Cohen gave them to me - he kept them till then. He had said he would not prosecute her, or I should have taken her on Saturday.
 - WARTERS. I produce the handkerchiefs which Cohen gave me at the office on Monday, as Levy had refused to take them, because they had been so long in Cohen's possession.
   SAMUEL COHEN. They are mine.
 Prisoner. I leave my defence to my Counsel.
 Letter read.
 "MR. GASHEER, - This is to inform you that I received a character of a servant from you, whom your wife said had lived two years with you, and that she allowed her to receive money. Now she has robbed me, and you know she has given me a false character. I expect you will make good the property I lost. I shall proceed against you for it, as my solicitor directs.
 S. COHEN."
   SAMUEL LEVY re-examined. When the coin was found on the prisoner, I do not recollect Cohen saying he knew them by any mark.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1122.  JESSE BOWTELL was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , one watch, value 1 l.; one chain, value 10 s.; one seal, value 5 s., and two keys, value 5 s., the goods of Thomas Ingram , from his person.
   THOMAS INGRAM. I live in Harley-street. On the 18th of August I was standing at the entrance to Spring Gardens to see her Majesty pass. A rush came instantly at me. The prisoner came with his arms and held against me with great force. I felt my watch go, and saw the chain hanging from his hand. I seized and kept him till the officer took him. Some gentleman produced my watch.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Did not the gentleman say,
 "Here is your watch, you have got the wrong man" - A. No. The prisoner dragged me into the middle of the road.
   WILLIAM TATE. I am a tea-dealer, and live in the City-road. I saw the prosecutor rush towards the prisoner, and saw the chain in his hand. I attempted to seize him, but five or six came round, cried Rescue, and threw me into the middle of the road. I saw a gentleman take the watch from the prisoner's hand, and give it to Mr. Ingram.
   JAMES HAUDLEY. I am a constable. Immediately as the Queen's carriage passed I saw a general rush. There was a cry for an officer. I saw two or three gentlemen struggling with the prisoner, and secured him. Several men endeavoured to rescue him. One of the gentlemen who had hold of him gave me the watch. He made great resistance, and it was with difficulty I could secure him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was taken to a public-house. The watch was brought in by a man.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Life.
