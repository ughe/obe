MARY JACQUES. The prisoner married my mother. I am sister to
the prosecutor's wife--I have lived at home with my family. I remember
living in Tredegar-square-Mrs. Roberts kept the house there Mr.
Hinton and the prisoner hired the house, I believe I should think we re-
mained there nine months—there are five of us in family during that
time the prisoner bad the receipts for the bills made out in his name
be used to pay for what was wanted my sister is eighteen-I have seen
money pass between the prisoner and the prosecutor several times I have
seen the prisoner give the prosecutor money several times (I am sixteen
years old)—that was at Leytonstone-I never saw it in Tredegar-square-T
should think I have seen it six or seven times I do not know to what amount
-I think it was silver--the prisoner paid the bills at Leytonstone I do not
know whether he paid them out of his own money-he was doing nothing
at that time-he has been married to my mother seven years he has al-
ways supported us since his marriage-my mother has some property the
prosecutor lived with us at Leytonstone-I do not know how he lived
with us—I never heard any agreement made about payment for board and
lodging-I never heard him speak about it I do not remember his ever
paying anything for board and lodging—from Leytonstone we went to
Mrs. Roberts's, in Tredegar-square again the prosecutor did not live with
us there-I believe he came there twice on a visit—I do not know that
the prisoner has been greatly reduced.
