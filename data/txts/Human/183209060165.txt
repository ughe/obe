 Steel. I met these two girls, and asked them to go with me to buy a pair of shoes - Humphries touched this pair - they fell down; I took them up, and before I could put them up the officer took us. Witness. They had got fourteen or fifteen yards from the shop; Clark had a jacket on, and he put his hands into the pockets, and extended his arms as if to conceal them - I found the shoes under Steel's arm; I put her into the shop, and Clark used the most disgusting language, which collected a mob, and I took him - Steel and Humphries stood close together, and the other two close behind them; I had watched them for twenty yards previous to their going to the door.
 Humphries' Defence. I was coming down Shoreditch and met Steel; I touched the shoes and they fell down; she picked them up, and the officer took us.
 Wiley. I was with Humphries - we met Steel; she asked us to go and buy a pair of shoes - they were a little before me, for I stopped to look at a window; I did not know what was done till the mob collected. Witness. I had watched them for twenty yards previous to their going to the shop; when I took Steel, Clark said I had no business to take her - I said if I did wrong I must answer for it; he was very violent, and a man stepped up and assisted me to take them - Steel then wanted to shuffle the shawl from her shoulders, and I assisted her, in order that the other might come to the station - Clark then took off his hat, and I asked Wiley to carry it for him, which she did; I then met another officer, and we took the other two prisoners.
 ELIZABETH CROW. I am servant to Joseph Fennel. I was in his shop, and saw Humphries take hold of the toes of the shoes and turn them round - she said they were a nice pair, and then Steele took them down and put them under her shawl; they had got twelve or fifteen yards before they were taken - I gave an alarm; my master went out and Steel was brought in - I was at the back part of the shop; the shoes were hung on hooks.
 JOSEPH FENNEL. About seven o'clock in the evening my servant gave me some information - I went into the street and saw Steel in custody of the officer, who was taking these shoes from under her shawl; they were twelve or fifteen yards off - Clark then came and was taken - they are my shoes, and worth 4s.
 HENRY COLTON. I had seen all the prisoners together in deep conversation for twenty minutes before; and I had seen the whole four together before that evening,

 HUMPHRIES -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 WILEY -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 CLARK -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.

 2162.  ANN SWEENY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of August , 1 tippet, value 4s., the goods of Mary Ann Waters.
   MARY ANN WATERS. I am single; I live at No.35, Frith-street- the prisoner was servant to a lodger in the house; I lost my tippet on the 2nd of August from a back parlour -I saw it again on the prisoner on the 30th of August.
   ELIZABETH HOEPFNER. My husband keeps the house in Frith-street. Waters is my servant- the prisoner is servant to Mrs. Rymer, a lodger; this tippet and a gown of my own were taken on the 2nd of August, when the prisoner's mistress took her to Margate; I described my gown to her mistress, and she found it on her person.
   FANNY RYMER. This tippet was in possession of the prisoner on the day we returned from Margate.
   JAMES WARD(Police-constable C 172). I took the prisoner at the prosecutor's house on the 30th of August; this tippet was under her gown and petticoat, tied round her waist. Property produced and sworn to.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2143.  ANN SWEENY was again indicted for stealing, on the 30th of August , 1 gown, value 8s.; 1 cap, value 1s. 6d., and 1 handkerchief, value 6d., the goods of John Augustus Andrus Hoepfner.
   ELIZABETH HOEPFNER. I am the wife of John Augustus Andrus Hoepfner - he keeps the house No.35, Frith-street; the prisoner was servant to my lodger. I lost my gown on the 2nd of August, and the cap about a week after.
   JAMES WARD. I am a Police-constable. I found this gown on the prisoner on the 30th of August.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Transported for Seven Years longer.
 There was another indictment against the prisoner.
 2144.  MARGARET MURRAY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st September , 1 gown, value 2s., 1 pair of stockings, value 9d., and 1 shawl, value 6d., the goods of Michael Crawley.
   MARY CRAWLEY. I am the wife of Michael Crawley; we live in the parish of St. George, Hanover-square. I hired the prisoner to mind my children; she came on the 1st of September - I had my tea with the prisoner at five o'clock, and then went out to market, telling her to put the children to bed - when I came back I missed these articles from the front kitchen.
   JOSEPH KENDRICK. (Police-constable F 42). I took the prisoner at two o'clock the next morning, in Bowl-yard, St. Giles'; she had this shawl on, which is all that has been found.
 Prisoner. She allowed me to wear the shawl on that Saturday afternoon, and then got me to mind her things while she had her tea.
 MARY CRAWLEY. No, I never lent her any article of dress; she agreed to stop with me for 1s. a week.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Six Weeks.
 2145.  JOHN LAWRENCE was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , 27 yards of carpet, value 12l., and 1 hearth rug, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Dowbiggin and another.
   MICHAEL DRISCOLL(Police-constable H 133.) On the 18th of August, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, I was on Tower-hill - I saw the prisoner with a parcel on his shoulder; he was going on between a walk and a run - I suspected all was not right, and followed him; he dropped the parcel - I called to two men to take care of it; I followed and took him in the Minories.
   SAMUEL EMMERSON. I am clerk to Mr. Thomas Dowbiggin and another, of Mount-street, Grosvenor-square - this parcel contained the articles stated, and is their goods; I gave it to one of our porters to pack, and he gave it to two carmen - it is addressed to Lord Kinnoul, Duplen-castle, Perth.
