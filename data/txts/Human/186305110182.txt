   MR. DICKIE . Q.  Is there any pretence for saying you were tipsy when in this house? A.  None whatever; the officer will prove I was quite sober. WILLIAM CUMMING( Policeman, M  196). On 24th April I went to  the house of prisoner Frost, at 17, Revel's-row, at 7 o'clock—I did now see her then—at 8 o'clock I saw her there with Mason—I told her I wanted her for robbing a man of a quantity of money—I commenced searching the place, and she said, "It is no use, you won't find it"—I said, "Find what?"—she replied, "Nothing"—I had another constable, I told him to take Mason, and she ran up stairs. LOT SAWYER( Policeman, M  175). I went with the last witness to take the prisoners into custody on the 24th April—I saw them both—Mason ran up stairs, and I followed her, and I saw her breaking the string of a new pair of foots—I asked what she had got, and she said she had bought them the day before in Suffolk-street—I told her I should take her into custody for being concerned in robbing Mr. Wyatt—I took her to  the station, and afterwards went to Mr. Durrant's with the other constable, and made inquiries about some boots—I afterwards made inquiries at the Skinner's Arms, and I saw the witness, Hussey.
