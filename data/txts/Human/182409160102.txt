   SAMUEL SEDDON. I am a watchman. I heard an alarm of fire, and went to the house about twenty minutes after ten o'clock, and saw fire in the cupboard in one corner of the room, the whole cupboard appeared to be on fire; it was rather abated by water being thrown on it. I got a lantern, and saw a bed and mattress on the floor, with one laid against the cupboard. I took a small heap out of the cupboard on a large shovel; it happened to be some paper shavings, a pair of stays, and the wood of a pair of bellows, they appeared all to have been on fire, but put out by water. On the morning of the 26th, to the best of my recollection, about a quarter or ten minutes after five o'clock, I saw the prisoners in Turnmill-street; Pickering seeing me, came over and said
 "Watchman, people may think I have been out all night, being out so early in the morning, but I have not; I have been sleeping at a house which I have lately taken, for there is a bad lock on the door, and I was afraid to trust it, and now I am come down to see how this house goes on, for I have 200 l. worth of property there." Between a week or a fortnight before that, I saw some furniture carried out of her house early in the morning, it was loaded on a cart or van, she helped to carry some things; Shannon was with her. I went off duty, returned in about twenty minutes, and the cart was gone.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it on the morning before the fire that you saw her in the street - A. To the best of my recollection it was, but she told me before the Magistrate that it was Sunday. I have seen a number of persons collected round her house. The paper shavings were like some I have seen in grates.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am a constable. On the night of the 26th of August, as soon as the fire went out, I went with Rice to No. 15, Slade's-place; Pickering had taken that house, she was going to bed there - she asked what I wanted her for, I said I would tell her as soon as she got to the watch-house, and when we got there she asked me to be as good as my word. I said she was charged with setting her house on fire; she seemed much alarmed, and asked for water, and before that could be obtained, she went into hysterics. I went to the house next day, and brought away the cupboard, shelves, some burnt paper and rags.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far is Slade's-place from the house - A. Three or four minutes walk. I knew where to find her.
 RUEBEN RICE. I was with Clark, and apprehended the prisoners - they had lived in Slade's-place for nearly a fortnight. I have the cupboard shelves, stays, bellows, a gown, and pieces of paper.
 Cross-examined. Q. The gown is a stuff one - A. Yes - it is not a thing which easily burns. She had slept at Slade's-place for a fortnight before the fire, and had moved part of her furniture there. I saw Pickering in Castle-street between three and four o'clock in the afternoon of the fire. I have frequently seen persons collected before the house, and she has sent for me to disperse them - she was obliged to move in consequence of it.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1503.  JAMES HAYES was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of September , two carpets, value 8 l., and thirty yards of stair carpet, value 3 l., the goods of William Ginger , in a certain out-house, belonging to his dwelling-house.
   DAVID DAVIS. I am servant to William Ginger, who lives in College-street Westminster, and is a bookseller. On the 4th of September, about ten minutes past seven o'clock, I found the back door open on coming to the house, and on going further I found the warehouse door forced open. Some carpeting which I had left there a few nights before was scattered about the place, and about 12 l. worth gone. The warehouse does not join the dwelling-house, but is enclosed in one fence with it. The carpet belonged to the house.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. What do you use the warehouse for - A. We keep books there; the carpets were placed there as the family were out of town; they were kept in the warehouse or outhouse - we deposited books and paper there.
   BENJAMIN TIMBRELL. I am an officer. On the 4th of September, about half-past five o'clock. I met the prisoner in Peter-street, with a bag on his shoulder, and asked what was in it; he said nothing. I found it contained carpeting, and took him to the watch-house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he not say that he did not know what was in it - A. No; his answer was
 "Nothing;" he said nothing about finding it.
   ELIZA COOK. I am housemaid to Mr. Ginger. I missed these carpets out of the warehouse.
