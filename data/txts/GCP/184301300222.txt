said, “ No, it will be no forgery, unless we swear to it" - I said I felt myself
very ill treated, and I should not let it rest where it was--the prisoner said he
was going to Chatham, where he had a house of business, and should return in
three days-in the course of conversation about witnessing the deed, he said
that his uncle had filled in the vacant names, and they had witnessed it at his
(the prisoner's) house, at Camberwell--the prisoner kept a draper's shop at
Camberwell-my father was never there.
Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS. Q. When were you lucky enough to
have this conversation? 4. About a week before Christmas, 1841, in Craw.
ford-street-I rather think there were lodgers in the house there-I mentioned
this conversation to Mr. Petch, and I mentioned it to Mr. Boys, the following
year, in 1842-I mentioned it immediately to my friends, of whom Mr. Petch
was one-he is a housekeeper, in St. John-street-I had the prisoner taken up
a very short time ago I never saw him after this conversation, to have an
opportunity of doing so before I was not indicted by him before I charged
him-either be or his wife appeared against me at Union Hall-I appeared
at Union Hall on a charge of having committed a forcible entry--that
was at the instance of the prisoner's sister-she appeared there--it was after
that that I had the prisoner taken up-I was not taken into custody-1 had
gone to a house in which the prisoner's sister lived--I went with Mr. Fluker,
the attorney's clerk, not with other persons--the house was not broken into in
my presence-I found the door broken.
Q. Did you not go in the dusk of the evening, and after you had sent per
sons forward, find the door broken open? A. I sent no person forward-
was invited by Miss Ann Blackwell to take away all the furniture I found there
-she brought me the keys to unlock all the doors- I do not know whether
that was after the outer door was broken open-I did not know whether there
was any door broken open-the door was open-I do not say it was broken
open-I found it open-I found Miss Ann Blackwell there, and Phæbe
Blackwell, and her nieces- I saw no one else-I saw Mr. Fluker-there:
were a great number of persons--there was no riot-We had applied to Mr.
Cottingham for a warrant, and he considered it was the subject of an action
--we were not three minutes about the premises before I found the door open
-I will swear my friends were not waiting there the whole afternoon to get
in-Mr. Fluker was not, to my knowledge.
Q. How came you to go there that evening? A. 1 had been there for
some days before the property was mine, and they were conveying it stealth-
ily away-I had been watching for some days before I had not been
watching that afternoon-I was visiting at a house opposite, at Mr. Clifford's,
the assistant overseer-Rutland, the officer, was in the house with me
did not know who he was then, but I afterwards knew he was an officer-I had
taken tea at Mr. Clifford's--I had been there in the course of the afternoon,
