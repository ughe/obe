MESSRS. CRAWPURD and MEAD conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM PENSTON. I am a butcher, of 133, Cromer Street-on 6th
March I saw the prisoner in my shop-my boy served him with a balf-pound
of beef sausages, which came to 24d.-he paid with a half-crown--the boy
called out "Change, take 21d."-my wife took the half-crown, and said
“I don't think it is a good one "-I tried it with my teeth, and found it
was bad-I had never lost sight of it--as I had thrown two half-crowns
into the fire previously, I said to the prisoner, “I have had enough of these."
and gave him in charge with the half-crown.
John CROCKER (Policeman E 112). On 6th March I was fetched to Mr.
Penston's shop, and received the half-orown—the prisoner said he did not
know it was bad, he took it at the London Docks, where he had been
working-I found nothing on him he was remanded to the 13th, and then
discharged—he gave his name, Charles Wright.
ARTHUR Dawson. I am barman at the Cranbourne Tavern, St.
Martin's Lane-on 31st March, about 8.30, I served the prisoner with a
glass of cooper, which came to 11d.-he put down a bad shilling, and I
gave him in charge—I had seen him there that afternoon, between 3 and
4 o'clock, in a different suit of clothes, when he gave me a shilling, and
shortly afterwards a bad shilling was taken out of the till-there were other
shillings there—I gave it to Mr. Silcock, who gave it to the constable, who
broke it.
Prisoner. I was only in the shop once that day. Witness. I am sure you
are the same person.
