To Mr. Marshall Hall. I suggest that the identical notes have
been traced out of Smith's account into Perryman's account, by their
numbers.
To Mr. Muir. Taking CS 5, with regard to the £1,680 in notes, I
find from the exhibit that notes to that amount, the numbers of which
ere given, were drawn out of Herbert Smith's account with the Lon.
don City and Midland Bank, and that notes with those numbers were
paid into Perryman's account with the London City and Midland
Bank to the amount of £1,580. I have traced those notes by their
numbers out of the one account and into the other. In the same way
I traced notes out of the Smith account with the London Joint Stock
Bank, Finsbury, notes to the amount of £190, and by their numbers
I traced £150 of those notes into Perryman's account. They are the
identical notes. I have seen the notes and the numbers. In the same
way I have traced out of Chalmers and Cox's account with the Lon.
don and Trading Bank notes to the amount of £360 by their numbers,
and £285 of that to the account of Perryman at the London City and
Midland Bank. Taking one as an example November 10-you will
find in Exhibit 226 that £50 was drawn out in notes, a £20 note and
£30 note, from Herbert Smith's account. I have seen those two notes.
In Exhibit 209, which is Perryman's account with the London City
and Midland, there is a credit on November 10 for £60. Mr. Hudson
produced to me the statements from the Bank of England.
Mr. Marshall Hall said there was a missing link with regard to the notes, #
Exhibit 212 A did not break up the notes further than October 22. There were no
details with regard to November 10, and what the witness said was not strictly
evidence.
Mr. Mair said the only document be had of the credits going into Perryman
account was 178 A, which went from January 4, 1906, to June, 1907.
Mr. Marshall Hall submitted there was no evidence before the Court of the pay.
ment into Perryman's account of those notes.
To Mr. Justice Lawrence. In saying that those notes were paid in
to Perryman's account I am relying on two things, one, that the bank
clerk told me a certain fact, and, two, that on the corresponding date
in the Exhibit of Perryman's account I find a credit for a sum at least
as large as the item in question.
Mr. Marshall Hall said the bank clerk had stated that notes of corresponding
numbers had passed through the London City and Midland Bank, but he could not
say they had passed through Perryman's account.
Nr. Justice A.T. Lawrence said that that criticism went to the value of the
evidence, and not to its admissibility.
At the request of Sir C. Mathews, his Lordship made the following note of the
objection: Mr. Hall and Sir C. Mathews object to the evidence because the
bumbers of the notes are not traced into the London City and Midland Bank to
the account of Perryman. I rule that the evidence is admissible, but that the
objection goes to value."
