JOHN WHITE (policeman, R 180). I was on duty at Sir Thomas Wilson's
on the afternoon of 14th July, there was a fete there I saw the prisoners
together, watched them about a quarter of an hour, and saw them go both toge-
ther, and stand beside Mrs. Rees-Williams lifted her pocket, or her dress-
Johnson covered him-tbey then left-I went and spoke to her, and then
took Johnson- I said, “ Hand me over that purse" -he said he had no
purse--he then took it from his pocket, and threw it on the ground-I called
to my brother officer to take it up.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALPE. Q. Did you see it when he took it
out? 4. Yes, and tried to get it-there was not much of it visible--I
saw it was something red-when it was on the ground I could see that it
was a purse-Gladwin picked it up-I was tustling with the prisoner-I
saw a form with ladies and gentlemen on it-Mrs. Rees stood with her back
to it-there were persons in front of her-there were persons on the form
looking over her bead, and of course over Williams's head. si
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. Were you behind Williams?
A. Yes—Johnson bid him-I took Johnson from twenty-five to thirty yards
from the spot.
WILLIAM GLADWIN (policeman, R 122). I was with the other officer at
Sir Thomas Wilson's I saw the prisoners together-Williams went to Mrs.
Rees, and apparently put his hand into her pocket-Johnson covered him
Williams took something from the pocket, and gave it to Johnson, who put
it into his pocket, and they both walked away-I spoke to Mrs. Rees-I
then saw Jobpson in White's custody-I took Williams-White said, “ Look
out, he has dropped the purse on the ground"-I found this purse on the
ground-it has shillings, sixpences, and fourpenny pieces in it.
Joun CARPENTER policeman, R 84). I was on duty, and watched the
prisoners-Williams attempted to pick a lady's pocket-he then went behind
Mrs. Rees- I stood behind the form, and separated two ladies' dresses with
my hand, looked through, and saw Williams take from Mrs. Rees's pocket a
red purse-- he had a handkerchief in his hand wiping his mouth with it-he
wrapped it in the handkerchief, and passed it to Johnson-- I took this hand-
kerchief from Williams--I heard Johnson say he had no purse.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALPE. Q. Did you state this before the
Magistrate ? A. Yes, on cross-examination-I was in plain clothes--I be-
long to the Detective Force of the R division--there were five or six thou-
sand persons there.
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. Were you behind the others?
4. I was behind the form which was between me and Mr. Rees; I have been
. policeman nearly twelve years I believe that I did right to state that I
saw Williams try one pocket before I believe it is my duty to state all I
saw the prisoners do on that occasion-I should not have allowed them to
commit more than one robbery if I could have helped it, but if I could not, I
should have stated what I had seen-I am aware that to attempt is an
offence, and a summary conviction would have been the consequence-the
form was five or six feet long I was about the middle of it, or if anything,
rather nearer to the end where the prisoners were it was perhaps a yard and
a half from Mrs. Rees-I was looking to her left side.
