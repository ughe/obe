 228.  JOHN WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of December , 1 jacket, value 10s., the goods of John Simonds.
 JOHN SIMONDS. I am a  seaman. On the 1st of December I came on shore from my ship, at five o'clock at night- I left my jacket and other things in my hammock; I employed a man to go for them, and he employed the prisoner - they were brought to King James-stairs, by the man; I did not see the prisoner then - I saw the hammock was undone, and I said to the waterman, "Something is amiss - you had better go back;" he put back, and we told the officer of it - the officer took the prisoner, and the duplicate was found on him.
   RALPH LANCASTER. I was employed to go on board the Peggy, and get the hammock - the prisoner (whom I have known from a child) went with me to Greenwich; we could not get up, and we went on shore at Deptford -I gave him some grub; I then went to the Peggy, and the mate gave me the things - I brought them to King James-stairs, and saw the prosecutor; I left the prisoner in the boat - he then came on shore: I said to him, "I promised you 6d., and I will give you something more," but he was very shy, as he had helped himself - the prosecutor and I then went into the boat; he saw the hammock was undone, and said, "Avast here. Ralph, shove back - my hammock has been unlashed;" we put back, but I could not find the prisoner - we told Mr. Wilson, who found him.
 Prisoner. Q. Was I not on the stair-head when you put off? A. Yes.
   WILLIAM WILSON. I am a patrol. I took the prisoner on the night of the 1st of December, in the White Hart public-house; I told him I wanted him for stealing a sailor's jacket - he said he had had no jacket, and knew nothing of it; he said, "I will go down with you to Lancaster," and in Farmer-street he tried to get away, but he fell down, and I took him to the watch-house - I found this duplicate on him.
   WILLIAM CANE NOOK. I am a pawnbroker. I gave this duplicate for this jacket; it was pawned on the 1st of December, between six and seven o'clock at night, but not by the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The duplicate was given to me, and that is all I know; the person said, "You have got a very poor jacket on your back - I have been pawning one for 3s.; here is the duplicate."
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 229.  HENRY RICHARDSON was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of November , 1 wheelbarrow, value 12s., the goods of James Wrigglesworth.
   JAMES WRIGGLESWORTH. I lost a wheelbarrow some time before the 4th of November; one of my men found it that day in posssesion of the officer.
   JOHN BARNES. I bought the barrow of the prisoner about nine weeks ago.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Did not he bring another man with him? A. Yes - but the other man did not say it was his; the prisoner told me he had an old barrow to sell - he did not say for another man; I paid 4s. and a pot of beer for it, at the Dublin Castle public-house, Park-street - I laid the money on the table; I do not know which took it up: the prisoner bargained to sell it to me.
   WILLIAM COLTON, I found the barrow in possession of Barnes, at the Scotch school, in the New North-road- he said he got it from Richardson, and I took him.
   WILLIAM BARTLETT. I understood that the prisoner was wanted - he was taken, and asked what I wanted with him; I said on suspicion of stealing a barrow - he said,"That barrow which Barnes the gardener had of me?" I said, Yes - he said, "I found that in one of the shrubberies in the Regent's-park - I sold it to Barnes for 4s. and a pot of beer.
   JOHN WRIGGLESWORTH re-examined. My barrow was lost from the brick-field, in the road near Holloway - it is worth 10s. or 12s.; the place was trenched round to keep strangers out - he must have got it at night.
     GUILTY. Aged 41. Confined Three Months.
