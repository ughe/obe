   Cross-examined Q.  Were you told of three men that ran away from the wagon? A.  I heard there were two—I did not try to pursue them—my brother constable did, but he did not catch them—he did not bring any man back—I saw from seven to eight person in the wagon. JAMES HALLIDAY. I was in this wagon—I saw the prosecutor and the two prisoners there, and several others, but they are not present—I re collect three men getting out of the front of the van—they kept putting my hat over my eyes, and the prosecutor's too—there was another young man sitting with the prisoner—I saw them do nothing, barring  having three silver skewers in their hands, passing them from one to another—I turned my back to them, and was afraid to them, and was afraid to turn round, as I was threatened to have my head punched  —the one that kept hitting me over my head got out first, and two others after him—the prosecutor's hat was knocked off, and I called to the wagoner to stop—he stopped and gave it him, and he got out; and then one of them put his hand round him, and he a kind of black book in his hand; and after that I saw the skewers handing about—the two prisoners had them in their hands—I do not know what became of them—the officer came up in an hour, I should think—the prosecutor got out, and went a different road, and then I saw the skewers—I expected the man had been robbed of them—I would not go away; and then the officer came up and the prosecutor, and said that he had been robbed of three sil ver skewers. GEORGE WOODS re-examined. Q.  What money did you find on either of the prisoners? A.  I found 18 d . and a four-penny piece on Stone, and on the other two knives and a box, but no money.
   MR. PHILLIPS to JAMES HALLIDAY . Q.  How many men ran away from the wagon? A.  There—it was one of the three I saw with a black book, and taking something from the prosecutor—I do not know whether every body in the van might have seen the skewers—I had my back turned to them—the three fellows ran off—they were not pursued by the prosecutor and the policeman—I sent them after them—I do not knew how many person there were in the wagon—I should think twelve.
   COURT . Q.  How long before you got to Clapham Common did you see the skewers? A.  Between two and three o'clock—I cannot tell the name of the place.
   Ward.  This man said he saw the man take them from the gentleman, and Stone said, "Let me look," and he did, and said, "Here comes the man and the officer," and then these three men got out.
   Witness.  I saw the man that ran away had something black in his hand, after that I saw the skewers handed about—I said I saw the man have his arms round the prosecutor—he had something black is his hand.
   JURY . Q.  Did you see them in any body's hands, but the prisoners? A.  No—that was before those men ran away.
   MR. PHILLIPS to JEAN BAPTISTE ALARY . Q.  Were these skewers in paper? A.  I had a leather memorandum book which I lost.
