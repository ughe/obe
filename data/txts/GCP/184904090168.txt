the prisoner, but I received twelve shares of him on 12th Aug., and three on
the 19th-he gave me this memorandum on 12th Aug.--the body of it is my
writing, the signature is his-he acknowledges the receipt of 201., and
authorizes me to sell the shares within three weeks, if the money was not
returned—on 19th Aug. he gave me this memorandum, acknowledging the
receipt of 301., and authorizing me to sell the shares if the money was not
returned-I had bought the goods and fixtures at 3, Pancras-lane, for
about 181.- I bought them of the landlord's brother, who had seized for rent
--there was no stock of wine amongst the goods-I took a bill of sale of the
broker, and left the prisoner in possession--the prisoner was insolvent in
Feb., 1848—the goods which were upon the premises in Aug. were those
for which I had taken a bill of sale in the previous Feb.-I removed them in
Aug.--the landlord wished me to have them away-he wanted to have the
house--the bill of sale I think was dated about 18th Feb.-- he gave me the
shares on the 12th and 19th Aug.--I gave bim 201. on the 12th, and 301. on
the 19th, making 501. together, and he was a debtor to me before.
Cross-examined. Q. What does he owe you ? A. About 1801.-I could
not take these shares without advancing him money, because he would not
give them up-he asked me for a loan on these, and agreed to allow 51. when
the shares were sold, to go off the old account-he gave me permission to
sell the shares if in three weeks he did not redeem them-I am willing to
give up the shares now, if the advance is paid me-Mr. Walker brought, an
action against me in Oct.
Mr. Bodkin. Q. I believe you offered to give up your shares without
the money? A. I did at the Mansion-house,
Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Are you willing to do so now? A. No-it was
offered by the defendant to give me a Judge's order for the amount-he was
willing to do it at that time-that was on 12th March.
JOSEPH MANN. I am a clerk. I became acquainted with the prisoner in
June, 1847-I was in his service a short time I remember his insolvency
in 1848-I was a creditor of his to the amount of about 1001.-he was
cleared by the Insolvent Court in March -- there was no dividend paid--I
have seen this circular about these wines—there was the same thing when I
was with him he was then in Pancrass-lane-he had no stock at that time-
I occasionally went to the docks to taste wines—I was with him five months,
and left him on 23rd Nov., 1847.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you clerk to Mr. Stannard, or master, which?
4. I was clerk-I saw an advertisement in the paper, which I suppose
was his-I paid 1001. for integrity when I went in his service in June, 1847
-I did not sketch this circular, not a line of it-I recommended a printer to
him to print them I knew it was a circular-I had seen it on his table, and
knew the purport of it, I knew it was quite false-I did not take it to be
printed-1 procured bim a printer.
MR. BODKIN. Q. What salary were you to have ? A. To commence at
701. a year, and to be raised to 100l.
SAMUEL JARMAN. I am clerk to Mr. Humphreys-I produce these docu-
ments from the Insolvent Court-they are certified copies under the seal of
the Court in the matter of Edward Stannard-one is the balance-sheet--the
amount of debt is 15101., and the other is the adjudication on 17th March
an order was made to discharge him from prison.
