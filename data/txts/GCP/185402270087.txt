Emma Hudies Gooch. I am the wife of Edward Goochhe keepe a
public house in the Old Bailey--the prisoner has been employed as a char
woman at our house-in consequence of something that happened I marked
some money on Sunday, 19th Feb.--I put it into a box in a drawer. I
locked the drawer, and put the key in my pocket-I marked 1. 188. 6d., all
in silver, and put it into the drawer about half past 1 o'clock the prisoner
was that day in my house as a charwoman-about half past 3 o'clock I went
to the drawer, and missed two shillings I did not say anything to the
prisoner till she was leaving, about 6 o'clock in the evening-I then asked
her if she had any money-she said yes, she had three shillings I asked her
to let me dook at it she turned her purse out, and there were four shillings
-two of them were the marked money-these produced are the two. I
save them to the officer-here is a out across the neck-I made the same
mark on all the shillings I told my husband, and the prisoner was given
into custody.
WILLIAM JARVIS. I am a detective officer of the CityI took the
prisoner on the 19th, and received these two shillings from Mr. Gooohthe
prisoner said Mrs. Gooch had paid her the money.
EMMA HUGHES Gooch re-examined. I paid her no money on Sunday,
and this money was not marked till Sunday--what I paid her was on
Saturday night I then paid her & sixpence, and the rest in halfpence.
Guilty. Aged 24.-Confined Six Months.

WILLIAM HART. I am shopman to John Thomas Hart, & currier, in
Commercial-street, Spitalfields. On 31st Jan., a boy presented a paper to
me that is not here but on 1st Feb., another order was delivered to me
by a boy named Coventry- this is it (read "Sir, please let the bearer
have five of the same kind of roans that you sent yesterday. Arthur
M'Namara, Paul-street, Finsbury") Mr. MNamara was a customer of my
master's I had delivered to the first boy three skins the day before in
consequence of this order I delivered to Coventry on 1st Feb. five sking
they were my master's property--the boy signed the book-I afterwards
went to a house in Skinner-street-I found the elder Fossey and a boy in
the shop-I found on the premises behind the door four of the skins I had
given to Coventry--we asked the elder Fossey how they came there, and
who brought them- he said he did not know, they were brought by a person
who worked for him occasionally,
