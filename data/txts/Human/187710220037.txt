followed him to the house in Queen's Gate, but saw him no more that day—on the 26th July I again watched him—Prior was on duty, and Tilke passed direct to the platform without taking a ticket, and at Gloucester Road he gave up what was presumably a ticket which I had never seen him take—I watched him to Queen's Gate and he returned to Gloucester Road—he passed to the Hammersmith platform without going to the booking office—he travelled back to Earl's Court and gave up a ticket—neither going nor returning did he take a ticket—on the 27th July I again we watched him, and exactly the same thing occurred—Prior was collector that day—on the 28th July he was again collector—Tilke arrived at the Earl's Court station—I saw Prior go to his ticket table and remove a newspaper, under which there were two tickets—he removed one and took it to Tilke on the platform without going to the booking office—I went to Gloucester Road with the object of watching Tilke again—he did not get out at Gloucester Road that day, but I believe went on to South Kensington—I watched him return from the direction of Queen's Gate—he went to Gloucester Road, I following him—I don't know whether be showed a ticket at Earl's Court—he made a motion to Prior—at Gloucester Road he showed a ticket to Finch and passed on to the platform without going to the booking office—I watched him back to Earl's Court—I fol  lowed him from the station to a public-house, where he remained a few minutes, and left—directly he had left Prior arrived and went into the public-house, and after a short time came and looked out of the door as though looking for some one—he then went in and remained.
   Cross-examined.  When I watched Tilke I went to the place of business, Mr. Scarlett's—I never lost sight of him—his taking a ticket was the exception—I don't recollect any other occasion than I have given—on that occasion I saw him speaking to Brooks, and then he left the station—I went in the train and he followed by the next—he might have taken a ticket in the interval, and if so it would be necessary for him to take one on his return journey—sometimes there is a good deal of traffic at Earl's Court, and sometimes it is quite clear—he could not have obtained a ticket except on the one occasion without my seeing him—I have seen him carrying meat in the ordinary course of business and deliver it at the house in Queen's Gate.
   Re-examined.  I was instructed to watch. GUILTY. TILKE received a good character and was recommended to mercy by the Company as being the instrument of the Collectors.— Six Months' im prisonment each.
