Q.  Did the dog bark then in the same way it wed to do, or had it become much more quiet? A.  If strangers came into the ground it barked, it would not bark at young Bridgman—the dog constantly ran in and out the burial-ground, and seemed acquainted with the whole of the family—I do not know whether it barked at them after this, it might occasionally—it did not some times—when young Bridgman returned it, the lodger offered to give it to him—he said he thought the horse was broken to the dog, and he had no fur ther use for it, that they were going to dispose of the horse, to they would not wish it any further—on Thursday, the 21st of Sept., I was in my garden and saw Mr. Bridgman digging in his garden—I saw the spot where the coffin was afterwards found—it was not at that spot I saw him digging—it was on the same side, but nearer the stable—he was spreading the earth about with his spade—there was nothing hanging there at the time—I saw a coun terpane or sheet hanging there the day before, but he was not there then—the coffin was found, I should say, three or four yards from where I saw him digging—it was in the same direction.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was this on the 18th of Sept, which has been spoken of? A.  I do not know the day of the month—it was on a Tuesday, I think, about eleven in the day—I never saw young Bridgman's dog—I heard him say he had one, but it would not bark, and therefore he wished to borrow one to accustom the horse to it—the fence dividing our premises from Mr. Bridgman's, has gaps in it—the palings have been broken down in places, which the dog could get through—he had been in the habit of going in and out of Mr. Bridgman's premises as he liked, ever since we have been in the house—it had the same opportunity of being familiar with Mr. Bridgman's family before it was borrowed as after.
   Witnesses for the Defence. MOSES REDDING. I am a baker, and live at No. 8, Staverton-row, Wal worth-road. On the 6th of Sept. I remember seeing the elder Mr. Bridgman at Ramsgate, at the door of Cavendish chapel, about a quarter to nine o'clock, within a few minutes; I cannot say to a minute or two—I was with him for a quarter of an hour at least, in conversation.
