 1921. THOMAS JOHNSON was again  indicted for stealing 150 pieces of veneer, value 10 s  .; the goods Charles Watson , his master; and HENRY HART for feloniously receiving the same: to which
   THOMAS JOHNSON  pleaded GUILTY . Aged 17.— Confined One Year .
   MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES WATSON. On the morning of the 2nd of July I found a quantity of veneer being removed—I went in the course of that morning to Mr. Melton, mentioned the circumstance to him, and he showed me 200 or 300 veneers, which I am quite positive were mine—they were very bad ones, and were worth about 12 s .—I received information, and went to Hart's lodging—I found him in bed—I asked him where those veneers were which he had offered Mr. Melton for sale—he said he had shown them to a man named Jennings—we went to Jennings', and found about 500 veneers—they were all mine—I went to Coker's, and found, I should think, 200 or 300, which were worth about 30 s .—those at Jennings' were worth 7 l . or 8 l .—I found some locks and hinges of mine at Mr. Bayley's worth 14 s . or 15 s .—Hart is a cabinet-maker, he has worked for me. THOMAS MELTON. I am a cabinet-maker—I produced some veneers to Mr. Watson—I bought them of Hart for 6 s .—there were from 200 to 300—I told him they were of no use to me—he had offered me a lot of veneers previous to that, but I did not buy them, I did not see them—he said he had a lot to sell, if I wanted to buy them—I bought this lot of him—I asked him if they were come honestly by—he told me he bought them, and he was going to work some himself—I said, "They are of no use to me; if I buy them, I must take them to Mr. Watson." JAMES COKER( a prisoner. ) I remember the prosecutor coming to my house with the policeman—I produced some veneers to him—I had bought about 200 of them of Hart—he took wood in exchange for them—he said they were his own, and it was all right—I can hardly tell what day he sold them to me—it was about five weeks before Mr. Melton called on me—I had two dealings with Hart, the second time I bought about sixty of him. EDWARD JENNINGS( a prisoner. ) I remember the prosecutor finding some veneers at my house—I bought them of Hart, about a fortnight before the prosecutor came—I bought them in two separate lots, for about 25 s ., alto gether—I put them under the boards at my house—I had no other place to keep them from being spoiled.
   Hart.  I bought all these things of one man, who is not taken into custody. THOMAS DAVIS. I bought five dozen locks of Hart, on the Saturday night, as Epsom races were on the Monday; I sold them to Mr. Bayley; I took Mr. Watson to Mr. Bayley, and he recognized the property in my presence. CHARLES WATSON re-examined.  I saw the property at Mr. Bayley's, that and all the other property was mine.
   HART  — GUILTY . Aged 28.— Confined One Year .
