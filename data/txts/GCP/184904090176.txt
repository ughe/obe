1011. JOHN EATON and CAROLINE EATON, breaking and entering
the dwelling-house of John Purslow, and stealing therein 86 yards of cloth,
2 coats, and 16 waistcoat-pieces, value 281. 168., his goods.
Ms. COOPER conducted the Prosecution.
SABAH PUrsLow. I am the wife of John Purslow, a tailor, of 6, Upper
King-street, Bloomsbury. On the night of 22nd Feb., I fastened up the
shop-I saw the shop-door fast at eight oʻelock next motning about twenty-
five minutes past eight my daughter called me I found the shop open and
everything taken from the counter.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Had not you opened the shop door
that morniag? d. No, I found it open about ten minutes after I came
down--the shutters were not taken down-my husband was up-stairs-he
is not here the property has not been traced.
Saran Purstow, I am the daughter of the last witness. On 23rd Feb.,
about half-past eight, I opened the passage-door and saw the female prisoner
-she asked if my father wanted a waistedat-maker-I went up to ask him,
came down, and told her “No”-she said, " Thank you," and shut the door
- she looked very stout, and part of her gown was spread over the handle of
the door--in about five minutes I found the door wide open, and called my
mother down.
Cross-examined. Q. When you saw a stout woman at Marylebone, did
you say you knew her by her bonnet and breast 1 A. Yes; I had a good
view of her face, and am quite sure she is the party.
WILLIAM HARRISS. I am a sawyer. On 23rd Feb., about half-past eight,
I was taking down my shutters, and saw the male prisoner coming out of Mr.
Purslow's shop with a bundle of clothes, and give it to a woman under an
archway--I could see two rolls of cloth, and part of a coat-1 followed them
as far as Orange-street.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you ever seen the man before ? A. No, but I
cannot be mistaken in him-1 did not tell Mr. Purslow-I thought they were
parties who worked for bim-1 did not notice his eye-the right side of his
face was to me-(the prisoner's left eye was injured).
