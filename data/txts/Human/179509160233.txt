 Q. Did you promise him any thing if he would confess? - NO.
 Q. Did you make any threats? - Neither to my knowledge. I asked him if he was not ashamed to serve me in the manner he had done? He intreated me for mercy; he begged I would forgive him, on account of his children; that was nearly the expression he made use of; I told him he had no thought of me and my children, or else he would not have done an act of the kind; I said, I suppose let you tell the story, you would say this is the first time; he said, no, I don't say it is the first, it is the second; I said, you know I have put my hand in my pocket and lent you money, and when you have offered to pay me half of it, I said, you had better leave it and pay me when you can. He then went over to Bow-street, and was committed.
 Mr. Moore. You had a good character with this man? - I had, or else he should not have been taken into my house.
 Court. Has he a large family? - I never knew he had but two children; but that day he told me he had a third.
   JOHN LASHROOKE sworn.
 I live in Hungerford-market. On the morning of this day I was about a hundred yards from Mr. Freame's door, about ten o'clock in the morning. I see the prisoner come out of Mr. Freame's house with a pail at his back; he walked some little distance from the house; I see Mr. Freame come out of the house after him, which was a signal to me to stop him; I then crossed over the way then and came within six or seven yards of him, and by that time Mr. Freame had come up with him; I stopped him, Mr. Freame was behind him and I was before him. There were two quarters loaves in the pail, when taken off his back at the watch-house.
 Q. Did he say any thing? - I did not hear. Mr. Freame said, he had heard of people carrying iniquity in their bosom, but thou carriest it at thy back.
 Q. To Prosecutor. What is the value of these two loaves? - Ten-pence halfpenny a quartern loaf, and nearly all the money out of my pocket.
 Prisoner. I leave it to my counsel. My master entirely left the business to his daughter; he entirely looked after the bakehouse. I, that morning, had taken out a great deal of bread; I always went down into the kitchen for what bread I wanted; I was going to serve customers with this bread, they lay in my way that I was going for the yeast; it was a considerable wet morning, and I put these loaves in the pail, and my master came out after me. I always used to make it my practice to take the bread out of the kitchen, while there was any bread in the kitchen.
 Court to Prosecutor. Was he at liberty to go down into the kitchen and take what bread he pleased? - No, he brought it into the shop and put it on the counter when the shop was emptied, and it was counted.
 Prisoner. Were you ever there to do it yourself? - I was there, or somebody that I entrusted.
 Miss. FREAME sworn.
 Q. Do you ever act in your father's business? - I do.
 Q. Was it over the custom in his business for the bread to go out without being counted? - Never.
 Q. Might it not happen, by accident, that bread may go out and account given of it afterwards? - Never.
