 2207. HENRY CURSON , stealing 9 sovereigns, 6 shillings, 1 sixpens, and 2 pence; the moneys of William Atwooll Bennett .
   MR. PARNKLL conducted the Prosecution. THAMAR BENNETT. I am the wife of William Atwooll Bennett, the mate of the Clementine , plying between this country and Ireland. We live at 3, Queen-street, Horsleydown—the prisoner is my cousin by marriage—I left him, his wife, and my sister, Miss Curtis, in charge of the house, and went with my husband to Ireland—I was in the room with them on the evening I left, and counted out nine sovereigns, 6 s ., and 8 d . on the table, folded it in a receipt for the rates, and asked them to be kind enough to pay that to the landlord, when he called—both the prisoner and his wife said they would do—the wife took it up—they were to sleep in the front-room, first-floor—I returned on 16th Aug., between three and four o'clock—the prisoner was not at home—he came in about four—he told me I had no occasion to sit up, for it would be very late when he came in, he would take the street-door key—I objected to that, and said my sister should sit up for him—I went to bed between ten and eleven, in the back-room, second-floor—I heard no thieves in the house—soon after eleven I heard a knock, and the door unlocked—my sister came up—I went down, and saw the prisoner come in, with two con stables—next morning I gave the prisoner notice to leave—he said, "I have written to my father concerning the money that was lost last night"—I said "What money?"—he did not answer me—I said, "Have not you paid the rent?"—he said, "No"—I asked if the landlord had called—he said, "Yes; once," but his wife was not in, and he could not get the money—I asked him where the receipts were—he said the robbers had left them, and he gave them me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Have not you accused the people of the house of stealing a sable cape? A.  No; I lost it in the street. ELIZABETH CURTIS.
