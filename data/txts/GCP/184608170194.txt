NEW COURT.- Wednesday, Aug. 26, 1846.
Fifth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq.
1698. SAMUEL WILES was indicted for stealing 28., the monies of
William James Harris, his master.
WILLIAM JAMES HARRIS. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Charles-
street, Back-road, St. George in the East-the prisoner was in my service-
in consequence of suspicion, on the 13th of August, I marked five shillings.
and put them into my till about five or six o'clock-I went about seven
o'clock, and found them safe there-I then marked two more shilling
and a sixpence, and put them in the till-about eight o'clock I went to the
till, and missed one of the shillings-I then took the sixpence out, and let
the six marked shillings in the till-I sent the prisoner over to the ware-
house-he came back, and I went into the parlour at the back of the shop-
watched, and saw two children come in for half a pound of butter-they gave
him a shilling, and he gave them sixpennyworth of halfpence in retur,
which he took from the till there was plenty of copper in the till, but no
silver but what was marked-1 directly went into the shop, and there was but
five shillings in the till-there ought to have been seven-I took the pri-
soner by the collar and said, "What have you done with the two shillings you
have just taken out of the till?"-he trembled very much- I pushed him into
the parlour--he said he knew nothing about it-I told him I certainly should
send for a policeman-he then said he would give it me back if I would for
give him, I said, "I told you I was robbed a few weeks ago by the last boy,
and you knew very well what I intended to do with the next I caught"-be
said, “Pray forgive me on account of my mother"-I gave him into custody,
and two shillings were shaken out of his boot--one was marked the other
was not-this is one I had marked with a scratch on the top of the crown.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. How many shillings have you
marked ? A. I never marked any before I did not state before the Magis
trate that I marked six shillings-I marked five shillings first, and two sbil-
lings and a sixpence afterwards-this is my signature to this deposition-I
did not say I marked six shillings, and put them into the till, where thert
was no other but copper money-this was read over to me, but not about the
six shillings- I was watching the prisoner all day-I had a good opportunity
of seeing what he did.
Court. Q. Are you sure you marked five shillings at five o'clock? 4.
Between five and six o'clock-about eight I found the five shillings right-
then put in two shillings and a sixpence more marked-I sent the prisoner to
the warehouse, and about nine o'clock the children came, and gave him a shil-
ling-shortly after I missed two shillings, and two shillings were found in his
boot-one was marked, the other was not.
ROBERT BOARD (police-constable K 232.) I was called on the night of
the 13th of Aug. to take the prisoner-I told him Mr. Harris charged him
with robbing him-he begged him to forgive him for the sake of his mother
- I found two shillings, five pence, and a farthing, in his trowsers pockel
and in his boot two shillings, one marked, and the other not-be begge
Mr. Harris to forgive him.
Guilty. Aged 20.--Confined Six Months.
