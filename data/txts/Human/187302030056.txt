   Cross-examined by MR. ATKINSON . My business has been carried on now nearly forty years; twenty odd years by my father before me—we do 100,000 l . a year in shipping to Australia, South America, and New Zealand—there is nothing unusual in the practice to get advances on goods in the way that has been done in this case—I have over fifty firms in the City, of the highest respectability, for whom I ship in the same way—I never heard of any business transaction where they did not render account sales—they have not been rendered in this case, because I have only got a small return of goods—I have not got 300 l . out of the whole of the consignments—when goods go out the consignee exercises his discretion as to whether the market is in a proper condition for the purposes of sale—he would hold them till the proper season—whatever the return sales will be over and above what I have advanced the trustees will get, whatever it is; whatever balance there is will be given to the trustees.
   Re-examined.  I had dealt with Wilmius before, when he was in Fen-church Street, in the same way, before his bankruptcy—that was twelve months before he began as Cramer & Co.—I rendered account sales for the business done in Fenohurch Street to Coiner & Co., of Manchester, who were the trustees—goods are about four months going from here to Aus-tralia—the goods would get there about the beginning of the winter, but they ought to be there a month or two before the season comes on.
