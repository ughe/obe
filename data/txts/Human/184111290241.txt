I was a great deal engaged at the time in business, and was desirous of ascertaining all I thought he had taken—I sent for an officer—Cuthbert was brought—I said, "This youth has been robbing me, and perhaps you can get from him better than I can the ex tent of his depredations"—I certainly did not authorise him to hold out to the lad, that if he told the truth he would not be prosecuted—I did not tell Cuthbert that I did not wish to hurt him at all, and that it would be better to confess the whole—it never was my intention not to prosecute the lad, and when Cuthbert said, down stairs, "If you will promise not to pro secute him he will confess to all he has done"—I said, "I will make him no promise, but give him pencil and paper, and let him write down all he has taken." RICHARD HITCHMAN was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of November , 1 purse, value 1 s  .; 10 shillings, and 2 sixpences; the pro perty of Joseph Limperani , from the person of Christiana Judith Limperani . CHRISTIANA JUDITH LIMPERANI. I am the wife of Joseph Limperani; we live in Albany-road, Camberwell. On the 12th of November, at five o'clock in the afternoon, I was going home with my daughter from Walworth-gate —I got to the corner of Albany-road, between the wall and the Watch-box, exactly at the angle of the road, I felt the proximity of three young men who had been following us—I felt myself touched, and one of the men immediately passed me—seeing him put his left-hand into his pocket, and connecting that with having been touched, I put my own hand into my pocket, and immediately missed my purse—I saw a man pass me who I thought had put his hand into my pocket—I told my daugh ter I had been robbed, and my purse was gone—a policeman being in the immediate vicinity I gave information immediately—the man who passed me, and put his hand into his pocket had on a brown Taglioni coat, with pockets in front—I lost a purse containing, as nearly as I can recollect, 11 s .—next morning the prisoner was produced to me—from his personal appearance he bears a resemblance to one of the three men who were there—I did not turn my head to look in his face—I had got change from Mr. Townsend, and one of the shillings was excessively black between the head and the rim—I have since seen one which I believe to be that—to the best of my belief this is it—( looking at a shilling )—the purse has not been found—this shilling is the only one I can identify.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  I believe it was the shadows of the men you saw? A.
