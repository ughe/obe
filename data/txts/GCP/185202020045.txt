Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. After these goods bad been sent
to Woolf and Lyons, did you write a letter by their direction to Mr. Churchill ?
A. No; I took a letter to Mr. Churchill before they were brought in be-
lieve it was two or three days before I do not know whether I ever saw Mr.
Churchill-I saw a gentleman at Ellis and Bverington's- made no repre-
sentation to him; that was in the letter-I wrote the letter. (Mr. CLARKSON
slated that notice to produce the letter had been given, but Mr. BALLANTINE
was not aware of it, nor had he got the letter; the COURT, therefore, admitted
secondary evidence)-the substance of the letter was that Woolf and Lyons
would not give the price for the goods; but if they would take something less
(I cannot recollect what, but it was fd. or id., or something like that) they
would have the goods; and would Mr. Churchill let them know at once, as
Mr. Lyons was going to Manchester that afternoon-I took no other note
there there was another person in their employ who may bave done so, but
I do not know of it-I had no conversation with the gentleman I saw, on
the subject of the return of the goods.

SOLOMON A DRAHAM Hart. I live at 22, Bury-street, St. Mary Axe, and
am a merchant and warebouseman. I have not got my books here; I have
some invoices-on 29th Sept. I purchased some silks of the bankrupts at
their warehouse, and I should presume that I agreed to purchase them at the
same time I bad been in the habit of buying silks to make up into parasols
- I do not recollect exactly what time it was, but I think it was in the moro-
ing part, I generally leave home about half-past 10 o'clock--I saw Mr.
Lyons, and to the best of my belief Mr. Woolf also-1 did not at that time
know there were any boxes of silk-they showed me the silks, and asked me
if they would suit me--I bid for them—they were lying on a counter, or
table, up stairs, out of the boxes-I bought the lot, thirty-five pieces, at 18.78.
a yard; they had asked me 1d. or 2d. more they were faney silks, not
figured-they had a stripe down them-on 30th I went there to give them
part of the money, and bought a lot of coloured glacés-I laid out with them
on the two days 5431. 3s. 11d.- these are the invoices of the two transactions
(produced -1 do not know whose writing they are in-I paid the money at
various times to Mr. Woolf and to Mr. Lyons-) received the invoices, I
think, from Mr. Woolf-I had thirty-four pieces of figured or striped, 2,790
yards, and I do not know how many lengths of glacé-tbere would be 2,833
yards of glacé on the two items-I got two lots of glacé. 02
Cross-examined by MR. HEDDLESTON. Q. What price did you give for
the glacé? A. 23. 3d. for one lot, and 2s. 4d. for another-Lyons told me
he wanted to sell the silks because they did not suit the parasol trade-he
might have said be had tried them, and found that they fraved-) do not
recollect that he said he was obliged to keep them, because Ellis and Ever-
ington would not take them back; I do not recollect Blis and Everington's
name being mentioned at all—it was a thin, steezey silk, a soft silk-I showed
both the silks to parasol makers, and they would not buy them-I have not
been able to sell them for parasol purposes only offered them to two
houses--I had been in the habit of selling goods before that time to Wook
and Lyons.
