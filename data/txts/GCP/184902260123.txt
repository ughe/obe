George WEARE WAKEFIELD. I am a draper, and live at 8, Sun-street,
Bishopsgate. I took tliis note of Mrs. Hawley on 1st Feb.it has my
writing on it-I marked it when I took it.
EDWARD HAWLEY (policeman, K 209). I belong to the Stratford station.
I know the prisoner- he was the policeman on duty at Stratford-broadway
on the night of 9th Jan.-I was at home when he came in on Wednesday
night, 31st Jan.--he threw down a piece of paper on the table-I opened it,
and found it was a note-I said, « Charley, where did you get this note
from ?" - he said, " It is my own, I have saved it up "-he asked me to let
my wife go with him to buy some things.
Cross-examined. Q. You have known him as a brother-officer? 4. Yes
--I have the same opinion of him that my wife has- I would trust him with
anything I believe he has a good character in the force.
Guilty of finding the note and appropriating it to his own use. Aged 22.-

774. WILLIAM WILLIAMS, stealing 450 bricks, value 188.; the goods
of Anthony Storey Reed. Mr. Robinson conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE PERREY. I am a bricklayer, and work for Anthony Storey Reed,
a builder, at Walthamstow. He is carrying on some buildings near Wood-
ford-bridge--the prisoner is carman to Mr. Hooper-on 17th Jan., about
twenty minutes past seven o'clock in the morning, I met him about 150
yards from the buildings, with a horse and cart with bricks in it-be spoke to
me, and said, “You are going to have another turn"-I said, "Yes"-I
went tracking the cart-wheels to the heap of bricks, and the prisoner had
put the mud down over the tracks-I missed a number of bricks--I sent a
person after the cart--the prisoner saw him, and ran away-he did not
return to business.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. Who was the prisoner employed
by? 1. By James Hooper, a bricklayer, who is employed by Mr. Reed-
he had Hooper's cart to cart the bricks.
John KINSLEY. I am a labourer in Mr. Reed's employ-I saw the bricks
in the cart-I followed the prisoner with Hooper's cart when I came in
sight of him he ran away-I ran towards him.
THOMAS DELABETAUCHE (policeman, K 146). I went to Woodford-rond,
in consequence of information, and took possession of a horse and cart, loaded
with bricks-the eart is at the Green-yard: Hooper has been for it, but it
has not been given to him I produce one of the bricks from the cart, and
one from the head at the buildings they correspond.
HENRY Avis policeman, N 248). I received directions to look after the
prisoner, and apprehended him in Whitechapel on 2nd Feb.-- he said what a
fool he was that he had not run away further
Cross-examined. Q. Have you been to Hooper ? 4. Yes-he appeared
the first time, but did not appear afterwards.
(The prisoner received a good character). GUILTY. Aged 20.-
Recommended to mercy by the Jury.-Confined Eighteen Months.
