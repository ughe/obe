SARAH LOVELL. I live at 4, Maryland-buildings, Rotherhithe; my father, James Driscoll, keeps the house. About a quarter before one o'clock, on the 24th Sept., I was awoke by a noise—I threw up the sash and saw the prisoner getting out of the parlour window—he turned and held up his fist, and said, "I am sorry I did not do for the lot of you"—I went down stairs, and found the parlour window open; it had been safe between eight and nine o'clock the night before—the window was shut down, but not fastened—the prisoner lodged next door to us at that time, but the people have removed since he has been in prison.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q. YOU  say the window was shut the night before? A.  Yes, my sister fastened it—my sister has had a few words with the landlady of the house where the prisoner lived—the prisoner was brought back by the policeman immediately. ELIZA LILLIE. I am the sister of the last witness—I am the wife of James Robert Lillie—he is mate of a ship—I live in the house with my sister—I shut the parlour window shutters between eight and nine o'clock that night—I afterwards heard a noise, and went and awoke my sister. DAVID FIGG( policeman, M  33). I was on duty, and heard a cry of" Police!"—I saw the prisoner run out from the court—I asked him what was the matter; he said he did not know—he ran, and I ran and caught him—I said he must come back with me—he said he would not, and resisted violently—with the assistance of a private watchman I took him back—Sarah Lovell saw him and identified him directly—he was sober—he said he would knock my by brains out.
   Witness for the Defence. MARIA DRISCOLL . The prisoner lodged with me—I am no relation of his—he has borne a good character—he has been in the habit of coming home late and getting in at the window in my absence—there has been a little quarrel between Mrs. Lillie and me. NOT GUILTY.
