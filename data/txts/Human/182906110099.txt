 PATRICK BURK. I lodge at the prosecutor's. On the 11th of May, I was going up to my bed-room, and saw his door open, about eleven o'clock, and the prisoner with his keys in his hand, locking or unlocking the box.

 JOHN SPOONER. I keep a chandler's-shop on Saffronhill. On the 11th of May, between eleven and twelve o'clock the prisoner came and asked me to give him a sovereign for a sovereign's worth of silver - he had three or four half-crowns in his hand - I said I had not any gold.
   ALEXANDER PHILLIPS. I sell hats on Saffron-hill. On the 11th of May, the prisoner came about twelve in the morning, and purchased a hat for 3s. 6d. - he paid for it with a half-crown and a shilling - he pulled out a bit of paper, and I saw a sovereign in it - he said he had been going to throw it away to light his pipe with - I advised him, as he was in liquor, to get it changed into silver. as then, if he lost part of it, he might not lose it all - I gave him eight half-crowns for it - he went away, and left his old hat, which the prosecutor had the next day.
   ANN MARIA BAYLIS. I am a baker on Saffron-hill. On the 11th of May, about four o'clock, the prisoner came into my shop, and asked for a pennyworth of bread, and then asked me for change for a sovereign, which I gave him - he left me 15s. to keep for him till he called - I had seen him two or three times - he did not appear very tipsy.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I took the prisoner, and received this money.
   LUKE BRUNTON. This is the prisoner's old hat - there were no sovereigns in my box - there were four half-crowns and the rest in small silver - there was 1l. 10s. or 1l. 18s. in all.
 JURY. Q. Was the prisoner in the habit of putting money into your box for you to keep for him? A. No; I forgot the keys that day.

BRYAN MALONY was indicted for stealing, in the 14th of May , 1 plane, value 2s., and 1 jacket, value 1s. 6d., the goods of William Stevens ; 1 axe, value 1s. 6d.; 1 hammer, value 6d., and 1 apron, value 6d., the goods of George Healman ; and 1 plane, value 2s., the goods of William Waugh White.
   WILLIAM STEVENS. I am a carpenter. I was working in Charlotte-street, Fitzroy-square; I left my tools there on the evening of the 14th of May, and when I went next morning I missed a plane and a jacket.
   GEORGE HEALMAN. I was working at the same place. I missed an axe, a hammer, and an apron; I know nothing of the prisoner.
 WILLIAM WAUGH WHITE. I missed a jack-plane from the same place.
   THOMAS WALLACE. I am an officer. At half-past seven o'clock on the evening of the 14th of May, I saw the prisoner and two other men in Rathbone-place; the prisoner went on to Charlotte-street - I saw two of the workmen come out of No. 1; he followed them some distance, then returned, went into the house, and came out again with these tools - I took him, and asked if they were his own; he said they were; I then asked if he worked there- he said he should answer no more questions.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met two men, who asked if I would go over the way and get some tools; I asked if there was any danger of my getting into trouble - they said No, but they had been having some words with their master, and did not like to go; I said I was going to take them to those two young men - the officer saw them, but he did not take them.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined One Month.
