2074. WILLIAM ATKINS and HENRY KING, breaking and entering
the dwelling-house of Susannah Durman, and stealing 1 looking-glass, value
40.; her goods.
THOMAS DURMAN. I live with my mother, Susannah Durman, at the
Oral, Hackney-road. On Saturday morning, 19th Aug., a little before eight
o'clock, I saw the looking-glass safe on the mantel-piece in the front parlour
-the bottom sash of the window was down, but not fastened the top sash
was a little down-this is the glass-it is my mother's, who is a widow.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. When did you miss it? 4.
About half-past eight o'clock in the evening.
JOHN BATES. I live at No. 11, in the Oval, at Hackney, opposite Mrs. Dur.
man's. About eight o'clock in the morning of 19th Aug., I was in our front
parlour, and saw the prisoners walk up to Mrs. Durman's window-they looked
in, and pushed it up from the bottom, then walked away and returned
Atkins got in and handed the looking-glass out to King, who ran off towards
Hackney-road, and Atkins ran the other way.
Cross-examined. Q. Was your window open! 4. No I saw them
through the glass- I was not near enough to the window for them to see me-I
did not know tbem-King bad a blue frock-coat with a velvet collar, and
drab trowsers-Atkins was dressed as a bricklayer, all in drab-be wore a
cap-I saw them again at the Police-court-the policeman called and told me
they were there.
EBENEZER ORRIS DURMAN. I am a son of Susannah Durman; I live
with her. On 19th Aug., about eight o'clock, I missed the glass, and went
out-a little boy was bringing it back..
WILLIAM Fox (police-sergeant, N 5). I took Atkins-I told him it was
for stealing a glass-he said he knew nothing about it.
