DANIEL GEORGE ROBERTS. I am a hairdresser, of No. 47, Carnaby-street.
I know the prisoner and Skingsley– I have seen them together at my house,
at various times, from last summer--they were there in the beginning of
July-I saw several sums paid by Skingsley to the prisoner-168. 8d. for the
issue of a writ, 78. 6d. for re-sealing writ, 28. 4d. in addition for the same
purpose, 28. 6d. for stopping the issue of the writ by the defendant, and
38. 6d. for examining the defendant's plea ; Jarvis had a little bit of paper
in his hand, which he told me was the plea, but not at that interview.
COURT. Q. What passed when the 38. 6d. was paid, what was said by
the prisoner previously? A. He told me that it was for examining the
defendant's plea in answer to the declaration, as it was not sufficient to
answer the purpose of the trial, and that he would consult Counsel on the
subject, and shonld want 38. 6d. for so doing there was a bit of paper,
which he said was the answer of the defendant, but he did not read it to
me; he produced it, but whether it was before or after, I cannot say.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you rather an extensive business? 4. I have
- the news is sometimes talked over-I recollected this statement because I
was the unfortunate introducer of him to it I have known the defendant
since I called on him seven years ago-he represents himself as an attorney,
and has offices in Laurence Pountney-lane-I never saw any clerks-I
thonght it was a favour to introduce him, but found that not one case had
been properly done by him or done at all. I have no strong feeling in the
matter, no more than seeing right done I know nothing of Mr. Wright's
case, and was not at the police court to argue it--I have not referred
to the prisoner's little memorandum book, but he had it before the Magis-
trate-I have never seen this extract from the book ( produced).
COURT. Q. Are you sure the 38. 6d. was paid for him to consult
Counsel as to the sufficiency of the plea! 4. That is what he told me,
that the plea was not sufficient, and he should consult Counsel on the
subject--that was after the 38. 6d. was paid before it was paid, he said
that the plea was not sufficient, and that he should have 38. 6d. for examin-
ing it-he did not tell me, in Mr. Skingsley's presence, that the 38. 6d.
was to be paid for him to consult Counsel-he said that it was for him to
examine the defendant's plea he did not say that he had examined it, that
was wbat he had to receive the money for. (Mr. TORR here stated that he
could not carry the case further).
Not GUILTY.
