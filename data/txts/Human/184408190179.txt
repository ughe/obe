JOSEPH LOVELL , JOSEPH BULL , GEORGE WILLIAMS, and JAMES PHIPPS, were indicted for stealing 2 sacks, value 2 s  .; 4 bushels of oats, 13 s  .; and 5 bushels of beans, 5 s  .; the goods of George Stansby. LAWRENCE ALEXANDER( police-constable D  35.) At half-past five o'clock in the morning of the 4th of Aug. I received information, and went to Mr. Stansby's wharf, at Paddington—I found his stable door open and the inner loft door had been broken open—I went to a boat in the canal, and saw Williams and Phipps unloading some gravel out of it—I asked how, long they had been there—they said that they had been there all night, that they came at eight o'clock in the evening, and worked till half-past ten, then went to the Red Lion, came back at twelve, and worked till two, then laid down on a truss till daylight—I said, "I suppose you have heard about Mr. Stansby's place being broken, and the corn stolen?"—they said, yes they had—I asked if they had seen anybody—they said, "No"—I asked them if it was possible for any one to come there while they were at work, and they not see them—they said, no, it was not, and they had not seen any one—I went into the boat, and on the top of the deck I saw some loose corn scattered about—I lifted up the scuttle, and observed Bull and Lovell there—I asked what they did there—Lovell said he came to assist in unloading the boat; but that they had slept too long—I told them to get out—Lovell got out, but Bull seemed rather reluctant—I said, "Get out"—I saw something under him, and said, "What is that?"—he said it was nothing—I said it was something—he then said it was corn for the horses—I saw it was a sack—I then saw on the opposite side, where Lovell had been, some split beans—I asked who they belonged to—he said they were for the boat horses—I awoke the captain of the boat, who was asleep aft, and asked if he had got any corn—he said, "Yes, from three to four bushels" and he had two empty sacks, and a piece of a sack and a tow ing line—I asked if he knew about any beans—he said no, he never gave his horses any split beans, and if there were any they did not belong to him—I found about a bushel of beans, and a sack containing black oats, beside the sack of white oats I had found before—I sent for assistance to the station, and then the captain pointed out three bushels of black oats, which belonged to him—he said the white oats were not his—I had seen the prisoners pass me a few minutes before twelve o'clock—they were all together, and walking along the Wharf-road—when I found the place broken I traced beans from the stable to the boat. JOHN HARVEY WHITELOCK.
