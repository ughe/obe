William MILLER (policeman, F 80). On 14th Sept. the prisoner was
given into my custody by Mr. Webb's assistant, with this ring, which I have
produced- I gave it to Mr. Johnson, who assayed it, and gave it me sealed up.
Sutcliffe. I am an assistant to my brother, who is a refiner.
Mr. Clark's assistant brought me a ring; it was metal inside, cased with gold,
and worth 48. or 58.
- Johnson. I am an assay-master. I assayed one ring about the 10th ;
part of it was gold, and worth about 10s. an ounce-I did not weigh it, and
cannot tell how much it was worth-it was under half an ounce-I have no
doubt, from their appearance, that these rings are all of the same character-
(The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate was here read~"I always
thought the ring was gold, my husband used to tell me 80-my husband has
been dead two years—the reason I went to the pawnbroker's after I had been
cautioned was, because I wanted money.")
Prisoner's Defence. I did not mean to do any injury to the parties; I do
not know the manufacturer of the rings; if they do not prove to be gold
I cannot help it. GUILTY. Aged 40.-Confined Twelve Months.
2006. ROBERT YORSTON, unlawfully obtaining money of John Winston
by false pretences.
MR. M'Mazon conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN WINSTON. I live on my property, and reside at 84, Waterloo-road.
About 22nd Sept. my son was in custody on some criminal charge-on that
evening I met the prisoner at Mr. Butcher's shop, in the Waterloo-road-
I told him my son had unfortunately got into trouble-he said, "Well, what
is it?"-I told him I understood it was for stealing a looking-glass, and I
believed it was at the West-end-he said, anything he could do for me he
would-I said, "I have known you some time as an attorney, I suppose you
are an attorney ?"-he said, “Yes, I am, living at 10, Clifford's-inn"-be
said nothing about being a clerk to Mr. Townshend-I never heard that name
till I was at the Police-court, when the prisoner was there on this charge
I said I was very much inclined not to do anything for my son, but for the
sake of my own conscience I must employ one of you gentlemen (the Counsel),
and I asked him what money he wanted-he said 11. 38. 6d., or not exceeding
258. for the present-he said there would be 1l. 18. for the counsel, and
2s.6d, for the clerk's fee it was afterwards decided that he was to have 25s.,
and he was to meet me at Mr. Butcher's the next afternoon, when he was to
make out a brief-I did not meet him the next day, I was very ill of the
cholera, and was advised to go into the country till the trial was over-I went
to Mr. Butcher, told him to let the prisoner have 23s. 60. or 258., whichever
he wanted, and I went into the country-Mr. Butcher did not owe me any
money-I returned on the evening of 25th, the day the trial took place
I knew the trial would come on that day-I called on Mr. Butcher that
evening, to inquire what my son was done to-Mr. Butcher told me he had got
twelve months, and produced this letter to me-(read-“10, Clifford's-inn,
Tuesday afternoon. Dear Butcher, I wrote yesterday to Mr. Palmer, and
gave him all to-morrow (Wednesday) to pay the bill, and 58. expenses. Will
you tell Mr. Vincent his son was tried in the name of John George, and was
got off with only twelve months-what think you of that? Yours truly,
R. Yorston")-I called on Mr. Butcher again the next morning, and asked
him if he had paid the sum-he said, “Yes, I have, and here is the receipt
for the money from Yorston," and he produced this paper produced)-(read-
