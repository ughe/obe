First Session, 1884–85.
Coroner's Inquisition with, the wilful murder of Arthur Arnold.
Messrs. POLAND and MONTAGU WILLIAMS Prosecuted; Mr. PUROELL
Defended at the request of the COURT. DE
GEORGE NASH. I live at 8, Clarendon Street, and am a labourer-
on Sunday, 21st September, about half-past 12 o'clock in the morning,
I was with a man named Murphy and Arnold at the top of Clarendon
Street-we all three went to a coffee stall at the top of Bermondsey New
Road; we met on the road Harris, William Piggott, Margaret Good-
son, and Annie Keefe-I did not know her, but I know Goodson-
Goodson said to me “What cheer, Punch?"-I said “What cheer,
Mog?"-Harris immediately after that came round and struok me in the
jaw-I said “What did you do that for ?"-he then hit me again, and
said, “What did you want to kick her for?"--I had not touched her-I
hit him and knocked him against the shutters---Arnold then held me back
and said "I am more his equal than you "-I got away from Arnold and
struck Harris in the face-he then went to the shutters and pulled some-
thing from underneath the left breast of his coat and struck me in the
back-I then found that I was stabbed-Harris then ran away and Arnold
after him-I was taken to the hospital; when I got there I found that
Arnold was there he was wounded also.
AMORTIVA
Cross-examined. When I and Arnold came up the two girls were talk-
ing to the prisoner-I heard the prisoner say “What did you want to
kick her for ?"-I did not hear him say "Don't interfere with these
girls”-Arnold did not strike the prisoner in the face and make his nose
bleed, nor did he knock him down I did not knock him down, I am
quite certain of it- he did not say while on the ground "Let me get up;
oh, they are killing me."

JOHN MURPHY. I am a labourer, living at Tabard Street on the
night of 20th September I was with Nash at a friendly meeting, and
about 20 minutes past 12 we went to the top of Clarendon Street, and
there met Arnold --we all three then went along to Old Kent Road, to a
coffee stall-we met Harris with two or three women-Nash spoke to the
girls and said “Halloa, Mog"--with that Harris struck him in the jaw
and said “What did you kick her for?” --with that Harris stepped back
and Nash ran towards him-I stood on the causeway and saw Harris
running along by the shirt shop as if trying to get away from Nashhe
fell back towards the shops-Arnold ran after him, and I ran towards
him to see what was the matter-I saw Araold stagger back, and he said
"I am stabbed "-he then fell-I ran after Harris a gentleman threw
Nash's coat across my shoulders and threw his hat on the ground I ran
after Harris down towards Tabard Street-he was taken into custody as
he was walking up from Falstaff Yard, and I and the constable and Harris
went to the station-I was sober and so was Nash and Arnold. Jou
Cross-examined. When Harris fell back against the shop Nash was
near him; Arnold was at a distance from him I cannot say whether
Nash struck Harris; I saw he was bleeding when he came up from
Falstaff Yard-I never saw Arnold have hold of Harris-Arnold fell back,
and then Harris ran- I did not hear any one say “Let me get up; they
are killing me”-there were a lot of men round Harris; I could not see
what was going on inside the crowd.
