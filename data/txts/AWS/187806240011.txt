Mrs. Steele left one box here which she took away last Monday morning

having showed him a list which he read through and said I know
nothing of any of these they were trunks and boxes, and a chest of
drawers-I said 44 The bailiff must exeeute his warrant and search the

Mrs. Steele I shall certainly advise them to give up all their property and
money to the creditors."

EDWARD WILLAM SAWYER. I am a farmer, of Helmingham and am
Mr. Peck's brother-in-law-on 18th Oetober I went over Steele's farm,
and saw him and his wife-Mrs. Steele remained in the house, and when
Steele and I came back he, in Mrs. Steele's presence, produced an
inventory, and said that that was a memorandum of the things on the
farm-she took an interest in the affair, and put down the prices at
Steele's diotation- conversed with them both-I found fault with some
of the items-she said that the horses cost more money; she took an
active part in the transactions-I saw some books, and they were
referred to.
