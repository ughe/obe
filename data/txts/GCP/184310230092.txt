2906. WILLIAM SIBLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd
Oct., 5lbs. weight of bacon, value 2s. ; tbe goods of Henry Hart.
MARY HART. I am the mother of Henry Hart. On the 2nd of Oct., about
seven o'clock, I missed this bacon--Thompson brought it back--the prisoner
came into the shop in the evening for an errand.
RICHARD THOMPSON. I am a constable-I found the bacon in a frying.
pan, by the side of the prisoner, in Harefield's brick-field, about a quarter d
a mile from the prosecutor's, about half past seven o'clock-there were two
persons with him-he first said he had not got the bacon, and at last be suid
he had got it, he had taken it away.
Prisoner. I did not say I took it, I knew nothing about it; another chap
took it. Witness. The other man said nothing about it.
HENRY HART. I went home-I went with the constable, and found the
bacon by the prisoner's side-he said he had not taken it, and at last the
others said, "Give it up."
Guilty. Aged 17.-Recommended to mercy.--Confined One Month.
2907. JAMES HOY was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of Oct., 1 sack,
value 4d.; 1 basket, 3d. ; 5 pecks of pears, 48. ; 2 bushels of onions, &c.;
and 1 peck of apples, 8d.; the goods of Samuel Coombs, his master,
SAMUEL Coombs. I am a market gardener, and live at Enfield. The pri-
soner was in my employ-I lost a basket-these pears, apples, and onions
are mine-the basket is marked with my name--the prisoner had no business
with it. .
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. I believe you were in the habit ol
• lending him a basket? A. I lent him two about a month ago I cannot un-
dertake to say this is not one I had lent him-he bought two pear trees in
the garden close to mine, so that he might have got at them from my garden
-I can speak to the apples, by being of two different sorts-1 had not sold
them-we do not sell anything at home-there were only two trees of this
sort on my ground I will not say that there are not apples of the same sort
-I missed these from the heap-the prisoner has worked for me two yeans
and two months--the pear trees the prisoner bought were of the same sort a
mine-these could not have come from these trees, because I missed them
from the heap-I do not know when the onions were taken--the ground on
which the pear-trees stand is not separated from mine; it is part of my owo
ground, though let as a separate garden.
Court. Q. Have you looked at the onions ? A. Yes- I believe they
are mine, and the pears--they are not off his two trees-his own pears were
gathered a month before.
