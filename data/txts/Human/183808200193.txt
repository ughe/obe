A. , Yes; the prosecu tor said that six months before, the prisoner had said, "Have you got a pair of soiled shoes for my mother's servant?" and he gave him five pairs—that he brought four pairs back, and paid him for one—he said he might have told him one more pair—Mr. Bennett said he thought there had been a bona fide  transaction, and if he were on a Jury, he should have a doubt whether he did not take these boots for the purpose of selling them—the prosecutor's shop opens with folding doors—there is a blind goes before the cutting-board—the prosecutor had come to me on Friday evening, and requested me to witch—he said, very likely there would be something done then, as he had taken a pair the day before, and had not given him any account of them—I had been watching two or three days before—he requested me to come early on the Saturday, and follow the prisoner.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . to EDWIN BILTON . Q.  Was not the prisoner re gularly employed in selling boots and shoes in your shop? A.  Certainly; he was my clicker—he was employed to cut out leather, and if an extra hand was wanted, be went in to sell—he took money—I had employed him ten months. GUILTY. DANIEL EVANS. I am a servant, and live with Benjamin Hall. On the 30th of July I met the prisoner in Edgeware-road, about half-past eleven o'clock, or after—I was not quite sober—I entered into conversation with her—I cannot say whether she spoke to me first, or I to her—I took her to my room over the stable, in Old Church-street, Paddington—I gave her 2 s . 6 d .—we went to bed together, about twelve o'clock—I awoke about four o'clock the next morning, and she was gone—I looked on the table, and missed my watch—I went to the next room, and saw there was a shirt taken off the back of the chair, and this waistcoat—the watch was found—this is it—( looking at it ).
