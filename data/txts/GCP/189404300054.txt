THIRD COURT.—Friday, May 4th, 1894.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
443. JAMES ROLAND FITT, Forging and uttering a receipt for
£200.
MESSRS. C. MATHEWS, H. Avory and Biron Prosecuted, and MESSRS.
C. F. GILL and A. Gill Defended.
During the progress of the case MR. C. GILL stated that he could not
resist a verdict of Guilty upon the Count for uttering, it having been
decided that a person who knowingly put forward a false document which was
accepted as genuine was responsible for the offence, apart from any question
of intention to defraud. T'he prisoner stated in the hearing of the Jury
that he was guilty of uttering, and the JURY found him guilty of utter-
ing. There were other indictments against him for omitting material
particulars from and making false entries in certain books of the North
London Commercial Permanent Building Society, his masters, and also
for embezzling certain of their moneys. He received an excellent character.
-Five Years' Penal Servitude.
