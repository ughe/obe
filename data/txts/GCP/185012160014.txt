208. HENRY COOK, forging and uttering an order for the payment of
11. 158., with intent to defraud Thomas Vesper and another.--2nd COUNT
describing it as a warrant. MR. CAARTEEN conducted the Prosecution.
Thomas Jones CAVANNAH. I am manager to Mr. Thomas Vesper ; he
has one partner; they are outfitters, at Limehouse. On Saturday evening,
23rd Nov., between seven and eight o'clock, the prisoner came to the
shop, and asked if we cashed seamen's notes-I replied, "Yes," and he
presented this one (produced)-it has my writing on the back of it-it is
a note purporting to be from a master of a ship, directed to a firm, and
payable to bearer or order, provided the seaman sails in the ship-he said
he would lay out money to the amount of 11., and selected goods to the
amount of 11. 1s. 3d., and I handed him the difference, 138. 9d.-he did
not say the name of the ship, but I understood it, and said I would send
the things on board on the Monday morning, at one o'clock, when he said
he would be alongside to receive them, and he consented to that- asked
him who wrote the note-- he said, " The captain"-he called on the
Monday, at ten o'clock-1 had previously ascertained that the note was a
forgery-I did not send the things-I made the prisoner make his mark
on the back of the note-he said he could not write-I asked him how be
managed when he signed articles-he said he touched the top of the pen,
the captain holding it-I asked him his name, and he said, "Henry Cook,"
which is in my writing on the back of the note-(note read: "Advance
note. 23rd Nov., 1850. Three days after the ship Castle Lackland has
sailed from Gravesend, pay Henry Cook, or order, 11. 153., at Bright and
Green's, Philpot-lane. J. Bruce, commander.")
EDWARD SALTER. I am clerk to Mr. John Dawson, of 9, Billiter-
square, who is owner of the Castle Lackland, and also acts as the broker;
