MR. CHARNOCK called
Lucy CHAPPELL. I was there that night, and saw the beginning of the
affaireFlinn was not there at first-- he came afterwards, and the mob
passed up to him-I, being a cripple, stood aside-Flinn came and stood
by me--he did not move from me he did not put his hand into a police.
man's belt-he could not have done that without my seeing him-he stood
by my side all the time.
MR. CLARK. Q. How long did you remain? 1. I stood at the corner
of Nelson-street about a quarter of an hour, and during that time Flinn
stood by my side.
-- MARTIN. I live at No. 2, Nelson-street. The first I saw
was Hare in the field, challenging any one to fight-he then came back,
went up Nelson-street, and I went home I saw Flinn there, but he was
not within 100 yards of the disturbance-I passed him more than twice
or thrice-he was talking to a lame young woman-he was not near
enough to put his hand into a policeman's belt.
Mr. CLARK. Q. Did this take place opposite your window? 1. No,
I was at the door when the people first ran past-there was a disturbance
and a fight in Grosvenor-court-I went out to see them- the policeman
would not let them fight-I came back to my own door, and they passed
me towards the Windmill public-house-I was at my door when they
passed, and when they came back from the field too-that was not the
time I saw Flinn-I went into Windmill-lane and saw Flinn when the
mob was by the oil cloth factory--he was coming up Nelson-street-he
was alone-they were then trying to get Hare.
CHARLES HUSsey. I live at No. 5, Appleton-place. I am the letter
carrier of that district, and have been so for sixteen years-Michael Mannix
Jodges there-he was at home all that evening till eight o'clock-I was
there till eight o'clock, and then I went out.
COURT. Q. How far is this from the Windmill ? A. Half a mile.
ELIZABETH MILLER. I live at No. 5, Appleton-place, in the house
where Michael Mannix lives. I was there on Sunday evening, the 22nd
of June-I went to bed about ten o'clock--I had come home at four
o'clock in the afternoon, and did not go out at all that evening-Mannix
and his wife did not go out all that evening-my father and mother occupy
the two rooms up stairs, and Mannix and his wife the two rooms down
stairs- I was up and down sufficiently to know that Mannix did not leave
the house-I saw Mr. Hussey there.
MR. PAYNE called
SARAH CATlin. I am single, and live in Thomas-street. I go out
washing and ironing--I saw James Goodright with a child in his arms on
this occasion--gave the child to him I was there during the whole
time I saw the mob go past me--the three Goodrights were standing
close by me, at a distance off from the row, and James Goodright had the
child.
