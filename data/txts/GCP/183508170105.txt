1788. WILLIAM HOYLE was indicted, for that he, on the 20th of
April, at St. Peter's, Westcheap, London, feloniously did forge a bíl of
exchange, which is as follows, that is to say, “No. 104, 1097. 168. 10d.,
London, April 1st, 1835, Three months after date, pay to our order 1091.
168. 10d., value received. Thomas M'Mahon and Co. to Mr. G. Ormerod,
Rochdale, payable at Messrs. Cunliff, Brooks, and Co. George Or-
merod;" with intent to defraud Charles Ginnever Kewney and others.-
2nd Count, for feloniously offering, uttering, disposing of, and putting off
a like forged bill of exchange, well knowing it to be forged, with like
intent.--3rd Count, for feloniously forging an acceptance of a like bill of
exchange, which is as follows: "Payable at Messrs. Cunliffe, Brooks, and
Co. George Ormerod;" with a like intent-4th Count, for feloniously
offering, &c. the same, well knowing it to be forged, with like intent.-
Four other Counts, like four former, only not setting out the forged bill of
exchange.
Messrs. CLARKSON AND BODKIN conduoted the Prosecution.
CHARLES GINNEVER KEWNEY. I am a hosier, and live at Nottingham.
I have two partners--we had transactions with a firm named M'Mahon
and Co., of London-the prisoner was represented as one of that firm, and
was so, I believe--they owed us between 13,000h. and 14,0001-in April
last we made application for part of that amount, I afterwards came to
London--their place of business was in Wood-street, Cheapside- I went
there on Monday morning, the 20th of April—I saw the prisoner first at
our own warehouse, in Maiden-lane before I went there, I called his
attention to the account-he said he had paid part of it at our town ware-
house-that was not true in the afternoon of that day he made me a pay-
ment of between 5001. and 6001., in bills and cash-there was not sol. of
it in cash the rest was in bills-the bill in question, of 1091. 168. 10d.,
was among them- produced)-it was accepted as it now ishe made no
observation about it-it was presented for payment, and dishonoured
no such person was to be found.
JOHN TEMPEST. I am a manufacturer of flannel, and have lived nt
Rochdale all my life. I am very well acquainted with the inhabitants-I
know there is no such person to be found there as George Ormerod, ex-
cept a school-boy, about fourteen years old I have frequently seen that
boy--I have not seen him write.
Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS. Q. Are you well acquainted with
the boy? A. I have seen him frequently-his father has been dead these
four years--his friends are curriers-he lives with his brothers—there are
no other George Ormerods there - I have made inquiry.
JORN BROWN. I am clerk to Cunliff, Brooks, and Co., bankers-we
have no customer of the name of Ormerod, to my knowlege.
