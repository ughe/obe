I accused Allport of stealing a silver spoon, she denied it-I said I had the
property before-she said it was very strange, but I did not accuse her till
the 9th of May-I then gave her in charge-she was taken to the station
returned to my house with the policeman and searched the room-Johnson
then came home, and I told him Mrs. Allport was out, that she had taken
silver spoon out of my apartment and I had given her in custody, that I had
found the duplicate between the bed and mattress-he said he knew nothing
about it-I said the room was stripped-he seemed astonished-I said the
feathers were all gone out of the bed-he said he knew nothing about the
pledging at first, but he then said the feathers were all made into pillows
I gave him into custody-he said what could be done I said I had given it
into the officer's hands-he then said he had been to see a gentleman that
day to make arrangements to get the things out, and that he had got them
out again and again, but Mrs. Allport had put them away in his absence-he
said he had got them out over and over.
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. I believe when Johnson came
home the policeman Wood was there ? A. Yes—when I told Johnson, he
said, “ Bless my soul ! is that so ?”—he is a writer, or an author-I knew
nothing of his circumstances till he was given into custody-Mrs. Allpott
cooked for him, and she has said he was poorly—he complained of ill health
when he took the lodging, and said Mrs. Allport had been his nurse—she did
every thing for him-I washed the table-cloth and sheets-Mrs. Allport
asked me when I would have them, and she gave them to me—they have
been at all times forthcoming—I had the rent by instalments through the
winter, but lately more regularly-when I have missed any trifling thing I
have said to Allport or to Johnson, " As far as you are concerned, all I re-
quire is, that on the day before you leave, or on that day, you will permit me
to see that all my things are there "- I had no wish to prosecute-I believe
I mentioned to Johnson's father, that if the things had been all safe, I should
have thought nothing about it, I wished to get them out of the house quietly,

Cross-examined. Q. I believe they have constantly pawned and taken

—those things bave been pawned and redeemed once a week-I think this

JAMES Wood re-examined. I searched the room, but Johnson was not

Henry LJEBRICHT. I am assistant to Mr. Sowerby, a pawnbroker-I

Cross-examined. Q. I believe she has pawned articles at your shop almost
every day? A. This very kettle was pawned in the morning, and redeemed
in the evening, for about a fortnight previous to the 2nd of April.
