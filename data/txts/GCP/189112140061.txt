126. JOHN COYLE (24), Burglary in the dwelling-house of Lord
John Henniker, and stealing a teapot and other goods, his property.
MR. STEVENSON Prosecuted.
JOHN ROBERT KEMP (Divisional Surgeon of Police). I have this morn-
ing seen Police-constable John Street-he is not well enough to be able
to travel here to give evidence.
WILLIAM MORLING (17 CR). I was in court when John Street gave
his evidence before the Magistrate, the prisoner had an opportunity of
cross-examining him.
The de position of John Street (203 C) was read: “About 7.15 a.m., on
11th inst., I was called into 6, Grafton Street, I found the pantry door
locked. I burst it open and found the prisoner there; I asked him how
he got there. He said he did not know. I examined the back area,
door and found a pane of glass broken in it, making an opening large
enough for a man to get through it, and the door was fastened and
bolted inside. The prisoner gave an address at a common lodging-house
in Strutton Ground. He is not known there. At the station prisoner had
some blood on his hand; I asked him how he did it. He said he must
have cut it in breaking the glass. The blankets in the pantry had been
lain on. The opening in the door was 20 inches by 30 inches. The
glass was inside the hall. The prisoner was not drunk."
