JOSHUA FREDERICK CLEMENTS. I took the prisoner. This is the box.
 Prisoner's Defence (written). As I was coming down  Bond-street, I observed two men following a gentleman; my suspicions induced me to watch them - I followed them as close as I could, and saw the gentleman get into a coach; the said men were close to the carriage - I did not observe any thing wrong; turned round, walked a few paces, and then run - I saw them drop a box, which I picked up; a cry of Stop thief! was raised; the men got out of sight - I was returning with the box, which I gave him, and stated the case to him, but would hear nothing.
 JURY to  EDWARD CROCKFORD. Q. Did he come towards you? A. No, he ran from me, and I called Stop thief! I am certain he ran against me as I was getting into the cab.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Life.
 861.  JAMES MILTON was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of February , 1 bushel of beans, value 4s., the goods of Edward Kirby.
 EDWARD KIRBY. I am a  farmer at Ryeslip, beyond Harrow. On the 25th of February, at nine o'clock at night, I saw the prisoner take these beans from my barn- I was in a place adjoining to watch; he was in my service - he came into the barn, took some beans from the heap, and put them into a bag; he then went to the lower part of the yard with the bag - I waited till he came back and said, "Milton, you have stolen some beans;" he said he had not - I said, "I saw you take them, and you shall go with me;" he said, "I will go and fetch them back;" but I said, "No, that won't do, you have taken so many;" he said it was the first time - I went with him across the turnpike road, and found them under a bush.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had he any horses to feed? A. Yes; he had been with me about a week- my horses had split beans, but these were not split; they were to be split in the barn.
   EDWARD SCEENEY. I am an officer, and took the prisoner - he is married, and lives in the parish.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. - Confined One Month.
 862.  JOHN KEEGAN was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of April , 2 shoes, value 6s., the goods of James Stack.
   JAMES STACK. I am a shoemaker, and live in Holywell-street, Strand. About one o'clock on the 3rd of April, the prisoner came and said, "I want a pair shoes;" he took a pair off the hooks, and said, "These are about my size, but I can't have them now, I am going to Charing-cross;" I turned my back, and he walked out of the shop - I missed these shoes, and pursued him; he had got half way down the street - I went and said, "You scoundrel, you have a pair of shoes of mine;" I brought him back; he threw down the shoes, gave me a blow, knocked me down, and ran off - I pursued; a Thames Police-officer was coming up, and stopped him - he gave him a blow, but the officer got up and took him; he was quite sober.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not I give you the shoes back again? A. When I brought you back, you threw them down and struck me over the head - you said you were in the army, and was going to Cox and Greenwood's.
   JOHN GASKIN. I am a constable. I saw the prisoner running down Arundel-street; I caught him - he hit me in the eye, and made it bleed; I lost my hold of him, but I got him again and kept him.
 Prisoner. I was twenty-five years in the army, and a serjeant nineteen; I have been handsomely remunerated; it is not likely I should compromise a 35l. a year salary to get 6s.
     GUILTY. Aged 41. - Transported for Seven Years.
 863.  WILLIAM MARNEY was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s. 9d., the goods of Rachael Boulton.
   RACHAEL BOULTON. I lost a pair of shoes on the 24th of February from my shop in Pitfield-street; the prisoner came and asked for a pair - I said I kept no men's shoes; he said he thought I had some large enough for him; I showed him some, and while I was taking down another pair he ran out - I followed him, and he was taken by the Police-officer; he was brought back without the shoes, but in two minutes a young man brought them in.
   RICHARD BANKS. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner running and took him - I saw nothing of the shoes.
   WILLIAM WILKS. I picked up the shoes, which went from the prisoner when he was in the officer's hands.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I leave it to the mercy of the Court.
     GUILTY. Aged 17.

 864.  MARY JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March , 13 yards of ribbon, value 10s., the goods of Robert Pite.
   JOHN NOBBS. I am shopman to Mr. Robert Pite. The prisoner came in as a customer, and was there for twenty minutes, but bought nothing; the ribbons were in a drawer on the counter - I saw Mr. Pite take this ribbon from the prisoner.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. What is the reason Mr. Pite is not here? A. I do not know - he was not before the Magistrate; I do not suppose there were three words spoken which I did not hear - Mr. Pite did not say he had hung a man once for robbing him, and he would hang this girl if he could; I observed the prisoner appeared silly - I have not heard that she is so.
   JOHN CLARK. I took up the prisoner - I found nothing on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to ask for some ribbon - he gave it me, and the man came and shoved this under my arm; the officer took it from me - I laid 1s. on the counter.
 Two witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
