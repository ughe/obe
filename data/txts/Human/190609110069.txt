It had always been the rule that if a prisoner when celled upon refused to plead the Judge might direct a plea of not guilty to be entered, but it was very seldom that the conduct of a prisoner was of such a character that the trial could not take place in his or her pretence. It had, of course, been necessary to satisfy himself that she was in such a state of mind as to be capable of understanding the proceedings and pleading to the indictment by the evidence of the medical gentleman under whose careful observation she had been, and had that not been the case he would have directed her to be confined during His Majesty's pleasure.
 Verdict, Guilty.
