 1069.  MARGARET SHELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of March , 3 shillings, the monies of James Harris.
 JAMES HARRIS. I am a  dairyman, and live in Holland-street, Soho. The prisoner was my weekly servant, and carried out milk for me; I missed money from time to time, and got Murless, on the 5th of March, to mark a sovereign, five half-sovereigns, six shillings, and six sixpences - I put the money into a drawer in the desk in the parlour, and in about an hour I told the prisoner she had been robbing me - she said she had not; I sent for an officer, and three marked shillings were found on her.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you lock the desk? A. I did, and put the key into my pocket - I owe her 21s.; I always gave her money when she asked for it; the desk was not broken open - I paid none of the money away which Murless marked; I never said I suspected my sister.
   JOHN HARWOOD. I am an officer. I searched the prisoner, and found one marked shilling in her mouth, and two in her stocking, under her foot; no key was found on her.
   BARNARD MURLESS. I marked the money, and saw it put into the desk; these are three of the shillings that I marked.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Confined Three Months.
 1070.  FRANCES HUBBARD was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of February , 5 cups, value 5s.; 1 shirt, value 6d.; 1 penknife, value 6d.; 1 shirt-pin, value 1s., and 6 yards of ribbon, value 3s., the goods of William Wale , her master.
   FRANCES WALE. I am the wife of William Wale - we keep the  Bayswater tavern; the prisoner was about fifteen months in my service. On Saturday, the 18th of February, suspicion arose about some tea that was found, and on the Monday morning Mr. Wale sent for the officer, who searched the prisoner's box, and found some baby's caps, and the other things - the knife was in her pocket.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe the prisoner was very much in your confidence? A. I put every trust in her; she usually sits in the bar-parlour with me, as she attends the bar - I have played at cards with her - I am fond of spirits; we all know our faults; I do not get tipsy - I am not compelled to tell what quantity I take; I never accused my husband of putting these things into the prisoner's box; I never said to him,"You wretch, you put these things in the girl's box" - she frequently sat up all night with me when we had heavy dinners; I have slept in the same room as her; I never stuck my husband in the hand out of jealousy of the prisoner - she did not give me warning to quit; I never said I would hang her if I could - I had a great deal of plate which she could have taken.
   THOMAS TRINGHAM. I am an officer. On the 20th of February I took the prisoner, and requested to see her box; she unlocked it, and I found in it five child's caps, a child's shirt, and other things.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1071.  SAMUEL RALF was indicted for stealing on the 1st of April , 2 sovereigns and 6 half-crowns, the monies of William German , from his person.
   WILLIAM GERMAN. I am servant to Mr. Ranson, a baker, in the Minories. On the 1st of April, between seven and eight o'clock, I went into a public-house at the corner of Crispin-street, Spitalfields, and had in my left-hand waistcoat pocket two sovereigns and six half-crowns- I was sitting in a box with my head on the table, and fell asleep; I was not intoxicated - the prisoner, who I have known four years, went with me to the house; he was standing up when I fell asleep; he was close to the fire, not within reach of my pocket - I was awoke, and directly missed my money; he was then gone; I saw him in custody in about twenty minutes - he said he had got plenty of money, and produced two sovereigns, three half-crowns, a penny and a farthing, which he said were his own, and he could produce 10l. if it was wanted - he told me that morning that he had no money - he borrowed 6d. of me, and gave a duplicate as security for it.
   JOHN MADGE. I am a baker, and live in Petticoat-lane - I know the prosecutor and prisoner. I saw them both at the public-house; I went in after them; the prosecutor laid asleep, with his head on the table - the prisoner was standing by the fire, smoking his pipe, about two or three feet from German; nobody else was in the room; I did not see the prisoner do any thing - he left three or four minutes after I came in; we both left together - I returned directly, having heard something at the bar, and awoke the prosecutor, who complained of losing his money in about five minutes - the prisoner was out of work then, and had been so for some time.
