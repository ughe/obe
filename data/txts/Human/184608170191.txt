Within one house of them—they came out of No. 1, Rupert-street—I was opposite No. 3—I followed them because they were going the same way as I was—they went up the court, and I lived up the court—I went into my own door, and saw no more of them—they were in the court when I entered it. CHARLES DORKMAN. I live in Tollington-street, Whitechapel-road. On Friday night, the 17th of July, between twelve and one o'clock, I saw Webb, just by Whitechapel-pump, going towards Rupert-street, about two minutes' walk from it—I knew him—he asked me to have a pint of beer, and I refused—he and Gill lived at my mother's at the time.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did not he say, "Holloa, Charley, How are you?" A.  Yes, and asked me to have half a pint of beer. AMELIA SCARLETT. I am single, and have been staying some time with Mrs. Dorkman—I knew the prisoners Webb and Gill. On Saturday, the 18th of July, between four and five o'clock in the morning, I saw them at the Blue Anchor public-house, Whitechapel-road, drinking half-and-half—that is not more than a quarter of a mile from Rupert-street—I left them there—I had been staying at Dorkman's about a fortnight. ELIZABETH DORKMAN. I live in Thomas-street, and let apartments—tha prisoners Webb and Gill lived three weeks with me, as man and wife.
