Cross-examined. Q.Did he not tell you he was tired of them, and that another gentleman gave them to him? A. No, he did not say where he got them.
   GEORGE DICKENSON. I am a broker- I live in Tabernacle-walk. On the 22nd of February , in consequence of information from Foster and my wife, I went in pursuit of some boys to Old-street-road - I got sight of the two prisoners and another , going towards Shoreditch ; they turned down a street , then across Hoxton-market, and out by Hoxton new church - I went on to Pitfield-street, came up with the prisoners in Royal Oak-walk, and took them; these are my shoes and brush - I had not been out of the shop a quarter of an hour .
 Cross-examined. Q. I suppose you have some mark on them? A. I can swear to the shoes, by this break in the grain of one - they hung on the door; a person standing on the steps might take them without going into the shop.
   JOHN OWEN. I am a Police-constable. I saw the prisoners in Pitfield-street, and assisted in taking them at the top of the court; I received the shoes and brush from Rodman, tied together as they are now .
 Bailes' Defence. We were going along, and saw some boys with the shoes and brush - we heard a cry of Stop thief! they ran and we ran .
 Heath's Defence. I never saw the shoes; I was going for some potatoes .
 The prisoners received a good character.
 HEATH - GUILTY. Aged 13.
 BAILES - GUILTY. Aged 14.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury. Whipped and Discharged .
 986.  JOHN DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of March , 1 African dress robe, value 3l., the goods of Maria Lardner.
   MARIA LARDNER. I live in Acre-cottage, Camden-town, and am a widow. The prisoner was in my son's service; he is an officer in the Second West India Regiment - in September last my son and the prisoner left my house to go abroad, but in a few days the ship got leaky, and returned to Margate; my son went to an hotel, the prisoner left him, and came back to my house - he continued there till the 24th of March, when I sent him away , and when he was gone I missed the robe of an African chief, which my son had brought from abroad, and made me a present of - I went to the house of Martha Baggs, and got the robe from her.
   GEORGE BROWN. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner in Pratt-street, on the 24th of March; I told him I took him on suspicion of stealing this dress - he said his mistress gave it him to put on his bed, to keep him warm ; Martha Baggs is not here .
 COURT to MRS. LARDNER. Q. Do you know whether it was used on the prisoner's bed? A. He complained of being cold (as they are always very chilly), and wished to have more clothes - I think he had this on his bed; he has been always robbing me.
     NOT GUILTY.
