JOHN COOPER. I am a carman, of 44, Carpenter Road-on Saturday,
11th August, the prisoner came about 6 p.m., and wanted to sell the two
carts and some old harness and old horses for 201.- I told him I did not
want them; I had got nothing for my own to do-he went away-he
had nothing from me-about mid-day on Monday, 13th, he offered me
the two carts for 81.-on Tuesday he offered me the two carts for 61.-I
bought them, and paid him the money-this is the receipt: “ August 14th.
Bought two carts of Mr. Goff, for the sum of 61. Signed, H, Goff"-the
receipt my missus wrote-he also gave me this paper which he brought
ready written:-“London, August 14th.-Mr. Cooper to H. Goff: sold two
second-hand carts for the sum of 61. for the agreement for cart and
stabling for one month "-he asked me if he could put a horse in the
stable for a month, and would I lend him a cart; 80 I was to buy two
carts for 67., and let the horse stand in my stable for a month, and lend
him a cart if he had any work to do-he was to feed the horse-I fetched
the carts on Tuesday-he shot the horse out, and as soon as the cart went
up I saw "J.J." on the shaft-I said, "Some people have a funny way
of marking carts underneath "-he turned his back, and walked away-I
used the carts till Mr. Jeffery came to identify them on the 29th.
Cr088-examined. I will swear it was not Monday the carts came there.
JANE JEFFERY. This business is mine-I signed this agreement and
advaneed the money for the purchase of these horses and carts- I gave
the prisoner no authority to sell them.
The prisoner in his defence said that owing to the wet weather he had been
out of pocket every week since Easter, and that he was forced to sell some-
thing to get food for the horses.
