881. THOMAS DAVIES  (40), Robbery with violence on John Caffrey , and stealing a watch and chain, his property. JOHN CAFFREY. I am a commissioner—on 2nd September, between 12 and 1 a.m., I was in Chandos Street, and was suddenly struck on my mouth, rendered senseless, and my watch taken—I did not see who did it, but I saw the prisoner running, and pursued him, crying "Stop thief"—I fell over a step, and when I got up I found him in custody—I have not seen my watch since.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  At the time the man snatched my watch I saw nobody else there—a boy pointed you out—I saw no one running after you when I got up. WILLIAM FRAZIER. I live at 5, Turner's Court, Bedfordbury—I was in the Strand, selling newspapers, and saw the prisoner at the back entrance to Toole's Theatre; he struck Caffrey on his mouth, stole his watch and chain, and ran—I halloaed "Stop thief," and followed, and spoke to a policeman—he ran into a crowd, but I watched where he ran, and am sure he is the man.
   Cross-examined.  I sell newspapers—I had sold them all—my father is a painter; he is in prison for nine months. HENRY HIGGINS( Policeman E  254). On 2nd September, about a quarter to 1 a.m., I was on duty in Bedfordbury, heard cries of "Stop thief," and saw the prisoner running—I chased him along Chandos Street to Adelaide Street—he placed himself among 20 or 30 other men, and Frazier came up and pointed him out—I took him out of the crowd, and said he would have to go with me—he said "What for? I have done nothing"—I said "You stole the gentleman's watch"—I am sure he is the man I saw running, after I heard the cry.
   Cross-examined.  The prosecutor's mouth was flowing with blood.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went into the crowd to see what was going on, and stood there two or three minutes, and the boy came up and said "That is the man." The policeman said "Has he struck you?" He said "No, I fell over a step and hurt my nose." JOHN CAFFREY( Re-examined ). I said that the man struck my nose and made it bleed—I fell over the step afterwards. GUILTY. He then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction at Westminster on 23rd July, 1877, in the name of Henry Batts; thirteen previous convictions were proved against him, and he had still two years and four months to serve of a former sentence of seven years' penal servitude.— Nine Months' Imprisonment after completing his former sentence.
