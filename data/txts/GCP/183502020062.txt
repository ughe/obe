MR. PHILLIPS. Q. He gave you that account of the way he came by

COURT. Q. Are you sure that the lad told the prisoner his father

got down from my cab and looked at him, but he was not the same lad-
I cannot say when the purchase was, whether it was in January or Decem-
ber, before Christmas day or after it.
MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Would not the man who bought the bag recollect

ought to recollect it I did not go to the police office to tell this story,
but I told an officer in Farringdon-street the size of the boy.

524. WILLIAM HOWARD was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of
January, 1 coat, value 108., the goods of John Underwood Smith; and 2
spoons, value 68., the goods of Richard Smith.
MARIA SMITH. I am the wife of Richard Smith, of Winchester-court,
Monkwell-street. The prisoner came to my house, and slept one night-
he gave my husband a written direction to go to the Custom-house, to get
his luggage-he slept there that night, and had his breakfast and dinner
the next day-at dinner-time my son came in, I said, "How long will
your father be ?" he said, "Two hours, for he is gone to the Custom-
house "- the prisoner said, “I am very glad, for I shall have a change to
put on"-I went down, and the prisoner said, "I will put this coat on,
and go to see a young woman at the bottom of Silver-street, Falcon-
square"- I said, “You will not be long?" he said, “No; because I
expect your husband with my luggage"-he went off, and when my hus-
band came home, he said, “Where is the young man ?" I went to see
after him, but he was not there-- I then missed two silver spoons, which
had washed up with the tea-things that morning after breakfast I have
never seen them since.
