 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY of an Assault  . — Confined Twelve Months .
 2016. MARY ANN DALE , stealing 1 half-crown, 6 shillings, and 2 sixpences, of Henry Greenwood , from his person. HENRY GREENWOOD. I am thirteen years old, and live with my father and mother at Black-horse-place, Lower-road, Deptford—last Saturday week, I took half a pint of ale to Mr. Temple's, and he gave me half a sovereign to get changed—I got half-a-crown, 6 shillings, 2 sixpences, 5 1/2 d . in copper, and a halfpenny-worth of plums—the prisoner met me—I go of errands for her—she asked me if her husband was in our place—I said I did not think he was—she said, "Come along with me"—I went with her down Effingham-place—she knocked me down with her hand, knelt on me, put her hand over my mouth, forced my hand open, and took the money out, and said, "Don't you think I have robbed you," and then put the halfpence into my hand and run away—another boy was with me—it was about eight o'clock at night—I watched her home and told a policeman—he took her next day.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did not you accuse some one of taking 1 s ., which you had for some beer, and you said you had not got it, and your mother found it on you? A.  No—I do not know Scam, I have seen that man before ( looking at one )—he never gave me 1 s ., which I denied his giving to me, which was found on me—this was quite an open place—I did not tell Eliza Tripps that it was a tall big woman in a white shawl and a black bonnet—I do not know Tripps—I said she was a little woman with a black velvet bonnet and a light shawl—my father said if I did not get the woman I should catch it—I had never played with Holmes before—I had seen him—I do not know that he has been convicted—there is a large gas-lamp at the corner of Effinghain-place, the prisoner lives a little way from there. JOSEPH HENRY HOLMES. I am twelve years old, and live in Cross-street, Deptford—I was by the Prince Regent door about eight o'clock one day last week, and saw a woman in a light shawl and black bonnet knock Greenwood down—he had 5 1/2 d . in his hand.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you pick him up? A.  I caught hold of his hand, and he jumped up—I have been in prison at Brixton—we had not spent the money between us. WILLIAM THOMAS ARTHUR( policeman, R  202). On Saturday night about nine o'clock Greenwood complained to me—I went with him to dif ferent houses, but could not find the party—on Sunday evening about six he came and took me to the prisoner's house—she was standing at the door—I asked her if a female had been in there ten minutes or quarter of an hour ago, she said, "No"—I said, "Have you lodgers?" she said, "No"—she was very much confused—Greenwood said, "That is the woman you want"—I said, "Are you sure of that?"—he said, "Quite positive"—I asked the prisoner if she knew him—she said, "I believe he lives at the beer-shop over the way "—he said, "Yes, you know I do, you are the party that robbed me "—I asked her to produce the bonnet and shawl she had on yes terday—she put on a light shawl and black bonnet.
