1322. JAMES DORE was indicted for embezzlement. JOHN SIMPSON. I am a ginger-beer and soda-water maker—I live in King's-place, Newington—the prisoner was in my service—it was his duty to take money and bring it to me when he came home at night—if he re ceived the sums in question he ought to have paid them to me on the days he received them.
   Prisoner.  When you cast up my wages on Saturday night you said, "You have had so much money of me, "and deducted it. Witness.  No, I did not.
   Prisoner.  I never received this money—I always gave receipts. EDWARD FRANCIS. I paid the prisoner 1 s . for Mr. Simpson, on the 3rd of April—I have no receipt for it—I paid ready money. HENRY WILLIAM BARTON. I had a dozen of ginger-beer, and three bottles of soda-water of the prisoner—I paid him 1 s . 10 1/2 d . for it—I did not pay him at the time I had it, but I am quite sure it was paid—I am not indebted to Mr. Simpson for anything.
   Prisoner.  You were not at home; your wife took them in, and said, "When my husband is in the way, he will pay you. Witness.  I am well aware of that, but I paid you the next day when you called.
   COURT . Q.  Are you sure you paid him? A.  I would not swear it, but I believe I did. HENRY BUTTLE. I had six bottles of lemonade, and six bottles of soda-water from the prisoner—my wife paid him in my presence—I gave her the money—I cannot say on what day it was—there was a receipt given for it, but it is lost.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I have been about the town for Mr. Simpson for fourteen months; another person set up in the business; he heard of me, and offered me 5 s . a week more than I had of Mr. Simpson; I said I was satis fied, I did not want to change; this is done out of spite, because the other gentleman wished to get me away. NOT GUILTY.
 1323. JAMES DORE was again  indicted for stealing 12 bottles, value 1 s  ., and 3 quarts of ginger-beer, 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of John Simpson , his master. JOHN SIMPSON. On the 3rd of April I received information that the pri soner was taking out more goods than he accounted for, and on the 4th of April I watched him, and saw that he was doing so—I told the inspector—he told me to watch him on the following morning, and I did—I saw the pri soner and the foreman put the goods into the cart—the prisoner took the cart into the street—it was then taken to the station, and I found in it one dozen more than the prisoner had accounted for—he had done it by a very novel mode of packing—in the bottom tier of the crate he had managed to put four dozen and three bottles, four dozen and three in the middle tier, and in the top tier only four dozen—the officer found that also, and in consequence of that I gave him into custody.
   Prisoner.  When the crates were empty we went to the stock of beer and filled them; when they were full we thought there were twelve dozen; we never counted them. CHARLES MANN. I drive for Mr. Simpson. I took out the boxes with the Prisoner—they ought to have held four dozen in each tier—he ordered me to unpack one box that we did not sell, and there were six bottles over—I told him it was wrong, and I would not allow it while he was out with me—he said it was all right, it was only six over.
