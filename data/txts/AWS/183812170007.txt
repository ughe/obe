(MR. HORRY, the prisoner's counsel, stated, that poverty had led him to
commit the ofence, and that he threw himself on the merey of the Court.
JOHN FOWLER re-eramined. The prisoner was sent to me on the Friday
previous to the robbery by the overseer of the parish, with a note requesting
me to give him a night' lodging and some food-he was in the greatest dis-
tress indeed-ii is quite common in our parish to do so--to send them to
me as vagrant-the parish pay me for the meal, and I find them a lodging
in the station-house--I do that with the authority of the overseer-w do
not take them into custody-we do not lock them upwe put them where
there are rugs and things-they can walk out if they please-it has been the
custom in our parish ever since I can remember--the prisoner bad applied
for relief-there is a relieving district officer, or something, who sends people
to the station-house frequently, two or three nights a week-I do not know
under which of the Poor Law Acts he does that-I sent him next morning
to the Relieving Officer of the Poor Law Union, Mr. Hadlington, and he
brought me word that Mr. Hadlington desired him togo to Guildford, where
Isuppose he had told him he belonged to.

bed, and he was left quite destitute-] believe what he did was through
