EMMA COLLINS. I am the wife of Augustus Collins, a bookbinder--I
have known the prisoner about eighteen months-on Friday morning,
February 16th, I met him by arrangement at the foot of Blackfriars
Bridge, about 11.30—he said he had had & run of luck, and had won
some money, and handed me five sovereigns-we went for a walk up King
William Street, and from there we went to buy things—we bought a
jacket, a hat, and a silk handkerchief, which he paid for out of the money
he had handed to me--we went to a place of amusement that evening,
and spent the night together-he paid for the room out of the £5--he
left me in the Norman a short time on the Saturday, and came back
again between one and two--we spent the afternoon together, and he left
me in the evening.
Cross-examined. I put the money in a purse--- he paid for refreshment
and amusement out of the purse when he left me I had £1 left-ho
handed it to me, and I handed it back again---he said he would lay it out
on me-I do not know the actual amount he spent on me
he first bought a purse for 3s. 6d. out of the £5 he had given me
- he had all the money back--we had some drink and sandwiches, which
came to a few shillings-he then bought me a hat for 10s. and a jacket
for 12s., paying out of the £5; also a ring for £1 1s., boots 78. 6d., and
a silver brooch and ear-rings for 48.-We spent the evening at the
Alhambra-I cannot say whether he paid 2s. each or 18.; he paid ls.
