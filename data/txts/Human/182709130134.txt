 1905.  AARON FRANKS was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of July , 1 waistcoat, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Julian Jacobs.
     The waistcoat being the joint property of Julian Jacobs and his father, the prisoner was ACQUITTED.
 1906.  THOMAS PARKER was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 4 memorandum-books, value 1s. 6d.; 131/2 quires of note-paper, value 3s. 6d.; 21/2 reams of thick laid post paper, value 18s., and 20 copy books, value 9s. 6d., the goods of Henry Thomas Green.
 JAMES NEWTON. I am a  cork-cutter. On the night of the 29th of August, I saw the prisoner go into Mr. Green's house, in King-street, Snow-hill- he was very busy in taking the things out of the window, and putting them in a handkerchief on the ground; he came out, I followed him down Snow-hill; he heard me, dropped the bundle and ran - I pursued and took him in Fleet-market; a lad took up the paper.
 HENRY THOMAS GREEN. My attention was drawn to this circumstance, and I missed this paper; it was all in my window, and no one could get it without coming into the shop.
   NICHOLAS POWEL. I am an officer. I received this bundle of paper from Newton.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence (written). I went in for a sheet of paper, and knocked on the counter for a quarter of an hour; nobody came out, and outside of the door I found the bundle; I heard a cry of Stop thief! and threw it down, supposing it was stolen.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Two Months.
 1907.  JAMES EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 1 pair of boots, value 15s. 6d., the goods of John Alger.
   ROBERT HOCKLEY. I am shopman to Mr. Alger - he keeps a boot and shoe warehouse in Ludgate-street. At seven o'clock in the evening of the 24th of August, I saw the prisoner come to the shop door, and take a pair of boots off the floor; he did not come in - I pursued, and came up with him near the Old Bailey - he dropped them.
   JOHN ALGER. I am the owner of the shop - these are my boots.
   JAMES SNOW. I took him in charge; he said he was almost famished with hunger: I found nothing on him.
 GUILTY. Aged 30. Recommended to Mercy- Confined Seven Days.
 1908.  CORNELIUS McCARTY was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 3 handkerchiefs, value 12s., the goods of Thomas Hall.
   ANN HALL. I am the wife of Thomas Hall, a linen-draper, of Bishopsgate-street. On the 25th of August, between nine and ten o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner pulling these handkerchiefs off the line; several persons were in the shop; he put them under his jacket, and was walking out, when I caught hold of the end of them, and stopped him.
   THOMAS HALL. I was at home. I had driven the prisoner away from the shop twice within half an hour; he appeared much intoxicated, but when he was taken five men could not handcuff him.
   THOMAS SAPWELL. I am an officer. I had been watching the prisoner - he tried to get some other handkerchiefs, but could not; he then went into the shop, and took these, I secured him with difficulty.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went in to buy two yellow handkerchiefs - Mr. Hall would not let me have them.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Three Months.
 1909.  ANN PHILLIPS was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 46 pocket-books, value 20s., and 1 billcase, value 1s., the goods of Michael Aarons.
   PETER MONTELL. I am a hair-dresser. On the 31st of July, in the morning, Mr. Anderson, of Finch-lane, Cornhill, sent me in pursuit of a woman in a black gown; I ran out, and overtook the prisoner at the top of Finch-lane. with this property in a bundle.
   JOHN TAYLOR. I am an officer. I took the prisoner in charge. The Lord Mayor ordered me to return part of the property to the prosecutor; she said she was happy of being taken; that she meant to have destroyed herself that morning, and that she had been confined in St. Luke's.
 MICHAEL AARONS. This is my property - I had left it at Mr. Anderson's, in  Finch-lane; I sell the pocket-books about. I left it safe under his counter at six o'clock in the evening.
     GUILTY. Aged 42. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1910.  WALTER BURKE was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of John Dunning , from his person.
 JOHN DUNNING. I am a  tailor, and live in Cleveland-street, Fitzroy-square. On the 14th of August I was going up Cock-lane, Smithfield, about half-past eleven o'clock in the morning, a man came up, and pointed the prisoner and another out - I ran down Cow-lane; a man came round the corner, and took the prisoner with my handkerchief, which was in my pocket just before; I saw him running, but had not seen him near me; he was within twenty yards of me.
   JESSEE PHILLIPS. I am a glass-dealer. I was in Hosier-lane, going to a glass-warehouse, and saw the prisoner running towards me; I laid hold of him - he threw this handkerchief from his pocket: I took it up, and gave it to the officer.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a mob running, and this man said I threw the handkerchief down - I had not seen it.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Three Months.
