ELLEN FRENCH. The prisoner came to me in July, and was with me

Cross-examined. Q. How was this money shown to you? A. I was
standing at the corner of the table, and she took her purse, and paid me
for an article I purchased for her-she did not appear timid in showing the
gold.
ANN FITZGLIK. The prisoner came to live with me in Little Qucen-
street--the policeman came there to look in the drawers-the prisoner pointed
with her finger-I could not understand what she meant-she then said to me,
"Say it is yours"--the officer was then looking at a brown paper parcel – I
had seen the prisoner with some silver the night before, but no gold.
Cross-examined. Q. Did the policeman hear what she said ? A. Yes-he
said to me,"Is this yours?"-I hesitated about it, and then said, "Yes"-
he said, "Are you sure?"-I then said, "No"-I was rather confused-she
appeared quite calm, and spoke to the officer as if he were a friend.
WILLIAM MORTEN (police-sergeant, T 7.) I took the prisoner -1 charged
her with stealing 121.-she said, “I stole no money"-I asked for her
pocket-she gave me this purse, which contained some silver-I said,
"You had some money"- she said, "Who said so?"--I said, “Mrs French"
--she then said she had 101. given her by a gentleman she had lived with,
and there was a letter in her box would show it-I then searched a drawer
and found a parcel-I saw her make a sign to Miss Fitzglin-I found
dress and a number of other things in the parcel, all new I told her I un-
derstood she bought those things after leaving Mr. Bromley's—she said, yes,
but most of them were presents- I took her to the station-she said, "I have
not stolen Mrs. Bromley's money, but if you will go to Islington the money
will be paid."
