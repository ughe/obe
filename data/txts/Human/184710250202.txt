   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  You first thought you had taken your half-sovereign from a teacher that lived opposite? A.  Yes, and then I found I did not, it was a mistake of my brother's—I said I was confused, and did not know what I was about, and the prisoner might as well have taken the nine shillings, but she did not take them—I had taken my half sovereign of a person whose face was familiar to me, but not a teacher—I have been in the jewellery business some years and am a good judge of money, but I never heard of ringing the changes  before—I took my half sovereign out of my pocket—I had had it there one day. CHARLES MILES. I am in the service of Mr. Cockin. On the 2nd of Oct., about five o'clock in the afternoon, Mrs. Grant gave me a half-sovereign to take to Mr. Field—I took it him—he gave it me back, and I took it to Mrs. Grant.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What did Mr. Field do with it? A.  He bent it on the counter—he did not take it into another room. GEORGE FIELD. The boy Miles gave me a half-sovereign—I gave him the same back. GEORGE COFFIN. I got a half-sovereign from my sister, Sophia Grant—I gave it to George Beck, the constable. GEORGE BECK( policeman, V  164.) I produce the half-sovereign. MARTHA STANDNIG. My husband keeps a china-shop. On the 5th of Oct. the prisoner came and wanted a china mug, with the name of "Mary" or "Ann" on it—I looked and could not find one—she looked at the counter, and said, "I will take one of these custard-cups"—the price of it was 1 s .—she gave me a sovereign—I had no change, and went up to my cash-box, and brought down nine shillings and a half-sovereign—I did not think it was right, and I bit the sovereign before I took it up stairs, and sounded it before her—she said, "It is a good one"—I gave her the change—she was putting it away, I turned to put the custard-glass into a piece of paper, and she said, "This half-sovereign does not sound well; I think it is cracked"—I said, "Let me see it"—she hesitated for a moment, and I asked her again—she gave it me, and said, "It is a cracked one"—I took it from her hand, and said, "It is a very base one"—I bit it double, and said, "I am confident this is not the one I gave you"—she said directly, "Oh, my good soul, what could I have done with the other!"—I said, "I don't know; I am confident this is not the one I gave you," and directly she faltered, and said, "If you will give it me, let me see it; I will take it to my father, and he shall see it, or I will cut it in two before him"—I did not allow her to go—she said, rather than there should be a bother  she would leave the shop. WILLIAM COTTON( policeman, F  52.) I took the prisoner—this half sovereign was given me by Mrs. Stevens.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not the prisoner say she had no other half sovereign, it was the same the lady gave her? A.  Yes—this was marked before it was given me. CALEB EDWARD POWELL.
