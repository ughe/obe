Ann Warren. I am cook to Mr. George Worth, of Montague-street-
the prisoner lived at my master's mother's, as her maid. On the 10th of
October she came to master's house between twelve and one o'clock, and left
between three and four-I am sure of that-a female was with her-she
wanted to see my master-he was out of town she was in the front
kitchen-the plate was in a cupboard in the back kitchen, which is generally
kept open the doors of the two kitchens are quite convenient one to the
other-she did not call again till the day my master came home-the
policeman came on the 11th with Clark, and brought a silver fork--I then
missed a fork-this is my master's fork, Mr. George Worth's—these are
the sort of forks that were in the cupboard the morning the prisoner was
there--I opened the door to the prisoner the morning the policeman came
-I told her there was a piece of work about a fork, and the pawnbroker and
policeman were there I asked if I should announce to my master that
she was there-she said I might if I liked-I left her in the hall, and told
my master-she said she never did it, she never saw the fork-Clark said
he could swear to her.

Sixth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
3006. ANDREW GOODRUP was indicted for embezzlement.
JOHN GEORGE STROBEL. I am a baker, and live in Brick-lane. The
prisoner was in my service-it was his duty to take bread out, and receive
money-he ought to have paid me directly he came home-if he received
28. 6d. on the 1st of September, or 23. 11 d. on the 5th of September, he
has not paid me.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Do you keep a book? 4. Yes,
I settled my account with him when I discharged him on the 17th of
September-I took him into custody on the 21st-he had 168. a week-
the account of the day's bread he gave me on the 17th of September was
38. 3d., which he said he had received from James, my butcher, and a per-
son named Morley-he said he had sold six quarterns- I do not know
who he sold it to exactly-I sent him to get this money.
