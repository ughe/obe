 1791.  HENRY STEVENS was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of July , 1 watch, value 30s., the goods of Thomas Walter Pride , from his person.
 THOMAS WALTER PRIDE. I am a  tallow-chandler, and live at Paddington. On Monday, the 23d of July, about twenty-five minutes after ten o'clock at night, a person, whom I did not know, walked with me from the Hay-market to Crawford-street; I saw a person take my watch; the man immediately ran away; I called Stop thief! but he turned a corner, and I lost sight of him; the watchman brought the prisoner back soon after - I should not like to swear to the person, or the dress, of the man who robbed me, but he had something round him which appeared to me to be a black apron, but it proved to be a bag.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you sober? A. Not so sober as I am now.
   THOMAS CLARK. I am a watchman. I heard a cry of Stop thief; the prisoner was the first man who was running; I took him about two hundred yards from Crawford-street; others were running - I saw the prosecutor in a quarter of an hour - he was sitting down; he had been drinking, but was not to say drunk - the prisoner had something black round his waist.
   JOHN BOYD. I am sergeant of the night; I came up when the prisoner was taken; he dropped some keys from his hat - I found no watch on him.
   RICHARD HARTLY WALL. The prisoner was brought to the office; I saw something being poked through the wall; next morning I went in, and found this bag in his hat.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1792.  ELIZABETH GRAHAM and SARAH SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of June , 60 penny-pieces, the monies of James McGoverin , from the person of Richard Braham.
 RICHARD BRAHAM. I am  servant to James McGoverin, of Buckeridge-street. On the 12th of June I was in Buckeridge-street, and had these penny-pieces in a basket; I put it on a post to rest; Graham came and took them out - there were four more girls with her - she was the first who came up; I did not see her take them, but I missed them in four minutes: a woman behind me said I was robbed by one of those girls; I laid down the basket to go after them, and saw Smith, who was one of the four, drop the 5s. paper of halfpence - I took it up - I lost two papers, but only found one.
   MARY DONNELLY. On the evening of the 12th of July, I saw Braham resting his basket on a post, near the corner of Church-street, St. Giles' - I stood talking to him - these young women came up; Graham took the paper of halfpence out of the basket, and passed it to Smith; I told him of it, and helped him down with his basket, while he pursued them - I saw the paper brought back.
 Prisoner SMITH. Q. Was there not a fight in the street? A. There was a crowd, but I saw no fight; Braham had 10l. worth of copper in his basket. I at first thought she took it in fun, till she gave it to Smith.
   EDWARD HEDGES. I am a beadle. I was sent for, and took up the prisoners; I went up into their room, but had a great difficulty to take them.
 GRAHAM's Defence. I stood by, but did nothing to the basket; I said, "That man has taken something;" this woman came, and said, "How dare you say so? it is more likely to be one of you."
 SMITH's Defence. He struck Graham, and made her nose bleed; I took her up stairs to wipe her face, and the beadle came and took us.
   RICHARD BRAHAM. I struck at her, as she appeared to be coming towards the basket again, but did not hit her.
 GRAHAM -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 1793.  THOMAS JAMES and GEORGE BUTLER were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , 1 shirt, value 5s., and 1 pair of stockings, value 2s., the goods of Robert Potts ; and 1 gown, value 10s., and 1 shift, value 3s., the goods of Charlotte Potts , from the person of William Ponsden.
 SARAH PONSDEN. I live in China-mews. On the 18th of August, at nine o'clock at night, I gave my son William three bundles to deliver, one containing these articles, belonged to Mr. Potts, of Little Gower-place, whom I wash for; he was to take it there; my little girl, who went with him, came and said he was robbed.
 WILLIAM PONSDEN. I am eleven years old. My mother gave me a bundle to take to Mr. Potts; I had delivered two other bundles, and had Mr. Potts' left; I was in Little Gower-street - the two prisoners came running along, and James snatched at my bundle; he could not get it, and snatched again - I hallooed Stop thief! he then made a third snatch, and got it. I had seen him before - he had followed me two or three times; they ran away together, and James gave the bundle to Butler - they ran down Little Gower-place, and tried to throw it over the pales, but it fell down, and I took it up; I carried it on to Mr. Potts' - some persons were pursuing the prisoners.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did not the watchman ask you if Butler took it? A. Yes; I said James took it, and gave it to him; James always had his hand in his pocket; I never observed that he had but one hand - he only used his left hand to take it, I am sure.
   JOHN CAMPBELL. I am a street-keeper. I was in Gower-street; a boy said I was wanted; I went to Little Gower-place, and saw Butler in custody; the lad said a man had taken a bundle, and given it to Butler, who tried to throw it over the pales; and a gentleman said he saw him try to throw it over, and dropped it.
