Did not you think the answer she made was more like a drunken than a sober woman? - Yes, one would have thought she had been drunk by that, but she went along the street very well.
 (The pot was produced in court and deposed to by the prosecutor.)
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 I went into this man's house, and had a pint of beer; the gentlewoman asked me to get her some water. I did not intend to take the pot when the man laid hold of me.
 To the Prosecutor. Where did the pot use to stand? - In the passage with pump-water in it; there was water in it that had been brought by my boy from St. Paul's pump. She had been in my house and had a pennyworth of beer. GUILTY of stealing to the value of 10 d. W.
 Tried by the Second Middlesex Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
 386.  JAMES SMITH was indicted for stealing eight iron bars, value 10 s. the property of the   Mayor and Commonalty of the City of London, June 12th.
   JOHN DINMORE sworn.
 On the 12th of June, about eleven o'clock, I saw the prisoner come up Turnmill-street with two iron bars in a bag on his shoulder. Isaacs and Redgrave, two other constables were with me; we stopped him and asked where he got these bars? He said he found them near Smithfield, but could not tell directly the place. I secured him. He laid in gaol till the 15th when the magistrate sat at the Artillery Ground; he was then examined and committed for further examination; when we came to Bridewell in the evening, he desired Redgrave and I would call upon him in the morning; we did. He asked if we could be of any service to him? I said it was out of our power to promise him any thing whatever. He said, he had some more iron at home at his lodging in Long-lane, Smithfield.
 Did he say where he got the iron from? - He said some men and he got it from Newgate. We went to his lodging and found these other eight bars across the bedstead under the bolster and pillow of the bed where his wife lay.
 (The evidence of  John Dinmore was confirmed by the testimony of Thomas Isaacs and Jonathan Redgrave.)
   THOMAS FLETCHER sworn.
 Seven of these bars belong to the transom windows, which give light into Newgate. I know them by the size and length, and the mark where they go through; two of the bars belong to the steps of the stairs.
 Do you know when these bars were taken from Newgate? - No.
 You did not see whether the bars were left standing after the rioters were there? - No; a great many were destroyed and a great many were standing.
 What was the value of these bars? - When they were new between two and three guineas.
 Were they put up at the expence of the City of London? - They were.
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 I have some friends to appear for me.
 (The prisoner called three witnesses who gave him a good character.)
     GUILTY.
 Tried by the Second London Jury before Mr. RECORDER. [Whipping. See summary.] [Imprisonment. See summary.]
 387.  THOMAS CUBBAGE was indicted for stealing a wooden cask, value 3 s. and a copper funnel, value 6 d. the property of Thomas Langdale, June the 7th.
   JAMES MATTHEWS sworn.
 I am servant to Mr. Langdale.
 Look at that cask and funnel? - It is my master Mr.  Thomas Langdale's property.
 When did you see it last in Mr. Langdale's house? - The day his house was destroyed, Wednesday the 7th of June; it is my province to fill these casks; I use the funnel for the finings. I am sure it is Mr. Langdale's.
