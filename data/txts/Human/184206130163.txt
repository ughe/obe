 1883. MARIA SNELL was indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of May , 1 cape, value 1 l  .; 1 collar, value 15 s  .; 3 yards of satinet, value 15 s  .; 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  . 9 d  .; 8 yards of ribbon, value 3 s  . 6 d  .; 10 artificial flowers, value 1 l  . 7 s  .; and 1 yard of silk, value 3 s  .; the goods of Jane Hains , her mistress. JANE HAINS. I am single. I am a milliner, and live in New Bond-street—the prisoner was in my employ—I sent her away—I received in formation, and gave her into custody on the last day of May—I afterwards went to the station and saw her—she told me if I would go with her to her house she would give me my property—I went with her and the in spector to Hastings's-street—she produced several boxes, and showed me a muslin cape, a lace collar, nine or ten flowers, and some other things, which are mine.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Had you searched her boxes at her mother's before that? A.  Yes—on the 5th of April, I think it was—I know Mrs. Chafford, a milliner, who lives near me—I believe the prisoner went from my service to live with her, but I know nothing of that lady—the prisoner left me because I suspected her and sent her away.
 I am a police-inspector. I took the prisoner.
   Cross—examined. Q.  Did you say you were sorry for this girl? A.  Yes—I do not recollect that I told her it would be better to tell the truth—I told her she was my prisoner on suspicion of stealing some silk—she laid, "I stole nothing"—at the station she sent for me, and said if I would go with her she would give up the things—I did not tell her it would be better for her.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 17.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury.  — Confined One Week.
