Mr. CLARkson. Q. You said something about servants, what is that?
A. The wax ends are considered the perquisite of the butler or footman, and
when they bring them we give them less than we do to a collector, but what-
ever we receive from a collector, or from a servant, we make an entry in the
book of it, so that the book shows every thing—there is no separate sum paid
to a servant, except for the amount we buy-when the omission which has
been asked about occurred, I had not received any money which I had not
accounted for, but I had left out an item in the customer's bill, and my omit-
ing it caused the prisoner's displeasure.
Thomas MOSDELL SMITH. I am a partner in the house-Tarrant and
Murrell were in our employ-I recollect another servant going to Gravesend,
and on that occasion a communication was made to me indirectly by John
Tarrant.
Cross-examined. Q. Has it not very often happened that checks have been
cashed for your customers, or for neighbours ? A. No, it never happened
to my knowledge-my department is the superintendence of the cash account
and correspondence, not the manufacturing department-our house keeps no
account with any nobleman or gentleman for dripping or wax ends, to my
knowledge.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. What do you mean by saying there is no account
with any nobleman or gentleman ? A. I know there is one family where
the dripping which is supplied by them goes to their credit in general where
there is dripping it goes to the account of the cook.
FREDERICK Shaw (police-sergeant A 29.) I took the prisoner on the 11th
of Jan.- produce a paper which was found by Mr. Smith in the counting-
house-it was sealed up I directed Mr. Smith to break it open.
MR. SMITH re-examined. I found this paper at the bottom of the cash
drawer in the iron safe in the counting-house, the name of T. Sergeant is on it-
I broke it open-I delivered it to Mr. Blackmore in the same state as it was.
ROBERT BLACKMORE. I am attorney for this prosecution. I received this
paper and its contents from Mr. Smith, and delivered it to the officer-it con-
tains a 5l. note and a sovereign.
ISAAC BLOMFIELD. I am servant to Mr. Edridge, of Oxford-street. On
the 7th of Nov. last I went to the prosecutor's shop, in South Moulton-street-
I paid the prisoner, who appeared to be a clerk there. 91. 38. 10d.-part of it
was a 5l. note-this is the note I paid him that day-I know it by the writing
on it.
Cross-examined. Q. What is the writing? A. The name of Mr. Fresh-
field, 24, Rutland-street, Hampstead-road----when the prosecution was going
on they came to my employer, who is a master tallow-chandler, in Oxford-
street-(paper read)—"This 67. is money over in the shop cash, supposed to
belong to T. Serjeant, bis own money not being right, and no other account
can be given of it: it will be left in the cash drawer, to see if any thing else
comes to light on the subject. Nov. 4, 1843."
MR. SMITH re-examined. Q. Supposing there was money over or under
in the shop cash-book, what was the prisoner's duty ? A. To tell me of it
- here is the shop cash-book for the 4th of Nov.-here appears to have been
3d. over that day-the prisoner never gave me any account of there being
61. over in the shop cash.
Not Guilty.
