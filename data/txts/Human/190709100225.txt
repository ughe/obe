(Wednesday, September 18.) FRIEND  , John (34, labourer), and RILEY  , Henry (37, dealer) ; to the stealing a horse, the goods of Emma Munday and feloniously receiving same; both stealing one horse collar; one pair reins, and other articles, the goods of William Grant , and feloniously receiving same; both stealing one saddle one bridle, and other articles, the goods of Henry Harvery , and feloniously receiving same.
 Mr. Thatcher prosecuted; Mr. Joyce Thomas defended Riley. EMMA MUNDAY, Wife of Richard Munday, labourer, Clapton. On August Bank Holiday I had a bay horse at grass in Reader's field, close to Lea Bridge. It was safe at five o'clock in the evening. Early next morning, when I went to look for it to take it to Spitalfields Market, it was gone. I am a costermonger, and help to get my own living and the childern's. When next I saw it it was at Dorking on the 9th, and 18 inches had been cut off its tale. I know prisoner Riley. He came to me to buy some fowls and a set of harness, but I could not say for certain when that was. He did not buy the fowls as the price was too high. Riley saw the cob on two occasions and asked whether it was for sale. The value of the horse was £14. It had on, in the field, a leather head stall, which was found in the trap at Barking. There was also found in the cart a collar I had sold to Riley. The collar was too small to go over my horse's head. Later on I saw Riley at Stoke Newington Police Station, and charged him. I value the halter at a shilling. I was shown some of the harness which I did not recognise. I know nothing of the harness except the collar. There was a complete set and some head pieces. The harness was found at a livery bait stables, and I paid 7s. 6d. for the feed of the horse on 6th 7th 8th and 9th. When I saw the horse it was in the custody of the police.
 To prisoner Friend. I have had the horse 16 years. I am not accustomed to let it out to anybody.
 To Mr. Thomas. I do not exactly remember the date when I sold some harness to Riley.
 Re-examined. If someone else has declared that this particular collar was theirs, very likely Mr. Riley sold it to him. It was mine once. WILLIAM GRANT, 10, Ribstone Street, Hackney, fire-wood dealer Between August 5 and 6 I lost some harness. I identified it on the Thursday week following, at the Stoke Newington Police Station. I know Riley, but I cannot say whether he know where my stable is. A man named Harvey uses the same stable as I do, and I some times have lent the harness to him. Harvey sometimes lent me his harness. There was no lock on the stable, only a but outside. This is the first time I have loss anything from the stable. HENRY HARVERY, Brooksby's Walk, Homerton. I missed some harness on the Tuesday after Bank Holiday, from the stable I jointly occupy with Grant. I identified the harness at Stoke Newington Police Station. I know Riley, to whom I have sold two horses.
