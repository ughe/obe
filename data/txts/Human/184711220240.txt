ELIZABETH PARKER. I am the wife of last witness. I was standing at my window and saw Ward commit the robbery. JAMES BURTON( policeman. ) I was on duty on 22nd Oct. in the Old Kent-road, and saw Ward and others in Kent-street—shortly after, I met Parker—he gave me information, and I went after Ward and took him—as we went along, Smith joined us—I saw nothing, as several of their companions were round me—I received information, and called to Smith—he ran away—I said to Ward, "You are well aware I know you"—I ran after Smith and overtook him—he took out a sovereign and said, "That is what the man gave me"—the Magistrate desired me to say that Smith was allowed to go upon his own recognizance—he has appeared here to-day.
   Smith's Defence.  I was not at the place when the robbery was commit ted, and know no more about it than a child. SMITH  — NOT GUILTY .

 263. JOHN MANGAN , stealing 6 shillings and 4 sixpences, the moneys of John Bone , from his person. JOHN BONE. I am potman at the Prince of Wales, in Brixton. On 22nd Nov., I went with a female friend in a cab  to White Hart Inn, in the Borough—the prisoner asked me to change my cab , and I got into his—I had 11 s . in my pocket, all but three farthing—he agreed to take me to Brixton for 2 s .—he said be would save the gate, and go round by the Wellington; but he weat through, and insisted on my paying the toll—when we got through ken nington-gate, he came into the cab  and dabed  against me, shoving  me from one side to the other—I asked what he meant by it—he left a boy on the box to drive, and came inside—I missed my money—I spoke to him about it—he denied it, and punched my head—I said I would give him in charge, and told him to drive to the station, but he stopped on the way—I said I would go—we went there, and he denied it—he was searched, and 13 s . 8 d . was found on him—no one had been near me but him—I had been half asleep and half awake—I did not ask him to go in the cab  with me—I asked what business he had there.
   Prisoner.  You got out; I demanded 8 d . for my fare, which you would not give me. Witness.  It is not true—I did not get out. WILLIAM JOHN GIFFORD( policeman. ) I was at the station—the prisoner and Bone came there together.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did not I drive him to the station? A.  Yes—you denied the charge.
   The Prisoner called GEORGE FITZGERALD. I live in Redcross-court, Borough. I am out of employment—I worked for Mr. Cullum, driving a horse and cart—I cleaned horses in his stable—I know the prisoner—on 22nd Nov. I was with him—I never drove his cab  —he never went into the cab  until Bone got to his jour ney's end, and then he woke him up—it was about two o'clock in the morn ing—I went that distance with him, as I had nothing to do—on my oath did not drive the cab  on that or any other occasion—I do know his horse—the road was heavy—the prisoner let his horse walk, because he sweated. NOT GUILTY .
