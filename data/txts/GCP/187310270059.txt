MR. COLLINS submitted that there was no evidence to support the indictment
Firstly, with regard to the Counts for false pretences, it was necessary that
a false pretence should have soine effect on the mind of the person to whom it was
made, otherwise it was no offence: that although the defendant wrote to
the prosecutrix, saying, “ The three shares were offered to me in one lot, and I
thought myself fortunate in securing them for you, and I have no doubt you
will ratify what I have done," that did not appear to have had any affect upon
her mind to induce her to part with her money, because she said “The reason
I sent the extra money was because I believed he had purchased three Bonds
instead of two, and I was satisfied if he thought it was for my advantage;"
therefore the false pretence was not sustained in any one particular. Second y;
the indictment was framed under 24 and 25 Vic., c. 96, sec. 75, which enacted
that “Whoever is entrusted as a broker, or attorney, or other agent, with a

the terms of such direction, convert the same to his own use and benefit, dec., shall
