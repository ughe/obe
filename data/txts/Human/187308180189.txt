were unfinished—I afterwards went to Mr. Edwards, the doll maker, in Waterloo Road—he showed me some papers, and I showed him a sample of wax—I did not see any of my wax there; it had been used—I received a communication from a person named Rumble, which I communi cated to Croome with certain instructions, and he came back to me and brought two pieces of  yellow wax, each weighing six or seven pounds, which I identified—it was in an unfinished state and not as it would be sold—it was worth about 18 d . a pound—about the same time I received information from a person named Gadsden, and went to the place of Mr. Williams, 108, Minories, where I saw the witness Addis—I had a conversation with him, in eousequence of which I went to Howe's shop in Cambridge Heath Road, on 28th April—Croome spoke first;
