 910. HARRY JAMES, Feloniously forging and uttering an order for the payment of 9 l  . 8 s  ., with intent to defraud.
   MR. MEAD Prosecuted; MR. HORACE AVORY Defended. GEORGE RICHARD TOOKE. I am managing clerk to Messrs. Smith, Stenning, and Co., of Aldermanbury—the prisoner has been in their employ about eight years—on 12th May 9 l . 8 s . was due to Mr. Heath for fees—the prisoner drew this cheque by my directions for that amount ( produced )—the body of it is in the prisoner's writing, and this is Mr. Stenning's signature—the prisoner's duty was to pay it to Mr. Heath—I did not see it again until, I think, 6th July, when my cashier called my attention to the endorsement, apparently in Mr. Heath's writing—I know his writing; it is not his, though very much like it—I showed it to Mr. Heath—after that I asked the prisoner what made him do these things—he said he was very sorry—there were several cheques drawn on the 12th May.
   Cross-examined.  The prisoner came to us from Kirby, Gedge, and Millett, the Solicitors to the School Board, with 6 years' character—he was their assistant Chancery clerk—we entrusted him with lump sums of money to disburse—he would have to render us an account, not at stated times, but not more than a week would elapse before he did so—if he used any of this petty cash for his private purposes, and then accounted for it at the end of the week, no particular objection would be made, unless damage resulted to a client.
   Re-examined.  We did not recognise his taking cheques drawn for par ticular purposes and cashing them, or putting other people's names on cheques—there were other acts of dishonesty on his part—certain papers were not to be found which were found afterwards.
   By MR. AVORY . That was one of the matters I complained to him about when I spoke to him—there was a question of his not stamping some deeds, and the whole matter was investigated by Mr. Stenning, not in my presence—there was some salary due to him, and it was set off against that.
