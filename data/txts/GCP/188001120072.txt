Cross-examined by MR. GRAIN. As a shipping-agent I consign goods
abroad-I believe it is the custom for large banks to make advances on
documents of title and invoices attached-I advanced cash on these goods
which were going to Paris, and before they could deal with them their bills
had to be met.
Re-examined. I think there were only two instances out of about 21
in which the goods went to Paris before I was paid I claim 2,7007. As due
to me, not as a creditor--that represents the difference between 2,7001. and
4,5001., the amount of the goods redeemed by them.
By the Court. About 21 repayments were made to me by the firm from
22nd May to 18th September; but in only two cases were the goods after-
wards redeemed-there was a running account I did not keep a debtor and
creditor account of each parcel—the invoice value of the goods I had when
they failed was about 3,1551.
MICHAEL CAUCHAL. I am a commission merchant, at 113, Fenchurch
Street-I was introduced to Learoyd, Wilton, and Co., by Mr. Penny on
18th March, to raise 2,0001. on the Zanzibar-on Saturday, 19th July, Mr.
Penny called on me, and said “Will you oblige Learoyd and Co. with
4201.-I saw Wilton with reference to it on the 21st, and agreed to advance
it on the security of woollen goods-he produced samples-he sent them to
my office, I think by Griffiths--on examining the goods with the invoices I
found they did not correspond, so Dunderdale called to explain it and set it
right--the goods were at Southgate's—they remained there to my order
I had a transfer order, and they held the goods for me-there were seven
advances altogether, amounting to 2,7341.-Wilton negotiated all the trans
actions with me; he sometimes came to me and sometimes sent for me when
they wanted to redeem some goods--they did redeem some, and gave me
cash-at the time of their failure 1,7591. 138. 6d. was due to me--I had
goods in hand as security, but they owed me 1,2131. besides-I sold them
some goods myself before they failed, and that included the amount, but I
did not deliver those goods-1,2631. is due to me for advances on those
goods, and there were the warehouse charges- I have sold those goods; I
realised 1,7501., in luding my goods; deducting my amount, it comes
to about 1,2631.--there is another bill for me to pay-altogether. I
shall be either a winner or a loser of about 101. out of my actual
capital exclusive of my commission--the pledgings were for about 30
days; the shortest was 14 days—this was the first transaction I ever
had in advancing money on goods—I am not a money-lender—I did this to
accommodate them.
Cross-examined by Mr. WRIGHT. I had, I think, seven separate trans-
actions with the defendant's firm-they renewed some of the bills-in
two instances the goods were redeemed, and in one there was a substitution
--the total of my advances from 21st July to 5th September was about
2,1001.- they redeemed goods by paying about 8001., and I delivered them
goods to the value of about 1,5007.---some goods were left in my hands at
the time of the failure-I don't understand selling woollen goods, but I
know who I can employ-when they stopped payment I sold them con-
ditionally, provided the trustee and receiver would not take them, because
there were bills running-I employed Cave, of Barnstaple, to sell, and be
sold them to Samur! Fletcher, of Manchester.
Re-examined. These are the invoices I received, and I gave them to the
