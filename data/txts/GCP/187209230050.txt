GIBBONS, Mayor.
ARTHUR NEWTON FORBES Gordon. I am an officer in the 79th High-
landers, now statioued in the Isle of Wight-I inserted an advertisement
last June in the Isle of Wight Observer and Hampshire Independent, as I
wished to sell a bay mare I had, and any reply that was made wis to be
addressed to the adjutant of the regiment a letter was received by the
adjutant, which he gave to me-I wrote this letter in answer-I afterwards
received this letter, signed William Cartwright-I sent this letter, saying
that I did not wish to sell the mare unless Mr. Cartwright, or some friend
of his, had seen and approved of her, and I did not chose to have her sent
back on my hand I got this answer, and then I sent the mare by the rail.
way-tbe clothing that I sent with her was returned, but I have not seen
her since, nor have I had the money-I parted with the mare, believing
that I was sending her to a salesman and contractor in Smithfield.
Cross-examined. I never saw the prisoner till I saw him at the Police
Court-I don't know his writing the letters that I sent I addressed to Mr.
William Cartwright, 25, Cock Lane.
WILLIAM WRIGHT (re-called). There are five letters of Major Gordon's,
which were found at 22, Naylor Street, the prisoner's private address.
CHARLES WILLIAM LACRELL. I occupy the house, 22, Naylor Street-on
10th June the prisoner came to live in two of the rooms, in the name of
Beck-I received one letter from the postman for him, which I gave to
his wife.
Cross-examined. I am the landlord of the house-I saw the prisoner very
often after he came to live there another man lived there with hira, that
was not Beck-I don't know what his name was, I only heard the name
" William"-they went away in the morning and came back in the evening,
like business men.
CHARLES UNDERWOOD (re-called). I produce a railway ticket for a horse,
which I found in the prisoner's pooket-book-it is dated 2nd July, in the
name of Cartwright, for a horse sent from Portsmouth.
GEORGE FRANCIS. I am an estate agent, at Eaton Chambers, Buckingham
Palace Road-on 12th July the prisoner came and signed an agreement to
take a house at No. 19, St. John's Lane-this is the agreement-he signed
it in the name of William Cartwright-he pave two references before the
preliminary agreement was sigued; one was to a man named Beck, of
Naylor Street-I wrote to that address, and got an answer by return of
post—this is my letter to Beck.
Cross-exumined. I only saw the prisoner at my office on 12th July
I have no doubt that he was the man who signed the preliminary
agreement.
