I met the three prisoner together, about a quarter past two o'clock, coming down the Harrow-road, with the horse, not more than forty yards from the field—Condon was riding it—I knew the horse, as my father has borrowed it—Condon had a red comforter for a bridle, and a black handkerchief for the reins—it was a very quiet horse.
   O'Donnell.  That boy was fetched up the first time that we were remanded, but he did not say what he does now; he said he could not swear to us; the Magistrate would not take his evidence, and they fetched up a ginger-beer man, who said it was two young men that entered the field, and he did not see them take the horse out. Witness.  The Magistrate did not refuse to take my evidence. WILLIAM YOUNG re-examined.  The prisoners were remanded, as the pro secutor said that two lobouring men in the field had told him that three hod-boys had taken the horse—we could not find those men, but a ginger-beer man said he saw two boys go into the field and take the horse out—he could not swear to the prisoners; and then the Magistrate asked for the boy—he was not there that day, and I subpoena'd him.
   Hogan. Q.  Did not we follow you to the station-house? A.  You did.
   O' Donnell's Defence.  We found the horse running down the Bayswater-road; we stopped it, and put our comforters round him, intending to take it to the station-house or the Green-yard.
   Condon's Defence.  We were out looking for work, and saw the horse in the road; some gentleman asked us to stop it; we ran after it, and caught it, and tied the comforters round its neck; we went along with it, asking of every cab  man we met if he knew of a Green-yard.
   Hogan's Defence.  We stopped the horse in the road, took off our com forters, and tied them round his neck. JANE HOGAN. I am the prisoner's sister. He was at work on the Mon day before this, and on the Tuesday he went out, and was not gone ten minutes before I heard he was taken—I saw the other two boys not a quarter of an hour before, and am sure they had not time to go very far. NOT GUILTY .
 1929. MICHAEL JOHNSON was indicted for feloniously assaulting Richard Brannan , and stabbing, cutting, and wounding him on his left arm, with intent to do him some grievous bodily harm. RICHARD BRANNAN. I am a porter, and live in Red Lion-street, White-chapel. On the 7th of Aug., about a quarter before twelve o'clock, I was in Grace's-alley, Wellclose-square, and saw the prisoner there striking a young women—I said, "Why not leave the women alone?"—he then left her, and seized me by the neck-handkerchief, twisted it round, and nearly choked me—a constable came up, and sent him one way, and me another—when I had got about 150 yards from the spot, he came running back—he ran past me about a yard or two, then turned suddenly round, and aimed a blow at me, which I thought was with his fist—he then stepped little way back; and as he rose his hand a second time, I caught the reflection of the blade of a knife, or some sharp instrument—it was under a gas-lamp—I then hit him, and knocked him down—he aimed a second blow, which cut me slightly on the neck—I found my arm cut about an inch, or rather better—I was taken to the hospital, and am there still.
   Prisoner.  I had no knife on me, nor was any found—there were sailors and others round—he had me on the ground, and two men were on me—they took my handkerchief and cap from me, and he collared me.
