JAMES CLARKE. I am a surgeon, of 22, Arbour-place, Kingsland-road.
On 11th Feb. I was called in to examine the body of the deceased - I made
a post mortem examination--the lungs exhibited evidences of long-standing
inflammation---the stomach was inflamed throughout its surface, and there
were traces of incipient ulceration--the state of the lungs was certainly not
the cause of the ulceration in the stomach-the ulceration proceeded, in my
opinion, from some acrid substance being taken into the stomach-there was
a fluid in the stomach at the time I examined it, amounting to three ounces,
and of an olive green colour-that fluid was saved, and was afterwards given
to Dr. Letheby to be analyzed-my opinion is that the proximate cause of
death was disease of the lungs and stomacb; there was disease enough in the
lungs to account for death-there was an effusion of something like sixteen
Ounces of fluid in the cavity of the chest, the result of inflammation of the
lungs-seeing how the woman was, I should say lobelia was not a proper
thing to be given; it would be likely to hasten death-cayenne pepper wat
not a good thing to administer to a person in that state-lobelia, cayende
pepper, and stimulants of that nature, would be apt to hasten the death of a
person diseased as she appears to have been.
Court. Q. Are eayenne pepper and lobelia stimulants of the same sort!
A. Not of the same character; cayenne pepper is a powerful stimulanti
lobelia is used in medicine in some diseases; it is of the tobacco tribe-it
not like stramonium exactly the tribe is not called datura; it belongs to
the tribe lobelia-when I said it was of the tobacco tribe, I meant having
similar effects on the constitution.
