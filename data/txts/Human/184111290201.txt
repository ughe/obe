   Q.  On your solemn oath, was not your wife drinking brandy at the public-house yesterday? A.  In the public-house after standing some time, I believe I got her some—she tasted it—I found her sitting there by her self—I said, "It is very foolish to sit here among the smoke, you will get overcome"—I blowed  her up for being in a smoky-room—she had got damp in her feet.
   Q.  When had you seen the forged note? A.  I do not know that I had seen it for twelve months, but I cannot say how long—I do not think it is two years—it belongs to my wife—her mother had taken it—she is not here.
   COURT . Q.  Where was your money kept? A.  In the left-hand corner of a drawer in my bed-room—the drawer was not locked—the money had not been there long—I suppose about six or seven weeks—sometimes the door was locked, and sometimes not—there was no key of the drawer—I had a sister of my wife's in the establishment—she is not here—she re sorted to the room, and my wife, myself, and the servant.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you mean to swear the girl did not on two occasions give you notice to leave, and at last say she would quit on the 7th of the month, when her quarter was up? A.  Never—I never offered her two sovereigns.
   COURT . Q.  How do you know what money you had? A.  I cannot say within a few pounds, between 40 l . and 50 l . I do not know the 10 l . note, or the three 5 l . notes—they have not been found—no note has been found but the forged note—I had talked about the forged note, it had "forged" stamped right across it—the prisoner can read.
   JURY . Q.  Did you ever pay the prisoner any money? A.  Not one farthing. not for wages—I dare say my wife has advanced her some.
   MRS. BEAGLEY . I never paid any wages to the prisoner—I never gave her any money.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Will you swear you never paid her any money during the six or ten months she lived with you? A.  Let me recollect myself if you please—I believe I did, I think it was about 5 l .
   COURT . Q.  Where did you keep your keys? A.
