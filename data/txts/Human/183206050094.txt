 1626.  THOMAS HAYNES was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of May , 1 hat, value 5s., the goods of Samuel Gravenor.
   JAMES WEARING. I was in Brick-lane on the 31st of May, and saw the prisoner take a hat from Mr. Gravenor's shop, opposite Hanbury's brewhouse; I did not pursue him, but he was taken afterwards - I lost sight of him; I know he is the person.
   JOHN KNIGHT. I saw the prisoner run away with the hat; I cried Stop thief! I know he is the boy.
   ROBERT WARE(Police-constable K 170). I was at my own door at ten minutes past eight o'clock; I heard a cry of Stop thief! and saw the prisoner run by and another boy after him, with the hat; I pursued, and took the prisoner - the other boy told him he had stolen the hat.
   JOHN HUMPHRIES. This is Mr. Samuel Gravenor's hat - I had it to sell for him.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1627.  WILLIAM GARSON was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of June , 1 pocket-book, value 2d.; 2 skeins of thread, value 1d.; 2 half-crowns, 2 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the property of Michael Stanley , from his person.
 MICHAEL STANLEY. I am a  navigator- I have known the prisoner two months; he worked for the same master. On the 17th of June he and I went to a public-house about one o'clock; I had a pocket-book in my waistcoat pocket, containing two skeins of thread, two half-crowns, two shillings, and two sixpences - I fell asleep for near three quarters of an hour; when I awoke I missed the book and its contents - I inquired of the landlord about it: I saw the prisoner again at a quarter before three o'clock, up the street; I taxed him with taking my book - he denied it; I met an officer, who took him; I saw him searched, and one of the papers, which had been in my book, two skeins of thread, and a bit of blue ribbon were found on him, and the money stated.
   WILLIAM BARROW(Police-constable G 158). I took the prisoner, and found on him the money and these articles.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   BENJAMIN WALKER. The prosecutor and the prisoner came to my house, and had a pot of porter - the prosecutor pulled out a pocket-book, and gave me a shilling; in about three-quarters of an hour he said he had been robbed of his pocket-book; I said, "If you have been robbed, it is by your mate:" he went out to look for him, and brought him back.
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor asked me to go and have some beer; I had 10s. in my pocket, and was going to buy some clothes.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Confined Twelve Months.
 1628.  FREDERICK FYNN was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of February , 3 coats, value 6l.; 1 pair of trousers, value 1l.; 9 yards of kerseymere, value 1l., and 2 yards of woollen cloth, value 2l., the goods of Felix Neil , his master.
   FELIX NEIL. I am a tailor. The prisoner was in my employ for two years and a quarter; I had stock taken, and missed some property - I employed an officer, who took the prisoner.
   ALEXANDER TATE. I am a pawnbroker. I produce all the property, which was pawned by the prisoner's wife, I believe.
   GEORGE AVIS. I am an officer. I looked for the prisoner, and found him on the 5th of June; he said his master's property was safe - I went to the Two Spies, in Catherine-street, where I received nine duplicates, which relate to the property.
 GUILTY. Aged 27. Recommended to Mercy- Confined Six Months.
 1629.  WILLIAM EDMONDS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of June , 1 snuff-box, value 3s.; 18cigars, value 12s.; 1 canister, value 8d., and 6 ozs. of snuff, value 3s., the goods of John Schofield.
 JOHN SCHOFIELD. I am a  tobacconist, and live in St. John's-lane. The prisoner was in the habit of coming to my house for cigars and other things - on the 25th of June he came early in the morning; I stood on the stairs, and watched him - I saw him take a snuff-box, then a lot of cigars, and then a Scotch snuff-box from my window, and put them into his coat pocket; I sent for an officer, who took him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was he tipsy? A. I do not consider that he was; I think he had been drinking - it was about seven o'clock in the morning.
   WILLIAM ALLEN(Police-constable G 38.) I took the prisoner - I found the property on him; he stated in going along that he had been carrying on the game for a fortnight; these things were in his coat pockets.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he not tipsy? A. I did not perceive it.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 48. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Weeks.
