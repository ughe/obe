   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Did they walk all four near to one another, and appear to be in company? A.  They appeared to be in company—Claridge and Mrs. Haggerstone were about a yard or two behind the others—they were all in one company—Hedges appeared to be intoxicated—Mrs. Haggerstone had a loaf under her arm—I just saw them pass me on the road—that is all—I believe Mr. Haggerstone had a bonnet on, but I did not take particular notice—I never saw the prisoner at all that evening—I never saw Claridge with Mrs. Haggerstone before.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  How long would it take to walk from the White Hart to where you first saw there persons? A.  Not above a minute, I should think—it was about twenty minutes before twelve o'clock—Mrs. Haggerstone was nearer to me than Mrs. Hedges when Mrs. Hedges called out to her—she could have heard her—she made no answer, but remained with Claridge—I believe Haggerstone was taken up, and afterwards liberated—I do not know how many days he was at large.
   COURT . Q.  You were very near to Claridge and Mrs. Hedges? A.  Yes—I soul think it impossible she could have been there, without seeing Claridge and Mrs. Haggerstone together—they were together—they came up to them—I was examined before the Coroner on the Tuesday after it happened, and Claridge was shown to me there—I am sure he is the man I saw talking to Mrs. Haggerstone. GEORGE HONE. I am foreman to Miss Paynes. On the 16th of April I lived in a little cottage just by Miss Paynes's—next to the farm-house—I was going home that night, from the White Hart—I had been down there to do some business—I left there as near twelve o'clock as could be—it was about ten minutes after twelve o'clock when I got home—just opposite Saunder's beer-shop there is a turning to the left, which leads to the Winding-shot field, and to the hut where Haggerstone lived—when I got a short way up that lane, I saw a man and woman at a stile on the right-hand side—I should not know them again to swear to them, it was very dark—I can swear it was not the prisoner—the person was taller than the prisoner—the man moved from the stile when I came up, and I passed between the two—I did not get over the stile—I never saw Claridge till after this—as far as I cam judge, the man was about the size of Claridge—he was dressed in a lightish coloured frock, or flannel waistcoat—I could not tell which, but it was a light dress—my house is about a hundred yards from the well in which Mrs. Haggerstone was found—shortly after I got home, I heard two persons walking through the gate into the little field, and talking together very gently—that little field leads into the Winding-shot field—it appeared to be a man and woman talking—I went to bed a few minutes after—my lodger was afterwards called up, when Mrs. Haggerstone was found in the well.
