SECOND COUNT, for stealing 1s., the money of Daniel Connel.
   DANIEL CONNEL. I am a cow-keeper, and live in Hackney-road. On the 19th of June the prisoner was employed by me to cart bricks from Messrs. Rhodes', whose country house is in Balls'-pond- I did not send my account in for a week after; I do not know how much was due, as the prisoner gave me a false account - he used to tell me every day what work he had done, and I settled with Messrs. Rhodes every Thursday; on Wednesday morning I gave him the account to take to them - he was not to receive the money; I was to call for it next day, but I did not call till the Thursday week, and finding I had no money to receive, I gave him in charge - he said he had received 8s. 4d., and that was all; that was one day's work - he said he had bought a pair of half-boots with the money, and would pull them off his feet if I would forgive him; he had received one week's wages, and had 5s. advanced towards the next.
   JOSEPH BANKS. I am junior clerk at Messrs. Rhodes. The prisoner carted bricks for us; he asked for employ in the name of Laig, and did not say he was the prosecutor's servant - on Thursday, the 21st of June, I paid him 8s. 4d. for one day's cartage; Mr. Connel applied for it on the 28th of June - here is the receipt the prisoner gave me.
   RICHARD HAWKES. I received the prisoner in charge - his master asked him for the account which he ought to have delivered to Messrs. Rhodes; he then gave me the account, and said he had received the 8s. 4d., and bought a pair of half boots with the money.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Three Months.
 1667.  JOHN FISHER was indicted for stealing on the 26th of May , 3 gallons of turpentine, value 13s., the goods of Thomas Green.
   GEORGE NORRIS. I am a shopman to Thomas Green, an oilman, in Little Bath-street, Clerkenwell. On the 26th of May, about eleven o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came to the shop, and ordered three gallons of turpentine, to go to Mr. Powell's, Lower Islington - I sent it by Miles, who was to bring the money back - the prisoner went with him; he took it in two cans, which came back empty.
   JOHN MILES. I am in Mr. Green's employ. I was sent with the prisoner with three gallons of turpentine, in a cart, which I had the care of; the prisoner said it was to go to Mr. Powell's ( Lower Islington, and when we got to Powell's), as the prisoner called it, he asked me to wait outside, while he took the turpentine into Powell; I gave it to him, out of the cart, which stood on the opposite side of the way to Powell's, who is an oilman - I saw him take it in; he brought the cans out, and said Mr. Powell would not have the turpentine - he set the cans on the pavement, and said he would go to another customer, and see if he would have it; just after he went, the cans were opened, and contained nothing but oil of tartar - I never saw him again till he was taken; I am sure the cans contained turpentine when I gave them to him.
   JAMES FRITH. I am an apprentice to Mr. Green. I saw the turpentine put into the cans; the prisoner drove the cart, pointed out the shop, and told us to wait opposite - he came out once or twice, and said Powell was busy, and kept us waiting half an hour; he then brought out the cans full of pearl-ash and water, which is called oil of tartar - I thought there was something wrong, and opened them immediately; I pursued, but could not find him.
   WILLIAM RODD. I sell pies in the street. I was passing Powell's, in Lower Islington, and heard Powell halloo out Stop thief! and saw the prisoner running - I pursued, and he was taken; Powell told me a young lad had given charge of him about some turpentine - he said nothing to that; this was about a month after the transaction.
   FRANCIS FEATHERSTONE. I am a Policeman. On the 28th of June I saw Rodd pursuing the prisoner, and stopped him - he was charged by Powell's boy with stealing 9s. 6d. - this charge was made at Worship-street.
   THOMAS GREEN. Oil of tartar is about 3d. a gallon - the turpentine cost me 4s. 3d. a gallon; Powell was held to bail with the prisoner on this charge.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1670.  HENRY WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Robert Hitchcock , from his person.
   ROBERT HITCHCOCK. On the 26th of June I was walking, with my brother, about half-past nine o'clock in the morning, in Regent-street. A gentleman behind us said, "That person crossing the street has picked your pocket;" at the same moment, my brother exclaimed, "Somebody has picked your pocket - we crossed the way;" I saw the prisoner, and seized him - he had rushed between us and the wall, as we thought very rudely; this is my handkerchief.
   ROBERT STONE. I saw the prisoner pick the pocket - he then crossed the road - the gentleman seized him, and he threw the handkerchief down.
   WILLIAM HACK(Police-constable C 34) I took the prisoner.
 Prisoner. I hope you will have mercy upon me.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Six Weeks.
