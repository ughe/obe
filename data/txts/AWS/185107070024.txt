ANN RICE. I live at Notting-hill-square. On 4th June I wrote this letter
to my daughter, and put this 10l.-note into itthis is a memorandum of the
number and date, which I made at the time-I fastened the letter, and also
stuck on a black medallion wafer-I took it to the post-office at Notting-hill,
kept by Miss Underwood, and gave it to her, with a 6d. for the registration
fee, desiring that it might be registered-Miss Underwood was busy, and
put the letter into a glass book-case behind her, and asked me to call again
for the receipt-I asked to see the letter again, to see that I had left the right
one, and the prisoner handed it to me-I merely looked at it, and left it in
her hand-I then left the shop, and called in half or three-quarters of ad
hour for the receipt, which I received from Miss Underwood- do not recol
leet whether the prisoner was present-I afterwards heard that the letter was
not right, and made an application to the Post-office.
SOPHIA UNDERWOOD. I live at Notting-hill, and keep the post-office.
The prisoner was my servant, and used to assist me in getting up the letters
and stamping them-or 4th June Mrs. Rice handed me a letter-I believe
this to be the cover of it (produced)-I was making up the 4 o'cloek
delivery- received the 6d. for the registration, and asked ber to come again
for the certificate- put it into a glass-case behind me, in the prisoner'
presence, till I had an opportunity of registering it-Mrs. Rice came again,
about 5, and I marked 44 Registered letter' on it, and stamped it-this
is my mark in red chalk- tied it round with string, entered it on the
bill, and gave it to the letter-carrier when he came-there was no other
registered letter that dispatch-the prisoner had access to the glass-case-I
may have been out of the shop at the time the letter remained at the
office I do not recollect it-the prisoner was with me nearly the whole of
the time.
