Q. Who are they? A. I do not know I am sure I do not know where
the house is—I do not know whether it is in Hanway-yard - I have heard
that some one has taken a house for them I do not recollect the person's
name-he does not act for me I have been in prison ever since, and have
had no communication-I do not know who has taken the house or where
it is-I have been in prison, and have not troubled my head about it-
I do not know that it was my brother-in-law, Mr. Rees, that took it-I
bave every reason to believe it is not, or I should have heard it, I do not
know who is the owner of the house in Hanway-yard-it is not my brother-
in-law-he has no houses there he did live in Hanway-yard before I went
to prison-he lived there in a house of his own my wife and family have
not taken his house-they have taken a house there, but not his house.
Q. Pray how long was it after Alice Abrahams was examined that the
remorse struck you, and your conscience began to work? A. Directly
after I had bought the first gold- I would have made reparation and told
the truth if I could have got out of it then-I did not like to expose
myself that I was in it-it was about a week or ten days after Mrs.
Abrahams was examined that I made the disclosure-it might have
been a fortnight-my conscience had begun to work before that-I only
sent one statement-I was examined privately before the Magistrate once,
and then Mr. Hardwick told me I must consider that statement was a vo-
luntary statement, and that was the way I gave it-it was drawn up in
writing by my solicitor, and my signature was attached to it--it was the
substance of the truth that I was to swear to-I swore to it afterwards be-
fore the grand jury, not before the Magistrate-I do not remember swearing
to it before the Magistrate- I gave it in--they considered it a voluntary
statement-I did not swear to that statement before the Magistrate I was
examined by the Magistrate after I gave that statement in- I was ex-
amined afterwards on oath before the Magistrate.
