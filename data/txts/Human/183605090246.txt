I was at the baker's—I did not are them come out—I never saw them after went in—while Mrs. Haggerstone and I were waiting for them, we stood in the same place—I was called up on Sunday morning by the constable and overseer, and was taken into custody, but not when they first came for me—I went to work on Sunday morning after they called me up—they told me about Mrs. Haggerstone being found in the well—I went to work after that—I was taken into custody about nine o'clock that morning—Haggerstone and I were at the White Hart together about twelve o'clock in custody, on the Sunday, in the parlour of the White Hart—and we were there on the Monday—he then told me not to say any thing of what happened on the Saturday between him and his wife—I was then under charge myself—that was before I went before the Coroner—the Coroner came on Tuesday, and I made my first statement on the Thursday following—I was discharged as the Monday night after I made my second statement—I was kept in custody from Sunday, the 17th till the Monday in the following week—I was not discharged till after I had made my second statement.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  When you made second statement on the Monday, did you give the same account as you had given on the Thursday? A.  No, I did not—I did not say any thing about the prisoner's striking his wife, till the Monday night, when I told the constable about it—I did not tell the Coroner of it till Monday—I was in custody on this very charge—the prisoner wished me not to do it.
   COURT . Q.  You, who were under examination, chose to obey him, though you had seen all this cruelty to his wife? A.  Yes.
   MR. DOANE ? Q.  Were you on oath? A.  No: the reason I kept it back was because the prisoner told me to—that was my reason.
   COURT . Q.  Why, at the time you had no doubt in your own mind that he had done this—at the time you kept it back? A.  I could not tell—I did not see it myself—I could not say he did it.
