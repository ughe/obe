   ROBERT LOCK. I am the officer. I have the silk which Mr. Machu desired me to take away - it laid on the box - the prisoner was there; he said he was a lost man, and he did not care what become of him; it was tied up in this handkerchief.
   JAMES BAMFORD. I am a foreman to Mr. Machu. I know the prisoner - on the 10th of September some silk of this description came from the dyer's; I weighed it, and there was 58lbs.; it was then put into the silk-room, which is detached from the warehouse; I have seen this parcel of silk weighed; it weighs 5lbs., and I have weighed the silk that came from the dyer's since; we had parted with about 9lbs. 11 oz. of it, but allowing for that there is 5lbs. deficient - I have seen the prisoner with a handkerchief of this pattern, about five or six days before; it was dirty on a shelf, and he said "This is mine, I will take it home, and have it washed;" it struck me immediately that it was the same handkerchief; I have often seen a handkerchief of this kind in the warehouse.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went out with a paper, and the boy was left in the warehouse; when I returned I was occupied full an hour in putting things in order; the prosecutor was in his house, and I did not see him till I went out to my breakfast; he then came and asked, if I had seen the silk; I said I had not; he then told me to search, which I did, and it was found in the cotton-room; but I told him I knew nothing of it.
 COURT to PROSECUTOR. Q. Did he say he knew nothing of it? A. He said it was not his - I said it was not, it was mine.
 Six witnesses gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 33.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Twelve Months.
 1623.  JACOB MEE was indicted for embezzlement.
   GEORGE OWEN. I am a boot and shoe-maker, and live in Cross-court, Carnaby-street. The prisoner was my apprentice, and was employed to receive money for me; when money was paid him, he was to account for it immediately on his return; he gave me no money on the 1st of June, as received from Ann Smith; and to the best of my knowledge he gave me no money on that day - Mr. Smith owed me for the repair of a pair of boots for one of his children - on the 26th of August I told the prisoner to go for the money, and he said he recollected that Mr. Smith had called in my absence, and paid it, and he had given it to his mistress - my wife denied receiving the money; he did not say any thing else, and I sent for Mrs. Smith, who came and said she had paid him when he took them home; when I sent him with them I was in the shop, and on his return he said he gave them to Mrs. Smith, who did not ask him the price; he has been with me four years.
 ANN SMITH. I am the wife of  Thomas Smith. The prisoner brought home a pair of boots for my child, and I paid him 1s. 4d. when he brought them home.
   THOMAS GOOK. I was sent for to take the prisoner; his master gave him in charge for receiving 1s. 4d. of Mrs. Smith, which he had not given him; he said he was very sorry, and hoped his master would forgive him. GUILTY. Aged 17.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Months.
 1624.  JOHN MURPHY was indicted for stealing 3 axes, value 5s., and 1 hammer, value 4d., the goods of John Hinxman.
   MARY BUVINI. I am in the service of John Hinxman, who lives in Tottenham-court-road- his house was repairing on the 11th of August - the prisoner was a labourer there; I saw him go to dinner with a sack, which appeared heavier than it ought to be; I called him back, and asked him what he had got; he said, "Tools of my master's, which I am going to carry home;" I found these three axes - I claimed them as being Mr. Hinxman's property - he has a quantity of iron and lead, and other things at the back of his premises, of which I had the charge.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Was this man working on the premises? A. Yes; with his master; he offered no resistance - he said he had been desired to take them by his master - he emptied the bag without any hesitation.
   JOHN TECTOR. The prisoner was working for me at Mr. Hinxman's - I did not desire him to take any axes or any thing else from there.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long did he work for you? A. Ten days; I never saw the tools.
   JOHN LEWIS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and found this hammer in his breeches pocket - he said at the office that his master gave it him to put a handle to it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. He put all these things in the bag, and told me to take them home before I went to dinner.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Confined One Month.
 1625.  GEORGE PALMER was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Edwin John Grafton , from his person.
 EDWIN JOHN GRAFTON. On the evening of the 30th of July I was walking with my brother and a friend, near  Northumberland-street, Strand- we passed five or six persons at the corner of St. Martin's-lane; I felt something behind me, and missed my handkerchief; I saw the prisoner walking from me; I took hold of him, and was about to take the handkerchief from his hand - he had not met me, but was walking as if he had - he ran across the street, I pursued, and he dropped it; I am quite certain I saw it in his hand - the gentleman who was with me took it up.
   WILLIAM NETTLETON. I am the officer. I took the prisoner at the watch-house.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. (written.) I was proceeding home, and heard an alarm - I went to ascertain the cause, but finding it was of no consequence I returned, when I was stopped by a watchman at the end of a court - he took me back to the prosecutor, who was completely intoxicated - the handkerchief produced is the property of my sister.
 Mr. GRAFTON. I was not at all in liquor.
