WINCHESTER, Mayor.
MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you not sworn to tell the whole truth 1 A.
Yes, I was; but it was after that that she told me this-before that I
mean.
Q. You swore you were examined after the woman stated this; is that
true ? 1. It is true what she said to me--I have told the truth-I cannot
give any reason why I did not disclose to the Magistrate what I have told
here to-day, about this woman asking forgiveness and offering to give up
one share-I believe I was sworn to tell the whole truth, but I thought
Mrs. Smith would turn up the money I have spoken the truth and no
more-I told the truth, that is all the answer that I can give you-I have
left iny tongue behind me at Manchester.
Q. Do you know any thing of the solemnity of an oath, or do you care
what you swear; is it true that you kept that back, and thought that Mrs.
Smith would turn up the money? A. I have told the truth; I can say
no more.
Q. I ask you, in the presence of the Jury and 'of your God, did you take
an oath at the police-office or not? 4. I spoke the truth and stated the
truth, Sir.
Jury. Q. Would there have been any prosecution if this money had
been returned ? A. I know nothing, only that she said she would give up
hers, if Mrs. Mully would give up hers.
MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Now you heard the question the foreman of the
Jury put, would you have prosecuted or appeared in this business, if the
money had been paid ? A. Yes, I dare say we should-Mr. Jennings was
the gentleman who put the prosecution forward-we should have done
what he directed us-I did not send any message by Mr. Jennings to the
prisoners, nor did my husband-I never gave him any money to give the
prisoners, nor did my husband-he went with us to the prison--it was left
entirely to him-I might desire him to say something to the prisoners, or
I might not, I cannot say.
Q. Will you swear you did not? A. Perhaps in this length of time I
have forgotten it--you ask me so many questions, I cannot give you an
answer-I have no more to say-I never said I would settle this for 2001
nor for any sum, I swear I never did I never went with Mr. Jennings to
the prison in my life.
Q. I thought you said you went? A. Yes, when he took us altogether
in the coach to Worship-street-I did not desire him to say any thing to
the prisoners on the subject of this prosecution, but he was the conductor of
it ; that is all I know.
MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. At any time, when you were at the police office,
did any body put a book into your hands, telling you to give true answers
and take down what you said ? A. Yes, sir, the first time, that wa
when the prisoners were taken: I had no authority to settle this, it was not
in my power; the property did not belong to me, it was my husband's.
