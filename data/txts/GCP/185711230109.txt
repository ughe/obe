WILLIAM SOUTH (Policeman). On Saturday night, 31st Oct., I was
called into Mr. Whitter's shop the prisoner was standing against the
window, looking in-he seemed confused when Mr. Whitter asked him
something-Mr. Whitter said, “I give him into your custody for robbing
me" I said, “We had better go in at the private door, as there are people
passing "-we did so, and Mr. Whitter said, “Search him now" I put my
hand in his waistcoat pocket, and he pulled several pieces of silver out of
his trowser's pocket, and this marked shilling and sixpence with them--he
said, “Pray forgive me, for the sake of my friends," or “Will you forgive
me, for the sake of my friends ?"-I took him to the station.
Cross-examined. Q. Was it not, “Do not locke me up for the sake of my
friends ?" A. I am sure he said, “Forgive " he did not give me an
answer immediately-he seemed confused, and as though he could not speak
for a minute. (MR. COLERIDGE stated that the little girl was too young to be
examined.)
