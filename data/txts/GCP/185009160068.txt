FARNCOMB, Mayor.
NEW COURT.-Friday, September 20th, 1850.
PRESENT-Mr. Ald. KELLY; Mr. Ald. HOOPER; Mr. Ald. WILLIAM
HUNTER ; and Mr. COMMON SERJEANT.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant, and the Fifth Jury.
1647. GEORGE WREN, WILLIAM BRACE, and HENRY DAVIS.
stealing 108 trusses of straw, value 41. 48.; the goods of Nancy Brown and
another, the mistress and master of Wren and Brace.
MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES STAFF. I am clerk to Mrs. Nancy Brown and Mr. Masin.
They are hay and straw salesmen, in the Old Bailey-Wren and Brace were
in their employ, as carmen-Messrs. Elliott, the brewers, deal with us for strax
-on 27th Aug. I sent three loads of straw to their brewery-I sent Wren
and Brace with it in two carts, about four o'clock-I saw them leave Smith
field with it I directed tbem to deliver it at Elliott's and Co., at Pimlico-
it would take them about an hour to go-I saw Wren and Brace again the
same evening about eight-Brace produced to me the receipt for the two loads
of straw--they were worth 288. a load, four guineas altogether--this is the
receipt-(read-27th August, 850, received three loads of straw. 7. Johnson.)
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. How long have Brace and Wren bech
in your service ? A. I believe Brace about twenty years, and Wren about
twelve; they have always been carmen-as far as appearances go, this stamp
is in the ordinary course of business-this stamp is the stamp of the brewery,
JAMES TITCOMBE. I live in Buckingham-cottages, Westminster, and an
horse-keeper at Messrs. Elliott's. It is not my business to see what hay and
staw arrives, but I am generally there when it arrives-on 27th Aug. Ia
there from half-past four till six o'clock-no straw arrived in that time, and
not in the day at all.
Cross-examined by Mr. PARRY. Q. It is not alone your duty to do this!
A. No; I am horse-keeper-I look after the horses and clean them, and sach
as that I was from half-past four till six o'clock within twenty yards of where
this straw would bave gone, if it had come to go in the loft- I was not stand-
ing still, I was backwards and forwards from one stable to another-I had a
horse to clean, he is sometimes a teaser-I had not above one pint of beer
that afternoon-Mr. Thomas allows us a pint a piece at four o'clock-one of
us fetches it I did not fetch it that afternoon-I had my share, about two
half-pints.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You are subject to the gout, are you
not? A. I have not had it these two years--I am not in the habit of drink
ing-our premises are large-there are but two places where they put straw
but it is very seldom put in one of them, I do not believe there has been
straw there these six months when I was in one stable I could see what
was done in the other I was looking at the horse, but sometimes I turti my
head another way.
