 1342. MATTHEW ANDERSON was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Henry Wade Smith , on the   16th of April , at   Greenwich , and stealing therein 1 glazier's diamond, value 30 s  . the goods of Robert Hyde Aish . ROBERT HYDE AISH. I am a glazier, and live at Mr. Smith's, at Royal-hill, Greenwich, I work for him. I left off work at six o'clock on Satur-day evening, and put the diamond into a little drawer in the shop—I went away, and returned on Monday morning, and it was gone—it is worth 30 s .—I do not know the prisoner—he had no right to be on the premises. CHARLES WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker. This diamond was brought to me by the prisoner on the 18th of April, and I stopped it, the prose cutor having been to me in the course of the day—I sent for him, but he was not at home—I told the prisoner it was stolen—he said he found it—I said he had better come again.
   Prisoner . I never was at your home. Witness. Yes, I kept you at the shop till the evening—the prosecutor did not come home—I let you go, and told you to come in the morning. WILLIAM COLLINGS. I received information, and got a description of the man that offered the diamond go pledge—I went in pursuit, and found the prisoner with another man, named Barrett—I said to the prisoner, "Who gave you the diamond you offered to pledge at Mr. William's?"—he said, "Barrett"—I said, "Where did he get it?"—he said, "Of Heath"—I said, "How came Heath by it?"—he said he gave a wild rabbit for it—I then asked Barrett—he said all that he knew about it was, that he had seen it in the hands of Anderson. WILLIAM WOODGATE. I am in Mr. Wade's service. I was going up Royal-bill, on the 16th of April, and saw Henry Heath, nearly opposite my master's premises about eight o'clock in the evening—I did not see the prisoner there.
   Prisoner's Defence . I got it from Barrett; and Heath was convicted last month for stealing a horse-cloth—he brought this diamond and rabbit into the park—Turner and Barrett went to Heath, and asked him to buy a wild rabbit—he said he had no money—they asked what he had got—and he gave me 2 d . and a wild rabbit for the diamond. NOT GUILTY.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant .
 1343. SARAH SMAILL was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the   28th of April , of an evil-disposed person, 2 candlesticks, value 8 s  .; 2 basins, value 11 s  .; 2 ewers, value 1 s  .; 1 salt-collar, value 1 s  .; 6 cups value 10 d  .; 6 saucers, value 10 d  .; 5 plates, value 2 s  .; 2 tea-pots, value 4 milk-pots, value 6 d  .; 5 mugs, value 1 s  . 9 d  .; 1 box, value 1 s  . 2 d  .; and 1 fountain, value 1 s  .; the goods of Joshua Freeman Strang . JOSHUA FREEMAN STRANG. I am a china and glass-dealer, living at the corner of Richmond-street, Woolwich. These five tea-cups and other articles, produced by Butterfill, are all mine—I missed them on thee morn ing of the 28th, when we were about to fill up the vacancies for goods sold on the preceding days—they are all mine—but this little mug I could not swear to.
