 1008.  WILLIAM PECK and JOHN WILLIAMS were indicted for stealing, on the 3d of April , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of William Johnston , from his person.
 WILLIAM JOHNSTON. On the 3d of April, soon after seven o'clock in the evening, I was in  Fleet-street, nearly opposite Chancery-lane- I was coming towards the City, and walking quick; a person accosted me, and said my pocket had just been picked, by a person who had run across the road - I pursued up Chancery-lane, and met Peck in custody of two men - I said, "Is this the person who picked my pocket?" "Yes (said one of the men), and I have got your handkerchief;" I came down with them to the watch-house, but it was not open - I gave my address and left them, as I was on business - this is my handkerchief.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Do you know when you had your handkerchief in your pocket? A. Yes, in passing by Messrs. Twinings' there was a crowd, and a quarrel with some coachmen - I stepped into a druggist's shop, and took out my handkerchief there - I then came through Temple-bar, and was nearly opposite Chancery-lane, when my attention was drawn to the circumstance.
   WILLIAM WHITTINGHAM. I am an officer. I saw Mr. Johnston in Fleet-street - Williams took the handkerchief from his pocket - the street is narrow, and I was just opposite to them - Williams gave it to Peck the moment he took it, and Peck ran up Chancery-lane - I had followed them out of the Strand - I ran over and seized Williams - he was the farthest from me, and I thought he had not had time to give it away, but as I got close to them. I saw Peck receive it out of his hand, and run away - I told a young man to follow Peck, which he did.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. I understood you that the moment he took the handkerchief, he gave it away? A. Yes; when I crossed over it was handed instantly - my object was to take the man farthest from me - I seized Williams on the spot.
   THOMAS CUP. I was in a public-house in Chancery-lane, and heard the cry of Stop thief! I ran out, and saw Peck running - I followed him, and he was taken. I saw him drop this handkerchief - I took him to the watch-house - there was nobody there, and I took him to the Compter - he denied having had any thing to do with it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 PECK's Defence. I met my fellow prisoner in Lincoln's-inn-fields, he asked me to go with him to Fleet-market to purchase a hat - I said I would if I could be back by half-past 7 o'clock - I saw when I got to the clock that it was 7 then, and I told him I must leave him - I went up Chancery-lane, and I immediately found myself in custody.
 WILLIAMS' Defence. I met my fellow prisoner, and as soon as he got to the clock, he bid me good bye and left me - an officer then took hold of me.
 PECK - GUILTY Aged 18.
 WILLIAMS - GUILTY. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Six Months.
 Before Mr. Recorder.

 1010.  EDMUND WRIGHT was indicted for embezzlement.
 SECOND COUNT, for stealing four promissory notes for payment of 5l. each, value 20l., the property of  John Winskill.
   MARGARET WINSKILL. I am the wife of John Winskill, who keeps the White Hart public-house, Giltspur-street- the prisoner was in his service nearly three months, and was intrusted to take money. On Saturday, the 10th of February, I sent him with four 5l. Huntingdon notes, to go to Esdaile's to change them, and go to the Bank and get silver for them - he went about ten o'clock, and I directed him to be back by twelve o'clock - he said he should have plenty of time to return, but he never did return. My husband brought him back on the Saturday fortnight, and he was taken to the Compter - he never returned any thing that he got for these four notes.
 JOHN WINSKILL. I was not at home when my wife delivered these notes to the prisoner; but I endeavoured to find out where he came from, and I found, by a letter, that his father lived at Harlstonham - I went there, and found him at Yarmouth - he said he was very sorry for what he had done; I found on him three sovereigns, and 14s. in the bag which his mistress gave him for the money - he had purchased a great coat for 3l. 10s., a hat, and a cloak, and taken a cloak out of pawn, and paid his expenses on the road; he said the money I found on him was all he had left - I had a character with him from Mr. Williams, of Aldersgate-street.
   GEORGE HASLEWOOD WORRALL. I am an officer of St. Sepulchre's. I received the prisoner into custody at Mr. Winskill's house. I have the watch and great coat - he told Mr. Winskill this money was all he had left.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.
