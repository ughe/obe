 1254.  EMANUEL RUSSELL was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , one waistcoat, value 3 s., and one pair of stockings, value 1 s., the goods of Thomas Clarke.
   ELIZA WOODS. The prisoner lodged with me seven or eight months. On the 29th of August, about twelve o'clock he came in and said he was rather the worse for liquor, and must go up stairs and lay down. He came down in about an hour, and went out with a bundle. I went up and missed these things from the cupboard, ran out but could not overtake him. I have never found the things.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1255.  ELIZA PURDIE was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of August , two frocks, value 2 s.; three napkins, value 1 s., and one bed-gown, value 9 d., the goods of Isaac Dowling.
   ANN DOWLING. I am wife of Isaac Dowling, we live on Bowler's-green. These things were stolen while I laid in. The prisoner nurse d me. I had the linen from St. Andrew's Society, and during the month five napkins and a small frock were stolen, and many things of my own; she was a fortnight with me - I charged her with taking them; she said, she had carried them to the mangle - I mentioned my mangler's name, she said, it was not there, it was another person who she knew, and she would fetch them, she left, and I saw no more of her. A neighbour found them at the pawnbroker's; she sent me word that she would see me d - d before she would send me the duplicates.
 Cross-examined by MR. HONE. Q. You do not know where she was - A. No. I have got the things to make good. Every thing was marked, I know she can read, and she had the same linen when she laid in herself.
   ANN CROSSMAN. The prosecutrix is a neighbour of mine, she sent to me on missing the property; I met the prisoner by my door, she said, Mrs. Dowling had lost three napkins, that they were taken out of the yard, and asked me to go to Nicholls's with her, to ask if they had been pledged, I went, she said to Nicholls "Has any linen been pawned her belonging to St. Andrew's", he said, No. I went with her to Crees, another pawnbroker, and enquired there, and she went back to Dowling. I then went alone to Nicholls's; he said if any were pawned there, the prisoner was the woman who pawned them. I went to Armstrong's, and found some things pawned in the name of Glass, which I redeemed - they were wrapped in one of my aprons. A little girl fetched me into Leather-lane, where I saw the prisoner with her child, and asked why she took the things, she said she would make them good. I said "Why did you take them, you knew she was distressed, and has six children; give me the tickets and I will take them out." She said she would see her d - d first.
   JOHN LIMBRIC. I was sent for and took her, and found the duplicates on her of things pawned at Mr. Nicholls's and some at Armstrong's, which he gave up, also a bed-gown and frock.
   SOMERVILL TELFER. I am shopman to Mr. Nicholls, of Gray's Inn-lane. I have a frock, pin-cloth, two napkins, and a child's blanket. I was present when the prisoner pawned the pin-cloth and napkins in the name of Ann Glass. The duplicates found upon her are ours.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. She knew I pawned them.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Six Months.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1256.  WILLIAM M'COY was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , one pair of trowsers, value 9 s., the goods of Joseph Harrison , privately in his shop.
   EDWARD HULL. I am servant to Joseph Harrison, a pawnbroker, who lives in Tottenham-court-road. On the 10th of September, between eight and ten o'clock, these trowsers were stolen off the counter.
   CHARLES PERRY. On the 10th of September, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came to my master's shop, (Mr. Hulm, of Museum-street,) and offered to pawn these trowsers for 7 s., my master said they were stolen; he said it was no such thing, that they were his father's who lived in Dudley-court - he persisted in saying so; we said we would send for the beadle, he said we might do so in welcome; he wanted to go with the boy for the beadle, but we kept him, he then said he lived in Lassells-court, he tried to get out, I held him, he beat and kicked me - an officer came and took him.
   JOHN RICE. I took him in charge; he told me at the watch-house, that he stole them from Glass-house-yard, we went there, they missed nothing.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. A woman came up to me in Church-lane, and asked me to pawn them, and offered me sixpence. I said, "If they ask any questions, I will say they are my father's."
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Life.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
