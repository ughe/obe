   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE Q.  Perhaps he could not pay hit bills? A.  He always paid very well—there was no waiter but me—there was a female servant—she has served Mr. Shackleford with half-a-pint of wine, when he has been up stairs with his lady, and I perhaps was out of the way at the time, cleaning the pots—I do not know her name—she did not wait on company, only when I was busy—I have had nothing given me for coming here—I should not have come if I had not been made to come—I am now engaged at the Great Western Railway, looking after some horses for Mr. Saunderson—I lived with my father at Buckingham five years ago, and helped him in the stables—I then went to live with an aunt at Baltown—I have lived with Mr. Chantry, at Colchester—I misbehaved myself, and he dismissed me—I then went to Mr. Saunder son, who sent me to the Bull and Mouth tap—I was there six months—I cannot say when these gentlemen first began coming backwards and for wards—many strangers used to come—I cannot tell the first or last day I saw either of the prisoners—they came frequently before I, left—I can not say how often—I took no more notice of them than of other cus tomers—I noticed the parcels—other persons took notice of these things as well as I did—a mail guard did, and Mr. Smith pulled off his coat to fight him—the guard came and told my mistress that she ought not to encourage them there, they were a pack of swindlers, and nothing else—Mrs. Saunderson was my mistress—she is not here—she did not attend on the gentlemen in the parlour, that was my business—it was at the bar that she forbade Mr. Shackleford the house—they never went into any room but the coffee-room—Mrs. Saunderson might take a thing in now and then—perhaps I have been in seven or eight times, in the course of the day, answering the bell—Mr. Shackleford never used to go and sit in the bar—mistress did not like him well enough—she did not approve of him, nor of any of them, so she said—I never saw any customers sit down in the bar—there is a bar maid—she may have gone into the coffee-room if I was out of the way—I heard my mistress tell Mr. Shackleford that she did not wish him to come there "at all, and she has said to me that she wished they would go somewhere else, and
