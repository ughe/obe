   BENJAMIN TIMBRELL. I received information from Sarah Grange, that she had some cloth belonging to the prosecutor - I went and got it, and then went to the prosecutor; I found this table-cloth, this piece of linen, and this handle of a knife in the prisoner's pocket.
   SARAH GRANGE. I go out charing, and go by the prosecutor's regular every morning to fetch spring water; the prisoner called me and asked me if I would go on an errand for her; I said yes - she asked me to pawn this cloth; I took it to Mr. Williams', where I had pawned a gown, and he would not take it because it was not made up - the prisoner said I must take it. as she had no where to put it: I heard in the evening that it was wrong, and told all about the matter.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I am a serjeant of Police. I received some information on Sunday fortnight; I went to a woman named Britton, and found these articles, which I produce; I said I should take Britton - she said she could state who she had them of, and named the prisoner; I went to the prisoner the next morning and asked her - she said she never took any thing but what she had seen yesterday; I showed her some duplicates for pieces of cloth, two pledged in one day, and told her I found the duplicates at Britton's- she then said, "I did take them through distress;" I asked her about a ring, which she said she had taken, and I should find the duplicate.
   ELIZA BRITTON. I received a piece of cloth from the prisoner, to make a child a frock, but it was not enough, and my husband pawned it for her.
   THOMAS COX. I have a piece of cloth and a waistcoat-piece, pawned by a man in the name of John Britton.
   EBENEZER HEMBRY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a remnant of cloth, pawned by a man in the name of John Britton.
   JAMES WALLIS. I have a remnant of cloth and a ring pawn by Grange.
   SARAH GRANGE. The prisoner gave them to me to pawn, and I gave her the money.
 DANIEL RIERDON. This is all that has been found, a great deal is lost.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Transported for Seven Years.
 199.  ROBERT COLLINS was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 29th of November , 1 cheese, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Zephaniah Simpson , well knowing it to be stolen; against the Statute.
   LAWRENCE MAYER. I am a coal-whipper. On the 29th of November, I saw the prisoner and another boy opposite Mr. Simpson's window; the other took the cheese, and the prisoner was close to him - I ran and took the prisoner; he had the cheese then, and let it fall.
     NOT GUILTY.
 200.  WILLIAM COLLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of November, 1 bundle of bristles, value 6s., the goods of William Poke.
   PETER HUNTER. I live in Fisher-street, Red Lion-square, opposite Mr. Poke's shop; he is a shoemaker's grinder- I went into his shop on the 18th of November, and saw the prisoner there with his face towards the window; he turned to go out - I said, "Young man, what have you got?" he made no answer - I attempted to follow him, but could not go fast enough; I called Stop thief! and he was stopped.
   TIMOTHY BRITTON. I am a Police-constable, I heard the alarm, and saw the prisoner run, a postman stopped him; I went up and took these bristles from his hand - he struggled very much.
   ISABELLA POKE. I am the wife of William Poke - these are his property; I did not know the prisoner, but I saw him in the shop.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had seen a person come out of the prosecutor's shop and drop the bristles which be picked up. He received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 18 - Confined One Month.

 202.  JAMES ASPEY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 1 shawl, value 6s., the goods of Caroline Brooks , from her person
   CAROLINE BROOKS. I am single, and live in St. Andrew's-terrace, Waterloo-road, I am a dress-maker. On the 9th of November, I was in Arundel-street, between five and six o'clock in the evening. I had a shawl on, and all on a sudden the prisoner suatched it off my shoulder; I turned, and cried Stop thief! I am sure it was him - I turned immediately, and he ran down the street - there was no person but him who could have taken it; he had no companion as far as I could judge - I ran after him and never lost sight of him, till he turned Water-street; I then turned, and he was in the hands of two officers - one of them saw him drop the shawl - I had not lost sight of him for a moment; I am sure he is the man.
   SAMUEL PRICE. I belong to the Thames Police. I heard the alarm, and turned into Water-street; I found the prisoner in custody of a brother officer - I went up, and the prisoner dropped this shawl from his person, I am sure he is the man.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me drop it? A. I did, I was not a yard from you; that was about three hundred yards from where the prosecutrix lost it - she had not come up then.
   JAMES LILL. I heard the alarm, saw the prisoner run, and I took him; he said, "My tar, if you will make it all right for me, I will give you two sovereigns;" I had him by the collar, and he had his hand behind him, but I did not see him drop the shawl - I found a pair of scissors on him, and a watch-key.
 Prisoner. Q. What did I say to you? A. You said you had lately come from sea, and had 4l. 15s. in your captain's hands, and you would give me two sovereigns; you said you were running after the man that stole the shawl, but there was no one near you; there was no one in the street but you - there had been some rioting just before, and the people followed them.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was with the mate of a vessel, and he knew I was not the man who took it; I am a native of New South Wales, and was never in London before.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Life.
