I searched the prisoner and found a marked shilling in his trowsers pocket—I showed it to James Covill, and he said it was a shilling which he had marked—I also received another marked shilling from Hoath, and she marked it in my presence—these are them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who sent for you? A.  One of Mr. Covill's sons, but not James—he gave the prisoner in charge—he said he supposed he had been robbing the desk of the marked money. JAMES COVILL re-examined.  A marked shilling was also found on Munns—these are two of the shillings I marked—the money I paid him on the Sunday I got from a different desk. GEORGE MUNNS. I had a marked shilling among my other money—I do not know how I got it—I had received a shilling from the prisoner in the course of Thursday—I won 1 s . of him—there was no quarrel between my cousin and the prisoner in my presence.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you been playing with your cousin as well as the prisoner? A.  Not that day—I did the day before—I had been in town about a week—I do not know bow many times we played at this game—we had nothing else to do to amuse ourselves—I had not won any money of my cousin, nor he of me—I cannot say whether he lost any thing to the prisoner—we took turns in putting the money on the sticks—I think my cousin put sixpence on—I played at skittles once at the Druid's Head with my cousin—I do not remember who won there—I was at one other place with the prisoner—I never had more than 1 s . from the prisoner—I never had any money from my cousin on any occasion—I do not re member whether I knocked any money off which he put on the stick. NOT GUILTY.

ELIZABETH EDDS was indicted for stealing, on the   19th of April , 1 shawl, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 3 shillings, 4 pence, and 16 halfpence; the goods and monies of Mary Mahoney ; and that she had been before con victed of felony. MARY MAHONEY. I am a widow, and live in Harding-lane, Woolwich. On the 9th of April I went up stairs to put my child to bed, and left my shawl and this money down in my room—the prisoner lives next door to me—I came down in ten minutes, and my shawl and money were gone—it was between six and seven o'clock in the evening—this is my shawl, I am certain—( looking at it .)
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not lend me a shawl that morning? A.  No, I never had any thing to do with you—I had only lived there seven days. FRANCIS CHANDLER. I am shopman to Mr. Davis, a pawnbroker at Woolwich. On the evening of the 19th of April, Millar pawned this shawl with me. PHILIP MILLAR. I am a baker. I was at the Duchess of Wellington public-house—the prisoner called me outside, and asked if I would take the shawl and pawn it—I said yes, and did so—she gave it to me off her shoulders.
