EDWARD DOWNING. I am a surgeon of Deptford. Sergeant Croxon
brought Hearn to my house, between one and two o'clock on Monday
morning-| examined him-be was bleeding very profusely-he had
a wound on his left cheek, about five inches and a half in length, in a hall
circle, going from the ear to the mouth-one part of it at the middle was
to the bone-it was not generally deep-I applied pressure to stop the
hemorrhage-being early in the morning I was very much fatigued, having
been up the whole night before, and with great difficulty I stopped it-about
seven o'clock the same morning the prosecutor called me up again, and I
was then obliged to make use of ligatures, as it was bleeding afresh--be
was faint-I was obliged to give him a stimulant while I was dressing it-
it was certainly a dangerous wound, and if it had been a little lower,
I think it would have been fatak-it appeared to be inflicted by a sharpi
strument-the knife produced was likely to have done it-I consider
it dangerous, if it had not been attended to immediately-he is convalescent,
and there is not any fear now.
Prisoner's Defence. After I went home, he began to ill-use me, and

hands round my neck, trying to choke me-as I was trying to get up again
in the struggle, I cut him about the face with the knife, but did nut intend
to do so.
