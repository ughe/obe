THOMAS JONES. I am a waterman lighterman. On Friday, the 26th
of Oct., I was navigating a barge on the river I was going to stop it at
the Upper Tunnel-pier-I was going up with the tide-I saw a schooner
aground against the Lower Tunnel-pier, which I belong to that schooner
caused an eddy, about twice the vessel's length, round her-it was up and
down the river too-it caused a circular eddy-it ran right round, astern
of the vessel in particular-she laid athwart the tiver--the eddy extended
a good way out, about two barges' length from ber into the river my
barge got into the eddy, and it stopped me from going to the place where
I intended, the Upper Tunnel-pier--it turned ber round twice when I
found she was in the eddy I let her go her own road, and put my oar in,
finding it was no use-I began to feel the eddy soon after I came to the
acbooner, almost directly I came opposite her.

MR. BALLANTINE. Q. The eddy was merely the course of the tide

lying aground on the causeway, stops the tide flowing its original course,

to stop at the Upper Tunnel-pier-I could not get to the Tunnel pier
without going through the eddy.
