JOHN SPARROW was indicted for stealing, on the   20th of October , 1 ring, value 10 s  .; and 1 necklace, value 10 s  .; the goods of Thomas Irving ; and WILLIAM SHAW and RICHARD SHAW were indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen, against the Statute, &c. ELIZABETH IRVING. I am wife of Thomas Irving. I live at Lambeth—on the 14th of October, I missed, from the drawers in my bed-room, a ring and a coral necklace—there was an odd bead in the same paper with them—these are the ring and necklace—Sparrow lodged in the house with his parents.
   Cross-examined by MR. MAHON . Q.  Had you always the same im pression that you lost these things from your bed-room? A.  Yes; my husband wished me write to Bath respecting them, which I did—I had been at Bath seven weeks previous to my missing these things—I had them with me there, and to satisfy my husband, I wrote there, but it was not to satisfy myself—I had an answer directly, that I had not left them there—I know I put them in the drawer in my bed-room, on the 12th of October—I let the first and second floor of my house—Sparrow's father and mother lodged in the house, and he with them—my bed-room door was locked at night, but I left the key in it—my lodgers were out the day I missed these things—I have no servant—I have a char-woman—she has cleaned my bed-room, but did not at that time. CHARLES DAVIS. I was standing minding my mother's stall—Sparrow came up and showed me a ring on his finger—I asked him where he got it—he replied, "That is my business"—he showed me one coral bead rather larger than a grey pea—I asked him to give me that—he said, "O no, it is worth 3 d ."—Mucklow came up at the time—this ring is like the one he showed me—I believe it is it, but cannot swear to it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You know nothing about the necklace? A.  No; I have not seen rings like this in jewellers' windows—I cannot swear to it—I did not have it in my hand. PETER PALMER. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I produce this coral necklace, which was pawned by William shaw for 3 s . 6 d ., on the 23rd of October.
   Cross-examined. Q.  This is a mere common necklace? A.  No, it is a very good one—one bead of coral resembles another—our shop is in New Park-street, South wark—no one came to pawn a necklace that day, but William Shaw—I could not swear to every one who came; but I know him—he had pawned for his mother before. JOHN WALKER. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I have a ring pawned for 3 s ., but I cannot recollect by whom—this is the duplicate I gave. EDWARD LANGLEY( police-constable L  148.)
