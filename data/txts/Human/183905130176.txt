   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you mention that before the Magistrate? A.  I did—my deposition was read over to me and I signed it.
   MR. BALLANTINE called WILLIAM FORD. I am a hackney-coach driver. On the day in ques tion I drove a glass-coach for Mr.Ward, my master—Clarke merely had a ride with me down to Epsom—he is a servant of Mr. Ward's—coming back, one was a job-horse—when we got to Tooting we gave two gentle men a ride, but the horse gibbed,  and we stopped at the Castle at Tooting, and the gentlemen got out—the party we took down to Epsom had come back in another conveyance—I saw the prosecutrix about a hundred yards from the Castle at Tooting—we were on the off-side of the road, trying to get the horse on—she came up and said, "If you will let get on the box, I will get them along"—she said, "Will you give me a ride to town? I have been to Epsom and have lost all my money, and lost my friends"—I said, "I can't give you a ride to town, I can't give ourselves one I am going to walk"—I meant I could not get the horses along—I had two children with me, one my master's and one belonging to a person named Bonner—one of the ladies who went down with me was to ride back in a gig, and I was to take the two children back—the prosecutrix at last got into the coach—Jackson was at that time standing about there, and he agreed to help us back—he was not in the coach—I said, "will take the two children and walk, we cannot get them on"—there were two policemen there, also a man who said he was a knacker, and a man in a blue jacket; who
