JOHN LLOYD. I am a policeman. I was called to the house, went up stairs for the prisoner, and told her the prosecutor suspected she had robbed him—she produced the duplicate—I asked if she objected to my searching her boxes—she said, "No," and gave me two keys—I found nothing in her boxes—I took her to the station—she was searched there, and this pair of gloves found on her—she said she picked them up in the room.
   Cross-examined. Q.  She told you, did not she, that she pawned the things intending to get them again? A.  Yes. GUILTY  . Aged 38.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Nine Months.

   MR. CHARNOCK conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS FULLER. I am in the service of Mr. Searl, of Stangate-street, Lambeth. The members of the Oxford University Aquatic Club have a room on our premises—it is my duty to superintend the room which the gentlemen use to dress in, when they go out in the boats—they keep their clothing and things in lockers all round the room—on Monday, the 4th of April, at half-past nine o'clock, I locked the place up all safe—I returned on Tuesday morning about ten minutes to six, and found the gate open, also a small counting-house, where other gentlemen dress, and saw some of the clothes lying about, some of which belonged to the Club, and had been brought down to that room—I went up to the Oxford room, and found the door wide open—it had been opened with the key which I always leave there, in case the gentlemen should come while I am away—it is twelve o'clock sometimes before they come home—on the morning of the 4th I missed several articles belonging to the gentlemen—I saw some things afterwards produced by the policeman. CHARLOTTE AUSTIN. I am the wife of Charles Austin, of Whiting-street, Lambeth. I know the prisoner Warren—she came to lodge with me on the 3rd of March, and left on the 5th of April—on the morning of the 5th of April, between ten and eleven o'clock, I went into her room to insist on her leaving it, as she was so noisy, and had so many fol lowers, and I said I would not leave the room till she was gone—she left the room, and wished me to wait a few minutes till she returned—the prisoner Logden was in the room at the time—she did not lodge with her to my knowledge—Warren left the room to return in ten minutes, as she said, if I would wait, and Logden was to wait till she returned—she did not say what she was to return for, but said she would come back in ten minutes—finding she did not come back, I suspected something! and turned down the bed to see if the bed clothes were all right, and found in the bed twelve gentlemen's coats or jackets, one pair of trowsers, and eleven gentlemen's rowing shirts, tied up in a bundle—Logden was there—there was also a blue silk handkerchief, and a gentleman's cap, and in the drawer I found two drinking horns—there was a pair, of mittens also
