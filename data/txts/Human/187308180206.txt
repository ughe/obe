 (  Re-examined ) When travellers come round they give the names and addresses of their principals. EDWIN WALKER. I am manager to Messrs. Field, at the Star Night Light Works—I have known Witham in the neighbourhood of those works for some years—after the apprehension of Ralph and Mrs. Castle, I met Witham in Union Street—he said "What do you want with me?"—I said I thought he could give some information relative to the robbery of the wax from Mr. Field's—he said "Yes, I can, I know all about it; I bought three-quarters of a cwt. some odd pounds in Castle's shop myself'—I asked him if he would come and tell Mr. Field—he said "No, I do not want to see Mr. Field, but I shall be at the Court next hearing, and I will tell the Magistrate the same as I have told you"—next evening I met him at the same place, and I then told him that I found he had offered wax for sale—he at first said "I—No"—I said "It is no use for you to deny it, because I can tell you to whom you offered it"—he asked me who—I said to Mr. Hall's assistant, and also to Mr. Steane"—he said "Well, yes, I did," and he said he would tell that to the Magistrate—I again asked him if he would go to Mr. Field—he said No, ho would be at the Court next day, and he would tell the Magistrate the same as he told mo—I saw nothing more of him till Whit-Tuesday, when he came to me at the works in Paradise Street—he asked if I wanted to see him—I said "No, but if you have anything to say to me, I am quite ready to hear it"—he asked if I was not going to treat him—I said "Yes, you may have what you like"—we went together to the Bell public-house in Church Street, and he had half a quartern of gin—I left the house for a few minutes, and when I came back he had gone to the Crown—I called him out; he was not perfectly sober; and in a rambling manner he said he would tell Mr. Field that the man whom he discharged knew nothing about the robbery; that the right man was locked up, and two other men were at liberty—I then took him to Mr. Field, and after a conversion with him, I followed him out—he stopped and told me he could not say anything unless I locked him up; situated as he was he could not say a word out there, because if he did, he should never get another day's work among this class of people.
   Cross-examined by MR. LYON . I mentioned that at the Police Court—a lad named William Maunder, a nephew of mine, first brought him to me—I sent him for him—when I first saw him in Union Street he went into the Dolphin public-house with one of the Castles—I did not give him any money on that occasion—I lent him a shilling the night I took him to Mr. Field—he said he had no money, and asked if I would give him a shilling—I said "No"—he said "Lend me a shilling"—I said "I will lend you one if you like, but I shall never get it again, I suppose"—that was understood I think. EDWARD DAVIS. I am warehouseman to Mr. Hall, an oilman in Lambeth Road—I know Witham—about twelve months ago he came and offered me some wax for sale—he said he had about three-quarters of a cwt.—he left me a sample—I showed it to my employer, but none was bought—it was yellow wax—I have since seen the sample that he brought me.
