CHARLES JAMES ABBOTT. I am a solicitor, of 8, New Street, Strand-
I produce my appointment as Under-Sheriff of the county of Surrey-I
am authorized to hear writs of inquiry, to assess damages, and perform
the office of a sheriff-I was so acting on the 27th Ootober, at the City
Terminus Hotel, and I produce the notes taken by me at the inquiry-
I have not the writ-it belongs to the plaintiff, and has been returned
to his solicitor-the prisoner was the plaintiff in an action against the
London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway Company, and was sworn by
me-he had a "good" jury on the occasion-(The plaintif", evidence was
read; he swore that he had been to the Crystal Palace on the day of the
Licensed Victrallers' Fee, in June last, and was injured in an accident
which happened to the train coming home; that a collision occured near
New Cross, in which he was thrown from one seat of the carriage to the
other; that his head came into collision with something; that he then found
himself on the rails, and felt very sick, and had a pain in his inside ; a
servant of the company asked him if he was hurt; he stayed at New Cross
for about half an hour, that he lost a light over-coat worth 258.; that he
rode home from London Bridge in a cab; that the next day he went in
a cab, and saw Dr. Simpson, who examined him, and told him to get a
trun, as he was ruptured; that he was' afterwards examined by Dr. Heath,
on the part of the Company; that he had pains in his head, and that
his knee and ankle were painful for upwards of eleven weeks; that he hurt
his ankle in getting out of the train, that at the time of the accident he
was earning from 21. 108. to 31. a week, as a travelling draper; that
for eighteen weeks he had not earned more than 21. altogether; that he lodged
in Winchester Street, Pentonville, and that his wife was in business in
Trinity Street as a mantra-maker; that he never wed a truss before the
accident, and could not stand upright afterwards).
Cross-examined. l. Were the notes taken in shorthand ? A. No-I
took notes of the evidence of Dr. Simpson, as also of Mr. John Gay, sur-
geon to the Great Northern Hospital - I had not the slightest doubt at
the time as regarded the plaintif being injured-he gave his evidence
in a straightforward manner.
FREDERICK JULIAN PAGE. I am a solicitor, and act as clerk to Messrs.
Baxter, Rose, & Norton, who are solicitors to the Company-I produce
some of the papers in reference to the action of Dickeson and the Railway Com-
pany-we first of all received a letter from the secretary of the Company, enclos-
ing one from the prisoner, which he had forwarded after the accident-then
we had a copy writ, and the usual course was taken with regard to the
particulars of claim, including the medical expenses, and such like-the
plaintiff was stated to have received injury to his system, with bruised
ankle, inflammatory symptoms, and hernia-notice of trial was given for
the assizes at Croydon, on the 2nd August—but the Company with-
drew their plea of not guilty, and afterwards a writ of inquiry was
issued at the instance of prisoner's solicitor- I was present at the inquiry, and
heard the prisoner say that he had been confined to his bed for eleven weeks
through the accident-he said that he was invited to the Crystal Palace
on the day in question
