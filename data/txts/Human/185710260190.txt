   Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY . Q.  Where was your room; was it over the room in which the prisoner and his wife lived? A.  No; on the same floor—my door was not opposite the prisoner's; it was on the same floor—I could easily hear what passed in the prisoner's room, if the door was not fastened—I could not hear a word without speaking very loud—amongst other things, I heard the prisoner complain of the children being left, neglected and crying—the children were about four or five months old—he asked her what money she had in her possession—she told him, I think, 12 s . or 13 s .—I am sure she said as much as that—she did not show it—I have known the prisoner from a child—I have known him and his wife living together about twelve years—as long as I have known him, his conduct towards her has been kind and affectionate—the only blow I saw was a slap on the side of the face. ELIZA HUDSON. I am the wife of Bryan Jessie Hudson, a hatter. I live in High Street, Vauxhall, next door to Welch, the prisoner. On 20th Sept., about half past 10 o'clock in the morning, I was sent for by Welch—I went in, and saw Mr. and Mrs. Welch—she was in bed, and he was applying leeches to her temples—she appeared insensible—her face was much swollen on the left side, and discoloured—she was rather restless, and moved herself about, but I do not think she was conscious—I did not remain—I left imme diately, without saying anything—at my husband's desire, I went in again in the evening, in consequence of what I heard—she was still in bed, and he was sitting down by her side—I then applied a blister to the back of her neck—he requested me to do so—she was still insensible, but rather restless—the prisoner said, "Mrs. Hudson, this is a bad job; she went out drink ing, and I was very much exasperated, and when she returned home, I gave her an open handed blow, and she fell; and that was the cause of this"—I
