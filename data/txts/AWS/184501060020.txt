Prisoner's Defence. I did not steal the meat, neither did it come from
Mr. Oak's shop I bought it at Newgate-market, and was coming home,
when the policeman collared me and asked what I had there I said
Meat;' he held me by the collar very tight, and I might make a little dis-
turbance, but nothing to signify he said, You had better walk with
me;" I said I would do so, and I did I never tried to get away I do
not know Mr. Oak's shop, nor the gateway, neither have I ever seen Mr.
Oak before he came here.
Mr. OAK re-examined. All this was gone into before the Magistrate-
the prisoner has had plenty of time to bring any one from Newgate-market.
Prisoner. When a butcher goes to Newgate-market to buy anything,
he does not take particular notice of the name of the person be buys of;
I have been out of employ some weeks ; I do not take notice of the sales-
man I buy of; I pay my money.

OLD URT-Wednesday, January 8th, 1845.
Third Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esg.
310. ANN BOCKMASTER was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of
Dec., at St. Join, Wapping, 1 searf, value 21. 2s. 1 gown, 2l. 2s. 1
habit-shirt, 3s. 6d.; 1 cape, 4s.; 1 petticoat, 2s. 1 pair of stockings,
1s. 1 pair of stays, 1s. 2 pairs of gloves, 2s. 1 shawl, 1s. and 1
handkerchief, 1s. 6d. the goods of Robert Garbutt, her master, in his
dwelling-house.
SARAH GARBUTT. I am the wife of Robert Garbutt-we live in the
parish of St. John, Wapping-he is the householder-the prisoner lived
servant with us for four months. On the 22nd of Dec. I went to cbapel
about a quarter to six o'clock in the evening-I returned about a quarter
past eight-I first missed my cap off my bed-I went up into the prisoner's
room, and seeing her clothes I thought there was something amiss-she
was not there-I then went to my room again, and missed from my drawers,
which were open, the articles stated-these now produced are what I
missed, and are my property-they had been in separate drawers in the
same room, except a half-sovereigo, which was taken from my son's room
-that is not included in the indictment-my drawers were not kept locked
-I gave notice to the police, and saw my things again at the police-office
-the value of the property is about 51.-I never gave the prisoner any
of the things, or gave her any authority to take them-I had a good cha-
racter with her.
CHARLES JEcks (police-constable T 20.) I apprehended the prisoner
on the 23rd of Dec. at Lambton, near Hounslow-I told her the charge-
she said she was sorry, and that the devil had tempted her-these clothes
were on her back-she was dressed in them.
