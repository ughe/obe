   MARIA WILSON. My husband's name was Robert, he is the drawer of this bill; and that in his signature - my husband died on the 25th of January, 1831.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. This is your husband's signature? A. Yes - I know nothing about the bill - I never saw or heard of it before - the indorsement is my husband's also- I knew Mr. Richmond when my husband was living - when he lived in Gutter-lane - between four and five years ago - I did not know him myself long before he left - my husband knew him - my husband was brought up as a lawyer and practised as such in the country, but had not taken his certificate out in the city - he lived in Trinity-street, Borough - he practised in the north.
 COURT. Q. What did he do when he lived in Southwark? A. He was not doing anything - I cannot exactly tell how long it was since he practised - he was not doing anything in 1829 - he was not living in the rules of the King's Bench - he was living in Trinity-street, Trinity-square, over London-bridge, nearly opposite Union-hall, leading out of High-street - he got his living with the assistance of my friends.
   JAMES PALMER. I am a sadler, living at Star-corner, Bermondsey. I knew Robert Wilson - I have seen him write - I believe this signature to be his hand writing - I believe this indorsement to be his hand writing - I have frequently seen him write - he used to write for me frequently.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long ago? A.  MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you receive any letters from him? A. No - he mostly made out my bills - he wrote not only figures but words - I paid him for it - he assisted as a kind of clerk.

 Before Mr. Baron Gurney.
 1402.  JOHN TURNER was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying Elizabeth Greswood.
   THOMAS GRESWOOD. I am the father of Elizabeth Greswood, and live at No. 7, White Lion-street, Seven-dials - I saw her last, about a week before this happened - she died in Middlesex Hospital - she was six years old last June.
   JAMES WHITE. I sell fruit in the street. On the 12th of August, I was in High-street, Bloomsbury, selling my fruit within a short distance of the church - about forty yards from where the accident happened - I saw two omnibuses both coming down towards Holborn, driving at full gallop - they appeared to be racing - I saw the deceased going across the road - she got clear of the omnibus next her - she was going from the near side to the off side - she passed the first omnibus, and was run down by the other, and both wheels ran over her; both omnibuses were pretty near abreast at the time, there was very little difference between them - I don't know the driver - it was Bardell's omnibus - the other was Shillibeer's.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was there anybody with the  child? A. Another child was playing with her, before she started to cross - it was not a regular crossing place - the child ran across - the first omnibus was very near her when she attempted to cross - I think the man pulled up as soon as he possibly could - the child was a good deal more than a yard or two from the omnibus when she attempted to cross, but they came at such a rate the child could not cross - she was able to run very well - it is a narrow part of the street - the street was not dirty; I think Shillibeer's omnibus went a good distance before it pulled up - the man by whom the accident happened pulled up as soon as he possibly could.
 COURT. Q. Did he pull up before the child was run over? A. No - both wheels went over the child.
   JOHN CONROY. I am inspector of nuisances for the parish of St. Giles's. On the 12th of August, I was near St. Giles's church about a quarter to two o'clock - I saw two omnibuses, but the driver of Bardell's omnibus was in the act of pulling up, when I saw it - Shillibeer's passed on thirty or thirty-five yards, and then stopped - I had not seen either of them before - I saw Bardell's in the act of pulling up - I saw the child taken in to a surgeon's - I took the numbers of the omnibuses - Bardell was No. 194, and drawn by two horses.
   OSBORNE BARKER. I am a farrier, and live in Hampshire-hog-yard. I was in St. Giles's, and saw both the omnibuses before the accident, driving at a moderate pace - I was standing outside of the Angel Inn - the omnibuses were going by - the child appeared to pass Shillibeer's, and the other omnibus came in contact with her - the near horse knocked her down, and the two wheels passed over her body - I could not see any blame that could be attached to the driver, it was so sudden - I instantly took the child up - I was examined before the Coroner - there was room sufficient for Bardell's omnibus to go by - when I saw Bardell's it was about passing the other - it was in the broad part of the street at that time, and there was sufficient room - the Angel joins the church - the omnibusses were beyond me a few yards
