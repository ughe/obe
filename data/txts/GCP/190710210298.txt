Office, Southend, Essex, in an envelope dated January 24, 1907. I
also received a letter addressed to me from 54, Shaftesbury Avenue,
dated February 17. I believe I received it the next day. I received
the telegram produced on Saturday night, September 7, and a letter

snob," on the Tuesday, I think. I received all those from the pri-
soner.
WILLIAM JOHN MANN. I am a solicitor of the firm of Mann and
Rodway, at Trowbridge, where I hold several positions of an official
character. The prisoner is my wife's nephew. My wife's Christian
name is Julia, but she is commonly called "Dolly" by her sisters.
Before the prisoner's arrest there was some correspondence between
the prisoner's mother and her sister with regard to the prisoner. The
prisoner was convicted on his own confession of libel on Septem-
ber 14, 1905. In my presence he then gave the undertaking to
Mr. Justice Bucknill which has been read. That, however, seems to
have been followed up, in December of 1906, by the false allegation
with regard to how it was that the prisoner's elder brother died of
starvation in Canada. I sent a copy of the telegram to the prisoner's
mother and made inquiries. In January, 1907, I learnt that this
course of persecution had commenced afresh. I received the letter
of March is from the postman and handed it to my wife; she read
it in my presence. The whole of it is in the prisoner's handwriting
and also the envelope. I was the recipient of a form of typewritten
notice circulated on March 11. That came to me, although it was
addressed to "Mr. M. Mann." The letters produced are in the pri-
soner's handwriting, also the postcard addressed to the proprietor
o! the "Wiltshire Times."
His Lordship intimated that he would consult with Mr. Justice
Bucknill on the subject.
Mr. Morris asked that the mental condition of the prisoner might
bo inquired into while he was under remand, to which his Lordship
assented.
(Tuesday, November 12.)
Dr. Scott, medical officer, Brixton Prison, said that prisoner was
suffering from chronic alcoholism, but could not say he was insane.
He was sentenced to Eighteen months in the second division.
