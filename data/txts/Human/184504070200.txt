GEORGE SATCHELL. I am a seaman on board the Union.  On the Thursday morning after this I met Harrison in Church-lane, and gave him in charge. JOHN BURKE( police-constable H  81.) Harrison was given into my charge—he offered at the station-house to show me who he took the watch to if I took him to Cooper's-row—I did so, and Cormack was then appre hended. JOHN CONDON( policeman. ) I went with Harrison to No. 2, Cooper's-alley, and saw Cormack sitting in the house—Harrison said, "That is the man I sold the watch to"—Cormack said he knew nothing of the watch or Harrison—the boy Norton was there, and said, "It is no use denying it; I saw him buy the watch at the straw yard; he gave 5 s . for it." JOSEPH NORTON. I have been sleeping in the straw-yard in Ratcliff-highway—I know Cormack—he slept in the same place—I saw Harrison and him together on a Wednesday evening, just by the fire-place, and heard Harrison say to Cormack that he had a watch to sell him—Cormack told him to keep it till the morning, and he would look at it—Harrison asked him 10 s . for it—Harrison slept in the straw-yard that night—next morning they came into the room where they sell coffee—I then saw the watch, and took it in my hand—it was silver, with a little steel chain, and a seal, marked, "One penny"—I saw Harrison sell it to Cormack for 5 s .—I saw the money pass—Cormack borrowed it from a man.
   Cormack. Q.  When did you see me with Harrison? A.  On Wednesday night, just after they all went in—you borrowed the money of Joe Collier, who belongs to the Marine Society, and you paid him the money out of a sovereign.
   S . PEARSON re-examined.  There was a steel chain to my watch, a key, and a seal with "One Penny" marked on the lower part of it.
   CORMACK  — GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Six Months .
