JOHN LANGTHORN. I am one of the engineers employed at the South-Western Railway terminus, at Nine Elms—the prisoner has heen in the Company's service, I think, nearly three years, as a carpenter—the Com pany had such brass tubing as this produced, for the purposes of the engine—the prisoner used to work close to where this was put—it is part of the tubing introduced into the boiler, for the steam to pass through, by which notice is given of any eruption.
   Cross-examined. Q.  This is a patent of one of the makers of the loco-motive? A.  Yes, all the locomotives have tubing, but not the same as ours—this is the last patent—I don't know whose patent it is—it may be used on other railways—I believe the prisoner has always borne a good character. CHARLES BUSH. I am a smith. I made two iron brackets similar to these produced, for the prisoner, to put in a coke-wagon on the railway—to the best of my knowledge it was in Oct.—I never made more than two for the Company, to my knowledge.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you been in the service? A.  Six years—the prisoner has been there three years, and always bore a good character—there are no marks about these brackets by which I can swear to them—I cannot swear they are my work—I do not know how many hundred coke-wagons there are on the railway—there are nine smiths in the same shop with myself—I never saw any brackets of this description made—I have made other kinds—I cannot swear these are what I made in Oct.—they are very much like them, but there are many smiths besides myself that make them.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Have you ever seen any two brackets of that form manufactured at the railway, except the two you made? A.  No, and they were to repair a coke-wagon with. HENRY BAKER. I am a brazier, employed by the South-Western Rail way Company, at Nine Elms. In Nov. I worked at 128 brass tubes to fit this gauge—the prisoner worked about twenty yards from me—I produce one of the tubes which I so prepared—the broken pieces produced, to the best of my knowledge, have been prepared by me to fit this gauge—( The witness here fitted the tubing to the gauge )—it fits this gauge—the mouths were made smaller to fit the fire-box of the engine—these three pieces at one time formed one entire piece, but the whole length is not here—it is altogether useless in this state, except as old brass—a whole one would cost about 15 s . or 16 s .
   Cross-examined. Q.  You do not swear to these pieces? A.  No—I have been a year and ten months on this railway—I formerly worked on the Northern and Eastern—I have been accustomed to make these sort of tubes about seven years—I never made any of this exact kind before—they have come up within the last two years and a half—I have seen them before I came on this railway, but not of this size—there are different diameters—they are used on other railways, but not of this gauge—I could not speak to it without the gauge—if tubing of the same gauge used on other railways was produced, I could not tell it from this.
