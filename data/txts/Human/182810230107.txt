ANN ADAMS. I am the wife of Philip Adams - he lives in Portland-town; I take in washing. On the 24th of September I had a large basket of linen in my yard; the shirts, table-cloths, and towels, stated in the indictment, were among them - there is one table-cloth and 1 shirt here now, which were in the basket; I cannot say when they were safe, but I heard an alarm about ten minutes before seven o'clock in the evening.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I suppose you are married? A. Yes, Sir, and no one has a right to doubt it: I am living with my husband - my name is not Long; I have never gone by that name, nor do I know a person of that name - I never was married to a person named Long.
   MARY WARDELL. I live in Portland-terrace. This table-cloth belongs to my daughter, Ann Rickett's; she is a widow, and sends her things to Mrs. Adams to wash.
   FREDERICK HICKS. I am a pawnbroker. I have a shirt, pawned with me on the 27th of September, by a woman - I do not know who, in the name of Harriet Jenkins.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I am an officer. In consequence of information, I went on the 13th of October, to No. 2, Little Church-street, where the prisoners were living as man and wife: I found this table-cloth in the room - Kimpton said there was nothing there, but what her mother had given her, and she had bought; the table-cloth was in the bed, but I did not take it then - I went again and took it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you looked at it the first time? A. Yes, and I thought it was a sheet; I returned in the evening and took it - it was not removed.
   PHILIP WEBSTER. I was with Wall; I received from Langley's hand this purse, containing eleven duplicates, and one of them is for this shirt, pawned at Smith's, in Edgware-road, on the 27th of September, in the name of Harriet Jenkins.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 KIMPTON'S Defence. I had the tickets given to me by  James Brown, who was going to Edgware, and I said I would take care of them.
 LANGLEY -    GUILTY. Aged 21.
 KIMPTON -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.

 2200.  JOSEPH GREEN& JAMES COX were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of September , 1 pair of steelyards, value 5s., the goods of Benjamin Dixon.
 BENJAMIN DIXON. I am a  farmer, and live at Ealing. On the 20th of September, I lost a pair of steelyards from my rick-yard - I had seen them the day before.
   JOHN KING. On the 29th of September, the two prisoners came into my shop in company, and asked me to buy these steelyards; they said they had had nothing to eat since the Saturday morning - I said I kept a shoe-shop, and these were articles I did not deal in, but they pleaded so hard that I gave them 1s. 6d., more out of charity; I hung them up behind my door, meaning to let them have them again for the 1s. 6d., as they said they would come: between eleven and twelve o'clock that night, after I was gone to bed, Mr. Farrant and Mr. Dixon's man came to my house.
   JOHN FARRANT. I am an officer, and took the prisoners in charge.
   JAMES COMPTON. The prisoner Green told me he sold the steelyards to King, as we were drinking together.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 GREEN -    GUILTY. Aged 25.
 COX -    GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Three Months.
 2201.  MARY ANN GOLDING was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of September , 1 shawl, value 10s., the goods of Abraham Smith.
   ANN SMITH. I am the wife of Abraham Smith - we live at Islington; the prisoner lodged in my house, till the 17th of October: I missed a shawl on that day, but I had not seen it for three or four weeks or more - I sent for an officer; she gave me the duplicate.
   THOMAS COPE. I am an officer, and took the prisoner - Mr. Smith gave me the duplicate.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. The shawl was pawned with me on the 22d of August.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress. GUILTY. Aged 19.
