 MR. ANDREWS. Q. Did not Mr. Leslie pay you 4 l. 2 s. 3 d., on one occasion? A. Yes; it was for beer and spirits - and I believe there was some borrowed money. There was no part of it for money I had lost in playing with him.
   WILLIAM ROBINSON Re-examined. Q. Who do you live with now? A. Mr Berry. I once lived with Mr. Leslie about twelve months. I then went to live with Mr. Smith, for about eighteen months - I went in 1822, and left in 1824. When I lived with Mr. Leslie, he was carrying on the coal trade - he often went to the public-house kept by Mr. Smith. I used to go and order the boy to bring the beer for Mr. Leslie's coal-heavers, to the water side; he used to have coals in every two or three weeks, and then there was a certain allowance of beer, which was chalked up at Mr. Smith's over the beer engine, sometimes by Mr. Smith, and sometimes by Miss Smith - this went on regularly and fairly, while I lived with Mr. Leslie, and afterwards, while I lived with Mr. Smith - when the chalk-place got full, it was put in the book. I have heard Mr. Leslie say, it had better be put in the book, as the place was full. I cannot swear that he said that more than once in my hearing, this was while I lived with Mr. Smith - I do not know that I saw Mr. Leslie go more than once to look at the chalk score; he spent his evenings there frequently, but I never heard any dispute about the chalk score, nor saw any account in writing between them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you ever fetch any beer for the coal-heavers? A. Yes; it was for them - but Mr. Leslie used to tell me it was to be put up for them - he never gave me any money for it. - I never saw him but once look at the chalk-score - I never heard him say to Mr. Smith, that he thought the coal-heavers had too much beer, and not to trust them so much.
   JOHN WYARD. I am a coal-porter. I assisted in delivering coals to Mr. Leslie, when he carried on business - they then got their beer at Master Smith's - the allowance was according to the quantity of coals - I remember one night, some time ago, Leslie coming to Mr. Smith's house and saying, he never would pay for this gallon of beer we had had - he said it, I suppose, about a dozen times in the room where I was - he called Mr. Smith to bring the bill, and he did bring it - it was 15 l. 1 s. 8 d. - I did not hear him say whether the account was true, or whether it was not - I did not see him pay anything; nor hear him say what the difference between them was.
 Cross-examined. Q. When did you leave working for Mr. Leslie? A. I have not worked for him this twelve months, or more - I had no dispute with him - I will swear that he never charged me with any misconduct, or with taking any of his property; we parted on good terms - I never saw Mr. Leslie pay for any beer that we had, nor did I ever receive any money to pay Mr. Smith. - I never heard Mr. Leslie say we were having too much beer, and not to trust us too much - I am employed by the parish in which I live, when I have nothing else to do. I go on Fridays and Mondays, and have 1 s. a day for it - Mr. Smith never advanced me any money when I was in distress - I do not owe him a farthing.
 COURT. Q. Who was present when the bill was brought in? A. Mr. Baker, and three or four other folks - I did not take any particular notice.
   JOHN BAKER. I was at Mr. Smith's about November, 1823, when there was a dispute about a gallon of beer - Leslie said he never ordered it, and would not pay for it - he had a bill in his hand; I understood it was 15 l. 1 s. 8 d., - he did not dispute any thing but the gallon of beer - Mr. Smith asked him to balance the bill, and said there was a balance due to him of 7 s. or 8 s. - he said that Leslie's mother-in-law had reported about the neighbourhood, that he owed him for a five of coals, and he could not get the money; and he wished the account to be settled between them - Leslie denied having the gallon of beer, but said nothing against the balance of 7 s. or 8 s.
 Cross-examined. Q. What was going on in the house? A. The men were sitting drinking their beer; I saw no game going on, nor any pack of cards - I saw the bill in Mr. Leslie's hand, and he handed it round to the company and said here's a "Bill delivered" - and "Bill delivered" - "What does bill delivered mean?" and he asked if that was a proper bill to be delivered; but he did not object to pay any thing, only the gallon of beer. I understood that gallon of beer to be in the bill - because he said it was not legal, and he would never pay it. I am in the habit of having beer there, but I do not owe 1 d.
 COURT. Q. What did he say about it? A. He held the paper in his hand and said, here's a "Bill delivered" - and "Bill delivered" - I don't understand it?" He did not say any thing about any gambling debt that I know of - he did not say any thing about this being for money lost at play.
   JAMES JOHNSON. I am a carpenter. I was at the public-house the night the last witness was there. I saw Leslie, and heard the dispute about the gallon of beer. I saw only one account which was handed over, and he asked me to look at it, but I did not; some words occurred, which caused the bill to be brought forward, concerning what Mr. Smith owed Mr. Leslie. Smith said, "What is the reason you suffer your father-in-law, or mother-in-law, to say I have had five chaldrons of coals, and you can't get the money for them - you have my account, and I have your's, and there is very little between us;" the bill was held up by Mr. Leslie, who said "Here's a pretty bill, what does it mean." Mr. Smith said, "I can't give the bill every time, but if you look at the different times you will see what it is for - I let your men have beer from time to time, and considered it the same as you."
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you have the bill in your hands? A. No; I did not see any of the items of it - he did not complain of any of the items; he read these words from the account. After he had read "Bill delivered," and
 "Bill delivered," he said "Here is a gallon of beer, I shall never pay for."
   ISAAC BEST. I am a carpenter. I remember being at Mr. Smith's in 1819, or 1820, when he kept the Lord Exmouth, in Catherine-street. Leslie was there and Mr. Smith, and several other persons were sitting in the parlour.
