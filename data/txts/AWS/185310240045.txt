HORACE DEBENHAM. I am house surgeon, at the London Hospital. The
body of the deceased was brought there on 24th Aug., covered with blood,
her clothes were saturated with blood there was a wound across the front of
the throat about six inches long, and on the face and neck there were ten other
smaller wounds; there were also wounds on the fingers of both hands-the
wound in the throat divided three of the jugular veins, but not the arteries
that caused her death-there was a post mortem examination on the following
day the organs of life were healthy-I examined a woand across the front
of the prisoner's throat, it was between five and six inches long-it opened
the wind pipe in two places, and divided one of the principal arteries, it
was a dangerous wound-] did not think he would live an hour when he
came in-the wounds on the woman and on the man were such as might have
been inflicted by these knives.
