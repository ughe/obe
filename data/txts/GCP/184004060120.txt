1165. JOHN KELLY and MARY CONNOR were indicted for a mis-
demeanor.
WILLIAM JAMES. I keep the Bell public-house, in York-street, West-
minster. On the 19th of March the prisoners came, about half-past four
o'clock in the afternoon, for a quartern of gin, and the man put a shilling
on the counter-I examined it-it was bad- I put it into my pocket,
where there was no other silver they went out, and about half-past nine
o'clock in the evening they came again, and called for a quartern of gin-
the woman put down a shilling-1 saw it was bad- I took it into my
hand, and came round and fastened the door-I sent for a policeman, and
gave them both into custody--I kept the first shilling in my pocket, and
the second in my hand, till I got to the station-house.
Kelly. Q. Did you not say you knew me by a black eye? A. No-
I knew you beside that there was another man there.
GBORG. Robe (police-constable B 29.) I took the prisoners on the 19th
of March-I received these two bad shillings from the prosecutor.
Mr. Joan FIELD. These are both counterfeit shillings.
Connor's Defence. I went to the prosecutor's house with two men ; one
of them gave me a shilling to pay for a quartern of gin, and that man was
not taken.
Kelly's Defence. I never was in company with this woman at all-
was there ten minutes before any person came.
KELLY-GUILTY. Aged 40. lConfined One Year.
CONNOR-GUILTY. Aged 43. S
