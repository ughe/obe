JOHN JUSTICE and WILLIAM PALMER were indicted for stealing, on the   4th of February , at St. Mary, Newington, 26lbs. weight of soap, value 8 s  .; and 21lbs., weight of candles; the goods of Jabez Pike , the master of John Justice; and WILLIAM MILLER was indicted for felo niously receiving 12lbs. weight of soap, value 4 s  . 6 d  .; and 21lbs. weight of candles, value 8 s  ., part of the said goods, well knowing them to be stolen, as aforesaid.—2nd COUNT  , for receiving of an evil disposed person. JABEZ PIKE. I am an oil and colourman. Justice was in my employ, as porter—I sent him on errands to deliver goods—Palmer was the servant of a Mr. Holraan, my neighbour—my premises open upon Holman's yard—on the night of the 4th of February, I received information from Holman, and accompanied him to a cart-shed in his yard; and under the cart, in a box, I saw a quantity of soap, which I had every reason to believe was mine, from having a similar description of goods on my premises—Holman went into the loft, and there was a quantity of candles in a canvass bag—I have every reason to believe they were mine, having candles of a similar description on my premises—I got a policeman, and went to Holman's premises again, and in the interval the property had been removed—the candles from the loft, and the soap from the box—I found near the box five bars of soap afterwards in a nose-bag—in consequence of information we went to a shop in Arbour-street, within fifty yards of my premises, and there saw Miller—I told him I had come for the soap and candles which Holman's boy had left a few minutes before—his reply was, "I have no candles"—I said, "It is no use to deny it; we know all about it;" and then he stooped down, opened a drawer, and produced the soap, and after wards the candles, in a canvass bag—I believe the bag to be the same as I had seen in Holman's loft—I got a policeman, and gave Justice and Pal mer into custody—I value the candles at 8 s ., and the soap at 8 s . or 9 s .—Miller keeps a chandler's shop—Holman is a carter.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  I believe you stated to Miller that what you wanted was some soap and candles, which Holman's boy had brought? A.  Yes—the prisoners were all before the magistrate—Justice and Palmer were detained—I undertook Miller should come next day, and he was let go on that—he has been a neighbour of mine many years—he came according to promise next day—I offered the magistrate to become bail for him—the magistrate said as the Grand Jury were sitting, it was not worth while—I have supplied Miller with goods, but not for months past.
