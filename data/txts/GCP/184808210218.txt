Mitchell, the Irish patriot, who has been condemned and transported
felon for freely explaining to the Irish people what was their duty, what were
their rights, and how they were to obtain them, has been sacrificed, and
made the victim, for promulgating those principles which I am quite glad to
see at present are so prominent among the Irish people. Ireland has been
taught by the Apostle Mitchell, and I rejoice to see that, although the Gover.
mert has removed him, there are other individuals equally as patriotic
himself, who have nobly and manfully come forward and taken the people bir
the hand, and declared that they will to the death lead them on, if possible
to victory. Mr. Chairman, I rejoice that such has been the case; and i
recommend to the Irish people to follow implicitly the commands of their
leaders. I would call also upon the English people who desire liberty, who
desire justice, and who desire equality, to stand by the Irish people in the
hour of need. As soon as the Irish people are up, as soon as the ingor
rection in Ireland becomes general, (as I believe in the course of a very fer
hours it will become general), I trust that the people of England will rise to
support them. Mr. Chairman and fellow working men, for whatever I say
care not, whatever may follow, I as an Englishman consider that on all
occasions, and upon every occasion, when my conscience dictates to me that
I have an opportunity of speaking to my fellow men in public or in private,
I have an unbounded right to tell them what is the honest conviction of my
mind; and if I suffer for so doing, I shall only be another added to the band
of patriots who have been crushed; and if, working men, you do allow those
men who do nobly come forward and tell you your rights, and how to obtain
them, if you allow them to be picked away, if you allow them to be trane
ported, to enjoy, I was going to say, but rather to be placed in a dungeon
there to suffer all the indignities which it is possible for a base, bloody,
and brutal Government to inflict, you do not deserve the name of men, but
you ought to be incarcerated yourselves, your families ought to starve
around you, and you ought to go howling through the streets as a base
and cowardly race of men. Gentlemen, I am not inclined to take up much
of your time this evening. In what I say, however humble it may be,
remember I am only a working man, and bave not much time to spare to
study to make use of fine grammatical expressions. I sincerely and honestly
co-operate with the friends of liberty as much as those who have more time
to spare and to devote to the cause. But, gentlemen, allow me to ask you
if you are men, will ye act as men? (cries of "Yes.") If ye are called on
to-night, to-morrow, or at any other time, do not show you are cowards, and
flinch, but rally round the standard of liberty, defend it with your lives, and
may God Almighty grant we may be victorious, that our enemies may be
crushed, that they may hide their heads in the dungeons in the hulks, and
that our honest patriots may be restored to us, and that they may take their
places is my sincere wish."
