 1594.  SARAH WATSON and JANE COLLIER were indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , a shawl, value 18 s., the goods of Henry Keymer.
   WILLIAM BILES. I am in the employ of Mr. Henry Keymer, linen-draper, of Bishopsgate-street. On the 19th of July, about six o'clock in the evening, the prisoners and another person came into the shop, and wished to look at a silk shawl from 20 s. to 25 s.; I shewed them several, and after they had sat some time the other person went out, saying the shop was so hot, she must go out; I then shewed the prisoners some more, till my attention was called to the door to a person who inquired the price of a print; while there I looked round, and saw Jane Collier standing to hide the other, and the shawls moving on the counter; I then went back to the prisoners, and Collier said I need not give myself any farther trouble; what they saw were very pretty, and she persuaded Watson to have one; she then asked what was the lowest price; I replied 25 s.; she said she had not come out with an intention of purchasing, and had not money enough, but left 2 s., and said she would call on Saturday; I put the shawl into a bit of paper, and while they were going from the back of the shop to the door, I missed the shawl now produced; I told my employer to detain Collier, and I pursued Watson; I took her, and under her petticoats I found the shawl.
 Prisoner COLLIER. Q. Did I attempt to move out of the shop while you went after Watson? A. No.
   JOHN GREEN. I am the officer, and took them up.
 Prisoner WATSON. My fellow-prisoner is innocent - she did not know what I was doing.
 Prisoner COLLIER. I did not know what was taking place.
 COLLIER -    NOT GUILTY.
 WATSON -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 1595.  CHARLES PREFERMENT was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of July , a pair of cloth trousers, value 2 l., the goods of James Adams and Thomas Wight.
 THOMAS WIGHT. I am a  tailor, and live in Fenchurch-street. I am in partnership with James Adams. These trousers are our property. On Saturday, the 2d of July, they were in the window, hanging on the rail, before five o'clock; they were brought back to me, with the prisoner, some time after.
   AMOS DEACON. I am a carpenter. On the 2d of July I was working in Fenchurch-street; I saw the prisoner and two others walking along, and one of his companions leaned into the prosecutor's shop; the prisoner stood close to him with a bag, which was doubled up, and they all stood looking about; I went over to the prisoner, and asked him what he had got in the bag; he said Nothing; I said "If you have got nothing the better for you, for you must go back with me to that gentleman's shop," and then I believe he said, some person gave them to him; I saw the bag opened, and the trousers taken from it.
   WILLIAM MOORE. I have heard Deacon's evidence, and know it to be correct.
   WILLIAM YOUNG. I am an officer - I took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. The witness says he saw me take and put the trousers into the bag, which is quite a falsity; the person who took them gave them to me, and asked me to carry them.
 One witness gave the prisoner a good character, and engaged to employ him.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Fined 1 s. and Discharged.
 1596.  JOSEPH PATTERSON was indicted for embezzlement.
   Robert Govan. I am a baker, and live in Redcross-street. The prisoner was in my employ for three years and two weeks - he had authority from me to receive money for the bread he delivered - I have a customer of the name of Robinson - on the 26th of July, he accounted to me, about five o'clock, for money to the amount of 4 s. 8 d., but did not account for any received of Miss Robinson - if he had received any, he ought to have accounted for it.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. From whom did he say he had received this 4 s. 8 d.? A. From four customers, whose names I do not recollect, but I can positively swear Miss Robinson was not one.
   MARY ANN FISHER. I am an apprentice to Miss Robinson. I paid the prisoner 3 s. 11 d. for Mr. Govan, on the 26th of July; I gave him 4 s. and he gave me 1 d.
 Cross-examined. Q. How do you know it was the 26th? A. I recollect it perfectly well - I went before the Magistrate on the 2d of August.
   WILLIAM PAYNE. I went on the morning of the 28th of July, to Mr. Robert Govan's house. I took the prisoner, and found five sovereigns, and one shilling in his fob, and some copper in his pocket behind.
 ROBERT GOVAN re-examined. Q. Had you ever asked the prisoner about this 3 s. 11 d.? A. Yes, on the 26th of July, about five o'clock, when I booked the bread; he said, No, she had not paid.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you not part with him for a short time? A. Yes, and took him back again, at an advance of wages - when he went away, he did not leave 12 s. in my hand in case there should be a shilling or two deficient.
 Prisoner's Defence. When I first left my master, I left money in his hands. I left him on a Wednesday - I called on the Friday, I went with the new man to the customers, and when I came back, my master asked me to come back, and he would allow me 3 s. a week more.
     NOT GUILTY.
