I am the wife of last witness. On Wednesday night, the 6th of Sept., my attention was called to what took place in the chapel-yard next to our house—I saw three men engaged at the tomb—one of them was undressed, and had a cloak on—the other two, I think, were in black—two had cloaks on—I saw the dog in the ground, it was quiet—I saw the horse come out of the stable—on Monday, the 18th of Sept., from ten till one o'clock in the day, I was at our back window, and saw the elder defeudant at the wall of his ground, which separates our two houses—it was formerly a garden, but is now part of the chapel-yard—he seemed to be gardening, mak ing a border—I am not sure whether I saw a spade—he bad no coat on—I cannot say for certain that he was digging, as my husband was gardening at the time, and it might have been his spade that I heard. HANNAH COURTNALL. I am the daughter of Mr. Courtnall. About a fortnight before the 6th of Sept. last, I was walking in the garden, and heard Mr. Bridgman, senior, saying that some men had been seen in the burial-ground the night before—I do not know who he said it to—on the night of the 6th my attention was called to what was going on—I slept in the same room with Priscilla Terry, the servant—on the night alter I saw a man come out of Mr. Bridgman's house, and saw my brother follow him—shortly after, I was looking out of our second-floor window, which is even with Mr. Bridgman's second floor, and saw a female in her night-dress, looking out of the window—I am not able to say it was Mrs. Bridgman, but I think it was—at the same time I thought I saw a man with a hat on, looking out of the other window—I do not speak with any positiveness about that—that is their bedroom—I heard somebody talking in Mr. Bridgman's first floor, and heard some one say, "As long as my name is Bridgman"—it was a roan's voice—I thought I heard the voices of two men—I know the elder Mr. Bridgman's voice, and I thought it was his voice—I do not attend his chapel—on the following morning, about nine o'clock, or between nine and ten, I saw Mr. Bridgman's horse and chaise at the door; and between ten and twelve that same morning I saw Mr. Bridgman coming out of the chapel in West-street—he passed me—I saw his face distinctly—I was at home about ten o'clock in the evening, while my brother was watching, I saw the two men at the tomb, but could not distinguish young Bridgman—I saw my brother go out after one of them.
   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN . Q.  The horse and chaise, I suppose, was one Mr. Bridgman, the elder, was in the habit of riding in? A.  Yes—I was looking out of our first-floor front window when I saw the chaise—I saw it come, I did not see anybody in it—I beard it come to the door, and some one had just jumped out of it when I saw it—I heard them jump out—I had to go out twice that morning, and I do not remember whether it was the first or second time that I saw Mr. Bridgman—I passed him in the open street—I did not speak to him, nor he to me—I had to go about half a mile, and then I returned home.
