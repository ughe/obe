1649. ELIZABETH HINCHCLIFFE was indicted for stealing, on
GO
the 13th of June, 2 wine-glasses, value 28., the goods of James Wood.
James Wood. I keep the Three Compasses, at Mile-end. I have known
the prisoner some months—she lived near me, and came to my house
almost every day on the 13th of June, she came and asked for half-a-pint
of ale, and while I was drawing it, I saw her put a glass into her pocket
I had her apprehended on the 13th I have since seen two wine-glasses
in the pawnbroker's possession.
THOMAS SAFFRON. I am shopman to Mr. Folkard, a pawnbroker, in the
Commercial-road. I produce two wine-glasses- I took one in pawn from
the prisoner, on the 9th of June, for 3d. I have the counterpart of the
duplicate --Mr. Folkard took the other in- I have the counterpart of that
here-she gave the name of Nelthorpe.
HENRY LOCKWOOD (police-constable K 140.). I took the prisoner into
custody on the 13th, at Mr. Wood's, and found two duplicates on her for
these glasses, among others pawned in the name of Nelthorpe,
JAMES WOOD re-examined. I have had one of these glasses for years--it is
worn very much at the bottom-- I have very lately purchased half-a-dozen of
the others, of one Lawrence-there is nothing uncommon in the pattern.
one pattern has not been in vogue many years it is a full half-quartern
glass, and the other is a three-ounce glass- I am satisfied they are mine
-they correspond in pattern and appearance with those I lost.
Prisoner's Defence. These glasses were my husband's before he died,
which is more than five years ago. I never took one of Mr. Wood's glasses
-I was obliged to make money for my children by pawning them.
GUILTY. Aged 52.-Recommended to merey --Confined Ten Days.
