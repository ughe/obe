THOMAS ISRAEL BUNDY. I am servant to Mr. John Chatfield, who lives
in Aldgate. On the 12th of August I put this bag of coffee at his door,
inside-I saw it safe at ten o'clock, and soon after the street-keeper spoke
to me, I then missed it.
GEORGE TEAKLE (police-sergeant H 8.) I stopped the prisoner with
this bag of coffee, in Essex-street, Whitechapel, about eleven o'clock in the
morning-there was another man with him, whom we took, but he made
his escape-the prisoner said he received it of that man.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON, Q. How far is it from the prose-
cutor's shop to where you took the prisoner? A. About five minutes
walk--the prosecutor's sbop could not be seen from there.
NOT GUILTY.
2224. ELIZABETH JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of
August, 1 shawl, value 10s., the goods of George Liddimore.
MARY LIDDIMORE. I am the wife of George Liddimore; we live in
Old Gravel-lane. The prisoner lodged with me I went out and came
home on the 14th of August-the prisoner left my house that evening-I
missed my shawl-I spoke to her about it-she told me where she had
pawned it, and I found it there.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. I believe her husband is your
own brother? A. Yes I gave her in charge through my husband-I be-
lieve she would have redeemed it-she was in distress.
NOT GUILTY.

2225. GEORGE HOLLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 19th
of August, 2 gowns, value 78. ; 1 shirt, value 1s. ; 1 pair of stockings,
value 3d.; and 1 handkerchief, value 3d. ; the goods of Ann Woodman;
and that he had been before convicted of felony.
Ann WOODMAN. I lodge at Mrs. Hurst's. I found the prisoner in my
room about four o'clock in the day, on the 19th of August-he was partly
undressed-he had a pair of my stockings on his legs, and my handker-
chief in his pocket-Mrs. Hurst had found these gowns and shirt before
she called me-they are my property-the prisoner had no business in my
room.
Maria Hurst. I live in Wentworth-street. The prisoner came to
lodge in my house on the night of the 18th of August-I called him at
nine o'clock the next morning-he got up, came down, and appeared very
ill and very sick-I said, "You don't seem well, would a bason of tea be of
any use to you ?"-he said, “ Yes, and thank you too"-I said, “You had
better go and lie down again"-he went up, and laid till four I then
went to him to know if he would get up he said, "Yes"-I went up
again in about ten minutes, and could not see him in his room-I looked
in another room, he was not there-I then looked in the further room, and
he was standing by Mrs. Woodman's bed-side-I said, " What are you
doing ?"-he said, " I don't know"-I found this gown in his bat--I said,
You are robbing the woman"-I locked the door, ran down, and called
Mirs. Woodman up--when I went up again he was half undressed-he was
dressed when I first saw him, and I suppose he had these things on him, as
there was nothing on the bed when I went down, and when I came up
again some of these things were on the bed, and the rest on him.
