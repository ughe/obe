MARK YOUNG. I am servant to Thomas Johnson of Richmond, He is a chimney-sweeper—he directed me to sift some dust in a field at Mortlake—it is coal-dust—Swabey had a heap in that field—about the beginning of January, I saw Ellis take one barrow full of dust from my master's heap, and eleven barrows the next day, to Swabey's heap—Swabey was not there the whole time—I only saw Swabey give 6 d . for one barrow, and that was at the last—Ellis offered me 1 d .—I refused it—Ellis was in my master's employ.
   Ellis . He is saying false, Swabey gave me 6 d . that he owed me, but not for this—I took four barrows from there a month ago—it was all done is one afternoon—he was as bad as I was, he carried two baskets, and he said if I gave him half, he would not say any thing. Witness.  He was at work for Mr. Johnson two days, and he took one one day, and eleven the next—when Swabey came in, he said to Ellis, "How many have you taken there, one?"—he said, "No, a dozen," and Swabey saw him take the last one.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  How long was it before you told of this? A.  About a week after—I was afraid of his whopping me—there was no dispute about 3 d .—the 1 d . was offered to me in copper—he got the sixpence changed at the Bricklayers' Aims—my master and Swabey are both cinder-collectors—I know of no dispute between them—them are about one hundred yards between the two heaps. THOMAS JOHNSON. I collect ashes and dust. The barrow holds about two bushels, and the twelve were worth about 3 s .
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long after this supposed loss was it that Young told you of it? A.  About a week, I believe—I know Swabey collects a little dust, and he has employed Ellis since my dust was taken.
   ELLIS— GUILTY. Aged 29.— Confined One Month.
   SWABEY— NOT GUILTY.

TIMOTHY CRAWLEY and CHARLES SILSBURY were indicted for stealing, on the   19th of January , 1 cape, value 20 s  ., the goods of Benjamin Croot : and SARAH DAY for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. BENJAMIN CROOT, JUN. I was in the Borough on the 19th of December, with my father's chaise—I had a cape in it belonging to my father—I left it while I went some where, and was absent about ten minutes—when I came hack it was gone—I saw it in a marine store-shop in the Mint, about twenty minutes after it was stolen—I found the prisoner Sarah Day, and another female with a child—the officer asked for a cape, and it was, immediately produced—there was no denial that it was there—Day said, "It was brought in, put into the scale, and sold as old rags," and she was the person buying it—it has a rent under the arm.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  I believe you know that Day is but servant in the house? A.  I do not know it, I have heard so, and heard the contrary—there was not the least concealment about her—there was no time—I put no question to her—the policeman did, and she said we bought it for old rags, it being torn, and she said what she gave for it—she said, "Crawley put it into the scale"—she had not time to put it away.
