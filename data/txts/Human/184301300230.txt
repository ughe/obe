   Q.  Then in March you had a paper given yon, dated back to December, which you signed? A.  Yes, I did not send the retainer by post; I took it to Mr. Boys.
   MR. BIRCH re-examined. Q.  Upon whose application did you part with the deed said to be forged? A.  The application was in writing in the first instance—this is it—it is signed by the prisoner. THOMAS HILL re-examined.  This signature, "F. C. B. Blackwell," is the prisoner's writing.
   MR. BIRCH re-examined.  This is dated, "Southampton-street, Camberwell, 27th of Oct. 1841," and is addressed "Messrs. Sutcliffe and Birch"—it is—( reads )—"Gentlemen, As devisees and executors of the will of the late Mr. Joseph Stanley, deceased, we request and hereby authorise you to deliver the papers and documents which have belonged to him and his estate, particularly the deeds relating to the Lewisham estate, to Messrs. Hale, Boys, and Austen, on their receipt; and we will thank you to furnish them or us with the particulars of the demand you have against the late Mr. Stanley's estate."—This is signed by the prisoner and Phoebe Blackwell—that ultimately led to giving up the forged deed; but something else passed between the letter and the giving up of the deed—on the receipt of this letter, I think, we sent the prisoner Stanley's bill, and then we gave up all the papers belonging to Stanley, but we did not give up the deed in question, as we had not re ceived the bill against Mr. Constable—Hale, Boys, and Austen then took out a summons, which I hold in my hand—they were then acting for the prisoner—it is a summons before a judge, to show cause why we did not give up the deed—the judge discharged the summons with costs—he said we had a clear lien on the deed for the debt against Constable, on the assumption that Constable having ordered the deed to be prepared would have had to pay the expense of it—Hale, Boys, and Austen ultimately paid the amount, and the deed was given up—I think I was in the way when the forged deed was taken away—I was not in the room when it was given up—I was in my own room—I did not see the parties who came to claim it—I gave Coward, my clerk, instructions to give up the deed—I authorized him to part with it, in consequence of the communication which he made to me—I gave it him for that purpose—I knew from Coward that the prisoner
