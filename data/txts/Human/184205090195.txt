   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe you authorised the prisoner to act for you? A.  The first time I went to him was at his entreaty, at Ley ton stone—he asked me to let him look at the will, and how I got on—I told him I did not know—he asked tie to let him have the will—I let him have it, and the advertisement, where he could go and find out if there was any chance for me, and what—he went up to London with the copy of the will, and the advertisement—I saw the prosecutor several times during the course of a year or two—he sent for me many times at the Anchor—I only saw him once up at the warehouse—me and my relations went; there—I was not satisfied with the prisoner's goings on, and I was determined to see who the solicitors were—I went up to him and told him, "Mr. Hitchcock, let us know who the solicitors are"—ne said "I have orders from the solicitors not to let you know who they are"—he said, "Go home and I will bring you some money"—I entered into an agreement—he has done nothing whatever—he has brought me into great difficulties—I have seen the prosecutor alone as well as in the prisoner's presence.
   MR. BALLANTINE called MARY JACQUES. The prisoner married my mother. I am sister to the prosecutor's wife—I have lived at home with my family—I remember living in Tredegar-square—Mrs. Roberts kept the house then—Mr. Hinton and the prisoner hired the house, I believe—I should think we re mained there nine months—there are five of us in family—during that time the prisoner bad the receipts for the bills made out in his name—he used to pay for what was wanted—my sister is eighteen—I have seen money pass between the prisoner and the prosecutor several times—I have seen the prisoner give the prosecutor money several times—(I am sixteen years old)—that was at Ley tons tone—I never saw it in Tredegar-square—I should think I have seen it six or seven times—Ido not know to what amount—I think it was silver—the prisoner paid the bills at Leytonstone—I do not know whether he paid them out of his own money—he was doing nothing at that time—he has been married to my mother seven years—he has al ways supported us since his marriage—my mother has some property—the prosecutor lived with us at Leytonstone—I do not know how he lived with us—I never heard any agreement made about payment for board and lodging—I never heard him speak about it—I do not remember his ever paying anything for board and lodging—from Leytonstone we went to Mrs. Roberts's, in Tredegar-square again—the prosecutor did not live with us there—I believe he came there twice on a visit—I do not know that the prisoner has been greatly reduced.
