WILLIAM Webster. These balf-crowns are both counterfeit, and differ
from those usually produced in Court, inasmuch as these are struck from dies,
and then plated the second process mentioned in the paper just read
would electro-plate them, which is the modern process-the first is the old
process of colouring them.
Cross-examined. Q. Which do you call the electro-plating process ? A.
That commencing "1 oz. syanide potass;" that is for giving & colour to
copper or brass, but not to white metal—these are made of brass- I do not
know what this one means, “clean the metal," &c.-this, "to silver,
moisten in common salt," &c., is merely to cleanse either brass or copper-
that would be used in any business where they have copper or brass.
MR. CLERK, Q. To what does this apply, "add 60 drops of sulphuric
acid, and fasten to the pole of the battery ? A. To electro-plating I have
no doubt that that is the way in which these half-crowns have been treated
there is no other way to do it. MR. BALLANTINE called

JOSEPH Bush. I am a saw-naker, of 1, Somerset-street, Aldgate--I
have known the prisoner eleven years-he has always been a very upright,
honest young man.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLERK. Q. Who is he now working for? A.
He had a shop of his own a few weeks ago, in Walworth; till within the last
five weeks, I believe I have never been there he has told me he employed
one man- I have no means of knowing what he has been doing during the
last three months.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Has he given up this shop since this charge was
made against him? A. A little before that.
