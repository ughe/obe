   Cross-examined by the prisoner.  I did not say I only got a sovereign for the things—I had no money at all. BERNARD JACOBS. I am assistant to John Jacobs, a pawnbroker, of I49, St. George's Street, E.—on August 14th Cohen came with these things—I refused to take them—the police send us a list of things stolen every morning, and it contained the description of these. GEORGE LAMBERT( Police-Sergeant K ). On August 12th I examined Dr. Milne's premises—an entry had been effected by breaking into No. 30, which is next door, then getting into a loft and through a window on to a roof, and then on to the roof of Dr. Milne's place—there the thief or thieves had removed the whole of the top of the w.c., and dropped down into it—the roof was not screwed down—Jacobs handed me these three silver things—I charged the prisoner, and on August 31st he was picked out by Cohen and his son—on September 7th he was charged at Arbour Square—in reply he said, "I admit trying to sell the things, but I did not break into the place."
   The prisoner, in his defence, said that he found the things in the w.c. of a beer-shop, and took them to Cohen, but did not know anything about the burglary. GUILTY.— He then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of felony at this Court on October 22nd, 1894, and six other convictions were proved against him.— Three Years' Penal Servitude.
