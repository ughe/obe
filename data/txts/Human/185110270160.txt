 1964. THOMAS CHIDLOW , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. SCRIVEN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. JAMES NOBLE. I am conductor of one of Wilson's Favorite  omnibuses. On 9th Sept., about 11 o'clock at night, the prisoner was a passenger by my omnibus—he got on at Islington and rode to the Eagle in the City-road, about three-quarters of a mile—the fare was 4 d ., and he gave me a bad half-crown—I asked him if he had any more, and he gave me a good one—I told him I should not give him the change till I saw a policeman, and I kept the bad one also—he rode on the steps to the Artillery-ground gate, about a quarter of a mile, where I saw Allen, and I gave him the bad half-crown—he was searched, but no more found on him—I asked him for his address, and he said he could be found at Mr. Bowen's, 23, Cullum-street, Fenchurch-street—I asked him if his name was Bowen—he said no, but he could be beard of there, and his name was Chidlow—I gave him the change out of the good half-crown, and Allen took the bad one—the prisoner was then allowed to go.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Have you been to Cullum-street? A.  Yes; I went to see if there was such a number in the street, and saw the name of Bowen on the door. GEORGE ALLEN( policeman, G  46). On 9th Sept. I was on duty in the City-road, and about 5 minutes to 11 o'clock saw Noble and the prisoner-Noble said he had given him a bad half-crown—I asked Noble whether he would charge him, but he would not as he could not leave his omnibus—I took the bad half-crown and marked it—this is it ( produced )—I have had it ever since. SAMUEL WILLIAM FENN. I am conductor of one of the Atlas  omnibuses. On 13th Sept. the prisoner was a passenger by my omnibus—he got up it the Bricklayers' Arms at 5 or six minutes to 10 o'clock at night, and rode for about three minutes, a quarter or half a mile—the fare was 4 d ., and he gave me a counterfeit half-crown in payment—I said, "Halloo! what have you got here?" and told him it was a bad one—he said he was not aware of it—I left my omnibus, and walked with the prisoner to the Elephant and Castle, where I showed the half-crown to the time-keeper, who asked him for his address and he refused to give it—I gave the prisoner into custody to Hyde, and gave him the half-crown.
