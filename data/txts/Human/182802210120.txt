WILLIAM MITCHELL. I am an officer. On the night of the 13th of February I heard a cry of Watch! up Barleymow-court; I ran up; Ashdown had hold of the prisoner, and said, "She has robbed me;" I took hold of her hand, and said, "Have you robbed him?" she said No; I held her, sent for a light, and found a half sovereign, three half-crowns, a shilling, and six sixpences; the prosecutor said next morning that he lost 1l. 12s. 6d. I took her to the watch-house, and 18d. was found on her; she said he had offered her 5s.
 Cross-examined. Q. In what state was the prosecutor? A. He was not sober; he gave us some beer after we had locked her up - he said that night that he had lost a considerable deal of money; he seemed too drunk to tell how much he had lost - he said he could not tell till he looked at his books - they were about ten yards down the passage.
   CHARLES COOK. I was with Mitchell, and found the money.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he say what he had lost? A. He said he could not tell till he looked at his books at home.
 Prisoner's Defence. I heard some money fall, as he took it from his pocket; he asked me for 2s. back which he had given me; I refused; he seized me, and they fell from my hand.
     NOT GUILTY.
 700.  HENRY MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of February , 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s., the goods of George Borough , from his person.
 GEORGE BOROUGH. On the 10th of February I was at the  Castle public-house, Uxbridge, leaning over the bench, speaking to a person, and found the prisoner's hand in my pocket; I took hold of it, and said, "You are taking my handkerchiefs - you had better let that alone; "I sat down, and said,
 "I will have them again;" I gave him in charge as he was abusive; I saw them again next morning; one of them was torn to pieces.
 Prisoner. Q. What were you doing? A. Speaking to a book-hawker - I was not taking his watch out.
   JOHN FARRANT. I am a constable. I was sent for, and took the prisoner; the prosecutor gave me the handkerchiefs next morning.
 GEORGE BOROUGH re-examined. They were brought to me next morning by a young fellow, who had been to see the prisoner in the cage; I heard the prisoner examined, and saw him sign this account - (read.)
 "The prisoner admits he took the handkerchiefs, but it was only out of a lark."
 Prisoner's Defence. We had been drinking together all day; he was leaning over, and taking a man's watch - I took his hankerchiefs, and sent them to him next morning.
     NOT GUILTY.
 701.  SAMUEL PAYNE was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of December , 80 lbs. weight of lead, value 12s., belonging to Thomas Lynn , and fixed to a certain building of his; against the Statute.
 THOMAS LYNN. I am a  builder, and own a house in Brunswick-street, Hackney-road, in the parish of Shoreditch. On Thursday morning, the 20th of December, I missed some lead from the gutters, which I had seen safe two days before; part of it was afterwards produced - I could not match it, as the rest was gone; I know it by my own dressing, as I am my own plumber - here are marks which I put on it about six months ago, when I put it down on the gutter of Nos. 5 and 7, in the street - I lost lead from other gutters before, but it was older than this - I am certain this belongs to No. 5 or 7, and I saw it safe two days before; the mark on the lead of the other houses is quite different.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is this the lead of a whole gutter? A. No, only part of it - it has been cut in pieces; I marked the other in a different place.
   ELIJAH BEASLEY. I live at the Roebuck public-house, Hackney-road. On the 20th of December Pool called to me and my fellow-servant to stop the prisoner; we found this lead in his possession; I said,
 "You have been and stolen this lead from Mr. Lynn's houses;" he said, "You are a poor fellow as well as myself - you had better let me go."
   GEORGE HARDING. I and Beasly were called, and took the prisoner with the lead.
   EGGETT POOL. I was selling fruit at the corner of Haggerston-lane - the back of these houses come down to this lane. On the 20th of December, Mr.Lynn asked if I had seen any body with lead - I said No; but about half past six o'clock, I saw a young man loaded with lead - I ran over to the public-house; but he had got down a back street; I should know him by his clothes - In about an hour, I saw the prisoner come with some lead - I ran to the public-house, and the witnesses came and took him.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long is it since you were a witness here? A. I never was a witness; I was here, but did not do the misdemeanor; I was brought here about some beef, and imprisoned six months - I was tipsy.
   JAMES BROWN. I am an officer, and received the lead from the witnesses, and I told the witnesses if they saw the prisoner to take him, and a night or two after, I went with them and took him - I went to the houses, and could not match the lead except by the width of the gutters which seemed to match.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not you see footsteps on the ground? A. Yes; I took the prisoner's shoes off, but did not match them, as I was informed he had got a new pair; the marks were of a shoe with part of the heel worn off.
   ELIJAH BEASLEY. I left the prisoner with Harding while I went for assistance, and he escaped - I had seen him before about.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did the prosecutor attempt to swear to the lead before he spoke to you? A. He spoke to me before he went in; the Magistrate did not reprimand him in my hearing.
   GEORGE HARDING re-examined. I knew the prisoner before - he struggled and got from me - some persons round said, if I did not let him go they would make me.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not the prosecutor speak to you? A. He did, outside the office; he told me to be confident to the lead, and not swear to the wrong lead, as there was other lead about; the magistrate reprimanded him for speaking to me - Beasley was present, but he had sworn to it before he spoke to me - I told the magistrate I had seen the prisoner before.
 The witnesses deposition being read, did not contain that statement.
 I told the magistrate so I am certain.
