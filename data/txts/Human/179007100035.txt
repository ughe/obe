I trust you will be told by his lordship, that there has been no sort of evidence to fix any thing upon me; that there has been no meeting proved with Shanley; no meeting proved with Tronson and myself, for the purpose of framing this conspiracy: you will well consider that I find myself very unequal to go on, therefore I shall submit; I shall call the witnesses that I have to tender to you, and only request that you will not be prejudiced by any thing that you have heard against my character: my character I admit has been very much injured indeed; my fortune has been destroyed; my reputation gone; but by whom has it been destroyed? by Mr. I'Anson: I'Anson indicted me for forgery; Mr. I'Anson indicted me for perjury: I proved in the Court of Chancery, and he dare not attempt to deny it: I have not been so fortunate to find out the rest of the witnesses on this occasion; if so, I might unravel the whole of it upon that occasion; I feel myself very unequal to say any more; I only request that you will well consider the consequence of this prosecution, it has been pointed out to you as a serious one; and it is a serious one, I admit, and of the greatest magnitude; I should rather stand here to be tried for my life, I should rather stand here charged with felony. I have been in great habits of intimacy with the warden of the Fleet; there was a time before he became acquainted with Mr. I'Anson, that he thought me a man of the strictest honour; but the moment he became acquainted with Mr. I'Anson his opinion altered; he certainly thought himself attacked in a very serious manner by Mr. I'Anson, and he thought it material to obtain his friendship. Gentlemen, I have undergone a trial of great length, and I have been astonished and shocked at the testimony against me; and I trust that I shall be supplied by his lordship as to what I have left deficient, and that the evidence I shall call, will be sufficient to set aside the evidence against me, and that I shall be entitled to your acquittal. My lord, I trust the warrant of attorney being proved, will be sufficient to prove the debt from Shanley.
 Court. It is material to you to prove it.
 (Witnesses for the defendant.)
   FRANCIS SEARLE sworn.
 I am a grocer in Oxford-road.
 Mr. Jacques. Upon your oath, from the knowledge you have of Mr. Clipson, would you believe him on his oath? - No, I do not know that I should.
 Court. Do not know! - No, I would not.
 Mr. Jacques. I have no right to ask his reasons; the learned gentlemen on the other side may do that.
 Mr. Silvester. How long have you known Jacques? - I think it is between two and three years.
 How long have you known Clipson? - About two years.
