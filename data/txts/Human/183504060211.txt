WILLIAM BRADBURY was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of April , at   St, George the Martyr , 1 half-crown, the money of Henry Chester , his master; and GEORGE TYLER , for that he, before the said felony was done and committed, to wit, on the same day and at the same parish, did feloniously and maliciously incite, move, procure, counsel, hire, and command the said William Bradbury to do and commit the said felony.
   Bradbury  pleaded GUILTY.— Confined Eight Days. HENRY CHESTER. I am a bread and biscuit baker, and live in St. George's-circus, Southwark. Bradbury had been about six weeks in my service—on Friday, the 3rd of April, I was absent from home, and, on re turning in the evening, Mrs. Cheater gave me information. ELIZA CHESTER. I am the prosecutor's wife, Bradbury came to me for money to fetch 1 cwt. of salt—I gave him two half-crowns—he said we wanted salt—when he came back he brought me 1 s . 6 d .—when Mr. Chester came home I told him what I had sent for—Tyler has lived with us for about a year and a quarter, and was in our service at the time—he was a trusty servant, as we thought—I thought the salt was wanted for the baking—I supposed Bradbury had bought 1 cwt. of salt, as he gave me 1 s . 6 d . change—Tyler was in the bakehouse at the time—I do not know whether he could hear what passed.
   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  What is the price of salt? A.  Three shillings and sixpence per cwt.—I have sent for salt before—we trusted to Tyler as to whether salt was wanted—Mr. Chester attends to the shop—this was on Saturday—Tyler's wages would be due on the Wednes day—no wages are due to him now—he was paid on the Saturday before he left the house—I did not hear any dispute between him and my hus band about wages. WILLIAM MOORE. I live in the London-road. I sold Bradbury half a cwt. of salt, for which he paid me 1 s . 9 d .—he gave me half-a-crown, and I gave him 9 d . change. HENRY CHESTER re-examined .
