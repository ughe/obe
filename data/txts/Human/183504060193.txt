Sixth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant .
 1093. JOHN PARISH was indicted for embezzlement. GEORGE BANHAM. I live at Montpelier-vale, Blackheath, and am in partnership with my father-in-law. The prisoner was in our employ—on the evening of the 13th of March, I marked some trusses of hay—I saw the same trusses safe on the morning of the 14th, which was Saturday—I concealed myself in the stable, and about seven o'clock the prisoner came into the stable, fed his horses, and then sat down on a truss of straw—some time afterwards, a man, whom I could not see, came to the door, and the prisoner said, "Come along, I just want one dram this morning"—he asked, the man if he had a handkerchief—he said he had, and he could have a little bit that morning—he went to the manger, but whether he took any thing or not I cannot say—the prisoner said he had not got much that morning, for his mistress had been peeping through that b—y hole—they then went away—I went for an officer, but could not find one—I went home, and the officer came some minutes after—I said nothing to the prisoner that morning, but he went through his work as usual—in the evening I went to the stable to see that every thing was safe, and missed two trusses of hay; but one of them I had ordered him to take to a cus tomer—I went and got a search-warrant—we then went to Mrs. Loft's, where I found the truss of hay I had missed, with a stick in it, which I had put—I had directed him to take a truss to Mrs. Bevis', but not to Mrs. Loft's—it was his duty to sell hay; but when he did, he was to bring me the money for the hay so sold—he sold hay to different people—after he was in custody, he said he had trusted Mrs. Loft for four trusses of hay—I had not had the money.
   Cross-examined by MR. MAGUIRE . Q.  Was he allowed to trust any one who came for hay? A.  I always cautioned him against trusting any one without my knowledge—he sometimes accounted to me for hay the evening he sold it, and sometimes not till the Saturday night—he has sometimes given my wife money for hay—I cannot swear that he had not given her the money for this. LUCY LOFT. My husband is confined to his room. I dealt with the prisoner—I had a truss of hay of him on the 14th of March, which was to come to 3 s .—I did not pay him for it—I had fifteen trusses of him, and paid 7 s . off them. NOT GUILTY.
 1094. JANE SHAWL was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of April , 1 pair of shoes., value 1 s  ., the goods of Mary Smith . MARY SMITH. I am a widow, and live in the New-road, Woolwich. On the 4th of April the prisoner came to me and asked me to buy a pair of shoes—she said she wanted 1 s . for them—I said that would not do—she said, "Will you give 6 d .?"—she then took up a pair of shoes from my table, and said, "What are these?"—I said, "A shilling"—she staid some time, and I turned to the fire, and she flew out of the shop with my shoes—I followed her as well as I could—she was taken and brought back with these shoes in her hand—they are mine. SAMUEL NICHOLLS. I am constable of Woolwich. I took the prisoner about half an hour afterwards—I found these shoes in her bundle, and an other pair of her own—she said she took then by mistake, and asked for giveness.
