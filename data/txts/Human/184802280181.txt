961. WILLIAM ROBERTS , stealing 1 pewter pot, value 1 s  ., the goods of John Dooley . JOHN DOOLEY. I am a publican, at 61, Old Bailey. I missed this pewter pot about 11th Jan. ( produced )—I believe I had seen it safe that day. GEORGE WILD( policeman, M  94.) I received information, and went to a house in Hope-place, Borough-road—the prisoner lodged at the top of the house—I saw the landlord, and waited till the prisoner came home at past eleven o'clock at night; and when he opened his room door I walked in after him—he tried to prevent me, but I got in and found two pots—I took him in charge, and on the following morning I found this pot there is a hamper, under a lot of things—he said some one had put it into his room, or I had put it there myself.
   Prisoner. Q.  What time were you there? A.  I had information three days before I went to the house—when you got in, you said, "Good God, I wish I was out of the place"—I said, "I am a constable"—you said, "I thought it was my landlord"—you showed me the room—you did not tell my where the pot was to go. MARY ANN LEECH. I saw the prisoner bend up pots between two pieces of rag on the night before he was taken.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The landlord look the house where I reside, and next day he gave me notice to quit; it did not suit me to move, and he had annoyed me in every way; he took away some furniture of mine and gave me no account of it, and then I was charged with pot-stealing; I was at the Auction mart, and never went home till past eleven o'clock; I could not have taken this pot. GUILTY . Aged 42.— Confined Nine Months .
 962. HENRY TURNER , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. BALDWIN conducted the Prosecution. MARIA DYER. I am servant to Mr. Powell, of Lambeth. On 8th of Feb. the prisoner came for a pennyworth of milk, and gave me half-a-crown—I put it to my teeth; it went like a bit of sand—I sent it next door for change, which I gave the prisoner—the half-crown was afterwards brought back—I could tell it was the same by two marks I made by biting it—the prisoner came again for some more milk—I said, "You gave me a bad half-crown"—he said, "Did I?" and ran away—a man in the yard ran after him—he was caught, a policeman was fetched, and he was given in charge—I marked the half-crown again and gave it to the policeman—this is it—here are the two little dents I made with my teeth.
 ----  QUADIN.  I followed the prisoner, caught him, and gave him in charge—I asked him for the change of the half-crown—he said he gave it to his brother—he wanted me to let him go to his brother's to get the change. WILLIAM SMITH. I am the son of William Smith, a tripe-dresser. On 8th Feb. Dyer gave me a half-crown—I took it to Mrs. Croft—she gave me change. MARY CROFT. William Smith came to me for change—I gave it him—I found the half-crown was bad after he was gone—I took it back to Mr. Powell's—I am sure it was the same. WILLIAM HENRY MAINWARING( policeman, L  86.) It took the prisoner—I found a shilling in his right boot—I gave it to the inspector—I found no other money on him.
