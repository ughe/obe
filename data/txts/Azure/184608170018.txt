JOHNSON, Mayor.
1512. THOMAS LIVERMORE was indicted for embezzlement.
MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
ABRAHAM JOHNSON I keep the Angel tap at Edmonton. The
prisoner was in my service as pot-boy from Nov. last—it was his duty to
receive monies for beer of mine which he supplied, and he was to account to
me for the money at the time be received it—he left my service in July—
after be was gone I found Mrs. Tuffnel had paid him Is. 6d. for beer—he
never gave me that money, nor accounted to me for Is. 7d. from Mr.
Woodward.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. When did the prisoner leave
you? A. On the 7th of July—he took out pots of beer—he was obliged
to account for them in money, except what he left with parties whom I
authorized him to trust—if he served regular customers be need not bring
back their money, till they paid him their week's score—supposing he did not
account for the beer in money, he had to account for it in the book, which I
have here—the names of Tuffnel and Woodward both appear in this book as
persons whom he has trusted—I was not aware that he had trusted a great
many persons without entering it in this book.
LOUIsA TUEPNEL I am the wife of Thomas Tuffnel—we live at Edmon-
ton—I am a customer of Mr. Lamprell's—l used to take beer of the prisoner.
On the 6th of July I paid him 18. 6d. for a week's beer—I gave him half-a-
crown, and he gave me two sixpences out.
THOMAS WOODWARD. I am a customer of tbe prosecutor's—l occasionally
had beer of the prisoner. On the 5th of July I paid him Is. 7d. for beer.
ROBERT BOYLE. I am an offcer of Newgate—l saw the prisoner in cus-
tody there—he handed me this letter—he put his mark to it—it was written
by some fellow-prisoner—I asked if he was satisfied with the contents of it
—be said, Yes," and I put my initials to it.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you know whether it was read to him ? A. No,
I cannot say that.
NOT GUILTY.
