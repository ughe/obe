MILLER, Charles (61, merehant), and SEAR, Joseph (32,
labourer) both stealing six brass pipe connections, the goods of the
Great Western Railway Company, and feloniously receiving the same.
Mr. J. P. Grain prosecuted; Mr. W. H. Leyeester defended Miller.
HENRY JOHN GAZARD. I am chief foreman at the Great Western
Wagon Works at Wormwood Serubbs. On February 13, at six a.m.,
I was on duty looking round unused coaches and horse boxes standing
at the Serubbs Lane siding I found a number of these conneetions
had been taken off. They are connected to each end of the coaches
for the steam to pass from one to the othersteam pipe conneetions
or vacuum connections (specimens were produced in Court) I feel
certain that this piece was cut off for seven-eighths of the way through
and ripped off. The other portion of the rubber that exactly con-
neets with this is in the possession of the company, The removal of
these connections would render the steam heating apparatus useless.
I communicated with the police. I have seen all these things on the
premises of the defendant Miller.
Cross-examined. There would be a very serious accident if our exs.
miners did not notice they were eut away before the coaches went
into traffic. There had been previous thefts of the same kind. These
two big pieces were not found at the prisoner's premises. (The wit-
ness withdrew in order to examine, for the purpose of identification,
other parts produced.)

15 last I went with other officers to the shop of Miller, 82, Silchester
Road, Notting Dale. It is a rag shop. The name up is "C. Miller,
rag dealer." I first saw his son when I walked into the shop. I then
went into the kitchen, where I saw Mrs. Miller, the wife of prisoner
Miller, sitting on a chair. I told her we were not satisfied and we
were going to make a second seareh. I requested her to get up from
the chair, which she very reluetantly did. Under the chair Was a
pail containing broken metal-the one produced by the last witness.
