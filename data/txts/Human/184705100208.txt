 1308. JAMES FLANAGAN and GEORGE ELDERKIN were indicted for stealing 9 bagatelle balls, value 30 s  ., the goods of William Rogers . SAMUEL WELLS. I am waiter to William Rogers, at the White Hart pub lic-house, in St. Giles's—he keeps a bagatelle board—on the 24th of March the prisoners came there to play, in the afternoon, and went away again—they came again after eleven o'clock—I left them playing about half-past eleven—I was called away, and was away about seven minutes—I then returned, the prisoners were gone, and the gas was put out—on one else had been in the room—the nine bagatelle balls which had been there were gone—they have not been found.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  You went down to attend to the bell? A.  Yes, on the first floor—the bagatelle balls were on the second flour—I had not been playing—I was standing there—there was no one playing but the prisoners—there had been several persons there, but they were all gone—no one else could have gone up without my hearing them NOT GUILTY.
 1309. JAMES FLANAGAN and GEORGE ELDERKIN were again  indicted for stealing 9 bagatelle balls, value 30 s  .; the goods of Charles✗ Runey . CHARLES RUNEY. I keep a beer-shop at Vauxhall. On the 1st of April the prisoners came there about four o'clock—they went into a room where I keep a bagatelle board—they asked where the balls were—I gave them two red balls and seven white ones—they called for a pint of ale—soon afterwards Flanagan came out for another pint of ale and a screw of tobacco—he took it into the room and shut the door—they were there some time, when a neigh bour came and told me they had gone out of my window—I pursued them—they ran and I ran, but I lost them—when I came back my bagatelle balls were gone—no one else had been in the room.
   FLANAGAN  — GUILTY. Aged 20.
   ELDERKIN  — GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Six Months .
 1310. RICHARD LETTS was indicted for stealing 1 hat, value 1 s  .; 1 pair of shoes, 6 s  .; 4lbs. weight of beef, 4 s  ., 1 loaf of bread, 4 d  .; and 1 to bacco-box, 8 d  .; the goods of William Cowland ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. WILLIAM COWLAND. I live at Pleasant Retreat, Brixton-hill. I am a brick maker—the prisoner came to lodge with me on the 27th of Feb.—he lodged there twp nights, and left just before four o'clock in the morning, on the 1st of March—I came down and missed my hat, my shoes, 4lbs. weight of beef, a 2lb. loaf, and a tobacco-pipe—no one else had been there—I found the prisoner in April, at the Three Goats' Heads, in the Walworth-road—he had my hat on his head—he had left his own hat on the table at my house—this is my hat.
