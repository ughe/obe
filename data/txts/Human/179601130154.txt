 153.  JOHN CHESHIRE was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 14th of January , a woollen cloth cloak, value 10s. the property of William Brown.
   SARAH BROWN sworn.
 I am the wife of William Brown; my husband is a  stone-mason; he lives in West Smithfield: On the 14th of January, about a quarter before eleven in the forenoon, I came home with some potatoes from market, I went out again for some turnips; I pulled off my cloak, and hung it over a chair; I returned in two minutes; and when I went in at the door, I saw that young man with my cloak doubled up under his arm, coming out of my room; I directly laid hold of him, and asked him what he was going to do with it; he said he was only going to look at it, and I would not let him go, till he threw down the cloak; he knocked me down several times, but I never let him go till I got assistance.
 Q. Did you know any thing of him? - A. No; I never saw him before that I know of.
 - WYLLIE sworn.
 I am a patrole belonging to St. Sepulchre's; I was sent for to take the prisoner into custody; he is a drover in Smithfield, (produces the cloak.)
 Prosecutrix. This is my cloak, I am very certain of it.
 The prisoner made no defence, but called  Jonathan Crow, a foot-dealer, and Thomas Cox, a drover, who gave him a good character.
     GUILTY. (Aged 16.)
 Tried by the London Jury, before Mr. COMMON SERGEANT. [Imprisonment. See summary.] [Fine. See summary.]
 154.  WILLIAM BURRAMS was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 24th of December , a silver watch, value 1l. 1s. the property of John Fuller.(The prisoner being deaf and dumb, the Jury were sworn to try whether he stood mute of his own obstinacy. or by the visitation of God.
   SARAH BURRAMS sworn.
 I am the mother of the prisoner; he was born deaf and dumb.
 Q. You can make him understand you by signs? - A. Yes; I asked him about the watch, and he told me he did not steal it.
 Q. He can write? - A. He writes from a copy, but he never writes his name right.(The Jury found that he stood mute, by the visitation of God).(The prisoner's mother was sworn as an interpreter).
   ROBERT BLASSON sworn.
 I am a shopman to Mr. Fuller, a hardwareman, at Smithfield-bars: On the 24th of December, between nine and ten in the evening, the prisoner came into our shop and pointed to the window; I thought he wanted some buckles, I shewed him some, and he shook his head; he then pointed up higher to the watches, and I took down three or four watches and shewed him.
 Q. Did you know him before? - A. No, I never saw him before; he opened some of the watches and then pointed to some more, and while I was turning about to get another, he ran away with a silver one; I ran under the counter as fast as I could, and cried out stop thief; I ran across Smithfield, and just before I came up to him, he chucked it down as hard as he could; it was a moonlight night, and I was very near him, and somebody picked it up, I don't know who, and gave it to me; I did not stop to pick it up, because I was determined to catch him; here is a mark in it where he threw it down; I delivered him to the constable, and he was taken before a Magistrate.
   SAMUEL BLADEN sworn.
 I am an extra watchman; I heard the cry of stop thief in Smithfield; the prisoner was taken before I came up, the prosecutor had hold of him, and gave me charge of him; I took him to the watch-house, and he was committed to the Compter, he wrote his name in the watch-house.
   SAMUEL ROBERTS sworn.
 The prisoner was brought to me to St. Sepulchre's watch-house, and was charged with stealing a watch; I understood that he was deaf and dumb; I said, how should I know his name, he immediately got up and wrote his name, (produces it); whether he heard me, or whether it was by the motions of my mouth, I cannot say. GUILTY.
 He was recommended to mercy by the Jury, on account of his infirmity. Whipped and discharged.
 Tried by the London Jury, before The LORD CHIEF BARON.
