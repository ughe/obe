 1815.  DANIEL McLANE was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 1 hat, value 5s., the goods of David Hyams.
 DAVID HYAMS. I am a  clothes-dealer, and live in Wardour-street. I was at my door on the 8th of August - I saw somebody put a hand in, and take a hat from my door; I did not see who it was, but I went out and saw the prisoner running - I called Stop thief! and my neighbour brought him back; the prisoner ran about thirty houses - I saw him stopped; I have not recovered the hat - there was another boy whom I saw.
   WILLIAM RUFFLE. I live with my father near the prosecutor's. On the 8th of August I saw the prisoner take the hat out and give it to his companion, who ran off; I did not see which way he ran, but I saw the prisoner run - I am sure he did not walk; I was looking out of window - I came running down to the door; I am sure the prisoner is the boy who took it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was walking up the street, and saw people running - I ran, and a man stopped me.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 1816.  THOMAS PEGG was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , seven 5l. Bank notes, the property of Duncan Thompson.
 SECOND COUNT, stating them to be the property of  Jane Thompson.
 MR. SHARPE conducted the prosecution.
 MR.  JOSEPH WINNY GULL. I did live in Upper Seymour-street. but I removed from there, and left Jane Thompson, who had been my servant, in care of the house. I did not know the prisoner was there - I never gave him permission to reside there; I paid Jane Thompson 15l. in the middle of July, and at the beginning of August 20l., taking then a receipt for 35l. - they were in 5l. notes, some of which came from Messrs. Barclay and Co., and some from Mr. Lanson; I did not take the numbers.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you given this young woman permission to let any man sleep in the house? A. No - I knew a man of the name of McKenzie, but he is dead; I have known the prisoner since the beginning of 1826 - he was my servant for ten or eleven months in 1826, and I gave him a good character, and since then have taken him into my service.
 MR. SHARPE. Q. Was not McKenzie a servant of yours? A. He was - I made no stipulation for either the prisoner or McKenzie to stay in the house; I thought it better that Thompson should have some female to stay - McKenzie was not then in my service; I remember now that there was an accusation against the prisoner - a female servant in my house accused him of having stolen two guineas, or 2l.; I investigated the matter, and found no proof - I thought it extremely improbable, and dismissed it from my mind.
   JANE THOMPSON. I lived with Mr. Gull as housekeeper; I received 35l. from him; my husband was a captain in the West India service, and sailed in a vessel which has never been heard of - McKenzie had been in Mr. Gull's service, and he was at that time up from Scotland to get a situation; Mr. Gull gave him leave to be in the house, and the prisoner came to the house - I told him to stop and have a cup of tea with me after the family were gone; he said Mr. Gull sent him there to see McKenzie, who was there at tea - he said he did not know where to get a lodging, and I said he might stay in the house; the last time I saw my money was at one o'clock, on the 4th of August, when the landlord and his wife were at the house with four ladies - McKenzie came and knocked at the door, and asked if the prisoner was ready to go out; my money was at that time in a trunk in my room; I took it away at night, and put it there in the morning - the prisoner took a shirt to go up stairs; I went up to the room, and saw him sitting on the bed cutting his toe-nails - I retired from the room, and went up stairs with the ladies and the landlady to look at the house; the prisoner went into the room a second time - McKenzie said to him, "Are you not ready yet;" they then went out: about six o'clock I missed my money - no other person had been in the room but the prisoner; I found my two trunks tumbled about - the prisoner had seen me put my money in the trunk the day I received it; when he left the house he said he should not be long, but he never returned.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you permission from Mr. Gull to let any man sleep in the house? A. No, but I told him they were going to sleep there, and he never said they were or they were not - he said he would send a woman to sleep there; I will swear I told Mr. Gull that I was going to let the prisoner and McKenzie sleep in the house - he made no answer, or if he did, he made such an answer as no one could understand; I told Mrs. Gull I was going to pay a bill with the notes, but I paid it with some other money - I told her I was going to pay 20l. to a person in Regent-street, but I did not pay it out of the notes; there was no other person in the house, but the prisoner and McKenzie, the landlady, four ladies, and a little girl; a man came to the door for some linen. but he did not come in - the washer-woman was not in the house that day; I never said so, that I recollect - if I did say so before the Magistrate, it was a mistake; a man came after the furniture, but he came no further than the hall - I gave no warning when I went into the room, where the prisoner was cutting his toe nails; he was preparing to dress himself - his trousers were on; he came down into the kitchen, a minute or two afterwards; before that day he had dressed in the butler's pantry - he had slept in the room till he took the money, and then he never came again: his boxes were left in the house with his clothes and all his things - they were searched, but nothing was found; Mc Kenzie was taken up - he was acquitted and died in three days afterwards; I always said he was innocent.
 MR. SHARPE. Q. On the morning of the robbery, the prisoner was in the house? A. Yes, at half-past eleven o'clock, in the room where the trunk and money were; no other person was in the room that day - he did not go out dressed - he left his shirt in the room.
