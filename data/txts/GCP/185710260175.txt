CAROLINE LEE. I am the wife of James Lee, who lives at Stratford.
On Wednesday, 21st Oct., & pig came into my house I was ligbting my
fire, and I heard a noise in the front room; I turned round, and saw two
pigs thereI drove them out; I had lighted my fire when one came in
again, and I drove it out into the back yard—I let it remain until my hus-
bånd carne home-it broke a glass lamp, standing on the table, and a case of
stuffed birds- I was trying to drive it out with a broom, when my husband
came home I told him a stray pig had come in; he asked me what mischief
it had done-I told him; he said, “Let it remain," and he would send to
the police station, and give information about it-it was there from the
Wednesday until the Friday—on that day the prisoner came for the pig my
husband was not at home, and I gave him the pig to keep until the owner
was found; that was the agreement between my husband and him.

JAMES LEE. I live at Stratford, and am foreman in Mr. Burford's manu-
factory. I have been there ever since I was nine years of age when I
came home on the day in question, I asked who broke the case of birds;
my wife said a pig came in the place and ran underneath the table, and upset
the case I was very angry, and said I would keep the pig until I found
the owner, and would make him pay for it-I asked my lodger to write a
letter for me to take to the station house, on the Thursday; he said
he would take it; be delayed it I asked him for it on Friday, he gave it
to me, and I took it to the station as soon as I had done work, about
9 o'clock-I went to where my brother-in-law works, and asked him if he
would take the pig until I could find an owner, as I had got no place to
keep it he did so—when I went to the station they said they knew nothing
about any pig.
