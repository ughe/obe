In his defence, Cross said that he acted honestly, and had given notice
twice, and therefore his conduct was satisfactory.
Heywood said the shortness in cash and stock was caused by mistakes and
stock used in the house which was unaccounted for, and by over measure.
He got a character from Watson, who had been his manager, and he had
acted honestly.
Ballard said that he had worked for Cooksey on and of nearly eighteen
months, and had tried to retrieve his character. He had had no barmen
visit him, and his landlord, Rushin, knew of his prepious trouble. He was
charged with stealing sixpence, and punisherl, but had nothing to do with
any conspiracy. The falling off in stock was due to the drinking habits of
the landlord's family, and barmaids and potmen, who had ten to fifteen
drinks a day.
