(Defence.)
MORRIS RUBINI, 14, Umberton Street, Commercial Road, boot-
maker. Prisoner has been in my employment seven weeks as a boot-
maker. On Friday, July 19, I paid him a half sovereign in gold at
dinner time. He said he wanted to buy a piece of leather.
SAMUEL Low, 34, Winthrop Street, tailor's presser. On July 19
I met prisoner in the Lane. He bought some cherries, changed &
half-sovereign, and received silver like the coins pfoduced, which
be showed me and I thought it was good.
Cross-examined. The coins produced ring well to me. I went
to the Lane to buy a suit for my boy. Prisoner asked me to look at
the money and see if it was good. There were three florins, two
shillings, coppers, and some sixpences. I said it was good money.
I have known prisoner six or seven weeks. He is a bootmaker.
Verdict, Not guilty.
SAINT, John (42, labourer); uttering counterfeit onin twice on
same day well knowing the same to be counterfeit.
Mr. Sands prosecuted. Mr. A. J. Lawrie defended.
ELIZABETH WILKINSON, of the "Albion," St. Paul's Road, Burdett
Road. I have known prisoner for some time as a customer. On
July 29 between one and two p.m. he came with some friends and
was served with drink. He asked for the bagatelle balls to play at
the table, and gave florin produced as a deposit, which I put aside.
Cross-examined. I know Smith as a customer and a companion
of the prisoner.
RICHARD SMITH, 19, St. Paul's Road, carman. On July 29 I was
with prisoner in the bagatelle room at the "Albion." He asked me!
to get a pot of ale on the game and gave me a 2s. piece which I took
to Wilkinson, who said it was bad.
Cross-examined. Prisoner said he must have got it from his em
ployer, and that perbaps the one he had given to Mrs. Wilkinson
was bad too. Wilkinson then looked at the other 25. piece and sent
for a policeman. Prisoner waited. He asked for the coins to take
them back to the place he had got them from.
THOMAS WILKINSON, licensee of the "Albion." On July 29 Smith
brought me the florin produced from the prisoner. I went into the
bagatelle room and said, “This is a bad one, Jack." He said, "I.
know where I got it from. Give it me back." I said, "I cannot do
that." He said, "I gave the wife one as a deposit on the balls. Take
out of that." That was for the beer that had been called for. I
then took the other coin from the wagon where it was placed and said,
* This is another one-& bad one too." Prisoner said. "Give them to
me back. I got them from my governor." I said, "That is more
than I dare do. I am going to break them." Then to convince him
they were bad I put some acid on them, and said, "If you demand
them, if you must have them, I must send for the police." Prisoner
said, "Send for them." He was swearing. I then fetched a police
man.
