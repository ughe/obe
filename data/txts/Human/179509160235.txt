   WILLIAM MILLER sworn.
 I drive the Tottenham stage, for Mr. Kimpton; I have drove it near these twenty years; I live at  Tottenham. The 15th of this month, Tuesday sevenight, when I came out of my back door, I see a man standing with a bundle under his arm; I says to him, what do you want? he makes answer and says to me, nothing. That was Smith I believe, but I am not certain, because I did not take him, and I did not take particular notice when I was at the gate. I see Mrs. Gillam's gate open and door open; it was the back part of the house; there is a little bit of ground called a yard to it; and I calls out Mrs. Gillam! and nobody answered. I went then to the back door, and as I went to the back door I see two men come out of the shop, and I asked them where Mrs. Gillam was? and they made answer up stairs; one of the two did; but positively I could not tell which in that time, and I says to him, then you shall stop till Mrs. Gillam comes; when they both made for the gate to get away as fast as they could; I catched hold of one, the beggest of the two, Tooke, and I never broke from him, nor he from me till such times as I brought him down and was on him.
 Q. You secured him? - Yes, by myself. The other ran away; there was nobody near at all to assist at that time; there were several handkerchiefs on the ground as he lay, as we were both down.
 Q. What is Mrs. Gillam? - She keeps a linen draper's shop, nothing else.
 Q. Do you mean to say that the handkerchiefs dropped from this man? - Yes; they dropped from him out of the breast part. The constable has them.
 Q. Do you know which way these men got in this house? - At the back door.
 Q. Could they have got in at the front? - No, it was fastened.
 Q. Do you know any thing of the other man that got away? - Not to swear to him.
 Q. What time of the day was this? -About a quarter after eight in the morning, as near as can be.
   FREDERIC TANSLEY sworn.
 I am a shoe-maker, at Tottenham. About eight o'clock in the morning, or a trifle after, Tuesday week, the 15th of this month, I see Smith go by with a bundle under his arm, by my house; I live about six or seven doors from Mrs. Gillam, about seventy yards, not much more. I suspected that he had stole this bundle. As such Miller sent for me to take Tooke in charge, and he informed me that Mrs. Gillam was robbed; and as such I said, I see a man go by with a bundle under his arm, and I did not doubt but he was the person that stole the property; as such I sent a young man, named Wagstaff, after Smith immediately; and he took the man with the property under his arm, against the place which we call the George and Vulture, in Tottenham; as such he brought him back to me immediately, and I searched his pockets, and in his pockets I found five pair of stockings, and three pair of childrens shoes buttoned up in the flap of his breeches. I have got the property now in court.(Produced.)
