WALTER LIZALL( Police Serjeant, L ). On March 31st I was called to the New Bridge House and found the prisoner in the bar—Mr. Huss said he had tendered a bad five-shilling piece—he said, "What! Do you think I should do that with two constables outside?"—I searched him, and found one shilling and threepence in copper—I took him to the station, after which the other charge was mentioned, and Miss Perry was sent for—she picked him out of eight others—the public-houses are about a quarter of a mile apart. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to Her Majesty's Mint—these coins are both counterfeit, but from different molds.
   Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate: "I own to uttering the first, but I did not know it was bad. The second I know nothing about. I do not know the man; I have a witness as to that."
   Prisoner's defence:  You know men are liable to get drunk. I thought the coin was good, but was given in charge. When I was at the station the first witness saw me between two constables, and when she came to identify me she said, "That is the man "Why did she not identify me in the street? There are plenty of people in the street like me. GUILTY — Nine Months' Hard Labour.
 343. JOSEPH WINNING  (19) , Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. PARTRIDGE Prosecuted. FLORENCE HILBERT. I am a draper with my sister at 136, Mintone Road, Wimbledon—on March 25th the prisoner came in for a 2 1/2 d. handker chief and gave me this crown—I gave it to my sister—she went out of the shop and I wanted to detain the prisoner—I kept him in conversation—I wrapped the handkerchief up, but be went out before my sister came back, and ran away without waiting for his change or the handkerchief—when my sister came back I gave his description to the police—I after wards picked him out from twelve others—I am sure he is the same boy—he never came back for his change. ANNIE HILBERT. I am sister to the last witness—on March 25th I came into the shop and saw a lad—I am not quite certain of the prisoner—I took the five-shilling piece to get change—I saw the prisoner after wards with four others at the Police-court—I was not certain; but I thought he was the boy. ROBERT WILLIAMSON( Detective Officer ). On March 25th Florence Hilbert gave me a description—I circulated it among the police—she also gave me a crown piece. FLORENCE DUCHAL. I assist my father, who keeps a fancy shop at 40, North Road, Battersea—about 7.30 one evening the prisoner, to the best of my belief, came in for a threepenny mouth organ, and gave me a bad five-shilling piece—my mother came in—I passed it to her—she said it was bad—the boy said, "Oh, is it?" and picked it up and walked out—I gave information—we had two bad pieces before that—Carter, the constable, has them—my father went to the station—I was sent for, and picked the prisoner out from about twelve others.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  This was about seven o'clock—it was later than 6.30—I cannot say whether it was past seven.
