   MR. CARTISN . Q.  Was it the first time you saw the letters on the rule that you spoke to Byfield about it? A.  Two or three daya after, the first time I got sight of him—the indictment was found on the Monday in the last Sessions, about twelve or one o'clock—I applied for the warrant on Tuesday, and it was put into my hand about four o'clock—it was executed that night, I believe.
   JURY . Q.  Did you find the rest of their tools? A.  Yes, but I would almost as lief decline answering anything—I bad missed property, and had accused them—we generally find tools, but in this case I was to find every thing, except wax for casting, and the other tools carried in a basket. DAVID REEVE. I live at Brixton. I have worked for Mr. Cooper—I made this rule, and sent it to the building—I know it—( looking at it )—it is just as it was when I made it—I bad some words about it with Mr. Cooper—I said it would never be fit for a floating-rale—I said it was a very shaky  piece, not proper for the purpose—I am sure this is the rule—it is not exactly in the same state, it is a little narrowed at each end—it was straight all along when I gave it to the boy to take down—I made it about twelve months ago last April.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is it worth? A.  Not a great deal, something under 10 s .; I suppose it is worth about 2 s . 6 d . or 3 s .—what I know it by is this flaw, which it shakes with—I described it as not fit for the purpose—I am quite sure I did not swear that I made it three yean ago—I did not all the Magistrate it was three years—I do not know that I said it was two years—it was about twelve months last April.
   JURY . Q.  Do you know it by anything but the shake? A.  No, sir—I can swear to the wood, as being the wood I prepared. JOHN MILLER. I am a bricklayer. I worked for Mr. Cooper last May—I worked for a short time at the building where the prisoners were at work—I remember, about May, Byfield came there, and Mr. Cooper accused him of stealing the rule—he asked him why he put bis name on the rule—Byfield said it was his—Mr. Cooper said it was not, it was a rule belonging to himself—I do not recollect anything else. PETER CHARLES. I am a bricklayer. I was at work at Mr. Cooper's buildings in May. I remember Mr. Cooper asking Byfield what business he had to put his name on his rule—Byfield said it was his rule; and he said as far as this, that it was bis intention to take the rule away—Mr. Cooper said, "It is mine, and you may depend, if you take that rule away, I will lock you up"—Byfield said he would insist on taking it George Brydges (police-constable P 128.) I was in Coalharbour-lane on the 7th of August—I saw Haines with this rule, and I took him into custody by Mr. Cooper's direction—I afterwards saw Byfield, and took him—I took both the prisoners to the station—I was at the police-office afterwards—the Magistrate said Mr. Cooper might take his rule.
