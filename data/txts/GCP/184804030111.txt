Siæth Session, 1847–48.
1124. HENRY NICKS, stealing 1 watch, 1. key, and 1 box, value 211.;
the goods of Henry Wilson; in his dwelling-house.
Mr. Platt conducted the Prosecution.
MARY ANN WILSON. I am the wife of Henry Wilson, who keeps the
Collett Arms, White Horse-street, Limehouse.On 22nd March, between
twelve and one o'clock, I had a gold watch on the mantel-piece, in my bed-
room, on the first floor between three and four, Mr. Jackson, a customer,
who I had known some time, and the prisoner, came into the house, and
went up stairs into the long-room, which is on the left of my bedroom-after
they were gone, about six o'clock, I went up and missed the watch it was
in case, and had a key attached to it-this is it produced)-it is worth
twenty guineas--I should think this produced) is the key--it is very
similar, and winds the watch up.
Cross-examined by Me. METCALFE. Q. How do you know the watch ? A. It
was made for me, and I have handled it daily since 1847-Henry Lads, who
is here, is the maker--I had seen the name on it before I lost it, and the
number is 1487-I had noticed that many times. It
HENRY LADs. I am a watchmaker, at 65, Murray-street, Hoxton-
made this watch, "No. 1487, Henry Lads," for Mr. Wilson-I always
sumber them--there is also "Mary Ann Wilson" inside it, spelt with only
one "7."
JOHN JACKSON. I live at Clark-street, Stepney. On 22nd March I accom-

with Mrs. Wilson's permission we went up, I in advance I had been up

the landing, and the bedroom door being partly open, I saw him walk round
at the foot of the bed, and be met me in the passage-he came from the room
-I said, "Nicks, it is very odd of you ; what business had you in that room?"

had no business there, I only brought you to look at this room"-we went
into the room, which is a club-room, looked round it, came down again, and
we parted
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known him? 4. Several years
-he was a publican on Clerkenwell-green--the last house I knew him in
was the Cricketers, at Ratcliffe-cross-after that he was in a beer-shop, in
Union-street, Bishopsgate- I have nothing to say against his character-I
know his mother very well; she is a retired victualler, and a woman of con-
siderable property, I believe I have seen the prisoner drunk occasionally-
