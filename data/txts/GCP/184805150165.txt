1441. JAMES DOWDING and JAMES HILLESLEY, stealing 18lbs.
weight of copper, value 8s.; the goods of William John Loat, their master.
Mr. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution.
William John Loat. I am a builder, at Clapham---Hillesley was my
wagoner four or five months, and Dowding my gardener and odd-man. On
30th March I directed Hillesley to take the cart to Nine Elms; and on 6th
April, in consequence of something I heard, I went to a stable on my pre-
mises, and found a piece of copper, worth 78. or 88.-I had not missed it; it
had been in a closet or cupboard, in a shed, which was always locked-the
padlock appeared locked and perfect-it was fastened by a screw-staple, but
a person could take it out and put it much in the same state that it was
in before.
Cross-examined by Mr. Robinson. Q. Have you the copper here ! A.
This is it (produced) I did not know how much there was in the place
probably I never should have missed any, but this had been the cover of a
roof-this is a very common form for old copper to be in-Dowding has been
in my service seven or eight years I had a very high opinion of him.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did Hillesley drive the cart ? A.
Yes-- he lived with his family, at Balham-hill-he has been bailed, and has
surrendered to take his trial.
