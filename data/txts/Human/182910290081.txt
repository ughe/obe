 2046.  JAMES CARTWRIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of October , 1 trowel, value 2s., the goods of Joseph Taylor ; and 1 trowel, value 1s., the goods of Thomas Taylor ; and that he was in July, in the 8th year of his present Majesty's Reign, convicted of felony.
   JOSEPH TAYLOR. I am a bricklayer, and was working at an unfinished building in Percy-street, Kingsland-road. On the 16th of October, when I went to dinner, I left my trowel concealed in a new drain in the back yard - the prisoner had no right there; when I returned I missed it - I went to several pawnbrokers and could not find it; I then went to Mr. Thimbleby's, and the prisoner was there offering it to pawn.
   JOHN WILLIAM WINTLE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - this is the trowel.
   HENRY FREAK. I live with Mr. Thimbleby. The prisoner brought this trowel, and pawned it on the 16th of October: he came again and brought this other; one the prosecutor came in and claimed it.
   THOMAS TAYLOR. I am a bricklayer, and was at work at the same place. This is my trowel, which I lost from there.
   RICHARD CONSTANTINE. I am a constable. I have a certificate of the prisoner's conviction in this Court in July, by the name of James Bates; I took the prisoner, and know he is the person - he was ordered to be whipped and discharged.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Transported for Life.
 2047.  SARAH ELIZA TATTON was indicted for bigamy.
 ANN TATTON. I have a son named  William Tatton- he was married to the prisoner about seven years ago, on the 28th of August, at St. Mary, Lambeth; she was married, as a widow, in the name of Sarah Eliza Webb- I do not know what she was, but I believe she took in needle-work; my son was a porter- he had been acquainted with her four or five months; she had one child five years old when she married - they had no children; I do not know whether they separated by mutual consent, but he allowed her 5s. a week after they had lived together two years; I have a copy of the register, which I got from the church myself on the 3rd of July, 1828; the reason I have kept it so long is, that she was in prison, and denied knowing such a person as William Tatton; I was present at the marriage (certificate read).
 Cross-examined by J. ALLEY. Q. Who did you get the certificate from? A. From the minister himself - his name is on it; my son went to sea three years ago - he was gone fourteen months, and returned in the Ganges; the prisoner did not tell me after he was gone, hat she found a letter of his, saying he had left her for ever - I do not know whether he allowed her any support while he has been away the last three years, but the first voyage he did - he is here now.
   BONDFIELD BRYANT. I am a carpenter. I married the prisoner at St. Mary, Islington, on the 2nd of October, 1827- she represented herself as a widow, by the name of Sarah Webb; I heard Mr. Webb was dead - I did not hear of the other husband; that she stated she had plenty of furniture, but I found none at all.
   WILLIAM MORGAN. I am a Police-officer. I have the certificate of the two marriages; I took the prisoner at her brothers's house on the other side of Stepney church (certificate read).
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Six Weeks.
 2048.  SAMUEL ADAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of October , 1 coat, value 12s.; 1 hat, value 8s.; 1 snuff-box, value 1s., and 1 pocket-book, value 1s., the goods of James Collins.
   ABRAHAM BARKER. I keep the Bacchus, public-house at Hoxton. I was in the skittle-ground about five or six weeks back; Mr. Collins had been there two or three times; and that day he pulled off his coat and hat, left them in the Dutchpin box, and went into another ground; I heard a person had come over the wall; I went out and saw the prisoner; he gave these things into my arms and ran away; I had known him very well by his coming to the ground.
 Prisoner. Q. What time was it? A. From half-past five to half-past six o'clock; it was between the lights - you threw the things into my arms - I did not stay three seconds; you are the person who gave them to me - I took them back to Mr. Collins; I did not see you again till you were at the office - it might be four or five weeks afterwards.
   JAMES COLLINS. I was at the skittle-ground on the 14th of October, and took off my coat and hat in the Dutchpin ground - I went to another ground, and heard a person drop off the wall on the other side; I looked, and missed my things - I had not seen the prisoner then, but Barker gave me my things; the coat had the pocket-book and snuff-box in the pocket.
   JOHN FRYER. The prisoner was described to me; I found him on the 15th of October in Hoxton - he ran away, and was stopped by another person; when I came up, he said, "Do you want me for the Bacchus job?" I said Yes.
 Prisoner's Defence. I believe another officer can prove to the contrary; I never was in the skittle-ground that night - do you think, when he saw me only three seconds, and did not see me again for five weeks, he could know me again.?
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Nine Months,
