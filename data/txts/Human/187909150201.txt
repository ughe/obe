   Cross-examined.  He was very fond of his wife, and a very good husband, and very patient—he came into the street twice to try to induce her to come indoors—she would not go, and he could not move her in—I did not see him come out undressed—he is a labourer—I was not drunk, nor was she—we had been two hours in a public-house with one pot of beer. TIMOTHY DALY. I am a labourer, and live at 4, Currie Street, Nine Elms—I knew the prisoner and his wife—on Monday night I was going home, and saw Mrs. Kilby and Mrs. Norman sitting on the kerb—Mrs. Norman bad the baby—she spoke to me; I could not tell whether she was sober or not—I sat down by them—shortly afterwards the prisoner came out of his house in his night-shirt without his trousers or boots—he asked his wife whether she was coming indoors—she said "No, go away you b——thing"—she used some very bad language to him—he said nothing, but walked upstairs—in about 10 minutes he came out again dressed, and he had his boots on, and I saw him come up and give his wife a kick somewhere about the groin—I could hardly tell what sort of a kick it was—she fell back, and did not speak—afterwards she said "Bill, Bill, you have done for me"—he made no reply—I don't know what became of him—a constable came up shortly afterwards—I saw blood where the woman was lying—a doctor was sent for—I afterwards heard her say "Bill, Bill, kiss me," or "speak to me"—he had come up from round the corner—he did kiss her—she was then taken to the hospital.
   Cross-examined.  I did not see any one making a mockery of the prisoner when he came out in his night shirt—she used bad language to him before he gave her the kick. JOHN GARLAND( Policeman W  383). I was on duty in Currie Street, shortly before 2 o'clock—I saw the deceased lying on the pavement, as if she had fallen back—she said, "He has done for me"—she bled very much from the lower part of her body—I raised her up, but I saw it was doing injury and I laid her down again—a doctor came and examined her and ordered her removal to the hospital, and when we arrived there in the cab she was dead—I took the prisoner into custody. THOMAS BAKER. I am a student at Charing Cross Hospital—I live with Dr. Sutcliffe, of Battersea Park Road—on the morning of 26th August, abont 2 30, the police fetched me; I found the woman lying in Currie Street in a dying state, she was in a state of collapse—she had lost a large quantity of blood, I should think three pints—it came from the vagina—I had her put into a cab and sent to the hospital; it might have been caused by a kick. KANIERO POKAKO. I am one of the house surgeons at St. Thomas' Hospital—on Monday night or Tuesday morning I received the deceased from the police—she was quite dead and her clothes were saturated with blood—I afterwards examined the body—the organs were healthy—there was a lacerated wound on the left side of the vagina, an inch and one-eighth in length and about an inch deep—it was such an injury as might be caused by a violent kick—she died from loss of blood arising from that injury.
   The Prisoner received a good character. GUILTY  . Strongly recommended to mercy by the JURY on account of the provocation he received. — Three Months' Imprisonment.
