408. EDMUND WEBBER was indicted for embezzlement.
MR. BODKIn conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES Kirton. I am a corn-chandler, and live in Charles-street, Middle-
sex Hospital. I am a customer of Giles and Co.-on the 11th of Dec. I
owed them 561. 6s., which I paid to the prisoner, in notes and gold, on the
11th of Dec.-I had known him as Giles's clerk some years.
Henry Aste. I am a corn-chandler, and live in Tottenham Court-road.
I deal with Mr. Giles-on the 5th of Oct. I purchased to the amount of
6261. 118. 4d., which became due two months after, on the 5th of Dec.--I paid
it to the prisoner on the 4th of Dec., being market-day-on the 7th of Oct. I
purchased to the amount of 7181. 11s. 10d. of Mr. Giles, which became due
on the 7th of Dec.- I paid that to the prisoner on the 7th of Dec., by this
check.
FREDERICK KERCHEVALL. I am chief clerk to Mr. James Sharpe Giles-
he has no partner. The prisoner has been in his employ about eight years-
he was a clerk under me-it was part of his duty to collect money from cus-
tomers- this is what we call our long-book, used at market, containing the
original entries of what sales are made on the particular dates-I find on the
5th of Oct. a sale to Aste, 6261. 11s. 4d; and on the 7th of Oct.,
7181. 11s. 10d.--those sums became payable at two months after the sales—it
was the prisoner's duty to collect them he kept the book produced for that
purpose-it is his collecting-book-it is his duty to make it out from the
market-book-to copy it from the market-book-here is no entry in the col-
lecting-book, on the 5th of Oct., of Mr. Aste's transaction_his name is not
mentioned-on the 12th he has an entry, 6261. 118. 4d., as if the sale took
place on that day-there is no entry whatever on the 7th- there is no entry
of the 7181. odd on the 7th, and that is not in the book at all-bere is another
book, which we call the market cash-book, containing the various sums received
from the different buyers on the different dates stated, together with the trans-
actions to which it refers-whoever received money at market would give it to
the prisoner, and before he went to the banker's, in the afternoon, he would
enter the various sums, which is done by patting the checks on the City
banker's in an alphabetical list, first casting them up, and then entering the
amounts, making the exact amount received-it was bis duty to enter all
sums he received on account of Mr. Giles—the other clerks who received
would enter in this book, and give the money to the prisoner-there are
several handwritings in it-the prisoner would see that he had the money, and
write on the opposite page the different kinds of money of which it was com-
posed, checks, notes, or cash-he ought to enter on the credit side of the book
the sum paid into the banker's-I find no entry of 561. 6s. from Kirton,
neither the name nor amount--the entries of that day, except two, are in the
prisoner's writing the discharge on the other side is his writing-be paid
into the bank that day 1,508l. 14s. 3d.-here is another amount afterwards of
