941. FRANCIS VERENDER, stealing 1 gallon of rum, value 12. on

942. JAMES GARRATT, stealing 1 box, value 28. ; and 841bs. Weight
of cocoa, 21.; the goods of the London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway
- 2nd Count, of Francis Chambers and another.--3rd Count, of Charles
Pascoe Greenfell and others; having been before convicted.
MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution.
JOSHUA NUNN. I am warehouseman to Francis Chambers and another,
in Tower Royal, wholesale tea and cocoa-dealers. On 26th Jan. I sent
box, containing cocoa, by our carman, to the Nag's Head, Borough
received this receipt for it-it was to be sent to the railway-station-this is
it (looking at it)--the value of the cocoa and box was about two guinea-
this was a tea-chest originally-if it bad tea in it, it would have been more
valuable.
JAMES Woods. I am warehouseman, at the Nag's Head, Borough. On
the evening of 26th Jan. a chest was brought there for Stone and Son, Nen.
haven--next day, about eleven or twelve o'clock, I put it on a van, to go to
the Newcross-station.
GEORGE GROVES. I live at Newcross, and am porter to the South Coast
Railway. I was on duty at the station, on 27th Jan., when the carman
brought the chest, about twelve o'clock in the day-it was put on the bottom
of the platform, by itself, as it was the only thing we had for Newhaven-1
saw it there between four and five o'clock, and I was the only porter on the
platform--the prisoner had come down with a load of butter for Brighton,
and while waiting to be uploaded he came on the platform, where I was
working, and looked at the chest-he had no business on my platforms
goods were on the Brighton platform-I was at the other end, loading some
iron-I kept any eye on him-he looked at the chest I cannot say whether
he weighted it or not, but there was a bag of coffee close by, and be went
and felt that - he walked off, and he went and unloaded the butter on the
Brighton platform-I missed the chest in about a quarter of an hour.
Cross-examined by MR. Parry. Q. Were you on the platform alone!
A. I was the only porter there the man who was delivering the iron to be
was there, and there were three or four other carmen there with their carts
waiting to be uploaded the platform is from eighty to a hundred feet long
and I was at the end the prisoner came up the steps at the other end, where
the chest was standing there were not many bales and boxes of gouds-
there was a bag of coffee and the chest; I cannot say what else he would
not have seen me if he had turned his head round, I was in a truck, and the
sides were high-not more than a quarter of an hour elapsed between By
seeing this chest and missing it-on seeing the prisoner looking at the chest
I went down to the other end, and it was gone-I went to the Brighton
platform, and the prisoner was gone-he had not uploaded his cart when I
saw him looking at the chest-he could roll the butter out of his cart in bre
or ten minutes .be Brighton platform faces the one I was on-1 gare
information.
