877 JOHN THOMPSON was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 12th of April , 4 handkerchiefs, value 15s., the goods of George Lowe and another, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute.
   ENOCH JONES. I live in the City-road. I lost a piece of handkerchiefs on Friday, the 12th of April; we suppose there were six handkerchiefs in it: we sold one, and it corresponds exactly with that found on the prisoner; I am partner with George Lowe.
   THOMAS CASSEDY(Police-constable N 71). In consequence of information, I went to the prisoner's house in a court in Hackney-road, between two and three o'clock last Friday; I asked him if any property had been brought to his room - he said No; I said "Perhaps you do not understand what property it is - it is some silk handkerchiefs;" he said "There was no silk brought here;" I saw a hat on the table; I asked him if it was his - he said Yes; there was an old handkerchief over it, I took it off; and found this piece of handkerchiefs in it; I took him to the station, and then I and my brother officer went back to the house, and found this guinea note. this duplicate, and this bottle - this skeleton-key was found on his person.
   ALBERT MARTIN(Police-constable N 72). I went with Cassedy - I found some files, a key, and a centre-bit, in a box, on the ledge of the prisoner's window.
   THOMAS APPLEFORD. I am shopman to the prosecutors; on Friday last I was standing near the shop door - I heard a noise, and a woman pointed to three boys who were then running away; I ran and overtook two of them, but neither of them had any property; they were given to the officer, and they told him where the prisoner lived.
   ALAN ZOUCH. I am shopman to the prosecutors; I have one handkerchief which I bought of them - it corresponds with the piece found.
 Prisoner's Defence. The key is of my own room - the guinea note is of no use, it was picked up as waste-paper; I had only been three weeks, and these handkerchiefs were brought by a lad; I am a smith, and the files and centre-bit have been in my nail-box for five or six years; I had been finishing a stove that day, and worked till past my dinner-hour - it was half-past two before I left the shop; I went and got my dinner, and as my wife was not at home, I went up stairs to feed my fancy; the boy had left the property there unknown to me, and he called to know if I was at home.
   THOMAS CASSEDY. The boy took me to the place, and showed me where the property was; he then called to the prisoner that the Policeman was coming; the prisoner took up a poker, and came to the door, but I told him I had my cutlass, and he dropped the poker.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 871  ROBERT SMITH and JAMES CARROLL were indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March , 11 sheep, price 20l., the property of Henry Matthews ; 2 sheep, price 4l, and 2 lambs, price 4l., the property of James Taylor Murtrie.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY MATTHEWS. I am a butcher, and live in Newgate-market; I keep my sheep at Mr. Murtrie's stable, in Sharpe's-alley, Cowcross. On the 13th of March I was in that stable about eleven o'clock - I had sixty-one sheep there - I took out forty, and left twenty-one; the door was fastened by a padlock on the outside; the next morning I received information - I went and missed eleven sheep; I know both the prisoners by sight - Smith is a master drover; I have not seen my sheep again.
 Cross-examined by  MR. CHURCHILL . Q. Are there not two ways into Sharpe's-alley? A. No - there are posts at the end, which sheep cannot get through well, but I have driven them through; it is not far from Smithfield.
   WILLIAM BIRCH. I am in the employ of Mr. Matthews. On Thursday morning I went to the stable, and saw only ten sheep there - the lock of the stable had been undone, and hung by the staple - I know Smith is a drover.
   EDWARD SCALES. I am in the employ of Mr. Murtrie. On Wednesday, the 13th of March, I was in the stable between ten and eleven o'clock; he had twenty Down sheep and two lambs there - I locked the door; the next morning two sheep and two lambs were missing - I have not seen the sheep, but I saw the lambs at the station; on Friday, we had them and killed them - the officer has the skins; I can swear to them.
 Cross-examined. Q. What short of sheep were they; A. Ours were short woolled sheep, some of Mr. Matthews were long woolled.
   WILLIAM MLLES. I am in St. Sepulchre's school. On the 13th of March I was in Sharpe's-alley, about half-past seven o'clock in the evening - I know Smith the prisoner; I saw him about twenty yards from the stable; he was minding some sheep - some of them were long woolled, and one of them had a black face; I threw a turnip among them, and the black faced one eat it - I went for some starch. and as I was coming back I saw Smith again; he told me to go to Greenhill's-rents, and tell the boy there to stop there with the sheep till he came with the lambs; I went there and saw a person - I could not see whether it was a boy or not, but I delivered the message; there were fourteen or fifteen sheep in Greenhill's-rents - on the next morning I went with the officer, and pointed out Smith to him; he had a white dog with a mark round his neck, which had been with him on the Wednesday night.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you live in Sharpe's-alley? A. Yes; only three people keep sheep there - there is one gas-light there; there are not many persons - I saw there was some person with sheep in Greenhill's-rents, and I called over to him.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.You noticed one black faced sheep? A. Yes, it came and cat my turnip-tops - there might have been more; I saw a man with Smith, who had a very white coat on, who was doing something at the lock of Mr. Matthews' stable door outside.
