FRANCIS SANDERS. On the 25th of June I was in the Black Lion, public-house, Castle-street, Bloomsbury. My waistcoat was in my hat on the seat. I was having a pint of beer. I went into the back-yard, leaving it there. The prisoner was sitting in the same box. A boy came into the yard, and I missed it out of the hat, and the prisoner was gone. I went after him, and saw him five days after by St. Giles's church. He confessed it.
   GEORGE LEE. I am a pawnbroker. On the 25th of June, between six and seven o'clock, the prisoner pawned the waistcoat for 1 s. 6 d.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1270.  JOHN CLARK and JOHN JOHNSON were indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , two handkerchiefs, value 7 s., the goods of Vincent Cross.
   VINCENT CROSS. I am a linen-draper, and live in Kingsland-road. These handkerchiefs laid in my window. I missed them, jumped over the counter, and seeing Johnson in the shop, I seized him. Both prisoners were in the shop, but Johnson was nearest the window. I observed him throw the handkerchiefs from him. Clark remained in the shop. They had asked for two black silk laces, and tendered me a 6 d., for which I gave change.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I am a constable. On the 8th of September I took charge of the prisoner. I found the laces in Clark's hand. Neither of them had a farthing about them, although the prosecutor had given Clark 3 d.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 JOHNSON'S Defence. I never had them. He searched me, and not finding them, said I had thrown them over the counter.
 CLARK'S Defence. I was not with Johnson.
 JOHNSON -    GUILTY. Transported for Seven Years.
 CLARK -    NOT GUILTY.
 1271.  HENRY MEAKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , a lamp-shade, value 2 s., the goods of George Woolford.
   GEORGE WOOLFORD. I am the Blackwall carrier. On the 1st of September, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, this lamp-shade was tied to the tail of my cart, which was going on between Whitechapel and Commercial-road. I had a parcel to deliver; and going round the cart, found the cord cut, and the shade gone, with a prickle which it was in. Jones brought the prisoner to me with it a week after; it was safe in Leadenhall-street. The burner was in the basket with it. I bought it of Messrs. Pellatt and Co.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am a patrol of Bow-street. On the 1st of September I was in Gulston-street, and observed the prisoner coming along with this glass-shade in his hand, done up in brown paper. I asked what he had there. He said a glass bowl, and that it was made for him by one Abrahams, of Wheeler-street, Spitalfields, and he gave 7 s. 6 d. for it. I sent to Abrahams, and in consequence of what he said, I detained the prisoner, and found the prosecutor out a week afterwards. He had not got the prickle.
   FREDERICK MOSS. I am mounter to Messrs. Pellatt and Co. On the 1st of September I sold the lamp, with this shade to Woolford. I know it to be the same.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought it for 7 s. 6 d.
     GUILTY. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Jury, Before W. Arabin, Esq.
 1272.  WILLIAM HOOPER was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , three spoons, value 6 s., the goods of John Edbury.
   JOHN PARTRIDGE. I am beadle of Whitechapel. On the 25th of August I was in Rosemary-lane, and saw the prisoner offering three spoons for sale to a Jew. I asked him where he got them from. He said from Mrs. Noon, of Ailiff-street. I took him in custody with them, and found out the prosecutrix.
 Cross-examined by MR. WALFORD. Q. He was not sober, was he - A. No.
   MARY EDBURY. I am the wife of John Edbury, and live in Little Aliff-street. The prisoner was recommended to me as a lodger, and was one night in my house. I did not miss the spoons until they were brought to me. They were taken off the back-room table. He was at my house a quarter of an hour before.
 Cross-examined. Q. He might have taken them in a joke - A. Very likely.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. She sent me out for rum, and I do not know whether they were put into my pocket or not, for I never saw them.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Months.
 1273.  HENRY HOLDER was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , twenty-four lbs. of soap, value 14 s., the goods of Joseph Mote , his master.
 MR. BRODRICK conducted the prosecution.
   JOSEPH MOTE. I am a soap manufacturer, and live in Hill-street, Finsbury. The prisoner was my confidential clerk for three years. After soap is manufactured, it is put into the store-room; in consequence of suspicion, I employed two officers to watch: he occupied two rooms in my house. On the 30th of July, I ascertained the quantity of soap in the store-room. I reckoned 38 cakes to a cwt., and by that calculation, there were 2 l cwt.; I ordered him to deliver 6 1/4 cwt. to a customer; only a labourer, myself, and him had access to the soap. The labourers were under his controul. The soap was moist on the evening of the 29th - it was then all correct; he was to deliver out the 6 1/4 cwt. On the 30th, the officers took him into custody; on that day I went with them into his room; I found eight cakes of soap on the floor, between the bed and wall; it was moist, similar to the pile; I went to the store-room, and found eight cakes deficient.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. When had the pile been manufactured - A. I cannot exactly say; I think not a fortnight or three weeks - there might be two or three tons weight made. I sold Mr. Freeman 56 lbs. of each; the prisoner said it was the residue of a 1/4 of cwt. he had bought of Freeman; he bought soap of me occasionally; there was a closet in his room, where he might have hid it. I had Freeman apprehended.
