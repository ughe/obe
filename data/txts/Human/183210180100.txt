 2443.  HARRIET EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of October , 1 pair of shoes, value 4s. 6d., the goods of Thomas Perean Morgan.
   THOMAS PEREAN MORGAN. I live in Chesterfield-place, New-road. On the 5th of October, about one o'clock, I missed a pair of new shoes from under my window; I went to the first pawnbroker's shop in the same place, and found the prisoner at the counter, with them in her hand - she said they were hers, and she had given a man outside 1s. for them; I asked if she could point him out; she pointed to a man, and when I got up to him she said it was not him, it was another farther on; and when we got to him, she said it was not him - I gave a man in charge, who she said she had them from; the Magistrate discharged him.
   CHARLES MUMFORD. I took the prisoner into custody.
 Prisoner's Defence. The man asked me to buy them; they would not fit, and he asked me to pawn them.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. - Confined One Month.
 2444.  WILLIAM FAWCITT was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of October , 1 picture, framed and glazed, value 5s., the goods of John Pratt.
   ELIZABETH PRATT. I am the wife of John Pratt, a fringe-maker, in Exeter-street, Portland-market. On the 17th of October this picture was taken; I had seen it safe in the parlour the day before - the shop door is open in business hours; I never saw the prisoner in the shop - I believe he was in distress.
   HENRY BYFIELD. I am shopman to Mr. Gideon, pawnbroker, Stafford-street, Lisson-grove. On the 18th of October I was informed this portrait and frame were stolen, and shortly after the prisoner came and offered it in pledge; I detained him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I bought it.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Two Months.
 2445.  WILLIAM JACOBS was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of October , 2 shillings, the monies of John Cross , from his person.
   JOHN CROSS. I live in Boswell-court, Queen-square; the prisoner and I lived in the same room, and slept together for three months. On the 18th of October, having suspicion that he was robbing me, I stopped out, and did not go home till six o'clock in the morning; I was employed at some stables - I was quite sober; I put my waistcoat on a chair at the foot of the bed; when he got up, I pretended to be asleep; I saw him take my waistcoat up, and put it down again, and directly he was gone, I missed two shillings; I went out after him - I got a Policeman, and gave him into custody; he denied it; nobody else slept in the room; his clothes were not on the same chair as mine, that I observed.
 Prisoner. He came home in liquor; I helped him off with his great coat. Witness. I feigned to be drunk, wishing to find who was the thief - he denied it, but when he got to the station he said he found the two shillings on the carpet; it was day-light; my waistcoat had sleeves, but his had not.
   CHARLES WELLDING. I am a Policeman. The prosecutor charged the prisoner with this; I told him to produce his money; he produced two half-crowns and some shillings; among them were two marked shillings, which Cross claimed; the prisoner denied it at first, but said at the station that he found them on the carpet.
   JOHN CROSS. These shillings are mine - I marked them overnight.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Confined Six Months.
