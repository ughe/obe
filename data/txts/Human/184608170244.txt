WILLIAM CHATTERIS. I am employed in Stamford-place. On the evening of the 5th of Aug. I was there—I do not know the Larkins—I saw all thepri. Boners there—I saw the whole of the disturbance—I did not hear Mrs. True body say anything, but I saw her run after Mrs. Larkins and Miss Smith—I did not see her do anything to them—they came to me and complained, and I took them both in and protected them—I was charged by the policeman to aid and assist—I went up to Finn and he said, "you b----old—, if yw do anything to me, I will kick your b----guts out"—he kicked me violently on the knees—I had the handle of a chaise riding-whip in my hand, and struck him over the head with it.    Finn's Defence.  The old gentleman has been speaking very false to say I kicked him; I own I struck Larkin.
   John Hart's Defence.  My wife came and told me that Mr. Larkin had been threatening to hit her with a big stick; I went up to see what it was about, and there was the policeman and several others; I asked Larkin what be meant by it; he said he would give me in charge; Finn came up, and a quar rel took place; I went to get them away, old Larkin came up, with a big stick, and I shoved him away; his son came and laid hold of me, and held me by, the handkerchief for a quarter of an hour; and I held him; we weie fight ing a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes; I asked him to let me go; I took my knife out and cut my neckcloth; I was never near Parsons.
