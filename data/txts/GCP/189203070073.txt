Mr. Jones submitted that the Counts relating to forgery were bad, as they
disclosed no offence, a postage stamp not being a writing as alleged; that the
foreign stamps were not more than labels. As to the Sandroich Islands stamp, it
was proved that no such stamp.ever aristed, and therefore it could not be counter-
feited, and that it was no more forgery to copy such a stamp than it would be to
copy a patent medroine stamp or the label on a reel of cotton.
MR. WILLIs also submitted that the offence was not one at common law ; the
question was whether any of the stamps produced was a document or instrument
in writing. Some of these stamps had been actually med. so that there was no
intention of injuring the person who originally wed them. The Sydney View
stamp being no longer in we, did not constitute a contract between that country
and the Postmaster-General, and therefore the first twenty-three Counts could
not be sustained. (Casos referred to, Reg. v. Closs, 7 Cox, p. 494 ; Rog. v.
Juhn Smith, 8 Cox, p. 586; and Reg. v. Gordon 11 Cox, p. 672.)
