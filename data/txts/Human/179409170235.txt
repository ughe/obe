565. STEPHEN LANGLEY was indicted, for feloniously stealing, on the 20th of September , six pounds weight of iron nails, value 2s. two ounces weight of Singlo tea, value 6d. the goods of the   East India Company.
 MINNIKIN sworn.
 Q. Do you know any thing of this transaction? - Yes, thoroughly. I am a labourer in the East India Company's warehouses, in  Fenchurch-street; between the hours of eleven and twelve on Saturday last, I was coming down stairs, and on the first floor, along side of a nail box, I saw the prisoner. He is a bricklayer's labourer, employed by the Company; he was along side of the nail box; he was lifting the nails from the box, and putting them in his pocket; he observed that I see him put some in his pocket, and he dropped some on the stairs, and said that he wanted them, to put them in his shoes. I said nothing to him, but I went and told my fellow labourers that Longley had got some of the Company's property; and my fellow labourer went to the counting house to this gentleman, Mr. Duree. I went back to my employment, because I was requested to be back as soon as possible.
   JOHN DUREE sworn.
 I belong to the East India Company. I am what they call an elder there. On Saturday last, about twelve o'clock, I believe it was, I was informed by the last witness, and another man, that the prisoner Langley had been taking some nails from a box on a stair case, belonging to the Company. Afterwards he went on the top of the house to work; I went to the top of the house after him, and called him down. I asked him what he had in his pocket? he said, nothing. I told him I was sure he had some nails of the Company. He then emptied the nails into an apron, belonging to one of the men, out of his pockets. I asked him if had got any more in his pocket, he said, he had not. I put my hand into his waistcoat pocket, and found there was some tea, two ounces and a quarter weight of Singlo tea. I sent for a Custom house officer that is here, and he took it from him; and he has got it in his possession. I asked him where he got the tea? he said, he thought there was no harm in it; and he went up, and pointed out the chest where he took it out from.
 Q. Did you make him any promise? - I did not.
 Q. Did any body else? - Not to my knowledge; I was there all the time.
 Q. What was the value of the nails? - Very likely three shillings. I gave them into the custody or one of our men.
 - HETCHELL sworn.
 I have got the nails; the prisoner took them out of his own pocket and delivered them into my possession; likewise I see the officer take the tea out of his pocket.
   Q. What may be the value of the tea? - Eight shillings and six pence.
   Q. What sort of nails are they? - What they call small congou nails.
 Q. How many nails are there? - Six or seven pounds.
 Prisoner. I had a piece of tiling off, and the bricklayer was not in the way to lath it on, and I went down and took some nails on purpose to lay it on as soon as possible I could; the lath nails don't run very little larger than them; I did it to get it lathed in as soon as possible I could, because of the rain; and when I went down to get some lath nails there was some tea laying down along side of the chest, and I scratched it up and took some of it, and they asked me what chest it came out of? and I shewed them.
 The prisoner called three witnesses, who gave him a good character.
     GUILTY. (Aged 35.) Privately whipped.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. COMMON SERJEANT.
