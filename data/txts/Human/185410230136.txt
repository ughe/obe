   COURT . Q.  I suppose in an animal which had died from suffocation, the flesh would be more likely to get bad than another? A.  Yes, became the blood is not drawn from it, and still more if it was trampled on—if a man does not mind a beef steak being tough, it would be bad on account of the animal having been suffocated, and it is likely to mortify in a very short time—if you cut beef steaks, like the savages do, out of the rump as soon as the animal is dead, it is quite unfit for human food—I should say that it is unfit at any time, if the animal dies from suffocation—if a bullock jumps overboard and is drowned in getting to shore, I should not be afraid to eat it. NOT GUILTY.
