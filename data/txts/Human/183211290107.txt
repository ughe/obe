THOMAS FENTON. I was sent by Harris to the prosecutor, with the prisoner and the trousers.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. My brother came home and asked me to go to buy him a waistcoat - I was going down Petticoat-lane, and saw a man with these trousers; he asked 6s. for them, and taking some of my brother's money, I went to pawn them, to get the money back.

 246.  GEORGE JORDAN was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of October , 4lbs. of lamb, value 3s., the goods of John Williams.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I had this lamb in my hand at the corner of Milton-street; I turned to speak to two girls, and the prisoner snatched it from me - I pursued him, but I fell down; he got off, and I did not see him again till he was in custody - I knew him very well.
   PAUL PLANT(Police-constable G 156). I took the prisoner for smashing, and as I thought he resembled the person who took the lamb, I took the prosecutor to him.
 Prisoner's Defence. He was very much in liquor - two women had told of his arm; he asked me to have something to drink - we went into the Paul - I staid there some time, and then he said I had got his shoulder of mutton; I said I had not - he said, "Has Jenny got it;" I said, "I don't know;" we then came out - I was afterwards taken on another charge; I saw no mutton with him.
 JOHN WILLIAMS. I had not drank with him, nor had any intimacy with him; I was sober - I changed a sovereign at the Paul, and lost 9s. 6d. there.
     NOT GUILTY.
 247.  CHARLOTTE VINCENT was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of November , 1 shirt, value 4s., the goods of Robert Farrow.
   ELIZABETH FARROW. I am a laundress, and am the wife of Robert Farrow- he lives in Luton-street. This linen was brought into the house for me to wash, and in about three minutes it was gone; I went to the station-house, and then to some pawnbrokers, and found the shirt - I did not see the prisoner; my child described the prisoner to me - the things were taken from the parlour, the door of which was open.
   HENRY SWAIN. I am a pawnbroker - I live about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's I have a shirt which was pawned by the prisoner, on the 24th of November, about nine o'clock at night - this is it.
   WILLIAM JOHNS(Police-constable D 41.) The prosecutrix came to me, said she had lost a shirt, and gave me a description of the girl and her dress - I went to Little Church-street, and found the prisoner at a temporary playhouse.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Fined 1s. and Discharged.

 249.  ROBERT HERN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Peter Dunne , from his person.
 PETER DUNNE. On the 27th of November, about ten o'clock in the morning, I was in  Compton-street, Soho- a witness said my pocket had been picked; I then felt, and missed my handkerchief, which was safe five or ten minutes before, when I left my employer's house; I have not found it.
   GEORGE ISRAEL. I am a jeweller. I saw the prisoner lift up the tail of the prosecutor's coat, and draw his handchief out; he was with three others - he was about to put it in his bosom; I lost sight of them for half a minute, then went and informed the prosecutor - we came up to the prisoner at the corner of Walker's-court; he made great resistance - I am sure he is the one who took it; he was the shortest of them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you know him before? A. No.
   THOMAS WILSON. I am a constable. I saw the prisoner making a desperate resistance - I came up and took him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never saw the handkerchief.
     NOT GUILTY
