1003. WILLIAM BALLARD, was indicted for unlawfully removing
and concealing part of his estate within sixty days of his bankruptoy, with
intent to defraud his creditors.
MESSRS. POLAND and GRIFFITHS conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAS HENRY BROWN. I am an officer of the Court of Bankruptcy-
I produce the original proceedings in the bankruptcy of William Ballard
-the petition is dated 22d October—it is the bankrupt's own petition-it
has the seal of the Court—the adjudication is dated the same day-there
is an affidavit, by the bankrupt, verifying a list of debts, filed on 24th
October—the bankrupt's examinations are on 4th and 11th November,
1862, and 31st January, 1863—they purport to be signed by the bank-
rupt, and bear the seal of the Court.
Cross-examined by Mr. GIFFARD. Q. Did you see the bankrupt on 22d
October 4. I did—he at that time made a statement to the messenger
of his property for the purpose of his seizing it-this is the statement-
“furniture at Dockhead, about 151. stock of ironmongery, about 851., and
fixtures ; some ironmongery at Minty's, plumber and glaziers, York-road,
Lambeth, worth about 4001.; book debts about 407. ; books at home"-İ
have not been at Minty's-we took possession of the goods there-this
statement was made at the same time be brought in the petition.
WILLIAM FIDO. I live at 45, Red Cross-street, Southwark, and keep a
ccal-shed there about the middle or end of September last, I removed
some ironmongery goods from 58, Dockhead, by order of a man, numed
Keats, a tailor-the prisoner was present, and handed me the goods-I
took them to a coal-shed, at Mrs. Hickey's, 31, Red Cross-street-they
were removed in two vans-I went two separate mornings—there were
two van loads I was ordered at 6, but did not get there till a little
after-Keats paid me.
Cross-examined. Q. Where did Keats carry on his business as a tailor
4. In Union-street-I had known him living there for some time-I
can't say that I knew him carrying on business there since March, 1862
-he is now dead-the second removal was about 8 in the morning, two
or three days after the first-it did not take me more than about half an
hour to load it was about 10 when I had done.
BRIDGET SLATTERY. My name was formerly Hickey-in September last
I kept a greengrocer's shop and coal-shed at 31, Red Cross-street, South-
wark-I knew Keats, a tailor, in Union-street-in September he came
and took a workshop of me at the back of my house-the rent was to be
two shillings-sometime afterwards two loads of ironmongery goods were
brought there by Fido-they remained there nine or ten days, and were
thea fetched away in some vans- I do not know the prisoner.
