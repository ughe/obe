He was carrying on the business as Her  bert Smith; that is after Smith's death. I do not remember that an order for security of costs was made and not complied with and the action dismissed with costs. I do not even remember that I was sued as Herbert Smith in respect of those coats. There was an ap plication for leave to issue. I presume I answered the affidavit in support of the application by an affidavit, if you say so. I attended to the legal documents through my solicitor; very likely I swore an affidavit that I was not a partner in nor proprietor of Herbert Smith's business. If you give me the affidavit I will tell you whether it is right or wrong. The action was settled—Smith paid the money, or Harris, I really do not know. I asked Smith to settle, and pay his debt under the issue. I have never seen the affidavit produced re Ashworth against Herbert Smith. "The fact a deposed to are within my knowledge true, and I am authorised by the defendant to make this affidavit, and am advised that the defendant has a good defence to this action." Harris ought to have been the defendant there. In regard to the sentence, "I, George Harris, etc., manager to the above-named defendant," though Smith himself had been dead a year, I was not the defendant, and Harris knew I was not Smith. In Leslie v. Smith there were some proceedings in bankruptcy, in the course of which I was asked about the action. It is true I said there that I remembered an issue being directed to try whether I was, in fact, Herbert Smith, and that I asked Smith to settle it. Smith bad been dead a year, but it was Harris I asked. I did not disclose that the original Smith was dead, because the business was being carried on. I think if a man takes the business and liabilities of Smith he becomes Smith, for the purpose of a business. The ques tions asked were affecting Smith's business. It is true I said I first met Smith some years ago; about five. Although I said here that I had known Smith 15 years, that other matter was affecting his out side broking operations, which began about 1900. Concerning that particular business, I said I had known him about five years. I said, "I have known him about the City, met him there. I could not tell who introduced me; he was an outside broker then." "Then" means in 1900. Twenty years ago Smith was in the tea trade; I was in the same trade. I had not seen him for 10 years before 1900; when he was in the outside broking business. That action was a blackmailing one, and I was not giving any gratuitous information to anybody. It simply referred to the, outside broking period. I also said I had last seen Smith at Moorgate Street about a year ago; that was a year before 1905. I said I had not been to Moorgate Street much lately, that I ceased going when they got attacking me in the action, that I did not think I had been there that year, and that pro bably Smith went there after I did for all I knew.
