 677.  JAMES BRADFORD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of February , 1 copper, value 12s., the goods of William Read.
 WILLIAM READ. I am a  wheelwright, and live in Princes-street, Lisson-grove, I lost this copper from a shed at the wharf - I cannot exactly say when; my apprentice first missed it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did he not say he missed it on the 8th? A. No - he mentioned no day.
   RICHARD STRATTEN. I am apprentice to the prosecutor. The copper was safe at the wharf on the 31st of January - I missed it on the 3rd of February, and found it at Nash's on the 14th.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you ever say you missed it on the 8th? A. No.
   JOSEPH NASH. I am a broker, and dealer in rags and copper. I bought this copper of Mr. Boxall, for 12s. 6d. - Webster came and had it from me: when Read came he had a doubt of it, and I took him to Boxall.
 Cross-examined. Q. He had his doubts? A. Yes - he said he could not swear to it himself, but would find a man who could; he said there were three letters on it -Webster cleaned it, but could not find them.
   WILLIAM BOXALL. I sold this copper to Nash for 12s. 6d. - I am a rag-merchant, and sometimes deal in stores; my landlord Arbour brought it to me to sell for him.
   THOMAS ARBOUR. I am a carpenter. I bought this copper from the prisoner, who was a tenant of mine, on the 5th of February - I set 9s. off his rent for it; he said it was his own - I did not ask where it came from.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was any body present when he sold it to you? A. Yes, his wife - he told me he had it from a man who owed him money.
 WILLIAM READ. I cannot swear to this copper.
     NOT GUILTY.

 678.  ELIZABETH CLARKE was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Thomas Wilkins , on the 19th of February , and stealing 1 gown, value 7s.; 1 frock, value 1s.; 1 quilt, value 1s.; 1 apron, value 4d.; 1 shift, value 3d., and 1 handkerchief, value 1s., his property.
   NATHANIEL WILKINS. I am the son of Thomas Wilkins, who lives in Long's-buildings, Whitecross-street, and is a watchmaker. The prisoner was a stranger there; these things were in a drawer on the first floor - the street. door is kept open for the lodgers; the first floor room was locked, and the key left on the stairs - my mother locked it, as she says, but I do not know that; I stopped the prisoner at the bottom of the stairs, and she dropped the frock on the floor - I did not see it fall; it was found near to where she stood - the other things w0ere found in her apron: my mother claimed them - the prisoner wanted to be let go, to fetch the person who she said had put them into her lap - we did not let her go.
   SARAH WILKINS. I am the witness' mother. This gown and frock were on a press bedstead, by the side of the drawers - the other things were in the drawers; I always lock the door when I go out - I had gone out that morning about a quarter-past ten o'clock; I have no servant - my husband was in the workshop on the second floor; I am sure I locked the door, and left the key on the stairs, where I always put it - it was within reach of any body; I returned at a quarter-past eleven, and found the prisoner in custody - the things had been taken from her.
   WILLIAM HOOPER. I am a constable. I was sent for and took charge of the prisoner - the property laid on the table, and was claimed by Mr. Wilkins, in the prisoner's hearing; the first words the prisoner said to me were "It was not me opened the door - why don't you go after the person who opened it;" I said, "We will search you first, and then go after her;" I only found 1d. on her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress. GUILTY of stealing only. Aged 60. Confined One Year.
 679.  CHARLES GAST was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 5th of January , of a certain evil-disposed person, 1 writing-desk, value 3l., the goods of Peter Wood , well knowing it to have been stolen.
 PETER WOOD. I am a  writing-desk manufacturer, and live in Portugal-street, Lincoln's Inn-fields. On the 5th of January I went out at nine o'clock, and left these dressing-cases safe in my shop window, close to the door; I returned about one, and they were gone - I saw them at Marlborough-street office about a month after.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. On the 5th of February I went with a search-warrant to the prisoner's house in Hanway-yard, and saw this dressing-case standing in the corner of the shop parlour; I went to the house again in about half an hour, and told him in consequence of what I had seen I should take the work-box, as I called it - I think I called it a writing-desk; I went to where I had seen it, and it was gone - I asked where it was; his wife said, "It is here" - I found it moved to the other end of the parlour, on the table: he said it was not his, and that he did not know how it came there - his wife said the same.
     NOT GUILTY.
