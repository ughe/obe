1931. JOHN BURGESS was indicted for unlawfully assaulting Charles Payne .
   MR. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution , CHARLES PAYNE. I am apprenticed to a carpenter, and live in Great Wild-street, Lincoln's Inn-fields—on Wednesday, the 1st of July, between twelve and one O'clock, I was in Fleet-street, and saw the prisoner I was pass ing him on the kerb, and he struck me in the eye with a stick, which cut my eye, and he then struck me twice on the wrist, and broke the bone of my wrist—I had not spoken to him or annoyed him in any way—he was taken into custody, and I was taken to the hospital, where I remained three weeks—I have pretty well go the use of my arm again—there were ten or more boys following the prisoner at the time, but I did not see them annoying him or say anything to him. JAMES JOY( City-policeman , 347.) I saw the prisoner in Fleet-street, and some boys following him, but they were not annoying him in my presence—I cautioned them not to do so—he had this stick in his hand—I saw the prosecutor—he was not following or annoying the prisoner, but as he passed, the prisoner turned and struck him, as he described, over the eye, and three times over the arm—I took him to her hospital, as one of the bones of his arm was broken.
   COURT. Q.  Do you know anything of the prisoner? A.  I know him about the streets, dressed in a very peculiar manner—the boys tease him—I did not see them do so on this occasion—I do not know how he gets his living—he was remanded for me to get a certificate from the surgeon—he is in his right state of mind. JOHN BEMERSYDE HAY. I am house-surgeon at St. Bartholomew's Hos pital—Payne was brought there on the 1st of July—one of the bones of his arm was broken—he went out on the 21st—he is quite well now.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The boys were annoying me and throwing stones at me, which caused me to use the stick. GUILTY .*— Confined Three Months , and to enter into his recognizance in 20 l  . for three years

 1933. THOMAS DUGDALE was indicted for unlawfully selling and publishing obscene prints.
   MR. E. LAW conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE WILLIAM SHARP. I am clerk to Messrs. Pritchard and Collett, solicitors to the Society for the Suppression of Vice, No. 57, Lincoln's Inn fields. On the 27th of July last, about half-past nine o'clock in the evening, in consequence of instruction I received, I went to the station-house at Bow-street, and after being there searched by a policeman, I went to No. 51, Holywell-street, Strand, followed by the policeman—I went into the shop and asked for a book, which the prisoner showed me, and which I produce—I paid a guinea for it—I then left the shop, and went, closely followed by the policeman who had searched me, to the Society's chambers, where he took from my pocket the book I had bought of the defendant.
