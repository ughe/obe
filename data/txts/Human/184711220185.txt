CHARLES THORNE. I am a clerk at the Kent and Essex wharf. On 12th June I received from Holdsworth and Seager six packages of rope, in the name of Seager—he signed my book, which I have here—nothing was said about any other person—I did not ask whose goods they were—the name of the person who deposits the goods is put in this first column—in this case it is the name of the person to whom the goods belong, which is the same thing—on the following Monday, about the middle of the day, he came again and had the goods away in Mr. Ayrst's cart—Davidson was the carman.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  He delivered the things tem porarily at your wharf? A.  Yes—we always have the signature of the party taking the goods away, as a receipt. THOMAS DAVIDSON. I was a carman, in the employ of Mr. Ayrst. On 14th June last Seager came to my place in Thames-street to hire a cart—he went with me in the cart to the Kent-wharf, where several bales were put into the cart—I cannot say how many—he then took me to Mr. Booth's, the auctioneer's, in Watling-street—as we went along, and before we got there, he asked me to assist him in taking some of the wrappers off—they had marks on them—some of the wrappers were taken off, and we went on to Mr. Booth's—I left the goods there, but not the wrappers—I was not paid—Seager said that Mr. Ayrst was acquainted with the thing, and that would be quite sufficient—I came away from Booth's, leaving him there.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  Did he give you card? A.  No—I did not see him give one to Mr. Ayrst—I saw no card—I went by Mr. Ayrst's direction—I did not see Gardiner and Holliday while going with the cart from the wharf—I did not see anybody speak to Seager, or see him speak to any one—I understood afterwards that whoever had given the order was a customer of my master's, because, when I asked him about the payment, he said Mr. Ayrst knew of it.
   JURY to CHARLES THORNE. Q.  Was anybody represented to you as the proprietor of the goods? A.  Seager told me to put the goods in the name of Seager, and he gave a receipt in the name when he took them away—the signature indicates the ownership—they were left on Seager's account. ROBERT BOOTH.
