 1987. ROBERT COULDERY was indicted for stealing, on the   19th of May , 1 purse, value 1 d  .; 3 shillings, 1 sixpence, 4 pence, and 5 half-pence; the property of Samuel Hall , from his person. SAMUEL HALL. I am a Greenwich pensioner. I was in the Infirmary and got leave to go out for a few hours, on the 19th of May—I went to the Red Lion—I had a pint of beer—I paid one shilling, and had sixpence and four penny-pieces in change—I put my beer down in the parlour, and went into the tap-room to light my pipe—I had then 3 s . 6 d . in silver, four penny-pieces, and 2 1/2 d ., in a leather purse, in my waistcoat pocket—probably the string might be hanging out, I cannot say—I was not three minutes gone into the tap—I then returned and drank my beer up—I went out, and did not walk above three yards before I missed my purse—I do not know who took it—I am nearly blind.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Are you certain you had the purse when you went in? A.  Yes—I cannot swear it might not have dropped out of my pocket, but I was not taking any thing out of my pocket—I saw no person near me—I went back to the Infirmary, and on the Sunday following a policeman and Abraham Young came to me. ABRAHAM YOUNG. I am a dustman. I was at the Red Lion that day, and saw the prisoner take the money out of the prosecutor's waist coat pocket—it was in a leather bag—I did not say any thing to him—there were two or three more persons with him—I do not know when I first gave information of this—he put it into his pocket—I knew two or three chaps  that were there—I heard that one of them had been in Maidstone Goal—I did not see him give any of the money to any person there. NOT GUILTY.
