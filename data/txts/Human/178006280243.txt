 Did not you expect to have some of that fifty pounds? - No.
 Did you hear no talk of it? - No.
 Did not your friend  Moses the constable tell you of it? - No, he is no friend of mine.
 You say that the boy the man took the gun from brought it out of the closet and the sword too? - Yes.
 Whose property were they? - Mr. Lebarty's, I believe.
 Counsel. Was your daughter in the room when the prisoner did what you speak of? - No, she went down stairs.
 You was not in this room till your daughter went down stairs? - Yes, when she went down stairs.
 What did the man do with the gun and the sword? - Threw them out at the window.
   JOHN LEBARTY sworn.
 Did you see the prisoner at any time on Wednesday the 7th of June? - Yes, I saw him, I think he was at my house to the best of my remembrance, between eight and nine o'clock, he came to get change for a half guinea.
 That was on the evening your house was demolished? - It was.
 Did you go to the justice's with Mary and  Elisabeth Frazer, to give information against the prisoner? - I did not give information against the prisoner, I was summoned by the justice to go there, and I went.
 What time was that? - I do not recollect the day, I was so disturbed in my mind I cannot recollect.
 Did you say any thing to  Mary Frazer for her not to give information against the prisoner? - I spoke that I would let it alone, I would have nothing to do with it, as I was a stranger in the nation I thought I would let it alone.
 Cross Examination.
 You say you saw him between eight and nine o'clock, you are not exact to the time I suppose? - I do not know, he and two more came in for change for half a guinea.
 You knew him before? - I certainly did.
 Your servant knew him and knew his name? - I believe so.
 Court. How came you to leave your house so early that afternoon? - A message was brought to me that the mob were going to burn my life, therefore I left the house between ten and eleven o'clock.
 You did not think of arming yourself against them? - What could I do, they were all against me?
 Had you any arms in the house? - I left a gun in the closet I believe.
 Prisoner. I leave my defence to my counsel and my witnesses.
 For the Prisoner.
   WILLIAM WHITWAY sworn.
 Do you know Mary and  Elisabeth Frazer? - I have seen them before. On Thursday the 15th of June, in the morning, they came to the King's-Arms, East Smithfield, opposite Justice Clarke's office; they and Lebarty came together, and began to relate the matter about the prisoner to me and some others; I told her I had seen him that morning, and if I had known it I would have stopped him. I understood she was going up to the Rotation-office about some other business concerning the rioters.
 Who said so? - I do not know, I believe all three, that they were going up concerning the examination of other rioters. I was going up there myself. I told her to go up and I would endeavour to find Downs, and if I found him I would bring him up.
 What did they say to that? - They said it was very well; if I found him to bring him up. I went to his house; he happened not to be at home; as I was going I saw him on the opposite side of the way; I beckoned him; he came to me; I told him what the woman said; he declared he was innocent, and went with me voluntarily. When he came there the women were at the Rotation-office; there were some altercations between them, but the particulars I cannot remember; there were Justice Sherwood and Justice Clarke; I related the story to them; the magistrates ordered me to bring the prisoner up; I did so.
