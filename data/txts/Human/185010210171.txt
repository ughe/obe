I was in Drury-lane when they brought in the different articles to divide"—in the course of conversation he mentioned a brooch, a silver pencil-case, and a quantity of shells, which he said were lying on the table, and were in dispute as to who should have them—I took particular notice of that, as they had not been missed—I mentioned them to Miss Plowman, a search was made and they were missing—Adams came five or six times—on the following day, or a day or two after he said, "I think I shall be able to get you the box; 10  l . has been offered for it; I shall see it again next Sunday, and I think 12 l . will get it"—I said, "I suppose you are aware of the reward I have offered (50 l .); and I shall be glad to give anybody that sum if they get it"—he went away with the intention of getting it—he came three or four times after that—he never produced the box—he told me Costello was in custody. EDWARD THOMAS SHORTER KIGHTLEY. On Thursday last, I served a notice on Costello, in the other Court, to produce a letter dated 25th Sept., 1850, addressed to "Simon Connell, for James Costello." WILLIAM TROUGHTON LANGLEY( policeman, F  42). I was with Attwood on Wednesday morning, 26th Sept., in Drury-lane, and saw Taylor and Adams—on Sunday night, or Monday morning, two nights previous to the robbery, I saw Gosling and Costello in Lincoln's-inn-fields.
   Adams's Defence.  I knew nothing of it till I heard a man named Johnson say he had a pencil-case, brooch, and some shells, and asked me to go to Mr. Stiff.
   Costello's Defence.  Taylor called on me, and told me to call on Mr. Martin, as he wished to give me some work.( Taylor received a good character. )
   TAYLOR  — GUILTY  . Aged 17.— Strongly recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. — Confined twelve Months .
   ADAMS  — GUILTY. Aged 32.— Transported for Seven Years. GOSLING  — NOT GUILTY. COSTELLO  — GUILTY .* — Transported for Ten Years, to commence at the same period as the former sentence( See page  759).
