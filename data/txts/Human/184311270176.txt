   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What did she change? A.  She changed a sovereign at the Swan, Blackheath-road, before she went to the Clock House—I have been living with a young man for about ten years—I am now twenty-four years old—I have walked the streets for the last eight weeks, as he is in trouble for beating a policeman—he is at Maidstone, and was tried here—I do not think he has been gone three months—I have not walked the streets for nine or ten months—my name is Cook, but they call me Byrne, sometimes, which is my grandmother's name—I was never called Match Kit  —a relation of mine is called so—I have been in trouble for beat ing the police and taking a drop of gin, nothing else—I was sent to Maidstone gaol for three months—a person named Richards was there at the same time for taking a bed-gown—my mother sent me there then—I was charged with stealing it, and sent to Maidstone for two months—I have been in custody three or four times, but have not been in trouble for these three years—the prosecutor was not a moment holding my hand—Harman would not let him—I made him no answer; Sophy spoke  to me too quick—I went out that night about eight o'clock from Mill-lane—I left there with the prisoner—she walked rather before me, as she did not like Johnny  to see me with her—I walked with her till ten o'clock, and went straight home after leaving her—I was at home a little after ten—we both went home together, and I went to bed—I informed the policeman of this about five days after, and before that told Carry Hawkins , who lives in the same house as me—the police have looked after her, but cannot find her—I did not see her that night—I saw her the night after the robbery.
