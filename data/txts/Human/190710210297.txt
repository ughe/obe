 (Tuesday, November 12.)
 Verdict. Each prisoner Guilty on all counts, Perryman being the instigator throughout, and Tyler the least to blame.
 Inspector OTTAWAY, in referring to the history of Perryman from 1884, said that while he was a member of the Sports Club it was alleged—Mr. Marshall Hall protested against statements where "it was alleged." Mr. Justice A.T. Lawrence said he was afraid he must receive it and that he must be trusted to know the meaning of it.
 The Inspector (continuing) said it was alleged that Perryman attempted to swindle a bookmaker by backing a horse after knowing the result of the race, and it was decided to expel him, but that decision was reconsidered. Soon after he started a bucket-shop business, and later on in the year promoted a bogus concern in which the public were defrauded. In 1890 he was expelled from the National Liberal Club in consequence of his connection with a gold mining company which was exposed by the "Financial News," who were sued by Perryman in the Queen's Bench Division, when the jury found the company to be a swindle. He was subsequently at this court sentenced for libel to two months' imprisonment as first  class misdemeanant. Numerous instances were given by witness as to the shady transactions in which Perryman had been mixed up, and he stated that many complaints had been received in connection with the present business.
 There had been no convictions against Harris or Tyler.
 Mr. Marshall Hall asked the Judge to state a ease on the point of the reception of the whole of Hawkes' statement, which he, Mr. Hall, considered had prejudiced the jury. Mr. Justice Lawrence said he could not state a case on that.
 Sentences: Perryman, four years' penal servitude; Harris, 18 months' hard labour; Tyler, nine months' hard labour.
 The jury commended Inspector Ottaway for his efficient conduct of the case.
   BEFORE MR. JUSTICE A.T. LAWRENCE .
 (Monday, November 4.)
