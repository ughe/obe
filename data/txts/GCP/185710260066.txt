WILLIAM LEDGER. I keep the Coach and Horses, on Holborn Hill. On
Friday, 18th Sept., about 20 minutes before 12 o'clock at night, the prisoner
came for a glass of 6d. ale; it came to 14d.-she placed a shilling on the
counter, I noticed that it was bad, and I told her so --- she said she thought
not—I put it into the detector, and bent it double--I left it on the counter
-the prisoner gave me a good balf crown, and I gave her 28. 41d. change
- she did not drink the ale, and was very abusive - I called for a policeman
—as she did not drink the ale, I gave her back 14d.; I gave her the full
change---I gave her liberty to go, but she would not; she kept saying the
shilling was not bad--there was time for her to have gone away if she had
liked, but when I went round the counter to call the policeman, she could
not have gone; I was between the prisoner and the door-the policeman
was near the door outside ; I called him, and gave her into custody the
prisoner took the bad shilling up; I grasped her wrist, and the policeman
opened her hand, and it dropped on the counter-this is it. In t he
Prisoner. I was in liquor at the time. Witness. She was a little in liquor.
