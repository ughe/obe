WILLIAM ROSS. I am a policeman. I was with Wilkinson, and took Fairey to the station-house. WILLIAM NYE. I know Fairey by sight—I had seen him near the pro secutor's shop several times at the door, the beginning of December last, and gave information to Mr. Putley.
   Peacock's Defence.  When the prisoner came to me on Sunday morn ing, I gave him the basket, with my linen in it—he said he wanted some meat—I weighed this meat, and made it ten pounds—I put the apron and cloth into the scale—I gave him the meat—he gave me 2 s . 6 d .—I said it was not enough—he said, "You owe me 7 s . for washing, and whatever there is to pay, you must settle it"—I put the half crown on the desk, with 2 s . 6 d . more, which the meat came to—I told him to go to the Blue Mead tap, and I would come to him, and pay him what I owed him for my washing. JOHN WILKINSON re-examined . The door was opened to me in about two minutes—Fairey had got the door of the Blue Mead tap in his hand, when I took hold of him—when I got into the prosecutor's house, the maid-servant was at the foot of the stain—I found a watch and some money in Peacock's box. MARY WEBB. I am the servant. I heard the bell ring—the prisoner was in the kitchen when it rang, and went down to answer it, but he did not go till it rang three times—he went down the first time it rang—it rang three times more, and then I went down, as he did not open it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know Fairey was in the habit of coming for the dirty linen? A.  The bell never rang in the morning, and I never saw any body come for his dirty linen.
   Fairey's Defence.  I went to the shop for his linen—sometimes I ring at the bell, and sometimes I do not ring—when he came out, I have said to him, "If you have a bit of meat to suit me, put it in, that will do instead of the washing"—I said that morning, "Seven or eight pounds will do for me this morning"—he put this in—I gave him 2 s . 6 d .—he said it was not enough—I said, "You pay the difference, and I will settle it when you come to me"—I was going into the public-house door, when the sergeant of the police caught hold of me, and said, "I have been looking for you a good while, I want what you have in that basket"—I said, "What I have is my own, I have paid for it"—he said to the policeman, "You take him, I will go after the other"—at the station-house, he asked how much the meat came to, and I said, I gave half-a-crown for it.
