 Q. Is it true that you allowed him and your daughter one shilling a day for their support? A. It is not true; they fared with me - I maintained the prisoner; at the time he absconded I missed property to a large amount and a cheque for 34l., in fact, property of every description: they robbed me of every thing.
 COURT. Q. You say "they," as if others were concerned? A. I only say that from a letter written to me by my son - I had no reason to suspect he was going to leave me till he went.
   RICHARD ROSS. I am shopman to Mr. Tunis, a pawnbroker, of Liquor-pond-street. Here are five hearth-rugs, pawned by the prisoner, between the 2nd and 23rd of May - he came alone; I advanced 10s. on some, and less on others.
 Cross-examined. Q. This is more than three months ago? A. Yes, I am certain he is the man.
   SAMUEL HOLLINGSWORTH. I have no doubt of these being my property - I never sold them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you not a large stock of such articles? A. Yes - my porter has been convicted of stealing articles of this description; I have not sold rugs of exactly this pattern.
 COURT. Q. Have you any recollection of seeing that property about, shortly before May? A. Yes: it is a particular pattern, which I do not suppose could be got.
 MR. BARRY. Q. Do you manufacture them? A. No- it is an old pattern, which has been out of work many years; it was made expressly for me - I have not sold any of this pattern for years, and had but this one; these rugs had a shop-ticket on them.
   JOHN LIMBRICK. I am an officer. I took the prisoner up on the 9th of July; he said he was very sorry, and that the porter and his wife had brought him into this trouble; this was on his being charged with stealing carpet and rugs.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was his father present? A. He was; I made him no promise or threat; the father had no private interview with him to my knowledge - he told me this at No. 50, Baldwin's-gardens: he was in a wretched plight when I took him - I do not know of his being married.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was forced to do it, for my father kept me short on Saturday, during his absence. GUILTY (of stealing to the value of 99s. only.) Aged 18.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Year.

 1759.  MARY FITZGERALD was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND, and SCARLETT conducted the prosecution.
   ANN CRAMPTON. I am niece to Joseph Pullen, who keeps the  Blue Last public-house, in Broadway, Blackfriars. On the 16th of July the prisoner came in, and had a glass of porter, which came to 1 1/2d. - she gave me a shilling; I saw it was counterfeit, and laid it on a shelf over the engine, by itself - she came again in about half an hour, had another glass of porter, and paid me a shilling; I laid that by the other, as I suspected it, but being alone in the bar, and my uncle not at home, I could not detain her - he came in in half an hour, and I gave him both the shillings; he laid them on the same shelf again, apart from any others; they laid there till the 27th of July, when the prisoner came again, about a quarter to ten o'clock at night, in company, and asked for a glass of porter and a glass of gin; she paid for both with a shilling - I told her it was counterfeit, and it made four counterfeit shillings which she had passed at our house; she said it was quite unknown to her if they were counterfeit - I gave Mr. Pullen that shilling; a constable was sent for, and took her in charge; I marked that shilling directly I took it - I gave the constable three shillings, only one of them was what she had paid me before; the other had been mislaid - one of those I gave the constable I had from Mrs. Pullen.
 Prisoner. I did not know they were bad; I said if it was bad I was willing to change it. Witness. She said so at the watch-house, but not before.
   SARAH PULLEN. I am the wife of Mr. Pullen. On the 27th of July, at a quarter-past nine o'clock at night, the prisoner came in alone, and had a glass of porter - she paid me a shilling; I gave her 10 1/2d., and she left - I put the shilling by itself, and gave it to Camplin.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not put it into a drawer? A. I put it into a part of the till where there was no silver whatever.
   JOSEPH PULLEN. My niece gave me two shillings which she had received; I put them in the bar, and gave them to her again.
   SAMUEL DOWDNEY. I am a constable. I was sent for on the 27th of July, and took the prisoner; I received from Crampton three counterfeit shillings, which I produce- I have kept them separate ever since; I diligently searched the prisoner in the watch-house, and found on her 3s. 6d. in good silver, and 1s. worth of copper.
   WILLIAM CRAMPTON. These are the shillings; I know them by the marks I put on them.
   JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of coin at His Majesty's Mint. These shillings are all counterfeit, and are cast, two from one mould, and one from another.
 The prisoner delivered in a written Defence, declaring she was not conscious of the money being counterfeit, and that she had received them in the street, where she sold thread and tape.
 JURY to MRS. PULLEN. Q. What was your reason for putting the shilling in the till? A. I thought she was the person who had passed bad money before, and I put it there separate from any other; I took it out as soon as she went out, which was in a moment.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. Confined One Year, and to find Sureties.
