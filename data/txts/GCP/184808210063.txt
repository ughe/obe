Mungo Park. I am a surgeon. On 6th Aug., about half-past one, I
vas called to the deceased she appeared dead, but I was not quite certain
-I had her removed into a house, and opened a vein--very few drops of
blood flowed-I could find no external marks of violence-on 19th, I made
a post-mortem examination with Mr. Courtnay-there was a great extravasa-
tion of blood at the base of the brain-the larger quantity was on the left
side-it appeared to arise from the rupture of a blood-vessel-blow with a
denched fist on the left side of the head would be likely to produce it-it
might not have left an external mark the brain was not very healthy-
blow to a subject of that kind, though not very violent, might rupture &
blood vessel--the extravasation of blood was the immediate cause of death
the brain was slightly congested, so that less violence would produce serious
consequences.
Cross-examined. Q. The brain was very much diseased? A. Yes; that
vas of long standing-I imagine from the effluvia, she had been drinking all
the morning-if she had been very drunk, the exertion of walking would not
be so likely to produce extravasation as if she had fallen down--if she had
resisted a person, it is possible the excitement would have caused it, but not
90 probable as a blow-if her head fell back against the railings, that would
very likely cause it.
