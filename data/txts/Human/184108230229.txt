JOHN FOSTER. I am the prosecutor's brother. This is hit waistcoat—he never gave it to me.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you ever been in your brother's employ? A.  I was there at the time this waistcoat was there—I was a tailor—the prisoner was groom—my brother was in the habit of wearing this waist-coat, perhaps for nine or ten months—I remember, on one occasion, re quiring a horse to go to Romford—I asked the prisoner to get me the horse—he did get it—I did not give him this waistcoat for getting me the horse. AMELIA BUTLER. The prisoner kept company with me for some time—we were likely to be married, and he has refused to marry me—I have seen him wear this waistcoat.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where are you living now? A.  With my father and mother—I knew a man named Bates some time ago—I left my father to be married to the prisoner—I think it was the week before Easter—I left in the evening—I know persons are not married after twelve o'clock—I never lived with Bates, nor with any body bat the prisoner—I staid with him three weeks—I told my father that the prisoner would not marry me—I told Mr. Foster that I had got a bit of this satin in my bonnet. CHARLES STEWART. I am a police-constable. I took the prisoner at Mr. Lock's, at Lambeth, his last master's—I found this waistcoat there on the counter. GUILTY. Aged 31.— Confined Nine Months.
