ELIZA CHAPMAN. I am the prosecutor's servant—I saw the prisoner in the shop on the 16th of February—I left her there alone—I was not away more than a minute—I did not sell her any flour.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How many bags of flour did you sell between December and February to different customers? A.  It is very seldom that I sell flour—I do not think I sold a dozen or half a dozen—it is put on the shelf ready weighed to sell to any one—if I happen to be in the shop I sell it—I served the prisoner with bread frequently—she did not pay me—I told my master of it. JOHN WALKER( police-constable R  182.) The prosecutor sent for me on the 19th of February—I went to the prisoner's and found this bag of floor—as we were returning from the Magistrate's, the prosecutor said he was not satisfied, we went to the prisoner's house again, and he found this marked bag, empty, in a drawer.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you went first to the house with the prose cutor did you not search to find a marked bag? A.  Yes, and I could not—we took the prisoner to the Magistrate—he said the evidence was insuffi cient, and he would remand her, and before the prisoner got home, the prosecutor went to her house to get the linen she had to wash, and there he found this marked bag in a drawer in the front-room—that was the room in which we at first found the bag of flour—I did not then look where this marked bag was—it was in a drawer of an old chest of drawers, as it ap peared to me—I did not take notice when I first went, as I saw a paper had been torn up, and I said to the prosecutor, "It is in rain to look for a bag."
   Q.  Is it true or not that you "looked carefully for a marked bag?" A.  Yes, and when I saw a bag torn up on the floor I said it was no use to look for it—I did not perceive the torn bag on the floor the instant I en tered, not till I looked round the room and saw that flour had been shifted from one bag to another—the first-thing I did was to look about the back-room, and then I opened a cupboard in the front room and saw this bag of flour—we then took the prisoner, and the prosecutor, not being satisfied, went again—he found his linen one corner of the back-room—when I first went to the house the prisoner aid she had no flour at all of Mr. Floyd's—when I found the bag of flour she said the servant had served her with it—I saw nothing in the cupboard—the flour had been shifted from the bag that had been torn up, into a cloth bag—I found two or three quarterns of flour in a cloth bag—there was a py named Tinsley at the Magistrate's office—he gave some evidence, and it was put in the depositions—neither he nor I were called in before the Grand bury—but he is here, as he was bound over. WILLIAM FLOYD re-examined. Q.  When did you mark this bag that you found empty? A.  On the 15th it was filled with flour, and I lost it.
