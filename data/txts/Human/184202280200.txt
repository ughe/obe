   Q.  Upon your oath did you not strike your wife most brutally in pre sence of this gentleman who is here? A.  No—I will not swear that did not—will that gentleman swear she was not drunk?—when my master went out he did not agree with me about the time he should come back—I have had things from my master's shop—I expect to pay for them—I have not paid them out since—I do not know whether I pay the cost price or the price a customer would pay—I have got a bill of them from Mr. Stedall—I got the bill the first night I was going to pay money on them, which was last Saturday night week—I think it was on the Saturday he fore the prisoner was taken—I have paid money two Saturday nights—I have never had many things—I had some candlesticks, and a fender, and a kettle and saucepan, and various little things of that sort—I did not come so high us getting an oven.
   COURT . Q.  Did you take them, and tell your master after you took them? A.  No, I asked him to let me have them, before I had them at all.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Where was the oven when the boy took it? A.  Along side of him in the shop—I did not stay below that the boy nights go out with it—I went out so that I should have nothing to do with it, because the prisoner wanted me to give it to the boy—I knew it was gone—when I went down stairs to my bench to work I lifted up my bead and saw the boy go past with it—I waited at my bench, looking through the window—I expected to see the boy go by with the oven.
   MR. CLARKSON to MR. STEDALL . Q.  You made out a bill for Blake, was that after the prisoner was in custody or before? A.  I believe it was previously—I will swear I made out the bill before the prisoner was is custody—I did not give Blake the bill—it is now on my desk—I did not charge him cost price. GEORGE WEEKS. I am errand boy to Mr. Stedall. Between four and five o'clock on that day the prisoner said to me, "George, I have got as American oven down stairs; I bought the tin, and Blake is making is for me; I want you to take it to my house"—he said, "I have got some things going up that way, and I will get you to take it"—then he said, "Blake has done the oven, go down stairs and fetch it up; your basket is down there, put it in your basket and bring it up here"—he then said "Look sharp, mistress is out of the way," and then I took it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Why were you to look sharp as mistress was out of the way, if the prisoner told you he bought the tin? A.
