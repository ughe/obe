THoMas MoRGAN. In August, 1894, I lived at 175, New Cross Road,
and was post-master at 177, where there is a letter-box opening on to
the street-I left No. 177 on August 14th, 1894, about seven o'clock,
and went to 175, where I sleep, leaving the premises secure-l had just
got into bed, and heard a very loud sound like a collision, or the hursting
of a gas meter-I opened my window, looked out, and saw people running
towards the Post-office, calling out that it was on fire-a I opened the
door of 175 I smelt a sort of gunpowder smell-I went to 177; there was
a very great crowd-I found the cross plate of the letter-box very much
bent outwards, but hanging on by a corner, the window broken, and the
letters in the letter-box and the stationery in the shop window on fire-
a railway inspector and a constable were engaged in extinguishing the
fire-Sumner, Cox and Mr. Clegg appeared to be guarding the box from the
public-the box would be cleared at midnightit had been cleared at
nine Oclock-my neighbours and I took possession of the contents of the
letter-box-the damage done to the shop was estimated at 265, but that
is not correct; I think the bill was E45 ; I can produce it-everything in
the Post-office was knocked about all over the place-I think this was
on a Tuesday, and on the next Monday I received a letter in this en-
velope (produced) unpaid, and 0. H. M. S." outside-I had made some
statements which got into the press] was very much pressed, and I am
afraid I yielded too much-one old lady and two young ones were living
on the premises.
