PEROY YOUNG. I was in partnership with a Mr. Brown as wholesale
stationers in November, 1879, we were introduced to the prisoner, and
he asked us to make advances on some paper in the Surrey Commercial
Docks and Globe Wharf; about 37 tong-he said "I can let you have it
at a price, but can't give you the warrants, having got advances from
the Globe Company and Levy and Co."-he sold us the paper at 131. 108.,
and we paid 401. in cash on 28th November, 1879, and two bills for the
balance-we paid off the loans, 8331.-in January, 1880, the prisoner
brought a warrant for cigarettes and some potted meat-our transaction
with the prisoner came altogether to about 8001.
Cross-examined. We advanced you about 301. or 407. to start with-I
cannot tell, without the books, whether our first transaction was for
1291. 198.-I have no right to the books now-I had principally to do
with the banking part of the business ; Mr. Brown has the books about
10th August you asked me to send you some money-you may have
offered me this paper at 161. or 171. a ton-I was not the buyer, Mr.
Brown bought it-on 20th August we received from you a debtor's
summons for 3021., and we filed our petition for liquidation after that,
not in consequence of it-I came to your office with my father on one
occasion to know how much we owed you. (Letters of 10th and 14tk
August, 1880, read.) I never promised to go into partnership with you
--we never had as much as 6001. worth of goods from you-you made a
most ridiculous claim, and we should have prosecuted you, but Messrs.
Williams took it out of our hands-we had goods from you-I gave
you cheques for 501., 301., and 201., they were in exchange for your
cheques-I was not present at the meeting of creditors-we did not owe
you 3001., you owed us money.
Re-examined. The prisoner attempted to prove for 3027., but his proof
was rejected-all the cheques I gave the prisoner were honoured'; his
last cheque was not, and we stopped the goods-our transactions were
from November, 1879, to June, 1880—he owes us 4741.
