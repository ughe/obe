   Cross-examined.  I had lived with the prisoner about 18 months, during which time he was a kind good man to me and my children, except when he got a drop of drink, which he did now and then, and it made him like an insane person—when he came to me on the 7th August he was beastly drunk—the wound was very slight and did not prevent my going on with my work—I should not have taken notice of it if he had not come back with the constable.
   By the COURT . I do not remember him saying he wished it had gone through my heart; he might have said it to the constable, but not to me. GEORGE HOLLIS( Policeman P  121). About 1.30 in the afternoon of the 7th August the prisoner came to me at Camberwell Gate and said he had stabbed a woman at the laundry in Albany Road—he was very much excited, but not drunk—I went back with him and another constable, and went to the ironing room at the laundry, where I saw the last witness, and she said in his presence she had been stabbed, and she should charge him with it, and he said "I wish it had gone through her b——heart "I was given this knife ( produced )—the prosecutrix said it was done with this knife, and I took him to the station—on his way he said he wished it had gone to her b——heart, and that would be an end of her—the other constable wrote it down.
