 261. PHILIP SPARKS was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of November , 30lbs. weight of potatoes, value 1 s  ., the goods of Samuel Cornell . SAMUEL CORNELL. I am a farmer, and live at Hill Farm, Woodford-bridge, Essex. On the 17th of November I went to Romford—I returned, and received information about the prisoner, who had been in my employ for about seven years—I had directed him that morning to put two sacks of potatoes into my cellar—I got a policeman, and went and found the pri soner in the road with a bag of potatoes—I examined them—they were exactly like what I had in my cellar—the sacks in my cellar were defi cient in quantity—I found thirty pounds of potatoes in the prisoner's bag, but I missed more than that.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did you measure the pota toes that were left in the cellar? A.  No, but I could tell that I missed more than 1cwt. ELIZA MOTT. I am in the prosecutor's service. On the 17th of No vember I was in the dairy, and saw the prisoner employed in taking pota toes into the cellar—I noticed, when he came up from the. cellar, that he had some potatoes in a sack on his back—he then emptied them out of the tack into a bag—the sack was such a one as my master uses for potatoes—I told my master when he came home. GEORGE ABRAHAM MOORE( police-constable K  76.) I went with the prosecutor, and met the prisoner with the potatoes on his shoulder—I asked where he got them from—he said from where he worked. GUILTY. Aged 46.— Recommended to mercy .— Confined Fourteen Days.
   KENT CASES .
