NEW COURT.-Monday, September 21st; Tuesday, 22nd; Wednesday, 23rd;
Thursday, 24th, and Friday, 25th, 1868.
Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Esq.
830. SARAH RACHEL LEVERSON (43), Unlawfully obtaining goods
and money value 6002., by false pretences, from Mary Tucker Borradaile.
Other Counts-for conspiring to defraud her of 30001.
MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE, MR. M. WILLIAMS, and Mr. STRAIGHT conducted
the Prosecution; and MR. DIGBY SEYMOUR, Q.C., MR. SERJEANT PARRT,
MR. SERJEANT SLEIGH, and MR. BUTLER RIGBY the Defence.
MARY TUCKER BORRADAILE was examined from the Sessions Paper (See p. 334),
and repeated the evidence there given ; she also gave the following additional
evidence on cross-examination : Madame Rachel mentioned 1000 guineas as the
price of being made beautiful for ever-that was the charge for enamelling,
not that she enamelled me, for she did nothing of the sort, she said that
it was necessary I should be made beautiful for ever" before I was
married to the rich and good man, meaning Lord Ranelagh-I took more
than a hundred baths by her direction-I swear that I never borrowed any
money of her—this letter is in my writing : “4, Francis Street, London
Street, Paddington, May, 1866. My dear Rachel. My husband, Colonel
Borradaile, died the 17th of May, 1861. Will proved by Mr. Nelson, City
solicitor, whose brother lives in Gracechurch Street, to whom I refer
you, and he will arrange everything to your satisfaction. I was left
sole executrix to all he possessed in the world. He left one will in London
with his nephew, Mr. J. C. Borradaile, Blackheath, and the other in India
I refer you to my mother, who resides at Sealey, Ham, near Haverfordwest,
Pembrokeshire, South Wales. I am related to Lord Kensington, who resides
in London; therefore, my dear Rachel, you may feel assured I am mistress
of my own actions, and may do what I think proper with my own, and this
will at once convince you that I can fulfil my promise, and carry out
any arrangement with you that I have entered into." -That letter
was written at Rachel's shop, at her request, but she told me to
direct it from Francis Street, where I lived for a few days—I do not

fan-light, over the door-you can say nothing against my charao.
