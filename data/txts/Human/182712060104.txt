MR. CHURCHILL contended this was not a felony, but only an obtaining of the goods by false pretences.
 CAMPBELL's Defence. If I have committed myself in the purchase of these goods, I must bow to your decision; but I am in duty bound to say Lewis did not know what I had purchased, or any thing about them; he had been acquainted with me some time - I met him in Cheapside, and asked him to walk with me - in consequence of my not meeting some friends, and the parcel being large, I determined to take it and divide it, that I might send it next day. Mr. Berens was to see me next morning, and if I had paid him that evening, I should have paid him for some goods which I had not received, as I was to have a parcel from him in the morning, and as I had to meet him in the afternoon it would do: for if I called on my friend who was to lend me the 200l. he would have lent it; I have not seen that gentleman in Court - I was not informed till last night that I was to be tried this afternoon, and the person who was to have lent me the money may not be here - I only called to select a few goods - Mr. Berens pressed me to buy these - I should have called on him in the morning, and paid the money; my friend, who was to lend me the money, was here last night.
 CAMPBELL - GUILTY. Aged 25.

 225.  HENRY BUTCHER was indicted for embezzlement.
   JOHN RUCK. I am in partnership with Cornelius Ruck; we are cheesemongers, and live in the Borough; I paid the prisoner 7l. on the 13th of October, on account, for Messrs. Williams.
   BENJAMIN NICHOLAS WILLIAMS. I am in partnership with Joseph Richard Williams; we are ship and insurance-brokers; the prisoner was our clerk for two years; he was employed to receive money for us - he was to enter it on his return in the cash-book, and pay it immediately; I have not the cash-book here, but I have examined it - he did not pay 7l. on the 13th of October, as received from Mr. Ruck.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is not your knowledge of this derived from your cash-book? A. Yes.
 COURT. Q. Had you any conversation with him about this? A. Yes, about this and other sums; I mentioned there were two sums, amounting to 7l. for freights, which he had received from Messrs. Rucks; but I cannot recollect whether I stated that it was composed of two items - I charged him with receiving 7l., this was on the 1st of December; I showed him this paper - the conversation began with himself - he said, "I am very sorry my accounts are deficient" - I then put this paper into his hand, which amounts to 98l., which I took from the cash-book, not from his book - I said I had discovered various sums, amounting to 98l., and I feared it was to a much greater extent - he said he did not know the exact amount he had received - I put this list into his hand, and Mr. Ruck's 7l. is two of those items - he said, "He thought he had taken about 70l. more than was due to him as salary:" my partner had told him he had better give an account of all he had received.
     NOT GUILTY.

 228.  JAMES GROVER was indicted, for that he, on the 19th of November , at St. Dunstan in the West, in and upon John Williams , feloniously, wilfully, and maliciously, did make an assault, and with a certain sharp instrument, feloniously, &c. did stab and cut the said John Williams, in and upon the left arm of the said John Williams, with intent, feloniously, &c. to kill and murder him, against the statute, &c.
 SECOND COUNT, with intent to disable him, &c.
 THIRD COUNT, with intent to do him some grievous bodily harm.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am a tailor, and live at No. 9, Mark's-place, Fetter-lane. On the 19th of November, about seven o'clock in the evening, I heard a disturbance of several persons, as I thought, running down the place, where I live; I opened the door to see who it might be, as there is no thoroughfare. I saw the prisoner at the bar, pursuing his wife; he at that time lived in the court, on the opposite side of the way. I did not at that time see that he had any thing in his hand - I only saw him and his wife; when I opened my door, he turned, came to me, and said "You bl-y rogue, I will murder you," and shoved a knife as I suppose it to be into my left arm.
 Q. Was that the first word you heard uttered? A. Yes; I found I was stabbed; I put my hand to my arm, and the blood was gushing out; he attempted several times forward with the instrument which he had in his hand to stab me again. I stepped back into the house, and put my hand a little behind me, where I knew there was a yard measure, and took that in my own defence; he did not come in - he was at the door; I struck him with the yard, and drove him back into the court; he still continued thrusting at me, till I broke the yard measure into short pieces, in my hand; he then ran away into his own house. I heard him say he would stab his wife also - he would murder she also. I sent for an officer, who took him, and a surgeon, who dressed my wound.
