 545. WILLIAM WALTON WILKINSON was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of January , 5 half-crowns, the monies of Ralph Needham , his master. RALPH NEEDHAM. I am a linen-draper, and live in Shoreditch. The prisoner was my shopman—I had some suspicion, and marked half-crowns, shillings, and sixpences, to the amount of 3 l .—on the 29th of January and put them in ht till, about nine o'clock—I then went upon a lead-flat, where there is a sky-light, through which I could see into the shop—I saw the prisoner go round the counter, pull the till out and put his hand in what he took out I cannot tell—I came down, and counted the money and found one half-crown short—I sent for a policeman, and gave the pri soner in charge—this half-crown is mine—it is marked with a cross behind the head.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Where were they taken from? A.  Four were found in his box—three of them were marked—this is one that was in the till that night—I missed this half-crown.
   Q.  Can you take upon yourself to swear positively that there have not been shillings and sixpence to an equivalent amount put into your till? A.  I can; the other four half-crowns I had not missed, but I can swear to these being mine—three of them are marked with my initials behind the head—I know the prisoner had a particular fancy for half-crowns. THOMAS SEAL( police constable G  16) I took the prisoner into cus tody—I took him to the station and received from him the key of his box—I went and found in it forty half-crowns, one of which was marked and Mr. Needham identified it as his—they were sealed up in a white paper—I searched further and in another part of the box I found in a leathern purse 4 l . 15 s . in half-crows, three amongst which were marked—the prisoner declared that all he had taken was 17 s . 6 d ., all in half-crowns. WILLIAM BROOM COSS( police-constable G  217) I was called in to take the prisoner—I found on him this half-crown—I then went with Seal, and found this other money in his box.
