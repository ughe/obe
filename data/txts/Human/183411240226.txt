   Q.  You have stated that the prisoner went out of the room, as you sup posed, for a necessary purpose, but before he went, he said he should keep this till the game was played out—I suppose you did not ask him what he was going out for? A.  No, but I went out in a minute or two after wards—I found at the Spotted Dog, that he lived in Cheyne-row—I did not go to his house—I thought I should not be able to find him—I was at a loss how to proceed—I have not seen Buxton here to-day—I did not employ him at all in this transaction.
   Q.  You have stated that the cards were dealt—that you left yours on the table, and went to get a pot of ale—upon your oath, when you were before the Magistrate did you state that? A.  I believe I did—I do not know—if I had been asked the question I should—I believe my cards had been improperly dealt with while I was out—I do not know whether I said a word about that before the Magistrate—I should expect I did, for I stated every particular.
   Q.  On the first occasion before the Magistrate, did you say a single syllable about your having been playing at any game whatever? A.  I consider that I must—I explained how the thing occurred—I will swear I did.
   Q.  Upon your oath, upon the second examination, did not Mr. Min shull complain of your having omitted to state any thing about the game till you came to the second examination? A.  I never heard any com plaint.
