   JASPER FLETCHER. I am a watchman. I was going my round about eight minutes past ten o'clock, and I made a stand, as I always do, at one particular corner, I saw the prisoner come out at the wicket gate, with this malt on his shoulder; the gate opens into Brewers'-green, where Mr. Chamber's premises are - I went up to him, and asked what he had there; he said, "Malt." I asked where he was going, he said, "Round there;" I said, I should go with him, and he went round to No. 12; he made a stand, and a little girl came out. I felt the sack - I told him to put it down, and asked if Mr. Chambers knew of it; he said he did - I said I should take him and the malt to the watch-house, till I had authority from Mr. Chambers; he asked me if I knew him - I said, No; he asked if I knew his mother, Mrs. Bunn; I said, I did, by passing through my beat - he asked if I would allow him to send for her; I said Yes: by that little girl, which he did - the mother came, and asked what was the matter, and told me if I would come round the corner, she would explain things to me. I went round, and as soon as I got in at the door, she wished me for God Almighty's sake, to have nothing to do with it; as soon as I found how things were going on, I retreated away; the prisoner had then made his escape. I took the malt to the watch-house, and the prisoner was taken in bed, about twelve o'clock by the patrol.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where did you leave him? A. By the wicket; I was not away above five minutes - I am sober now, and have drunk nothing but a pint of porter; I do not recollect whether I have been to more than one public-house.
 Prisoner's Defence. The malt was not Mr. Chambers'. I cart malt and ask for the sweepings; I had saved it and meant to brew with it.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.

 1064.  GEORGE COLE and JAMES READ were indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 1 sheep, price 36s., the property of James Lee.
 MESSRS. ADOLPHUS and CRESWELL conducted the prosecution.
 JAMES LEE. I am a  drover, and live at Mile-end. On Sunday, the 24th of February, I received sixty-nine sheep from Mr. Gay, a Norfolk drover. I marked twenty of them along the back, and forty-nine on the shoulder, with red ochre, and forty-five of them had an O made with orange-coloured paint - I gave them to my brother to place them in a lair at the Globe public-house, at Mile-end, where they were to be left for Mr. Gay's man to drive to Smithfield.
   JOSEPH LEE. I am a drover. On the 24th of February I assisted my brother to mark sixty-nine sheep, forty-nine of them on the shoulder, and one score on the back, with red ochre; I assisted John Denham to place them in the lair; that was on Sunday morning, about nine o'clock - I counted them by my brother's order, and put them into a place of security.
   JOHN DENHAM. I am a drover, in the employ of Mr. Gay, who lives in Norfolk. I came up with these sheep, and assisted to put them into the lair - I went on Monday morning, at two o'clock, to take them; I found them in a different lair to the one I left them in: I counted them out of the gate, and there were but one hundred and twenty-seven - I should have had one hundred and twenty-eight; there were fifty-nine which I had from Mr. Barrell, marked with what is called a bounce mark on the shoulder, and forty-four or forty-five marked with an old brand-mark on each side, and three or four with an old mark, that we could not say what it was; they were put altogether on the Sunday evening; when I got them out of the lair I missed one; I was going up Mile-end-road, and met two of Conder's men coming back for another flock; they told me they had got one of mine at Smithfield; this was about half-past two o'clock, while I was driving the sheep to market; I got to Smithfield about four o'clock; I met Mr. Lee in Long-lane, and said to him, "I am one sheep short - I do not know whether it is one of yours;" Conder then told me the sheep was in Mr. French's pens in Smithfield; I parted Mr. Lee's sheep from the others, and found it was one of his that was missing; whether it was branded over, or whether it was the old brand-mark, I cannot say; I did not go to Mr. French's pens, for Mr. Lee said if the sheep was there he would let it be till it was day-light, as it was safe enough - I then went away.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Are you sure it was one hundred and twenty-eight sheep you had in the whole on the overnight? A. Yes; they were put in the lair about five o'clock; I saw there were forty-four or forty-five with the brand-mark.
   JAMES CONDER. I am a drover. On the 24th of February I had a great many sheep to come to Smithfield; I put them into the Globe lair, Mile-end; I moved them from there at eight o'clock at night; Denham had a morning drove, which laid next to mine; when I got to Smithfield I found I had all my sheep and one over, which was marked on the shoulder with red ochre; I expected it belonged to Denham's drove, because his drove had been mixed with mine; I and my two men parted them; I mentioned that to Lee when I saw him.
   JAMES GUIVER. I am a drover in the employ of Mr. Lee. On the 25th of February, between two and three o'clock in the morning, I saw Cole lead a sheep across Smithfield-market; it was marked with red-ochre between the shoulders - he took it into the lower alley, nearly opposite the Ram public-house, he put it into the second pen on the left-hand - after the man was gone, I went and looked at it, and saw the mark; I do not know what became of it afterwards.
 Cross-examined. Q. What time was this? A. Between one and two; it was dark.
