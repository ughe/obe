   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How far did you watch the man? A.  From the top of the Minories at Aldgate, up to the Mint—I have tried to get into the City Police, my father being a liveryman—I did not suc ceed, on account of my height—I was never accused of any thing wrong—I know Esther Haynes—I was not employed to whitewash a room of hers—she never accused me of taking 1 l . 5 s . out of her drawer—I know Charlotte Lawton—I never pretended to her that I was a widower, that my wife was buried in Stepney-churchyard, and want her to marry me—I did not show her my wife's grave—she came after me—my wife was living at the time, and is now—Lawton lent me two sovereigns—I paid her again, and have the receipt to prove it.
   Q.  Why not stop the man before, and not wait till he took the box; why not tell the carman somebody was trying to rob him? A.  I cannot tell my reason—the box was very heavy, he could hardly carry it—I will take my oath he had it on his hip. JOHN RUSHBY. I am a builder, and live in Lincolnshire. I was with my van, the carman was driving it, who I employed to take the goods to the wharf—I was called to stop, which I did, and missed the box—I saw the prisoner in custody of a policeman, and the box was produced—there was a coat of my own in it, and the other property belonged to my sister, Elizabeth Smith.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What sort of a van was it? A.  A four-wheeled van—the box was at the back part of it—not fastened—it could not have fallen out, for it was too far in, and the tail-board was up. JAMES SMITH. I am a policeman. I heard the cry of "Policeman," and saw Lee holding the prisoner in one hand and the box in the other—he charged him with taking it—the prisoner immediately said he did not take it, that it fell from the van and he picked it up.
