Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Do you live with your husband
at this place ? A. I do when he is in town, but he is now an invalid in
the Bath Infirmary-he went from me on the 13th of April last-he had
been living with me till that time-he never left me in his life I came
down stairs as Mrs. Mully was opening her room doorher room and
Mr. Roberts's were both on a floor-I saw all that she did she first
opened the door and looked in, and I looked over her shoulder, or by her
side-I did not see it in any other way I did not look through the
key hole, but Mally did- I looked through the crevice of the door that
was after she had opened the door-it was a box that Mrs. Smith was
kneeling at-it might be about half as far off as you are from me--it is a very
small room-I did not stop till Mrs. Smith came out I went up stairs,
but I came down immediately I saw Mrs. Smith rise from the box-she
put the bag into her pocket-I did not see her put any thing back into
the box-when Mrs. Mully opened the door, she just put her head into
the room-I know Mrs. Payne; she lived servant to Mrs. Mully I can-
not say how long she had lived so, but suppose about three or four months
-she did not sleep in the house, but she took her meals with Mrs. Mully.
she was there that morning, and it was from her I heard that the old
gentleman was dead-I believe she was in the passage when Mrs. Mully
was peeping in I am sure Mrs. Mully said she should wait till Mrs.
Smith came out, and let her know that she had seen her at the box-she
said that slowly-Mrs. Smith was in the room at the time I did not hear
Mrs. Mully say she never kept a servant-I do not know whether she
called Payne her servant or not, but she did her work-Mrs. Smith told
me that Mr. Roberts was coming to town, and got me to clean the
parlour-I knew there was a letter sent by Mr. Hyde-I did not know
what day Mr. Roberts was coming--my maiden name was Duffield
have been married eighteen years I do not recollect where I was in 1818
or 1819-I was living at Thetford, in Norfolk I have been in Thetford
court many times to hear the trials, but was never under any charge, or in
any trouble-- was once in a watch-house, about four months ago,
because a woman and I had some words--I never was in any difficulty
about a pocket-book, that I can swear.
MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Is Mrs. Payne any relation to these prisoners ?
4. She is Mr. Smith's own sister-I have never lived apart from my
husband in my life, and never was under any charge.
