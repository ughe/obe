 1746.  ELIZABETH DUGGAN was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , 1 ring, value 14s., the goods of Edward Storr.
 The witnesses did not appear.    NOT GUILTY.
 1747.  HENRY COOKE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , 1 sheet, value 3s., the goods of Arthur Graham.
   ARTHUR GRAHAM. I lost a sheet on the 1st of September from off the bed which the prisoner slept on at my house, the Northumberland's Head; I found it the same morning at Mr. Sowerby's - the prisoner stated he was in great distress, and I believe he was so.
   WILLIAM EDWARDS. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a sheet, pawned by the prisoner on the 1st of September, about eight o'clock in the morning.
   THOMAS HART. I took up the prisoner, and found this duplicate on him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 22. (See page 763.)
 1748.  JOHN BENNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , 1 axe, value 3s., the goods of William Hamling.
   ROBERT FANCOURT. I took up the prisoner about eight o'clock in the morning of the 28th of August, with this axe.
   RICHARD BLATCHFORD. The prisoner had been working with me and William Hamling. On the evening of the 26th of August I saw Hamling using this axe - on the morning of the 27th it was missed; suspicion fell on the prisoner - I took him the day following with the axe under his arm; I told him what I charged him with, and he said it was an old axe he had picked up in a stable - he took it out of a bag and said, "Is this it?" I said Yes.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Confined Two Months.
 1749.  HANNAH ALLEN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 1s.; 1 gold pin, value 7s.; two rows of beads, value 2s.; 1 silver stopper, value 2s.; 1 pair of ear-rings. value 2s.; 2 pockets, value 2s., and 20 half-crowns, the property of Marry Allen, widow ; and 1 bonnet, value 25s., the goods of Matilda Jones.
   MARY ALLEN. The prisoner is my daughter - I live in the Edgware-road; she has not been in my house, to my knowledge, for sixteen or seventeen months - I do not know who took these things, but I missed them on Monday, the 27th of July.
   MATILDA JONES. I am the prosecutrix's niece, and live with her - I found the bureau was ransacked, the things turned out, and the articles stated missing.
   SARAH PRATT. I am the wife of Thomas Pratt- we live in Windmill-street, Tottenham-court-road; the prisoner lodged with me, and was taken on Monday, the 10th of August - her box was found in my house, which had been brought there with her.
   HANNAH MUNDAY. I am the wife of Jacob Munday - we live in Burn's-place, Edgware-road; I can see from my bed-room into Mrs. Allen's house. I saw the prisoner on Mrs. Allen's fence on the 27th of July - the fence broke, and she went over into another garden - she then stood upon the fence some time - I called to her, but she did not answer me; she then went into Mr. Burke's wash-house, got on the files, and into the loft of Mrs. Allen's house - I waited about an hour and a half; she did not come out - I then went to bed; I told Mrs. Allen of it on the Friday evening.
   MARY ALLEN. On the Sunday night, the 26th, I tried a little room by the side of my bed-room, and found it locked - I thought my niece had taken the key; in the morning, when she came, I said, "Why did you lock that door?" she said. I did not;" she opened the door, we went in, and missed her bonnet, a cornelian necklace, a pair of ear-rings, two brooches, a silver knife, two shirt-pins, and a great many other things, which I have never seen since - I had seen them safe on the Saturday or Sunday, in the bureau; I know the bureau was fast on the Sunday - whoever took the things must have secreted themselves in the house; the bureau was wrenched all to pieces - the prisoner knew the bureau; and in her father's life time, if we had a little money, it was always put there - I went to Mrs. Pratt's house, and saw the box which my daughter had brought there; I saw a great many things of mine there, and a great number of duplicates - the officer took her; I do not recollect that she said any thing.
 Prisoner. I leave it to the mercy of the Court and my mother.
 JURY to MRS. ALLEN Q. Did you turn your daughter out of doors? A. No; she has been robbing me these five years successively - I have seen her but once since her father's death, and then she was in a situation.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Seven Days.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
