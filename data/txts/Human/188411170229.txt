MESSRS. POLAND and MONTAGU WILLIAMS Prosecuted ; Mr. PURCELL
   Defended at the request of the COURT . GEORGE NASH. I Live at 8, Clarendon Street, and am a labourer—on Sunday, 21st September, about half-past 12 o'clock in the morning, I was with a man named Murphy and Arnold at the top of Clarendon Street—we all three went to a coffee stall at the top of Bermondsey New Road; we met on the road Harris, William Piggott, Margaret Good son, and Annie Keefe—I did not know her, but I knew Goodson—Goodson said to me "What cheer, Punch?"—I said "What cheer, Mog?"—Harris immediately after that came round and struck me in the jaw—I said "What did you do that for?"—he then hit me again, and said, "What did you want to kick her for?"—I had not touched her—I hit him and knocked him against the shutters—Arnold then held me back and said "I am more his equal than you"—I got away from Arnold and struck Harris in the face—he then went to the shutters and pulled some thing from underneath the left breast of his coat and struck me in the back—I then found that I was stabbed—Harris then ran away and Arnold after him—I was taken to the hospital; when I got there I found that Arnold was there—he was wounded also.
   Cross-examined.  When I and Arnold came up the two girls were talk ing to the prisoner—I heard the prisoner say "What did you want to kick her for?"—I did not hear him say "Don't interfere with these girls"—Arnold did not strike the prisoner in the face and make his nose bleed, nor did he knock him down—I did not knock him down, I am quite certain of it—he did not say while on the ground "Let me get up; oh, they are killing me."
   Re-examined.  I picked up a hat afterwards in the crowd—I gave it to Constable P 267. JOHN MURPHY. I am a labourer, living at Tabard Street—on the night of 20th September I was with Nash at a friendly meeting, and about 20 minutes past 12 we went to the top of Clarendon Street, and there met Arnold—we all three then went along to Old Kent Road to a coffee stall—we met Harris with two or three women—Nash spoke to the girls and said "Halloa, Mog"—with that Harris struck him in the jaw and said "What did you kick her for?"—with that Harris stepped back and Nash ran towards him—I stood on the causeway and saw Harris running along by the shirt shop as if trying to get away from Nash—he fell back towards the shops—Arnold ran after him, and I ran towards him to see what was the matter—I saw Arnold stagger back, and he said "I am stabbed"—he then fell—I ran after Harris—a gentleman threw Nash's coat across my shoulders and threw his hat on the ground—I ran after Harris down towards Tabard Street—he was taken into custody as he was walking up from Falstaff Yard, and I and the constable and Harris went to the station—I was sober and so was Nash and Arnold.
   Cross-examined.  When Harris fell back against the shop Nash was near him; Arnold was at a distance from him—I cannot say whether Nash struck Harris; I saw he was bleeding when he came up from Falstaff Yard—I never saw Arnold have hold of Harris—Arnold fell back, and then Harris ran—I did not hear any one say "Let me get up; they are killing me"—there were a lot of men round Harris; I wild not see what was going on inside the crowd.
