January 15th, 1901, the prisoner handed this document to Mr. Moffatt,
who handed it to me-(This was signed by the prisoner, stating that the
condition of the accounts was solely due to him, expressing deep regret for
the defalcations, that he had no defence to offer, but desired that they might be
debited to him, stating that it was only done to facilitate business, and
admitting a deficiency of £10,000.)-I made my report about January 31st
--I found that the firm was insolvent, and could not continue to trade, and
that the creditors must shortly be called together, and Mr. Moffatt sold the

Cross-examined. I have not examined the books prior to 1899-the order
begun with May 1st, 1899-the firm appeared to be solvent on paper in
January, 1901, but they would be forced to pull up, as the manufacturers
would not give them credit.
