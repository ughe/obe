MARY ANN HUDSON. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw the eleven sove-
reigns and 13s. 6d. in the prisoner's box on the Friday afternoon, and
that evening I marked two shillings and two sixpences- I gave the two
shillings to Mr. Farrington, and the two sixpences to Mrs. Pritchard, to
lay out at our shop-on this sixpence produced here is a scratch at the
nose, and a piece cut out at the side, in a line with it I know the mark,
and the date of the sixpence was taken as well-I can speak to this as my
own mark- I never marked any other sixpences in my life.
WILLIAM FARRINGTON. I am a baker, and live in Windsor-place, Old
Kent-road. My wife showed me two marked shillings-I sent the servant
girl with them to the prosecutor's shop for some articles--I took a me-
morandum of the date and reign of the two shillings--I received two
marked sixpences from Mrs. Pritchard's sister-I put down the date and
reign of them--they were marked on the top of the nose and on the edge
--I sent the boy out with both the sixpences-this sixpence is one of them
-it is of the year 1840—it is one that Mrs. Pritchard's sister gave me,
and one I sent by the boy.
JOSEPH BIGGS. I am employed occasionally by Mr. Farrington to go
on errands. On that Saturday morning, about twenty minutes to eight
o'clock, he gave me two sixpences, to go to Mr. Hudson's shop, to fetch
2oz. of tea and 1lb. of sugar-they came to 18.-I paid the two six-
pences for them that I had from Mr. Farrington-I did not notice them.
BENJAMIN — (police-constable M 107.) I searched the prisoner's
box with Mr. Hudson-I found in it eleven sovereigns, fifteen shillings,
four sixpences, and a lot of coppers-I heard Mr. Hudson say to the pri-
soner, "There is no doubt the sovereigns belong to me; if you will give
me the sovereigns, you may take the silver, and go about your busi-
ness"-the prisoner said part of the money belonged to his brother--this is
the box-it has been a lucifer-box, I believe this is the marked sixpence
I had of Mr. Hudson.

MRS. MERRYWEATHER. I am the prisoner's mother. I know he had
money in his possession about last June-I saw it-there were six sove-
reigns and some silver-he was of a remarkably saving turn-I know of his
having a suit of clothes nearly two years he got them at his first place-
they were left by his master for mourning.

he had six sovereigns, besides some silver, which I did not see counted
he had a black coat, black trowsers, and a blue satin waistcoat.
