702. RICHARD WANDSLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of
February, 1 box, value 1s. ; 1 neck-chain, value 5s.; 1 watch, value 11.;
1 cross, value 18. ; 2 rings, value 48. ; 1 brooch, value 108. ; 1 pencil case,
value 1s. ; 1 printed book, value 1s.; and 1 pocket-book, value 1s. ; the
goods of Sophia Priscilla Harris, bis mistress, in her dwelling-house.
Sophia PRISCILLA HARRIS. I live in Royal Opera Arcade, Pall Mall,
and keep a bookseller's and circulating library. I am single, and all the
property in that house is mine-the prisoner was my errand boy-at half-
past nine o'clock on Friday, the 3rd of February, the prisoner came up
stairs and said he wanted some butter--I gave him a shilling, and told him
not to go just yet-in a few minutes I heard my brother's voice saying,
"The door is open-no one is here"- I waited till twelve o'clock the
prisoner did not return, but I bad told him on the 2nd, (in consequence of
i communication I had from a gentleman,) that he should leave on the fol-
lowing day-I missed all these things in the afternoon I wanted to know
the time; my watch was gone, and all the other things, which are worth
51. 168. together-I had seen the watch the night before the cross was
fastened to it—the brooch had been in this box at nine o'clock on the morn-
ing of the 2nd, with the other things-they were all locked up--the box
was broken open-I had not seen that from nine o'clock the morning before
-the watch was not in the box-a gentleman had come in at three o'clock
the day before and told me something, and the prisoner asked to go up
stairs and dust the room-I said it did not require it; but he ran up-
these are my property.
JAMES MORRIS. I am constable of Plumstead. On the afternoon of the
3rd of February, I saw the prisoner coming over Shooter's-hill, with a
hat-box on his shoulder-I said, “What have you got there?"-he said,
"An old jacket and shirt "-I opened the box and saw it was so I asked,
"Where are you going?"-he said, "To Gravesend to get a ship"-I said,
"What have you got to keep you there!"-he said, "I have got 60."-I
put my hand into his pocket and found this book- I was looking at it and
he ran off down the hill-there were some gravel diggers at work he fell
down and plunged his hand into some mud-I found this snuff-box and
pencil-case in the mud, and the watch and other things on him.

703. JOHN CARROLL was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of
February, 6 hats, value 51. 8s. : 6 caps, value 6s; and 1 box, value 38.;
the goods of Joseph Stainburn and another.
JOSEPH STAINBURN. I live in Southwark-square, in the Borough, and
am a hat maker. I am in partnership with William Bolland--these hats
are mine.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Are you swearing to these or to
the paper in which they are ? A. I swear to the marks on the paper-I
had seen them the evening before they were lost-I did not give them out
they were going to a customer at Cambridge when I sell hats I always send
them out in that way I can swear to these hats being in our warehouse
the day before, by the name of “Palmer" on them, the person we sold
them to.
