Prisoner's Defence. I am a cabinet maker by trade, and when going
home I had a drop more that I ought to have had. I met this other man,
who asked me to lend him 4d. to get him a lodging: I refused, and to get
out of his way went into the Calthorpe Arms. There I tendered a bad
shilling, which was returned, and I put it into my pocket. On coming out
this man again bothered me for 4d., and eventually I went into the Union
Tavern, where I treated him. I was then taken by the policeman into
sustody, and the same shilling was found in my possession.
Guilty**-Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
16. THOMAS SMITH (36), and CAROLINE ADAMS (26), were indicted
for a like offence.
MR. RADCLIFFE COOKE conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD LANCASTER. I keep the King's Head public-house in James
Street, Covent Garden - about 9.30 on Saturday 23rd, October,
Smith tendered to my barman a bad 18. in payment for some stout--I said
to the prisoner " Have you got any more like this ? "- he said “What do
you mean, is it bad !"-I said " You are old enough to know whether it is
good or bad"-he took it back and gave a good 18,- I followed him, and when
he got to Russell street he joined two females, Adams, and one not in
custody-the woman not in custody went into the Beaumont at the bottom
of Wellington Street, while the other two waited outside-afterwards they
all three walked along the Strand as far as the Adelphi Theatre, and all at
once I missed Adams-I saw the other two waiting at the top of Savoy
Street-Adams came out of the Savoy Palace public-house, joined the other
two, and all walked along the Strand-Adams went into Mr. Joyce's at the
Adelphi Stores--I followed her in--she asked for some gin, and paid a bad
shilling to the barmaid for it-I examined the shilling, and gave it back to
the barmaid--the prisoner left and joined the other two-I went to a con-
stable and told him what I had seen, and the two prisoners were taken in
charge-the other female got away-Smith wanted to know what he was
charged for, and I said "I suppose you have not been trying to pass bad
money" he said "NO" I said "I suppose you have not been in James
Street to-night, Covent Garden "-he said "I don't know James Street"-
I went to the police-station.
ALFRED FRUITERIL. I am barman at the King's Head-Smith tendered
me a bad shilling on the evening in question-my master followed him
afterwards.
CAROLINE WHITE. I serve at the Adelphi Stores-I remember Adams
giving me a bad shilling, which I handed to Mr. Joyce, who gave it to the
policeman.
WALTER JOYCE. I keep the Adelphi Stores-Mr. Lancaster made 8
sign to me when in my bar--I saw Adams, and marked the shilling W which
she passed in payment for some gin-I gave it to the constable-this (pro-
duced) is the shilling.
JOSEPH PRICE (Policeman E 284). With the assistance of another con-
stable I took the two prisoners into custody--Mr. Joyce gave me the bad
shilling which I produce-I searched the male prisoner and found upon him
three sixpences, two shillings and 14d. in bronze-Adams handed a purse
to the other constable, which he gave to me.
