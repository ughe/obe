Q. Supposing Bray had not opposed any of your omnibuses in the way
you have stated, should you have put on an extra omnibus? A. No: if
he bad not run against our omnibuses, we should not have put the extra
one on against his.
Court. Q. Do you mean if he had not put it on at all, or run in the
manner he did ? A. If he had not run in the manner he did against us-
if he had run against other proprietors, it was other people's business.
MR. JONES. Q. Before you put on that extra omnibus, had you or
your father, to your knowledge, consulted with any other proprietors about
putting it on ? A. No, we had not-in fact, my father never gave me
orders to put it on, I put it on on my own account.
Court. Q. Do you mean this “ Albion, 177?" A. Yes; I took it
off the New-road to do it --my father has three omnibuses on the New-
road, and I took that off, to transfer to the stones the New-road omni-
bus had been paying its way, and this was a losing concern, but I took it
off to protect the others- I took that one because there was no other omni-
bus in the yard fit to go to work.
Mr. Jones. Q. Did you put on another in the New-road? A. Yes,
most certainly.

Court. 0. When did you start this? A. On the Monday-Bray had
been three journeys against our regular omnibuses that day-I then sent
this extra one out-he went down with our buses the fourth journey both
of his continued for about three days to run at our regular times--then one
of Bray's left us--the other still continued at the regular hours—his buses
sometimes stopped where ours did I gave the men orders, if he did not
go with ours, not to go with him; but he continued in our time for about a
fortnight-some of the men told me that he started from one starting-point in
the road, and would go a little way, turn down a street, and come out in
front of another one ; wait and let one go by, and then come out in front
of another; and they asked me what they were to do--they told me Bray's
men dared them to come with them I said, "Well, if they dare you to
go with them, go with them wherever they go."
ROBERT WILLIS. I am a driver in Mr. Trevett's employ. I drove the
"Old Albion," No. 174-the first time it went was at twenty-one minutes
past nine o'clock; the next was three minutes past twelve o'clock; and the
next, twelve minutes before three o'clock-I remember Whit-Monday-I
went off that morning, at my usual time, from the Wheatsheaf, at Pad-
dington-there was another omnibus of ours that went down the yard
with me, or nearly so-Mr. Trevett's omnibus, the "Triumph," was there
-When I got to the Wheatsheaf, I saw, in two or three minutes, two
omnibuses, with the name of "Aaron Bray" on the side I had about
nine minutes to stop-they came up, and said, “Now for the old cobbler ;
we will give him a turn"--this was at the Wheatsheaf door-when I
started at my time, one of Bray's omnibuses started with me; and where I
stopped, they stopped-I first stopped at the corner of the New-road, at
the Crown-Bray was before me at that time-our omnibus always stops
there-Bray stopped there till I started, and then he started too-I went
next to the New Inn, and stopped there; and Bray stopped too he was
