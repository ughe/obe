Q. Would the effect of that eddy, on a small boat, coming from the
southward to the northward, if it got into it, be to carry it out again into
the stream, instead of enabling it to go to Wapping Dock-stairs? 4. Ob
yes, and it would affect a ship as well as a small boat I was in it no later
than this passage up-if it had not been for the eddy I think the skiff would
have made the stairs easily enough-I saw the skiff when she turned round
in the eddy, towards the steam-boat that was the effect of the eddy on
her-that eddy would not be perceivable to a vessel coming straight up the
river, not till she got past us—the schooner's stern would shut out the ap-
pearance of the eddy, all the way above it.
Q. Then, in your judgment, from the effect the eddy had on the skift,
could the accident have been avoided by the commander of the steamer ?
A. I think the accident was all occasioned by the eddy if the boat had
not turned round towards the steamer, I think she would have cleared it
the eddy was the occasion of turning it round-I do not think the master
of the steamer could have avoided the accident-he could not have per-
ceived the eddy until he came past us- I did not observe the helmsman
starboard the helm when the small boat was seen-I could not see any
negligence or carelessness on the part of the captain-I am in no way in-
terested in steam-boats--they are as much injury to me as they are to any
person--if the small boat was coming in the direction that has been stated,
the effect of the eddy would be to catch her head, and cant her round, and
saw that.

MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You say you think the master of the steamer
could not have avoided it? 4. Not from the time of my seeing it--they
were that close together when I saw them, that he could not have avoided
it-I should think they were not more than the length of the boat apart-
I think there was not then sufficient time to back the steamerif she had
been coming at the rate of but five or six miles an hour, I do not think it
could have been avoided, they were too close together when I saw them
- they were almost in the act of striking--there is, perhaps, about 200
Tathoms' breadth between the Tunnel-pier and the tier of vessels on the
other side of the river, but the steamer was coming to the pier where she
was going to land her passengers.
JURY. Q. Would the eddy have any effect on vessels seventy or
eighty feet from yours ! A. Yes-straight upwards, but not outside
the tide is very strong at spring-tides.
