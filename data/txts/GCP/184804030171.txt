WILLIAM Acton. I am superintendent of the London, Brighton, and
South Coast Railway Company's police. Charles Pascoe Grenfell is the
chairman--the prisoner was a plate-layer in their employ, at the West Station,
Croydon-- I took him, and said it was on a charge of stealing the Company's
property; that some iron pipe had been found at Stevens' house, which he
said he had bought of him the evening before-he said he was very sorry :
he did not think he was doing any harm; he picked it up on the line--it is
worth 1s. 6d.—it was worth 2s.6d. or 3s. before it was broken up the
Company do not sell their pipe, or break it up the prisoner had no right to
break it up, or to sell things found on the line.
Cross-examined. Q. Did not be say he found it in the lane? 4. No; on
the line-I found about 18cwt. of stolen iron at Stevens's.
CHARLES BONNER. I am deputy superintendent of the locomotive depart-
ment. This piece of iron pipe belongs to the Company-I believe it was
recently broken when I first saw it-we do not break up iron or sell it.
Cross-examined. Q. What is done with pipe accidentally broken! 4. It is
put on one side, and I suppose would be sold, but we never had a sale before
yesterday.
