 2712. JOHN SHAYLER was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of October , 1 sixpence, 12 pence, and 32 halfpence; the moneys of John Allen Butler , his master. JOHN ALLEN BUTLER. I am a baker, living in the Hackney-road—the prisoner was in my service for fourteen months. In consequence of circumstances I marked 5 s . worth of coppers, and put them into the till—about seven o'clock in the morning on the 23rd of October I came down—the till was locked as I had left it—I opened it, and missed 2 s . 4 d . in half-pence and a silver sixpence—I sent for the policeman—he desired the pri soner to turn out his pockets, which he did, and there was one silver six-pence and 3 1/2 d . in copper, all marked.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You marked some shillings that you found all safe? A.  Yes—the prisoner has four children—I do not wish to press this against him. GUILTY. Aged 45.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Three Months.
 2713. EMMA WATTS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of Oc tober, 1 frock, value 5 s  .; 1 pair of boots, value 3 s  .; and 1 petticoat, value 2 s  .; the goods of Elizabeth Cox . ELIZABETH COX. I live in Charles-street, Drury-lane—I have known the prisoner about a fortnight. On Thursday night, the 21st of October, I was with her in her room, and another young woman who slept with me—I took my clothes off, and put my frock and petticoat on the top of the bed—in the morning I missed them and my boots—these are my boots.
   Prisoner.  The person who lived with us sent me to pawn the frock and petticoat. Witness.  I did not know the other girl. WILLIAM CRUSH. I am a pawnbroker, in Museum-street. I took the boots in pledge of the prisoner, to the best of my belief—my duplicate corresponds with this one. JOHN TALBOTT. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner, and received these duplicates from her—one of them is for the boots. NOT GUILTY.
