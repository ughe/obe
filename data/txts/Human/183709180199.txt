I was at Deptford on the 18th of September, in the Broadway, and went to the Saxe Coburg public-house with Sedger—Thorn was on a bench in front of the house—we called him aside, and asked if he had received any hay from two men belonging to Barclay and Perkins—(Green is in their employ)—I went the same evening to New Cross, and saw Green coming with a dray—I asked if he belonged to that dray—he said he did—I said he must go along with me—he said, "What is to become of my horses and dray?"—I said, "I have seen some of your firm, and have brought a man to take care of the dray"—he said, "You will allow me to speak to him"—I said, "certainly," and he did so—he then asked me on what charge I took him into custody—I said on suspicion of disposing of a truss of hay belonging to his employers, and that he had better not say anything to me about it, as he would come before a Magistrate—he said he had nothing to say, but what he had stated at our station that morning. JOHN FREEMAN. I am horse keeper to Barclay and Perkins. It is usual for men going any distance to take out hay with them—I did not see whether Green took any out—I recollect when the dray went away, turning round and seeing some on the dray—it is usual to take hay when going as far as Welling, and the quantity I saw.  2163. JAMES COLLINS , MARY COLLINS, and PETER KIRTLAND were indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of August , 1 sovereign, and 6 half-crowns, the monies of Julia Sophia Beattie . JULIA SOPHIA BEATTLE. I now reside in Waterloo-road. On the 22nd of August I lived with Mrs. Glenister, at Lambeth—Mrs. Collins fetched me out of Mrs. Glenister's room into hers, which was the next door—I took out my money to send for something to drink, and gave Kirtland half-a-crown to get some gin and beer—I had one sovereign and six half-crowns in my purse then Mrs. Collins once or twice felt my pocket, but she did not take the money out herself I heard Eliza Glenister say she would not have it, but I heard no further—James Collins and Kirtland took me into the adjoining room, where Kirtland's wife laid asleep; and Kirtland held me by the arm, while Mr. Collins put his hand into my pocket, and took out 1 l . 15s—Mrs. Collins was not in the room at the time, she was in the other room, holding Mrs. Glenister down there to prevent her coming to help me. JAMES COLLINS. Q.  Is there not a party wall between the two rooms? I think there is—Kirtland's wife laid on the floor—I saw nobody else—I did not send for a second quantity of gin. JAMES COLLINS. She came into the prison to me, and asked me what I meant to do about it, and brought me half a pound of meat and a loaf.
