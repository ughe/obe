 2186.  JAMES THOMAS was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of August , 1 waistcoat, value 4s.; 1 tablecloth, value 2s.; 1 pair of scissors, value 2s.; 1 jacket, value 1s. 6d.; 1 pair of trousers, value 1s., and 1 handkerchief, value 6d., the goods of Charles Goodman ; and that he he had been before convicted of felony.
 CHARLES GOODMAN. I live in  Margaret-court, Oxford-market, and am a tailor. I lost these things on the 23rd of August - they were worth about 12s.; they had been safe in my house the night before, and I missed them between six and seven o'clock in the morning from the back room, at the top of the house, where I work, and where my children sleep.
   GEORGE JOHN RESTIEAUX(Police-constable E 49). I took the the prisoner into custody on the 23rd of August, between six and seven o'clock in the morning; he had in his basket the articles stated in the indictment; and he had, concealed in his sleeve, three keys and a purse, with 12s. 6d.; one of the keys opens the door of the house in which the prosecutor lives - it is a latch-key; I asked how he got the things, but he would not say any thing, but pretended to cry - he was particularly reserved.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN VENNEL(Police-constable S 140). I have a certificate of the conviction of the prisoner, which I got from Mr. Clark's office; I was present when the prisoner was tried on the 16th of February - he is the person, and was three months in Newgate.
     GUILTY. Aged 37. - Transported for Life.
 2187.  SELINA TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , 1 half-crown and 1 shilling, the monies of William Tocknell , from his person.
 WILLIAM TOCKNELL. I live in Blenheim-street, Oxford-street, and am an  engraver. On the 1st of September I was near a dead wall in Old-street-road; the prisoner was there talking to another person - I passed her; she came and asked me to go home with her - she put her hand under my arm and put her hand into my trousers pocket; she took out a half-crown and a shilling; I asked her to give me what I had lost- she asked me several times what it was, and kept walking with me till we came to a public-house; she went in there, and asked what I would take - I asked for my money; she got between two men and got out- the men prevented my following her, but I got out at another door; I gave an alarm, and the officer took her - I am certain she is the person.
 Q. Have you not before said that you saw a female, and you really believed she was the person who robbed you? A. I have said so, but I am certain she is the person - she was taken and one half-crown, a shilling, a sixpence, and some halfpence were found on her; I had felt it safe ten minutes before I lost it - in my way home a young man followed me from the station as far as Finsbury-square, and he offered to lay down his life if I would take the money of him, and offered me double and treble the amount - he spoke so feelingly, that I took 3s. 6d. from him, and he wanted me to take more - I went to the Police-office after that, but I thought it would be all settled; I did not mean to go any more, but I was sent for on Wednesday.
 Cross-examined. Q. When did you meet the prisoner? A. On Saturday, about eleven o'clock; I did not say I would not be sure she was the woman - I expressed some doubt of her identity; I went by her dress as much as any thing at the time, but I know quite well she is the person - she had another dress on, but I can be positive as to her features,
 Q. Did not the gentleman who came up to you say it was not the prisoner who took the money? A. No, he said he was not present at the transaction; I took the 3s. 6d. of him, and I might have had a larger sum.
 COURT. Q. Was your doubt respecting her dress? A. Yes, I had no doubt about her person - I saw her under the wall, and again at the public-house.
   GEORGE KEMP(Police-constable N 82.) I was applied to on the night of the 1st of September to take the prisoner - I had before heard a cry of Stop thief! and ran, and the prisoner ran by me; I knew her well- the prosecutor was following two or three yards behind; he said that was the person, and I pursued and took her to the station, after great resistance - I searched her, and took her pocket from her; I found a half-crown piece, a shilling, a sixpence, and some copper in it.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2188.  JOHN TYLER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , 4lbs. weight of beef, value 2s., the goods of John Harradence.
 JOHN HARRADENCE. I  keep a butcher's-shop in Goswell-road. On the 1st of September the prisoner purchased a piece of beef, and paid 1s. 8 1/2d. for it -I had been watching him - he brought in one piece to be weighed, but had taken two off; I saw him put the other piece in his jacket - when he had walked out I followed, and took him into custody; I found the other piece of beef on him, tied in a handkerchief - it weighed 3 lbs. 2 ozs. - I knew it from its appearance.
 Prisoner. When I went into the shop I asked him what it was a pound - he said 7 1/2d.; I said, "If I take two bits will you let me have them for 7d.?" he said No - I took one piece, and put it into the scale; he then said, "You shall have the two pieces for 7d.;" he said they came to 3s. 9d. - I gave him two half-crowns, and he gave me 1s. 3d. out. Witness. It is not true; I never sold him the two pieces - he brought it under his coat, and did not pay for it.
   WILLIAM CLIFTON(Police-constable N 143.) I stopped the prisoner, and found on him two pieces of beef - they were produced before the Magistrate, and sworn to; the prisoner said he had bought them both.
