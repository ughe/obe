THOMAS CAPEL. I live at No. 4, Benton Street, Oxford Street-in
November last I worked for Mr. Glew, off the premises-after the strike I
went there for materials as usual-on the 30th November, as I was going in
to ask for work, I saw Emmett, sen., and Cook-a man spoke to me, and
asked me if I was going in-I afterwards saw Cook in communication with that
man-I did not go into the factory then, but went away--I should have
gone in if the persons had not spoken to me--four or five days previously
Emmett, sen., spoke to me as I was standing by the gateway where the
workpeople enter-he said, “Are you going in after work?" I said, "Yes"
-ho then asked me if I was aware that the shop was on strike, and I said
"No"-he said, " The shop is on strike about a reduction of wages"- he asked
me if I would go up to the meeting-upon that I went away for about an
hour and a half, and then watched my opportunity to go in and ask for work
-I did not care about going in in the presence of the defendant.
