   MR. HAWKINS . Q.  During the time you were in negotiation with Mr. Johnson you received the anonymous letter? A.  Yes; three clergymen were named on it as persons to whom I might refer—I wrote to one, and Mr. Taylor to the other two, and I showed him the answer I received; that was all that took place between Mr. Taylor and I as to the anonymous letter; nothing in the shape of a trap was suggested—these are the envelopes in which the two letters came. WILLIAM EMERY SLACK. I am a clerk in the employment of Mr. John son—I have known the defendant for several years—he has been in the habit of coming to Mr. Johnson's office—I am acquainted with his hand writing—I have seen him write—I believe this letter and envelope of 15th October to be his handwriting.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How often have you seen him write? A.  Once I can say for a certainty, and I think twice—I can swear to once.
   MR. POLAND . Q.  At your office have you seen letters from Mr. Birch? A.  Yes; continually—I have had business transactions with him spreading over more than three or four years; besides having seen him write, I have become acquainted with his handwriting.
