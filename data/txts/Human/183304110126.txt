 884.  SARAH GROVES was indicted for wilful and corrupt perjury on the trial of William Pedley and Joseph Smith.
 MESSRS. PHILLIPS and CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY BUCKLER, short-hand writer of the Court, read his notes of the evidence given by the defendant, on the trial of Pedley and Smith, for which see First Session, December, 1832, page 29. The defendant had deposed, among other things, that on the 23rd of June, about five o'clock in the afternoon, she saw a cart backed into the premises of the said William Smith, into his yard, and that the gates were shut, and enclosed the cart in the said yard; "and about an hour and a half after she saw the said William Smith come out of his yard in another cart with two packing-cases."
   RICHARD JOHNSON. I am a labouring man, and live in Castle-ride, Castle-place, near Castle-alley. On the 23rd of June last, I was in Joseph Smith's premises, who then lived in Castle-alley; there is no number on the door - I was at work on his premises all that day; he had a taxed cart there and harness - I was cleaning the harness that afternoon, inside the gates, in his warehouse or shed; Smith was not at home that afternoon, but there was a cartand a van belonging to him, both at home that afternoon - they never were absent from the premises; there is no entrance for a cart or horse but through the gates, and they were not open all that afternoon - I was on the premises all the afternoon, but at dinner time; if any goods had come and a cart had been backed into these premises, I must have heard it; there was no cart came near the premises in my hearing - there was no room for any cart to have come into these premises for the cart and van which were there; I know Allum - he lived in Castle-place; I cannot tell the number - I know the house; it is up in the corner, in a narrow part.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q.Where do you live now? A. At No. 10, New Market, Finsbury. In October last, I lodged in Castle-ride, Whitechapel; I made an affidavit for Mr. Smith to get him bailed, by desire of Mr. Harmer's clerk - Mr. Smithers went down with me; this was in October last - the trial did not come on here till November; I was here, but was not called as a witness - I came by my own wish, to see my friend done justice by.
 Q. Had you stated in your affidavit that you were there all day, that Smith was not at home, and that it was impossible he should have received the goods? A. Yes, I stated it at Mr. Harmer's; I know the premises very well, and no cart came near the premises that afternoon.
 Q. Were you ever taken into custody on these pre- premises? A. Yes, I was; I cannot tell the day - it might be on the 25th of June; I was charged with letting a cart out - the man who hired it came as I was going to bed, and wanted a cart to bring a box to the Bull-inn, Aldgate; I do not know whether there was a cart let out, for driving of which, I was charged with stealing goods.
 COURT. Q. You made this affidavit - was it for the purpose of Smith being let out on hail? A. Oh no, quite different - it was not to procure bail for him, it was to tell the whole truth.
 Q. Was it not that Smith might, by your telling the truth, he let out on bail? A. No, to tell the real truth - I know no other reason.
 Q. Now I will give you five minutes before you go into the dock; look at that paper - upon the oath you have taken, was not that affidavit made by you for the purpose of getting Smith let out on bail? - answer that question. A.What question, Sir? I mean to speak the truth.
 Q. Do not trifle, or I will commit you to Newgate - Did you make that affidavit in order that he might get out on bail? A. Yes, Sir.
 Q. Now I will ask you one more question - When you were stating the facts in favour of Mr. Smith, in order that he might go out on bail, did you mention a word that he was not at home on the 23rd of June? A. I cannot exactly say.
 Q. Read it - Now, when you were sworn for the purpose of Smith being let out on bail, did you mention one word about his not being at home on the 23rd of June? A. I said as that represents, and no more.
   ELIZABETH WARNER. My husband is a brewer's servant; we lodge at No. 2, Castle-place - I can see Smith's premises from my apartment. On the 23rd of June I was looking out of my apartment; I saw a large towncart turn up Castle-alley, and it backed down Castle-alley again, to the bottom of Castle-place; it was then very near to my window - it was about a quarter-past five o'clock; there were two large packing-cases in it, two large bags, and a sack - the cases had large letters on them; the first thing that was taken out of the cart was a canvas bag or sack - it was carried to James Allum's, No. 4, Castle-place; Allum does not live there now - I have not seen him since Smith was in custody; I had seen him about a week before - the cart did not back at all to Smith's premises; Mrs. Gray is my landlady - she was at her window.
