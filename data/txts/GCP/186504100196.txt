GEORGE CLAYTON. I am station master at Charlton— I was on duty there
when this accident happened the ballast train was detained some time, and
after it had left a passenger train from Maidstone came into the station-it
ought not to bave stopped there, but it did so because the signals were at
danger-it arrived at 4.20, and was detained about twelve minutes-the
passengers were very impatient, and I telegraphed to Blackheath, to the
station-master, by words, "Train waiting"- that is a customary thing when
a train is kept a long while-I got no answer, no further than in the signal-
box the "All clear" signal was given at 4.32, which was almost immediately
before I could leave the office--that would be from Tucker's place the train
then left--that would be about 4.334.
Cross-examined. Q. My friend asked you whether you telegraphed to the
station-master at Blackheath ; are you aware that the telegraph station there
is in charge of a boy! 4. Yes-important messages like that are entrusted
to his care-the ballast train had not been signaled to me through the
speaking instrument, nor had I telegraphed it on by message to Blackheath;
it was not my place to do so—the passengers in the express were impatient,
and I gave a reminder-a ballast train leaving Charlton arrives at Black-
heath sometimes in seven, eight, or ten minutes, but eight minutes had
elapsed on that occusion-I telegraphed, "Train waiting"-we make it as
short and explicit as we can-I could have telegraphed, “ Has ballast train
passed ?" ---that would have given some notice to the signalman of a ballast
train--I called his attention very distinctly to the second three beats I did
not give him notice of that special ballast train, which I might have done
when I said, "Train waiting the signal was against me I have sent many
such messages before and since, but I do not know what man I sent them to
