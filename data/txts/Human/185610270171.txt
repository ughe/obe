CHARLES MAYO TALMADGE. I reside at Greenwich, and am agent to the Globe Insurance Company. On the night of 20th Sept., in consequence of a communication made to me, I went to examine these premises—Mr. Moore showed me the cupboard—I took the candle, looked into it, and well examined the wall—I found that the bricks had evidently been removed very recently—we then went on to the stairs, and through the hole he had made into the loft—I took from there a portion of the remaining fragments of rags—a good portion of the paper and rags had been burnt, about one third of it—I produce what I found—there is a piece of striped shirting amongst it—it is a very common pattern at Greenwich—I afterwards went into the passage that divides the two houses—I there found some bricks lying down in the passage—any person putting their hand through the hole in the cupboard could throw the bricks below, or they might have fallen from the blow which thrust them out.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner is not insured? A.  No—I do not know whether Hodges is. SARAH WILSON. I am married, and live at No. 6, Bennet-street, Wool-wich. I know the prisoner—I saw her on the Saturday, the day of the fire, several times—between 3 and 4 o'clock I picked up a pewter pot in front of her house, and took it over to a beer shop opposite Mr. Moore's—she said that the b——old b——had been after the rent, and she would see her in flames before she would pay it, for anybody that paid rent was fools; for six months and a fortnight she could keep her house, and then she would set fire to the b——building, and if the old b——came by, she would take and throw her b——carcase into the flames—she was perfectly comfortable when she said this—I mean she was not intoxicated—I do not think she had been drinking—about a month or six weeks before that she said the old b—had been after the rent, and she would not pay any, she would see her at—first—she had better put the money on the clothes—her husband had taken a house, and she had refused the key, she was not going to be such a b——fool as to take another house until she had done her work—we had not been drinking together on the day in question—we were on friendly terms, so far as neighbours, only good morning and good night, never holding any conversation, only when she thrust her conversation in like that.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were not always on very friendly terms? A.  I do not know of anything to the contrary—I never had an angry word with her—I know Mr. Mayberry by sight—I never had a quarrel with the prisoner ( Mayberry was here called in )—I never said that I would do any thing I could to spite and injure the prisoner—I never said that to Mayberry at the police court—Miss Moore was in and out from the yard to the bar while the prisoner was saying this to me—she did not hear what passed. ISABELLA COLLLNGTON. The house in question belongs to my son, Henry Alfred Collington. The prisoner has paid two weeks' rent, and she owes twenty-four next Monday—I have applied to her for rent two or three times every week—I had not applied to her on 2nd Sept.—I had the Saturday before. NOT GUILTY.
