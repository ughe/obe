CHARLES GILBERT. I live with my father, at 92, Royal Mint-street Tower-
hill-he keeps a cart for the purpose of being hired_I know the prisoner
Smith by sight, by doing a little job now and then--I have seen him
several times when he has come for the cart-I recollect his coming to
my father for a cart-- I cannot say bow long it was before this robbery, or
how long it was before I was examined before the Magistrate I cannot tell
how many weeks, days, or months-it was a week or two, or a month before
I was examined-Smith was not in the cart : he walked alongside of it-I
went in the cart to Crutched-friars-I know Seething-ane; it is in Crutched-
friars, through the railway arch, on the left hand side I saw a bale put into
the cart; I cannot say by whom-it was lowered down by a crane from
some warehouse ; from a high place, a loophole-Smith was out of my sight
then-he bad not led the horses up to this place I had ; I drove the cart,
and he walked by the side he told me to follow him and I did so, and he
walked up to this place I stopped because he told me to-I saw him after
the bale was lowered; as soon as I had got out from the yard. I had drawn
into a yard and turned round --when Smith came down he told me to go ou
home-I knew where "home" was I knew he meant to go on to his house
I knew where be lived I went to his house the walked when we got
to his house we unloaded the cart and left the bale at Smith's shop-about
five or six days afterwards, we removed some sacks from his house, and took
them to Limehouse-there was sometbing in them ; I cannot say what the
bale that was lowered into the cart was in a sack.
Smith. The father and the boy came down with me in the morning.
Witness. My father was with me one morning, but not this morning that I
have been speaking of there was one man with you, not all the while.
COURT. Q. What part of the time was he with him A. When I

one asks, you may tell them this is Smith's cart" - I said, "All right."
