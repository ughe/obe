MICHAEL HAMILTON. I was an apprentice on board the Royal Tar. I
went with the eldest apprentice to fetch the men from the Dutch boat-I was
not one of the first party-I do not know what time it was when they left
the smack it was after dinner-they came back with me, and the eldest
apprentice-they were all tipsy-they brought three bottles of liquor on
board with them-they had had some gin before they went on board the
Dutchman-they did not drink anything after they returned. they went
down into the cabin-I was in the same cabin-I was there when Toombs
was burnt-I was neither awake nor asleep I was half asleep and halfawake
-I was roused up by a light, and then 8aw that Toombs was on fire-iDun
ning was the nearest person to him at that time-Charles Turner was in the
cabin, and a youth named George Carlisle was asleep in the bed cabin-he
did not wake up during the whole of it-William Whittick was also
asleep in the stern-the only persons there awake were myself, Dunning
Toombs, and Turner-Toombe laid still on the settle after he was on fire-
went out and called the eldest apprentice-he came down, but he was no
sooner down than he woke the man up, and was out again-Toombs then
came on deck, ran along the lee seupper, and fell down against the fore
hawse-the eldest apprentice took two buckets of water and chucked over
him, and the fire was put out-Dunning was sitting on the hatches when I
saw Toombs on fire-he remained in the cabin-I did not see any light there
in anybody's hands-by 44 the hatches,' I mean the cabin floor-there was
light in the sconce in the fore part of the cabin-that was an oil lamp, it
had no glass round it-it was in a socket not hung up-it was not above
two or three feet from Toombs-I could stand under the lamp-my head
used to touch it when I did-a bottle of naphtha was kept in a corner there,
and was used for the signal lights-there had been no quarrelling at all that
evening.
