GIBBS, Mayor.
MR. HUDDLESTON conducted the Prosecution.
William Shaw. I am the son of Thomas Shaw, he is a linen-draper
--the prisoner was his housemaid. On Saturday night, the 18th of Oct.,
I, with a policeman, searched the prisoner's box-she was not present, but
the policeman was, and Mr. Rogers-1 believe it was the prisoner's box
-- it was in a room adjoining her room, which is merely used to hold boxes
and a chest of drawers- I found in the bottom of the box this book,
which is mine-I also found three lengths of new calico, containing about
ten yards-it was my father's, and had our private mark on it-I had
missed my book about a month.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. I suppose the mark was
on all the calico you sold ? 4. Yes we have not had any young men
left lately, the last who left was Mr. Thomas, about six months ago
he might have sold this calico--this is my book-the last time I saw it
it was in the bookcase.
MR. HUDDLESTON, Q. Has any other person left since the calico was
in the stock? A. No--this was in the stock when Thomas left.
WILLIAM Geist (police-constable M 223). I was called in to Mr.
Shaw's on the 18th of Oct.-after I took the prisoner to the station, I
searched a box-I had asked her in the station for her keys-she said
she had thrown them into a drawer-I found a chest of drawers in the
lobby, where the boxes were-I found four keys in one drawer-1 tried
one to a box, and it opened it-I found the book and the calico--the
prisoner was in the station on the other charge-- (see page 1033, No. 2177)
the bookcase from which this book was taken was merely open shelves,
WILLIAM SHAW re-examined. Q. What was the cost of this book ?
4. I cannot remember— I had not left it about that anybody might have
taken and read it-I had left it on the shelves.
WILLIAM ROGERS. I saw the box searched-it was the prisoner's-I
know it from the position in which it was placed, and I had seen her
locking and unlocking it.
Cross-examined. Q. How could you see the maid at her box? A. Our
warehouse was opposite the room door, and I had to pass it several times
a day—I do not know of her having bought any calico-the servants have
bought things, I believe.
Court to William Shaw, Q. Where do you keep the calico ? A. The
lengths that are cut are below-the maids have to pass through there to
fetch coals-these are each a fag end, and have our marks on--the prisoner
had been a year and three-quarters or two years in my father's service, and
was going away in consequence of our missing some spirits.
GUILTY. Aged 25.-- Recommended to mercy by the Jury.-Confined
Three Months.
