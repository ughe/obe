   OLD COURT .— Tuesday, May  5 th , 1891.

 391. WILLIAM HOPKINS  (58) , Feloniously wounding William Ward , with intent to murder. Second Count,  with intent to do grievous bodily harm.
   MR. LAWLESS Prosecuted. WILLIAM WARD. I am a carver and gilder, of 12, Bradstock Road, South Hackney—the prisoner is my uncle, and lived in the same house—about one in the early morning of 7th April I heard a disturbance in the room occupied by my uncle, George Hopkins, who is an invalid, and was in bed—there are three rooms on the first floor; his room was the middle one—I went up, and in that room I found the prisoner and a woman named Martha Arnold, who had no business there; they were annoying my uncle—I said, "Let him alone; give him a chance;" and I said, "If you don't go out of the room I will put you out"—the prisoner said, "I will give you a chance," and he went out of the room, went upstairs, and got this razor or knife, and came back with it; it is what is called a weaver's knife; it is very sharp; sharp enough to shave with—he struck me about the head and neck with it—he hit me twice in the head and knocked me down, and then in the neck—I don't know what he was saying—my mother came in and got him away from me; a policeman was fetched, and he was taken to the station—he was net drunk or sober; he had had a drop, the same as I had, but I knew what I was doing.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I had been drinking with you that day—I can take drink as well as anybody else—I pushed Martha Arnold out of the room; she fell down—I don't think I kicked her; I am sure I did not—I was in the house to protect my mother against you—you have threatened her life—you hit your brother when he was in bed, and you went away for ten days and took half a sovereign.
