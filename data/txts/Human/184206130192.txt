JOHN HEFFERMAN( police-constable D  99.) I took the prisoner, and asked how he became possessed of these handkerchiefs—he said the yellow one belonged to Thompson, and the other was his own.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I saw the handkerchief down the area; Thompson said it was his, and if I would go and get it, he would give me 6 d . for myself; I got down, and he walked away; I came up, and ran after him. GUILTY. 18.— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Four Months.
 1924. JAMES FORD was indicted for embezzlement. CHARLES SMITH. I am a baker, and live at Chiswick—the prisoner was my servant. On the 16th of May I sent him to Strand-on-the-Green, to Mr. Jeffries and Mr. Heman with bills—he was to receive the money from them—he returned, and worked that week—on the 23rd I sent him out with bread, and to deliver bills again—he returned at half-past six o'clock—next morning I met him, and asked him where he was going, and he said he was going to send a man to do his work—he had not then paid me either of these sums.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did you ask when he returned on the 16th, if he had received the money? A.  He did not receive these sums till the 23rd, when he went out with his bread—he returned, and set his sponge, but did not book his bread—I did not see him again till the Thursday when he was given into custody—a person named Burt offered me the money that same afternoon, and I said I would settle it before the Magistrate, and he would not allow it—I was on friendly terms with the prisoner—I gave him notice on Saturday, the 21st, to quit me on the Saturday following.
   COURT . Q.  This offer of the money by Burt was after the prisoner was in custody? A.  Yes. ABRAHAM JEFFRIES. I live with my father, at Strand-on-the-Green, near Kew-bridge. My father deals with the prosecutor for bread—on the 16th of May the prisoner brought a bill which was not paid—on the 23rd he brought another, and I paid him 14 s ., all in silver, for the two bills.
