time, and I took charge of the gates-& number of police came after-
wards—the names and addresses of the different visitors were taken,
and Cunningham was afterwards detained and taken into custody inside
-on the first day he was brought up I went to Bow Street and say
Cunningham there in a room with a number of other people; I am

Cross-examined by MR. RICHARDS. This produced) is the ordinary
guide-book which is sold inside the Tower gates in the ticket office, most
strangers would buy one-I have been on duty in the Tower nine or
10 years—this guide-book says, “On quitting the White Tower the
visitor would proceed across the Green to the Beauchamp Tower"-that
is the way in which visitors would generally go after coming out of
St. John's Chapel and the Banqueting Hall, it is the general route-I
don't know how many people went out between a quarter past I, when I
saw the prisoner go in, and 2 o'clock-I came back from my dinner
about 25 minutes to 2 o'clock, I can't say how many people wentout between
that time and two o'clock, but I know it was a very slack day; that
day slacker than usual, some people went out I didn't look at the clock

strike 2 o'clock-when I heard the explosion I ran and closed the gates
-that was about three minutes after; that was not by order, I used my
own discretion-there was an alarm given that there was an explosion
in the White Tower, and directly I heard the alarm given I ran and
closed the gates, the alarm was given to me by a military man in the
Tower-he ran out and said there was an explosion in the armoury it
the White Tower; I expect he ran from the White Tower down to the
gates, so that there would have been an interval of about two or three
minutes as near as possible—the explosion didn't excite me, it is i
general occurrence to hear the guns towards Woolwich, but we went
