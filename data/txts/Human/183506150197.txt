   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Are you serious in saying you do not know why this man called on you in the King's Bench? A.  Yes, quite serious; after he had called on me, I employed him to seck out an attorney—I do not remember the name of the attorney—I mentioned the name to the policeman, but now I have forgotten it. I was not acquainted with him enough to know his name—I found his name by information that I had given me—it is an odd name—Mackarel is the name—I have seen him—the prisoner found him out for me, and he did what I wanted done—I paid the expenses myself—the prisoner only went to Hoxton for him—I had plenty of money when I left the prison—I had had the property in the trunks several weeks—it was put in at different times—it would be impossible to say when I bought the property—I took the benefit of the Insolvent Act the day before Good Friday—this was not the property of the assignees—nothing was in my other trunk but old invoices—it was a port manteau—there were two or three hundred invoices—I returned this property in my schedule—it is not the property of my assignees—I did not swear that this was not my property—the question was not put to me—I never swore it was not my property—I was going to Huddersfield—I called at a place named Goole—I did not tell the prisoner that I should have only 7 s . 6 d . left, when I left Goole, and desire him to pay my expenses at the tavern—I swore to my shedule, and these trunks were in the room when the person came to take an inventory of what I had—he saw them I read the schedule before I swore to it—whatever is in the schedule is correct—it is a good while ago—I cannot tell whether the trunks were in it or not—they were, to the best of my knowledge—I would not swear it—I swear I never gave the prisoner authority to go and fetch these things—there were no money transactions between us—he had no business to pay Mr. Wallis—he did not know what was in the trunk or portmanteau—he got them both—the invoices were of no value to anybody—I had known the prisoner five years—I do not know how he knew I was in the Bench—he saw my name, very likely, in the newspaper—it was two or three days before I came out, that I employed him to go for Mr. Mackarel—I never received any letter from the prisoner down in Yorkshire—Mr. Wallis told me that the prisoner had paid him 7 s . 8 d .—in two separate sums—he paid 4 s ., and got the trunk, and then he got the portmanteau, and paid the remainder—I know Squires and Tolkington, but I paid them all that was due—I have the receipt in my pocket—I heard the prisoner say that he had written to me, but I never received his letter, certainly.
