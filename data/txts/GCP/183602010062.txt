GEORGE BAKER. I am an undertaker, and live in Salmon-lane, Lime-
house. I was at the King's Arms on the 8th of January, from seven
eight o'clock-I saw the prosecutor in the parlour intoxicated--the prisoner
was there when I went into the parlour--the prosecutor had been exhi-
biting his back, and said something about flogging-I sat down, and had
a glass of half-and-half-the landlord found that the prosecutor was in
that state that he did not like him to be in the parlour-he was called on
to pay his reckoning, and had no money--the landlord threatened that he
would not let the prisoner quit the house till he was searched-heal-
tempted to leave, and refused to be searched-I stopped him as I was al
the bar-two policemen then arrived-he had struck me on the side of the
head with his fist-I cannot say whether it was clenched or not-thens
party coming by called in to have half a pint of porter, and seeing me ille
used, forced the prisoner into the tap-room-he went into a corner, and
seated himself in the further box, where he took out of his pocket, some
pieces of paper, one of which was a £5 note-I saw the word "five" written
on the note, but I could not see the number-he then tried to destroy
paper, and was stopped by Mr. Robey at the fire-place.
