 2548. GEORGE BARKER , CHARLES BROCKWELL, and JOSEPH WILKS were indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Sarah Thornhill , about two o'clock in the night of the   24th of June , at West Ham , with intent to steal, and stealing therein, 24 bottles, value 7 s  .; 4 gallons of brandy, 6 l  . 10 s  .; 4 gallons of gin, 2 l  . 10 s  .; and 18 quarts of wine, 1 l  . 15 s  .; her goods.
   MESSRS. BALLANTINE and HUDDLESTON conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE VOOGHT. I live with my sister, Mrs. Sarah Thornhill, in the parish of West Ham, Essex. There is an orchard at the side of the house, between it and a row of houses, called the Printers'-buildings—the nearest house of that row is about a hundred yards off—they face West Ham-lane—there is a passage down the side, along the back, and up the middle of the houses, with a wall about eight feet high—on Saturday night, the 24th of June, I went to bed about half-past ten o'clock—the house was then safe—I was awoke by the policeman, about twenty minutes or half-past two—I found the back door open, and a hole bored into it, about the size of a quil—the door was not forced open—it was opened from the inside—some tiles were taken off the roof of the back kitchen, and a hole cut through the ceiling, by which a person could get through—the back kitchen communicates with the cellar, and we had lately had some bottles of Marsala, and spirits in stone jars placed in the cellar—when I came down in the morning I went with the policeman into the orchard—it was just getting light—we saw some tracks, and followed them—they led to a corner of the wall opposite the Printers', buildings, and by the side of that wall in the orchard I found about three dozen of wine in bottles, a loin of veal, some gooseberry-pies, and the princi pal contents of the larder—on the wall were some bottles of wine in a basket of ours, which had been in the back kitchen—we lost the tracks at that place—I afterwards examined the cellar, and missed from it four gallons of brandy, and four gallons of gin, in four stone bottles, and about a dozen and a half of wine besides the quantity we recovered in the orchard.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  How many houses are there in the Printers'-buildings? A.  Thirty-two, I believe—No. 1 is the nearest house to mine—my sister is not in any business. HANNAH CARR. I am single, and live at No. 3, Printers'-buildings, with Mrs. Craske, my aunt.
