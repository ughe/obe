HENRY COUGHLIN. I am a hammermanon March 8th, about eleven
p.m., I met the prisoner Cain-I did not know him, but he said he knew
me, and worked with my father at the Arsenal, and he had no money, or
he would give me a glass-I said, 4 You can have a drink went
into the Duke of Sussex, and Wood came in, but only Cain and I drank-
Cain said, 44 You are late home, and you had better come and sleep with
me at my mother's "I said, No, I will go home said, 44 Go home
in the morning "-I went with him till we got to a dark place, where
there is a passage leading from the streetWoo was on the path,
walking about-I refused to go into Cain's house-he caught hold of me,
and said, 44 Come on; what are you frightened of "-Isaid, I won't go
in there "Wood came behind me, and seized me with hoth hands by my
throat, put his knee in my back and threw me to the ground; and Wood
knelt on me, put his hand over my mouth, and held me there while Cain
put his hands in my pockets and took my purse, which I had taken out in
the public-house to pay for the drink, and they saw it-it contained a
half-sovereign, a half-crown, and a wateh keyhe put his hand in another
pocket, and took some sixpences and coppers-I had a watch on, but they
did not take that-they went off, leaving me on the ground-I went to
the Police-station and reported Iknew Cain as a boy, and his father
and mother before him-Wood was a stranger to me-I was with them
in the public-house about an hour and a half-I was not altogether sober,
but I was quite sensible-I saw both prisoners at the station, about an
hour afterwards, and identified them-I was at the station, and one
policeman brought in Wood and another Cain-they were charged with
robbing me-neither of them made any reply.
Cross-eaamined by Cain. I did not say before the Magistrate that I only

Cross-examined by Wood. You did not speak to me in the public-house,
but you spoke to your comrade-] saw no doctor, as I had no money to
go to one, but I am suffering still from my throat.
