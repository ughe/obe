WILLIAM CROSS. ( policeman, C  121). On the morning of 26th April I saw the two prisoners just before they came to the County Fire Office in Regent-street—it was about a quarter before 5 o'clock—it would take them about two or three minutes to walk from there to Mr. Blanchard's, the Scotch Stores.
   Cross-examined by MR. POLAND . Q.  Were they together? A.  Yes—it wanted a quarter or 20 minutes to 5 o'clock—they were a few yards distant from Marylebone-street. WILLIAM BLANCHARD. I keep the Scotch Stores, in Beech-street On 25th April I went to bed about half past 1 o'clock—I was the last person up, and all was then safe—I was called at half past 6 o'clock—I found the cords of the fan light so broken that anybody could get in—they had been safe the previous night—the doors were unfastened—I missed some silver mounting; and about 39 s . worth of coppers, in pence, halfpence, and farthings; and about 5 l . worth of silver; and they had passed into the cellar, and taken some wine—this bag belongs to the Union Bank; I had had it on the Saturday previous, with 100 l . worth of silver—it was in my house that night, and was missed in the morning—I had placed some of the coppers in paper, similar to this paper which is wrapped round some of the money—I missed 130 or 140 cigars—I have brought some to correspond with what are here—the copper was not in this bag. EDWABD PLUMRIDGE. I am porter to the Scotch Stores. I came down about a quarter before 7 o'clock in the morning of the 26th—I have heard what my master has said, and I say the same about the fan light.
   Crawford.  I said before the Magistrate that I was in a coffee shop that morning till half past 4 o'clock; he told me to consult my legal adviser; the sergeant went to the coffee shop, and I believe he told the persons not to come. WILLIAM GLASS. re-examined.  I went to the coffee shop—I did not desire them not to come.
