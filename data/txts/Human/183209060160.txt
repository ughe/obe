 NEW COURT. FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 14.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 2120.  JOSEPH POWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 15 yards of linen cloth, value 1l. 5s., the goods of Thomas Baker. - To which he pleaded GUILTY. - Confined Six Months.
 2121.  ANGELO PHILIP was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , 11 bronze vases, value 30l., and 5 pieces of bronze, value 20l., the goods of Anna Maria Gordon.
   ANDREW CASTELLARI. I attend the Egyptian Hall, Piccadilly; I have the management of the business for Anna Maria Gordon. I missed eleven vases, and five pieces of bronze on the 11th of August; they are worth 50l. - I have known the prisoner twelve or fifteen months; he was in the habit of coming, as a friend of mine.
 Prisoner. He gave me these vases to sell. Witness. No, I did not.
 COURT. Q. Did you deliver any pieces of bronze to him to sell? A. No, I did not; I did not employ him to sell any thing.
 Prisoner. My Lord, he said to me,
 "As soon as you have sold these vases, come and tell me, and who you sold them to. Witness. No, I did not say a word about it.
 COURT. Q. Do you mean to say, upon the solemn oath you have taken, you never employed the prisoner to sell any thing, or give him any thing to sell. Witness. I never spoke one word about it.
   WILLIAM TILL. I live in Great Russell-street, Covent-garden; I deal in coins, metals, and anitquities. I bought these articles of the prisoner at different times; I gave about 10l. for them - I observed something particular in their appearance; I went to the Musenm, and saw similar articles - I bought some on the 11th of August, and for three or four days before - there were some tickets on them, and I saw some tickets on those at the Museum; I have kept the tickets of those I bought - they are genuine antiques about 1800 or 1900 years old.
   JAMES KENNERLY(Police-constable C 30). I was on duty in Piccadilly; I took the prisoner on the 13th of August, at the Egyptian Hall, and received these articles from Mr. Till - I found on the prisoner two gold rings, two medals, and some money.
   WILLIAM TILL. I missed these medals and some coins.
 Prisoner. I dined with Castellari after I sold these things.
   ANDREW CASTELLARI. I dined two or three times with him, but he did not say he had told the things.
 Prisoner's Defence. He has not told the truth, and now I leave it to the Court.
 JAMES KENNERLY. When I took the prisoner I took off his hat, and there was a dent in it, in which, probably, some of these things had been, and some hay in it, and some hay was in these things; he said he had been playing with some horses in a stable.
 WILLIAM TILL re-examined. Q. How came you to buy of him? A. I had bought some things of him before, of what they call Terra cotta; I then lost sight of him for some time - I then saw him again, and he sold me these things; they ask 4l. or 5l. a-piece for them at the Museum, which is a ridiculous price.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2122.  THOMAS BROWN was indicted for embezzlement.
 The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 2123.  JOHN BELL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , 4 razors, value 5s., the goods of George Handley.
   GEORGE HANDLEY. I live in Crown-street; I am a hair-dresser. The prisoner was occasionally employed in my shop, and was so about one month before the 4th of September; I left fifteen razors in my shop on the 31st of August, and missed four - the next morning I went to the prisoner's shop, in Holywell-lane - I asked what he came to my shop for; he said to sell a couple of boards, which we generally hang outside our doors, and I asked him about the razors - he said he knew nothing about them, but one which my boy took from him.
   THOMAS FORD. I am shop-boy to the prosecutor. On a Friday evening, the prisoner came to the shop; he took a razor out of the rack - I took it out of his hat; he said it dropped in, but I saw him snatch it.
   JAMES YOUNG(Police-constable G 203). I took the prisoner.
 Prisoner. I was in great distress, and had two boards to sell - the razor fell into my hat.
 GUILTY. Aged 22. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Months.
 2124.  ELIZA ALLEN was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of September , 1 pair of boots, value 2s., the goods of John Davis , from the person of Matilda Davis.
   MARY DAVIS. I am the wife of John Davis- he is a weaver, and lives in Hare-court. On the 4th of September I sent my little girl Matilda, and my little boy Joseph to take a walk; they had their boots on when they went out, and came back without them; from what they said I went after the prisoner, and found her in York-street; I asked if she knew any thing of the children's boots - she said No, she did not; I gave her into custody, and she told me where they were.
   LEWIS HARRIS. I am shopman to a pawnbroker, in Brown's-lane. I have a pair of boots, pawned on the 4th of September, in the name of Ann Watson- I cannot swear to the prisoner, but this is the counterpart of the duplicate I gave for them.
   MATILDA DAVIS. I am eleven years old. The prisoner took my boots off my feet on the 4th of September, when I was in the street; she told me my mother said I was to have the boots off, and she was to take them home - I was to have another pair for working days, and a new pair for Sundays.
   CHARLES EAST(Police-constable H 33). I received the prisoner, and the boots.
 GUILTY. Aged 15. -    Judgment Respited.
