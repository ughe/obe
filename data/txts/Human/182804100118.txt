 1061.  DAVID GARDINER was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 1 bridle, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Eddy.
   WILLIAM WAKEFIELD. I am a carman to Mr. Thomas Eddy. I was coming out of his place into Blenheim-mews on the 7th of April, and saw the prisoner go by with this bridle on his arm, which I knew was Mr. Eddy's, as I had been in the habit of putting it on the horse every day - I said to a person named Kitchen, "That man has got a bridle, and we had better go and take him," which we did- I have known him some time; I believe he had an honest character.
   JOHN KITCHER. I am in the employ of Mr. Thomas Eddy. At a quarter-past seven o'clock, we saw the prisoner go by with this bridle, and took him at the corner of Blenheim-street - he said he had bought it.
   ANGELIOUS BETRAUN. I am an officer, and took him.
 Prisoner's Defence. Necessity and poverty led me to take it. I had been to borrow 6d. of Mr. Eddy, and he was not in the way.
     GUILTY. Aged 52. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1062.  WILLIAM CONDEN and JOSEPH WATERS were indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March , 3 stocks and centre-bits, value 10s.; 2 stocks and pin-bits, value 7s.; 5 stocks and spoon-bits, value 17s.; 1 stock and counter-sink, value 4s.; 1 stock and sinking-bit, value 5s., and 2 stocks without bits, value 4s., the goods of William Adams.
 WILLIAM ADAMS. I am a  carpenter; my shop is in Fox-court, Gray's Inn-lane. I left work at half-past ten o'clock on the Wednesday night, and next morning I missed the articles stated; I have known Conden from a baby - he lived very near me.
   SARAH ADAMS. I am the wife of William Adams. I went into the shop, and saw some planes lying in the yard; I then looked, and missed the articles stated, from the shelf.
   HANNAH CRUMP. I live in Blewitt's-buildings. On the 13th of March, I went into Elizabeth Carr's room, No. 4, Pheasant-buildings, Gray's Inn-lane, about a quarter before seven o'clock, to give her a light; I saw Waters there, counting over some tools; as I went into the room, he pushed the basket under the bed, and one of these tools fell out, which I knew was Mr. Adams', as I had seen him work with it so frequently; I met Mr. Adam just before eleven o'clock, and asked him if he had lost anything; he said Yes, his stocks and bits; I said if he went there he would find them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you in the habit of going to his shop? A. Yes; I go for chips, and see him at work with these tools. Waters never kept company with my sister; he did with Mr. Carr's sister - I did not say anything to Waters about the tools; the tool I saw fall out, was the same as this centre-bit, but larger and darker. I do not know that Waters is a carpenter; I only know him by his coming to Mrs. Carr's - I was in the room about five minutes; I did not take up the tool.
   HENRY WAGSTAFF. I did live in Pheasant-buildings. On the 13th of March, about nine o'clock in the morning, I met the two prisoners at the end of the Buildings, they asked me to give them something to drink; I said, I did not want anything myself, but I gave them some money - Conden took a stock and bit from under his coat, and asked if it was not a shame that he could not get more than 6d. for it; he said they had broken open a house, at Camden-town, which they thought was empty, but it was not - they were disturbed, but got fourteen of these tools, which they had been offered 6d. a-piece for.
 Cross-examined. Q. He did not say at Fox-court? A. No. I have gone by the name of Hughes and White - I have been tried here, and was imprisoned three months. Hughes was my mother's name; and I took that after I got out of trouble. I never went by the name of Crutch.
   ELIZABETH CARR. I lived in Pheasant-buildings, on the 13th of March. My husband is a fishmonger; Waters lived next door to me - on the 13th of March he brought a basket of tools into my room, about five o'clock, and asked me to let him leave it a short time; and about a quarter before eight o'clock, he came, and took it away - it had tools in it; he is a carpenter.
   JOHN DAVIS. I am a constable. I took Conden on the 14th of March, and Mr. Platt took Waters; we went to Water's lodging, and found some skeleton keys; when I saw Conden, I tapped him on the shoulder, and said I wanted him for house-breaking; "Very well," says he,"I will go with you."
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you not likely to get into trouble yourself, for taking paper from the Record Office? A. No; I was employed to sell some waste paper - Mr. Allen spoke about it; he could not make a charge against me.
   JAMES PLATT. As Davis was going along Gray's Inn-lane, with Conden, on the evening of the 14th, Waters came up to see if he was in custody; and as soon as he saw us, he ran away down Laystall-street - I followed, and took him; when I got him to the watch-house, I asked him where Conden was; he said, what the h - ll was that to him. Conden was then brought to the watch-house, and I said, was it not a shame to rob the poor man of his tools; he said, "You b - r, you may want a tool, before you die."
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you an officer? A. No; I am a watch-case maker - Davis asked me to assist him; the tools have not been found.
 WATER's Defence. They were my own tools; I left them there when I went to breakfast.
     NOT GUILTY.
