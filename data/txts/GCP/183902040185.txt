JOHN WARREN. I live with Mr. James Ashman and Son, pawnbrokers,
in Long-acre. On the 29th of January, the prisoner Inglis came and asked
for Mercer-street-he had not been gone two minutes before I saw bim
and Jewell looking in at the window close to the goods--Inglis came in again
--- he saw the shopman, and ran out, and in about balf an hour I missed a
pair of trowsers--these are them.
WILLIAM HOWARD. I am a policeman. About two o'cloek, on the
29th of January, Warren described the prisoners to me. I saw them
looking into another shop, and took them-Jewell said, “ You can search
me, I have nothing about me"--on Inglis I found 78., and on Jewell a
large knife, and 3d.--they were remanded till the following day, and
then Inglis told me where I could find the trowsers, and he took me
to No. 88, Monmouth-street, to a cellar-I found a girl she called her
mother, who said she had bought them for 28., that they had been seated,
and she had sold them for 38., but she would get them if I would let her
--she then went out with the girl, who came back with the trowsers, and
said her mother was ill, but she had sent her on, not to keep me waiting
--the woman did not appear before the magistrate, and the girl said her
mother was taken ill with the fright of my going, and was compelled to
go to the Lying-in Hospital.
Mary DEVANNY. I live with my mother at No. 88, Monmouth-street.
She buys and sells second-band clothes-- I was in her place when Inglis
brought these trowsers, and asked half-a-crown for them-Jewell was
waiting for him close by--my mother gave Inglis 2s, for them.
(The prisoners received good characters.)
INGLIS-GUILTY. Aged 9.2 Recommended to mercy.
JEWELL-GUILTY. Aged 11. Whipped and discharged.
