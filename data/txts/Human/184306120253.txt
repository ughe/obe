Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 2043. WILLIAM HOLMES was indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of George Benjamin Edwards , about two in the night, on the   28th of May , at St. John, Southwark , with intent to steal, and stealing therein, 300 yards of ribbon, value 16 l  . 11 s  . 6 d  .; 90 yards of silk, 20 l  .; and 3 handkerchiefs, 12 s  .; his property; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 18.— Transported for Ten Years .
   Before Mr. Justice Cresswell.
 2044. WILLIAM MONAHAN was indicted for feloniously assaulting George Luxton , on the   22nd of March , and stabbing and wounding him in and upon his left leg, with intent to do him some grievous bodily harm.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE LUXTON. I am a glover, and live in Church-street, Lambeth. On Wednesday morning, the 22nd of March, about one o'clock, I was at the Coburg Arms—I saw the prisoner there—he and I were fighting—a man named Bon ner was fighting with him—when I went in at the bar, and as I was passing through the bar, I was shoved  against him by Bonner—he struck me, and I struck him again—he struck me in the face several times—I went away, and had something to drink at the bar, and in ten minutes afterwards I found I was stabbed just above the ankle—I examined my trowsers next morning, and found they were cut through in four or five places—I had only one wound—when I discovered that I was wounded the prisoner was gone—I searched for him before I found myself wounded, as I found Bennett was wounded—he was taken to the hospital, and so was I.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  There was a number of persons there? A.  Yes, twenty at least—I believe there was a general scuffling and fighting going on—in passing between them I was shoved  up against the pris oner, who was fighting with Bonner, and while they were fighting I got the blow—I know the Coburg Arms, by going three or four times there—I have often gone there, but not lately—I live in the neighbourhood—I had been to the theatre that night, and went there to have something to drink—I do not go to the theatre very often—I work for Mr. Bolton, but have not been able to work for him for three months—my wife works for him now—I had hardly time to see whether the prisoner and Bonner were drunk—I was not there a minute before I was fighting with the prisoner—it appeared a general row  —I walked away from the prisoner—I did not see him fight with anybody else—I stood at the bar, having something to drink—the scuffling appeared to stop—I hit him several times, and he struck me—he fell down—I was not knocked down.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  When the prisoner was down was there anybody as near you as he was? A.  Yet, they were standing all round—I did not notice any one else on the ground—there were others round him—I believe he was hustled down more than by my blow—there was a quantity of people rushing about the bar. ALFRED COLLINS. I am a house painter, and live in South-street, Hercules-buildings. I was in the tap-room of the Coburg Arms, and heard a noise in front of the bar—I went there, and saw the prisoner in the act of striking Luxton, who returned it, and a fight commenced, which lasted some minutes—Bennett was alongside me, and witnessed it—Luxton was a friend of Bennett's, and Bennett said he would put a stop to the fighting—I saw nothing happen to the prisoner more than the exchange of blows between Luxton and himself—they were standing up fighting all the time till the prisoner slipped down.
