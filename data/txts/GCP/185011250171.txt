BENJAMIN SAUNDERS (policeman, R 220). On Sunday morning, 6th
Oct., a little after twelve o'clock, I was on duty with Frampton, in the
Lewisham-road-the prisoners were making a disturbance, and Frampton
told them to leave-he took out his watch, and gave them five minutes,
at the end of which they ran up to a wood-stack, to draw some sticks, but
they did not-Marlow said, “We mean to give it you to-night, yon
r”-we followed them to see where they were gone, and they met
Frampton, and Marlow struck him in the breast twice, with his double
fist-Frampton then took Marlow by the arm, and Marlow ran his head
between Frampton's legs, and tried to throw him down-Ererett and
Benjamin Marlow then went to a wooden fence, and drew a paling each;
Everett threw one at Frampton, which took him athwart the nose and
right cheek-bone, and he fell down senseless-they immediately ran away
-Frampton bled very much indeed-Marlow was taken a few days after-
wards-when Everett threw the paling, Marlow was about three feet from
him-he had just got away from Frampton—it was a wooden paling, with
square edges, about three feet and a half long, and about an inch and a
half thick-he stood about six yards from Frampton at the time he threw
it-the middle part of it struck him.
Cross-examined. Q. Where was Benjamin Marlow at the time? A.
He stood close by Everett - I was about nine feet from Frampton-the
paling went past me- I was between them, only a little on one side-the
prisoner Marlow was then at the side of the road, between Frampton and
me-Frampton struck Marlow twice across the arm with his staff-I did
not hear him cry out-it lasted altogether about five minutes, it might
be less.
Court. Q. Were these men very drunk at the time? A. No.
