1810. JOSEPH TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August, at St. Ann, Westminster, 9 lampirons, value 10s., fixed in certain street and places there situate, and dedicated to public use; against the Statute,&c.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to be 396 lbs. weight of iron, value 10s., the goods of, and belonging to  Daniel Niven and others, and then being fixed to certain buildings.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS varying the manner of stating the charge.
 MR. BARRY conducted the prosecution.
   GEORGE CLUBB. I am a smith, and live in Mitcham-street, Paddington. I have known the prisoner twenty years: he came to my shop on the 7th of August, to ask if I would go and give him a hand in the morning to do a job - he said he could pay me four or five shillings. and I could be back to my work by ten o'clock; I called him at three the next morning, and went with him to great Chapel-street- he raised the ladder against the wall, and said he had to take those lamp-irons down; he pulled a paper out of his pocket. and said he had so many to take down in that street, and some in King-court; Orange-street, St. Ann-court, and Carlisle-street - I believe Dean-street was mentioned; we went to work to pull them down - they were fixed up with nails and staples; we were about three hours, and pulled down eighteen in the whole - we put them down in two loads, and he said"We will take a load a-piece to where they are to go;" I took one load, and went with him to Newman-passage, to a man's house, named Burrows; I believe I carried four loads, and he took one, and the second load he was stopped with - I carried two loads after he was stopped; I afterwards saw him with Lucas, near a builder's yard, in Great Chapel-street; there was another gentleman with Mr. Lucas - the prisoner went into the yard; I said to him "Joe, what are you carrying them here for?" he said "Go and mind the other load, as there is a screw loose;" I did not know what it meant, but I thought it did not sound very well - I stood and watched him some time; I then went and worked at the heap, and took the second load - afterwards two men came up, and asked where I was going; I told them - they said I must go with them - they took me to Mr. Heron's, the builder's yard; I was taken to the watch-house, and from that to Mr. Burrows - I pointed out the lamp-irons, and was then taken to Marlborough-street, and there was admitted as a witness.
 Prisoner. Q. What money have you received from the gentleman since my commitment? A. Sometimes I received 1s., and sometimes 2s. 6d.; I do not know how much in all - I called on you in prison, and you asked me to bring you a bit of tobacco; I never told you then what I had received - nor said that I was to go for more on Saturday; I certainly called on you on the 5th of September, but I was not drunk - I said I had received 2s. 6d. - you asked me why I had not gone away, and I said I could not; you said you would have gone on glass bottles to have got away - I did not, to the best of my recollection, want half a sovereign of you; I have been two or three times to you, but not to get money - you had been in the first regiment with me; I did not shew you my coat sleeve, and say I could not go into the country without a coat.
 MR. BARRY. Q. Have you been out of work? A. Yes; and I got some money of the parish.
   WILLIAM WHITEHOUSE. I am a labourer in the employ of Mr. Heron, of Carlisle-street. The prisoner was brought to our yard about seven o'clock, on the 8th of August, by Lucas, who is the inspector of nuisances; the prisoner had some lamp-irons on his shoulder - he said he had been taking lamp-irons down, and he brought him to know if he was authorized to take them down; the irons were left with me - the prisoner went away with Lucas, and said the Company had employed him.
   WILLIAM CRAG. I am an officer of Marlborough-street. On the 8th of August, I executed a search-warrant with Lucas, at Burrow's house, in Newman's passage; I saw some lamp-irons there, which appeared to have been fixed - I went the second time with Clubb, and took them away.
   FRANCIS MACE. I am inspector of the parish of St. Ann's, Westminster. In the month of August I went with Clubb to Great Chapel-street, St. Ann's-court, Carlisle-street, and that neighbourhood - he pointed out places where lamp-irons had been taken from by him, and the prisoner; I had seen the irons there before, and I saw irons at Burrow's, which appeared to me to be the same.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that a man named Houghton of No. 6, Great Chesterfield-street, who represented himself as foreman of the gas works, had employed him to take down the irons and dispose of them.
     GUILTY. Aged 39. Confined Three Months.
 1811.  JOHN BARCLAY was indicted for bigamy.
 MR. BOLLAND conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM WILLIAMS. I am parish-clerk of St. Mary, Newington, Surrey. I find by this register, that on the 16th of December, 1824, John Barclay, bachelor, and Elizabeth Cornelius, spinster, were marriedin that church, by bans, in the presence of Charles Brand, and Elizabeth Gilliard.
 ELIZABETH BRAND. My name was formerly Gilliard. I saw the prisoner married to Elizabeth Cornelius, in December, 1824; she is still living - I have not a doubt but the prisoner is the man.
   ITHIEL PRICE. I am parish-clerk of St. Dunstan, Stepney. On the 25th of June, 1827, there was a marriage in that church of John Barclay, bachelor, and Mary Phillips, spinster- I am quite certain the prisoner is the man who was married on that occasion.
 Prisoner. Q. Did the Minister ask if we were of age? A. No. If he considers it a clandestine marriage, he does, but you brought two witnesses with you, and it appeared a proper marriage.
 MR. BOLLAND. Q. Should you remember the woman if you saw her? A. Yes; she is in Court.
 MARY PHILLIPS. I lived with the prisoner as his wife, after our marriage, for twelve months; he made away with my clothes, and the tickets of my property.
 Prisoner. Q. What age were you? A. Twenty.
