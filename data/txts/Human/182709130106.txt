WILLIAM BENNS. I am a special constable. At a quarter before six o'clock on the morning of the 18th of August, I saw the prisoner and another walking to and fro, in Torrington-square- I watched them, and saw the prisoner take a cord from his pocket, and drop it down the area of No. 4; he drew up this kettle, put it under his arm, and gave the cord to the other - I went up and took him with the kettle; he said he had done it for a lark.
   MARY STALLAN. I live with Mrs. Rebecca Southey(who is single), at No. 4, Torrington-square; this is her kettle - I had set it out about a quarter of an hour before; the constable came and asked if it was mine.

     GUILTY. Aged 27. Transported for Seven Years.
 1787.  HENRY SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 13 yards of silk, value 2l. 9s.; 11/2 yards of calico, value 1s. 3d., and 1 piece of cord, value 2d., the goods of William Sweet.
   SOPHIA SWEET. I am the wife of William Sweet - he is a servant in Mr. Harford's family, Piccadilly; we lodge at No. 9, Bryanstone-street, Portman-square. On the 5th of July there was thirteen yards of silk, one yard and a half of white calico, and some cord, lying on the kitchen dresser, near the window - I had seen it safe between twelve and one o'clock that day, and missed it in about a quarter of an hour - I saw it again at the pawnbroker's, about the 10th of July.
 Cross-examined by MR. QUIN. Q. Had not you lost several other things? A. Yes; there was a servant in that house taken up on suspicion, but he was discharged; I never saw the prisoner near the house.
   GEORGE WILLOUGHBY PERUZZI. I am a pawnbroker. I have thirteen yards of silk, some calico, and some cord, which were pawned by the prisoner on the 5th of July - this prosecutrix called in a few days afterwards, and claimed them as hers - the prisoner came again on the Monday, and brought a coat for sale, and I stopped him; he gave his direction, No. 32, William-street.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know the prisoner? A. Yes; I had seen him before, and am positive of his person.
   JOHN ANDREWS. I am an officer. I was fetched to the pawnbroker's; I found the prisoner there; he had brought a coat and some shoes to redeem some silk, as I understood.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been after a place, and as I returned home, a man came and asked if I wanted to buy any silk - he wanted 30s. for it, and I gave him 26s. - there was a person with me, who is here.
   HENRY SANDERS. I live at No. 17, Circus-street - this silk resembles some silk I saw a man offer for sale at the Stingo-gate, in the early part of July, I cannot tell the day - it was offered to me, but as my wife had a silk dress I did not buy it - it was not the prisoner who offered it - it was an elderly man; he offered it to several people there; I am there every day, to take up the coachmen's tea - there was a coachman here of the name of Hood, but he is gone.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Confined Six Months.
 1788.  WILLIAM WOODHALL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 1 coat, value 3s., the goods of William Pascoa.
 WILLIAM PASCOA. On the 31st of July I was carrying my coat about for sale, and a man, who I believe was the prisoner, took it from me, in  Little Earl-street- he ran off with it, and I followed some distance, but did not give an alarm. I saw it again in the officer's possession some days afterwards - I believe he is the man.
   JOHN TURNER. I took the prisoner to the watch-house for an assault, on the 31st of July - he had this coat partly on when I took him - I asked where he got it; he said it was stolen out of a drunken spree.
   PHILIP RILEY. I was at the watch-house; the prisoner told me he had bought the coat for 1s. 6d.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined One Month.
 1789.  ANN MATTHEWS was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , 1 watch, value 35s., the goods of John Cody , from his person.
 JOHN CODY. On the 4th of August I was a little tipsy, and went with the prisoner to a house - I had seen her before once or twice - I staid there about a quarter of an hour; I did not see her take the watch, but missed it as soon as I got away. I saw her again in two or three days afterwards, and asked her about it; she said it was pawned - I am not sure I did not give it her. She took me to where it was, and I promised to let her go if I got it, but the pawnbroker would not let us have it that night, and I was not certain of its being mine.
   WILLIAM SHEPPARD. I took the prisoner into custody - she gave up the duplicate, and said the prosecutor had promised to let her go if she gave it up.
   JOSEPH KING. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned by me, but not by the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.
