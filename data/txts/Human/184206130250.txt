   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Why did not you stop him A.  I had a number of papers on the table when he came in, I was writing for the evening post—as soon as he was gone I gathered up the papers, looked for the money, and it was gone—I missed my money about a mi nute and a half after he was gone—I sent my servant after him—there was no other man with him when he received the money—there was during part of the time—he refused to leave the goods unless I signed the book and paid him the 20 s .—I did not say I would not pay him more than 17 s . 3 d .—I said nothing about 17 s . 3 d .—he threw the money on the table himself, and said he would take back the goods—I afterwards sent my sister-in-law to Pick ford's—I did not desire her to say that she had no doubt that a mistake had occurred, by my having had a part of the money at the time in my hand, which I unthinkingly handed to the prisooer—I never desired her to say any such thing—I went to Pickford's the next morning—I bad an interview with the prisoner there—I did not ask whether 20 s . was the charge he was directed to make—I afterwards complained that 20 s . was more than he ought to have charged, but not in the first instance—I will not swear that I did at all.
   COURT . Q.  You must know whether you found that the 20 s.  was a wrong charge? A.  It was a wrong charge decidedly, which I intend to put them in the Court for—it is an overcharge—I do not mean to say it was a wrong charge on his part—I did not say to the prisoner, when I went to Pickford's the next morning, that unless he gave me the 7 s . 6 d . I would give him into custody—I said nothing of the kind—I said, "You took 7 s . 6 d . yesterday that was not your own"—he said, "I did no such thing"—I said, "Then I shall give you into custody"—f sent my sister-in law for a policeman, and the moment I did so some of the clerks there did their best to get him out of the place—they sent him to another part of the office, and I followed him—the prisoner told me he had only received the right money; so did the clerks—he did not say he would refund no thing—I swear he never said so—I do not recollect his saying he would not give me any thing back—I believe he did not.
