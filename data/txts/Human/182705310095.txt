 1249.  EDWARD WALKER and JOSEPH EDWARDS were indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , 1 half-crown, 1 pair of shoes, value 3s.; 1 handkerchief, value 6d., and 2 knives, value 1s., the property of David Profit , from his person.
   RICHARD KENDRICK. I am a watchman. On the morning of the 8th of April, at half-past one o'clock, I was in Wellclose-square, and turning into Parson's-street, I saw David Profit on the ground, very drunk - Walker was taking off his shoes; Edwards had his hand in his right-hand pocket - I sprung my rattle, ran and overtook Edwards turning into Well-street, but found nothing on him - I lost sight of Walker for some time, but am certain of him.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. What distance were you off? A. Seven or eight yards; Walker was at his feet, and he put something under his arm, I cannot say what; I did not see the shoes; Profit had himself been taken to the watch-house on that night before.
   ISAAC PORRITT. The prisoners were brought to the watch-house, and searched, one half crown was found on Edwards, but nothing which the prosecutor could swear to; he is now at sea - he had no shoes on.
   MATTHEW HALL. I heard an alarm, and took Walker, who was running.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1250.  JOHN BARNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of April , 2 sovereigns, the monies of Nathaniel Skuse , from his person.
   WILLIAM HEATH. I am a watchman of Whitechapel. On the 23d of April I was in Osborne-street, at half-past 12 o'clock at night. I know Nathaniel Skuse, he lives in Wentworth-street; he was in Osborne-street, very drunk - and the prisoner leading him. I followed and saw the prisoner take his right-hand from Skuse's right-hand pocket. I went up and asked if he was his friend - he said he was - I said, "I think you have robbed him." I took him to the watch-house - Skuse, said in his presence, that he had lost two sovereigns. The officer found two sovereigns in the prisoner's fob pocket.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did you not see a friend of Skuse's there? A. Yes; the prisoner did not say he had asked him to assist; he is not here, nor is Skuse.
   THOMAS HAILES. I was officer of the night. Skuse was brought in, and said he had been robbed of two sovereigns by the prisoner, who had laid hold of his arm, and put his hand into his pocket - the prisoner said he had only got 7s. I searched him and found that. I then said"I'll search your fob," which I did, and found two sovereigns. I then searched the prosecutor and found some silver on him, his right-hand breeches pocket was turned inside out - he had a bundle of papers.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where was the silver? A. In his waistcoat-pocket; his friend was not bound over.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming home and saw this man laying insensible on the ground - a man, calling himself his friend, asked me to assist; the watchman came up, and asked if I was his friend - I said "No; but I was assisting;" he said he thought he saw my hand come from his pocket. I can prove how I got the 2l. 7s.; the prosecutor afterwards said he had lost nothing. I declare my innocence - the prosecutor was locked up himself, as he was drunk - his friend has not been heard of since.
   WM. HEATH . The prosecutor was locked up - he did say afterwards, that he had lost nothing; his friend was close by him.
   WILLIAM YOUNG. I am a carman and live in Mercer's-row, Shadwell. On the 23d of April I paid the prisoner 2l. 10s. for a suit of clothes. I gave him two sovereigns and a half in gold - this was about seven o'clock in the evening.   GUILTY. Aged 23. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1251.  ISAAC JACOBS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of John Wood , from the person of Jane his wife.
   JANE WOOD. I am the wife of John Wood. I was in Church-row, Whitechapel on the 1st of May, about half-past nine o'clock at night, the prisoner came and took this handkerchief out of my basket. I was running at the time, and collared him immediately, he begged of me to let him go.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BARRY. Q. Where was this? A. Near a sugar-warehouse - there were some ballan-singers there - I was running past, my handkerchief was picked up - I lost two sixpences from my basket.
   ROBERT COMBER. I heard a cry of Stop thief! the prisoner was running - he had got from the prosecutrix. I collared him and took him to the watch-house.
   THOMAS POWELL. I saw a gentleman pick up the handkerchief. I shewed it to the prosecutrix and took it to the watch-house. I returned and saw something shining on the ground. and found two sixpences - that was between nine and ten o'clock at night.
   THOMAS HAILES. I heard a cry of Stop thief! and found the prisoner in Comber's hands.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I heard an alarm and was stopped.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
