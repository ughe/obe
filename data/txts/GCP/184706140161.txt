ELIZABETH Cook. I live at No. 90, Drury-lane, the prisoner is my bus-
band. On the 20th of May he came there with a stick in his hand-be
held the stick over his head to strike me--I rose from the bed I was on, I
laid hold of the stick, and Phoebe Morgan and I got the stick from him-he
then took a knife off the table- I went into the bed-room and shut the door
- he forced the door open, and rushed into the room; be threw me
down first, and then stabbed me in the back of my head with the knife-I
held his hand with both my hands, and he could not get his hand away, and
he screwed my face round with the nails of his other hand-he scratched me
and hurt me terribly-he heard some footsteps coming, dropped the
knife by the side of the bed and left me-Phoebe Morgan stopped him from
going down stairs-he went up stairs, and was taken on the top stairs of
the house-he was quite sober-we had been parted twelve months-he
bad threatened the day before that he would come with a knife.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. He bad seen you before this? 4. Yes;
he said, “You have had your frolic, but you don't see Whit-Sunday though it
is very close to it"-we had been married thirteen years-he had only come
out of the workhouse the day before I had a daughter by the prisoner,
she is eleven years old, she lives with me, I carry on business in the name
of Cogan-I write up “Cogan late Cook"-I am not living with any man
-Mr. Cogan used to come backward and forward, the same as any of the
workpeople-my husband in general has a stick-the knife was in the room
-it was about ten minutes before twelve o'clock in the day when he came
-I was in bed, because he had threatened my life the day before, and I was
up the greater part of the night-I had seen Mr. Cogan on the Monday
before he came to tell me my husband had come out-my husband raised
the stick - I got it from him before any blow was struck- I have been carry-
ing on business with “Cogan late Cook" written up, for twelve months-my
husband left me in the street with a child-T did not go to the place, take
the rent-money out of the drawer, and then send in a broker--the prisoner
did not say he did not come to kick up a row, but to see after his daughter
- he did not say anything of the kind there; he did at Bow-street-I did
not strike him first-Morgan and I got the stick from him-Morgan had
brought the knife into the room and laid it on the table.
Q. Will you swear she had not the knife in her hand, and you were all
struggling, and so you got the cut? A, I can swear she had not the knife in
hand; I saw him take it up.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Have you been cohabiting with Cogan? A. No,
no more than the protection of the name- I have been obliged to work for
myself, getting no assistance from my husband for twelve months I lost a
great deal of blood, but the wound was not a large one.
PHEBE MORGAN. I was at work at Mrs. Cook's when her husband came
she was in bed-he bad a stick in his hand-he went through the shop -I told
him there was no one at home-he went to the bed-room-he held up the
stick to hit my mistress- I took the stick from him and broke it-he ran to
the table, took up the knife, and stabbed my mistress on the back of her
head, and scratched her face---my mistress is a glass-grinder-Mr. Cogan is
my master-he brings work there-he does not live in the house-there was
no man in bed with Mrs. Cook.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you break the stick over the prisoner's back?
4. No; he clung to the stick, and broke it into three or four pieces- he had
it over his head to strike.
