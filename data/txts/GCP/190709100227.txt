JOHN FRIEND (prisoner, on oath). On Bank Holiday night (August
5) I was in Les Bridge Road, accompanied by my brother and my
Lister in-law, when I met a man named Lovell leading a harnessed
lsrse with a rug over it. He said, "My trap has broken down up
the road and I have left it. Do you know where I can borrow one!"
I said, "No, I do not." Outside the "Ship Aground" I met Riley
with Gardner. I said to Riley, "Do you know anybody that can lend
chap a trap." Riley said, "My landlord has got one. He will lend
it to you for a bit of silver." Meanwhile we went to Nesbit Street,
where Head lives, ana dragged the trap round to the "Stag" public-
house in Brooksby's Walk, where Lovell said he would meet me
with the horse and harness. Lovell put the horse in the trap and
way he went, saying to me, "Do you know anybody that will buy
the old cob. I am a bit short of money and want to go to the market
tomorrow." On the following morning I met him in Old Ford Road,
and he asked me, "Did I know anybody who would buy his horse1"!
I told him "Yes." He aen said, “Here, it is; do what you like
with it. I want £10 down for it or you can have an exchange for
another one, as I want some money." I got up into the trap, and
going along the Old Ford Road I met Gardner and Riley. I said,
Hallon, would you like to go for a ride1" They said they did not
mind and got up and accompanied me to Dorking. When I got to
Pixham Lane I left Riley and Gardner at the top of the lane while!
I went down to where I was going to sell the horse. I am perfectly
innocent of having stolen the horse. I had seen Lovell on the
Thursday before at Spitalfields Market with some bananas on his cart
with a horse exactly like the one he gave me to sell, and I thought
it was the same horse when I saw it on the Monday night.
Cross-examined. After I had sold the horse I intended to put
the cart on the rail and send it up to London Bridge. I think I had
10jd. in my pocket. That would not have paid for sending back
the cart, but I should have had money after I had sold the horse.
It did not strike me to ask any questions about the harness or the
borse because I had seen Lovell with it on the previous Thursday.
He keeps a fruit stall somewhere in the Bethnal Green Road. When
I w arrested at Dorking they asked me whə this Lovell was, and I
told the detective as far as lay in my power. I did not know Lovell
very well, only through having seen him up and down, Lovell
promised me a bit of gold if I sold the börse. I was to meet Lovell
again after I had sold the cob at the corner of Briok Lane, Bethnal
Green Road the same night. I have never seen Lovell since as, of
course, I have been in custody, whereas if I had been out on bail I
might have had a chance to look about for him.
