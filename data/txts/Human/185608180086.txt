   Cross-examined. Q.  How long was the prosecutor before he recovered his sensibility? A.  Two or three minutes—he did not mention his having seen two men in a public house—I remained with him about five minutes, but it took me longer to get him home—I mentioned to him my having seen these two men, but did not tell him how they were dressed—I asked him to describe them—I never mentioned the public house, nor did he—he men tioned it at the court for the first time—I went with Gray to take the pri soners—he knew where they lived, but I did not—they were searched at the station, but they only had a few halfpence.
   MR. M'AUBREY . Q.  Were you there when the prosecutor was taken to the police office next day? A.  Yes; the prisoners and about twenty others were all together—I asked Beaver if he could identify them from other men, he said that he could—I took him in, and he pointed them out immediately—there might have been one or two women and boys among the twenty.
