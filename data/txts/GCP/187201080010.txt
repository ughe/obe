master of the Grammar School at Stockwell—he bore the character of being
a gentleman of great learning and classical attainments, and of being a
kind and humane man-I always understood that he was very punctual in
the performance of his duties as head master of the school-1 bad never
attended him, or had much personal intercourse with him; he has not had
much illness, I believe-I had often met him in the street, and three or
four months previously I met him at a luncheon, at King's College distri-
bution of prizes—he did not know me at first, he was very absent—he was
a very reserved man, and very self-absorbed--when I was called in to see
him in this unconscious state, I have no doubt that he had taken poison
