 MR. LAW. Q. Have you any reason to believe Mr. McVicar has lost any of those sent him? A. No.
   HENRY ROBERTS. I am a pattern-drawer to Messrs. Chippendall; part of this new pattern is my drawing - I made an alteration in this new pattern - I cannot say when, but I can tell from that, that it is their pattern.
 Cross-examined. Q. You will not say nobody else printed that pattern? A. I do not think it possible - I do not know how many they may have sold.
   JOHN PERROTT. I am manager to Messrs. Wilkinson and Co., packers. I have delivery notes of Mr. McVicar; I packed all the shawls received from him, and received the delivery notes from him; I delivered the goods, packed, to our carmen, Burn and Wood, to take to Phillips; they were packed in tin cases, and soldered; I believe these shawls were folded in our house, and formed part of that package - there was a ticket put into the outside fold, and part of it still remains; I cannot speak to the ticket, but it is on precisely the same spot as we place them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean that you packed that very shawl up? A. I believe I did, and that they were folded in our house; an Excise-officer was in attendance. The tin-case might be taken out, opened, and put in again, with a little trouble - it would take much trouble to repack them properly, but we have nothing to do with the shipping.
 MR. LAW. Q. If the property is out, you conclude the case has been opened? A. Yes; I know the folding done in our house; this shawl bears every appearance of having been folded in our house, and I believe was packed by me. I have a peculiar method of packing.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How many examinations have there been at the office? A. Four or five; the shawls were then opened.
 COURT. Q. Look again at the ticket - do you believe it to be part of your ticket? A. It is the place where we put the ticket - it has been torn off evidently; there are fifteen shawls in a piece - we always put a ticket on the outer one.
   ROBERT WOOD. I am a carman in the employ of Wilkinson and Co. I was employed, with Burn, to carry these packages to Galley-quay, to Mr. Phillips - I delivered them to Joseph Payne.
 Cross-examined. Q. There was no appearance of their being opened at all? A. No.
   JAMES BURN. I assisted Wood in carrying the goods; we delivered them as we received them, at Galley-quay, Thames-street.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you take any of them? A. No - the boxes were nicely packed up, and had no appearance of being opened nor broken.
   JOSEPH PAYNE. I have heard Wood and Burn examined - I received the goods from them at the Galleyquay - they were lowered into the lighter in the state I received them; Mr. Phillips' men had the care of them - I cannot say who they were, or whether the prisoner was one. I gave an account of them to Chisley, and he entered them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you lower them down into the lighter? A. Yes; I did not see the prisoner there - I believe he was; some of Mr. Phillips' men were there; I delivered them as I received them. I know one Aldridge - I cannot say whether the goods were ever in his craft.
   WILLIAM OLDING CHISLEY. I received from Payne the items relative to the packages deposited in the lighter, and booked them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see any of these packages after they were deposited on board the lighter? A. No - I cannot tell whether any of them were broken.
 MR.  WILLIAM WADHAM COPE. I am one of the City Marshals. I took the prisoner at the prosecutor's counting-house, on the 15th of September; I asked where he had those shawls from, which he had to ship for Mr. Phillips; he said he had not a shawl in his possession, and did not know anything about them; he said he never had a shawl in his possession in his life. On being taken to the Mansion House he said he lived at No. 6, Norwich-court; I went there, and saw his wife - I inquired for Hall - she said he was at home, but she was his wife; I found one of these shawls on her neck at the time - I then searched the house, and found up-stairs four whole shawls and five half shawls.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you go to Mr. Venables? A. Yes, where I found four other shawls of the same pattern, which the manufacturer identified; I never saw the prisoner and that woman together. I did not find any shawls in the trunks - they were in the drawers.
   WILLIAM PHILLIPS re-examined. There is not much difficulty in opening these packages. I have examined some of a similar description, and I find, by very common means, it might be done, and closed, to all appearance, as it was before; the tin-cases are to preserve them from sea water. I know the prisoner's wife - the place where she was found is where he lived.
 Cross-examined. Q. Could you have opened the case yourself? A. Yes, with a pair of pinchers, a hammer, and a chisel; and if they closed the wooden case, the tin lining would not be seen - it was a deal case with iron hoops - they could break open the tin without its being discovered in this country. I asked one Smith to turn evidence; he said he knew nothing about it - the cases are sealed.
 MR. ANDREWS. Q. If it was opened, would it be discovered before they arrived at Calcutta? A. No; the prisoner might perhaps have access to the goods only a a few hours, or perhaps a day or two - they were all night and part of the next day in our lighter; the prisoner was generally there as our confidential servant; it was his duty to see them delivered on board the ship.
   WILLIAM VENABLES. I am a linen-draper, and live in Whitechapel. I bought forty-four shawls similar to these on the 26th of August, of Mr. Shave, at 36s. a dozen - these being found, let to this prosecution.
 Cross-examined. Q. Who is Shave? A. A linen-draper living in Graces-alley. I had no suspicion of them; I considered him a respectable man - he is now in Newgate. I never saw a shawl of this pattern before.
 MR. LAW. Q. Was not Shave charged with this offence? A. Yes; he had failed, and was in private lodging; not keeping a shop at that time.
