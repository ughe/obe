to me on the afternoon of 19th Dec.—he said he wanted the assistance of the
police, he was going to turn Coffeld out of possession—I said I could not
allow the police to go—there were other police about the neighbourhod, and
if there was any breach of the peace they would inte&re in it—be said he

that he ought to have an ejectment—he made some remark that he should

risks—be did not say to me that he was going with these men to repair
the house.

—I cannot of my own knowledge say whether it did business after August.
CHARLES CRIPPS. I am a licensed victualler, and live in Coppice-row,
Clerkenwell, about five doors from the Horseshoe and Magpie. On 19th
Dec. there was a great deal of noise, and a lot of people running—I went out
of doors in my slippers—I saw a mob of persons, and Miss Coffeld was in
the window, partly outside and partly in, and some door was being nailed
against her legs inside—it was taken away, and she fell, and was carried
away—she appeared to be fainting—I saw Mr. Terry was inside, squeezing
this shutter against her—his head was bleeding—I did not see any one strike
him, to prevent their pushing the shutter—there was a mob outside, endea-
vouring to e'tricate Miss Coffeld—l did not see any one outside strike any
of the persons inside—the conduct of the persons inside appeared violent—
they were pressing the shutters to, and the persons outside were pressing
against it—Miss Coffield's legs were pressed with the shutter, and the persons
outside were attempting to rescue her, and then Mr. Terry raised himself up,
and asked the police to protect him.
