 973.  WILLIAM GEORGE SHARP was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 1 pair of graining-plates, value 1l., the goods of William Crawford.
   WILLIAM CRAWFORD. I live in Walbrook-buildings, Hoxton, and am assistant to my father. Mr. William Crawford, of Peerless-row, a book-binder. On the 16th of February the prisoner came to my father, and asked for the loan of a pair of quarto morocco graining-plates for Mr. Knock - I lent them to him, and he never returned them.
   GEORGE KNAPP. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Golden-lane. On the 16th of February the prisoner brought two pairs of graining-plates, which he said he had brought for his father, whose name was James Sanders, No. 13, Whitecross-street, and his own name was John Sanders.
   JOHN KNOCK. The prisoner was my apprentice, and left me on the 23rd of January - I did not send him to Mr. Crawford's to borrow any thing on the 16th of February.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I hope you will be as favourable as you can.
     GUILTY. Aged 16.
 974.  WILLIAM GEORGE SHARP was again indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 1 pair of graining-plates, value 25s., the goods of Alexander Crawford.
   ALEXANDER CHAWFORD. I live in Peerless-row. The prisoner came to my house on the 16th of February, and borrowed a pair of morocco graining-plates for Mr. Knock - I let him have them; these are them.
 Prisoner. Q.Did you not say you gave me the plates? A. Yes, I gave them into your hand, as you stood at the door, for your master.
 COURT. Q. You did not intend to part with the property? A.Certainly not, only to lend them to his master - he had borrowed them for him before.
   GEORGE KNAPP. I took these plates in pawn with the others, and lent 2s. on them.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Three Months, and Whipped.
 975.  ELIZA HURRY was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of March , 3 half-crowns, and 1 crown, the monies of John Gardiner , from his person.
   JOHN GARDINER. I live in Lamb-alley, Bermondsey-street, and am a skinner. On the 6th of March I went to a public-house in Wentworth-street - I had a pint or two of beer; I asked for a bed, and they could not let me have one, but recommended me to a private house just by - I went there, and went to bed; I counted what money I had before I went to bed; I had two crowns and three half-crowns, and I put them into my trousers pocket - there was a candle in the room; when I had been in bed about twenty minutes, I saw a woman come into the room with nothing on her but her shift - she came to my bed-side; I asked what she wanted - she said to come to bed to me; I had placed my trousers, with my money in them, under my head, but when I looked round I saw them on the floor, near to the prisoner - I got out of bed, examined my trousers pockets, and missed the three half-crowns and one of the 5s. pieces; I accused the prisoner of robbing me - she could not speak, on account of having something in her mouth; I seized her, and in the scuffle the candle went out - I called the people of the house, and sent for a Policeman; when he was coming up the stairs the prisoner put a crown-piece and a half-crown into my hand, and said she would make it all up before the Policeman came - I said I would not till she gave me the other two half-crowns; I saw she had something in her mouth - I seized her throat, and grappled with her, but I have never seen the other two half-crowns since; I detained her till the officer came in- I had had a pint or two of beer, just to refresh myself.
 Prisoner. I was in bed when he came into the house; he asked if there was any woman in the house - he heard me cough, and said, "That is a woman's voice." Witness. No, I did not; I did not get out of bed and let her in in her shift.
 COURT. Q.How came you to go there for a bed? A. I had been to some friends, then went to the public-house, and as I felt myself fatigued I thought I would get a bed there.
 Prisoner. Did I not say, "If you think I have any money, where have I got to put it?" and then you asked the woman to send for an officer; I did not offer to go away - I did not put this money into your hand. Witness. Yes, you put the money in my hand in the dark.
 JURY. Q.What hour was it? A. A quarter before eight o'clock - I had never seen the prisoner before, and did not know she was in the house till she came to my bed-side; I suspected it was a house of ill-fame when I got into it, and that made me count my money - I never was in the house before; I am not married.
   JAMES WALTON. I am a Police-constable. On the night of the 6th of March I was in Wentworth-street; I was called, and went to the house, where I found the prisoner and the prosecutor on the second floor - the prosecutor was standing in his shirt, and the prisoner in her shift; I took a light with me - there was no light in the room; the prosecutor said she had robbed him of 12s. 6d. - he held out his hands, and said, "Here is what she has given me back," holding a crown-piece in one of his hands, and a half-crown in the other - I took the prisoner to the station-house, but found nothing on her; the prosecutor appeared sensible of what was going on - there was a partition between the room his bed was in and the other; that house makes up from seventy to eighty beds- they are all in narrow slips; the partitions only go up about six or seven feet high - it is more like a rabbit-warren than any thing else; any person might throw any thing over the partition; I have had several charges from that house.
 JURY. Q. What is the sign of the public-house? A. The Two Brewers.
   HENRY MARQUARD. I was with Walton - I received the crown and half-crown; we could not find any more -I did not observe that there had been a candle in the room, which had been put out.
