SURREY CASES .
   Before Lord Chief Baron Pollock ( with Mr. Baron Martin and Mr. Justice Willes ).
 1125. ANTONIO DE SALVI  (29) was indicted for the wilful murder of Robert Henderson Robertson . ( See page  568.)
   MESSRS. BODKIN . and CLERK . conducted the Prosecution.
 (  On the part of the prisoner, a plea of autrefois acquit was pleaded at the last Session, to which the Crown demurred, and the matter now came on for argument. )
   MR. BODKIN . submitted that the plea was bad in point of law, as it did not aver that the crime with which the prisoner was now charged was identical with that upon which he had been previously tried; it only alleged that the cause of death was the same, which would not support the plea. It was not a necessary ingredient in murder that there should be an intent to commit that offence; murder might be committed where it was manifest the party had no such intention, as in resisting a lawful apprehension, where the only intention might be to escape, or in administering drugs to procure abortion; yet in such cases, if death ensued, the crime would be murder; and an acquittal, on a charge of wounding with intent to murder, would be no answer, in the form of the present plea, to that charge. MR. EDWIN JAMES., Q. C., in support of the plea, contended that the wounding with intent to murder, charged in the first Count of the former indictment, and of which the prisoner was acquitted, was in effect the same accusation as the present. It was not disputed that it arose out of the same transaction and from the same wounding. It was clearly laid dawn in Chitty's Treatise on the Criminal Law, p.  455, that it was not in all cases necessary that the two charges should be precisely the same in degree; it was sufficient that an acquittal of one would show that the defendant could not be guilty of the other. The present case fell within that principle; the wounding was the Same, the penalty was the same, death in either case. The mere fact of the death of the party wounded did not affect the question; for, although not the ordinary course, there was no reason why, if the death had occurred at the time, the prisoner should not have been tried for the very offence of wounding with intent to murder, of which he had been acquitted; nor would it have been any answer to such a proceeding to say that, because the man was dead, therefore the prisoner could not be tried on such a charge. It was analogous to the case of a party acquitted of a common assault, which he might plead in bar to any future indictment for a felonious assault, there being but one assault, and the felonious intention and the transaction being identical with the assault of which there had been a previous acquittal. As an authority for this proposition, see the Crown v. Robinson , 5 Deas and Anderson's Reports , 26 l . also  5 Law Chronicle, Regina v. Walker, 2 Moody and Robinson , 446. upon these and other authorities ( to be referred to ), he contended that this case came within that principle of the law which declared that no man's life should be placed in jeopardy twice for the same offence.
   Upon MR. SERJEANT PARRY . proceeding to address the Court on the same side, the LORD CHIEF BARON . expressed the opinion of the Court that only one Counsel could be heard.
