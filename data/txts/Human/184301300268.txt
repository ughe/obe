I was acting as cashier on the 10th, 11th, and 12th—in my absence for meals per sons act for me—I did not on either of those days receive from the prisoner 3 l . 18 s . 6 d .—I was there from five o'clock till eight on the evening of the 10th—I can say positively that 3 l . 18 s . 6 d . was not paid to me by the pri soner on any one of these days—it is my duty to see the cheque-book kept by the prisoner—the men call over their books at night, the money is counted by the cashier, who takes it to the bank in the morning—the prisoner's book is compared with the sheet of the day, and if he had paid this sum in that day, the money in the sheet would not have balanced, which it did correctly—it would have been 3 l . 18 s . 6 d . more.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you got your book here? A.  Yes, and the sheet—my accounts are quite correct between the 10th and 16th—they never failed to balance on any occasion within a shilling or two—I keep on my paper an account of what each person pays on a small cheque—the prisoner paid me various sums between the 10th and 16th—the sheets will show it—this sheet is for the 12th—on that day I received various sums, amounting to 17 l . 2 s . 6 d ., as the sheet states—on the 11th I received 35 l . 1 s . 5 d ., and one cheque was 30 l .—on the 13th I received 11 l . 8 s . 5 d .—on each of these days I received some money from him, varying in amount, but not that sum—I swear he has never paid me more by mistake than he should—I think on one occasion I paid him more than I ought to have done, and he returned it
   COURT . Q.  Supposing him to receive several sums, are they separately entered, or only the total sum? A.  They are separately entered—if he received 2 l . 10 s . and 1 l . 10 s . they would be entered as two separate items. JULIANA BURTON. I am occasionally employed to receive cash during the absence of Williams—I did not receive 3 l . 18 s . 6 d . on the 10th, 11th, or 12th of Jan. from the prisoner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you got your sheets? A.  There are no sheets but these, and these would contain the amount of what he paid me as well as Wil liams—they are made up at the end of the day—these papers are in Williams's handwriting. ELIZA WILLIAMS re-examined.  The separate sums are put into his book, then they are added together, and the total amount is put on the sheet.
   COURT . Q.  Is there a book in which the prisoner's separate payments would appear, and to whom he paid them; would it appear how much he paid to each person? A.  No, it would be proved by the sheet that the prisoner paid in the money—the book would represent the various sums he paid in, and this paper or sheet is the cash account of the day, under the names of all the persons paying in money—the total that each person paid—the fractional parts appear in the prisoner's book.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Look at this book kept by the prisoner; look on the 11th of Jan., and look at the sheet made up on the 11th, what is the sum of money apparently received from the prisoner according to the sheet? A.  35 l . 1 s . 5 d ., and the total in the book is 35 l . 1 s . 5 d .—it appears that 3 l . 18 s . 6 d . has been interlined after the 35 l . 1 s . 5 d .—if this 3 l . 18 s . 6 s . had been in the book when it was brought to me on the 11th, it would not have ap peared right—I should not have allowed the book to have been made up with an error of 3 l . 18 s . 6 d .—it is not added with either of the days—I did not on the 16th, or any day before the 16th, receive this sum.
