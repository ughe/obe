 Did you make use of any promise to induce her to confess this? - No; after she had confessed, I said, I want nothing but my things, if I get them I will do you all the service I can. I got a search warrant to take up Mrs. Fieldhouse. I went to Ann Fieldhouse's to look for the things, and a constable with me, as soon as I got the warrant. I believe I found my child's muslin cloak cut and part made into a cap, the remainder was there; it was a muslin cloak worked with lace all round it; I found my bag there.
 Was the prisoner there at the time? - No, she remained in the Compter; I went to a pawnbroker's and there I found five yards of my silk, and three pieces of lace; they denied every thing else. I went in a coach with Mrs. Fieldhouse to her sister's; there I found a gown and coat. The prisoner had told me that the person where she lodged had sold all the things to Mrs. Fieldhouse's sister.
 When was it the prisoner told you this? - The first time.
 Before you had made her any promise? - I made no promise till the next day, I went again. I went to Fieldhouse's sister on the Friday night. She keeps a pastry-cook's shop on Snow-hill; her name is  Jane Thompson. I found there a garnet colour sattin gown and coat, and a black cloak pulled all to pieces, and a necklace.
   JANE THOMPSON sworn.
 I have here some things I bought of Mrs. Clark, by the consent of the prisoner, who was present; she said they were her's; the prisoner lodged with her. She told me that they were her mother's and that she brought them from Ireland, that Mrs. Clark had fetched them out of pawn, and was to keep them till she could pay for them.
 Are these the things you bought of Clark by the prisoner's desire? - Yes, no more nor less.
 (They were produced in court and deposed to by the prosecutrix.)
   JOHN CRAWFORD sworn.
 I am servant to Mr. Windsor. I have about three yards and a half of silk and two remnants of lace; they were pledged at our house in the name of  Ann Fieldhouse. I never saw the prisoner.
 (They likewise were produced in court and deposed to by the prosecutrix.)
 - HUSSEY sworn.
 Mrs. Holbird went into the country, on the 9th of April, about nine o'clock; as I worked for her, she desired me to stay in the house with the prisoner till she came home; the prisoner went away, on Monday morning at eight o'clock; she told me she was going to a gentleman, expecting to get some money to carry her over to Ireland, she would come back before dinner, but she did not come back.
   ABRAHAM SOLOMONS sworn.
 I am a constable. On the 24th of May Mrs. Holbird brought me a warrant for  Ann Fieldhouse. I have here the things which were found in Ann Fieldhouse's house; here is a bag, some pawnbroker's duplicates, and a white cloak; the duplicates were brought to the Alderman; they were first pawned in the name of Fieldhouse, and then altered to the name of the prisoner; when she came before the alderman she brought the lace with her. (That likewise was produced and deposed to by the prosecutrix.)
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 On the Monday I went to Mrs. Clarke's. I was there three days; then I went to Mrs. Fieldhouse's, and she took and pawned the things; I gave her the laced handkerchief to pawn for seven shillings, and she would not own it afterwards. I have no witnesses; I rely upon the mercy of the court.
     GUILTY. Imprisoned 6 months.
 Tried by the First London Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
