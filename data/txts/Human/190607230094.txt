When goods are ordered which are not paid for at the time, the assis tant should make out a charge-up order and pass it on to me to charge up to the customer. I enter it in my day-book, and post it on to the petty ledger, or ledger, according to the amount purchased. The ledger accounts and the petty ledger accounts are treated in the same way. If the customer orders goods which are delivered and paid for on the same day it does not come through me. Where a charge-up order has been entered in our books we issue monthly statements, and an official receipt is given which is kept for that purpose. I have to take it from the cashier, who passes me her book the follow ing morning, and from that I mark off the various amounts. The cashier's book shows who has paid the money. As regards the list of amounts paid by Bullard, King and Co., to Darling on behalf of Horwood and Co., from October 13 to June 15, none of those amounts are in the ledger. The same remark applies to King and Foa. The British Dominions Insurance Company have not got a ledger account, and none of the items in the list of amounts paid by them to prisoner from January 30 to May 18 are in our accounts.
