 2985. MARY BLABER was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of Sept ., 2 water bottles, value 5 s  .; the goods of James Blenkinsopp , her master. JAMES BLENKINSOPP. I live in Christopher-street, Finsbury, and am as oil merchant. On the 10th of Aug., the prisoner came into my service—I missed two water bottles, a wine decanter, and a great many other things—these are my water bottles—( looking at them )—they have my mark on them. ELIZABETH IMPET. I bought these bottles of the prisoner, one a fort night after the other. JAMES BRANNAN( police-sergeant G  20.) I took the prisoner—she said she took the bottles, but she knew nothing about the spoon.
   Prisoner.  Mrs. Blenkinsopp gave the bottles to me—my master knew where to find me. Witness. Mrs. Blenkinsopp was examined at Worship-street on her oath, and she swore to the contrary before the prisoner.
   MR. BLENKINSOPP re-examined.  When she had been with me about a month she came home drunk—I went into the kitchen, and missed a great number of things, and on the 16th of Sept. I discharged her. GUILTY. Aged 43.— Confined Six Months .
 2986. MARGARET THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of Oct ., 2 shirts, value 5 s  .; and 1 gown, 5 s  .; the goods of Cuthbert Elsdon . JANE ELSDON. I am the wife of Cuthbert Elsdon, we live at Shadwell. On the 5th of Oct. I was absent about ten minutes, and missed from my back kitchen two shirts and a gown—these are them. JAMES MOODY. I am a pawnbroker. I took in these things of the pri soner and another woman—the prisoner had half-a-crown for the two shirts, and the other woman bad 2 s . for the gown. MART ANN GULLY. On the 5th of Oct. I saw the prisoner go into Mr. Elsdon's house—she had nothing with her, but when she came out she had something in her apron—in five minutes Mrs. Elsdon missed her things. JAMES ROOKER( police-constable K  243.) I took the prisoner from the description of Mrs. Gully and the pawnbroker—I took another woman first—I found the prisoner at the Thames Police Court, where she was charged with being drunk and disorderly.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did not you take me to a broker's shop, and did not the woman say I was not the person? A.  It was a second-hand shop—they said it was another woman who offered them for sale—I took you to the pawnbroker, and he identified you—Mrs. Gully identified you the moment she saw you. NOT GUILTY.
