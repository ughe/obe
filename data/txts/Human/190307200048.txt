governor"—I asked what it was, and he said that there was a man in Russia who had the position of overseer in the Siberian Mines, and as he col lected the gold from the prisoners each day he was able to take some for him-self, and that he had 70 lbs. of gold dust to sell, and proposed that my gover nor should be the buyer: that the man who used to buy it was dead, and they were looking for another—I said I should see my governor about it, and made an appointment for the next day—I then went and reported the matter to the police at Arbour Square—I met him the next day and told him that my master wanted to see a sample of the gold—he gave me a small nugget and told me the bulk was equal to it—I left him then, having arranged to let him know what my master would do in two or three days—I then gave the nugget to the police—a few days after I saw Soulman again, and told him that my master had tested the nugget and was satisfied with it and wanted to know all the particulars—he
