 Mr. ADOLPHUS. Q. What made him tell you so? A. I missed some boot-trees - he said he had lent them to one White.
   BENJAMIN HAYDON. I am servant to Mr. Alger's lodgers; I was in the cellar between eight and nine o'clock, sitting on a tub as it was warm weather; I went there to cool myself; I could see into Mr. Alger's cellar; I saw the prisoner come down a trap hole, drop on a bench, and take at lest six pairs of soles; he then cut a piece of leather off a large piece - he then threw them up the hole, and got up himself.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is the cellar under ground? A. Yes - it was dusk; I mentioned this about three weeks afterwards, when he was leaving, as he was suspected; I quarrelled with him once; I never said he only took two pairs - I said he put two pairs down his trousers at another time.
 Mr. ADOLPHUS. Q. Why did you not mention it before? A. I did not like - I had only been there three weeks.
   MARY ANN THOMPSON. I am related to Mr. Alger. I went to Vauxhall, and told the prisoner to be sure and not send the boy out for any thing, and particularly for pump water.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you ever send the prisoner into the cellar? A. Once - when my brother was out with the key - the prisoner said he could get down the hole, and did so.
   SAMUEL CLARK. When Mr. Alger went to Vauxhall, I was left in the shop - the prisoner, at half-past eight o'clock, sent me out for pump water; I returned in ten minutes, and he was at the cupboard where his coat was kept - he walked to the end of the room in a hurry; Miss Thompson had desired me not to leave the shop - the prisoner then sent me home.
   JAMES WHITE. I am a boot-closer, and live in Water-lane. I got a pair of boots, from Holton, by Alger's desire - I have them here.
   WILLIAM HENRY HOLTON. I am a poulterer, of Leadenhall-market. The prisoner made these boots for me two years ago.
   WILLIAM DRINKWATER. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner by Guy's Hospital; I got into his lodgings a few days before he was taken, but found nothing.
 Mr. ALGER. I had left a quantity of soles in the collar; I could not tell how many by fifty pairs.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1764.  CHARLES KNIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of John Todhunter , from his person.
 JOHN TODHUNTER. On the 5th of July, about half-past ten o'clock in the evening, I was walking with a friend in  Fleet-street, and felt a handkerchief drawn from my pocket- I turned round, and collared the prisoner with it in his hand.
   EDWARD KING. I was with the prosecutor, and saw the prisoner drop the handkerchief.
   JOHN CARVILL. I am an officer. I took him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to)
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 20.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Months.
 1765.  JOHN ROACH was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of John Denny Clunn , from his person.
 JOHN DENNY CLUNN. I was in  Duke-street, Smithfield, on the 14th of August, and was informed I had lost a handkerchief - the prisoner was pointed out; I secured him, but have not found the handkerchief; several persons were about.
   WILLIAM GROMET. I was in Duke-street on the 14th of August, and saw the prisoner and two others; the prisoner took up the tail of Mr. Clunn's coat, took out his handkerchief, and gave it to one who appeared like a sailor; he came through a public-house into Bartholomew-close; I pursued, but could not catch him; I was on the other side of the way.
   THOMAS BARKER. I am an officer. I was passing, and heard a scuffle - I turned, and saw a sailor going through the public-house - he got away; I returned, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going to see my mother at the Hospital, and was stopped.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1766.  JOSEPH TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of December , 26 yards of linen, value 3l. 7s., the goods of Richard Fawcett , his master, in his dwelling-house.
 RICHARD FAWCETT. On the 7th of December I missed some linen from my shop in  Cheapside- it was safe three days before.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. Was not the prisoner in your service till the 21st of August? A. Yes - he was then apprehended.
   EDWARD BEACHAM. I am a pawnbroker. I have a piece of linen, pawned on the 7th of December; I do not know who by.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is it not common for people to buy duplicates? A. Yes.
 The officer being absent, through indisposition, the case could not proceed.
     NOT GUILTY.
