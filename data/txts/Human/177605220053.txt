 Did the landlady know what you took him away for? - No; I did not tell any body in the house, for fear they should tell the son.
 Did she know you was an officer? - She understood that I had got him upon an arrest; she said, I hope the debt is not much? I made answer, it is not five pounds; I went back and staid till between seven and eight o'clock; then the son came home; he went a few steps up stairs, and seeing me coming after him, he turned back again; I turned him about, and shoved him up stairs before me; when I came to the door. he pulled the key out of his pocket himself; but, in his fright, and being rather in liquor, he did not open the door: at this time  Charles Jealous came to assist me, and Halliburton soon after; then I took the key out of his hand, and opened the door myself; we shoved him into the room: Jealous searched one side of him, and I the other; out of his right hand breeches pocket I took two ten pounds Bank post bills; then out of his waistcoat pocket I took eighteen guineas; Jealous took the money out of his other pocket, and the watch; then we searched the room; upon the table between the two windows I found some papers, and four bills of exchange, and in the window there lay some leaves of Mr. Mattison's bill book.
 Mr. MATTISON. These leaves were torn out of my bill book; the entries are my own hand-writing.
 MORANT. We found five pieces of sattin in the drawer, and two at the bed's head under the bolster; then we tied up all the things that we could find, that we thought belonged to Mr. Mattison, and brought the young man away: I forgot to tell you that I took the key of the street door where the lodging was out of the father's pocket.
 Did you observe either of their hands cut? - No.
   CHARLES JEALOUS sworn.
 I was at the lodgings at the time young Rowney came in; I found upon him this gold watch (producing it) I found 14 guineas and a half in gold, and in his pocket I found a watch-maker's bill, and a note for seventeen pounds; in a little box in the same room I found this remarkable piece (producing it).
 Mr. MATTISON. It is mine; it is a medal of king Charles on horseback.
 JEALOUS. I found seven pieces of silk and sattin; two of them were under the bed.
 Prisoner. I had eighteen guineas in my pocket when they took me.
   WILLIAM HALLIBURTON sworn.
 I was at the searching of the room; I found these tapes, these memorandum-books, &c.
 Mr. MATTISON. I believe these are my property; they have taken off the marks, so I cannot be positive to them.
   JOHN WIGHTWICK sworn.
 I am a watch-maker in Ludgate-street.
 Do you remember selling any watches to the prisoners? - Yes;  Thomas Rowney, the young man, had a repeating watch, the name Hughes, the other a gold horizontal watch, the name, Roger Haselwood, they were both second-hand watches; it was about nine o'clock in the morning; the name that he gave me was Thomas Rosewell; he paid me with the bill of exchange that I produced a little while ago; I gave him thirty-two pounds one shilling in change; I gave him two bank notes, one of twenty pounds, and the other of fifteen pounds; there was a man with him, but I don't know who that man was: he said he came to me because I had used him well in a watch he bought some time ago, that he had an order from a friend who desired him to buy two very good gold watches, one a gold repeating watch; he further desired that I would change them, in case his friend did not approve of them; when he had agreed so far, he said he had not ready money, he gave me that bill, and desired I would be satisfied that it was good before I took it; and when I was so satisfied, that I would wait upon him at Staple's Inn coffee-house at eleven as near as I could that day; he desired I would be particular to eleven o'clock, because he had other business to do; I took the bill to Sir William Leman's to enquire whether it was good, I shewed it to a gentleman who is one of the partners, and upon looking over it, I found it was accepted, payable at the Bank; he told me it was very good, but I was not satisfied,
