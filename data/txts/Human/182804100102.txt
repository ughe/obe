 MR. ALLEY conducted the prosecution.
 HENRY BROOKS. I live in the Strand, and am a  window glass cutter. On the 26th of January, I received an order to send these goods to Mr. Joseph Cooper, at Reading - there was some stained glass among it. I sent it on the 4th of February, by Ford, to Kinner's waggon.
   GEORGE FORD. I am Mr. Brooks' servant, and took the box on the 4th of February, I delivered it to Kinner's waggon.
 THOMAS KINNER. I am  part owner of the Reading waggon. I received this box, and loaded it in the hind part of the waggon; which I drove, and saw it safe at the further end of Brentford. I missed it when I arrived at Hounslow; the cords had been cut to get it.
   JOHN SELLS. I am a carpenter, and live at Kensington. The prisoner is a stable-keeper, and lived over Mr. Dyer's stables, at Kensington; on the morning of the 16th of February, (as near as I can judge) he came to me in the front of my house, and asked if I knew anybody in the building line, who wanted any window-glass. I said I had a house to glaze, and I would look at it - he said if I would call in about half an hour, he would shew it to me; that he had it to sell for a poor man who was in the Bench- he called in about half an hour and we went up to his room over the stable. I saw Matthews in the room, looking over the glass; I did not examine it, for I was afraid of breaking it; it looked very good. Matthews said, if he had all the money in the world, he would not give more than 6l. for it; the prisoner was in the room at that time, I think. I said nothing to him about the price - I never offered him any price; he never asked what I would give for it.
 Q. Did you ever propose any price to the prisoner? A. Never; till I left the money in the publican's hands; and said if he would take 5l. or five guineas for it, I would have it.
 Q. How came you to leave that message with him? A. I was obliged to go to town - I gave him four sovereigns, and asked him to lend me 25s. I got the glass out of the room on the Sunday morning afterwards; I carried it home, and had it glazed - I bought nothing but plain glass of him; the prisoner had mentioned no price in my hearing.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did not the prisoner tell you he had it from a person at Kensington, who had it from a man in the Bench? A. No; I had known the prisoner for about a fortnight before.
   JOHN MATTHEWS. I am a painter and glazier. On the 16th of February, the prisoner told me he had some glass to dispose of, for a person in trouble, and asked if I could get a customer, or buy it myself; he would let me have it reasonable - I went, and while I was measuring it, Sells came; I should call it second glass. I saw some orange coloured, and some ground glass there - there was about 120 square feet altogether; the largest square I saw was twenty-two inches by fifteen.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did the prisoner keep a liverystable, in Earl-street? A. Yes; I know one Cox - I do not know where he is, or whether he has absconded; the prisoner did not tell me it came from Cox.
   JAMES JONES. I am a publican. I paid the prisoner five guineas, which Sells left for him; he did not say it was for glass.
   JOSEPH DYER. I am a publican; the prisoner lived in some premises of mine - I took him a pot of hot one morning, and saw a quantity of glass of different sizes; I did not notice any coloured. I asked where he got it; he said he had it to sell for one Rodman, who had bought it at a sale, at Hammersmith and left it for him to take care of, as he could not take it home; and if he got a customer he might sell it for him - the prisoner rented a stable of me, and, I believe, deals in horses.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was the glass concealed? A. No; I am sure he mentioned Rodman's name.
   ROBERT DUKE. I am an officer, and apprehended the prisoner; I said I had come about some glass - he said he had none. I said "No; it was some you sold to Sells;" he said he had sold some to Sells - and that he had it from a person who was now in the Bench. I asked his name, he declined answering any more questions.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1006.  ANTHONY WEST& THOMAS SINGFIELD were indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 7 live tame rabbits, price 20s.; 3 live tame ducks, price 7s.; and 1 live tame drake, price 3s.; the property of AEneas Barkly.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   JAMES AMBROSE. I am a gardner to Mr. Eneas Barkly; he lives at Highbury Grove; he has a farm-yard, where he keeps rabbits and poultry. On Sunday afternoon, on the 24th of February, at half-past four o'clock, I saw the poultry all safe, and next morning, at a quarter-past six, I missed seven rabbits, three ducks, and a drake; I have seen two of the rabbits since in Cox's custody; they were taken from a butch in a shed, which was not locked; the ducks were all white and very large, the drake was the largest ever seen.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did your master have hand-bills printed describing them? A. Yes; I cannot read. I can swear to one rabbit by a slit in the ear; they had black spots on their faces and all over them.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. How long has your master had these rabbits? A. About two years; I attended to them.
   JOHN COX. I am gardener to Mr. Barkly. On the 24th of February (Sunday) these rabbits were stolen; I went to the pales about seven o'clock in the morning, and saw some footsteps; I noticed one particularly - there was one particular nail in the fore part of the heel-piece, and I traced the marks from the premises, over one field, to Hornsey-lane, about a mile towards Holloway; I do not know the prisoner - I have seen two rabbits here, which I am sure are master's.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is this one of the hand-bills? A. Yes? I believe the rabbits are not correctly described, as to the colour.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. Were you present when any shoes were fitted to the marks on the ground? A. Yes; they fitted exactly.
