   Cross-examined by MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  The prisoner had three weeks before his notice would expire? A.  Yes—I do not know that a former per son who had been in my employ had left through not being able to agree with the housekeeper—Mr. Norris was my foreman, and it was customary for per sons in my employ to have articles from the stock for their own use, with the foreman's permission, and to have the price deducted from their wages, when I settled with them—they have the goods at what we call cost price, from the unredeemed pledges, or any property I have on sale—a young man would have no business to take an article till he had had it of the foreman—if there had been a simple transaction of his taking one, and then saying," Put it down," I would have had no objection—there was 5 l . 15 s . due to the prisoner, I think—I was at Ramsgate five or six weeks this year—I left Mr. Norris in care of my business—he always had the care of it—I do not reside in the house myself—I do not know that Norris went to Gravesend as soon as was gone to Ramsgate—when I first charged the prisoner, he said, "All I owe for, Mr. Norris knows of," or words conveying that sense—while he was in my service, he used to go home to his father's, as I supposed—he used to go home every other Sunday, and he could go at other times if he wished—when the persons bring back the duplicates, we make a mark in the book, to show that the articles have been redeemed—that is done before they are filed—I knew these articles belonged to my sale—stock, by marks which are on them—on this watch here are two scratched marks of my own—I knew it to be the property of my stock—here are marks on the other articles.
