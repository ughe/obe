THOMAS GOLDSMITH. ( Policeman Y  140). On 11th May, about 2 o'clock in the morning, I was on duty near the Bald-faced Stag, at Finchley, and saw the prisoner going in the direction of the rick, and about 500 yards from it—about ten minutes afterwards he came running back—I stopped him—he said, "Do you see that light?"—I looked, and said, Yes—he said, "I have set that on fire"—I asked him what it was—he said, " A.  hayrick"—I took him into the station—I found on him two boxes of fusees, fourpenny, a knife, and a bit of copper wire—he said he set the stack on fire with the fusees—he gave no address.
   Prisoner. Q.  When you took me, or rather when I gave myself into custody to you, did I say that I set the rick on fire, or that I see it on fire? A.  You said you set it on fire—I will positively swear to that. WILLIAM WENHAM. ( Policeman Y  194). I passed close by the rick about 1.45 on this morning; it was then quite safe—I did not see anyone near it—about 2.10 I was about a mile away, I saw the light from the fire, and ran back and found the stack completely covered with flame.
   Prisoner.  I don't wish to say anything. GUILTY  .— Judgment respited. The Prosecutor recommended him to mercy, believing him not to be of sound mind.
