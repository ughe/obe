JONATHAN WHICHER, (police-sergeant, 27 A.) On Wednesday afternoon.
the 22nd of Sept., I saw the prisoners and another woman in Westminster.
road-Mason and the other woman went into Mr. Harvey's shop---they left
there, and went to Mr. Kingston's - Mason and the other woman went in-
the other prisoners remained outside, near the shop-door-I saw Mason or the
other woman, I am not certain which, but I believe Mason, come out and go
to Weaver, and, as it appeared to me, give ber something, which Weaver
put under her shawl-the woman then returned to the shop directly, and
Weaver and Brown went down Phænix-street, which adjoins Mr. Kingston's
shop-I told Langley what I had seen, went into the shop, and saw Mason
and the other woman sitting at the counter-I told the foreman I was an
officer, and I gave him instructions to retain the women, which it seems they
did not attend to-I then came out and joined Langdon-we went down
Phenix-street, and about 200 yards down, Brown and Weaver were standing
talking- I said to Weaver, “What have you got under your shawl?"-she
said, "Nothing"-I lifted up her shawl, and found three cards of lace under
her arm-I said, “What do you call these ?"-she said she picked them up
in the street-I took hold of Brown, and said he must go to the station-
Langley took hold of Weaver-I had seen the prisoners together about
twenty minutes before they went to the shop- they were all walking together
--at the time Mason or the other woman brought something out, and gave to
Weaver, Brown was close by, within two or three yards of the window-
it is a corner house-- he might be half a dozen yards from the door-I found
on Weaver a purse, nine shillings, and a penny, four or five pieces of ribbon,
two small bits of lace, and a pair of child's socks, all new-I went with
Langley about three hours afterwards to the Swan public-house in Bishops-
gate-street, saw Mason there, and told her I apprehended her for taking
some lace that afternoon in Westminster-road-she said I was mistaken, she
had not been in Westminster-road, nor on that side the water am certain
she is the person-I know her well--there was a new scarf found on Brown
- this is it.
Cross-examined by Mr. PARTINGBR. Q. When you took Brown, he said
he knew nothing of the robbery? A. Yes; he said before the Magistrate
that he accidentally met Weaver.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Where were you standing! 4. I
passed them first on the same side of the way, then went over the way to
watch them-there is a pawnbroker's shop at the opposite corner to Mr.
Kingston's-Brown and the woman not in custody live as man and wife.
