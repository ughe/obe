Fifth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq.
96. MARY ANN M'HUGH was indicted for stealing 1 looking-glass,
value 108. ; 3 blankets, 9s.; 1 poker, 18.; 1 pair of tongs, 6d. ; 1 shovel,
6d. ; 4 sheets, 6s. 6d. ; 4 pillow-cases, 2s. ; 3 towels, 18. ; 2 flat irons, 18.;
1 bolster, 58.; 2 pillows, 68.; and 10lbs. weight of feathers, 12s.; the goods
of William Thompson ; and that she had been before convicted of felony.
WILLIAM THOMPSON, cabinet-maker, Circus-street, Marylebone. The pri-
soner lodged in my house for six weeks-on the 27th of October I went into
the room, and missed a swing looking-glass, which was let to her with the
room-I asked where it was-she said the plate was broken, and it was gone
to have a new plate--I also missed some sheets, blankets, and the bed-tick
was cut, and the feathers gone-I shut the prisoner in the room, and went for
an officer-these are the things-they are mine-this is the glass--the plate
is not broken.
Thomas Ayres, apprentice to Mr. Gideon, pawnbroker, Stockton-street,
Lisson-grove. I took this swing glass in pledge of the prisoner-I can swear
to her-I have a blanket and two pillows, one of them pawned by the prisoner
- I cannot swear who pawned the others.
smo
JOAN GRANGER (police-sergeant D 21.) On the morning of the 28th of
Oct. I was fetched by the prosecutor from the station-house-I saw the pri-
soner there I had some conversation with her; in consequence of which, she
produced these eleven duplicates.
THOMAS Ayres re-examined. Here are the fellow-duplicates to the pro-
perty which I produce.
ISAAC PLATTs, pawnbroker, Upper York-street, Bryanston-square. I pro-
duce two sheets and a set of fire-irons-I do not know who pawned them-I
gave two of the duplicates produced for them.
GEORGE THORNTON (police-constable D 109.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction, from Mr. Clark's office-(read)-the prisoner is
the person, and she had been convicted before that.

GUILTY. Aged 19.-Transported for Seven Years.
97. JOHN CASEY was indicted for stealing 9 cheroots, walue 10d., the
goods of Thomas Wilson; and that he had been before convicted of felony
MATTAEW HESWELL. I am in the service of Thomas Wilson, tobacconist,
in Brook-street. On the 30th of Oct. I was in the parlour adjoining the shop,
about a quarter to nine o'clock at night, I saw the prisoner come in, and,
before I could get into the shop, he had the cigar-box lid off, and took these
cigars out--I spoke to him-he made no reply, but knelt down and begged
pardon, and said he would not do it again.
STEPHEN TAYLOR (police-constable S 11.) I went to the shop, and took
the prisoner-I saw the cheroots under his feet-he was crying, and begged
to be let off.
THOMAS DOWNING (police-constable E 47.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction, from Mr. Clark's office-(read)--the prisoner is
the person.
