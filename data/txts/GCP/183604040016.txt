880. RICHARD ABSOLAM, WILLIAM ABSOLAM, and WIL-
LIAM BISHOP were indicted for feloniously assaulting Joseph Prior
on the 20th of March, at St. Luke, Middlesex, putting him in fear, and
taking from his person, and against his will, 1 bag, value id.; 4 half-crowni,
9 shillings, and I sixpence; the goods and monies of the said Joseph Prior.
Josepu Prior. I am a carman, and live at No. 21, Hatfield-street.
Goswell-street. On the 20th of March, I was at the Benbow public-
house, in Golden-lane-when I went in, it was near upon twenty minutes
after twelve o'clock at night I called for a pint of beer-it was served,
and I paid for it-I drank it at the side of the counter, by the barthe
prisoners William and Richard Absolam stood at the side of the bar toge-
ther--they were drinking-William Absolam bad a pint pot in his hand, and
asked me to drink -- I told him no, I had got beer of my own--he asked
if I would let him drink out of my pot-I told him no, and told him to
keep his own company, and I would keep my own-he asked me if he
might drink out of my pot-I did not choose to make acquaintance in
that way-no sooner had I spoken, than William pushed me down, and
fell on the top of me-he put his knees into my belly, and the other,
which was Richard, fell on the top of me as well-I had not spoker to
him--one of them kicked me in the side, which it was I can't say--and
then they ran one of their hands into my left-hand breeches pocket-I cried,
"Murder," and a police officer came up and took them off me-- he took
William and Richard off me- I did not see Bishop while this was going
on-as soon as I got up, I told them I was robbed of my money, 195. 6d.,
which was four half-crowns, nine shillings, and one sixpence- I missed my
money-it had been in a little fustian purse, in my left-hand breeches pocket
-no money was found there there was the same pocket as the hand had been
introduced into-I had my money in my hand, and my hand in my pocket,
before I paid for the beer-I paid three-halfpence out of it for my beer-
the Absolams saw me pay for the beer-they stood before me at the time-
I had received 188. 10d. that night from my master, Henry Dodd-I had
18. in silver before I received that I had laid no more out-only three-
halfpence--my master gives me a ticket every night for a pot of beer, and
I only laid out the three-halfpence-I was quite sober--I had not done
work till nearly half-past nine o'clock, and I went over opposite master's
yard, and had the pot of beer, and stopped there till nearly ten minutos
before twelve o'clock-two of us drank three pints of beer-that included
the three-halfpenny-worth-I dare say I drank half the three pints, but
I did not drink half the pint which I paid for before I was pushed down
- when I paid for the three-halfpenny-worth of beer my money was safe
there was another person pressing upon me, when I was pushed down,
but I can't swear to more than two-- there was more than two or three
persons had me down.
