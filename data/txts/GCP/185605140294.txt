Cross-examined. Have you any doubt ? A. Well, I do not feel to
have any doubt I have no doubt but what he is the man I saw him on
three occasions at the house in Oakley-crescent; the first time for five
or six minutes, the second time for about the same, and the third not so
long—he had a hat on and he had spectacles on, white ones once he had
not got them on, but he put them on when I went into the room-he was
a minute or two without them; he went to the window, took them out,
and put them on-I solemnly believe him to be the man—I went with
Forrester to the House of Detention for the purpose of identifying him
Mr. Mullens was not with me-Forrester did not give me any clue or hint
-the gaolers told me not to stop at the cells, but to look and pass on-
there was only one person in each oell___the gaoler did not say when I had
passed the last cell but one, “You have passed him"-he did not say so
till I had passed them all—I said there was no one I could identify, with
the exception of the last man, and he was something like the man-I do not
believe the gaoler said, “You have passed him," before I said that after
we had passed them all, I believe he said, “That is all, have you seen him?"
-he said “You have passed him," when I came to the end-it was then
that I said “The last man was something like him”—I said if Iwas compelled
to swear to the man, I would not say it was him, by the time I had seen
him-on leaving the prison, I believe I told Forrester I had seen them all,
but there was but one there that was like him I do not recollect saying,
“My impression is I have not seen the man"-I will not swear I did not
-when I saw him at Marlborough-street he was in the dock-I had then
heard of his being committed on another charge. I then said he was the
man-I have never mentioned before to-day that the man I saw at Oakley-
crescent had spectacles on-I told Mr. Mullens so early in the case I did
not say so at the Mansion-house - I did not repass the cells at the House of
Detention, after having once looked into them.
MR. SLEIGH. Q. About what is the size of these cells? A. About as
far as from me to the door—they are light-each prisoner got up as the
gaoler opened the door and said, “Come to the front"-they faced me-
their back was towards the window of the cell - none of them were brought
out of the cells--I recognised him directly at Marlborough-street- I had
then been told that there was a man brought up charged with forgery-I
was told that there were several, and that I should have a chance of seeing
them-I was told that I should see a man who had been identified on
another charge-before I looked into the cells of the House of Detention I
had not been told that there was a man there like the description I had
given-I was asked by Mr. Mullens to go and see some prisoners I do not
positively know whether or not he said there was some one like the man.
