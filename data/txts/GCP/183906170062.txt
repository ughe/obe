GEGRGE SEAL. I am a coal-merchant, and live in Blackfriars-road. I
bought a horse of Mr. Miller, on the 24th of May, for sixteen guineas,
which I paid him.
Cross-examined. Q. You live very near Miller ? A. About three or
four hundred yards off-1 frequently buy at his auctions, if any thing suits
me-I am a sheriff's-officer, and a farmer as well.

and Seal delivered me the horse the same day-Biggs saw the same pro-

FREDERICK WESTON. I keep the Pier Steam-mill at Deptford. The
prisoner has no residence at that mill-1 live at No. 18, Union-street-I
never authorised him to give my address as his-I advertised for grinding,
and the prisoner answered my advertisement-1 stated in my advertise
ment that any one who wished to grind at my mill could reside at my
house, if they wished, as I wanted to let part of it--the prisoner never
came with corn to grind-he said he expected to send some down.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he call on you? A. I was to see him at the
Castle I saw him about six months ago, or more-the first time, I dare
say, was five years ago I told him, when I saw him about this, that be
could have part of my house, if he wished it, and he expressed a desire to
do 80-he asked if he might have any letters addressed to my bouse, and
I said yes, I have seen him within the last six months about the cort-
market, apparently engaged in business-he did not tell me he had any
place of business in Mark-lane-if anyone had asked me, in April and
May, if I knew him, I should have said I did, but not as regards business
-my mill is sometimes called Pier Mill, as it is near the pier.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Did you give the prisoner authority to have a card
engraved with your address on it? A. No-I think there is another
No. 18, Union-street, besides mine.
NOT GUILTY.
