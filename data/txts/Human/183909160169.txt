MR. PAYNE . called WILLIAM WOOOFORD. I am a carpenter, and live in Alstead-place Wyndham-road, Camberwell. I know the witness Brown—I hare never had any quarrel with him—I would not believe him on his oath.
   COURT . Q.  Have you ever heard him give evidence in a court of jus tice? A.  No—I have known him state what was contrary to the truth with reference to many things, stating one thing at one time, and contra dicting it at another time—I have always known him to be an indifferent character—I never paid any respect to what he said—I believe he was in the habit of erroneously talking in most of his cases, bragging about acts which he has been guilty of perpetrating—he affects to be a tailor—I put a garment into his hands to make for a boy of mine, and he never executed it, nor ever paid me—I have heard him say he had stolen dogs, or obtained them in some improper way—the prisoner has worked for Mr. Deacon, of Nonfood, since he was tried before—he worked for me a long time—Brown owes me 5 s ., and his father 7 s .—he never acts honestly to wards me.. WILLIAM BULLARD. I know Brown—he told me, about eighteen months ago, that he had been tried at Horsemonger-lane, that he stood there, I think, six hours, against a lady he used to work for at Kennington oval, whom he robbed of a watch, and that he pleaded his own cause—he said he was put into the dock, and tried for felony—he mentioned the lady's name, but I forget it—he was tried and acquitted, according to his asser tion—he said this in Mr. Banbery's public-house, and also in the George public-house parlour—there were many persons present, and they said he was great ass to expose himself—I thought he was in earnest. HENRY BROWN. re-examined.  Bullard says he has known me eighteen months, I can prove he has known me for years—they cannot bring a re spectable person to give me an ill name—I defy any one to prove a charge against me—Woodford gave me some doth to make a suit of clothes—I found no prospect of getting the money, and gave him back the cloth with out making it—I owe him nothing. NOT GUILTY.
