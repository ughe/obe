1107. JAMES WATSON was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , one butt rope, value 14 s., the goods of Henry Meux, Thomas Stirling Benson, Florance Thomas Young, and Richard Latham.
   JAMES PEACH. I am a drayman at the Horse-shoe Brew-house, St. Giles's. On the 6th of September our drays stood by Monmouth-court. Brooks came to me. I found the prisoner in Bowl-yard, with our butt-rope. My masters' names are, Henry Meux, Thomas Sterling Benson, Florance Thomas Young, and Richard Latham.
   THOMAS BROOKS. I saw the prisoner come from the dray with the rope on his shoulder.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 52. Confined Six Months.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Reynolds.
 1108.  BENJAMIN WHITTEY and WILLIAM LILLEY were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of July , one coat, value 30 s., the goods of Daniel Bristol.
   DANIEL BRISTOL. On the 4th of July I left my coat in the hall, at No. 19, Holles-street, at twelve o'clock, at noon, and at six the officer brought it.
   JAMES TURNER. I am an officer. I saw the prisoners going along Gray's Inn-lane with a basket, which had willow shavings on the top, they shifted it from one to the other. I took them into custody. I took the basket from Lilley, and found the coat under the shavings. Whittey ran away. Cadby brought him back.
   JOSEPH CADBY. I was with Turner, followed Whittey, and brought him back.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 WHITTEY -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 LILLEY -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1109.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 3d. of July, one watch, value 3 l.; one seal, value 15 s., and one key, value 1 d., the goods of Edward Fricker , from his person.
   EDWARD FRICKER. I am a tailor, and live at Bexley, in Kent. On the 3d of July I was with some friends in Portman-street at half-past three o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner and several more stood at the curb. I was stopped by the crowd, and turned to go back. Then the prisoner and two or three more, closed upon us, and forced us back into the crowd. I said to my friend,
 "Stand back a few minutes, and we shall be able to get back." The prisoner came before us, and in an instant put his hand down. I felt my watch move from the bottom of my fob, and saw him take it out. I immediately seized him, and he gave it to one of the others. They tried to rescue him, but I kept him, and Gall took him.
   THOMAS GALL. I am a constable. The prosecutor gave the prisoner into my charge.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was near him, and he collared me. I declare I am innocent.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Life.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1110.  ELIZA MOSS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , one trunk, value 6 s., the goods of James Wyer.
   GEORGE WELLS. I am servant to Mr. James Wyer, a trunk-maker, who lives in Oxford-street. On the 24th of July, about half-past seven o'clock in the evening, the trunk was outside of the door. I heard it move; ran to the door; a woman met me, and said the thief had ran down Berwick-street. I followed, and overtook the prisoner at the corner of Noel-street.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. A woman asked me to carry it to Compton-street.
     GUILTY. Aged 48. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1111.  ROLAND TYLER was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of September , one seal, value 7 s., the goods of Lambert Gauron.
   JOSEPH BOWDEN. I am servant to Mr. Lambert Gauron, who lives in Drury-lane. On the 2d of September, about one o'clock, the prisoner and another man came into the shop, he went outside the window, and pointed out two seals, which he wanted to see. I shewed him one, and said it was 5 s. - he returned it. I gave him the other into his hand, and asked 10 s. for it - he told me to brush another seal up. I went to get the leather and brush, and saw him put his hand behind him, and the other man put his hand into the prisoner's hand - he had not delivered me the other seal; my master came into the shop, then the prisoner's companion asked him what a dozen seals would be - he went out immediately - the prisoner then said,
 "What can you do these seals at a dozen?" My master told him, and, I believe, he grumbled. I went to get another seal, and recollected what I had seen, and told my master, that he had not returned the seal I shewed him; he stopped the prisoner - he said he had given me the seal, which was false - the officer took him. I should know the other man again. I am sure they were companions.
   LAMBERT GAURON. I came in while the prisoner and another man were in the shop, his companion said he had come about a man who was in trouble, and asked what I could make seals at per dozen? I said 3 l. 16 s.; he said very well, he would call again on Monday, and immediately left the shop. The prisoner looked at a seal, and asked me the lowest price - I said 5 s. I asked the boy to reach a seal from the window, at 10 s. 6 d., he said he had shewn the prisoner that seal, and told me what he had seen. The prisoner said if I would let him go, he would tell me where the man was who had got the seal.
   CHRISTOPHER JACKSON. I am a constable. I took the prisoner in charge. He used great force to get away as I took him to Bow-street, and looked behind, as if he expected a companion.
 Prisoner's Defence. The boy has been taken up several times for robbing his master.
   LAMBERT GAURAN. I never charged him with robbing me.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
