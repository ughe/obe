Green Dock, Rotherhithe on the 8th of March, I shipped the prisoner
on the 3rd, at the wharf-I left him in care of the ship on the 4th of
March-I returned on the 8th to get my clothes, and missed my quadrant
-I taxed the prisoner with it-he said he knew nothing about it-T over-

police, and on the 9th, I went on board again the prisoner was then gone
-I found him on the 15th-he had my waistcoat and shirt on, which
I had missed-these now produced are mine-some have my name on
them in full--they were all in the ship when I left him in charge - I have
not found the quadrant.

1411. JOHN COLEMAN and WILLIAM WATSON were indicted
for stealing, on the 1st of April, 20lbs, weight of lead, value 5s., the goods
of George Bainbridge, being fixed to a certain building.–2nd Count,
not stating it to be fixed.
COLEMAN pleaded GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined Three Months.
Joux STROTHER. I am foreman to George Bainbridge, a timber-mer-
chant, at Rotherhithe-he has an engine-house-on the morning of the
Ist of April, I observed the lead was stripped off the hips of the building,
and some from the sky-light-I went to a police-station, and got some
officers-between our own and another yard we found this lead concealed
-it is Mr. Bainbridge's-1 set two watchmen to watch.
EZEKIEL HENRY GILBERT. I watched the Ten foot way, where the
lead was--I saw the two prisoners coming down a street in company--they
went up the Ten foot way direct to the spot where the lead was placed-I
saw them look round to see if the coast was clear-Coleman went round
the corner of the Ten foot way on to the stone wharf-I went a little farther
up the street to see if I could see him remove the lead, and the next I saw of
him was with the lead placed under his arm-Watson was then standing in
the Ten foot way, about six or eight feet from the lead, apparently on the
watch-I called for the key of the stone-wharf, and on approaching Cole-
man I saw him throw the lead down, close to a shed under some paling-I
took Coleman, and gave him in charge of a policeman, went back to the
spot where I saw him throw the lead, and there I found it-when Watson
was taken he said he knew nothing about it.
GEORGE REDGROVE. I am a policeman. I watched, and saw Watson
at five o'clock in the evening in the Ten foot way-I saw Coleman on the
premises where the lead was flung-as soon as Gilbert told me had taken
Coleman I took Watson-I did not see Coleman with the lead.
