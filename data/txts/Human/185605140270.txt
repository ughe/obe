   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.  Porter to Mr. Blackwell, I stand outside—I am a barker. HUGH ANDREWS. ( policeman, H  62). On Wednesday, the same day that I was before the Magistrate, I saw the prisoners together in George-street, Spitalfields—I took Weaver, and told him that I wanted him for a watch which was stolen at Shoreditch—he said that he knew nothing of it—I had seen the prisoners and another party about 9 o'clock on the night of the robbery standing about thirty yards from where it took place, in High-street—I had a good view of them—I might be three or four yards from them—I knew them before, and have no doubt about them—Weaver is one of the three.
   Cross-examined. Q.  While the examination was going on, did you hear Weaver say, "I am innocent, Clouse done  it"? A.  Yes.
   COURT . to GEORGE WOOLLARD CLARE . Q.  At the time this rushing took place you did not see Weaver? A.  Yes, at the moment the watch was taken—he was at the back of Clouse, close by, but I could not see whether he struck Sharwell—he was not near enough to touch him, he was about a yard from him—I am quite sure of Weaver. JOSEPH DUBBLE. ( policeman, H  195). I was with Andrews on Wed nesday in George-street, and saw the prisoners—as soon as Clouse saw me he ran—I ran past Weaver, telling Andrews to take him—I took Clouse, took him to the station, and charged him with stealing the watch—he looked at Weaver, and said, "We know nothing about it"—after a witness came to identify him, he said, "I did do it, but Weaver had nothing to do with it"—I know them, they are always together.
   WEAVER  — GUILTY.**† Aged 20.— Confined Twelve Months .
 533. ELIZA DAVIS , stealing 1 watch, value 6 l  .; the property of Samuel Hassan , from his person.
   MR. LAWRENCE . conducted the Prosecution. SAMUEL HASSAN. I live at No. 4, Worship-street. On 15th April I was going from St Paul's down to Farringdon-street, about 10 minutes past 9 o'clock—the prisoner spoke to me, and I walked with her a short distance—my watch was then safe in my pocket—we turned up Wheatsheaf-yard,. she remained talking to me there two or three minutes—a man came up, and she ran away—the man tried to hold me, and began to ask me about some chambers—my suspicions were aroused, I felt for my watch, and found that it was gone—I followed the prisoner, and saw her running on the other side of Farringdon-street—I ran across, took her by the arm, and accused her of taking my watch, and she put it into my hand, but said nothing—a policeman came, and took her—I found the handle of my watch on the ground in the yard—the chain was left behind—the watch is worth 6 l .
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How long were you with her? A.  Five minutes, but not two minutes up Wheatsheaf-yard.
   MR. LAWRENCE . Q.  You did not give it to her? A.  No—she is one of my people ( a Jewess ), but I did not notice that at the time—I do not know whether she is a foreigner; she speaks English like a native—I am from Gibraltar. WILLIAM ROWLAND.
