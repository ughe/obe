237. SAMUEL PATRICK BRYANT (30), and PETER SULLIVAN
(29), Stealing 2 dead hares, and part of a dead fish, the property of William
Loveland.
MR. STRAIGHT conducted the Prosecution.
ROBERT WEBB (Policeman, P 6). On 20th January, about a quarter past
6 in the evening, I was on duty in Sydenham, and saw the prisoners in front
of me--they both looked back, and then stopped and put something through
a hedge into a gentleman's garden-28 1 went past, Bryant said, “Oh, he is
all right, he is nobody"-I was in plain clothes--they walked half-a-dozen
yards, and then stopped-I crossed over to the other side, and saw them go
back to the spot, and search about in the hedge, apparently looking for
what they had put away-Bryant went to the Greyhound public house, and
I saw him have some beer, and get some lucifers, he then went back to
Sullivan, struck the lucifers just inside the hedge, they searched for a
quarter of an hour, and Bryant then pulled two bares out of the garden
they walked sharply up Laura-park-I followed them till I met two other
constables--I then stopped the prisoners, and asked Bryant how he became
possessed of the hareshe said, "I bought them " I said, “Where be
snid, “I shall not tell you on the way to the station he said that he had
sold half-a-dozen hares, and had those two left-he produced 58. 7d. at the
station, and the sergeant said that it was very little money if he had sold
half-a-dozen bares -- he said, “We are obliged to spend a great deal, and we
want a little beer "-he then said that he had sold no bares, but half-
dozen rabbits, and that he bought the hares in Billingsgate-market that
morning, for bs. 9d. of a Mr. Davies-I asked Sullivan when he saw Bryant
- he said at 4 o'clock in the afternoon-I asked him if had the hares then
he said, “No," I found on Bryant two herrings, which the prosecutor said
were bought at his shop.
Bryant. Q. Where were you when you saw me put the hares into the
hedge ? A. Just by the cab-rank, between thirty and forty yards from
you-I saw you take two hares out-you went across to a butcher's and
Sullivan waited outside that was before you went to the Greyhound-I
saw you in there by looking through the glass door-there is a flick in the
hedge, where you pulled the hares through.
Sullivan, Q. Have you known me to be a hard-working man? A. Yes
-I have known you eleven years, but I do not know much of you for the
last three years, as I have been away.
JURY. Q. Were they both together when the bares were put into the
hedge? A. Yes, and they both stooped together—it is about forty yards
from Mr. Loveland's shop, to the hedge where the hares were put.
