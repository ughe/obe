Yes - I did not ask her to go with me; but she was going the same road, and I went across the fields in my road home - when I missed my half-sovereign, I told her if she would give me my half-sovereign, I would say no more about it - I had not been at work that day; I had been to Blackfriars - the prisoner was alone with me when I missed my money, but the woman she said was her sister was a few yards from us; the prisoner took off her pattens before I came up to her, and after she left me she went up to the other woman and gave her her pattens - it was dark; when we walked down the Bird-cage-walk; I felt something at my pocket which I did not take notice of; I had my umbrella under my arm, and the hand was withdrawn in a moment from my pocket - I had not time to put my hand to it - I don't know what she did with the half-sovereign unless she swallowed it - I had had a little rum and shrub with them before I went into the Bird-cage-walk; I did not ask them to go and have it - they asked me to treat them; I paid for that out of my waistcoat pocket - I kept my hand on the pocket where my half-sovereign was while I was at the ballad-singers; I told her it was a nasty song, and she said she never heard such an one.
 COURT. Q. Might not this have dropped out of your pocket when you were standing together? A. We did not stand.
   CHARLES GRANT(police-constable H 25). The prisoner was given into my custody; I found nothing on her; her companion came down to the station-house, but nothing was found on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. This man came up to me and my sister as we were going along, he asked us to have some rum and shrub, and then he asked me to go across the fields with him, which I refused; we went down Bird-cage-walk to get from him, but he followed me; he wanted me to go down a court, which I refused; he then said I had robbed him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 153.  WILLIAM PHILLIPS and WILLIAM HATTON were indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of November , 1 coat, value 18s., the goods of John Wright. Hatton pleaded.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
   JOHN WYLES. I live with Mr. Smith, of Rufford's, buildings, Islington. On the 2nd of November I saw Hatton minding a cart and horse, and Phillips stood against a wall; I saw Phillips go up to the cart, take a coat out, and run down George-yard, and Hatton after him: I went up to the cart and waited till the prosecutor came; I told him of it.
   JOHN WRIGHT. I live in Bowling-green-lane. I left my cart and horse in care of Hatton - when I returned my coat was gone; Wyles told me of it - I went down George-yard, but could not see the prisoner; Wyles afterwards pointed Phillips out and he was taken.
 Phillips. Wyles has been tried himself.
 JOHN WYLES. Twelve months ago, I was destitute of a home, I went out thieving, and stole beef and bread.
 Hatton. He buys anything; he bought that cap he has in his hand of a boy.
   JOHN WYLES. I bought this for 1s. 6d. of a boy, but I don't buy stolen things; I have been in the House of Correction about three times.
 COURT. Q. What were you there for? A. Once was for a pair of boots.
 Hatton. He was there once for stealing a till with two more boys; one of them was the boy he bought that cap of, and he is in this goal now.
 PHILLIPS -    NOT GUILTY.
 152.  JAMES FORD was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of October , 1 watch, value 30s.; 1 guard-chain, value 9s., and 1 watch-key, value 3d., the goods of Simon Goodman Jones.
   SIMON GOODMAN JONES. I live shopman at No. 155, Tottenham-court-road. On getting up on Saturday, the 19th of October, I left my watch in my bed-room; at seven o'clock in the morning I saw it on the hook where it usually hung; I desired a servant to put it into a drawer, which she did - at four o'clock in the afternoon I went to inquire about it and it was gone; the prisoner was porter in the house; he was taken, and my guard and key, and the duplicate were found on him.
   SARAH PULLEN. I brought down the watch, and put it into the drawer, at ten o'clock - I looked for it at four o'clock and it was gone - there was no one but the prisoner in the kitchen when I put it into the drawer.
   ALEXANDER BROWN(police-constable E 67). I took the prisoner - I found this guard chain in his pocket, and the key in this bag, on his person - this duplicate was by his side - he said, he had taken the watch.
   WILLIAM ALFRED PUTNAM. I am a pawnbroker - the prisoner pawned the watch with me.
 Prisoner's Defence. A man named Taplin was in the habit of calling there for the shopmen's linen; and on that day he called for 17s. 6d. due to him, from Mr. Ware, but he found he had been discharged. He then met me at the gateway, and asked me to pawn his watch for him, as he had not received his money - and said, he would give me the guard and key, if I would pawn the watch; and he would see me again in the evening, and receive the money, and the duplicate. I never saw the watch till Taplin gave it to me, and the witness cannot deny that he had been in the kitchen, where she says she had placed the watch.
