Robbery with violence, together with other persons unknown, on William
Rissler, and stealing a cigar-holder.
MR. W. J. ABRAMS Prosecuted.
WILLIAM RISSLER. I live at 11, Lintoll Street, Bermondsey-on 10th
July, about half-past one a.m., I was walking at the corner of Tower Hill,
quite sober, with a young woman-the prisoners and two others came
and stood in front of us, and spoke to my young woman, who answered
them-I said, “What do these men want?" and I was struck at the back
of the neck and thrown down, a hand was held over my mouth, and my
pockets were rifled, and a meerschaum cigar-holder was taken out of my
waistcoat pocket--the man that held me got away; I took this scarf
from his neck-they broke away, and were brought back by & constable
-I am sure the prisoners are two of the men; I lost sight of the man
that held me, but not of the prisoners.
Cross-examined by Franklin. Both of you were at my trousers pookets
and side pockets, all at the same time.
Cross-scamined by Griffithe. You were leaning over me; when I sang
out you both ran at the same time.
LOUISA NEWMAN. I was with Rissler on this morning-I do not
recognise the prisoners.
EDWIN WHITE (Detective City). About half-past one a.m. on 10th July
I s&w the prisoners in the company of two other men in the Minories,
going towards Tower Hill: on Tower Hill they spoke to Rissler and
Newman, whom I then saw on the ground I ran up-when the
prosecutor got up the two men not in custody got hold of his shoulders
from the back, while the prisoners felt in his pockets---some men passing
in & cart stopped and shouted "Police !" and the prisoners and the other
two men ran away-I followed them to Savage Gardens, when the two
prisoners ran into Crutched Friars, one turning to the left and the other
to the right-they were stopped by constables--I never lost sight of
them.
JOSEPH COOPER (762 City). About twenty minutes to two a.m. on
10th July I was in Crutched Friars- I saw the two prisoners run out of
Savage Gardens into Crutched Friars-& whistle was blown, and Stop
them !" was called out-I stopped Griffiths, who said nothing when
charged.
WILLIAM OODEN (755 City). About twenty minutes to two on this
morning I was in Orutched Friars; I heard a whistle blow and saw the
prisoners running, and I caught Franklia-he said nothing-at the
station when charged he said he was innocent.
Franklin, in his defence, said that he spoke to Norman, and that these two
men came up and knocked the prosecutor down, and that he stood looking :
and when the constable came and the men run he ran too. Guttry.
Franklin PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of felony in October, 1891.-
