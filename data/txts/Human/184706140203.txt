 1531. SARAH RYAN was indicted for stealing 1 basket, value 1 s  .; 1 lock, 3 d  .; 1 pair of boots, 5 s  .; 2 handkerchiefs, 1 s  .; 2 aprons, 1 s  .; 1 petti coat, 1 s  .; 2 pairs of stockings, 8 d  .; and 5 3/4 d  . in copper; the goods and monies of Honora Collins . HONORA COLLINS. I am a market-garden woman, and live at Mortlake. On the morning of the 24th of May, I left my boots, my handkerchief, and other things, in the room where I lodged—I had locked them up in a basket—I afterwards met the policeman with the basket and all the things in it—this is it—the things are in it now. CATHERINE LEE. The prisoner and Collins lodged with me. On this morn ing the prisoner went out, and I followed her—she had this basket locked up, and her own bundle too—the basket was not broken open.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I had no work, and I took the basket for a bit of bread; she turned me out, and then she said I was taking away the basket. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months .
 1532. JOHN JENNER was indicted for stealing an order for the payment of 197 l  . 10 s  .; the property of Edward Ransby . EDWARD RANSBY. I reside at Bawsey, in Suffolk. On the 26th of April, I came to town to receive money on my father's account—I received a check for 197 l . 10 s ., payable at Messrs. Prescott's—this is the check—I had about 18 s . or 19 s . in my pocket besides—I went on that day, about twelve or one o'clock, to the Yorkshire Stingo beer-house in the Blackfriar's-road—I saw the prisoner in front of the bar—he was attending apparently as master—I had a pint of beer—while I was drinking it, a party challenged the prisoner to play at skittles for a sovereign—they went out to play—after they were gone a little while, the prisoner came and invited me to go down in the yard at the back of the house, to see them play—I went and saw them—the land lord won the game—I heard him ask the man for the sovereign—the man said he had left it with his wife in the bar—after that the prisoner asked me to play a game or two of skittles for a pint of porter—I played a game or two—I lost every game—I then played with another man and lost—Leary ( See page  414) was there, but I never saw him play—I played for half-an-hour or three-quarters—before we had finished, several of the men went away—I staid in the skittle-ground—one of them came down and invited me to go up to hear a song—I went up, and after the song, they began to toss for stout and for money—I tossed for stout first, but ultimately for money, with three other men, not the prisoner—when there was a small game I generally won, but when there was a large sum I generally lost—that soon disposed of my 18 s . or 19 s .—I was then going—the prisoner came in and said, "Do not go, you will win your money back"—I said I had got nothing but a check—he
