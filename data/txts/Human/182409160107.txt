I never wore any diamonds since Mr. Duland was here; I would not dress myself like a lady to be in the showroom; Miss Hudson saw some of my jewellery in my drawer; I may have taken her to show it her, but I do not recollect. I had a coral neck-lace, but I never said or swore that it was one of the things that was taken from me. I have said, that Bertrand had some other Billet de Banque and some gold. I live now in Berwick-street, at Mrs. Broadfoot's house. I never told Mrs. Broadfoot that I was married to Bertrand; but she is a woman to swear that which is untrue, because she is paid for it: I know it, a man told me so; I do not know his name, but it was the man that arrested us for the lodging; it was a distress, and my attorney has been kind enough to pay, because Mr. Askew has taken my all. The day Mr. Askew was taken to the Office, Mrs. Broadfoot's young boy was there, and said all manner of things. The man said that he saw another man who came to Mrs. Broadfoot's house, and spoke to Mrs. Broadfoot, and she said she would swear anything against me. Mrs. Broadfoot's son said he got 5 s. and his mother 5 s. and their victuals, and a coach to fetch them, and so they would swear any thing, and that is the truth. Mrs. Broadfoot never asked for her rent until Mr. Askew was arrested; that was in the presence of Bertrand; it is since Mr. Askew went to Newgate. I have not told her upon any occasion that I had 15 l. in my drawer at Askews, and with that I would pay her; but when we came from Cheltenham, Mr. Bertrand said, we have no money, but if she would be kind enough to let us into her house, we would pay her in November, or when we got our furniture and things from Mr. Askew's. When Mr. Askew had refused to deliver my things, I have said
 "That is 15 l. out of my pocket;" because Mr. Askew had not delivered to me my furniture, which was worth 15 l.; I paid for it, and have got my receipt. I have said to Mrs. Broadfoot
 "I will pay you with 65 l. which is in the hands of a man at Cheltenham." I had a letter from Mr. Askew, communicating the contents of this French letter, and I was afraid of it; it was not a long time before he was arrested; it was before I sent Mr. Hubert to demand my goods, but after I applied to Mr. Abrahams. The night that I came from the Office, I saw Mrs. Broadfoot at her house; it was on the 15th of September Mr. Askew was committed; I saw her every night. I never said,
 "I will have my revenge; I will hang him," though he has robbed me, it is not my wish to hang him. I never said to her,
 "Askew has gone to Newgate; I will have my revenge! I will have his life!" Mrs. Broadfoot did not say,
 "Stop, stop, not so fast! he is not tried yet." I know Mr. Unit, but I never told him that Bertrand had brought over 2,000 francs only, and had changed them at a shop in Regent-street; but I told Unit, to prove that Bertrand had brought some money with him, that I myself had changed 200 francs for him in St. James's-street. Bertrand never told him that he had brought over 2,000 francs only; but he told him, in my presence, about the 200 francs. I did not tell Unit that the produce of these 200 or 2,000 francs was locked up in my drawer at Mr. Askew's, nor did Bertrand; but I said I had exchanged 200 francs about three weeks ago. Mr. Askew said, his reason for forbidding Bertrand his house was, because Bertrand was my friend, and it was on account of jealousy. I had none of the jewellery at Cheltenham.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You said to Mr. Adolphus that you had lost 15 l. through not having the furniture; is this the receipt for it? - (Looking at it) Yes. I told Mrs. Broadfoot, after I had a letter from Mr. Askew, that I would pay her in November, with the 65 l. in the Sheriff's hands at Cheltenham. The letter from Mr. Askew is not signed, but his own servant brought it; here is a translation of the French letter. I went to Cheltenham in July; Mr. Askew and I had quarrelled. Bertrand was then receiving things from Mr. Askew; he was still trying, notwithstanding the quarrel, to get  Bertrand  a situation. In July, when I went to Cheltenham, I had but one key to my drawers, and I took it with me. I am sure I left the bills and jewellery in the drawers. When I was with Bertrand on Saturday night I did not know I was to go so early as six; Askew never told me the hour. Mr. Abraham never got me any thing, and then the French Consul recommended me to Mr. Hubert, and I employed him. Some of the jewellery was not in the inventory in the drawer; it was only of the things I brought with me to London. I heard the Russian gentleman called His Excellence, and that is why I called him a Nobleman. When I was travelling in the coach with Askew, he was very kind and attentive to me, and I objected to it, because, I said,
 "You are married, and I am engaged to Mr. Bertrand." I used to write to Bertrand very often, and Mr. Askew said,
 "You must call him your brother." I said,
 "He is not;" and Mr. Askew said,
 "Never mind, you must call him so." Miss Dulan came with Mr. Askew; I did not desire it. I don't know whether she was examined on my part or not.
 A. WATTS. I  drew  this agreement (looking at it), by the mutual instructions of the prisoner and Miss Perrot; Bertrand was there but gave no instructions. It was left in the possession of Miss Perrot; there was a copy of it, which Mr. Askew had. I don't know whether Miss Perrot had it then; for while it was in our custody, a message came from Mr. Askew, not to deliver it. That was about two days after we had it. It was five days or a week before Miss Perrot got it: she had called twice in that time. I mean positively to swear that there was a copy of it, and Mr. Askew had one part of it at the time of the execution, and I think he put it in his pocket. I am not certain whether I took it away with me, or it was given to us afterwards by some other person, one of the parties. I do not know how many times Miss Perrot and Bertrand called for it before they got it. I will swear they did not use threats to get it; there were two parties to it. Mr. Askew had one, and the other party one; it has not been altered in any way since I wrote it.
   VICTOR BERTRAND. I came to England in the end of April this year. I know Miss Perrot. Three or four days after I came to England, I put into her hands seven billets de banque, of 1000 francs each, and five of 500 francs each, to take care of. I have never got them back.
