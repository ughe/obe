GEORGE GARDINER was indicted for stealing 1 box, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 8 sovereigns; 1 half-sovereign; 24 half-crowns; 110 shillings; and 60 six pences, the property of Martin Ohler in his dwelling-house. MARGARET OHLER. Iam the wife of Martin Ohler, a baker. On the 11th of Nov. I left our cash-box standing on the chimney-piece in my sitting-room—it contained about 20 l .;—there were eight sovereigns, a half-sovereign, about twenty half-crowns, some shillings, and sixpences in it—Iam sure it was safe about five o'clock, when Iwent into the shop—while Iwas engaged, the prisoner walked in through the shop, through the sitting-room, to the bake-house—his father was in our employ—Iobserved nothing in his hand when he went in, but when he came out Isaw that he had something in his hand be hind him—he looked me sharp in the face, and kept his hand behind him under his coat—thinking it was a loaf, and knowing he was a poor fellow, a long time out of work, Isaid nothing—about half-a-minute after he was gone Imissed the cash-box—no one was there but the prisoner and two lodgers, who came down through the sitting-room, from the time of seeing the cash-box safe, and missing it—it has not been found.
   Prisoner.  Ihad nothing in my hand—Itook in some yeast—the lodgers came through to shake their carpets at the same time. MARY ANN TAPLIN. Ilodge in the prosecutor's house. On the 11th of Nov., about five o'clock, Ipassed through the sitting-room—Isaw the prose cutrix in the shop, and saw a man standing by the fire-place in the sitting-room in a stooping position—Icould not swear it was the prisoner, as Idid not look pointedly at him—neither Ior my sister touched the box—we did not see it—we did not stay in the room—we only passed through to shake a carpet. LUCY TAPLIN. Ilodge in the prosecutor's house—Ipassed through the sitting-room with my sister on this evening—Idid not touch the box—Isaw the prisoner standing in the room close by the fire-place in a stooping posi tion—we went through the bakehouse into the yard, returned in about five minutes, and, as Iwas going up-stairs, the loss of the box was discovered—no one had been there that Iknow of besides the prisoner. PHCEBE WRIOHT. Ilive opposite the prosecutor's. On the 11th of Nov., about five o'clock, Iwas at my bed-room window, and saw the prisoner come out of Mr. Ohler's shop with something under his arm wrapped in something—it was about the size of half a quartern loaf—Icannot say what it was—Isaw his features—he stopped to speak to his father who was at the barrow and went on—he had on the cap now produced—Icannot speak to any other part of his dress.
