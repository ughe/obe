HENRY JOHN CRAWFORD. I am clerk to Mr. Wontner, the solicitor.
I subpoenaed Mr. Lewis, the attorney for the prisoner, on Friday night
it was a subpæna duces tecum—the original subpæna is at the office, I
believe-(the witness was desired to fetch it)- I now produce the original
writ, a copy of which I served on Mr. Lewis-I served it on bim person-
ally, at his place of residence in Grosvenor-street, Bond-street--I showed
him the original subpæna.

CHARLES LEWIS was called on his subpoena, and did not appear.
MR. JOHNSON re-examined. Q. Did you, at any time, see in the hands
of Mr. Lewis an IOU for 17891. 108. ? A. Yes, I did, on the 28th
of Feb. last, when I served him with a summons for a bill of particulars
-that was at the Albany, Piccadilly, where his offices were at that time
-I was referred to him by his clerk, and took the summons up-at the
same time I observed that it was a ridiculous idea for him to commence
an action against Mr. Rogers at the suit of Hunt, for the family of the
Hunts owed Mr. Rogers some thousands, to my knowledge-he said it
was nonsense, and opened a drawer, from which he took an IO U for
17391. 10s., dated 6th Oct., 1840, which I told him was not Mr. Rogers's
signature - I was in attendance at the Bankruptey Court on the 6th of
Oct., 1840, with Mr. Cranch-I saw Mr. Rogers there he was with me
about nine o'clock in the morning-we got there about eleven, and were
there the whole day, till between seven and eight-I have known the pri-
soner five or six years-he was generally working for his father--he
appeared to be in very bad circumstances generally. b a
