COURT Q.  You have observed these omnibuses, and the "Em peror" and "Albion" constantly; upon your oath, can you state that the "Em peror" and "Albion" have been impeding Bray's, or did Bray's annoy them? A.  I have seen the "Emperor" oppose Bray's omnibus—that is what I swear to—I could not swear to the driver of the "Emperor"—I put down the number—It is 352.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  When did you make any communication to Mr. Norton? A.  I do not know him—I was supcenaed to attend to give evi dence—I never gave evidence but in this Court—I have talked of it to my neighbours—I saw Bray last night—I never saw him before—I did not speak to him.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  What is the number of the "Albion?" A.  177—I marked it on the back of my subpœna. RICHARD TEMPLE. I am a brazier and oilman, and live in Prince's-street, Cavendish-square. I know Mr. Bray's omnibus—I have seen it when one of Bolton's omnibuses has been before it, and one of Bardell's along with it—I spoke to young Bray—he nodded to me—he then turned round and went back again, up Oxford-street, and Bolton's followed him—I told the coachman, who happened to be young Mr. Bray, that what I had seen I should be most happy to say in any Court afterwards—I have seen Bolton's following Mr. Bray's about—I saw it last Saturday. JOHN RAWLINGS. I am a linen-draper, and live at No. 133, Oxford-street. I have noticed Bray's omnibus, in their way from Paddington, along Oxford-street, for some months—I have seen it almost daily—I have noticed the conduct of other omnibuses towards it, the "Emperor" for one—they were chasing Mr. Bray's; one was before and one behind—I cannot recollect what was the name of the other omnibus—the one before ob structed him in his buisness, and the one behind interfered with the pass engers who wanted to get in—I have seen that frequently—they pulled the people away frequently—their pole was nearly close to the door, so as to prevent any person getting in—I cannot say that I have noticed any other omnibus interfere in this way but the "Emperor" and one more—Bray's omnibus was obstructed in its free course by them—I do not recollect that I have seen Bray's act improperly to them, but I have seen the opposition strong against Bray.
   COURT Q.  Did Bray's ever act so as to oppose them? A.  I never noticed that. WILLIAM THOMPSON DICKENSON. I am a chemist and druggist, and live at No. 147, oxford-street. I know Mr. Bray's omnibuses—I have observed them for about two or three months—I have observed two other omnibuses, with the name of "J. Bolton" on them—Chapel-place is a place where they generally stop, and if Bray's omnibus has stopped there to take passengers, or any passenger attempted to get in, the others drove in such a manner as to prevent their getting in—I have observed that fre quently with those omnibuses—I cannot speak as to others—I have seen Mr. Bolton's more particularly.
