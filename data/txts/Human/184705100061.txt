RALPH F ROLLS( police-constable G  248.) I took the prisoner—he denied having the pin—between four and five o'clock the next morning I found the gold part of the pin in the gutter, partly buried in mud—I saw the proseeutor find the stone of the pin within a foot of the spot where I found the stem of it—that was in the direct road from the prosecutor's house to where the pri soner was taken to.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Hour long have you been on that beat? A.  I am not on that beat, I am on the opposite side of the street, and have been for eighteen months—I never saw the prosecutor before—I know the Hog in Armour—I never saw or heard of any boys and girls going there to dance—I have heard singing as have passed by when on duty in the street—I did not know the prosecutor at the Hog in Armour—I never noticed his name being up at the Nag's Head—I cannot recollect the name of the policeman who is on the beat where the Nag's Head is—I found this gold part of the pin a few yards from the Nag's Head. NOT GUILTY.
