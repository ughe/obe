JOHN MORRIS, In March, 1856, I was in the employment of Mr. Isen
berg, as foreman of the shoe department On 27th March I saw some
cases packed-there is an entry in the book, made by me at the times it is
an entry of six cases, containing children's and women's boots, about 500
pairs I made a memorandum of the marks and numbers of the cases--they
were "G. O. C. and Co.," and 1,084 to 1,093-I find those marks on this
Case, and the No. 1,090-it is on the same part of the cases as those I saw
packed.

Goodman's Fields, at the corner of Rupert Street And Lambert Street

Christopher Court the young woman who was then living with me went
with me; also Maybury, Collins, George Boyce, and a man named
M'Carthy--they were altogether at the public house when we got to &
house in the court, the cabman went on first, and the young woman and I
followed, and we just got there in time to see two bags of boots put into
the cab by M'Carthy and Collins; Maybury was then up the court the
bags were brought out of a house about the centre of the court, but I do
not know the number I did not go into the house when I first went to
the house M.Carthy introduced me to Maybury and Collins, and Asid that
it was all right, and I knew all about it, and I was to take them away:
Collins held out his hand, and said, “If you give me the money you can
have them"_that was the money for the boots and shoes what I have
told you was said in the presence of Maybury, the boots were mentioned
