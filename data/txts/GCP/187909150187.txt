LYDIA DRIVER. I am single, and live at 9, Windmill Street-on Sun-
day, 3rd August, at 10.30, I was standing with my mother and sister in the
street: the deceased came down and I went and got some beer for her. I
gave it to her and she went upstairs with it she had been drinking, but
she could walk straight-soon after she went up I was standing under the
window of her room, which is the first floor front and about 8 feet above
my head, and leard the prisoner say to her, I will put your light out before
the night is over-I went to get my supper and heard no more, nor did I
hear any one go upstairs, but I saw the deaf and dumb boy come in at'a
quarter to 11; I did not see George that night.

night, 3rd August, I was standing outside the door with my mother and
sister and heard the prisoner and his wife quarrelling upstairs; he said, “I

JAMES WALSH (1 209). On 3rd August, after midnight, George Pilfold
and his dumb brother came to me together-George spoke to me and I
went with them to 9, Windmill Street, to a room on the first floor, where
found the prisoner partly undressed and apparently asleep; a woman was
lying on her back on the part of the bed next to the wall, her clothes were
raised up to her knees--I saw she was dead-I saw some blood on the
hearth, and sent 40 L, who was with me, for an inspector and a doctor-the
prisoner lay 'still asleep, and in a short time another constable came, bringing
the dumb son, who had left the room, and who woke his father up and
pointed to the dead body of the woman---the prisoner said “What is the
use of taking notice of that drunken cow, she has been drunk like
that for the last month, and a good job if she is dead; who the
- hell fetched you two great fellows to me in my own room
I suppose it was that lazy young swine, George; how is it that he has not
gone to work if you don't let me out I will jump through the window"
-- he went towards the window and partially raised it up, but was prevented
by another constable--the inspector then came, and Mr. Wilson, the doctor
-I found this pearl-handled knife under the dead woman's body when
Louisa Marks was taking off the clothes for the doctor to examine-I also
found some halfpence and a farthing on the bed and took them-nobody
had interfered with the bed till the inspector came the woman was on the
hed, as if she had been put there she was on her back, and her legs were
stretched out straight.
