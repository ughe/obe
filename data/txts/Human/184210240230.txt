   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you take her to several chemists' shops in the neighbourhood? A.  I did, among others to Mr. Jones—the cup, or mug, in which the rhubarb was said to have been fetched, was taken with her—Mr. Jones could not call her to mind at the time, but when I produced the cup he recollected that, and he then asked her what quantity she had—she said, "Three-penny worth"—he said, "How far would the three-penny worth come up to in the cup?"—she then pointed to a certain mark, and Mr. Jones immediately told the assistant to put so many grains of water in, and it corresponded exactly with what she said—this was on the 27th, I think, the Monday week after his death—I went to the house from time to time for the whole three weeks the Coroner's jury were sitting—I always found her at home, and most willing to accompany me wherever I suggested—on the same day that I took her to Jones I took ber to Hornblower's—I had been there before that day, and had seen the wit ness Page—I asked him if he thought he should be able to recognize her again—be said he thought he should—I intimated to him that I would bring her—I cannot positively say that I told him I would bring hw—I did not say any thing about when I would come—from the conversa tion he of course would expect me to bring her—she had a cotton gown on, and the bonnet that she said she was out with when she went to get the tincture of rhubarb on the Friday—she herself told me how she bad been dressed on the Friday—she took off her widow's cap and dress and put thw one on—a man named Lowring accompanied us to Mr. Page—she wai the only female—I remember Page saying, when he saw her, "I think I hive seen you before"—it was the first thing he said—I do not remember bw saying any thing about being taken rather at a nonplus  —I then asked him whether that was the party that had applied to him for arsenic, and be said he had no doubt that she was—when I first went in, he said, "I have seen you before"—I will not be positive whether he said, "I think," or not—
