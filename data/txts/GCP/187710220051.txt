RICHARD BROWN. I live at 3, Devonport Street, Commercial Road,
and am a stevedore on 18th October I was playing at billiards with Mr.
Arnett, and saw the prisoner come in-he wanted to play Mr. Arnett for
half a sovereign, but Mr. Arnett would not the prisoner took up a cue
and said “This is the way to hit the balls!”-Mr. Arnett took a cue and
did not hit the balls the marker said "I cannot have this nonsense,”
and took the cue away-the prisoner went up to a young man and said
"I demand my half-sovereign I deposited with you " he said “You
deposited no half-sovereign”--the prisoner got into a fighting attitude
the marker got between them, and the prisoner was put out-I was after-
wards told that he had a pistol in his possession-I went down, saw a
little scuffle in the passage, and the prisoner was shoved out on to the
pavement—he got up, put his right hand behind him, and drew a revolver
and pointed it at Mr. Levy-I caught him by the hand, and took hold of
the revolver, and the nipple came down on my hand; here is the wound
it made, so that it must have been at full cock-he was given in castody
-I had not seen him give any money to any one.
Cross-examined. I said before the Lord Mayor “There was a tussle,
and the hammer came down ”-there was little tussle in getting it away
-I do not think I could have cocked it in that way-you had a pistol
pocket, made for the purpose of carrying a revolver.
WILLIAM COOPER. I rent the billiard-room at the Woolpack-on 18th
December the prisoner came in about 3 o'clock-four gentlemen were
playing at billiards, and he challenged the worst player of the four to
play for a half-sovereign, but playing for money is not allowed-he went
down and came up with his coat off, and began knocking the balls about
-I picked up the balls as he was talking a lot of nonsense, and said
"This won't suit me; you had better go somewhere else and play"-he
said to a gentleman there “I will take my stake money”-he said “What
do you mean?” and the prisoner squared up at him-I went between
them-he tried to seize me by the throat, but I was too quick for him,
and seized him-I asked a gentleman to ring for the potman, who came
up, and I said “Show that gentleman outside"-I did not see the prisoner
produce any money.
ALFRED BLACKMAN. I am potman and waiter at the Woolpack-I went
upstairs when the bell rang, and they had got the prisoner outside the
door-he asked me the marker's name I said "I will tell you downstairs.”
--he went down and asked for the bos, meaning the landlord—I said
"He is upstairs lying down”-he placed his left hand on my shoulder and
