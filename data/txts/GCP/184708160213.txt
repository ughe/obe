1942. ELIZABETH SMITH was indicted for a misdemeanour.
FREDERICK WILLIAM HAMMOND. I am the son of Francis Manesty
Hammond, of the Churchill-arms, Kensington. On the 18th of August, at
half-past six o'clock, the prisoner came and left a bottle, and said she would
call for it in a few minutes-she came again, and wanted it filled with vinegar,
and wanted a bottle of the best rum, for Mr. Smith, of Bedford-place-I know
him, he deals with us regularly-I gave the prisoner the rum and vinegar,
believing she came from Dr. Smith-she went away I watched her up
Bedford-place-she did not go to Dr. Smith's, but turned into the New-road
I stopped her, and told her that was not the way to Dr. Smith's-she said
she was going to call as she came back-she put the rum and vinegar on the
path, and said if I did not believe ber I might take them myself, and was
going to walk away-I said she should not go, unless she went with me
this is the rum and vinegar- produced.)
Thomas BROWNING ŠMITH. I live in Bedford-place, Kensington Gravel-
pits, and deal with Mr. Hammond, of the Churchill-arms. I never sent
the prisoner there for any rum or vinegar-she had never been in my service
-I had never seen ber before.
SAMUEL PUDDEFOOT policeman.) I was present when the prisoner was
taken, and received the rum and vinegar--the prisoner said she was sorry
for what she had done; she knew it was quite wrong.
Guilty. Aged 27.-Confined One Month.
