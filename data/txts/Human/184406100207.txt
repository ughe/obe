 1787. PHILIP CHILD was indicted for stealing 4 sovereigns, 8 half-sovereigns, 3 half-crowns, 12 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of Charles lon.
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES ION. I am a milkman, and live in John-street, Vassal-road, Brixton. The prisoner married my sister—he was out of work some time ago, and I allowed him, his wife, and children to remain in my house for about six weeks or two months, without paying any rent until he should be enabled to get work—I used to keep my money in a little box, locked up in a chest under our bed—I saw it about nine o'clock in the evening of the 14th, and there was then 8 l . in sovereigns and half-sovereigns, and 1 l . in silver—I had put it there to pay my rent with—on the 16th, I went out about half-past four to my work—I came home about eight to breakfast, about twelve to dinner, and when I came home at night the prisoner was gone—he had given me no intimation of his leaving—he did not come home that night—I sus pected something might be wrong, and my wife missed the money—I directly went to the station, and gave information of the robbery—on the 2nd of May, I received a letter of which I took no notice—a friend came to me last Saturday week, and in consequence of what he said, I met the prisoner that evening at Kennington church, and gave him into custody—the person who came to me was with him.
   Cross-examined by MB. PAYNE . Q.  What is that person's name? A.  I do not know—I took a policeman with me—I never made any effort to find the prisoner—he has gone away when out of work on former occasions—I cannot say where to—he might have gone into the country in search of work—I have got a trifling subscription among my friends to repay the money—I had no conversation with the prisoner when I met him—I gave him into cus tody directly—I had made no arrangement with the person who came to me—I merely agreed to go to the place. MARY ION. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw the money safe on the 15th of Jan., at two o'clock, when I went to the box and took out 2 s .—I left the 8 l . in gold, and 1 l . in silver—locked the box up safe, and put it under the bed down stairs—I went out, leaving the prisoner and his wife in the house—I returned at half-past five in the evening, and he was gone—his wife and children were there—I did not see him again till he was in custody—next day I went to look for my money, and it was gone—the box was locked, and in the same place I had left it—the key was in my pocket—a false key must have been used—it is a common lock.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you quite sure the prisoner was not at home that night? A.  I am positive—I went into the room at half-past five o'clock when I came home, and many times after, but did not see him—their room was next to mine in which the money was—there were lodgers in the house, but they did not come home till between nine and ten—they do not have to pass our room—it is not far from the stairs.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Did either of the lodgers run away next day? A. NOT GUILTY.
