1335. THOMAS FLETCHER and JOSEPH HAMSON, stealing
1 jacket, 1 pocket-book, and 1 piece of paper, value lös. 1d.; the goods of
John Howes : Hamson having been before convicted.
JOHN Howes. On 22nd June I left my cart in Upper Thames-street,
with my boy, while I went to sign for some goods I had been loading, and
when I returned I missed my jacket from off the cart-it had a pocket-book
in it, containing an order for fifty dozen of wine--it belonged to Messrs.
Pickford and Co., but was in my care—the pocket-book produced is mine,
and bas my name on it.
William KENCHAT (policeman, G 199). I took Fletcher into custody
on another charge, and found on him this pocket-book and two papers.
Samuel MABERLY. On 22nd June I saw Fletcher buy this pocket-
book of Hamson-he gave him ls, for it-I knew them both before.
Fletcher. Q. Was I not at work all that day with you? A. Yes, and
you bought the book about nine o'clock in the evening.
Fletcher's Defence. I bought the paper that was in the book for 18.; it
had on it that half-a-crown reward would be given to anybody bringing it to
the place mentioned on it, and he gave me the book into the bargain.
JAMES GRIMBLEY (policeman, N 90). I produce a certificate--(read-
Joseph Matthews, Convicted April, 1848, and confined three months)- I wa
present-Hamson is the person so found guilty.
