sixpences, and a fourpenny-piece, one penny-piece and three halfpence--he
said he got it by strapping horses on the Derby-day at Epsom, and he
gave silver for the sovereign at a booth at Epsom-I asked him where the
booth was-he said he did not know what booth it was, nor where it was
-Clarke claimed the money he had the prosecutrix showed me her
stocking-I have it here-it appears to have been either torn or cut just
above where her boot would come, on the right leg-she told me before
any thing was found, that she had lost two sovereigns and 198. in her
purse, and fourpence halfpenny and one shilling out of her pocket-I asked
Clarke if he knew Jackson-he said he had known him for years by being
about the Marsh-gate.

WILLIAM FORD. I am a hackney-coach driver. On the day in ques-
tion I drove a glass-coach for Mr. Ward, my master-Clarke merely had a
ride with me down to Epsom he is a servant of Mr. Ward's coming
back, one was a job-horse-when we got to Tooting we gave two gentle-
men a ride, but the horse gibbed, and we stopped at the Castle at Tooting,
and the gentlemen got out-the party we took down to Epsom had come
back in another conveyance-I saw the prosecutrix about a hundred yards
from the Castle at Tooting-we were on the off-side of the road, trying to
get the horse on-she came up and said, “If you will let me get on the
box, I will get them along"-she said, “Will you give me a ride to
town? I have been to Epsom and have lost all my money, and lost my
friends"--I said, "I can't give you a ride to town, I can't give ourselves
one, I am going to walk" -I meant I could not get the horses along--I had
two children with me, one my master's and one belonging to a person
named Bonner-one of the ladies who went down with me was to ride back
in a gig, and I was to take the two children back-the prosecutrix at last
got into the coach-Jackson was at that time standing about there, and he
agreed to help us back-he was not in the coach-we got the horses on about
one hundred yards, and then they stopped again-I said, "I will take the
two children and walk, we cannot get them on" -- there were two policemen
there, also a man who said he was a knacker, and a man in a blue jacket; who
