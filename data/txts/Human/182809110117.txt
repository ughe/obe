 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 BENJAMIN CARTER. I  keep the George inn, at  Silso, in Bedfordshire. On the 19th of June. I had a bay gelding pony; at nine o'clock that evening I gave it to the boy to take to the paddock near my house; I did not see it in the paddock; I saw the boy take it, and he brought the halter back; there is a gate and a lock to the paddock; I made some inquiry for the pony, and a little boy said he could not find it; in consequence of some communication, I came to Hampstead on the following Monday, and found my pony in possession of John Hinton, who keeps ponies and gigs; I have got it since - it is not worth more than 5l. in the market.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is the boy here? A. No, I could have had him here; the paddock may be about one hundred yards from my house.
 COURT. Q. What age is your poney? A. Upwards of twenty years, but he was a great favorite with my children.
   JOHN CULLEY. I am ostler at the Holland Arms, Kensington. On Friday, the 20th of June, I saw the prisoner there a little after eleven o'clock in the morning; I was out exercising a horse, and met him going up towards my stable - he had a little bay gelding, which he put into the stable, and in a little while after a man named Wilkinson came by with some potatoes; the prisoner brought the poney out, shewed it to Wilkinson, and rode it up and down Back-lane, and then put him in again; soon afterwards, the prisoner went after Wilkinson; when I went out, about twelve o'clock, I saw him and Wilkinson together; I heard Wilkinson say "I have bought this poney of Collins for 4l. 10s.;" the prisoner made no answer; they all went away together about two o'clock.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did he go away with the poney open in the public road? A. Yes, there was no concealment of any kind.

 1751.  JAMES COLLINS was again indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , at St. Mary Abbott's, Kensington, 1 gelding, price 20l, the property of George George.
 GEORGE GEORGE. I live at  Eden-bridge. Kent; I lost a horse on the night of the 28th of June - I had seen it in the close that day as late as nine o'clock in the evening- I missed it the following morning between six and seven; it had been in my yard, adjoining my house; I found it again on the 15th of August, at Kensington, in possession of William Bennett, the constable - I know it to be mine - it was worth upwards of 20l.; I have got it again - I had bred him; my farm is about thirty miles from Kensington.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you any other Christian name? A. No - it was about six weeks before I saw it again; I keep a farm-yard, and have many servants; I retired to my house on the 28th of June - I left my servants about - I did not go out myself; I do not know who was last in the yard that night - two men and a boy were in my house; I cannot tell what time they went to bed that night - whether any of them might have left the gate open I cannot tell - my servants are in health, but are not here.
 COURT. Q. What is the fastening to your farm-yard? A. A common hasp to the gate - it was fastened when I was in the yard, but I cannot say of my own knowledge whether it was open or shut when I went to bed - I was up an hour or more after I saw it, but did not go out again.
   WILLIAM BENNETT. I am a constable of Kensington. I had occasion to go to the Holland Arms public-house, on Sunday, the 29th of June, about eleven o'clock in the morning; I found the prisoner there, and took him into custody- I told him it was on suspicion of stealing a horse from Bedfordshire, and I asked if he did not sell a horse ten days back to Mr. Wilkinson; he said Yes; I then asked where he got that horse. which was then in the stable; he made no answer; I took him and the horse - the ostler told me, in the presence of the prisoner, that he had come there with this horse that morning - it was the ostler who had fetched me- I took him into the public-house, and searched him; I kept the horse in my possession till the 15th of August, when I found who it belonged to; when Mr. George appeared at the office, the horse was given up to him by the Magistrate's order.
 Cross-examined. Q. When had you this conversation with the hostler? A. On the 29th of June; the hostler said, "This is the horse he sent here by a little chimneysweep boy, and he gave him a penny for it;" I knew the prisoner before, and he knew me perfectly well as a neighbour - I suppose he knew me to be an officer; I leave no doubt of it - he worked for Mr. Cobbet, in his garden.
   JOHN CULLEY. I am ostler at the Holland Arms, Kensington; I know the prisoner. On Sunday, the 29th of June, he sent this horse to our house by a little boy; I did not think it was to come to our stable - I thought the little boy had made a mistake. and I hitched the horse on the outside; in about a quarter of an hour the prisoner came into the stable - I went up with him, and unlocked the stable door, and he brought the horse into the stable - this was before he said anything to me about the horse; the boy was then gone - I asked him if it was his horse; he said Yes; the horse was then put into the stable, and I went to the public-house and fetched a pint of half-andhalf, I called Richard Toy. our watchman, out, and told him to go for the officer - the officer came, and took the prisoner: I do not know where the prisoner lives - he had been at our house before; the last time I had seen him before the 29th was on the 20th.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you been acquainted with the prisoner before? A. Yes - I had known him better than two years.
 COURT. Q. What is he? A. When I first knew him he was at home with his father, and sold fruit in Covent-garden; I do not know where he has lived for the last two years.
 Prisoner's Defence. When I was going up towards their house on the 20th; Culley was drinking with some men, and he asked me if I would toss with him for some beer - I said No, I did not want any such thing; I was not inclined to toss for it - he then ordered me to take the horse up into the stable: then he asked if I would give 2l. for it, and then Wilkinson came up.
 COURT. You are now tried about George's horse.
