COURT . Q.  Do you know what trowsers he had on? A.  I do not, but I believe they were those he has now—they were lightish, but are now, from wear, rather brown—he had on the same dress he has now—I saw him at my house about eight o'clock—he was in the kitchen with the servant—he has known the servant ever since he was a child—I have to pass the kitchen frequently, and the door is generally open—I saw him there two or three times within half-an-hour—I think when I first saw him it was a quarter or twenty minutes before eight o'clock—he was in the habit of coming to my house of an evening when he had permission to leave the vessel—I recollect the day the prisoner was at my house, from the circumstance of the accusation, and then I recollected it was on the Saturday, from a conversation that took place in my coffee-room on the same evening, which caused me to come out to ask a question in my bar, and then I saw the prisoner, and I think that was before eight o'clock—none of my servants are here. JAMES CANNON. I am a labourer to Mr. Watts, the high-constable. I have known the prisoner about two years—he was always a very good character, and very honest—I remember seeing him on Saturday evening, the 16th of Nov., about half-past seven o'clock—he had a pair of canvas trowsers, painted brown, and a blue frock.
   COURT . Q.  Where did you see him? A.  At the Coach and Horses, and from there he went to Mr. Hodson's—we had a couple of pots of beer together, and the prosecutor too—he had not left him then—we came back again to the Coach and Horses—we met Stevens there, and then we all went together to Whale's, and had some drink. ELLEN WHALES. I live right opposite the Ship and Half Moon, at Woolwich; I am married. The prisoner has been with me, off and on, for about five years—he has been honest—he was at my house that Saturday evening about a quarter after nine o'clock—he had lightish trowsers on, and a blue frock, tucked inside his trowsers, the very same as he has on now. NOT GUILTY.
