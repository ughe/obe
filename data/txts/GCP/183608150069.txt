JAMES HOLLIS. I live with my mother, in Rochester-Tow.On Mon-
day morning I went out at seven of clock, and I came home at eleven o'clock
at night-I missed three coats and a waistcoat, and a pair of my mother's
boots-next day I saw the three prisoners together, just by Westininster-
bridge, going into a public-house, about a quarter past twelve o'clock at night
-I turned back, and saw a policeman just by Astley's theatre, and he
took them up.
JAMES Watson. I am servant to Mr. Lloyd, a pawnbroker in Strutton-
ground, Westminster. I have a pair of woman's boots which were pawned
by Richard Hollis, to the best of my belief, on the 27th of June, in the
middle of the day, in the name of Richard Hollis, for his mother lent
him 2s. 6d. on them.
JOHN WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Great Smith-street,
I have two coats, which were pawned for 88, by Cain, on the 27th of June
in the name of John Cain, No. 2, Union-place, for John Taylor.
Cain. When I pawned the things he asked if they belonged to me
said, "No;" I pawned them for James Hollis, and that he put on the du.
plicate. Witness. He gave me the name of Taylor-my lad wrote the
ticket, but I took them of him, and that was the name be gave.
