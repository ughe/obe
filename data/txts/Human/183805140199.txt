   Q.  Who do you work for? A.  A man of the name of Robert' Rhodes cannot tell exactly how long we were altogether at the Cattle, not any time—I did not notice the time-when I left the Castle I went to the Globe—I cannot say whether that was one of Mr. Badnall's houses—I do not know whether it was Mr. Miller's, or De Horsey-s, or Badnall's—there were different people in the house—I do not know whether House is the mas ter of the Globe, or Mr. Hope—I do not know whether the house is in the in-terest of Mr. Badnall or not—I found some persons there—I cannot say whether they were the friends of Mr. Badnall or Mr. Miller—they might be fiends of both—I cannot say whether they were friends of one or the other—I do not know.
   Q.  You. went down after this; it is not true that the constable was ceiled, and you were put out? A.  No; I had not had any ale that day—I was perfectly sober, and Gallimore the same, and Wall—I did not sec any rea son to say they were intoxicated.
   MR. JONES . Q.  Were you in work up to the time you were taken into custody? A.  Yes; and I left about fourteen shillings' worth of work when I was taken—I have been, in Newgate ever since I was first apprehended, which was six weeks last Friday night—I was in bed, and a person came and called me up—I do not know that he was a constable—he is assistant overseer of the poor-house—I went from the Castle to the Globe—I do not know whose house that was—I stated at the Globe the conversation I had heard between Mr. Miller and Mr. Mason at the Castle—I saw Mr. John Brostock and Mr. Thursfield there—there was a xlarge room full of persons.
   MR. KELLY . Q.  Was what you said there taken down in writing? A.  I did not see it myself.
