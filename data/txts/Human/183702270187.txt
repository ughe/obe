I received information about these coppers from Mr. Whiffen on Friday, the 17th—I had some conver sation with Fraser—I went to Mrs. Jackson's on Friday, the 17th—I asked her to show me the copper she had previously shewn to Fraser—she stated the had not the copper by her, she had sold it the day before to Myers, a rag merchant in Spitalfields—I asked who she purchased it of—she said of two boys—I asked her to show me the book—she said she knew the boys names, it was Parsons'—I asked where they lived—she said in Dowling-street, Deptford—nothing was said about any other copper than that shewn to Fraser—I went to the prisoners' mother in Dowling-street, and there we took Henry—I took James at the tide mills in Church-street—we kept them apart at the station—J asked James where he got that copper which he sold to Mrs. Jackson—he said, "I bought it of a roan at Lewisham-bridge for a half-crown"—in going along, I said, "Where did you get the other one from?"—he said, "I bought that at a house at Lewisham-bridge," or "Lewisham watch-house," I will not swear which—I asked him what he gave for that—he said half-a-crown—after that I went to Mrs. Jackson again, and told her about buying the second copper, and asked what she gave for the first which she bought of these two boys—she hesitated—I said, "Consider what situation you stand in; what you state I must state again"—after I told her that the boys had said they had sold her two, she stated she had bought one previously of the two boys Parsons, and had sold the two to Mr. Myers, of Rose-lane, Spitalfields—I went to Myers, and got these two. MARGARET THOMAS. I lodge in Mrs. Jackson's house. I was washing for her on the 10th, and these two boys brought a copper on their heads, about four o'clock in the afternoon—she gave 9 s . 11 d . for it—the other came on the 14th, in a little dog-cart of Mrs. Jackson's—she gave 8 s . 5 1/2 d . for that—the largest was brought first.
