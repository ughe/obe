   JOSEPH FOSTER. I am a constable of Tottenham. A man named Newsom brought the prisoner to me - I took him to the watch-house, and found these two latch-keys concealed at the bottom of his trousers: I asked what he did at the bottom of the field - he said he was picking up caterpillars, and his father was a bird-fancier.

 1790.  WILLIAM BRACKLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 1 mat, value 2s.; 1 bag, value 2s.: 1 rasp, value 6d.; 3 nail-bags, value 1s. 6d.; 1 screw-driver, value 3d., and 1 file, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Henry Grainge and another, his masters.
   WILLIAM GRANT. I am apprentice to Henry and Daniel Grange, who are ironmongers. The prisoner was in their employ on the 25th of August; I know this mat to be their property - they had received it with some things a few days before; I had not quite unsewn it, and when it was returned it was exactly in the same state - I cannot speak to the other things, but one of them has the name on; they are all such articles as they sell.
   ROBERT BARNES. I am shopman to Messrs. Granges. On Monday, the 25th of August, I was desired to follow the prisoner, which I did to his own house; I then asked him how he came to take the mat, which was then under his arm - he said he thought it was of little or no value; it was charged to us about 2s. or 2s. 3d. - these tools were found in the prisoner's house by the constable, in my presence; I believe them to be my employers'.
   JOHN FERNE. I am a constable. I went to the prisoner's, and found these articles.
 Prisoner. Mr. Barnes came to me, and said I had got a mat that did not belong to me; I said I would take it back - he said it did not matter about my taking it back.
   ROBERT BARNES. He did say so; I said I would not advise him to do anything.
   WILLIAM GRANT re-examined. Q. What was the right thing to do with that mat? A. To take it to an out-house, where we keep such things - we use them in packing, but not very often; the prisoner had been three or four years in our employ, and had a good character.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1791.  MARY ROLLS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , 1 sovereign, the money of James Dancer , from his person. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1792.  JAMES TREVILLION was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 1 tea-kettle, value 3s., the goods of John Bullock.
 JOHN BULLOCK. I am an  ironmonger, and live in Featherstone-street, City-road. On the 26th of July, I was out; I had seen this kettle before nine o'clock that morning, and I saw it again at the office - it has my privatemark in it.
   HENRY WILLIAM TREVIS. I am apprentice to a ropemaker in Featherstone-street. On the 26th of July, I was looking out at a window up stairs, and saw the prisoner looking into the prosecutor's window - he then took the kettle, and walked up the street with it; there was another person with him - I told a man who worked in the room with me; I went down and pursued him, and took him in the City-road with the kettle - I lost sight of him for about five minutes, but I am sure he is the same person.
   GEORGE WOODGATE. I am an officer. I received the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going to my dinner, and saw a lad turn the corner of Featherstone-street, and put down the kettle at a door - I took it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
