HARRY WILLIAM SMITH. I find in this inwards to pay book 51. 48. ld.
enclosed as a supplementary account in the prisoner's writing as received too
late to be accounted for in the previous month-that ought to have been
entered in the office receipt-book, but it is not in consequence of not having
received it, I heard Mr. Pratt ask the prisoner for an explanation-he said
that he had not collected the amount, and I then wrote this letter: "En-
closed I beg to hand you your account for carriage of goods; an early reply
will oblige." -- the Board of Works then produced to me this receipt for
61. 48. 1d.- in the Inwards to pay book for June 5th, I find an entry of 98. 2d.
against Edward King for gravel from Thornton Heath, invoice 90. Which is
referenced to the office receipt-book, page 112, in the prisoner's writing; but
here is no such entry at page 112-I have not received the 98. 2d.--I caused
an application to be made to Mr. King for it, and he produced his receipt
--there is no entry in the books of 144 trusses of hay received by Albert
Martin, but here are the waybills showing that he received them the
prisoner did not account to me for 188. 6d. received on June 8thhe said
that he had not received it, and made out an account in Mr. Prates presence,
which was handed to me for collection-I went to Mr. Martin, and he presented
his receipt.
Cross-examined. When the conversation was going on, the prisoner
knew that accounts were going to those people, and he said he believed he
had not received them and knew nothing about a deficiency, and he came
and wanted to see the books-goods sometimes arrive before the invoice, we
then deliver them without charge, and the charge follows I cannot say
whether this gravel arrived before the invoice, and I do not know whether
in such a case an assumed charge is made without waiting for the invoice
I cannot tell what the goods clerk does it was the prisoner's place to
collect accounts as well as the goods clerk's, when the goods porter could not
get them in-this 51. odd occurs just about the date of Mr. Gasson's account
-the company have sent in their bill to the Guarantee Society.
Re-examined. The amount of it is 251. odd—King's, Martin's, and
Gasson's accounts were specially brought to the prisoner's notice by Mr.
Pratt, and the prisoner made out in my presence the second account to send
in to Mr. Martin-Mr. Pratt took it from him and handed it to me.
