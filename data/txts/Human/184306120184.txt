King-street, Long-acre. At half-past five o'clock, on the 2nd of May, I was in bed—I was awoke by a person taking the pillow from the other side of the bed—I jumped out of bed immediately, and saw the prisoner by my bedside with a small bundle under her left arm—she got out—I screamed and ran to the street door—the market people were going by, and two or three ran, but they could not find her—she took the key of the back parlour with her—I then missed these things—I had the lock taken off the back parlour door, and the door bolted inside—at a quarter before three o'clock in the morn  ing, of the 27th of May, I awoke my husband, and said there were footsteps in the passage—he said, "Lie still, you are always fancying something"—in about seven minutes I heard the key put into the back parlour door—I turned and put my hand to the street door, and the key was going round the wood—I said, "It is there now, come and hold the person"—he came and held her, till I slipped on my gown, and got the policeman—it turned out to be the prisoner—I swear she is the same person that came before—she had on this shawl which is mine, and which I lost on the 2nd of May—this key was found on her, and it is the one I lost on the 2nd of May, and fits the lock of the door.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was not near the place on the 2nd of May; I bought this shawl in Monmouth-street; the key belonged to my room. GUILTY. Aged 45.— Confined Nine Months .

JOSEPH STOKES and THOMAS LEACH were indicted for stealing, on the   7th of June , 1 cask, value 5 s  . 6 d  .; and 5 bushels of cement, 7 s  ., 6 d  .; the goods of Elisha Allsop and another, their masters, and JOHN NICHOLAS for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen, against the statute, &c. ELISHA ALLSOP. I am a builder, and live in Back-road, St. George's. I was at work at St. John-street, Sidney-square—Leach was foreman—I sent him two casks of cement at half-past eight o'clock in the morning of the 7th of June—I received some information, and went to John-street—I missed a cask of cement—I had sent two that morning, and they both ought to have been there—I then went in search of Leach, and found him at the Tom  and Jerry  shop in Charles-street, with Stokes—I called Leach out, and asked him to bring back the cement which he had taken away—he said he had taken nothing away—I said if he did not bring it back, I should give him and Stokes in charge—I met Squires, I told him, and gave Leach in his charge—I then sent Squires to the Tom  and Jerry  shop for Stokes—Leach then said he had sold the cement to Nicholas for three pots of beer—Nicholas was at the Tom  and Jerry  shop at that time—we proceeded to Nicholas's house, and at the back of his premises found the empty cask, which I identify by a mark on the bottom—it was one that I sent that morning by Leach—I did not see Nicholas till he was at the station—he said he knew nothing about it, and Stokes said he was innocent—I have seen some cement which I think was the same, but I would not swear to it—Leach and Stokes were very drunk—if Leach had been sober he would not have done it—he worked for my father many years.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did not Nicholas say he had left his shop in charge of somebody? A.  No—Mrs. Nicholas lives there, and when her husband is out she has charge of the shop. DAVID ALLSOP. I went to the job at Sidney-square, and saw there were no men—I looked into the cask of cement, and found there was about a peck gone—there was no cement used there that day—there were two casks there.
