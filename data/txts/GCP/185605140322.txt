563. ISABELLA STUART, stealing a necklace, the goods of John
Townley, from the person of Emma Townley : and ANN STUART.
feloniously receiving the same; to which
ISABELLA STUART PLEADED GUILTY.-Discharged.
MARY ANN TOWNLEY. I am the wife of John Townley, a blacksmith at
Woolwich I have a little girl eighteen months old—on 13th April, about
half-past 12 o'clock in the day, she was standing at my front door-she had a
coral necklace on—I saw Isabella Stuart there I had not known her before-
she spoke to my child, and remained there about a quarter of an hour-she
took her up in her arms, and then put her down-I heard the child cry,
and went, and missed the necklace off her neck-I then went after the
prisoner, but did not see her she was afterwards taken to the police station
fushe then said she lived in St. Mary's-street-first she said No. 25, and
afterwards No. 27—I went there, but found nobody belonging to her.
JAMES ATTWOOD (policeman, R 313). The child Isabella was brought
to the station and given into my custody-I told her she was charged with
taking this coral necklace from a little child—she said, "I took it and
chucked it under a box in the room my father and mother occupied
asked her where she lived-she said, "No. 25, St. Mary-street" -I after-
wards went to No. 56, Warwick-street-I went there in consequence of
finding the name of Stuart in a prayer book which the child had in her
possession—I knew persons of that name living at No. 56, Warwick-street
-I went there, and found the prisoner Ann Stuart-I asked her if she had
a daughter named Isabella-she said she had-I asked her whether Isabella
had been out in the morning-she said, "No"-I asked her that again, and
she said, “Yes, she was out about an hour, about dinner time"-I asked her
if she had a little boy-she said, "Yes"_I asked where he was she said
he was gone to the barrack school-I asked her whether either of them had
brought anything home this morning-she said, “No, nothing"_I said,
“Are you sure of that 1"-she said, “Yes”-I then said, " Isabella is at the
station, in charge for stealing a coral necklace with a gold snap, and I must
search your room, as Isabella says she took it, and it is here"—she said, “Oh,
my little boy brought home a necklace, and said he had found it," and she
went to a cupboard and brought out this necklace which I now produce-I
told her she must go to the station along with me, which she did.
Ann Stuart's Defence. At a quarter to 2 o'clock my little boy went out
to play in the back lane; when he came in he showed me these beads, and
said he had picked them up; I asked where he got them; he said, "Mother,
I will show you," and I went with him, and he showed me where he found
them; I put them in my cupboard, intending to make inquiry about them;
about half-past 4 o'clock the prosecutor and the policeman came, and I told
them the same; I did not know they were stolen; the policeman waited
till my little boy came home, and he told the policeman where he had
picked them up.
JAMES ATTWOOD re-examined. The little boy came home just before I
left-he was asked where he found them, and he told me, in the back lane
-he is about eight or nine years old-he is here—the prisoner's husband is
a pensioner, and works at shoe making. ANN STUART-Nor GUILTY.
