Legg to the Cross Keys, in Wood-street-I found Ford standing at the door
of the booking office I was in plain clothes—I went with him into the

heard of it, but I know nothing about it_I said, "It contained a diamond

WILLIAM JAMES MITCHELL (City police inspector). In consequence of
being sent for by Ford, I went to the cell in which he was confined he said
he wished to make a statement to me- I told him that whatever statement
he made must be entirely voluntary, and that I should make use of it, and
asked if he had well considered the matter-he said he wished to give me
the full particulars he said that when he left the office at abont 20 minutes
after 8 o'clock on the 17th, in passing up Wood-street, be kicked his toe
against a small parcel, that he picked it up and put it into his pocket, with-
out examining the contents ; that upon his return to the office about a
quarter past 9 o'clock he examined the parcel, and found it to contain a
diamond ring, and two gold lockets; that he said nothing about it to any
one until the Saturday, and he then asked Taylor if he could dispose of it
for him; he said that Mr. Mott appeared very anxious to have the lockets,
and he could tell me where they were concealed, they were hid between the
rafters and the tiles in the hay loft of the stable in Wood-street-I sent an
officer there-Ford also said that on the night in question he had counted
out forty-seven parcels, and he was not aware that this was one of them,
because there was no mark on it, and all their parcels were numbered.
GEORGE LEGG (City policeman, 440). In consequence of directions from
Mr. Mitchell, I went to a loft over the stable in the Cross Keys yard, and
between the rafters and the tiles I found these two lockets (produced) -
this book was found on Ford by Evans at the station.
