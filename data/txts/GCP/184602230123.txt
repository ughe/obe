MARY ANN MILLIGAN re-examined. Q. Had you gone to the water-
closet that night? A. No-the servant is not in the habit of going there
it was fastened by a handle--I am sure it was fastened when I went to bed
at night.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean you tried the door at all?". It was
not fastened on the outside—there is no lock to it-it shuts as any other
common door does-I know it was shut, because I passed it.
Doyle's Defence. At half-past twelve o'clock that day, I was standing at
King's-cross; I met two wagoners ; one of them said, "Have you anything
to do?" I said, “No;" he said, "I will give you bread and cheese, and 6d.
to come and get two loads of dung ;” we got it, and then it was twelve at
night; I went into the hay, and slept with five or six more; he gave me 9d.
in the morning; I laid cut 14d., and kept the 7fd. till there was a marine-
store shop open, to get me a pair of shoes, as I had left my own at home.

758. JAMES JOHNSON was indicted for stealing 1 coat, value 7s. Bd. ;
1 handkerchief, 1s. 6d.; and 1 pair of gloves, 1s.; the goods of Edward
Elnes Gardner.
EDWARD ELMES GARDNER. I live in Bedfordshire. On the 25th of Feb.,
at a quarter-past six o'еlock in the evening, I stopped with my cart at the
gate of the Windmill-inn, St. Jobn-street, Smithfield-I was in the house
about five minutes- I saw the prisoner near my cart-I came out, and saw
him come away from it with my great coat under his arm followed,
and got close to him-I looked to see if it was my coat-he dropped it and
fan off-I picked it up, and followed, and a gentleman caught him I am
positive he is the person-this is my coat, my handkerchief and gloves are in
It-he walked when I first saw him--when he dropped the coat he ran.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doane. Q. Where had you left it? 4. On the
front board - I had laid it on--it did not hang over-anybody passing might
see it.
