GEORGE REED. I am a tailor, and live in Northumberland-place.
The house, No. 12, New-street, where Moss lived, belongs to ine-New-
street goes down by the side of the London Hospital-on the 25th of
March, Moss owed me two quarters' rent-he left on the 26th-he had
given no notice whatever-Bradley, the servant-maid, brought me round
57. at ten o'clock at night-it was unusual for him to send the servant with
it-he generally paid it himself—the rent was 201. a year-there was 10l.
due.
JOSEPH JEPSON. I live at No. 29, Mansel-street. Moss took a lodg.
ing at my house on the 26th of March--they moved in some things the
same day-a truck came, and next day they moved in their furniture.
LUCY MARSHALL. I am a widow, and live at No. 13, New-street,
Whitechapel-road, opposite the house Moss lived in. I knew Moss very
well-on Monday, the 25th of March, I saw him go to his house about
three o'clock in the afternoon-he had something very weighty with him,
I cannot tell what-he came in a direction from Stepney way-that would
not be from Whitechapel-road, but from Commercial-road-he could
scarcely get what he had, up the steps-he went into the house with il-
next morning I missed the plate off his door-about five o'clock that
Tuesday evening, two men went with a truck, and took away some things
-there were three or four boxes, a bed and bedstead, and a few brooms-
one of the boxes was corded, and appeared very heavy-it was the shape
of that box-(the one produced.)
Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. You say it looked heavy-I
suppose he staggered under the weight of it? d. It was as much as two
men could put into the truck, it was so heavy-it was very weighty-I
have no judgment what weight it would be I should think it was full
two cwt.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. But you have no judgment what weight it would
be? A. No.
HARRIET SMART. I am the wife of George Smart, a tailor, living in
New-street. I know the house where Moss lived-I saw some moving
from there on Tuesday, the 26th of March-I know Ellis Caspar-I saw
him on the Sunday before this moving, at Moss's door, and I saw him on
the Monday-I saw the younger Caspar there one Sunday, to the best of
my recollection, but I could not swear to him.
Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS. Q. Did you ever see that before?
A. No-I did not lodge in the same house with Moss, but at No. 16, op-
posite-I had never seen the person I say was Caspar, before that Sunday
-I only had a view of him while he stood at the door to be let in---that
might be about a minute or two minutes- I cannot say what time of the
day it was it was towards the evening, getting dusk-it is not a wide street
--it is as wide as the length of this court-I cannot speak positively to the
width- I was at my first floor window-it was in the dusk of the evening
saw him the second time, and from my first floor window-he had his hat
on-I cannot say positively whether he was going away when I saw him,
or whether he was going in- I had a view of his face-I saw him convers-
ing at the door--the door was open.
