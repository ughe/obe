MARY BUTLER. I live at No. 2, Booth-street The prisoner came to me on
Thursday afternoon, the 21st of March, between two and three o'clock-she
had her child Charles with her-she said she would leave him with me, and
she went and fetched the younger child George-she remained with me three
nights on the Sunday morning she got up and asked me to give her a drop
of water to wash the baby-I gave her some--she said she was in a great
deal of trouble; and addressing the baby at breakfast time she said, " This
day will end it"-she went away between ten and eleven o'clock-she took
the younger child with her, and left the elder one with me-she said before
she would return back to the Union at Bethnal-green with her children, for
the ill treatment the children had received, she would make away with herself
and her two children the child that was left with me was sore, and George
had a breaking out on the face, and one on the leg, about the size of a
sixpence.
CAROLINE GIBBS. I have been an inmate of Bethnal-green work house
more than twelve months. I remember the prisoner being there she was
there when I went, and was there during the whole time I was there-I ob-
served her young child, called George Furley, while she was there--I have
not seen it since the child was in very good health, except now and then it
bad a breaking out-she bad the care of it herself during the whole time-
was at the workhouse on the day she left- I did not hear her say any thing
about herself or the child on that day.
Emma WEAVER. I saw the prisoner shortly before the death of her child-
she came to our house between twelve and one o'clock on the Sunday—the
child George was with her-I have not seen it since she dined with me on
that day, and left about twenty minutes after eight o'clock in the evening
with the child-she said many people talked but did not do, and it would be
her fate and her infant's to bave a watery grave-she mentioned that several
times on the Sunday-she said she left the workhouse because her children
were ill treated by them, and she told me she had been turned out of the
workhouse.
Thomas GARDNER. I am a lighterman. On Sunday night, the 24th of
March, about nine o'clock, I was by the side of the Regent's-canal, near
Mile-end-bridge--my attention was called to the back of the premises by the
noise of a dog- I went there, and after waiting a minute or two I heard a
moaning in the canal-I hastened to the place from whence the moaning
seemed to proceed, and saw what appeared to be a bundle in the water-I
supposed from the moaning it was a person in the water-I ran for a boat-
hook, and pulled the supposed bundle to the side, and there found it was the
prisoner-when I got her to the side and was pulling her out, she exclaimed,
"Oh my child!"- I asked her where it was—she said, “In the water"-I felt
down by her side, and the child was under her left arm, which was still in the
water at the time I had hold of her right arm holding it up- I took the child,
apd laid it on the side of the canal, and then took the prisoner out-the child
seemed to all appearance dead-it was quite motionless, and turned out to be
dead-I ran with it to the Globe tavern-Mr. New, the surgeon, attended, and
measures were taken by putting it in a warm bath to restore it to life-10
time was lost, but it was all in vain-that was the child that was seen by Mr.
