1662. SUSAN JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of May,
1 shawl, value 4d. ; 1 box, 18.; 1 pair of stockings, 1s. 6d. ; 1 cap, 1s. 6d. ;
2 pillow cases, 1s. 6d.; 3 towels, 4d.; and 2 table cloths, 6d.; the goods
of Elizabeth Sullivan.com
ELIZABETH SULLIVAN. I live in Twine-court, Shadwell. On the 5th of
May the prisoner came out of the workhouse, and told me she had neither
home nor habitation-I said if she could make shift with me and my boy,
she might come-on Wednesday she went out, between eleven and twelve
o'clock, and came back between seven and eight in the evening, with a man-
I went to my next door neighbour for about ten minutes--when I came back,
the man was still there; but I missed my box and a shawl which had been on
it-on the Friday after I met her in the street, with my shawl on—I said, “ For
goodness' sake give me what was in the box, or acknowledge it; I won't
burt you"- she said she would not-I gave her in charge-this is my shawl.
Prisoner. You sold my shawl to get tea, and said, "You can have the
loan of my old shawl," and I said, "Very well." Witness. I did not-I
did not sell her shawl to Emma Perkins-she left a few things with me for
board and lodging-she never paid me anything.
John BUTT police-constable K 197.) The prisoner was given in charge-
she refused to give up the shawl, and said she would sooner break it in pieces
than the prosecutrix should have it-she came to the station without an officer,
Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutrix lent me the shawl, when she had sold
mine to Emma Perkins.
Guilty. Aged 48.-Corfined Two Months.
