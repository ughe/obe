 1400.  THOMAS BRIGGS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of May , 1 necklace, value 18s., the goods of William Clarke , from the person of Emma Clarke.
   WILLIAM CLARKE. I live in Butterland-street, East-road, City-road, and am a watchmaker. I went with my wife and children to Whitecross-street, on Saturday evening, the 12th of May; my wife went into a butcher's shop: I went on with the two children - I saw the prisoner pass me two or three times: my daughter Emma had a necklace on - I had hold of her hand, and felt her flinch; I missed the necklace from her neck, and saw the prisoner running round the corner - I went up to him, and he threw it down; I took it up, and took him to the officer - several of the bends were lost by the fall.
   JOHN HAYFORD(Police-constable G 184). I took the prisoner from the prosecutor between six and seven o'clock on Saturday evening, the 12th of May; I have the beads which I received from him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1401. The prosecutrix did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1402.  ELIZABETH EMILY WARD was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of May , 1 counterpane, value 3s.; 2 blankets, value 6s., and 1 pillow, value 1s., the goods of Peter Sapet.
 SARAH SAPET. I am the wife of  Peter Sapet, of Little Russell-street, Bloomsbury. The prisoner lodged with us from the beginning of March - on the morning of the 18th of May, I got a Police-constable, and missed these articles from her room - she said she took them from want.
 WILLIAM CRUSH. I am a pawnbroker of Museum-street, Bloomsbury. These articles were pawned with me by the prisoner - she has borne a good character.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM WILSON(Police-constable E 74.) I took the prisoner, and found these duplicates on her.
 The prisoner pleaded poverty. GUILTY. Aged 46.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1403.  ANN WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of May , 4 sovereigns, and 3s., the monies of John Brown , from his person.
 JOHN BROWN. I am a  seaman. I came on shore a fortnight ago last Monday; I met the prisoner on the 16th of May, when I had been paid; I went home with her - she said the place belonged to her, and she put her hand into my pocket and took out my sovereigns; I asked what she did that for; she said to take care of them - I would not let her have them, but she would in spite of me; I still stopped, and a man came in and wanted some money of her; I rested still till the man knocked me down - I had given them the last 1s., I had to get some beer - he said she was his sister, or sister-in-law.
   MARY CHALKLEY. I live in Hamblin-street. The prisoner lodged at my house; I remember the prosecutor coming - he went with her to the front room; they were there some time; the prisoner then went out for pork-chops; I saw some sovereigns in her hand, and said I hoped they did not belong to the man she brought into my place - Crouch, a man who had lived with the prisoner, came in - he knocked the prosecutor down, and I picked him up; he then knocked me down, and almost put my shoulder out - the prosecutor asked the prisoner for his money; she said if he wanted his money he might go elsewhere - he said he did not want to hurt her, he would be contented if she gave him one sovereign.
 Prisoner. Q. You saw him give me two sovereigns out of his pocket, when I went to get the chops? A. No, I did not; the usage he had from your young man was shameful; every thing I had in my room was broken to pieces.
   WILLIAM OSMAN(Police-constable K 61.) The prosecutor fetched me to the house - I found the prisoner there; he charged her with robbing him, and said the man had knocked him down; the prisoner came to the table, took up a knife, and said, "If you charge me with such a sort of thing, I will stick you, or rip you open;" she said the prosecutor gave her the money, and he denied it - I found no money on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the prosecutor; we went to another house, and I slept with him - the next morning he asked me to go with him and get some money.
 JOHN BROWN. I slept with her the night before, but I paid her half a sovereign - she tore the pocket off my new jacket.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Transported for Life.
 1404.  ELIZABETH SAUNDERS was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of May , 1 cap, value 5s., the goods of Thomas Redpath.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am shop-boy to Thomas Redpath, of Upper Seymour-street, St. Pancras. On the afternoon of the 18th of May, I was outside his shop; the prisoner came up to me, I went round the corner, and when I came back I missed a cap - I went after her, and asked if she had bought a cap; she said No - I asked where she got this from; she said she did not know; I gave her to an officer - this is the cap, it has the ticket on it; it had been on a bed, on a bedstead outside the shop.
