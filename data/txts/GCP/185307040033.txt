JAMES ABRAHAMS (policeman, S 296). On 21st June I was on daty in
plain clothes-I saw the prisoner in company with another one I had known
the prisoner previously-I watched him, and saw him go into the last witness's
shop-I went in and took him-he said, “Well, you may take me, and lock
me up till Saturday, and then I shall be turned up"-Saturday is the day on
which all persons passing bad inoney are remanded to, and if there is nothing
known of them then they are turned up, that is, discharged at the station
the prisoner said, “You can search me, I have got no more on me; I am too
clever for you"-I found he had no more, only two good shillings.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This half crown is a bad one.
Prisoner's Defence. What the constable says is quite false about my
saying I might be locked up till Saturday, and then turned up; he certainly
knew me, from my being in custody four years ago, and by seeing me about
Somers-town. Guilty. Aged 22.-Confined Twelve Months.
