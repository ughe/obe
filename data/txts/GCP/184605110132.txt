1169. HENRY WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing 1 half-crown, 2
shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of Sarah Parkins.
Sarau PARKINS. I live in the rope-yard, Woolwich. On Saturday night,
the 18th of April, I met the prisoner in Powis-street--- he went home with
me, and gave me half-a-crown and a sixpence, which I put into a teapot,
where I had 38. 8d. besides-another man came into my room, and the pri-
soner went into an adjoining room--the other person did not stop, and when
he was gone the prisoner came into my room again- I then went to bed, but
not before I had counted my money-the female who lives in the next house
to me saw me count it-I put it into a teapot, and put it into the cupboard
-the next morning the prisoner made an excuse to go out-he desired me
not to get up, but he dressed himself and went away- I thought it strange,
and went to my cupboard- I found the teapot lid was off, and half-a-crown,
two shillings, and a sixpence gone-no one could have taken it but the
prisoner-1 locked my door and bolted it after the other man was gone.
Cross-examined by Mr. MELLOR. Q. What kind of a teapot was it? 4.
A brown teapot, with a cover to it-I went home with the prisoner between
twelve and one o'clock-1 received the money from him directly-1 did not
go to bed immediately-my friend was not in my room a quarter of an hour
-he called for an article he had left with me, and I expected the prisoner to
return-I placed the teapot on the bottom shelf in the cupboard-the cup-
board-door does not shut very tight-there was nothing before the teapot
there were a few other things in the cupboard - I had never seen the prisoner
before I never charged any one with a robbery before. T ERADEN
Court. Q. Did the prisoner see you put the money in the teapot? A.
I do not know, he might-I am sure I counted the money after the prisoner
returned to the room, on account of my being back with my rent-Ann Jones
saw me count it and put it into the teapot, after the other man had left. 10
Ann Jones. I live in the next house to Parkins- I was in her room that
Saturday night and saw her count the money--the other man was then gone,
and the prisoner was undressed and in bed.

JAMES PARRY (police-sergeant R 8.) I was passing the cell where the
prisoner was confined-be asked me at what time the Magistrate would sit-
said about three o'clock-he said, “ Would the Magistrate allow me to give
her half a sovereign ?" I said I did not know, it was a serious offence-he
said, “I have been served the same, and they would serve me so again if they
had a chance."
Not GUILTY.
