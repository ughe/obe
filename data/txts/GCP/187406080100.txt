The letters were dated 25th and 28th July, 1873, from Mrs. Easterby to her
sister Rachael, stating that it was her solicitor's fault that she was taken
away, and mentioning "Wentworth 7." "Wentworth V" is Wentworth
Villa—the trial was then postponed to September, and then to Ootober,
but we were not able to find Mrs. Easterby, and in her absence, at the
October Session, the case came to an end; the Common Serjeant would
not postpone it any longer---I heard nothing more of the matter till the
little boy made a statement to me, early in February, and two days after
that, Mrs. Easterby came to my office-I reported the matter to head-
quarters, and was requested to prosecute the persons who had got her
out of the way—these informations attached to the depositions are signed
by Mr. Harris-I produce some letters signed by Harris, relating to Mr.
Martin. (These were dated 27th February, 25th March, 27th March, and 23rd
April, 1873, one of which acknowledged the receipt of a cheque for 51., and

case-I also produce two applications for post-office orders in Harris's
writing. (Each of these was for 51., made payable at the Poste Restante, Paris,
to Cecil Fortescue, from Charles Kidman of Barnet, and dated July 8th and

Cross-examined by MR. STRAIGHT. I think there were five or six exami-
nations at Highgate when Mr. Harris had charge of the case, but I don't
know-it was on 3rd June that he brought me the papers-he had a
lengthened conversation with me, both on the 2nd and 3rd-he told me
he had been put to considerable expense, and I told him I thought very
likely he would get part of it, but not the whole, but I had no authority
to say that I tried to convey to him that he would get some of his
expenses, and when he left me he seemed satisfied.
