HENRY CHAPMAN. I am a licensed victualler, of the Fulham-road. The
prisoner was my potman-he came in April, and left me on 18th May-it was
his duty to take out beer to my customers, and to receive money on my
behalf-he was to pay it to me, not particularly on the same day, but at the
end of three or four days.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. What were his wages? 4.
Five sbillings a week, his board and lodging, and a halfpenny a pot on the
beer he sold-he had a book, and I have a journal in which I kept an account
of everything that went out; it is not here-he was in my service about six
weeks- 1 paid him all his salary, except on the Saturday on which he
absconded with the whole of the money.
GEORGE Woop. I live at Parson's-green. I have my beer of Mr. Chap-
man-I pay once a week-I know the prisoner--the last time I paid him I
gave bim a half-crown-it was the latter end of May-I cannot say how many
pots I paid for.
SAMUEL WINKWORTH. I have beer of Mr. Chapman-I paid the prisoner
a half-sovereign on the Friday evening that he went away-it was the latter
part of May.
Mr. CHAPMAN re-examined. The prisoner did not pay me the balf-crown
or half-sovereign, or give me an account of it.
