EDWARD BOWYER. On 7th February I saw my wife serve the prisoner
- he threw down a half-crown, which she handed to me, and I saw that
it was bad-I asked him where he got it-he said that his master had
just paid him 58. for his wages--I sent for a constable, and the prisoner
attempted to kick me, and made two attempts to run away, but I gave
him in oustody with the half-crown-he was taken to Southwark Police-
court, remanded to the following Wednesday, and discharged
DAVID LARKING. I took the prisoner at Mr. Bowyer's shop, who
gave me this half-crown. I searched the prisoner and found the change
on him-he was remanded, and discharged.
MARIA JOHNSON. I am & clerk at the post-office, 225, Westminster
Bridge Road-on 4th March, about 11.80 a.m., I served the prisoner
with 88, worth of postage stamps-he laid down a half-crown and a six
penoe- I took it up and passed it to Charlotte Olark, who took it away;
and as she passed me he said, “What is the matter?"-I did not answer
him-he slipped the stamps back and rushed out of the office-I gave
information to the police, and next day saw him in custody with a num-
ber of people, and picked him out-I have no doubt about him.de
CHARLOTTE CLARK. On 4th March the last witness gave me a half-crown
-I took it to Mr. Miller
WM. HORATIO MILLER, I keep a stationer's shop and post-office-on
4th March the last witness gave me a bad half-crown-I went into the
shop, but the prisoner had gone-I gave information, and afterwards
gave the coin to Sergeant Nelson.
JOBRPH NELSON (Polio. Sergeant D). On the evening of 5th March I
saw the prisoner in Blackfriars Road and told him I should take him
on suspicion of uttering a counterfeit half-grown the day previous in West-
minster Bridge Road- he said that he knew nothing about it. I placed
him with five or six others, and Miss Johnson identified him immediately
-I received the half-crown from Mr. Miller.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER, These half-crowns are both bad.
Prisonor's Defence. I know nothing about it; I was never in the post
office.

MR. POLAND Prosecuted.
MARY SOPPETT. My father keeps the Beehive, Warner Street, New
Kent Road on 6th March, about 7 p.m., I served the prisoner with a
glass of bitter-six, which came to 11d. he gave me a bad florin-I gave
it to my father, who spoke to him.
JOHN SOPPETT. I keep the Beehive on 4th March my daughter called
me into the bar and gave me a bad florin-I saw the prisoner there and
asked him if he was aware he was tendering bad coin-he said that he
was not-I doubled the coin up, and am sure it was bad-I gave it back
to him, and he paid me id., and said that that was all he had got-I
followed him some distance and gave him in custody-he was searched
- he said that he had thrown the florin away as it was no good-he was
allowed to go as he had no other coin on him-I saw him again next day,
and am sure he is the man,
