On July 12 I received the telegram which has been produced by the Post Office that two horses were consigned to see at Dorking Station. I paid for the two horses as described, and they were taken round to my shop. I valued the two ponies I gave in exchange at £20, and that with £9 in gold makes £29, which I con  sidered a fair price. The bay mare that I sold to the co-operative Society had a large knee, and that, in my opinion, reduces its value. In purchasing horses from dealers in the ordinary way I do not get any warranty; there is a certain amount of risk about such pur chases. The Stratford cart was not sent to the carriage builders for its appearance to be altered. It is not correct to suggest that I ordered the maker's name to be obliterated from the wheels. I sent it to be thoroughly done up. The first time I had inquiries about the two horses was on the Saturday before the August Bank Holiday, when I saw Mr. Brown. Up to that time I had no suspicion that the horses had been stolen or dishonestly come by. I told the detective next evening how they had come into my possession. I had up till then only known Friend as J. Smith. I had Smith's address at Drewton Street, Commercial Road through him writing to me from there. When I saw Friend at the police-station I identified him as the man Smith from whom I had purchased the horses. He was arrested close to my shop. My son had him arrested. I purchased the animals in good faith.
 Cross-examined. When I saw the horses they struck me as being very good ones. The mare improved wonderfully after I had her. It did not strike me as being a curious deal or anything out of the way. I have had altogether three transactions with Friend—a cob, a cart, and these two horses. The cob I sold to a man named Ford, who lives at Hedley. Friend had a standing order to bring me any decent animal he could get hold of. I have not done anything with horses until this year. I have not seen "Tom" since I bought Henderson's cart of him that I know of. You can call it a nice little lot; it seized me very well. My wife fancied it. "Tom" was known to Swift, who kept the "Three Tuns," where the periodical sales are held and I asked him about him and thought I had satisfied myself that he was all right. I purchased three carts within about a month. My instructions to Sherlock about the Stratford cart were that I wanted it done up properly. I told him the colours. I tried to sell the Stratford cart. Everything I have is for sale. After my son fetched it home it stood in an open shed and I chucked a sheet over it to keep the dust off.
