minster, about a quarter past 4 on the afternoon of 13th November, on
plain-clothes duty-I saw Sergeant Williamson there-I saw the prisoner
strike him with the appearance of a paper pareel, on the right side or
his hend; it was a hard blow, and then he dropped an oyster koife out
of his left hand; this produced) is the knife; I picked it up-William-
son staggered; he was assisting a constable at the time who had been
knocked down-after Williamson was struck another plain-clothes officer
took him away-seeing the prisoner drop this knife I halloaed out
"Williamson, are you stabbed ?" and I picked up the knife-I went
after the prisoner, and he was in the custody of Sergeant Honey when

EDWARD DAY (Policeman L 166). I am now specially employed as &
plain-clothes officer on this Sunday afternoon I was on duty in Bridge
Street, Westminster, in plain clothes-Williamson was with me, also in
plain clothes-about 4.15 I saw the prisoner in Bridge Street; I saw him
run from the crowd and strike Williamson with some weapon; I noticed
that he had something, I thought it was a roll of paper-it was a blow
right under, like that (describing)-as soon as he did it he ran back to
the crowd-Honey knocked him down, and he was taken into custody,
and taken to King Street Station-Honey took from him the roll of
paper, and it turned out to be this (a piece of iron piping)-almost
immediately after the prisoner had been taken away I found this weapon,
a kind of poker or bar, on the ground near the place ; it was not wrapped
in anything; it had this string to it--from the time the prisoner struck
the blow until Honey took him I did not lose sight of him.
Cross-examined. I said at the station that I saw the prisoner strike
Williamson with & weapon, I would not swear I did not say an iron
weapon; it sounded solid, like a piece of wood-I found this weapon on
the scene, and took it to the station- it was produced at the police-court
-the prisoner ran from the direction of Parliament Street from the crowd.
