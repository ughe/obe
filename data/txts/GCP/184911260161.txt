Osmotherly's Defence. Thompson told me she had left the box ; she wrote
to me to send it to London, and I asked a man the price to take it, and he told
me if I would take it to the corner we should not fall out about the price; I
fetched it to the corner, and the sentry told me the carinan had been gone about
twenty minutes; I went to a public-house, and put the box in the parlour
I afterwards took it, and it fell off my back and burst open ; I took it to the
railway station, and put it upon a wall; the policeman was behind me, and some
of the things fell out in the street; I took them under my arm, and a mate and
this woman came along and I asked them to assist me; the man said he was
going to stop out all night, and the box would be all right if we took it into this
house; we took it in, and in about twenty minutes Sutton came and asked where
I got it from, and I told him; I said it was the first time I was ever in custody,
and if I got out I would have a spree for it; the other marine told me the
policeman followed me.
Johnson's Defence. He brought the box to my place, and asked to let it
remain there till the morning.
GEORGE SUTTON re-examined. I did not follow them there were three
hours from the time the box was moved till I found it-I never saw it before
I saw it in the room.
(Charles Hancock, sergeant of the Marine Artillery, gave Osmotherly a
good character.)

Before Mr. Common Serjeant,
153. WILLIAM JACKSON and HENRY ROGERS, stealing 87 lbs.
weight of lead, value 168.; the goods of John Blyth, fixed to a building.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN BENNEY (policeman, R 356). On Wednesday night, 31st Oct., I
was in the Wool wich-road, at a quarter before ten o'clock - I heard a noise in
an unfinished house there - I looked up and saw Jackson at the attic-window
-I went to the back of the house, and found Rogers in a trench, made in
the area- I asked him what he was doing there-he said he came for a neces-
sary purpose-Jackson was still at the window-I asked Rogers if he knew
there was any one up there-he said he did not-I told Jackson to come
down, and he did -I asked him what he was doing up there-he said,
“Nothing"- I took them into the road, and said they must come with me-
Rogers made his escape-1 took Jackson to the station-in going, he said he
had been there, and cut off about 6 lbs. of lead, and I should find it at the
back of the house - I locked him up, and gave a description of Rogers to
another officer, and he was brought in about half-past two-after I had locked
Jackson up, I went back to the house-I found another officer there who had
found the lead that was cut off-I matched it, and it fitted to the roof-there
was about 8 lbs. of lead found on the ground, near where Rogers had been
1 matched that with the lead on the roof, and it all matched-I found three-
quarters of a cwt. more lead on the top cut off ready to throw down.
