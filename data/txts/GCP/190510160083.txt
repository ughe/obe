The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that owing to being nearly always
kept short of funds he had to use cheques and postal orders of customers to
pay the expenses, which not only consisted of expenses in the office but large
amounts he incurred in travelling and pushing trade; that he did not on
all occasions render accounts in which these expenses were detailed, but
that he kept a special expense book in which he entered all sums spent on
travelling, which book had been left in the office with the others; that he had
always informed Miss Stamp when he had used cheques in that way, of
which fact the prosecutor always knew; that the prosecutor's account of
the conversation in Ribémont was absolutely false; that the prosecutor had.
endeavoured to ascertain how much money he had inherited; that he told
him he was going to America, that he had never used any moneys received
for his own purposes, nor had he intercepted any letters nor taken books
from the office prior to his arrest , that 3,000 of his inhalers had been ordered,
and that he believed the prosecutor was in his debt. turn
Guilty. Three months in the Second Division.
