JOSEPH JAMESON. I live at 217, Kent Street, Borough, and am a pic-
ture-frame maker-I am brother-in-law to the prisoner – I remember meet-
ing Mr. Reed in Cheapside early in July; it must have been on the 3rd,
as the 2nd was the wedding-day, and it was the day after that I know
nothing about the things being fetched away in the cab, I was not there-
when I met Mr. Reed, on the 3rd, there was the prisoner and a young man
we went to a public-house opposite the railway station in Farringdon
Street-I do not know the name of it-Cohen said to him, "I shall want
some money, Mr. Reed "-he said, “ Have you got a bill with you ?” and he
said, “Yes," and took it out of his pocket--this is it, the one for 21. 18s. 6d.
-Mr. Reed said, “Very well, I will sign it for you:" at least he did sign
it, whether he said those exact words or not I can't say-he signed it in
the public-house, that is he put his initials “H, R.” at the bottom-he told
Cohen that he would see him at four o'clock, and they went away-this
was on Wednesday, the 3rd July—he got the pen that he signed it with
from a man standing at the bar, a sort of commercial man, rather shabby-
looking, rather inclined to be stout-he carried the pen in front of him
with a little bottle.
Cross-examined. Q. How long were you in the public-house together
before this paper was signed ? A. Not very long—it might be about ten
minutes, I can't say exactly-it must have been about one or two in the
day, as near as possible-Reed said, “If you will produce the bill I will
sigu it"-I can say positively he said those words, something to that
effect, I can't say the very words-he did sign it-the shabby man
was not behind the bar, he was in front of it-he was not the bar-
man--I might know him again if I saw him-we have been to I don't
know how many places to find him the other young man was &.
foreign-looking man- I did not know him previously-I happened to
make his acquaintance and asked him to have a glass of something to
drink-he was a Jew, and I befriended him because he was in want
-I did not know his name or address at the time I found it out since
-I saw him at a barber's shop where he engaged himself, in Kent Street,
just opposite where I lived, about a month ago, but he is not there now-

the day before was the prisoner's wedding-day-I remember the name of
the public-house now, it was Lockey's—I did not instruct the lawyer to
