134. CHARLES BROWN, feloniously killing and slaying Charles Nelson;
he was also charged upon the Coroner's Inquisition with the like offence.
Mr. O'BRIEN conducted the Prosecution.
ELLEN WILsoN. I live at No. 31, St. George's New-town, Southwark
On Monday, 30th Oct. I was in company with Elizabeth Hazle at a public
house-I don't know the sign of it-the prisoner and Nelson came in,
and we afterwards went together to another public house-there was some
quarrelling there-we took a cab from there-there were five of us got in
the prisoner, Nelson, my companion Elizabeth Hazle, a man I knew as an
Irishman, and myself-we drove into Mint-street, where Hazle lived-we
had some drink there, and they quarrelled again-the Jrishman and Nelson
were quarrelling firstNelson then called Brown a son of a b-, and told
him that he was no mate not to take his part-I believe they were ship-
mates-Nelson then caught hold of the prisoner by the collar or handker-
chief, until he was nearly black in the face-upon that I ran for the police
-I left the Irishman and the two men in the room-when I came back I
heard an Irish girl who lived in the next room, Mary Ready, halloo out that
he was stabbed- went into the room and saw Nelson lying dead on the
ground he was bleeding-there was only Brown, Nelson, and the Irishman
in the room-the deceased was lying on his side and a dagger was sticking
in his neck I pulled it out and put it in my pocket, and afterwards gave
it to the policeman-whe I left the room I left Brown on the bed nearly
strangled by the deceased-I can't say that the Irishman was drunk, but
Nelson and Brown were very much in liquor.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. You are an unfortunate female, I
believe A. Yes-the two mates were very drunk at the public house-
they had very little at our place-] think the deceased was more in fault-
he first began the rou with the Irishman at the public house-he afterwards
called the prisoner a son of a b-, because he did not join in the rouo with
the Irishman-he called him that twice-the prisoner offered no violence to
the deceased at the public house-I do not know whether they had just
come from the same vessel-after they got to Hazle's place, the Irishman
and the deceased still kept quarrelling and calling one another bad names-
the prisoner and deceased were very much in liquor indeed-th deceased at
last seized Brown, and threw him down, and hit him several times with a
cane-Brown had not done anything to him then-he had caught hold of
Nelson in the cab, and told him to be quiet, and not quarrel-w had only
been to two public houses-w were in company about half an hour-after
they got to our place Nelson kept sparring at Brown, and calling him names
-before I went for the police Brown was black in the face-I told Nelson
if he did not let the man go I would go for a constable, and he said he did
not care one God d-n-he had got Brown on the bed, and was upon him,
holding him by the handkerchief-] left him on the bed when I went for
the police-as far as I saw, the violence was on the part of Nelson towards
Brown, and not of Brown towards Nelson.
