   MARY HARWOOD. I am single, and live with my sister Deborah Turner - she keeps a house in Chapel-street, Pentonville, in the parish of St. James' Clerkenwell- the prisoner had been her servant for twelve months, within a few days, before the 11th of July - on that day she was sent out to buy some pork; she did not return, and in the evening we heard she was in custody - my attention was then directed to a 50l. note, which was my property, and which I had had in a chest of drawers in my bed room; the drawer was locked, there was other cash in the same drawer; I did not miss the note till my attention was called to it - I had received it from Grote's, the bankers, on the 20th of June; I had only that one 50l. note - I saw the broker write a pencil memorandum of the number of that note, and the policeman has that memorandum - after I had received the note I compared the number and date of it with that memorandum, and I saw it was correct; it was No. 9139, dated 14th May, 1833 - it was my sole property, and that was the note I lost - I had not sent the prisoner to buy any linen, or to buy anything with that note.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had your sister a part property in that note? A. No; nor in any of the money in that drawer - I don't know how the drawer had been opened; there was the appearance of the mahogany being chipped off a little, but I found it locked.
   GEORGE READ. I keep a linendraper's shop, at No. 40, Upper-street, Islington - on the 11th of July the prisoner came to my shop, about eight o'clock in the evening; she said she wished to purchase a few goods, but she could not unless I could give her change for a 50l. note - I said I could give her change, but I asked where she came from; she said, from No. 14, Camden-street - I told her it was a larger amount than I liked to give change for to a stranger; she said she could get change at the grocer's just above - I then said I would give her change, and she purchased goods to the amount of 2l. 4s. or 2l. 5s.; she gave me the note, and I wrote on the back of it "Miss Turner, 14, Camden-street," which was the name and address she gave; when I gave her the change I said I would send a young man with her to see that all was right - she then said she was not going home, that she was going to the dress maker's, and the dress maker knew her - I told my young man privately to follow her, and not leave her till he had seen her home; my young man is not here - I afterwards found the prisoner was in custody - I had kept the note and given her the change; this is the note.
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you sure it is the same note? A. Yes; it has my writing on it.
   JAMES FOX(police-constable N 196). I received information that a 50l. note had been passed at Mr. Read's, by a young woman who had given a false address, and who had gone off with two young men - I followed, and took the prisoner in Chapel-street, about ten o'clock - I found these articles, which had been purchased at Mr. Read's, some on her person, and some at a dress maker's - I found on her four 5l. notes, one 10l. note, seventeen sovereigns, and 19s. in silver.
   GEORGE DYER. I am a clerk in the Bank - this note was brought in on the 19th of July, and paid it in cash.
 MARY HARWOOD. This is my note.
   GEORGE READ. This is the note; I paid it away for goods to Bishop & Hall, in the Borough - I wrote the name on it in the prisoner's presence, and I took the number before I paid it away - I gave her 47l. 15s. in gold, notes, and silver; this property is what I sold her.
   William Crossley, a tailor, at Carshalton, gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Life.
 1371.  ELIZABETH WILLIAMS and SARAH EDWARDS were indicted for stealing, on the 30th of June , 1 pocket-book, value 6d.; 1 half-sovereign; 4 half-crowns; 7 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of James Catchpool , from his person.
 JAMES CATCHPOOL. I am a  compositor, and work at Mr. Lewis', Finch-lane, Cornhill. On the 30th of June, I called in at the George and Dragon, in Hackney-road, about ten o'clock in the evening; I had a glass of sherry at the bar - while I was there, Edwards came in and asked if I would treat her with a glass of anything to drink, and on her renewing her application, I said she should have a glass of anything she liked; she had a glass of rum and shrub, and I paid for that and my sherry and came out, but I had not got more than twenty yards, when she came running after me, and asked if I was going home, I said, "Yes;" she then put her hand round my waist; I said, "You are going to rob me;" she said, "No I am not, upon my soul;" I said,"Go away; if you follow me I will give you in charge of the first policeman I meet;" I went on, she still followed me; I told her if she dared to follow me, I would give her in charge; she took no notice, but still followed me, and I struck her; I then met a friend of mine and told him of it - she then went away, and I went with my friend to another wine vaults and had a glass of sherry; while we were there, the two prisoners came in and Williams came up to me and said, "I don't think she meant to rob you;" I said, "She has not, and if she follows me I will give her in charge;" Williams then said,"I have not offended you, give me a glass;" I said I would not, but my friend said, "You had better give her a glass and have nothing more to do with her;" I then gave her a glass - Edwards then went away - I came out, Williams came after me, and asked me to give her a sixpence to get some gin; I gave her one and bade her good night; but as I was going down the street, she came after me again and said, "Give me another sixpence before I go;" she then put her arms round my waist, and I missed my money from my pocket; I then put my hand to my pocket and missed my pocket book - I said, "You have robbed me;" she said, "I have not, I have nothing but this sixpence;" the policeman was coming by, and I gave her in charge; she was taken to the watch-house - she still denied having anything, but when she was going to be searched, she said, she had 6s. or 7s.; I had lost a half-sovereign, which was in a small bit of paper, and four half-crowns and some silver, which I had taken in change of a sovereign;
