E. S. DORÉ (Re-examined.) We buy the cloth at so much a yard-the
hop-sack would be 78. 6d. a yard, the grey would be 88. 6d., and this about
88. 60.--that is the wholesale price to us.
JAMES COLLINS (City Detective.) On September 6th I took the prisoner
to the police-court-on the way he made a statement in fairly good English
- he said, “ Potkins has brought me a lot of cloth in pieces; I have made
it up, and he told me how much to charge for it; nearly all the trousers
in that box are from Dore's, one or two of the coats that are finished I
bought at another place."
ALBERT HENRY POTKINS. Until I was taken into custody I was employed
by Doré and Sons for about two years, first as a trimmer and then as under-
cutter-I received 358. a week-- when I wanted cloth for the purpose of
cutting out, I filled in a form for the amount I required, which would be
sent to my father, who was the manager, or to Mr. Townley, to sign-it
was then sent by a messenger to the place where the cloth was kept, when
the amount would be returned to me; when the orders came back, signed
by my father, I would insert additional amounts, and so get more cloth-
from time to time I obtained pieces of cloth in that way, I first resorted
to dishonest practices at the prisoner's suggestion-he asked me if I could
get him a length of cloth for a pair of trousers--we were in the basement
at Doré's, when he said that, about the middle of last June--the prisoner
