ALBERT PEDDER (Detective Sergeant, E). After Ancourt was in custody
I received this letter from him (produced), and received permission to
visit bim in prison, when Foster and I both took down his statement.
Ancourt's statement before the Magistrate. “I can call evidence at the
trial to prove that I obtained that cheque in betting transactions, and
that I am innocent. The witness Hillier has committed gross perjury."
Ancourt, in his defence, stated that the man gave him the two chequies to
cash for him, and said that he would give him £l; he complained that his
witnesses were absent, and stated that the cheques produced were not prove
to be the proceeds of the burglary.
Guilty. He then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction at the Mansion
House on October 11th, 1897. Several other convictions were proved
against him.-Judgment on both prisoners respited.
