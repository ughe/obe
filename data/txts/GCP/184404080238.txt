1816. ELIZABETH NICHOLLS was indicted for stealing 2 handker-
chiefs, value 9s. 6d.; the goods of Richard Wade and others.
RICHARD WADE. I am a linendraper, and live in High-street, Deptford,
in partnership with my brothers. The prisoner was in our shop on the 30th
of March, and I received information - I told the young man to go on serving
her and let her go-after he had served her she was attracted to the other
side of the shop by a young man who was folding some shawls- I got into
conversation with her, and invited her to come out and look at our window,
which she did--I said there was one article I should like her to see- I took
her again into the shop to another part-I then jumped over the counter, and
said my young man told me she had got some handkerchiefs, and asked if she
had any objection to be searched-she said "No"-I said to my young man,
"Are you sure she has got the handkerchiefs ?"-he said, "Yes, I will show
you the same pattern in another colour"- these handkerchiefs were then lying
at the prisoner's feet-I am sure they hail not been there before--that part
was three or four yards from where she stood at first--they are mine.
Cross-examined by MR. WILKINS. Q. Is yours a large shop? 4. Yes-
there was another woman with the prisoner they sat at a counter, and White
was showing them handkerchiefs- they would be taken out of the box to be
shewn-there were other customers in the shop there were not less than four
young men in the shop-it was supper time---the other shopmen were on the
opposite side of the shop, but they were in view--I do not know how the
handkerchiefs got at her feet, but they were not there when I took her to the
place-the other woman was standing by her side when I asked to be allowed
to search them.
CE
STEPHEN WHITE. I am shopman to Mr. Wade. On the 14th of March,
a little after ten o'clock in the evening, the prisoner and another woman came
to look at some handkerchiefs-while I was showing them some, my attention
was aroused by seeing the prisoner try to draw the handkerchiefs from the
counter-she then asked for some lighter ones, and while I was going to fetch
them I communicated it to my employer, who told me to go on and serve
her ---I returned with the light handkerchiefs, and the prisoner left
the other woman, to decide on the handkerchief-I then saw the prisoner
draw the handkerchiefs off the counter-shortly after she stooped, and I sup
posed picked them up, she made a wry face, as if she had a pain in her leg.--
after that she had some ribbon, which she gave to the other woman-Mr. John
Smith was at the desk, and took the cash-he asked if I was certain about
the matter, and I looked behind the counter and missed the bandkerchiefs-
these are the handkerchiefs I saw her draw from the counter-I swear to them
- she was then invited by a young man on the opposite side to see some
shawls, and then Mr. Wade took her outside- I looked at the place where
she had been, and nothing was there-- I looked at her dress, and nothing was
clinging to it-she was brought back, and a place was cleared for her to go
to-Mr. Wade said, “My young man has missed some handkerchiefs, will
you allow me to search you ?"- they said "Yes"-he searched the other
woman first-I then saw the handkerchiefs on the ground in a crumpled
state,
Cross-examined. Q. How many young gentlemen were serving in the
shop? A. I did not notice there might have been four.
Not GUILTY.
