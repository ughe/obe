 2194.  RICHARD HUDSON was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 1 coat, value 2l.; 1 waistcoat, value 5s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 4s., and 1 hat, value 4s., the goods of William Henry Dickinson.
   WILLIAM HENRY DICKINSON. I am a shoemaker, and live with my mother, Mary Hudson, who is the prisoner's mother - I am the prisoner's half brother; I am the elder brother; the prisoner lodged with my mother in Luntly-place, Brick-lane; we slept in the back room first floor. On the 3rd of September I went out, locked the parlour door, and had the key - I found the door had been forced open when I returned; I missed my coat, waistcoat, hat, and shoes from my bed-room - the prisoner was then out, and did not return till Thursday morning; he then came home, and denied taking the things; I had not lent them to him.
   JOSEPH HAWES. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a coat, waistcoat, and shoes, pawned on the evening of the 3rd of September, by the prisoner - I am confident he is the man; it was between seven and eight o'clock.
 Prisoner. Last Monday week I was at Barnet-fair between eight and nine o'clock, which is the time he said at the office that I pawned them.
   JOHN BROOKS(Police-constable H 64) I took the prisoner at his mother's, where he lodged.
 GUILTY. Aged 19. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Six Months.

 2196.  JOHN EVANS and ANN PRESSER were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , 1 pillow and case, value 3s.; 1 sheet, value 2s., and 1 flat-iron, value 1s., the goods of Elizabeth Reily.
 ELIZABETH REILY. I live in  Little Windmill-street, St. James'; I am a widow. The prisoners took a furnished room of me as man and wife - they slept there that night, and the following; and when they came the third night I got an officer, who took them, and we missed the articles, which had been in the room when they took it.
   DANIEL CHAPMAN(Police-constable C 38). I received the two prisoners; I asked if they had pawned the property, and if they had got the duplicates, to give them up: Presser put her hand into her bosom, pulled out the duplicates, and gave them to me.
   GEORGE TURNER. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, which I took in of a female on the 7th of September, in the name of Mary Evans, No. 3, Windmill-street - I do not remember whether it was the prisoner; one of the duplicates was the one given.
   SAMUEL HOSLOCK. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pillow-case, pawned on the 8th of September, in the name of Ann Evans, No. 7, Earl-street; this is the duplicate I gave.
 Evans' Defence. I knew nothing about them till I came home at night, and then I said they should be fetched out directly.
 Presser. I pawned them, but intended to get them out that evening, but it was too late.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 EVANS -    GUILTY. Aged 37.
 PRESSER -    GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 2197.  JOHN ELMER was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of July , 1 jacket, value 10s., and 1 handkerchief, value 18d., the goods of Henry Alexander Reatt.
   WILLIAM HARK(Police-constable C 34). On the morning of the 11th of July, at two o'clock, I stopped the prisoner in Regent-street, and asked what he had under his coat; he said Nothing - I pulled it open, and found this jacket, and the handkerchief in the pocket of it - these are the articles the prosecutor swore to at the office, but he is not here; he gave his name as Henry Alexander Reatt.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. Were there any other persons about? A. Yes, several.
   MARY ANN JONES. I live in St. Martin's-street, and do needle-work. I saw the prisoner take the jacket off the flags in Regent-street- there was another person with him when he took it; I saw Mr. Reatt there - he took the jacket off, and gave it to another person, who put it on the rails; it fell off the rails, and the prisoner took it - he was pursued, and taken.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you know any thing of Mr. Reatt? A. I was in company with his brother, who is not here; I heard the prosecutor called by the name of Henry Alexander Reatt - I only knew him by the name of Henry; I always said I saw the prisoner take it.
 COURT. Q. What is Mr. Reatt? A. I do not know; he had no other jacket on - he had a scuffle with some other person; he took his jacket off, it fell, and the prisoner came and took it - he ran away, and I told of it.
 WILLIAM HARK. Reatt is a young gentleman, about sixteen or seventeen years old - I believe he had been struck, and he took his jacket off to return it; he gave his address in Charlotte-street - I went there, and heard he was gone abroad.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never ran away with the jacket - I was walking along.
