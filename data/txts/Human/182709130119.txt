 MR. CLARKSON called -
   JEFFREY EAST. I am a sawyer. and live in Wright's-buildings; the prosecutor is a tenant of mine - he has been badly off for the last five years. I never saw him with a watch in my life; he told me he had been robbed of one; I said it was odd, for I never knew him have one; he said,"Oh! Yes, I took it out of pawn yesterday morning from Philpot's, at Bermondsey." I know he often borrowed money to get victuals - I would not believe him on his oath.
 COURT. Q. Why? A. I believe him a very bad character - he has no lease of my house; I want to get rid of him- I never knew him swear false; he is given to drink; I think he was fresh when he spoke to me; he had come home in the night in question quite tipsy; I would not believe him if he was sober, for I believe he never had a watch.
 SAMUEL MARTIN re-examined. I was once taken to Union-hall for an assault, and was acquitted; this man's wife once took me there, but they were glad to make it up. I was never there at any other time; she charged me with indecently exposing myself.
 COURT. Q. Were you ever charged with any thing else? A. Never.
   THOMAS JACKSON. I live at No. 9, Wright's-buildings, Spa-road, Bermondsey, and live on my income. I have known Martin ten years; he was always in very indigent circumstances. I never saw him with a watch; I saw him on the 27th of August, in the morning, and told him I had read in the newspaper of his having been robbed of a watch- he said it was true, and that he had been hustled by three youths; I said I never saw him with one; he said, "I don't wish all the world to know what I do with my property;" he said he took it out of pawn at Thomas', which is now Philpot's; I said, "You must get a document from the pawnbroker to satisfy the Court that you had it;" he said there was no necessity for that; he afterwards said, "That is already done;" he said the person he was taking the geranium to, could prove that he saw it three-quarters of an hour before; I said, "Why, you was going there?" he said, "Yes, and while I was there, a person came in who had been robbed of 30s." I know Cribb's parents, but not till after this transaction.
 COURT. Q. Have you quarrelled with Martin? A. No.
   SAMUEL MARTIN. This man says very wrong. I was coming up the street, and he was very much inebriated, and called me an old rogue, and a d-d b-g-r, because I did not speak to him.
 - SHEEN. My husband lives in Gloucester-terrace. Martin came to my house with a geranium; I do not exactly know the day; he did not complain of his loss to me, or in my presence.
   LUCY PHILPOT. I keep a pawnbroker's-shop in Bermondsey-street. I know nothing of the prosecutor - I had no watch pawned in the name of Martin. I have searched the books to see; he has applied to me since, to ask if we give tickets; I said, "Yes, when we take pledges;" he appeared confused; I said perhaps he alluded to a watch which was inquired about; my husband said, "We have examined the file, and find no such thing."
 SAMUEL MARTIN. I had pawned a watch there some years ago, in the time of Mr. Thomas, and wanted to know if they could find the ticket.
   MARTHA EVANS. I am the wife of William Evans - we live in Wright's-buildings. I have known Martin five or six years; he is a very poor man - I never saw him with a watch. On the day the prisoners were committed, he came into my house and sat down, gave my son a paper and said, "There John, read that; your mother do'nt seem to believe I had a watch;" I said, "I never knew you had one;" he said, "Read that paper, it will convince you I took it out from Thomas the morning I lost it;" I did not see the paper.
   JOHN OVERILL. I live in Amelia-row, Spa-road, Bermondsey. i have known Martin twelve or thirteen years; he was always a poor indigent man; he has a pension of 9d. a day, as a soldier - he generally gets drunk when he receives it, and is then in want.

 1839.  JOHN COSGROVE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of James Lloyd Jones , from his person.
 JAMES LLOYD JONES I am a  draper, and live at Charing-cross. On the 5th of August I was in St. James'-park- two boys were going to fight, which caused a mob; a boy told me, a boy had taken my handkerchief - I felt and it was gone. I found the prisoner in Bertraun's charge. he pulled two handkerchiefs from his trousers; one of which was mine - I had not seen him near me.
   ANGELIOUS BERTRAUN. I was in the Park, and saw the prisoner take this handkerchief from the prosecutor; he walked round the crowd; I took him and found two handkerchiefs in his trousers - Jones claimed one.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you sure you saw all this? A. Yes.
 GUILTY. Aged 10. Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
 1840.  DENNIS CONNOR was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of August , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Edwin Henry Thomas , from his person.
 EDWIN HENRY THOMAS. I live at Birmingham. On the 2d of August I was in  Coventry-street, and heard a cry of Stop thief! from a gentleman who was with me; I felt my pocket, and my handkerchief was gone - I had had it a very few minutes before; I turned round and accused the prisoner of taking it - he denied it, but put his hands behind him, and dropped it on the pavement - I called the watchman, who took him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
