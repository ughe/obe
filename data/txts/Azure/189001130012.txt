MARY DEELEY. I live at 21 , Wimpole Mews—I am a widow—during

White 's four children whi I e his wife, the prison er, was in an lum in

still continued to take charge Of the children ; she helped me to wash

in about five minutes I heard screams in the other room as if

he fetched Gibbons, who forced it open, and went in, and I followed him
—I saw the second boy lying in the crib dead—I did not see the elder
boy—I fetched the baby, who is about six months old, and remained with
it till the police came—the prisoner was confined in July—after the
risoner came back on 10th December she several times mentioned about
Ying been sent to the asylum, but she did not see why she should have
been ; she did not complain of it; that was about all she said—I did not
know her before she went to the asylum—I was in the country—when she
came back she would wash and dress the children, but she did not take
particular notice of them—what she did was done kindly.
