which they sold by contract to the public, so you see they saw their
chance and quaked at it like a couple of pigs, but they are wrong,
for they know nothing-simply what they learnt from the police. Let
them beware of me in the future, for I am becoming dangerous against
people who injure me with lying stories. And let them all remember
that if I am deaf I am also clever. This is all I have to say against
my case, but nevertheless it is the solemn truth. (Signed) Carl Edward
Green. This is my statement written by my own hand.'

BEFORE THE RECORDER.
(Wednesday, April 27.)
FAWCETT, Selwyn (24, salesman), stealing one roll of silk, the
goods of Pawsons and Leafs, Limited.
Mr. L. A. Lucas prosecuted.
CHRISTOPHER LANE, warehouseman, Pawsons and Leafs, St. Paul's
Churchyard. At two o'clock on April 7 prisoner came in and asked
for a pattern of Wedgewood coloured silk. While I was getting the
pattern I saw prisoner put this piece of silk (produced) underneath
his coat. When he saw he was observed he pulled it out and put it
back on the counter. I called Mr. Smith, the head of the department,
and prisoner was given in charge.
