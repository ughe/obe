CHARLES Cox (Policeman, P 346.) On 12th April, abont twelve o'clock,
I was in Clifton-fields—I met Luscombe in Pomeroy-street- I remember
the people turning out from the Victoria public house, and among others
the prisoner-we were then standing on the other side of the street-the
prisoner crossed over, pointed to Luscombe, and said, “That is the b-
that took me to the station; it would take forty b- policemen to take
me there to-night; if ever you put a hand upon me again it will be worse
for you," or " I will do for you * -Luscombe told him that he had nothing
to say to him, and said, "Why don't you go away home ?"-a man then
came out of the public house with some beer; he asked for a drink-he
said, “Why don't you drink and go home?”-the prisoner said, “The police-
man did more than his duty last night;" and he said that he would keep
in the street all night if he liked-I then went on my beat, and left Lus-
combe to go on his- a few minutes afterwards Crutcher called me-I went
up the street, and found the prisoner in Luscombe's custody-Luscombe
was staggering back, and said to me, "Look out for the knife"-I made a
catch at the prisoner to catch hold of him very quickly, and I received a
blow from him on the side of my head with something very hard : I believe
it was not his fist-I did not see anything in his hand, but I think there
must have been something beyond that which nature gave him-a stone in
a handkerchief would produce such an effect as I felt; I was knocked down
senseless two or three minutes after I came to myself I found that Lus.
combe had taken the prisoner-I followed them to the station, and found
Luscumbe bleeding from the back-I saw the prisoner at the station, and
found on him this stone (produced)—it was done up in this handkerchief
in the way it is now; there was a knot tied above the stone to keep it there
-the prisoner was charged with stabbing Luscombe-he said, “You have
not found the knife that was all he said-he had been drinking, but was
not drunk ; he knew what he was about.
