 1527. CHARLES PEMBURY was indicted for stealing 1 watch, value 2 l  . 10 s  ., and 1 watch-chain, 1 d  .; the goods of Thomas Davis and that he had been before convicted of felony. THOMAS DAVIS I live in Sun-alley, Woolwich, and am a sawyer. On 26th of May, about eight o'clock in the morning, I went into the White Lion public-house—the prisoner was there in the parlour—I took my watch out of my pocket to wind it up—the prisoner said he would take it to the bar, and have it put to rights for me—I said, "No, let it alone; I shall put it to rights myself"—he took it out of my hand, and went off with it.
   Prisoner.  He gave it into my hand, and told me to go and pawn it. Witness  No, I did not—he did not bring anything back for it—I was not the worse for liquor—I have known him ever since he was born—I thought he might bring it back. GEORGE BARTON. I am in the employ of Mr. Carter, a pawnbroker, at Greenwich. On the 26th of May the prisoner came, and brought this watch—he pawned it for 12 s ., in the name of Thomas Smith—he said it was his own. JOHN GRIFFIN( police-constable R  229.) I apprehended the prisoner on the 3rd of June—I told him he was charged with stealing a watch from the prosecutor—he replied that he had settled matters with the prosecutor—I said I should take him to the station, and I asked what he had done with the watch—he said he had pawned it at Greenwich, and had lost the duplicate—he did not tell me at what shop he pawned it. JOHN WALKER( police-constable R  298.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction at Maidstone—( read—Convicted  9 th March , 1846, and confined six months )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY Aged 28.— Confined One Year .
   SURREY CASES .
   Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 1528. JAMES COOK was indicted for stealing 4 bushels of oats, value 15 s  .; and 1 sack, 2 s  ., the goods of Henry Dunkin , in a barge on the navi gable   river Thames .
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. DANIEL DAVIS. I live in Two Kings-row, Horsleydown, and am a watch-man in the employ of Messrs. Courage and Donaldson. On the evening of the 20th of April, about nine o'clock, I was in the neighbourhood of Horsley-down-stairs—the prosecutor, Mr. Dunkin, had two or three barges lying of there—I met the prisoner and James Bennett, who has been convicted—Bennett had a sack of oats, and the prisoner had an empty sack under his arm, and a hat, which I believe was Bennett's—(Bennett had no hat on)—the prisoner was in front—I stopped Bennett—the prisoner turned back, and asked what I had to do with Bennett, and held up his fist to me—I called out, "Police"—the prisoner dropped his empty bag, and ran away—the sack that Bennett had, contained oats.
