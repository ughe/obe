 1405.  ANN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 1 shirt, value 4s., the goods of Owen Parry ; and that she had been before convicted of felony.
 MARY COTTERELL. I am a warehouse-woman to  Owen Parry, Quadrant, Regent-street. On the 14th of April, I took home some work; while I was there the prisoner came and asked for some Denmark sattin - Mr. Parry said he had not any, and asked her if it was to make shoes; she said Yes; he said he did not sell that sort - she then turned out of the shop; he asked me what she had got; I said I did not know; he told me to go and see - I went after her; she dropped this shirt, and said "Here is your shirt;" I took her back, and laid the shirt on the counter.
   JOHN CORLEY(Police-constable C 144). I received charge of the prisoner on the 14th of April; I found on her a quantity of ribbon, a cotton handkerchief, several gilt seals, and a duplicate.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   CHARLES HENRY WATERS. I have a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, by the name of Ann Reed, which I got from Mr. Clark's office; I was present at her trial in September last, and know she is the person.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Seven Years.

 1406.  JOSIAH HUGH EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 1 mare, price 4l., the property of Joseph Shurley.
   JOSEPH SHURLEY. I live at Portland-town. On the 3rd of September I asked the prisoner to take my mare to Barnet fair, and sell her, or swap her away for another; he was not to take less than 4l. for her - I did not see him again till the 30th of March, when I met him in Smithfield I said, "Don't you recollect taking my mare to Barnet?" he said, "Oh, your name is Shurley;" I found he had taken it to Barnet, as I desired, but he never brought me the money.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1408.  MARY SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of April , 32 sovereigns, 8 half-crowns, 15 shillings, and 8 sixpences, the monies of Patrick Keefe , in his dwelling-house.
   PATRICK KEEFE. I lodge at Mr. Marriot's, Wild-court, Great Wild-street- the prisoner lodged in the same room with me and my wife. I missed this money from my box about two o'clock on the 9th of April; I had seen it safe on the Saturday fortnight before - it was in a green purse; I have three beds in my room, and let them out; my wife kept the key of the box: on unlocking it the purse and money were gone, with my marriage certificate, which was in the purse, between the silver and gold; it was all enclosed in a white silk stocking - the prisoner was taken up on the 18th; she slept in my room till then - my marriage certificate was found in her pocket, but only 1d. in money- four persons besides her slept in the room; the prisoner said the devil a farthing should I ever see again; she went out drunk on the Friday before, and was out all night, and on the Sunday night she was drunk.
   ELLEN KEEFE. I kept the key of my husband's box in my pocket. On the Friday before the money was missed, I left the room to fetch something for my husband - I left the key in the box; the prisoner was sitting in the window, and there was an old woman, who was very ill, and unable to get out of bed; I was out about five minutes, and when I retuned the key was still in the box; I had seen the money safe that day - I did not examine the box till Monday: when I told the prisoner the money was missed she said nothing about it.
 JOHN SUTTON. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner on the 18th of April, and found Keefe's marriage certificate on her; Mrs. Keefe directly said, "Oh, Sullivan! you are guilty, for that is my marriage line" - the prisoner replied, "The devil of a farthing of the money will you ever see again;" she was rather forward in liquor.
   PATRICK KEEFE. This is our marriage certificate.
 Prisoner's Defence. They were fighting on the Monday, this man, his wife, and brother-in-law - the wife took up the box and swore in a most desperate manner she would never live with him another day; she threw the box about- he took his clothes out of the box, and said, "You may go, but shan't have the money;" he then looked, and said,"Oh! I am robbed of all my money" - next day they went to a fortune-teller, then got the Bible, and turned it on me- as to the certificate, she knew I had got it, for I picked it up with some papers which the prosecutor tore; she told me to take care of it - the woman who slept in the room is not bed-ridden.
 MRS. KEEFE. I never saw the certificate loose about the room - it was between the gold and silver in the purse- the woman could not get up without help.
     NOT GUILTY.
