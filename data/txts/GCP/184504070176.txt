MARY Davis. My husband is a vellum-binder, and lives in Brunswick-
place, Backchurch-lane I know John Caffyn was in Brick-lane from half-
past five till turned half-past eight on the Monday evening-he was in the
lane, and had got a toasting-fork which he had bought to take home to his
mother-I had his little brother, five years old, at my place, to mind, I
took him home, and was coming back when I saw him in the lane--that
was about five o'clock, and it was about half past eight or a quarter to
nine o'clock when I saw him with the toasting-fork-he was not in the
lane three hours—he was doing nothing at five o'clock-he merely said,
"There you are, Mrs. Davis," and I said, “There you are"-he was walk-
ing along the street-I said, “Is your mother at home," and he said,
"Yes, she is not very well"-I did not see him all the time between
a quarter past five and half-past eight o'clock-I cannot tell what he was
doing in the mean time.
John Cox. I am a labourer and live in Boundary-street, at the back of
Shoreditch church. I was in company with John Caffyn on Monday after-
noon, this day week, and was with him at a public-house, at the corner of
Booth-street, Brick-lane, from eight o'clock in the evening till ten-I do
not know the eign of the house, or the landlord's name-I do not often go
there--we were up stairs-there was a set-to, sparring, for the benefit of a
young man- I dare say there were fifty people there--the room was full,
but no one else that I knew-we had a drop of porter--there was no one
with him but me.
John NEWELL re-escamined. Brick-lane is about twenty minutes' walk

PETER CAFFYN. I am the prisoner's father, and am a chair-maker,
