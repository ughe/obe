   MR. KELLY . Q.  I think you say it was about half-past six o'clock that you left the Castle hotel to canvass with Mr. Miller, and others? A.  It was—I cannot say exactly how long Mr. Miller remained canvassing in my company—I have no recollection of seeing Mr. Miller that evening after eight o'clock, or about that time—he continued canvassing with me, to the best of my recollection, till about eight o'clock—I shortly afterwards returned to the Castle—Mr. Miller was not with me—I did not set eyes on Mr. Miller for the rest of that night. MARY MASON. I am the wife of Mr. Mason, of Newcastle. I remem ber the last general election in July—I had been absent from home in the Isle of Man—I returned home on the Thursday before the nomination—Mr. Miller resided at our house, from the time of my return till the election had taken place—he slept there every night—he dined there on the Friday and the Sunday, and the Monday too—he did not dine there on the Satur day—I remember Saturday, the nomination day—he left home that day very early in the morning, as soon as he had his breakfast—I was at home during the evening of that Saturday—Mr. Miller came home that evening after eight o'clock, but not half-past eight o'clock—I first received him in the lobby of the house, he then went into the drawing-room—it was not half-past eight o'clock—he took tea with me in the drawing-room, and passed the remainder of the evening with me—he did not leave the house again before he went to bed-when he went to bed it was after ten o'clock, but it could not be more than half-past ten o'clock—he could not have left the house so as to be at the Castle between nine and ten o'clock, and come back, without my knowing it—I do not remember when my husband came home that night—I was gone to bed—I went to bed after Mr. Miller.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . I suppose you looked at your watch or clock? A.  I looked at the clock just after it struck eight—it had finished striking eight—Mr. Miller was not come in then—I felt hungry, having had no dinner that day, and ordered my servants to get me a cup of tea, and I would have some more with Mr. Miller when he came in, and while I was taking the first cup of tea he came in—I cannot, say whether he dined at our house on the Thursday—I was not at home—he dined at our house on Friday, with several other friends, and on Sunday he dined at our house—he went out to church on Sunday morning, and re turned and took a lunch, and then went into the pottery—we got our dinners—Mr. Miller told us not to wait, and we had just finished dinner when he came back—I never heard it said at all that Mr. Miller and my husband had been bribing—I never heard from my husband that he had been bribing voters at former elections—I cannot tell how far our house in from the Castle hotel, but I can walk it in about seven minutes—I cannot walk it in one or two—I can walk it in five—sometimes I walk it in seven sometimes in five—I cannot tell whether it is fifty yards from our house—it is more than twenty—we do not live in a street, we live on the London-road, and the Castle is in High-street—I mean to swear Mr. Miller was is our house at half-past eight o'clock, and was never out of my sighs till half-past ten o'clock—it was not half-past ten o'clock when he went to feed—I swear he was in our house at half-past eight o'clock, and never left the room till he went to bed-ours is a private house—there are two outlets to it.
