Cock and Johnson—( read, "H.S., No. 2, 2nd April, 1839, double gold assay"—that is my writing as far as that—this "W 4 1 5/8" means "worse 4 carats 15/8"—that is Mr. Johnson's writing—that means less standard, or worse than standard 4 carats 1 gr. 5/8—this "No. 1 H.S., 3rd April, 1839, double gold," on the other assay paper is my writing—the "W 4 2-1/8" means worse 4 ounces 2 grains, and 1/8—they have each Mr. Johnson's signature to them—On Monday, the 8th of April, I remember obtaining some sovereigns from Messrs. Bult's—there were from one hundred to one hundred and twenty, as near as I can recol lect—I got some from Messrs. Ransom's on the same day—I do not recol lect getting any more at any other place that day—during that week I got some from the shop in Coventry-street—I do not recollect How much gold I got from Ransom's—I got it from various cheques—I do not recollect what I got from Coventry-street—it was by Mr. Solomon's directions that I got this gold—I changed notes with the trades people in that week—with three or four people—I remember taking a £100 note to Ransom's during the week beginning Monday, the 8th—I took one there to get change, and one to pay in to Mr. Solcmon's account—one was changed for notes and sovereigns.
   Q.  I do not know whether you are able to speak positively to the bars or not, but are these other four bars such as you took from your roaster's? A.  Yes—I will not undertake to swear to them, as they have been out of my possession—they varied in size as these do, but I made no mark on them—I believe them to be the same.
   COURT . Q.  Do you say the same of the bar alloyed with silver? A.  Yes.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You speak positively to the silver bar? A.  No, I will not undertake to swear positively to either bar, as they have been out of my possession.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Where were you last night? A.  After I left the court I went home with Selim and Isaac Solomon—not a word was said about what transpired yesterday—I con tinue in their employ—I was the general melter to Mr. Solomon—he melted a great quantity of watch cases, outsides and insides—it is safer to melt than to sell them—Mr. Solomon had a great deal to do in melting—not to the amount of 10,000 l . a year—we used to send what we melted to Messrs. Bult's, and Cock and Johnson's—I do not know to what amount we sent them—it was not 8 or 10,000 l . a year—I should think about half that.
