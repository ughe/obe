 (Wednesday, June 28.) SEPP  , August (23, wrestler), was indicted for and charged on the Coroner's inquisition with the wilful murder of Ah Ling,   otherwise Sea Heng : also indicted for feloniously wounding Lee Fong , with intent to murder him, or with intent to do him grievous bodily harm.
 Mr. Bodkin and Mr. Leycester prosecuted; Mr. Roome defended.
 Police-constable  WAYLIFF , 12 KR, proved a plan for use in the case. FLORRIE PHILLIPS, factory girl. On the night of May 6 I was walking in West India Dock Road with Catherine O'Shea and Emma Goddard. We turned into Pennyfields, and outside No. 5, which is a Chinese boarding-house, we saw three or four Chinamen, who addressed us and followed us up the street. Lee Fong was talking to O'Shea. Three foreign sailors came up and began insulting the Chinamen. Then one of the foreigners, a fair man, rather thick build, struck Lee Fong; I cannot recognise this foreigner. I tried to separate the men but could not, and I ran for the police. I after wards saw the body of Ah Ling; I did not know that man; he was not one of the men who followed us up the street. On May 7 I went to the Scandinavian Home and saw a number of men; prisoner was not amongst them; there was no one there resembling the foreign sailor I saw. On May 9 I went to the police station and saw a number of men; I picked out prisoner as resembling the man I saw fighting with Lee Fong. CATHERINE O'SHEA. I was walking with Lee Fong when three foreign sailors, who were behind us, said something rude to him; he said, "What for do you call this; I never speak to you nothing." I afterward saw the tallest foreigner of the three strike Lee Fong.
