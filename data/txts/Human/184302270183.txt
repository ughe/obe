Yes; but when they are very small, and we cannot sling them, they are carried down by hand—there was no necessity for this case of brandy going through the loop-hole—I have known the prisoner many years—he was in the emply of a carman named Elsom—I never knew any thing against him.
   COURT . Q.  Do you allow the carmen to take the cases out of No. 10? A.  No; we order one of the gangs of men to deliver them to the carmen. GEORGE SMITH. I am a labourer in the Docks. On the 7th of February I was called by Mr. Evans to load for the prisoner—I came down from No. 11 to No. 10—while I was coming down I saw the prisoner standing at the end of the gangway, at the foot of the stairs that lead to No. 11—I passed on to Mr. Evans, and Watkins came along directly after—in consequence of what Watkins said I was sent to where the cart was loading—I found the prisoner standing at the tail of his cart, unfolding a tarpaulin in the bottom of the cart—the case of brandy stood at his feet, under the tail of the cart—I looked at the case; he turned round, and said to me, "I think I have made a mistake; I want No. 8, this is not it"—he took a paper from his pocket—this little case could have been got conveniently into the curt, under the tarpaulin. ROBERT WATKINS. I was going up the stairs, from No. 10 to No. 11, on that day—I turned round, and saw a person similar to the prisoner, with a straw hat on—I saw him go our, with a case on his shoulder—I called out—we went round, and saw the prisoner with a case, at the tail of the cart—it was such a case as I had seen the man carry—the prisoner said a man had brought it out of the ground floor, with a paper cap on.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How neat were you to the person? A.  About thirty yards from him—I was going up the stairs—I could see any person going out. EDWARD CULBERT( police-constable H  200.)
