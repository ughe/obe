THIRD COURT.- COURT.-Wednesday Yednesday and Thuraday, April 23rd and 24th, 1890.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
383. CHARLES SEALEY (24) and TOM SMITH (25), Burglary in
the dwelling-house of Frank Oxley, and stealing twelve cigars and 3sry
his goods and money.
MR. HEDDoN Prosecuted, and MR. K. FRITH Defended Smith.
SAMURL RODWELL (Policeman 371). About 1.40 a.m. on 28th March I
was on duty in Orehard Street, Ball' Pond Road-I received information,
in consequence of which I went to the Britannia beer-house-] found the
taproom window broken-I pushed open the window, and got through
by standing on a tub that was beneath the window--I found both
prisoners sitting on the roof of the house smoking eigars- blew my
whistle- said, 40 What are you doing here re?"- Smith said, 44 You need
not blow your whistle, we will go quietly another constable came up
on the roof, and between us we took the prisoners into custody, and to the
station-] found on Smith 1s. 21d. bronze in his trousers poeket, a table
knife, two latehkeys, a pocket knife, and a begging petition-wher
charged he made no reply-I found this cigar on the roof afterwards, and
I found these three cigars in the street.
Cross-ecamined by MR. K. FRITH. Smith works for his lather, I know
nothing against him-he was admitted to bail-he was perfectly sober-
I don't think he had been drinking-I cannot say one way or the other.
THOMAs BRYsox. At 1.45 on 28th March I heard a police whistle-aI
went in the direetion of the sound, and on the roof of the beerhouse I
found Rodwell detaining Smith-I took Sealey into custody-I said, "I
shall take you to the station he did not speak-he said nothing at the
station-] searehed him and found 1s. 10d. bronze in his trousers pocket
-Sealey was perfectly sober-Smith was sober, as far as I could see.
FRANK OxLEY. I keep the Britannia beer-house, Orchard Street-on
the night of 27th March I left my house safe about eleven o'clook,
having fastened it up-the taproom window was not broken, it was all
right--I did not sleep on the premises that night ; the house was
quite empty-about eight o'clock next morning, in consequence of
information, I went to Dalston Police-station, where I saw some cigars
like these and about 3s. worth of coppers- went to my beer-
house-I found the taproom window broken- had left a dozen or
fifteen cigars in the bar-this is the class of cigar I was sellingI
had left 2s. in the till and 1s. in the back room, all in coppers found
they had gone.
