CARISTOPHER EMMETT. I am in the service of Mr. Brotherton, a chemist
at Islington-about 4 o'clock on 28th August the prisoner came for two
ounces of Epsom salts-she offered me a penny-I told her they were three
halfpence, she said she only gave a penny at other shops- I told her they
were three halfpence, and she said, "Then I must change half-a-crown" -
she put a half-crown down-I took it up and gave it to Mr. Brotherton
- he said it was bad- he came into the shop where the prisoner was-he
spoke to her, and asked her where she got it-she said she got it from a
cabman the night before-I went for a constable and when I came back the
prisoner was gone from the shop-I went to a greengrocer's shop, and saw the
prisoner come out-I pointed her out to the constable, and she was bronght
back to Mr. Brotherton.
Cross-examined. Q. Who was the greengrocer 4. Mr. Gerrard-he is
not here- I did not see the prisoner go in there—I heard she had gone in
--I fetched the constable-we waited about and I saw the prisoner come
out of that shop.
