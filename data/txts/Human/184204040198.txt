I am a City police-constable. On the 29th of March about half-past eight o'clock in the morning, I was on duty in Hounds ditch, and saw a cab  stop at the corner of Cutler-street—the prisoner got out, paid the man his fare, and brought out of the cab  this bag, with this clock in it—he held it till another party got out, and then gave it to him, and both went up Cutler-street—I followed them, and when they found I was following them they dropped it and run away—I took it up, cried "Stop thief," and saw the prisoner stopped by a brother constable—I never lost sight of him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not the other man look round and see you? A.  The prisoner looked round—he was not carrying it when it was dropped—they had gone through Cutler-street, nearly to the bottom of Whitecross-street, about 300 yards, before they dropped it. THOMAS MALIN( City police-constable, No.  74.) On the 29th of March I was on duty in Petticoat-lane, heard a cry of "Stop thief/' and saw the prisoner and another run down Harrow-alley from Cutler-street—I stopped the prisoner—the other made his escape—the officer was behind with the clock—the prisoner said, "It is not me, Sir, it is the other one;" and the other one said, "It is not me, Sir, it is this one."
   Cross-examined. Q.  But the other did not say so in the prisoner's pre sence? A.  He said it in my presence—I had quite sufficient to take this one—if I had taken him I should have lost the prisoner—the prisoner said he had met the other man at six o'clock in the morning, at the Cat and Mutton public-house, Cat and Mutton Fields, that he waited there till the other fetched a bag, and they went together to a stack of bricks, and there found the clock..
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined One Year.
