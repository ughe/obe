fives; I met Ball in High Street, Borough, a day or two ago, and he told

soner said, “ Ball had no right to say that; he cannot prove it"-I said I
should take him to Bow Lane Station and we should see-I took him
there, and he was charged with uttering a 201. note at the butcher's, in
Great Trinity Lane-the inspector said, “Oh! Mr. Curtis's ?”– I said,
“Yes"-the prisoner said, “That note I changed with Mr. Curtis I re-
ceived from Mr. Gordon, Park Farm, West Croydon," and produced the
book again-Mr. Curtis was sent for and identified the prisoner, who gave
his name, John Lockwood, Prince of Wales beer shop, Church Street,
Borough-he said he had seen Gordon last Sunday night, 31st March, at
his house.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you been in the police ? A.
Thirteen years I have been examined many times I met Ball and asked
him where Lockwood was-he said that he was keeping out of the way-
I told Lockwood that, and he said that Ball had no right to say so and
could not prove it-I asked him about the 51. note-he said, "I received
that from Mr. Waters, 126, Waterloo Road, a customer who very often
comes to my house in a trap with two others."
GEORGE SCOTT (City Detective Sergeant). On the evening of 5th
April I was at Bow Lane Station and heard the prisoner say that he got
the 201, note from John Gordon, of Park Farm, West Croydon-I searched
him and found the pocket-book produced and another one-I have looked
through them, but find no entries of the numbers of bank notes.
JOSEPI TYRRELL CURTIS (re-examined). I have brought the book in
which I entered the address the prisoner gave me : "Mr. Gordon, Park
Farm, East Croydon "-I wrote that down at the moment in blue ink.
