654. THOMAS JAMES GILL was indicted for stealing 2 pairs of
trowsers, value 68., the goods of Samuel Shaw: and 1 jaoket, value 34.
the goods of Thomas Dobson; in a certain vessel in the navigable river
Thames.
SAMUEL SHAW. I am master of the schooner Vigilant. On the 26th
Dec. she was lying at Simmons'-wharf, Tooley-street-the prisoner was my
apprentice-I missed him on that day, and not ten minutes after he was gone
I missed out of the cabin two pairs of canvass trowsers--they have not been
found-I had four men on board beside the prisoner.
Thomas Dobson. I missed a jacket and a satin waistcoat from the fore
castle of that vessel- this is my jacket--I had not lent it to the prisoner.
GEORGE WALKER JUDGE. I am a Thames police-inspector. I went to the
prisoner's father's house, and found this jacket there--I apprehended the
prisoner there, I told him he was charged with stealing a jacket, a waistcoat,
and two pairs of trowsers-he said he had pawned them at a house in White
chapel -- he said Tom bad lent him the things; he meant to get the waistcoat
out, and give it to him.
JAMES Gill. The prisoner is my son. I delivered this jacket to the
officer-my boy took it off in my house, and I took possession of it.
Prisoner's Defence. I asked Dobson if he would let me take the things at
shore; he said, “Yes."
Thomas Dobson re-examined. I did not-it was not likely when I wa
going to sea.
Guilty. Aged 18.--Confined Nine Months. rs
