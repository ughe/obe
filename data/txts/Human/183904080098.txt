1285. RICHARD DYER was again  indicted for stealing, on   15th of March , 4 spoons, value 4 s  .; and 1 pair of sugar tongs, value 6 d  .; the goods of Jane Lane ; and that he had been previously convicted of felony. JANE LANE. I am a widow. The prisoner lodged in my house—I missed these spoons and tongs from a tea-chest down stairs—it was locked, but the key was in it—these are them. WILLIAM HALL. I searched the prisoner—he gave me the duplicated the spoons, which was sewn up in his small-clothes. THOMAS BARFORD. I am a pawnbroker. I took in these spoons of the prisoner on the 16th of March—I believe I gave him a half-crown, but I cannot swear. JOHN PARNELL. I am an officer. I produce a certificate of the pri soner's conviction—I got it at Mr. Clark's office—( read )—he is the man.
   Prisoner.  I was in distress—I intended to redeem them—Mrs. Lane said she would forgive me if I told her. GUILTY. Aged 31.— Transported for 14 Years.
 1286. EDWARD GARDENER was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of April , 1 tea-kettle, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Mrs. Whitlock . GEORGE FELTHAM. I am a police-inspector. I met the prisoner last Saturday night, at eight o'clock, in the Harrow-road—he bad a round frock on, and was carrying this kettle under it—I asked what he had got—he said what he had was his own—that he brought it from his boat, and was going to take it round to another boat—he then said he was going to take it to be repaired—I told him to take me to the boat he had takes it from—he took me to a boat, but the man said no kettle had been takes from there. WILLIAM WHITLOCK. I am a foot-boy. My mistress gave me this kettle for clearing out two cellars—I put it under a water-butt—the prisoner had been employed in doing jobs, and we never missed any thing before. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months.
 1287. EDWARD MANNING was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of April , 1 hat, value 14 s  .; and 1 hat box, value 3 d  .; the property of Henry Sidney Down , his master. JAMES PINK. I live with Henry Sidney Down, a hatter in Holborn. The prisoner was his porter. On the 6th of April, the boy in the shop gave the prisoner a hat in a box to take to Mr. Beeton's, in Bloomsbury—the gentleman came afterwards to inquire for it. WILLIAM MURDOCK. I am a pawnbroker. I took in this hat and box is pledge of the prisoner last Saturday night.
   Prisoner.  I pawned it, being Saturday—I did not think it was particular to go home that night—I did it to make up a sovereign I lost, for fear I should be turned away. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months.
   OLD COURT .— Saturday, April  13 th,  1839.
   Third Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
