MR. EDWIN JAMES, Q.c., in support of the plea, contended that the
wounding with intent to murder, charged in the first Count of the former
indictment, and of which the prisoner was acquitted, was in effect the same
accusation as the present. It was not disputed that it arose out of the same
transaction and from the same wounding. It was clearly laid down in Chitty's
Treatise on the Criminal Law, p. 455, that it was not in all cases necessary that
the two charges should be precisely the same in degree; it was suficient that
an acquittal of one would show that the defendant could not be guilty of the
other. The present case fell within that principle; the wounding was the
same, the penalty was the same, death in either case. The mere fact of the
death of the party wounded did not affect the question ; for, although not the
ordinary course, there was no reason why, if the death had occurred at the
time, the prisoner should not have been tried for the pery offence of wounding
with intent to murder, of which he had been acquitted ; nor would it hare
been any answer to such a proceeding to say that, because the man was dead,
therefore the prisoner could not be tried on such a charge. It was analogout
to the case of a party acquitted of a common assault, which he might plead is
bar to any future indictment for a felonious assault, there being but one
assault, and the felonious intention and the transaction being identical with the
assault of which there had been a previous acquittal. As an authority for this
proposition, see the Crown v. Robinson, 5 Deas and Anderson's Reports,
261; also 5 Law Chronicle, Regina y. Walker, 2 Moody and Robinson, 446.
Upon these and other authorities (to be referred to), he contended that this case
came within that principle of the law which declared that no man's life should
be placed in jeopardy twice for the same offence.
