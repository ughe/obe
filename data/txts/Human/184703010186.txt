COURT . Q.  Tell us exactly what occurred at the time your money was taken? A.  After coming in from the bar I had the money in my hand—I counted it after getting change at the bar—it was all right—I had 17 s . or 18 s .—I had part of it in my pocket, and the other in my hand—I took out a sove reign, to pay for some cold meat, and bread, and porter—I took in my hand what change I had got, and put it into my pocket—when I went in he saw me putting my hand into my pocket, he took hold of my hand, and took what I had in my hand, and what was in my pocket.
   Q.  But you have said before, that he asked what money you had got? A.  Yes, about five minutes before I changed the sovereign he asked what money I had got—I was putting my hand again into my pocket—I had my pocket up, not to make a noise—I let it go willingly, because I thought he would not take what was in my pocket at the bottom, and he took it all—he had got the money from me before he assigned the reason that it was to pay for what was to happen in the church—he got it from me without that explanantion—it was against my consent—I only did not take steps to recover it because I believed at the time what he said.
   Q.  Did you not say on another occasion, that when he asked what money you had got, you said, "I have not much;" he asked you to let him see how much, and you took some of the money out, and was counting it? A.  He asked five minutes before, had I much money—I said, "I must look sharp, I have not much money now, scarcely as much as will free us from the Church"—I was not counting it—I did not say I was counting it when he took it out of my hand—I was counting it at the bar, and I was looking at it to see if it was right—as I came from the bar I was putting it into my pocket, and he thrust his hand across, and took hold of it—I did not intend to part with my money to him when he took it, but he quieted me by telling me it would be spent in the Church—he took it without my consent. MARGARET MALONE. I am the sister of Catherine Malone. I was at the public-house in Aldersgate-street with them—I was standing by the side of the table—the prisoner was sitting by my sister's side—he ate his breakfast—my sister had one knee on the top of the other—the prisoner put his hand in her pocket, took up the money, and put it in his own pocket—she made an attempt to take the money out of his hand—she got him by the two hands and tried to get it—he put it into his pocket, and said he should wear the breeches—I went with them to Lambeth—there was a church at the side of the street, and a public-house at the other side—he stopped the cab  man at the public-house—we got out of the cab  —he pointed and said, "That is the Church"—he went into the public-house and called for half-a-pint of rum—I followed him and brought him out by the body—the publican asked what out intention was—I said they were going to be married, and he said, "You have not many minutes to delay"—it was only ten minutes to twelve o'clock—the prisoner said, "Where is Kitty ?—I said, "There she is"—he followed her up, and between her and me he ran away—he had got all my sister's money, and goods, and everything else—she had not a farthing left.
   Cross-examined. Q.  In the public-house did you not hear the prisoner whisper to her, and then take the money? A.  No—he did not like her well enough to whisper—I never saw a young man that had such a dislike to a young woman in company—he did not whisper before he put his hand in her pocket—this is my mark to this deposition—( read  —"On the day they were going to be married I went with them to the public-house in Alders gate-street—the prisoner whispered to her, and then put his hand down to her pocket."
