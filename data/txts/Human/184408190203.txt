I worked for Mr. Patrick at the time in ques tion and live in the Waterloo-road. On the morning of the 1st of July, when I was going to work down to my master's yard, I saw something lying just outside the gate of Mr. Patrick's yard—there are two gates, and this was the inner one—it is a wide road—there are two yards within one large gate—the wall of Mr. Bowerman's house runs down by the side of the gate—I did not know what it was, but on going near to it I thought it was a child—I communicated what I had seen to a person named Glaire—I went quite close to it—I thought it was a child—it was naked—it was lying about two yards from the wall.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time in the morning was it? A.  A quarter to nine o'clock—it was two or three yards from the side of the wall, some dis tance from the wall—this gateway is a private way—it is open for any one that works for Mr. Patrick—not for strangers to pass through—people do not go down there—it does not lead anywhere—it is all open there—there is no covering at all—I should say it is as wide as thit Court—anybody in the main road could see down the road and see what I saw—it was six, seven, or eight yards from the main road—I did not see any other per son there—there is an outer gate which comes into the Belvidere-road—that is open before six o'clock. EDMUND GLAIRE. I am one of Mr. Patrick's labourers. On the 1st of July I received a communication from Moody, in consequence of which I went into the roadway leading to my master's premises, and saw a new born female child—I picked it up and put it in my apron—the navel string was exposed, and through that I observed its not being many moments born—on the way to the doctor's I looked at it, and it was foaming at the mouth—I observed a lump on the side of its head, and the shoulder very much dis figured—I took it to Dr. Carruthers's shop, by the York-road, Lambeth—a policeman was sent for—the child was alive.
