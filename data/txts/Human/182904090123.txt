 957.  ANN KING was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of March , 13 yards of printed cotton, value 7s., the goods of William Kemp Hodges.
   CHARLOTTE HODGES. I am the wife of William Kemp Hodges, linen-daper, of the Minories. The prisoner came to our shop, on the 6th of March, and asked to look at some prints - I shewed her some; she looked at about three, then took a piece of cotton from the counter, and ran away with it; I gave an alarm, she was pursued, and brought back with it.
   RICHARD BOOTH. I heard a cry of Stop thief! and took the prisoner, who was standing still, about one hundred yards from the shop - she had this print hanging on her arm.
   WILLIAM KEMP HODGES I saw the prisoner in my shop on the 6th of March; I was in the counting-house; Mrs. Hodges made an alarm, and the prisoner was brought back - the print has my mark on it.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 958.  SUSAN JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of March , 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of George Knapp.
   GEORGE KNAPP. I am a pawnbroker. and live in Golden-lane. The prisoner came in on the 30th of March to redeem an apron; she asked the price of another apron, and pulled out some others; Hare watched her -I heard something snatched from the door, and she was brought back with this handkerchief, which is mine.
   GEORGE HARE. On the 30th of March I saw the prisoner pull the handkerchief from the door; she was then on the threshold - she put it under her shawl and was going off; I took hold of her - she said "Don't, don't;" I brought her back - the handkerchief has my master's mark on it.   GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 959.  WILLIAM HILLMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of March . 3 jugs, value 2s. 6d.; 12 pap boats, value 3s.; 4 busts, value 5s.; 65 egg-cups, value 1ls.; 58 cups, value 6s. 6d.; 54 saucers, value 5s. 6d.; 29 basons, value 7s.: 1 milk-pot, value 3d.; 7 glass cruets, value 14s.; 144 toys, value 2s.; 148 plates, value 18s. 6d.; 54 saltcellars, value 8s.; 12 coffee-cups, value 3s. 6d., and 1 china dog, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Mansfield and another, his masters; and EDWARD PAYSON was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, he well knowing them to have been stolen.
   JOHN MANSFIELD. I manage the business of the prosecutors, who are earthenware manufacturer s, and live in Barbican. Hillman was in their employ for three or four years, as a kind of porter- I was going along Smithfield on the 26th of March, and overtook Hargrave with a basket of goods on his head; he did not give me any information, but I sent a person to Payson's with my young man, who is not here; he brought a pattern of some goods; I went to Payson's lodging on the 2d of April, in Three Tun-court, Redcross-street, and found him there - I told him I had accused my young man, Hillman, of robbing me, and he had confessed it, and said that Payson had received the goods and paid 6d. per dozen for them; he said he had not, but that he had received the goods for gown pieces, which Hillman had had of him, and he had given him some money besides; the price of the goods were two-thirds less than they could be purchased for on his own account - I saw the two prisoners afterwards together; Payson asked Hillman whether what I had said was true - Hillman said it was, about his paying 6d. a dozen for them; Payson denied having paid any money for them, except two or three shillings - there was property found in both their lodgings, cups, saucers, and a great many other things; the greater part was found in Payson's lodgings.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. What did you find at Hillman's? A. The officer has it; I had a communication with Hillman before I saw Payson, but not before I sent to him - I did tell Hillman that if he would confess the extent to which he had been robbing us, I would not prosecute him; and he said, what Hargrave had was all he had robbed us of.
   GEORGE HARGRAVE. I deal in earthenware, and live in Sandy-street, Bishopsgate. I have seen the prisoners, but not in company - I dealt with Payson once only, which was on the 26th of March; I bought twelve dozen of plates, five dozen of egg-cups, and four dozen and half of salts; I paid 1s. 3d. a dozen for the plates - it was no very great bargain - I could buy them at Mr. Mansfield's for 1s. 4d.; I have the articles now at home - I have sold about a dozen egg-cups; Mr. Mansfield saw me going along, and told me they were his; Payson told me he had bought them at Mr. Bullen's sale.
 Cross-examined by MR. RYLAND. Q. Where does Mr. Mansfield live? A. In Barbican; I bought them between eleven and twelve o'clock, and he met me about one - I had seen Payson the day before in Petticoat-lane, where I stand every afternoon to sell goods; he asked me if I would buy some blue muffin-plates, and told me he lived at No. 3, Three Tun-court, Redcross-street - I offered him a 5l. note; there was some difficulty about changing it, and I went to Mr. Mansfield for change as I deal there - I told him I should go there; he made no objection - I returned in about two hours, and he was still there.
