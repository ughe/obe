On the 16th of June, about ten minutes past nine o'clock in the evening, I went into the shop of my son James William Benson, a silver  smith and jeweler, on Cornhill—I saw the prisoner sitting in a chair at the counter—my son was behind the counter, showing him some watches—the prisoner said he should not have the chain that night, but would come back to-morrow evening—I sat in the chair next him—he had watches in his hand several times—he rose, said, "I shall take this," took a watch in his left hand, and ran out of the shop on to the pavement of the Royal Exchange—I called, "Stop thief!"—my son caught him—I called the young man out of the shop to assist his master, as I was afraid to leave—he went out—as we were bring ing the prisoner across the road the watch fell, or was thrown from him—my son picked it up, gave it to me, and I gave it to the policeman—this pro duced is it—the prisoner was given in charge—he said, "You Have found no watch on me"—it had been dropped then. JOSEPH DAVIES. I am shop man to Mr. Benson—I heard the call of "Stop thief," went after the prisoner, and helped to secure him—I saw the watch picked up—this produced is it. EDWARD HOPDUP( City policeman. ) I took the prisoner in custody—the watch was given me by Mr. Benson.
   Prisoner.  I plead guilty. GUILTY. Aged 21. Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor  .— Confined Six Months .
 1496. WILLIAM HUNT was indicted for stealing 1 pint of linseed oil, value 3 d  .; the goods of Joseph Gordon Davis , his master. JOSEPH GORDONO DAVIS. I am a builder, and live at Pimlico. On the 14th of June, in the morning, I found this bottle of oil secreted in an iron cistern on my premises—I suspected a man named Cotterell, but about twelve o'clock I saw the prisoner coming out of the front door of my house with this oil—there is but one door—I had seen him three or four minutes previously at the part of the house where the oil was—I taxed him with it—he pulled it out of his pocket, and said he brought it from home—I said, "It is false, it is my oil"—he said his wife gave it him, that she bought it at Roberts's in Store-street—I went with him to Roberts's, and sent for his wife—both his wife and the shopman in his presence denied all knowledge of it—I sent for a policeman, he had not told a lie, he said "Yes"—the bottle is his own.
   Prisoner. Q.  How can you prove I saw you there, and at No. 14, in the next street, there was a trace where somebody had emptied oil—I did not see you take it. ANDREW SWANTON CRIPPS( policeman. ) The prisoner was given in my charge—the oil was given me by Mr. Davis—as I went to the station with the prisoner, he told me he had to oil some paintings for his mother, and that it was his own—he did not say how he came by it—at the station he said he had bought it of Mr. Scott, at Walliam-green, on Friday evening last. CAROLINE SCOTT. I live at Walliam-green, Fulham—I saw the prisoner on Friday—he had some oil and turpentine of me mixed together, but I did not sell him any plain oil.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The bottle of oil belongs to me; I bought this oil on Thursday, and emptied it into a jar; it was on Friday night that I bought some oil and turps. JOSEPH GORDON DAVIS re-examined.  I can swear this is pure oil. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined One Month .
