He said he had saved it from his wages last week—I think he said "got" it, but I will not be certain which—it was one or the other—I knew him by sight, but not by name. WILLIAM SMEE. I am a constable attached to the Post Office. On the morning of the 15th April I was watching this water closet—I relieved Peak about half-past 6 o'clock—it was one of the letter carriers' closets, the second from the corner, next the iron gate—I remained there until a quarter past 7 o'clock—I could see the entire of the closet through the grating—there is a gas light in the closet—up to that time nobody had entered the closet—at a quarter past 7 o'clock the prisoner came in—he turned himself round, and fastened the door—he then arranged his clothes and sat down on the seat—I then saw him put his left hand into his left coat pocket at the side of him (it was a shooting coat with two pockets)—he took something out, of the appearance of a letter, and brought it in front of him—he tore something with his hand of the appearance of a letter, but he sat in such a stooping position that I could not exactly see whether it was a letter or not—the paper was torn up in small pieces—I then saw him put his right hand behind him to the pan, and drop pieces of paper of the appearance of a letter—I saw a post stamp on part of it as it was felling—it was a red stamp—I directly turned off the water, to prevent them being washed away—after about a minute or so, the prisoner got up, arranged his clothes, and went out, and directly afterwards Blackburn came in—Peak and I were both together all the time—he left me for about a minute, and returned directly—he had the opportunity on his return of seeing into the water closet—he had not so good a position as I had—he could see the person in the closet—he had a better sight of his face when he came in than I had—when Peak went for Blackburn I remained watching, to see that no one else came in—no one but the prisoner came to that closet up to the time of Blackburn's coming—when he came he commenced taking out the fragments of a letter—I saw writing on them—it looked like a letter—it was all in small pieces—I went with him into his shop, which is in the vaults, and there he put them into a bucket of water, and washed them—we then put them together, and saw the address on them—I afterwards delivered them to Mr. Sculthorpe, after Blackburn had put his initials on some portions of them—I have here on this piece of tissue paper the papers I saw Blackburn take out of the pan, and also a portion of two cards—they are the same pieces that I received from Black-burn—one of the cards bears the name of "Mr. G. T. Attwater," and the other "Mrs. Frederick Davidge"—they had been torn up, but have since been put together—I afterwards gave information to Mr. Gapes, the inspector of letter carriers, and was in the office when he took the prisoner into Mr. Sculthorpe's private room—I heard Peak say to him, "You have been down to the water closets this morning"—the prisoner said, "Yes, I have"—Peak said, "The second one on the right from the door, in the letter carriers' closets?"—he said, "Yes"—Peak then said, "We saw you tear up a letter this morning"—the prisoner made no answer to that—I then searched him—in his left trousers pocket I found a purse containing a sovereign, half a crown, and a sixpence, and in his right trousers pocket
