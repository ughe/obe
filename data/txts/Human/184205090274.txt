   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You cannot tell how many people might have access to the room? A.  They have a great many acquaintances I believe—the street-door is left for anybody to come in and out who has got a key—it is a street-doorkey, and not a latch-key—I have opened the door myself at times to persons who come—I slept till six o'clock—I generally get up at seven—I get out and open the shutters sometimes before I get up. CHARLES STEWART. I am a policeman. On the 5th of April I went to No. 9, Whiting-street, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, and received some articles from Mrs. Austin, which I produce—I inquired if there was anybody in the room—I went up, and found Petty and Logden in the same room as the bedstead was—it was about ten o'clock in the morning—whether it was the front or back-room I cannot say, as it is a curiously-constructed house—Mrs. Parker and Mrs. Austin stood in the passage—when I went into the room I told the prisoners they must go with me to the station—they said nothing—I searched them, and found nothing—I searched the room again next day, and found several articles belonging to the club.
   MRS. AUSTIN re-examined.  These are what I found in the bed—I took them into my room, and the policeman took them out of my room—he took Petty and Logden in the room Warren lodged in—I removed the articles into the room I occupied myself at the back of the house built over the yard—I gave them to him in my room, not in Warren's. CHARLES BURGESS GOFF. I am a policeman. I took Warren into custody on the 19th of April, in High-street, Borough—I told her it was for receiving twelve jackets and other articles, stolen from Mr. Searle's, and found in her room—she said, "I know nothing about it; if they are found there, somebody must have put them there." RICHARD SIMMONS. I am a painter, and know the prisoner Bromell; he is a waterman, and has been in the habit of going out with the Oxford Club at times, when they were short-handed. On the 4th of April, about four o'clock in the afternoon, I saw him and Petty pass the front of Mr. Searle's premises—they only passed as I went out, walking together—I cannot say they were in company, but walking by the side of each other.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Is Mr. Searle's house in the open street? A.  No—there is a thoroughfare where they passed, it is Bishop's-walk—anybody might pass there—they walked along as any body else might—there was nothing remarkable—it is a very usual place for watermen to pass. JAMES BROOK. I am a policeman. I took Bromell into custody, on the 5th of April, in the Waterloo-road—I told him I wanted him for a rob bery at Mr. Lankester's—I searched him, and found this cap on his head—I produce a bunch of skeleton keys, which I received from sergeant Jones at the station—he is not here.
   MRS. AUSTIN re-examined.  These are the keys I gave to the sergeant—there are sixteen and a small crow-bar—I am certain they are the same, I particularly noticed them.
