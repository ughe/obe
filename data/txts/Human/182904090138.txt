THOMAS ASHBY. I know the poor-house. On the 16th of March, about six o'clock in the evening, I saw Elizabeth Charles, Stokes, and Purdy, on the top of the premises - Hill was at the bottom, throwing up the bundles to Purdy; the matron was out of the way at the time; the prisoners left the house the same evening.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 CORDWELL's Defence. We were washing on Monday afternoon; after tea, Stokes asked us to go away, and said she would get us work, and keep us till we got it; Charles then went into the yard, and got the linen - they were put in bundles, and at night we got over the wall: we went to Putney, and there they pawned two sheets; we then went to Wandsworth - we slept at the King's Arms; on the Monday morning they pawned one sheet at Mr. Butcher's, and two flannel petticoats, one shirt, and three hankerchiefs; Charles pawned the handkerchiefs in her own name, and Stokes the rest: we went to Chelsea next day, and then Stokes took two old sheets, and sold them, but I do not know to whom: on the Tuesday they ran away, and I never saw them till Thursday, when I saw Stokes, and asked what she had done with the duplicates; she he had said, burnt them; I was away a fortnight, and then I thought I had better give myself up, and suffer a little, and give up the company I was in.
 PURDY's Defence. Stokes asked if I would run away? I said, I had such a bad jacket; she said, "I will buy you a jacket in Chelsea;" Stokes tied up the bundles; I saw them take three pieces of soap, which they sold in Jew's-row; they then went to the Rose and Crown public-house, and took the shirts - I was going away, but they would not let me.
 HILL's Defence. I was down in the garden while the other prisoners were getting over; I asked where they were going? they did not answer; I never went away.
 CORDWELL -    GUILTY. Aged 30.
 PURDY -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 HILL -    NOT GUILTY.

 979.  JOHN TAYLOR and BENJAMIN TAYLOR were indicted for breaking and entering a certain building, within the curtilage of the dwelling-house of Edward Chattaway , and occupied therewith, but not being part thereof, on the 11th of April , and stealing therein 5 live tame fowls, price 16s., his property.
 MR. DOWLING conducted the prosecution.
   EDWARD CHATTAWAY. I keep the  workhouse at Edmonton. On the 10th of April I had some fowls in a hen-house at the corner of the workhouse yard; it is surrounded with a wall nine or ten feet high; on the 11th I missed five fowls, which I had seen the day before - I have not seen them again; the tiles of the hen-house were taken off: they had tried to get in by the door, and could not. I saw the prisoners apprehended; Benjamin Taylor had a plaid cap on.
 Prisoner  BENJAMIN TAYLOR. Q. Is there not more than one cap of a kind? A. Yes - I cannot say that I ever saw you with a cap on before; your brother wore one similar so it.
   ELIZABETH HART. (This witness being deaf and dumb, gave her evidence through a person who could communicate with her.) I am a pauper in the workhouse at Edmonton; I know both the prisoners - they had been paupers there: on Saturday morning last I got out of bed and looked out of the window - it was a beautiful morning; I saw one of the prisoners on the wall, and the other on the ground, within the wall; there was another person with them, whom I did not know - one of the prisoners had a fowl, with its beak in his hand; the wings fluttered and he put it under his arm - I am quite sure the prisoners were two of the men: I rapped at the window, and described the prisoners to the master; Benjamin had a striped cap on his head.
 Prisoner  BENJAMIN TAYLOR. Q. Did you see me on the top of the wall? A. I saw him getting over the wall, I am quite certain - I did not see him take the tiles off.
   JOHN CAMP. I am a constable. I took Benjamin Taylor, with this cap on his head; I have seen him wear it before.
   JOHN TAYLOR'S Defence. I was in bed and asleep at the time.
   JAMES TAYLOR. I am the prisoners' father; they lodge at my house. On the night of Friday last I went home at half-past eight o'clock, and inquired if Benjamin had come in - my wife said he had: while I was eating my supper John came in, had his supper, and went to bed - when I got up the next morning, at six o'clock, they were both in bed: my house is a mile from the prosecutor's.
 - TAYLOR. I am the prisoners' sister. My brother Benjamin was at home when my father came in; John then came in, and sat down - when they went to bed I locked the door, and took the key out; when I got up in the morning they were in bed - the key had laid on a chair in my bed room.
 MR. DOWLING. Q. Were you ever in the House of Correction? A. Yes, once, for a few potatoes, and once for some gooseberries, which I had given to me.
     NOT GUILTY.
 980.  JOHN GOODRICK and ROBERT GOODRICK were indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Harry Phillips on the 3d of April , and stealing therein 12 drinking-glasses, value 18s.; 12 plates, value 12s.; 5 dishes, value 10s., and I looking-glass, value 10l., the goods of the said Harry Phillips.
 MR. CHURCHILL conducted the prosecution.
   MARY ANN BROWN. I am servant to Mr. Harry Phillips, and had the care of his house, which is Brandenburgh college, at Fulham, Middlesex. On the night of the 2d of April I locked up the house all safe - the next morning I went to open it, and found the door open and a chair against it; the shutters were put too, but the padlock which I had put on at night was broken off and gone; I looked in the kitchen, found the drawers all open, and the china and glass gone, which I had seen right the night before - I missed the chimney-glass from over the drawing-room fire-place, where I had laid the fire the day before; this iron bar laid across the fire-place, it appears to be the same the house was broken with.
