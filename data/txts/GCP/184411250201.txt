MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you see the schooner ashore at the Low

WILLIAM JEVPORD. I am a waterman. On Friday, the 25th of Oct. I
was on the Lower Tunnel-pier, and saw the Waterman, No. 6, coming on
the river-she was going at the rate of eight or nine miles an hour-id
not know whether that is her full speed or not-there was a schooner
moored to the Lower Tunnel-pier--I had moored her there some time before
- saw a skiff and a man rowing--the steam-boat struck it-she was not
above her own length from the skiff when I first saw her-she was just on
the point of striking- I did not see whether it had at all altered its direc
tion before it struck the boat-the skiff was twenty-one feet long-the
speed of the steamer was not diminished to my sight-I had seen the
steamer before-at the time I saw the skiff the steamer was going in the
same direction as she had been before-she had not altered her course
from the time I first saw her when I first saw her she was about 150
feet from where I stood-it was not half a minute between my first seeing
her and her striking the skiff-I have been acquainted with the river for
some time.
Q. What is, in your judgment, the proper rate for going in the Pool!
1. Four miles against the tide, I think it is; and six miles with it -- I dare
say the tide itself would drive a barge along at the rate of four miles
hour, with a person just now and then to straighten her or turn her round
-in my judgment there ought to be an increase of only two miles a
hour put on by the steamers--the schooner that I had moored caused
some eddy; but at the time the skiff was overturned she had not come
within the influence of that eddy.
Cross-examined by Sir John BAYLEY. Q. Are you employed at the
Lower Tunnel-pier? 4. Yes-the "Waterman" boats do not run to that
pier-I did not see the steamer starboard her helm-she might have done
so, in the course of the way she was going-I think she ought to have
starboarded her belm, to have cleared the boat-I saw the schooner
aground-she caused a little eddy above her-she caused more eddy when
she was aground than when she was lifted-a good many steamers come to
the Lower Tunnel-pier, as many as to the other--they travel at the same
speed as the others--there is very little difference in any of them--one is
as good as the other there is never above a minute difference in speed in
any of them- sometimes one is first, and sometimes the other is first.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. I suppose you are only employed as a servant
at this pier? 1. That is all I have no share or interest in the boats-1
think if the helm had been starboarded, it would have enabled the steamer
to avoid the boat.
John CHAMBERS. I am a master mariner, belonging to the schooner
Ann-on Friday, the 25th of October, she was lying at the Tunnel pier-
whilst there I saw the skiff and steam-boat, and saw them come into colll-
sion--the steam-boat was going, I should think, at about eight miles an
hour, as near as I could judge, when she was passing our ship-when the
boats came together I was, I should think, between eighty and ninety feet
from them.
