1241. EDWARD BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , three pewter pots, value 2 s., the goods of James Clark.
   JAMES CLARK. I keep the  Camden's Head, public-house, Camden-town. On the 16th of August, I was within twenty yards of my door, and saw the prisoner with three pots. I sent my man after him, he brought him to me with them. I gave him in charge.
   JOHN SEAL. I am a baker. I was in Baham-street, Camden-town, and saw the prisoner take two pots off the rails of No. 32.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before W. Arabin, Esq.
 1242.  PHOEBE BENJAMIN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , one watch, value 15 s.; one seal, value 3 s., and one key, value 2 s., the goods of George Buckley from his person.
 GEORGE BUCKLEY. I am  steward of an East Indiaman. On the 15th of August at half-past two o'clock in the morning, I was in Whitechapel, going home to Kennigton-lane - I had been drinking, but knew what I was about, and met the prisoner. I am sure she is the woman; we chatted together for three minutes, she asked me to give her something to drink, I gave her 6 d., my brother, mother, and sister, were about four yards a-head, agreeing for a hackney coach, she kept me in conversation three minutes, I then left her, she was close to me, when I gave her the money, a man said feel if you have got your watch, I felt and missed it; it was safe just before - we went after her with the watchman, who overtook her - she swore she had not got it, he overhawled her, and found it in her bosom.
   SAMUEL STOCKTON. I am a watchman. Buckley came to me; I overtook the prisoner and asked if she had got the watch, she said she had not. I found it in her bosom.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. He gave it me to keep till morning, I walked off with it.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Life.
 First Middlesex Jury, before W. Arabin, Esq.
 1243.  JOHN BLAZER was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , one shawl, value 35 s., the goods of George Sparing.
   CAROLINE SPARING. I lodge in Pear-street, Westminster. On the 20th of August, I met the prisoner in Queen-street; I knew him by sight before, I asked him to drink, as I knew his brother; I never saw him before, but by his features, I asked if he was not that man's brother, he said, Yes; we went to the Cooper's-arms, public-house, and had two pots of beer - it was about half-past ten o'clock at night. I was taken very poorly there, and a friend of mine who was there took me home, the prisoner went with me, they both stripped me, and put me to bed, he sat on the chair by the bed, I asked him to go home, he pretended to be in liquor; he took and folded my shawl up, I asked what he was doing with it; he then laid it on the table, I went to sleep, and felt him taking my pocket from under my head; I said, "Young man, do not take my things;" and he went out with my shawl, leaving my pocket on the table, and no money in it, there was a candle in the room all the time.
 Q. Had you sent for any doctor - A. No; I am married, my husband's name is George, he has left me - I have not found the shawl, I saw him next morning, and asked him for it, he pushed me down, and went off.
 Prisoner. Q. Were you not in liquor - A. No; not at all, I had not been drinking all day with him.
 COURT. Q. Do you mean to say he did not meet you before half-past ten o'clock - A. I believe it was between six and seven o'clock, I met him standing at the door of the Hoop and Grapes, public-house, he treated me with a pot of beer there, we had nothing but three pots of beer.
   ANN BRANSTON. I was present. The prosecutrix has spoken a number of untruths.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First Middlesex Jury, before W. Arabin, Esq.
 1244.  MARGARET BARLOW was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , one watch, value 2 l.; one seal, value 1 l.; one key, value 2 d.; one ribbon, value 1 d.; one pocket-book, value 6 d., and one rule, value 6 d., the goods of William Harrison , from his person.
   WILLIAM HARRISON. I am a journeyman stone-mason, and live in Tooley-street. On the 13th of September, about two o'clock in the morning, I met the prisoner in the Strand, we went to several public-houses together, and about four o'clock, I accompanied her to No. 22, Clement's-lane. I put a washing tub against the door, and laid on the bed with my trowsers on, and put my watch in my right hand breeches pocket, I gave her what silver I had. My pocket-book, containing 2 l., which was in my coat pocket, was across a chair. I awoke about eight o'clock in the morning, and found her gone, and missed my watch, put my coat on, and found I was fastened in the room - I made a noise at the door, and old man came up, and I asked him to let me out, he said he could not. Sometime after, the prisoner came and let me out; I charged her with robbing me, she denied it, and said, I never had a watch - I got an officer (after going to several places) who searched the room, and found my pocket and rule on the table, they were not there when I charged her with stealing my watch - nothing was found on her, the watch has been found - I had been at the Play till eleven o'clock, then went to a public-house or two, I might have drank two pots of beer before I met her - I was not intoxicated.
   JOHN LANGFIELD. I was sent for between nine and ten o'clock in the morning. The prosecutor was sober then, he charged the prisoner about the watch, she denied it, I found the pocket-book and rule on the table, and found the watch on Crampton.
   JAMES CRAMPTON. I am a porter, and live in the same house as the prisoner, the landlady gave me the duplicate of the watch, and I redeemed it.
   MARGARET MOORE. The prisoner lodges at my brother's. I picked the duplicate off the kitchen floor; I do not know who it came from.
