1818. WALTER GRIGG and GEORGE AUSTIN were indicted for
stealing two loaves of bread, value 10d. ; the goods of Richard Trew, from
the person of Charlotte Trew.-2nd Count, stating them to be the property
of Charlotte Trew.
GRIGG pleaded GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined One Month.
CHARLOTTE Trew. I am the daughter of Richard Trew. I live at Wim-
bledon, with my father and mother--on the 6th of March, I was at the bottom
of the white houses, at Wimbledon, about half-past twelve o'clock-I had my
little sister Eliza with me-I was carrying four half-quartern loaves home
the prisoner Grigg came up to me-Austin was about twenty yards behind
me-he was near enough to hear what Grigg said–Grigg said, “What
have you got ?" he pulled my apron open, and snatched out two loaves-I
said, "Grigg, I know you - Austin said, "Do not take it, she knows
you"-Grigg ran to the back of the white houses--the groom ran after him.
JAMES Roots. I am groom to Mr. Wilson. I came up, and Charlotte
Trew was crying-Austin was standing there-Grigg ran down a turning
I ran to take him, but he got off–he had no bread then-the policeman
came up-I showed him which way the boy ran, and he saw the prisoners
and another sitting in a valley-we went and brought Austin-Grigg ran
away--the policeman brought him off the farm, and he showed us where they
had put the bread.
SET 5
EMANUEL STOCKLEY (police-constable V 291.) I found the prisoners-
Austin said, he never touched the girl, and had nothing to do with it.
AUSTIN-Not Guilty.

1319. JAMES SMITH and GEORGE ANDERSON were indicted for
stealing 1 iron shaft, value 31. ; and 1 iron wheel, 11.; the goods of Charles
Keene and others.--2nd Count, stating it to be 378 lbs. weight of iron.
per MR. METCALF conducted the Prosecution.
BENJAMIN NICHOLLS. I am in partnership with Mr. Charles Keene and
Christopher Nicholls--we have a manufactory in Granby-place, York-road,
Lambeth--the way to it is through a pair of gates, through a passage, and
then through another pair of gates to a yard, formerly in possession of Mr.
Baker, which has been lately bought by the South-Western Railway Com-
pany--this piece of iron was a shaft-it is our property-I ordered it to be
placed in that yard it was taken out of the store, and left in the vacant
ground about the 8th or 10th of April-1 think I last saw it safe on Thurs-
day afternoon, the 15th of April, and on the Monday following I missed that
and a packing-case-in consequence of what I heard, I endeavoured to discover
where the packing-case and shaft were gone I went to the premises with an
ollicer, from there to a field, and from thence to the back of a house, some
distance off, where I found the packing-case-I saw the prisoner Anderson
There-I asked him where the shaft was-he declared he did not know,
mheither had he seen it-I told him what shaft I meant, and asked him four
for five times, and he constantly denied it—the officer, John Ould, was with me
I went the next day to Mr. Knowlden's, in Guildford-street, and the shaft
was shown to me-I am quite sure it was mine-Anderson was afterwards
brought to me in custody by Ould-Anderson said it was a bad job, he
