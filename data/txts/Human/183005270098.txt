 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. How far were you from the prisoners? A. They stood close to the gentleman, and I stood close to them - I did not take them immediately; there were many persons - I might have been knocked down; I do not suppose they observed me - they moved away; I took one, the other went a few doors he then stopped, and turned round; I swear they are the persons I saw behind the prosecutor - I did not see the handkerchief in the prisoners' possession, till the prosecutor said he had got it; there were eight or ten persons, but they did not watch them as I did.
   GEORGE BUSH. I was coming down Hosier-lane, and saw the prosecutor hold Newman - I did not see the other till a little while afterwards, when Toole brought him out of King-street; the prisoners were charged with taking the handkerchief out of the pocket - they did not say any thing; when I took hold of Newman the handkerchief dropped from him - I took it to the watch-house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you see him? A. In Hosier-lane, ten or twelve yards from Cow-lane - I had only just come out of the watch-house, and was going on duty; I collared Newman, as he was against the shutters; the handkerchief dropped behind him - no persons were behind him.
   WILLIAM TURNBULL. I am an officer. I received the prisoners in custody at the watch-house; this is the handkerchief.
 The prisoners received a good character.
 DEWERT -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 NEWMAN -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 1240.  JOHN CARRINGTON was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of May , 1 canvas bag, value 2d.; 7 sovereigns, and a 5l. Bank note, the property of Thomas Tomlinson , from his person.
   THOMAS TOMLINSON. I deal in potatoes and coals, and live in St. John's-lane, Clerkenwell. On the 10th of May, I lost this money out of my left-hand pocket, as I was in Newgate-market, about seven o'clock in the morning - there was a 5l. note, and 7l. in gold, in a canvas bag; I have never got it again; I know the prisoner, and saw him that morning two or three times near me - I had seen him near me about three minutes before I missed my money.
 Prisoner. Q. When I was brought before you, did you not say you did not know me? A. I had seen you, but I did not know who took it.
   ELIJAH WHITMORE. On the 10th of May I was in the market, filling up a half sieve of gooseberries - I looked up, and saw the prisoner's right hand in Mr. Tomlinson's pocket - he pulled out a very dirty bag; I went to tell Mr. Tomlinson, but my master said, "Come home, I want you;" the prosecutor afterwards came and said he had been robbed - I said I knew the person; the prisoner had got out of the market before the prosecutor said that to my master, but he was taken in the afternoon.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you say your master told you? A. Yes, he said Mr. Tomlinson had been robbed, and I said I knew the person who robbed him; I knew him before, and knew his name.
   THOMAS HANDLEY. I am a headborough. The prosecutor came to me on the 10th of May, and said he had been robbed in Newgate-market; I went to look for the prisoner, by the description he gave, to Battle-bridge, where his uncle lives - I met him in the afternoon going up St. John-street, and charged him with it; he said he had not been in the market all that morning, but when I took him to the station he said he had been at the market, but not till eight o'clock - he had only 4s. 6d. on him, but he had a new suit of black on.
 Prisoner. I know nothing at all of it - I was out at work that day.
 JURY to ELIJAH WHITMORE. Q. How soon after you saw this did you tell of it? A. Directly he told my master - not above two or three minutes after.
 THOMAS TOMLINSON. I went across the market, and had occasion to pay some money - I then missed my bag; I went back to the stall I had been at, and then round the market - it was a dirty bag; I am in the coal business.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1241.  WILLIAM WEBBER was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of April , 1 shirt, value 9s., the goods of Richard Cooper.
   RICHARD COOPER. I am a tailor. On the 20th of April I had left my shirt on a box at my lodging when I went out at seven o'clock in the morning; the prisoner lodged in the same house - when he came home at half-past twelve he told me he had pawned it, and said it must be a d-d good one, as he had got 4s. on it; I said I should get an officer, but, in consequence of some of his friends coming the next morning, I let him go.
 Cross-examined by Mr. LEE. Q. Do not you know he had been out drinking the whole of that day? A. I do not know - he came to me while I was in bed and said he had pawned it; I had told him before this not to take any of my things - he did not say he took it by mistake; he said he would get it out - 4s. was put down, but I never accepted it; he was taken in bed on the 21st of April - I said if he got it out I did not care.
   STEPHEN STEPHENS. This shirt was pawned with me by the prisoner.
   FRANCIS GITTENS. I am an officer. I found this duplicate of the shirt on the prisoner.
 Witness for the Defence.
 MRS. RIGBY. I am landlady of the house; the prisoner was very much intoxicated from the Monday - he came home that Tuesday very drunk; he went up stairs for half an hour, then I saw him no more till he came home at night - he went to the prosecutor, and said he had taken a wrong shirt.
     NOT GUILTY.
