   MR. HUDDLESTON conducted the Prosecution. ROBERT COLLINS. I am a hop-factor, 17, Maze-pond, Southwark—I know the prisoner—he came to me in the beginning of Oct.—I showed him a sample of hops that went by the name of "Andrew's hops" from the grower, the bulk of which that was a sample was lying at Messrs. Nash, Hicks, and Co., Pickle-herring wharf—I saw him again on 8th Oct.—he said he could sell those hops if I would take 70 s . per cwt. for them—I had mentioned 75 s . as the price—I told him I would take 70 s ., provided they were sold to a respectable person, and delivered and sold in my name, and for me to receive the money—he said he had sold them for 72 s .—2 s . was his profit—he said he had sold them—he had not bought them—he said he could sell them at 72 s .—I said, "Whatever you sell them at beyond 70 s ., shall of course be your profit"—he then said he had sold them for 72 s ., to Messrs. Haynes and Son, maltsters, of Paddington—he did not say anything about the invoice till the Tuesday or Wednesday—I referred to the " Directory ," to see who Messrs. Haynes and Son were—I found their name there, and said to the prisoner they had been living there seven or eight years—he said he had known them twenty years, and that they were respectable—I said, "Will you give an undertaking to pay for them, if Messrs. Haynes do not?"—he said he would, and he did give it—the policeman has it=the hops were said he would, and he did give it—the policeman has it—the hops were worth about 22 l .—he asked for an order for their delivery, and I told him I would send the order to Mr. Haynes, and inclose him the invoice—on that he said that Mr. Haynes had not any hops by him, and that he wanted them immediately, that he was going to Paddington, and would take the order with him—I gave him this order ( produced )—this writing, which is on the back of it, was not there then—I believe it to be the prisoner's writing.
