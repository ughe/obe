PATRICK JAMES MASON. On the 27th of August, I was sent for from Worship-street, this boy and another came; the prisoner was walking by - the boy pointed him out, and said, "That is the man who stole the stove" - the prisoner appeared fresh. The parties were examined before the Magistrate the same day at two o'clock. I heard the boy examined, what he said was taken down and afterwards read over to him, and I saw him put his mark to it; I heard the woman examined, what she said was taken down - it was read over to her and she put her mark to it; I heard her state what she signed.
 Cross-examined. Q. What office do you belong to - A. I belonged to Bow-street. I have given it up to  Barnard Gleed.
 Q. On your oath were you not dismissed for misconduct - A. I was not. I was discharged for being drunk on duty. I am now a willow cutter. I was an officer at this time.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming by and am entirely innocent of knowing any thing was stolen. I had been drinking with a shopmate.
   BARNARD GLEED. I am an officer, and have been so twenty years. Mason brought the prisoner and stove to the office - the Magistrate desired me to take care of it. I heard the boy examined - what he said was taken down by the clerk; it was read over to him, and he made his mark. What the woman said was also taken down - she made her mark to it. The prisoner certainly was extremely drunk.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he so drunk as to be insensible - A. I think he would not have done it if he had been sober.
 Q. Were there not so many people about, that the boy might be told by somebody that the prisoner was the man - A. He might.
 - GIBBONS. I saw the prisoner on the day in question, he appeared very drunk.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1246.  EDWARD ALLEN was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , two brushes, value 5 s., the goods of George Boxhall.
 GEORGE BOXALL. I  keep a toy warehouse in Dean-street, Soho. On the 30th of July, about five o'clock in the afternoon, I was from home, and on my return I missed the brushes.
   THOMAS M'CORMICK. I am clerk to Mr. Allcock, who lives opposite to Mr. Boxhall. On the 31st of July, I saw the prisoner and another boy walk into Boxhall's shop - I saw them come out - I ran after them down St. Anne's-court, and seized the prisoner; I unbuttoned his coat and took two brushes from under it.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming along, a boy gave me the brushes and ran down the court, the gentleman came up and took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Confined Three Months and Whipped.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1247.  ANN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , one handkerchief, value 2 s.; one waistcoat, value 4 s., and one pair of stockings, value 6 d., the goods of John Dixon.
   JOHN DIXON. I live in George-street, St. Giles's. On Thursday, the 13th of September, about half-past eleven at night, I was at the Black Dog, George-street, and had these things in a bundle, I laid them down on the counter of the bar, the prisoner was there, while I was drinking some beer it was gone - I have not seen it since; I did not see her take it - there were three of us there, myself, my companion, and the prisoner - nobody else, except the landlady was there at the time. I saw her again on Saturday in Drury-lane, and charged her with stealing it. She said I could not take her up without an officer. I saw nobody else come into the house.
   THOMAS EDWARDS. I am an officer. The prisoner was given in my charge, I searched her, but found nothing on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I drank with him and was in his company four hours, and went with him to several different houses, and went to a public-house where he was dancing - I came out and left him there.
   JOHN DIXON. She did not drink with me and was not in my company.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1248.  CATHERINE SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 11 s., in monies numbered, the property of John Naish , from his person.
   JOHN NAISH. I am out of place, and live in Charles-street, Westminster. On the 25th of August, about three o'clock in the morning, I was returning from Peckam, where I had been waiting. I was coming down Drury-lane with a friend, and met the prisoner, she went with me to Church-street- my friend had another female, we both went to the same house of ill fame. My friend was in the next room to us. I objected going with the prisoner, and offered her a shilling to go when the light came up, as she had two black eyes. I put eleven shillings in my handkerchief in my breeches pocket, and went to bed; and in three quarters of an hour I awoke, by hearing her calling to be let out. I jumped up and knocked against the wainscot to awake my friend - he came, and the prisoner came up stairs, as she could not get out. My friend fetched a watchman who came up; she strongly denied it, and took out 1 s. which I had given her, and a few halfpence; she declared it was all she had in the world, and wished her Maker might strike her dead if it was not - she stripped herself, the watchman put his hand behind her head, she screamed out, and he took the eleven shillings from among her hair, tied in a piece of a handkerchief.
   LEWIS ROBERTS. I am a watchman. I was called to this house, No. 1, Church-street, St. Giles's. The man charged the prisoner with stealing his money - they were both in the room; she denied it, and said she had no money belonging to him. I at last found it in her hair, wrapped up in an old piece of handkerchief. I found a handkerchief on her which the corner matched.
