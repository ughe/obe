HE
Ann Hogan. I am the wife of Edmund Hogan, and lodge with the
last witness--the prisoner and deceased occupied a room on the same floor
as ours- there are three rooms on a floor-on Tuesday night, 26th Decem-
ber, boxing-night, I heard the prisoner come home, aliout 10 o'clock, or it
might be a few minutes after--the deceased was not with him --I had not
heard her come up--the prisoner came up stairs, and went into his own
room-I beard hím open the door, go in and shut it ; I did not hear
it looked about four or five minutes afterwards I heard him open the door
Again, and come out on the landing to fetch a pail, which they kept there
-I heard him move it, and I heard the deceased say "Oh, Jack, don't !"
--I had heard no noise or anything before that he went back into the
room, and shut the door agrin-a few minutes afterwards I heard a great
noise in the room, as if someone was dancing and jumping about, and it shook
my room-I did not herr anyone speak-my husband came in soon after
10 o'clock, and he began to sing ; he had been out, being boxing day, and
I remarked to him "Ned, you are singing, and Harriet and Jack are
dancing" -I thought they were dancing--it was quiet then for about ten
minutes, and my husband and I went to bed, and when I was going to
lie down I heard the noise again, as if someone was dancing, and it shook
my floor, and as if someone fell up against the partition that parted their
room and mine-I suppose that was about 10.30 or 10.40-I heard no-
thing inore that night--next morning Mrs. Manners called me into the
prisoner's room, and I saw the decensed lying in the middle of the bed,
with the clothes covered up over her-she had on her stays, an apron,
petticoat and shift--the stays were laced up-her mouth was split right
open down to the chin-her eyes were both blaok and closed, and there.
was a great out on the eyebrow-I believe it was over the right eye-I
spoke to her two or three times, but got no answer there was a red mark
all round ber neck, as if something bad been tied tight round the neck-
there was a black and blue mark round her neck-it could not have been
caused by any part of her clothing, or the bed clothes--her face was very
much bruised all over-I saw two great places of blood on the floor, and
splashes of blood on the tablo-there was no cloth on the table--I saw two
or three splashes of blood on the fire-place-there were no fire-irons--the
blood on the floor was dry-it looked as if it had been wiped over with
something I know that they had a pail, which they used to keep on the
landing, and I saw that pail in the room in the morning--I had not noticed
the pail on the landing the previous evening—it was a wooden pail, with
an iron handle, and I believe three iron hoops round it-on the table I saw
a public-house can it was half full, or perhaps more, of beer-rit would
hold a quart, not more, I think it looked like beer-I did not taste it-
when the potman came for the can I flung the beer away, and saw a great
mark, as if somebody had laid hold of it with a bloody hand--there wero
three or four finger-marks-the can was not painted or apenned, it was a
clean tin can-I saw a broken chair in the room, with spots of blood on it,
that was under the bedstead, pushed up against the wall-the back was
broken right off, and the bottom was all broken up; it had no covering to
it; it was & cane-bottomed chair-the deceased and I were on friendly
terms--we had been out together on the boxing-morning, and had two pints
