Court.Q. Was there, on the part of the patient herself, a dislike to food!
A. I should say that she would take food which would not do her any good
and not take the food which was ordered for her; many people lose their
lives like that, but I cannot form any opinion whether that was the case
here—I can only say that if she had had proper food the condition of her
body would have been better than it was.
MR. BRINDLEY submitted that the deceased was not a child of tender years, but
a girl of nineteen, who was able to go on errands and discharge the duties of
life, and that the duty of taking care of her did not at that age devolve upon her
parents. THE COURT considered that the state of her bodily and mental health
must be taken into account, before the parents could be excused from the duty of
taking care of her.
