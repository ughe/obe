OLD COURT.— Tuesday, Aug, 22nd, 1848.
PRESENT-Mr. Ald. GIBBS; Mr. RECORDER ; and Mr. Ald. FARNDOM,
Before Mr. Recorder and the Second Jury.
1788. ALBERT BROWN, stealing 1 watch and guard-chain, valne 141;
the goods of William Vaughan, in the dwelling-house of Charles Wyatt.-
2nd Count, in the dwelling-house of Elizabeth Wyatt.
WILLIAM VAUGHAN. I live at 15, Skinner-street, St. Botolph-without,
Bishopsgate -my mother, Elizabeth Wyatt, keeps the house-- her husband,
Charles Wyatt, has not lived there for some time-the prisoner lodged there
about a fortnight-he absconded on 20th July, and that morning I missed
my watch and chain-I afterwards received this note- I then found the
prisoner at his father's house, 15, Bartholomew-square, Old-street-be said
he had written the note, and told me that the watch was at Chatham ; that
he had offered it to a Jew for sale, and that he had been given into charge
tbere on suspicion of stealing it-he said he had pawned the chain in the
Westminster-road--this is it, and the watch produced.)
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Did the prisoner tell you that be
wrote this note ? A. Yes-(read~" Aug. 3, 1848. Sir, I beg to inform
you that your watch is in the safe custody of the Magistrate at Chatham, but
not the guard, which is parted with. I write this to save you the 5l. reward;
and other information I will oblige you with, through your advertisement an
the daily Times, and I will answer it through a letter.")
WILLIAM EDWARD ARCHBUTT. I am a pawnbroker, at Bridge-road,
Lambeth. This chain was pledged by the prisoner.
John NATHAN. I am a clothier, at Chatham. The prisoner offered me
