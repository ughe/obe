JOHN MIDDLEDITCH. I am a watch-maker. On the 27th of June, the
prisoner brought me this broken spoon to sell—he said he had broken it in
cleaning it, and his master sent him to sell it, and he had to replace it by
another, which would cost him 38. 6d.-I knew it would cost more than
double that, and I said this one would mend-be said he would rather have
another, and sell that-he then tried to get the spoon back; a scuffle
ensued, and he ran away-he had given his name as "Thomas Flyn, at Mr.
Brown's, a potatoe-salesman."
GUILTY. Aged 14.-Confined Three Months.

1667. CHARLES MARKS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of
July, 1 handkerchief, value 38. 6d., the goods of Thomas Cope, from his
person:
THOMAS COPE. I am the son of Thomas Cope, of Cheapside. I was
in Fleet-street on the evening of the 6th of July, and felt something-I
turned, and saw the prisoner take my handkerchief-1 followed till he was
taken in Shoe-lane by the officer—this is my handkerchief.
THOMAS ILSLEY. I saw the prisoner that evening at the corner of Shoe-
Tane, at half past nine o'clock-I saw him at the prosecutor's pocket
- he ran past me till he got to the Morning Herald office-be there threw
this handkerchief down on the rails--the prosecutor was running after him.
JOHN HUCKLE. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner running in advance,
and several persons running after him-I ran out of my shop, and threw him
down on the steps of the Morning Herald office--this handkerchief was
taken up, and given to me.
GUILTY. Aged 21.-Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor.
