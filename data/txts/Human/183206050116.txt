 1713.  NATHANIEL HALL was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , 1 ivory image, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Charles Nicholas.
   CHARLES NICHOLAS. I am a broker, and live at Hoxton. On the 29th of June I saw the prisoner, with another boy, as I returned home; one of them stooped, which made me suspect them - I went home, and missed a carved ivory image of a lion, which is two hundred and thirty years old - I pursued, and found it in the prisoner's pocket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The other boy gave it to me.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Whipped and Discharged.
 NEW COURT. THURSDAY. JULY 12.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before J. Mirehouse, Esq.
 1714.  MICHAEL JORDAN, THOMAS WILKINSON, and RICHARD HAMMOND were indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Duff William Rae , from his person.
   WILLIAM COLTON. I am a constable. On the 5th of July, about ten o'clock at night, I was in Goswell-street, and saw the three prisoners following two gentlemen - they then went into St. John-street, where I saw the prosecutor and another gentleman walking; they passed them, then turned, and followed them - I saw Jordan take a handkerchief from the prosecutor's pocket, and give it to Hammond, who crossed the road with it, leaving the other two; I took hold of Hammond, who threw the handkerchief down - Cadby took up the handkerchief, and took the other two prisoners.
   JOSEPH CADBY. I am a constable. I was with Colton - we followed the prisoners for some time; when they got to St. John-street they followed the prosecutor and another gentleman, and just as they reached a tobacconist's shop I saw Jordan take the handkerchief out of the pocket, and give it to Hammond - Jordan and Wilkinson then sat down on the step by the tobacco-shop, which the prosecutor and his friend went into; Wilkinson must have seen the pocket picked, as he was close by; Hammond then crossed the way, and Colton took him - I picked up the handkerchief, and took the other two.
   DUFF WILLIAM RAE. I live in Bride-lane. I was walking in St. John-street, and lost this handkerchief from my pocket - I did not miss it till I was told of it.
 Jordan's Defence. I saw it on the pavement - I took it up, and gave it to Hammond, who crossed to see what it was.
 Wilkinson's Defence. I do not know either of these lads; I was sitting on the step waiting for a person.
 Hammond's Defence. I saw Jordan pick up the handkerchief - I said I had a right to have half of it, and he gave it me to look at.
 JORDAN -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 WILKINSON -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 HAMMOND -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Whipped and Discharged.
 1715.  JOHN BIGGS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 15 shillings, and 2 rings, the property of George Perry , from his person.
   GEORGE PERRY. I was lodging, on the 3rd of July, at the Marlborough Arms, St. Pancras. I went to bed about eleven o'clock at night - the prisoner slept in another bed in the same room; I slept alone - when I went to bed, I had 15s. in shillings, and two sixpences in my pocket; I took it out, put it into a bit of paper, and put it into my right-hand breeches pocket, and a letter with it - I put my clothes in a chair by my bed-side - I awoke between seven and eight o'clock in the morning; the prisoner was then getting up - he seemed in a great hurry to dress; when he had left the room, I got out of bed, and found my clothes, which I had left in the chair, on the prisoner's bed - I felt in my breeches pocket, and my money and letter were gone; I could not tell the money, but I know the paper it was in.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not take a hand-bill in Oxford-street? A. No, it was given to me in Holborn - it was the only paper I had; I did not put my clothes on yours when I went to bed - I put them on the chair - there was nothing else there; I awoke while you were dressing.
 COURT. Q. Did you find the letter or the money? A. I apprehended the prisoner myself, in about a quarter of an hour, or twenty minutes, and a Police-officer, who is not here, searched him in my room; he found on him this paper, in which my money had been, and 13s. 6d. - I found this letter, which had been in my pocket, in his bed; he afterwards said he gave 2s. 6d. for this new waistcoat - he made some resistance when the officer searched him.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Confined Six Months.
 1716.  JAMES YOUNG was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of June , 2 pillows, value 5s.; 2 sheets, value 3s.; 1 quilt, value 1s. 6d., and 3 knives and forks, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Thomas Williams.
   MARY WILLIAMS. I am the wife of Thomas Williams; we live in Vine-street, near Tabernacle-square. About May, the prisoner came to lodge at our house - he had a furnished room, at 4s. a week; he brought a person with him, who he said was his wife - they staid a fortnight; one Saturday night, in June, I ran up stairs, to go into another room, and saw his wife there - I then heard the door shut - I went up stairs again, and they were both gone - it was then about ten o'clock; they had left one candle on the stairs, and one on the mantel-piece - I missed the articles stated - I saw them safe when he had been one week with me; he did not pay me any rent - I have not seen any of the things since; my husband took the prisoner afterwards; he brought him to our house, and gave him in charge - he did not say any thing in my hearing.
 Prisoner. Q. What time did I leave your house? A. About eight o'clock you came, and about ten you left - no one went into the room after you left; I went up in ten minutes, or in five minutes after you were gone; I then missed the articles - the door was wide open - you left me without notice.
   SAMUEL TAYLOR. I am an officer. I took the prisoner.
