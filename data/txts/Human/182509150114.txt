 JOHN SWEENEY. I am the last witness's husband. On Sunday night, the 12th of June, I went out and took the key in my pocket; and as I came home, I saw the prisoner in Gunn-lane, Limehouse - my wife and Sullivan were with her - it was about eleven o'clock. I did not see Brown. I left them talking to her at the corner of the street.
 Cross-examined. Q. At what time of night did your wife come home? A. Not till seven o'clock in the morning; she said she was robbed by Goggen, Brown and Sullivan; that the prisoner decoyed her out of her way - it happened in the centre of a field, which is a thoroughfare - there are no watchmen; she could not be heard if she called out - the place is noted for robberies.
   LUCY BRIAN. I was at the public-house, when the prosecutrix came to look for her husband, and in about half an hour I saw her at the top of the street, with a man, whose arm was round her neck. I saw the prisoner go towards her, and know no more. I saw the prosecutrix next day - she was ill used.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was she struggling with the man? A. She seemed inclined to go home - he said No, she must go with him.

 Before Mr. Justice Gaselee.
 1528.  GEORGE HAMPTON was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , a looking-glass, value 30 s.; a piece of needle-work, value 30 s.; a pair of trousers, value 3 s.; a half handkerchief, value 1 d., and two aprons, value 3 d., the goods of William Peters , in his dwelling-house.
 WILLIAM PETERS. I live in  Grove-street, Hackney-road. The prisoner lodged with me for six weeks; I took him in distress, having known him. On the 27th of July, at six o'clock in the morning, I came down stairs, and found the shop door open - it was fastened the night before. I could not find him in the house. I had seen him go to bed the night before; he usually opened the shutters, which was not done. I missed this property, and found the watch in pawn. I waited at Mr. Laws, West-street, Smithfield, from seven o'clock till half-past five, when the prisoner came in with a bundle. I asked what he had done with the property he had taken - he did not tell me; he had got my hat on, and had left his own behind. I said he was not going out till the officer came; he went backward to the privy for ten minutes, and was just coming out when the officer came, and found on him, a pair of trousers, two aprons in a bundle, and a half handkerchief in his pocket - a duplicate was found in the privy.
   WILLIAM WILLIAMS. I am an officer. I was fetched, and in the privy I found the duplicate of a piece of needlework; pawned in Helmet-row, Old-street. I found 24 s. and a handkerchief in his pocket.
   FRANCIS GIDDENS. I was with Williams, and have a duplicate of a looking-glass pawned at Chapman's. Mr. Laws gave it to me.
 JOHN LAWS. I live in West-street, West Smithfield. I found this duplicate in my privy, after the prisoner was gone.
   JAMES FURRELL. I am shopman to Mr. Chapman, pawnbroker, St. John-street. On the 27th of July, about ten o'clock in the morning, the prisoner pawned this looking-glass for 12 s. in the name of John Clark. I gave him the duplicate produced.
   CHARLES LEPLAISTRE. I am shopman to Mr. Cresswell, pawnbroker, Old-street. On the 27th of July, the prisoner pawned a piece of needle-work for 7 s.
 (Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 25. Of stealing to the value of 39 s. only. Confined One Year.

 1530.  CATO COURTHOUSE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of July , at St. Bridget, alias St. Bride , 20 lbs. of lead, value 3 s., belonging to the Mayor, Commonality and Citizens of London , and fixed to a certain dwelling-house there situated.
 EIGHT OTHER COUNTS varying the manner of laying the charge.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND and BERNARD conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN THOMAS KIRKUP. I am in possession of two houses, No. 4 and 5, George-alley, Fleet-market. On the night of the 7th of July, I was in No. 4 - I had information, and came home about eight o'clock at night, and between eight and nine, I saw the prisoner look over the wall leading out of Rose and Crown-court into George-alley - he was on the top of the privy - I had seen him before. I got my stick, mounted the wall, and found him crouching down with his hands on this lead. I secured him, and another person took the lead; we gave him to Johnson, and next day, by order of Mr. Montague, I compared the lead with the top of Mr. Eastwood's house in Shoe-lane- it corresponded exactly, nail holes and all; the house had been taken possession of by the Corporation - it was taken from the back gutter.
   ALEXANDER JOHNSON. I am a constable, and took charge of the prisoner, the lead weighs twenty lbs. - I matched it with a short gutter on the roof of No. 27, Shoe-lane - it corresponded.
   WILLIAM MONTAGUE, ESQ. I am the City surveyor. This house was taken by the Corporation; Mr. Eastwood occupied it, and Mr. Watson, was the freeholder - the prisoner was not employed there.
