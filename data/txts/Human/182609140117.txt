 COURT. Q. How came he to be taken up? A. Because the young lady, who came to tell me I had lost it, said she would point out the man - she did so, and he was taken.
   LOUISA HUGHES. I am a waistcoat-maker, and live opposite this shop. Between eight and nine o'clock I saw the prisoner, in company with two men; they stood next door but one; the prisoner went into the shop; I am quite sure it was him; it was broad day-light, and my window was open; he had nothing with him that I saw; the other two then crossed; the prisoner then came out of the shop, and, in a few minutes, went in again, and the second time I saw him draw a coffee-biggin from the corner of the shop, and put it under his coat; he and his two companions then went away; I was so much alarmed I could not cry out, but I went over and told what I had seen - on the following Tuesday I pointed the prisoner out; I am sure he is the man; I had never seen him before, but I particularly observed him.
 Cross-examined. Q. Then you did not see any thing in his hand when he wentin? A. No; he could not have had a spectacle case in his hand; he had his face towards me for about five minutes - when he came out of the shop with the biggin, they all walked away; I was but a little way from him; it is not a wide street - he had lightish-coloured napkeen trousers on, and a blue coat; I observed him particularly; he has a particular walk, and is rather a bow legged man; this was on a Saturday; I saw him again on the Tuesday, when they all three went past the house again, I went and told Mrs. Tiddeman - she went to try to get an officer, but could not; they got away - the prisoner was taken on the following Saturday; I could see the coffee-biggin in the shop as I sat at my window; it was just as you go in on the right hand.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was at work at Hackney-common.
   JAMES PLOWMAN. I am a constable. I took the prisoner on Saturday, the 2d of September, he made no resistance - Mrs. Tiddeman told me of him; they were all three walking together.
 Mrs. TIDDEMAN re-examined. He is the man who came into my shop; he took the spectacle case out of his pocket - I have no doubt he is the man; I was sure it was him; I said, "You know me, I suppose;" he said, "I don't know you by God;" I told him if he would give me my property back, or the duplicate, I would not proceed.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.

 Third Middlessex Jury - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1580.  JOHN HENRICK was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 38 yards of cotton, value 1l., the goods of William Dobinson.
 WILLIAM DOBINSON. I am a  draper, and live in High-street, Shadwell. This piece of cotton is mine, and has my private mark upon it; it was in my shop on the 21st of July; I had been showing it to a customer; I did not miss it till the officer brought the prisoner back half an hour after I had seen it; I placed it on a pile with others, a yard and a half from the door; the shop-ticket was found in his right hand.
   JAMES BEECHEY. I am an officer. I stopped the prisoner with this cotton, at the corner of King David's-lane, eighty or ninety yards from the shop, on Friday, the 21st of July; he was walking very fast, and putting his handkerchief over the cotton; he spoke Englis very well.
 Prisoner's Defence. (through an interpreter.) A man gave it to me to carry, and said he would pay me to take it to his house. GUILTY. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury, being poor and away fom his own land. Judgment respited.
 1581.  RICHARD MYATT was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 2 chairs, value 5s., the goods of William Taylor.
   ELEANOR BILLETT. Lewis Billet, my son, is fifteen years of age; he was sent with two chairs by his master to take them to Hoxton.
   LEWIS BILLETT. I live with William Taylor; he is a chair japanner, and lives in Charlotte-street, Fitzroy-square. On the 31st of July he sent me with two chairs, which I was to try to sell at the broker's shop; I took them as a sample of the half dozen; when I was at the corner of Charlton-street, the prisoner, whom I knew before, said he would go with me; I left the chairs at Mr. Jessup's shop, at Somer's-town; I went with the prisoner along the City-road; a mad bull came by, and I lost the prisoner; I went to the City, and when I came back to Jessup's, the chairs were gone.
   JOHN FARREN. I am twelve years of age. I am in the employ of Mr. Jessup; I was in the shop on the 31st of July; the prisoner and Billett left the chairs; the prisoner afterwards came back, and sat on a chair; I told him to go off; he did not say any thing, but got off and sat on the step of Mr. Grove's door; he went to sleep, and I awoke him; he went to sleep again for two hours; he then said,"The other don't seem to come, so I shall take the chairs;" he said he was going to take them home to his master, with whom he had lived about a week.
   ROBERT WILLANS. I am the officer. I took the prisoner - the chairs have not been found.
 Prisoner. The boy has taken a false oath; I was not at the place at all.
 JURY to FARREN. Q. Can you positively swear to the prisoner being the person who took the chairs away? A. Yes; he put one upon the other, and took them away.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.

 SECOND COUNT, stating it to belong to  Ann Saunders,
 ANN SAUNDERS.
