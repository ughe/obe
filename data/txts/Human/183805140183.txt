   Q.  When did you first go to the Castle, after half-past six o'clock? A.  bout eight o'clock—I cannot speak positively within half an hour—I can within an hour, to the best of my recollection—I should have no hesita tion in saying that it was not later than half-past eight o'clock—Mr. Miller was not with me at the Roebuck and heard Mr. Badnall—I was not at the Roebuck—I passed by it—not when he was speaking—I was at distance—I was certainly within sight and hearing of Mr. Badnall—I do not know that I could understand the words, but I could hear the the sound—I will swear Mr. Miller was not with me—I have no recollec tion of being at the Royal Express beer-house with Mr. Miller—I will swear I was not at half-past eight o'clock—I do not think I was at eight o'clock—I have no recollection of being there at all—I really cannot say that I was there with Mr. Miller as late as eight o'clock—I will not swear that I was not, because I cannot recollect every where I went—I was in every house in the town during the election—I do not know Boot, the tenant of that house—I cannot say whether I went with Mr. Miller to this house, and there canvassed Boot for his vote—I cannot say whether I did or not—I have no recollection of it—that house is in Lower-street-if I was in that house I must have gone into Lower-street—I cannot say the exact course I took now—I told you before I cannot swear to every abode I was in daring the election—I went back to the Castle that evening, but whe ther I went up Church-lane I cannot say—I did not, on my solemn oath, go back with Mr. Miller to the Castle that evening—I cannot recollect whether I was in Church-lane or not—I went to the Castle again—it might be eleven or twelve o'clock before I left the Castle that night—some of the committee were there that night—I cannot say it was a formal meeting.
   Q.  Was Mr. Miller in the habit of going to and fro to the Committee? A.  I do not recollect seeing Mr. Miller in the committee-room, but when the committee was formed he was there—I never saw him there when any one but the committee was present—I do not recollect his being there at all—he did not walk arm-in-arm with any person into the Castle on that night after half-past eight o'clock, to my knowledge—I went out with Mr. Miller to canvass—I have some recollection of being in Paradise-street—it is impossible for me to tell who I canvassed that night—I went to a per son's house, I believe, of the name of Watkins—Mr. Miller was there—we went together with a whole party of gentlemen—Mr. De Horsey, and the whole party—I never saw Mr. Miller at the hotel after that canvass—it is very improbable he would be there—I do not know that I went back with Mr. De Horsey after the canvass—Mr. De Horsey went to the hotel, because it was his house—I might be in his company after the canvass, but I can not recollect that he was arm-in-arm with any one—he certainly might have been.
   Q.  On your solemn oath, after you went into the hotel, and canvassed that evening, did not Mr. Miller address a very few words to the crowd from the balcony or window? A.
