ALICE RUDOLPHINE D'ALTERA, 12, Waverley Road, Folkestone. In
December, 1905, I received circular (produced) from Financial
Guardian and Mining Market Mirror, Share Department, offering
Golden Rhodesias. I did not purchase. I afterwards got circular
(produced) signed "H. K. Ablett," offering Canadian Pacific shares,
and on February 16, 1906, I sent cheque (produced) for £30, which is
endorsed by "#. KAblett," and has been honoured. I received con-
tract note showing that 50 shares had been purchased for me at
1768. On March 1 I received letter (produced), and on March 2
sent another £30 as further cover. I afterwards sent a bundle of
cheques (produced) for small amounts for contango amounting to £61
in response to fortnightly accounts (produced), signed "H. K. Ablett."
I wrote for the money several times, and on March 4, 1907, I received
letter (produced) from George Martin, stating that Mr. H. K. Ablett
had been away some time, and he would try to get into communica-
tion with him. On March 25 I received letter and account (pro-
duced) showing a debit balance against me of £21 Os, 3d. The ac-
count shows a charge for 167 days contango from October 11 to
March 27 amounting to £54 15s. 3d. I replied, and on March 27 I
received a letter signed by "H. K. Ablett," stating that I had been
credited with bonus to the extent of £110, and the account would be
made up later. I received nothing from the "Financial Guardian."
I thought it was a genuine firm,
Cross-examined by Mr. Wellesley Orr. I believed that I was
buying the shares with the £30, and that according to the circular
"there was no further liability." I sent the second £30 because I
thought it was needed to keep them going. I did not know the
market price of the shares. I do not know what "taking up a
share" means. I did not write that I had already bought the shares
and there was nothing more to pay. I have not taken legal proceed-
ings. I intended to do so, and I believe I said so in the last letter I
wrote.
