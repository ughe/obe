1992. HENRY JOSEPH KILLERBY was indicted for feloniously
sending to Elizabeth Challenor a certain letter, threatening to kill and murder
Elizabeth Spriggs, the daughter of the said Elizabeth Challenor; and that he
had been before convicted of felony.

MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH CHALLENOR. I am the wife of John Challenor, and live at No.
45, White-street, Borough. I have a daughter named Elizabeth Spriggs, by
a former husband-she is thirteen years old-I know the prisoner-I had
oocasion to make a complaint of him some time ago, with reference to my
daughter--he was imprisoned for a year--he came out of prison on the 26th
of Feb., I think-I received the letter in question by post, on the 18th of
Aug.--this now produced is it-I had received one previously, on the 20th of
July, and one before that—I do not know whose writing they are, but I had
many letters before which were proved to be in the prisoner's writing, and I
took these to be the same writing-the letters that were the subject of the
previous complaint were in the same handwriting.
baitz
EDWARD KENDALL (police-serjeant, A 30.) I am acquainted with the
prisoner's handwriting—I have seen him write on one occasion-I believe
these letters of the 20th of July and the 12th of Aug. to be the prisoner's
writing.
COURT. Q. What was it that you saw him write ? A. The direction of an
envelope and his signature-it was at his father's house, before I appre-
hended him on the first occasion-it was done for the purpose of ascertain-
ing his handwriting—I did not prove his writing on the first oceasion--there
was another witness who had seen him write on more occasions, a foreman
in an establishment where be worked he is not here to-day-the Magistrate
said it was not necessary, and that I was sufficient-I believe these letters
to be in the handwriting of the prisoner. bon zahar

Cross-examined by Me, BRIARLY. Q. You have never seen him write but
once ? A. Never, that was merely a direction and his name it was in
Sept., 1845-I requested him to write it, I have got it here that is all I
know of his writing, but I have plenty of writing here wbich was sworn to as
being his, on the former trial.

Mr. Payne. Q. Was it when you went to apprehend him in Sept., 1845,
that you got him to write ? A. No, some time before, when I was making
inquiry to detect the person who had sent the letters--he wrote the direction,
"Miss Elizabeth Spriggs" and his own name, in my presencer having seen
him write that, I have not the shadow of a doubt of these letters being his
the character of the writing is particular-I paid particular attention to his
writing- I went for that purpose.
