MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
   JAMES POWELL. I am clerk to Richard Henry Cox and two others. On the 14th of December last, I saw the prisoner at our house - we had a correspondent at that time, named Captain Gordon- the prisoner produced this letter to me - I asked him to favour me with his name - he said his name was Wainwright - I said, "Captain Wainwright?" - he answered, "Yes;" and added, "Not Captain Wainwright, late of the 47th" - I then told him, as the order was to pay the money on the sale of the stock, I could not pay it him then; but that he must come again in a few days' time, when the stock would be sold - he asked what day he should come - I think Wednesday was mentioned - this was on a Saturday - he remonstrated at not being paid then, and stated that he had received the order from Captain Gordon for a particular purpose, and that it was of a pressing nature - I said I was sorry to put him to inconvenience, but I could not pay the money till the stock was sold - he then left - I saw him again on the 18th of December; I think it was Wednesday (it was a few days after this) - he asked if I was ready then to pay the money - I said, "Yes," and asked him his Christain name - he replied, "Charles" - I then filled up the order, conceiving he was the proper person for whom it was intended, and whom he represented himself to be - this is the order (looking at it) - he signed it in my presence - this order which he signed was the authority for paying the money - in the course of our business, the party applying would be called on to sign such an order as this before it was paid - we do not pay the money in my department of the house - we should not part with the money unless that cheque had been signed.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Have you ever seen Captain Gordon since he wrote that letter? A. No; I know nothing of the prisoner except in this transaction - we were agents for Captain Wainwright, late of the 47th- Captain Gordon is also of the 47th - Captain Wainwright's name is not Charles - we were agents for him while he was in the 47th - he is on half-pay now, and not in any regiment.
 Q. The prisoner brought you that letter which was your voucher or authority for payment of this money? A. Yes, on his signing the cheque; the letter was my authority to charge it to Captain Gordon's account - if Captain Gordon asked for a voucher, I should have to produce the letter and the receipt - the letter is my authority, supported by the receipt.
 Q. Do you use and treat that paper which you have produced in any other way than a receipt for the payment of money, which Captain Gordon had previously ordered to be paid? A. I treat it as a draft in acknowledgment of the money - Captain Charles Wainwright, if there is such a person, had no money in our hands to give an order to receive - the letter does not mention any Christain name - I am not aware that there is such a person as Charles Wainwright, who has any money in our hands to which an order could apply - I do not know all the clients of the house - I paid the money on the faith of Captain Gordon's letter to the party who said he was Captain Wainwright - Captain Gordon is at Mullingar - he corresponds with us from Ireland.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. The letter of Captain Gordon was the authority on which you sold out his stock? A. It was; the cashier pays the money on authority of the draft - he does not see the original order, when he pays the money - he pays it on production of the draft which the prisoner signed - my handwriting is on the draft.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. The cashier knows your handwriting on the draft? A. Yes; my initials are on it - that forms a voucher or authority to the cashier to pay the money - he would not pay it unless it was marked by the clerk, who has charge of the account.
 COURT. Q. If the draft was not signed, your initials alone would not be the authority to pay it? A. No; the draft must be signed before the cashier would pay it.
 JURY. Q. Did you consider you were paying the money to Captain Wainwright late of the 47th? A. No: I did not pay the money - I knew Captain Wainwright, late of the 47th, and supposed the prisoner to be another Captain Wainwright.
   FREDERICK WEEDON. I am cashier to Messrs. Cox and Co. I paid this draft on the day it bears date, from the funds of Cox and Co.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner on the Wednesday preceding the examination - I do not remember the day of the month - I took him in Titchbourn-street, Coventry-street - I knew him as Prescott - I met him accidentally in the street - he made a stop and said, "How do you do?" - I said, "I had been looking for you some time, I want you" - he said, "For what?" - I said,"I have a warrant against you" - he said, "What is it for?" - I said, "For obtaining money by fraud" - he asked where it was from - I said, "From Cox and Greenwood" - he said, "I know nothing about it - I will go there with you" - I said, "Do you know a person named Wainwright?"- he said, "I do - how much is it for?" - I said, "I really do not remember the sum" - he said, "Oh, it is for 50l.: here, I will give you the money" - I declined to take it, and he offered to go to Cox and Greenwood, to take the money there - he also wished me to take him to the Solicitor's in Harley-street, and he would give the money there - I declined to go there with him, and took him to the office - he complained of his friends neglecting him, and said he was obliged to do it - he had no other means of living.
 CAPTAIN  HENRY MAXWELL WAINWRIGHT. I was lately a Captain in the 47th. I am acquainted with Captain Gordon, who is in Ireland, and have been so for eighteen years - I never heard of any other Captain Wainwright with whom Captain Gordon was acquainted - I have left the 47th three years - I have been acquainted with the 47th since 1808 - I was in it twenty years - I never knew any other Captain Wainwright, but myself - I do not know of any Captain Charles Wainwright, in the army - Captain Gordon was at my house about half a year ago - I did not authorize any person to sign that order for me - I knew nothing of the transaction till after this discovery.
