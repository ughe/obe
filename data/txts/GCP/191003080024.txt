Charles Robinson, 69, Glaslyn Road, Crouch End, confirmed the
evidence of last witness.
Police-constable FREDERICK ANDERS, 804 Y. I was on duty at 3
a.m. on January 2. Hearing a police whistle I went to the junction
of Holloway and Liverpool Roads. I saw the hansom cab driver and
Mrs. Holmes. The defendant was standing on one side. Lott said.
“I think I have broken my leg.” I attended to him and Mrs. Holmes.
put them on a vehicle and sent them to the Great Northern Hospital.
I saw prisoner was drunk and took him to the station. The divisional
surgeon was called. Prisoner was charged. He said, "I am not
drunk." I did not see the Robinsons there; they had gone.
Cross-examined. The station is about 10 minutes' walk from where
the accident took place. I asked prisoner for his license. He produced
it at once. He appeared to half know what he was talking about.
He gave his name and address. He seemed to know all of it. He did
not seem greatly diſtressed. By the appearance of his face I should
bay he was drunk. Looking at him now I do not think he has a glassy
stare in his eye. I do not think his eyes are bloodshot. He had a
klassy stare that night and bloodshot eyes.
Dr. ROBERT JAMES HARBINSON, 44, St. James's Road, Holloway, divi.
sional surgeon. I saw prisoner at Caledonian Road police station about
3.15 a.m. on January 2. I examined him and had a conversation with
him. I made him walk up and down. I looked at his eyes and came
to the conclusion that he was drunk. I asked him if he had had anything
to drink. He denied having had anything. I said to him, “Now, be
careful." After he said, Little of anything," I said, "You have
been drinking four ale, bitter, beer, or stout." Afterwards he admitted
having about four glasses of beer.
Cross-examined. The station sergeant, in the presence of the pri-
soner, told me what I was brought there for. One of the symptoms
of drunkenness is the state of the eyes in conjunction with other
symptoms. The condition of a person's eyes is not exactly symptomatic.
His eyes were conjested and bloodshot. I should not be surprised to
find a man's eyes in that condition if he had been driving a car in the
streets of London the whole day and the greater part of the night.
His pulse was full and bounding. I asked him to walk along the line.
He was on his feet when I got there. He was not being held up. I
do not think the knowledge that on his walking might depend a serious
criminal charge would have the effect of making a man unsteady in his
gait. He was unsteady in his gait-staggering. I said, "You have
not walked along that line straight." He said which line was it! !
pointed it out again. He went up and down again. He staggered
again. If this man had been drinking I would not expect it necessarily
to have been apparent half an hour before. It might have been
apparent an hour or an hour and a half before. It might not have been
apparent to anybody. If a man had only had four glasses of beer over
the whole day the last one being at 12 p.m., I might have expected him
to be in that condition.
