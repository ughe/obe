RICHARD SCOTT AITCHESON. I am a chartered accountant, of Edin burgh, and was appointed official liquidator to the London Scottish Banking Corporation on January 16th, 1895—I came up to London about January 18th or 19th to the office of the bank, 35, Lombard Street, where I took possession of such of the books and documents as I could get—I think I mentioned at the Mansion House that I saw Wain there, but on referring to my notes afterwards I found that I did not see him at the bank on that occasion; I tried to arrange two meetings with him, aud he disappointed me on both occasions; but I saw Lambert afterwards, not at the bank, but at the hotel—I also arranged that Lambert should be at the bank meeting, but he did not come up; I saw Beall as the bank, I did not see him on the first occasion, I saw F. J. Baker—I had been up once before; I was formally appointed liquidator—I did not collect any books before I was formally appointed—I only came up once for the purpose of collecting the books—I had the assistance of a clerk from the office of Clarke, Rawlins and Co.—I was not acquainted with the English style of doing business, so I got the assistance of a clerk—I made a list, which is— Exhibit  252, of tho books which I had possession of, and which I after wards handed over to the Director of Public Prosecutions; it was a list made when the books were sent up to him—the letter-books which I got began with letter-book No. 3—I never saw letter-books 1 and 2—letter-book No. 3 commences with March 14th, 1893—I never had any share-holders' ledger, or a list or account of any sort of the holders of founders' shares—I never saw any account of the founders' shares at all—I never saw any minutes of the meetings of the signatories prior to the appointment of directors or governors or any agreement of March 10th, 1892—I did not find among the papers any account showing the position of the company at the date when the 7 per cent, dividend was declared—the petty cash in hand was £10s. 8d—at my instigation the directors and F. J. Baker and Mr. Beall were examined in Scotland b fore Lord Stormonth Darling in February, 1895—shorthand notes were taken of their examination—I was present the whole time of their examination, and I have since checked the transcript of the shorthand notes—I do not say that that transcript produced is a correct transcript of the evidence given by Lambert, Wain, and Beall—that is the actual transcript I checked, and it is certified by Lord Stormonth Darling as correct—I did early in my appointment communicate with Messrs. Abbott, who had audited the books—I obtained from them a copy of their report and balance-sheet, and also some books—I prepared from the books and from Messrs. Abbott's balance-sheet a bal ance-sheet up to January 16th, 1895, the date of my appointment— Exhibit  253 is the balance-sheet which I prepared, with a profit and loss account from March 31st, 1894, to January 10th, 1895—the profit and loss account shows £2,739 odd—my profit and loss account shows that the total amount earned in that, period was £152 1s.—I think there was tid., toomuch, and did not go back over the older account; I took Messrs.
