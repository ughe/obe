   MR. PARRY . Q.  Before this charge was preferred, had you missed pro perty? A.  I had—I have no idea of the amount, there are many tons of metal, it would be impossible—the whole value I charge is 6 s .—the value is depreciated by being fused—this metal might be fused metal-type—I do not identify this as my property—a man has shown me some casting of the prisoner's—this is a portion of a stereotype-plate—part of it has been melted down—that is an act which the prisoner would have no authority to do—the part that has been separated on this block, is the writing part at the top and bottom—this reporter is a friend of mine—I did not see him' take a note—there is not the slightest pretence for saying that I employed a reporter—the prisoner was going to be employed on the Patent Journal  —there is not the slightest pretence for saying that it was in consequence of that I took him. EDWARD HICKS. I reside in Vine-street, Waterloo-road, and am a moulder, in the service of Mr. Silverlock—I have been with him ten years next May—I have examined these plates and block—this block is my master's pro perty—this is a plate of my casting, I am not positive when I cast it, but I think in Sept., 1847, or about that time—this plate, which has been partly melted, that  has "Court-plaister" on it, answers to one that I have missed a long time, and here is the back of the other—there were two missing, and these answer to them—here are some leads that I know—they have the mark of a plug which was fixed in a damaged place in the mould—here is a particular mark on this Patent Journal  stereotype—I was" sent for by Mr. Lucas—he com plained that it printed very thick, and he asked me to clear it out, which I did, and there is a nail-mark which has appeared in the printing for five or six months—here are the Royal arms—this is a cast of mine, taken from a large bill—here is the bill of it—I know these brass rules by the nicks at the back—they are not all nicked in this way: some are—I know them, they were cut for the Literary Gazette  —they are all marked, but not all in the centre—as it regards these leads, I have the mould here in which they were cast—I can identify them by the plug-mark in this mould—these are the leads that were cast in this mould, and by me—here is the plug-mark, and there is the same mark in the mould—there might be a mould taken of one of these leads, and then it would be recast, but then it would be a lighter colour—if this were recast I do not believe it would have the plug-mark on it in the same way it has now, it is so slight.
   Cross-examined. Q . I suppose there were thousands of pounds of pro perty of your master's about? A.
