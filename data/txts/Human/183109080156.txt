 1918.  RICHARD CRIBB and ELIZABETH WARNER were indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 2 pillows, value 8s.; 1 sheet, value 2s.; 1 pail, value 1s., and 1 candlestick, value 6d., the goods of Daniel Parker.
   JANE PARKER. I am the wife of Daniel Parker, and live in Laystall-street. The prisoners lodged with me above twelve months as man and wife, at 4s. 6d. per week; they never owed a week's rent - the man is a picture-frame maker; the man left the room, sent the rent, and to say he was not coming home, that he would not live with the woman any more, and that part of the goods were gone - this was on Saturday, the 3rd of September, I then went up into the room, and these things were gone; Warner had not left.
   JOHN SPENCER. I am servant to Mr. Brown, a pawnbroker, in Gray's Inn road. I have two pillows, pawned on the 4th and 7th of March, and a sheet on the 21st of January, for 1s. each - they were pawned by a man in the name of James Warner, No. 20, Laystall-street - the person who took them in has left.
   WILLIAM COX. I am a dealer in marine-stores, and live in Little Gray's Inn-lane. I bought a pillow-case, a candlestick, and pail of Warner, on the 1st and 2nd of September, and the 23rd of August - she was alone.
   THOMAS DANEY. I am a Policeman. I received the prisoners in custody - Cribb produced the duplicates to me, and said voluntarily that he had pawned the two pillows and sheet in Gray's Inn-lane.
   JOHN SPENCER. These are the duplicates from my master's shop.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Cripp's Defence. The things were sold without my knowledge or consent - I slept at my employer's for nine weeks, while the family was at Brighton; during that time she took the things out, and pawned them - I sent our foreman to pay the rent, and to say I should not live with her any longer.
 WARNER -    GUILTY. Aged 38. Confined Fourteen Days.
 CRIBB -    NOT GUILTY.
 1919.  CHARLES DOYLE was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of August , 1 steel-roller, value 2l. 10s., the goods of Noah Rous.
   NOAH ROUS. I am a smith. On the 22nd of August, between ten and eleven o'clock at night, my premises were shut up - this steel roller was there on the bench in the shop; when I came down, about six o'clock in the morning it was gone; it must have been taken before the shop was open - it must have been opened by a false-key; there was no violence used - I found it at Marlborough-street on the 29th, and am quite certain of it.
   GEORGE MYERS. I live in High-street, Bloomsbury, and am an iron-merchant. On the 24th of August, between four and five o'clock, the prisoner came into the shop - I asked what he wanted; he took this roller from under his apron - I examined it, found it belonged to some machine, and asked where he got it; he said he found it among some rubbish - I asked what kind of rubbish; he said shavings - I asked what employ he was in; he said he was out of place, that he had no parents, and was living with his aunt - he gave the name of John Jones, No. 33, John's-court, John-street, Haymarket; I asked if he knew it was stolen - he gave me no answer; I told him there had been a person to inquire after it - he said, "Has he?" I said I should detain it, and send a man with him to see if his address was correct, but before I could get round the counter, he ran out - I lost him, and did not see him again till the 29th, when he was apprehended; nobody ever came for it.
 ROBERT HUSON. I am an officer. The prisoner was given into my charge on the 29th, by another officer, who took him - I inquired, and found the direction he gave was false.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming down stairs one morning, to get some pieces of wood from some carpenter's rubbish, and among the shavings, under some large pieces of timber, I found this roller.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1920.  GEORGE DOVEY was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 1 sack, value 2s., and 4 iron weights, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Joseph James Lake.
   JOSEPH JAMES LAKE. I live in Drummond-crescent, St. Pancras, and sell vegetables in the street. On the 6th of September these weights were in my cart, which stood opposite my premises in a yard; when I got up at six o'clock in the morning they were gone - the gate of the yard had been broken off its hinges before; I found the prisoner in custody with them the same day - he is a stranger.
 Cross-examined by MR. BALL. Q. Your house looks into the yard? A. Yes - the gate was down which made the yard open; the sack was at the back of the cart, and the weights under my goods - nobody had any business in the yard; there is a piece of waste ground near there.
