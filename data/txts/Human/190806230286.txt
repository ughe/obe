On the last occasion when he was at North London Sessions he denied all his convictions, and he was put back till the following sessions in order that, warders might be brought from various prisons to identify him, and Mr. Loveland-Loveland said he was the most confirmed liar he had ever met with in his life.
 The Recorder observed that prisoner caused great inconvenience here at the last sessions. A jury had been sworn and the trial com  menced, when prisoner asked that it might be postponed in order that he might have the opportunity of calling witnesses to prove his identity. The jury therefore had to be discharged, as otherwise prisoner would have said he had been prevented calling witnesses by the wickedness of the court.
 Detective Weston said that prisoner after his last sentence peti  tioned the Home Office, stating that all the witnesses had committed perjury, and that the women employed, respectable hard working women, had put the sheets on him. Prisoner had never done any work. Witness had known him about eight years. He went from place to place robbing lodgings. He worked by himself, and usually disposed of things to dealers with second-hand stalls. He did not drink, and was very quiet. Witness was unable to say anything in his favour.
 Sentence, Five years' penal servitude.
   BEFORE JUDGE RENTOUL .
 (Monday, June 29.) BRAY  , John (43, meter inspector), who pleaded guilty last sessions of a common law forgery and uttering (see page 160) came up for judgment.
 Mr. Metcalfe, appearing for the prisoner, stated that communication had been made to the West Ham Corporation sitting on Thursday, and they came to the conclusion that having regard to the number of their employees they would not be justified in taking prisoner back into their service.
 Sentence, Three months' imprisonment in the second division.
