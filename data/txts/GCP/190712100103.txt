Mr. Marshall Hall. I am going to ask you one question before I
go into detail. Did you kill Emily Dimmook!
Prisoner: Of course, not. I mean it is ridiculous.
Mr. Marshall Hall. I wish for a direot answer.
Prisoner. No.
Mr. Marshall Hall. I do not wish it to be said that I did not put
the direct question. I put it.
Prisoner. No. I made her acquaintance in the Rising Sun
on the Friday mentioned in my statement, which is perfectly true.
I have heard the evidence of the man Crabtree. It is utterly false
in fact, it is dastardly. I hope God will destroy me this minute if I
ever knew Crabtree or have ever been in his house. I live within
stone's throw of the Rising Sun." I knew Ruby Young when she
was living in Liverpool Street, but I did not know that Phyllis Dim
mook lived nearly opposite. On the Friday night deceased asked me
for a penny for the gramophone, and I gave her one. I paid for
drinks. Later a boy offered some postoards for sale. They were of
A very common, inartistio kind. I produced some more artistio earde
from my pocket, and Dimmook chose the card which has been pro
duced. She said she collected them, and asked me if I would send
it to her, and write something pleasing on it to give it interest. I
signed the card with the name "Alice" at her request. She said
that was the mame of a friend I saw her again the following nicht
in Grest College Street, as I was on my way to the Ous Company's
offices in Camden Rond. On the Sunday night I did not see her, but
on the Monday night I saw her for some time in the "Rising Sun."
The man Roberts was there Mrs. Smith and Mrs. Lawrence were
there also. We had drinks and were all very friendly. I did not
take Dimmock to the Holborn Music Hall that night. I had a sketch
book, and she amused herself looking through it. She had some
intelligence, and I may say that appealed to me. I did not hear
Dimmock say that we had spent the evening in the "Adam and Evo"
public-bouse. I have never seen Dimmock in the “ Pindar of Wake
feld." It would be some time after 11 that I left the Rising Sun
on the Monday night. She called my attention on the Saturday night
to the fact that I had not sent the postoard. I posted it on the Sunday
night, I think, in the pillar box in Museum Street, after I had left
my brother's place. On the Tuesday night I was not in the Rising
Sun" at all. I was on my way to Red Lion Street to see about some
ink for a stylo pen when I met my brother in Theobald's Road. After
we had been to the public library and to the barber's, I accompanied
him home and had supper, and he afterwards came back with me to
Frederick Street to see father. I did not go out again after that
On the following evening. September 11, I was in the Eagle" publie

came in, and I introduced her to him as a "merry girl friend."
