138. JANE LEWIN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of Novem-
ber, 1 cloak, value 4l. ; 1 pair of stockings, value 6s.; and 1 towel, value
1s. the goods of George Alexander Morgan.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. This
SOPHIA MORGAN. I am the wife of George Alexander Morgan, and
live in Hadlow-street, Burton-crescent. The prisoner was at my house
Dearly a month, and left on the 8th of November she was very ill and I took
her in, knowing her before she left without telling me she was going-
about half an hour after she left, I missed a clouk and some pieces belong-
ing to it, a pair of stockings, a towel, a diamond ring, and a coat-I did
not see her again till the 21st, when she was in custody these stockings,
cloak and towel, I know to be mine (looking at them.)
Cross-examined by Mr. DOANE. Q. Have you any mark on the
stockings 1 4. No- there is a mark on the towel-it was made by my
daughter I knew the prisoner before I took her in out of mere charity-
my husband is a tailorwe occupy all the house we have no lodgers
I had none when she was with me my daughter made this clonk-it was
not made for the prisoner-I swear that positively-1 know Margaret
Newman-she is a washerwoman I was never in company with my
daughter and the prisoner and her she used to come backwards and forwards
to the prisoner--we never had gin together the prisoner never said that I
had got the cloak for her, nor any thing of the kind I never received a
sixpence from the prisoner in payment of the cloak.
Mr. Payne. Q. How much is the cloak worth? 4. It cost me 4L
before it was made-I took her in from charity.
Joun WALL (police-constable C 63.) I received charge of the prisoner,
on the 19th of November, from Mrs. Barrett, in Regent-street-she denied
the charge--I asked where she lived-she told me, and, after taking her
to the station-house, I went to the address she gave me, which was "No.
31, John-street, Edgeware-road "-I opened the door of her room with a
key she had given me, and found a pair of stockings wrapped up in a
towel, which I have produced—she had told me that she had the cloak-
that it was made for her, and it was had on tally, and that it was pawned.
Cross-examined. Q. When you say she denied it, she was charged with
stealing it? A. Yes--she did not tell me where it was pawned-Mrs.
Newman gave me a quantity of duplicates.
MR. PAYNE, Q. Mrs. Newman is the washerwoman? 4. Yes--she
gave up sixteen duplicates, after some remonstrance, without the cloak;
and on my telling her she would get herself into trouble, she went out for
the one for the cloak, and produced it.
MO
RICHARD AVANT. I am shopman to Mr. Ridpath, pawnbroker, in
Upper Seymour-street. I produce a cloak, which was pawned with us on
the 10th of November for 1l. 108., in the name of Mary Hunter, 15, Drum-
mond-crescent, by a middle-aged person, not the prisoner.
HARRIET BARRETT. I am the wife of John Barrett, of Weston-street,
Pentonville-he is a traveller. I made this cloak for my mother, the pro-
secutrix, and she wore it-I made an ilet-hole in this towel, as I could not
mark, and I know this is my mother's towel.
(MR. DOANE, for the prisoner, stated that the prosecutrix kept a house of ill
Jame, and had agreed to sell the prisoner the cloak to walk the streets with.)
MARGARET NEWMAN. I am single, and live in Lancaster-street with
my mother, who takes in washing. I know the prisoner and prosecutrix -
I recollect seeing them together about a fortnight or three weeks ago in
