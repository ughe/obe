JOSEPH WILLIAM PARKS. I live in Kent-street, Borough—the prisoner was in my service. On 20th Nov. I sent him to Mr. Smith, of Lambeth-walk, for some iron—I gave him two half-crowns and 13 shillings—he did not re turn—I afterwards has him taken—I said, "What have you to say in answer to this charge?"—he said, "I did not do it with intent to steal, you can't lock me up"—I fetched a policeman, and took him to the station—I found on him 4 s . 10 1/2 d . JOSEPH SMITH. The prisoner did not come to me for any iron on 20th Nov.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I met with two young men; I got drinking; I lost part of the money; I had part of it about me when I was taken. GUILTY . Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .
 394. ISAAC HOARE and JAMES BEAZLY , stealing 1 loaf of bread, value 3 d  .; the goods of James Scott . JOHN JOHNS. I live with my mother, at Wandsworth. On 26th Nov. I saw Hoare take a half-quartern loaf out of a baker's barrow—he ran a long way—I did not see what he did with it—Beazly was about three yards and a quarter away from him when he took the loaf—he walked down after him. WILLIAM GREGORY. I was with Johns, and saw Hoare take the loaf and run down by the French Horn—he stopped under the railway arch—I saw Beazly three or four yards away from him.
   Hoare. Q.  Did you see me with the loaf? A.  Yes, you put it under your coat. ALEXANDER SCOTT. This was my father's James Scott's, barrow—I left is in the street—I missed a loaf when I came to book my bread at night.
   Hoare.  I know nothing of it.
   HOARE  — GUILTY .
   BEAZLY  — NOT GUILTY .

On 29th Nov., at a quarter past nine o'clock, I left my horse and chaise in care of West—I left a cloak in the chaise—this produced is it. HENRY WEST. I am horsekeeper at the Falcon, at Kingston. About nine o'clock that night the prosecutor told me to put a cloth on his horse—I then saw a cloak in the chaise—I doubled it up, and laid it on the cushion—when I came back with the cloth it was gone. WILLIAM READ( police-sergeant, V  18.) I found this cloak by the door of a cottage, in a private road. JAMES STEDMAN( policeman, V  217.)
