61. WILLIAM HARPER (41). Stealing nine bagatelle balls, the pro
perty of Frederick James Salmon.
MR. SATFORD conducted the Prosecution. A
FREDERICK JAMES SALMON. I keep the Star in the East public-house,
at East Greenwich-on Wednesday, 11th November, the prisoner was em
ployed as & painter at my house - I noticed my bagatelle balls safe in the
billiard room on that morning--after the prisoner had gone to his dinner
missed them-next morning, when he came to work, Paocused him of
having taken them, and told him to bring them back and I would not say
any more about it-he said he did not know anything about them-1 gave
information to the police, a detective came, and I went to the station with
him, where I saw the balls and identified them I can speak to four of them
by the spots on them- they are worth about 208,
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. It was on the Wednesday this took
place you went away, and did not come to work in the afternoon.
BENJAMIN POWLING. I am assistant to Mr. Phillips, Pawnbroker 62,
High Street, Deptford-about 3.30 on the afternoon of Thursday, 13th
November, the prisoner came to my shop with those nine bagatelle balls to
pledge-I asked him to whom they belonged, and he said they were his
from what my foreman said to me, I went with the prisoner on the way to
the station - he went into a public-house to get some beer, and escaped
leaving the balls in my possession I went to the station, and gave & descrip-
tion of him to the police.
WILLIAM BARRY (Detective Sergeant R 141). I received a description of the
prisoner, and apprehended him-I charged him with stealing nine bagatelle
balls-he made no reply.
Frisoner's Defence. Mr. Salmon says I was away from work, the book
was produced here, it would prove the contrary, that I was working all the
day on Wednesday, having half an hour to dinner and I riever left the
house from 7 o'clock in the morning till 5 in the evening. GUILTY.
He also PLEADED GUILTY to having been before convicted in May, 1870-
