ANN BARKER. On the 25th I went to the rabbit place—I saw a nail on the water-butt lid, and a knife by the shed door—it had been undone, and every one of the rabbits were gone from the shed. JAMES BRANNAN( police-sergeant G  20.) On the 26th of Feb. I went to the prisoner's lodgings in Golden-lane—I knocked at a room on the first floor—one of the prisoners opened the door—they were both in bed—I said, "I have come after some rabbits"—George covered his head over with the bed-clothes—John said, "We have no rabbits"—I said, "Yes, you have; come, George, get up"—he said, "We have none here; the gentleman next door keeps rabbits, perhaps they are there"—I waited till they dressed—the other officer called out, "The rabbits are here"—I took the prisoners down stairs, and the rabbits were in a cupboard—I said, "I thought you had no rabbits here"—John said, "Yes, my brother came in with a man who was going to be distrained on, and I bought them of him"—I said, "Where is the white buck you were hawking about yesterday?"—I meant George—John said, "I killed it, as the saying is, to save his life"—I found the dead rabbit in the cup board, and the doe and the young ones my brother officer took out of a bag in the cupboard—it was John Gibson's shop—he is a turner—I have repeatedly seen him at work in the shop—John said to George, "I told you it would come to this."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know that George was at work for Mr. Clark? A.  No—their house was half a mile from the prosecutor's.
   George Gibson's Defence.  I thought it was the landlord come after me for the rent.
 (The prisoners both received a good character.)
   GEORGE GIBSON  — GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Six Months .
   JOHN GIBSON  — NOT GUILTY.
 1029. MARY JONES was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of Feb ., 2 shillings, 3 sixpences, 1 groat, 4 pence, 6 halfpence, and one farthing, the monies of William Noonan . WILLIAM NOONAN. I live in Plough-court, near Field-lane. I have known the prisoner three weeks—about one o'clock in the morning of the 28th of Feb., she knocked at my room door, and said she had been turned out of doors by the party she lodged with—my wife got up, and let her in—I had two shillings, three sixpences, and the other money stated in my pocket—I let her bide in the room, and went to sleep—I saw her go out about seven o'clock, and soon after I perceived my trowsers, which I left on the back of the chair, had been removed, and my money was gone—I afterwards met her in Whitechapel—I asked her if she was not ashamed, for robbing me—she began to cry, and offered me part of the money—I would not accept of it.
