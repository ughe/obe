 817. WALTER PATEMAN  (19) and THOS. REYNOLDS  (18) , Robbery with violence on Thos. Schofield , and stealing a hat, some veal, and other goods.
   MR. CANDY conducted the Prosecution; and MESSES. RIBTON and PURCELL the Defence. THOS. SOHOFIELD. I am an engine-driver in the London, Chatham, and Dover Railway Company's employ—on Saturday, the 14th July last, I took down an engine to fetch a train from Swanley Junction—I had some refreshment that evening at the Bull public-house, near Birchwood Corner—it is kept by a Mr. Hutton—I left that house, a little before nine o'clock, to return to the station—I was not due to leave the station till eleven o'clock—I took a parcel of food from the Bull for my fireman—as I was going along the road to the station I saw two men come towards me on donkeys—I was then about half a mile on my way—I made way for them to pass, and just as I thought they had passed I received a blow on the back of the head; I stumbled forward, and as soon as I could recover myself I turned to look who it was—I saw the faces of both men—one was on a donkey, and the other standing then—they were quite close to me, and I made the remark that it was cowardly for two men to attack a little man like me, and the one on the donkey (Pateman) said "That's the b——y Yorkshireman"—with that they both fell to beating me with sticks and kicking me about the body and head, Pateman having got off the donkey—I was in the road, and they kicked me when I was standing up and when I was down—my hat was lost—they continued to kick and beat me—one of them said "What have you under your coat?" and I told them it was food for my mate—one of them pulled open my coat and took the parcel from me, and by some means or other I managed to get up—as I was running away I received three blows on the side of my head with a stick—the third blow knocked me down again—when I recovered I saw the men going up the hill in front of me—I followed them as well as I could to the Bull—just before they got to the Bull they turned down a lane to the right—that is a lane leaning towards Dartford—one donkey was a lightish one, and the others were more of a brown, darkish—there were three donkeys, but two men—Mr. Hutton took care of me, and had me washed and drove me back afterwards to the station, and I went up to town with my fireman on the engine—I went to Dartford the nest day and lodged a complaint.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON .
