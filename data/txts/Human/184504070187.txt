   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  You say he was to have taken them home to a gentleman that evening? A.  Yes—they are completely finished—I saw him in custody between three and four o'clock on the day I went to the shop—these trowsers, which he was to have taken home on the Saturday night, were then on him—they are worth 18 s .—I have not found any of the other missing articles—when the prisoner was at my house at nine o'clock that night he bad a pair of new boots, but he had not these trowsers—I expected him to have taken the trowsers home before he came to me—he did not take them home, and the gentleman refused to take them in—the prisoner never offered them to him.
   COURT . Q.  How do you know that? A.  Because I was obliged to make the gentleman another pair instead of them—I do not know that he did not give them to the prisoner—Richard Harvey occasionally worked for me—I owed him 7 s .—I gave the prisoner a 5 s . piece, and a half-crown to pay to Harvey, about six o'clock that evening. RICHARD HARVEY. I live in Drory-lane. I am sometimes employed by the prosecutor—previous to the 22nd of March he owed me 7 s ., the balance for making a coat—I went on that Saturday evening to bis shop for the money, about eight o'clock—I found the shop closed—I have not received any of the money. HENRY SMITH( police-constable A  28.) I apprehended the prisoner at a beer-shop in Cromer-street, on Monday, the 24th of March, about half-past three o'clock—I told him it was for absconding with a coat and trowsers, and some money—he did not say anything—he was drank—these trowsers were on him.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Six Months .
