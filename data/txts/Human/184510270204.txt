 2175. WILLIAM BAINES and WILLIAM REYNOLDS were in dicted for a like offence. LOUISA JULIAN. I keep a tobacconist's shop. On the 22nd of Sept. Baines came in for a pennyworth of tobacco—I served him with it—he paid me with a 4 d . piece—I did not look particularly at it—I had looked at the till shortly before—I had no silver but a shilling in it—as Baines stepped out the policeman stepped in, and in a hurried manner said, "What did that man pay in?"—I said, "A groat"—my son went and took it out of the till, bit it, and gave it to him.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  How did you put the 4 d . piece in the till? A.  I opened the till and put it in—I saw there was only 1 s . in it—I examined it in one minute afterwards. JOHN JULIAN. I am the son of Louisa Julian. I came into the shop directly Baines went out—the officer came in—I took the 4 d . piece out of the till and bit it, and handed it to him—there was only 1 s . in the till besides. SARAH AITKIN. I am the wife of Charles Aitkin. We live in Great Suffolk-street, in the Borough—on the 22nd of Sept. Reynolds came into our shop for a 1 d . saveloy—he paid me with 4 d . piece—I put it into the till—the officer came in—I took it out and gave it him—I had no other silver in the till.
   Reynolds. Q.  Did you give me the change? A.  I laid it on the counter—I do not know whether you had it. JOHN WARD. I am barman at the Red Lion public-house at the corner of Great Sutton-street, Southwark.
