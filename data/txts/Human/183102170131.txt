 655.  JOHN TOWERS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 1 waistcoat, value 1s. 6d., the goods of James Kevill.
 JAMES KEVILL. I live in  Lucas-street; the prisoner was servant to Captain Pearson, and lived in the same house. I saw a waistcoat at the Police-office, which was mine; I had not missed it - it had been in a trunk in my room.
   DAVID MOSES. I am in the employ of my brother, who keeps a slopseller's shop, in High-street, Shadwell. On the 16th of February the prisoner came, and brought a waistcoat in his hat, which he asked me to buy - I asked whose it was? he said his own - he asked 2s. for it, and I gave him 1s. 6d.; I gave the same waistcoat to the prosecutor.
   HENRY LEE. I am a Police-constable. On the 16th of February I took the prisoner, in the Back-road, St, George's in the East - I said, "Don't you live at Mr. Pearson's?" he said Yes - I said, you must go with me; he said he had taken the waistcoat.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I lived with Captain  Pearson  some months - I was sick, and I took the waistcoat to get some medicine from the hospital; I came from Spanish-town, Jamaica.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s., and Discharged.
 656.  JOHN WATTS was indicted for embezzlement.
   THOMAS DAFFURN. The prisoner was in my employ to do odd jobs, for which he receives 7s. a week from each coachman. On the Wednesday before the 5th of February he was sent out with a cart - he was to remove some furniture; one of my men sent him to the job - I do not know what passed between the man and him; I saw the prisoner when he came back, and received 3s. 6d. from him - he said that was all he could get, but the job came to 5s. 3d.
   WILLIAM DAIRY. On the 2nd of February I told my wife to pay the prisoner 5s. 3d. for three hours and a half, at 1s. 6d. an hour - it was for moving some goods in Mr. Daffurn's van: the prisoner had the management of it -I saw the money paid to him.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Have you a person in your employ named Charles Franklin? A. No-I had sent a person to the yard for the van, but he is not here; I do not know who he delivered the message to.
 MR. DAFFURN re-examined. Q. Had the prisoner any permission to attend with the cart? A. Certainly, if a job came in; I had not that day given him authority to go with it - I was not at home; he had a general authority to go with it, and receive money - he had been a regular servant at one time, and has worked there two or three years.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you a person in your employ named Charles Franklin? A. Yes, but he is not here; it would not have required any authority for the prisoner to go with the van.
 Prisoner's Defence. Mr. Hancock came to me with a message from Mr. Dairy to remove some goods; I went to do the job on my own account - I met Mr. Franklin, and asked him for a van, which he said I could have; I went, received 5s. of Mr. Dairy, and there was a man with the van who had 3d. - I went home, and paid my master 3s. 6d. for two hours and three-quarters, at 1s. 3d. an hour - I kept the other 1s. 6d. for my trouble.
 MR. DAFFURN. If he had asked me to let him do it, I, perhaps, should not have refused him, but he ought to have asked me - he would have received 6d. an hour for the job.
     NOT GUILTY.
 657.  LEONARD JAMES MITCHELL and ELIZABETH MITCHELL were indicted for stealing, on the 30th of October , 2 pillows, value 3s.; 1 bolster, value 3s.; 1 piece of patchwork, value 2s., and 1 looking-glass, value 1s., the goods of John Crawford.
 JOHN CRAWFORD. The prisoners were lodgers of mine, and passed as brother and sister. I missed this property on the 17th of January - the prisoners had been taken into custody on another charge on the 15th of January; they had lodged with me from the 29th or 30th of October, and the goods had been let them with their lodging.
   JAMES SPENCER. I am a pawnbroker. I have a piece of patchwork and a looking-glass, which I took in of a female on the 4th and 5th of November.
   WILLIAM BROOKS. I am a pawnbroker - I live in Bulstrode-street. I have two pillows and a bolster pawned on the 30th of October, for 3s., by the male prisoner - I am sure he is the person; I had seen him before - the prosecutor could not identify them before the Magistrate.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I am an officer. I took the prisoners on another charge; I found this duplicate in a box in the room which they occupied.
 Elizabeth Mitchell. My brother went to Mr. Brooks, and he refused to take some things of him on account of his youth, and then I took them - I have pawned things there, and taken them out again.
 WILLIAM BROOKS. Yes, she has, but she did not pawn these things; the male prisoner did, and said they were for his sister.
 Elizabeth Mitchell's Defence. I intended to redeem these things - I have stripped myself of every thing to support my brother, whom I have brought up; I could not turn him into the street - I have been three days without food, and always paid my rent.
   JOHN CRAWFORD. They paid the rent, but I believe they pawned my things to do it - her brother was not present when I let her the lodging.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. When I went into the room the bed was stripped, and they were in the greatest distress - there were fifty-four duplicates in the box.
 The prisoners received a good character.
   E. MITCHELL - GUILTY. Aged 28. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
   L.J. MITCHELL .   NOT GUILTY.
