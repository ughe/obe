Thomas Grant. I took him into custody, and have produced the yarn-
on the road to the station-house, he said he put the yarn in his breeches as
he had a bad leg, and he thought it would ease the pain.
Prisoner's Defence. I had been ill with a bad leg, and had taken physic
in the morning-I wanted to ease myself, and I put the yarn in my
trowsers until I could get to the privy-it was not done with the intention
to rob master.
Thomas PEARSEY re-examined. When I stopped him, he was not going

Jane, 1 printed book, value 6s. 6d., the goods of James Johnson. 7
ETWALL HEBBARD MARTELL. I am a student in law, at Lyon's-inn,
I was at Johnson's shop on the 4th of June, in High-street, St. Giles'
I saw the prisoner scufiling with Mr. Crouch, outside the shop-he got
from him I followed the prisoner into Seven-dials-he ran into a gin-
shop-I saw him directly afterwards in the custody of Carle.
WILLIAM CARLE (police-constable F 77.) I was on duty in Seven-
dials, on the 4th of June, and heard a cry of "Stop thief I saw the
prisoner running down Great St. Andrew's-street--he ran into a gin-shop
--I caught him as he came out at the other door, and held bim until Mr.
Martell came up and gave him in charge- I received this book from John-
son's shop.
JAMES Johnson. I am a bookseller in High-street, Bloomsbury.
This book is my property-I did not know it was lost until afterwards. Y
DANIEL NEWTON CROUCH. I am an engraver, and live in Greek-street,
Soho. On the evening of the 4th of June I was in High-street, St, Giles's
--I saw the prisoner looking at some books in Johnson's shop window
I suspected and watched him-I saw him take a book, slip it down his
side, and walk off-I followed him about two yards and collared him he
threw the book down, got from me, and ran up Compton-street- I took it
back, and afterwards saw him in Carle's.custody- I am certain he is the
man.
Prisoner's Defence, Distress drove me to it.
