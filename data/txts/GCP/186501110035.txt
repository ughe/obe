ALFRED RYDER. I am a warehouseman in the employ of Mr. Johnson,
of London-wall-I remember the prisoner coming with a van, but cannot
recollect the night, because he calls every night-I received a barrel of
oysters from him-there was no address-card on it-he laid it on my
packing-board, and I paid him the price of the oysters, 48.--the barrel had
Sweeting's brand on it.
COURT. Q. Had you asked him to bring them ? A. No-I was not
surprised-I never bought any before—he brought them in and said,
“Ryder, I can sell you this barrel of oysters"-I said, “Well, what do you
want for it?"-be said, 48.—I gave it him and he left.
HENRY WILLIAM THORN (City-policeman, 209). I took the prisoner in
custody, on 23rd December, at the Bull and Mouth-I searched him at the
station, and found this card and this gold watch in his fob, also the address
paper in which it had been packed—I charged him with stealing the parcel
containing this gold watch-he said that be bought it in Leadenhall-street
-I said, “How did you get this paper in which it was packed I”-he said
he could not account for it.
SAMUEL JACKSON. I am a watch manufacturer, of Red Lion-street,
Clerkenwell on 23rd December I sent this gold watch packed in & box
(produced) like this addressed to Mr. John Ching, Launceston, Cornwall —
here is an inscription inside it which I know perfectly well-it was wrapped
in wadding and properly secured, and sent to the Great Western Railway
booking office.
HENRY WING. I am in Mr. Jackson's employment-on 23rd December
I received a parcel from him to take to the Bull and Mouth-this box is
about the size of it, and this cover (produced) was wrapped round it-it was
securely packed, I delivered it in the same state to Mr. Crutcher between
7 and 8 o'clock, and my book was signed.
