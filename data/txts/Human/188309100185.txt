I submitted it to Mr. Pound; he would not entertain it—I came back and told the prisoner so, and that a warrant was out for his apprehension—since the new manager came in there is an increase of over 1  l . a day.
   Cross-examined.  I have the books that show that; they are in my writing—that is the only book I have here which shows it—the takings were larger in corresponding weeks in September last year than this year—the weeks vary from time to time—I nave been with Mr. Pound five years, and have been managing clerk to this house since its opening in October, 1881; previous to that I had had no practical experience of public-houses—I was a sergeant major in the army, and had a good deal to do with the stock in the canteens and officers' mess—I Know the ordinary way of taking stock—we hadn't a proper gauging-stick for the spirits; we used a deal lath; it answered the purpose—you could get it within a quarter of a pint—the manager after a little calculation would give the quantities—I put it in the book, and worked it out afterwards—on 24th June I put down the quantities as called out and decided on—in this book all these are "quarts," because they were decanted—the original entries I made were in pencil; they have been entered in ink in his  presence since—this ( produced ) is the rough book—ink is written over the pencil in every entry in this book—every item in this book was prepared previous to going to the hotel, except the figures—the ink was an exact fac-simile  of the pencil, only the figures were written over—to the best of my belief every one of the figures were originally in pencil—on 26th August all the figures were not previously in pencil; they were copied from this book—it was not done in the same way in August as in June—I have repeated the conversations from memory; I have not been tutored in any way—I did not tell the prisoner that if he would only put the matter right I would be his friend, or that if he would find 15 l . and pay the rest by instalments I would get Mr. Pound to put the rest right—I suggested that he should rake up some money to reduce the deficiency, and if he had I would have taken it—the figures 108 l . 6 s . 10 d . were the result of my own working out—I did not take stock at all between 24th June and 26th August—books were kept at the hotel showing a proper retail account of wines, spirits, ales, mineral waters, and cigars—I several times got goods of the prisoner and paid for them—the only time no money passed was when I joined a Christmas club there—I did not get the spirits which I said I would take instead of the 1 l . 12 s . 8 d . due to me, and so I wrote and told him to send me certain things, and no money passed—I don't know whether his club accounts were right—I know his wife took to drinking, and had as much access to the till as he had—I believe her drinking bouts lasted for a long time—he repeatedly told me they were a great source of trouble to him—I have never heard of one spot or blemish on his character.
   Re-examined.  There were only a few bottles and casks broken into, and it was only in those cases we used the gauge—I took the figures from the defendant himself. WILLIAM ANDREW BASNETT. I am the last witness's son, and clerk to Mr. Pound—I accompanied my father on Sunday, 26th August—I assisted at the stocktaking; the prisoner was present—I heard what was said at the interview on Tuesday; what my father has said about it is correct.
