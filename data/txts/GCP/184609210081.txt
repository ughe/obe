JAMES SAMUEL. I am the resident engineer on the Eastern Counties Rail
way. I prepared this plan-it is a correct representation of the part of the
line where this accident happened it was prepared under my direction, to
show the working of the signals-it does not show the curve--it was made to
a scale--there are two bridges before leaving the Stratford station--this
signal (looking at the plan) was about 259 yards from the hindmost carriage at
the station where the train stopped-it can be seen by the engine-driver, if he
keeps a proper look-out, at a distance of 479 yards--more than a quarter of
a mile the point A in the plan is the spot from which you can first see the
signal, that is, at a distance of 220 yards from the signal; and from B, where
the signal is, to C, where the station is, is 259 yards you can see the signal
from the windlass. so sis

Cross-examined by Me. CLARKSON. Q. Are you quite sure of that? A. I

curye between the signal and the windlass of a radius of about a mile, and
there are two bridges intervening--the white part shows itself to the man at

- the signal has never been out of order since I have been there I have
been there since the 1st of Jan. I believe the windlass is not above ten yards
from the station, but I cannot speak positively; I have never measured it
it has never been reported to me that the drivers have made complaints of
the signals at Stratford being dangerous--if it was a subject matter of com-
plaint to Mr. Scott, he never communicated it to me-I do not consider it &
dangerous part of the line's not a bit more dangerous than any other part-I
do not consider it safe for a train of trucks to go without a guard, or without
a bind breakit may be safe without, because many trains do go 80, and no
accident occurs; but, in my judgment, I believe that it is inconsistent with
safety, or rather, that we run a risk in sending out a train without a hind
break.
COURT. Q. Will a break on an empty truck at the end have much effeet ?
A. It would have very little effect in stopping the train the effect of a break
depends a good deal or mainly on the weight of the carriages—the weight
naturally increasing the friction-two of these trucks contained some iron.
