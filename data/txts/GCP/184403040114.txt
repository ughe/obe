Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
1010. JOHN FELSTEAD was indicted for stealing 1 shawl, value 108.,
the goods of Elizabeth Ann Donovan.-2nd Count, stating it to be the goods
of Charles Alfred Read.
MICHAEL CASEY (police-constable K 278.) On the 27th of Dec. I received
information, and went to Mr. Phillips's, a pawnbroker's, and found a shawl
-I stopped it-I searched for the prisoner, and took him at Hyde Park
corner, on the 12th of Feb.-I told him it was on a charge of stealing a
shawl on the 21st of Dec. last- he said yes, he did, and his wife would
make up the money to compromise the case-I went to his lodging, and he
gave me the duplicate.
REBECCA Isaacs. I am assistant to Mr. Phillips, pawnbroker, Stratford.
On the 22nd of Dec. the prisoner pawned this shawl-I gave him this
duplicate.
ELIZABETH ANN Donovan. I am single. This is my shawl-I had
been at my cousin's, Mrs. Read's, at Stratford-I came away and left it
there on the 21st of Dec.-on the 23rd Mrs. Read put it into a parcel, and
sent it to my mother-I opened the parcel, and missed the shawl.
SARAH READ. I am cousin to Miss Donovan, She was at my house, and
on the 21st of Dec. she left-I made up a parcel of her things, and put this
shawl in it-I asked the prisoner to take the parcel to her mother, and I de-
livered it to him.
Prisoner's Defence. I took the things to her mother, and as I had nothing
to do I took the shawl, but my wife was to have made it up.
Guilty. Aged 31.-Confined One Month.

Before Mr. Recorder.
1011. JOHN BOTT and JOHN HAWKSWORTH were indicted for
stealing 3 gowns, value 12s., the goods of Charles Weir.
CELIA WEIR. I am the wife of Charles Weir, of Jackson's-lane, Woolwich,
and am a laundress. On the 25th of Jan., between ten and eleven o'clock in
the morning, I put three gowns out to dry in the drying-ground-they were
missing between six and seven in the evening.
Rosetta WEIR. I am daughter of last witness. I missed these gowns
between six and seven o'clock on the 25th of Jan.
THOMAS STANTON. I am coachman to Sir George Whitmore, and live in
Jackson's-lane. On the 25th of Jan. I met two persons dressed in fustian
clothes and caps-Hawksworth was one-I knew him well before, but not
the other they were coming out of the lane between six o'clock and ball
past; and as I came back again I saw them lower down the lane, going over
the rails close to the drying-ground—the lane is no thoroughfare.
GEORGE HALL, shoemaker, Jackson's-lane. On Thursday evening, the
25th of Jan., I saw the prisoners standing near the drying-ground-1 keer
them well before, and have no doubt of them - I did not see them do any
thing--they were standing in the lane--they had no bundle that I saw.
