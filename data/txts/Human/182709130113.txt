 1814.  JOHN HITCHCOCK, CHARLES HAWKINS, and JAMES PAGET, were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , at St. Leonard, Bromley, 1 gelding, price 21l., the property of Joseph Kain.
 MR. JOSEPH KAIN. I am a  shipowner, and live at St. Leonard's, Bromley, in Middlesex. On the morning of Tuesday, the 12th of July, I lost a gelding out of a field, at the back of my house - I had seen it secure about ten o'clock the night before; the gate was then locked. On Tuesday morning my gardener came and told me the gate was broken open, and the gelding gone - I had handbills printed, and advertised it in the Times Newspaper - and on the Sunday evening, three Bow-street patrols came to me - I know the gelding to be mine; I know nothing of the prisoners.
   FREDERICK GROSSMITH. I am a Bow-street officer. About half-past two o'clock in the morning of the 12th of July, I was with McWilliams and Tyrrell, in Church-row, Bethnal-green; it was just then getting light. I saw Hawkins riding on a gelding, at the corner of Busby-street, which leads out of Church-row - I suspected all was not right, and ran after him; he was only walking - I pursued him up Busby-street, and when I came up with him, I asked him whose horse that was; he hesitated a minute or two, and then said, he was a drover, and was coming from Romford, that about a quarter of a mile below Bow-bridge, a man in a fustian coat hired him to ride the horse to Church-street, Bethnal-green, and he was to have 1s. - but he was then riding away from Church-street - Tyrrell had got up by this time; he took Hawkins; I lead the horse to the corner of Brick-lane; McWilliams went to the farther end of Church-street, near Shoreditch, where Hawkins said the man was to wait for him - he returned in about ten minutes, and said there was no one waiting; that nobody was there but the watchman - we then took Hawkins to Bethnal-green watch-house. Tyrrell and I took the horse away, and at the corner of Hare-street, Brick-lane, we saw Hitchcock coming along - this was about six hundred yards from where we met Hawkins; we took him, and Tyrrell found a quantity of oats in his pocket; he was walking toward Church-street - we took him to the watch-house - we asked him where he had been - he said in the brick fields all night; we said nothing to him before we searched him. We asked Hawkins, in his presence, if he was the man who gave him the horse; he said No; we locked them up. I shewed the same horse to Mr. Kain, who claimed it.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I am an officer. On the 12th of July I saw Hawkins go down Busby-street, with the horse, as my brother officer has stated; we saw Hitchcock; he was dressed as Hawkins had described - I stopped him and found some oats in his pocket. I then took off his hat, and found in the lining of it, the remains of some oats - I asked Hawkins if he was the man; he said No.
   JOHN McWILLIAMS. I am an officer. I was with the other officers - I left them at the top of Brick-lane, and went to the end of Church-street, where Hawkins said he was to meet the man. I waited there ten minutes or more, and could see no man; I enquired of the watchman; then came back and told them nobody had been seen there.
   JOSEPH ADAMS. I am a street-keeper. I received information from Worship-street, that Paget' was wanted - I met him on the 28th of July, took him, and told him I wanted him for horse-stealing; he said, rather than go to the watch-house, he would go to Garton, as he knew it was all right - we went part of the way and he ran off, down an entry; I called out, and he was stopped at the end of Longalley - I took him to Garton, who said he was the person wanted.
   WILLIAM WILLIAMS. I am a watchman. At a quarter to ten o'clock on the 28th of July, I heard Adams calling Stop him! I stopped Paget in Meeting-house-court, Longalley; he said "Let me go, it is the street-keeper after me."
   JOSHUA ARMSTRONG. I am an officer. After Hawkins was taken, I went to Paget's house but could not find him at home.
   THOMAS GARTON. I was with Armstrong; in consequence of Hawkins' confession before the Magistrate, I went to take Paget.
   THOMAS WATERS. I am an officer of Worship-street. I went into the lock-up room, and Hawkins said it was very hard he should suffer as an innocent man, and wanted to tell me something - I said, "Don't say any thing to me; if you have anything to say, I will take you in to the Magistrate;" what he said was taken down - I made him no promise or threat.
   RICHARD SKILLERN. I am a constable of Bow-street. Hitchcock was apprehended on the 12th, and discharged; after that I heard he was wanted - I went and took him again on the 13th.
   WILLIAM KAIN. Grossmith shewed me the horse; it was mine - it is worth twenty guineas.
   JOSHUA ARMSTRONG. (Looking at the examinations returned by the Magistrate.) This is signed by Mr. Twyford; and I saw Hawkins make his mark to it (read.)
 "John Hitchcock came to me yesterday, at one o'clock, and told me Mr. Paget wanted to speak to me about a horse, it was a job to fetch a horse. I went, and Hitchcock and Roberts were there; they asked if I would go to fetch the horse - we went to the bridge at Mile-end, and Hitchcock told me to wait there while he went into a lane; when he returned, he led a chesnut-coloured horse, and told me to ride on to Church-street, Bethnal-green, and when I got to Church-street I was stopped - Hitchcock told me it was a Justice of the Peace's horse.  CHARLES HAWKINS X his mark."
   RICHARD SKILLERN. The other prisoners were not present when the statement was made - they were not in custody.
 HAWKINS - GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 24. Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor, believing others were concerned with him.
 HITCHCOCK -    NOT GUILTY.
 PAGET -    NOT GUILTY.
