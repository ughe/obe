I'll tell the truth, I can't be worse than I am: This Rogers brought the box to me about four o'clock in the afternoon to my lodgings, and bid me keep it till the morrow. The next day was a rainy day; he said he would not be kept under by his brother, and so brought his things away. He lay in my bed that night. Then he said he had lost the key of his box, and did not know how to get at his things, and desir'd me to get a hammer. I did, and he bid me tell my landlady it was my box; she gave me the hammer and I took out the things; said he, this woman may make a discovery that I am going to pawn my things. I said I'll take and carry them out. I went with them to the pawnbroker's; and he said he'd go and stay in the cook's cellar till I return'd, and said I might put them in either his or my name. The pawnbroker ask'd me whose goods they were, I said they belong'd to another man that had need of money. He said you must come and release them, then I said he might as well put them in my name. On the Wednesday following Rogers came again, and said, will you take care of the watchman till I pass him, for fear he should take me up with this box. Said I what are you afraid of; he said it was his own box, and he wanted to lodge with me that night. I said I could not carry the box in, they were all in bed. He came and gave me the box at the door, and left it in the room. The next day he came with a hatchet in his hand, and said, now Riley I have a secret to relate to you, if you will keep it I will make a man of you, and you will live well. Said he, I saw the ostler taking out money from a purse in a box, and I thought the first box was it, but now I have it, and there are a great many cloaths in it, and you and I will go away to Portsmouth, and I'll be under no dread of my brother. He fell on his knees to me, and prevailed on me not to tell; said I, see and get some money that will redeem the things already pawn'd, and carry them back. He said he brought them down a ladder three story high, and would not carry them back: I would not have brought the boxes down that ladder if you would give me twenty thousand pounds.

 349. (M.)  Robert Murray was indicted for the wilful murder of Edward Clark. He likewise stood charged for the said murder on the coroner's inquest, July 24. +
   Thomas Russel. I can't tell the day of the month, it was on a Sunday morning; as I was coming down the back lane the clock struck six: This was about a quarter of a mile from the prisoner's cellar door, in the back lane. When I came there I heard a noise. There was a man about buying of a shirt of the prisoner.
 Q. What is the prisoner?
 Russel. He  sells shirts and old shoes, and such things. Hearing a great noise I stop'd. I heard the man that is dead say, '' If you did not like '' the money that I bid you, you might have bid '' me go about my business, and said, you rogue, '' you have done me an injury, which, I think, I '' shall never get over; you had no occasion to '' use me so ill; here is money if you would have '' taken it.'' There was a sort of a skirmish in the cellar. Clark the deceased made haste up out of the cellar. Murray followed him, and push'd him with his right elbow several times in the stomach and bowels in the street. Clark said he would make him pay for it the next day. As the prisoner was following him I went away, and was absent the best part of half an hour. After that I saw the deceased, about a quarter of a mile from Murray's cellar, heaving up blood. I saw him do it three times.
 Q. In what place was he then ?
 Russel. I can't tell the name of the lane. He turn'd himself round, and I said, God bless me have you received some inward blow? He made me no answer. In a quarter of an hour after that he was dead.
 Q. Whether Clark made any resistance when this elbowing was?
 Russel. No, he made none.
 Cross Examination.
 Q. Are you a coal-heaver?
 Russel. No, I am not. I am a craft.
 Q. Who was there besides you?
 Russel. There were several people, but all strangers to me. I neither knew Murray nor the deceased before.
