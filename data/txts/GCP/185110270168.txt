Great Charlotte-street-Frost was in company with me; he was in plain
clothes ; I was in uniform-l followed the prisoner and the others with Frost
-Frost followed them first, and I followed after Frost, to the corner of Wel-
lington-street-Frost was running after the prisoner-the other two then
crossed the road, and one stepped before Frost on the pavement, bit Frost on
the head, and knocked him down.
Court. Q. Had the prisoner crossed the road? A. Yes; Frost followed
the prisoner; the other two then crossed the road, and one of the two got
before Frost-Frost seized him, and he knocked Frost down with a blow on
the side of the head, as well as I could see-Frost fell, and the man that
struck him fell down with him that was not the prisoner-I sprung my
rattle, and ran to Frost's assistance-the man who struck Frost jumped up.
and ran away directly-the other men ran up Blackfriars-road-I saw a hat
lying near Frost-I did not see it picked up-the prisoner had a cap on-1
have known him the last three months; I have seen him several times-
I have no doubt he is one of the men I saw at that time-I could not take
him-he took the first turning in Wellington-street, and ran towards St.
George's Church-we had a long run for it-I could not catch bim--the next
time I saw him was on Thursday, 2nd Oct., at the station-he spoke to me
first-he said, “You know me very well; you saw me at the coffee-shop"-
he did not say what coffee-shop-he said it was on Saturday night I had
seen him at half-past 1 o'clock on Sunday morning, at the Albert coffee-
shop, in Great Charlotte-street-I am sure I saw him there.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you see the bat fall from the party it belonged
to? A. No, I did not, it was not the prisoner's hat; he had a cap on when
I first saw him, and he had when I was running after him-I did not see him
strike or kick Frost-I have neyer said that I doubted whether the prisoner
was one of the party- I was examined before the Magistrate against this pri-
soner and Allen-when I saw the prisoner at the station he said I saw him
in the coffee-shop-he denied the robbery-he said he knew nothing about
the robbery, and had nothing to do with it-I do not think he said he was at
home at the time-I cannot swear it.
FRANCIS IRELAND (policeman, L 119). I was on duty, in Great Charlotte-
