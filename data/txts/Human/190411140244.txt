HERBERT GEORGE THOMAS. I was appointed by the directors a director of the Electric Construction Company in September, 1900; I cannot-call to mind the names of the directors at the time—I was a director two years and two months; I left in November 1902—this is my signature to this cheque to Sir Henry White as a dividend on his shares—as a director we had announced that we were in a position to pay a dividend of 12 per cent.; I cannot go any further than that—it was announced at the dinner that we were in a position to pay that: it was spoken of generally at the Board meetings—we resolved at our meetings that we were in a position to pay a dividend if necessary—I was never present at any resolution that a dividend should be declared: I was told at Bow Street that a dividend had been declared—my authority for paying £226 of the company's money on that cheque was that the recipient of it was the only man who had ever put any of his own money into the company, and he was deserving of a certain amount of interest—Mr. Paine had not put in any, that I am aware of—Sir Henry White had purchased shares for cash in the company—the company did not get it—I paid Sir Henry White money of the company because Sir Henry White was holding shares in our company, I presume—Mr. Paine held shares;
