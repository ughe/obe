   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  The purse fell through your dress, did it not? A.  I cannot say where it fell, or whether it fell under my dress—I saw it on the ground—I was examined at the Magistrate's—I did not say it fell from under my dress—what I said was taken down in writing, and read over to me.
   Q.  What you said was, "I saw the purse fall from under my dress." Is that true? A.  Yes—I examined my pocket—it had not been cut, Banks. Q.  Which side of Day was I standing? A.  I cannot say. JOHN WHITE( police-constable R  180.) On the 4th of February I was on duty about half-past two o'clock—I saw the prisoners arm in arm toge ther near the Arsenal-gate—I saw Day put his hand into the prosecutrix's pocket, and as he was drawing out his hand I caught hold of him by the wrist—I said, "Where is your hand now? does that pocket belong to you?"—on drawing his hand out from the pocket, the purse fell on the ground, I believe—I saw it on the ground, but I did not see it fall—I told the prosecutrix to pick it up.
   Banks. Q.  Where was I? A.  On the left side of Day, and his hand was in her pocket. CHARLES HEARNS( police-constable R  228.) I was present. I saw Day's hand in the prosecutrix's pocket—I saw something fall between her clothes on the ground—I could not tell what it was, but it proved to he this purse.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you in plain clothes? A.  Yes—I saw the purse fall at the time I saw Day's hand in her pocket. EDWARD FARNELL. I am a barman—I was near the Arsenal-gate—I saw the policeman holding Day's hand in the pocket—I did not see Banks till afterwards, when he was in custody—I saw the purse lying on the ground—I did not see how it came there.
   JURY to ELIZA KING . Q.  How long before had you removed the money from your pocket? A.  Not ten minutes—I had not moved at all—my gown was torn very much behind—I am sure I returned my puree into my pocket.
