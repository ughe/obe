JOHN GILL (Police Sergeant H.) I arrested Edwards--he was charged at
the station with being concerned with others in wounding with intent to
murder Myer Edgar and Thomas Tamplin on the evening of March 5th-
he said, "I was there, but I did not put a hand on either of them."
ANDREW NELSON SIMMONS, M.R.C.S. and L.R.C.P. I am clinical assistant
in the surgical outpatients' department of the London Hospital—I examined
Myer Edgar there on March 6th-he had three cuts on his head about an
inch long-that is all he complained of, and all I took any notice of - I saw
him again on the 10th and to-day-the wounds have healed-they were
likely to have been caused by these broken pieces of a jug.
Cross-examined by Mr. HUTTON. He had lost a considerable amount of
blood from clean cut wounds & billiard-cue would leave marks-it would
take a very heavy blow to break an ordinary man's skull—I should expect
to see a bump-it might easily cut the skin— chopper would not account
for the marks unless the sharp point was used.
Cross-examined by Sharper. It is conceivable that if he fell with the
jug in his hand it might have caused the cuts if the jug smashed first, but he
wouldve to tumble head foremost to do it.
Cross-examined by Edwards. I do not remember seeing you at the
hospital-I have not had your subpoena-I have had many serious cases
-if you had given me the card number I could refer to my notes.
The prisoners, in their defences, stated that there was a quarrel and a
general fight, and that the witnesses were committing perjury. GUILTY.
They then PLEADED Guilty to previous convictions, Bonn, at the Great
Midlothian Sessions, on January 25th, 1897, in the name of George
Marriott ; Sharper, on April 2nd, 1901, at Clerkenwell ; Brooks, at Clerken-
well, on June 20th, 1899; and Edwards, at Clerkenwell, on March 20th.
1900. Several other convictions were proved against each prisoner ; also
that they belonged to a dangerous gang. BONN, SHARPER, and BROOKS
-Five years' penal servitude each ; EDWARDS--Pour years' penal

OLD COURT.-Thursday, April 10th, 1902.
Before Mr. Justice Grantham.
322. WILLIAM WILSON (24), PLEADED GUILTY to the manslaughter of
Sarah Wilson. Five years' penal servitude-And (323) EMMA BURTON
(34), to unlawfully wounding George Ronald Burton. Judgment respited.
For other cases tried this day see Essex and Surrey cases.
NEW COURT.-Thursday, April 10th, 1902.
SE Before Mr. Recorder.
324. MICHAEL WHITTY (50) PLEADED GUILTY to obtaining the
execution of a certain security by false pretences. He received an excellent
character. Judgment respited. There were six other indictments against
him for forgery, to which he pleaded not guilty.
