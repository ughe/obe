Mr. Charnock conducted the Prosecution.
MARY BETTs. I am the wife of Stephen Betts, a butcher, at Putney.
The prisoner was in my service about three months-on the 31st of Aug., in
consequence of some circumstances, I required to search her box-the police-
man was sent for ; and on her consenting, I went up to her box in her bed-
room, and she unlocked it-I found in it two aprons belonging to my son
Stephen, and a sheet cut to pieces - I have put it together to see if it would
make a sheet- I can swear to it by a B which I put on it myself, and a little
dot-I found a long muslin curtain, which was an apron of my daughter's, and I
joined it together myself, and made a curtain-it is my own work-I found a
night-shirt of my son's in the kitchen table-drawer, which I can swear to by
8 B that was on it-the letters have been picked out, but I can see that there
has been the cotton there, I asked the prisoner how she came by the aprons-
she said she had made half-a-dozen aprons for a young man, and be had given
her these two-she said the curtain was her own-I said I could swear to it-
she said it was hers, and she had more like it - I told her to show me one like
it, and she showed me one which was clear muslin-the sheet, she said, she
bought of a housemaid at Streatham, where she lived servant-I had never
given her these things-she had no right or authority to have them in her
box.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. What is there on the aprons that
enables you to know they belong to you? 4. I had a dozen of them, and I
am two short out of the dozen-I missed them after I had searcbed her box
these are exactly like the others, and the person who made them is here
this shirt is torn--it is not an old one-here is a darn that I know it by, down
the seam, and here is a B en it.

CHARLES CANHAM ( policeman, V 58.) I was called in to be present at

Streatham-I went there to make inquiries.
