SAMUEL ROPER. I am a gold refiner, of 7, Gardner Street, Clerken-
well-I have known Clapham four years-throughout the year 1891 I
was purchasing gold and silver articles from him, watch-keys, chains, and
rings-I knew him as a pipe-mounter-on 30th October he brought me
four lumps of molten silver, and asked me to give him an estimate
-I said it would have to be re-melted and assuyed, and if he would call
the next day I would give him the price ; he did so—the weight was
129 oz. 5 gr., and I paid him £23 58. 6d., and gave him a ticket with
the amount on it-I had made other purchases of him during the month.
Cross-examined by Mr. BURNIE. I saw nothing improper in the trans.
action-he always behaved as a respectable man in my office.

PHILIP WILLIS (Policeman G). On 8th December I arrested Clapham,
and told him he would be charged with others in custody for house-
breaking and receiving-he said, "All right”-I sent him to the
station he gave his address, 17, Taplow Street-I went there; he
occupies the first floor froat-I afterwards went to Britannia Road, Isling-
ton, to a shop which had been used by Clapham; I know that from his
brother-Olapham gave me an address and a key, but the door was
unlocked-I asked him if he wished to make any reply; he said, “Not
at present”-these shears and four crucibles were brought to the
station.
