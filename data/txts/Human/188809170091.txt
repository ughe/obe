SAMUEL GUNNER. I am assistant clerk at Clerkenwell Police-court—this declaration was made before Mr. Barstow, the Magistrate, on June 2nd—the man who made it declared it to be true, and that that was his name and writing—I cannot say whether the prisoner is the man. WILLIAM CARTER. I live at 3, Adam's Cottages, York Road, King's Cross—I know the prisoner as working for Mr. Bennet for three months—letters in the name of John Smith were taken in for him at my house, and I gave them to him, but he did not live there. HARRY DIDDUMS( Police Sergeant G ). On 1st July I found the prisoner detained at King's Cross Police Station—I held a warrant for his arrest for making a false declaration—I read it to him—he said "I wrote to Mrs. Sweeney to ask her if she had my papers, and she wrote and said no." BRIDGET SWEENEY( Re-examined ). The prisoner never wrote to me, and I never sent any such answer—he left on Easter Monday—I did not get the papers from him when he was drunk.
   The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. "I wrote to my brother to ask Mrs. Sweeney for the identity certificate; he did not get it, and she said it was lost."
   Prisoner's Defence.  If Mrs. Sweeney had my papers she must have got them when I was drunk; I do not remember doing so; I have been in prison seven weeks. GUILTY.— Two Months' Hard Labour.
