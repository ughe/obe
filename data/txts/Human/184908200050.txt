   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Had you any occasion to examine the bag? A.  No; I was applied to to come here on Saturday; a friend of Mr. Sim mons sent me a subpoena—I have known him ten or twelve years—I heard he was in trouble about a fortnight ago—I did not attend before the Magi-strate—I have not seen the man since who brought the cloth—he was a respectable-looking man, in a dark frock coat—I have dealt with the prisoner for ready-made clothes.
   COURT . Q.  Do you know where he lived? A.  Somewhere in Holborn; I do not know the house exactly—I mentioned this transaction about a fort-night ago, to some friends of his—Mr. Jones was one person; he lives at the Clothes Exchange—I did not intimate to the prisoner that I could give this information—I had my own business to attend to—I do not recollect who else I mentioned it to—it is a month ago. JAMES DEVEREUX. I am a painter, of 24, Little Queen-street, Lincoln's-inn-fields. On 26th July, I was painting Mr. Solomon's shop—it was on Thursday, the wet day, when there was such a storm—a person came and offered some tweed for sale—I was graining the counter-front, and told him to take it off there, as the paint was wet—the mahogany front had been stained and oiled, and would have marked it.
   COURT . Q.  He did not put it down there? A.  No—it was a little after one o'clock; I go to dinner at twelve—he took the cloth out of the bag, and tossed it on the floor, I expect—I do not know, whether it was unrolled—I heard money bid.
