I returned at four in the morning, went in to go to bed, and directly I shoved the outer door open, I found Whitaker's room door open - I then went up stairs, and found mine all safe; I called to a woman who lived in the right-hand room.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do not you know that Hough and the prosecutor are very intimate friends? A. I have seen them drink together, and I have seen Hough about there; I have never seen him working in the yard.
   WILLIAM NORTON. I am a watchman. On Friday morning, the 9th of November, about half-past twelve o'clock, I saw the two prisoners and Lock come out of the King's Head-yard; Hough had a bag under his arm, I had not the least idea of it being stolen; I thought they had been at work down there, as Abdell, the nightman, lives down there. In the morning, I received information from Whitaker, went to his room, and saw the pannels broken, and the lock off the door.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long have you been a watchman? A. About a year and a half. I had no suspicion of them; I was in search of them for four days before I could find them - they were all three absent.
   JOHN LOCK. I was taken up for this felony, on Saturday night, the 10th of November. I went up to the King's Head, public-house, to get a pint of beer, on Thursday night, the 8th November, and Whitaker offered me a glass of rum and shrub; when I had drank it, I was going home; but Longman said "Don't go yet, I will put 1l. into your pocket;" when the house was shut up, we all went out, and stopped against the gate; Longman and Hough then went down the yard; the outer door of the house, where Whitaker lives, was open; Longman and Hough put their feet against Whitaker's room door, and broke it open; Longman went in, and brought out a hat, a coat, several waistcoats, trousers, handkerchiefs, and some harness; he gave them to Hough, who put them into a sack; we all went out of the yard - it was about twelve o'clock, or half-past; we went to Constable-alley, and Longman put the property under a house; we then went to Darkhouse-lane, near London-bridge, and staid their till four o'clock; we then returned to Constable-alley for the parcel, and I took it to a man named James Pagett, who brought a sovereign to the Golden Heart public-house, in Phoenix-street, kept by Van, that morning; I got it changed, and we had some steaks, and beer - the landlord took the reckoning out of it; in the course of the day, Pagett paid some more, which was put on the table and divided. I fetched the steaks myself from a butcher's in Brick-lane, and the landlord dressed them; the property was sold to Pagett.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you come here in custody? A. Yes, from the House of Correction; this is the second time I have been in a Court - I was here before, but only as a witness. I was in trouble myself last Session, on suspicion of carrying a parcel - I was not tried; the gentleman did not come against me - I was at the bar.
 Q. What did that parcel contain? A. Harness; I was never in custody on any other occasion. I did not break the door, nor carry the goods - I swear I did not break the door.
 JURY. Q. Did you sell this property? A. No; I saw Pagett about selling it; he gave 2l. for it all; he asked Longman how much he wanted - he told him 2l.; he lives at No. 2, Hope-street, Spitalfields. I had 5s. out of the sovereign, and we had 5s. each afterwards, and the beef steaks and drink among us. I took the things to Pagett, between six and seven o'clock in the morning - Pagett is not here.
   THOMAS WATERS. I am an officer. I took Lock at the Job's Castle public-house, near Shoreditch; I have been after Pagett several times - he has absconded
   THOMAS VAN. I keep the Golden Heart public-house, Phoenix-street, which is not more than twenty yards from Hope-street; I remember Lock and the two prisoners being there on Friday morning, and Pagett came in while they were there. I changed a sovereign at the bar for Lock - I did not see from whom he received it - I heard nothing pass between them; they had some beef steaks, which Lock brought - I cooked them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen Lock before? A. Yes; I knew Pagett - he was a near neighbour.
   GEORGE SMITH. On the 9th of November I was called up, and told of the robbery. I went with Norton and Whitaker, and saw the door was broken open; the watchman told me what he had seen: I went in search, but nothing has been found; Pagett's house was searched on Tuesday afternoon.
   HENRY WHITAKER re-examined. I took Hough into custody; he came into a public-house where I was sitting, and I charged him with this robbery - he said he had not done it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you not say, "You had better come before a Magistrate, and clear yourself?" A. Yes- he said, "I am agreeable," and went. The witnesses stated what they have now, but Hough gave no account of himself.
 COURT. Q. Do you hire these rooms of your master? A. Yes, to take care of some property, and I intend to live in them; I had been living in the public-house, and continued there till this house was fitted up.
 Q. Had you the key of the outer door? A. Yes.
 MR. BARRY. Q. Had not Mr. Milledge a room or two in the house, for his own use? A. No - he let it all in seperate tenements. There are four rooms - I have two, Abdell one, and a widow has one; the street door was open when I went out; I had closed my own door, and am positive that I locked it.
 LONGMAN'S Defence. We were all drinking in the public-house that evening.
 HOUGH'S Defence. Norton says I was carrying the sack; Lock says Longman carried it. I stated to Whitaker that I went down to the Dun Horse, public-house, where I had lodged a few nights.
   THOMAS WATERS. He did not state that in my hearing.
 Two witnesses gave Hough a good character.
 LONGMAN -    GUILTY- DEATH Aged 22.
 HOUGH -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 19.
