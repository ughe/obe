On one Friday morning, in August, about half-past five o'clock, he came and knocked at the street-door, (it was on the day the officer came and found the property) I opened the door, and Richard Taylor stood there; he asked me to leave the door open for a few minutes, for he was coming into my room. I left it open - he was in the street, and had nothing in his hand then: I was not quite dressed. I went to the door again when I was dressed, and he came down and brought a bundle, set it down, and there it remained till the officer came at night and found it in my room. I had been out all day and do not know what was in it; it was candlelight when the officer came. I did not notice the handkerchief it was wrapped in.
 Prisoner  RICHARD TAYLOR. Q. Was I not in bed on the night of the robbery - A. I cannot say, but he wished me good night, the same as on other night.
   JAMES PERRY. I live in Union-place, Bagnigge-wells, and rent the house. The roof of my wash-house joins the wall of the house; the tiling comes against it. There is a passage between the dwelling-house and wash-house, which is covered over by a continuation of the roof of the wash-house; you do not go into the air to get to it. There is a yard behind the house; it all belongs to me. I rent it all of one landlord, and under one lease.
   ELIZABETH PERRY. (Looking at the property produced by Colton.) These are all ours, and were stolen from the basket in the wash-house on that night. Here are two petticoats, a handkerchief, four childs' shirts, two pockets, nine pinafores, nine caps, a shirt, a habit-shirt, a frill, and silk handkerchief - that is all the bundle contains. They are worth 1 l. 1 s. 6 d. altogether. The two table-cloths and two shifts pawned at Button's are also ours, and the name is picked out of one of the table-cloths, but the date of the year is on it, by which I know it. The handkerchief pawned at Button's is ours. Here are three shirts and a shift pawned at Croft's. The two gowns pawned at Mills's are also ours. Besides what have been produced, I missed five sheets, two handkerchiefs, five day-caps, four night-gowns, two petticoats, two table-cloths a pair of sheets, and three pairs of drawers, value together 3 l. 4 s. On the day before the robbery two dust men applied at the door to take the dust away - I let them through the house into the yard, and the linen was then drying in the yard. The prisoner. Timothy Taylor, I know to be one of the dustmen. I never pawned anything with Mills.
   JAMES PERRY. We always use the wash-house. I never pawned anything at Mills's.

 Prisoner  RICHARD TAYLOR to COLTON. Q. Did I not tell you where the gowns were which I had bought - A. I said so - he told me he had bought them of John Barry.
   RICHARD TAYLOR'S Defence. It is customary for me to get up about 4 o'clock in the morning, and as I was going to Battle-bridge, about half-way down Gray's-Inn-lane, a person stopped me, and asked if I wanted to buy any shirts. I asked to see them. He showed me four, and wanted 10 s. for them. He put some more things out of his bundle - petticoats and other things. I said they were of no use to me; he then said he would sell me the whole bundle for 2 l. I said I had but 30 s. I bought them of him for that. I was in bed on the night of the robbery.
   TIMOTHY TAYLOR'S Defence. This man asked me where he could pawn these things. I took him down to this woman's house, and she pawned them.
 CLIFFORD'S Defence. On the day in question I went to see  Eliza Harris, and while there these two men came in, and asked me to pawn the things. I asked if they were their own? They said Yes; and I took and pawned them in my own name; where I am well known. I never lived with either of the men, but merely had a slight acquaintance with them. I gave them the money and duplicates.
 One witness gave the prisoner,  Richard Taylor, a good character, and one deposed the same for Timothy.
 RICHARD TAYLOR - GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 17.
 TIMOTHY TAYLOR - GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 21. Of stealing in the dwelling-house, but not of burglariously breaking and entering.
 CLIFFORD -    NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Justice Littledale.
 1503.  MARY PICKERING and SARAH SHANNON were indicted for arson.
   JOHN JACKSON. I am a cut-glass manufacturer, and live in Silver-street, Clerkenwell. The house No. 3, Castle-street, Clerkenwell, is my property. The prisoner Pickering was the occupier at the time in question, she is the widow of John Pickering, who has been dead about three months; he lived there for three years, she continued to live there after his death; he was my tenant, and had no lease: he paid twenty guineas a-year; he was a workman of mine; there was nearly a quarter's rent due at the time of his decease, she paid part of it, and I took one of his tools: she continued my tenant: her quarter's rent is not due untill Michaelmas: the house is my freehold.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. The husband was formerly in your employ - A. Yes, several years, she herself was nearly ten years in my service, and married from me. I have not seen the house since this happened, for I could not get in: Shannon lived with her, and I believe as a servant: Pickering had come to me about disturbances being made about the house, and I know that the windows are broken. I requested her to leave, and expected that she was about to leave.
