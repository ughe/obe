a few minutes after that my husband came in, and he stood by the table--be

my husband came in he went through my room to the privy that might be a
minute after my husband came in-it might be two, I cannot say-wben he
came back he said, " I don't feel very well, I suppose I am horror-struck like
the rest"-he then asked my husband to fetch him a drop of beer, and he
fetched him some beer, a rasher of bacon, and tobacco-while my husband
was gone for the beer, I said, “This is a shocking affair, Richard"-he
said, “It is; I know very little about it"-I asked him where he had
been that day-he told me he had been and called on his aunt, or seen his
aunt, I do not know which, and he had had good luck, and the old lady
was glad to see him and he had called on his cousin, and his cousin had
a pair of shoes that were too large for him, and he had given them to him
--my husband came in during the latter part of this conversation with the
beer and things he had been sent out for- I put the rasher on the fire, and
broiled it for the prisoner, and he ate it-we then went on talking together
-I asked him where he had been over-night-he said he had been to the
Rainbow public-house, that he went in with a pint, and came out with
pint-I said, “You could not have been there till that time in the
morning" he said, “I was gossipping"- that is all he said there was
other conversation-I do not know of any thing particular-he said, in
speaking of his situation, “Many poor creatures like me would rather be
hung than transported" — he was speaking of the difficulties he was in,
that he was out of work, and in trouble, and a good deal of conversation
to that effect—he had often said he had never known so much trouble as
he had done this winter-I saw the stocking in which the money was found
in the privy.

Q. Do know to whom that stocking belonged ? A. I saw a pair of
stockings rolled up on a box, at the side of the prisoner's bed on Monday
afternoon, at the time I made the bed—I cannot say whether it was five
o'clock, or what time it was in the afternoon- it might be five o'clock
it was while the prisoner was out-I took hold of the stockings and laid
them down, and thought to myself, they are Richard's stockings--I knew
them to be similar to what he always wore-I washed for him—they were
exactly like what he wore, and did not belong to me--they belonged to
Richard—they did not belong to me, and are like what I used to wash for
him-I believe them to be his-he had two pairs of stockings when I
washed for him, before he went to Mr. Bartlett's I washed for him three
weeks after he went there--I had washed a pair of stockings similar to those
for him, just before Christmas-that is all I know my children's clothes
were not kept in that room-nobody's clothes but the prisoner's would be
kept in that room-they were two odd stockings, I had mismatched them
when I washed them, I think it must have been six week before Christmas
when I mis-matched them-these were formerly two pairs, and I put one
stocking of one pair to another.
