GEORGE TASSELL , HARRY ADAMS, and DAVID RICE were indicted for stealing, on the   7th of September , 1 box, value 3 s  .; 1 pair of boots, value 7 s  .; 1 pair of clogs, value 6 d  .; 1 work-box, value 3 s  .; 1 brooch, value 3 s  .; 1 thimble, value 6 d  .; 3 gowns, value 14 s  .; 17 aprons, value 12 s  .; 4 pairs of stockings, value 3 s  .; 3 bed-gowns, value 5 s  .; 6 shifts, value 7 s  .; 7 petticoats, value 13 s  .; 2 pairs of drawers, value 3 s  .; 7 handkerchiefs value 4 s  .; 8 towels, value 6 s  .; 1 printed book, value 7 s  .; 1 shawl, value 3 s  .; and 3 pairs of cuffs, value 6 d  .; the goods of Elizabeth Smith : and ELIZABETH RICE and MARY LUXTON , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Sta tute, &c. ELIZABETH SMITH. I am a servant, and live in East-terrace, New Kent-road. On Saturday evening, the 7th of September, I was going to my situation, from the neighbourhood of the Elephant and Castle public-house—I had a bandbox with me, and another box, containing the property stated in this indictment—I saw the prisoners Tassell, Adams, and David Rice, near the Elephant and Castle public-house—as far as I could judge, they were all in company together—I asked Tassell if he could carry my box to my situation—he said, "Yes," and I gave him the box containing the pro perty stated—the other two men were present—I carried the bandbox myself—at the beginning of St. George's-road I told Tassell that he must cross the road—I crossed myself first, and walked a short dis tance, thinking he was behind me, but when I turned round it was an other young man, and Tassell was gone—David Rice was behind me, and followed me down the street—Adams followed at a distance, but I did not see him—I left my bandbox with Lloyd while I went back, and looked for Tassell, but I could not see him—I saw some of the property afterwards, but not all—this is part of it—( examining it. )
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You addressed Tassell, and asked him to carry your box? A.  Yes, and I entrusted it to him to carry. JOHN LLOYD. I am a butcher, and serve Mr. Polden, in whose em ploy the prosecutrix is—I was at my door on Saturday, the 7th of Sep tember—the prosecutrix came to me, and gave me her bandbox—David Rice was standing there—he said, "What is the matter?"—I said she had lost her box—he said, "What a pity!" JOSEPH DAY. The prisoner Adams lodged in my house for three weeks—on the Monday after the 7th of September I saw him carry away a box—I do not know what it contained—the box produced, I am quite sure, is the one he had. ELIZABETH ELVERSON. My husband is a hatter. On Monday, the 9th of September, I saw Adams in Fleet-street, with a box on his shoulder—he was bringing it from Fleet-street to the corner of a court, that leads down to Gun-street—I do not know what he did with it, but I afterwards saw some boys breaking the box, and told the policeman—this is the box.
