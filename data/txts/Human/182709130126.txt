 SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 19.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1869.  MARY GATES was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , 1 table-cloth, value 2s., the goods of John Webster.
 The prisoner pleaded GUILTY. Confined Seven Days.
 1870.  ROBERT OSBORN and THOMAS BRACE were indicted for stealing, on the 3d of August , 4 bushels of oats, value 12s.; 2 bushels of chaff, value 1s., and 1 sack, value 1s., the goods of Charles Batley.
 CHARLES BATLEY. I  job horses for the coal merchants. I have a stable at Mr. Beetham's wharf, near the canal at Hoxton. I missed some corn and this sack, which was in the granary about a fortnight at three weeks before the officer took the prisoner, I missed five quarters of corn in the space of a fortnight.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was it not mixed with peas? A. I do not know.
   CHARLES MIELL. I am an officer. On the 3d of August, about half-past two o'clock in the morning, I was coming over Kingsland-road bridge, and saw the prisoner with another man coming out of the prosecutor's stable - the prisoners had each a sack; the third man opened a door to see if any body was coming - he did not see me; he said something to the prisoners - they came into the road, and went into a stable belonging to Brace. I followed, and saw them throw down the loads in the stable. I pushed the door open, went in, and there saw Brace, who had been holding the door, so that we should not come in - he keeps a horse and cart. I asked him what they had got - he said a bit of corn and chaff for the horse - we took them in charge; another patrol went after the third man, but he was gone. I went and took Batley's horse-keeper, but he was discharged - here are the two sacks which I found in Brace's stable.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where were you when you saw them? A. By the wall, about thirty yards from the stable. I could see the door by the light of a fire from a coak burner. I saw them within two yards of the stable door; the third man was in the stable.
   JOHN STEVENSON. I was with Miell. I went and took the prisoners in Brace's stable.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were they not discharged on bail? A. Yes.
   CHARLES BATLEY. I have too many oats to miss any; both these sacks were in my stable, and one is my own. I have no doubt that the oats and chaff are mine - there is none there but what is mine.
 OSBORN's Defence. I met Brace on the road with two sacks - he told me to carry one which I did.
 BRACE's Defence. I bought the sack of chaff the overnight.
   ROBERT EDWARDS. I am a carman. I occasionally sell corn and chaff - I live in Kingsland-road. I sold Brace a sack of chaff - I cannot tell the day of the month, but it was the night before he was taken - I really believe this to be the same chaff. I sold him about five bushels.
   JOHN SMITH. I lent Brace a sack five or six days before he was taken - this is it. I know it by the corner being tied, which I did - the name of Love is on it.
 OSBORN -    GUILTY. Aged 35.
 BRACE -    GUILTY. Aged 55. Confined Six Months.
 1871.  JOHN PROTHEROE and WILLIAM JONES were indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , 1 fixture, i. e., 1 stove, value 5s., belonging to John Davis , and fixed to a certain building of his.
 SECOND COUNT, calling it the property of  Samuel Wilson and William Blackmere Noble.
   GEORGE WEST. I live with Mr. Franks, of Cumberland-street. At nine o'clock in the evening, on the 28th of August, I was going to master's stables, and heard a noise in Mr. Davis' stable; I opened the door, and saw the prisoners there, bringing a stove down stairs, and there appeared to have been one taken from the room over the coach-house. I called the watchman, who took them - Protheroe said a man had sent him for it, but he could not tell me his name; he said he was to meet him in Bond-street.
   WILLIAM SOMERFIELD. I was fetched to a stable in Cumberland-mews, and took the prisoners; the stove was close by them. I found on Protheroe a turn-screw and three keys; and with the stove was a large poker. I had been in the room the morning before, and saw a stove fixed there; I went to the room next morning, and found a porter's knot, which Protheroe said he took to carry it on.
   GEORGE DUNN. I am a watchman. I assisted in taking the prisoners; I said it was a bad job; Jones said, "Yes, it is, it cannot be helped now;" he then said he was employed by Protheroe to take it to the corner of Bond-street, and was to have 2s. for carrying it.
 JOHN DAVIS. This was my coach-house, but at that time it was in possession of Samuel Wilson and William Blackmere Noble, my assignees - the stove was fixed there.
 PROTHEROE's Defence. I met a man who asked me to fetch the stove from the stable. I found it at the top of the stairs; we moved a coach which stood in the yard. I got this man to help me, being unwell.
 JONES' Defence. This man asked me to help him.
 PROTHEROE -    GUILTY. Aged 19.
 JONES -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months.
