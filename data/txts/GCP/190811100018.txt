Mr. Huntly Jenkins, who prosecuted, said that this was about as
bad a case of forgery and larceny as it was possible to imagine. Pri
soner for about 26 years had been employed by Messrs. Rose, John-
son, and Hicks, solicitors, 13, Delahay Street, Westminster, and had
gained their complete confidence. He had induced his brother-in-
law, Mr. Black, to advance close upon £5,000 upon various securities
which prisoner had forged, and, under the pretence that the money
was invested on Mr. Rose's authority, prisoner representing that his
brother-in-law was a client of the firm. By this means Mr. Black
(a clergyman) had lost his entire fortune.
Sentence, Five years' penal servitude.
COHEN, Solomon (19, tailor), pleaded guilty of obtaining by false
pretences from Jacob Marks, divers of his moneys to wit, the sum
of £2, with intent to defraud; obtaining by false pretences from
Abraham Lipman, divers of his moneys to wit, the sum of £2, with
intent to defraud.
Prisoner had been bound over by Judge Lumley Smith at this
Court, on January 8 of this year, for a similar offence. (See Vol.
148, p. 424.) It was stated that he had probably been led away by
bad company.
Sentence, Six months' imprisonment.
