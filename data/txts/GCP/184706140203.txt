1531. SARAH RYAN was indicted for stealing 1 basket, value 10.; 1
lock, 3d. ; 1 pair of boots, 5s. ; 2 handkerchiefs, 1s.; 2 aprons, 18.; 1 petti-
coat, 1s. ; 2 pairs of stockings, 8d. ; and 54d. in copper; the goods and monies
of Honora Collins.
HONORA COLLINS. I am a market-garden woman, and live at Mortlake.
On the morning of the 24th of May, I left my boots, my handkerchief, and
other things, in the room where I lodged-I had locked them up in a basket
-I afterwards met the policeman with the basket and all the things in it
this is it-the things are in it now.
CATHERINE LEE. The prisoner and Collins lodged with me. On this morn-
ing the prisoner went out, and I followed her-she had this basket locked up,
and her own bundle too-the basket was not broken open.
Prisoner's Defence. I had no work, and I took the basket for a bit of
bread; she turned me out, and then she said I was taking away the basket.

1532. JOHN JENNER was indicted for stealing an order for the payment
of 1971. 108.; the property of Edward Ransby.
EDWARD RANSBY. I reside at Bawsey, in Suffolk. On the 26th of
April, I came to town to receive money on my father's account-I received a
check for 1971. 108., payable at Messrs. Prescott's-this is the check-I had
about 188. or 198. in my pocket besides-I went on that day, about twelve or
one o'clock, to the Yorkshire Stingo beer-house in the Blackfriar's-road-I
saw the prisoner in front of the bar-he was attending apparently as master-
I had a pint of beer-while I was drinking it, a party challenged the prisoner
to play at skittles for a sovereign--they went out to play after they were
gone a little while, the prisoner came and invited me to go down in the yard
at the back of the house to see them play-I went and saw them the land-
lord won the game--I heard him ask the man for the sovereign-the man
said he had left it with his wife in the bar after that the prisoner asked me
to play a game or two of skittles for a pint of porter-I played a game or
two- I lost every game-I then played with another man and lost--Leary (See
page 414) was there, but I never saw him play-1 played for half-an-hour or
three-quarters-before we had finished, several of the men went away-I
staid in the skittle-ground-one of them came down and invited me to go up to
lear a song--I went up, and after the song, they began to toss for stout and
of money-I tossed for stout first, but ultimately for money, with three
other men, not the prisoner-when there was a small game I generally won,
but when there was a large sum I generally lost that soon disposed of my
108. or 198.-I was then going--the prisoner came in and said, "Do not go,
