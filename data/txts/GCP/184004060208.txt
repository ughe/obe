JOHN COLLINS (police-sergeant.) I went to Mr. Templeman's cottage on
Tuesday morning, and was there with the surgeons I have produced the
splinter of wood-it was cut at the police-office-I have had it examined,
according to the prisoner's request, by a turner, who gave his opinion that it
is "red saunders—he described it as a very heavy close wood, coming from
New South Wales-I searched the drawers in the deceased's sitting-room-
the top drawer on the left hand side had been broken open apparently by
a chisel or small crow-bar or something I found in it two boxes, a
quantity of papers, and two notes of the Bank of Elegance-one for 50l.
and the other for 51.-(producing them)—this is the mahogany box-it has
a lock to it-it is not broken-it was shut when I found it, but unlocked
I found the key of it in the deceased's trowsers pocket I do not think
any violence had been offered to the lock-there are also a quantity of
papers, bills, and memorandums-I found no money in the drawers, but
in his trowsers pocket was a purse with three sixpences—that was in the
same pocket with the key-the papers were apparently tumbled about a
good deal—these notes, folded up in this way look like notes of the Bank
of England -I thought they were so when I first found them-behind the
door of the sitting room I found a chopper and a hammer in the corner
between the outer door and the bed-room door- I do not think they had
been used—I also found a hammer in the cupboard on the top of
some clothes-that did not appear to have been used—I found no piece of
wood except this—I found no weight except two clock weights--they
were round at the bottom and flat at the top they were in the farther cor-
ner of the sitting toom, attached to the clock-I did not find any other
weights in the bed-room. 3208 SOUD
