JAMES MUNDAY. I am a butcher of Eyre-street, Woolwich—the prisoner was in my employ—on the evening of 5th April, there was but little meat, and there was not the slightest obstacle to my seeing to the scale where the prisoner was weighing a piece of meat for a woman—Stride was inside the shop—I saw him hand a piece of fat to the prisoner—he then whistled and pointed to the prisoner's left hand and I saw some money in it—he pre tended to wipe his nose and dropped the money into his pocket—I then heard the chinking of silver—he put his hand back and I said, "George, what have you put in your pocket?"—he said, "Nothing, you may search me"—I went in, and he took out four half crowns and four shillings and twopence before I asked him how much he had—I said, "How came you by that?"—he said, "It is what I have saved out of my wages"—I said, "How can that be, when you only have a crown a-week?"—he said, "I have saved it"—I boarded and lodged him—he said, "I went over to Mr. Fry's, had a quartern of gin, changed half a crown and he gave me 2 s . 2 d . out"—the silver I saw in his hand appeared to be two shillings and a half crown by the gaslight—there are three compartments in the till; there was 9 l . or 10 l . on the silver side—I went for a policeman and gave him in charge—he had been in my service seven or eight weeks.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What light was there? A.  Five or six gas lights flaring in different parts of the shop—the woman paid for the beef but not to the prisoner—it was one pound and a little more; I handed it in, the prisoner weighed it, and my wife, who came in, received the money—I am a cousin of the last witness—there was a bit of rag on one of the prisoner's fingers; his hand was slightly open—I believe the prisoner had received money that night, but he was not instructed to do so—I had told Stride to give a signal, and I believe I said a whistle—this was on Saturday—I appeared before the Magistrate on the Monday—I was not talking to Stride about it on Sunday.
   MR. LOGIE . Q.  Did the prisoner say anything to you about having received any of that money from customers? A.  No. WILLIAM BUTCHER. ( policeman, R  85). I took the prisoner—I told him the charge—he said that the money he had was what his father had given him as pocket-money, and some which he had saved up—there were four half-crowns and six shillings, and in his blue pocket, two shillings and some halfpence.
   COURT . to JAMES MUNDAY . Q.  Where was the money? A.  In his breast pocket; butchers do not usually use that pocket for money—it is only a slop  pocket for a handkerchief; it was not a coat—it was a slop  —I do not know whether he had a breeches pocket as he scarcely ever had his slop  off. WILLIAM BUTCHER. re-examined.  I found 14 s . in his trousers pocket, and 2 s . and 2 d . in his slop  pocket.
 (  The prisoner received a good character. ) NOT GUILTY.
