 2418. ALEXANDER VICKERY was indicted for stealing, on the   25th of July , 1 gown, value 2 l  . 10 s  .; 4 shirts, value 15 s  .; 4 table-cloths value 13 s  .; 1 pair of shoes, value 4 s  .; 1 night-gown, value 1 s  .; 2 petti-coats, value 3 s  .; 3 sheets, value 6 s  .; 5 towels, value 2 s  .; 3 knife-cloths value 1 s  .; 3 shifts, value 6 s  .; 1 curtain, value 6 d  .; 1 pair of drawers, value 1 s  .; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 bolster-case, value 6 d  .; 1 pillow-case, value 6 d  .; 1 apron, value 1 s  .; 1 watch-guard, value 1 s  .; 1 cap, value 6 d  .; and 2 pinafores, value 1 s  .; the goods of George Parker . ESTHER PARKER. I am the wife of George Parker, of Ship-street, Deptford New Town, a coachmaker. I have known the prisoner some time by coming to visit a friend of ours—on the 25th of July, about five minutes past eleven o'clock, he came to my house and wanted me to go and borrow half a crown for him at Mrs. Knowldon's of High-street—he said she would have it all ready—I proposed my little girl should go, but he said he would rather I went—I went, but did not get the half-crown—I was absent ten or twelve minutes—when I returned my little girl was crying—I was in duced to go up stairs, and missed all this property—he was not there—he had said he would wait for the half-crown—they were all safe when I went out. FREDERICK NORMAN. I live at Mr. Nash's, pawnbroker, London-street, Greenwich. I have a pair of shoes which were pawned on the 25th of July by the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  I did not pawn any shoes that day. GEORGE PARKER. I am the husband of Esther Parker. These are my shoes—I found them at the pawnbroker's in about an hour—I took the prisoner on the 11th of this month at Brixton tread-mill, where he had been committed—I told the constable to take him—he asked him, "Do you know that gentleman?"—he said, "I have seen him"—I have found nothing else. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Transported for Seven Years.
   Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
