 Court. Was what you have said now all he told you when he came out? - Yes.
 Then he did not tell you the particulars of what had passed? - No, only that he had demanded the money, and he was ordered to go to Mr. Dyson's, and that they thought it was a false will.
   PETER MAYNE sworn.
 How long have you known this young man? - Not any considerable time, it may be two or three months.
 What is his name? -  Thomas Peters.
 How do you know that is his name? - By hearing him called so in different companies.
 Mr. Garrow. Do you belong to the Commons? - No, Sir, you know that very well.
 To the Navy-Office? - No.
 Clerk to the Solicitor of the Admiralty perhaps? - No.
 What are you? - I attend a publick office.
 A runner to the East Smithfield office? - Yes.
   WILLIAM YOUNG KNIGHT sworn.
 Are you the Mr. Knight that this man went to? - I am.
 Have you the probate? - Yes, it was on the 4th of April.
 Did the prisoner produce that probate to you? - He did.
 Did he tell you why he came to you, who sent him? - He came to me with one  Henry Moses, a Jew, he said that he had been to the Pay-Office with this will for the wages, that there was an objection to paying the money, and that he was referred to Mr. Dyson; I questioned him as to the execution of the will and the proof of it, and I first asked him where he lived, he said on Green-Bank, Wapping, and had come home three months ago from Newfoundland; I asked him how long he had been absent, he told me he went out the beginning of summer; I repeatedly asked whether it was before June, he said it was the beginning of the summer, early in the summer; this conversation passed on the 4th of April, he came over from the Pay-Office to my house, he said that this will was executed by Sir John Carter, who is the mayor of Portsmouth; I saw by the will that the signature was a mark, and I asked him if the deceased could write, he said yes, he could, and he saw him write his name to the will, he came to me in the name of John Murphy.
 Mr. Garrow. I believe neither Mr. Dyson or you had any authority to pay these wages? - No.
 Then the only reason of coming to you was to remove any objection to his being paid? - Yes.
 In the course of the conversation that you have had with the prisoner at the bar, did he at that time acknowledge to you that the will, of which this was a probate, was a forgery? - No.
 Did you know Banks? - No.
 Did you know whether he wrote? - No.
 Have you inspected the books of the Navy Office of the Stirling? - I have not.
 Do you know from these books or any other document that is authentick, whether he could write his name? - I do not.
 Do you know whether this is the true will of that  Thomas Banks? - I do not.
 Do you know the subscribing witness? - No.
 Do you know these are the signatures of the persons who are the witnesses? - The prisoner informed me that one of the witnesses was a publican, and the other was a salesman at Portsmouth, I wrote to them.
 Do you know at all whether the signatures are the signatures of the persons they import to be? - I do not know.
 Then I ask you whether from any circumstance you know whether that is a true will or a forged will? - I do not.
 Court. He did not come to you for the purpose of receiving the wages of course? - No.
