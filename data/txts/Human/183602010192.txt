   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How long has he been in your master's service? A.  Twelve months—Nelson spoke to me the morning before about this JOHN HENRY MACHU. I am a silk-manufacturer, and live in Twisters-alley, Bunhill-row, St. Luke's I have heard the statement made by Nelson—it is correct—I said, "Every thing is not right here, there is some thieving going on," or something to that effect—the prisoner, with others, was sitting round the fire at the time—I took a candle, and went towards the gas-meter cupboard—I said, "Come here this moment"—he did not move—I said, "Ben, came here"—he then did not move—I said again, "Come here"—he then came, and I said, "Pull every thing out of this place, "Which he did, but I thought rather reluctantly—I said, "Come, every thing out" and he pulled out with ropes and dirty paper that was there, a wrapper containing the silk laces—I said, "How came they here?"—he made no answer—I said, "You rascal, you put them there"—he still made no answer, but appeared very much confused, and I saw a tear dropping down his face, and when I went in he turned exceedingly pale, and put up his arms—the value of the laces is 3 l . 10 s .
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is that the wholesale price. A.  Yes—there are fifteen dozen of them—I have no partner in this business—I do not wish to press the charge in any way—he had half-a-guinea a week and a penny a night for his beerhe is not married—he slept in the warehouse for security.
