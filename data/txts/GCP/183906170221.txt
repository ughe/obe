Isaac SOLOMONS. I am the son of Henry Solomons. My father had
a shop in the Strand, and another shop at No. 9, Coventry-street-I assisted
him in the business at the shop in the Strand—I was at that shop on
Easter Tuesday last--the prisoners, Mrs.-Abrahams and Mr. Moses came
there-Moses came first-- I was in the front shop next the street when he
came-my father was there--Moses spoke to him--he merely said he wished
to speak to him, and they went to the passage, and went up stairs--they re-
mained up stairs about ten minutes---Moses then left-about three-quarters
of an hour after he was gone Mrs. Abrahams came--she went into the back
shop--my father was there with her--there are furnaces in the back shop-
I do not know whether there was any fire in them at that time, I believe
there was—I did not remain at home all the time Mrs. Abrahams was
there—my father directed me to go out-in consequence of directions from
him I went to Ransom's, his banker- I returned and told my father what
had happened there-- I went out again, and took a cheque for 801. with
me, drawn by my father on Ransom's-I took the change I got for it to
my father-I know Woolf, a tailor in Regent-street--in consequence of my
father's directions I went to Woolf, and received a cheque of 1001. from him
on Ransom's-I had also received three £5 notes and five sovereigns from
Woolf-I went to Ransom's with the 1001. cheque of Woolf's, and received
the amount of it I do not remember what I received from Ransom's in
payment of that cheque-I delivered it to my father in the same state I
received it-I took a bar of gold to Cock and Johnson's the same day,
and they gave me a cheque for 4001. on Williams and Labouchere's bank
on account of that bar-I presented it for payment, and received a £300
note and a £100 note this is the £300 note I received (looking at the one

£10 notes, and twenty £5 notes-I changed the £100 note for a hundred
sovereigns-I carried them back to my father-I found Mrs. Abrahams
