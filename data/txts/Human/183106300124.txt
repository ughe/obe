 1453.  JEREMIAH SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of September , 1 wooden partition and door-frame, value 2l. 10s.; 200 bricks, value 5s.; 1 piece of leaden pipe and cock, value 10s., and 1 dust-bin, value 10s., the goods of William Robins , and fixed to a building of his; against the Statute, &c.
   WILLIAM ROBINS. The prisoner rented a house of me in Harley-street - he paid me three quarters rent, but when I went again he was gone, and the house shut up; I went to him for the key, and he would not give it without I would give him a receipt, which I at last did - he had had the house on an agreement, which is not stamped.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1454.  WILLIAM SELLWOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of May , 1 bag, value 6d.; 1 sovereign, 1 half-sovereign, and 7 shillings, the property of Edward Bishop , from his person.
   EDWARD BISHOP. I live in Noble-street, St. Luke's. On the 21st of May, about six o'clock in the evening, I was in Old-street; I saw the prisoner, whom I have known many years - I called him to have part of a pot of ale; there was a fight; we went into the crowd, and while there I noticed him very close to me - I then walked up as far as the Pitt's Head; I there missed my money - I came back, and found the prisoner; I accused him of having my money- he said he knew nothing about it; I got an officer, and then he said, sooner than be given in charge he would give me the bag and money back again - I had a sovereign and a half in this outside waistcoat pocket, and seven shillings in a bag in my under waistcoat; I have not seen any of it since, for directly the officer came to take the prisoner, he got away, and was not taken till the Monday morning.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe you were all drunk together? A. No, I was not drunk - I had had part of a pot of ale.
   THOMAS BRADFORD. I live in Long's-buildings, and am a headborough. On the 21st of May I received information, went to a public-house in Golden-lane, and found the prosecutor and prisoner sitting on a seat together - the prosecutor charged him with this robbery, and the prisoner denied it; I asked if he would give charge of him - he said he would rather have his money; I hesitated some time, and the prisoner said, "If you or Bishop will go with me, you shall have your money back again" - I did not countenance that, as there were some of his companions about there: I had heard mention of a bag and seven shillings in silver - the prosecutor said he had lost upwards of 30s.; I took the prisoner - he gave me a thrust away, and ran off.
 Cross-examined. Q. You found no bag nor money? A.No.
   FREDERICK MILES. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner - I said I wanted him for stealing a canvas bag and seven shillings from Bishop, in Old-street, on the 21st of May - he said there were but six shillings in the bag, and that it was hanging out of Bishop's pocket when he took it; he said the same to the inspector, at the station-house - he said before the Magistrate, "I don't deny it."
 Prisoner's Defence. He has falsesworn - I said nothing about the bag at all.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Two Months.
 1455.  PETER REYNOLDS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of May , 1 coat, value 6s., and 1 cap, value 2s., the goods of Edward Richardson.
   GEORGE RICHARDSON. I am the son of Edward Richardson, and am eleven years old; my father is a jeweller, and lives in Coram-street. On the 14th of May I was in Oxford-street, and saw the prisoner; I had seen him before in our street - he asked me where I had seen him last; I said at Russell-square, at the pump - he said he had been to the East Indies since that, and the mate of a ship had given him two poll parrots and two other birds - if I would go with him he would give me one of the parrots; I had some jewellery in my possession, which I took to Holles-street - he waited till I came out; he then spoke to me again, and we went to St. Martin's church steps; he then said I must give him my cap to put the parrot in, and my coat to wrap it in - I asked him if my waistcoat would not do, and he said No, it would fly out at the armholes; I took off my coat and cap, and gave them to him; he ran off - he out-ran me, and got away.
 Prisoner. Q. Which way did the person go after he had received your clothes? A. Towards the Strand.
   CHARLES MAYHEW. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - he denied the charge.
   EDWARD RICHARDSON. I am father of this lad - he returned without his coat and cap, and told me what had happened; I know the prisoner, by seeing him about the neighbourhood for a long time, and I had him taken.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am quite innocent; if the father and son knew they had seen me within three months, how was it likely I could have been to the East Indies?
     GUILTY. Aged 19 - Confined One Year.
 1456.  WILLIAM REEVES was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of June , 1 hat, value 20s., the goods of John Perring.
   ROBERT PATTENDEN. I live in Tibberton-street, Newington-causeway, and deal in fruit. At a quarter to seven o'clock in the morning of the 22nd of June, I saw the prisoner in the Strand, near Mr. Perring's - he walked to and fro, then went into the shop, and took two hats off the floor, in front of the door, walked towards Charingcross with them, and went away; I saw him and the shopboy in conversation in the Strand next day, before the shop was open, at a quarter to seven o'clock - I am certain of his person.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You thought he had stolen the hat? A. Yes - I was going to endeavour to stop him, but he went off; I was placed there to watch - I was four or five rods from him: I waited to see another person go in, who was walking to and fro - I had not turned my head before he was out of my sight; he had the hats under his arm - I sell fruit in the Boroughmarket; I saw the prisoner for several minutes before he took the hats, and am certain of him.
