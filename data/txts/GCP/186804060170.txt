AEXANDER MOCAUL. I am superintendent of the police at Glasgow-in
January last I saw a document sent from London-I read it, atid went to
McNulty on Sunday, 26th January-I saw him, and asked him some ques-
tions, noting down his answers in this book (produced) I had not known
him before what he stated was in answer to my questions_I told him
who I was-I asked him whether he had heard of the Clerkenwell explosion

for about four months, and was introduced to him by a man named
Campbell- he said " O'Neil came into my house for 23. 6d. on the Friday
morning of the affair, and James Lewis, shoemaker, was in the house at the
time, and I told O'Neil to call in the evening for payment, he called
sume evening, between 4 and 5 o'clock, and he did not get payment, as I
had not got money for work. On Saturday morning he called in the fore-
noun, and I gave him a shilling, and took as much tea as made up the
shilling. I remember the date of the Clerkenwell affair from the fact that
O'Neil left, on Saturday morning, that morning's Herald; and James
Lewis came in after 2 o'clock, and read the account of it which had hap-
pened the day before. When O'Neil was in on Friday, Lewis was the only
one in the house but my own family, there was no one in when O'Neil
called on Saturday. I never had any men working to me, and certainly
none about that time. I never heard Barrett's name, nor do I know him
