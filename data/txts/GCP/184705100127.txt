1241. JOHN PRICE was indicted for embezzlement.
Mr. PRENDERGAST conducted the prosecution.
JAMES ARCHER. The prisoner called on me for rates due to the Commis-
sioners of Whitechapel Trust-Iwas not prepared when he called - I afterwards
carried the money to him-I found him in his own house-I paid him the
amount of 188. on 25th May, 1846-he gave me a receipt-this is it
(read—" Received, 25th May, 1846, of Mr. James Archer, 188., for two
quarter's pavement rate, due at Christmas last, one-third being assessed on
the landlord. John Price, Collector.")
Cross-examined by Mr. COOPER. Q. Then this rate was due at Christmas ?
4. Yes; that was the time I ought to have paid it-I never collected rates-I
know that with some persons they are not paid till the end of the year--I have.
known the prisoner thirteen or fourteen years- I believe he would pay rates
for me, trusting to me to pay him again-I never knew anything against him
- his wife died three or four years ago-I believe that preyed very much on
his mind-he has a family.
EDWARD RAMSDEN. I am an inhabitant of the parish of Whitechapel.
The prisoner called on me on the 19th June, last year, for some rates - I
paid him 11. 58. on account of my brother and myself - this is the receipt he
gave me-(read—“Received, 19th June, 1846, of Edward Ramsden, 11. 58.,
for four quarters' pavement rate, due at Christmas last, one third being as.
sessed on the landlord. John Price, Collector.")
Cross-examined. Q. This rate was due at Christmas; had it been called
for before June? A. I do not think it had been called for above once--it
has been the habit of the parish to pay these rates five or six months after
they become due--I do not know that the rates have gone longer than that
I have known the prisoner eight or ten years- I never heard anything
against him.
THOMAS EDWARD DAVIS. I am in the employ of Mr. Edward Went-
worth. The prisoner called on me for the rates, on 7th of August, 1846
I paid him by a check, 71. 68. 8d. - I received this receipt from him- this is
the check I gave him, it was not crossed then-(read_" Messrs. Barnett,
Hoare, and Co., pay to pavement of Whitechapel, or bearer, 71. 68, 8d. E.
WENTWORTH.") This check was returned by the bankers as paid.
WILLIAM WYNN WILSON. I am clerk to Messrs. Spooner's the bankers
I cannot identify this check; but a check for 77. 68. Bd., drawn on Barnett
Hoare and Co., was paid in with other checks, and money, in a sum of
541. 158. 3d., to the credit of the Whitechapel Road side commission, on 8th
August, 1846-this is the pass-book-it is here entered 541. 158. 3d,
GEORGE STARKINS WALLIS. I am one of the treasurers to the commis.
sioners for paving Whitechapel-road-I am one of the commissioners,
there are others--the prisoner was collector of rates for the commssioners-it
was his duty to account to the Board every time they met, for the amount of
rates he bad collected up to that period if he received, on the 25th of May,
188. of Mr. Archer, it was his duty to aceount for it at the next meeting of
the commissioners, which took place on the 5th of Jupe--I was present when
the entrys were made in this minute-book-the collector was present-these
entrys were made in his presence, from the margins of his receipts-be did
not account for this 18s.-he accounted for 201. 08. 4d, as what he had received
up to that day for Charlotte-street, where Mr. Archer's house is rated--this
account will not tell how that account is made up-Mr. Archer's money ought
to have been entered in this book, and it is not this is a book which was kept
by the prisoner, and was in his possession, but he neglected posting it up
