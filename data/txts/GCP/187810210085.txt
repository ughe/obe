ROBERT WILLIAM SCHREIBER (Reexamined). As receiver, I acquainted
myself with the condition of the business—I examined the books-in 1869
the gross sales were 27,8321.; in 1870, 29,6421.; and in 1871, 29,4981.-
then the monthly sales in 1872 were in January, 2,6151. ; February, 1,6421;
March, 1,2841.; April, 2,028l. ; and up to the 18th May, 18781., that being the
month in which the business in Worship Street was opened from the 18th
May to the end of May, 8041. ; in June, 8501. ; in July, 7861. ; in August,
up to my appointment, 6427., including the alleged sales to Keeling, Patton,
Balchin, and William Saull-I have gone through those alleged sales to find
out the prices at which they took place—there is a total loss of about 301 in
hard cash on the cost price of those sales, excluding all expenses--that is
roughly on 2,0001.- I can give the particulars of the prices when I was
appointed receiver, the liabilities of the concern amounted to a little under
1,9001. I believed they were all incurred after the dissolution-I won't be
positive about it, but the greater portion were-I paid every one of them-
among others there was a payment of 1051. to Mr. Almeda-I sold some
goods to pay it—those were part of the goods purported to be sold by
Keeling, or one of those persons, and which ultimately found their way to
Brown-I had at one time to advance about 5001. out of my own pocket-
about 3rd of June, 1872, the hills receivable by the firm of Saull, Brown,
and Godfrey, amounted to about 9101. 158.3d, -I find an entry of
8161. 178 6d., including interest, on the 29th August, paid to Mr. William
Saull, of Northampton-on the same day I find bills were discounted to the
amount of 8971. 98.-I believe the entry of the debt due to the family, and
the payment of it by the members of the existing firm, is correct.
