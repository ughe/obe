 First Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Recorder.
 1834.  EDWARD HAYGREEN and WILLIAM DUTTON were indicted for feloniously assaulting Hannah Rogers , on the King's highway, on the 7th of September , putting her in fear, and taking from her person, and against her will, 1 frock, value 2s,; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 piece of ribbon, value 6d.; 1 pair of stocking, value 1s., and 1 lace collar, value 3s., her property.
   HANNAH ROGERS. I live at Bushey, with my father, and am single. On the 7th of September I had been to London with my father, and as we returned we went into the Crown, at Hendon, between ten and half-past ten o'clock at night - it is about four miles from London; I staid there about an hour - I saw Haygreen there, but not Dutton - he asked me to sing, and I did attempt a song; I did sing, but it was out of fear, because I thought I was in bad company - I staid there because it rained hard; I left the public-house with my father, and had not got three hundred yards when Haygreen, and another man whom I do not know, interrupted me - Haygreen caught me round the waist, and said I should not go home; I had never seen him till he was at the public-house - when I saw they would not let me go, I instantly called to my father to fire his pistol, as I thought it would intimidate them - I knew he had not got one; while my father was in the act of taking a knife from his pocket, a bundle was taken from between my father's hands and mine - the bundle was under his arm, which I had hold of; when I asked my father to fire his pistol, he said he had one in his pocket that never missed fire, and while he was drawing a knife out the bundle was drawn away by one of the two; I could not see which took it - they ran away, and I have not seen it since; it was very dark, but there were three officers opposite, who called out "What are you at, can't you let the people pass quietly?" in ten minutes I saw the prisoners in custody; we were going back to the public-house, and met the officers with them - when I saw them in the public-house I was quite sure of Haygreen, but I cannot speak to Dutton.
 Cross-examined by MR. BOLLAND. Q. What time in the morning had you left Bushey? A. At twelve o'clock- we stopped at the Crown on our way to town, about three o'clock, and called for a pint of porter, but had nothing else; we had some porter in London - it might be three pints; I did not drink a pint, I am certain - we had nothing more till we got to the Crown; there were a great many men there - we had a pint of porter, and Haygreen asked me to drink; I drank with no other man, to my knowledge - I am not certain whether I drank with any body else; I will not swear it - I might perhaps; I do not think I did - if I did, it was not with more than one; I began two songs, and did not get through the third - I do not exactly know what the songs were; the one I sang through was, "Yes coachmen all of London-town" - I will swear I only sang two, and part of a third; a woman asked me to sing - I said I did not make a practice of singing in public tap-rooms, and it was a long while before I attempted to sing; Haygreen put his arm round my neck - I did not put my arm round his waist, or round any man's waist; Haygreen attempted to kiss me, but I do not know whether he did - I begged of him to take no liberties: nobody else kissed me: I had a black veil on, and did not pull it up for him to kiss me -I did not go into the passage and beckon him out, nor any other young man; I knew Haygreen by his voice - I did not say it was a dark man who robbed me, but I could not tell who it was - I said it was Haygreen; I told the Magistrate he was the man, but I did not know his name - the patrol asked me if I could swear to him; I said Yes, if I had a light; it was a very dark night - all the company came out of the public-house together; we had a pint of porter outside the house before we went in, and sat under a tree with it - that was for a very few minutes; we went in for shelter - Scott, the ostler, brought us the beer; he did not advise us to go home, as it was late - I asked him for some water; he said had I not better have porter - I said I had had porter in town; I do not recollect his saying any thing about my going home.
   THOMAS ROGERS. I am the prosecutrix's father. I left home with her between eleven and twelve o'clock, and went to Windmill-street, Haymarket; we stopped and had a pint of porter at two houses - it was dark when we got to town; on our way back we stopped at the Crown, which is about four miles from town - Bushey is thirteen; my daughter had some water at the Black Lion in Kilburn, as she did not like the beer - I had two pints of beer in town; when we got to the Crown, the servant brought us a pint of beer, and the girl some water - we had it under the tree; it began to rain very fast, which induced us to go into the house - there might be six or eight persons in the tap-room; I cannot say how many - Haygreen was one; I do not know Duttol - we sat there an hour or more; one of them wished my daughter to drink - I thought as they were hard working people, there was no harm in it, and I did, out of fear, persuade her to drink; she drank with nobody but Haygreen - there were two women in the house; the servant had orders to shut the house up, and all the company went out together; Haygreen wanted to kiss my daughter - I told him to let her alone, that she was my daughter, and very tired; they would hardly believe she was my daughter - she sang merely to oblige the people, out of fear; she attempted two songs, but could not sing - I could tell by the beating of her body that she was in fear - she afterwards sung three verses; they said that would do - when we left the public-house, I had her bundle in my arm, and her arm was linked in mine; when we got about two hundred and sixty yards, we heard some people running after us - it was very dark; my daughter said"These men are coming after me" - I said, "Never mind, hold my arm;" Haygreen then caught hold of her, and
