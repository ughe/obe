274. JOHN WAKEMAN, stealing 3 pecks of oats, value 2s.6d.; the
goods of George Robinson, his master.
GEORGE ROBINSON. I live at Chiswick, and have a granary at Strand-
the-Green-the prisoner was in my service, he drove a one-horse cart-
plied him with oats for his horse-he had no business at the granary,
had no right to the key of it-the oats produced correspond with mine
cannot tell whether I have lost any, as I have a very large bulk.
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. How long had the prisoner been
in your service! 1. Four or five years, on and off-we have a man who
supplies all the carters with oats every Saturday night I do not know what
journey the prisoner had been on, as it varies very much-sometimes he i
out all night--the foreman leaves the counting-house at six, seven, or cielt
o'clock.
WALTER FORD (policeman, T 225.) On Tuesday, 30th Nov., about
seven o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner carrying something from Mt.
Robinson's granary-on seeing me he put it down on the ground, and stood
still until I came up to him-I found a quantity of oats in a nose-bag, and
asked what he was going to do with them-he said they were for his horse
I said, “ Your stable is not on this side the road "-he was going across the
road from the granary; he said, "No, I brought them from the granary"-1
said, “Does Mr. Robinson allow you access to his granary when you like?"
-be said, “No, do not say anything about it, I got in with a key which I
found "-he gave me a key, which I tried; it fitted the lock--there were
three pecks of oats in the nose-bag.
GEORGE ROBINSON reexamined. He had no business with any key-he
only had corn on Saturday evenings-he had a regular allowance for his
horse as far as I know. GUILTY. Aged 39.-Confined Six Months.
