 1916. HENRY SPINKS was again  indicted, with JAMES BECK  WITH , for stealing, on the   17th of May , 1 half-crown, the money of Charles Gurney and another, the masters of Spinks.
   MR. PHILLIPS conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES GURNEY. Spinks was in my employ as bar-man—Beckwith had been in a great many times during the day in question—on the 17th of May, among other things, I marked this half-crown, which Mrs. Kershaw got, and the officer marked it also—here are two black marks on the top of the crown—it is marked differently from the others that were put into the till—Brannan brought Beckwith into the counting-house, and this half-crow was taken out of his pocket, and some halfpence—I do not recollect any thing else—the officer said to him, "Do you call these halfpence?"—Spinks then said, "I gave him change for a 5 s . piece, for half-a-pint of stout"—they were both sober—there was more silver on him than the change for a 5 s . piece.
   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN . Q.  When was it you began this system of marking money? A.  On the 14th of May—I marked twelve half-crowns all together, and put the marked money into the till on the Sunday and Monday, and four half-crowns on the Tuesday—I found a 5 s . piece in one of the tills—I cannot say which till it was in—I took it out—I took another customer into custody—he was searched, and nothing but half-pence found on him, and we let him go.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What wages were due to Spinks? A.  quarter's wages—I cannot say how much more—there may have been more—I paid him his wages at the end of every quarter, if he wanted it—we made no particular agreement—I paid him every quar ter—if our servants want the money they ask for it.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did he ever ask you for any wages? A.  No, I should have paid him if he had—I commenced this system of marking, be. cause I suspected I was being robbed.
