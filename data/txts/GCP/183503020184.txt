JOHN MCKAY. I live near Temple Bar, and am a stone-mason. On
the 10th of February, I put up some dirty linen, consisting of the article
stated in this indictment—they were delivered to Mrs. Way the day after
—these are part of the articles.
LOUISA WAY. I am the wife of John Way, and live at Kennington. I
wash for the last witness. On the 11th of February I received a bundle
of linen from him, but I never saw it opened-I do not know what was in
it-my son, John Way, the prisoner, went with me, and I gave him the
bundle to carry for me, to assist mewe got as far as St. Bride's Church,
and I desired him to wait there, or to follow me I was going to Farring-
don-street for his father's linen- I went on, and he seemed to be following
me-I went on to Farringdon-street, and when I returned to where I had
left him, he was gone-I could see him on Blackfriars'-bridge, which
was in my way home-I went on, and inquired of the policemen at the
corner of Stamford-street, if they had seen a youth of his description--they
said he was gone straight on, but I saw him no more.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Was he not unwilling to go
with you? A. Yes, but I wished him to go, as the boy who was to fetch
the linen did not come in time.
Court. Q. In what capacity did he go? A. He went to inquire after
a situation, and I asked him if he would walk with me into the City to
see if Mr. McKay's linen was ready-he said, "I wish to go home, I am
not very well"-I said, "Do go, John, for fear I should not overtake the
boy"-he went to the house-I gave him the linen-I said, "John, take
up this bundle and carry it, and we will go on to your father"-he took it
up and carried it to the end of Fleet-street, and I told him he might either
stop there, or follow me there was nothing said about home--I went to
his father and left the bundle with him the next time I saw him he was
on Blackfriars'-bridge, which was on the road home-I went home, and
he did not arrive.
NOT GUILTY.

874. ELIZABETH HARRS was indicted for a misdemeanor.
MR. CHAMBERS conducted the Prosecution.
CAROLINE SMITH. I am the wife of James Smith, he lives at Brixtos,
and keeps a baker's shop. On the 7th of February, the prisoner came
there between one and two o'clock in the day-she bought a quartern loal,
which came to 7d., and gave me half-a-crown-I put it in the till, in a
bowl which we have there I am quite certain there was no other hall-
crown there, and I did not put any other half-crown in before that was
taken out-I saw my husband take it out--no one had been to the till, and
I am quite satisfied that the one my husband took out, was the one -
ceived of the prisoner-on the Friday following I was sent for to go to Mrs.
Mather's, about six or seven o'clock in the evening-I saw the prisoner
there, and said she was the person who had been in my shop-she said she
never had been there-- I gave the half-crown which I took of the prisoner to
Woodward, the constable.
GUILTY.
