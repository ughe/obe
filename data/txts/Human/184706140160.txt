   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  What size is this granary? A.  A very large place—the prisoners came for a morning or two to sweep it—I do not know how long they had been in the employ of the Company—I was on the top of one sack and behind another—I was higher than the Pri soners—I could have reached them with my hand—I followed them till I met the foreman—they went down stairs, and I lost sight of them for three or four minutes—they were not taken into custody in my presence—about two hours elapsed before they were taken—this sack was loose, but it was not open—I had not measured or weighed the flour—I judged of what had been taken, by the hole that was made—there were hundreds of sacks there—I had examined this sack that morning—we set it there on purpose, and the string was cut loose on purpose—I believe it was laid like a trap. SAMUEL GREEN( police-constable H  61.) On the 22nd of May the pri soners were brought to the station—there was nothing found on them; but Kynaston's waistcoat was stained white with flour—this is the waistcoat—I went to the prisoners' houses—I found at Miller's two dishes of flour, which weighed 8lbs. 6 ounces.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Did you not find some flour bags also? A.  No.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  You found no marks on Miller? A.  No; only on Kynaston—this waistcoat was under his smock-frock—he stated the way it got stained, was by sweeping the floor—I have had the waistcoat ever since—it is exactly in the state it was—I found no flour at Kynaston's. JAMES HUDSON SPARKS. I am a foreman at the Railway. I gave the prisoners into custody—I observed that Kynaston's waistcoat was stained with flour—I asked the prisoners where they lived—I was present when the flour was found at Miller's—I have not compared it with the flour in the sack it was taken from. RANSOM READ. I am delivery foreman at the Eastern Railway Company's warehouse, in Brick-lane. Dyson pointed out a sack to me—I weighed it—it was 2cwt. 1 qr. 17lbs.; the proper weight was 2 cwt. 1qr. 25 lbs.—I took a sample of it to Worship-street, and compared it with the flour in the dishes—I cannot say that it was the same; it was a similar sample.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  What are you? A.  I was bred and born a miler—I know this granary—I am there seven times a-week—there are about 1,500 sacks on the floor—I took three samples out of three sacks that had been robbed by somebody—the flour found in the dishes were like two of the samples—it was not like the third, that was another man's flour. NOT GUILTY.
