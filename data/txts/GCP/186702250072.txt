HENRY WINDET. I am the prosecutor's clerk-on 4th December 1

prisoner accompanied me; when we got to the top of Mrs. Ablett's street
he said he would deliver it, and I gave him the letter--on the Monday ho
borrowed 58 of me, and on the 4th 11. 108.-he has not paid me.
Cross-examined. Q. Has he borrowed money of you before? A. Yes,
and has paid me I think he left on 5th December
HANNAH ABLETT. I am the wife of William Haywood Ablett, of 41,
New Gloucester Street, New North Road—the prisoner served me with
subpæna on 26th November-I signed this list-he did not pay me the
218.-he took his purse out and said he had not sufficient money then, but
he would call that night or the next morning--he did not call, and I wrote
this letter, but did not receive an answer-I received the money on 4th
December
Cross-examined. Q. How did you receive the money? 4. A man

WILLIAM EDWARDS. I live at No. 9, Leonard Square, Finsbury--the
prisoner served me with a subpæna on Monday, 3rd December-he did
not pay me any money—the matter was mentioned to Mr. Shepherd at the
trial, and I was then paid 58.-on the evening of the same day the pri-
soner came to me and said, “I understand you have been paid your col-
duct money”-I said, "Yes, I have"-he said, “Well, will you be good
enough to receive this 58. from me?"-I said, "I do not know whether I
am doing right, I bave already received it from Mr. Shepherd”-he said,
“You take this from me and say it was left during the day”-I took it.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he not say, "I have paid a man more than I
should have done by mistake, and I hope you will take the 58. ?" A. He
did-some one called at my house on the Wednesday, but my wife did not
know who it was-when he served me with the subpæna he asked me to
take a glass of ale, and we went next door and had one-he said he had
not enough money with him to pay me, and that it would make no differ-
ence to me if he left it till the next day-I attended the trial.
THOMAS CHANDLER, M.R.C.S. I live at 77, Sun Street, Bishopsgate
Street-I am the attesting witness to the will of Mr. Vincent Phillips
which was in dispute in the case of Hastelow v. Stobie—the prisoner served
me with a subpoena four or five days before the trial-he did not pay me the
21s.-he looked into his purse and said, “Oh ! I have not quite enough,
but I will give it you to-morrow"-the day after I went to Mr. Hincks's
office, I saw the prisoner and asked him if Mr. Hincks was at home, be
said no, he was not; Mr. Shepherd came up and said, “Mr. Hincks is at
home," and showed me into his room, and I saw him on the evening
before the trial the prisoner saw me coming out of my door; he said,
"Take this as with the subpæna, and do not say anything to Mr. Hincks
about it," and gave me 21s.
JOHN HAYNES (166 G). I apprehended the prisoner on a warrant on
the 26th January; he asked what it was for, I told him for stealing 68.-
he said he knew nothing about it-I found him at Messrs. Waterlow's, in
Birchin Lane.
