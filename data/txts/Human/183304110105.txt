 858.  JOHN SOUTHGATE was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM WILTON. I am a bookbinder, and live in New Turnstile, Holborn- the prisoner was my servant, and was entrusted to receive money. On the 20th of February I sent him to receive money of Messrs. Vizetelly and Co., of Fleet-street - he never paid me 1l. 4s., as received from them.
   GEORGE WRIGHT. I am clerk to Mr. James Henry Vizetelly and Co., No. 76, Fleet-street - they are printers. I paid the prisoner 1l. 4s. on the 20th of February, on account of his master; I gave him a sovereign and some shillings.
 Prisoner. My master was an oppressive man to me, and I consider this prosecution cruel and unjust; I worked for him from morning till eleven or twelve o'clock at night, and on Sunday till eleven, and had then to go and cultivate his garden; it is a matter of no consequence to me now in what clime I may breathe out the remainder of my existence.
 MR. WILTON. I boarded and lodged him, and paid him 2s. or 3s. a week - he never returned to me when he had received this money.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined One Month.
 859.  WILLIAM BROAD was indicted for embezzlement.
   EDWARD HARBUT. I am a livery-stable keeper, and live at No. 41, Upper Rathbone-place- the prisoner was my ostler, and was in the habit of receiving money from Mr. Fountain for me; he took the bill to him every week: he told me at one time that the clerk was busy, and he could not get the money.
   JOHN DUNN. I am clerk to Mr. William Fountain- he lives at No. 38, Percy-street, and is a wine-merchant; he kept a horse and chaise at the prosecutor's. I was in the habit of paying the prisoner every Monday morning. 1l. 2s. - I paid him on the 4th of March, on the 11th of March, on the 25th of March, and the 2nd of April, and every week - I have his bills and receipts.
 Prisoner. I acknowledge it, but you did not give me any chance of paying the money.
 MR. HARBUT. I did not know you had got it till last Saturday.
   HUGH CARRIGAN(Police constable S 112.) I took the prisoner - he acknowledged he had received 6l. 12s., and said if I would allow him two hours to go to his father's he would pay the money - I said it was too late.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in trouble, and made away with the first week's money - I could not make that up, and I went on to the last.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. - Confined Three Months.
 860.  GEORGE AXTELL was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of April , 9 shillings, the monies of Jesse Axtell.
   HANNAH AXTELL. I am the wife of Jesse Axtell - the prisoner is my son; I gave him 9s. and a duplicate to fetch a pair of sheets from Mr. Chapman's, in London-street - he never brought me the money, nor the property, and I did not see him till the Wednesday following.
   RICHARD HONEYWELL AVANT. I am shopman to Mr. Chapman , a pawnbroker. I have the two sheets, pawned in the name of Axtell - the prisoner did not come for them.
 MRS. AXTELL. The prisoner was employed to do any thing for me - when he was taken he said he had spent the money at Greenwich fair.
     NOT GUILTY.
