MR. SMITH. Q. Do you remember, on the evening of the 17th of March, going on an errand? A. Yes, my mother sent me about half-past ten o'clock at night: I had to pass through the passage of the house we live in; there is a partition between Mr. Sandell's parlour and the passage - I saw a chest in the passage, and John Ballard standing by it; I know him-that is him; I have no doubt about it - I am sure it was him; there was another person standing on the step of the door - that person was George Hawkins;that is him: I sawMrs.Ballardcome down - John Ballard told her she was always a-bed; she had no light-she asked for one from Mr. Sandell; John Ballard said, "We want no light here" - I saw John Ballard and Hawkings take hold of each end of the chest and take it up stairs; it appeared to me to be a heavy chest: before I came down, while I was up stairs, I heard something fall very heavy in the passage - I went on my errand, and returned in about five minutes; when I re turned I heard a very heavy knocking in Ballard's room- I was in my own room, which is about three feet from Ballard's house; the noise appeared to be like something very hard - like something knocking against iron;Ihave been near a blacksmith's shop, and heard the noise directly I came back; I went to bed when the noise had ceased,
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you examined at the Police Office? A. Yes; I stated there that Mr. Sandell came to the passage, and the light was asked for.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BODKIN. Q. Do you live in small house? A. No, they live in a small house in the yard; I knew Ballards before -I had never seen Hawkins before; Mr.Sandell staid with the light till they were up stairs - that was two or three minutes, or more than that; it might be five minutes he said at his own parlour door in the passage; and there is a passage, and then there is another passage goes down; and his door is in that - Hawkins was at the step of the door at the end of the passage next the street, a good way from the candle.
 COURT. Q. How could you see him so well? A. I saw him when he came along the passage with the chest- when I came back they were up stairs.
   ANN OSBORNE. I am the mother of this witness. On Tuesday night, the 17th of March, I sent him out on an errand; I heard a very heavy fall in the passage before he went down, and I kept him a minute or two till I heard the sound of voices, and then I sent him down; this was a little after ten o'clock - after he came back I asked him what that swearing was, which I heard in the passage, and in a few minutes I heard something like a very heavy breaking; I cannot exactly describe it - it was very heavy,like knocking against something very hard, very loud, and it shook my room; it continned about twenty minutes, more or less, and appeared to come from the back house, which is out in the yard, and which is occupied by the Ballards - there was no other house from which it could come.
   MARY DENTRY. I am a grand-daughter of Mr. Sandell, and live there. On the night of the 17th of March I saw two men about twenty minutes after eleven o'clock, just at the corner of Dorrington-street; I do not know who they were - they were going from the yard, up towards Brook-street; one of them had something on his shoulder, which appeared very heavy by the way he carried it: When I saw them they were near our door,about six feet from the passage.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You cannot tell whether they had been in the yard? A. No.
   JAMES HANKS. I am a carver and gilder, and live at No. I, Dorrington-street, Brook's-market. On Wednesday, the 18th of March, about seven o'clock in the evening, I was at the street door in company with Culmore, and heard the cry of Murder! which seemed to come from the back premises occupied by Ballards; I know old Ballard and his wife well - Culmore and I went to the back premises, and when we got into the yard I heard the voice of Mrs. Ballard, which proceeded from Ballard's apartments; she said Joseph Ince should not have the money; I heard a kind of scuffle and other voices in the room - I heard Joseph Ince say if any person was mistrustful of his having the money he would give it up to any one they chuse; we then heard three or four voices say, "Let old Ballard have it;" we then heard the counting of money, which appeared as if counted on a table or a board; and it appeared, by the sound, to be sovereigns - we heard luch count one, two three,till he came to twenty-one sovereigns; and then there were two half-sovereigns, which he said made 22l. - we then heard a young woman's voice say she did not expect her brother John to wear two faces under one hat that morning; when the voices said Mr.Ballard should have it, Ballard said he would take care of it, it should not see either sun or moon.
 COURT. Q. Are you sure it was old Ballard's voice said that? A. Yes, it was then nearly eight o'clock,
 Cross-examined by MR.PHILLIPS. Q. These people must have taken uncommon pains for you to hear them; did they talk very loud? A. They did talk loudish; the upper door was closed - I know the sound of sovereigns from shillings if they chinked, that I will swear; I was at the bottom of the stairs; there are ten stairs - I was about three yards from them; I have always gone by the name of  James Hanks, unless they call me Jem; Hanks is the surname I have always gone by, never by any other; I was never here before, nor in the other Court - there was a charge made against me at Hatton-garden, by Mr. Dupree, of No. 10,Dorrington-street. a composition maker, for taking a sixpence and 3d. in halfpence; I am sure there was never any other charge made against me; there was no charge about jewellery;I was tried at Hicks-hall upon Mr. Dupree's charge, and acquitted - it is seven years ago; I am now nineteen years of age; Mr. Dupree was my master then - I live up stairs in the front house at No. 1, Dorrington-street; there might be twelve or fourteen people living in the front house or more; the back house is not more than four feet from the front house - I cannot say whether the people in the front house could hear this; they might be out.
 MR. SMITH. Q. Have you any doubt of your being correct in what you have stated? A. What I have stated I did hear.
