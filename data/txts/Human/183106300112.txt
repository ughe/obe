 1419.  ANN ANDREWS was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of June , 1 half-crown, and 13 shillings, the monies of William Carter , from his person.
 WILLIAM CARTER. I  drive a cab. On the 30th of June I was in Gray's Inn-lane, between two and three o'clock in the morning; I had spent the evening with a friend, and drank a little, but knew what I was about - I was going home; the prisoner accosted me, and asked me to walk with her - I walked with her; I went out of my way - in a quarter of an hour I was about leaving her, and felt my money was gone from my breeches pocket- it was thirteen shillings and a half-crown; I am sure it was safe when I met her - I accused her of robbing me; she denied it, and said she had no money about her, that I must have made a mistake - I laid hold of her arm; a constable came up, and I gave her in charge - as she went to the station-house I saw my money drop from her pockets; I am sure she said she had no money about her- I had taken no liberties with her.
 THOMAS DUNSFORD. I am a Policeman. I was on duty, and saw the prisoner and prosecutor - he charged her with robbing him of 15s. 6d.; I asked what she had done with the money - she said she had no money but 2d., but when we had got about a hundred yards towards the station she put her left-hand into the pocket-hole of her gown; I said, "Keep your hand from your pocket" - as I drew her hand out a half-crown and six shillings dropped on the ground; she then began to kick and be disorderly - I was obliged to hold her arms; I got my fellow-officer to pick the money up - she fell down in struggling, and seven shillings more dropped from her.
 Prisoner's Defence. This man met me, and asked if I was going home - he put his hand across my shoulder; I had the money in my bosom - he said, "You have got money;" I said, "That is immaterial to you" - he asked where I lived; I said he could not see me home - he walked with me to the middle of Laystall-street, and gave me a shove; the Policeman came up, and he gave me in charge, but said he did not know what money he had lost- he dragged me along, and the money being in my bosom, the string of my gown broke and it fell; his pocket had a hole in it, so that he could not have money in it.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Transported for Life.
 1420.  WILLIAM TIGHE was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of June , two pewter pots, value 18d., the goods of George Young ; 2 pewter pots, value 18d., the goods of John Gurr ; and 1 pewter pot, value 6d, the goods of John Bennett.
   CHARLES HAYWARD. I am servant at a public-house, in George-street, Manchester-square. I was coming up an area in Manchester-square, and saw the prisoner with two pots - I went and asked what he was going to do with them; he said he was starving - I took him to Young's, and these other pots were found on him.
   GEORGE YOUNG. I keep a public-house, in Duke-street, Manchester-square. Two of these pots are mine; I know nothing of the prisoner.
 GEORGE BANCHINI. I live with Mr.  John Gurr, of the Albion tavern, Duke-street. Two of these pots are ours- I know nothing of the prisoner.
   JOHN BENNETT. I keep the Red Lion, in Gilbert-street, a quarter of a mile from Manchester-square. One of these pots is mine - I do not know the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1421.  HENRY BLOW was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , 1 shirt, value 2s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 7s.; 1 pair of stockings, value 1s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 2s.; 1 handkerchief, value 3s.; 100 cigars, value 3s., and 1 snuff-box, value 6d., the goods of Thomas Birkenshaw.
   CHARLES CRUMPTON. I am a Thames Police-officer. On the 10th of June, at six o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner at the entrance gate of the West India-dock, with a cocoa nut in his hand - I asked what ship he came from; he said from the Ruth, which lay in the Exportdock - I said I did not believe he belonged to any ship; he then said he did not - I asked how he came into the ship; he said he got on board the previous evening, and stopped there all night - I asked what he had in his hat; he said Nothing - I pulled it off, and found about fifty cigars, and he had fifty more round him; I found a shirt and trousers on him, which he said he took from the ship.
 THOMAS BIRKENSHAW. I am chief mate of the Ruth. The prisoner was quite a stranger - he might have been on board; the property produced was in my chest in the cabin, which was locked, but not the chest - he broke open the sky-light.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1422.  JOHN WATSON was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , 1 pair of shoes, value 4s.; 2 solderingirons, value 18d.; 1 hand-staff, value 1s.; 3 hammers, value 9d.; 1 nail-tool, value 1s.; 3 chisels, value 6d.; 1 pair of scissors, value 3d.; 3 punches, value 9d.; 1 mallet, value 6d., and 1 budget, value 18d., the goods of James McGuire.
 JAMES McGUIRE. I am a  tinker. On the 27th of June I was at the Horse Shoe and Magpie, Saffron-hill, and had this property in the passage, in a leather budget - I went in to light my pipe, came out in ten minutes, and it was gone; I have not got it since - I did not see the prisoner there; it was every thing I had - I told the landlady.
   ANN CRICK. My husband keeps this house. The prosecutor called for a pint of beer - he went into the taproom to light his pipe; while he was there the prisoner came to the bar, and called for three halfpenny worth of gin, which he drank and went towards where the budget was - he returned and went out, with the budget under his left arm; when the prosecutor complained, I told my husband what I had seen, and he was taken in five minutes, but the property has not been found - I had seen the prisoner several times, and am quite sure he is the man; I knew the budget - the prosecutor usually leaves it at our house after he has done work.
