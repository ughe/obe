 OLD COURT. FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 24.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1833.  WILLIAM SULLIVAN and JEREMIAH CORCORAN were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , 1 coat, value 10s., the goods of John Nichols.
   JOHN NICHOLS. I am a labourer. On the 18th of July I was at the Rose and Crown, St. Giles'- it was a very wet afternoon; I went to have a pint of beer - my coat was wet; I pulled it off, and while I was unbuttoning it Sullivan said, "What do you want for that old coat, old chap?" I said I would not take less than 10s. for it; he came and laid hold of it, and said, "Let me fit it on;" he took it, and put it on - Corcoran was sitting by his side, drinking with him; he was going out with the coat on - I pushed him back, and said, "Don't go away with my coat;" Corcoran instantly pulled me back, and held me back while Sullivan ran out with the coat, and then he followed him - I went into the passage, but did not know which door they ran out at; I described them to two Policeman.
   EDWARD DAVIS. I am a Policeman. Nichols described the prisoners to me: I knew them from his description, and found them both in bed together, at one o'clock in the morning - I took Sullivan, but did not then know Corcoran was concerned - he was afterwards taken; I found the prosecutor's coat on the bed.
 Sullivan. The man himself put the coat on my back, and offered to sell it.
   JOHN NICHOLS. It is not true.
   THOMAS KENNEDY. I keep the public-house. I remember seeing Sullivan in the tap-room; I remember seeing Nichols there - I do not know Corcoran: he might be there - I saw no part of the transaction inside the house: I was in the yard - Sullivan passed me, and went out at a very quick pace, with a great coat like this on; another man followed him out quickly, but I did not see his face.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Sullivan's Defence. His coat was not wet at all - he took it off, and offered it for sale to another man, and then to me; I had not enough to purchase it, and he said if I would sell it he would stand something to drink.
 Corcoran's Defence. He put the coat on Sullivan's back, and he went out with it - I went in about ten minutes, and met Sullivan very much intoxicated.
 SULLIVAN -    GUILTY. Aged 26. Transported for Seven Years.
 CORCORAN -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Three Months.
 1834.  WILLIAM SPRY and THOMAS BROWN were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 6d.; 1 cap, value 2s.; 1 knife, value 6d.; 1 key, value 4d.; 1 sovereign, and 1 sixpence, the property of John Jones , from his person.
   JOHN JONES. I am a dealer in provisions, and live in Great Eastcheap. I got rather the worse for liquor, and was robbed of this property, between eleven and twelve o'clock at night, as I was going home - I recollect very little of the circumstance.
   WILLIAM CLEMENTS. I am a Police-officer. I was on duty in St. John-street, St. Sepulchre's- I had information that something was going on wrong, and went to the spot: I found the prosecutor standing with his back against the wall, very drunk - the two prisoners were surrounding him up a court, which is not a thoroughfare; they had got their hands about his person - I asked what they were doing with the man; they said they had merely brought him there to ease himself - I asked what business they had to bring him up there; they said they did not like to see him do it in the street - I asked what they had got about them - they said they had nothing; I searched them, and found in Spry's hat a handkerchief, and another handkerchief inside - I asked if both belonged to him; he said they did- I turned round to Jones; he said the red handkerchief was his - I took both the prisoners into custody; they wanted to rush past me - I said if they did I would cut them down with my truncheon, and if I had not been resolute they would have got away; I found a black cloth cap in Spry's hat, which Jones did not claim at the time, but next morning he inquired for it - when I got Jones to the watch-house, I asked if he had lost any money; he said he did not know, for he was very drunk.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. The prosecutor was drunk? A. Yes, and did not know what he was doing - some of his buttons were undone, and somebody had certainly been there for the purpose they stated; I will not swear their hands were in his pockets, but they were about them - they were not assisting to button up his braces; I do not consider that it was their duty to assist him, there were plenty of Police about; I found nothing on Brown - I found a towel and handkerchief in Spry's hat: Jones did not claim them - there is a gas lamp in the court.
   JAMES LEE. I am a private watchman. I found a knife and key on the window-cill, near the spot, directly after the prisoners were brought to the station.
 Cross-examined. Q. Many other persons might have been in the court? A. There is a wicket-gate at the end of it; it was about twenty minutes past eleven o'clock.
   THOMAS ENGLAND. I am a carpenter. I was the first who went up the court after the prisoners were taken - I found a sovereign and a sixpence lying there on the ground.
