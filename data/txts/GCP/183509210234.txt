COURT. Q. Was there an omnibus called the “Tally-Ho!" A. That
was before the 10th of November-there has been no opposition since, but
by Mr. Blower-his is an odd bushe takes his time regularly at the Bank,
following out the buses that he follows in, and that is the regulation in the
City, or else we should all be in confusion I can't tell when Blower had
bis time my fellow time-keeper can tell that I mean the same Robert
Blower, who has been called he does not take regular time-be goes
when it suits him.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. That is, when he comes in, you order him to
go at his time? A. He does not always come in, and take time he goes
up Bank-street, and takes passengers--if a person comes in, and takes time,
I can only insist that he should set off at his regular time--if a man comes
in to oppose the regular omnibuses, and remains at the Bank for the time
specified, he must go he is liable to be punished under the Bank Actif
he goes when I direct him, he must run against one of the regular
omnibuses, which causes so much confusion and obstruction, even on the
pavement.
Q. If an opposition omnibus, not coming in and taking the regular
times, thinks fit to come, for instance at twelve o'clock, must he not neces
sarily oppose himself to one of the omnibuses running at that hour!
4. Yes; and by that means he creates confusion.
COURT. Q. The times are regulated by the proprietors ? A. Yes:
there are twenty-two to fill these times if two more came on, it would
not only create opposition, but confusion-if the time was made twa
minutes, it would be no use, it would not pay them--we have had very
great complaints of so many omnibuses being in the City-if the other
omnibuses were as well regulated as ours are, there would not be 80
much confusion.
