751. ISAAC PERRY and THOMAS ANTHONY BURT, alias Thomas
Anthony, were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering a building
within the curtilage of the dwelling-house of Thomas Henry Whitmarsh,
at Lewisham, on the 9th of January, and stealing therein, 6 shifts, Value
21. ; 6 shirts, value 31. ; 4 bed-gowns, value 17. ; 1 shawl, value 1s.; 3
pairs of drawers, value 38. ; 1 pair of socks, value 6d.; 5 pairs of stock-
ings, value 38.; 2 aprons, value 28. ; 2 table-covers, value 48.4 1 pillow
case, value 2s.: 1 towel, value 6d. ; the goods of Thomas Henry White
marsh: and 4 shirts, value 21.; and 1 waistcoat, value 58.; the goods of
Thomas Whitmarsh.

Esther GREEN, I am housekeeper at the Green Man public-house,
Black heath, in the parish of Lewisham, which is kept by Thomas Henry
Whitmarsh. On Thursday afternoon, the 9th of January, I was in the
laundry, which is in the garden-I cannot get to it from the dwelling-house
without going into the open air—it is partly under the ball-room--after pass-
ing the great entrance of the house, there is a paling, which goes entirely
round the premises, enclosing the whole building, till you come to Dartmouth-
road and round to the great entrance again--the laundry-door opens into
small yard and you go out of that yard into the garden a man could get
over the paling I should think-the yard is in the garden, only separated
by a gate--the fence goes all round the garden--my master's and mistress's
linen, and some shirts, and waistcoats, and a bag, belonging to Me
Thomas Whitmarsh, who is the father of Thontas Henry Whitmarsh, was
in the laundry that afternoon--it was there to air--the policeman has since
produced a bundle to me containing these articles --some were marked in
fall, and some in initials--I left the laundry between four and five o'clock!
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Do you know any thing about
the parish the house is in? 1. I know very little about the parish-I be-
lieve it is called the parish of Lewisham-I always understood 80s the
laundry is attached to the house - I go through the scullery to it, and
through the yard--the brick wall of the laundry and kitchen join, but there
is no entrance from the house to the laundry, nor any covered way
should think the laundry has been added since the kitchen was built--Mr.
Thomas Whitmarsh has retired from the business-he kept the house till
within these nine months,

THOMAS HENRY WHITMARSH. I am the owner of the Green Man pub-
lic-house, and have been so wholly since May 1889. My father's name is
Thomas-he does not reside with me, and is not a partner at all—it is in
the parish of Lewisham-the laundry is under the ball-room and is part
of the building—there is no access to it but going through the garden and
yard—the laundry-door opens into a small yard close to the scullery-
this yard is enclosed, and the garden is enclosed by pales six or seven feet
high.
Cross-examined. Q. Does the floor of the ball-room form the ceiling of
the laundry ? A. Yes-it is not disconnected from it, except that there
is no doorway-Burt was in my father's service three or four years ago.
