   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Was not the main-sail of the lighter towards the boat? A.  Yes, between the boat and the person steering the lighter—if she had put her helm round she would have got the boat out in a shorter space—I heard no cry out—I was some distance off—there were several vessels tacking about—it required management to steer—the Peter-boat was still going down the river—I do not know whether the boy tried to get a tow of the ballast-lighter—if the boy had stopped his boat in time he might have got out of the way. LABIN TILBROOK. I am a fisherman, and live at Greenwich. I belong to a fishing-smack, in which I was sailing down the river with eight others—I saw the boy in the boat rowing down the river—we stood to the south—we asked him how far he was going—he said, "To Rainham"—my brother, who is master of the smack, said he would give him a tow if he pulled along side, but he could not catch us—we saw no more till a boy in our boat said, "Oh, master, he is run over"—I and another man hauled out our boat directly, and when I got within two strokes of him he went down—I put the oar down into the water, but could not feel him—at that time the lighter's boat came to the spot, within a minute or two—I think if they had heaved the vessel round directly, and jumped into the boat they might have saved the lad, but they kept right on till they got right over the river—they did not heave about directly—we saw hands on board—if there had been a look-out, I think it might have been prevented—I saw the wreck of the Peter-boat—I do not know who navigated the lighter.
   Crosss-examined. Q.  A ballast-lighter does not answer the helm so well as yours? A.  Yes; it will come round quicker in a brisk wind, as there was, but not generally speaking—I thought they lost time—they were a good dis tance behind us—I knew the boy, his name was James Brown. GEORGE TILBROOK. I am master of the smack, I saw the boy in the Peter-boat, and spoke to him a few minutes before the accident occurred—I was walking the deck, and the lad said, "The boy is run over"—I turned, and saw the boat—I consider, if the lighter had been looking out, it might have been prevented—I can venture to say, if I had been looking out in my boat, and they told me they saw a boat in time, I could have cleared her—it is the duty always to keep a look-out—they got out a boat afterwards.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Their boat was not long after yours? A.  No—it might have been the act of a moment—I had spoken to the boy three or four minutes before—there was no Vessel in the way to obstruct just at that time. WILLIAM BLACKBURN.
