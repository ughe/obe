Sheen to WILLIAM CURTIS. Q. What did you find on me! A. Three
shillings, a sixpence, and a fourpenny piece—that was after you had given the
half-crown back.
Sheen's Defence. The female states that I changed a half-sovereign, and
gave her two half-crowns; that left me with one half-crown, a sixpence, and
a fourpenny bit. The 8d. which I spent in wine would exactly make the
half-sovereign, and there was not a farthing more found on me. It matters
not to the policeman whether persons are convicted, so long as he gets them
convicted. I should have escaped when I had the opportunity if I had
been guilty. Colonel Henderson has the police to back him, and supplies
them with funds to commit perjury. A man cannot come here and get
justice. It is only proved that there was one piece, and that I did not
utter with a guilty knowledge; and I have never been convicted of tender-
ing counterfeit coin.
Guilty.
