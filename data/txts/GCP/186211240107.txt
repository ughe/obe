71. JAMES WANTAGE (20), Stealing a leather bag, containing divers
articles, value 102, the property of Henry de Lezeu.
RACHEL WASP. I live at 5, Barking-ohurohyard a little after 3 in the
afternoon of 7th November, I was in Cannon-street, and saw the prisoner o
up to a carriage which was standing by the kerb, put his arm in at the
window, and take a leather bag out-he then walked slowly away-I told
the coachman, and he instantly got down and went after him- the prisoner
ran then I stopped till he was brought back, and then the conchman and
the policeman asked me if that was the man who took it, and I said, "Yes",
-- he had dropped the bag, I believe I did not see him drop it.
Prisoner. O. Are you quite sure it was me that took it out of the car
ringet A. Quite certain ; you walked down towards the King William
statue—where you turned then, I cannot say.
EDWIN JOHN JARVIS. I am a carrier-I was just turning down Nicholas
lane, when I saw the prisoner coming along, running very fast, with a
leather bag in his hand there was nobody after him-I made way to let
him pass I thought he was trying to catch a bus or something-I am sure
It was the prisoner--that was about 4 o'clock, or half-rast, I should think
he was running from Cannon-street to King William-street.
Prisoner. . You said at the Mansion-house that when I was running
by you, a gentleman was calling, “Stop thieft" 4. There was no cry of
"Stop thief !”-I heard the ory of Stop thief!" after you passed me, or I
should have stopped you-directly I heard the cry, I turned round and gave
chase, followed close behind you, down King William-street to Lombard-
street, and you were stopped by some persons just by the post-office-you
threw the bag away on your passage.
Mr. Henry de Leeu, the owner of the bag, was called on his recognizance; but
he did not appear to identify the property stolen, and the Court directed the
Jury to acquit the prisoner on that ground.
Not Guilty.
