RICHARD WILDEY (Polios Inspector). From information received I went
to Chelmsford on 30th July and took the prisoner into custody there-he
know me before I said "I shall take you into custody for stealing some
watches "-he stopped me and said "He wrote a letter to my wife telling
her to come and ask me what had become of the pawn-tickets, and gave
her 58. to come and see me; and because she told him I knew nothing
about them he said he would swear to me"-on the way to the station he
said “Where were those watches from, Barking way?-I said "Bark-
ing Road”-he said "Yes, my wife told me so" - I had not mentioned
Barking Road to him previously-descriptions of the watches have been
circulated; they have not been recovered the prisoner was living in
Ocean Street, Stepney, three or four miles from the prosecutor.
Witnesses for the Defengs.
Louisa WALTON. I am a tailoress, of 66, Ocean Street, and am
married-three weeks before 3rd August I was lodging in the same
house with the prisoner's wife, occupying the front room upstairs-on
the Tuesday the prosecutor called to see the prisoner's wife-I sent for
her, and then went to my room upstairs, where I could hear what was
said in the kitchen-the prosecutor said "Is your husband at home?" -
she said “No”-he said "He is locked up -she said "Yes, I know
he is "-he said "I saw him in the Barking Road once, but I let him
pass me, because I was not sure he was the man"-he wrote on a piece
of paper which I saw afterwards the number of the watches, and his
name and address I know his hand from a letter he sent, which I saw
a week afterwards-when he left the paper on the table he said "Be sure
you don't show this to any one."
Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHBGAN. I have been lodging with the
prisoner's wife two years, and am doing so still the prisoner was living
with his wife in June; he went out at 7 in the morning and came back
at 5-I don't know where he was working, I never asked him-when I
first went he worked for his brother-on 15th June he went out and came
home about the same time we have lived at three addresses together
during the two years I said at the police-court that the prosecutor said
he was not sure the prisouer was the man--the prisoner's wife gave me
no papers to bring here-she had the paper the prosecutor left on the
table, and the letter he wrote afterwards--I live in the same house still
they have left-I owe the prisoner's wife no money-we paid 68. 3d. a
week for the house; I paid 38, for the top room.
