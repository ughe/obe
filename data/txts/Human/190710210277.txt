Neither Harris nor Hawkes drafted circulars for Herbert Smith that I know of. The usual way is to copy some other broker's circular. I do not know that I gave him any to copy. Atkins was the principal man at 43, Moorgate Street. He was a director. I believe I was on the voters' list. I cannot tell how I got there. Miss Long was Smith's clerk. Harris was manager of the business from the beginning. Herbert Smith took an active part, and at far ss I know was there daily. I cannot name any person who is here as a witness who has ever seen Herbert Smith. I do not believe that Bleach saw him only once, and I think that the story that Bleach had to have Smith pointed out to him by a lady clerk is an Invention. As to Mrs. Strick having only seen him once I do not suppose she has ever been there more than once. I never saw her there at all. Jessie Oyler was employed there about twelve months. If she thought Harris was Smith I cannot help that. There was no necessity for Smith to go into the office where the young lady clerk was, and she might have been there twenty years without seeing him. Besides she sat with her face to the window, and would not see anybody com  ing into the room. Parkinson, who identified the photograph of Smith, used to come about once in three months. As to the statement of Mr. Gash that £12,000 came into, the business, it passed through the banking account, but that is not money coming into the business. I consider the foot that Smith only had 4s. a day quite consistent with his controlling a business of this magnitude. With regard to my own position towards the business, I simply dealt through Herbert Smith. I was his client. I opened stocks for myself on the Stock Exchange, and they were debited to Smith's account, and the con tracts went to Herbert Smith, in the name of Herbert Smith. Herbert Smith also opened stocks in his own name for his own protection. I met the brokers at Smith's office, and the stocks were put down to him with the knowledge of the brokers. I never gave orders to stock brokers to cover for Herbert Smith. The volume of business Smith did gave him credit. I attended on pay-day, and picked out the stocks that were mine. Without a technical knowledge of the neces sities of this business it is very difficult for anybody to follow it. I hare technical knowledge, but counsel have not very great know ledge, end his Lordship is rather strange-to it. Herbert Smith, either by himself or through Harris, opened stock on the Stock Ex change to protect his clients, and I opened stock on the Stock Ex change through the same broker who was dealing for Herbert Smith, and those contracts went down to Herbert Smith's account. When pay-day came round I had to take up my three or four loans in order to know what I had to pay or receive, and the balance of the account belonged to Herbert Smith. If there was a profit on the account I got it from Herbert Smith, why in turn got it from the broker; if there was a loss the broker had to look to Smith for it.
