   Cross-examined. Q.  You did not mark it till the 30th of March? A.  I presume that was the day—it was nearly two months after I received it—this is very unlike a good half-sovereign—the impression on the head side is like, but the gilding and appearance is not—there is no other person who has access to the shop but Mr. and Miss Smith and myself, only to-day, as we are compelled to attend here—the till was always settled every even ing, but this coin was never taken out—I saw it every day—an eighteen-penny tooth-pick is very uncommon in our shop—I saw the prisoner at the Compter about a month after the 9th of February, that might be a week previous to my marking the coin. WILLIAM FERRISON SMITH. I am a cutler—Day is my shopman. I first saw the counterfeit half-sovereign the evening it was passed—after looking at it I put it under some paper at the back of the till, in a sepa rate compartment, and it has remained there ever since, as far as I know—only Day and myself have access to the till—my sister lives in my house, she comes down at night to add up the amount of the money, in pre sence of myself or Day, but is never in the shop alone—she is not here, being ill, and under the care of a doctor. HENRY LAMBERT. I am shopman to Mr. Farthing, a cutler, in Corn-hill.
