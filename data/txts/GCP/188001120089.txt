meeting of creditors in March—he proposed to pay 68. in the pound at once,

I acted as representative for Hyde and Co., and this prosecution was at

Cross-examined. I sold some shawls to Michell on my own account on
26th October, value 1031., which was paid—that made the second pareel
that was paid for the St. James's Bank stopped about 12th September, but
there was nothing there to his credit - I may have declined to look at the
securities at first, but being pressed I looked at them-I am sure he said
1,6001.- I did not know whether his wife's sanction had been given--this
looks like the document which I said appeared to be a will (an indenture)
I am not familiar with legal documents—I put the 691. bill into the hands
of Messrs. Plunkett and Leader, who I have known 14 years, some time in
January, and in due time received notice of Michell filing a liquidation
petition-I attended the meeting, and was chairman-I took no other steps
till April 1st-I do not know whether the composition was to be secured by
the wife--the general feeling was that the creditors would not take less than
10s.--as the 58. was refused Mr. Vanderpump suggested that time should be
given—I do not remember hearing Mr. Waddington say that if 10s. in the
pound was not paid bankruptcy proceedings would be instituted, or that a
prosecution would probably take place, or that the Bradford creditors would
prosecute, nor do I remember stopping him and correcting him I do not
remember a statement being made by any creditor that he would prosecute
if 108. in the pound was not paid the committee of inspection are Mr.
Newstead, who is, I think, Mr. Lovering's manager, Mr. Lovering, who is
Keighley's trustoe, Mr. Hopkins, one of the managing clerks of Plunkett and
Leader, and myself.
Ro-examined. I have heard nothing against Mr. Hopkins's, or Mr.
Newstead's, or Mr. Lovering's characters, or Messrs. Plunkett and Leader's
--the prosecution has been undertaken on public grounds—there was a very
lengthened inquiry at the Barkruptcy Court, which made an order to prosecute,
and another inquiry before the Alderman, before a committal took place ---
I have known Mr. Lovering a great many years, he is a highly respectable
accountant-this (produced) is his appointment of Messrs. Plunkett and
Leader as his proxies-I never read the document which I thought was a
will--the same proposal of 58. was made on 25th March, and it was
adjourned at Mr. Vanderpump's request to give him the opportunity of seeing
whether he could carry it out-no resolution was passed the proceedings
in bankruptcy were left to the trustee, Mr. Everett I never interfered in
any way as to threatening to prosecute Michell no one was present
when the documents were shown me but Keighley and Michell—I did look
at one document about the consent of the wife.
