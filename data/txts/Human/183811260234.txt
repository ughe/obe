   Q.  Why have you not preferred charges for those bills? A.  I did not care which, I left it to my professional man to choose—my present at torney lives in London—I did not direct the two bills of Phillips's to be brought to London.
   Q.  Was not the bill on Morris, which induced you to send Mr. M. R. James to the prisoner in gaol, due in October? A.  That was for another 100 l ., which I never heard any more of.
   Q.  Then there seems to have been thousands according to your state ment? A.  I cannot say—I did not send Mr. M. R. James to see if he could get any admission from the prisoner—I sent him to know the extent of the forgeries—he was examined as a witness for me on the trial last summer, and he swore, in my presence, that the prisoner admitted the forgery, and the Jury found for the plaintiff.
   Q.  Pray have you got any security besides that you have produced, either from the prisoner, or his family? A.  I have security from his mother—I got that, I think, at the latter end of 1837—I have not brought it to London—I received it, I think, in October, after I sent Mr. M. R. James to the prison—it is a mortgage—it does not state any amount—I took it after I found there was another mortgage, I mean Biddulph's mort gage—I do not know the amount of that—I have heard it is 600 l ., but Mr. Hulme, the clerk, said yesterday he thought it would cover the other 100 l . also—I know his mother's property lets for 55 l . a-year—I have a general charge, without any amount stated—there is no incumbrance on it—she gave me that as security that I should not he injured by her son—I think Mr. Gwynne was the prisoner's detaining creditor—I did not know he was going to arrest him—he was examined on the trial as a witness—Mr. Hulme was not—the case was tried before Mr. Baron Gurney.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Before you went to the prison with Mr. Morris, had he requested of you to avoid any particular subject? A.  Yes, he had—I was the defendant in the 200 l . action—the prisoner was a witness.
