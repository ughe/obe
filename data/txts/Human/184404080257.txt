Before Mr. Justice Williams.
 1338. ANN CARROLL was indicted for the wilful murder of Elizabeth Hobbs ; and on the Coroner's inquisition with manslaughter.
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM HOBBS. I am a boiler-maker, and life in Holland-street, Borough. At the time this happened I lived at 2, Bird Cage-alley, where Carroll and his wife lived—there were several lodgers in the house—I and my wife occupied the front-room second floor—I had been there four or five weeks when this happened—Carroll was my landlord—on the 2nd of March I left my wife in the Black friars-road as I went to work—we had been at a public-house, and had half-a-quarters of rum together—she did not drink quite a quarter of it—I left her at five minutes before six in the morning—she was in good health—I never knew her in better health—when I came home at night I found her dead—her name was Elizabeth Hobbs. SARAH BRAY. I lived at this time at No. 1, Bird Cage-alley, next door to this house. On the morning of the 2nd of March I heard cries of murder proceeding from the up-stairs window of Carroll's house—I heard people quarrelling very much—I went to the street-door, and demanded for the door to be opened—I did not see the prisoner at first, nor her husband—when I first saw the prisoner I said, "Mrs. Carroll, what have you got about? or what do you make this noise about? why don't you send for a policeman? if you cannot agree with this woman, have her taken up, don't ill use her like this"—Mrs. Carroll held two pair of boots in her hand, and said to the deceased, "When you pay me for my broken things I will give you your boots back, and not before "—the blood was then running down the left side of the deceased's neck from the back of the neck, and I saw a mark of a blow on her temple—it seemed a very heavy blow—the deceased had a fork in her hand—the prisoner's husband was standing by her side—the deceased beat the fork on the stairs, and called Mrs. Carroll very bad words indeed several times, and she said, "I wish it was one o'clock, for my husband to come home, I would have my revenge for beating me like this'—she addressed herself to Mr. and Mrs. Carroll—I did not see anything in Mr. Carroll's band—he might have had something, and I not see it—I went into my own room—I then heard the deceased woman repealing these bad words again, saying, "Don't strike me with a poker, I am not a man, I am a woman"—she addressed herself to Mr. Carroll—she said, "Mr. Carroll, don't strike me with a poker."
   COURT . Q.  Was the deceased in liquor? A.  It is my opinion she had a little liquor; but I don't think she had more than to know what she was about—she stood correct on her feet—I saw nothing in the prisoner's hands, except the boots. HARRIET MOSEA.
