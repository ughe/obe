o'clock? A. Yes; it was quite evident they were attempting to commit
an offence-they attempted another shop before it was impossible for

Q. Were not you and your companion lying about on purpose to have
a felony committed, that you might take them ? A. We are sent out on
purpose in coloured clothes to pick up what we can regarding that, for two
months past-I do not know that the policemen's allowance used to be
paid to the head establishment-I always received it myself, here or at
the station-house I believe there has been an alteration lately-it comes
directly into the policeman's hands now-I always received it myself-
we have received it here and afterwards at the station-house, and now!
understand we are to receive it here againthat is all the alteration that I
know of.
GEORGE TEAKLE. I am a policeman. I was with Savage at about
half-past seven o'clock-I saw the prisoners go to Mr. Corss' shop-they
both stood close together-I saw Ward's hand working, as if he was
cutting or boring something, but they stood so close together, I could
not see what he was doing--in about ten minutes they went away, and
stood under an archway-they remained there five or ten minutes, and then
returned to the shop, to the same pane of glass-Ward's hand worked in
the same way-all in an instant they both darted from the window and ran
across the road-we ran over, and found a pane of glass out of the window,
and a piece of cloth partly out of the window-I went in the direction
they ran, and went to one or two public-houses, but could not find them
-I am quite certain they are the two persons.
Cross-examined. Q. If you had run after them before you went over
to the shop, you would have saved the property ? A. I did not know
they had committed an offence till we went over.
