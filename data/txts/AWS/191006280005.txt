1910.
CENTRAL CRIMINAL COURT
KNILL, MAYOR
NINTH SESSION.
LONDON AND MIDDLESEX CASES.
BEFoRe THE RECORDER
(Tuesday, June 28.)
JOSEPHS, Ethel Miriam (28, parlourmaid), pleaded guilty of
feloniously forging on April 18 last an order for the payment of 215
with intent to defraud and uttering the same, knowing it to be forged.
Sentence, Two months' imprisonment, second division.
RIDGELEY, May Frances, pleaded guilty of feloniously marrying
Edward Albert Williams, her husband Arthur Ridgeley being then
alive.
Williams was convicted last session (see page 197) of feloniously
wounding the present prisoner, when Mr. Justice Grantham expressed
the opinion that she should be prosecuted for bigamy.
Mrs. FLIza UNDERHILL, prisoner's mother, stated that she
had looked after her daughter as well as she could, and undertook to
take proper care of her in the future.
It was stated that prisoner had falsified the register on the occasion
of her going through the form of marriage with Williams.
Sentence, Six months' imprisonment.
HENSHAW, Henry (45, writer), and HENSHAW, Alexander (24,
traveller, son of Henry Henshaw), pleaded guilty of demanding and
obtaining from Rose Gladys Laughrin the sum of E1, the moneys of
His Majesty's Postmaster-General, under, upon, and by virtue of a
certain forged instrument, to wit, a Post Office Savings Bank Deposit
book, well knowing the same to be forged and altered.
It was stated that in 21 cases the prisoners had opened a Post Ofice
Savings Bank account in a fictitious name by depositing small sums,
which they afterwards altered to larger amounts. In 14 cases they had
sueceeded in drawing these sums.
HENRY HENsHaw stated in mitigation that he was not guilty of
getting the 14 different sums as alleged, and that he had been out of
work for 12 months.
