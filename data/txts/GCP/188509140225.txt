910. HARRY JAMES, Feloniously forging and uttering an
order for the payment of 91. 88., with intent to defraud.
MR. MEAD Prosecuted; MR. HORACE AVORY Defended.
GEORGE RICHARD TOOKE. I am managing clerk to Messrs. Smith,
Stenning, and Co., of Aldermanbury-the prisoner has been in their
employ about eight years-on 12th May 91. 88. was due to Mr. Heath for
fees--the prisoner drew this cheque by my directions for that amount
(produced the body of it is in the prisoner's writing, and this is Mr.
Stenning's signature--the prisoner's duty was to pay it to Mr. Heath-I.
did not see it again uptil, I think, 6th July, when my cashier called my
attention to the endorsement, apparently in Mr. Heath's writing I know
his writing; it is not his, though very much like it-I showed it to Mr.
Heath-after that I asked the prisoner what made him do these things
- he said he was very sorry-there were several cheques drawn on the
12th May.
Cross-examined. The prisoner came to us from Kirby, Gedge, and
Millett, the Solicitors to the School Board, with 6 years' character-he
was their assistant Chancery clerk-we entrusted him with lump sums of
money to disburse he would have to render us an account, not at
stated times, but not more than a week would elapse before he did so-
if he used any of this petty cash for his private purposes, and then
accounted for it at the end of the week, no particular objection would be
made, unless damage resulted to a client.
Ro-examined. We did not recognise his taking cheques drawn for par-
ticular purposes and cashing them, or putting other people's names on
cheques—there were other acts of dishonesty on his part-certain papers
were not to be found which were found afterwards.
