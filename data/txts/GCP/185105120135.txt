MR. HORRY conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE REEVE. I am a carrier, living at Royal-hill, Greenwich. The
prisoner was in my service about fifteen months-in Aug. last my daughter
left town on a visit, and the prisoner had the care of the plate- I did not pay
particular attention to it till 15th April, when my daughter returned—I then
asked the prisoner where the spoons were-she said she did not know; she
supposed they were all right-I said they were not in the plate-basket; and
after some time she said she had put them away; she refused to tell me
where--I searched a box of mine in her bedroom, and found in it two dupli-
cates- I missed other articles, and gave her in charge-I saw the constable
find this pocket between the mattress and sacking of her bed-there were
forty-three or forty-four duplicates in it-she said she intended to redeem the
articles when her wages were paid her.
WILLIAM WELLS (policeman, R 875). I searched the prisoner's bedroom,
and found this pocket under the bed-she said she put the things away,
intending to redeem them when she had her quarter's wages.
EDWARD Challis. I am shopman to Mr. Delaney, a pawnbroker, of
Church-street, Greenwich. I produce a pair of spectacles, pledged in the
name of "Mary Powell, Church-passage, for Ann Johnson," I do not know
who by.
Robert KENDRELL. I am shopman to Mr. Nash, & pawnbroker, of
Greenwich. I produce a silver spoon, pledged on 15th June, in the name of
Ann Powell, by the prisoner, I believe; I am not positive-I produce a
baby's shirt, two pinafores, a handkerchief, two sheets, two pieces of merino,
and two pieces of flannel, the duplicates of which correspond with those found
by the officer—the linen is pledged in the name of Mitchell.
George Reeve re-examined. These "articles are all mine-some are
marked with my name in full.
Prisoner's Defence. I pledged them, because I was not ball fed.
(There were two other in dielments against the prisoner.)

1214. VICTORINE DE HOWARD, stealing 2 tumblers, value la. 6d.;
the goods of Charles Thomas Green.
CHARLES THOMAS Gesex. I keep the George and Dragon, Woolwich.
This tumbler is mine-it has “George and Dragon" on it-I missed it on
Sunday evening, 27th April, about ten o'clock, with one of another pattern
-in the morning I found one of a similar pattern, which did not belong to
me, left instead of it-the prisoner was there between eight and nine that
evening.
JOHN NEWELL (policeman). I found this glass, and two others, in a room
which the prisoner said was hers.
GEORGIANA BESTER. I am servant to Mr. Green. I saw the prisoner there
between eight and nine o'clock-she was drinking out of a pot-I saw her
drop a glass on the floor by ber sidesbe picked it up and put it on the
table-this is it-it is not my master's; it was left instead of bis.
Prisoner's Defence. I bought the glass of a little boy; if I had stolen it
I should have locked it up in my room, or made away with it. Nor GUILTY.
