hedge--the man who ran down the lane could very well have returned and
come over that bank-I did not see him come over the top of the bank, but
I saw him run from the bank into the middle of the high road, across the
footpath-I did not see him come over the bank he came from the bank
- he came into the road with his hands under his coat flap, and walked
coolly down to the old gentleman and me I was not making any words,

Q. Who was the young man with his hands under his coat? 4. The pri-
soner-1 said, “This gentleman has just been robbed of his watch" he
said, "Oh, that be d-d, nothing of the kind"-I then said, "He has, for
I saw it all"-I said " I will have nothing to do with it," and I bade them
good night-I ran up towards Croydon canal bridge to see for a policeman
-I did not see any, and I returned back again-I did not see either the pri-
soner or the prosecutor-they were gone I saw the prisoner again about a
quarter of an hour after, below the Newcross turnpike-gate, opposite Mz
Hardcastle's gate nearly--it leads up to what I call the front coach-road,
further down from London-he was by himself walking along the road—I
did not give him in chargeI told the policeman, when I saw him coming
down Mr. Hardcastle's coach-road, what had happened—I gave a descrip-
tion of it to the policeman, Ledger, of the R division, and he took him in
my presence.
Q. You observed the height, figure, and dress of the man ; what is your
belief as to the prisoner! 4. He is the same man by dress that passed me
at the end of the lane from the old man, and came up again-he has the
same dress, and is the same height, as near as I can guess, but I did not
see the young man's face it might be another man in the same dress
cannot say,
