There was a disturbance certainly, for the police to come to the assistance of this man—that was the cry, and when Peter O'Brien was on the policeman, it was to release him—it was about the middle of the bridge. HENRY STANDAGE. I am a waiter at Cremorne. I was coming by Battersea Bridge from the gardens after having left my work at a quarter past 12 o'clock—I heard a disturbance and cry of "Police!"—I came up and saw Whittaker on the ground—there was a number of persons, and I saw both the prisoners—Peter had got his hands in Whittaker's stock, and was kneeling on him—Simon was scuffling about, and another man got hold of him—Peter was shaking Whittaker up and down on the kerb stone—Mr. Henry said, "Don't kill the man"—he caught hold of his arm, and I caught hold of his arm, and we got him away—he was quite exhausted, quite gone—he could not move for several minutes—I did not see Simon do any thing to the policeman—I was not there in time for that—two of the A's came up, and one of them tried to get Peter away from Whittaker, and they scuffled and fell—Peter hit him as hard as he could, and told him he would serve him the same.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you hear anything said by either of the prisoners about their wives and families being insulted? A.  I heard them say so at the station—I rather think they were the worse for liquor. JOHN HILLIER. ( Policeman, A.  282). In consequence of a cry of "Police!" I went on this night or morning to Battersea Bridge—I found Whittaker there, and Peter O'Brien holding him by the stock—he appeared very nearly strangled, he could scarcely speak—I requested Peter to release his hold—he said, "No, I will choke the b—and throw him over the bridge"—I then threw Peter on the ground, and succeeded in releasing his hold—Whittaker appeared very exhausted; he was obliged to rest on the kerb a few minutes before he could get up—Peter struck me several times and said, "You b—, I will serve you the same"—Simon was lying on the ground—some parties had hold of him—I did not see him do anything.
 Cross-examined,  Q.  Did you take Peter to the station? A.  Yes—he said at the station that he and his wife had been insulted by some parties on the bridge. JOHN CLARK. ( Policeman, A.  273). I came on Battersea Bridge that morning—I took Simon O'Brien in custody—there were three civilians with him trying to keep him from making his escape—he said to me, "If so many of you had not come up, I would have killed the b—"—he made several attempts to strike me, but I kept him off with my truncheon.
