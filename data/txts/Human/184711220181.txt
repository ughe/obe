DAVID PATON. I am porter to Messrs. Benton and Thoms. On 6th Jan. I took a liqueur and cruet-frame to No. 10, Theberton-street, Islington—a little girl opened the door, and I left the goods—two or three days after, I took the invoice of those goods to 6, Pinner's-hall, Old Broad-street, and found that Holliday and Co. had left—I saw a female, who keeps the chambers, and she told me so, and could not inform me where they were gone—on finding that, I went back to 10, Theberton-street—I saw a female there, who referred me to Cross-street, Islington—I do not know the number—I went there, but could not find them—I found the name of Holiday on the door.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q.  Did you find that a Mr. Schulk hauser had been living at Pinner's-ball? A.  No. CHARLES REEVE. I am a pawnbroker, at Chapel-street, Lisson-grove. In Jan. last, I was assistant to Mr. Boyce—on 19th Jan. Holliday pawned this liqueur-frame and cruet-frames there, for 2 l . 5 s . each, in the name of Smith—these two duplicates ( looking at two produced by Kibblewhite, the officer ) are what I gave him. SAMUEL STUCKLEY BENSON re-examined.  These are the frames I sold to Gardiner and Holliday. EBENEZER KIBBLEWHITE( City policeman , 645.) On 5th Sept. last, after the prisoners were taken into custody on this charge, I went and searched Gardiner's lodging, at 19, Mount-street, Charlton, near Woolwich—I there found seventeen duplicates and among them the two I have produced.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q.  When you took Gardiner into custody, I believe he at once gave you his address? A.  Yes, and it was in consequence of that address that I went and found the duplicate—I did not break open his drawers—these duplicates were sealed in a piece of paper—I broke the seal.
   MR. BODKIN. Q.  Where was Gardiner when you took him? A.  On the second-floor landing of 52, Lime-street, leading to Cusell's office.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q.  Outside Cusell's door, do you mean? A.  Outside his officer-door—I do not know that Cusell is very ill—I should like to know where he is—I have seen him a few times, but not very latterly—I had no warrant when I went to Gardiner's lodging—I did not break anything open—I carried off these papers and love-letters, and some documents belonging to an old lady—I acted under my own instructions in seizing them—I considered that I had a right to take them—Gardiner and Holliday had been apprehended at that time, not for a misdemeanour, but for obtaining goods under false pretences—I had received no instructions from a Magistrate to go into his room, or any written authority—I seized nothing else there but what I have produced—I took other things belonging to him at the office, 4. Pancras-lane, and on his person—I produce the books which I found at the office in Pancras-lane—I am not on intimate terms with the attorney in this case—I never knew him till this trial came on—he did not tell me to seize all Gardiner's papers, or to search his lodging—I said nothing to the attorney about going to the lodging, until after I had been—I did not pro duce all these papers to the attorney, I took some of them to him—I believe I have read them all—I saw the landlady at Gardiner's lodging—she was in the room when I seized the papers.
