   Cross-examined.  The rent was paid by Johnson up to 21st June, and no rent was due till 29th September—he would have 21 days after the 29th to pay it—I do not know he was arrested on the 29th—I do not think he has lost the 21 years' lease by it; it was from year to year—I do not think the 21 years is at his option; he was going to buy the place of us—I negotiated for it to be a shop where they sold beer off and on; that the British Empire Company would not agree to—they allowed beer and wines to be sold off on sufferance, as Bendall did; there was no agreement—I made an agreement with the tenant that he should sell wines and spirits off, but he knew all about it—the British Empire would not have put in an injunction if he had done it as it was before—he never said he was going to sell beer; he said he was going to make it a depot—I contend lie could not put up the beer-engine and sell wines and spirits by this agreement or make it a beerhouse.
   Re-examined.  The British Empire Company had not given me permission to put up a beer-engine or excavate—we have nearly lost our interest in it—they did not consent to draught beer being sold there—I did not explain to Johnson the difference between the two terms; I told him what Bendall sold—it was explained to Johnson he could carry on a grocer's business with the sale of bottled beer. GEORGE JOHN SAYER.
