Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. When an article was delivered
out of your warehouse on an order of this kind, did you book the order to
the prisoner? A. We book it to the party we deliver it to, not to the
prisoner-we did not hold the prisoner responsible for the amount in any
way-he did not receive any commission on sales—we took him on com-
mission in May, but in Nov., from some circumstances, we found it right
to alter the plan, and to give him 31. a-week instead of commission--be
received his 3l. every Saturday night-we began that in the early part of
Dec.-we have paid bim nothing in commission since that, the commis-
sion he had from May to Nov. was fifteen per cent. I will swear he was
not to have ten per cent. after that--the arrangement was made by myself
and partner-there was an arrangement that if the commission on his sales
would have exceeded 1561. per year he was to have it made up in commis.
sion, but his sales were far less than that-there was no time named when
we were to ascertain whether his sales had or had not exceeded 1561.--We
did not contemplate any time, considering that he would hardly make it to
the amount--if he had, he would have been entitled to a commission of
fifteen per cent on the excess of sales-this order was for Thomas East,
Goldsmith-row, Hackney-road, and I presume our carman found Mr. East
at that direction-I was at the police-court-Mr. Price attended from
Messrs. Potter and Collingridge as our solicitor-he called Mr. East, on
the part of the prosecution, but he varied much from what he stated to me
in the first instance-there have repeatedly been complaints made of orders
having been obtained by the prisoner, and the persons afterwards refusing
to take the goods--I have blamed the prisoner for it exceedingly-we never
told him to the effect that if it happened again he should be at the cost of
the articles, nor conveyed that to him-we blamed him for getting fictitious
orders when the people would not take thein in, and he had not seen
the people-we took him last May-we exacted security from him to the
amount of 3001.
Q. Did you receive securities from him to the amount of at least 800h.?
A. Decidedly, from inquiry they are not worth that I should think 3001.
was the outside of the value of them-I believe they were papers connected
with his wife's property, but I have not seen them they are in the possession
of Potter and Collingridge-they made out the deed-I cannot say person-
ally that I have ever seen that deed-my partner has- I have been told
he has been obliged to effect a life assurance to the amount of 2001.-I
have not seen it-very likely my partner has it-1 know the prisoner pays
an insurance, which is to keep us secure against any defalcation of his-we
have been applied to by the prisoner or his solicitor for a statement of our
account, pro and con.-we have not given it, and I can give you a reason
why-after the charge was made against the prisoner, East went at
nine o'clock one night to our foreman's wife-I did not see him, but
I received a communication from our foreman, who is not here-I
have not the least doubt that East offered the money-when we
