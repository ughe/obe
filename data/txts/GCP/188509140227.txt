ALFRED WARD (Police Sergeant). I received information on the morn-
ing of 11th June and made inquiries, and on 7th August, about 1 a.m.,
I saw Dixon at a coffee-stall in Blackfriars Road-I told him he answered
the description of a man I was looking for for a robbery on 10th June-
he said "I think you have made a mistake”- the prosecutor saw him at
the station next day and identified him, not positively, but to the best of
his opinion, as the man who ran off with a darker man on the night of
the robbery-he said on the way to the station "If you will get the
Magistrate to admit me to bail I will take you to the shop where the
locket was sold for 58.; I did not steal it myself, I only ran after Gree to
see what he had got"-on the morning of July 27th I saw Gree at the
police station-I said "I have been looking for you, Gree, some time,"
and told him what he was wanted for-he said " I don't think that is
quite right," but when he was before the Magistrate he pleaded guilty
to the robbery-he was placed with others, and the prosecutor identified
him.
