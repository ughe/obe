 1213. JOHN BAXTER , stealing 1 brass tap, value 3 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of John Beard ; having been before convicted. DANIEL HALEY( policeman, M  181). On 24th May, I was on duty in Kent-street, at a little before three o'clock in the morning—I stopped the pri soner coming out of Hunter-street, and asked what be had in his pockets—he said a pint pot which he found near the Borough-road—I searched his pocket, and found in it three brass taps—this is one—he said he bought them of a man in Blackfriars-road for a shilling—I took him to the station, and went to Mr. Beard's, and showed him this tap—it had been in the yard at the back of his house—I observed some footmarks—I took the prisoner's shoe, and put it into the marks, and it corresponded—I saw the impression before I put the shoe in it. JOHN BEARD. I am a gas lighter—this tap is mine—I saw it safe in the water-butt in my yard on the night before, about nine o'clock, and missed it about four next morning.
   Prisoner.  I bought the three taps of a man in the Blackfriars-road. WILLIAM WEAVERS. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's conviction at Newington—( read—Convicted March, 1850, and confined two months )—I was then a constable, and was present—he is the person. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Twelve Months .

 1214. JOHN WHITE , HENRY PAULTON, and GEORGE WIXCEY , stealing 1 purse, value 6 d  .; and 1 half-crown, 1 shilling, and 1 farthing; the property of Emily Amelia Hunt : Wixcey having been before convicted. EMILY AMELIA HUNT. I am tingle; I live in Manner's-street, Black friars-road. On 19th April, I went into a coffee-house in Waterloo-road, about two o'clock in the moruing—I had a cup of coffee—I sat down by the side of White—I had not known him before—in ten minutes or a quarter of an hour he got up and left the house—I missed my purse, and my pocket was cut—I had had in my purse a half-crown, a shilling, and a farthing—I had it safe when I went into the coffee-house, and paid for the coffee out of it—I spoke to the people of the coffee-house, and then went to the police—I have not got my money or purse since.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  What are you? A.  I have been in service, but I have been very ill the last month or six weeks, and not able to go—I had been to the theatre, and had had a glass of ale—the theatre was over at twelve o'clock—we had a walk—I think it was a little before two when I got to the coffee-house—I was not there longer than half an hour—I had no conversation with White, nor he with me—there were a great many people there; I spoke to none of them—I was not asleep; I was half-dozing—my eyes were shut.
