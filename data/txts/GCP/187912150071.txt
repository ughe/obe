15 years, I had not applied to other loan offices-I cannot say whether I
had, in the year preceding, 'applied to borrow money and been refused
I will not swear that that did not occur in January last-I was not being

Cross-examined by MR. JARVIS. When I paid Kelly the 158, I understood
it was for the expenses of inquiry,
EDWARD LOOKE. I live at 2, Market Place, New Malden, and was a
canvasser, but am now out of employment--in April last I was living at
Bow.--I saw Buer's advertisement in the Daily Telegraph, and went to his
office on the 19th, when I saw Kelly; who passed as Buer; I also saw Airey
--I said I was about to take a situation, and wanted cash security for that
purpose, and required a loan of 401., which Kelly said I could have, and I
was to call again, I think on the next day-I called again within a day or
two, when I saw Airey, who said there would be a guinea to pay as inquiry
fee -I asked him if he would give me a receipt for it, when he said, “If
Mr. Buer were here he would show you the door; we have been established
here for 30 years, and no one has any reason to doubt our honesty in such
matters;" so I paid him the guinea-he said he would communicate with
my father, who is a fishmonger and poulterer, and whose name I had pre-
viously given him as a surety—my father afterwards gave me this letter.
(Read: "19, London Bridge Station Approach, opposite the booking office
at London Bridge Railway-station. April 18th, 1879, Re loan. Sir, You
have been proposed as surety for Mr. E. Locke, jun. I must trouble you
to correctly fill in and return the enclosed form at your earliest convenience,
and oblige yours faithfully, H. W. Buer."). After I had seen my father I
went to see Kelly, and asked him why he did not tell me it would be
necessary to have a bill of sale on my father's furniture, as required by the
form enclosed, as if I had known that I should not have attempted to have
anything to do with it, and he said, “We cannot afford to give you or your
father 402.”—I believe I asked him for my money back again, and he said
he would look over my account and would let me have a bill of it in a few
days I had the bill, saying he had looked it over and found there was 38. 9d.
to his account-I never got the loan or my money back-I threw the letter
in the fire and thought no more of it.
