688. GEORGE TRENT, stealing 1 book, value 78.; the goods of Robert
Riviere, his master.
ROBERT RIVIERE. I am a book binder, in Great Queen-street. The pris
soner was in my service for twenty months or two years he left in the begin.
ning of Jan.-on 25th Jan. I saw this book produced) at Mr. Stibbs, in
the Strand-it was one which I had care of for a Miss Levy-it was sent to
be bound in 1842-I had not missed it-I am positive of it-it must bave
been in my possession at the close of 1845, because I built some workshops
and this book was stowed away there-I produce a tool which fits the orna.
ments in the corners- I bound it myself-8 man robbed me formerly, but it
was in my possession after he left-I know I moved it when the worksbop
was completed,
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. How long did you find the
book after the prisoner left? 1. Three weeks or a month - I saw it acei
dentally-I buy books there-it has my own peculiar tooling on it-I cut
away the bottom part of this tool, to make the design to put on this par
ticular book-they tally exactly I have never used the tool since the
prisoner worked for me in 1843 or 1844, not in 1842–he left, returned
in 1846, and remained till Dec. 1847..
ALEXANDER ROBERTSON. I am a bookseller, at Queen's-place, Great
Queen-street. Two or three months ago Shenton brought me this book-1
sold it to Mr. Stibbs for 78., for Shenton, and gave him the money.
Cross-examined. Q. Is Stibbs here? 4. No; he lives at 831, Strand
I did not keep the book a quarter of an hour.
EDWARD SHENTON. I live in Green-street, Blackfriars. This book w
brought to me by the prisoner about two years ago, or rather more-I gave
him 78. 6d. or 108. for it-I knew him as a customer three or four years before
I had had it six or eight months; I had to make up some money, and
pledged it, I afterwards got it out, and gave it to Robertson to sell for me,
not quite three months ago he gave me 78. for it.
Cross-examined. Q. Why did not you sell it yourself? A. Because he
was in the trade, and knew more about it than I did - I was not to give
him anything-I did not sell any more-he has sold books for me before—it has
been used considerably-I always thought the prisoner a respectable person.
WILLIAM Pocock. I took the prisoner-I asked if he had been in Ms
Riviere's service-he said “Yes”-I said he was charged with stealing
book from his premises--I received this book from Mr. Riviere.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
NOT GUILTY.
