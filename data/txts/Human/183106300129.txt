 1473.  THOMAS ROACH was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of May , 1 jacket, value 5s., and 1 pair of stockings, value 1s., the goods of Henry Hart.
   HENRY SOUTHAM. I am a shopman to Henry Hart, who keeps a clothes-shop. On the 14th of May the prisoner and a man, who called himself his father, came to the shop - I had seen the prisoner on board ship before; they asked Hart to trust them with a pair of shoes and stockings- he took down the father's address, and during that time the prisoner went away with the stockings and shoes; I then missed a jacket and another pair of stockings, which were not sold - the prisoner came back; I said, "You have stolen a jacket;" he said he had not - I afterwards found the prisoner at the station-house.
   EDWARD STERN. I am a shopman to Mr. Hart. I stood at the door, and after he had served the prisoner I saw him - go out with the jacket under his arm, but did not know it was stolen - he returned in ten minutes; I said,"Why did you not bring the jacket back which I saw under your arm?" he denied it - I said, "Who did you give the shoes and stockings to?" he said, to his brother; I found his brother with the jacket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 GUILTY. Aged 19. -    Judgment Respited.

 1474.  MARY DIXON was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of June , 1 shawl, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Nichols.
   JOSEPH HUETSON. I am in the employ of Thomas Nichols, a pawnbroker, in Gray's-Inn lane. A person passing alarmed me, and I missed this shawl from the door, which I had seen safe a quarter of an hour before, pinned strongly up - I went out and found the prisoner with it folded in her apron, about twenty yards off; she said she was sorry for it.
 BENJAMIN PHILLIPS. I am a Policeman. I saw Huetson stop the prisoner and take the shawl from her apron - she said distress had caused her to do it.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY Aged 48.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Two days.
 1475.  JOSEPH PERKINS was indicted for stealing on the 18th of May , 1 coat, value 2l.; 1 bag, value 1s., and 1 handkerchief, value 2d., the goods of George Whitworth; and that he had been previously convicted of felony.
   GEORGE WHITWORTH. I work at a stable in Clarencegardens, Regent's-park; I lodge on the second floor there. I have worked with the prisoner once or twice, at groundwork - he knew where I lived; I was out of employ, and went to Epsom Races on Monday, leaving my coat and things at my lodging, in care of my wife - I returned on the Friday night, and they were gone; I never sent the prisoner for them.
   ANN ELIZABETH WHITWORTH. I am the prosecutor's wife. He went to Epsom; the prisoner came on the Wednesday morning and told me he came from my husband, who had sent him for his coat, as he had come to town for some horses, that he had to go further, and would call again in the afternoon - he came in the afternoon, and asked me if the coat was ready; I fetched it down, and gave it to him in a bag - the handkerchief was in the pocket.
   ROBERT DIXON. I am a Policeman. On the 18th of May the prosecutor gave the prisoner into my charge, for stealing a coat, handkerchief, and bag - he said he knew nothing about it, and in about two hours he said he had sold the coat for 6s., and it was gone far enough away; he said next morning that he had pawned it at the corner of East-street, Manchester-square - I found it there.
   JAMES MOODY. I am a servant to James Ross, a pawnbroker. I took this coat in pawn from the prisoner, for 8s., on the 18th or 19th of May; and on the 21st he brought a man to buy it - a few hours after a second and third came to look at it - I have had it ever since; the handkerchief was pawned with it.
 JURY. Q.Did you ever see him before? A. Never; he said he had been to the races, and wanted some money to take him back.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. If I had not been in liquor I should not have done it.
 THOMAS FERGUSSON. I know the prisoner. I saw him tried here in October, 1827 - he was found guilty.
   RICHARD HARTLY WALL. I am an officer. I have got a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, from Mr. Clarke's office. - (read.)
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1476.  EDWARD FITZGERALD was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of May , 1 blanket, value 3s., and 1 sheet, value 2s., the goods of James Power.
   MARGARET POWER. I am the wife of James Power. The prisoner lodged in our first floor back-room, with his two children; he came as a widower, and worked as a plasterer- he was there about nineteen months, and owed me nearly 7l.; he was out of work a long time - on the 22nd of May I asked him for some rent, as he was in work; he said I could have none that day - I said I thought my things were not all right, and while I was examining, he went out; his children followed - I missed a blanket and sheet; I never allowed him to pawn them.
   JOSEPH HUETSON. I am a pawnbroker. On the 9th of May the prisoner pawned this sheet for 1s.; I knew him before - the blanket was pawned at our other shop, but we cannot find it; we believe it has been redeemed.
   DANIEL HUMPHREYS. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner; he said he had burnt the duplicates of the blanket and sheet - I found ten duplicates on him; one of them related to the sheet.
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
 GUILTY. Aged 45.
 Recommended to Mercy. -    Judgment-Respited.
