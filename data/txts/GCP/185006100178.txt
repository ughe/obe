William VILLMOWATH. I superintend the colour-department of Thomas
Bradram and others, at Rotherhithe. On 5th Feb., I directed the prisoner
to go to be loaded-between eight and nine o'clock Witted came to me
respecting this keg of lead--after the conversation I followed the van with
Witted, and saw the keg produced by the prisoner-when I first began to
follow the van, it was going up the field towards the road it was in sight of
where I was- I followed it just outside the gates of the factory-it was
stopped at the public-house, a few yards from the premises - we went up to
the van, and Witted said, "I want that keg of lead you have in your van"
the prisoner hesitated a short time, and then lifted up the tarpaulin and
produced it, and placed it on one of his casks of white lead I said, “Moss,
this is a very bad job"-he said, "It is," and he wished to return to the
factory and wait till Mr. Andrew Bradram came-as there were a crowd of
persons come up, I and the prisoner went into the public-house-I came out
to get one of the witnesses to bring the keg in-the prisoner followed me out
in a leisurely manner, and very shortly afterwards I missed him-I went to
the corner of a street a few yards from the spot, and saw him running as fast
as he could-the van was put into the care of another carman--the prisoner
did not return to the factory--I did not see him till the 17th of May, at the
police-court at Greenwich-he had been one of the regular carmen, and been
there nearly nine years.
