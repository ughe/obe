 Prisoner's Defence (written.) On Monday, the 16th of August, I was in bed about ten o'clock at night, when my mistress came to my bed-room door, and desired me to get up - my master followed her, and insisted on my getting up, as he had missed a sovereign; they searched my pocket, and found nothing in it but an old purse - they then searched my work bag; in it was a pair of black silk stockings that I had washed and mended, but had omitted taking them up stairs; in my box was a waist ribbon I had purchased in the shop of my master after I had been there about a month, a black handkerchief I had bought in the country, a small ganze scarf I gave 1s. 3d. for in Charles-street, Soho-square, all of which they have sworn to as their property, as well as a collar I had given to me by a friend, and was in pledge, with some other things, for 10s.; my master and mistress both said if I would say where the sovereign was they would not send for an officer, but pay me my wages, and I should return to my friends; next morning, when they searched the shop, the sovereign was found on the floor; though they had found it, they sent next morning for an officer.
 SAMUEL HARRISON. I told her if she did not tell me where the sovereign was, I would send for an officer; she then came down into the shop, and took it up from a corner, as she pretended, but I think she had it in her mouth; she brought me down 10s., which she said she had picked up, but I do not think she had; I had frequently missed money out of the till.
 Prisoner. I took the lamp into the shop to have oil in it- I saw the sovereign lay in the shop; I touched it with my foot, but not with my hands - I thought it did not lay in my way, and I did not meddle with it; they called me up, and took my key - my mistress unlocked my box, and searched it; I told them I saw the sovereign in the shop - my mistress took it up, and put it into my master's hand.
   SAMUEL HARRISON re-examined. Q. At what time did she produce the sovereign? A. In about half an hour; she said her mistress would not know where to find it.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 FIFTH DAY. TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 16.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1783.  WILLIAM DYSON was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , 1 coat, value 3s., the goods of John Fish.
 JOHN FISH. I am a  glazier, and live in Bishopsgate. On the 27th of June I left my coat in a house at Hackneygrove, where I was at work; I went out at twelve o'clock, to dinner, and when I returned it was gone - I saw it again in a few days, when the prisoner was in custody; he had been working on the premises as a labouring gardener.
   ROBERT FISH. I am the prosecutor's brother, and was working in the house. On Saturday, the 28th of June, I saw the prisoner in Sun-street, Bishopsgate, with my brother's coat on; I spoke to him for some minutes, and a person came by - I collared him, and asked that person to assist me; the prisoner said it was all nonsense - we had a scuffle, and he ran away; I pursued, and cried Stop thief! he was taken by Sapwell.
   CATHERINE DOGGET. I was servant at No. 7, Hackney-grove, next door to the house the coat was stolen from. I saw the prisoner on the 27th of June, come out of that house with a flag basket and a bundle; he hung the basket on the pales, and then got over and took it away; I believe the workmen were gone to dinner - it was between twelve and one o'clock.
   THOMAS SAPWELL. On Saturday, the 28th of June, I was at tea, between four and five o'clock; I went out, and saw the prisoner running down Lamb-alley - I pursued, and took him; Robert Fish came up, and said he had got his brother's coat on; I said, "How do you know it is your brother's?" he said, "If you look on the right shoulder you will see it is sewed, which it is; the prisoner said he bought it in Petticoat-lane.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was returning from the Londondocks, and went to Petticoat-lane; I bought this coat, and exchanged my own old one; I then went up Sun-street, and stopped Robert Fish; I talked to him a little while, when he said I had got his brother's coat on; I said it was all nonsense, and walked away - he called Stop thief! and I ran, not wishing to be locked up all day on Sunday.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Months.
 1784.  THOMAS CARTWRIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of George Witt.
 GEORGE WITT. I am  in the service of the East India Company. On the 25th of July I was on the Paddington stage, opposite Regent-crescent; I felt a jerk at my pocket, looked round, and saw a boy getting down from behind the coach; I felt my pocket, and my handkerchief was gone; I saw the same boy stopped - I lost sight of him in getting down from the coach; I then saw him running, and several persons pursuing him; I think I should know him again; the handkerchief was found in the crescent, which he had passed; a girl who was drawing some children in a chaise threw it to me.
   SAMUEL WITT. I was with my brother on the coach on the 25th of July; he clapped his hand to his pocket, and called out Stop thief! I turned round, and saw the prisoner running away - he threw the handkerchief over the pales; I lost sight of him while I got off the coach, but I observed him enough to be certain that the prisoner is the boy.
   RICHARD HARTLY WALL. I am an officer. I produce the handkerchief, which Mr. Witt gave me; I found 1s. 6d., a knife, and a tin box on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going home to my dinner, and running to make haste, I heard the cry of Stop thief! one gentleman was trying to get over the rails, and a girl threw the handkerchief over the rails; I know nothing of it.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Fourteen Years.
