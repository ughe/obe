   Prisoner.  She did not accuse me; a gentleman came to me and asked if I knew Mr. Gainsford, I said "Yes;" he asked if I had been to him last week or the week before; I said, "No;" he asked if I knew Mrs. Kent; I said, "Yes;" he said, "Have you heard she has lost two 10 l . notes?" I said, "No;" he said, "You are suspected of taking them;" I said, "I did not;" he said, "I shall call a policeman, for you don't seem willing to confess, and I shall have a cab  and take you;" he then said, he would come on Monday, and make me confess more than I was disposed to; I told him; I would confess at the Court; Mrs. King never mentioned the subject to me; the gentleman said, I should be made to confess at the Old Bailey; I denied ever having touched the money; Mrs. King said, "Why are you such a fool Clara, why don't you confess it, think of your children?" she said if I made 10 l . good to her it should be all secret, and I should hear no more about it; I said, if I sold all I had in my house I would not take it. SAMUEL VINCENT PRICE PHILLIPS. I am a clerk in the Bank of Eng land—on 20th Oct. I paid Mrs. King fourteen 10 l . notes, Nos. 10855 to 10868, dated 10th Sept. 1847—the Bank never issue two notes of the same number and date for the same amount—Mrs. Kent afterwards came for the numbers of the notes—two of them were 10867 and 10868—they were paid in through Barclay's Bank, on 27th Oct. GEORGE ASHMORE. I am shopman to Messrs. Gainsford and Good, linendrapers, of High-street, Southwark—this note, No. 10867, has my writing on it—the prisoner paid it me on 25th Oct.—I was not certain before the Magistrate, but now I recollect the circumstance perfectly well, and what she bought—she is the person—she bought a black Ottoman scarf, price 2 l . 10 s ., and a wool shawl, 17 s . 6 d ., and I gave her 6 l . 12 s . 6 d . change—the amount is put down—she gave her name Mrs. Anderson, Clapham-park, but did not give me the number—she appeared rather confused—I wrote her name on the note.
   Prisoner.  You could not swear to me at Kennington-lane. Witness.  I recollected you after taxing my memory—I have served a good many people since—I did not Like to destroy you unless I was sure. JOHN HAMBLIN( policeman, P  18.) I took the prisoner—she said some thing to me in going to the station, and I took her to Messrs. Gainsford and Good's—she pointed out Mr. Ashmore, and he was taken before the Magis trate—in consequence of what the prisoner said, I went to Mr. Bremming ton's, at Clapham, and also to 6, Bedford-row, a respectable window's house, where I found this shawl and a scarf—( produced. )
   MR. ASHMORE re-examined.  This is the scarf I sold to the party who changed the 10 l . note, and this shawl is of the same pattern and value, but our mark is taken off.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I gave 10 s . for the shawl, at Mr. Gainsford's, in July; that was the last time I was there. GUILTY . Aged 35.— Confined One Year .
