Wullum TOAKLEY. I am foroman of the carpenters in the employ of
Nors Webb. I remember seeing the prisoner and Charles Butler I Saw
the prisoner unbutton his waistcoat, and Butler took a quantity of lead
from his waistcoat.

DeadA. He did not say any thing to him in my hearing the prisoner
went on his knees and begged to be let go-Butler told him to go away as

were the words, as near as I can recollect.
SANUEL ROBERT BISHOP. I am clerk in the prosecutor's employ. I saw
the lead that was taken from the prisoner-I had seen it on the 22nd of Sep-
tember, in the cellar of the premises--this is the property of Messrs. Webb
--it was afterwards produced to me.
Grorex Webb. I am a builder, and live in St John's lane, Clerkenwell,
I have two partners. The prisoner was in our employ-we have three
of four hundred men-there is a great deal of lead used by us-1 had
missed a quantity, and had given directions to some of the workmen to
I look out for the depredators--one of the foremen bad appointed the pri-
soner to watch the lead is worth about 8s, or 10s.-I saw the prisoner
about a fortnight after the 22nd of September he came to our oflice
drunk, or nearly so, he went down on his knees, and begged to be forgiven,
and acknowledged that he had taken the lead.

JOHN CHARLES BRINE. I keep the Saxe Coburg publie-house in the
Kent-road. When my attention was drawn to this I missed some pots-
there two are mine.
JOSEPH Joun LEWIS. I am a Thames police-officer. I saw the prisoner
enter a marine-store shop, in Mint-street, Borough, with a bundle-1 fol-
lowed him, and said, "What have you got here?"-he pointed to a woman,
and said, "This belongs to you"- I said, "That won't do for me; let me
See what they are "-I found eight pots, with the bottoms and handles cut
off-I took him into custody.
