WILLIAM BOWERMAN( City police-constable, No.  249.) I produce the shilling which I received from Mrs. Chandler on the 16th of June. JOHN LAYTON. I am a victualler, and live in the Old Bailey. On the 17th of June, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my house and asked for three-halfpenny worth of gin, and threw down a half-crown—I saw it was bad, and asked if she had got any more money—she said no—I said, "Then don't drink the gin, for that won't do"—she said she had a penny, and would have a pennyworth—I kept the half-crown in my hand, and asked where she lived:—she said, "In Black Horse-court"—I repeatedly asked if she had any more money, she said no—after a few minutes she went away—I followed her into Farringdon-street, and gave her into custody, and gave the half-crown to Harding—I knew her before. EDWARD HAKDING( City police-constable, N , 274.) The prisoner was given into my charge by Mr. Layton, who gave me this half-crown, which I produce—I found nothing on her. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—this half-crown and shilling ire counterfeit in all respects, and are both made of Britannia metal.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was very much intoxicated, and did not know what I was doing. EDWARD HARDING re-examined.  She was not much in liquor. JOHN LAYTON re-examined.  She was not much in liquor—she seemed to know what she was about. GUILTY.* Aged 27.— Confined Twelve Months.
