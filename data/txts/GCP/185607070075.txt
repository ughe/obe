Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. Q. When did you make any communi-
estion first to the police ? A. I think it was about January, it was shortly
after the meeting at the lodge Towers is my wife's brother, and Horwood
married my wife's sister-I have not known Horwood above two years; I
have known Towers a longer time I have been convicted; a quart pot
was bent one day, and I took it out of the house, and was going to throw
it over a wall-there was nobody on the other side to catch it it was con-
sidered that I stole the pot, and I was convicted, and had six months, that
is eleven years ago. I have never been convicted before that or since, but
have got an honest living-I am a plumber, painter, and glazier-I work
for customers whom I have, respectable people in Jan. last I had many
oustomers, I was at work. I know the policeman Langdon by his being a
policeman-I never was a witness for him in any case, nor has my father to
my knowledge-nobody took me to this lodge meeting at the King's Arms,
I went by myself ; there is a meeting every week, it is the Loyal United
Friends--I saw Fleckart there, he came with Horwood, I did not see
them come in, but I should naturally suppose they came together, they were
acquaintances-I know that, because Horwood introduced Fleckart to me
as his friend on that evening-Horwood is a member there were a few
people in front of the bar whom I knew, being members of the society-1
told the policeman what Horwood told me, and I told the Magistrate-I
told several policemen at the time--I shook my head at Horwood, I thought
it was a shameful thing for him to endeavour to do such a thing I thought
it was very imprudent to talk openly of such matters ; I thought, as an
honest man, that it was very imprudent of him-I did not think it might
interrupt the harmony of the meeting the robbery was committed in Feb.
or March - I hardly knew where Mrs. Humphreys' house was, I know
now; I did not know exactly where it was situated in Jan.--I heard of it
first by Horwood telling me that his wife had robbed Mrs. Humphreys
while she lived with her, and that he intended to have some more of her
money– I first heard of the robbery when I was told by a policeman at
Hoxton- I cannot say exactly whether that was in Feb.-I did not tell
that policeman of the conversation at the lodge-I had told other policemen
before that, one named McNallen and one named Jefferys, that it was their
intention to rob the house; I told them that in Jan., twice or three times
-I mean to say that I told those two policemen in January that Horwood
and another man intended to rob Mrs. Humphreys' house-I did not tell
Langton, I did not happen to meet with him nor Bovis I have not seen a
single policeman here that I told I think it was a man named Watts that
stated that the house was robbed, I am almost certain; he knew that I had
told the police beforehand that it was their intention to rob this house-I
know that because he told me so-he told me that the house had been
robbed; I suppose that was because he had heard, very likely he might
have heard it I had not told him at the conversation at the King's Arms
I am not certain that Watts did tell me; it was either a man named
Watts or Jefferys I do not know what made them tell me.
