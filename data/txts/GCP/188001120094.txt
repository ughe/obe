Trustort, Mayor.
THOMAS GARNETT. My partner is dead, and I am now the firm of
Gillies, Garnett, and Co., stuff merchants, of Bradford-I never knew John
Keighley, or that the firm had business transactions with him, but this bill
is dated 10th October; it is due 13th December--it is a two months' bill
for 1841. ls, by us and accepted by Keighley and Brother-it was not paid
when it fell due--I had heard of Michell from Mr. Ramsden before we drew
the first bill, and a day or two after it was due Ramsden and Michell came
-Michell had announced his arrival two or three days previously, and I
expected him-when he came in I said “What is the reason that this bill
is protested ?"-he said “Mr. Keighley has mismanaged his affairs; I have
been looking into them; I find him perfectly solvent, and I intend to take
up that portion of the business, and I intend to introduce an amount of
capital which will make the concern perfectly right, and I have come to ask
you if you will grant us an extension of time, so that it will make it cons
venient for us and perfectly safe for you; I shall put 4,0001. or 5,0001, into
the business, and your bill will be met when it becomes due " I said If
you have 4,0001. or 5,0001. why do you allow the credit of your intended
concern to be blasted by having these paltry sums of 1001. coming due and
not being met -he said “I am entitled under & will to this money; the
trustees have a little difficulty," or "there is some little technicality about
it; they cannot get it for a few weeks, and if you will give us” or “me
that time it will be ample, and the money will be paid "-he did not say
“I'm a virtual partner;" he said "I am intending to be a partner, and I
will hold myself responsible for Keighley and Brother's liability as far as
you are concerned "-I said " I hope the arrangement will be carried out;
and that it may continue to lead to further business"-I agreed that there
was to be a new bill from December 1, but it was not drawn, and two or
three days afterwards I received this letter: "London, 23/12/78.-Dear
Sir,-The arrangement made with Mr. Michell when he saw you in Brad-
ford we shall be glad if you will carry out, but we should prefer if you
would renew for three months." A bill was sent with a letter in reply to
that with an explanation of what was required, and it was sent back with
Michell's name on the back. (This was for 1851. 168. 8d. Accepted, J.
Keighley and Brother. Endorsed, F. Michell.) I made that new bill
entirely upon Michell's representation that the goods represented by the
first bill had been sold for cash–I have recbived 18. in the pound from
Keighley's estate through the Bankruptcy Court-I never saw Michell again
till I saw him here yesterday--that was the single transaction in which Michell
was concerned.
Cross-examined. The conversation was on a market day about 20th
December; I have no memorandum of the data-I wrote a letter as to the
nature of our case I think between July and December last-Michell said
between 4,0001. and 5,0001., but I understood that Mr. Ramsden said before
the Magistrate that Michell said between 3,0001. and 4,0001.--I told Mr.
Ramsden that what he said to me was between 4,0001. and 5,0001., and
that I should state that that was the fact I am prepared to swear it-I
believe our cashier can confirm it-we have a great inany people in and out
of the office on market days.
