WALTER Mason Hill, 29, Friar Street, East Ham, bricklayer, and
Seoretary of the Commercial Road branch. Prisoner was appoint.d
treasurer of the branch on October 6, 1906, and was given the
traasurer's books and a bag. He rendered a true account at the
audit on January 3, 1907. I sent him notice of the audit on April 3,
at 8 p.m., held at the Hayfield Tavern, Mile End Road. Prisoner
did not attend, but his wife came with the bag and the books and
£6 15s. 6d. I knew from my books, and also from his own, that the
balance should have been £26 8s. 10d. He sent also two letters to
me: "Dear Walter,- I cannot attend the audit to-night as I have
not got the cash balance. I cannot see you to-night as I am too ill.
I hope you will not try to see me. I will write to you to-morrow,
I have only £6 15s 60. left, but I hope to make it good next week.-
Fred. You will find everything in the bing" The other letter is:
"Dear Walter,--I lost my bag about three weeks ago containing
about £11, and since then I have suffered something terrible, and
rather than any more of it I had better be dead and out of the way
I hope you will try and do your best for me for old times sake-
Fred." (To the Judge.) He told me nothing about the loss of the
Dog. There is a weekly meeting, and he would take home the money
collected. If he had come and reported the loss of the bag the
ciety might have given him time to pay. It would be gross care
Jessness. He is not bound to use the bag for the money; it is for
the purpose of carrying the books. We have received nothing from
the prisoner beyond the £6 15s. 6d.
