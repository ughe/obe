 Cross-examined by BARRY. Q. Was he in military clothes? A. Yes; I am sure he is the man - we do not very often have soldiers call for cheques; I do not recollect that I had seen any soldier for some days before, but it is not uncommon.
 COURT. Q. How came a recruiting draft payable at your house? A. We do it on the faith of the officer who draws it.
   THOMAS ROBINSON. I am a serjeant in the Coldstream Guards - I was not at Leeds at this time; I know the prisoner, and have seen him write frequently; the body of this draft is the prisoner's writing - I do not know whose the signature is; I cannot say whether the writing on the back of it is his.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long has he been in the regiment? A. About fourteen years - he had generally a good character, but he deserted in October last.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Then if Captain Hope had given him drafts before that, would he have ceased to do so after he had deserted? A.Certainly; if the name of  James Compton on the back of this draft is the prisoner's writing, it is disguised.
   ALFRED WILSON. I am clerk to Cox, Greenwood and Co. This draft was presented from Brown, Janson and Co., and I paid it on the 5th of January; I concluded that the name James Hope on it was genuine.
 Cross-examined. Q. Can you say why it should be presented and paid at Leeds? A. I believe it is a very common thing for persons to go to bankers in the country, and get cash for drafts.
 THE  HON. CAPTAIN JAMES HOPE. I am a captain in the army, and lieutenant in the Coldstream Guards. The body of this draft is certainly the prisoner's writing; the signature I do not know - it is not my writing.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is the name like your writing? A. It is certainly an imitation - I am adjutant of the regiment, and sign drafts for recruiting parties; I believe the person who wrote this  James Compton, wrote this James Hope.
 MR. COLLINS. I saw the prisoner write this  James Compton- he apologized and said, he was a bad writer.

JAMES HALL and THOMAS STUBBS were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the warehouse of Edward Stubbs and another, on the 4th of April , and stealing 11 coach-springs, value 22s.; 33 cushions, value 33s.; 3 gallons of japan, value 30s., and 4 carriage-linings, value 4s., their property.
   EDWARD STUBBS. I am a coach-maker, and live at No. 244, Whitechapel-road; I am in partnership with Mr. John Dickson Hancock. On the 4th of April I saw the prisoners in custody of a person in George-yard, which belongs to me and my partner - I gave charge of them; I saw some coach-springs laying about the ground, and some cushions, and varnish in various places on the premises in different parts to where they had been in our warehouse - the hasp of the warehouse lock was broken off.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. How long before had you been in your warehouse in George-yard? A. We do not often go there - I cannot say that I had been there for several weeks, but if it had been in that state, I should have heard of it.
   EDMUND STUART. I am in the prosecutor's employ; I live at No. 20, Oxford-street, Whitechapel. On the 4th of April, between six and seven o'clock in the morning, I went to the warehouse; I put the key into the lock, and it turned round - I felt some obstruction against it - I pushed it away, and got in; while I was getting some timber, which I wanted, I heard some footsteps - I looked up, and saw a man going towards the gate; I called out; "Who are you? and what do you want?" he went out - I went into the yard, and on returning to the premises again, I saw the two prisoners; I called for assistance, and they were taken; there is only one gate to the premises, which is the one I went in at; some of these articles were taken from below, and some were up stairs; the lock of the gate was not broken, but the staple was broken off; I saw eleven springs on the ground, which had been taken from carriages; I had seen them on the Monday before, and this was on the Thursday; we found thirty-two or thirty-three cushions in different parts, and the linings of four of the carriages appeared to have been cut; I saw a quantity of japan in an iron pot, which had been put there from a stone bottle and a carboy - this screw-wrench was found in the yard.
 Cross-examined. Q. How must they have got in? A. I did not see them get in, but they must have got over a wall, or the gate, twelve or fourteen feet high; the prisoners were taken on the spot - two men came to my assistance.
 Stubbs. Q. Where did you find the holdfast of the door? A. It was hanging down on one screw; I did not tell you the door had been forced, and show you the holdfast on the post.
 MR. STUBBS. I saw the cushions and springs; they were mine and my partner's.
   THOMAS OWEN(Polic-constable H 40). I received information, and found the prisoners on the premises - I have a hand-screw, a crow-bar, and a picklock, which were given to me by Mr. Henry Stubbs.
 HENRY STUBBS. I found these things in the right-hand corner of the yard.
 Cross-examined. Q. When did you find them? A. On the 4th of April, which was the day the prisoners were taken; I did not produce them on the first examination - I was not there; I did on the second examination.
