1332. SAMUEL BRICE BADGERY, stealing 18 cwt. of potatoes; the
goods of William James Nokes, his master. 2nd COUNT : the goods of the
Great Northern Railway Company,
MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM JAMEs NOKES. I live at 5, South-street, Spitalfields, and am a
potato-salesman. At the latter end of May received a communication
from Messrs. Cox, of Doddington, near Spalding, and some potatoes arrived
by the Great Northern Railway—I told the prisoner, who was in my
employ, to go and put the potatoes, which were loose in the trucks, into
sacks, but he had no business to remove them off the railway premises—he
never brought me any ; if he took any away, they have not come to me—
the potatoes are about 18 cwt. short by the bill of parcels.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You do not know how many potatoe
there were ? A. No.
JOSEPH FRESHWATER. I am clerk in the goods-omce at the King'*-cross
Station of the Great Northern Railway. I produce two invoices, marked
197 and 1247—on the same day that we received them, •e received 12 ton,
14 cwt. 2 quarters of potatoes, the quantity mentioned in tbe invoice, but I
did not compare them—they are weighed at the country-station, and the
invoice gent up—I communicated with Mr. Nokes on 3rd June—a man, I
cannot say who, brought me an order from Mr. Nokes, which entitled him to
remove them.
WILLIAM FRosr. I am Mr. Nokes's foreman. I remember the prisoner
being sent with the carter about the potatoes—he had no right to bring any
away, only to sack them.
Cross-examined. Q. You cannot ascertain the missing of a few sacks from
the stock ? A. No.
JOHN AKcocE. I am a porter, in the service of the Great Northern
Railway Company. On 3rd June I received these two delivery-notes (pro•
duced) from the prisoner, who had another man with him—I pointed out
three trucks of potatoes to them as those referred to in the delivery-order,
and they put them into sacks, loaded them into Patrick's van, and took
those away—the prisoner came again on the 4th, with a boy, and took about
eight sacks more away—the boy drove the cart—I gave information.
NOT GUILTY.

RICHARD JAMES TODMAN (Thames-policeman, 65). On 15th June I was
passing Mr. Green's Repairing-dock, at Blackwall, and saw the prisoner on

copper, and a hammer—I asked the prisoner where he got them ; he made
no reply—on the way to the station, he said the chap that was with him
asked him to get some copper—there was another chap with him,
who escaped.
