My daughter, Elizabeth, lives with me—the pri soner is my son—he did not live with me, he used to come sometimes to see me—he came on Sunday, the 11th April—he used always to behave very kindly to me when he was sober, but when he is drunk he is very outrageous at times—he does not often get drunk—he was sober when he came, but went out, and at a quarter to six came back drunk—I and my daughter were there drinking tea—I got up to let him sit down—he said I did not act as a mother to get up to let her child sit down, that I ought to let him get a chair himself—he asked me if I would take anything to drink, and said he would fetch me something—I said I had much better not, but if he would give me a pint of beer for my supper I would much rather—he seemed very angry at that, and upset my table with the tea-things on it—I took up the poker, and said if he did not go out of my place I would make him—I did that because, if he sees me frightened when he is tipsy, it makes him think worse of it—I did not do anything with the poker, but said I would use it if he did not leave the room—I saw a knife in his hand during the transaction—I do not think that was before I caught hold of the poker, but I was so agitated at the time that I do not Know; but when I saw it, I laid hold of his arm, and told him to drop the knife—I cut my thumb and finger very slightly—they bled, but not
