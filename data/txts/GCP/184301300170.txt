ELIZA WILLIS. I am servant to Mr. Hill. He went out that evening-
he left me and my mistress at home-about eight o'clock I went out for the
supper beer-when I came back I missed a fender from the window-I will
not be sure that it was safe when I went out.
HENRY Wood (police-sergeant K 44.) On the evening of the 24th of Jan.
I was on duty in Ropemaker's-fields, Limehouse-I saw the prisoner with
this fender under his arm-I asked where he got it-he said he bought it at a
broker's, named Burns, in the Highway, for 23., and he was going to take it
to his sister's I said he must go with me to the shop-he walked about two
hundred yards, and then made a cut at my legs with the fender-it got between
his own legs-he tripped, and ran away-I fell down-he turned into a court,
which was no thoroughfare, and I caught him.
Prisoner. I said I bought it of a man named Burns, I did not say a
broker. Witness. He said a broker, named Burns-I knew there was no
such person.
Prisoner's Defence. I do not know where Burns lives, but it is in Poplar,
Guilty. Aged 18.-Confined Two Months.
752. JAMES KENNY was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of January, 1
pistol, value 43.; and 2 handkerchiefs, 38.; the goods of John Painter.
John PAINTER. I am a butcher, and live in Pierpoint-row, Islington. I
lost a black silk handkerchief similar to this one, but I cannot swear to it-
missed it last Sunday morning week, from an apartment in a house I sleep in
- I live in one house and sleep in another-I went down to the landlady, and
told her-I then looked for another handkerchief, and missed that-that hand-
kerchief is here now, and I can speak to it-I also lost a small pistol, similar to
this one, but I cannot swear to this, I cannot tell whether I lost these things
at one time-I did not miss the pistol till I heard the prisoner was appre-
hended-1 had seen him about the premises the last time I saw him was on
the Saturday.
THOMAS FIELD. I live in Adelaide-square, Islington. I saw the prisoner
in the evening of this day fortnight-he asked me to buy this black silk hand-
kerchief of him-I did not want it, but he came after me again, and I bought
it-I afterwards took it to the station.
Prisoner. This lad was here for stealing a quartern loaf, and had three
months in the House of Correction. Witness. Yes, I had.
JAMES Cook. I am shopman to Mr. Goodburn, a pawnbroker. I pro-
duce this other handkerchief, which was pawned by the prisoner-I am sure
of that.
GUILTY. Aged 16.-Confined Three Months.
753. GEORGE LESTER was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of Jan.,
4 planes, value 6s.; 3 saws, 58.; 1 augur, 18.; 1 screw-driver, 1s. ; 1 chi-
sel, 18.; and 1 square, 28.; the goods of John Pearce.
THOMAS PEARCE. I live with my father, John Pearce, at Shepherd's
Bush ; he is a carpenter, and is finishing the house where we are residing; the
prisoner is a plasterer, and was employed there by my father-we had some
tools, which I put into a box, on the 20th of Jan., which was put into the next
room to the one we live in there were four planes, and the other articles
stated in the indictment in it-these are the tools -- they are my father's.
Prisoner. I removed them from the kitchen up to that room. Witness.
No, I took them up myself.
