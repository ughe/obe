 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You did not see whether the young man gave the prisoner the lead? A. No.
   JAMES TOGG. I saw the prisoner come, on Wednesday night, to hire the truck; I had known him as pot-boy at the Three Crowns public-house, Mile-end-road.
   JOHN BROWN. I saw the lead at the watch-house; to the best of my knowledge, it was the same I had worked some of for Mr. Marshall - there are no marks on it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it fixed? A. No - it was lying in the skittle-ground; if I saw it at a distance, I could not swear to it.
   ROBERT CHRISTIAN. I saw the lead brought to the watch-house; I showed it to Brown, and to Mr. Marshall.
   GEORGE HENFREY. I took the prisoner from the skittle-ground, at Mr. Marshall's, the same evening; Marshall told me to come down, between six and seven o'clock- I went, and saw the prisoner; Mr. Dyke said he was one he saw with the truck - I charged him with it; he said he knew nothing about it - Mr. Dyke said he was certain he was one; he said he was at home, and in bed, at the time.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was at home, and in my bed; I did not get up till eight o'clock - I went to the skittleground in the evening, and then went home; I went there again, and was taken.
   MARY ANN LOVELL. I am the prisoner's sister-in-law- I married his brother. On Thursday morning last, he was in bed from six to eight o'clock; I live in the same house with him.
 COURT. Q. How can you swear to that? A.Because I was up with my baby, who was ill; I went down, to put some water into the kettle, between five and six o'clock, and saw him in bed - I passed the bed, and could not help seeing him.
   MARY LOVELL. I am the prisoner's mother. On the night before he had given his trousers to me to mend, and I found them where I left them, after eight o'clock in the morning - he had but that one pair to wear; I sleep in the back-room; he could not get out without my knowing it.
   ALICE LOVELL. I am the prisoner's sister. He was at home, and in bed, from six to eight o'clock last Thursday morning; I am a dress-maker - I got up at six o'clock to work; I went for a candle, and he was in bed, and appeared to be sound asleep - my sister was up at the same time.
   ELIZABETH LOVELL. I am the prisoner's sister. Last Thursday morning, my sister and I got up at six o'clock, to work; I went into the kitchen, to wash my hands, about five minutes past six o'clock - the prisoner was then asleep.
   JOSEPH LOVELL. I am the prisoner's brother - I sleep up stairs; I got up about five o'clock, and struck a light, as the child was crying - my brother was then in bed, and the doors had not been opened; I went into the kitchen between five and six o'clock, and the doors were fastened as I left them - I had occasion to go to the yard.
 COURT. Q.What doors are there to the house? A. The kitchen-door and the street-door; the key of the street-door is generally in the lock when we go to bed.
   GEORGE GOOD. I live with Mr. Marshall; my mistress said there were two suspicious characters come to the house; I went to inquire on Stepney-green, if they lived there, and they did - I went to Mr. Dyke, and he said he could not exactly swear to Lovell, if it had not been for Togg's boy.
   THOMAS DYKE re-examined. Q. Did you say you should not have known him. but for Togg's boy? A. No; Good came to me and said "Have you not made a mistake in the person you have sworn to?" I said No; he said"Mrs. Marshall says there are two very suspicious lads in the skittle-ground, and she would be glad if you would come and look at them;" I said I should not; I was quite certain of his person - he was between two very large gas lamps that morning. and I had him in my grasp five minutes; if I were going to die I could take my oath of it.
   ROBERT CHRISTIAN re-examined. Q.Were you before the Magistrate? A. Yes; and Marshall, Dyke, Togg and Brown - Good was not there; I know him well - I am afraid he is a dangerous boy from his general character; I would not believe him on his oath.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q.Why would you not believe him? A.Because I have known him tell great untruths; I never knew him on his oath before - but Mrs. Marshall says she never sent him at all - it is from what she said that I state what I have on this occasion; I have had him once or twice at the watch-house for little rows, and there he proved himself a great story-teller, but I cannot charge my memory with one.
 MR. MARSHALL re-examined. Good is in my employ; I have a very bad opinion of him - if any loose characters come there he is always with them; I was going to discharge him on Saturday.
 JURY. Q. Did your wife send him to Dyke, to come and look at the persons in the ground? A. No, she did not.
     NOT GUILTY.
