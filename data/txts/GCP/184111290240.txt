BENJAMIN M KNIGHT (a Prisoner. See page 198.) I was in the prose.
cutor's employ-I took twelve lengths of velvet from his premises --eleven
were sufficient for a waistcoat, and the twelfth for two-I afterwards pointed
out to Evans some of the patterns which I took--I first saw the prisoner one
Saturday night, about three weeks ago, at his shop at Newington Causeway
-I had nothing with me then-I asked him if he would buy some lengths
of figured velvet for waistcoats-he said, "Yes"-I went again on the Mon-
day, I believe, and took the twelve lengths which I have mentioned with
me- I got there about half-past eight o'clock in the evening-I found him
there there was a boy present in the shop-he was bringing in some
waistcoats as they were going to shut up-he went out and came in again-
I opened the parcel--the prisoner asked what I wanted for them-I did not
mention any price, but he said he would give me 9s. for the lot-- he said
nothing more-I said, "Would he give more than that, it was not enough
for them"-he said no, he was a man to his word-he said nothing else
-I took the 9s, which he paid me.
Cross-examined. Q. Is it true that your master and Cuthbert gave you
two hours before you made your statement, after they told you they would
prosecute you, if you did not tell what you knew? A. I do not think it
was quite two hours.
Mr. Bopkin. Q. Did any body tell you you should be prosecuted
if you did not tell this story? A. Yes, Mr. Cuthbert-he told me it
would be much better to confess every thing, that Mr. Saltmer did not wish
to hurt me at all that was on the occasion when I was given two hours to
consider what I had to say.
COURT. Q. Is it true you were threatened to be prosecuted if you did
not tell this story? A. Not in those words - I do not remember its being
said that I should be prosecuted unless I did tell-Mr. Saltmer told me to
confess every thing, and that he did not wish to hurt me--that is all I re-
member he said I was likely to be prosecuted if I did not told him this
transation-it was not suggested that I was to be made a witness of.
JAMES CUTHBERT. I was a ward-constable; I have resigned that office.
I was called to Mr. Saltmer's before M Knight was taken into custody-I
saw him-I should wish to remark that I had some conversation with Mr.
Saltmer before I saw the lad-I should not have acted upon that, had I not
been deceived by Mr. Saltmer-I should not have lent myself to any thing
-Evans came to me when I was at home and said, “We have another job
at our house"-I said, "Well, you had better have an officer, for if it is
a prosecution I shall have nothing to do with it, as I have resigned office
- he said, “Oh, it is only just to frighten that lad of ours"-I went and
saw Mr. Saltmer, and had some conversation with him--he said it was
merely to know to what extent, and he would start him I went into
the cellar where the lad was, and said to him, “This is a sad affair"-
he caid, "Yes, it is”—I said, “Well, Mr. Saltmer has every reason to
believe you have taken more goods than what you say"-he said, “No, I
have not"--I said, "I am confident Mr. Saltmer is of opinion that you
have, and if he would not prosecute you, would you tell all you have
taken" -he directly turned round and said, "Has he said so ?"--I said, "I
will call him down, and he shall say so"- I left Evans and him together in
conversation, and called Mr. Saltmer down-there was some conversation,
and Mr. Saltmer said he had made no promise-I then walked away to the
other end.
