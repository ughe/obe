NEW COURT, Thursday, February 6th, 1873.
Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Esq.
189. JOHN WILLIAMS (25), Feloniously forging and uttering an order
for the payment of 71. 78., with intent to defraud.
ME. A. B. KELLY conducted the Prosecution
JAMES Woods. I am a tailor, of Custom House Terrace, Victoria Docks
I know the prisoner he came to me on 6th January, and said that he
came from Welch, the steward of the Ferdinand de Lasoels, and asked me
to cash this advance note for 72. 78.-I did so, and gave him 42 2s, in
money and the remainder in clothes, and charged 58. for eashing the note
-it is payable at 63, Fenchurch Street-I went there, but have not got
the money.
Prisoner. Q. Did not I ask you if you did any business for Mr. Welch
or not? 4. Yes, and I replied that I did you told me you had been
second mate of the steamer which he was steward of—the clothes were for
yourself.
.
Prisoner. I got the order from a man named Watson, a sailor, who I
have met in Calcutta and Bombay. (The order was for 71. 7s., being a
month's advance wages, payable to the order of I. Williams three days after
the sailing of the ship Amaranth, provided he sailed in her.)
JOHN OLIVER. I am clerk to Gilmore & Co., of 63, Fenchurch Street
this order was presented to me it is not genuine--it is dated 6th January,
1873, and we sold the Amaranth five or six years ago, therefore, I cannot
tell who sailed on board of her—the prisoner was in one of our ships two
years ago as second officer-I know him as John M. Williamsblank
forms like this are sold-I never heard anything against the prisoner's
character.
CONWAY HARPER. I am deputy superintendent of mercantile marine -
the crew of the Amaranth signed articles in my presence in December-the
name of John Williams does not appear.
