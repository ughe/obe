964. WILLIAM GRANT (22), and JOHN COOPER (24), Burglary in
the dwelling-house of George John Cooke, and stealing 1 dlook and other
articles.
MR. EDMUND PALMER conducted the Prosecution, and Mr. WARNER SLEIGH
defended Grant.
MARY DOWNHAM. I am servant to Mr. Cocke, of Tredegar-square-on the
evening of the 14th of September, about half-past 9 o'clock, I heard our little
dog bark, and I went out into the area to see who was there-I saw Cooper stand-
ing on the steps- I asked him what he wanted-he called out, "Jack, Phil,
Jack, they are coming"- a bundle was dropped from one of the windows, and
Cooper picked it up and ran away,I went into the kitchen and got a light,
and when I came back I saw Grant coming down the pillar of the portico
my master and mistress were in the dining-room, and I called out to them
that thieves were in the house-I opened the door and run after Grant-my
mistress followed—I called out, “Thieves !" and "Police !" as loud as I
could---my mistress never lost sight of him-he was stopped by a policeman
I saw Cooper again at my master's door the same evening, when Grant was
bronght back, and I said, "That is the man."
Cross-examined. Q. Has the area got railings over the top ? A. Yes I
saw Cooper standing on the steps, and I thought he was looking for the
number of the house, and I asked what he wanted—it was dark, but I saw
him pick up a paroel and run away-I had a paraffin lamp.
