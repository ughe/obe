CHARLES HOOD. I am shopman to Mr. Lowther, of Tottenham Court-road. About half-past one o'clock, on the 1st of October, the prisoner and another girl came and offered this shawl in pledge—I could not swear which offered it, but I believe it was the prisoner—the witness came, in a very violent manner, and said, "That is mine"—I gave it him—he said, "Send out for an officer"—I said I had nothing more to do with it, and he said he would take them himself.
   Prisoner's Defence . I went with the young woman to get her bonnet, when we got out she called me to go with her to pawn the shawl—I never saw it till she put it on the counter. GUILTY.* Aged 17.— Transported for Seven Years.

RICHARD NORDEN. I live at the Horse and Groom public-house, at Earling. Some of these articles are mine—they had been sent to Mrs. Weston's to wash. MARTHA WESTON. I am the wife of James Weston—I am a laundress, hung out all the articles stated, in my yard to dry, at three o'clock, on the 9th of October—part of them were my own—I saw them at the station-house at three o'clock the next morning. JOHN POOL( police-constable T  152.) I was on duty about one o'clock the morning, on the 10th of October, and saw the prisoner in a garden, with these articles and three other bundles of clothes.
   Prisoner . You stated that there were four or five walls climbed to get these things. Witness . I did not—the garden might be entered in three four ways, but you could not get to where these things were without getting over one wall—I took you in Mr. Weatherby's garden.
