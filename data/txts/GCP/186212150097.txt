EDWARD Low. I am a lighterman, and live at 8, Model-cottages, Victoria
Dook-I knew the deceased Arthur Melton-on Monday evening, 24th
Nov. about 8 o'clock, I saw him at the Tidal Basin station on the North
Woolwich railway-I remained with him till 10 o'clook-we then came off
duty, and we went together to the Dock-house public-house-Harvey and
Dyson were in our company--we remained at that publio house till about
half-past 11-1, the deceased, and Dyson came out together-we were all
sober-Melton and Dyson were going home-Melton stopped behind by
the side of the Dook-house for a certain purpose, and I and Dyson went on
a little way-we had got about 50 yards when my atteution was drawn to a
noise which appeared to come from where we had left Melton -I went back
and found Melton standing about two yards from the lamp post, near the
house-I then saw the prisoner Mitchell with three or four other seamen
-Mitchell struck Dyson, who was with me, on the shoulder, and said,
“I know you"-Dyson replied, " If you do know me, don't strike me in that
way"-Mitchell then turned to me and gave me a blow in the mouth, which
felled me to the ground-when I got up I saw Melton lying at full length OD
