I went to the tap with the man who gave me the information, and found the two prisoners there. I asked the price of the grey horse; May said 4 l. I asked if he would go in harness - he said Yes, he had worked him at Thane for four years. I then asked the price of the two horses together, and May said 14 l. I said "Let us go and look at them;" May, the man who gave me the information, and I, went to look at them. I said the black horse looks as if he had lain at grass. He said "Yes, I took him out this morning at four o'clock." I then went to Moore, who was in the house, and asked him how far they had ridden the horses - he said from this side Thane. I said, "Then you have been travelling all night - what time did you set off - at nine o'clock?" - he said Yes. I said, "Was it dark?" - he said Yes. I then went to the door and called May in, and said "I will see if I can deal with you." He came in - I said I had strong suspicions of their being stolen, and I should take them into custody, and detain the horses. I searched May, and found nothing on him - I found on Moore three spoons. I took them before the Magistrate at Hillingdon. Mr. Bowell saw the horses in my presence - the black one he saw was the one they had attempted to sell me. While they were in custody Moore said he knew nothing of the horses, but he was coming along, and that May asked him to have a ride.
 MAY'S Defence. I was coming down the street in Oxford, and I met  Abraham Hyams and Thomas Jackson, who had the horses with them. I intended to have come by the waggon to London, to get advice for my arm and leg, but I came with them; and just before we got to Uxbridge, they said they were authorised to sell the horses for 14 l. or 15 l., but they would not turn 14 l. away. Mr. Ingram saw the two men I had them from.
 WILLIAM INGRAM re-examined. Q. Did he give you this account when they were taken? A. No; I saw two men at the cage door, but they said they knew nothing of them.

 1519.  WILLIAM ROBINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of August , at St. George , a chest of drawers, value 8 s.; two pairs of trousers, value 8 s.; two silk handkerchiefs, value 9 s.; a scarf, value 10 s.; two gowns, value 10 s.; two bird cages, value 3 s.; twenty dozen of fishing hooks, value 10 s.; a sieve of currants, value 3 s.; three frocks, value 2 s.; two shirts, value 2 s.; two brooches, value 6 s., and seven sovereigns, the property of Joseph John Harrad , in his dwelling house; and MARY COLDECOTT was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
 JOSEPH JOHN HARRAD. I am a  green-grocer, and live at No. 1, James-street, Commercial-road, St. George's, in the East. I and my wife went out to market, about half past four o'clock, in the morning of the 2d of August - I left a widow woman, her son, and my child at home - I returned about a quarter before eight, but my wife remained at the market - I got the key into the lock, and opened the street door, but could not get it out again - I sent for a smith, who took the lock off, and got it out - I opened the front of the house, and took in some things off a waggon; my little girl went into the room, to put some play things on a shelf - she then came to me, and gave me information; I went into the room and found a small mahogany chest of child's drawers gone, which had in them some beads, some fishing tackle, and seven sovereigns, and some silver, and a pocket-book which had been locked in one of the drawers - I had seen the money and the pocket book safe the night before, but did not count it - it was safe that morning, before we went out, when my wife took some money to go to market with - I heard it chink in the drawer, but did not see it - I then missed some birds, and in a short time afterwards, a sieve of white currants. I sent my little girl to tell my wife, who came home and opened the large drawers, and found that they had been opened, and some things taken out.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When did you see the prisoner in custody? A. I think about three weeks afterwards.
   MARY ANN HARRAD. I am the prosecutor's wife - I kept the money and the pocket-book in the top drawer of a small chest of drawers - on the morning of the 2d of August, I saw seven sovereigns and the book, which I left there with some silver; there were some handkerchiefs, and other things, taken out of a large chest of drawers - the small chest was altogether gone, and two birds and cages, a sieve of currants, and three frocks.
   MARIA MINTO. I am the wife of John Minto, and live at the corner of James-street, opposite Mr. Harrad's - on the 2d of August, about a quarter before six o'clock, I was standing at the window, and saw the prisoner Robinson, come out of their house with a light pair of trousers on, and a large bag, which appeared to be half full in his right hand; he shut the door with his left hand - I had not seen him before, but I particularly noticed him, because I knew they had a new lodger, and I thought it was him.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far were you from him? A. Just across the street - he opened and shut the door gently - he was as much as three minutes shutting the door, and crossing the road - I suspected something, but did not give an alarm - he had his hat on, and a flannel jacket, and was marked with the small pox.
   SUSANNAH BOWERS. I live in Wentworth-street - I have known Mrs. Coldecott for three years - I was accustomed to deal with her - I have seen Robinson at her shop - he has served me and bought the bones which I took there to sell - he went by the name of Mr. Robinson.
