BURGESS, Frederick (20, labourer), was indicted for and charged
on coroner's inquest with the wilful murder of Annie Lydis Fletcher.
Mr. Travers Humphreys and Mr. Bigham prosecuted; Mr. David
White and Mr. Sydney É. Williams defended.
P.C. SPENCER, 194 A, proved a plan used in the case; Detective
BOUSTIELD proved some photographs.
ELIZA ROBBINS, 21, Prospeot Road, Child's Hill, Prisoner came
to lodge with me on June 9. On the 15th he went to work as usual
in the early morning, and came back between 5.30 and six; he then
washed and brushed himself; later he asked me for a needle and
thread to mend his trousers; he went to bed about 10. Next morning
he left about six o'clook; I did not see him after that.
Cross-examined. Prisoner was very quiet and well-behaved while
he stayed with us.
ROBERT BELL, timekeeper to the Hampstead Garden Estate,
Golder's Hill. Prisoner worked on the estate on June 12 and 14. On
the 16th he asked for his money, saying that he had got a better job
at Whitbread's bottling factory at Willesden. I gave him 5s., and be
left at nine in the morning. To get to Whitbread's he would pase the
end of Pound Lane, Willesden.
Cross-examined. Prisoner seemed a quiet reserved man, and did
not associate much with the other workmen.
WILLIAN RAWLING, tram conductor. On June 15 I was going on
my tramoar from Willesden Green towards Edgware. About 2.20
p.m., & man and a little girl got on to the car at Willesden Green;
they sat on the top. On my collecting fares the man asked for two
threepenny tickets; he had his arm round the girl's neck. At Crickle
wood corner when I again went on top the two were in the same posi-
tion. They left ine car about 2.50 just by Goldbeater's Lodge. On
June 19 l'identified the dead body of Annie Lydis Fletcher as that
of the girl I speak of; I cannot identify the prisoner as the man.
JOHN HOWARD FRAMPTON, driver of the tramcar, corroborated Raw
ling. Witness identified both the girl and the prisoner.
