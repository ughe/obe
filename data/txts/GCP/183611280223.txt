JAMES WATSON. I am the landlord of the White Horse, within half a
mile of this place. On Sunday night the 27th of last month, I recollect
seeing the prisoner at my house - he was there about three o'clock
in the afternoon, and staid there till ten minutes before eleven o'clock
--that was the time he went-I went into the tap-room between eight
and nine o'clock, he was then asleep-when I went in at ten o'clock
he was in company with one or two more, drinking some beer at half-past
ten o'clock I took him in a pot of beer-he said, "I shall not pay you for it,
I did not get my money on Saturday night I shall receive my money
from Mr. Ledger, at Deptford"-at ten minutes to eleven o'clock he came
to the bar to my wife, and asked what beer he had had he said he would pay
to-morrow-I went into my tap-room, and it was ten minutes to eleven o'clock
-I shut up at a quarter before eleven o'clock-I am quite positive that
between a quarter past ten o'clock and ten minutes before eleven o'clock
the prisoner was on my premises.
Mr. Bodkin. Q. How far is your house from the wood ? A. About
half a mile-within about fifty yards--the turnpike is a quarter of
mile, and the wood is about three hundred yards farther it would
take seven or eight minutes to walk there, if he walked fast-I have
not heard that Mr. Farquhar walked it in four minutes--I served the
prisoner with a pint of porter at three o'clock, just before I sat down
to dinner-I cannot go in without seeing the clock-I saw him next at
five o'clock, when I took the candles into the tap-room-he was sitting in
company with two or three others- I did not look at the clock then I
