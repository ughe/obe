   MR. PAYNE called HENRY PECK. I am employed in a wholesale Berlin warehouse in Cheap side, and carry on business for myself at Kingsland—the prisoner was in my employ, up to the time he was taken into custody, eighteen months altogether—I had given him a holiday on Easter Monday—to the best of my belief Old-street is a quarter of a mile from where the copper was taken from—he was on his way to me to ask for another day's holiday. WILLIAM TILLIER, re-examined.  I knew the other lad to be associated with thieves—he has kept out of the way—I know the prisoner by sight—I know no wrong of him. NOT GUILTY.
 1296. JOHN DRURY was indicted for stealing 1 pair of boots, value 2 s  . 6 d  . the goods of James Stocken . JAMES STOCKEN. I am potman at the Craven Arms, Queen's-road, Bays water. I missed a pair of blucher boots from the top of a beer-tray in the yard—they were safe a little before ten o'clock—I saw the prisoner in the tap-room that day after three o'clock—about six o'clock I taw the prisoner and John King together—King had got the boots—I asked the prisoner how he came by them—he said, "A man gave them to me crossing the fields." JOHN KING. I am a potman at the Oraren Arms. I saw the prisoner in the tap-room—he had a flag basket with him—he went out—I afterwards beard Stocken had lost his boots—I met the prisoner in Queen's-road with the boots concealed under his jacket—I asked what he had got—he said, nothing belonging to me—I asked him to give me the boots—he said, "I have got no boots"—I said, "Let me look"—he unbuttoned his jacket, and took the boots out—I said, "They are not mine, they are my fellow-servant's"—he said, "B—your eyes, if you don't let go of me I will smash you"—I let go, but being surrounded with a mob of people he could not go—he was 100 or 200 yards from the Craven Arms—the boots were ten or twelve yards from the tap-room close to the bar parlour window.
   Prisoner.  I said if you came with me I would show you who gave them me. Witness.  No, you said you would show me the man that gave you the handkerchief. JOHN LODDER( police-constable D  207.) I took the prisoner into custody—he was not much the worse for liquor—King delivered the boots to me—the prisoner tried to get away, but tome people came to my assistance—he tried to kick my legs.

   Prisoner 11 Defence.  A man in the Craven Arms bought half a gallon of beer; after I drank, I went to sleep, and never awoke till ten minutes after five o'clock; the men were then all gone out; just outside the door, I met one of them, named Peter Ashburn; he said, " Jack , if you go  across the field you will find an old pair of boots in the hedge, and we will have some more beer," which I did.
