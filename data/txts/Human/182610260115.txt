2042. HENRY CLEAVELAND was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of November , 1 Bible, value 3s., and 1 Prayer-book, value 1s., the goods of the   Mayor and Commonalty of the Citizens of London.
 MR.  JAMES SPENCER. I am keeper of Whitecross-street Prison. This Bible and Prayer-book belongs to the Corporation - I have the care of them; I missed them on the 19th of October; the prisoner was there: a letter was found in his bundle - I do not think him in his right mind.
   SOPHIA WATTS. I am nurse at the Infirmary at the Prison; the prisoner was there; I saw him pack up his linen to go to wash; I looked into his bundle, and found this Bible, which belongs to the prison; I found a note in it, which Mr. Spencer desired me to put back; it stated that the Prayer-book was at his mother's - the prisoner tore it up the next day; I do not think him in his right mind. - This letter was found in his bundle. (Letter read).
 October 17th, 1826.
 Notwithstanding your behaviour towards me, during the time I have been here, I have got better treatment than I met with from you of these late years: which you may depend upon being returned by that behaviour of mine when I come home, which will be on the 11th of November. Now, as I have this to say, that as me and you for these late years past not been agreeable to each other, I will have that explanation, how I am to act, before I leave this place, either from you, as being my mother; but of your actions to me like - my actions, being very bad; but not so, that which you have thought fit to lay them out to - . I have wrote to Mr. Noke, but I have not sent it until I hear from you about this business altogether. I do not want you to settle any thing for me, as I now have got nothing more to settle; the weight of which, with your treatment, having been on my mind for ever since, and of which, as I was told by Dr. Babington here, is sure of bringing on that affliction; and, as I believe, that from your treatment and T. N. has been the whole cause, both of that and my present situation, in this life, and of which being unjustly, I shall hold to the last. I have only to say that I, during the short time I have been here, have had several advices, and all to a right purpoe, which way to go on when I come out, either to your advantage or disadvantage. I will not do any thing rashly, while here, towards you, although in my power so to do; I shall take consideration before acting -. There is a person here who has got two bills of Evans', in Spital-square, which Norman lent to a friend of his, money to the amount, one bill 37l. and 49l., which are put in his schedule as doubtful; but you never knew any thing of them; but you will (if not already) be applied to about the payment of them, therefore you may know which way to answer him. As I am glad I come here, for one point, you may depend I have not come here for nothing, but to learn the most I could while here, which you will see hereafter. I shall expect some money from you, and I am comfortable enough here, if for a year hence. Keep this book clean of mine, and Razors, and Prayer-book, and them things up-stairs from thieves that sleep there, George W. and Geo. Lish, &c. I am, H. CLEAVELAND.
 White Cross-street, October 17th, 1826.
 Prisoner's Defence. The Bible and Prayer-book lay on the mantel-piece, and inadvertently I put the Bible on the basket, but not into it. I have Bibles and Prayer-books enough. NOT GUILTY, being Insane.
 2043.  CATHERINE KIDNEY was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of October , 41/2lbs. of cheese, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Edward Gainsford.
 JAMES SNEED. I am shopman to Edward Gainsford, a  cheesemonger, of Ludgate-hill. I was in the shop on the evening of the 26th of October: the prisoner came to the window, took half a Stilton cheese, and went away - I ran, and found it in her apron.
   JOHN PARRY. I took her in charge.
 Prisoner. I meant to ask the price of it - a man came and said I stole it.
     GUILTY. Aged 71. Confined Seven Days.
 2044.  JUDY DOCKERTY was indicted for feloniously assaulting Mary Dryden , on the King's highway, putting her in fear, and taking from her person, and against her will, 1 handkerchief, value 1d.; 11 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the property of John Dryden.
 MARY DRYDEN. I am eight years old; my father, John Dryden, lives in Red Lion-market, and is a  shoemaker. Last Tuesday week I took some shoes to Mr. Miller, and received 11s. 6d., which I put into a handkerchief; I had 2d. in my other hand; I had met the prisoner in Beech-street; she followed me to Miller's, and then back to Long-lane- she then asked me to lend her 1s.; I told her to come to my father, and perhaps he would; she then put her hand to my mouth, took my money from me, and ran towards Smithfield; I could not call out, I was so frightened - she said if I made a noise she would knock me down; she came back - I am sure of her; it was four o'clock - a gentleman had stopped her.
 Prisoner. She came by me crying - I said, "What is the matter?" she said it was a basket-woman who took the money; her father came to my house, and asked if I she was the woman - she said, No. Witness. I said so, but covered her face. I told my father a basket-woman took it.
   WILLIAM ANDREWS. I saw this child crying in Long-lane; there was a crowd of about thirty people; the prisoner was outside, either drunk or pretending to be so.
 JOHN DRYDEN. I am the child's father. I took her to the prisoner's house, which is a dark alley; she pulled her hair over her face, and the child said she was not the woman.
     NOT GUILTY.
 SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, NOVEMBER 1.
 First London Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
