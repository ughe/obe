   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Does your father send greens to market? A.  Yes—he slept at home on Thursday night.
   COURT . Q.  You never bought hay before? A.  Not in Smithfield-market—we generally come to Smithfield to buy it cheap—the man I bought it of had about a load and a half in his cart—he threw nine trusses off the cart—I did not notice any little pieces of paper amongst it—hay was 5 l . a load—a man bought it before me, with intent to sell it again—I think he was a corn-dealer—I never asked him for any written document—I met a neighbour of ours on the road, who saw me take it away—he lives about half a mile from us.
   JURY . Q.  It being a long distance from Smithfield, at what watering-house did you stop on the road? A.  At the World turned upside down, and gave my horse a drop of water—I did not see any ostler—I had not left the house long before I met the witness and spoke to him.
   C. S. WARDEN re-examined . The prisoner was allowed by the Magis trate to leave the office on Saturday, and come on Monday—he gave two sureties for that. TABITHA TAYLOR. I am the prisoner's daughter. I remember my father and brother being taken into custody—I remember, some days before that, my brother bringing home some hay about one o'clock—it was put into the stable—my father was gone from home, but when he came home at night he examined it, and said it was rather dusty, and told my brother to shake it—I saw it shaken by my brother in the stable—my father slept at home on Thursday night, and my brother too—the superintendent came on Saturday morning, about seven o'clock—I heard the dog bark, and went out—I saw the man in the stable by himself, examining the hay—I asked him what he wanted—he came out of the stable immediately—the two other policemen were at a distance from the stable—he asked where my father was—I said at work at Charlton, and he would return at dinner-time, which he did—the policeman said he had been informed there was some hay stolen, and it had been traced to my father's premises—I denied it—I said my brother bought it in London, and I had let him in at the gate with it—the policeman told me that this was not the hay—he came again about three o'clock in the afternoon, and asked if my father was at home—I said not, but if he had come at dinner-time, as I told him, he would have seen him—I said my brother was at home, and I would call him—he came to him, and they went out together—my brother had been lying down—he went into the yard to them—I do not know what passed between them.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Your brother bought some hay on Tuesday, and you let him in? A.
