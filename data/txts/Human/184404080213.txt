 1285. JAMES JACKSON was indicted for stealing 1 pair of boots, value 2 s  .; the goods of Job Matthews . JOB MATTHEWS. I live in Frederiok-place. Go the 30th of March, be tween eight and nine o'clock, I was is the parlour behind my shop, and saw a man in the act of stooping—I went into the shop, and the prisoner ran out—I ran and took him—he said he would pay for them—I was not then aware that he had taken any thing—these are my boots. THOMAS BISHOP. I live at Brewer's-green, I saw the prisoner throw away these boots—Mr. Matthews picked them up.
   Prisoner's Defence.  Two more lads stood there; a woman said, "There he goes," and I ran. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Month .
 1286. WALTER WILLIAM SILVER WOOD was indicted for stealing 1 sovereign, 1 half-sovereign, 2 half-crowns, 6 shillings, and 6 sixpences, the monies of William Walter , his master. WILLIAM WALTER, JUN. I am the son of William Walter, a cork-cutter, in Drury-lane. The prisoner was his shop-boy—on the 13th of Sept., at half-past seven o'clock in the morning, I gave him the key to open the shop, and sweep it—I afterwards found he was gone—I went to his lodging, and he was not there—I came back in about ten minutes, and I round the till in the front shop was broken open, and missed one sovereign, one half-sove reign, two half-crowns, six shillings, and six sixpences—no one was in the shop from the time I went out till I came back—I did not lock the shop.
   Prisoner.  I was not in the shop that morning—I was up in the top shop. Witness.  I am sure I saw him there—Sherrard had opened the shop when I returned. JAMES SHERRARD. I came to the shop at half-past eight—I found the till broken open, and about 2 l . 4 s . taken out—I did not open the shop—it was open when I came—I do not know who opened it. NOT GUILTY.
