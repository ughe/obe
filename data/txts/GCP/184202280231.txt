WILLIAM KIMBER. I am in Mr. Rolls' service, and act as foreman over
the workmen--it was my duty to keep the keys of the gate, and let the pri-
sober in and out with his cart-on Monday afternoon, the 7th of February,
the cart was loaded at four o'clock, and it left the premises about half-past
four-I left the factory that evening at eight-the prisoner had not returned
- I locked up the gates, put the keys in a little box, and took the box to
Mr. Rolls' house, in Marlborough-place-I came to the factory at seven
the next morning, and found the prisoner's cart there there is only one
tilted cart belonging to the establishment--the cart was in its usual place
standing shaft upwards-I passed close to it-I cannot say whether it
was full or empty-I did not look into it-I went to breakfast about eight,
and returned about half-past eight-the cart was then gone—the prisoner
had had no orders to go-he returned about ten with the cart and horse,
and some timber-I did not authorize him to take these 146 yards of
cloth.
