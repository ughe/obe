1501. BENJAMIN RIMELL was indicted for stealiing, on the 8th of September , fourteen dozen of pottle baskets, value 7 s., the goods of Allen Malcolm.
 ALLEN MALCOLM. I am a  cowkeeper, and live at Brentford. My father is a market gardener; I had a number of baskets in my father's potatoe cave. The prisoner is his carter. I cannot swear to them.
 HENRY MALCOLN. I am the prosecutor's brother. I was sitting in doors with my father, when a girl said there was some person among the pottles; I went out and found the prisoner in the stable, with the pottles among the horses.
   JAMES MALCOLM. On hearing my female servant say there was some person among the baskets, I went out; I did not see the baskets in the stable, but I saw them afterwards in the potatoe cave - I am certain that they had been moved and put back again.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Can you swear to these baskets? A. No.
   HENRY MALCOLM re-examined. Q. Did you see the prisoner move any of these baskets? A. I did not see him bring them out; but I saw him take them back.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1502.  PETER WIGMORE was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , two brooches, value 10 s., the goods of James Franklin.
 JAMES FRANKLIN. I am a  jeweller, and live in High-street, Bloomsbury. The prisoner came to my shop on the 10th of September, between one and two o'clock; I was not present when he came in, but I afterwards saw him standing by the counter, begging - my wife said she suspected him of having taken something; I did not miss any thing, and sent him away - I missed the brooches almost immediately; I then pursued, and brought him in out of the street; he at first opened his waistcoat, and said I might search him. I found the two brooches in the inside pocket of his coat. GUILTY. Aged 9.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
 1503.  WILLIAM WALDRON was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , a watch, value 1 l., the goods of John Jones. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1504.  JAMES WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of July , a handkerchief, value 5 s., the goods of William Skinner , from his person.
 WILLIAM SKINNER. I am an  apothecary. On the 4th of July, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, I was in Saffron-hill; I felt a little pull at my pocket, and missed my handkerchief; I turned round, and the prisoner was close at my heels - I said he had picked my pocket; he said he had not; I said, "It is of no use to deny it." I put his arm aside, and found my handkerchief under his arm.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Months.
 1505.  WILLIAM ARTHUR was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , a tin box, value 2 d.; seven half-crowns, and a shilling, the property of James Cripps.
 JAMES CRIPPS. I  keep a cook's-shop, in Chapel-street, Mary-le-bone. On the 15th of July I heard a noise in my shop, and saw the prisoner by the side of the counter - he asked for some pease-pudding - I said I had none, and on going round the counter I saw my till was out, and the money taken from it, some of which had been in a tin-box, and some loose in the till - I had seen it about a quarter of an hour before, and no other person had been in the shop; I then looked on the counter, and saw the tin box, and the prisoner's hand under the scale on the counter. I said, "You have got my money;" the money I had in the till was two half-crowns, a shilling, and a sixpence; he said, "I have 6 s. 6 d. of my own." The whole of the money in the till and box was five half-crowns, some silver, and some copper.
   J - WILLS. I am an officer. I took the boy, and found upon him two half-crowns, a shilling, and a sixpence. GUILTY. Aged 14.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Six Months.

 1507.  ROBERT JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , a coat, value 20 s., the goods of Walter Phillips.
 WALTER PHILLIPS. I  keep the  White Hart public-house, Little Windmill-street. The prisoner lodged with me - I hung my coat up on the Monday night, and missed it on the Wednesday morning; he was taken up on another charge, and a duplicate of the coat was found - he has been with me three months, and conducted himself well.
   JOHN VIRGO BUCKLAND. I took up the prisoner, and found the duplicate of a coat upon him, which had been pawned at Mr. Tate's, in Cambridge-street.
   THOMAS BAYLEY. I live with Mr. Tate, a pawnbroker. I have a coat, pawned by the prisoner, I believe, but I cannot swear to him. This is the duplicate which was given.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met two men who used the house - they had got the coat; we went and pawned it together.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Three Months.
