Cross-examined by the Prisoner. There was one other young man in the
bar-I was not there when you gave the first sovereign.
FRANCIS PEPWORTH (Policeman E R 13). The prisoner was given into
my custody by the last witness, who gave me two bad sovereigns produced)
I found on the prisoner 78. in silver and 4 d. in bronze-he said he
passed one sovereign, but he did not know anything about the first one-he
said he knew where he got it, but he should not tell.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These sovereigns are both bad—they are made of
white metal, and gilt.
The Prisoner, in his defence, stated that he only passed one sovereign, and
was not aware that it was bad, and that as to the other, the barmaid must
have mistaken him for someone else. GUILTY-Two Years' Imprisonment.
245. SARAH BEAUMONT (33), was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS, COLERIDGE and DE MICHELE conducted the Prosecution, and
MR. BESLEY the Defence.
FREDERICK HORTON. I am an oilman, at 52, Leverton Street, Kentish
Town-on 21st March, at 5.30 p.m., the prisoner came in for a 2d. cake of
fanoy soapshe placed a sovereign in front of the counter in payment-my
brother asked me for the change for the sovereign; I gave him a half-sovereign,
98. 6d. in silver, and 4d. in coppers-my brother counted the change over
to her—he turned round, and was going on with his work, and the prisoner
suddenly said “Did I give you & sovereign I intended to give you a
shilling; if you will take it out of the shilling, and give me the sovereign
back; I did not want to change that sovereign," and she placed the shilling
by the side of the change-my brother took the 2d. out of the shilling, and
I gave him the sovereign out of the cash-box to give to her, and he placed
the change on the desk by my side—the prisoner asked the price of the
Swiss milk a tin, and I said “Eightpence"- she said she would have a
tin, and she paid for it out of the 10d. change from the shilling-she
then went out-I counted and examined the change, and found a bad
half-sovereign-I can swear that was not the silme half-sovereign that I
had handed to my brother-I showed it to my brother, and he bent it
between his finger and thumb-we both ran after the prisoner, and he
said “This is not the half-sovereign I gave you; this is a bad one," and
he returned it to her she drew her hand from her muff, and threw another
one down on the pavement from the same hand, and put it back in her
muff, and said “What do you mean ?"-I picked that half-sovereign up;
it was a good one-I gave it to my brother, and said “That is not the
half-sovereign you gave her "-he said “No, that is not the one; that is
& good one"-we followed her about 300 yards till we got a constable,
and gave her into custody.
• Cross-examined. I was attending to the books—the shop is rather dark
- I took the sovereign from my brother-I counted the change out of the
cash box myself and gave it to my brother, and he counted it over on the
counter and left it there-I did not see the prisoner touch the change at all,
and when she said she would pay with a shilling my brother took the change
off the counter where it had been placed, and she remained some few
minutes being served with the Swiss milk-she walked down the street at
the ordinary pace-she was not running at all-she had both her hands in
her muff-she withdrew one hand, and the very instant my brother passed
the coin to her, the other fell on the ground.
