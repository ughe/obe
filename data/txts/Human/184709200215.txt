DAVID COTTON. I saw all the prisoners that night, between six and half-past six o'clock, by the corner of the Duke of Clarence, in the Circus, leading up from the London-road—I saw Pound take the two Lawsons into custody, and I saw this purse drop from Johnson.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you refer to Murphy as being there? A.  Yes—I had not known him before—I am sure it was him—I never saw him till he was taken into custody—that was after the lady had been robbed—Pound took the two Lawsons, and called to Stroud, and said, "Take these other two men"—I saw nothing of the lady—I saw Johnson drop this purse—I did not hear any cry of "Police." JOHN BAKER( policeman, L  128.) Pound pointed out Murphy, and I took him into custody—he was not with the other three prisoners when I took him—he was with them when he came out of the St. George's tavern—he had been pointed out to me when he was in the tavern—they came out, and walked about 100 yards, and then Murphy left the other three, and went back, and I took him—I found on him 1 s ., a knife, and two penny-pieces—on Johnson I found 4 s ., two sixpences, 9 d . in halfpence, and a comb—among the sixpences was this battered  one.
   Cross-examined. Q.  All you know is, that Pound pointed out Murphy and the others being all together? A.  Yes, they were close together when they came out of the tavern. JESSE CANNON( policeman, L  69.) I produce a certificate of Murphy's former conviction at this court—( read—Convicted the 7th of April , 1845, and confined two years )—I was present at the trial—he is the man.
   MURPHY  — GUILTY . Aged 28.— Transported for Seven Years .
