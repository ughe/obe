   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Is this your hand-writing to this deposition? A.  Yes—I did not tell the Magistrate that the prisoner said he hoped I would deal leniently with him, because the question was not asked me—I first saw these three bars at seven minutes past seven o'clock—I afterwards saw the prisoner in the factory, standing close to Dove—I remarked it in my own mind—I mentioned it at a quarter to six o'clock in the evening—I and Dove have never had any conversation with respect to Dove's wages—I had understood by a man in the factory that the prisoner charged 18 s . a week for Dove's wages, and gave him only 15 s .
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Do you know what wages were allowed to the pri soner for Dove? A.  18 s . CHARLES DOVE. I am a labourer, and worked for the prisoner. On the 27th of February, in the evening, he gave me directions to call at his house on the following morning—I did so, at twenty minutes before seven o'clock, and found some bars of solder in the passage—he had left his house, and gone to the factory—I took the bars there, and saw him there—he told me to put the eight bars of solder into the scale, and I did so—it was weighed in Mr. Munro's presence.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were there any other bars of solder lying there besides the eight? A.  No—I cannot swear there were not—MR. Munro did not make any observation to me about them—I told him I had 15 s . a week from the prisoner—on the Saturday morning, the 29th, Munro said it was a great shame that I should only have 15 s . a week, when the pri soner had 18 s . from the firm—I was never taken up for any robbery—I was accused of one by a person who is here to give the prisoner a cha racter—I considered myself innocent—I was sent from his employ, and taken in by the prisoner out of charity. NOT GUILTY.
