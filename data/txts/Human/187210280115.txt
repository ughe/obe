   The Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate.  I reserve my defence. I have Hannah and Margaret Leary, Mary Mahoney, and Johanna Driscoll I will call at my trial.
   The following Witnesses were called for the Defence. MARY REGAN. I am the wife of Dennis Regan, and I live at 1, St. George's Square, on the ground floor—the prisoner is my daughter; she lived with her husband on the first floor—Timothy Began is my son—he did not live with me—he was living in some place, I don't know where—he came into my place when the policeman rushed in after him on this Sunday morn ing—I saw my daughter on that morning when she came out of her bed from the policeman rushing into her room—I did not speak to any constable that night except one who came and broke the door, and I told him "Don't break open the door"—they broke three doors—my daughter was there in bed with her husband—I don't know Sergeant Woods—I heard somebody call out that a constable was cut, and a cab was called, and he was taken to the hospital—I did not see my son Timothy throw a ginger-beer bottle, nor did I see the constable struck with it—I did not ask him to interfere—I saw nothing of the row  before the police came to my house and knocked at the door, and broke it to pieces, and they went up stain into the prisoner's room—she jumped out of bed, and came down in her nightgown and petti coats, but no boots—there was a young man in my place, and she said to him "They will break all my things;" and he said "No they won't," and then the went out into the court clapping her hands and crying—that is all I know about it.
   Cross-examined.
