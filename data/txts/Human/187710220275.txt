HENRY VERRALL. In April last I was employed at the Phoenix Iron Works—I remember some iron coming in the barge Andrew by this entry—my duty was to weigh it as it came in from the barge—I did so, and made entries in this landing-book ( produced ).
   Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY . The barge came on the 3rd of April. EDMUND VICTOR WHITLING. I am the City manager to the Phoenix Iron Works Co., at 60, Gracechurch Street—on Saturday, 28th April, Porter called there for payment of his account, and I paid him 90 l .—I said that Mr. Freeth was not at all satisfied with the weights of the iron delivered, and he intended going over to Messrs. Penn to see if the amount charged was correct—he said, "For God's sake don't do that, telegraph to Mr. Freeth to see me before he goes"—I declined to tele graph and Mr. Freeth came up—before that I told Porter that Mr. Freeth had sent Andrews and Mr. Verrall together, and he thought the weights were not correct—later on the same day Mr. Freeth came in—on 1st May Porter called again for payment of his balance, and I ultimately paid him—I said that we believed we had been robbed in conjunction with Andrews and Verrall—he said, "So help me God, I have only charged you the same weight as Penn's have charged me"—I produced an invoice, turning down a part and showing the weights only—after looking at that I was satisfied—he said, "These are the weights, perhaps you will now be satisfied"—we discharged Verrall—shortly after Porter left I saw Mr. Freeth, who showed me a paper—after that I saw Porter at the wharf—I believe we had then made a claim upon him for 32 l . 12 s . 8 d ., the difference between the weight Penn's gave to Mr. Freeth and the weight that Porter had charged us—I said I was very much sur prised after a man taking his solemn oath that he had given it, that there was such a discrepancy, because I considered that he had shown us the original invoice—he said that we should not lose any thing, or some incoherent statement; but of course I did not believe a word he said after that—he said that I should have to get half of the amount back from Mr. Douglas—he called on me four or five days later at the City office, and promised to pay the 32 l . the next day at twelve o'clock—the last day of May, or early in June, I was in the Lombard dining rooms and saw Douglas—I said, "Porter has not paid that money yet"—he fell into a great temper, and seeing he was getting very boisterous I left him sitting where he was, and went into the innerroom—he came after me and sat down by me and said, "Don't you think you are going to frighten Harry Porter out of that 32 l .?"—I said that I did not want to frighten any one, I only wanted what was right—he said, "You have had your fair weights, you have not been robbed"—I said "If we have not been robbed, who has been robbed"—he said "Penn and Sons have, and they have been for years"—I said, "Well, if Penn and Sons have been robbed, pay them back the money, we only want what is right"—he said."
