 1835.  MARY ANN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 1 watch, value 3l., the goods of William Saucutt.
 WILLIAM SAUCUTT. On the 17th of July, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, I met the prisoner in  Leonard-street, Shoreditch, and while I was speaking to her, I found her hand in my waistcoat pocket - I felt, and missed my watch; she denied it - I called Watch! she then asked if I thought she could be guifty of it - I took hold of her hand, and said I would not let her go; she then put her hand behind her, and gave it to me; she used bad language, and I gave charge of her.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. Did you not take liberties with her? A. I spoke to her - I asked who she was waiting for - she said her friend had got her key, and she could not get in; nothing more passed - I am a cabinet-maker - I was not in liquor at all - I did not ask her to go to a house with me.
   JOHN RENCH. I am a watchman. I heard a call of Watch! and took her in charge - the prosecutor appeared sober.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you hear her say she was a respectable person, and he had no business to speak to her? A. No - she said he was not robbed, he had got his property again.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that the prosecutor had taken very indecent liberties with her, and as she had resented them, he charged her with the robbery.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1836.  JOHN WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of August , 6 shutters, value 9s., the goods of Frances Corbell.
   HARLEY ARCHER. I deal in building materials, and live in the New-road. On the afternoon of the 3d of August the prisoner brought me three shutters - I did not buy them, for while I was waiting on another customer, he left them, and went away; he said he had bought them of a Mr. Scott, in Shakspeare-walk.
   JOHN SCOTT. I know nothing of these shutters. I employ the prisoner at times.
   GEORGE DEVERELL. I took the prisoner in charge; he said he hoped I should take young Lazel, as he was as much to blame - I took him; he was bound over to prosecute, but is not here, nor is the prosecutrix.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1837.  CHARLES POPE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of May , 1 pair of shoes, value 6s., the goods of Joseph Bordey, his master.
 MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the prosecution.
   JOSEPH BORSLEY. The prisoner was my clicker; he used to cut out, and deliver goods to workmen, and assist in selling in the shop. In consequence of information, I had him taken - I never gave him authority to part with these shoes.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. You are not always in the business? A. No; my nephew and neice act as cashiers; they are not here; they could not give him authority - I had the shoes in my hand, the day after they were made, and complained of the work; we had not above a dozen pairs of boys shoes in the house.
   CHARLES OGLE. I am a tailor, and live in North Audley-street. The prisoner came to me about a pair of shoes, which I ordered of him, and he sent, or brought them home - I was making clothes for him, and did not pay him for them.
 Cross-examined. Q. You do not know who brought them? A. No - they were never worn, as they were too large for my son - I kept them for about eleven months - I never settled with him; my son may have worn them in the house; but never went out in them.
 MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you see him after they came? A. Yes, and told him they were too large, but thought they would fit him bye and bye; the prisoner saw them then.
   JOHN LACEY. I made these shoes for Mr. Borsley, about Christmas, and know them.
   CHARLES OGLE. I cannot say whether I had them before Christmas or after, but think it was before.
 GUILTY. Aged 28. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Months.
 1838.  GEORGE CRIBB, JOSEPH HANKINS, and JAMES MORLEY were indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 1 watch, value 2l. 10s., the goods of Samuel Martin , from his person.
 SAMUEL MARTIN. I was in  Whitechapel-road on the 17th of August, it was towards the evening, I received a very heavy blow on my back; I looked round, and, on turning, my watch was snatched from my fob. I turned round, and laid hold of Cribb, who was close behind me; whether he struck me I do not know.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. What are you? A. A gardener - I sometimes work as a porter; it was a silver watch, and belonged to my son. I had worn it before, but did not wear any seals to show it. I was going along with a large geranium - I put the watch into my pocket about twelve or one o'clock, when I cleaned myself and came out - I know Jeffrey East, to my sorrow; he is my landlord; I did not speak to him that day - I have not seen him since; he never said I never had a watch. I saw Jackson next day; he called me into his house to try to draw me; I mentioned my loss to him. I did not tell him Sheen had seen me with the watch. I never told Evans I had taken it out of pawn that day from Philpots - I have worked for Mr. Payne, of the Bank.
   THOMAS SMITH. I am beadle of Whitechapel. On the 17th of August I was in the road, and saw a mob - I found the prosecutor and Cribb having an altercation; he said he had shoved against him, and two more had stolen his watch. I took Cribb to the watch-house. I found no watch.
