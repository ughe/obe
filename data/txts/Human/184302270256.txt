No—I was to give him the 1 l . 1 s . 6 d . for bringing him home—I asked him about my things on the Tuesday—I did not on the Friday. WILLIAM RENTON. I am a tallow-chandler, and live in Park-place, Wal worth. On the 12th of Feb. I accompanied Mr. Ballance, Miss Shepherd, and Mrs. Renton, to Croydon—we were overturned in the Norwood-road, between ten and and eleven o'clock at night—I was slightly hurt—the prisoner came up, and assisted in taking up Mr. Ballance—he gave me his card, and said he was a particular friend of Mr. Ballance—I saw him take the watch and guard from Mr. Ballance, and the pin—I demanded them of him, and he gave them to me—I asked him to give me back the property, and he gave me three sovereigns—I had not see him take any money from him—I went home in the fly  —I do not know who paid for it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he not give you the purse with the half-crown and half-sovereign in it? A.  He did. WILLIAM BENTON. I am coachman to Mr. Retford; he keeps flies.  On the 12th of Feb. a chaise was upset, and I volunteered a fly  —I saw the pri soner take some money out of Mr. Ballance's pocket—I heard him count it—he got out of the fly , and said he would take the gold from the silver—he counted five sovereigns.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was Mr. Renton there? A.  There was only Mr. Ballance, the cabman  and me—Mr. Renton was in the stable-yard, assisting the lady who was hurt—I was quite sober—the policeman did not take any notice of me, that I am aware of—I was not taken up for being drunk—I took Mr. Ballance home—I then took the prisoner back to Walworth, and he asked me to go and have something to drink, which was ale and gin, and by the effect of that I was stupified—I never received any money—I do not recollect taking any money at all. ABRAHAM TEARSE. I am a house-agent, and live at Norwood. I was near where this accident occurred, on the night of the 12th of Feb.—the party had just left my house before the accident happened—I saw the prisoner standing behind Mr. Ballance, assisting him after the accident, and I saw him take the ring off his finger—I cannot tell what he did with it—Mr. Chapman, the sur geon, was attending to Mr. Ballance at the time.
