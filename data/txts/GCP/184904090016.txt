WILLIAM SAUNDERS. I am a chandler, in Hertford-street, Fitzroy-square.
On Wednesday, 21st March, the prisoner came to my shop about two o'clock
for a loaf and some other things, which came to 94d.-he gave me a bad
58.-piece- I cat a piece out of it, and went round to the street door-I asked
my wife if she would get change for a crown-piece-when she came to the
door I said to the prisoner, "I shall give you in charge; you know this is a
base crown-piece"-he made a rush, and pushed my wife against the door,
and ran out-I ran and called, "Stop thief!"-he was taken—I gave the
crown to the policeman.
George Davis (policeman, E 29). I took the prisoner – I received this
crown from Mr. Saunders.
Caleb EDWARD Powell. These four crowns are all counterfeit, and cast
in the same mould.
Prisoner's Defence. On the 12th I went to Mr. Hopkins's, and gave him
a sixpence; on the Monday I went and had a glass of ale; I gave him 2d.;
I afterwards met a young man, and went and had a glass of ale and a glass
of stout, which he paid for—I have never been in his house since the
Monday night. 10. Witness for the Defence.
CHARLOTTE Brown. I am the wife of William Brown, a bricklayer and.
plasterer,he is in the country-I live in Southampton-street, Euston-square.
On the Sunday the prisoner was not out all day-be was repairing a coat
and making a pair of trowsers, and in the afternoon he went to lie down-I
went into his room, and said to his mother, “Is Tom gone to bed ?"-she
said, "Yes."
Cross-examined by Mr. ELLIS. Q. How many Sundays is that ago? 4.
Three-be is a tailor by trade.
Prisoner. I received the crown that I tendered to Mr. Saunders two hours
before, not knowing it was a bad one.
