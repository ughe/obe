I was told he had taken fox-glove, and the symptoms agreed with that; they could not have deceived me if they had tried, as he had taken such an immense quantity - I ordered him braudy every ten minutes, gave him medicine, and ordered him blisters; but he died while they were preparing. I afterwards opened his body, and there was an absence of all disease that might have produced death. Death is produced by vegetable poison, not by occasioning disease, but by lessening the energy of the nervous system, so that the heart and all the energies cease to act. I have no doubt the decoction of fox-glove caused his death; it is a medicine which is admiaistered in similar cases. The boy had been under my care for two months, and I had repeatedly administered it to him, properly guarded; it was a case in which I judged it right - I administered it to him successfully, I think, as his health was restored, and he returned to work. I cannot say how much would be sold for 2d. or 3d.; the prisoner told me, in the Coroner's Jury-room, that he had boiled down 4 ozs., which was a greater quantity than I thought - from measuring the cup, the boy must have taken about 51/2 ozs. of the stuff, as 61/2 ozs. are left in the bottle, which makes 12 ozs., which 4 ozs. would produce. It is impossible such a quantity could be given without danger; from calculation, I find the quantity of decoction twenty-four times the strength our College orders us to make, and then he had given him six times the quantity beyond our extreme dose, and twelve or fifteen times the quantity beyond the common dose. Nothing but complete ignorance could have led to such a dose being given - in my opinion, it must have produced death - I should have thought five or seven drops of this mixture a proper dose. I have seen the book the prisoner has published, and am sorry to say, it will produce much mischief, if acted upon. I remember a case where an undue and uncertain quantity of fox-glove was given, and was unexpectedly beneficial, but I do not know how much.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2007.  JOHN TIFF was indicted for killing and slaying Daniel Baker,
 MR. ALLEY conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM SNOW. I am a waterman. On the 30th of September I had brought a lady from a ship, with a quantity of luggage - she was landing at Blackwall-stairs; the prisoner was there, acting as a porter; Baker came up, and said to the lady, "Do you want me, ma'am;" she said to me, "Yes; give the chest and hammock to that tall man;" he came to the boat, took hold of the rope, and the prisoner struck him the first blow - they put themselves in a fighting attitude - Baker fell on the gunwale of a boat, in the third round - I heard him say, Oh! but they fought two more rounds afterwards, and Baker died in about twenty minutes.
   WILLIAM JONES PERCEVAL. I am a surgeon. I was fetched to the deceased, and found him dying - after his death I opened his body, and found five or six pints of extravasated blood in the belly; there were several external bruises, and one corresponding with the rupture of the spleen - he died from the extravasation of blood occasioned by that rupture - the spine had been diseased before.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Would his fighting occasion it? A. Not without receiving a blow.
   JAMES LEWIS. I saw this man after his death - the surgeon saw the same person.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you present when he examined it? A. No; he was carried to the King's Arms public-house, and laid on the table; there was no other dead man there - I did not search the house.
 Prisoner's Defence (written). I called out to know if the lady wanted a porter to carry the things on shore; Daniel Baker also plied her; she nodded, and on the boat reaching the shore the waterman called to me, to pull her in, which I did. I then took hold of the hammock, which the deceased perceiving, attempted to take from me; and in so doing, he pushed me into the water. I got out of the water, and, in the irritation of the moment, struck him, which was returned, and we fought four or five rounds. He being older than myself, I rather endeavoured to keep him off, than to strike him; but in the last round he unfortunately fell on the nose of the boat, which hurt the spine and caused his death. I had no animosity whatever towards the deceased: indeed I hardly knew him.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Two Months.
