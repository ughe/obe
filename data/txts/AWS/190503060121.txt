LEONARD OsBORN. I am a carman-on the evening of January 7th
Green came into the A1 while I was there-somebody in the bar threw
s paper bag at him, whereupon he turned round and told the man he
did not want to be made a target of-the prisoner then turned round and
asked Green if he was after trouble and asked him to have a fight-I
turned to Green and said, 44 Come on. You don't want to fight. Come
out of it"-I took him up the street and came back and went into the
Albion-I then heard a noise at the back and went out and saw Green and
the prisoner quarrelling with words-Green said, 46 Why don't you shake
hands, and let us be friends, or let us fight and finish it 1"-I Said to
Green, 44 You do not want any fight. Come on out of it," and I took
him as far as the Al, and the prisoner went to the other side of the road-
while at the corner he began jeering and sneering and Green came across
and said, 66 Come on, Tom, if it is like that, let us finish it," and he threw
off his coat-I turned round and caught hold of Green, when suddenly
the prisoner rushed from the corner and struck him across the head-
Green was standing 8O that he could not see the prisoner coming-the
prisoner hit Green several blows on his head with a hammer, or a piece
ofiron with a big head-Green was knooked down and I closed with the
prisoner and brought him to the kerb, and his sister took the hemmer
away from him as I held his arms-I then released him and went over to
pick up Green, whom I took to my house-I then fetohed a cab which
took him to the hospital.
