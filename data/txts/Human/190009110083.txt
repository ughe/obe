   The Prisoner, in his defence, said, on oath, that his wages were 24s. a weak, 3s. for his son to come on the cart, and a commission of 4s. for a 4d. per quart customer, and 2s. 6d. for a 3d. per quart customer; that £6 19s. 6d. was due for commission, and that he took over the round at the prosecutor's request, and worked it up to about 220 customers, and paid in, in lump sums, all the moneys that he received, trusting to the customers to pay; that he had £3 14s. to come to him from customers, and that accounts were continued in the books after the milk had ceased to be supplied because the prosecutor was going to sell the business. ALICE WHEELER( Re-examined ). The reasons I gave the prisoner for paying for the milk every day were that my husband was not doing much work, and that the bill was not for the right amount. JOSEPH CARIER( Re-examined ). It is not true that accounts were continued in the books after milk had ceased to be supplied—I did not tell them to enter such amounts, nor that he was to take the round over on his own account—he never did take it over. REBECCA CARTER( Re-examined ). I never arranged with the prisoner to take the round over, nor to enter accounts that were not going on—he never told me he was paying money in out of his own pocket. GUILTY  .— The JURY added that they found that the prosecutor contributed somewhat unfairly to the prisoner's guilt. He then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of felony at the Thames Police-court on July 9th, 1898.— Six Months Hard Labour.
