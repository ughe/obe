   Cross-examined. Q.  The recess is very handy to the prisoner, as he was doing business in the shop? A.  Yes—if a person wanted change suddenly he should not give it out of his pocket, and then take it from where it might be—it is not the custom for our young men to have coppers in their pocket—they should be put into the till.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  The recess was not the usual place to put coppers in? A.  No—it was put there for the purpose of trying him—if a person wants change they go to the desk for it—there is a person at the desk for that purpose. JOHN COOK( police-sergeant M  8.) On the 18th of Jan., about half-past two o'clock, I was called to the prosecutor's—I went up stairs, and searched a box, after I had searched the prisoner—he opened it—I found in it 18 l . 13 s . in gold and silver, and 1 l . 11 s . 1 1/2 d . in loose coppers—I took charge of the copper, and produced them to Johnson George—he picked out these six penny-pieces.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You went to search for some copper? A.  We went to see if we could find any thing of Mr. Gainsford's—we took all the money we found in the box, which I produce here. GUILTY  . Aged 25.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Nine Months .
 821. JOHN NORMAN was indicted for embezzlement. MARY JACKSON. I am the wife of John Jackson. The prisoner was in our service—on the 24th of Dec. I sent him with half a hundredweight of coals to Mrs. Hands—he was to bring back 9 d . for them—on the 31st I sent him with some coals to Mrs. Fenton—he was to bring back 1 s . 6 d .—he did not bring me either sum—he said they would pay next time—he did not pay 1 s . 6 d . from Gay. JOHN JACKSON. The prisoner never paid me either of these sums. MARTHA HANDS. I received some coals on the 24th, and sent 9 d . back by the prisoner. ELIZA FENTON. I received some coals on the 31st of Dec.—I sent 1 s . 6 d . back by the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Four Months .
 822. JAMES SMITH and THOMAS POWERS were indicted for stealing, on the   12st of Jan ., 30lbs. weight of rope, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of John Churchin , in a vessel on the navigable   river Thames . ROBERT BARKER. I am mate of the Richmond Packet , lying in the Thames—John Churchin is the master. This rope belongs to that packet.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Who had the care of this vessel? A.  The captain, Mr. Churchin—I was absent from the ship about an hour. JOSHUA JUDGE. I am an inspector of the Thames Police. On Saturday night, the 21st of Jan., I saw the prisoners in a boat—I saw Smith get on to the Richmond , and soon after jump back—I boarded their boat, and found this rope in it—I said, "Where did you get this?"—Smith said a lighterman had sent them for it—I said, "Do you know his name?"—he said, "No"—I said I should take them to his vessel—Smith said, "It is no use to go, it did not come from there."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Smith seemed the active person? A.  Yes—Powers took no part in the conversation—I cannot tell whether Powers was employed to row Smith.
