ROBERT DOUTHWAITE. I am a boot and shoemaker, and live in Tot hill-street, Westminster. On the 10th of April, between eleven and twelve o'clock the prisoner came to my shop in company with another—he showed line two pairs of boots, and asked if I would purchase them—I asked where he got them—he said they were his own goods, and he was on the fuddle  —I offered him 5 s . for them, which was the full value—they were not new, and had been repaired—he went away for half an hour, or three quar ters of an hour, returned, and said he would take the 5 s .—I produce the boots—he promised to return in an hour or two with some more—I had not bought them above half an hour, when Mr. Berry came and identified them—next morning a man came with these for sale—Mr. Berry said they were his, and if the man came, I was to detain him—he came again—I looked outside, and saw the prisoner down below—I immediately sent for a constable and had him apprehended.
   Prisoner.  I sold the two pairs of boots, but the other man received the money. Witness.  No, you did—I gave you two half-crowns, and you said, "We will go and have a drop of beer, as we are on the fuddle"  these are the boots. JAMES BARNICOATT( police-constable P  149.) The prisoner and another were given into my custody—I received these two pairs of boots at the station, from the inspector on duty.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I pleaded guilty to that small pair as soon as I was taken into custody; I said it was my work, but I know nothing of the others; they are the other man's. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Three Months .
