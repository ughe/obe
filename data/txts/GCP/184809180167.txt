2197. FRANCIS HENRY WILLES was indicted for embezzlement.
Messrs. BALLANTINE and PARNELL conducted the Prosecution.
BENJAMIN TEMPLING. I am in the service of the Waterman Steam-
Packet Company as collector, at the City Pier, London-bridge-it is my duty
to issue tickets to persons going up and down the river (it would be the pier-
man's duty to receive them)-this book (produced) was in my possession for
that purpose on 30th Aug.--this ticket, No. 145, was issued from it that
morning, for the purpose of going down the river.
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Do persons who come for a
ticket sometimes change their minds after you have toru the ticket out of the
book? A. Yes, it does happen-if a person takes a ticket for Woolwich,
and then say they will go to Greenwich, I give them a ticket for there, and
give the other ticket to the next Woolwich passenger-I might do that in
five minutes or in five hours a ticket might be cast into the place where I
stand, instead of being delivered at the station- I do not know that it has
occurred at my place if it does happen, those tickets would be given to the
pier-man in a minute or two, or a little longer--the issuer of the tickets ooca-
sionally takes the tickets which the pier-man ought to receive; that is, by
persons coming past the pier-man, and getting off at another part-those
tickets ought not to get mixed with the others-my stamp is No. 8-the
prisoner would issue a different number-it would be impossible for the pier
man to distinguish the number on the ticket, but they know the colour of the
ticket--we have different colours--the colour of that day was drab.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Are you quite sure this ticket left your pier that
day? A. Yes; it was issued from my station, and I have the counterfoil.
