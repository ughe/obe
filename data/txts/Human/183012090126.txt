 Cross-examined. Q. How were the cheeses removed? A.    WILLIAM BEAMAND. I am in the employ of Harbin and Larkin, in Whitechapel.  Cross-examined. Q. Is not H.4. your general mark on all cheeses? A. It is of that dairy - I can only swear that Mr. Cass bought the whole of that dairy.
 JOHN SULLIVAN. I searched the prisoner, but found no knife on him.
 Prisoner's Defence.  JOHN SULLIVAN. There is a reward on conviction, but nothing was said about that.

 265.  JOHN WAPSHERE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of December , 1 bag, value 1d.; 1 crown; 40 half-crowns, 140 shillings, and 38 sixpences, the property of Alfred Coy Hows.
   ALFRED COY HOWS. I live in High-street, Shadwell. On the 7th of December, about ten o'clock in the morning, I had 13l. 4s. in silver in a bag - there was one crown-piece, upwards of twenty half-crowns, more than one hundred shillings, and more than twenty sixpences - I gave the bag to my lad to take to a place where I generally send my silver - he put it at the furthest end of the counter, from the door; I saw the prisoner about ten o'clock that morning - he came immediately the lad had gone down stairs; he asked for 1lb. of sausages - I served him with them; he gave me 1s., and I put 8d. in copper, which was the change, into the meat scale, but he did not take it up - he was at that time standing within reach of the bag- he left the shop without his change; a woman came in before he left - she stood about three yards from where the bag of silver was; the lad came into the shop while she was there, and the bag of silver was missed; I ran out to look for the man, and when I returned, the woman was still there, and they said had been there all the time - no other person but the prisoner and the woman came into the shop after the bag of money was put down till it was missed - I could not find the man.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe you found the man afterwards by his coming to your shop to ask you if you accused him of taking any property of yours? A. Yes, he came with another person, and I charged him with it - he asked if I had any charge against him; I gave him in charge - he was let go, and came of his own accord to the office next day, but I understood there was some guarantee for his appearing; the woman was not searched - another man surrendered; I did not describe him as a tall man - I did not say so to Mr. Lamonte; the prisoner said he worked at the engine, at the London-docks - I went there, and Heatley was taken, as he worked at the engine.
   EDWARD HEALE. I am in the prosecutor's service. On the 7th of December he gave me a bag of money - I laid it on the end of the counter; there was a scale about a foot from it - there was no one there but my master; I went into the cellar for a piece of beef - I returned in five minutes: my master told me to take the bag, and it was gone - there was only one woman in the shop, two or three yards from where the bag had been; my master went out, and the woman remained till he returned. On the evening of the 8th of December the prisoner came to the shop, said he had heard that master had been robbed, and that he was the man who had been in the shop at the time; I told him my master was not at home, but he would be in half an hour - he went out, and returned at that time.
 Cross-examined. Q. This was after your master had been making inquiries at the London-docks? A. Yes, and a man named Heatley had surrendered.
