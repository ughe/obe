KATE BAXTER. I keep a confectioner's shop at 866, Garratt Lane,
Wandsworth. I remember a man coming into the shop on the after
noon of December 14 and asking for twopennyworth of cough drops.
He was alone (John Smith, Ernest Lee, and George Leo came forward
in the dock). I do not recognise the man. I served him; he gave
me a florin which I put into a box at the back of the shop, and
handed him the change. There were no other florins in the box.
After he left a police officer came in, and I showed him the florin,
which he examined and then returned to me, and I put it into the
box again. The same officer came again afterwards and I handed
him the coin and he put his initiale on it. He came back again
the same evening and I gave him the coin which he had marked,
and be took it away. I identify the packet of cough drops.

ra Lane. I remember a man coming in on the afternoon of Del
cember 14 and asking to be shown some socks. I identify him as
George Lee. He asked me for a 2 d. pair of socks and gave me
piece of silver and I gave him the change. I could not swear it
was a two-shilling piece he gave me. I put the coin into my pooket,
where it remained until closing time. After that a police officer
came, on the following day, and I showed him the coins I had in
the box where I had put wbat I had taken on the previous day. The
coin I had from the man for the sooks was there. Mr. Gilan
selected a coin from three others and said it was a bad one. There
were three or four florins in the box. He took one, said it was
bad, and marked it. I identify that coin. On the following Satur
day I went to the police station and identified one man as being
the one to whom I had sold the sooks. The socks before me are the
kind I sell, and the same price.
