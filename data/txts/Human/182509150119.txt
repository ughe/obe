 EDWARD JONES re-examined. Q. What made you so confident that he would be acquitted? A. Because I thought him innocent.
   FREDERICK WEIGHTMAN. I am Emma Weightman's brother - she is a servant out of place - it is my painful duty to say that she is not a person fit to be believed on her oath - I could not believe her myself.

 1545.  JEREMIAH SULLIVAN, JOHN KEMP, and RICHARD BUTLER, were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , nine pairs of stockings, value 12 s., the goods of William Kipling.
   JOSEPH WILSON. I am in the employ of Mr. William Kipling, haberdasher, of Cheapside. On the evening of the 22d of July, between seven and eight o'clock, I saw Kemp and Butler come into the shop - I was at the farther end of the shop, tying up a parcel - they asked to see some braces, and tendered a shilling to pay for them - the officer then came in and took them. Sullivan was brought in soon afterwards with these nine pairs of hose - I had seen them safe in the shop, within about a foot of the door - any body going by might have taken them.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. Did the prisoners select a pair of braces? A. Yes, and paid a shilling for them - they were at the end of the shop - I was astonished when the officer came in and took them.
   CHARLES HERDSFIELD. I am a City officer. On the evening of the 22d of July I saw the three prisoners in company in Leadenhall-street. I followed them to Mr. Kipling's shop. Butler had this bag in his hand. When they got to the corner of Queen-street they remained there some time - then went up Bow-lane and returned, and went into a shop three or four doors before they came to Mr. Kipling's. They then looked at another shop - a boy came out and looked at them. Butler and Kemp then went into Mr. Kipling's shop. Sullivan took the stockings from the door, and put them into the bag, which he rolled up under his arm, and crossed the road - I followed, and took him - I got assistance to secure him, while I took the other two.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you go into the other shop which they went into? A. Yes, and they had bought there a pair of stockings. which they paid for - I searched Kemp and Butler, and found the stockings.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 SULLIVAN'S Defence. I was going to Fleet-market to meet my brother. I saw this parcel of stockings lying on the ground. I took them up, and put them into my bag. The officer came and took me. I had never seen them others before.
 KEMP'S Defence. I met Butler, he said he was going to buy stockings, and asked me to go with him, which I did; he gave two shillings and sixpence for them. When we came out, I said, "You want a pair of braces, you have forgotten them;" we then went into this shop to buy them, and gave one shilling for them; while we were there the gentleman came and took us.
 BUTLER'S Defence. I met Kemp at the corner of Castle-street, Whitechapel. I said I was going to buy stockings and braces. I went and bought the stockings, but forgot the braces till I got out of the shop. I then went into Mr. Kipling's to buy them, and was taken - but I know not for what.
 SULLIVAN -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 KEMP -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 BUTLER -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months.
 1546.  EDWARD KNIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , a table spoon, value 10 s., the goods of Thomas White.
 THOMAS WHITE. I am a  carpenter. I know the prisoner - he was a seaman- he frequently visited me; he came on the 1st of August, but I was not at home; the next morning there was a silver table-spoon missing - it had been used on the Sunday, and was in the dishes - he had dined with us on the Sunday - and invited himself to dine on the Monday.
 Prisoner. Q. Can you swear I came there on Monday to dinner? A. Yes.
   METCALF HOPGOOD. I am a silver-smith, and live in Bishopsgate-street. The prisoner brought this spoon to me, on Monday, the 1st of August, between three and four o'clock, for sale.
   JOHN GREEN. I took the prisoner into custody. He said he would take me where the spoon was.
