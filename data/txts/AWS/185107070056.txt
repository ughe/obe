1501. JOHN SCHOFIELD and JOHN EFFERANCE, stealing 1 hand-
kerchief, value 28. the goods of Charles Lacey, from bis person Schofield
having been before convicted.
JoHN LARKE. I live in Goswell-road. On 7th July, about 3 o'clock in
the afternoon, I was in London-wall, and saw the prisoners behind a gentle-
man, and saw Efferance put his hand into the gentleman's pocket-Schofield
fell back, so as to screen him as much as possible from my observation-the
gentleman turned into Basinghall-street-I went after him, and asked if be
had lost anything- then ran after the prisoners, when they saw me they
began to run, and I called out 'Stop thief! kept them in sight down
Pbilip-lane, Addle-street, and Aldermanbury, where I lost sight of them
for a few minutes, and then Efferance was taken by Hodges-I went to
Moor-lane station, and gave the charge, and Schofield was brought in.
Eferance. Q. Can you swear to us by our faces A. Yes; I lost sight
of you for a second.
Rev. CHARLES LACEY. I am the rector of Allhallows, London-wall On
7th July, I was in London-wall, I turned into Basinghall-street, and the last
witness came to me, and in consequence of what be said, I found I bad lost a
silk pocket-handkerchief, which I had when I left home, a few minutes before
-this (produced) is it-I know it by its general appearance- turned
round when Larke spoke to me, went into London-wall, and be pointed out
two men to me, who were walking along the pavement towards Wood-street,
a little distance before us-Larke called out Stop thief ef!"-the two men
instantly began to run, and I saw them run round the corner of Philip-lane,
and the mob followed-] went down Philip-lane, and saw a woman holding
up a handkerchief-] went, and saw it was mine I took it, and gave it to
the police-sergeant.
ANN FRY. I am the wife of Jobn Fry, of 8, Philip-lane. As I was
coming down-stairs, I saw a handkerchief in my passage, I picked it up, and
gave it to the last witness-this is it-my door always stands open.
