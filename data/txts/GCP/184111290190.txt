227. JAMES GIRDLESTONE was indicted for stealing, on the 18th
of November, 1 box, value 6d.; 1 coat, value 78.; 1 pair of trowsers,
value 2s. ; 2 pairs of breeches, value 48. ; 3 shirts, value 3s. ; 3 cravats,
value 18.; 2 shirt-fronts, value 1s. : 3 stockings, value 6d. ; 3 handker.
chiefs, value 6d. ; 1 printed book, value 6d. ; 1 pair of gloves, value Id.;
and 1 pack of cards, value 4d.; the goods of John Coker.
JOHN COKER. I am a private in the 16th regiment of Lancers. I left
a box at No. 25, Dean-street, Westminster-it contained the coat and other
articles stated this now produced is my box and the property in it.
John Moore. I live at No. 25, Dean-street. On the 18th of Novem-
ber, about half past ten o'clock in the forenoon, the prisoner came and said
that John Coker had sent him for the box-I gave it him and its contents.
JOHN COKER. I did not send him for the box--I had known him for
about ten days.
JAMES CLIFT (police-constable A 121.) I found the box in Castle
street, Long-acre--it was not locked--the prisoner said at the station
that he had not been sent for it-he said it was at Castle-street, Long.
acre.
Prisoner's Defence. A young man told me to go for it; I gave it to
him, and he was taken in Charles-street, Long-acre.
JAMES CLIFT re-examined. The other man was taken, but released.
GUILTY. Aged 21.-Confined Four Months.
228. THOMAS REDDINGTON and MICHAEL JENNINGS were
indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November, 1 chair, value 8d., the
goods of John Hargrave; and that they had been before convicted of
felony; and CHARLES UNDERWOOD for feloniously receiving the
same, well knowing it to have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.
WILLIAM VICTORY. I live with my father, in New-street, Westminster.
I was in Husband-street, St. James's, and saw Reddington take a chair
that belonged to Mr. Hargrave-he gave the chair to Jennings, who
carried it across the road to No. 2, Hopkins-street-Reddington went up
Hopkins-street, and went away-it was between seven and eight o'clock in
the evening.
ELEANOR HARGRAVE. I am the wife of John Hargrave. I missed a
chair like this on that day, but I have not sworn to this, it is so very frivo-
lous- I do not wish to be hard on the boys.
JOHN WILLS (police-constable C 125.) I went to Underwood's, in
Hopkins-street-he has an apartment there-I asked him where he got
this chair from-he said some of the boys had brought it-he did not know
what boys--I found it near ten o'clock in the evening.
Reddington's Defence. I was not out after five o'clock that evening,
which my aunt can prove. The officer took me as I was going to bed,
at half-past eleven, in my aunt's room. Victory is a thief. I was going
down the Haymarket one night, and he asked me to pawn a handker-
chief for him. I got 23. on it. I did not see him that night, and I wanted
something to eat; I spent a shilling, and the next morning I told him
bad spent the money– I gave him the ticket.
Jenning's Defence. I was not in the parish that night till ten o'clock.
GEORGE KEMP (police-constable N 82.) I produce a certificate of
Jenning's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office-(read)-he
is the person.
