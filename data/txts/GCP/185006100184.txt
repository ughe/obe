MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you tell him you could not take him on that
charge? A. No; I was willing to take him, but he seemed to hesitate about
charging him—the carman fetched me, I do not know his name- he told me
to come to Ann's-place to take a man into custody-he said his master wanted
to charge a man; I asked what for, and he said he did not know-I swear he
said that I went with him, and waited there a few moments-there wa
some conversation-the foreman first said he would charge him, and then he
said he would not said, "If you charge him, you take the responsibility
on yourself"-- he said he wished to have it settled some other way, he wished
to have his money-they had some conversation about the goods—I did not
hear exactly what they said-upon my solemn oath I took no gin-and-water.

Cross-examined. Q. What about the bill? A. 1 met Mr. Edwards that
morning, and at his request I went to the prisoner about it-I am a commer-
cial agent-I have no office-I have no place of business-I do not practise
on 'Change-I am a general agent- have lived at 4, Ely-court, a few
weeks-I merely occupy the bedroom there on the ground-floor-I never
saw the prisoner till 1st January-about three weeks afterwards I had to go
to Whitecross-street prison and I saw him there I have lived three weeks
where I do now I lived before that at Camden-town-I was coming to

town I think I lived (upon my word, I almost forget) at 2, Pemberton-tom,
