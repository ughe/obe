I got to know Perry man about eight years ago, and the company has been doing printing for him since that time. He was then connected with the "Mining Herald, in old Jewry, which we printed. Harris was the "second" (clerk) in the "Herald" office. Perryman paid the ac count by cheques. Afterwards Perryman was to be found at Herbert Smith's in Moorgate Street. I also saw Harris there. The photograph produced is not the Herbert Smith I was introduced to. He was dark, about 27 years of age, and had a black moustache. This would be about 1902. I was introduced to Herbert Smith by Perryman. I printed circulars for Smith. We did not set up the type. The formes were sent and we only did the machining. We had the formes from Bleach, printer, of Sumner Street. Harris gave orders as to how many to print, and the accounts were paid by cheques drawn by Herbert Smith, until the guarantee by Perryman in Sep tember, 1904. I asked Perryman for the guarantee because we were not getting paid. The original was handed to Messrs. Lewis and Lewis. I had nothing to do with it after I had passed it into my directors' hands. After the guarantee Perryman always paid—by cheque. In all £931 4s. was debited to Herbert Smith. We also did printing for Messrs. Chalmers and Cox, for which Harris gave the orders. We had £55 from Cox. Chalmers and Cox were introduced by Perryman. I should not have given them credit otherwise. If they had not paid I should have looked to Perryman. We also did printing for A.W. Fraser, of Lombard Street. Fraser used to pay by cheque. In all the account was £376 9s. 6d., and in this case also Harris used to bring the orders. I never had conversation with Perryman about Lombard Street and Fraser. The printing was of the same character—circulars; but I never read them. We did not do any printing for "The Financial Guardian." We did printing for Tyler and Co. to their order. Tyler was introduced, I think, in 1905. His account was £304 4s. I had no conversation with Perry man about Tyler and Co. The delivery note produced shows that in March of this year I was directed to deliver some circulars of Tyler's to "The Financial Guardian." The cheques produced from August, 1905, to August, 1906, are drawn to my company by Perryman. Mr. Perryman's guarantee did not become operative until January, 1905. The company afterwards brought an action against Perryman, Messrs. Lewis and Lewis acting for them. Judgment was signed, and Perry man was served with a notice in bankruptcy at the suit of the Argus Printing Company for £351 14s. 9d.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Marshall Hall.
