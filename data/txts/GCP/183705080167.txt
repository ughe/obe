Richard LARTER. I am pot-boy at the Castle public-house, which is
about two hundred yards from Mr. Gotsell's. On Monday evening, the
3rd of April, about ten minutes or a quarter past nine o'clock, I saw the
prisoner drinking a pint of ale at our bar with a cab man—they stopped
about ten minutes or a quarter of an hour I did not see them go away,
as I was sent out, but I saw them there for that time I had seen the cab
man once before, and the prisoner often before, but not at our house I
know the time by going out with my beer. 10

Joan Pocock I am a policeman. In consequence of information, on
the 3rd of April, I went to Mr. Gotsell's house, about eleven o'clock
the private door was open-I saw the prisoner in the shop, and several
gentlemen there, with Mr. Gotsell and two or three policemen I asked the
prisoner, distinetly, who he had spoken to outside the house after his
master left-he said he had spoken to no one, nor opened the door after
bolting it when his master left-he said he had bolted up all the doorg
and secured the house immediately that his master left pointed out to
him the impossibility of any person going into the house without his know
ledge-I observed that there were no marks of violence in any part of
the house he said they might have been concealed in the house--Mr.
Gotsell heard part of this conversation, but there were a good many people
about-I told him to be careful, and said, "You are not obliged to answer
these questions, but you had better tell the truth" Mr. Gotsell was asking
him how the thieves got into the house, and I wished to know, that I
might detect the robbers- I did not know who the parties were I had
not taken him into custody then I had not my police dress on, but he
knew who I was-1 came in with the sergeant-(looking at the examina-
