Cross-eramined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Had you known Penny at all!
A. No- I swear he is the person, because I took such particular notice of
him-1 do not go from his dress in particular, but from his appearance and
features—the place I saw him at, was not a quarter of a mile from the prose-
cutor's.
Court. Q. Have you weighed the lead ? A. Yes, it weighs fifty-four
pounds.

Cross-examined by MR. METCALE. Q. What does that purport to be
(handing a paper to the witness)? A. “Bought of T. A. Hockley, one
quarter and 22ībs."-1 know nothing of Hockley--the weight here does not
correspond with the lead I found, it is four pounds lighter--the date corre-
sponds- I went into Garrett's cellar, and saw a cistern in Garrett's cellar-
whether it was being built I cannot say, or whether leaden pipe of this sort
would be used in making it, he said, before the Magistrate, that the leaden
pipe was to fit a pipe that was in his cistern ; but that pipe has never been cut
-when I first saw the lead, it was standing by Penny-I cannot say where
Garrett came from— I first saw him put the lead on his shoulder-I am sure
he was not there before I was looking round the corner, and I saw the lead
put on bis shoulder, and saw him walk away with it-I had then been there
five minutes- I cannot see any mark on the lead.
Court. Q. Have you any doubt about Penny being the same man?
A. None at all, and I followed Garrett to his very door-I found Penny at
work, at a job in Regent-street, for his master, Mr. Coorze.
MR. PAYNE to John RICKETTS. Q. Did you not say this weigbed about
fifty pounds ? A. Fifty-four pounds-I said before the Magistrate that it
was fifty pounds, because I did not wish to convict the man, and I hope the
Court will deal leniently with him, he has a wife and three children-I had
the weight of the lead chalked down two or three days previously, I weighed
it myself, as there was to be an estimate sent in.
PENNY--Guilty. Aged 30.--Recoramended to mercy by the Jury-
Confined Three Months.
GARRETT-GUILTY. Aged 27.-Confined One Year.
