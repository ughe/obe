 763.  JOHN ARUNDELL was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of February , 1 cart, value 3l., the goods of George Martin.
 GEORGE MARTIN. I am a  sawyer. On the 3rd of February, about seven o'clock in the morning, I left a cart at the corner of New-street, Bishopsgate; I chained it to a scaffold-pole about four that evening, and missed it the next morning, about nine o'clock; I afterwards found it in Wentworth-street, at Spooner's, who keeps a livery-yard- I know nothing of the prisoner; I got information, and went to the prisoner's house, in Widegate-alley - he is a sweep; I asked if he had not got a cart in Wentworth-street, and he said he had - I asked how he came by it; he said he bought it on the Saturday morning, about seven o'clock - the officer then took him to the watch-house; he did not say where he bought it.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. You found that he is a sweep? A. Yes, and I believe he keeps a donkey or two - he said he bought the cart of two men on Saturday morning; I am sure he did not say Sunday morning.
 COURT. Q. Did he say who they were? A. No; he said he knew two men, and he was on bail a week to find them.
   JOHN SPOONER. I keep a yard. On Saturday, the 4th, I found this cart in my yard, which I had never seen before- I inquired about, but could not hear any thing of it - the prisoner afterwards came to me about it, and asked if I was not surprised at seeing that strange cart there; I said Yes - he said, "It belongs to me - I bought it this morning for 13s.;" I knew him, and was then satisfied.
 Cross-examined. Q. What has been his character? A. An honest man.
   THOMAS HALL. I saw the cart from five to ten minutes after seven o'clock on Saturday morning.
   WILLIAM LAW. I went to the prisoner with the prosecutor - what he has stated is correct.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought it between seven and eight o'clock on Saturday morning - it was rather too large for me, and I took it to Mr. Spooner's.
     NOT GUILTY.
 764.  FREDERICK BRIDGE was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 1 trunk, value 2s., the goods of William Illman.
   THOMAS STANDOVER. I live opposite Mr. Illman's, in Dean-street, Soho. On the 16th of February I saw the prisoner and some others near the house - I saw the prisoner take a trunk - he put it under his arm, and ran away; Mr. Wheeler followed him - I know he is the boy.
   JAMES WHEELER. I was going out at the door, and Mr. Standover said the lad had run away with a trunk from Mr. Illman's - I went and took him with it.
   JAMES LUFF. I took the prisoner, and have the trunk.
   WILLIAM ILLMAN. This is my property, and was in one of my windows.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.

 767.  ELLEN BLYTH and CATHERINE BARRY were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 1 bag, value 2d.; 4 shillings, 1 sixpence, and 3d. in copper, the property of William Nitingale , from his person.
 WILLIAM NITINGALE. I live in Lisle-street, Leicester-square, and am an  engraver. On the 12th of February I met the prisoners in Hanover-street, Long-acre, about three o'clock in the morning; they wished me to go home with them - I refused, stating that I had no money; I then felt Blyth's hand draw my bag and money out of my pocket, as I was standing opposite the house of an acquaintance, considering whether I should wake him up to let me in; I thought Blyth gave the bag and money to Barry, but I did not see her do it - I had 4s. 6d. in silver, and 3d. in copper in it - I said if they did not give me my money, I would give them in charge; two Policemen were coming by, and they took the money up from the ground - both the prisoners said they had not got it; I am sure it was Blyth's hand which took it from me.
 Blyth. Q. Did you not meet me in Hanover-street? A. I was standing there, and you came up to me - I was not so sober as I am now - I did not say I had a small trifle, and give her the money.
   JOHN BENNETT. I am a Policeman. I was in Belton-street, and heard Blyth abusing the prosecutor, and using very bad language - I was going up to remove them for being disorderly, and when I got near, the prosecutor called Police - I went up, and seized Barry; she threw from her hand a purse, enclosed in this handkerchief, which she afterwards denied being hers - I said, "This is the woman that had the purse," and the prosecutor, whom I did not consider at all tipsy said, "This one (meaning Blyth) took it from me" - I took both the prisoners to the station, and found on them 4s. 6d. in silver, and 3d. in copper.
