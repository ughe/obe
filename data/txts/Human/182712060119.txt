 JAMES BROWN. I live at  No. 4, Queen-street, Chelsea, in the parish of St. Luke, and am a coal-heaver. The prisoner is my son-in-law, and has been to sea; I married his mother - he came to my house when he pleased; he slept there on Monday and Tuesday, the 3d and 4th of December; and on Wednesday, after dinner, he went off, to go to Gravesend. I missed twenty-three sovereigns, and 15l. in Bank notes, on Thursday, when I came home to dinner - it was in a box; I may not have seen it safe for four or five months before - my wife found the box broken open.
   JOHN JONES. Between five and six o'clock last Friday, the watchman brought the prisoner to the watch-house, charged with robbing Brown of 38l.; I asked Brown how he knew it was him; he said, "Because nobody else was there;" his wife was not there: he said, that in coming home the prisoner had ran away - he called Stop thief! the watchmen caught him, and they brought him to me - I found seven sovereigns and 16s. 1 1/2d. on him. When I took the charge, the prisoner said he had taken the money at different times; he said, "I took eleven sovereigns last Wednesday;" he said so to Brown, when I was taking the charge.
 JAMES BROWN re-examined. I went to Gravesend after him, and brought him back; he offered me seven sovereins to let him go, but did not then say he had taken the money.
 Prisoner. I went to look after a ship, and on Thursday my father came and took me.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 22.
 256.  JOHN CHAPMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of November , 1 sheep, price 30s., the property of Richard Franklyn.
 SECOND COUNT, for wilfully and feloniously killing the same, with to steal the carcase.
   WILLIAM JOHNSON. I am carter to Richard Franklyn, who lives at Totteridge, and has land at Hendon. On the 14th of November, between five and six o'clock at night, I saw all his sheep right - there were twenty: I went next morning, between seven and eight o'clock, and one was gone - I saw some blood on the ground, and a few nuts at the gate of the next field; I saw an ass had been tied to a post, near the gate; I know it was an ass, by the hoof marks; I saw some wool on the little quick hedge, as if a sheep had been driven through. I got a warrant, and went to the prisoner's house at Edgware, and found a leg of mutton in a cupboard - it had been in the pot - it was boiled a little, and all greasy. I found a sheep's head and some fat in a basket - it appeared fresh killed; we found the skin and entrails in the place behind; the skin was not well taken off; they were Leicester long-wool sheep, two years old - we call them two-teethed wether sheep; I found such teeth in the head; I believe the skin to be the skin of master's sheep; and the ear of it, which is marked, is here.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is your master here? A. No; I know he has no other name.
   THOMAS PARTRIDGE. I am a farmer, and live in Hendon parish. I went to the prisoner's house with the patrol and constable; he is a green-grocer, and keeps an ass and small cart; we found the cart in the yard, cleanly washed out, and set up to dry; we went into the house, and saw a woman, who said "For God's sake, what do you want?" we found a leg of mutton in the cupboard, and what Johnson has stated.
   DANIEL FRANKLYN. I know Richard Franklyn, and his farm - I work on the road as a labourer. On the 14th of November, about a quarter-past eight o'clock in the evening, I met the prisoner leading an ass, drawing a load in a cart; it appeared to me to be bed-clothes - it was on a Tuesday; he was between Edgware and Hendon - he was going towards Hendon, and on the off side of the cart, I saw written, "John Chapman, Little Stanmore," and behind the cart was a man named Samuel Weedon, shoving; he knew me, and said "How are you old man;" I said "How are you?" it was on Tuesday - I cannot say whether it was the 13th or 14th.
   STANBRIDGE GINGER. I am a butcher. I went to the prisoner's house, on the 15th of November, with a search-warrant - I found the leg of mutton, with a loin to it, parboiled, it was not killed in a butcher-like manner - I afterwards went, and saw the other sheep in the field; they were all marked, as this is, in the ear, as long-wool sheep generally are; each shepherd marks his own sheep.
   WILLIAM JOHNSON. I saw all the sheep safe on Wednesday, the 14th, and missed one next morning; here is the sheep's ear; it has a cut on it; it is the same mark as that on the other nineteen, and there is a mark on the rump of the skin, but it is nasty, as the entrails have been wrapped in it, and it is probably rubbed out; here is the remains of a pitch-mark, and the head was marked with ruddle.
 Cross-examined. Q. Will you swear to any mark in the ear? A. Yes.
 MR. PHILLIPS to STANBRIDGE GINGER. Q. Are you not an officer? A. Yes - I know Weedon; he is gone away somewhere - I have not looked for him, but other officers have; he lived at Finchley. The prisoner bore a very good character.
 Prisoner's Defence. Weedon brought the sheep to my house; he came to me, and I let him a room, and next day took my cart to get his goods - I went into a public-house; Weedon and his brother came in, and sat there from four to ten o'clock; we then got into my cart, and Weedon drove it home, and when we got within a quarter of a mile, he hoisted me out of the cart, as I was tipsy, and asked if I knew what was in it - I said No; he then said, "It is a woolley bird;" I felt in the cart, and said it was a sheep; he said he had picked it up, and it would do for his dogs - I said, next day, "We shall have a piece of work about it;" he said No; he skinned it next day.
 STANBRIDGE GINGER, He said nothing about Weedon, when we were at his house.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did he not mention it to Mr. Miller, the Magistrate? A. Yes - somebody was sent to find him.
   THOMAS PARTRIDGE. We have a warrant against Weedon respecting this sheep - we searched his house, at Finchley, but found nothing particular there; the prisoner said he had been drinking at a public-house, and got very tipsy - I have known him five or six years, and never knew him in trouble - I have known Weedon in trouble.
     NOT GUILTY.
