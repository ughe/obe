   Q.  Did you tell young Newman, when he came with the cow, if he dared to touch the cow or the place you would make him suffer for it? A.  No; nothing of the kind—I used no violence to him—I told him they had gives me a great deal of trouble after the money—I considered it only a fair bargain—I have had the cow ever since—the gates are open all day—I did not shut them before the boy went away—I went to the public-house when I was sent for—Newman or Eldridge sent for me—I did not desire the boy to go in and drink—I swear the boy did not demand to have the cow back, and I refuse—he said he would have the full amount, 11 l . 10 s .—when I did not place the full money before him he said he would not deal with me for it—nobody demanded the cow—he did not say he would not go away without the cow or the money—he went away contented—he might have had the cow if he chose—I did not stop it—he might have had the full amount of money—they applied for it afterwards to Mrs. Eldridge, but not to me—if he had come for it, or any body had applied for it, he might have had the cow or the money—I will deliver it to the constable now, if you please—Mrs. Eldridge was applied to for the money, and she referred them to me—if they had come for the cow or money, they might have had either.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did they ever come to your house to demand the cow? A.  They did not—I have had three houses in the neighbourhood. NOT GUILTY.
