 Verdict, both Guilty, and both confessed to previous convictions.
 Detective-inspector  SIMMONDS , D Division, stated that in 1901 Riley was sentenced to three years' penal servitude at the North London. Sessions for horse stealing. On January 22, 1894, at the same sessions he was sentenced to five years' penal servitude also for horse stealing, and there was a prior conviction for horse stealing on Feb ruary 4, 1889. Altogether he had had 13 years' penal servitude for horse stealing, and was at the present time reporting to the police on his licence. For years he had been extensively engaged in stealing horses from Hackney Marshes and the surrounding districts, which he know intimately. With regard to Friend, the police had great difficulty in detecting a man of his class, as he was engaged in horse dealing. Witness had pulled him up taking horses along which had turned out to be correct horses, and Friend had then made a com plaint to the Commissioner that he had been prevented from getting an honest living.
 Detective-sergeant  HAWKINS  said that Friend had against him only one conviction of three months for horse stealing, with the excep tion of the conviction yesterday for stealing a mare and a gelding, but he was the associate of notorious horse thieves, one of whom was sentenced to four years' penal servitude and another to 18 months last Sessions. There was a great deal of this horse stealing going on, people turning their horses out to grass in the early part of the year, and the sufferers were generally poor people who could ill afford to lose their animals.
 Judge Lumley Smith said he was satisfied that Riley was a con  firmed horse stealer or dealer in stolen horses, and he could not pass a less sentence than Five years' penal servitude. Friend, Three years' penal servitude for each offence, to run concurrently.
