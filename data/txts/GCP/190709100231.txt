BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT.
(Tl.ursday, September 19.)
WHITE, Henry (58, butcher); feloniously receiving one Ralli car,
be gelding, one set of harness, and other articles, the goods of
William McGinty, well knowing the same to have been stolen.
Mr. Sydney E. Williams prosecuted; Mr. Purcell defended.
WILLIAN MCGINTY, 718, Barking Road, Plaistow, laundryman.
On July 22 I visited my stables at Portland Road, and found them
all correct. The next day the stable had been broken into, and the
gelding, car, harness, and lamps stolen. On August 13 I saw the
car at Minories Police Station.
JOHN BRYCE, 417, Holloway Road. I have stables in Holloway
Road. On Sunday, August 11, between three and four, prisoner
drove up to my house with my boy. He had my horse in the trap,
my boy having lent it him to trot out with the trap. Prisoner
wanted me to buy the trap for £16. I went for a run with it, and
it seemed all right. I eventually agreed to give him £10 and my
old trap for it, which I did on the Wednesday evening at six o'clock.
Meanwhile on the next two days I went out with the trap, and on
the second one I was stopped by a constable, with whom I went to
the Minories Police Station, and was detained for six hours till my
statement was investigated, on which I was released. On the next
day, the 16th, prisoner came about 5.30 to receive the money. As
he was signing the receipt Detective Eustace oame in and arrested
him.
Cross-examined. I had never seen prisoner before the Sunday
that he called. Prisoner first called at my stables and borrowed
the cob to drive the trap to my house. I am pretty well known in
the neighbourhood, and sometimes buy traps in the course of busi-
Dess. After wanting £16 prisoner came down to £13 before accept
ing £10. My old trap was worth about £5. I told prisoner I had
Bat the money at the time, and he agreed to wait till the Wednes.
day. He gave me his name as Henry Jones, 29, Halton Road,
Islingtoa. I arranged to pay prisoner in cash. When he had
written the receipt," I said, I will go outside and blot it." Whilst
I was going out, prisoner said, "I am glad you are satisfied with
the trap, and I will do anything for you in the future." I then
handed the receipt to Detective Eustace, who asked who prisoner
was. I told him, and he informed prisoner that he was a police
officer, and understood that the trap had been stolen, asking him
where he got it. Prisoner said he got it from & man, outside A
public-house in Holloway Road or Caledonian Road on August 9
for £13. The man was a stranger. The market in Caledonian Road
& well known place for buying horses. Prisoner said there had
been horse in the trap and he had taken it out and pulled the
trap round to his stables.
