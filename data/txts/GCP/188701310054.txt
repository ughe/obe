THIRD COURT.-Wednesday and Thursday, February 2nd and 3rd, 1887,
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
249. KATE ROE (32), Unlawfully obtaining by false pretences from
William Acland May 291. 158.; also conspiring with other persons to
defraud Her Majesty's Postmaster-General of 291. 158.
be MESSRS. BAGGALLAY and GILL Prosecuted; MR. MUIR Defended.
THOMAS GOLDING. I am & post office receiver at the Lupus Street
office, Pimlicoon 23rd June the prisoner came in to make a deposit of
18.--I handed her a declaration form, and requested her to fill it in with
her name and address and occupation-she wrote on it, and handed it
back-I noticed she had only written Ann Dean, and her signature at
the bottom "Ann Dean"-she asked that the acknowledgment should
be addressed to the post-office, Lupus Street, and she would call for it-
I said "I see you have not filled in your address on the declaration," and
I asked her for it-I wrote that in myself she said "10, Grant Road,
Battersea”-I thought it strange that she should ask for the acknow
ledgment to be sent to my office, the address being so far away, and that
fixed the matter in my mind-I entered the number of the book and the
number of the deposit on the sheet, and handed the savings bank pass-
book to her, and asked her to put her signature in it- she then gave the
book to me, and I entered the deposit of 18.-the book was number 6100,
with the name of the Lupus Street office on it, and her name I am not
sure if I put her address on the book-I saw her write the name of Ann
Dean on it-I made the entry in the book of 18., and put on it the office
stamp and my initials-she said she was a widow, and I entered that on
the declaration sheet while she was writing her name in the book this
is the ordinary deposit-sheet for the 23rd June-ordinary deposits are
those paid where the account is opened the sheet is not in the same
state now as when I made the entry, my figure of ls. has been tampered
with, and apparently 301. has been written by the side of it-there were
only three deposits on that day; 18., 18., and 128. I put the total 148. at
the bottom of the sheet-since it has left my place 301. has been added, and
the "1" has been tampered with-301. is the limit of the amount of a
deposit on one day-the prisoner never paid me any other sum-I have no
doubt whatever she is the woman-when the matter was being inquired
into I gave a description of the woman who made the deposit, to Mr.
Everett-a depositor can draw out money standing to his credit at any
office he likes, by simply sending up a demand note, when a warrant 15
sent to pay it at any office.
