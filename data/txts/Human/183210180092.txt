 COURT. Q. Do we understand that she never asked you to bring her any thing, but accepted what you took her? A. She once asked me to bring her a weddingring, to persuade her landlady that she was married; I asked for her in the name of Mrs. May - I never gave her any name of my own.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Had you not told the landlady that you were married? A. No, Sir; I never spoke to her only to ask if Mrs. May was at home - I had robbed my father before I saw the prisoner; I called at her house one day, when she was not at home - I sent her one letter, but no more; I had given my father's property to a woman of the town, named Lee, before I knew the prisoner - I had visited Lee; I had been in the habit of robbing my father for five months - I was sometimes without any thing in my pocket to give to women; I remember telling the prisoner, shortly before our acquaintance terminated, that I had overheard my father, and mother talking about sending me to sea - I told her that I had got away my boxes, and I said perhaps I might get a situation; I have been to bed with May and with Lee - what I gave them was the price of their prostitution; I used at that time to wear a frock-coat - I told May that I was going on for eighteen years of age, on purpose to deceive her; she wore some of the ornaments I gave her about the street - I remember taking her to the theatre one night; and when we came away I told her she had better not go home, as I thought it not unlikely my father might come - I have taken tea at her house, but I never dined or supped there; I have drank there, we have sent for brandy, and it has been paid for out of the money the things fetched - I had been in custody on a charge of robbing my father before I mentioned this.
 COURT. Q. Did she have the silk dress? A. Yes, she did.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long had you been in custody before you told this? A. Three or four days; I had been in the New prison, Clerkenwell - I came from there this morning; May had seen me in my father's shop, after I gave her some things - Phillips once said,"I wonder your father does not find it out - if he does I shall get the worst of it;" I thought May looked into my father's shop rather hard the second night I - spoke to her, and I told her not to look in so hard, as I lived there, and she said she would not - Saunders was with her that night; I never told her that my father kept the shop, but his name was over the door; I told the prisoner my name after the Sunday I speak of - she was to pawn the goods I took her, to buy a silk dress; the goods pawned might come to more than 10l. - I took her two pairs of candlesticks.
   EDMUND DAVIS(Police-constable E 86). I took the prisoner on the 31st of August, in High Holborn; I had been to her lodging, in Pettit's-court, Hanway-street - I knew her before, and saw her remove her goods there; I found on her a pair of bracelets, a pair of pearl earrings, a wedding-ring, two caddy-ladles, a buckle, a coral necklace, a coral negligee, and a duplicate for a pair of gold ear-rings; I told her I took her on suspicion of being concerned in the robbery at Mr. Steele's - she said they were the things that young Steele had given her.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was any one with her? A. Yes, Phillips.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating, that the prosecutor's son had been in the habit of visiting her - that he represented himself as nineteen years of age - and that he had the property from a young man who owed him 10l.
     NOT GUILTY.
