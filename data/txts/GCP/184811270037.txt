Peter GoWLAND, I am house-surgeon at the London Hospital. The
deceased was brought there on the evening of 26th June-I attended him-
he had a contused wound about an inch above the left eyebrow-it had bled
-it was about half an inch in length, and penetrated to the bone-it was a
most common wound, such as we see every day, not serious at all--he
remained in the hospital, and went on very well, until the 6th of July-erysi-
pelas then extended from the wound, and he got worse; the brain became
affected, and he died-erysipelas is a common consequence of a blow on the
head-it certainly was in consequence of this wound, and of that he died-he
was very drunk when he came in I heard that he had been in the habit of
drinking a great deal—I did not know him before-a fist would not have pro-
duced such a wound; there would have been contusion all round, and a dif-
ferent wound altogether.
