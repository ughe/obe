2986. JAMES TOOLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of Oc-
tober, 2 bushels of oats, value 138.; and 1 sack, value 28.; the goods of
Sarah Ellis, his mistress,
SARAH ELLIS. I am a widow, and live in Salisbury-street, Portman
Market. The prisoner was in my service-I missed a sack of white Poland
oats from my granary on the 18th of October.
JOSEPH ABBOTT. I saw the prisoner about six or seven o'clock on the
evening of the 17th of October, carrying a sack, with something in it,
through Portman Market, in a direction from Mrs. Ellis's granary.
MILES CHILTON. I saw the prisoner on the evening of the 17th of
October, between six and seven o'clock, carrying a sack of white oats in a
direction from Mrs. Ellis's—the sack had holes in it.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Were you standing in Portman
Market? 4. Yes, be came so near to me that I could have pat my hand
on the sack— I was talking to Mr. Treadaway-I did notice the sack par-
ticularly.
John Davison. I ordered a sack of oats of the prisoner, and he
brought them to me-I gave it to the policeman.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you tell the prisoner to bring the oats?
4. He asked me on the Thursday before, where I bought my oats-I said
of a man in the Harrow-road-he said his mistress had got some very good
ones, which she could sell for 148., and I told him to bring me a sack on
the Saturday- I had dealt with Mrs. Ellis before he brought them on the
Monday- I did not pay him-I intended to pay Mrs. Ellis.
JOHN GRAINGER (police-sergeant D 21.) I went to the prosecutrix's
house--the prisoner came in and I said, “Your mistress has a serious charge
against you ; on Monday evening you took a sack of oats from her"-he
hesitated some minutes, and then said, “I did take them to Mr. Davison's."
SARAH ELLIS re-examined. I have examined this sack and the oats, and
I believe them to be mine-the prisoner never sells oats for me-if he had
he ought to have entered them, and these are not enteredI knew nothing
about them-I missed a sack on the Tuesday-I then made inquiries, and
found about this.
Cross-examined. Q. Is the book here? A. Yes, and these are not
entered—I had not sold Mr. Davison any thing for twelve months--the
last he had was a truss of straw-I do not send in my bills weekly, but
when they are asked for.
GUILTY. Aged 25.-Confined Six Months.
