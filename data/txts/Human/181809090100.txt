 1381.  RICHARD RUFFEN and GEORGE BOYCE were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of June , one handkerchief, value 5s., the property of the Rev. Charles Cecil Bates , Clerk, from his person.
 THOMAS WESTBROOK. I am a labourer. On the 20th of June, I was standing opposite the hustings, in  Covent-garden, and saw the prisoners in company. Boyce took a silk handkerchief out of Mr. Bates's pocket, and gave it to Ruffen. I told him of it, and pointed the prisoners out to the prosecutor; he took one, and I took the other to Bow-street - He gave them in charge to an officer, who found the handkerchief in Ruffen's breeches. I had seen them in company before. The prosecutor attended before the magistrate, and swore to the handkerchief in the presence of the prisoners, they said nothing to it. I know the prosecutor's name is Charles Cecil Bates.
 WILLIAM WESTCOAT. I am an officer. The prisoners were brought to me by Westbrook and Mr. Bates, who gave them in charge. I searched them, and found one handkerchief in Ruffen's hat, and another in his breeches, which the prosecutor claimed - I produce it.
 RUFFEN -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 BOYCE -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Life.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1382.  ELIZA RICHARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , one shawl, value 2s., and one bonnet, value 1s., the goods of John Byrne.
   SUSAN BYRNE. I am the wife of John Byrne, we live in Clement's-lane, Strand. On the 20th of August, about five o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the prisoner come out of my room with my bonnet and shawl; I followed her into the next passage, and asked her what she had got? she said it was nothing to me. I found my shawl and bonnet under her own shawl, and gave her in charge.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I took the prisoner in charge - She was intoxicated, and said if she did take it she did not know it. (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Confined One Month.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1383.  JOHN MAINWARING was indicted for stealing 36 caps, value 2l. 5s., the goods of Stephen Pige.
 STEPHEN PIGE. I am a  furrier. The prisoner was my servant. He went out with thirty-six caps to sell for me he never paid me the money, but absconded. I expected to receive the money, but not to see the caps again.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1384.  JOHN MORRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , two firkins, value 2s., and 10 gallons of lamp-black, value 14s., the goods of William Francis.
 HENRY COLLINGTON. I am shopman to Mr.  William Francis, who is an oilman and lives, in Long-acre, and has a warehouse in Mercer-street, the prisoner was his porter. The keys of the warehouse hung in the counting-house. I missed them about eight o'clock in the morning of the 27th of June, went to the warehouse, and saw the prisoner come down with two firkins of lamp-black under his arm. I watched him into Short's-gardens - He took them into a house belonging to one Hambleton. I met two officers, who went with me, and took him and the lampblack.
 RICHARD ROW WICKS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner and lamp-black at an old iron-shop in Short's-gardens; he said he took it from his master's warehouse.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. My mistress told me to take it to Soho-square. I left it in Short's-gardens while I got my breakfast.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.

 1386.  FRANCIS JORDAN and GEORGE RICE were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , from the person of William Stewart , one shirt, value 6s.; one handkerchief, value 3s.; a 1l. bank note, and the sum of 2l. 2s. in monies, numbered, his property.
   WILLIAM STEWART. I am a sailor. On the 7th of August, I was in company with the prisoners. I went to bed about two o'clock in the morning, I awoke in about ten minutes, and saw them feeling in my pocket; they put out the light, and ran down stairs. They were taken next day. The property was never found.
   PETER PETERSON. I am a watchman. I heard the alarm, and saw the prisoners running, I took Jordan; Rice got away - He was taken next day. Nothing was found on them.
 RICE'S Defence. He was drunk. I never touched his clothes.    NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
