   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Did you look at all the tubs in which dripping was usually put? A.  Yes, and I examined them all on my return—these tickets are given by the person in charge of the shop at the time, to those who bring the goods instead of cash, as perhaps we might not have change at the time—there was no deficiency of cash on the 9th of Sept.—if any of these tickets had been given after twelve o'clock that day, the money would appear among the cash—one ticket is given to the person, and one is put into the till, and in the evening, when we have sufficient change, the money is rolled up in the paper for the persons when they come for it—I am a yearly servant, and am paid quarterly—my wages have been raised from the 1st of Jan.—they were 16 l .; a-year, and are now 18 l .;
   JURY . Q.  Did you when you returned examine all the tubs? A.  Yes—there was only one that had dripping in—I looked into the others, and none of them had any. JOHN TABRANT. I am shopman to Messrs. Tucker and Co. I went to Gravesend on the 9th of Sept., about twelve o'clock—on the evening of the 8th I balanced the cash-book—I returned on the 25th, and on that evening Murrell made a communication to me, and on the 26th I communicated with one of the partners—the money in the till on the evening of the 8th corre sponded with the cash-book, when I balanced it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do not some of the nobility and gentry keep an account at your house for dripping and wax ends? A.  Yes—I do not know that the servants are allowed 1 d . a pound on dripping or wax ends brought to our house—if we buy the wax ends of servants, of course we give them the money—there is something allowed to the servant for himself—it is entered in the book as for goods bought—I have never seen the prisoner make entries in the evening, of goods that he had purchased in the morning—I will swear that he has never done so—I go to dinner at one o'clock, and to tea at five—I sometimes go to the Bank, and sometimes to collect money—I am perhaps absent two hours—the prisoner has found fault with me for what he said Was negligence—he has reprimanded me for neglect in making entries in the book—I am Mrs. Tucker's nephew—I remember some time ago omitting to enter 721bs. of candles sold to Messrs. Ferguson and Co., of Ox ford-street, and some sold to Watkins'—I do not remember omitting to enter goods sold to Hughes, in May Fair.
   Q.  Do you remember receiving goods from Mr. Clarkson that you did not enter till some days after, when the mistake was discovered? A.  I do not know whether I omitted it, the porter might have done so as well as myself—when Clarkson brought his invoice, he said there was something omitted, and I entered it then, and he paid the money to me—it does sometimes happen that parties deliver dripping and wax ends, and receive a ticket for them and do not get the money for several days—in that case the ticket is put by, and entered in the book on the day the money is paid sometimes, and sometimes not.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You said something about servants, what is that? A.  The wax ends are considered the perquisite of the butler or footman, and when they bring them we give them less than we do to a collector, but what ever we receive from a collector, or from a servant, we make an entry in the book of it, so that the book shows everything—there is no separate sum paid to a servant, except for the amount we buy—when the omission which has been asked about occurred, I had not received any money which I had not accounted for, but I had left out an item in the customer's bill, and my omit ing it caused the prisoner's displeasure. THOMAS MOSDELL SMITH. I am a partner in the house—Tarrant and Murrell were in our employ—I recollect another servant going to Gravesend, and on that occasion a communication was made to me indirectly by John Tarrant.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Has it not very often happened that checks have been cashed for your customers, or for neighbours? A.  No, it never happened to my knowledge—my department is the superintendence of the cash account and correspondence, not the manufacturing department—our house keeps no account with any nobleman or gentleman for dripping or wax ends, to my knowledge.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What do you mean by saying there is no account with any nobleman or gentleman? A.  I know there is one family where the dripping which is supplied by them goes to their credit—in general where there is dripping it goes to the account of the cook. Sergeant is on it—I broke it open—I delivered it to Mr. Blackmore in the same state as it was. ROBERT BLACKMORE. I am attorney for this prosecution. I received this paper and its contents from Mr. Smith, and delivered it to the officer—it contains a 5 l .; note and a sovereign. ISAAC BLOMFIELD. Iam servant to Mr. Edridge, of Oxford-street. On the 7th of Nov. last I went to the prosecutor's shop, in South Moulton-street—I paid the prisoner, who appeared to be a clerk there, 9 l .; 3 s . 10 d .—part of it was a 5 l .; note—this is the note I paid him that day—I know it by the writing on it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is the writing? A.  The name of Mr. Fresh field, 24, Rutland-street, Hampstead-road—when the prosecution was going on they came to my employer, who is a master tallow-chandler, in Oxford-street—(paper read)—"This 6 l .; is money over in the shop cash, supposed to belong to T. Serjeant, his own money not being right, and no other account can be given of it; it will be left in the cash drawer, to see if anything else comes to light on the subject. Nov. 4, 1843."
   MR. SMITH re-examined. Q.  Supposing there was money over or under in the shop cash-book, what was the prisoner's duty? A.  To tell me of it—here is the shop cash-book for the 4th of Nov.—here appears to have been 3 1/2 d . over that day—the prisoner never gave me any account of there being 6 l .; over in the shop cash.
