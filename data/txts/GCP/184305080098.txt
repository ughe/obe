ROBERT MALKERN. I keep a general sale-shop in Albion-street, Lambeth.
On the 4th of April I paid the prisoner 158. on Mr. Whitworth's account-
this is the receipt he gave me.
Cross-examined by Mr. Crouch. Q. How long have you dealt with the
prosecutor ? A. I cannot say-it was previous to the prisoner's coming-he
collected once a week-he took orders at the time I did not require the
prosecutor's receipt for any of the monies—(read—“ Received for George
Whitworth, 158., on account; 4th of April : JOSEPH HOLLYHEAD. Balance
due, 68. 103d.")-it was usual to leave a balance-he was to receive this
balance the next time.
JAMES STEVENS. I live in Wootton-street, Lambeth, and deal with Mr.
Whitworth. On the 4th of April I paid 11s. 6d. to the prisoner on his
account-he gave me this receipt.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you dealt with the prosecutor ? A.
About twelve months—I did not know the prisoner previous to his coming
into Mr. Whitworth's employ.
GEORGE WHITWORTH. The prisoner has not accounted for 15s. of Mr.
Malkern, or 118. 6d. from Mr. Stevens.
Cross-examined. Q. How long had he been with you? 4. Ten or twelve
months-he formerly came on commission-I was to give him 68. a gallon
for pickles, and so on-his time for being paid, was every Saturday night-
he was on a salary at this time when he came to me, I do not know whe-
ther he had any customers of his own-he went round to get customers-he
has no commission now, there is no commission due to him-I allowed him 15s.
a-week wages--when he was on commission he earned about 68.-he has a
wife and family-he retained some money about two months ago-it is put
down in the book, and he was to pay it off, and he paid about a quarter of it-
I never let him have money on any other occasion-he was then a defaulter
to the amount of 31. 8s. 4d., but in consideration of his wife and family, I
took him again-he did not return to me every night, but it was his duty to
do so-he has remained absent a day or two before—when he was a defaulter
before, he staid away a day or two-I do not know what orders he received
-when I took him he handed me a paper, but I had been after him, and had
taken the orders- I do not remember that I ever applied to my customers
for money which had been paid--the prisoner had a sale of hops besides what
he did for me, I had a good character of him from a customer-it is not
usual to have characters when persons come on commission.
Not GUILTY.
1531. MARY THOMAS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April,
1 gown, value 10s. ; 2 pictures and frames, 1s. 6d.; and 1 shilling; the pro-
perty of John Crowson.
Ann Crowson. I am the wife of John Crowson, of Hungerford-street,
Commercial-road. The prisoner lived servant with a lodger of mine at Christ-
mas last, and I took her as a lodger afterwards-she was to pay 18. a-week
for lodging and board-she worked at slop-work in the day, and went out in
the evening, but I do not know where she quitted my house rather ab-
raptly, and I missed two pictures, a gown, and a shilling- I found the pic-
tures at Raphael's, and the gown was at an eating-house-they are mine.
ELIZABETH RAPHAEL. I am the wife of Abraham Raphael, of Harper-
place, Back-lane. I know the prisoner by her using my shop-she came and
said she wanted to sell these two pictures for 1s. 6d. to make up her rent.
