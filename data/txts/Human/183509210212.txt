Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Do not you recollect re ceiving orders from Mr. Bray to go against Trevett? A.  I do not re collect it—he said he did not understand the business; he left it entirely to his men to go in what times they liked—I received a direction to go in whose time I thought proper—I do not recollect receiving positive direc tions from Mr. Bray to go against Trevett—I was conductor to the om nibus driven by Lee—I do not recollect going with Mann—there has been two Mann's at work—we did not receive directions from Mr. Bray to go against Trevett in particular—he said Trevett's time was a very good time, and so was Mr. Bird's, and wer could not do better than go in them—Lee was present when Mr. Bray gave those directions—I did not tell Teevett I had orders to go in his time, nor I did not hear Lee tell him so—I first went with the omnibus on a Monday—the regular line is from the Wheatsheaf to Bank.
   Q.  On that day did you go down Market-street, to the place where Mr. Trevett's omnibuses put up? A.  I do not know whether it was the first day—I certainly went to a public-house there, to dine, close by where Trevett puts up—that is out of the line of road—I remember going there several times.
   Q.  Do you remember when you went triumphantly down Market-street, saying, "We will soon dish up  the old Snob,  we will soon melt his wax for him?" A.  I did not say much a thing, nor hear such a thing—there was no one in my omnibus—I have not boasted that I would melt the cobler's wax.
   JURY . Q.  Had you orders from Mr. Bray to go down Market-street? A.  No, Sir; we went where we pleased to drive, and they followed us.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  But you followed them when you went to their house? A.  It was Mr. Gamble's house we went to—I know Trevett's men go to that house occasionally—I dined with two or three of them there that day—It is a house where coachmen and omnibus-men generally go.
   Q.  Do you mean to swear that they go out of their line of road? A.  We were not plying for passengers—we went to have our dinner.
   JURY . Q.  Was the observations about the time when you first began, or afterwards? A.  Afterwards.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Had you know Mr. Bray before you were en gaged by him? A.  I had never seen him—I was recommended to him by Mr. Davidson, and am in his employment still—I have been in the ring—there is not a warrant against me now for manslaughter, for killing a man in a boxing match—I do not deny that I was severn years ago at Hammersmith—I remember Paddy  Flynn and another being transported—I have fought two or three battles.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You have said the pole of the "Emperor" was driven into your pannel A.  Yes; that was in conse quence of my pulling up suddenly, to prevent running over somebody, I believe.
   Q.  Now with respect to this public-house near Trevett's stable, did you not wait there, colse to Trevett's yard, till his omnibus came out, and then go with them? A.  We went with them to town and back.
   COURT . Q.  The question was, did you wait? A.  We did not parti cularly wait for them—we went with them when they went.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did you not follow one of Trevett's? A.  I do not recollect seeing one of them—we came away at the same time.
