Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 898. GEORGE WAITS was indicted for feloniously receiving, of a certain evil-disposed person, 1 waistcoat, value 12 s  ., the goods of Richard Scott , well-knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD SCOTT. I am a tailor and draper, and live in Westminster-bridge-road. A boy named Phillips came into my employ on Monday, the 23rd of January, and he remained till the Saturday following—I then gave him into custody—I missed the waistcoat in question at that time—this is it—the prisoner had been in my employ for two days about six weeks before, and knew what articles I dealt in—I discharged him.
   Cross-examined by MR. JOKES . Q.  Did you discharge him because he was idle? A.  Yes; that was one thing—I found him asleep three times in one day—I did not discharge him for having robbed me—I did not then know I had lost any thing—I did not see him as he was coming out of Newgate after being acquitted here before—I saw him in our neighbourhood—Iemployed Mr. Case to conduct the prosecution—I believe he is clerk to a solicitor—he did not appear as the prisoner's legal adviser at the police office to my knowledge—I told him at the office that I should employ him—I did not say to the prisoner after he was acquitted, "Now you have been acquitted will you tell all you know"—all that transpired was in the Court by the direction of the Judge—he allowed me to ask him if he had the tickets of some articles; and I never saw the boy after—I will not swear I did not ask him to tell all he knew, but I do not recollect it—Mr. Case did not speak to him in my presence at all—the prisoner said, while he was in the dock, that he had acquainted me with all he knew—I did not say, if he did not tell me more I would indict him for receiving—I swear that—I did not see him after he left the Court.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did all that transpired take place between you in the Court? A.  It was all in the Court—the Jury said he ought to be indicted as a receiver. JOSEPH NUGENT. I am shopman to Mr. Causton, pawnbroker, Mount-street, Lambeth. I produce a waistcoat which was pawned on the 26th of January, by the prisoner for 4 s ., in the name of Brown—the duplicate was destroyed in the other Court—I have not found it since the trial—the officer of the Court went and looked for it but could not find it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What was the waistcoat worth? A.  5 s . to us—he only asked 4 s . on it—it was not worth 12 s . to us. RICHARD SCOTT re-examined.  I heard this paper read over to the prisoner, and saw Mr. Wedgewood the Magistrate sign it, and also Phillips and the prisoner ( read )—"The prisoner Watts says, I had the waistcoat of Phillips, and pawned it, and we shared the money; I had 5 d ."—the pri soner phillips says, "I gave the waistcoat to Watts to pawn, and I had part of the money,"
   PEARSON . I am a policeman On going to the station-house the prisoner told me that he had recommended Phillips to the prosecutor's place, that Phillips gavehim the waistcoat, and he went and pled ged it, and received part of the money—he did not mention the place. NOT GUILTY.
