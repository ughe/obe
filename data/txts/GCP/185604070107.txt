WILLIAM WRIGHT. I am a watchmaker, and live in Tooley-street. I
lived there in 1841-in Feb., 1841, two women, came and brought mes
watch-this is the watch I cannot recollect the date-I recognised the
watch—I had been in the service of Mr. Carter this watch was made by
him-I knew it had been sold to Mrs. Atkinson of Blackheath I observed
something peculiar, about it, and I detained it and gave information--I sent
to Mrs. Atkinson-181. had been the cost price of the watch it had been
altered-Mr. Carter's name had been filed out, and another name had been
engraved, and also the number was altered, and there had been two A's in
old English--they had been filed out, but there is a portion of them which I
can trace now-it had been sold to Mrs. Atkinson seven or eight years before.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. . I suppose you know that the value
of a watch after eight or ten years very materially alters? 4. Yes I do
not suppose a pawnbroker would advance above 77 or 8l. on this watch-
I would not give above 91. for it.
