I am a furrier, carrying on business at 44, Gresham street, in partnership with my son—France was in my employ as a porter—I went with him to the house of Mrs. Shenstone, in the Goswell Road, on the 11th October—she has never been a customer of mine—the three dozen skins found at her house are my property—their value is 30 s . per dozen, 2 s . 6 d . each, wholesale price—I also identify the two dozen and nine skins as my property—they are of the same value—I had last seen all those skins in my warehouse—France had no authority whatever to remove them—I deal very largely in this business, especially in this article—this particular parcel was 1,500 dozen—we never sell skins. STEPHEN MARONI( Detective G Division ). On the 11th October I went to a warehouse in Charterhouse Buildings where Cain was employed, and took him into custody, and in consequence of information received I went with the other officers to Mrs. Shenstone's shop.

   MR. PURCELL conducted the Prosecution; and MR. FULTON  the Defence. FREDERICK LEICESTER( Policeman E  121). I was in Gray's Inn Road on 28th September at about 2.15 a.m., about four yards from No. 347, and saw the prisoner chuck the grating back and come up out of it, and run away—I was near enough for him to see me—I was in uniform—I followed him, and came up with him in about 100 yards, and laid hold of him—he struck me with a jemmy  across the helmet, got out of my hands, and tried to get over a fence of the Great Northern Railway—I never lost sight of him—I got him down, and he bit my arm—I took the jemmy  from him, and placed it in my coat pocket—I could not have mistaken a jemmy  for an umbrella—I have not the jemmy now—I called out for assistance, and two porters and two or three other men came up—he said he had been quarrelling with, a young woman, that she hit him in the mouth, and he hit her and then ran away—I found on him 7 s ., some bronze, a key, a knife, a silver match-box, and an almanac.
