   JAMES CHAMBERLAIN. I am a constable, and was placed outside Mr. Morris' shop, by his order, on the 7th of July, to stop the prisoner when he came out, which I did, just about one o'clock; I said he must not go, as I suspected he had some property belonging to his master about him; I took off his hat, and in it I found this worsted; I then proceeded to put my hand in his pocket, and took out this bobbin of silk - he staggered across from one side to the other, and said it was his first offence, and he did it for want; I told him he made his case worse - I then took him to Covent-garden watch-house; he said he hoped his master would forgive him - it was the first time.
   WILLIAM MORRIS. I gave directions to the witness to do what he has done; I heard the prisoner say it was his first offence, and he hoped I would forgive him; this property is worth about 6s.; I then caused a search to be made at his lodgings; I had property of this description on my premises.
 Cross-examined by Mr. CURWOOD. Q. How long had he worked for you? A. Eight years and a half - I would wish to speak on his behalf, in consideration of his wife, who is pregnant, and has five children.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1587.  RICHARD PARKIN was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , 36 silver spoons, value 26l., and 24 silver forks, value 24l., the goods of John Turner and Charles Turner.
 Mr. BRODRICK conducted the prosecution.
 JOHN TURNER. I am a  silversmith, and live in New Bond-street. I am in partnership with my brother, Charles Turner; the prisoner was our apprentice three or four years; he left us nearly four years since - he had not served his time out. On the 17th of July a letter was received, which was handed to me on the 18th - this is it; there was then the other half-sheet containing a direction; I gave direction to Vogler, my shopman, to get the order done immediately, according to this letter; I think the value of the articles sent was about 49l.
   EDWARD VOGLER. I am shopman to Messrs. Turner. In July last I put up three dozen silver spoons, and two dozen forks - they were all the fiddle-pattern, and worth about 50l.; I gave them to William Simmons, to be taken to Mr. Buck's, No. 13, Cumberland-street, who has been a customer of my masters' many years; I was at Messrs. Turners' at the time the prisoner was an apprentice there, and I know his hand-writing; I believe this letter to be his writing, but it did not strike me then, as I had it not long in my hands before the order was executed; I think I destroyed the other part of it, and put this part on the file.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ANDREWS. Q. Why did you destroy the other part? A. It is customary in our business; the other part had contained the direction, "Messrs. T. and C. Turner, No. 58 and 59, New Bond-street, London; by favour of Capt. Montgomery;" it came by the two-penny post; this is the prisoner's hand-writing - I have no hesitation in swearing it.
 COURT. Q. Are you sure it came by the post, and had Capt. Montgomery's name on it too? A. Yes; it had his name on the fore-part, immediately over the direction, and yet came by the post. (read.)

   WILLIAM SIMMONS. I am porter to Messrs. Turner. On the 18th of last July I received a parcel, which I conveyed to the house of Mr. Buck, in Cumberland-street, and gave it to Elizabeth Fry; it was the same parcel I received from Mr. Vogler.
   ELIZABETH FRY. I am servant to Mr. George Buck. I was taking care of his house, in Cumberland-street, Mary-le-bone; Simmons brought a parcel on the 18th of July, about two o'clock - the prisoner had called before that morning, and said his name was Captain Montgomery; that he was going to Glass Hayes, and Mr. Buck had ordered a parcel which he would send his servant for, as he was going to Lindhurst the next morning by the stage; he came himself again, about five o'clock, and asked me for the parcel - I fetched the same parcel and gave it him in the same state I received it; he said it was a large parcel, he understood it was to be small - I asked if I should send it - he said No, he had only to go into Park-street - I gave him two letters beside - he called again on Tuesday, the 8th of August, but a person had called before that to tell me what course to take when he came - he asked if there was not a parcel to go to Lindhurst - I said there was, and my fellow-servant had got it - I gave information, and he was taken by Whitear.
   GEORGE AVIS. I am a constable of Marlborough-street. The prisoner was brought to the office, and when I was about to search him, he delivered to me one duplicate first; I left him, and then returned and ask where the others were - he said he had thrown them down the gutter coming along - I went to inquire, and then I came back, and said, I was come to search him all over; he then gave me six other duplicates, which he said were all he had belonging to Messrs.
