937. THOMAS BERRY, stealing 3 handkerchiefs, value 178. 6d.: the
goods of Mary Ann Harvey.
GoRGs Gray. I am a licensed victualler, of Broadway, Barking. On
25th July the prisoner came in as a customermy sister, Mrs. Harvey, fol-
lowed him in, and made a communication to me; in consequence of which I
sent to the station-a policeman same, and I gave the prisoner in charge for
stealing some handkerchiefs-he said he had not stolen any he knew nothing
about them-as the policeman took him out, he put his hand under his smock
and drew a bundle of handkerchiefs from it-I gave them to the constable-
these are them (produced)th prisoner said nothing.
Prisoner. I was intoxicated. Witness. He was not.
MARY ANN HARVEY. I am single, and keep a draper's shop in Barking.
On 21st July, about 5 o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came, and asked
to look at some cotton pocket handkerchiefs- showed him some; he did
not buy any-after he had left, I missed some silk handkerchiefs off the
counter-there was no one else in the shop at the time-he stood in front of
the counter- followed bim to my brother's, and a policeman was sent for
-I did not see the handkerchiefs dropped-I have no mark on them, but I
lost such, and believe them to be mine.
Prisoner. I faney I paid her for one. Witness. No you did not; but you
offered me 1s. for three cotton handkerchiefs, marked 4)d, each, but I would
not take it.
ROBERT WHaTiINg (policeman). The prisoner was given into my charge
at Mr. Gray's-| did not see him with the handkerehiefs-he was sober.
