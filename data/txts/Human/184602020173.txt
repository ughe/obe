   Cross-examined. Q.  When you went for the surgeon you left the girl in the yard in care of somebody? A.  Yes. MARY STEDMAN. I am the wife of Marshall Stedman, and live next door to the prisoner. I went to his house at the same time as Mrs. Jones—I fol lowed her up stairs—I saw Celia coming down stairs, with the child in her arms, and a wound on her forehead—I took the child from her—she then went into the yard—we washed her head, wrapped it up, and sent her to the hospital—in about a quarter of an hour she was quite sensible, and said her father had done it with a poker—I saw the father lying on the landing. MARY ANN CLARKE. I am the wife of John Clarke, a coal-porter, and live in the same house as the prisoner, in the front parlour—I heard a noise in the prisoner's room, which is the front room, over mine—I went to the foot of the stairs, and called out, "Mr. Overy, what are you doing?"—no answer was made—he came out of the front room, towards the stairs, and threw him self down on the landing—I cannot say whether he saw me—I saw Celia come out of the front room, bleeding from the head, and go into the back room—I went to the door, and called "Murder"—I did not see her after wards—I have frequent opportunities of seeing the prisoner—I saw him every day, unless he was in the country, where he was several times during the summer—he was generally at home—he has not worked while I have been there—he has been ill all the time—I do not know that he ever kept his bed all day through—I do not know what was the matter with him—he had a doctor from the dispensary—he used to go to him—he has not been able to work during the eight months I have been there. GEORGE HENRY EDWARDS. I am dresser at Guy's-hospital. On the 13th of Nov. Celia was brought there with three wounds on her head—one just by the orbit, and two below it—the one above the eye-brow was the most severe, and exposed the bone, which was fractured—the skin was divided—all wounds in the head, are dangerous, especially compound fractures of the scull, which this was—I think this poker would inflict such injury—she was partly sen sible, in a state of collapse, but could answer Questions when aroused—then the would partially relapse—she has been in the hospital ever since, as an out-patient. DONALD MURRAY( policeman. ) I apprehended the prisoner on the 19th of Nov., at his house—I do not know why he was not taken before—I had a warrant, which I read to him—he was then in bed—it was between four and five o'clock in the afternoon—I told him I came to take him on that warrant—it charged him with assaulting Celia, his daughter, with a poker—he made no reply—he said he was very ill, and not able to be removed out of his bed—this was on the 18th—I got Mr. Martin, the parish surgeon, to see him—he wrote me a certificate on the 19th, and I took the prisoner away.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe he was removed to the infirmary? A.  I took him to goal in a cab , and had strict orders to see him placed in the in firmary—I do not recollect hearing him say anything before the Magistrate.
