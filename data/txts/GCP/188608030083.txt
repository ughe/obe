THOMAS PARDON. I am a labourer, of 10, Collingwood Street, Wool-
wich I was on the wharf at Woolwich on 24th June about 5.30 p.m.-
I saw Ranton holding an argument with Southgate-Ranton called
Southgate a long, narrow-gutted b ; I did not hear what Southgate
said, but I heard him make a noise talking--they were equal in bad
language to one another-Southgate left the wharf and went to his
beerhouse with two men-there is a lobby there; Ranton went in there
-I heard a noise and scuffle in the lobby three or four minutes after-
wards- I went to the lobby door, looked in the centre of the lobby, and
saw Ranton lying two feet from the threshold of the door, which opened
inwards-he was bleeding very heavily from the head; his face was
downwards in the muck, in the coaldust; he was insensible-Southgate
was looking at him with his face towards the man's back, and standing
at his feet-I raised up the man's head, and put the palm of my hand

his head I said, “Mr. Southgate, you have killed this man, you will be
