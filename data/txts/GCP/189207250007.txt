GEORGE SMITH INGLIS. I carry on business in Red Lion Square, and
have studied handwriting for some years I have examined letter A,
which is the order for the belts, and B, O, and D, and the altered postal
order for 158., and have formed the opinion that they are written by
Miss Illingworth–I compared them with the letter which she said at
the Police-court that she wrote, and it is the same-she writes various
hands--this diary is written in a small hand-the lines are very close
together, but it is the same writing as the others the second stroke of
the small "h's" turns round backwards instead of the other way, and
finishes with a small knob-I find that in the diary, too, on 28th
February, in the word " birthday," and here is another "h" in the word
"three" on document D, and the same in "inches" and "further
the diary has on the fly-leaf the name and address of the ownerI have
compared that with the “ 118, Bt. James's Road” in the order forms.
and with the signatures-- there is a variety now and then; sometimes
one letter is more perfect than the other, but they are by the same
hand.
Cross-examinod. Handwriting is a matter about which even people
who have made it a study may be deceived-Mr. Netherolift and I
differed; he relied on the "h, showing that it was the writing of
another person-I said before the Magistrate, «There is a little varis-
tion in the writing”-the letters to the company are in the same writing
as the admitted letter; they are not disguised, and it she did not write
them somebody else imitated her writing-I have not looked for dis-
similarities.
By the Court. The letters in the former trial (866 Vol. oxi. p. 259) were
different ones to those which are before me now.

681. MARY AGNES ILLINGWORTH was again indicted for un-
lawfully sending by post certain postal packets and postcards having
thereupon indecent words and matters.
Mr. PHILBRICK, Q.O., and Mr. CASSALLY Prosecuted, and Mr. O. F. Gu
Defended.
ALBERT WILLIAM HARRISON. I am the father of Miss Harrison, who
has appeared as a witness this morning-I formerly lived at Maisonette,
Ramsden Road, Balham–I do not remember receiving postcards there,
but some were forwarded to me from there to Bournemouth-these are
them produced)—the matter on them is offensive to my daughter and
myself.
JOHN PHILLIPS. I am chief of the Private Enquiry branch, General

the secretary, and signed Mary A. Tllingworth-it is written on the
