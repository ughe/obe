 1541.  JOHN SANDERS and GEORGE GASKIN were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , 13 chisels, value 13s, the goods of Thomas Cubit , and others, and 1 hammer value 1s., and 1 trowel, value 1s., the goods of Martin Caulfield.
 MARTIN CAULFIELD. I am a  mason. I lost seven chisels, a hammer and trowel, and thirteen chisels of Mr. Cubitt's, from an unfinished house in which I was working in Frederick-street; I had put them up carefully in the back-kitchen, at half-past five o'clock on the Saturday night, and missed them on Monday morning, the 24th of July.
   THOMAS WAKEMAN. I was ill in bed - my wife brought me some tools - I knew them by the initials; the prisoners came back to sell some more, and I got out of bed to take them, but I could not overtake them; I know Sanders was one of them.
   ELIZABETH WAKEMAN. I received these tools from Gaskin, who came to our shop alone, on Monday morning, the 24th of July, about eight o'clock, and asked if I bought such things; I said, I bought any thing if it was not stolen; he had not the goods then, but came again with Sanders, and brought them; he asked 3s. for them - I took them to the bed to my husband, and he told me to offer 2s. 6d. for them - and they took 2s. 4 1/2d. for them.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. What shop do you keep? A. A marine-store-shop. I never saw either of them before - there were twenty-five tools brought; I did not know what they were worth.
 THOMAS WAKEMAN re-examined. Q. What was the worth of these things? A. They might have been worth to Mr. Cubitt 14s. or 15s., but I might have got only a 1d. or 2d. a piece for them; I have kept that shop about fourteen months - we do not buy such things of boys without asking where they get them; Gaskin told my wife he was a stone-mason, out of work, and forced to part with his tools.
   THOMAS MARKHAM. I took up the prisoner at the Red-lion, public-house, in Rosamond-street. They were eating and drinking - Gaskin asked me to drink; I said,"I want you two;" they said, "For what?" I said, "I believe it is some chissels;" they said they found them in Wilmington-square, and did not know they were stolen.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you tell the Magistrate that they both said so? A. Yes.
 GASKIN'S Defence. I found the tools in an empty house.
 GASKIN. -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 SANDERS. -    NOT GUILTY.
 1542.  WILLIAM TAYLOR and MARY TAYLOR were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , 6 handkerchiefs, value 19s., the goods of Samuel Fisher.
   JOHN ALDRED COOPER. I was in the employ of Mr. Fisher, a linen draper. On the 27th of June, at a quarter past one o'clock, the two prisoners came in together; the man desired to look at some blue silk handkerchiefs; the woman took a seat, and the man stood beside her; I took a piece of handkerchief out of the window, he examined it, and desired to look at some others - he opened one piece, and covered the others that were on the counter; he paid me 4s. 6d. for one, and I saw the woman slip her hand under the piece I had opened - the man then said he did not wish to have that one, but another; I said I would exchange it - he said, No, and they went out; I missed the handkerchiefs, and pursued them - I took hold of the female, and brought her back - she made some resistance. and said she was not going to run away; I took hold of her two hands in the shop, and at that moment saw the piece fall from her.
   JOHN VANN. I am a patrol, and was sent for to Mr. Fisher's, and found the female prisoner there; she cried, and said she knew nothing about it; I was going with her across Finsbury-square, the man came up, and said,"What is the matter?" she said she was going to the office for nothing; he said, "What are you about?" I said, I was an officer, and if he made any attempt to oppose me, I would take him also; he wanted to fight me, and I took him to the office - just as we got there, he put put his head on one side, and said he would give me 2l. to make it right; I found the handkerchief he had bought, in the woman's basket - he said that was his property.
 Prisoner MARY TAYLOR. I went to buy a handkerchief - the man followed me, and said I must go back; I went back, and the young man in the shop was told to throw the handkerchiefs on one side; this piece of handkerchiefs laid on the floor, and he said, "There it is."
 JOHN ALDRED COOPER. I desired the young man to move the other handkerchiefs, that this piece might not mix with them - she dropped this; I did not see it fall, but I am certain it was not on the floor when I went out, because I passed the same ground in going out and coming in; she could not have brushed it from the counter, for they were removed before she got back.
 COURT. Q. Then she had left the shop? A. Yes - and I missed the piece, pursued, and brought her back - after some time she dropped it.
     NOT GUILTY.
