   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know Nash? A.  Very well—he was sent for, and was before the Magistrate.
   Q.  Was he not asked before the Magistrate whether he had told Wigley to move a truss of hay out of the front stable to the back, and did he not say, "Yes?" and did not they send him away? A.  He denied it, but his deposition was not taken—not in my presence—he said he did not tell Wigley to move the hay—I never heard him say that he did tell him to move it—I do not know who produced Nash before the Magistrate—I am not aware that he was remanded—I have not seen him this morning—he was not taken into custody—he was gone out when the hay was removed—I did not see any one else, at the time that Bennett took the hay from the wagon to the outer stable.
   Bennett.  I put the hay by the side of a brown horse—that is all.
   Witness.  It was five-yards from the horses—they appeared all brown but one, which was grey. JAMES HAIRSINE( police-constable R  37.) I went after Bennett, and overtook him on Blackheath—I said I wanted him respecting some hay he had left at the Halfway-house—he said he had not left any hay there—I said he must come back and prove it—he said the only bay he left was what his horses would not eat—I took him, and then went to the stable and saw Wigley—I said I wanted him respecting some bay that Mr. Weatherly's wagoner had left there—he said he had left none—I said, "I must take him"—he then said he would tell the truth—he removed the hay by order of Mr. Nash, his employer.
