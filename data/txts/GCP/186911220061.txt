56. WILLIAM KETTLE (28), PLEADED Guilty to stealing a lamp, and
other goods. He was further charged with having been previously con-
victed of felony, on the 7th September, 1866, to which he PLEADED NOT
GUILTY.
JOSEPH SMITH. I produce a vertificate, dated 7th September, 1866, for
burglary, after a previous conviction—I was present at the trial—the
prisoner is the same man-he was tried in the name of Frederick Schleter.
GEORGE AGAR. I am a warder, of Holloway Prison-I was present, and
heard him tried-I am perfectly certain he is the same person.
Guilty." -Seven Years' Penal Servitude.

58. MARTIN BIRMINGHAM (20), ALFRED BROWN (20), CHARLES
HYATT (20), CHARLES AUSTIN (18), THOMAS DAY (18), and
FREDERICK HOWARD (20), Robbery, with violence, on William Thomas
Brown, and stealing a chain and a pipe, his goods.
MESSRS. COLLINS and Hunt conducted the Prosecution; MR. WARNER SLXIGH
defended Birmingham and Brown, MR. COLE defended Austin, and
MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS defended Howard.
WILLIAM THOMAS Brown. I live at 11, Lodge Road, St. John's Wood,
and am a clerk in the City on the evening of the 5th November I was on
Primrose Hill with Thomas Moore, a friend--we were surrounded by a mob,
and I received a blow on the hat-I saw Birmingham and Brown in the
crowd---my friend came to my assistance, and I got away—they followed us
--we made a run to get to the bottom of the hill, and we were followed by
the crowd-one of the men ran at the side of my friend and said, “What
is the matter, sir 1"-I then saw him receive a blow-I did not see who
struck it, I was dragged down on the ground, and I lost a gold albert chain
and a meerschaum pipe the chain was attached to my waistcoat in the
usual way, and my coat was buttoned up-it was worth about 21. 108.-I
do not know who robbed me--I saw Brown and Birmingham while the obain
was being taken-Brown had hold of me all the time I was on the ground,
