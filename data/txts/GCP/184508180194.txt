1793. PETER CHRISTOPHER RING was charged, on the Coroner's
Inquisition only, with feloniously killing and slaying James Anderson.
WILLIAM TOLLER. I live in Southwark. On the night of the 13th of
July, at half-past twelve o'clock, I was returning home-the deceased and
the prisoner and two women were quarrelling in the street-I stopped to
listen-they got to high words—the quarrel appeared to rest between the
prisoner's wife and the deceased, the prisoner not saying a word-I heard the
deceased call the prisoner's wife a b-y w- -the prisoner then said, " If
you call my wife a w- again, I will knock you down"-the word occurred
again, and then the prisoner raised his arm, and the man fell-I assisted
him up, and the prisoner assisted - I said, “You have killed the man"-
he said, “I hope I have not"-I said, “You had better run for a doctor,".
which he did, and as we were taking the deceased to the hospital the doctor
met us, and said, "Take him to the hospital directly."
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Might not the deceased have
stepped back to avoid the blow, and have stumbled ? A. I cannot say-
they were tipsy-I saw the prisoner's arm raised-whether it was a push
or a blow I cannot tell.
MARY ANN Ryder. I was going on an errand- I heard Mr. Anderson
call the prisoner's wife a w- several times, and she called him a d-d
vagabond and a rogue--the prisoner then said if he called his wife a w-
again he would knock him down-the prisoner then raised his arm, and
gave him a push or a blow he was struck, but I cannot say whether it
was with his hand or his fist.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you quite certain he was touched at all, or
might he have stumbled back? A. I am quite certain he was touched-I
was standing about a yard off, at the corner of the street-there were not
many people I did not see Toller till the deceased was down-the de-
ceased did not step back when the prisoner lifted his hand.
ELIZABETH BRITTON. I had been with Mr. and Mrs. Ring- I had
not seen the deceased-we were coming down the street, and two or three
words occurred, but I do not know about what I walked on before them
-I saw no blow.
RICHARD WHEATLEY (police-constable M 161.) I saw no blow--I saw
the man bleeding, helped him up, and took him to the hospital.
WILLIAM SEDGWICK SANDEN. I am a surgeon. I was at St. Thomas's
Hospital when the man was brought in, bleeding very profusely over the
left temple, as if from a fall, not by a blow- he was insensible for five
days, and then died--the cause of his death was the fall.
