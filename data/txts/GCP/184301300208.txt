Taylor was black, like a sweep-I saw marks of white on his knees, shoulders.
and arms, at the station, such as a person would have by climbing up a white
wall-there were also marks of black on the inside of Penns wall, such as a
sweep would make by getting up-marks of a person's toes-the wall is
about twelve feet high-there is a urinal in the yard, a water-closet door, and
& wall above, then the leads, so that it is like a pair of stairs-he could get
on the leads very easily, and on that lead the window opens.
Cross-examined. Q. Whose feet would you swear made the marks ? A.
I cannot swear-they appeared to be marks of toes--I do not swear they
were-I almost swear the marks on the knife were marks of putty-it is a
common sort of a knife-I fitted it in the wood where the putty was scraped
away, and it corresponded-no doubt any common knife would produce the
same mark.
AMBROSE MUGFORD. I am a policeman. I was at the station, about half-
past seven o'clock, when a boy came- I was ordered to go up to Mr. Penn's
house--I went, and Penn gave Ryan into custody- I took him to the station
-I was going back to the house, and saw a brother constable with Donoghue,
and assisted in taking him to the station- I went to Penn's house, and brought
the goods to the station in a bundle-1 have seen Taylor and Ryan before-
Taylor is a sweep, and is generally black-I never saw Ryan with a black
face before.
Taylor's Defence. His statement was quite different at Union-hall; they
convicted one another there ; one of the boys said he saw Ryan throw things
over the wall, and a man underneath in a brown coat caught them, and ran
up to the van; one of the other boys said he saw Ryan throw them into the
van himself; he did not say so at Union-hall; one swore it was a man in
a brown coat, and the other said it was Ryan; I never had the knife in my
possession; I had been in the house an hour and a half; I went backwards,
and bad not been there five minutes before the policeman came.

812. WILLIAM TAYLOR was again indicted for stealing, on the
23rd of Jan., 1 keg, value 158.; and i quart of peppermint, 2s. 8d.; the
goods of Philip Penn.
Philip Penn. I keep the Duke's Head. On the 23rd of Jan., ab ou
half-past six o'clock, I was in the bar-Kelly brought me a keg- I said,
"Who took it?"-he said, “Taylor"- I took it from him, and put it behind
the bar-there was about a pint of peppermint in it-it was mine-I saw it
that evening on a corner of a shelf inside the bar-it could be reached from
outside the bar, from the staircase-it had a brass tap to it-I went for a
policeman, but could not find one.
Prisoner. Q. What did Kelly say to you? A. He at first told me he
picked it up off the floor-he then said he took it from underneath your
arm-I never saw you till afterwards I did rot go into the tap-room-the
other robbery was committed about ten minutes after, and then I hardly
knew what I was about-I was busy at the time-there were other people
outside the bar, but not inside.
WILLIAM KELLY. I was waiting at the bar for a pot of beer, which Mr.
Penn was going to draw for a customer-I saw Taylor going in the direction
of the door with the keg under his arm-I immediately took it from him, and
gave it to Mr. Penn, telling him who I took it from-he was not drunk.
