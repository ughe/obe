12th, and went to Mr. Jones's shop, 81, Gravel Lane, Soutllwark, four or five miles from the prosecutor's place—I saw both the prisoners there—I told Lepine I was a police inspector, and had come to see him respecting a watch which had been seen in his possession on the previous Saturday—he hesitated a moment, and then said, "I have not got it; 1 have sold it for 7  s . 6 d ."—Mr. Reeve came in at that time—I said, "That watch is part of the proceeds, of a burglary"—I asked Jones to show me several watches which were in the window, and he produced 14 of the stolen watches, and one which I got afterwards made 15—Mr. Reeve identified them as they were shown to him—I asked Jones how he came in posses sion of them—he said, "I bought them on Tuesday or Wednesday last as a job lot over the counter of a tall man who I do not know—I gave him 6 s . each for the yellow ones and 8 s . each for the white ones"—I asked if he had any entry of the transaction in his book; he said, "I keep no books whatever"—I searched his premises, but found no other property connected with the robbery—I only found some very old account books—we took the prisoners into custody.
   Cross-examined.  I understood Lepine to be Jones's servant—he keeps a small, dirty shop—one or two watches were in the window, and six were in the shop—there was no concealment—the police serve all jewellers with a notice of stolen property, and I have a notice describing some of these as silver watches, a copy of which was served on Jones on the very morning that he alleges he bought them—they are described as silver, and their numbers are here—the notice is dated March 8th; it gives the actual numbers engraved on each watch.
   Re-examined  The notice does not say whose watches they are; it says, "Any person offering the undermentioned articles for sale or in pledge will be given into custody"—the name of "Jones" is over the shop. GILSON REEVE. I am a pawnbroker, of 8, Falcon Market, Battersea—on Saturday morning, 5th March, I found my shop broken into—I missed a large quantity of property, amongst which were some watches—I identify these watches produced by my initial scratched on the cases, and the numbers of them correspond with the numbers in this police notice—I gave information—there are silver ones among them, valued from 8 s . to 15 s ., and aluminium, about 6 s . each—I went with the last witness to Jones's house; he said he bought the watches of a strange tall man.
   Cross-examined.  The most expensive watch is 15 s .; it is real silver—I found six silver ones on Jones. STEPHEN BANNINGTON. I am a whitesmith, of 135, Trafalgar Street, Walworth—on 12th March I bought these watches of Lepine, at my house, for 7 s . 6 d .—he had done a little job for my daughter—he brought a watch I had given him to clean, and said, "I have got a very nice watch to suit you; it is a good one, a good movement; I gave 6 s . for it"—he did not say to whom—he said he took it to a pawnbroker on the Saturday night, who offered him 5 s . for it.
   Cross-examined.  He said it was Mr. Reeve's, and I knew him, there was no concealment—I have known him upwards of ten years, and know that he has lived in Walworth Road thirty years—I always believed him to be a respectable man—I knew him latterly as Jones's servant—I am not a customer of Jones's—I was never inside his shop.
