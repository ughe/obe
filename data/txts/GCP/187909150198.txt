ing her child-I said, "You are not called upon to say anything before I
read the charge, but should you say anything, what you say will be taken
down in writing, and used as evidence against you"-she burst into tears,
and then made this statement, which I wrote down--(Read :) "I only wish,
to say that I did not murder my child, and I was in hopes he was alive; I

shilling, that was all the money I was possessed of, and she promised me

BENJAMIN BILLING. I am porter at the Richmond Union Workhouse
I keep the book of children admitted to the workhouse—there was no child
left at the workhouse without the mother about this time in July-one
child was admitted in July; the mother is now in the Asylum and the
father took the child away.
ROBERT COCHRANE MULLINS. I am a registered medical practitioner--
on 18th July last I was living at Mortlake, and was assistant to Dr. Mar-
shall there--the constable Warne came to me, and in consequence of what
he said I went to the mortuary at Mortlake, and there saw the dead body
of a male child-I could not recognise the features; the body was in a state of
putridity-I could not form any positive opinion as to how long it had been
dead; I should say probably about fourteen days; it might possibly here
been more; I don't think lesss; it might be--the atmosphere might have s
certain degree of influence there were no marks that I could trace; from
the appearance of the body I should not be able to state whether there were
any marks of violence; I found none--it was a well-developed child-I
could not form any opinion as to the cause of death; it was quite impossible
to do so; from the state of the body I could form no judgment whether it
had died from natural causes or any other--it was difficult to give an
opinion as to its age; I did form an opinion that it was between 3 and 6
months old, probably 4; that was not a positive opinion- I came to that
conclusion from the structural development-it was impossible to give an
opinion as to whether it might only have been 2 or 3 weeks old; one child
at 3 or four weeks old may be quite as well developed and as large as one
of 2 or 3 months; we meet with such cases every day; it is quite possible
that this child may have been born at the beginning of June; my impres-
sion was the contrary; it is not probable, it is highly improbable-I could.
not trace any marks of vaccination; it was impossible to do so, it was s
putrid body.
