   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  When you went to the lodging the prisoner was at your premises? A.  Yes; it is not quarter of a mile from our premises—we have a number of people in our employ—our shopmen are allowed to have goods on account by entering them in tie day-book, we don't allow them to pay for anything at the counter—it a the duty of Mr. Wilson to keep the day-book—he is not here—I have not got the day-book here, but there was no entry of the carpet—I believe the carpet was made expressly for our house—we have sold a great deal of it—I do not believe any of our shopmen have had any of it—the day-book would show that—the prisoner has been in the habit of dealing in the shop—we have no servants but what sleep on our premises, except Mr. Wilson—the prisoner sleeps there—they are allowed to deal for any of their friends—we enter goods in the day-book, and stop it from their wages-the prisoner did not mention that he had more carpets than one at hi lodging—there were several rooms in the house, but the woman who said she was his wife, was only occupying one room, which was the one she took us into—the prisoner has had about £3 or £4 worth of things from our shop—in some quarters this carpet is worth about three guineas. THOMAS WILLIAM REDFORD( police-constable L  52.) I found the carpet and rug at the lodging—on the following day, when going to Union-hall, the prisoner told me he had taken the three remnants of carpet, but had not made the entry in the book, and the same by the rug.
   MR. KINGSTON re-examined.  It is our practice to settle every quarter for things which they have—it is our invariable rule to have every thing entered, that they may not have an opportunity of saying afterwards, bought it, and paid for it"—but this carpet and rug we have lost for mew than twelve months—we had not another rug like this in our stock. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined One Year.
 224. JAMES BLAKE and JOHN PENSTONE were indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of November , 1 truss of hay, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Sir Felix Booth , Bart., the master of the said James Blake .
   MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM ROBY. I live at Great Ealing, and am a hay-binder in Sir Felix Booth's service. Blake was also in his service as carman—on the 22nd of November, I made up fifty-four trusses of hay to go to Brent ford—they were all my binding—I have since seen one of them, and can swear to the band I made, but not to the hay—this is the band ( looking at it )—only fifty-two trusses got to Brentford—I walked behind the team from Mortlake—I never saw Penstone before he was examined at Wandsworth.
