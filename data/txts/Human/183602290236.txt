   Cross-examined. Q.  You live in the same house, and that little girl played with you that afternoon? A.  No; I only saw her go out at the back-yard—I hallooed out, "There is a noise at the window"—I saw her once before go to fill her kettle, after her parents went out—she had to go about twenty yards to get water. HENRY TURNER. I know all the prisoners. On the 27th of January I was coming home from work, about twenty minutes past six o'clock—I passed the prosecutor's house—I saw Farringdon up against the wall, and Lavell stooping down, about ten feet from their yard they were in the street—I thought there was something up, and when I got to the top, I looked about half a minute, and then they were on the same spot—I then went home, and returned about half-past six o'clock—I saw Lavell pass with a box on his head, and three or four other men—he went straight down the street—one of the other men was Farringdon.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Lavell and Farringdon were near this yard? A.  Yes, it is; Mr. Hines yard, he lodges there—I call it his yard—Farringdon lives about 200 feet off—Lavell had a frock coat and white trousers—Far ringdon had white mole-skin clothes—I am sure of that—I cannot tell who the others were. SAMUEL LOCK I produce the box—I found it within two doors of my house, No. 4, church-terrace, under the church wall—that is a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's—it was in this broken state. JOHN HINES This trunk is mine—it was not broken—when I left it, it was locked—I had in it at two o'clock, when I left the house, a watch two coals, two pairs of trowsers, and a waistcoat—the rest of the pro perty belongs to my wife—I came home at eight o'clock—I had been home to dinner—the two Cranstons live in the same house—I work at Mr. Maltwood's factory at Lambeth.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Mrs. Cranston is the person to whom this house belongs? A.  Yes—the prisoners lived with their mother—I was twice before the Magistrate—I have not spoken to my girl about what she should say. nor threatened her—I left the house in her care. MARY SNELL I live in the same house with Mrs. Cranston—I was in her room when the sons came home to tea that night—they were in a hurry for it—George said to his mother, "I think I shall go down to Hines and boil my kettle"—Charles was in a hurry to go—George swore, and said, "What a hurry you are in."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you present when the little girl called coat to Mrs. Cranston, and the boy Hawkins? A.  Yes—Mrs. Cranston went down stairs, and asked what was the matter—I followed her—Mrs. Cranston talked about going for her father—the girl was afraid for her father to be sent for, as she said be would murder her. THOMAS WILLIAM BEDFORD( police-sergeant L  17.) I took Farringdon into custody. HENRY ROWS I am a foreman to Mr. Turner, of Waterloo-road. We had a watch offered as to pawn on the same evening, about seven o'clock, but did not take it in—we gave it back—two young men brought it, but I cannot swear to them.
