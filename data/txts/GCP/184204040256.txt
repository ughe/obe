1412. MARY FREEMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of
March, 1 crown, 11 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of William Ginn,
from his person.
WILLIAM Ginn. I live at Frogmore, near Wandsworth, Surrey. Be-
tween nine and ten o'clock on the 29th of March I was walking alone
High-street, Wandsworth, and met the prisoner-she said, "Holloa, old
boy, how are you?"-she gave me a shove, and pushed me up against the
wall, and began feeling me about, I said to her, “Go along, you hussy,
I don't want any thing to do with you," I parted from her all in a
moment, and found my trowsers were undone-I put my band into my
pocket, and 16s. 6d. was gone out of a little bag wbich I kept it in the
bag was left, but it was open-the money could be easily taken out-I
had before this taken out a sixpence, and changed it at Wandsworth-I
went up the town again, and met the prisoner on the other side of the way
- I said, "You hussy, you have robbed me of my money"-she said,
"Here," and returned 78. 60.--I said, “You have got a crown-piece and
some shillings; give me back my crown-piece, I don't want to get into any
bother, I have a wife and family at home"—she began abusing me " to all
eternity," and said, “Give me in charge of the police"-up came four po-
licemen-I was wrangling with her about the crown-piece-I was going
off, thinking it was better to lose the money altogether, as there would
only be hell upon earth at home with my wife-I went down the towo-
the prisoner followed me, and tore my shirt nearly asunder, and began
abusing me- the policemen took her, and asked what they should find on
her-I said, “Four shillings and a crown-piece"- she was searched, and
a crown-piece and four shillings found on her, which was the money I
lost-the crown was greasy with putting my hand into my pocket, being
in the soap line.
Prisoner. Q. When you met me did you not ask me to go up Chapel.
lane with you, and did not you ... against my consent? A. No-
was as sober as. I am now-I did not know you before-I was not aware
you were married.
WILLIAM JENNINGS. I am a policeman. I heard a wrangling on the
29th of March, and saw the prisoner with the prosecutor in High-street,
Wandsworth-the prosecutor said to her, “You have robbed me of
16s. 60."-I did not hear what she said he said, “You have only re-
turned 78. 6d., I don't want to get into trouble with you ; return me
the 58., and I will say no more about it"-with that she assaulted him-
she did not say any thing to that-(looking at his deposition)--this is what
I said before the Magistrate-it is true-(read)-"she denied it"--the pro-
secutor said, I will let you go, and say no more about it if you will return
me the money.
SARAH GARDENER. I am the wife of Thomas Gardener. I searched
the prisoner at the station, and found on her four shillings and a crowd-
piece--the crown-piece was greasy.
Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor gave me the money; I have a poor
husband, who has lost the use of his limbs, and one child; I did not
steal the money, nor give any back.
GUILTY. Aged 30.-Confined Three Months.
