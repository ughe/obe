 1654.  WILLIAM SANDERS was again indicted for a like offence.
   WILLIAM JOHN HADDON. The prisoner was my servant; I received this letter from Mrs. Parker, and sent my clerk to her house; I had no knowledge of the existence of such a person before I received the letter.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ANDREWS. Q. Where was the prisoner when the warrant was executed? A. In the brew-house yard - he had no previous notice of it.
   JAMES SHARP. It was the prisoner's duty to carry out beer, and to account to me in the evening for what he had received; it was the custom to deliver to the draymen these cheques to take out with the beer - this pile of cheques is his hand-writing, down to the 15th of June last, but those since then are not written on - he kept them till the book was full, and then returned it; I sent him out on the 11th of July; he did not account to me on that day for 5s. 6d. received from Parker; here is his book of that day, but no entry of this kind; on the 13th of July we received a letter, complaining of the beer, and desiring it might be changed - in consequence of this, I went there, and found a nine-gallon cask belonging to our brewery.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you go yourself in consequence of this letter? A. Yes; and the beer was changed; it was not my duty to call on the prisoner to account for it, but to report it to Mr. Haddon.
   ELIZABETH ANN PARKER. On the 11th of July I purchased a cask of beer of the prisoner, and paid him in silver; I paid him some shillings; I received this receipt for it. - (read.)
 Cross-examined. Q. You trust to your memory, I suppose, when you say you believe it was paid in shillings? A. I am sure of it; I did not ask him for a receipt with his master's name on it; he did not say, "If the beer is bad do not write to me or my master," but he gave me this printed receipt.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have so many customers, that I am obliged to put it down going along, and on a wet day it is very troublesome; it must have been booked to some old customer.
 JURY to JAMES SHARP. Q. How came you not to complain of his not filling up these books of receipts? A. We do not examine the book till it is quite full; what he collected was more than 1000l. a year. GUILTY. Aged 39.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined Three Months.
 1655.  THOMAS SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of May , 1 hammer, value 4s., and 1 pair of pincers, value 3s., the goods of William Jones.
 WILLIAM JONES. I am a  couch and sofa bedstead-maker, and live in Meed-street. The prisoner was my journeyman; these tools were on the bench; I missed the tools on the 24th of May; I had not seen the prisoner for a month before he was taken; I never suffered him to take them.
   THOMAS BAYLIS. I am employed by Mr. Attenborough, a pawnbroker. I have some tools, pawned at our house, in the name of Smart, but I cannot swear to the prisoner.
   THOMAS WELLS. I am a japanner. I was going to Jones' house, to work, on the 24th of May, and saw the prisoner running along; I said, "Where are you going?" he said, to Shoreditch. I saw him drop a pair of pincers, which he picked up; I went to Mr. Jones', and found the things had been stolen; no other man worked there but myself.
   SARAH JONES. I am the wife of William Jones. I was standing at the door on the 24th of May - the prisoner was working with the hammer and pincers - I told him to be careful of them, and the moment he was gone I missed them.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never intended to steal them. - Mr. Jones and I had a few words in the morning, which induced me to leave; he went to Paddington, and said he would prosecute me to the utmost, and I was afraid to go back. GUILTY. Aged 21.
 Recommended to Mercy - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1656.  SUSAN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , 1 sheet, value 3s. 6d., the goods of John Stott , in a lodging-room.
 JOHN STOTT. I  keep a lodging-house, in the New-way, Westminster. I let a room, furnished, to the prisoner, at 4s. per week; she was there about eight weeks - she paid me the rent sometimes, but the last time I went to her for 5s. 6d., she said, "I have not got it for you, Stott"- I said, "I must have it;" I went out in a passion, and she got up out of bed, and took all her things away - I got a constable, who took her close by; she said she had done it, and the duplicate was in the room.
   WILLIAM PATMORE. I am a constable. The prisoner told me the duplicate was on the table, but it had fallen on the floor.
   LEONARD GEORGE NEEDS. I live with Messrs. Graham and Stock, of Tothill-street, pawnbrokers. I have a sheet, pawned on the 11th of August, by the prisoner, for 2s.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I pawned the sheet - he came for my money; I went out to get some money to redeem the sheet, and when I came back, he had broken into my room. GUILTY. Aged 23.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined Twelve Days.
