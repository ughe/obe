JOHN GABLE. I am a greengrocer, and live in Passion-street, Spitalfields
-I know the Connorg-George Comor applied to me for assistance at the
latter part of May-I do not recollect the time he left London--he said he
had met with an accident, and was very poorly off, and that his son bad
been very ill for some time he asked me to lend him 17s. to purchase a
shed for his son-his son Charles came afterwards, wishing that I would
write to get a trifle for him, as they were in very needy circumstances--we
had a great many people at the time, and we could only afford him 1s. a
week-it is a Benevolent Society-he had employment for a few weeks
before that, but very little before that.
George CONNOR. Q. Was it me or my son that had the shilling a week
A. It was you-I volanteered that on account of your complaining of the
circumstances you were in I asked you whether you would accept of it,
and you thanked me-I also got John on the Stranger's Friend Society
without your asking me, but that was a year and a half ago I relieved you
about the first or second week in June of this year.
John Connor.Q. Did I ever make any application to get on this society
myself? A. No; your father did, not you.
JOHN WILLIAM FLORENOK. I belong to the Stranger's Friend Society -
on a Thursday in June I relieved George Connor with lo, and went a week
after that to relieve him again I had the money with me, but I found they
had left-I relieved him first in his own room, at 10, King Edward-street-
I was running up stairs, and some female behind me ran up, hollosing out
to Mr. Connor that somebody was coming up, and then the daughter came
out on the landing and asked me in--I went in, and saw they were just
having their dinner and tea together I told them I should not stop on
account of their having their meal—they persuaded me to sit down, and I
aut down-I gave him the shilling, and told him I would see him again.
NATHAN LEACH. I reside at 15, Stewart-street, and am a member of the
Sick Man's Assistant Society, for relieving poor and distressed people we
relieved the afflicted son, John Connor--he was at that time residing with
his father-we gave him 2s. a week-I think the last visit was 20th April
he had been on our society some months previous to that.
John Connor. l. Did I apply for the relief myself! A. I believe it was
interceded for by some one else--you appeared to be in very needy circum-
stances, and very thankful to receive it.
COURT. Q. How were they dressed? A. Very meanly at the time of
my visits.
