Rose HARRIS. I live at White Hart Street, Drury Lane, a coffee-
shop kept by Olarke, and the other three prisoners lived there - on
Sunday, September 15th, I went for a walk with the four prisoners-whe
we got to Chancery Lane Olarke and Jaokson parted-they went towards
Holborn, and Bassett, Whatton, and I went another way towards Holborn
-we turned to the right out of Chancery Lane, down the street where the
Railway Pre8s buildings are-Whatton and Bassett stood still there, and
said, 46 That is all right, is it not Said to Whatton, 46 What did you
mean by saying that is all right said, 44 Brown is going to break
into the Railway Press to-morrow night "-Jaekson goes by the name of
Brown-we were out about two hours-we three went back to White
Hart Street together, and Olarke and Jackson were there-Bassett or
Whatton said, 6 We have been down to that place of them, I can-
not say which, had a file, which they said was to be put in the fire and
hammered out down to a point-I communicated with Mr. Nicholson
next morning-Olark put the file in the fire, and Jackson hammered it
out on a flat-iron-on Monday night, the 12th, the prisoners went out,
but first of all they told me there were two people on the watoh-
Jaekson same in very late next mhorning-Jackson said when Olarke and
Bassett were there, that there were two coppers on the wateh-or the night
they were out, the first Monday night, Bassett said to Jaekson, 44 You
gave me the file in the street" the week before the burglary Olarke
broke off one of the prongs of this fork and put the other into the fire-I
said, "I know what that is for," and told him it was for undoing the
locks of drawers or desks-he said, 44 That is quite right"-o Monday
evening, the 23rd, Whatton, Jackson, and Miles, who is not in eustody,
went out from about 3 p.m.--Miles came back about two o'clock in the
night, and then Bassett and he went out together; they came back about
5 a.m., Whatton and Jackson about seven-when they were all five
together I said, 46 Where have you been ?"-Whatton and Jackson said
at first that they had been with two girls, but after breakfast there was
a conversation between them all, and one of them said they had been to
Southampton Buildings, and went upstairs and broke the desk and took
out the papers, that they opened the area window and Jaokson got in
first and Whatton after him, and went upstairs to the top floor and took
their boots off when they got into the passage-they said they had three
enamelled eloeks, and put them in a bag behind the front door, but did
not bring them out, and they oommitted a nuisance behind the stairs-
they had a cheque, and one of them said, 44 That is a wrong one it has
two lines across it," and it was torn up and burnt- could not under-
stand it, but I heard it said it had been forged-they were showing one
another this gauge, and said they had taken it to two or three shops, but
could not sell it or pawn it-after Olarke put the fork into the fire he
tried it on one of the loeks-on the Thursday I went to Mr. Nicholson
again, and then to the station, where my statement was taken down.
