1940. MARIA ELIZABETH LANGLEY was indicted for stealing
on the 7th of March, 2 pillows, value 48. 6d. ; 1 candlestick, value
1s. 6d. ; 1 pillow-case, value 6d.; I bolster, value 58. ; 2 curtains,
value 8s.; l quilt, value 2s. ; and 1 dressing-glass, value 8s. ; the goods
of Sopbia Ledbrook.
Sophia LEDBROOK. The prisoner lodged with me, in Dorset-crescent,
nearly six months-she sometimes went out nursing--I missed this
property when I went into the room with the officer, who came to me
with the duplicates--I never permitted her to pawn them—she was
in distress at times, and neglected by her husband.
GEORGE HANSON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a blanket, a
swing-glass, a counterpane and curtain---the counterpane and curtain were
pawned by the prisoner-the others I did not take in.
GEORGE THOMSON. I am a journeyman pawnbroker--I produce
a pillow-I do not know whether the prisoner pawned it, but I
know her.
John Lovick. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pillow pawned in March,
in the name of Ann Adams--I do not know who by.
CHARLES PALMER (police-sergeant G 19.) I took the prisoner on ano-
ther charge-I went to the prosecutrix's house, and she missed this pro-
perty-I found the duplicates of all the property on the prisoner.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
Prisoner. I was in distress through my husband's leaving me destitute
at home-it was my full intention to have restored every thing.
Sophia LEDBROOK. I was in the country for a month, and when I came
home I missed these things-her husband did not live there.
GUILTY.
