WILLIAM FRASER. I am of no occupation-in the year 1865 I was re-
siding at Weston-Super-Mare—this letter is my writing-(Read: "Dunro-
wan, Weston-Super-Mare, May 22nd, 1865. Dear Sir, Captain Langford
informs me that he has arranged with you to invest for me 8001. in the
Oriental Commercial Bank; beg therefore to enclose a cheque for that
amount, I am, dear sir, &o., W. Fraser ") I enclosed that in an envelope
addressed to “D. Pappa, Esq., Manager of the Oriental Commercial Bank,
Threadneedle Street" – I received a reply, dated 23rd May, which I
destroyed-(a copy of this letter was read from the press letter book of the bank:
« Oriental Commercial Bank, 31, Threadneedle Street, London, 23rd
May, 1865. Dear Sir, We have to acknowledge receipt of your esteemed
favour of yesterday's date, enclosing cheque for 800L. ; our mutual friend,
Captain Langford, has already introduced the matter to us, and we shall
have great pleasure in investing the money for you in shares of this bank,
as wished ; in due course we shall have the pleasure of forwarding you
further particulars, meanwile I remain, dear sir, Demetrio Pappa, manager")
- That was the letter, word for word-there was on the letter a receipt,
" 8001., 0. C. B., 23rd–5–65, P. H."--I had enclosed & cheque for 8001.,
and that cheque was met at my banker's—I heard nothing more of the
matter until my attention was attracted by something I saw in the news-
papers in March, 1866-I then wrote this letter (Read: “March 14th,
1866. Dear Sir, Having seen in the papers an account of the Oriental
Commercial Bank proceedings, I shall be glad to hear relative to the 8002
which I sent at the end of May last. I am, dear sir, &c, W. Fraser ") I
received a reply, dated 17th, which I destroyed-(The letter was read from
the press letter book : “17th March, 1866. Dear Sir, We have to hand your
esteemed favour of the 14th inst., and we are extremely sorry that your
certificate and warrant have been overlooked in the great press of business
at our transfer department : we will at once remedy this, and shall trans-
mit you both your certificate and dividend warrant on Monday. I am,
dear sir, &o., Demetrio Pappa, general manager ") I afterwards received
this letter-Read: “20th March. Dear Sir, Referring to my communica-
tion to you of the 7th inst., I have now much pleasure in handing you
certificates for 160 shares in the bank, bl. paid, and a cheque for 472. 118. 3d.,
being interest on same at the rate of ten per cent. per annum, from 28th
May to 31st December, 1865. I regret exceedingly that this matter has
been overlooked so long. I am, dear sir, &c., Demetrio Pappa, general
manager")-in that letter I received both the certificates for the shares and
a cheque for 471. Ils. 3d., which I cashed-these are the certificates
(These were certificates of shares numbered from 18859 to 19018, dated 20th
March, signed by two directors, witnessed by the prisoner) I received no
transfer deed, nothing but that.
