I was in the shop on Saturday, the 17th July, when two men came in—one of them went away rather abruptly, and I afterwards missed two boxes of ribbon—I have looked at the ribbons produced, and they are all my father's—I covered this box myself—after the prisoner was in custody, I went to his house with the officer and found about five pieces more of my father's ribbon—they were the same pattern as those we lost—I do not find any of my father's marks on these five pieces—I only judge from the pattern—they are worth about 15 s . THOMAS PARKER. I am in the house of Cheaper and Co., Wood-street, Cheapside—they used to serve the prisoner with ribbons. I only know from our books when we last supplied the prisoner with ribbons—I have the day-book here in which the goods are entered—we have a ledger as well, in which we book the amount of the different entries—the first entry is made in the day-book.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Is that in your handwriting? A.  No, but my name is at the bottom as having given the parcel in.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  When was the last time you sold him ribbons? A.  On the 21st of April, 1840.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I dare say yon will undertake to swear that there were no ribbons sold to him since 1840? A.  I will—there are about three of us who sell ribbons—this book is finished—there is another book suc ceeding this, but that is not here—this book goes down to May, 1841—we are still carrying on business.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Have you searched through the other book? A.  No, because we have not done any business with the prisoner since 1840, I know that of my own knowledge—the other book begins with 1841—we discontinued our account with the prisoner in 1840, and have not done any further business—ours is a wholesale-house—three of us manage the business, Mr. Jackson, Mr. Arnold, and myself—I see most of the goods that go out—I can fetch the other book. WILLIAM HOLLAND. I am a policeman. I went into the prisoner's shop with the prosecutor and another officer—some ribbons were shown to the prosecutor—the prisoner produced a file of invoices—I looked them over—I did not take them away—the prisoner said be had bought those ribbons of Cheaper and Co., 36, Wood-street, Cheapside, some months ago—I turned round to the prosecutor, and said, "This is a serious charge to make against an apparently respectable man, look at your ribbons again, and be satisfied beyond the possibility of all doubt, and be sure they are your a"—he looked again and said, "I am sure they are mine, they were in my shop a few days ago, my son papered the box, and previous to being papered with blue there was grey paper"—I looked, and there was grey paper under neath. HENRY WILLIAM DUBOIS. I am a policeman. I went to the prisoner's shop after he was in custody, and found five pieces of ribbon in one of the drawers behind the counter, which the prosecutor has seen—Hayward, a constable, was there at the time—the prosecutor's son recognized them. DANIEL STUBBS re-examined.  All the ribbons in this box are mine—they are not packed as they were when they were lost—there were more in quantity than are here, and some were in a different box.
