GILBERT MAUNDER. I live at 18, College Street, Camden Town, and am a signalman on the Metropolitan Railway—I was at the corner of St. Pancras Road—a constable called for assistance—he was struggling with the prisoner over a jemmy  —I know what a jemmy  is—the constable put it in his pocket—my mate came up at that time—somebody eke came up as if to assist the police, and the prisoner handed him something, and he asked the prisoner if he had got the hammer and chisel—I cannot say what became of the jemmy  after the constable took it—the man came behind the constable, I am certain—at the station I heard the prisoner say that he had been quarrelling with a young woman, and she hit him, and he hit him, and had run away.
   Cross-examined.  I was with Butcher—we assisted to take the prisoner in custody, but not the other man, we had quite enough to hold the prisoner—we were intent on keeping him—I spoke of the hammer and chisel before the magistrate, and I said that I saw the jemmy  —I have seen dozens—we use them sometimes—it was rather a foggy night—I should not mistake an umbrella for a jemmy. JOSEPH BUTCHER. I am employed on the Metropolitan Railway, and was with Maunder—I have heard his evidence—it is right.
   Cross-examined.  I was not the third man who was standing by—I was assisting the others—I heard some conversation about what I took to be a chisel and hammer—we did not secure the other man, because we were occupied in holding the prisoner—he was rather slippery—I saw the consta ble take the jemmy  from him and put it in his coat pocket—I did not take it out of his pocket—I know a jemmy  when I see one—the man was behind the constable for about a minute—it was a scuffle. GEORGE HEARN. I am a grocer, of 347, Gray's Inn Road—I was called up about a quarter past two on the morning of the 28th—I went down, examined the house, and found the top lock of the shop door picked, and the bottom lock forced—it could have been forced by a jemmy  —the top lock had to be repaired—everything was in disorder—a cab-mau's carriage candle was on the counter, warm, just having been put out—the till was picked to pieces—my desk was forced open, and 7 s . Taken from it, 2 s . 6 d . in bronze, and a gold pencil case—the grating is ordinarily fastened by a chain from the inside-a person getting in at the door could come out at the grating.
   Cross-examined.  The front door leading into the street was broken open, and anybody could get in or out—it had been locked so as to lock the cabman out, who had been trying to get into my house, and the cellar door had been forced to enable the prisoner to get up into the street—I think two were engaged, as the candles are peculiar, and would want another person to hold them—I count my money every night—I was disturbed by a noise—the cabman's wife knocked me up—they live on the top floor. GUILTY*— Eighteen Months' Imprisonment.
