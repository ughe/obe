782. WILLIAM CUTHBERTSON (23), Feloniously cutting and
wounding Eliza Newoombe, with intent to murder her:
MR. STRAIGHT conducted the Prosecution; and MR. CHARLES MATHEWS
the Defence.
ELIZA NEWCOMBE. I am nineteen years old, and am now in St. Bartholo-
mew's Hospital—in January this year I left my situation, and went to live
with the prisoner-he is a hairdresser's assistant-we lived at 27, Cowper
Street, Islington, in July, and I had charge of Mrs. Barth's baby, who
lodged in the same house, while she was out all day at her business the
baby was subject to convulsion fits on 25th July Mrs. Barth wanted me
to go and have the baby's likeness taken I did not tell the prisoner
where I was going, but I asked him whether I could go out-I gave him
the key in case I did not get back in time my sister went with me, and
on our way home I called where the prisoner was employed, and got the
key from him I went home and prepared tea, and he came home about
six o'clock, and said that I had been rather too long I said that I had
not been longer than I could help, and showed him the baby's photograph
-he said that I had no business to have it taken, wasting my money-I
said that my sister paid for it she was not present—we began tea, and
in the middle of it he asked to see the picture again, and he deliberately
broke it on the corner of a chair, and threw it on the fire, and smacked me
in the face once or twice I think I flew at him, it was in self-defence if
I did, and he struck me again in the chest with his fist, I believe until
he smacked my face I had done nothing to provoke him that I am aware
of-on the morning of the 26th he went to his work, and came home to
breakfast about 10 o'clock, having shaved off his moustache and whiskers
-I said "What have you done that for? what a boy you look” he
said "I have had a clean shave to swing for you in "-I said “I am
glad you let me know"--we breakfasted in Mrs. Barth's room that morn-
ing because the baby was so ill, but Mrs. Barth was not present, she had
gone to her work—as we were sitting down to breakfast the prisoner said
to me "If you have got anything to say you had better say it, as you have
not much longer to live; you may live another half hour, and you may
live till to-morrow”-I said “You have threatened me so often, I take
no notice of your threats "-he never said anything for ever so long, and
then he got up off his chair and threw himself across the hearthrug-I
asked him to get up, but he made no reply-I went downstairs, and
came up again, and he was still on the hearthrug-I said “Mr. Pick-
ford," that is his employer, “has sent for you, and wants to know if you
