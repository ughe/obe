because, while I held him, I felt his arm come against me—I saw nobody else when the light came—I then went into the bar, and found it strewed with various things—some silver spoons and forks were bent, and thrown about the bar—I found the bar door had been opened which I had locked at night, and taken the key to bed with me—I found the bar window had been forced by a crow-bar—I found one of the first floor windows open, which I am positive was shut when I went to bed—it was merely kept down by its own weight—I found the dust on the stone of the balcony scraped away by finger  marks, and the marks of a shoe with a nail—the balcony is about nine feet from the ground—a person could easily get up there, and from there get to the window—from a small cupboard in the bar, I missed three silver spoons, and a five shilling paper of copper—my bar-maid's work-box was forced open—the tills were emptied of four sixpences, and about four shillings of copper money—nearly five pounds weight of cigars were taken from inside the bar—I found many of them strewed about the passage leading to the stable yard, and one bundle, unbroken, in the stable yard—two stairs below where I struggled with Huggett, I picked up a life-preserver; and the 5 s . packet of halfpence I picked up where I threw Huggett—the life-preserver was close to the spot—about ten minutes after I went down stairs into the cellar, with the policemen, and heard a policeman say, "Here is another man"—he was in the cellar—we found the prisoner Jones in the coal cellar—after he was taken, we were searching among the coals, and on turning them over, found a crow-bar, which I afterwards fitted to the bar window, and it answered the marks exactly—I do not think any of the prisoners were in the house the previous night—the back-door was fastened before I went to bed.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Was it open when you got up? A.  It must have been, because assistance came to me—I did not go to it—the first time I went to it, I found it open; that was when I gave Huggett in custody, which was five minutes after I came down stairs—that door had been bolted—I did not bolt it myself—I looked at it before I went to bed, and found it bolted—it was about two o'clock in the morning—I am sure I was the last person up in the house—my establishment consists of three female servants, my wife, and a male servant—they had all retired—I did not go into every room in the house at two o'clock—I was in the room, the window of which was open, when I went to bed—it was the last room I visited—I am positive I closed the window myself—there is no fastening to it—I examined the marks on the balcony, within about an hour after the pri soners were taken—I am quite sure I kept hold of the man in the passage, and when the light came, I found it was Huggett—I only struggled with one man—the house is in the parish of St. Mary, Lambeth—it is all one parish, Lambeth.
