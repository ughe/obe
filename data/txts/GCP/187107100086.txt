Guilty
The Prisoner. "I have only to say that I am innocent, and that if I had
been guilty, in my own mind no punishment could be too great for me, if I
did that to him who for so many years has been all in all to me. I left my
husband for him. Mr. Moon knew me when I was in society, and in my
own family. Upon my husband leaving me, he wished me to get a divorce.
I returned to my husband at Boulogne to save him from a divorce. I re-
turned to this country again, and my husband followed and broke up every-
thing in the house, saying that all I wanted was a divorce, and that he
would not have it. Mr. Turquand wrote letters to my husband at the
diotation of Mr. Moon. After that his family persuaded him to leave me.
He did leave me. I went to John Moon and asked him to give Frederick
Moon a letter for me, and I would never see him again. He said 'No' and
that Sir Francis had determined that no letters should be sent. In the
meantime Frederick Moon returned, and it was agreed that I should belong
