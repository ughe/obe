WILLIAM HARRIS. The goods were delivered to this order - the prisoner produced this order to me.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Are you quite sure goods were delivered to this order? A. The goods were fetched from the wharf - I do not live there; I asked the prisoner if they were to be sent, and he said No, the party would send their own cart to fetch them; he had no hand in taking the goods away, that I know of.
 COURT. Q. Did the prisoner give you this bill of 18l. 5s. 6d. in part of payment? A. Yes. When Mr. Hipkiss came to London, I saw him frequently on the subject of these bills; I went with Mr. Hipkiss to present this bill for payment - it was refused; I am not quite sure whether we saw the prisoner on the road, or whether he went with us, but I had a bag with some tea in it, which I gave the prisoner to hold, and he waited in Compton-street while I took the bill - when I came out I told him that they knew nothing of any such parties; he said he knew not how that could be, because there had been a bill paid there; I think I left him then, but I saw him several times afterwards - I believe he frequently came to Mr. Hipkiss' inn. and he once or twice called on me; I said to him,"These bills stand in a very awkward state;" he said he would pay them this time and that time, but he never did.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I lived at No. 35, King-street, during the whole of 1828. I believe the prisoner's brother, whose name is John, lodged with me about fourteen or fifteen months, but not in 1828, it was in 1827; I never saw the prisoner himself there.(Bill read as in the indictment.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The moment Mr. Hipkiss waited on me I went with him, and had I had the least suspicion of these bills being forged. it is not likely that I should have gone there; my reason for saying I would pay these bills, was, that I considered myself in some way responsible for the loss occasioned by my brother's conduct.
     NOT GUILTY.

 998.  RICHARD HARPER GREEN was indicted for that he, on the 17th of July, at St. Botolph Without, Aldersgate, had in his custody and possession a certain bill of exchange, which is as follows, that is to say:
 £23: 14 Dudley, July 23,1828.
 Two months after date, pay to my order, Twenty-three Pounds, Fourteen, Shillings, value received, for  RICHARD FINCH. To Mr. James Lane, No. 4, Bull Head-court, King-street, Snow-hill, London. on which said bill of exchange was then and there contained a certain false, forged, and counterfeited acceptance of the said bill of exchange, and which said false, forged, and counterfeited acceptance is as follows, "Accepted, James Lane ;" and that he, the said Richard Harper Green, afterwards, to wit, on the same day, at the same parish, feloniously did utter and publish as true, the said false, forged, and counterfeited acceptance of the said bill of exchange, with intent to defraud Richard Finch , he, the said Richard Harper Green, at the time when he so uttered and published as true the said false, forget, and counterfeited acceptance, well knew the same to be false, forged. and counterfeited; against the Statute, & c.
   RICHARD FINCH. I am a fender-maker, and live at Dudley, in Worcestershire- the prisoner sent me orders down into the country for goods. On the 17th of July he said he owed me 38l. 15s. 6d; he said he would give me this bill for 23l. 14s., and he would pay me the rest - he gave me the bill, and I took it with me into the country; he agreed to pay me the 15l. 1s. 6d., but he did not - at the time he gave me this bill I was at Mr. Stevens', the Rose and Crown public-house, in Little Britain; this is the bill - my son endorsed the bill in my presence; I paid it away, and it came back to me in the country.(The bill was here read as in the indictment.)
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. This bill became due on the 26th of September last? A. Yes; I had it returned to me unpaid - I staid about a fortnight, and then came to town - I went to Bull Head-court, and inquired about Mr. Lane; they said no such person lived there - I saw the prisoner afterwards, and he said he would give me another bill; I said it would be of no use - he did give me a bill for 18l.; this is it - it is drawn upon the prisoner; his brother was with him - (bill read)
 £18: 0: 0 Dudley, October 28, 1828, Two months after date pay to my order, Eighteen Pounds, value received, for RICHARD FINCH.
 To R.H.Green, No. 11, Harper-street, New Kent-road. Accepted, R.H.GREEN.
 Q. Did not his brother promise to see this hill paid? A. Yes - I cannot say when I made this charge against the prisoner; it was about five or six weeks ago, since he was taken up - it was long after the second bill became due; I had been stopping at his house several times in the interval, and this last time I lived and slept there for nine or ten nights - I think not more; that was after the second bill was returned - he was taken on another bill before I gave charge of him; I have seen John Green since the prisoner was charged with this bill, but he had nothing to do with it; I had one bill before on Mr. Lane, which the prisoner sent down into the country - I paid it away, and it was paid; I sold  John Green some fenders, and I took a bill of him, drawn on Nicholson, for them.
 COURT. Q. Was there another bill, which became due in February? A. Yes; I was in the country when it became due - I saw John Green last about five weeks ago; I have since been there, and he is removed.
   THOMAS BROOKS. I am a housekeeper, and live at No. 4, Bull's Head-court, Snow-hill. I have kept the house these three years; no person named Lane has lodged there during that time - I let two rooms, but to no person of that name.
   JAMES JOHNSON. I live at the same house. I know no such name as Lane there; I know the prisoner, and he knew perfectly well where I lived; I have done little jobs for him now and then, when he has asked me, for the last two years - he has very often been to the house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he enquiring after a person of the name of Lane? A. No; he was at the house about a week before he was taken, but I had no conversation with him on that occasion - I am not master of the house.
 COURT. Q. Was he every there in the course of 1828? A. Yes, my Lord, I am certain of that; he has been there to send me on errands on such a thing as that - he lived in the New Kent-road.
