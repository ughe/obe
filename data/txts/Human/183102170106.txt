 598.  WILLIAM ROBROUGH GORNALL was indicted for bigamy.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   OCTAVIUS YOUNG THISELTON. I am parish-clerk of St. Pancras. I have the register of marriages in that church. On the 17th of July, 1823, William Gornall and Eleanor Elizabeth Prince were married there.
   ISAAC KIMPTON. I am an officer of St. Pancras. I know a woman who applied for relief, in the name of Mrs. Gornall, two or three months ago - I saw the prisoner with her; she got a warrant against him, and he was taken into custody - she claimed in his presence to be his wife; he said he was very sorry for what had happened - he acknowledged she was his wife.
 Q. Did he deny that she was his wife in any way? A. No.
 Cross-examined by MR. BALL. Q. Did he never state to you, that there was another William Gornall, a cousin of his, who was a mariner? A. Never, I am quite sure; I saw the prisoner and the woman I called his wife together, in Britannia-street, when I apprehended them - this conversation took place in the street, in going to Hatton-garden Office.
 COURT. Q. Did both the wives claim relief from the parish? A. No, the first only.
   HARRY PRICE. I am overseer of St. Pancras. A woman claiming to be the wife of the prisoner, applied for relief - I gave orders for a warrant to be issued against the prisoner; he was brought to the vestry-room - I saw him there with Isaac Kimpton; I said I thought it was a great pity the parish should be burdened with his family - he said he had allowed her 10s. a week for some time, but was unable to do so then; I said he must go before the Magistrate, which he did - his wife was there and identified him as her husband; the Magistrate remanded him -I applied to the parish-clerk of All Souls, Marylebone, and got a certificate of the register, which I here produce; I examined it, and it is correct. - (read)
 "In the district rectory of  All Souls, Marylebone, William Robrough Gornall, batchelor, and Hannah Palmer, spinster, were married by banns, on the 4th of October, 1829, by me, H. Nathan, curate, in the presence of Jane Watkins and William Watkins."
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you not confounding  Hannah Palmer with the other wife? A. No - they were both present at the two examinations, but I am speaking of the first time; he was then speaking of his wife, not of Hannah Palmer- he certainly did not speak about Hannah Palmer when he spoke of his wife; he did not tell me that Eleanor Elizabeth was the wife of a cousin of his, who was a mariner at sea, and that he had allowed her 10s. a week for him.
   HANNAH PALMER. I married the prisoner at All Souls, Marylebone, on the 4th of October, 1829.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had cohabited with the woman supposed to be his first wife, but that she was the wife of  William Gornall, who was now at sea; and that he had done his best to maintain both her and Palmer He received a good character.
   HANNAH PALMER re-examined. Q. Whose child is that in your arms? A. The prisoner's - I had been living at home with my father, before I married him; he never represented that he was married, and I never suspected it - he was an excellent husband to me, and a good father.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined Four Months.
 599.  JANE ROGERS was indicted for bigamy.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
 HENRY FOLKES. I produce a certificate of the marriage of  Jane Nowell and James Rogers, at St. Ann's, Westminster, on the 26th of May, in the 42nd year of the reign of George the Third.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Where did you get it? A. From Mr. Sawyer; I saw the register, and can take upon me to say that this is a literal transcript of it.
   WILLIAM NORTON. I am a chair-maker, and live at No. 11, Carnaby-street. I knew the prisoner in 1802, and before that; she was then single - I was invited to go to her wedding, in that year, but I could not go - she was to be married to a person of the name of Rogers; I had known him before - I think the wedding was about May; her master gave an entertainment, but I was not there - I think I saw James Rogers the next day, but the prisoner was not there; Rogers is alive, and is outside the door - he allowed her ten shillings a week, for some time, and I stopped the payment of it; her name was Jane Nowell before her marriage - she afterwards went by the name of Rogers.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know her before? A. Yes, by coming to see her sister - I was invited to the wedding by her brother-in-law, who gave her away; I do not know that she is out of her mind - I am told her husband has paid for her, as a lunatic pauper, in the parish of Clerkenwell; I had known Rogers perhaps two years before - I did not know that he had been married before.
   JOHN HUNTER MARLER. I married the woman at the bar, on the 31st of March, 1807.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know Rogers? A. No; I have paid eight shillings a week for the prisoner, for about two years - I did send her to a mad-house, but I was with her for fourteen or fifteen years, till I became in danger of my life. and I was advised to part with her; she has been living as a lunatic pauper in Clerkenwell.
     GUILTY. Aged 49. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
