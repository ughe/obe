JAMES TIDMARSI. I am a policeman. I took Driscoll into custody

Conolly's Defence. I was engaged to drive my master's horse and cart:
I was standing by the water. side to be hired ; a man came up and asked
me to take fourteen sacks of potatoes from his master's cart, which was
broken down ; I went and unloaded them, and while doing so the prosecu-
tor came up, and I directly missed the man that hired me.
Driscoll's Defence. I was coming along Maze-pond, and saw a mob of
people round the cart, and Conolly unloading the potatoes--that is all I
know about it.

1794. HENRY SAVAGE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of
June, 1 gelding, price 71., the property of William Bates.
WILLIAM BATEs. I am a water-carrier, and live at Richmond. I had
a gelding, which I turned out into the meadows at East Sheen, on
Saturday, the 5th of June, between six and seven o'clock in the evening--the
policeman called me up on Monday morning, between one and two o'clock,
and brought the gelding to me- I had not missed it-the prisoner is a
stranger to me.
William Rogers (police-constable V 57.) I was on duty at East Sheen,
in the parish of Mortlake, on Monday the 7th, about twenty minutes
to one o'clock in the morning-I was standing at the end of Blind-lane
-I heard the noise as if of a horse coming through a hedge--as I was
going towards the place, I met the prisoner in a potato field, leading the
horse-there is no road through that field-I asked what made him there,
it was no place for him to have his horse-he said as he was taking
pint of beer, his horse dropped over the wall into the field-- I told him to
come out on to the road, which he did, and I took him into custody- after
wards showed the horse to the prosecutor, and he claimed it-it was a
gelding.
Prisoner. He said if he was seen with the horse in his possession be
should be taken up by another policeman: I had not the horse in my pos-
session; I had nothing at all to do with it, neither was I in the field.
Witness. He was inside the hedge-I was standing on the footpath which
adjoins the potato-field, and is separated by the hedge-I never said what
he has stated-he was leading the horse by this halter---which was on the
horse-as I was taking the prisoner to the station he took this halter off the
horse's head, and said the horse might go on before us--I saw him fold the
halter up, as if to put it into his pocket, and he dropped it on the road!
got it next morning-we drove the horse on before us to the station, with
out any thing at all.
