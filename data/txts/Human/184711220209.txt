   Cross-examined. Q.  They were left there not to go by railway, but in a private warehouse? A.  Yes, for orders—I do not know whether they were to be re-delivered out to purchasers—they were not delivered there to be sent down the line. JAMES LYNCH. In Oct. last I was in Davis's employment—on the 9th Oct. I took an order from him to Mr. Hicks, at Pickle-herring wharf—I gave it to Mr. Hicks, in his counting-house—I received seven pockets of hops, and took them to the railway-station. EDWARD SAMUEL HICKS. I am in partnership with Francis Nash and another. In Oct. last there were seven pockets of Andrews's hops in my warehouse, belonging to Mr. Collins—they were delivered up to the bearer of this order—when delivered to any other person, the order is required to be endorsed by the party to whom the order is made deliverable—this order is made deliverable to Haynes and Son, and endorsed as from them, to be de livered to the bearer.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do I understand that in the course of business you never deliver without such an endorsement? A.  The order is always en dorsed—I cannot say there never was a delivery of goods without such an endorsement: but there never should be—the order is always endorsed when a party does not apply personally for them—deliveries do take place without endorsements, when the holder of the order applies himself—the prisoner did not himself apply to me.
   MR. HUDDLESTON. Q.  Would you have delivered up these things with out the order signed, "Haynes and Co.?" A.  Certainly not. THOMAS HAYNES. I am a dealer in malt and hops, at Paddington, and carry on business with my two sons. This order, signed "Haynes and Son," is not my writing—I know nothing about it—I never authorized any person to sign it—I know the prisoner—I saw him on 7th Oct.—he sold me a single pocket of hops by sample, marked "Andrews"—he brought the hops to me on the 9th, and I paid him for them at the rate of 78 s . per cwt.—they came to 5 l . 3 s . I think—he had two other samples with him, but I did not pur chase either of those—he sold me only the one pocket—he produced a bill of them made out in his own name, which I have here—( produced )—it relates only to the one pocket—I had no other dealings with him—he did not show me a delivery-order, or an invoice to Mr. Collins—I paid him the money—he did not mention Mr. Collins's name.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The bill is from Collins to you, is it not? A.  No, from the prisoner—I never knew the name of Collins till afterwards—I have known the prisoner twenty years—he has been a hop-factor during the whole of that time—I never knew him an any other capacity—on this occasion I asked him if they were his own hops, because we occasionally buy of growers through factors—I did not suppose he was a grower—he said they were his own—he had other samples with him—he said nothing about having more of Andrews's hops—I should not have been disposed to have bought more at any price—I did not want any more—he has been a respectable man ever since I have known him—his dealings have been so, as far as I have been concerned with him—I have frequently given orders where I have bought through fac tors, to deliver hops to my name, with an endorsement or a regular written order.
