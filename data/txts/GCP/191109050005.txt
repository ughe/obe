1911.
CENTRAL CRIMINAL COURT
VEZEY-STRONG, MAYOR.
ELEVENTH SESSION.
LONDON AND MIDDLESEX CASES.
BEFORE THE RECORDER.
(Tuesday, September 5.)
HARRISON, Cecil (19, printer), pleaded guilty of forging and
uttering, knowing the same t be forged, an order for the payment of
money, to wit, a banker's cheque for £45 10s., with intent to defraud.
Prisoner had previously borne a good character. His employers,
whose name he had forged, had, whilst he was on bail, taken him back
into their employ.
Sentence was postponed till next session.
LUKE, Frederick George Percy (32, packer), pleaded guilty of
maliciously publishing certain defamatory libels of and concerning
Charles Clement Hodges.
Prisoner gave as his excuse for writing three libellous postcards to
his late employer that he had been wrongfully dismissed after 15 years'
continuous service. It was stated by the prosecutor that he had to be
dismissed on account of his quarrelsome behaviour to his fellow em-
ployees.
Sentence, Three months' imprisonment without hard labour, at the
expiration of which he was to be bound over in the sum of £50 to be of
good behaviour for three months.
EDWARDS, George, ocherwise Williams, William (61, kitchen
porter), pleaded guilty of stealing £6 17s. 6d., the moneys of the
Carlton Restaurant Company, Limited, his master.
A long list of convictions were proved; Iprisoner had served, with
other sentences, 24 years' penal servitude. His downfall was
attributed to drink.
Sentence, 18 months' hard labour.
HALLIFAX, Septimus (50, clerk), pleaded guilty of stealing two
valuable securities, to wit, banker's cheques for £550 and £1,700 re-
spectively, the moneys of George Strachan Pawle and others, his
masters.
