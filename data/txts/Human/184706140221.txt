   COURT . Q.  When did you first mention seeing the bottles secreted? A.  On the Tuesday previous to Mr. Miller's accusing her—it was on the Thurs day before that I found her at the cellar—I was afraid to mention it before, as she had been there longer than I had.
   JURY . Q.  How long is it since the first of the twenty bottles was seen in the cupboard? A.  The first I saw was when I had been in the house about a week, the next a few days, after, and the third a few days after that—a bot tle lasted in the cupboard about three days—we have a shilling a week allowed for beer money, and I thought she might have bought it out of that—I am away from the kitchen so much that I could not watch where she got the wine from.  1550. HENRY SMYRKE was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Charles Clarke and another, at   St. Mary, Newington , and stealing therein 2 shawls, value 3 l  . 13 s  .; 1 half-shawl, 1 l  .; 24 handkerchiefs, 3 l  .; 8 neckkerchiefs, 1 l  . 10 s  .; 3 scarfs, 13 s  .; and 637 yards of silk, 60 l  . 7 s  .; their property. JAMES WICKENS. I am carman to Chaplin and Home, and live in Corn bury-street, Old Kent-road. On the 3rd of May, about a quarter-past six o'clock in the morning, I saw two men walking backwards and forwards in the Dover-road, close to Mr. Clarke's shop—one of them took off his top coat, hung it before his legs, and close by the area grating—the other one came behind him, lifted up the grating, and somebody from below put out a large sack—the one who lifted it up put it on his shoulder, and walked towards Napier-street—the prisoner was not one of those men—he was about fifteen yards from the place with a cab  —he saw the men coming with the bag, opened the cab -door, and the person who had it put it into the cab  —I informed the police—I had seen the cab  standing there for about five minutes—the prisoner could see what they were doing, if he was looking.
   Prisoner.  You stated before the Magistrate that I was half-way down Napier-street. A.  I said your cab  was a little way down the street—I do not know the length of Napier-street—I believe you were fifteen yards off Napier-street—I was about 11 yards from the shop, and you were about two yards down the street. WILLIAM RANDALL( policeman. ) On the morning of the 15th April I received information, went towards Napier-street, and saw a cab  which the prisoner was driving towards Kent-street—I saw a man running alongside the cab  —I called to the cab -man to stop—I was in my police dress—he was near enough to hear me, but drove on—the man by the side of the cab  went off in another direction—I followed the cab , stopped it in Long-lane, and asked the prisoner what he had got—he said he did not know—I asked where he had got it from—he said it was put into the cab  in Napier-street—I asked by whom—he said by two men who employed him in Aldersgate-street—I took him to the station, and found the property stated, in the cab  —I produce it—Napier-street is about twenty yards from Mr. Clark's shop—there are four houses in Napier-street.
