JAMES HENRY UITING. I am an upholsterer, living in Percy-street, Tot-
tenham-court-road. I have known Mr. Ovenstone for twenty years I have
perceived a very material change in his conduct and demeanour latterly-I
had opportunities of seeing him every day in June, 1847-I did not find him
the same that he was formerly-he did some work for a very respectable lady
in Tavistock-street-he expressed great apprehensions of not being paid-
there was no difficulty about the payment, I could not make him understand
his business latterly-his memory appeared to fail him continually-he bal
previously been of an exceedingly kind and gentle disposition-in June and
July he used to tell me to execute work in the evening and attend to certain
jobs, and in the morning he had forgotten it altogether-his conduct was the
harsh and unreasonable-he used bad language- I had not heard that before
-I know of his pecuniary losses-be spoke to me of the effect the marriage
of his sister produced upon his mind-it appeared to me to depress him-
had made observations upon his conduct, two months before I heard of this
painful occurrence.
RICHARD HOLT. I am a cabinet-maker, and live at No. 20, Melton-place,
Euston-square. I have known Mr. Ovenstone twenty years- I was at work
for him in May and June last-I have latterly observed an alteration in
manner and conduct-he did not appear to me to act in a business-like mannet,
quite the reversehe was formerly a man of accuracy and precision in giving
orders-latterly, although I could understand the orders when he gave them
he would alter them altogether in a few hours and not know anything at all
about it-I was at work at a house he was finishing in June, his conduct
not reasonable then-I remember his acting in a strange manner at the begi
ning of Aug.--there appeared to me to be a total failure of memory-severu
instances occurred, which I could mention if necessary.
