490. JOHN DUDLEY (24), was indicted for a like offence.
MessRS, POLAND and ORRIDGE conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES WILLIAM GIBBS. I keep the Admiral Vernon in Upper Ground-
street-On 28th April the prisoner came to the bar and asked for 14d.
worth of gin—he gave me a counterfeit shilling ; I examined it and found
it was bad; I told him so, and asked him if he had got any more-he said
he had not, and he pulled out some good silver-he paid me for the gin with
a good shilling- I gave him 10 d. change and returned him the bad shilling
- he left, and shortly afterwards a constable brought him back, and he was
searched in my bar—I saw the constable take one shilling from him—he
showed it to me and I broke it in two pieces—I laid the pieces on the bar,
and I don't know what became of them the constable found no more bad
money on him, and I allowed him to go.
SA
Prisoner. Q. Was I not in liquor ! A. You had been drinking a little,
but was not tipsy.
EDWIN WARD (Policeman, L 67). I took the prisoner on Saturday, 28th
April-he was about thirty or forty yards from the last witness's house
there was no one in his company-I took him back and searched him-I
found on him & bad shilling—I gave it to Mr. Gibbs—he broke it and put
it down, and by some means the pieces got removed from the counter-I
could not find them--I found some good silver on the prisoner, but no other
bad money-he was allowed to go-he had been drinking, but was not
WILLIAM NICHOLSON. I keep the Anchor and Hope in Great Charlotte-
street-On 28th April, between 6 and 7 in the evening, I saw the prisoner
in my bar-my barmaid called my attention to him, and she gave me a bad
shilling—I broke it in two-she said this man had just given it to her-I
Collared the prisoner, and before I took him out of the shop I saw Joseph
Levy pick up a bad shilling which was lying on the floor between the
prisoner's feet--he gave it to me, and he said, "I picked this up from
between his feet "—the prisoner said nothing - I broke that shilling also,
and took the prisoner to the station, and gave the pieces of the shillings to
the constable there.
Mary Ann INGRAM. I am barmaid to the last witness-I remember the
prisoner coming on 28th April—he asked for 1 d. worth of gin, and gave
me in payment a counterfeit shilling-it was between 6 and 7 o'clock-I
called my master and gave him the shilling,
JOSEPH LEVY. I am a sawyer, and live in Eden-street, Broadwall,
Lambeth-On Saturday evening, 28th April, I was at the Anchor and Hope
-I saw the prisoner taken in custody by Mr. Nicholson--I picked up a
counterfeit shilling from between the prisoner's feet-I gave it to Mr.
Nicholson.
John Cook (Policeman, L 112). I searched the prisoner at the station-
I found on him three half-crowns in good money, and one halfpenny-I
took his right boot off, and found in it this counterfeit shilling—he was not
drunk; he might have had a pint or two.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These are all bad, and the shilling found in the boot
is from the same mould as one of the others.
