the reason, but she asked me to wait until after her confinement.
Court. Q. Were any attentions paid by you to Mrs. Bowler? 4.
Not at all-there was no making love at any time-never anything of the
kind between us.
Mr. Bodkin. Q. I believe you were in the house at the time of the
death occurring? A. Yes, Mrs. Bowler's screaming called me into the
room-I went in, and found the deceased dead-Mrs. Bowler seemed al-
most in hysterics-she said nothing on my going in- I asked her what was
the matter-she said that her poor dear Joe was dead-those were the words
she used-within a minute or two I went for assistance, and brought in
the neighbours within a minute of each other, and Mr. Coulthred also
I was present the day that Clark came in after the inquest was ad-
journed-she first laughed, and Mrs. Parfitt and Mrs. Trott called her to
account for it, and she said they were as bad as other people until they
were found out-I do not know what that had reference to-I suppose it
was merely said out of aggravation-no other cause that I am aware of -
I told her I thought she was very ungrateful she said if I did not hold
my tongue, after the affair was settled, she would say something I did not
like-I wished her to say what it was, and urged her several times I did
not hear her say that she trusted to one, or that sbe was in it, and must get
out of it-I think she said at the same time that we would all hang her if
we could-I asked what we should get by hanging her-I have heard
since, that the death had been a good deal talked about in the neighbour-
hood--this was a fortnight after the inquest began the women who have
been here to-day as witnesses were in the habit of coming to the house
Mrs. Morley lived in the house-Mrs. Clark was there for a week-I was
examined before the Coroner-I was not bound over as a witness for the
prosecution.
