I went to the warehouse as usual, and I found a van at the door, with empty bottles and hampers—I went down into the cellar, and there was not the slightest appearance of any wine having been there—I stopped the van, and went to Mr. Exley's—on my return home I found this letter, by post, in the prisoner's writing—he did not call on me again—I went to Mr. Exley's office, and saw the prisoner there—he had given me an I O U about a week before, and he offered to meet me at Mr. Exley's—I never got my shares back—I had before ascertained from the prisoner that they were in the hands of Mr. Exley—the prisoner got me to sign a memorandum—he pressed me very much to allow him a week to settle the matter, and after a little con sideration I consented—his wife was there, and she importuned me as well—that was on the Monday, I believe, and on the Wednesday I was to meet him at Mr. Exley's, and he was to arrange with Mr. Exley that the shares were to be given up to me—on the 9th Sept. I was to have 9 l ., for salary given up to me, and on this consideration I said I would not take any pro ceedings against him—I never got my shares, nor the 9 l ., salary—I do not know what became of the prisoner, I lost sight of him then.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  My friend put into your mouth that the prisoner got you to sign a document; how old are you? A.  Thirty-six—I knew what I was signing, but it was only to be in existence for a week—I knew that I was promising to take no proceedings against him, and if I had received my money and shares I would not—I took proceedings against him about a month ago—I left nearly five months pass—I had not had any communication with him during that time—I met him in Dec. in Mr. Exley's office—I did not tell him that if he would give up the shares that would satisfy me—I had gone to Mr. Exley's that day for the purpose of coming to some arrangement with Mr. Exley—I said, if I possibly could, I would call the next day—I did not, but a friend of mine did.
