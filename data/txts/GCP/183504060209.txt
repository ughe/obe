MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is Grobecker the ground-landlord ? A. Certainly
not the freeholder ; nor a lessee under Hawkins.
Francis FAGAN. I am an inspector of the police - I apprehended
Moore on the night of the 17th of February, at hall-past twelve o'clock, at
the Britannia public-house, Hoxton-old-town-I asked him if his name
was Hustings Moore he said it was not-I repeatedly asked if it was
Hustings Moorehe denied that it was I said, "It matters not, you are
the man I want, you must come with me"-I said I must take him into
custody he said he was a man of independent property, and had pro-
perty at Knightsbridge, worth £15,000 on our way to the station, I
asked him him how long he had known Grobecker-he said, “I have
known him about five months"-he said, “This will complete my affair,
and make my action all the stronger against this man Griange-I never
performed a more mercantile affair in my life than this"-on the following
inorning I apprehended Grobecker, at the Retreat, South Lambeth as I
took him to the station, he asked me what would be the punishment
for the offence alleged in the warrant-- I told him I believed about a
couple of years' imprisonment-I asked him if he was ground-landlord
of Sidney-square, or the street near the Earl Grey-he said, “No ; 10
more than you are" -I said, "What, have you no land ?"- he said, “Nota
bit"-I asked him how long he had known Moore-- he said, About
eighteen months."
Cross-examined. Q. Moore denied his name being Hustings Moore? A.
Yes I had a private coat on, but he knew me very well to be a police-
officer, and I knew him well-I asked him his name, because it was
necessary, as his name was contained in the warrant-I read the warrant
to him afterwards he knew I had not a writ-it was on Sunday morning
-I know Greenwood-street-there is such a place as Sidney-square, and
there was so on the 26th of September-one part of it is built on-I was
not there for some time after the 28th of September--not where the build-
ings are, but some part of it was built some years ago I believe four car-
cases are run up to the first story—there may be five--I have not carried
away any bricks from there I have seen some carried away.
WILLIAM Lines. I am a bricklayer. I have been working for Moore,
at the buildings in Sidney-square-all he has done there--thirty-three or
forty thousand of Mr. Griange's bricks have been used there-not exceed-
ing that I have sold some of the bricks, by Moore's order-I sold thirteen
thousand to Mr. Gardener, at 17. 88. per thousand-I sold six thousand to
Perry, for 11. 98. per thousand-Moore ordered me to deliver them, which
did--I know Grobecker's hand-writing- I have seen two notes from
him, addressed to Moore, making application for money ; one demand was
for a sovereign, and the other sum was not specified that was since Mr.
Griange's bricks were delivered-Moore has shown me notes from Grobecker
and told me he had to meet him at different places-he sent me to sell
one or two thousand bricks, in order to raise the money to meet Gro-
becker-I have seen Moore at Abbott's beer-shop, in Mile-end-road, and
have heard him talk about meeting Griange, at Uxbridge-he said he bought
a certain portion of bricks that a man, named Burton, introduced him to the
field-he said he met Griange by appointment in Holborn, and a man named
Toovey acted as a partner at the time-he said he put Griange into a cab
