1965. JAMES PERCEVAL was indicted for feloniously assaulting
John Selby Watson, on the 6th of June, putting him in fear, and taking from
his person, and against his will, 7 keys, value 78. ; 1 purse, value 18.; 6 80-
vereigns, 4 half-sovereigns, 2 half-crowns, and 2 shillings, his goods and mo-
nies; and that he immediately, before and at the time of the robbery, feloniously
did beat, strike, and use other personal violence to the said John Selby Watson.
MR. BODKin conducted the Prosecution.
Jown Selby Watson. I now live at Maidstone, but at the time of this
transaction I lived at Eltham. On the 6th of June last year, I was going
home down Wellhall-lane, leading to Eltham, out of the Dover-road-it
was about half-past ten o'clock by the time I got to where I was robbed-
the lane is a mile and a half long-before I got to the spot I passed the
witness Leopard, who is lame- I got considerably ahead of him before any
thing happened when I got about two hundred yards up the lane I was
met by two soldiers, who separated in such a manner as to oblige me to go
between them the one on my right-hand said, “Good night," and the
other at the same moment, or nearly so, struck me with some heavy weapon
which I did not see, on the left side of my head I was stunned by the
blow and knocked down-I remained insensible for some time, and on
coming to myself I found the two men over me, one rifling my pockets and
the other holding me they had turned out one of my pockets, and were
proceeding to rifle the other-I attempted to cry out, but the one who beld
me down put sand into my mouth, and pressed my mouth to prevent my
calling out-they took from me money to the amount of 8l. odd, in six
sovereigns, four half-sovereigns, and some shillings--I had no opportunity
of noticing the features of either of them-I was lying on the ground at the
moment they left me—they ran off as hard as they could towards Wool-
wich-on recovering a little I got up and walked towards Eltham, but
thinking they might have left something on the spot I went back and looked
about for my things, and while I was looking Leopard came up I was
bleeding very much-with his assistance I proceeded to Eltham--the clock
struck eleven just as I entered Eltham-when I was lying in the lane
I laid along the road, in the same direction as the road it was rather a
wide part of the lane--it was a moonlight night, but the moon was behind
the men as they came up-there is a pathway there-I was in the pathway
when struck, but when I was lying down I was in the road-the path is on
the level of the road.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doane. Q. How long was it before Leopard
came up to you? A. Not more than five minutes after the outrage was
committed there is no turning between the end of the lane and the spot
where I was robbed-I passed Leopard within one hundred yards before I
got to the Dover-road-the end of the lane goes into the Dover-road-I was
robbed about two hundred yards from the Dover-road-it might be more,
but cannot be much more-I do not think it possible a person could walk
along the road without seeing me lying there I saw that the men were
Marines from their dress--they had scarlet jackets-I cannot say that I
