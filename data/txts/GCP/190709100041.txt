BENNETT, Frederick William (23, postman), pleaded guilty to
stealing a post letter containing a valuable security, to wit, & postal
order for 6s., the property of the Postmaster-General, he being om-
ployed under the Post Office.
Mr. Forster Boulton, M.P., prosecuted; Mr. Graham Mould ap-
peared for prisoner.
It was stated that prisoner had been in the army for 34 years, had
purchased his discharge, and had afterwards been in employment
for two years before entering the Post Office as auxiliary postman at
156. a week. Sentence, Nine months' hard labour.
KEMP, George (38, barman), ANDERSON, William (34, cook);
both stealing a silver watch, the goods of Thomas Langley Rose, from
his person. Kemp pleaded guilty.
Mr. A. H. Pocock prosecuted.
Tuomas LANGLEY Ross, 265, Franciscan Road, Tooting, clerk. On
Saturday, August 31, 1907, I was at Fresh Whart with my wife
waiting for the Bello steamer on which my son and daughter were
returning home. As the people were coming off the boat, Kemp and
another man were close to me. As I leaned over to touch my
daughter, my wife called out to me to mind my watch, and I caught
Kemp in the act of taking it from my pocket. It was afterwards
picked up and is the watch produced. I bought the wateh 27 years
ago for "35s., but it is an exeellent timekeeper, and I would not
change it for a £10 watch. Just at the time some one spoke to me
about the Kingfisher. I do not identify Anderson as the man who
spoke, but the first question he asked me at the police court was,
“Did not I speak to you about the Kingfisher ! This was said to
me about five or ten minutes before the watch was stolen, as the
boat was coming in.

with Kemp, and I saw Kemp whisper to him. They looked at my
