ALFRED GREEN (City-policeman). I went with Levy to Chicksand-street
first, and from there to Goldstone-street-in consequence of what he said,
I went into 42, and made inquiries if a person named Caufman was there
I was denied at every room-I went into every room and searched before I
found him I found him at the top of the house in & back room-I had
been in the passage of that house for half an hour before I found him-both
myself and a countryman of the prisoner's had been calling out for Caufman
-the only answer that came was, that he was not there-he must have
heard us call-I told him that I was an officer, and that he was charged
with robbing his employer, Mr. Moses, of between 51. and 61. in silver-he
appeared not to understand me-I repeated it to bim several times at length
he said, “Me no thief ; me no rob 18. I searched him, but found nothing
on bim-while I was searching him, the lad Levy said, “Caufman, it looks
very bad against you ; you have not been home to-night, and you have kept
away from your work-he replied, “Well, I have been very ill, and all the
tailors know it"- a female in the room said, “He has nothing to do with
me, I have not seen him for about a fortnight"-a little girl, who I believe
to be a relative of the prisoner, said something to me, but I cannot say
whether the prisoner heard it or not.
Cross-examined, Q. You stood at the bottom of the stairs, and called out
“Caufman ?" A. Yes; and went up stairs-somebody from that very room
answered that he was not there--I searched the room-I partly looked over
it while he was there ; bat after taking him to the station, I went back and
had a further search.
Mr. LEWIS. Q. You had no search-warrant from the Magistrate? A.
No; and the female denied his having anything to do with the room; that
it was not his room-the woman was not in bed-she lay down on the bed
afterwards.
