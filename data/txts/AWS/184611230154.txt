and 5 shillings: the goods and monies of John Dennis, from his person.
JOHN DENNIs. I am a leather-dresser, and live in West-street, Walworth.
On the 24th of Nov., between twelvea one o'clock, I was in the New Kent-
road, going home, and met the prisoner-she said, 46 Where are you going
-I said, I am going home me"-I was close against a door, and was forced
into a bouse-I think the door was ajar-] had had a little drink; but was
sober enough to know what I was doing-when I got into the house there
was another woman there besides the prisoner-I am sure she is one of them
-they both began to rummage my pockets-I had a purse containing five
or six shillings-I am quite sure I had it safe five minutes before I was pulled
into the house-they took the purse out of my pocket, stripped the coat from
my back, and forced me into the street-I called 44 Police policeman
came, and I gave the prisoner in charge.
HeNRy POYLE (policeman.) I heard a cry of 44 Police!" I went to the place,
and found Dennis standing in his shirt sleeves-I knocked at the door, but
could gain no admission-] forced it open, and found the prisoner in the
house--Dennis charged her with having rummaged his pockets, and taken
his coat from his back-she said she certainly took the coat, but did not intend
to keep it, and denied all knowledge of the money-I found no purse on her
found the coat folded up on the bed up stairs.
Prisoner's Defence. I was in the house when he came to the door with a
prostitute he asked if he could have a room ? I said, "Yes;' he then threw
sixpence on the table ; I fetched somne gin when I came back he said he
would have us both we went up into the bed-room with him, and he left his
coat with us for half-a-crown a piece, but he went to bed with her; when he
came down he said he would have his coat, or he would swear a robbery
against us.
