 953.  THOMAS HACKWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of March , 5 sovereigns, and 15 shillings, the monies of William Dowson.
   WILLIAM DOWSON. I am a sadler. The prisoner lodged in my house - my daughter gave him a cheque for 5l. 15s. on the 11th of March, 1829, to get the money; he did not bring the money.
   JEMIMA DOWSON. I gave the prisoner this cheque(read, drawn by D. Fletcher, on Messrs, Gillespie for 5l. 15s.) - I gave it to him to be at Messrs. Gillespie's by ten o'clock; I told him if he would go, and come back directly, I would give him something to get a breakfast- he did not come back; I did not see him again till last March - his wife was out at the time; he went away with his child - she returned in a short time, took all their things, and went away.
   WILLIAM COCKERILL. I was at Messrs. Gillespie's when the cheque was paid - I entered it in the book, but I cannot tell who had it; it was paid on the day it is entered - Mr. Dowson came in the afternoon, and asked if we had paid the money; it had been paid two hours before.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. If you had not looked at the book, would you have remembered any thing about it? A. I distinctly remember the payment, but not the person; I looked at the back to see if it was entered - Mr. Gillespie asked me the question, and mentioned the name of Dowson; I cannot say whether it was a man or woman received it.
 COURT. Q. Can you be sure whether the person who complained that the money had not been paid, was the person who presented it? A. My impression is that it was not, but I cannot be positive - it was paid in five sovereigns, but whether the silver was in shillings or halfcrowns I do not know; we keep sovereigns exclusively for the purpose of paying these orders, and never pay notes.
 Q. But if I wanted a 5l. note to send in a letter, you would accommodate me with it? A. It is a very unusual thing; I never knew it done.
 JURY to  WILLIAM DOWSON. Q. What lodging had the prisoner of you? A. A ready furnished room down the yard; there were seven or eight weeks' rent due at this time.
     GUILTY. Aged 39. - Confined Six Months.
 954.  PHILIP HUTCHINSON was indicted for bigamy.
   GEORGE JACKSON GREEN. I saw the prisoner married to a person of the name of Stubbs, about twelve years ago, at Bromley St. Leonard's, but the clerk is not here; I was sexton and am so still - James Brand was also present, but he is dead; there are no other witnesses here.

 955.  JOHN GRIMES was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of March , 1 ham, value 7s., the goods of Thomas Courtney.
 THOMAS COURTNEY. I am a  cheesemonger, and live in Drury-lane. On the 20th of March I lost a ham, about half-past eleven o'clock at night - I did not see it taken, but the Policeman brought it back, and I believe it to be mine; I had had it in my hand twice that day - I do not see any difference between this and mine; it is the same weight and size.
   JOHN RAWLEY. I stopped the prisoner on suspicion, three or four hundred yards from the shop, with this ham under his arm - I asked where he got it; he said at a shop leading to Clare-market, which is the prosecutor's - I lodged him in the watch-house, and took the ham to the shop; he said he bought it at 5 1/2d. per lb., and it weighed 10 1/4lbs., but it weighs 12 1/4lbs. - he was going from the shop.
 Prisoner. I said 6d. per pound.
 THOMAS COURTNEY. It would have been 7d. per pound.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw this ham and asked the price - they said 7d. per pound; I said I would give 6d. for the whole, and it was weighed to me - it weighed 10 1/2lbs., and I gave 5s. 1 1/2d. for it.
 JURY to JOHN RAWLEY. Q. Had he any money on him? A. He stated that he had none, but I believe he was not searched.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.

 956.  JOHN GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 8 live tame fowls, price 16s., and I live tame duck, price 4s., the property of Edward Sadler.
   ROBERT BATEMAN. I am a Police-officer. On the 7th of April, at a quarter before five o'clock in the morning, I met the prisoner on Stepney-green, with this duck wrapped up in a smock frock - I asked what he had got; he told me nothing - I found this duck, and then he said he had bought it half an hour before, of a sailor for 1s. 6d.; I found nothing else on him.
   MARY ANN BRUNEGAS. Mr. Edward Sadler lives at Laytonstone- I take care of his place, and had the fowls and duck in a hen-house; they were all safe and locked up on the 6th of April, and when I got up the next morning they were all gone - I missed eight fowls and this Muscovy duck; I know nothing of the prisoner - they had taken the tiles off, and got in at the top of the house; I know this duck very well, and he knows me.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had it tied up in my old smockfrock - I said it was a Muscovy duck I gave 1s. 6d. for.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
