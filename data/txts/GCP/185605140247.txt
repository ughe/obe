Cross-examined. Q. Were the prisoners drunk I A. Quaid was not
very drunk, Peters was rather drunk, but he was sober enough to say, "It
is mine," when he had got the staff.
GEORGE TAYLOR. I am a waterman at the cab stand in Moorgate-street.
I was there on 10th March, in the evening-I saw the prisoners coming
towards Moorgate-street, and Valler was standing on the pavement
Peters put his hand round his neck, and slapped his face- Valler came
back to speak to them, and Quaid shoved Peters on one side, and struck
Valler—there was a struggle, and they both fell on the pavement I did
not see much more—I saw Peters interfere, and they all went on the pave-
ment, and one of the bystanders pulled Peters from him.
JOHN MILLER (City policeman, 99). I was in Moorgate-street, and saw
Ginn struggling with the two prisoners- I took Quaid into custody-he
kicked me a violent kick on the leg immediately I laid hold of him-we
struggled violently together, and got on the pavement; he got up again-
I got assistance and got him to the station both the prisoners were very
volent indeed.

CHARLES SWALEY SMITH. I am assistant to Mr. Child, the surgeon to the
police. I first saw Ginn on the night that this occurred, he was in bed, and
his legs were in a very inflamed state-- he had been kicked very much about
the shins, and was suffering acute pain, so much so that he could not bear the
clothes on his legs--the skin was off in several parts he has been prevented
from going on duty till now, and will be for some time.
(The prisoners received good characters.)
