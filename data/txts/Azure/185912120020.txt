120. JAMES MOORE (32), was indicted for the wilful murder of Mary
Ann Moore. He was also charged on the Coroner's inquisition with the like
offence. MESSRS. CLERK and BEASLEY conducted the Prosecution.
MARY TURNER. I am the wife of William Turner, 17, King's-head-conrt,
Long-alley—on 23d November the deceased, Mary Ann Moore, took
apartments in my house—she was alone—she came with her husband, the
prisoner, the next night, Thursday, 24th November—I knew nothing
them before—they went on very quietly and comfortably indeed, for two or
three days—the woman appeared to be at home and very comfortable—
I did not see much of her, the time was so short—the prisoner was in and
out—on Monday morning, 28th November, I got up at a little before 8 and
had just lighted my fire when I heard a great screaming in the fint floor
front room, which they occupied—I went up, knocked at the door, and
to open it, but could not—the noise still continued, and I beat violently at
the door and said, You villain, are you going to murder the poor woman!"
—I said that several times but got no answer—the screaming continued,
and I told them I could not put up with such a noise as that—it was the
screaming of a woman—I heard a scuffing and knocking about of the
irons as if they were knocking them together—I told them I insisted on
their leaving my house, and that day too, as I could not put up with such •
noise, and I called to my children to fetch their father or a policeman, and
from that time the noise called loudly—I then went partly down
stairs, and as I came up again I could see under the door where there is
space, and I saw that there was a chair placed against the door—at thgt
time all the noise had ceased—I went up and spoke to him again, and Oid
that I wished him to leave the house—I went down stairs again and all wu
quiet—I had my breakfast, and between 9 and 10 0'clock I heard the door
closed—I did not go into the prisoner's room.
