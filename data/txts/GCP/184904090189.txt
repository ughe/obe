ALEXANDER DAVIDSON. This watch, trowsers, and handkerchief are mine.
and were taken from my dwelling-house.
Cross-examined. Q. What are you? A. A boiler-maker. I was present
when a bundle, containing a satin gown-piece, a watch, waistcoat, and other
articles, was found in the cesspool, when the house was pulled down.
JOSHUA THORPE. I am a pawnbroker. I produce these things pawned on
20th March, 1848, by a female, in the name of “Mary Taylor”- this shawl
was pledged in June, and renewed last January, in the name of " Ann
Chick;" here is the duplicate--the prisoner has frequently pledged in the
name of “M'Donald."
Cross-examined. Q. How lately? A. Not for nine or ten months- I do
not think I have seen her since May 31st-some of the tickets have been re-
newed since, by Clark, and other persons.
CHARLES MAY (policeman, R 296.) I took the prisoner, and told her the
charge-she said she had taken the shawl, but did not know anything about
the other things-her mother came to the station, and said to her, "If you
know anything about Mrs. Davidson's robbery tell me, and I will endeavour
to redeem them for her”- she said, "You can't redeem them all; there is a
coat and trowsers at Mr. Moore's; and when I saw the policeman coming
towards the house, I ran and put the things down the cesspool" - they must
have been there about twelve months.
Cross-examined. Q. You found her living in Mr. Dale's service? A. Yes;
he is a doctor, at Commercial Road East-they said she had lived there some
time-her mother is respectable. (The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to mercy by the Jury.
Judgment deferred.

1034. SARAH STABLER, stealing 1 sovereign; the moneys of Charles
John Tindal, her master.
Emma WHITE. I am in the service of Charles John Tindal, who lives in
Lee-road. The prisoner was in his service-she left on 3rd April-I used
to give her money from time to time to settle the tradesmen's bills--on 2nd
April I gave her a sovereign, and told her to give it to Mr. Beddall—it was
Mr. Tindal's sovereign.
JOHN BEDDALL. I live at Greenwich. Mr. Tindal was a customer of
mine– I was in the habit of sending a sovereign's worth of silver to bim-the
prisoner did not bring me a sovereign on 2nd or 3rd April.
John WATERS (policeman, R 177). I took the prisoner into custody. I
told her what I took her for-Mr. Beddall was with me-the prisoner said she
was very sorry for what she had done, and she wished to take Mr. Beddall
into a private room-I took her to the station, and two sovereigns were
found on her. GUILTY. Aged 22.-Confined Six Months.
