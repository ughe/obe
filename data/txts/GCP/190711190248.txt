To Mr. Justice Ridley. I was to pay the £52 the same day; I had
£23 for one man and the rest for the solicitor. I had to pay in the
money the next day, but I did not do so; I went to the detective and
showed him it, and then went to the bank. It was a claim I had to
settle; I settled it by cheque instead of cash. It was for a burglary
in Barking Road; I sometimes have these large sums in my pockets,
but it won't occur again. I will do it by cheque. The prisoners
know well I have this insurance business. There are any amount of
men I have claims for.
- CLAYTON, 164, Third Avenue, Manor Park, railway porter,
Upton Park Station. I remember the train coming in on the night of
October 2 at 12.35 from Aldgate. After it came in I saw Mr. Thomas
and the prisoners. They were at the far end of the platform. I
saw the four get out of the train. They did not seem to be in
hurry. I went to put the lights out. They went along, but Thomas
going first, and went up the staircase. I saw prisoners hustle him.
Then I went into the waiting-room nearest to the lavatory to put out
the light. Before that I saw the four go into the lavatory. I identify
the three prisoners. I went up to the gate, and Mr. Thomas was st
the side of it telling the collector about his watch. The three then
went out, and Stowe came back again and said, referring to Thomas,
"If he has not got his ticket I will pay." Thomas said, "I want
nothing to do with you; you have had enough; I am in the hands
of pickpockets." I did not hear Stowe say anything then. I went
and olosed the two further doors at the entrance to the station, and
the ticket-collector having finished his duty went away, and I was
left there with Stowe and Thomas in the booking-hall. Then Hayes
oame in, and after hustling him Hayes struck him a blow, knocking
him down, and they also kicked him. Then Thomas made a rush to
go out, making an attempt to catch a Union Jack 'bus that was
pussing, but he was prevented by Stowe and Hayes. He then turned
round and struck them with his umbrella. I rushed out and made
& noise like that of a whistle, and they ran away. Mr. Thomas camo
in and I shut the door and locked it. He was in a state of collapse.
then took him to the porters' room, where he was for about half-an-
hour, I should think, while I fetched & policeman, who came and
took him home.
