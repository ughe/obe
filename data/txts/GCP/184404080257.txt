Before Mr. Justice Williams.
1338. ANN CARROLL was indicted for the wilful murder of Elizabeth
Hobbs; and on the Coroner's inquisition with manslaughter.
MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
William Hobbs. I am a boiler-maker, and live in Holland-street,
Borough. At the time this happened I lived at 2, Bird Cage-alley, where
Carroll and his wife lived - there were several lodgers in the house and my
wife occupied the front-room second floor-I had been there four or five weeks
when this happened-Carroll was my landlord-on the 2nd of March I left my
wife in the Blackfriars-road as I went to work - we had been at a publica
house, and had half-a-quartern of rum together—she did not drink quite a
quarter of it-I left her at five minutes before six in the morning-she was
in good health nerer knew her in better health-when I came home at
night I found her dead-her name was Elizabeth Hobbs.
SARAH BRAY. I lived at this time at No. 1, Bird Cage-alley, next door
to this house. On the morning of the 2nd of March I heard cries of murder
proceeding from the up-stairs window of Carroll's house-I heard people
quarrelling very much I went to the street-door, and demanded for the door
to be opened - I did not see the prisoner at first, nor her husband when I
first saw the prisoner I said, “Mrs. Carroll, what have you got about? or what
do you make this noise about? why don't you send for a policeman ! if you
cannot agree with this woman, have her taken up, don't ill use her like this
-Mrs. Carroll held two pair of boots in her hand, and said to the deceased,
"When you pay me for my broken things I will give you your boots back,
and not before the blood was then running down the left side of the
deceased's neck from the back of the neck, and I saw a mark of a blow on
her temple-it seemed a very heavy blow-the deceased had a fork in her
hand-the prisoner's husband was standing by her side-the deceased beat
the fork on the stairs, and called Mrs. Carroll very bad words indeed several times,
and she said, “I wish it was one o'clock, for my husband to come home, I
would have my revenge for beating me like this"-she addressed herself to Mr.
and Mrs. Carroll—I did not see anything in Mr. Carroll's hand-he might have
had something, and I not see it-1 went into my own room-I then heard the
deceased woman repeating these bad words again, saying, "Don't strike me
with a poker, I am not a man, I am a woman" she addressed herself to Mr.
Carroll-she said, "Mr. Carroll, don't strike me with a poker."
Court. Q. Was the deceased in liquor? 1. It is my opinion she had
a little liquor; but I don't think she had more than to know what she was
about-she stood correct on her feet- saw nothing in the prisoner's hands,
except the boots.
