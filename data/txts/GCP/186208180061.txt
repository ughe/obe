THOMAS ROBERT BLOXHAM. I am a cashier at the Regent-street branch
of the Union Bank of London-I do not remember who brought this credit
slip of 230 June.
CHARLES HILL (re-examined). This slip is in the prisoner's writing-it is
dated 230 June--none of the five cheques are entered in it.
COURT. Q. Would the country cheques be entered in it 4. They
would-country cheques have been paid, but none of these five-after
I received them back I paid them into the bank-I endorsed one of them ;
that was after it came back from the prisoner-it was payable to order,
and was for 141. 18. -- another cheque for 141. 168. 6d., payable to order, had
beon endorsed by my brother Edward, previously-I know that, because I
remember the circumstance; it is simply endorsed Hill Brothers - the
others are all payable to bearer, except one, which was payable to our cus-
tomer himself, who endorsed it, and which amounts to the same thing-it is
for 487. 28. 6d.
Jury. . Were the receipts acknowledged on that day? 4. On that.
and the previous day—the cheques were kept in a cash-book, and were put
away securely at night-the key was under the prisoner's special charge.
GUILTY. --The prosecutors stated that their loss, as at present discovered,
amounted to 9001., and that they were still receiving letters relating to fresh
amounts-Ten Years' Penal Servitude.

John LoVELL DENNING. I am one of the clerks of the Provident Institu-
tion for sayings, St. Martin's-lane-the Marquis of Lansdowne and others
are trustees--persons deposit money there on the savings' bank principle-
it is the course of business to give a deposit-book to every person deposit
ing money there--in April, 1861, a person named Juste Masson deposited
206., the amount being aeposited, the depositor signs his name in a book-I
gave him a depositor's book with a number corresponding with the number
of the entry, 179,341, and I entered the 201.
EDWARD BOODLE. I am secretary and comptroller of this Institution-
it is unusual to require a week's potice of the withdrawal of any amount
deposited, but there are special ciroumstances which cause me to waive that
according to my own diseretion-on Friday, 21st June, 1861, a person
came to the bank with a deposit-book, and in consequence of what he said
I dispersed with the usual week's notice--I believe the prisoner to be the
person, but he has a beard now which he had not then--he said that he was
leaving England on the following Monday: he produced a book, and said
that he was the depositor and wished to have the money then-he spoke in
broken English- waived the usual notice and made this entry of the
transaction, 179,341, all, 201, foreigner leaving England on Monday," and
gave instructions to the ledger clerk to pay him the money.
