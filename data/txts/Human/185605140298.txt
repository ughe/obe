   Q.  You told me you did not remember it. A.  It went out of my mind just at that minute; at least, I quite misunderstood yon—I went to the bankers immediately after meeting him—I swear that I told them he gave the name of Hawkins—I could not say that he had any whiskers at all, if he had, they must be very fair, they were scarcely discernible—I have never said that I was not positive that he was the man, I am positive he is the man—I never said that I was not positive to anybody—I told the inspector I had a very, very slight doubt—I do not remember saying that I was not positive he was the man—I do not remember using such words—I will not swear I did not—I will not swear that I did; I may have said it, and not recollected it—this man's hair is darker than the man's hair was who employed me, but he kept his hat on all the time—his features are the same—if he had lighter hair he must have had a wig—no one suggested to me about a wag—I did not mention to Mr. Mullens that he had a wig—I did not know that there was a necessity for it—I might have said it at the police court—I might have said it to the police constable—I did not say so to Mr. Mullens—I do not remember that I said so to the clerk—he took down what I had to say.
   MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Did the police Magistrate allow you 17 s . 6 d . for your expenses? A.  Yes—I was not paid that at the office, but Mr. Mullens's clerk paid it me—I made a representation to him of my distressed condition, and he gave me 15 s . more—during the interview I had with the prisoner on the first occasion, he kept his hat on the whole time, that was what gave me the idea of his wearing a wig—I have no doubt that this is the man I saw in Stanhope-street, and the man I saw afterwards in Oxford-street—I am positive he is the man. HANNAH BARTON. I am servant at No. 16, Stanhope-street, Regent's-park. In March last some one came to look at the apartments—I let him in, and showed him up stairs—on the following day I let Bowells in—the same man was there then—Bowells afterwards went out—I let him in a second time—he went out again, and I let him in a third time—the man was still there—I did not see Bowells leave again, or the man—I did not see the man again until one Saturday, about a month afterwards, when the policeman brought him to the house in a cab  —I opened the door, Bowells was with them—as soon as the man came into the house Bowells asked me if that was the man that came to our house and took the apartments—I said to the prisoner, "If you are not the man, you are his brother"—he said, "I am not"—he became rather violent, and I fainted away.
