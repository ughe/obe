GEORGE Willis. I am a grocer, and live at Armley, near Leeds. On
Sunday morning, 28th Sept., about 8 o'clock, I was at Anderton's Hotel,
md saw the prisoner in the coffee-room-I saw a 201.-note in the waiter's
hand, who said that change was required- offered to give change--gave
him three 51.-notes and five sovereigns; the prisoner was about five yards off
- the waiter gave him the money into his hand, and pointed to show him
that two were country notes, but payable in London--there was a Salop note,
Provincial Bank of England note, a Bank of England note, and five sove-
reigns- put the note into my purse-I took it to Mr. Watson, of Chapter
house Court, a boarding house keeper, having beard that some Yorkshire

keep for me till I returned from France-I am sure I gave him the note
received at Anderton's Hotel-I had only one 201.-rlote.
Cross-examined. Q. Were there many persons in the room at Anderton's
Hotel ? A. I think not more than three, besides the waiter-I took it to
Mr. Watson, between 11 and 12 on the same day-1 left 61. or 71. more with
him, in his small room, and retained sufficient for my expenses in France-
returned from France on the Tuesday, I got back my note and money from
Mr. Watson, and gave it to Mr. Quick's clerk, of Fenchurch-street, a coffee
merchant- I called at Fenchurch-street, and asked Mr. Quick to send bis
clerk with me, and I went to Mr. Watson's, and gave the boy the 201.-note
in the room-I had retained about 5l. to spend on the continent-I went by
an excursion train-I was only in Paris from half-past 7 or 8 in the morning
till half-past 7 or 8 in the evening-I left the London station about a quarter
10 8 on Sunday night, and arrived in London again at 11 on Tuesday.
