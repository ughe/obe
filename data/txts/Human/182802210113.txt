 677.  JOHN LEARY and MICHAEL COLLINS were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 10 yards of rope, value 2s., the goods of William Smith.
   SAMUEL SMITH. I am son of William Smith, a carrier. I was in Whitechapel, about nine o'clock in the morning, with his cart, and missed this rope - I had seen the prisoners about Whitechapel.
   JAMES FOWLER. I am an officer. On the 12th of February I was in Whitechapel, and saw Leary take the rope from the cart, and give it to Collins - we secured them.
   FRANCIS KEYS. I was with Fowler. I saw Leary creep under the cart, take the rope, and throw it to Collins.
 LEARY's Defence. I asked this boy where I could sell it - I have no friends.
 COLLINS' Defence. He asked me where he could sell it- I was not near the cart.
 LEARY -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 COLLINS -    GUILTY. Aged 11. Whipped and Discharged.
 678.  JOHN MARCH was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of January , a pair of stays, value 3s, the goods of Sarah Moss.
 SARAH MOSS. I am a  stay-maker, and live in Prospect-row, Kingsland. On the 15th of January I was in the parlour, I heard a noise, came into the shop, and saw the prisoner about two doors off, with something in his apron - I missed the stays from the window; I was too ill to follow him, and he got away; I am sure he is the boy - I saw him again on the Saturday.
   ANN SARAH BARLOW. The prisoner came to me on the morning of the 15th, to my shop, which is a quarter of a mile from Moss' - he asked the price of tulip roots - I told him 4d. each - he went out, but came in soon after, and took a hyacinth glass from the window - I took it from him, and found these stays in his apron; he said he brought them from his aunt's, at Haggerston - I gave him in charge.
   GEORGE SMITH. I took him in charge, with the stays, which have the shop ticket on them.
 GUILTY. Aged 12. Recommended to Mercy. - Whipped and Discharged.
 679.  ELIZA NORMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of December , 1 gown, value 5s., the goods of William Willis.
   CAROLINE THOMPSON. I am William Willis' daughter-in-law - he lives in Stingo-yard. On the 11th of December the prisoner came and asked if Mrs. Thompson lived there; I said Yes, as that was my mother's former name; she asked me to show her the privy, which I did - she said she did not like to go there, and asked to go up stairs - I took her up; I stood on the landing-place - she pointed to my room, and asked if I slept there; she was scratching her leg, and said it itched, and I think she was then taking my mother's gown, for there was nobody there but her - it was a chocolate colour, with a chintz flower; she was gone before I missed it; I told Mr.Willis, when he came home, and in about two months I saw the prisoner in the street, and told my mother that was the woman - she sent for an officer, and he took her; I had seen the gown about an hour before she came.
   MARY ANN BASS. On the 11th of December I was standing at my mother's door, No. 2, Stingo-yard. The prisoner came and asked if I knew Mrs. Thompson, who kept a mangle - I said No, but a neighbour directed her to Willis' - the prisoner knocked at their door, and went in, and in about half an hour my mother sent me to Mr. Jenks', a pawnbroker, and while I was there the prisoner came in with Mrs. Willis' gown, which she laid on the counter, and asked 3s. on it - they would only lend 2s. 6d. - I said to her, "You found it out?" she said Yes - I said, "But she did not keep a mangle?" she said No, it was her mistake; that it was all right, and thanked me - I had seen Mrs. Willis wear the gown.
   JOHN EDWARD BENNETT. I live at Mr. Jenks'. The prisoner came and offered a chocolate gown, with a chintz flower - I would not lend her 3s., and she took it away.
   THOMAS HENRY THOMPSON. I am a Bow-street patrol. On the 16th of February, in consequence of information, I went to No. 4, Stephen-street, and found the prisoner; she said she was the most unlucky girl in the world, and offered to pay for the gown rather than be locked up.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going for something for dinner, when this child accused me of taking the gown; I never saw her in my life, and was never in the place; I took no gown to the pawnbroker's.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 680.  JAMES O'NEAL was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of February , 1 set of harness, value 2/., and 1 brush, value 6d., the goods of Joseph Duck.
   BAILEY DUCK. I am the son of Joseph Duck, of Plaistow. On the 2d of February he hired the prisoner to mend this harness; and next day two officers came, and produced it cut into pieces, and quite destroyed.
   JOSEPH NEWSOM. I am an officer. On the 2d of February, about half-past seven o'clock, I met the prisoner, with these pieces of harness, in Whitechapel-road; he was putting them under his coat; I asked where he got them; he said he bought them in Rosemary-lane: I took him to a public-house; and Tyrrell found the rest of the harness round his body, and inside his hat.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I was with Newsom, and found this harness bound round the prisoner's body; I found "J. Duck, Plaistow," cut on one of the pieces, and went there.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to Rosemary-lane to buy some old harness to make it up again.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Three Months.
