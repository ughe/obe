991. CHARLES FAULKNER, feloniously receiving 8,124 precious
stones, and other articles, yalue 381. 128.; the goods of Richard Willis.
MR. W. J. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
if
RICHARD CHECKLEY (police inspector, E). On 8th Oct, about half past
5 o'clock in the afternoon, I went to the prisoner's house, at No. 52, Mor-
timer-street, Marylebone_I saw him I said to him, "Your name is Faulk
ner"_he nodded, and I then said I wished to speak with him in private
- he keeps a jeweller's shop)—I passed round the counter, and handed him
my card, and at the same time I handed him a bill with the particulars of
the property stolen from Mr. Willis's, of Clerkenwell-green-this is the bill
-I said, “I must have this property"-he said, "I have no property" -1
said, "I have the best information that you have got it"he hesitated, and
trembled very much, and said, "I have got it, I hope I shan't get into
trouble-he then produced a drawer, there were some stones in it, and he
said, “That is all I have"_Mr. Willis then came in, and he said, "They
are not half"-I do not remember that the prisoner said anything in answer
to that-1 said to him, "You must consider yourself in my custody his
wife went up stairs immediately, and he followed her, and I followed also
when up stairs, be said to his wife, “Give me that brown paper parcel that I
gave you last night"-she took a paper parcel from the cupboard, and handed
it to him-I took it this is it it contains coral-I took him to the
station he asked me to shut up his shop before I left, and I did 80I saw
him at the station I went to the cell to him-I told him I had closed his
shop, as he wished me he then made a statement to me, which I took down
in writing I did not make him any promise or threat.
