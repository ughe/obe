Philip FORGARTY. I am a retired publican, residing at Plumstead, i
Kent. I paid 500l., in five notes, to Mr. Daly on 23rd Nov., 1852-I do not
know the numbers of the notes--I got them from Robarts and Co., No. 15.
Lombard-street--they were paid there, in exchange for a check of my own for
5501.--the other 507. I had 101. notes for-Mr. Danby was the clerk wbe
gave me them.
ROBERT DANBY. I am cashier, at Robarts and Co. I paid a check to
Forgarty on 22nd Nov., 1852—I do not recollect paying him, but I have a
entry of it in the book in my own handwriting-by looking at this entry!
can refresh my memory sufficiently to say that it was paid-bere is the entry
-I have no recollection of the transaction independently of the book- I can
only say that I paid certain notes for a certain check; I do not remember
paying these notes at all-by looking at this book, these notes must have
been paid for that check--from the fact of my having made this entry that
must have been the case-those notes were paid on that occasion by me.
Court. Q. All you can say is, “This is my genuine handwriting, and I
am sure I should not have entered this unless I had paid the notes ?" A.
Yes; but independently of seeing this, I have no recollection whatever I am
sure I should not have entered this unless I had paid the notes-it was for a
check for 5501., drawn by Seager and Co.--I paid five 1001. notes, numben
66042 to 66046 inclusive, dated 10th May, 1852-I paid them on 22nd Nov,
1852, and I paid the 501. in five 101. notes—that was the only check of that
amount paid that day by me.
GEORGE Tosh. i am shopman to Mr. White, a provision merchant, Nos
10 and 11, East Smithfield. I was in his employ on 5th August-I remember
some one coming to our shop that day to apply for a passage to New York-
we are agents for ships-it was a woman I cannot swear to the prisoner-
that woman were here that applied for the passage I should know ber-
believe the prisoner is not the woman-I received from the woman who did
come a 1001. note- I had to give her change-I obtained the change for it at
the Bank of England - I gave her the change the same day-I had to deduct
out of it about 541. for the passage of a person-471. 10s. went to the credit
of the ship that party sailed in the Hendrick Hudson-I took it on account
of that ship-I paid much money for passages in that same ship-I paid that
471. 108. alone to Mr. White-when I gave the change to the woman, she left
- I saw her again-on Saturday morning, 6th August, sbe came to my em-
ployer's in company with a man and a woman, who I am sure was not the
prisoner-on that 6th August there were some provisions bought-I gave a
ticket for them to the same person that I received the 1001. note from-this
is the ticket (produced)-it is "91. 178. 6d. on account of provision in the
ship Hendrick Hudson, for J. B. White. George Tosh."
