 726. FRANCIS EYRES was indicted for stealing 1 cloak, value 1 l  .; the goods of Elizabeth Mabon ; and that he bad been before convicted of felony. ELIZABETH MABON. I am a widow, and live in Seymour-place. I went to the prisoner's sister's house, on the 27th of Jan., and hung my cloak behind the door—I was there from ten o'clock in the morning till half-past nine at night—I then missed it—I went and told the prisoner's uncle and aunt of it—this is it. WILLIAM CRUSH. I am a pawnbroker. This cloak was pawned by the prisoner on the evening of the 27th of Jan.—he said it was his wife's.
   Prisoner.  It was from distress; I was out of work three months. JOSEPH NIFTON( police-constable D  118.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office—( read. ) JOHN LIOMIN. I was at the trial—the prisoner is the person then tried. GUILTY. Aged 24.— Transported for Seven Years .
 727. THOMAS SEXTON was indicted for stealing 2 1/2 lbs. weight of brass, value 2 s  ., 6 d  .; and 1 metal pillar, 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of William M'Do nald ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. WILLIAM M'DONALD. I keep a broker's shop, in Great Wild-street. On the 1st of Feb. I placed a metal gas-pillar outside my door—it was two feet six inches from the pavement, and tied with a string—I missed it—the po liceman brought it to me—this is it. ROBERT ADAMS( police-constable E  109.) I saw the prisoner with this pillar, in Belton-street, Long-acre, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, on the 1st of February—he said he picked it up in Long-acre—he might be about 500 yards from the prosecutor's.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I picked it up. CHARLES GRAHAM( police-constable S  21.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Transported for Seven Years .
 728. HENRY FRANCIS was indicted for stealing 1 bag, value 1 s  .; 14lbs. weight of tobacco, 2 l  .; 2 s  . 8 d  .; 1lb. weight of cheroots, 9 s  .; and 2lbs. weight of cigars, 13 s  .; the goods of Henry White and another. GEORGE WATTS. I am warehouseman to Henry and John White—I sent a bag out with 14lbs. of tobacco, and these other things, on the 2nd of Feb., by Meadows the carman—this is the bag and the articles—they are my master's. ELEAZOR MEADOWS. I put this bag into the cart—I was in Whitechapel—a woman ran and told me the bag was gone—I looked at the tail of the cart, and missed it—this is it.
   Prisoner. Q.  How far had you got from Osborn-street? A.  Perhaps 200 yards. THOMAS COCKRAN( Police-constable H  143.) I saw the prisoner running with this bag—when he got within ten yards of me he dropped it.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did I not fall over the curb-stone? A.  No, you did not. WM. COLMAN( police-sergeant H  2.) I was outside the Police-court when the prisoner was brought in—he said in a spontaneous sort of manner, "I am sorry that I have done this act—if I get over it I will take care it shall be the last thing I will ever touch."
