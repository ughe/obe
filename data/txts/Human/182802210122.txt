 FIFTH DAY. FRIDAY, FEBRUARY 26.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Recorder.
 706.  JEPTHA COOKE was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of February , 1 dead pig, value 5s., the goods of James Cooke, the elder.
 The prisoner pleaded GUILTY. Aged 22. Whipped and Discharged.
 700.  JOSEPH MADDOCKS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of January , 1 plane, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Joseph Owen ; and 1 plane, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Robert Cuthbert.
 JOSEPH OWEN. I am a  carpenter. I lost this plane out of the yard at the City-bason; my house is close by there, in Messrs. Snell and Co's. yard; it was in the yard, I believe; I saw it at the Carpenter's Arms public-house - it had G.O. upon it; the prisoner did not work in the yard; I knew him in the country, and he used to come and see me.
 ROBERT CUTHBERT. I work in the same yard, and lost a plane on the 16th of January; when I came to work, in the morning, it was gone - I had used it on the Saturday before; the shop is locked up at night; when I left I found out the man that had bought the plane, and then the prisoner was taken; he had no business to be in the yard.
 - HOWELL. I bought two planes of the prisoner, on Wednesday the 11th of January, between ten and eleven o'clock at night, at a public-house, in Old-street; we both lodged there; I knew he had no employment; I gave 1s. for the two; he was in much distress.
   JOHN CLARK. I produce the planes Howell gave me. I found the prisoner at the Merry Carpenters public-house - and he went with me to the watch-house.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined One Month.
 707.  ALEXANDER MACDONALD was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of December , 1 saddle, value 15s., the goods of Thomas Sell.
   JEREMIAH AISH. I lived with Mr. Thomas Sell, of Chiswell-street, in October last, when the saddle was lost; the prisoner worked occasionally there; the saddle was in one of the stables - I cannot say when I saw it last; Asbury came to me with the duplicate - it was pawned in Fore-street; when I saw it, I was not quite sure that it was my master's; Asbury asked me to buy the ticket - the prisoner never worked in the yard.
   JAMES ASBURY. I am a sadler, and live at No. 7, White-street, Little Moor-fields. The prisoner called upon me with the duplicate of a saddle, on or about the 12th of January; he wished me to buy it - I thought he was an honest man - it was pawned for 9s. or 7s. - I would have nothing to do with it; he said the duplicate belonged to another man; I told him if it was worth buying, I knew a person who would buy it - I went to Jeremiah Aish, and we went to Fore-street and took it out, and looked at it; he asked me what the person wanted for it, I told him 9s., he said he would give 5s., and something for himself; the prisoner was taken up that day.
   JOB VALENTINE WATKINS. I am shopman to Jacob Russel, a pawnbroker, No. 43. Fore-street. The saddle was redeemed by Jeremiah Aish; it was pawned on the 25th of December, by the prisoner, in his own name; I asked if it was his brother Harry's; he said it was; I advanced 7s. upon it - it is worth about 14s.; it was an old one; it was looked at first - about two hours after Aish came and took it out.
   JOHN BROWN. I am a constable. The prisoner was given into my custody, with the saddle.
   JEREMIAH AISH re-examined. I have no doubt at all but that the saddle is my master's; the prisoner worked in the yard when his brother sent him - his brother used to come into the yard.
 Prisoner's Defence. A man came and asked for Macdonald - he said he had a saddle to sell; I told him I did not think he wanted one - he asked me to buy it - I said I had not money enough, and he asked me if I would pawn it; I asked him where he got it - he said from a man at the White Bear public-house; I went to pawn it - I told him to put my name on the ticket - I bought the ticket, and told other people so.
 GUILTY. Aged 21. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Ten Days.
 708.  JAMES McCARTHY was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of January , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Nicholls.
   SAMUEL HAMPSTEAD. I am in the employ of Thomas Nicholls, a pawnbroker, of Gray's Inn-lane. This handkerchief was pinned inside the shop door, with about half a dozen others; a person must have put his arm inside the door, but he need not step into the shop; I had seen the prisoner there about five minutes before - I did not see him take it; Bennett pointed out a man to me - I jumped over the counter; I went out after him, but could not see the prisoner till he was at Hatton-garden, about half an hour afterwards; there was a ticket on it, of the price for sale; that had been taken off it.
   FRANCIS BENNETT. I am a labourer. About a quarter or twenty minutes before one o'clock, I saw the prisoner put his hand into the shop - he took a handkerchief, and put it into the flap of his trousers; I gave information of it at the shop directly; I pursued, and never lost sight of him; I am quite sure I took the same man, and took him to Hatton-garden. When I collared him he threw the handkerchief down - the officer picked it up.
   JOSEPH CADBY. I had just come out of the office in Hatton-garden; Bennett had hold of the prisoner, I saw him throw the handkerchief from him, and I picked it up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I deny all knowledge of the robbery; I never saw the handkerchief before.
     GUILTY. Aged 44. Transported for Seven Years.
