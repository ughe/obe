WILLAM MeGINTY, 718, Barking Road, Plaistow, laundryman.
On July 22 I visited my stables at Portland Road, and found them
all eorreet The next day the stable had been broken into, and the
gelding, car, harness, and lamps stolen. On August 13 I saw the
car at Minories Police Station.
JOHN BRYCE, 417, Holloway Road. I have stables in Holloway
Road. On Sunday, August 11, between three and four, prisoner
drove up to my house with my boy. He had my horse in the trap,
my boy having lent it him to trot out with the trap. Prisoner
wanted me to buy the trap for 216. I went for a run with it, and
it seemed all right. I eventually agreed to give him E10 and my
old trap for it, which I did on the Wednesday evening at six o'lock.
Meanwhile on the next two days I went out with the trap, and on
the the seeond Minories one Police I was Station, stopped and by was a constable, detained with for six whom hours I went till my to
statement was investigated, on which I Was released. On the next
day, the 16th, prisoner came about 5.30 to receive the money. As
he was signing the receipt Detective Eustace same in and arrested
him
Cross-examined. I had never seen prisoner before the Sunday
that he called. Prisoner first called at my stables and borrowed
the cob to drive the trap to my house. I am pretty well known in
the neighbourhood, and sometimes buy traps in the course of busi
ness. After wanting E16 prisoner came down to E13 before aocept
ing E10. My old trap was worth about E5. I told prisoner I had
not the money at the time, and he agreed to wait till the Wednes-
day. He gave me his name as Henry Jones, 29, Halton Road,
Islingtoa. I arranged to pny prisoner in cash. When he had

well known place for buying horses Prisoner said there had
