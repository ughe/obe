   KENT CASES .
   Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 2015. ROBERT ELLIOTT , for feloniously wounding John Roscoe on the head, with intent to do him some grievous bodily harm.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. JOHN ROSCOE. I am bailiff at the County Court, Greenwich. On Wed nesday, 26th July, I put a man into possession of the prisoner's house, at Plampstead—in consequence of a communication, I went to the house, between five and six o'clock in the afternoon—when I entered the house I saw the prisoner standing at the door—I said I was a bailiff of the County Court, and had a warrant to seize his goods—I produced the warrant to him—he said, "Get out of my house, you d----vagabond; if you don't I will kill you"—he had something concealed behind him, and he made a job  at my eye—it turned out to be a red-hot poker—it burnt my ear, and all my hair off there—he then struck me a violent blow with it at the back of the head, which caused a wound, and a great quantity of blood to flow—Holmes, who was with me, tried to take the poker from him, and he struck Holmes over the hat at the back of the head, which made a large dent in his hat—we at length succeeded in getting the poker from him—I went to a surgeon.
   Cross-examined by MR. COOK EVANS . Q . Where did you first see the pri soner? A.  At the back-door—he occupies the back part of the house, which stands in a yard—I was in the wash-house when I first saw him—I did not offer him the warrant to read—I did not read it to him—he did not give me the opportunity—he swore he would kill me if I dared to come near him—he appeared in a violent rage—I do not know "whether he was drunk—he was on the steps when he struck me—I was rather below him—there was no fire in the washhouse—I do not know where he had come from—I had not been talking to him a moment before this occurred—I was laid up for a week after—I went and got a cart to remove his things, and went about my business next day—I thought very little of it—Holmes was standing close to me when he was struck—he had gone with me. ALFRED SEPTIMUS HOLMES( policeman, R  93). I accompanied Roscoe, and saw him produce his warrant—the prisoner asked him what he wanted, and immediately struck him twice with the poker, once on the side of the face, and once on the back of the head—the last blow caused a great discharge of blood—I interfered, to take the poker' from him, and he struck me with it, which bruised my hat, and made a lump on my head.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you present when Roscoe went in ? A . Yes, I was standing against the wash-house door—he produced the warrant—he had not time to hand it to the prisoner, because he struck him directly—the prisoner did not ask for it, he saw it. DAVID HOLMES. I am a surgeon at Deptford-bridge—Roscoe came to me next morning—I found a scar on the left ear. and at the back of the head a wound about half an inch long—I did not probe it, as he had been to a surgeon before.
