Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. What did you get the 3s. for?
A. I do not know, unless he meant to lend it me--we have been in the habit
of lending and borrowing-I am quite innocent-I did not know whether!
was doing right or wrong-when I got the extra 28., I did not ask what it was
for-people do not often give me 2s. for nothing I did not know whether
he meant to lend it me-we do lend and borrow, without asking each other
for it-I left the sack with a little girl, and said, "Here is a sack of oats for
Mr. Gobby"--I have known Gobby to be a customer of my master's-I
have not lent him any sacks-I do not know whether he has had sacks of
my master-when he has bought things, he has brought his own sack-1
never knew sacks of my master's to go there.
MARY OAKFORD. I live in Hatton-garden. On the 1st of July, between
three and four o'clock, I saw a sack taken to Gobby's premises by Wakefield
-it appeared to be full-I could not see what was in it-it was a new sack.
similar to this one, and marked in red letters, "W. Powell."
WALTER POWELL. I am a corn-dealer, and live in Leather-lane-Gobby
has bought things at my place- the last oats he bought of me, was on the
10th of March-in consequence of information I went to his house with a
constable-when he came home, I asked him if he had any empty sacks of
mine-he denied it strongly-I said, "I think you have"- I went into his
stable, and he chucked a sack off a rail-I said that was not mine-he then
chucked down two sacks, which were mine-he said he did not know how he
came by them, he used them for chaff-I looked into one, and I said, “This
does not look like chaff; here are oats in this"-I then asked if he had
any more sacks --he said, “No, only those two empty sacks" - I said I was
quite certain he had got sacks up stairs-he said he had no place up stairs,
and he was quite sure he had no sacks at all up stairs—I then asked him if
he would allow me and the officer to go up-he said it was no use going,
there were no sacks there-we went up, and there was a sack in the pas-
sage-it could not be seen from the bottom-it contained about a bushel and
a half of oats - I looked at them, and said, “These are some of my oats" -
Gobby said, “Yes, I had them of George"-we asked him to let us go up
higher-he said he had nothing more—we went up, and found a sack with a
bushel and a half of beans-he said, "I did not have them of your man; I
took your sack to fetch them in"-I opened the sack, and I said, “These are
my beans"-- he said, “Yes, I had them of George," or “the man"-I said,
“What do you mean by allowing me to be robbed ?"-he said he hoped I
would forgive him, that he had seven or nine children, and he said, “George
told me he worked at a vessel, and he had got these overplus"-he said be
gave the man a sovereign for the oats and beans, through his spending a
shilling, and he borrowed part of the money at the public-house-I gave
21. 08. 6d. a quarter for the beans, on the 9th of Jan., but as they were
damaged, I should have taken perhaps 388. for them--they heated in the
sacks, and were damaged a little.
Cross-examined. Q. In fact they were damaged ? A. They were good
beans when I bought them they had heated, and there were a few sacks &
little damaged-those I found at Gobby's were so damaged, that I could not
send them to customers as sweet beans-there were plenty of them good
they were worth 4s. a bushel-I should be sure to get that for them if I had
them at home- might not have said at the Police office that he begged
I would forgive him I will not swear that I said anything about his wife
and children.
