   Thursday, June  27 th , 1839. GEORGE ROBINSON re-examined. Q.  Look at these notes. A.  I have seen them—they have been in my possession—they came into my posses sion for the first time on 14th April—I parted with them on the 17th at the New Inn in the Old Bailey—I delivered them to Moss—none of the prisoners were present when I gave them up to him—they are the same I received from him—this IOU was among them—I received them at my own house, in Moss's presence, with this I O U among them—they were all received at one time, and all parted with at one time.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You were concerned for Mrs. Abrahams? A.  Yes, for her alone, on the first occasion, and afterwards for Moss—he sent me several statements in writing—he made several statements to me—I never saw the letters—he mentioned to me respecting his forging the word Falmouth. HENRY SOLOMON. I am a goldsmith and refiner—on the 25th of March I carried on business at 53, Strand—I had another shop at No. 9, Co ventry-street—I know the prisoner Emanuel Moses—I have known him eight or nine years—it might be ten years—I have also known Alice Abrahams for some few years back—I have a son named Isaac, and a daughter Mary—on Easter Tuesday last I was in my shop in the Strand—Emanuel Moses came to my house, and said he wanted to speak to me—after some few minutes he said again he wished to speak to me, and intimated a wish to be private, and walked towards the end of the shop—I followed him, and we went up stairs into the sitting room together—my daughter Mary was in the sitting room when we came in, and seeing somebody with me, she got up and walked out—when she had left, Moses asked me if I would purchase some gold-dust, and what would I give an ounce for it?—I said gold-dust varied from 3 l . 15 s . to 3 l . an ounce—he then said he had a quantity of it—I said I would not take more than thirty ounces of it till I had tried it to see what it was—he said "Very well," and we agreed about the price—I was to give him 60 s . or 3 l . an ounce—I said one or the other—I do not know which, but the amount is the same—he said, "Very well, I will sand my daughter Alice with it in about half an hour"—he said, "Remember, you don't know me, and I don't know you," or, "I don't you and you don't know me"—those were the words he said before he left me—he then went away, to the best of my recollection—I do not remember his saying any thing more—about half or three quarters of an hour afterwards his daughter, the prisoner Abrahams, came into the shop—she said, "I have brought what my father spoke to you about"—I understood what she meant—she walked on towards the workshop, and
