 667.  JOSEPH MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 2 sacks, value 4s., the goods of John Smith.
   SARAH SMITH. I am the wife of John Smith, a costermonger. These sacks were taken from our stable at Kentish-town.
   JOHN HOLLAND. I am a Policeman. I met the prisoner about seven o'clock in the morning, on the 12th of February, with a bag, and asked what was in it - he said rags and bones; I made him turn it out, and he had these two sacks - he said he found one behind a house at Camden-town, he could not tell where - this was three-quarters of a mile from Smith's - I found out the prosecutor; the prisoner then said he had them from there.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received a good character, and pleaded poverty.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined Six Weeks.
 668.  JOHN IRELAND was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of January , 1 hat, value 16s, the goods of Jeremiah Spiller.
 JAREMIAH SPILLER. I am a  mahogany-polisher, and live in Carnaby-market. On the 23rd of January I was locked out of my lodging, and went to look for one; I saw a great many people at the door of the Crown public-house, Pulteney-street- I asked for a lodging there; I was returning home; a man in a flannel jacket ran against me, and as he passed me he took off my hat - I was running after him, and another man knocked me down; I should know him again - I went and saw a Policeman.
   WILLIAM ELLIS. I am a Policeman. I was going on duty at half-past six o'clock on Sunday morning, and saw about twenty-five people about the Crown, making a disturbance - I went up to them, and saw the prisoner with this hat just stuck on his head; it did not seem to fit - I took it off, and asked where he got it; he said, "What is that to you?" I asked the maker's name, and the colour of the lining; he said he could not tell - I was taking him to the station; he ran away - another Policeman took him; he was dressed in a flannel jacket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been all night at a wake - a man came and asked me to buy a hat; I gave him 4s. for it.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Transported for Seven Years.
 669.  MARY ANN HARBOUR was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of January , 2 half-crowns, 7 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of Thomas Smith , from his person.
 THOMAS SMITH. I am  carter to a gentleman in the Edgware-road. On the night of the 22nd of January, I went to the Horse and Sack public-house, between ten and eleven o'clock - I was quite sober; I had two pints of half-and-half there - the prisoner was there; she asked me for beer - I gave her some; she asked for gin, and I told the boy to bring a quartern; she stood by me, and I felt her hand in my trousers pocket; (I had seen her two years ago, but not since) I had 12s. 6d. in my pocket - she pulled it all out; I saw it in her hand -I tried to get it from her, but she threw it down in one corner; I saw two half-crowns and one shilling in her hand - there was a parcel of chaps in the room; I was afraid to look for the money - the people then shoved me about, and I got away as fast as I could; I am certain my money was safe when I went into the house - I never got any of it back; I went out for a Policeman, and when I returned the house was all shut up, and she was gone; I had been away a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes.
 JURY. Q. Could you hear any body in the house after you returned? A. No - I knocked, and nobody answered.
   THOMAS SHILVER. I am pot-boy at the Horse and Sack, kept by Mr. Moss. The prisoner came in on this Saturday night, before the prosecutor - he sat in the taproom for a minute, and then stood up by the fire; I saw the prisoner speak to him; he called for a quartern of gin, and gave me half a crown - I gave him the change; the prisoner then put her hand into his pocket, and pulled out the money; the men who had been drinking with her were laughing and talking together, and she dropped all but two half-crowns and one shilling, which she kept in her hand - the prosecutor accused her of the robbery; my master was serving in the bar, and knew nothing of it till morning - the house was cleared because it was twelve o'clock - I believe the prisoner went away with the men; I found no money on the floor - I believe the men picked it up.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met him by the Red Lion; he took me to this house, and paid for some fish for me there - I heard some money drop, but do not know where it came from; I got intoxicated, and do not know what became of him.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 670.  WILLIAM GADSEN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of February , 1 coat, value 10s., the goods of James Gadsen.
 JAMES GADSEN. I live in  New Nichol-street, Bethnal-green, and am a weaver- the prisoner is my son. On the 11th of February he was at home, and next morning I missed my great coat from the front kitchen; I never allowed him to pawn my things - I am obliged to prosecute him, as I can keep nothing.
   THOMAS COOMBS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Church-street, Bethnal-green. I have a coat, pawned on the 13th of February; to the best of my belief by the prisoner.
   MORGAN WATKINS. I am a Policeman. I was called to take the prisoner, and found the duplicate on him.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 The prisoner has been before convicted.
