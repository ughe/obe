Mr. Justice WILLIAMS felt some dificulty in saying that the form of repls-
cation which had been permitted here, was one that the case required; but
Counsel having consented to treat the issue of nul tiel record (raised upon this
record) as one which raised the question whether the prisoner could have been
convicted under the former indictment of the charge contained in the first and
second Counts of the present, he was content to rest his judgment upon that
footing. It had not been contended, on the part of the prisoner, that at
Common Law she could, under the former indictment, have been convicted on
the charge, which was the subject of the first and second Counts of the present
indictment; but it was said that she was in jeopardy, because she might have
been convicted under the 9th section of 14 and 15 Vic. He was of opinion that
she was in no such jeopardy; for that section would only enable the Jury to
convict of a charge, simpliciter, of an attempt to commit a felony, which would
only amount to a misdemeanour. The various portions of the section seemed to
narrow the expression, "Attempt to commit," to an attempt, simpliciter, to commit
the felony; the only verdict the Jury were authorized to find was simply a verdict
of guilty of an attempt to commit the felony, while on the record there was
nothing to authorize any punishment further than a punishment for misdemeanour.
The 11th section was different, and carefully provided for a punishment in the
same way as in a conviction of felony. For these reasons he was of opinion
that the judgment should be for the Crown,
MR. JUSTICE TALFOURD entirely concurred, and for the same reasons.
