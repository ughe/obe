 980. SAMUEL HOLDHAM was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of March , 2 pieces of board, value 1 s  . 8 d  .; and 1 piece of wood, value 4 d  .; the goods of Robert Webb and others, his masters. CHARLES EWE. I am foreman to Robert Webb and others, brick-makers and builders. The prisoner was in their employ—I missed two pieces if board and one piece of wood—they are here, and are my masters'.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How do you know them? A.  By matching them—the prisoner is a carman—there are a great many pieces of wood in the yard—there is a thoroughfare through the yard, but not public, it is for persons who come to the brick-fields, by day-time—I cannot say to a day when I saw this wood last—Mr. Webb would rather not pro secure him—he wishes to have him back in his employ—he has been about two years there, but still this is Mr. Webb's property—it was more than twelve months in the yard—I cannot say how long it had been out of the yard—I cannot speak to a week or months—it was taken away from a pig-stye, because I knew it so well.
 (  Property produced and sworn to .) WILLIAM SMITH( police-constable N  261.) On the night of the 16th of March I saw the prisoner in Coach and Horses-lane—he had two pieces of board—I asked how he came by them—he told me he purchased it at the stone-yard, Islington—I took him to the station-house, and then he said he had picked it up in the road—a quarter of an hour after, I went to the last witness, and told him—he came and said he was persuaded it was their property.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What did you find on him? A.  These two pieces—this one was at the pig-stye, and I brought it away, with these other pieces—it was concealed. GUILTY  . Aged 24.—Recommended to mercy by the Jury. — Confined Five Days.
