JOHN RICHARD GOLTON( policeman, M  62). I was on duty on the night of 5th Nov., near the Chapel in the Blackfriars-road—I saw Wright in company with two other boys, about a quarter-past ten o'clock—Leary was one of the boys. WILLIAM BROAD( policeman, L  82). I apprehended Wright on 8th Nov.—I told him the charge was assaulting this man, and robbing him of his hat—the prosecutor had two black eyes on the night of 8th Nov. when he gave me information—I knew Wright by the description he gave, and I took him about a quarter of an hour afterwards. AMBROSE FURMINGER( police-sergeant, L  42). I received information on 12th Nov., and took Leary. FREDERICK WILLIAMS re-examined.  I gave information to the police the same night—I should say that the girls who were there, were about twenty years of age. JOHN RICHARD GOLTON re-examined.  I received information from the prosecutor about half-past ten o'clock that night—he described Wright to me—I had seen him that night, but I did not know where to find him.

 176. ANN CHAPMAN , stealing 13 half-shawls, Value 7 s  .; the goods of Thomas Southon and another; having been before convicted.
   MR. W. J. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. GEORGE GERRY WILSON. I am a banker's clerk, and live at Peckham. On 21st Nov., about five o'clock in the afternoon, I was going along the Old Kent-road—I had not observed the prosecutor's shop before, but I did on that occasion—while I was passing I saw the prisoner standing looking in at the window, and as I passed she turned round to watch me—I walked on, and in a minute or a minute and a half, she ran past me, carrying something in her arms—I ran after her, and after running 200 or 300 yards I came up to her and laid hold of her arm—she immediately threw these shawls down—I told her she must come back to the shop from which she took these shawls—she said, "I did not take them; I picked them up"—I asked her if she picked them up why she ran away so fast—she said she never ran at all—she was running; I was quite out of breath when I came up to her—I took her back, and the prosecutor gave her into custody. THOMAS SOUTHON. I am a linen-draper, carrying on business with my brother, in the Old Kent-road. On the evening of 21st Nov. the prisoner was brought to my shop, with these shawls, which had been hanging in the lobby—I had not sold any shawls of this description that day—my brother and two young men serve in my shop—I saw these shawls safe about two o'clock—neither of the persons in my shop sold so many shawls as these, if any were sold it would have been only one or two—they were tied in the lobby with a string. JOHN VARNEY( policeman, P  333). I received the prisoner into custody—these are the shawls—she said she picked them up on the pavement.
   Prisoner.  As I was passing, I picked the parcel up; I ran across the road, but did not run away.
