AARON CARVALLO. I am the brother of Zadoc Carvallo-he advanced
money to the defendants-it was my mother's money, and he was con-
stantly pressed for the return of it I did not lend the defendants any
money-the 1151. which they paid me did not go into my pocket-I know
about the bulb trade; young Mr. Reens is correct in his statement about
what happened in that business-the 1,2501. which is alleged to be lent
was my mother's property; I received it from a notary who sold it for
her-the 1,2501. was paid to them in Haarlem, and 5007. was lent in
London-I paid Van Guilder 661.—he was the solicitor in Holland.
Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY. I saw the cheque for 1151. drawn in the
warehouse this is it (produced)-in this counterfoil cheque-book here is
& counterfoil on December 26th, 1882, “A. Carvallo, for Swade"- that
is the one I saw written-it is the same writing as the cheque-when I
got the cheque I went to the bank to fetch the money– I did not get
2371. on behalf of Swade on the day after Christmas Day as well as the
1151.--the counterfoil is 72324–I did not get the 2371. cheque-Car-
Vallo's cheque is 72324-I have seven of them the first is 72322, Car-
vallo 4001.--the counterfoil number is all right—72304 is for 3001., in
my brother's favour, dated October 27th, 1882—this is the corresponding
counterfoil; it has the same number-the next is 72307, November 4th
-I find the next number, 250, has a corresponding counterfoil, but not
the corresponding number—the last is in favour of my father, 72325, for
1211., on 18th December- this is my brother's cheque, and here is the
counterfoil out of the same book-Nathan's is 72325, on December 28th
-that is the last used cheque in the book-there are then a number of
blank cheques I brought the money over in English notes-I don't
know whether I got any acknowledgment-I changed them in Holland
as it was cheaper-I came from Amsterdam-I don't know whether there
is any difficulty in getting a bank draft on London there I agree in what
has been stated, that my brother was always making loans from 1879,
1880, 1881, and 1882, and for twenty years since Mr. Isaacs left the
partnership my brother sometimes gave them the money, and since my
mother's property was sold in 1881 and 1882 I have brought the money
over-it was not my mother's money before the property was sold-I
brought over 8001., mostly in English notes, in 1882-there was some
gold; my mother's property had been sold, but I had no idea that I
should have to come here I do not know that it struck me that I ought
not to part with trust money, the property of my mother, without some
acknowledgment-I never did get any acknowledgment.
