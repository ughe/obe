 1055. WILLIAM CRAWLEY and GEORGE PEACOCK , feloniously forging a warrant for the delivery of 5,000lbs. weight of flour, and 20 sacks, with intent to defraud the South-Eastern Railway Company .—2d COUNT  , for uttering the same.
   MR. BODKIN offered no evidence against
   CRAWLEY  — NOT GUILTY.
   MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution. JOHN FOTHEROILL . I am manager of the Goods Department of the South-Eastern Railway Company at the Bricklayer's Arms' station. In December last there was a quantity of flour placed there from Mr. Feltham, of Crawley Mills, consigned to Mr. Empson; according to the custom, that flour would be delivered to the written orders of Mr. Empson—some of it bad been delivered—we were regularly delivering the flour to the orders of Mr. Empson—Mr. Wells, our delivering clerk, is the person to whom such orders are directed. EDWARD CLAWSON. I am foreman in the employ of the South-Eastern Railway Company. This order ( looking at it ) was brought by Crawley—he was there when I went there, at seven o'clock in the morning, or it might be three minutes after; it was scarcely light—he had a man and a cart with him—in consequence of receiving this order, twenty-five sacks of flour were delivered to him—there were fifteen sacks of household flour, and ten sacks of No. 2—they were delivered by the order of Mr. Empson—here is the delivery-book—Crawley has signed it, in the name of John Roberts—on 20th Dec. Crawley brought this other order for twenty sacks—I delivered the quantity mentioned to him—he had again to sign the delivery-book for them—they were taken away the same as before—it was about the same time in the morning—they were there when I came—seven o'clock is the time to deliver.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  There were two occasions? A.  Yes, the 14th and 20th Dec.; but the orders are dated the 12th and the 18th Dec.—I did not know Crawley before—he did not give any account of himself, he only said he wanted the quantity of flour—he just gave me the orders, and I gave them to Blake—the flour was delivered according to the I order—we had only one sort of flour of this description, and these marks—if Crawley had been in the habit of coming with vans to the station be could see where these sacks were; they were all visible—I did not notice whether there was any name on the van, it was dark—Crawley was not alone on the first occasion; one of Boddy's carmen was with him—I know Mr. Empson's signature—I should say this signature to the order is not his—I might have given these sacks on a signature purporting to be his, which was not his, in the height of business—there might have been four or  five more vans waiting—I cannot say whether there were any more—I did not look particularly at it—I saw "T. Empson" on it.
