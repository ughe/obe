 149.  WILLIAM SHELTON was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 25th of December , an ox-hide salted, value 20s. the property of Richard Gearing.
 Second Count. Laying it to be the property of  William Alston.(The case was opened by Mr. Gurney).
   WILLIAM ALSTON sworn.
 Examined by Mr. Gurney. I live in Long-lane, Southwark; I am a  tanner, I delivered one, hundred and nine hides to be shipped on board a barge, marked T, directed to Charles Poole, tanner, Taunton.
 Q. Did you deliver any others? - A. Yes, fifty-two, directed to Mr. Poole, Overshaw, near Bridgewater; they were marked on the inside of the hide, on the fleshy side; there was a piece of sacking tied on, with a tar rope, such as is made use of to tie up raw hides.
 Q. To whom did you deliver them? - A. I delivered them to William Saunders; I saw them delivered.
 Q. Who is Saunders? - A. I believe he is servant to Richard Gearing; he likewise gave me a receipt for them.
 Q. Did you at any time afterwards see any hides that were delivered to him? - A. On the 29th, I was in Leadenhall market; Mr. Cork asked me if I had lost a hide, I said I did not know; he told me a hide was brought by a porter that morning, and desired he would sell it; Mr. Cork observed that it was a hide that was salted, and he supposed it had been stole or lost, and therefore he did not offer it for sale; he asked me if I had lost a hide.
 Q. In consequence of this, did you go to the barge? - A. Not immediately.
 Q. When you examined the hide, did you know it? - A. I knew it; I shipped the same on the 21st, in Saunders's barge, at Brook's Wharf, I went to the barge and found Mr. Saunders.
 Q. Did you look over the hides? - A. No, I did not.
 Q. Have you any doubt as to the hide? - A. No, I have not.
 Q. Was it one of the fifty-two? - A. Yes; I marked it, as I seldom mark them; I bought fifty for Mr. Poole, of Overshaw; I marked them with P, that I might knew them, they are falted hides, to preserve them from putresaction.
 Q. They are not saleable in this town? - A. No.
 WILLIAM CORK sworn.
 Examined by Mr. Gurney. I am a salesman in Leadenhall-market: On the 29th of December, a man, who was a stranger to me, throws down before my window, a hide, his name is Cranfield.
 Court. Q. Was that the same hide you shewed to Mr. Alston? - A. On the person's putting it down, I said, my friend, how came you by this hide, I am afraid it is either stolen or lost; he said, a man brought it to a public-house, at the Saracen's-head, in Friday-street, where he was porter, and asked him to bring it to market to some salesman to sell.
 Court. Q. Did he tell you who he was? - A. No; it remained on my premisses, and I enquired of three gentlemen, if they had lost any; upon enquiring further, a person told me he thought it was one of Mr. Alston's, and said, he would enquire, desiring me not to dispose of the hide.
 Court. Q. Was the hide you shewed to Mr. Alston that which you received from Cranfield? - A. The hide I received from Cranfield, I shewed to Mr. Alston.
   WILLIAM CRANFIELD sworn.
 I am a porter, at the Saracen's-head, Friday-street; between the 21st and the 29th of December, the day I went to Mr. Cork, the prisoner came to me at the Saracen's-head, Friday-street, and told me he had found a hide in Thames-street, and asked me where he could sell it; I told him the only way would be to carry it to a salesman; he said, I should be glad if you will carry it for me, you have no occasion to be afraid to carry it for me, for I came honestly by it; I carried it in a bag; as soon as I put it out of the bag, Mr. Cork told me it was either lost or stole.
 Court. Q. Are you sure that was the same hide you had from the prisoner? - A. Yes.
 Mr. Gurney. Q. Was the prisoner to come to you after you came from Mr. Cork? - A. He did not come then, I saw him afterwards.
