   Cross-examined by MR. DICKIE . ( For Brown ) Q.  Had you sent for a policeman? A.  Yes, I had sent my servant, and heard her putting the key in the door—the moment they heard footsteps, Brown dropped the rings—the door was opened by my friend, and there were the servant and two policemen.
   Cross-examined by MR. SMITH . ( For Kamble ). Q.  When they came to the house, I suppose you thought them respectable? A.  I had my doubts—I let my servant show them over the premises instead of going with them—there was no property in the bed-room which the servant showed them—her room is on the third floor, and mine is on the second floor—Kemble was the one that took the life-preserver—there was no attempt to strike me—I said if they gave up the rings I would let them go, because I thought it would give me time to send for a policeman—perhape when I made that promise I had the intention of breaking it—I intended to see whether they had robbed me of my property—when you have thieves in the house you do the best you can.
   Dickie. Q.  Did yon ever say anything about the life-preserver before to day? A.  I did to the policeman—I was not asked about it before the Magis trate—it was taken off the door in my presence—it was not attempted to be used—I am certain I saw the rings when last in my room—I had taken them off to wash my hands.
   MR. PALMER . Q.  Had you any other motive in making the promise but the laudable strategy of gaining time? A.  No. WILLIAM LAKE( Policeman, P  264). I was called, and look the prisoners at Hamilton-place—they made no statement—they were given in charge for stealing the rings—they gave a false address—there was another constable.

   MESSRS. POLAND conducted the Prosecution. EMMA KINGSFORD. I am waitress at a coffee-house, 10, Duke-street, Southwark—on the morning of the 8th April, about half-past 1, the pri soner came in and had some provisions to the amount of 7 1/2 d .—he gave me a half-crown, and I gave him Is. 10 1/2 d . change—I put the half-crown in my pocket—I had no other there—the prisoner went away, and in about twenty minutes he came in again, ordered similar provisions, and gave me another half-crown; I looked at it, saw it was bad, and told him so—he said it was a good one—I sent for my master, and took the other one out of my pocket and found that was also bad—I gave them both to my master—the prisoner was given into custody—he said he did not know the money was bad.
