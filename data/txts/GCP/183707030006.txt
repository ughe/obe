MARTHA STURGEON. I am a widow, and keep the White Horse. On
the 19th of June I went to bed before my son-I shut the fowls in at the
close of the evening, about six o'clock-they were all alive, I went to bed
between eleven and twelve o'clock-I had seen the house fastened up the
hen-house has a wooden shutter or door-it was nailed up that night-
next morning, at eight o'clock, I found it open, and six young fowls and
one hen gone-there were sixteen in the hen-house the night before-
should know them again-about two months ago one broke its leg, and
I tied two pieces of matches on the leg with some cotton, and the cotton
remained on-here is the leg (looking at it)—I had sown it round the leg
-the matches are worn off-I found an old cap in the hen-house-I saw
the prisoner about two years ago-he had nothing to do with my pre-
mises.
William Robert Garde. I am a policeman. I apprehended the
prisoner on the 21st, at the corner of Great Garden-street, Whitechapel-
searched him, and found a skeleton-key on him-I told him he was an in-
pudent fellow to come on my ground and commit a burglary, when I kner
him so well-he was very sullen, and would not speak-the fowls were
brought to the station-house, and I cut this leg off one of them, and
showed it to Mrs. Sturgeon-I did not see the fowls found-I did not see
them till the morning-they were locked up at the station-house, and I
went and saw them-Colyer had put them there.
Prisoner. You never said I was an impudent fellow, nor any thing about
a burglary, but put me into a cab and rode off with me. Witness. I took
you into a shop and searched you, and it was there I said you were 10
impudent fellow, and I wanted you for a burglary.
