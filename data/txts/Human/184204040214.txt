   Prisoner. Q.  Did not Hannah Robinson wash the shirt for me' A.  No. HANNAH ROBINSON. I am the prosecutrix's sister, and am living with her at present. I left my situation on the Monday before Good Friday—I was with her that day—I had some tea in my box, which was left un locked in the parlour—the hinges were off it—I had bought the tea—I missed it next morning—the prisoner had been left in the room alone several times while he was there—this is my tea.
   Prisoner. Q.  When you were at your situation did not you ask me to come there several times, and did not I refuse? A.  It is false—you never slept all night with me—you are a base young man—you never gave me 5 s . MARY QAMPBELL. I live in East-road, Limehouse. The prisoner was my lodger—he came home on the 25th of March about half-past ten o'clock at night—he had this shirt in his coat-pocket—he put it on the kitchen dresser, and when he went to bed he took it up with him—next morning I saw this tea in a bundle with the shirt—I said to him, "This is Mr. Kitchiner's shirt"—he threw it away in the street—I picked it up, and afterwards gave it to the policeman—the prisoner paid me 2 s . for his lodg ing and left—he said nothing—I did not know he was going away—he did go, and my husband followed him—I called the prosecutrix to my house, and showed her the shirt.
   Prisoner. Q.  When I came home, did not you say, "Is that the shirt that Hannah made for you?" and I said, "Yes?" A.  It is false. ROBERT BYLES. I am a policeman. In consequence of information I apprehended the prisoner at the Ship and Half Moon public-house, at Woolwich—he had this tea in his hat in a hat-box—he said he had bought it with some more things to send down to his mother.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I have been acquainted with Hannah Robinson about twelve months; she was always asking me to her master's home during his absence, and one Saturday, about three months ago, I stopped with her all night; on the Monday following she said, "I have got a good half-pound of  tea to  send to your  mother, and have bought you two shirts;" I refused taking them; I gave her 5 s ., and said that when the finished them I would give her the remainder of the money—about three weeks ago she came to my house and said she was in the family way, that she had told her master, and he had discharged her; she wanted me to marry her, which I was not able to do, but said I would marry her in the course of a month, and she has done this through spite; when I was at
 Mrs. Kitchiner's, on Good Friday, she gave me this shirt, and the hand. kerchief to take it home in; when I got home my landlady said, "Have you got the shirt Hannah made for you!" I said, "Yes;" I paid the landlady and went away, leaving the shirt on the bed; I went to Wool  wich to get employment, and was taken there; is it feasable I should steal the shirt and tea when I had 22 s . about me at the time? she gave it to me, and said she should not want the money, I should want it myself another time. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months.
