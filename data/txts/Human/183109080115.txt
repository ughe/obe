 1782.  RICHARD HORN was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   JOHN WALTER CROPLEY. I live in Wilmington-square. On the 1st of September, about a quarter-past nine o'clock, I sold seven loads of straw for James Jaggers, (of Broxbourne,) in Smithfield market, to Norris, of Chenies-mews, Russell-square; before ten o'clock the same morning, the prisoner, who was a stranger, came to me at the Coach and Horses, Bartholomew-close; he had a frock on - he met me in the passage, and asked if I was Jaggers' salesman; I said Yes - he said he had called for the money for the straw I had sold for Jaggers; he produced a note, which I have; I said, "My name is not Brocklin;" he said, "If you are Jaggers' salesman, that note is for you;" I said I should not pay for the straw till it was delivered, and if Mr. Jaggers would call after it was delivered, it should be paid; I gave him the note back - he returned about half-past twelve o'clock, for the money, and I said the man had not returned from the delivery, meaning the farmer's man, who had to deliver the straw - he said the man would not return, for he had gone home Whitechapel way; I said I was certain he would, for he had not had his spending money - he left, and came again about half-past five o'clock, and from information I had received, I asked him into the house, and said, "Give me the note;" he did so: I opened it, and said, "Who gave you this note?" he said Mr. Jaggers, that he got it at Broxbourn last night, and he had told him to receive the money: I asked if he was related to Jaggers - he said he had married his wife's sister, and lived within a few yards of him; I said I suspected he was a bad fellow, and that Jaggers did not give it to him - he said he did, and it was perfectly right; I sent for an officer, and gave him in charge - the straw came to ten guineas - (note read.)
 London, August 31, 1831.
 MR. BROCKLIN, - Please to deliver to the bearer of this note, the money for the seven loads of straw you have sold, belonging to  James Jaggers, Broxbourne, Hertfordshire.
 Richard Horn; bearer.
   JAMES JAGGERS. I am a farmer, and live at Broxbourne. I employ the prisoner a little - he married my wife's sister; I sent seven loads of straw to be sold by Cropley - the prisoner did not go with it; I discharged him twelve days before, and never gave him a note to the salesman, or authorised ahy body to receive it - this note is not in my hand-writing.
   FRANCIS DANNER. I am a plumber. I was at the Coach and Horses about half-past five o'clock, and saw the prisoner demand the money from Cropley for seven loads of straw sold for Jaggers; I saw him produce the note - he said he received it from Jaggers on the previous evening.
   JOHN PALMER. I took the prisoner in charge.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. - Confined Six Months.
 1783.  HELEN CASHMAN was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 25th of July , 1 pair of gloves, value 2s., and 1 fan, value 20s., the goods of Thomas Bunn.
 THOMAS BUNN. I live in Cheapside, and am a  haberdasher. In consequence of what Painter, my lad, told me I examined my stock, and know that I have lost these things - I do not know the prisoner - she informed me that she sold fruit opposite the Post-office; I have not found this property - Painter was four months in my service, and had access to my goods; I gave him over to his father the last Saturday in July, and on the Monday the father gave me information, which induced me to prosecute the prisoner.
   FREDERICK PAINTER. I am fourteen years old. I had known the prisoner about two months before I left Mr. Bunn's. On the 19th of July I took this pair of gloves from my master's, without his knowledge - the prisoner had asked me to get them for her two or three days before - she gave me 1d. for the fan; that was on the 19th of July - I do not recollect when I took the gloves; I did not tell master of this - I knew I was doing wrong.
 JAMES CARR. I am a stone-mason. The prisoner sold fruit opposite the Post-office; I heard her acknowledge that she had received a handkerchief from the boy.
     NOT GUILTY.
