2249. ROBERT JOHN ARNOLD was indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of September , 1 shilling, the money of Joseph Davis and another his masters. JOSEPH DAVIS. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Woolwich—I am in partnership with Lewis Davis—the prisoner was my apprentice. On the 2nd of September I found in his coat pocket, which was in his box, one sovereign, one crown, nine half-crowns, and four shillings—I put it back, and my shopman marked some money. CHARLES SOLOMON JACOBS. I am the prosecutor's shopman. I marked two shillings, and placed them on a ledge over the till, on Saturday morning, the 2nd of September, about eleven o'clock—about four o'clock I missed one—it was found in the prisoner's pocket, by Mr. Davis and my self—this is one of the shillings—I said nothing to the prisoner.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  What was this boy? A.  As apprentice—he never took in pledges—his duty was to keep the pledges in order in the warehouse—they want moving about continually—he has never taken in pledges, to my knowledge—I have been absent, and let him in the shop—if Mr. Davis was not in the shop, the prisoner was in call some one. THOMAS CHITTENDEN. I am a Woolwich police-constable. I took the prisoner, and have the shilling. JOSEPH DAVIS re-examined . The prisoner had no authority to receive pledges, but to receive money for goods that were sold—he might have had the opportunity of taking the shilling in giving change—he had as access to the till—he ought to have come to me, or our shopmen and asked for change—I was in town between eleven and four o'clock, on the 2nd of September—I cannot tell who was there that the prisoner might go to—the ledge is the general place where money is put for things that were sold—for change he would go to the pledge-counter, and ask the young men for it—he had a right to go there.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 16.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor . Confined Three Months; Three Weeks Solitary.
   Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 2250. JOSEPH CLARKE was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of September , 1 shirt, value 3 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 jacket, value 4 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Samuel Hitchcock . JONATHAN SHEPHERD. I am a policeman. I was on duty on the 17th of September, at Deptford, at half-past one o'clock in the morning and saw the prisoner on the road, near Deptford-bridge, about 300 yards from the prosecutor—my suspicions were attracted by seeing something bulky in his pocket—I asked him what he had got in his pocket—he said, "Some things"—I asked what—he said "A shirt and jacket," and he had bought them where he was hopping, below Westerham—I asked him to let me see them—he put the shirt out—I saw it was tumbled and took him to the station-house—I made inquiry, and found the owner as the Monday. SAMUEL HITCHCOCK. I am a slop-seller and out-fitter, and live is Broadway, Deptford. This is my property—it was taken from inside my shop on the Saturday evening—I had seen it about ten o'clock.
   Prisoner . I was not in the shop at all. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Three Months.
