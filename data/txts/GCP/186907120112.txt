696. JAMES ALBERRY (38), was indicted for embezzlement.
MR. COOPER conducted the Prosecution, and Mr. MONTAGU WILLIAMS
the Defence.
Thomas Lover. I live at 10, Shepherd's Lane Brixton, and am a cab
and fly proprietor-I owed Thomas Hatton, a veterinary surgeon, 48.-on
the 28th of February, the prisoner called on me and I paid him the 48.-he
receipted this bill (produced).
WILLIAM MOORE. I live at Rose Cottage, Brixton, and am a greengrocer,
in January last I paid the prisoner 11. 08. 6d. on behalf a Mr. Hatton, and
he gave me this receipt, and signed it—(Read).
JOSEPH SQUIRES. I live at High Street, Upper Norwood-I owed Mr.
Hatton a bill of 21., for repairing my cart-the prisoner called on me and
brought a bill with him-I gave him this cheque for 21., payable to Mr.
Hatton or order-I afterwards received a communication from Mr. Pope,
and the cheque was returned to me with a forged name at the back-I gato
Mr. Pope the 21. for the cheque-Mr. Pope had cashed the cheque for the
prisoner-Mr. Hatton and Mr. Pope came to Norwood to see me, and I gave
Mr. Pope 21.-he keeps an hotel at Brixton.
THOMAS HATTON. I am a veterinary surgeon at Clerkenwell Green-
had a business in the Brixton Road, of which the prisoner had the cbarge-
I paid him 258. a week, and he had rooms to live in over the shop--if he
received sums of money it was his duty to account for them to me, and to
pay me I was there twice every day-I have not received the 48., or the
11.08. 6d., stated to have been paid by the two first witnesses I have seen
the receipts--they are in the prisoner's handwriting-on the 26th February
he left my employ, suddenly, without notice-I never permitted him to
keep any money back--I s.w him again some two or three months after,
when he was in custody.
Cross-examined. Q. How long had he been in your employ! A. Twelve
months—he received several sums for me during that time--they were gene-
rally small sums--I have not got my books here.
PATRICK HOWARD (Policeman w 115). About 12 o'clock, on 30th May,
I was in the Vassal Road, Brixton, and I saw the prisoner--from information
I had received, I went up to him and asked if he had worked for Mr. Hatton-
he said, "Yes"-I told him he must go to the station with me for embezzling
some of Mr. Hatton's moneys-he said, “Will they take bail at the station1"
-I said, “Yes, perhaps."
