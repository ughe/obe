 1684. JAMES CUMMINGS was indicted for bigamy. SUSAN COLLIVER. I live in Church-street, Greenwich. On the 10th of January last I was married to the prisoner by license at Stoke Damerel Church, Devonshire. I lived with my mother up to that time at Stoke Damerel—she is a pensioner's wife—lived with the prisoner a week, and then some other woman came and claimed him—I had no money—he con ducted himself well—it is I that prosecute.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYEN . Q.  I believe you or friend paid the fees for the marriage? A.  Yes—the prisoner was in the Sappers and Miners—I never heard that his other wife had been guilty of felony and imprisoned, and that led him to enlist in the Sappers and Miners—I did not send word that if he would pay the money and wedding expenses there would not be anything done to him—I send to him to say I heard he was married—we lived together one week—he left me because the com pany came at Woolwich—he was obliged to go—I intended to follow the month after—he did not leave me—the first wife came to me herself—I did not learn from her how long she was absent from hi—I had known the prisoner seven weeks before I married him—he came down to that place with his regiment. THOMAS WINDSOR. I am a stonemason, living at Luton Abbott in Devonshire. I was present on the 4th of march, 1833, at the church of Abbott Kerswell, Devonshire, and saw the prisoner and Margaret M'Quinness married—I was saw her alive Wednesday morning.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you know the prisoner and Margaret M'Quin ness after they were married? A.  Yes, I know of no misfortune happen ing to M'Quinness—they lived in devonshire three years—I have seen nothing of them since then—I cannot tell anything that happened since they left Devonshire.
