 1689. CHARLES SIMPSON was indicted for stealing 1 bag, value 2 d  .; and 8 aprons, 1 s  .; the goods of James William Lee ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   MR. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution. SAMUEL DENYER( police-constable B  199) I have known the prisoner about eight months—on the evening of the 27th of July I met him in Belgrave-road about fifty yards from Mr. Cubit's brick-fields—he had a bag—I asked what he had got—he said, "Rags"—I took it and found in it these eight aprons. JAMES WILLIAM LEE. I am a working brick-maker. I was working at Mr. Cubit's brick-field at Pimlico—I have seen these aprons—two of them belong to me and the other six to men I work with—I saw them on the field on Monday, July 27th—they are worth, when new, about 3 s . each. JOHN BERRY( police-constable B  73.) I produce a certificate of the pri soner's former conviction—( read—Convicted  11 th of Feb. , 1844, and con  fined three Months)—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Nine Months .
 1690. THOMAS KILBEY was indicted for stealing two axletree-arms, 1 l  . 10 s  .; the goods of William Bridges Adams and others, his masters. WILLIAM OBADIAH CLARK. I am a marine store-dealer, and live at Marsh-gate-lane, Stratford. I purchased a pair of axletree-arms of Haydon, at Bromley—I had them in my possession about five months—I then lent them to Mr. Sorrell's to be altered—he sent for me, and I went to Mr. Adams—I told him I had the axletree-arms of Haydon, and told what I gave for them—I saw them afterwards at the Police-court and they were the same—I do not know anything of the prisoner. EDWARD CORRY. I am foreman to Mr. William Bridges Adams and others. Mr. Clark came to their premises, and I went with him to Mr. Sorrell's—I saw the axletree-arms which Mr. Clark claimed—they were my master's—Mr. Haydon was taken up for having them and not giving a proper account of their possession—he was discharged—the prisoner was in my masters' service up to the day that Mr. Clark came, and absconded the next morning—he came in about a month, and surrendered—he said he was an un fortunate man or an unfortunate sinner—I am positive he said "a sinner"—h said he did not steal the axletrees, but found them outside the works tied up in a leather apron—he hoped we would have mercy on him—he said he took his knife out and cut the apron, that he took one axletree home and then came and fetched the other, and that he sold them to Haydon—the man who made them is here—I found them at Sorrell's. GUILTY. Aged 45.— Confined Two Months .
