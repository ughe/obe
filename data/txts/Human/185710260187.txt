   Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY . Q.  You found that her father lived at No. 5, Gray's Buildings, did not you? A.  I did not know it was her father—I know it since then—it is in George Street, Lambeth—I told her that I wanted to see Stevens, and she then answered me that she thought Stevens would be there about half past 5 o'clock—I went at half past 5 o'clock, and there was no one in the house at all, and I went at 7 o'clock and found the prisoner there, and gave her into custody—there was no one there then—I told her she was given into custody for uttering a forged 10 l . note—she said she was not aware it was a bad one—she did not say she could not read or write.
   MR. GIFFARD . Q.  What did she say about Stevens? A.  She said she expected him there about half past 5 o'clock in the morning—she said they were going to open a new shop in Cavendish Terrace, and thought it would do very well because it was near the station. JOHN LOCK. I am Mr. Toby's shopman. On 12th of this month I went to Cavendish Terrace with some boots and shoes—a man opened the door—the prisoner was within hearing—the man asked me what I wanted—I told him I had brought some boots and shoes—he said to the prisoner, "Here is the man with the boots and shoes"—she said, "Tell the man to walk in"—I went in, put the boots and shoes on the floor, and gave her the invoice of them—she opened the invoice—the bill was 10 l . 7 s . 3 d .—she said that they were out—she did not mention the names, and there was only a 10 l . note left, thinking what little there was over would be discount—I told her it was nett cash, no discount could be taken off, and she called in the man who was standing outside the door, and as they had no change to pay me the difference, it was agreed on that they were to call and pay the balance the first time they were passing—the prisoner spoke—I then counted out the goods on the counter, and was paid with a 10 l . note by the prisoner—I did not observe where she took it from—the man was standing by the side of her, and I wished him to put his name at the back of it, and he put the name of "G. Stevens, Cavendish Terrace"—I gave that same note to my master.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The man was present, was he not, when the invoice was opened? A.  No; he opened the door and asked me what I wanted, and I told him I had brought the boots and shoes—I put them down on the floor directly I got in the shop—the man and woman did not count them; I did myself—I did not see where the note came from—the woman gave it to me—I did not see the man give it to the woman—it was not given to the woman in my presence—the man put his name on the back of it. JOHN WHITE. ( Policeman, L  39). On Tuesday, 13th of this month, I went to No. 5, Gray's Place, Lambeth—the prisoner was given into my custody—on the road to the station, she told me that a gentleman of the name of Miller gave her husband the note, but she did not know where he lived.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not she also say, when you took her, that the man with whom she lived had put his name on the note? A.  Yes; her husband, she said, and she paid it away—I did not know her before, nor her father.
