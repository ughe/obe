That is the fact—my sus picion was certainly strong enough to make me believe so, but I had no right to believe it was so—I had strong suspicion, that is the fact—I did not ask any question of Mr. Solomon about it—I went on melt ing—I had never melted things before which came in under suspicious circumstances—I never had any suspicion of any thing I melted before—there are three doors to our workshop—there is no place to go out into the street, except going through the shop—one door leads into a closet—you cannot get into the street from there—you can get into the open air from that closet—you go across the yard to that closet—you come out of the workshop and go into this back-parlour—there is a door out of that into a yard, and down that yard at the end there is a closet—that closet has no door outward—you can see the Strand from the workshop—you can see any body—any one who saw a person coming could go into that closet—any body who knew it—I suppose it was not communicated to every body—I am still with Mr. Solomon.
   Q.  Have you, or had you any doubt that he received this gold with a guilty knowledge? A.  I did not know How he received it—I did not know what was paid for it, nor How he came by it—I only know it came there—I have not heard him say any thing on the subject—I knew it when he was examined at the police office—I continue in his service still—the attorney came to us in the Haymarket, and asked us to make statements to him—he did not tell me for what purpose at that time—he told me to state the facts, as far as I was concerned—he did not tell me to what use they were to be applied—he never told me what use they were to be ap lied to until they came to the police office—he did not tell me it was to corroborate Mr. Solomon, who would not be believed if he was not corro borated—he told me it was to corroborate Mr. Solomon, but he did not tell me he would not be believed without—when I went to the private audi ence before the Magistrate, there were two or three gentlemen there—I do not know their names—I believe it was Mr. Hardwick and the clerks, and to the best of my recollection Mr. Abrahams was there—I do not recollect that there was another Magistrate there besides Mr. Hardwick—there were three or four gentlemen, but who they were I do not know—to the best of my knowledge Mr. Abrahams was there, but it is so long ago—I did not know it was important I should recollect—I cannot say How many persons there were—I do not distinctly recollect whether Mr. Abrahams was there or not, but to the best of my belief he was there—the two young Solomons were not there when I was there—they were waiting to go in when I came out.
   Q.  Had Mr. Abrahams taken down in writing what you were to say before you went there? A.  When he came to Moseley's, in the Hay-market, what I stated he took down in writing—he took down what the others said also—that which he so took down was put into the hands of the Magistrate's clerk, and I was examined out of it, and it was read over to me in the private room—I do not distinctly recollect whether I signed it then in the private room, but I signed it before the Magistrate—I did not sign it when Mr. Abrahams took it down—I do not recollect that I signed that paper at all—if I did not sign it in the private room I did not sign it at all—not that statement.
   COURT . Q.  Confine yourself to the statement you made to Mr. Abrahams at Moseley's. A.  I did not sign what he put down—I am not certain whether I signed it when it was read over to me before the Magistrate.
