I have been 35 years in business; I had a contract for one year with the Guardians for the supply of hosiery—about £100. That is the only tender I ever had. If it is said that I had it two years, on my oath I say I only had it one year, from March, 1906, to March, 1907. There was a small account of about £14 for goods supplied out of contract.
 Re-examined. When the Guardians want to make a contract, they usually advertise for tenders, which are sent in on a printed form. I have sent in three tenders, of which only one has been accepted. The £14 was merely an order, not a contract. ROWLAND HIRST(prisoner, on oath). I live at Wellsfield, Leyton stone, and am a licensed victualler. I was born in the Mile End Road and am 60 years old. I have had the "Three Crowns" 33 years; it is a very old licensed house. I have also the "Eagle Hotel" at Snaresbrook. I am also interested in "The Bank of Friendship," which is really my daughter's house; I am interested with my brother in the "Colville," King's Road, Chelsea, and in the "Free mason's Hotel" at Woolwich. I have held six licenses myself at one time. Apart from licensed property, I have a good deal of house property in the neighbourhood of Mile End and at Herne Bay. I have been associated with public life in the Mile End district for about 30 years. I was a member of the Vestry until it was merged into the Stepney Borough Council and for four years Chairman. On the creation of the Borough Council I was elected on that. I was one of the first Aldermen. I was Chairman of the Public Health Committee for the first three years; I was elected Mayor of Stepney in 1904, 1905, and 1906. I am a member of the Metropolitan Asylums Board, Overseer for Wanstead, and a member of the Mile Find Guardians. I first became a Guardian some 20 years ago, and since 1888 I have been a Guardian continuously. I go to the "Three Crowns" every morning at about half-past 10 and stay till about half-past 11; I have an office there in which I generally transact my business. Then I go out generally and get back at about half-past five or six, and stay as a rule till nine or half-past to catch the 9.45 train. The "Three Crowns" is a popular house, and has been used by the members of the Vestry and the Guardians the whole of the time I have been there; it is about 200 or 300 yards from the Guardians' office. I first knew Calcutt about 1900 or 1901; he was in troduced to me by my brother Robert. I never spoke to him about going in for the Infirmary job. I did not tell Calcutt that he had got the contract. I began to employ Calcutt myself in 1901 to do builders' jobs on my houses. I never received a diamond stud as a present from Calcutt, and Calcutt never, at any time when he gave me a present, said that it was for voting for him, or made any refer ence to my action on the Board. He gave me a pair of gold sleeve links at Christmas, 1903, at the "Eagle Hotel," Snaresbrook.
