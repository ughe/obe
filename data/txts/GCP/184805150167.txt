FREDERICK GAYDON. I am assistant to Mr. Loden, a watch and clock
maker, in Blackfriars-road. A little before Christmas, 1846, I pawned two
pairs of trowsers at different times, at Mr. Cockburn's; one pair for 12s.
or 148., and the other for 88.- about Christmas, 1847, I sent 23s. to redeem
them, by a post-office order, payable to the prisoner-my trowsers were sent
by the Parcel's Delivery Company in three or four days afterwards--some
time afterwards I met the prisoner in London ; I asked him what money
there was deficient-be said 9d. or 1s. 9d., and I paid it him he had
written to me about the post office order, and said it was 9d. or 1s. 9d.
deficient, and when I met him I paid it—be said he received the order ; it
was all right-I sent the duplicates with the order-I made the order payable
to the prisoner because I had been in Mr. Cockburn's service, and did not
wish him to know I pawned the things with the prisoner.
JAMES ROBison COCKBURN. The prisoner was in my service--the book
in which this money should have been entered as paid in 1846 is destroyed
I have examined my book-it is not entered as received at Christmas, 1847
-I have not received the Post-office order if these trowsers were desired by
the witness to be sent home by the Parcels Delivery Company, it would have
been the prisoner's duty to have entered them in this parcel-delivery book-
here are my entries, Mrs. Cockburn's, and the prisoner's, in it-here is no
entry of this parcel being sent-it would be the prisoner's duty to enter the
parcel, for my protection--if it had been lost I should have been exonerated,
because I sent it by the party that they had mentioned-I am sure the pri-
soner never accounted to me for this.
