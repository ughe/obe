STEPHEN WENTWORTH. I am in the service of Mr. Child, grocer, of Norwood. On 13th Oct. I was with his cart, at Norwood—I went into a customer's house, and the prisoner was brought there to me by a gentleman, who gave him in charge, and gave me a 1/4 lb. of tea—the prisoner said I was to give him a thrashing , and let him go—it is James John Charles Child's property. GEORGE ROUS( policeman ). The prisoner was given into ray charge. JOHN TANNER. I was at my window, and saw the prisoner come away from the cart—he was about twenty yards from it—I went, took hold of him, and the tea dropped from his coat—I picked it up, and gave it to Wentworth. WILLIAM WESTON( policeman ). I produce a certificate of the prisoner's conviction—( read—Convicted Jan ., 1848, and confined three months and whipped )—I was present—he is the person. GUILTY †.  2471. JOHN BENNETT , stealing 75 yards of ribbon, 3 yards of twist, 3 ounces of silk, and other articles, value 2 l  . 2 s  . 11 d  .; the goods of George Smith , his master.
   MR. PAYNE . conducted the Prosecution . GEORGE SMITH. I am a cap-manufacturer, of Union-street, Borough—the prisoner was in my employ—it was his duty to cut out materials for caps, and give them to the cap-maker—he had access to the stock—I received in formation, and on the 18th Oct. watched him, had him taken in charge, went with the policeman to his house, and found all these things ( produced )—I had sold him none of them, nor authorized him to take them away.
   Cross-examined by MR. WOOLLETT . Q.  How long had he been in your service? A.  About two years—I had no character with him—he said he would be a trustworthy servant, and I took him for the sake of his family—he has been in the habit of working off the premises—I cannot say when the last time was.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did you give him these seventy-five yards of ribbon, or the velvet, to make up? A.  No—whatever he had was put down by my clerk—I knew when I took him that he had had six months' imprisonment for embezzlement—I took him to give him a chance of redeeming himself. RACHEL SMITH. I am the wife of the last witness. I did not sell the prisoner seventy-five yards of ribbon, or this lace, silk, or velvet, or authorize him to take them away.
   Cross-examined. Q . Other persons serve besides you and your husband? A.  It is a wholesale-house—we should not sell a single article, only to the trade—we have a great many persons employed—my husband, myself, and the clerk, are the only persons who sell—the prisoner never had more than twelve caps; that was two or three months ago.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Do you sell ribbon, or velvet, or any of the things men toned? A.  No, only when made up. JOHN THOMAS GAIN. I am Mr. Smith's clerk. I did not give the pri soner authority to take any of these articles—he had no right to them without Mr. Smith's permission—he sometimes bought things, and paid for them by instalments—this is a list of the whole of them—( producing it )—none of these things arc mentioned in it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner has had caps to make at times? A.  Yes—there are several females in the establishment—there is a boy named James—he did not sell without Mr. Smith's orders.
