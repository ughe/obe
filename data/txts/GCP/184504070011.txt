Q. In consequence of directions from Messrs. Growcock and other credi-
tors, did you in July last go down to Portsea ? A. I did, on Saturday,
the 6th of July, I went to No. 77, Queen-street, which was a draper's
shop, in the name of Bannister-I inquired for him, of Mr. Coleman, his
shopman-I could not learn from anybody on the premises where he was
I saw his wife-I examined his books--this is one of them here is
Messrs. Growcock's account in folio 8-on the credit side is entered," May
5th, goods, 871. 178. 1d.; May 7th, 146. 118. 5d. ;" making together
1021. 8s. 60.-on the other side I find, " 4th June, bill at three months,
1001.," and,"cash, 21. 88. 6d.," balancing these two transactions on the
credit side," 4th June, 431. 108. ;" 6th June, 16s. 8d. has been scratched

paid-wben I examined the book at first the 481. 108. Was in pencil-it
is now in blue ink--the entries were in a correct and business-like manner,
except being in pencil -- I examined his books very roughly that Saturday,
but made out that he owed 14,0001. or 15.0001.- I have gone over them
since, and examined them with his balance sheet his debts are upwards
of 16,0001., besides 21281. liabilities for old partnership debts-within
seven or eight weeks of the 6th of July I can swear that upwards of 5000%.
worth of goods have been obtained, but believe it to be considerably more,
and within three months 17,0001, the whole-I found about 50001. worth
of goods on the premises-there is about 2601. owing to him I saw him
after he returned from Madeira, I think in August, and asked him to give
me the particulars of the goods he had sent to Oxenbam's, the auctioneer's,
for sale--he said he had no record of them in his books, but he sent a list
with the first quantity, and I could get it from Oxenham's, which I did
I asked what loss there was on them he tola me what he afterwards de
