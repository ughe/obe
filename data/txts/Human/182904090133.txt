On the 14th of February, about six o'clock in the evening; I went out to Wood-street, on business - I left my housekeeper, Maria Pritchard, in the house; the house door is kept shut at that time of day - I returned at half-past seven o'clock, and found things the same as I had left them, but I did not examine the warehouse at that time, and am not aware of any thing being gone, as the doors were closed as I had left them; the warehouse is part of my dwelling-house: after my return, I was sitting by my fireside, in the room over the warehouse, and thought I heard something like a bolt snap; I sent my son down with a light, to see what it was, as I was very tired - I trusted to what he said: I went down to the warehouse about half-past eight o'clock, and missed thirty-six yards of trimming, value 7s. 6d., and 20 lbs. of cotton, worth 3l., 10lbs. of worsted, worth 30s., and thirty-three pieces of ribbon, of thirty-six yards each, worth 15l.; I am quite certain all these things were safe when I was last in the warehouse - I saw twenty-four yards of one of the pieces of ribbon about a month afterwards, at a public-house in Wheeler-street; I was quite certain it was my goods - the prisoner was not then in custody: I found my back door had been forced open: it had been locked - I observed some grease just where the bolt went, and the mark of some instrument having forced it - the bolt of the lock was forced from the socket; I had left the warehouse at six o'clock, and gave my boy the key to lock the door; I locked the front door, but not the back.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. When had you seen the property safe? A. At the time I went out; I am sure the drawers were secure, and the goods in them; I had seen them in the course of the day - no persons but customers have access to the warehouse; I am in a small way of business - I may have six customers in the course of the day: the things could not be taken without forcing the door; I have seen some cotton and silk the same colour as I lost, but I cannot swear to that.
   JOHN TOWERS. JUN. I am fourteen years old next month. On this day my father went out about six o'clock, leaving nobody that was engaged in the business but myself; the back door of the warehouse was open when my father left - I shut it directly; he went and locked it - the back door opens into a yard - a person could easily get to the yard without going through the house; all was safe then - I locked the doors, gave the light to the servant, and went out directly; the street door was locked also - nobody could get in except by force; the servant could not open the street door, for I took the key; I returned with my father about half-past seven o'clock and went up stairs - I was playing with the baby, and my father thought he heard a noise; I went down with a light, and found the back door shut and locked the same as when I left it; I did not examine the warehouse - I went up stairs again; my father afterwards sent me down stairs to fill the tea-kettle - the kitchen is on the same floor as the warehouse - and as I went by I saw the back door about half way open; I called to my father, went into the warehouse directly, and perceived some drawers lying on the counters - they had been in their places before; all the things were gone out of them; they were the drawers from where the silk or bobbins were taken - my father missed the property; the ribbons were kept in the drawers on the left-hand side - those drawers were not taken out of their places; I had the key of the back door in my pocket - nobody could have got in without forcing it; I know the prisoner, he came to the warehouse that same evening, about two hours before I left the house, to pay me 5s. which he owed my father for cottons he had bought; he knew the house, and often came there - he used to come into the warehouse.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long did he stay in the warehouse that night? A. Two or three minutes; he has staid as long as that before; I did not suspect any harm of him; I locked the front and back door - the servant could go down a flight of stairs and go out without coming to the warehouse; it is a large house - there are four rooms and a shop.
 Q. A noise made in one part might easily be heard in the other? A. Yes; at night the servants were up stairs.
   JOHN APPLEBY. I live at No. 9, Vine - court, Lamb-street, Spitalfields, which is about ten minutes walk from the prosecutor's. I knew the prisoner - I saw him about three weeks after the robbery; he asked me to dispose of two pieces of work for him - he is a weaver; the work was silk and cotton - I had them to sell for him - they are hatters bands; I have a brother in the business, and told him I must send them to him - he told me to do so; my brother would not have them as they were not perfect work; I went and tried to sell them - in two or three days I exchanged one of the pieces with Mr. Holmes, of Whitechapel; I was sent for, and went to the public-house Towers has mentioned, and there shewed him a piece of hatters band which Towers claimed; I had received it from the prisoner, and told him so.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know the prisoner's business? A. Yes; he is a silk-weaver, and has manufactured his own work; I have known him manufacture hatters' hands - that is never called ribbon; Towers had heard I had the goods to sell - he did not suspect me.
 JURY. Q. Were you in the habit of seeing the prisoner frequently before the robbery? A. I had not seen him for fifteen months before, and then he was in my employ for two or three years.
 Q. Is there any difference between these goods and what he made? A. He could manufacture such goods; I cannot tell the difference between his manufacture and another.
