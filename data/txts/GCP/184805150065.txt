ELIZABETH ENNEVER. I am wife of William Ennever, of Charles-street,
City-road. On 23rd March the prisoner George took two furnished rooms in
my house--she came next day with Pulbrook, who she said was her mother,
and a man she said was her husband-on 7th April they did not come home
they left the door locked-I got in on 10th, and missed blankets, sheets,
spoons, and other things-they never came back-on 14th I found the
prisoners in custody.

Pulbrook. I lost the ticket going home, and came back to stop it.
Witness. Yes, you did.
ROBERT Moss. I am a pawnbroker, in Goswell-street. I produce a
bolster pawned by Pulbrook on 30th March, in the name of Marsh.
WILLIAM Brown. I am in the service of a pawnbroker. I saw my
master take a piece of carpet in pawn of Palbrook-she gave her name "Ann
Davis"-she afterwards pawned a watch-stand.
JOB SMITH (policeman, G 44.) I took Pulbrook in Seward-street, Goswell-
street, and George very near the same spot-they both said if the prosecutrix
would not prosecute she should have all her property returned-I told them
to be careful what they said, as it might be used against them--George said
she was sorry for what had been done.
Mrs. ENNEVER reexamined. All these things are my husband's, and
were let with the room.
Pulbrook's Defence. My husband was fitting himself out to go to India;
he was to send me some money, and I could have taken the things out, or
my father would have got them out for me.
