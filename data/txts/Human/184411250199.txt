   Q.  After seeing this careen , did you notice anybody on the paddle-box? A.  I did—it was the prisoner—it is usual to have a person on the paddle-box—he box—he directs the motions of the vessel—it is his duty to give the steers man a signal which way he wishes the vessel to go—I saw my father's body on Saturday morning—his name was Edward Everest—there was no dimi nution of the speed of the steamer before this careen .
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What is the name of the stairs that are close in on the Lower Tunnel-pier? A.  Wapping Dock-stairs—I believe those were the stairs my father was going to—I did not see the schooner till after the accident—Wapping Dock-stairs are very close to the Lower Tunnel-pier, and a boat going across the river to Wapping Dock-stairs would be shut in by the pier after she had got to a certain extent—if anything projected beyond the pier she would be more shut in. JOHN SHADRACK. I am a seaman, formerly of the Valiant . On Friday, the 25th of Oct., I got into a boat on the Thames—there were five in the boat altogether with the person who was rowing—I did not know him—we were going over to Wapping Dock-stairs—as we were going along we met with a barge going up along the tide—I sang  out, "Waterman, here is a barge coming," and he rowed astern of it—after that we met a schooner, and we went ahead of the schooner—after we had passed the schooner we saw a steamer coming up—we were sufficiently near to see the number—it was No . 6, Waterman  —we were between 100 and 150 feet from the steamer, as near as I could judge—she was coming up along the tide—when I saw her, I stood up and waved my hand for them to keep more out into the middle of the river, which they did not—she did not make the least alteration in her course after I made that sign—I saw a person on the paddle-box—I do not know who it was—he saw me wave my hand—he had an opportunity of altering his course so as to keep more into the middle of the river—when the waterman saw the steamer coming he rowed as hard as he could—the steamer's stem  nearly caught the boat, and knocked the scull out of the waterman's hand—he rowed round with his right hand, so that the steamer should not strike the boat right in the middle, so as to slant the boat along the steamer, and the fore part of the paddle-box struck
