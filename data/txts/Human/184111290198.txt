   COURT . Q.  When you sell an article at 4 s ., do not you state to your master, "I have sold this at 4 s .,?" A.  My employer happened to be in the shop at the time the prisoner bought it.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Are you quite sure this is the girl? A.  Positive—I have lived with my master eight years.

I had one answering that description, but I believe it is not included in this charge—it was property supposed to belong to the prisoner, and not produced before the Magistrate. NICHOLAS M'CABE. I am a coal and coke dealer, and live at No. 3, Bear-street, Ratcliff. The prisoner is my daughter—she was eight years in good service before she went to Mr. Beagley's—I believe she had 15 l . in her possession before she went there; I often told her to put it in the Savings'-bank, but she has said, "Oh, father, it is safe enough in my box"—I have seen the prosecutor since my daughter has been in custody—he came to the house I was at, not my house—he told me, on that occasion, that if my daughter got out of this scrape , he would take her again into his service; he said he would take her to morrow if her trial was over to-day.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was there any one present when he said that? A.  Yes, my wife and the woman who owned the house—I do not know her name—she is not here—I was overcome at the Court-house, and was forced to be carried out into a house; they gave me a drink of water, I recovered myself, and sat on a chair about an hour; and during that time Mr. Beagley came in to me, and said, "When the trial is over, I shall take her to morrow"—this was a little while before the trial came on, the day she had her first hearing at Marylebone—I cannot tell what time of the day it was—it was in the afternoon—I cannot tell what o'clock—it was in one of the little houses that join the police-office—a private house—I was overcome at seeing her at the office, and was taken there—she never put the 15 l . into any savings'-bank, or she would have told me so—I am certain of it—I often told her to do it, and she said her money was safe enough in her box—she told me so two or three months before she was taken up, or more than three months—I know she did not put it into the savings'-bank.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did she ever tell you any thing about being the barmaid? A.  She did—she gave me a reason for declining to be barmaid—that reason applied to Mrs. Beagley. ANN M'CABE.
