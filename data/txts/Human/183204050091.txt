 1007.  MARY ANN JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 1 hed, value 30s.; 2 pillows, value 5s.; 2 blankets, value 4s.; 1 sheet, value 2s., and 1 candlestick, value 2s., the goods of Elizabeth Shoebrook.
 ELIZABETH SHOEBROOK. I am a  widow, and live in Halford-street, Fitzroy-square- I let a furnished room to the prisoner; she was with me for fourteen weeks. On the 17th of March I went into her room, and missed the bed - I asked what she had done with it; she told me to be quiet, it was all right - I asked her for it two or three times, and then my lodger went and got two Policemen; I then missed all the articles stated, which I have found at the pawnbroker's - she got her bread by dress-making, and paid her rent regularly; she only owned two weeks.
   THOMAS PATO. I am a pawnbroker. I have these articles, which were pawned by the prisoner at different times.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, pleading poverty, and stating it to have been her intention to redeem the articles - she received an excellent character.
 GUILTY. Aged 43. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1008.  HENRY JOHNSON was indicted for stealing on the 20th of March , 1 shirt, value 4s. the goods of William Daffen.
   SARAH GORDON. I live in Ironmonger-row, St. Luke's. I had a shirt to wash belonging to William Daffen on the 20th of March - I hung it in the yard, where there were some other things; I have known the prisoner more than twelve months - be kept company with my sister; he was there the whole of that day, and he went into the yard - be then came up and finished tea; I sent my mother down for the shirt to iron it, and it was not there - I then went down and asked one of the lodgers if she had taken it in mistake; she said, No - the prisoner then closed his hands, and said, might his hands never unclose any more if he did not see her take it down, and he would go for a Policeman - he went, and was gone an hour and a half - he then returned, and said, he could not find one; I said, "You are the person who told me of it, and I will send for a Policeman, and have you searched" - he said, "You may search me yourself;" he pulled off his right stocking, and there was nothing there, but in his left stocking was the duplicate and the money - I said, "You good for nothing man, go about your business;" he said he would not, he would have my life, and then he would go to prison like a man - he then said, he had done that which would transport him, and I might do it and be d - d; I sent for the officer, and gave him in charge - I do not know what the prisoner is; my sister and I scarcely ever speak, but she came that morning and he came with her.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not ask me to take a shawl and pawn it for you - I brought you the money, and you said, "Get a quartern of gin?" A. No, I did not know of it till you brought the money, and laid down 9d., and said you wanted 3d. to spend - I took the duplicate, and forgave you that, because I did not like an exposure - I never said I would do for you some time or other; I get my living honestly - I do not know whether you lived with my sister or not.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am shopman to Mr. Latter, a pawnbroker. The shirt was pawned with me on the 20th of March, by the prisoner.
 Prisoner. The prosecutrix gave it me to pawn.
   SARAH GORDON. I never authorized him to pawn it; he accused a young woman in the house with taking it, and said, with bitter oaths, that he saw her take it.
 Prisoner's Defence. It is nothing but a conspiracy of the family against me, because I do not like so much drinking of gin; they drink from morning to night, and are never sober.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Six Months.
 1009.  WILLIAM MARTIN and JOSEPH YORK were indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of March , 2 pairs, value 3s., and 1 tub, value 1s., the goods of Henry Batchelor ; and that the said Joseph York had been before convicted of felony.
   THOMAS HOBBS. I am a constable. On the 3rd of March I was in Drury-lane, between one and two o'clock- I saw the two prisoners walking and talking together; I watched them down Queen-street, and into White Lion-street - I there saw Martin try a gentleman's pocket; I watched them on to Wilmot-street- they stood at the top of the street some time; Martin then went down, and stood at the prosecutor's door; York stood at the top of the street - I went round to the other end of the street, and saw Martin catch up two pails, run up the street, and give them to York, who was about twenty yards from the prosecutor's - York ran off with them down a mews: I took Martin, gave him to an officer, and ran after York - when I got near him he threw down the pails, and ran away; I pursued, and a gentleman stopped him.
   ROBERT BATCHELOR. I am a fender-maker, and wireworker. These pails and this tub, which was in them, are my father's - his name is Henry; they stood outside the door.
   THOMAS HOBBS. I have a certificate of the conviction of Joseph York, which I got at Clerkenwell, from Mr. Selby - I know he is the person.
 MARTIN -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 YORK -    GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.
