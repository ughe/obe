1927. LEONORA CROSBY was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of June , 1 bonnet, value 6 s;.  5 caps, value 1 s  . 6 d;.  1 towel, value 6 d;.  and 2 collars, value 6 d  .; the goods of Charles Evans ; and that she had been before convicted of felony. MARY ANN EVANS. I am the wife of Charles Evans; we live at Chelsea. On Wednesday morning, the 1st of June, the prisoner came to see one of my lodgers—I saw her going up stairs, and asked who she was going to—she said, "Mrs. Donohue"—I said she was not at home—she went up stairs, and returned in two or three minutes, then went away, and returned in twenty minutes or half an hour—she then went up stairs and stopped four or five minutes, then came down and went away—when she came first to our house she had something in her apron, but she appeared to go away the second time with a larger bundle than she brought in—it was then between eleven and twelve o'clock—I went up between two and three, to my bedroom, which is adjoining the lodger's room that the pri soner went to—I missed a bonnet, a towel, five caps, and two collars, which had been hanging outside my bed-room door—I had seen them safe be tween ten and eleven the same morning—these produced are them—this bon net is a tuscan bonnet, but it has been cleaned since it was taken from my house—I know it is mine—this is the lutestring that I lined it with—these are my collars and caps—they are my own make, and I can swear to them. Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  What particular thing can you swear to? A.  This is a French cap, and these others—I made every one of them myself, except this marked one—here is the ribbon that went over this cap, and I have a piece of the same kind of ribbon here—I know this bonnet more by the lining, but I know the bonnet is the same—there is only my husband and me, and one lodger up stairs, in our house. SARAH RIEORDEN. I live in George-street, Chelsea, and keep a straw-bonnet shop. On the 1st of June the prisoner brought this bonnet to me—I had not seen her before—it was lined with this silk that is here—she asked me to clean the bonnet and turn it for her by the following Satur day, and if she did not call on the Saturday I was to send it her—she gave her name as Mrs. Crosby, 28, New-road—I had to take it to pieces—I was cleaning bonnets at the time she came, and I did this one immediately—I afterwards saw the policeman, and he told me not to go on with it—I took it to the station.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How do you know it was the prisoner? A.  Be came she was very particular in trying on a shape—this was a very parti cular bonnet—it was short at the ears—it was quite a fright—they are not worn small now—the prisoner was with me about ten minutes—I am sure it was her—I knew her by her dress and her face. JAMES BRADLEY( police-constable B  134.) I apprehended the prisoner on the 1st June—I found these caps in her possession—I kept them—she was discharged, and then taken again.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where did you apprehend her? A.  In Lower Sloane-street—she lives in the New-road—I know her, and know her husband—he has tried to compromise this case—the prisoner said these things were her own, and stuck to it, till I took them from her at Queen-square WILLIAM BROWN( police-sergeant B  9.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Clerk en well Session-house ( read )—I was at the trial—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY Aged 28—. confined Twelve Months.
