Henry Rough. I live with my mother, who keeps a tobacco-shop.
The prisoner came to our shop about seven o'clock in the evening, on the
last day of January, for an ounce of tobacco, which came to 14d.-he gave
me a shilling-I told him it was bad he said he was very sorry for it-
I gave it to my mother-she looked at it, bent it, and gave it to the pri-
soner back again-he went out of the shop, saying he had no small change,
and could not have the tobacco I watched him thirty or forty yards down
St. John-street-road-be joined a woman, had a little conversation with
her, and went on to Mr. Petch's-I saw him come out, and I went in and
told Mr. Petch that a person had been offering bad money before, and
asked him if what he had taken was a bad one-he looked into his till,
and in a little bowl was a shilling and some small silver-I saw the woman
Walking up and down the street--I foHowed the prisoner down till I saw
the officer, and gave him in charge.
