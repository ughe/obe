THOMAS RUSSELL. I atn town traveller to Messrs. Watsons, of Holbord-
hill. I purchased the crimson damask of Messrs. Keith and Co-We have
one of the pieces--the other piece I sent on approval to Messrs. Druce and
Co., about 16th of Sept.--I sent for it again in Dec., I did not get it back
--the piece that we had in stock is now here.
WILLIAM WATKINS FRAY. I am manager of Messrs. Thomas Charles
Druce and another's business, 200, Regent-street-they have likewise an
establishment at Baker-street bazaar received & piece of this crimson
damask from Messrs. Watsons, on approval believe this is the piece
(looking at it)-it was sent for in Dec., and we could not find it. 10
Cross-examined. Q. Do you say that you have not sold it? 4. I have
not-we do not enter in our books what we receive on approval-il we had
got a customer we should have sold it, but then we should enter it in our
books-I believe our books are not here-myself and Henry Payne are in
Messrs. Druce's employ--there have been no others since Sept.-Henry
Payne is not here--this damask has not been at Baker-street- the establish-
ment in Regent-street is a large establishment-tbe one in Baker-street is
larger--there are four persons at the Baker-street bazaar--if a customer wanted
anything which they bad not got there, they would send to Regent-street
with a written order, and the goods would then be booked and sent--Mr.
Druce is in partnership with Mr. Broadburst, and has been for six years, I
believe-Mr. Druce is not a clerk to Mr. Broadhurst--the names are Druce,
Brock, and Co., at Regent-street-I do not know such a person as Brock,
the firm is Druce and Broadhurst.n et
Thomas COBLEY (policeman, K 65). On 14th Dec. I saw the younger
Arment in Whitechapel-road-I asked him some questions, and in conse-
quence of his answers I took him into custody on the following day I saw
some crimson damask at Mr. Jackson's, an upholsterer's, in Shoreditch I took
it into my possession, but not on that occasion--this produced is it-there
are from thirty-six to forty yards of it-within about a fortnight afterwards
I went to Mr. Hewetson, an upholsterer, in Tottenham-court-rond; I there got
about eighteen yards of the damask-these are the pieces.
GEORGE SPENCER WALL. I do not know the prisoner Chester, I have
never seen him I know the two Arments--I saw this damask about 12th
or 14th Oct., at the elder Arment's house, in the Tenter-ground-I had sold
something for him before, and on going to his house he produced this damask
- it was then in one piece-he asked me if I could sell it for him and I
never did sell any, but I would try--he wanted 6s. a yard for it_I took and
tried it more than a week-T could not sell it at that, being in one piece-I
took one yard as a sample-this is it-1 told the elder Arment, in three of
four days afterwards, at his house, that I could not sell it-I told him I was
only offered 8s. 6d. & yard for it, (Mr. Jackson had offered me that he said
he would not take that, and I never saw him again for several days afterwards
-I then asked him whether he had sold the damask-he said, "No, and he
was inclined to take the 88.6d." I went to Mr. Jackson, and told him he
said he would not mind taking it at 38. 6d.-he bad bought the whole of it,

son's, and they offered to buy some at 58. a yard-I met Mr. Arment as he
