ALBERT CHARLES VANPOOLE. I am a messenger, and live at 32, Great
Marlbourough Street-on 20th February, in the early morning, I was in
Whiteball, going home towards Charing Cross-I had a bad hand from a
poisoned finger, and had to carry my hand up-all at once I was surrounded
by four men-I received two blows; my coat was buttoned, but it was
opened by the first blow; the second blow was on my eye, and I have the
the mark now-I recognise the prisoners-it was Dunn who struck me,
but Cooke was the worst-& policeman eaught Dunn, and brought him to
me-I recognised him, and asked him what he struck me for, as I did not
interfere with him-I saw Cooke at Bow Street the same day-I recog-
nise bim, as he is tall and thin and pale-faced my eyes were all right till
the second blow-I have no doubt the prisoners are two of the men who
attacked memy watch was inside my coat in my left waistcoat pocket
they opened my coat, but I kept my hand tight against my chest. o
Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHEGAN. The first blow nearly stunned
me, but it was not in my eye-after that I felt a hand feeling over
me--they did not try to take my hand away.
Cross-examined by Mr. FRITH. I picked out Cooke from several others
-the policeman was standing by a pale-faced man-the Magistrate
asked me if I knew him and I said "Yes"-I did not see him till he
was in the dock-he is dressed the same now; he wore a round hat,
but I am not sure whether it was a hat or a cap, as it was all con-
fusion, and so quick after the first blow.
Re-examined. The man I saw in the dock had a pale face-I saw
him then in open daylight--the constable told the inspector that he saw
them try to take my watch.
