FRANK BOYton, surveyor and valuer. In March, 1908, I was a
partner in the firm of Boyton, Sons, and Trevor. We were instructed
io value the Hockley brickfield. I visited the property with my
partner, Mr. Trevor Abrahams, and we made the valuation in con-
function with Messrs. Brewett and Taylor; their valuation was
Slightly higher than ours and we took the mean. We sent in a report
to Benson, putting the value of the brickfields, plant, machinery,
stock in trade, and residential buildings, as & going concern, at
£15,270, stating that a further £10,000 would be required for work-
ing capital.
Cross-examined. I have had over 30 years' experience as a valuer;
I have myself been a brick manufacturer for 20 years. I did not
know you and had not seen you until this trial. It is difficult to say
what amount had been expended on the Hockley brickfield; it might
be, including land and everything, £30,000 or £40,000. I valued it
as it was at the time, as between a willing vendor and a willing pur-
chaser.
To Mr. Justice Darling. When I made the valuation I had no
knowledge of any previous valuation; I had not heard of prisoner's
statements in the affidavits in the Hobman action. I cannot for a
moment conceive the value being represented as £95,000.
JOHN TREVOR ABRAHAMS also spoke to the valuation, which he made
is conjunction with his partner, the previous witness. There were
two valuations sent in; in the first witness had inadvertently omitted
a reference to the "expensive and up to date machinery"; prisoner
pointed this out, and a second report was supplied containing this
reference.
Cross-examined. You did not ask me to rewrite the report; you
simply asked my firm to make a valuation, and did not suggest any
figures to me.
GEORGE TOWNSEND, clerk, in the Central Office, Royal Courts of

granted by prisoner to Kate Feltham, securing £100 on office furni-
ture at Copthall Avenue; and another, dated August 2, 1907,
granted by Gertrude Benson to Kate Feltham, securing £200 on
bousehold furniture at 89, Woodside, Wimbledon
ISABEL BENNETT, dressmaker, Dulwich. In May, 1908, I saw an
advertisement of Feltham's Bank; on writing to the bank I received
the yellow covered book, entitled, "Profitable Banking Accounts, by
• Bank Manager.” It stated that Feltham's Bank had "authorised
capital, £100,000." From this and the other statements in the book
I believed that the bank was carrying on a genuine bona fide business.
Prisoner submitted that as the book was "Issued by the directors of Feltham's
Bank Limited," he could not alone be made responsible for its statementa,
although the directors might in their corporate capacity be responsible.
Mr. Justice Darling said there was evidence that the prisoner took an active
part, and in fact, had the control of the business of the bank. It could not
e said that if a number of persons formed themselves into a limited
Ampany they could make any false pretences they pleased, and escape indivi-
dual liability for them.
