"How do you do, Mrs. Penfold?"—I said, "Is this the woman who pawned the napkins?—he said, "She is"—she said she had not, or if she had, she did not know it—Penfold and his wife were discharged.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did the Magistrate discharge him, though he had been a waiter there? A.  Yes; it was Mr. Halls, I think—there was not sort of evidence against him—were could prove the napkins were in his possession—there were about forty-one found altogether. JOHN DODDS re-examined . I do not know Penfold to swear to him—I had seen him—I know him now—I did not before—the address given was No. 9, Isabella-row—I went there, and found Penfold did live there—I always took the prisoner for Mrs. Penfold, because she gave that name. WILLIAM WATSON. I am butler to the Athenaeum Club, and have been so nearly six years—the Earl of Aberdeen is a member of that club, and a trustee—there are other trustees—I have examined the napkins, and have no hesitation in saying they belong to the club—the name of the club is marked in the damask. CHARLOTTE ROSE re-examined . I believe all these to belong to the members of the club.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know Penfold? A.  Yes; he was discharged from the club on suspicion of stealing a gold watch from a gentleman—these napkins have been found since that, but I lost them two years age—some of them were missed before his discharge.
   Prisoner's Defence . I am guilty of pawning them, but innocent of knowing they were stolen—they were given to me by Mrs. Penfold as her own property—I always gave her the money and the tickets, the moment I returned to her house—both Penfold and his wife, at the first examination, begged me not to acknowledge to it, or I should have done so—both thee butler and Mrs. Rose heard him beg me not to acknowledge it. WILLIAM WATSON re-examined. Q.  Did you observe whether Penfold and his wife made a communication to her at the office? A.  My back was turned to them—I heard a whispering—I cannot say what it was about. NOT GUILTY

HENRY CRICK. I am servant to Barnett and Dickens, pawnbrokers, I have two napkins, pawned in the name of Penfold—I know the prisoner by coming to the shop—I cannot say she pawned them. JOHN DODDS. I am a pawnbroker. I produce six napkins pawned by the prisoner in the name of Penfold.
   Cross-examined. Q.  She gave you Penfold's proper address? A.  Yes; Penfold was held to bail, to answer any charge that might be preferred. WILLIAM BALLARD. I am an officer. I questioned the prisoner if she had pawned any napkins—she denied it, and denied it to the pawnbroker, and also at the officer, when Penfold and his wife stood at the bar. WILLIAM WATSON. I am butler at the Atheneum—Lord Aberdeen and other are trustees—I know these to be the property of the club. NOT GUILTY
