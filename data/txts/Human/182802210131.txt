732. THOMAS BARRETT was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , 69lbs. weight of lead, value 13s., and 1 sack, value 2s., the goods of Frederick Charles Burton.
   THOMAS HENRY HURT. I work for Mr. Burton. I left his shop in Eagle-street about two o'clock on the 17th of January - he is a plumber and glazier; before I had left I had stripped off some lead - I believe this to be part of it.
 Prisoner. Q. Do you know the Cheshire Cheese public-house, in Dean-street? A. Yes - I use it every day for my breakfast and dinner.
   JAMES MEDLYCOT. I am an officer. I stopped the prisoner on the 17th of January, in Eagle-street, about half past six o'clock in the evening, with this lead in a sack; I asked what he had got - he said some lead, which he had brought from Penton-place, where he had been working for himself - that it was lead which he had left, and he was going to take it back to Mr. Ransom, in Bedfordbury, of whom he had bought it; I detained him, and went there; they said they knew him, but had not sold him any lead.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I am an officer. I was with Medlycot, and staid with the prisoner while he went to Ransom's; while he was gone the prisoner said to me, "I am very sorry I told him wrong - I have had the lead some time, and was going to sell it to get myself a pair of shoes."
   FREDERICK CHARLES BURTON. I am a lead-merchant. I had a quantity of lead on my premises; I have compared this with what remains, and I believe it to be the same.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that a man named Davenport had given him the lead to dispose of, at the Cheshire Cheese public-house, for which he promised to give him a pair of new shoes.
   THOMAS RANSOM. I am the brother of Mr. Ransom, of Bedfordbury. My brother has employed the prisoner for years, and he always had a good character.
 FREDERICK CHARLES BURTON. This witness called at my house, and told my brother that if I would go to a public-house in Broad-street, previous to the hearing, he could give me information of several men who had robbed me - I went there, and saw him; he then said if I would give him a sovereign he would tell me something; I said I would not - he then said he should not tell me anything.
 THOMAS RANSOM. I did tell him I could give him some information; but it was only what I derived from the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Confined Two Months.
 733.  THOMAS HOPKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of January , 1 sovereign, 2 half-crowns, 1 shilling, and 2 sixpences, the goods of Peter Wade , from his person.
 PETER WADE. On the 20th of January I was in  Whitechapel, about two o'clock in the morning, and had taken a drop too much; I was taken to the watch-house - when I got there I had one sovereign, three half-crowns, two shillings, and two sixpences; I had taken it for my wages about a quarter before twelve o'clock that night - I then went and drank, but did not pay for any thing - my master paid for what I had; I was put into a room where the prisoner and some other persons were; I lost my money in the night, and the next morning I saw some money taken from the prisoner's shoes; I was three parts drunk, and do not know what I had done before I went into the watch-house.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is your master here? A. No. I was taken near the church in Whitechapel - I believe they took me to the watch-house for safety; I had a long way to go home: I live at Stepney- my master is Mr. Cann, a hair-dresser, living in Fleet-street; I received my money at his shop, and then went with him to a public-house in Fetter-lane; I and three more drank nine or ten glasses of gin and water. In going from the public-house to Whitechapel a number of men and women spoke to me; several females came very near to me: after I left the public-house I showed my money to two of my shopmates, named Scott and Sinclair.
   THOMAS SMITH. I am beadle of Whitechapel. On Sunday morning, the 20th of January, the prisoner was brought to the watch-house, but I am not certain whether he was searched or not; while he was there I brought in the prosecutor from High-street, the worse for liquor; I do not know what he had about him, but about six o'clock the next morning he said he was very sorry, and hoped I would let him go home; I said, "Have you got every thing safe about you?" he then felt, and said he had lost a sovereign, some half-crowns, and some silver; I went into the room where the others were, and brought out the prisoner; I found a sovereign, two shillings, and two sixpences in his left waistcoat pocket, and one half-crown in each of his shoes - he said he had it from his father. I saw an apron taken out of a hat, but I do not know whose it was.
 Cross-examined. Q. What did the prosecutor say he was sorry for having done? A. I was dispersing some low men and women, and he came in among them, and asked what I wanted to do; he was a good deal the worse for liquor - he had a watch when he went in, but I did not see any money.
   WILLIAM HEATH. I am a patrol of Whitechapel. I assisted in taking the prisoner to the watch-house, for rescuing a girl from a watchman, in White Lion-street; I saw him searched the next morning, and the money found - Dungate took his hat off, and found in it this apron, which was claimed by Wade; I heard the prosecutor say he had lost 1l. 10s. 6d. - I think he said a sovereign, four half-crowns, and a sixpence; I did not search the other persons who were there, as they were only put in for being disorderly, fighting, and such like, which is frequently the case in that part of the town.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you search the prisoner when he went into the watch-house? A. No - I searched the other persons who went in; and one of them had no money when he went in, but the next morning there was half a crown and a shilling found upon him.
   JOHN DUNGATE. I am an officer. I took the apron out of the prisoner's hat, and found a half-crown in each of his shoes - I did not search him when he went it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see the prosecutor brought in? A. Yes; he certainly was very drunk - I was in an adjoining room; I was not in the hole with the prisoner. They have no candle nor fire.
     NOT GUILTY.
