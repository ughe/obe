3069. HENRY JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of Oct..2
pewter pots, 28. 6d.; the goods of William Coomber; and that he had be
before convicted of felony.
WILLIAM COOMBER. I am a licensed victualler, and live in Dorset-street. I
swear these two pewter pots are mine--the prisoner had no right to take them
JOHN HADDOCK. I was going down Harley-street last Wednesday even
ing--I saw the prisoner stoop down an area and take a quart pot-he put
it into his pocket-he went into the Park, took it out, took a knife out.
stooped, and appeared to be cutting it-he then returned, and took two pint
pots from an area- I gave information to an officer.
Prisoner. Q. What are you? A. A labourer-I have worked for Mr.
Grant, a coachmaker in Hatton-garden-I bave been convicted of pot
stealing myself.
WILLIAM JONES (police-constable D 59.) I received information, and
took the prisoner-I found on him these three quart pots and these two pints.
Prisoner. Haddock was the person who induced me to do anything of the
kind; and he was on this very occasion after the same property; if I had
not taken it he would.

3070. MARY RYAN was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of Oct,
1 box, value 3s. ; 12 yards of lace, value 18. ; 3 collars, 38.; 6 ostrich fea-
thers, 1s.; and 1 tea-caddy, 28.; the goods of Mina Wood.
Mina Wood. I am single, and live in Queen's place, Great Queen-street; the
prisoner was employed by me. On the 14th of Oct. I found her outside my door
- I looked at her very hard-she said, "Don't look at me; I have got nothing
belonging to you"- I said, “I did not say that you had "- I sent my ser
vant out for an errand-she came back and said did I give the woman two
boxes- I went after the prisoner, and found her with two boxes under her
apron-I asked her what she had got, she said, “Nothing"- these are them-
she had no right to them I had placed them under my bed.
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. What was she employed as !
A. A charwoman-she had got about eleven yards from my door-I keep
lodging-house-I do not inquire what my lodgers' friends are-I do not know
what my lodgers do-mine is not a house of ill-fame.
Jury. Q. Who admitted ber into the house? A. There is a latch and
a handle, which must be turned-I am quite sure my door was shut
THOMAS TICKNER (police-constable F 11.) I took the prisoner at the
prosecutrix's house.
Cross-examined. Q. The boxes were in the house? A. Yes, all the
prisoner said was, "You are quite mistaken "-I know nothing about the
house, it is not on my beat.
MARY HEIFY. I went out on an errand, and met the prisoner coming out
with two boxes- I asked her if she had seen my mistress-she said, "Yes,
it is all right" - I had cleared the room before, and saw the box under the bed
Guilty. Aged 40.-Confined Six Months.
