852. EDWARD HATTON was indicted for burglariously breaking
and entering the dwelling-house of John Dorrell, about the hour of five
o'clock in the night, on the 31st of January, at St. Saviour, Southiwark,
with intent to steal.
JOHN DORRELL. I am a dealer in marine-stores, living in Redeross-
street. in the parish of St. Saviour, Southwark have purchased things
of the prisoner. Last Monday night I shut up at ten o'clock all safe-
my wife and daughter and myself sleep in the house-my son and a friend
sat up to watch-I came down about five o'clock on Tuesday morning-]
thought it was six-I told my son it was six, and he might leave-he said
it was only five- went to bed again-I had not gone five minutes be.
fore this happened-it was a quarter-past five-] had lost money before
this, and could not account how it had gone.
RICHARD BRowN. I am a warehouseman, living in Cross-lane-Isa up two
or three nights at this house. Between five and six clock on Tuesday morn-
ing, I saw the prisoner get over the wall, which is ten feet high, and lower
himself down into the back-yard--he then moved a tub which covered a
grating, and lifted up the grating, and dropped himself down into the cellar
-a friend, who was with me, let the grating down-we took a light and
found the prisoner-he had gone from the back-cellar to the front-cellar,
and had got his shoes off ready to come up stairs-there is a way up from
there.
FREDERICK LONG (police-constable M 100.) I took the prisoner.
(William Hatton, a labourer, gave the prisoner a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 24-Transported for Ten Years.
