 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. The last was the only time the notes were specifically mentioned? A. That was the only time - I did not announce myself as a Magistrate; I acted as a friend of Dr. Farre, he being a professional man, and not able to attend - I was not acting as a Magistrate; I told her she had made herself liable - she was frightened before that; she could hardly hold herself still; she did not appear ill - I did not desire that she should be taken to Bow-street.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you go there as a Magistrate, or merely as a friend of Dr. Farre? A. As a friend; the little girl said she had slept with Mrs. Keefe that night.
 The papers proved to be in the hand-writing of Carter, which were put in and read as follows -"Mrs. Keefe died 11th."Mr. Dr. Farre, 11, Charter-house-square."
   SUSAN BROWN. I knew Mrs. Keefe. I used to go to make her bed every night before she lived at Carter's; I took her luggage to Carter's - Mrs. Carter paid every attention to her, and Mrs. Keefe was certainly attached to her for her kindness; in the course of the week I asked Mrs. Keefe if Miss Springweiler should be sent for - she said she could do her no good if she were there; Mrs. Carter said Mrs. Keefe did not want to see her - I said, as there was such an alteration, the family ought to know it; Mrs. Carter sent me away, and said I was not wanted any more - I know Mrs. Keefe used to knock with a stick when she wanted Carter to help her into bed; I said on the Friday night that Mrs. Keefe was altered, and I said the same on Saturday night - I was to stop and sit up with her on the Saturday night, but I could not.
   WILLIAM WOODBURY. I am an officer of Queen-square. I took Mrs. Carter into custody in August; on our way to the office she said that the notes were given her by Mrs. Keefe - that Mrs. Keefe had called her little girl and given her a 200l. bill, but the child could not read, and she took it away from her again; I then asked, if any of the notes should be traced with the name of Carter on them, what she would say? - she said, "It will be all very well; they were given to me - indeed she gave me all;" I understood her to mean all that was in the desk, and I think she said, "I am sorry I let the desk go." I afterwards apprehended Carter, Sen.; he said his wife had given him the notes; that he had passed them, and he was not ashamed of it - that his name was on them; that he had passed them at a public-house in his neighbourhood, and one at an ironmonger's - that his wife had not acted right, for she should have brought him the key of the room, and then he would have kept the whole property, for he understood her it had been given to her.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. You went to Mrs. Carter's and took her? A. Yes, it was mentioned that I wished to take up the son, and the father went and brought him to the office, and attended the examinations from time to time till I took him, which was a fortnight or three weeks after, and then I found him at home at his business; he is a master builder, and has a large family.
 MR.  WILLIAM WATERS. I am one of the firm of Barnard, Hoares, and Co. bankers, Lombard-street. (Looking at three bills of exchange), these were paid at our house at different times to a person named Keefe, by the receipt on the back of them - I have no recollection whether it was man or woman; two of the bills for 120l. and 180l. were paid together - here is a list of the notes I paid for them(handing it in).
   WILLIAM BEATON. I am a clerk in the Bank of England, and produce thirty-two notes which have been paid in there, and are cancelled.
   McCULLOCK ROSS. I am a clerk at the Duke of Bedford's office in Montague-street. The prisoner, Carter, Sen., holds a lease under the Duke, and paid me this 20l. note (looking at it) for rent - I cannot say which of them paid it, but think it was the father; I believe so, for I do not think it was the son; I have written "Carter" on it -I recollect that it was a man.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. You have no perfect recollection so as to swear positively which gave it you? A. No; seing the name on it induces me to suppose it was Carter; besides which I recollect asking him about a man named Wilson, with whom he had a trial; I should not have put the name on it if I had not received it from him - the note is No. 15,311, dated the 9th of June, 1825.
   WILLIAM BROWN. I keep the Compasses in White Hart-yard. I received this 30l. note, No. 10,855, dated the 20th of June, 1825, from Carter, Sen. on the 16th of July, and put his name on it; here are two 5l. notes, Nos. 11,560 and 11,561, dated the 9th of June, 1825, which I received on the 16th and 20th of June, either from the father or son, I cannot tell which; my house is within two hundred yards of Carter's.
   JOSEPH HART. I am a wholesale ironmonger. I received this 5l. note, No. 11,564, from Carter, Jun. on the 18th of June - he lives fifty or sixty yards from me.
   JOHN PARISH. I am collector of rates. I received these two 10l. notes, Nos. 3122 and 3123, from Carter, Jun. - I think on the 20th of June.
   JOHN TRIMBY. I keep a public-house in Drury-lane. I received two 10l. notes, Nos. 3120 and 7616, and a 5l., No. 11,558, from one of the Mr. Carters, I cannot say which, for I am in the habit of giving them change almost every Saturday night, as they live next door; I cannot say when it was, as I have only put the name on them.
