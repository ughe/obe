About one o'clock on Friday, the 31st of May, I went into the house, and
saw Bevan fall from the store-room window in front of the scullery window-
he got up, and run away– I then saw Cummings fall down immediately after-
he got up, and they both ran away-I ran after them—when I got outside the
gate I saw Cummings going down the Park-road into the Regent-park gate
Bevan was running down the walk in the park-I saw something under his
coat—he put it down into the late Marquis of Hertford's grounds—we brought
him back to the place—we went to where he put something down, and there
found these two cruet-stands--they are my mistress's, and had been in the
parlour fronting the area.
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. You did not pick them up?
No, I saw them picked upthe park-keeper took possession of them-I
caught Cummings first, and gave him in charge of the park-keeper-Bevan
ran a different way to Cummings-- he was taken a few yards from the pro-
perty.
James George. I heard a cry of “Stop thief”-I saw Bevan run-I
ran towards him—he dropped something in the late Marquis of Hertford's
grounds-I stopped him, and went back to the place—1 saw another person
pick up these two cruet-stands.
BEVAN-Guilty. Aged 23.-Confined Nine Months.
1667. FREDERICK GRANT was indicted for stealing 2 shillings and
3 pence, the monies of Mary Hart, from her person.
Mary Hart. I live in Rouse's-gardens, Queen-street, Chelsea. On the
evening of the 25th of May I went to Mr. Hyam's, a pawnbroker, at Fulham
- I had not been in above five minutes before I found the prisoner's left
hand in my right hand pocket-- I pushed him away, and he ran off-I had
38. 3d. in my pocket, and 2s. 3d. of it was gone-the policeman overtook
him—he gare it me back in the passage in scuffling with him.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. When was he brought back ? A.
In about three minutes - I am sure he is the person- I felt his hand in my
pocket.
James SKELTON (police-constable B 4.) I saw the prisoner running very
fast—he fell down-a man came up, and said, " Take him, he has committed
• a robbery in the pawnbroker's shop; I have tusselled with him till I am quite
out of breath”— the prosecutor then came up, and said, " That is the man that
robbed me "—the prisoner said two men had been fighting in the shop,
and torn his coat-this is the flap of his coat.
Guilty. Aged 21.-Confined Six Months.
1668. SUSAN SMITH was indicted for stealing 37 yards of printed
cotton, value 178.; 2 yards of union cloth, 6d.; and 7 yards of calico, ls. 9d.;
the goods of John Swaine, her master.
John Swaine, linen-draper, Chapman-street, Marylebone. The prisoner
was my servant for five weeks-after the 1st of May I missed these things-
they are mine.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. Where was the property found ? A.
At the pawnbroker's—it has my own mark on it-I know it had not been
sold-I have two men and a boy.
William Crow. I produce part of the property-I have the counter du-
plicates-I do not know who pawned it.
. GEORGE Wareham. I live with Mr. Mills, a pawnbroker, in the Edg.
ware-road. I produce the cotton and calico--I do not know who pawned it
- this is the duplicate of it.
