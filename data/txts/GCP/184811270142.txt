221. ANN GREY, stealing 1 bag, value 1d.; 1 half-sovereign, 1 half-crown,
3 shillings, and 1 sixpence; the moneys of George Turner, from his person.
GEORGE TURNER. I am a labourer. On 18th Nov., about three o'clock
in the morning, I met the prisoner in the New-cut-she asked me to go with
her-I said I would have nothing to do with her --she put her hand into my
pocket, and took 6d, out-1 crossed the road to get rid of her-she followed
and clung to me- I had had a little liquor, but knew what I was about-she
put her hand into my pocket, and took out this bag (produced), with a half-
sovereign, a half-crown, and 3s. in it.
GEORGE WATMOUGH (policeman, L 105) I took the prisoner, and found
on ber half-a-sovereign, half-a-crown, and 18.- was with a constable who
picked up this bag, empty-Turner claimed it.
Prisoner's Defence. He walked with me to the Waterloo-road, and told
me he had been with some girl, who robbed him of 6d. and some halfpence;
he wanted me to lie down in the street, and as I would not, he called
" Police!" and said I had robbed him; the money I had was mine.
GEORGE TURNER re-examined. It is false.
Not GUILTY.
222. MARTIN COOK, stealing 1 box, 1 gown, I snuff-box, 1 shawl, and
other wearing-apparel, value 18s. 60.; the goods of the South-Eastern
Railway Company. MR. BODKIn conducted the Prosecution,
RACHAEL ROGERS. In Sept., 1847, I had a box corded up at the Brick
layers' Arms station, directed, “ Miss Rogers, to be left at the Bricklayers'
Arms station till called for "- I had written to Canterbury to desire it to be
forwarded - I went to the station more than once, but could get no tidings of
it-it contained a mahogany work-box, about a foot long, some ear-rings, a
necklace, gowns, and other things-these are the ear-rings (produced.)
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Were there not two pair!
A. Yes-this pair are gold.
ELIZABETH Cooper. I am single. About nine months ago I was living
with my father, and the prisoner made me a present of these ear-rings.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you know Daniel Haines ? A. Yes; he is my
uncle-I believe he is employed by the railway-he used to come to my
father's-Cook did not say he got the rings from Haines-Haines brought a
mahogany box there, nearly a foot long, and about six inches broad.
MR. BODKIN, Q. Was it before he brought the ear-rings ? A. About
the same time. (MR. BODKIN withdrew from the Prosecution.)
Not Guilty.
223. CORNELIUS COLLINS, stealing 2 quarts of peas, value 6d.; the
goods of Edward Gripper and another, bis masters.
EDWARD GRIPPER. I am a wharfinger. The prisoner was in my service
- he had no authority to take peas- believe these split peas (produced)
to be mine.
Morriss RICHARDS. On 5th Nov., between nine and ten o'clock in the
morning, I saw the prisoner coming with his son from Mr. Gripper's ware-
house with this bundle under the boy's arm-I asked where he got it-be
said his father gave it him-the father was not there then I went to him,
and asked if the boy was his son-he said, "Yes"-I asked if he gave him
those peas-he said, "Yes"-I asked if his master allowed him to do so-he
said, "Yes" - I took him and the peas.
GUILTY. Aged 35.- Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Months.
ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, DECEMBER 1811, 1848.
