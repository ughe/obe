   Cross-examined. by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You did not happen to remem ber some things on the first examination? A.  I have not so good a head piece as some people—I mentioned about his "slitting" the second time, not the first—I do not know whether Ashlin was uneasy about my forgetting it—he said I had mistaken it—I called at his house, but I did not see him—he was not at home—I saw a person, I do not know whether it was his wife or not—she acted like a domestic woman—I believe I had some ale—I did not pay for it—that was the day before I went to the Police-court, and then the next day I told about the "splitting," and about the mud and the mire.

METCALF . Q.  How much ale had you? A.  One pint. HENRY BATEMAN HODSON. I am assistant surveyor to the London and South-Western Railway Company. they are extending their line from Nine✗ Elms—they have lately bought this yard, of Mr. Baker—Anderson was in the employ of the Company—he had authority to remove iron, or lead, or any stores belonging to the Company, to a place of safety, or into empty houses, where he could lock them up, and where they would be secure.
   MR. BALLANTINE to JOSEPH ASHLIN . Q.  Did you go to an attorney's office for Smith, and instruct him to appear on his behalf? A.  No; I went to a solicitor on my own behalf.
   COURT . Q.  What time did Anderson come to you? A.  At past twelve o'clock in the day.
   COURT to JAMES MILLBURY . Q.  What time did Smith come with the others to take these things? A.  I think it was after dinner, a little after two o'clock—I did not see Anderson. NOT GUILTY.
