1213. JANE TURNER was indicted for stealing 1 table-cover, value
45. 6d., the goods of John Askew.
JOHN Askew. I carry on business at No. 110, Blackman-street, Bo-
rough. On the evening of the 18th of April I was standing on the stairs,
and saw the prisoner sitting by the side of my counter-she took a table-
cover from the counter, folded it, and put it under her shawl-I came
down and waited about two minutes-she was sitting by the side of the
counter-I told my man to go for the policeman-she then took the cover
from under her shawl, and said, "What, about this "-she said she was
going to ask the price of it-she had not bought anything then.
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST, Q. The person who was with
her did buy something? A. Yes, and it was higher in price than this
table-cover, it was a hearth-rug, which came to about 58. or 68.- I cannot
say what the prisoner's intention might be-she brought the cover out at
once, as soon as I talked about the policeman-I believe her friends are
very respectable-I believe it was her sister who was with her-the pri-
soner is not married, her sister is-I said, “These people don't want to
buy a hearth-rug," and they had not bought one then, but after I sent
for the policeman they bought one and paid for it-I think I have stated
all that happenedI think the prisoner might be tempted to take this
cover, from being placed in favourable circumstances for so doing, as the
young men were out of the way, except the person who was serving the
rug-after I had told the young man to go for the policeman, the pri-
soner said she wanted to ask the price of the cover-I told her, and she
said it was too much- I was willing to sell it, but she would not pay the
price.
Court. Q. Do you mean that after you charged her with stealing it,
she asked the price? A. Yes, and I said, "You don't want to buy one;"
but she persisted in asking the price-I said it was 68.-she said it was
too much-it had cost me less than the rug did.
Not Guilty.
