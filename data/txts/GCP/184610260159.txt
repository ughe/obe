Before Edward Bullock, Esg.
2195. JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing 28lbs. of zinc, value 48. ;
the goods of Thomas Henry Green.
THOMAS HENRY GREEN. I am a general dealer. I live in Gloucester-row,
New Kent-road-on the 22nd of Oct. I was out collecting metal from diffe-
rent store-shop-about twelve o'clock at noon I called on Mr. Evans, a
marine-store-dealer-I left my cart at the door with two pieces of zinc in it,
and some baskets-while I was talking to Mr. Evans I heard the cry of
"Thieves "-1 went out in about three minutes and missed the two pieces of
zinc-they weighed about f-cwt.- I am sure they were in the cart when I
went into Mr. Evan's.
John CHAMBERLAIN. I live in Red-cross-street. I was standing near
Mr. Evans's shop-I saw a horse and cart there-I saw the prisoner reach
over the back of the cart, and take out something that looked like lead, and
give it to another boy, who walked away-the prisoner then took another
piece and walked away with it-I went into the shop-Mr. Evans came out
and the prisoner ran away-I knew the prisoner before -I am quite sure it
was him.
FRANCES Evans. I am the wife of James Evans. We live in Red-cross-
street-on the 22nd of Oct., between twelve and one o'clock, the prosecutor
came with his cart—he talked with my husband in the shop-the cart
remained at the door for a quarter of an hour, or twenty minutes—I went out
and saw the prisoner a short distance from the cart-he saw me and ran
away-I gave information to the police.
JAMES BARTON (police-constable M 272.) I received information, and
went, on Friday evening, the 23rd of Oct., to Mint-street. I saw the prisoner
with some others-he saw me and ran away--I took him, and told him I
wanted him on suspicion of stealing zinc-he said he did not know anything
about it.
S E GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined Six Months.
Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
2196. GEORGE BERGIN was indicted for embezzlement.
WILLIAM FOWNES. I am an omnibus proprietor. I bought the prison-
er's business and employed him afterwards-he was employed to collect
money for me-he was to pay it to me on a Saturday night-I afterwards
took the book over to Miss Jackson-the prisoner told me he had not paid
me an amount of 501. from about forty customers--he admitted he had not
paid them-if he received 8s. 6d. from Mr. Powell, 2s, 6d. from Mr. Palmer,
and 28. 6d. from Mr, Neal, be bas not paid those sums to me-he ought to
have paid them to me, or to Miss Jackson.
ELIZABETH JACkson. The prisoner was in the habit of paying me money
for Mr. Fownes. I have looked at the books-he has not paid me 88. 6d.
received on the 1st of Sept., or 28. 6d. on the 20th of Sept., or 2s. 6d. on the
27th.
Prisoner. Q. Did I ever pay you any money ? A. Yes, small sums
once or twice.
John Powell. I employed the prisoner-I paid him some money for
his master on the 1st of Sept.-I cannot swear to the sum it was above 18.
