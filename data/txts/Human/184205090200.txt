 1663. JAMES BOURNE was indicted for stealing, on the   8th of April , I saw, value 8 s  .; and 1 square, value 3 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Ben jamin Wildsmith . BENJAMIN WILDSMITH. I am a carpenter. I lost this saw on the 8th of April, from my shop, up stairs—I can swear to it, by a particular mark—this square is also mine—my name is on it—the prisoner formerly worked for me, and I always found him honest. JAMES ARCHER. I know this saw and square to be Mr. Wildsmith's. WILLIAM EPPS( police-constable N  303.) I met the prisoner in the Lea Bridge-road about eight o'clock, on the 9th of April, with this saw and square—I asked what he had got—he said he had bought it—I asked him Where—he said in Sun-street, Bishopsgate, about eighteen months ago.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to Woodford to look for work; as I was coming down Snaresbrook I met a carpenter, whom I had never seen be fore; he had the square and saw, and asked me to buy them, which I did, for 3 s .; when I found the name on the square it confounded me; I did hot know what to do; I thought I would take it to Bow, and send it to Mr. Wildsmith; I was never in trouble before.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 78.— Strongly recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury. — Confined Ten Days .
 1664. WILLIAM WINDLEY was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of April , 2 loaves of bread, value 9 d  ., the goods of John Macomie . JOHN MACOMIE. I had a wheelbarrow in the street—I went to a gen tleman's house—I left my bread in my barrow—when I returned I found two loaves gone. CHARLES PENNYPATHER. I saw the barrow at the gate—I saw the prisoner go away with two half-quartern loaves—I called him to stop—he would not.
   Prisoner.  The gentleman came to me; my brother was in doors; he said he stole the bread; my brother said he did not steal it. Witness. I did not charge the brother—there was another one with him, who also had a loaf—I could not swear to the other. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Months.
