NEW COURT .— Monday, November  27 th , 1843.
   Fifth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 96. MARY ANN M'HUGH was indicted for stealing 1 looking-glass, value 10 s  .; 3 blankets, 9 s  .; 1 poker, 1 s  .; 1 pair of tongs, 6 d  .; 1 shovel, 6 d  .; 4 sheets, 6 s  . 6 d  .; 4 pillow-cases, 2 s  .; 8 towels, 1 s  .; 2 flat irons, 1 s  .; 1 bolster, 5 s  .; 2 pillows, 6 s  .; and 10lbs. weight of feathers, 12 s  .; the goods of William Thompson ; and that she had been before convicted of felony. WILLIAM THOMPSON, cabinet-maker, Circus-street, Marylebone.  The pri soner lodged in my house for six weeks—on the 27th of October I went into the room, and missed a swing looking-glass, which was let to her with the room—I asked where it was—she said the plate was broken, and it was gone to have a new plate—I also missed some sheets, blankets, and the bed-tick was cut, and the feathers gone—I shut the prisoner in the room, and went for an officer—these are the things—they are mine—this is the glass—the plate is not broken. THOMAS AYRES, apprentice to Mr. Gideon, pawnbroker, Stockton-street, Lisson-grove.  I took this swing glass in pledge of the prisoner—I can swear to her—I have a blanket and two pillows, one of them pawned by the prisoner—I cannot swear who pawned the others. JOHN GRANGER( police-sergeant D  21.) On the morning of the 28th of Oct. I was fetched by the prosecutor from the station-house—I saw the pri soner there—I had some conversation with her; in consequence of which, she produced these eleven duplicates. THOMAS AYRES re-examined.  Here are the fellow-duplicates to the pro perty which I produce. ISAAC PLATTS, pawnbroker, Upper York-street, Bryamton-square.  I pro duce two sheets and a set of fire-irons—I do not know who pawned them—I gave two of the duplicates produced for them. GEORGE THORNTON( police-constable D  109.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person, and she had been convicted before that. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Transported for Seven Years .
 97. JOHN CASEY was indicted for stealing 9 cheroots, value 10 d  ., the goods of Thomas Wilson ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. MATTHEW HESWELL. I am in the service of Thomas Wilson, tobacconist, in Brook-street. On the 30th of Oct. I was in the parlour adjoining the shop, about a quarter to nine o'clock at night—I saw the prisoner come in, and, before I could get into the shop, he had the cigar-box lid off, and took these cigars out—I spoke to him—he made no reply, but knelt down and begged pardon, and said he would not do it again. STEPHEN TAYLOR( police-constable S  11.) I went to the shop, and took the prisoner—I saw the cheroots under his feet—he was crying, and begged to be let off. THOMAS DOWNING( police-constable E  47.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, from Mr. Clark's office—( ready )—the prisoner if the person. GUILTY. Aged 10.— Transported for Seven Years .—Parkhurst.
 98. WILLIAM PALMER was indicted for stealing 34 yards of satin, value 6 l  . 3 s  . 3 d  .; 1 boa, 5 l  .; and 2 pairs of cuffs, 10 s  .; the goods of Tho mas M'Dougal and others, his masters; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 23.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined Four Months .
 (The prisoner received a good character.)
