   HERBERT MAYO. On the 28th of April, about two o'clock, I was in Newgate-street; I felt my coat-pocket had been picked, and missed my handkerchief - I turned, and saw several persons; the prisoner was within one or two of me - I thought he appeared the likeliest person to have it; I watched him - he crossed the way, and then began to run; I followed, and caused him to be apprehended - I did not lose sight of him; when I came up to him my handkerchief lay at his feet - he had run about one hundred yards; I did not see where the handkerchief came from - he was about fifteen yards before me when the officer stopped him; none of the persons I saw near me had run with him.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. How many persons were within five yards of you? A. Eight or ten; I cannot say - he was the nearest; he began to run alone - I kept my eyes on him; I did not see the handkerchief drop from him - he did not walk some yards before he crossed the road.
   JOSEPH BATES. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner running, and heard Stop thief! called - I saw him take this handkerchief from his bosom with his left hand, and throw it over his shoulder; I was close to him - no persons were so near as Mr. Mayo and myself.
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you a regular officer? A. Yes; I belong to Farringdon-ward; the prisoner was ten or fifteen yards before me when I heard the alarm -I was sitting at my dinner at the corner of Warwick-square; he tried to knock one or two young men down; there was not a soul to be seen, but the mob who were following him - I took him in Warwick-lane, near Amencorner.
 MR. MAYO. I believe this to be my handkerchief - it is the same pattern.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is it not a common pattern? A. Yes; but I believe it to be mine; there is no difference between this and the one I lost.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 1236.  WILLIAM HAMPTON was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of May , 1480 sheets of printed paper, value 26s., the goods of George Eyre and others.
   RICHARD PARADISE. I am in the employ of George Eyre and Andrew Strachan, printer s. The prisoner came there to get some sheets for the binders - he came two or three times; on Saturday, the 15th of May, he brought a note from the bookbinders between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, and brought back a sheet which he had had the day before - he turned to go out, and said he would call again; as he was going out I heard a rustling at the warehouse door - I followed, and found this bundle of paper on his shoulder: I had seen it in the office that day - he had not got above twelve yards from our warehouse - our door opens into the passage; I made him bring it back - he said he had picked it up in the passage as he was going out; this is it - it is what we call waste: it is worth 1l. 6s.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did not he say he was bringing it back? A. No - I tapped him on the shoulder, and told him to bring it back; I had been in the room where the paper was, but did not see it taken - there was only another person in that office.
   EDWARD WYNNE. I am foreman to Messrs. Eyre and Strachan; Mr. Spottiswoode superintends the firm, as Mr. Eyre resides in the country, and Mr. Strachan is infirm -I left Paradise in care of the place in my absence - on my return I found the prisoner in custody.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is Mr. Eyre alive? A. He was a few days ago; I believe Mr. Spottiswoode is a partner -I never saw his name to any document; I have seen the names of George Eyre and  Andrew Strachan- I do not know whether Mr. Spottiswoode signs cheques or not; I am not in the counting-house.
   JOHN JAMES BERGE(one of the Jury.) I am a stationer, and deal with the prosecutors. We understand Mr. Spottiswoode is a partner, but I do not know that his name appears - I cannot say that he has ever signed his name.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do not you know that in the Acts of Parliaments the names of Eyre and Strachan only appear? A. Yes; I only speak from the information of others respecting Mr. Spottiswoode.
 RICHARD PARADISE re-examined. Q. Who gave the name of the firm? A. One of the clerks, by order of Mr. Mc Donald, the overseer.
   HENRY WILKINS. I live with Mr. Chip. We did not send this note.
 RICHARD PARADISE. He brought this note on Friday, and had a sheet - he brought it back again on Saturday, and said it was the wrong sheet.
   CHARLES EAGLETON. I took up the prisoner when I was sent for.
 JURY to  EDWARD WYNNE. Q. Suppose Mr. Spottiswoode signs orders, how does he do it? A. Eyre and Strachan - he never signs "Spottiswoode" for Eyre and Strachan.
 COURT. Q. Do the clerks sign the same way? A. Yes, always.
     NOT GUILTY.
