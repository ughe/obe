HENRY KEWELL. I am superintendent of the Tunbridge police on
Sunday morning, 17th October, the prisoner was brought to the police-
station on a warrant she was charged with manslaughter, killing and
slaying George Breeze-on 27th September she said "The landlord was
putting me out and I bit his leg, but I did not think it was going to kill
him."
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate._"I was going back to
the hotel after going to the station, when Mr. Breeze said You know
what you have done. I said that I was very sorry if I had done any.
thing to hurt. He said as long as I would apologise I need not think
any more about it.”
FREDERICK RANDALL. I am a constable of the Kent county constabu-
lary-I apprehended the prisoner on Sunday morning, 7th October, at
Tunbridge railway-station in a carriage-I asked her her name, she
gave the name of “Martin”-I told her I had a warrant for her in
that name—she first denied all knowledge of being known in Tunbridge.
and said that she did not know anything about the offence-I got into
the carriage and told her the charge, she again denied all knowledge of
it-I took her to the station, and she then said that she did bite the land-
lord, and he forgave at the time, and afterwards drew her half-a-pint of
rum in a ginger-beer bottle.
Prisoner's Defence. I had been drinking very heavily, and I did not
know anything that occurred till after I went back into the house again,
and then he said that I had bitten his leg-I asked him to draw me
half-a-pint of rum-he said "I suppose you know what you have done?”
-I said "No"-he told me, and I said that I was very sorry-he said
“So long as you apologise I shall not think any more about it."
GUILTY.** The officer stated that the prisoner had been taken in custody for
being drunk and riotous in 1876, on which occasion she bit the hand in two
places of an engine-driver who the constable called to his assistance, and also
assaulted the constable.--Six Months' Imprisonment.
