DAVID PHILLIPS. I am a watchman. On the afternoon of the 5th of September, I was at my door in Tufton-street. and saw the prisoner come round with the truck, and seventeen sacks, as he said; he offered them for sale for 5s. I told him, if he would go with me, I would take him to a person who would deal with him - I took him to the officer.
   BENJAMIN TIMBRELL. I am an officer. Phillips brought the prisoner to me, with the truck and sacks.
   THOMAS GILLEGAN. I am in the employ of James Holland. On the 5th of September I had been out, and collected these sacks. I left them at Pimlico for about twenty-five minutes, and they were gone.
 JAMES HOLLAND. I am a  salesman at Covent-garden-market. I sent Gillegan to collect these sacks.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had some beer at the King's Head, Public-house; some girls got on the truck, and I wheeled it down. I did not intend to sell the sacks - I meant to wheel them back.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. Transported for Seven Years.
 1807.  MARY HODGE was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of July , 7 gowns, value 30s.; 3 frocks, value 9s.; 5 caps, value 5s.; 1 shirt, value 3s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s.; 3 pinafores, value 2s.; 4 aprons, value 3s., and 2 shifts, value 1s., the goods of Patrick Hughes.
   MARY HUGHES. I am the wife of Patrick Hughes; we live in Union-place, Chelsea. On the 15th of July these articles were in our back parlour and front kitchen. I went out that evening at eight o'clock; I returned at twelve, and the watchman gave me information. I knew the prisoner, but had not seen her for four years.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   MARY HUGHES. I am the prosecutrix's daughter. The prisoner came to our house that evening, and sent me out three times for a young woman whom she knew. I did not go far the first time, but came back and said I did not see her. I went as far as the Star and Garter public-house the second time; and the third time, as I returned, I met the prisoner with a bundle in my mother's red handkerchief - this was a little after eight o'clock; she asked if I had seen the woman - I said Yes. I went home and missed the things, which were safe when I went out.
   JOHN CANNEY. On the 15th of July, I was in the back room of this house where I lodge; the prisoner came in and told a young man and I, who were writing a letter, to come into the front room, as we should be quiet - we did so, and in a few minutes we came into the front room. I saw her tying up a bundle - I knew nothing of her before; she brought the bundle, and put something on my box. I went up stairs, and as I came down, saw her going out - I followed and enquired for her.
   JANE ROBERTS. I am a publican. On the evening of Sunday, the 15th of July, the prisoner came and asked me to let her leave this bundle in my bar, which I did. I gave it to Allen.
   WILLIAM ALLEN. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner. I received the bundle.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I never saw the bundle.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 1808.  JAMES LEONY was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 1 looking-glass, value 40s., the goods of James Criswick.
 JAMES CRISWICK. I am a  carver and gilder, and live in Greek-street, Soho. On the 8th of August I went out, and as I returned, I saw the prisoner coming out of my shop with this glass - he dropped it at the door.
   WILLIAM CRAIG. I am an officer. I heard a cry of Stop thief! and secured the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   EDWARD HUDSON. I was with my master, and saw the prisoner come out with the glass. I am certain he is the man - I followed him.
 Prisoner's Defence. He met me and charged me with it, but I never had it.    GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Three Months.
 1809.  JAMES MATON, WILLIAM HUNT, THOMAS McCOY and THOMAS TOMLINSON were indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 2 silver-spoons, value 40s., the goods of Fanny Sanson.
   THOMAS KENWARD. I am servant to Mrs. Fanny Sanson, who lives at Hampstead. On the 9th of July, about half-past five o'clock in the afternoon, I saw Hunt in front of my mistress' house; another lad was with him - I asked what he wanted; he said, a drink of water; they went off directly, and did not shut the gate after them; they went down the road; I suspected something wrong - I went into the dining-room, where the cloth was laid, and from the corner of the table missed two spoons; the window was open; they could get in from the front of the house - I pursued immediately, and saw four boys together in a field - I caught Hunt, McCoy, and Maton - I found the spoons on Maton.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Does not Mr. Philip Sanson live in the house? A. Yes; he is Mrs. Sanson's son; it is her house.
   JOHN ADAMS. I am a constable. I received Hunt, Maton and McCoy in charge, with the spoons.
   JOHN BUSTARD. I apprehended Tomlinson the following day, from the information of one of the prisoners. I told him it was for a robbery at West End; he did not deny it - I took him at his mother's in Somers'-town - I said, "You were with the boys who were taken up at Mr. Sanson's;" he said he was.
 HUNT'S Defence. I took the spoons - I know nothing of the other boys.
 MATON -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 McCOY -    GUILTY. Aged 15. Confined Fourteen Days.
 HUNT - GUILTY. Aged 14.
     Judgment Respited.
 TOMLINSON -    NOT GUILTY.
