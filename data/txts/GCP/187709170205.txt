Cross-examined by MR. SERJBANT BALLANTINE. The appointment was
made with the prisoners--I do not know what has become of the other
ladies' letters-when I last saw them they were in the custody of the police.
Cross-examined by Mr. M. WILLIAMS. Our conversation was in French
--Golero had the management of a sinall line of coasting steamers, running
from Constantinople to Mudania on the sea of Marmora.
CHARLES BUTCHER (Detective Sergeant). Shrive and I were entrusted with
the execution of a warrant against the defendants-we attended at the
Café Monico, and the conversation being brought to an end, we took the
prisoners-Shrive found a number of pawn-tickets on Theodorides, and from
Golero I took this pocket book containing memoranda, amongst which were
these three receipts for rent and these three pawn-tickets. (The pawn-tickets
mere for a watch and chain, a suit of clothes, a pair of boots, and other
articles, pawned in New York in May and July.) I found these accounts on
Golero.
PHILLIP SHRIVE (Detective Officer C). I took Theodorides, and found on
him these pawn-tickets for jewellery, a coat, a vest, pin, and gold watch,
smounting to 151.; also other duplicates for table spoons, knives and forks,
for 16s., and a scarf pin, 68.
The following letter was translated: 18th July, 1877. From Paul Golero,
importer, to Miss Ernesta Slade: Miss,-Last Saturday upon the repeated
instances of Mr. Constantine Theodorides, I made it my duty to come and
see you personally about the sealed box in my possession which concerns
you. Although at the door I asked to see you, I was shown in to the Admiral,
and be said he could bear all concerning his niece. I have no doubt your
uncle communicated in detail the interview I had with him in reference to
the sealed box. Certainly I was ignorant of the contents, or else I should
have understood of what importance were the documents it contained, and
insisted on seeing only you and alone. A correspondence similar to that
between you and Mr. Theodorides is of too great importance to allow any in-
terference of third people. It is very astonishing, Miss, in spite of my great
delicacy towards Mr. Theodorides and yourself, you let pass the date for the
opening of the box without occupying yourself to take it off my hands. I
have read your letters in full, and I assure you I admire your indifference.
I expected to find some bonds or valuable objects. I have only found some
letters and other papers without value. However, I am more than satisfied.
I can now for the first time sleep soundly about my debt, and I shall know
how to get back my debt in making use of your letters. If you have a good
memory you must know what is in them, and who had such a correspondence
with you. I shall act against whoever it may be to get my debt of 6501.
Theodorides cannot pay, I know. Therefore I trust to you before address-
ing myself to third people. I regret that there should have been no witness
at the opening of the box. In case, Miss, I am not paid by the 20th, I
Bball commence more efficacious measures. Perhaps I shall send some
copies to your uncle, the admiral, and save the honour of the family before
publication. I shall see what I have to do, being complete master, and
nothing shall stop me. Take notice of what I say."
Miss SLADE (re-eramined). The names on this card found in Theodo-
rides' pocket-book) are those of friends and of two relations of mine-they are
in Theodorides' writing.
GUILTY.
