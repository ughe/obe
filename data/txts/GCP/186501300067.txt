WILLIAM DOUGLAS LAMB. I am a shipbroker in the City of London-I
produce the original charter-party of the Snowdrop-I chartered her in my
professional capacity--I have known Captain White as far back as I can
remember ; his character is first-class-The charter party was dated 1st Sep-
tember, 1864, by which it was agreed that the ship was to sail to Cronstadt in
ballast, and there load a complete cargo in grain or seed, to be taken at the
merchant's risk and expense at a freight of 38. per quarter, and proceed to
London, or some safe port on the east coast of Great Britain.

WILLIAM WHITE. I reside and carry on business at Newcastle-upon-Tyne
-in shipping I am a partner with other gentlemen ; in my business, as a
shipbroker, I am on my own account-I was part owner of the Snowdrop;
I had one-third of her; Mr. Robert Rowell and Mr. Johnson, who are
here, had the other two-thirds—we purchased the vessel in June, 1863, in
London-she cost 2,1257. in London, and we then coppered her at Shields
which cost 4201. more, making her first cost 2,6001., with the expense of
taking her down from here-after repairing her I appoiuted my brother, the
defendant, to the command-this produced) is the contract for the pur-
chase of the ship she first traded to the Mediterranean and Cuba--we
only had her two voyages; this was her second voyage—she left London in
September for Cronstadt, in ballast, ta bring home grain, under the charter-
party that has been produced--we insured the vessel for 2,0001.—we were
our own underwriters for 5001.-we had the option of doing 5001. more
with the same underwriters in Newcastle; they said they would do the
2,0001. at 6 guiners per cent, but if they did 2,5001. they would charge-8
guineas per cents, and we preferred to run the risk ourselves-I am not
speaking of the freight, but of the ship-the freight was not insured-I have
kuown my brother all his life; he has always borne the very highest
character for honour and integrity.
