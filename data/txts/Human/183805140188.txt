   Q.  What other servants were in the house besides you? A.  Henry Baguley and Jane Burn-nobody else—there was no other female service in the house at the time—I am quite sure of that; nor any male beside Henry—they paid my fare up and down to London, and something else—I do not know how much exactly—I think I received five pounds—Mr. Murray paid it to me in London—he did not tell me whose money it was-Jane Burn did not come to London.
   MR. KELLY . Q.  Did you first give notice of your intention of going away from Mr. Mason's before you came to London or after; try and re collect? A.  I gave notice before I came up to London, about a fort night before—I had no idea at that time that I was going to be sent for as a witness to London—I first heard I was wanted in London on a Friday and I left Newcastle to come to London on the Monday—I returned on the Saturday—the five pounds were given me for coming up to London, in cluding coach-hire and expense—it was to pay the coach-hire and expenses-Newcastle is one hundred and fifty miles from London-Jane Burn is not constantly in the house—she is occasionally there.
   COURT . Q.  Did you receive five pounds, or did you pay your coach-hire and expenses out of it? A.  I paid my coach-hire out of the five pounds. PHILLIP PETTIT. I am servant to Mr. Miller. I went down with him to the Newcastle election—I remember Saturday, the day of the nomina tion—I attended at dinner, at the Castle, that day—I remember my master Mr. De Horsey, and some others, going out soon after dinner—I think it was about twenty minutes past six o'clock—I went into the large room after my master left—it is called the speaking-room—I was there about seven o'clock, and remained there till half-past ten o'clock—there were no speeches made out of the windows of that room, or from that room, from seven o'clock till half-past ten o'clock that night—I expected my master back when he left, but he did not return—I know Thomas Baddeley—I saw him there that night, standing on the stairs.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Why did yon expect your mas ter back that evening? A.  Because he had not had tea, and I expected him back to take tea there, as he had dined there—he did not tell me he should come back, but I thought it my duty to stop—I am an in-door servant—I act as valet, or footman—I do not wear a livery—I did not go about with my master canvassing—I have gone out occasionally, but not to have any interest in it—I have heard him ask the poor voters for their votes.
