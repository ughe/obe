664. WILLIAM PAINE, burglariously breaking and entering the
dwelling house of William Purcell, and stealing 3 waistcoats and other goods,
value 16. 78. the goods of John Hammond.
Mr. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM HAMMOND. I am the son of John Hammond, and am in his employ.
He did keep a shop at No. 170, High-street, Shadwell, on 30th of May-
nobody slept in that shop; it adjoins a house kept by Mr. Joseph Pureell, you
can go from Mr. Purcell's shop to ours-] fastened the door of my father's
shop at half past 10 o'clock, on the night of 30th May-we were to get into
the shop in the morning by going through Mr. Purcell's-] have been shown
some waistcoats and other articles by the policeman, they are my father's
property; they are not in the same state as when I left them-I left them
in a wrapper, and at the back of the shop, when I saw them again they were
in a handkerchief- do not know the prisoner.
Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q. I suppose there is another door to
this shop A. Yes I fastened that door I drew it to after me, and
shut it with a lock and latch-I am sure I shut it.
ANN LEARY. I am in the service of Mr. Joseph Purcell, of High-street,
Shadwell-he slept there, and lived there on 30th May-the lower part of the
house is divided into two shops, Mr. Purcell occupied one, the other was
Mr. Hammond's clothes shop, I was there on 30th May-about half past 10
o'clock at night I thought I beard a noise, I went and opened the door
of my master's shop expecting my master or mistress- was just by the
door, and I saw this boy (the prisoner) come out of the next shop and run
away- hallooed out, Robbers, Robbers!"-some Robbers men were standing by
and they ran after him-I went to the station afterwards and saw this boy
there-I know him again-before I went to the station I went to Mr.
Hammond's shop, and the clothes were tossed about the shop, and some were
in a bundle, tied up-that bundle is here-the things were down on the floor
-there is a rail for hanging clothes on, and that was lying inside the door.
Cross-eramined. Q. How do you get into Mr. Hammond's shop A.
Through our shop-I tried the door to see whether it was fast-when the
man ran out he closed the door, and the rail which the clothes used to hang
on fell down-the door of Mr. Hammond's shop is close by my master's shop
door there is only just a partition between them-the man closed the door,
and ran away immediately- had no light in my and-there is a lamp at
the corner, three or four houses off-] had never seen this man before, to my
recollection-whe I saw him again he was alone-they told me they had
caught the man-I did not leave my master's to go after him-I stood a
little way from the door.
