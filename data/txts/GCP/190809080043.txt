(Defence.)
CHARLES HOGSDON (prisoner, on oath). On August 24 I was not in
the "Enterprise." I went to the "Horn and Chequers " with Cooklin.
After we arrived Saunders and Sullivan and two others came in.
Saunders, who was not sobor, started skylarking about; he and Bulli-
van were wrestling. Saunders's hat fell off ; Cocklin picked it up
and handed it to Saunders, who took hold of Cocklin's wrist and
started wrestling with him. The Governor (Cecil) came and stopped
it. It is untrue that I “rubbed down." Saunders; I never touched
him. I did not know whether he had any money or not; in fact,
somebody asked him to stand treat and he said he had no money.
Later he and Sullivan left the bar. It is not true that I and Cooklin
winked at each other. We left the house just after Saunders and
Sullivan and went down Thames Place. We were standing together
when Saunders came up and said to Cocklin, "Give us your hand.
Shall I show you a new grip?" and they started wrestling. Then
Bullivan came up and said, "It's nonsense for you to talk about
wrestling." I started laughing. He said, "Who are you laughing
w" and he gave me a blow in the mouth. I then got to close
quarters with him and as I struck him he fell down. After that me!
and Cocklin picked him up and set him against the wall. Cooklin
said to me, "We will go away, or there will be trouble." With that
We walked away. I never struck Saunders; never put a finger on
him.
