Witnesses for the Defence.
JOSEPH MILLER. I am an apprentice to Benjamin Cooke, of Salisbury.
court-I know the prosecutor I saw him playing with the prisoner on the
day of the accident-it was a Wednesday to the best of my recollection—they
were heaving things at one another, and running after one another in play
-I saw Stokes take that lad up and put him into a tub, in play-that was
after 2 o'clock, after dinner—they were playing together for an hour or an
hour and a half-they were heaving fine stuff at one another, the same stuff
that went into Stokes' eye, only it was harder I did not say anything about
it at the time it made their clothes in & grent mess, and Stokes was very
particular not to get any white on his clothes-this was in the shop after
that they went down stairs and brushed one another down, and Stokes
washed himself-after that I was in the kitchen, and Stokes was looking up
his tools-he said, "I wonder what Ted has stolen," or "prigged 1” do
not know which was the word, and then Edwin took up one of his chisels
and pretended to put it into the fire, but did not : that was in play I should
think, and Stokes took up his screw-driver and bit bim across the back as
hard as he could—the lad ran up stairs, and Stokes was going up stairs with
his tools when Stokes hit him he said, "Well, Ted, I did not mean to hit
you so hard as that ;" and I said, "If it had been in earnest, I should have
hit yon," but of course it was in play--the lad did not say before he went
up stairs, “ You b b , I will do something for you now," nor any
