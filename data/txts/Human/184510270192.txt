Decidedly—if it had been entered 5 l . 7 s ., I should not have discovered it—I should have taken it for granted that the money had been paid. SIMON BENNETT. I am the prosecutor's son—he is on the Continent—in consequence of what I heard from Harris, I had some conversation with the prisoner—I asked him what he had received from Mr. Hay—he said, "A cheque for 11 l . 5 s ."—I said "How much did you give my mother?" he said 4 l . 7 s ., and he gave Mr. Hay's man 5 l . 18 s .—I said, "How much do you make that?"—he said, "11 l . 5 s ."—I said, "How do you make that out? it is only 10 l . 5 s ."—he did not know what to say—he seemed very much confused, and did not seem to know how to account for the other 1 l .—his father was going by—I called him in and told him of it—he said, "I am very sorry, Mr. Bennett, but I am not surprised at it; he is a very bad boy; I could have got him into the Post Office"—the prisoner said he did not know how to account for the 1 l .; he must have lost it, and had reckoned it up wrong—I said I did not know what to do in it myself, as my father was on the Continent, and I was not in the business—I consulted with Mr. Harris, and we sent the prisoner away—next morning he entered an action against us—I believe the action was not brought by the prisoner's present attorney—I do not know. JAMES LEWIS( police-constable L  36). I apprehended the prisoner on the 11th of Aug.—he was given into custody by Harris—he said it was no use, he intended to bring an action against him. On the following day, in going to the police-office, he was met by an acquaintance of his own, who asked him what he had been doing, that he was locked up—he said Bennett's people had locked him up last night—he told him to go and get a solicitor—when he got a little farther, he met another person, who asked him what he was locked up for—he said he had made a mistake in a sovereign, and Bennett's people had locked him up. NOT GUILTY.
