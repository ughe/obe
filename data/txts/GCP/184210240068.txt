ing an acquittance and receipt for 6l. 98. 5d., with intent to defrand
Joseph Fowler and others.
MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
JOSEPH FOWLER. I am a labourer. I am a member of a society called
the Rechabites-there are other members-in March last I was stewand
of the Rose of Temperance Tent-what other societies call a "Lodret
we call a "Tent"-the money is the money of all the members jointly
there is a district of the society called the “London District," of which
gentleman named Russell was secretary last March-the prisoner applied
to me in March respecting a sum of 61. 16s. 11d., which it was necessary
to pay Mr. Russell from the funds of the club-I have a book in which
entry was made of it in the prisoner's writing-(reads)-"District retung
61. 168. 11d., 30th March."-At the time that entry was made I handed
him over that money with other money-it was his duty subsequently to
produce a receipt for the money, if he paid it-he handed me this receipt for
61. 9s. 5d. at one of our meetings- I cannot say exactly the date-he gave it
me as the receipt for that money-he did not state whether he had paid the
money-he only produced the receipt-I did not observe the sum at the
time, but, on looking over the book, I saw that it was 61. 168. 11d., and
asked why he had not produced a receipt for the whole of the sum-be
said he must have another receipt at home, for goods, or something, to a
count for the difference-I asked why he wanted two receipts for the por
ment of one sum of money-he could not account for it-he said it must
be for goods, or something.
