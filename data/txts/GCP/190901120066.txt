I have bought many hundreds of horns similar to those produced,
The price per pair is 5s. or 59. 6d. for the largest, and for the second
size 3s. 6d. to 4s. I buy them of Mr. Myers and Mr. Wilson. I
have never been able to obtain them for 1s, or is, 6d. per pair. The
smallest size we get as low as Is. 6d. I should charge £2 10s. to
the trade for a set finished and mounted, but, of coure, more to
private customer. I myself pay 7s. 11d. a yard for caracul.
obtained a diploma at the Crafts' Exhibition at the People's Palace.
I sold some horn hat pegs to the Duke of Fife at the Exhibition.
Cross-examined. The Duke never came came back to me for a
second set. They were ordinary horns of the smaller kind mounted
on mahogany.
EDWIN ERNEST ELLIOTT, 122, High Street, Walthamstow, and

NATHANIEL NATHAN (prisoner, on cath). I first saw Mrs. Mason
when I was put up for identification after being discharged at Enfield.
It is a case of mistaken identity. On May 12 I was in the billiard.
room of the "Black Bay,” Mile End Road, at the time I was stated
to have called on Mrs. Mason waiting for my governor to arrange
about going to Chester for the "Cuprace next day. His name is
Falk. I met him soon after twelve o'clock. It was arranged that
we should not go. Business, he said, was none too good and the
expenses were very great. The people amongst whom I was placed
for identification at Enfield were all of the labouring class. I have
never worn a beard in my life. I shave every other day. When I
was arrested at Enfield I had been with Harris barely three weeks.
I did not find selling horns good business. Harris asked me to go
with him, as I was not doing anything. I was to receive 5s. & day.
Harris told me what to say. I did not think there was any fraud in
what he was doing or I should not have gone out. I admit that I
said to Mrs. Saint that a Jew had offered Harris £11 for the horns,
which Harris would not take, because a Jew had once taken him in
over & watch.
Cross-examined. I was in Harris's employ about four months. I
have been to Ohester once, about three years ago. I have been to
other races with Falk. I used to use the "Black Horse." Falk
could find me there if he wanted to. I have never heard of a case
of this sort before. When Harris engaged me he told me I was so
wait outside the shop till he came out. Then I was to go in and
say he had been offered £10 for them and two suits of clothes. In
any shop I went into I was to say the lady was
Christian lady. I always said the lady in the shop was a Christian
lady. The first time we went out was to Bayswater, where we sold
one pair of horns for £4. We sold four sets altogether, including the
set sold to Mrs. Catling. Harris spoke with a fcreign accent. I
remember that on one ocoasion he said “My heart is brake; I do
not find my friend." I have seen him cry. I admit that I helped him
to get the money. If I had known the full consequences I should not
have done it. As to whether it was right to tell a lie to get money,
it is often done.
