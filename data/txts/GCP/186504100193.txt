Thomas Dowsey. I am an engine-driver, in the employment of the South
Eastern Railway Company-on Friday, 16th December, I was the driver of
a ballast-train from Dartford, consisting of an engine and tender, twenty-two
truoks, filled with gravel, and two break-carriages, one in front of the train
and the other in the rear-it started about five minutes past 3 in the after-
noon-it was an ordinary engine-I had no exact time to keep at the stations
down the line, but had to work in between the other trains, and, if necessary,
at some of the stations to go on to the sidings, so as to get out of the way
of the other trains-eight of the trucks were unloaded at Abbey-wood-we
took vine fresh trucks on between Dartford and Erith, before taking the
others off-we got to Charlton Station about 4 o'clock, and were stopped
there by a signal, and detained about a quarter of an hour the ballast
guard and the signal man at the station gave me the signal to start a man
named Lancaster was the guard of the train; I received my orders from him,
and he received the order from the signal man-I started and went into the
tunnel at that time I had twenty-three loaded trucks--seven or eight
plate-layers were riding in the brenk-carriage at the end of the train Jobn
Currie was one of them the tunnel is nearly a mile long, and is on an
incline-we went about three parts of the way through the wheels slipped
pretty well all through the tunnel - the greasy state of the rails was the
only cause of that-I signalled to have the breaks put on, to prevent the
train from slipping back-we had put some sand on the rails to try to get
along, but notwithstanding that the train stopped quite still, and we were
not able to get along, though the engine was powerful enough-Lancaster
then got down, but he had no time to get to the rear of the train before I
felt a shock--we were then on the curve-I knew that something bad come
into us behind, but could not tell what it was after that some one divided
the train, and we went on with twelve of the trucks to Blackheath for assist-
