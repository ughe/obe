MARY ANN WHITE. I live in Mount-row, Westminster-road. On the night in question, between ten and eleven o'clock, I was standing at the coal-shed, next door but one to Daley's house, and next door to Shears's—I saw the prisoner standing at Shears's door—I saw Godfrey come down from the end of the place, go up to Daley, and strike him in the face—after that I saw two or three men rush up, but what occurred afterwards I can not say—a mob collected, but I did not go from where I was standing—Daley did not strike Godfrey at all, before Godfrey and the other men struck him. EDWARD SKELTON. I am apprentice to Mr. Shears, a silk hatter. On this Saturday night, about half-past ten o'clock, I saw Daley and Godfrey struggling together, about six yards from my master's door—there were no others there at that time, but two or three minutes afterwards, two or three came from the corner, and a general struggle ensued—the confusion was so great that is all I saw. ISAAC HARWOOD. I am a printer. I did not see any thing of this affair—this is a printer's mallet—I saw a quarrel between Daley and God frey the week previous. FRANCES HARWOOD. I and my husband lodge in the prisoner's house. On the Saturday night this occurred I was at the shop between ten and eleven o'clock—I was coming out of Daley's shop-door, and saw Daley standing a few doors off, and Godfrey at the corner of the court—all of a sudden, Godfrey rushed towards Daley, apparently in a very great pas sion' and used the words, "You Irish b—, I will serve you out"—he then rushed on him, and hit him violently with both his hands—two or three other men then came up, and fell on him also—I screamed "Mur der," being very much frightened, and in about five minutes the police came up—he was then taken to the station-house, and I went with him, at his wife's request, seeing the whole occurrence—I did not see Daley strike Godfrey at all. GUILTY  of an Assault. Aged 31.— Recommended to mercy. Confined Two Months.

 1992. WILLIAM DUNN was indicted for feloniously killing and slay ing Ann Dunn . RICHARD THOMAS RUSSELL. I am a surgeon. On Saturday the 8th of June, I was called in to attend Ann Dunn, about two o'clock in the afternoon, she complained of a pain in her head—I did not examine her head at that time—I found her tongue was foul, and the state of the bowels confined—I sent her some medicine, and at one o'clock on Sunday morning I was sent for again—I found her in a state of insensibi lity, and I applied leeches to the temple—I saw her again about ten o'clock in the morning—she still remained insensible—she died on Thurs day, the 13th of June—she was insensible till the Wednesday afternoon—
