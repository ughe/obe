JOSEPH REECE ADAMS. I am a clerk at the Bank of England. I re.
member these four 1001. notes being brought to my office, which is the issue
department, on the 29th of Dec., before twelve o'clock in the day—I am not
certain of the time—I received them from, I think, the prisoner Kerp, but
am not quite certain—the name of Helen, No. 3, Bond-street, is written
on the back of the notes—we require the name and address to be•put on
the notes before we pay them—it was brought to me with that name and
address on it—I could not read the address very well, and enquired of the
man what it was—he said No. 3, Bond-street, and r wrote that again—the
notes were brought for gold, and before I paid them I took them to Mr.
Higman, who left the omee, at my suggestion, to go to Fuller's, as I had sus-
picion, and asked the man where he received the notes—he said at Fuller's
—bere is the name of Jones on one of the 501. notes—I cashed that note at
a different time, within five minutes before or after—it is No. 10826, 8th
Oct. 1846, N. B.—it was probably changed for the same man, but I took no
notice of the person then, the note being a small one—it might be the same
man, or another—the N. B. denotes the date—if the lower part of the note is
destroyed this would tell us the date—there is L. C. on the 1001. notes.
WILLIAM HIGNAN. I am principal in the issue department of the Bank.
On the 29th of Dec., between ten and half-past ten o'clock in the morning,
Mr. Adams brought these four 1001. notes to me, as it is not usual to be
asked for gold for so large an amount—I made inquiry of the man, and
noticed his person, and am perfectly certain in my own mind it Was Kerp—
he is the man; I have not a doubt about it—I asked him from whom he
obtained the notes—he said from Messrs. Fuller's—l stepped out at the back
door of the Bank while the matter was being arranged—I went to Fuller's,
and understanding all was right I ordered payment to be made.
