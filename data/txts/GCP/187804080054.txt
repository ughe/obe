440. JOHN CHAPMAN (26), Stealing a purse, a piece of paper, and
38. 5 d. the property of William Cox, from the person of Miriam Cox.
MR. HUMPHREYS conducted the Prosecution.
MIRIAM Cox. I am the wife of William Cox, an omnibus conductor
-on the afternoon of the 1st of April I was in Gracechurch Street carry.
ing a bag- I had a purse in my jacket containing 38. 53d. and a dupli-
cate-the prisoner came and put his hand in my pocket, took my
purse, and ran across the road-I told a gentleman, who ran after him
the prisoner ran into a constable's arms-I charged him.
WILLIAM JOSEPH HINES (City Policeman 743). On the afternoon of
1st April I was on duty in Bell Yard, and saw the prisoner running and
a gentleman shouting "Stop thief”-I followed, overtook the prisoner.
and took him to the station-I saw him throw this purse away-I found
7 d. on him.
GUILTY. ** He also PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of Felony in Feb-
ruary 1871 at this Court in the name of William Adkins.-Soven Years'
Penal Servitude.
441. EDWIN BURROWS (37), Feloniously forging and uttering &
request for the payment of 31. 108., with intent to defraud.
MR. HUMPHREYS conducted the Prosecution.
ROBERT JOHN HAYES. I am a publican of 8, Leman Street-on 23rd
March the prisoner came to my house a little after 11 a.m., and said that
he had been there the day before and his cousin had left 31. 108. with my
wife-I said "You know nothing about it, it was not 31. 108."-he said
" How much was it?"-I said "I will not give you any satisfaction, I
will not tell you what it was"-he said “I am going to see my cousin at
the Home and I will return"-he returned in about a quarter of an hou
and said "I have been down and found my cousin has been locked up fo:
being drunk, and he has given me this note for you; I have seen the Polics
Inspector and everything is all right”-I said "I should like to make in-
quiries to know whether it is all right or not, I will get you to sign you
name and your cousin's on this piece of paper"-he did so and I sent fa
a policeman-we walked to the station and saw the sergeant on duty, wh)
made inquiries—the seaman said he had never given any authority to
any one to receive the money.
MARY HAYES. I am the wife of the last witness-on 22nd March the
prisoner came to our house with a sailor who left 31. 108. in gold and
à 107. note from the Sailors' Home, for me to take care of-I saw the
prisoner sign this paper : “Please to let my cousin have the money
that I, William Stileman, left last night. Edwin Burrows. Willian
Stileman."
GEORGE LIGHTMAN. I am a sailor living at the Sailors' Home-on 22nd

had a couple of glasses with the prisoner-I had not seen him before-I
never sent him for the money nor authorised him to sign his name-he
is not my cousin.
