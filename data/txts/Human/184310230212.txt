   Q.  You understood from Mr. Bridgman's conversation that he had been to the chapel and heard the sermon? A.  No; we met at the door—I cannot tell what it was we said about the sermon, but I know we were speaking about Mr. Daniels taking up the subject—I can tell you the text, or nearly so; it was the promise of Jacob about going down to Egypt, "I will go with thee into Egypt, and be sure to bring you back," or something of that sort, to the best of my recollection, I forget exactly, I am not certain of the chap ter and verse; I think it was in Genesis, but I will not say positively—we conversed together for a quarter of an hour—nobody else was with us—we walked down the town towards where he was lodging, which, I think, was on some terrace, but I really do not know, I did not ask him—I did not see him on the following day—I went to Margate to meet ray friend, who I was to have met there on the Tuesday—I staid with my brother's family that night, and went away next morning—I came out of chapel with my brother's wife—I do not recollect that any one else of my brother's family was there—my brother's wife was at the chapel when I got down there, and I followed her—I did not take her home; I left her, and joined Mr. Bridgman—I do not know whether she saw me join Mr. Bridgratn or not, because I came out behind her—I was not with her in the chapel—I went into another pew where they put me—I did not know where to find her—I was never there before—I went to the chapel to hear a sermon—I knew my brother's wife was at the chapel, but not where to find her—the chapel was only three or four doors from where I was lodging—my attention was not called to this case for a week after I came back—I came back on the Monday after the 6th—I have not been asked about being in Ramsgate on the 6th of Sept. till I came here—I might have made a statement in consequence of inquiries about the 6th of Sept., but I do not know when, and I do not know whether I have been asked about it by anybody—I offered to come forward for Mr. Bridgman when I heard of it—I went and told Mr. Bridgman that I was down there on that very same day—that might have been a fortnight or three weeks ago, since there has been an inquiry at Union-hall—I went and asked Mr. Bridgman whether he could recollect that he was down there with me, and if he could recollect the day of the month—he said he could not exactly go into the particulars—I told him it was the 6th, and I understood, from inquiries at Union-hall, that it was the 6th it was com mitted, and I said, "I was with you on the 6th till nearly nine o'clock, and I will come forward and state it anywhere"—I did not attend at Union-hall at first—the last time I was there—I have never been examined by anybody as to the evidence I was to give, and I never would be—I went to Union-hall to hear the case, and if Mr. Bridgman, as I expected he would do, wanted evi dence to prove he was away from home, I intended giving it—I never spoke to any solicitor, or anybody else—I never was at Ramsgate but once before, and I think that is about ten years ago—I went this time to see my brother's family—my brother is a coach-builder, in Morton-street, Cavendish-square—before I was a miller I was a farmer, which I left twenty years ago—I am a housekeeper—I have known Mr. Bridgman from ten to fourteen years.
