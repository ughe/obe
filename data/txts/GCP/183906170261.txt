HENRY NORTON. I am in the 22nd company of the Royal Marines.
On the 6th of June last year, I went to Eltham to see my father and
mother-I came, in my way home, down Wellhall-lane, about twenty
minutes or half-past ten o'clock-I was alone-I met Evans, a postman,
as soon as I got outside my father's house, but met nobody else till I got
into the lane-I met there two soldiers- the prisoner is one
I do not know who the other was-they had round red jackets on, black
trowsers, and side belts, which is the regimental dress-1 pointed out to
the constable the place where I met them in the lane-they were coming
from Woolwich towards Eltham-I was going towards Woolwich-I said,
"Holloa Perceval, where are you going this time of night?"-he said, "I
am going to Footscray, will you come !"-I said, "I have no pass for
Footscray, I am going to barracks," and I wished him good night-the other
man said nothing-I have known Perceval three or four months-he is not
in the same company as me, but in the same barracks, and I have mounted
guard with him.
Q. Before you met these two men, in going down the lane did you see
Mr. Watson either walking or lying down on the road ? A. No, I did not
-after I parted from them I met Leopard, and bid him good night-I
knew him before, and he me I then went straight to the barracks- I was
out with permission that day I had a pass--I was to return by six o'clock
next morning-I was spoken to that night at the barracks after I was in
bed about the robbery, and I stated what I knew of it-I believe inquiry
was made for Perceval afterwards in the barracks—I was taken into custody,
and taken before the Magistrate, and examined as a witness in Perceval's
presence I have been abroad lately on duty-I was abroad last December
when Baker was tried-I returned about a month ago I do not know at
what time Perceval came into the barracks on the following day.
Cross-examined. Q. How soon after this night did you go abroad? A.
I cannot tell exactly-about two months- I was present before the Magis.
trate when Baker was there I never heard Baker charge me with being
implicated in the robbery-he never did so in my presence at any time I
was taken up the same night-I was in custody about an hour and a half
-I was sober that night I could see very well on a moonlight night-I
saw Evans and Leopard - I did not observe any gap in the hedge in any
part of the lane-I swear I saw nobody lying in the road.
MR. BODkin. Q. At what pace were you walking, slow or fast! A. I
was not walking very fast, at a moderate pace-my clothes were searched
at the barracks the same night, when I was charged..

of the 7th, or early on the morning of the 8th-he was absent a day longer
than his leave permitted him-Baker was absent without leave on the
