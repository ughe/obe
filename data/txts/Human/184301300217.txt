   Q.  Had you at that time the slightest intention of saying one single word about your suspicions, if you had been paid the amount of your bill? A.  Certainly I had—I should not have moved in the case, paid or not, unless I bad been subpœnaed or summoned—I cannot swear that I did not mention the case to anybody after writing this letter—I may have mentioned the case, but I should not have forwarded the case unless subpœnaed or summoned—I should not have come forward in the matter at all—I was acquainted with Miss Constable as long as I knew her father—I certainly thought that a deed of this kind would materially affect her interests—I certainly did not mean to do anything about it unless summoned or subpœnaed—I should have kept perfectly quiet up to the present moment, unless I had been summoned or subpœnaed—I did not, on the prisoner not paying my bill, go and make a communication without being summoned or subpœnaed—I might have mentioned it—I cannot tell to whom—I went to Mr. Hale before I wrote that letter—I did not make any communication after Writing that letter, till one of Mr. Kirk's clerks called on me for my information—I cannot tell how he came to do that—I think I had not made any communica tions previously—I was first summoned to Union-hall—I cannot say whether I had made any communication to anybody previous to that—I might have talked of it, but it must have been in the course of conversation, not to forward any criminal proceedings—my bill has not been paid—Miss Constable has not promised to pay it—I have not applied to her for pay ment since this affair—I did not apply to her for the picture—she left it at a house for me—I have had no communication about the payment of my bill since, with anybody—I made no further application after receiving no answer to my letter—I let the thing rest until I was summoned to Union-hall.
   MR. CLARAKSON . Q.  How long have you been practising as a doctor of medicine? A.  Six months—I was admitted as a member of the College of Physicians last June—before that I was a member of the College of Surgeons, and of the Apothecaries' Company as well—I have been practising in London nine or ten years—the payment or non-payment of the 30 l . would not have altered the evidence I have to give—Mr. Constable had made me a present of the picture about eighteen months before his death—I had failed to re move it from his house during the time he was at Lewisham—Mr. Stanley had often promised to bring it to town, but he never did—after his death I applied to the prisoner, as Stanley's representative—about two months ago Miss Constable sent it to a place for me, from which I removed it WILLIAM LISTER. I am a cloth-factor, and live in Basinghall-street, City.
