   RICHARD KIRKMAN LANE. I live in Argyle-street, and am a solicitor. I saw my clock safe in my clerk's office about six o'clock in the afternoon of the 27th of November - I went out, and missed it on my return at eight; I saw it at Marlborough-street-office lately, and was satisfied it was mine - I had had it about two years; I believe it to be worth 5l.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. What parish is your house in?  A. St. James ', Westminster - I do not know the direct way of describing the parish.
   MATILDA MOORE. I am servant to Mr. Lane. On the 27th of November, at nearer eight then seven o'clock, there was a knock at the door; I opened it, and saw a tall man, who asked if Mr. Lane was at home; I said he was not, and asked if he would leave his name, or any message, and I would deliver it as soon as he came in - he said there was no occasion for that, if I would allow him to write a letter in the office; I said Certainly - he went into the office; the clock then stood on the mantel-piece; I gave him half a sheet of paper, which he asked for; he then asked if I would allow him to bring a friend in; I said Certainly - he went and let his friend in, and left him on the mat in the passage; the tall man walked into the passage, and I also - I asked his friend to walk into the office, but he made no answer; I asked him again; he walked in - I shut the office door too, and went to acquaint mistress that such persons were there - my mistress had just come down stairs, and was in the back office; I merely told her; she said "Very well" - I shut mistress' door, went into the back hall, and was going down stairs, when mistress told me the gentlemen were gone; I then went into the passage, found the street door open, and looked into the office - they were gone, and the clock also - I saw Ellis at the office on the 7th of this month; he is the man; he is a very tall man - I saw him at the office, and know him to be one of them; I cannot be mistaken about him - I do not recollect the person of the other man who was with him, but am positive it was not Gast; it was a shorter, stouter man, dressed like a baker - I was as certain of Ellis at the office as I am now; I saw the clock there, and was sure of it; I went into the office in a short time in the course of that evening, and found master's hat was taken off the table -I have not seen that since; Ellis had left his own hat behind; I am sure it was his, because the other man's was all over flour.
 Cross-examined. Q. How many times were you before the Justice on this business? A. Three times - Ellis was present on the first occasion; I did not state then that I did not know Ellis - I was asked three times, once by a person near, whether I could identify both the prisoners; I said only the tall one - the person said, "I think you can, look at them again;" I said; "No, only the tall one;" it was my master's clerk who said this - I did not hesitate at identifying Ellis.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I am an officer of Marlborough-street. I had been to Gast's house, and went into his bedroom with him, to clean himself, and saw a clock standing on the drawers, on Saturday, the 5th of February - Ellis was at that time in custody on other charges; I said nothing to Gast at this time about the clock - I went with Goddard to Tottenham-court-road, leaving Schofield in care of Gast: when we returned he was in the parlour - he keeps a muffin shop in Hanway-street; I took him up stairs, and requested him to unlock the door, which was locked, and I believe, the key was in it - he unlocked it; I went, laid my hand on the clock, which was on the drawers, and said, "Now, this clock, whose is it?" he said that clock was his wife's before he married her - he repeated that two or three times over; I said, "Well at all events I shall take it, and you must account for it before the Magistrate;" the clock was identified by Mr. Lane and Moore.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was any body present when you had the conversation with Ellis about the clock? A. No - Schofield was down stairs; I saw Gast's wife - he keeps the muffin-shop; it is a small shop, but does a very great business.
   BENJAMIN SCHOFIELD. I am an officer. I went to Gast's house, after Ellis was taken, with Ballard and Goddard - I went into the first floor room on the left-hand; it is a bed-room - I saw the clock; I did not see Gast in that room - when Gast was in custody (after Ballard had left the house) he came to me in a side room, where I was waiting to prevent his going out; I then said it was a bad job, he having so respectable a business - I named several things which had been removed from his bed-room by Goddard; I named the clock slightly, but we had not removed that - he merely stated that he knew nothing that was brought into the house, it was all his wife's friends, and his wife's sister had brought the property in; I had not particularly noticed the clock to him then, as I then believed it was his own - after the examination I saw him in the passage of the office, and asked how he accounted for the clock being in his bed-room; I made no threat or promise to him at any time - there was only one clock there; he said that was his wife's, and that she had it before he married her.
 JURY. Q. Was any body else in the passage when he said this? A. It was crowded - I am not aware that any body is here.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You questioned him in the passage? A. Yes - any body by must have heard it; Gast's shop has been carried on for a number of years - he pointed out a woman there as his wife; I am informed Ellis is related to him by marriage, and had an apartment in his house - I have frequently seen him go there; Moore gave me a hat on the 10th of February - I have had it ever since.
   MATILDA MOORE. The hat has been kept in a cupboard in our house ever since, till I gave it to Schofield-I never saw it in our office till master's was taken; that has not been found - I dusted this clock every morning, and know it.
