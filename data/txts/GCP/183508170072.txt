1740. JOHN PETERSON and MARY PETERSON were indicted
for feloniously receiving, on the 14th of April, 1 flute, value 35. 6d., the
goods of William Savage, and another.
JAMES BROMLEY. I am the son of James Bromley, of Chichester-mews.
In December last, a little boy induced me to go to Windsor with him, and
then we went to Billingsgate ; & girl then enticed me away-she was Ann
Peterson, the prisoners' daughter-she is now under sentence of transporta-
tion-I was lodging at the prisoners' house about April-they sent me out
thieving- I went into Whitechapel-road with Ann Peterson, and she stole
a flute from Mr. Savage, and gave it to me-she told me to take it home,
and deliver it to the male prisoner--the man and woman both took it in
their hands—they both asked me where I got it-I said, “Out of White-
chapel-road--they said, “Oh, never mind where it came from, so long as
you have got it" -I told them I stole it I did not say I stole it from Mr.
Savage, as I did not know the name--they told me to go out, and see if I
could not get more-I lodged with them about three months, and they gave
me victuals--they knew that I had run away from my mother-I always
told them where I got the things from this is the flute which I gave them
-the man took it.
John Peterson. No; I turned you and the girl out together, when you
said how you had got it.
COURT. Q. Did he turn you out of the house, when you brought the
flute? 4. No; I was there three months, in April, May, and June.
