Eleventh Session, 1884-85.
AARON Comen. I am a fitter, of 26, Tiverton Street, Oakley Square
about midnight on 18th August I lost my train from Maize Hil, and
went to look for lodgings-I met the prisoner, who said, “Where are
you going to ?” -I told her I wanted lodgings—she said, "If you will
treat me I will direct you "-I gave her a penny and she directed me
sbout 10 or 15 minutes afterwards she came up again and said, “Where
are you going to now?"-I said, "I am going in the direction you
directed me”-all of a sudden I felt a hand in my trousers pocket-1
laid hold of her wrist and struggled with her she balloaed out, “Phil,
Phil," and a man struck me on the back of my head-I struggled with
him and held him down; he held me down by the throat while the
woman took my purse; I saw her take it-they both got away-I lay
for some time, as I was hurt-I only saw her about a second the first
time and about a minute the second time-I had a good opportunity of
seeing her face-I was not tipsy-I described her to the police, and
afterwards identified her from three others in a private house; she was
partly dressed and partly in bed-at the station she said, "I never see
the fellow before, I do not know him”-she took 168. from me; and
about 20 pawn-tickets, value 151., but I stopped those.
JOSEPH WILL (Policeman R 42). From a description of the woman's
features and her name "Phil” which were given to me I arrested the
prisoner--the prosecutor was sober.
JOSEPH JOYCE (Detective Oficer R). I have known the prisoner about
two years—I have made inquiries, and can find no woman named Phil
going about with another named Sophie-the prisoner's house is at
Greenwich.
The prisoner in her defence stated that she knew nothing about it and
never saw the prosecutor before, and that he could not identify her or say
whether she wore a hat or a bonnet.
NOT GUILTY.
