whether it was moonlight—I have been charged before a Magistrate with being tipsy, and making a bit of a noise in Brick-lane, Spitalfields, three years ago—I was then in custody and locked up—I was never tried before a Jury, nor ever charged with felony, or tried for anything—I have been living three months in the same house as Delaforce—I do not know the landlord's name—he em  powers a person to gather the rents—I swear I have only been in custody once, that was the occasion in Brick-lane—I recollect that this was on the 18th of July, because I had my wife at home, working at the cotton work, and when she receives work from her master, she always makes it out in a book—I was never a witness before—I do not know what Magistrate I went before—I went to Worship-street—Margaret Draper went with me, and the prisoner's master—we met there—we did not meet to go there—when I got there I found them waiting there—it was four o'clock in the afternoon when I got there—I waited an hour—I had some conversation with the master before I went there—he said his journeyman was in cus tody, and he was waiting to see if there was any character wanting for him—I did not say I had come to prove he could not be the person—I told Margaret Draper so—we were not before the Magistrate then—we were out in the street—we did not go in to the Magistrate at all that day—the depositions were made out for Newgate the day after he was taken up, and when I went before the Magistrate I was not examined—I told the master on that occasion, that I had come to prove an alibi  for him—I did not tell him that before I went there—he and Margaret Draper did not know of my coming—I did not meet with them till I came to the office—I had not seen either of them from the time Delaforce was taken till I went before the Magistrate—Union-walk, Kingsland-road, is a half or three quarters of a mile from where I live. MARGARET DRAPER. I live in Half Nicol-street, Spitalfields. I know Delaforce—I remember going with him to the play on Monday evening, the 18th of July—I was with him from twelve o'clock in the forenoon—we went to the play at six, to the Garrick in Red Lion-street, White chapel—we remained there till about twenty-five minutes to one, and then came straight home—I parted with him at the corner of Pool's-place, where he lives—I then went straight home—he was in my company all that time—it was one o'clock when I parted with him.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You live with your parents, I suppose? A.
