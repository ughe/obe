Perhaps a fortnight after the prisoner left me—I did not miss the lancet-case—I bought this ring second-hand—I believe the prisoner came to me about the 15th of May, and left on the evening of the 17th of June—I am a surgeon—I am not a member of the College—I belong to the Society—I am an apothecary and surgeon—I keep a shop—I deal in drugs and medical herbs—I am not what is familiarly called a "quack doctor" to my knowledge—I do not sell patent medicines—the prisoner came to me to take a shop of me which I had to let—that was the first I knew of him—I never engaged to give him any specified sum for his services, but very likely I should have done so—he was assisting me—he had his board—he was not assisting me the whole time—a fortnight or three weeks he was ill, and one day he went away and stopped all night, and came the middle of the next day—he went on towards the close of the time, and a female called—her name I believe was Emily Jones—he went home with her, and did not return till the next evening—he had a lodging of his own at the next door—my initial* are scratched at the end of one of the lancets—the prisoner was not in the habit of using these—this case was kept locked up in my desk in the shop, and 1 had a man to put a lock on, and the man was two hours or two hours and a half, while the prisoner held up the cover—the rim of this ring comes off.
