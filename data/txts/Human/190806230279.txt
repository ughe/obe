   ESSEX CASES .
   BEFORE THE RECORDER .
 (Wednesday, June 24.) BURRELL  , Robert (bookmaker); being the occupier of a certain house and room, using same for the purpose of receiving money as the consideration for agreements to pay money on certain contingen cies relating to horse races and games of football.
 Mr. Bodkin and Mr. Travers Humphreys prosecuted; Mr. F. E. Smith, M.P., and Mr. Ritter defended.
 Mr. Bodkin, in opening the case, said the question here was one of law, the facts, not being disputed. The prosecution was under-taken by the Commissioner of Police under what was generally called the Betting House Act of 1853, which made it an offence for any person to keep or use any house or place for the purpose of money being received by the keeper of the house or place as a consideration for a promise to afterwards pay money on events and contingencies relating to horse-racing or any form of sport, including football. Under the Act defendant had the right either to be tried by the magistrate before whom he was summoned or before a jury. As a bookmaker, he had carried on an extensive business in ready money betting, and seemed to have thought, with regard to this Act of 1853, that, as in the case of a great many other Acts of Parliament, one had only to apply a little ingenuity to its language to enable one to go on as if there were no such Act.
