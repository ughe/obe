ANNIE DAVIS (the prisoner). When I received the baby it had a rash,
was sore, extremely weak, had very little fat, was flabby; and the reason
given to me was it was sadly neglected, and I took it out of kindness-I
fed it, as the mother wished, on condensed milk and biscuits for nearly
three weeks, when she refused the food-I thought the milk was not
sufficient, and tried Dr. Ridge's food-in a few days it refused that—then
I tried cow's milk and a little brandy-it did not suffer from
diarrhoea before she left me I always kept it clean; I bathed it in the
morning or in the evening; I changed it, and laid it on my couch, where
the mother found it-simply spite has brought me here because I made
the mother take the baby away.
Cross-examined. I say the evidence is false-it might be dirty from
diarrhoea on 24th-it never missed its bath with me, because after its
bath was the only time it slept-I agreed to take the baby till I could
get one from the Home, and on the Monday the mother promised to
fetch it-it was not more ill on the Sunday, but it never seemed to repay
the trouble I took with it-the mother always expressed gratitude, and
said I did my duty till I suggested she should take it away- the child
suffered from diarrhea on the Sunday afternoon; it had not suffered
from it for some days; it had a touch of it-when I changed it on
Sunday at tea-time it was not sore-the doctor told me there was nothing
the matter with the baby when we took it to him.
