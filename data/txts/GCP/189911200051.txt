36. WILLIAM JEREMIAH REGAN (39), Feloniously cutting
and wounding Mary Regan, with intent to do her grievous bodily harm.
MR. METCALFE I'rosecuted.
Mary Regan. I am the prisoner’s wife—he is a coal-porter-up to
November 4th I lived with him at 2, Morcombe Street, Canning Town-
he came home drunk on this night about 10 o'clock-if he had not been
drunk this would never have happened-I was drunk too-he complained
that I had been drinking ;I denied it, but I had—we went to bed together
at a little after 11 o'clock-he woke up about 1 a.m., and wanted some-
thing to eat, but I would not give it to him-I got up, and got a basin
from the table and threw it at him--he was standing up-it did not hit
him-he hit me with his fist, and then with this piece of the fender (Pro.
duced) on my head two or three times--my head bled a good deal-I ran
out of the room and fell in the passage--I sat there till the police came,
and then went into my room-I was taken to a doctor, and then sent
to the infirmary, and remained there from Tuesday to Monday-I have quite
recovered now.
By the Court. I had been quarrelling with my husband for a week
previous—I was away from him ill four months-he accused me of
pawning things; I wanted the money to get drink.
SARAH TIMOKE. I am the wife of Henry Timoke, a dock labourer-
we live in the same house as the prisoner and his wife, below them-on
November 1th, between 12 and 1, my husband heard screams, and awoke
me-I went out and saw Mrs. Regan sitting outside in a pool of blood-
my husband sent for the police-I went into the room, but did not
speak to the prisoner; he was sitting on a chair-he had been drinking
very heavily all day, but he had had a sleep--I saw blood in their room,
