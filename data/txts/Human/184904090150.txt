 1000. ELIZA WAGHORN , feloniously cutting and wounding Charles Gibson on the throat, with intent to murder him.—2d COUNT  , with intent to do him grievous bodily harm. MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. JEMIMA BROWN. I am single, and occupy the first-floor front room at 2, New-court, Nicol-street, Bethnal-green—the prisoner lived next door, on the ground-floor. On the morning of 27th Feb., between two and three o'clock, a man named Sadler was in my room—he left me, and went down stairs in his shirt—Mary Day threw his trowsers down after him—I went down to look for a constable, and saw Gibson fighting with Sadler—Sadler came up-stairs again, and Gibson too—he was drunk—he tried to get into my room, but did not succeed—he then went over to Morris's room—I went out, and went down stairs—the prisoner rushed up-stairs into Morris's room, and said, "Oh you b—black mare!"—she ran to the dresser and took up a knife—Morris ran down stairs, and the prisoner after her, and she caught sight of Gibson, who was standing in his shirt, between Nos. 1 and 2 in the court, looking up to the window, and she drew the knife across his throat-Morris was at the bottom of the court, looking for a constable—it appeared to me that in the dark the prisoner mistook Gibson for Morris—when she had done it she said, "Oh my God! I have cut Charley  Gibson's throat"—she was drunk—neither Gibson or the other man were acquainted with her—they did not visit her—she wiped the knife with a piece of cloth; I think a piece of a child's frock, and flung it up into Sarah Morris's room—she then laid hold of Gibson's head and said, "Oh my good God! Charley , I shall never rest upon my bed again, for I have cut your throat"—Gibson had then gone into the prisoner's room—she took a washing-basin up full of water end flung it up to Morris's window, saying, "You b—black mare, I will give you this in your face,"—the basin fell down on Gibson and smashed to pieces—I do not know what quarrel they had had—Morris was in bed when the two men were fighting—she had no quarrel with the men—I am quite sure it was the prisoner who cut Gibson's throat—I saw the knife in her hand, but she endeavoured to make it appear that Morris had done it.

Between the door and the court—I was standing outside the door, and she was outside—( The wit  ness's deposition being read, stated she wiped the knife with a cloth in her own room)—it was in the court that she wiped it.
