ELIZA OLIVER. I live at 28, Cowper Street, and am a widow—I have the letting of No. 27—I asked the prosecutrix for the rent on 26th July, and the prisoner said that he would pay me two weeks at once on the Monday—I heard no screaming, but the prosecutrix afterwards called me and I went upstairs and saw her with her head bleeding and the prisoner standing by the window with his hands up to his eyes—I ran to fetch a doctor.
   Cross-examined.  The door of Mrs. Barth's room was open. BETSY GLADWELL. I am the wife of William Gladwell, of 27, Cowper Street—on 26th July Mrs. Bell called me and I went up to the first land ing and saw the prisoner standing with his hands over his eyes—I said, "What have you done?"—he made no answer—I asked him a second time, and the prosecutrix said, "I am dying, he has nearly killed me"—she was a few stairs up towards the next floor—I saw that she was wounded, and wiped the blood from her face—she asked me to let her go to my room—I said, "It is too far for you"—she said, "Oh, let me come," and she did go, and she was sent to the hospital—the prisoner said nothing and was locked into his own room till a policeman came. THOMAS MAYNARD( Police-Inspector G ). On 26th July I saw the prose cutrix in St. Bartholomew's Hospital—I then went to 27, Cowper Street, and found that the prisoner had been taken to the station—the counter pane, sheets, and the child's white frock were all covered with blood, and in the centre of the bed I found this piece of hair ( produced ), which appears to have been chopped off somebody's head—it is the same colour as the girl's hair—I found blood stains on the floor, and four marks made by a blunt instrument like this chopper, also several pieces of hair wet with blood, which appeared to have been chopped off, and at the foot of the bed I picked up another piece—the chopper had been taken away—I saw a small pan by the side of the bed with dirty water in it as if somebody had been washing, and some of it was upset on the floor. WILLIAM LONDON( Policeman G  158). On 26th July about 2.45 I went to a first-floor front room at 27, Cowper Street, and found this chopper ( produced ) lying near the bed with blood and hair on the blade and handle—I then went into the back room and found the prisoner sitting on the bed—I told him I should take him for violently assaulting his wife and striking her on the head with a chopper—he said, "I am very sorry, I know I have done wrong."
