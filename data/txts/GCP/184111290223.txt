the 15th of November I was in bed-I had been asleep, and was awoke
by the prisoner touching my toe- I got out of bed and made towards him,
and made a blow at him-he fell, not in consequence of my blow, but to
hide himself-he was very much in liquor-when I laid down again he
wanted me to go and fight with him outside in the court-I said I did not
want to fight him, if he wanted fighting I would talk to him in the
morning when he was sober-that would not satisfy him, he wanted to
fight right or wrong-he said if I did not fight him I had no business to
sleep, and he would cut our heels and our ancles off-there were other
lodgers in the room, but they were asleep-I said nothing--I knew I had
no business to stop in the room when he said this, and I dressed myself
and went down stairs-he had gone down before me-when I got down I
found him outside the door-be asked me what I wanted-I said I wanted
no quarrelling but to go to bed-I then asked what he wanted-he said,
"Come on, I am ready for you"-he was making towards me-I defended
him off by striking him somewhere in the face with my fist-I cannot say
where, I struck him a hard blow--his face did not bleed-I did not give
him a black eye--I bit him about the mouth I believe-I did not knock
his teeth out-he then struck me over the eye with a knife, and after-
wards cut me down the back of the neck and over my left ear-the
policeman parted us- I was instantly taken to Guy's Hospital, and got
my head dressed.
Prisoner. Q. You saw no knife in my hand ? A. I felt it scratching
down my neck-I could not see it-it was too dark.
Thomas HICKEY (police-constable M 152.) I was passing the court,
and heard a cry of " Take the knife away from him !-he has got a knife -
murder !-be is killing me with a knife!"-another voice said, “ There is a
policeman at the top, why not call hito?"-I went up, and found the pro-
secutor on the prisoner-they both had hold of each other he said. “Po.
liceman, he has got a knife in his hand; take it"-I caught hold of him,
and saw his left hand drawn out of his left pocket-I said, " Where is the
knife ?"- he said, “I have got no knife" I put my hand into his left
pocket, and found this knife-it was shut, and was not bloody-there was
a good deal of blood from the wound, all over the prosecutor's shoulder
and frock-I saw the prisoner fumbling in his pocket before he drew his
hand out-I did not look at the point to see if it was bloody-I took
him to the station.
Peter Lay. I am a dresser at Guy's Hospital. I saw the prosecutor
when he was brought in-he had three or four small wounds, and one cut
behind his left ear--the cuts appeared to be done with a knife-I think
this knife would do it-there was not the least danger from the wounds
there had been some bleeding, not much-the knife would not necessarily
have been bloody if it had inflicted the wound.
Prisoner's Defence. That night, as I was going to bed, I chanced to put
my hand on this man's bed, as it was so near mine; he told me to mind
what I was about or he would knock me down stairs; I said he should
not do it: he got up, shoved me across the bed, and said, if I wanted
any thing to come down stairs, and he would give it me; he was drunk
as well as me ; when we got down stairs he struck me, and I struck him ;
a witness stood by, and hallooed out to him, " Do you think he has got a
knife?" I had got a knife in my pocket, but I never used it or took it
out at all. If it was not a couple of pins I had in the cuff of my jacket
that did it, I do not know what else it was.
