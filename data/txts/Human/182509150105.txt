THOMAS GRAY. I am a Thames Police officer. I heard of this transaction about half-past five o'clock on the morning of the 4th of August. I went in company with Yates to a house in Higham's-buildings, Bluecoat-fields, and saw the prisoner Barton in bed undressed - two more men were lying on the bed dressed. The two men walked out of the room; and Ravenor who was also in the room followed them. Yates followed Ravenor and brought him back to me with his hat in his hand - he had hardly left the room a moment. I put my hand in the hat, and found a silver table-spoon, a tea, and salt spoon, and part of a metal spoon. I then searched him and found in his pocket a bunch of phosphorous matches. I asked him how he came by the property - he said he found it in Stepney-fields. Barton said "Here's a pretty job you've done for me." I asked Barton if he lodged there - he said Yes, and paid 8 d. a night for his bed. Yates then examined Barton farther.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. What kind of a morning was it? A. It was light. I do not know whether it had been raining in the course of the night - it was dry when I saw the prisoners. Barton was undressed - the others went away.
   HENRY YATES. I am an officer. I went to the place in question, between five and six o'clock in the morning with Gray. I saw the persons in the room, and knowing two of them in particular I said "Gray its not all right;" I then saw a pair of women's shoes under the bed. The other two, whom I knew, then walked out - Ravenor followed them, and I said to Gray "I think there's something in that boy's hat," for I heard something chink. I then went after him, and took hold of his hat, and two silver spoons dropped out of it. I took him back to Gray, who took the rest of the property. I then searched the bed, and found an apron on the top of it, which has been identified. I then asked Barton what he had got in his pocket - he said, nothing he was afraid of. I searched his pockets, but found nothing relating to this. I asked how he came possessed of the shoes, and he said Ravenor put them under the bed.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 RAVENOR'S Defence. As I was going over Stepney-fields, I saw a bundle lay, and I took it up and opened it. I found the matches, these spoons, and an apron. Two boys called out, "That is ours." I said it was not theirs or it would not have laid there - they followed me, and I went to ask Barton to let me in, as it rained very fast.
 RAVENOR -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 15.
 BARTON -    NOT GUILTY.
 1514.  MARY READ was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Whitcombe , about eight o'clock in the evening of the 11th of August , (no person being therein), and stealing a pair of trousers, value 5 s.; and a cap, value 2 s. 6 d.; his property.
   MARY WHITCOMBE. I am the wife of John Whitcombe, who is a drummer in the Guards. I live in Rochester-terrace, Vauxhall-road. I went out, about eight o'clock in the evening, of the 11th of August, to take my work home - I left no person in my house. As I went out I saw the prisoner standing near my door, and told her, if she saw my husband come home, to tell him I was gone home with my work. I returned in about half an hour; the prisoner was still standing at the same place - I found my door had been opened with a false key, but did not miss any thing that night; next morning I missed a pair of trousers, which I had left in my room, when I went out the night before, and a cap. I saw her that morning, about nine o'clock, and asked her what she had done with the trousers and cap, which she took from my room. She at first said, she had not seen them; and then said, she had sold them. I said, if she would tell me where she sold them, I would not hurt her; she then said she had pawned them. I went with her to one pawnbroker's - but she said they were not there, they were over Westminster-bridge.
   JOSEPH COOPER. I am an officer - the prisoner was given into my custody on the 12th of August, and I found this cap in her bosom - I asked her what she had done with the drawers; she said she had pawned them for 6 d., to get a breakfast.
 (Cap produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 16.
 Of stealing the cap only. - Confined Two Months.
 1515.  SARAH BUCKLEY and DENNIS CONNER were indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Jane Chapman , about eleven o'clock in the forenoon of the 11th of August , (she and others being therein,) and stealing a shirt, value 4 s.; a waistcoat, value 4 s.; three frocks, value 4 s.; two pairs of stockings, value 2 s.; two pieces of flannel, value 8 d.; a handkerchief, value 3 s.; and a cap, value 1 s.; the goods of Charles John Anderson.
   HANNAH ANDERSON. I am the wife of Charles John Anderson - we lodge at Mrs. Chapman's. I do not know her Christian name - it is in St. Giles's parish. On the 11th of August, I went out between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning - returned in about an hour, and missed this property - I had left some on a chair, and some on the floor. I left Mrs. Chapman at home, some persons in the garret, and people in the next room. I never saw either of the prisoners till they were before the Magistrate. The property I lost, was one shirt, one waistcoat, three frocks, three pairs of stockings, two pieces of flannel, a handkerchief and a cap, the whole was worth about 1 l.
   EMILY WINTER. I keep a clothes-shop in Monmouth-street. I bought a shirt for 2 s. 6 d., and a waistcoat for 1 s. 6 d., of a young woman, whom I took to be a flower girl. I went to the office, and signed my name to a paper, the contents of which were read over to me; I cannot swear I did not say that it was the female prisoner, as it was not explained to me; but I only know it was a short woman - I cannot say it was the prisoner.
   ELIZABETH MONCK. I am in the clothes line, and live in Monmouth-street. I bought a pair of stockings and two small pieces of flannel, which the officer has; of a person, who, I believe, to be the female prisoner, but she was differently dressed to what she is now.
