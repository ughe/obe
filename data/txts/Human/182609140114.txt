 1566.  SARAH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of August , 1 sovereign, 2 half-crowns, and 5 shillings, the monies of William Mudd , from his person.
 WILLIAM MUDD. I am a  tailor, and live in St. Helena-place. On the 13th of August, about a quarter-past twelve o'clock at night, I was going from Sadler's-well, to the top of Rosamond-street- the prisoner accosted me, and asked me to go with her; we got into conversation for about three minutes; she was throwing her arms about my waist - she then turned off from me, and though at first she wanted money, she did not then ask for any; I had taken liberties with her, and had not paid her, but I was to have done so; as she was turning away, I put my hand into my pocket, and missed one sovereign, two half-crowns, and five-shillings, from my left-hand waistcoat pocket - I seized her arm - a sovereign and a shilling were left; I asked her to give me my money - she denied having it - I took her to the watch-house, and stated to the night constable what I had lost; she said she would soon show what money she had - put her hand in her pocket, and pulled out the two half-crowns and five shillings; the officer told her to put her hand into her pocket again - she did so, and pulled out three penny pieces and between them was the sovereign.
   THOMAS MARKHAM. I am an officer, and was in the watch-house when Mudd brought the prisoner; he thought at first he had one shilling more, but he recollected afterwards he had spent a shilling; the prisoner said she had had good luck that night, and would soon show what money she had; she pulled out two half-crowns, five shillings, and a sixpence, and said that was all she had; I said, "Come, now I must have a search;" she put her hand into her pocket again, and pulled out 3d., and between them was the sovereign, which she said she did not know she had, but gentlemen were in the habit of giving a sovereign, instead of a shilling, in the dark.
 Prisoner's Defence. This man was in the company of two women - he left them and came to me - he took me up a court and offered me a shilling, which I took, and put into my pocket; he wanted to take very indecent libertles with me - I would not let him, and he gave charge of me.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1572.  EDWARD BUSH was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of June , 1 bed tick, value 6s.; 1 bolster, value 3s.; 1 curtain, value 2s.; 2 blankets, value 12s.; 2 quilts, value 10s.; four pictures, value 2s.; 2 sheets, value 5s., and 1 looking-glass, value 2s., the goods of Thomas May , in a lodging-room.
   SUSANNAH MAY. I am the wife of Thomas May, and live in Smith-street, Westminster. The prisoner's wife hired my lodging - he came afterwards; they lived together as man and wife from Tuesday till Saturday night; they went away without giving me notice - the door was locked, and I expected them to return - I missed all the property stated; the prisoner was taken on Westminster-bridge with some property, but not mine; I do not live in the house - it is all let out.
   JOHN CULLINGFORD. I am in the Coldstream Guards, and live in Smith's-rents, York-street. On the 14th of June I saw the prisoner and his wife turning down the bed together; my wife remarked, "those people are going to bed early" - but in half an hour I saw them get up and go out together, with each a large bundle; I saw them return in about an hour.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have no wife; I was going through Westminster, and the woman said, "Will you go home with me," which I did, and in the morning I paid her her due; I went again the next night, but I know no more of her than any one here.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1573.  THOMAS KENDRICK was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July , 1 blanket, value 8s.; 1 sheet, value 9s., and 2 pillow cases, value 3s., the goods of William Gilling , in a lodging room.
   SARAH GILLING. I am the wife of William Gilling - we live in Cow-cross-street, Smithfield. I let the prisoner, on the 24th of April, a furnished room, on the second floor, at 6s. per week; he stated he was married, and he brought a woman with him - they remained till the 10th of August; his wife asked me for a clean pair of sheets, which I gave her, and in about half an hour I saw her go out with something under her apron; I watched her to the pawnbroker's; the prisoner was at home at that time, out of work; I am confident he was in their room, it was between eleven and twelve o'clock in the day.
   THOMAS CARPENTER. I am apprentice to Mr. Croas, of Turnmill-street. I have a blanket pawned for 3s., two pairs of pillow-cases for 1s., and one sheet for 3s. 6d., by the prisoner's wife, in the name of Kendrick - I knew her before.
   JOHN LOADSMAN. I am an officer. Gilling sent to me on the 10th of July; the prisoner gave me up these duplicates, and said he had pawned the goods.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. It was distress that urged me to do it. GUILTY. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
