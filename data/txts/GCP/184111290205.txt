Richard Evans. I am foreman to the prosecutor, who has premises
in Friday-street. I heard of Coronel's coming to the warehouse the
next morning, in consequence of which I spoke to M-Knight-he made
a statement to me--Mr. Saltmer was not there-he came in about an
hour afterwards - M'Knight's father was present -- Mr. Saltmer gave
M'Knight two hours to consider to tell me all-at the expiration of the two
hours I went and fetched Cuthbert, and Inspector M'Lean was sent for
some patterns were pointed out to me by M Knight, which I have here
--he pointed out these figured satins with respect to the prisoner--they
are worth about 68. a yard- I have examined the stock, and three pieces
of that pattern, of nine yards each, are missing-I afterwards went with
M'Knight and the Inspector to Holywell-street--a parcel was made up to
carry-M‘Knight went in first and I followed him the prisoner came
in in about three minutes, and M‘Knight said, “That is the man I sold
the goods to"-I did not hear the prisoner make any observation-I di-
rectly came out to the inspector, who was outside-when he came in, the
prisoner said, “I suppose this boy has been stealing these goods which I
bought?"—the inspector then came in-he said, “Come, I want these
goods"-he muttered something--the inspector went round, the parcel was
Jying there, and he took possession of it-it contained the black quiltings
produced - I then asked him where the figured satins were-he said he had
never seen them-the inspector searched about, but found nothing more-
he was taken into custody and brought away.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. And afterwards bailed that
same night, was he not? A. He was these quiltings are cotton and silk
it is used for facing coats and waistcoats-this is very different that
might be bought for 18d., when this is 10s. 60.- this is all cotton-I
should say there is very little silk in it, if any-we do not keep any thing
of that sort--the prisoner muttered something I did not understand-the
officer went round the counter and took up these black things it is a small
shop-they laid on the back counter in paper-I cannot say whether it was
the paper they were sold in—when the officer said, "I want the things,"
the prisoner did not say, “They are there on the counter, in that paper"
- he muttered something about remnants-he said they were remnants-
he did not point to them when he muttered-M'Knight said, “I think
these are them"-it is a dark shop, and they were on the back counter-
the prisoner said in answer to the officer's question, that those were the
only goods he had ever seen from him, and he knew nothing of the satins
-We made a search, but never found a bit of satin.
