 1138. GEORGE COX, Embezzling 6 l  . 10 s  . and 6 l  . 6 s  ., the moneys of Lawrence Earnshaw and others.
   MR. GIFFARD . conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM MORRIS. I am an engineer—the prisoner was in the employ of a society, of which I am a member, called the British Engineers' Benefit Society—it is a registered friendly society—it was part of his duty to receive money on behalf of that society—in the event of his receiving cheques, it was his duty to pay them into our bankers, the London and Westminster Bank—I have the account he made out—I know his writing—this is his writing—here is no entry in this statement of 6 l . 6 s . paid by Mr. Hall, on the 28th May, or of 6 l . 10 s . paid by Mr. Hopkinson, on the 26th of May—this account goes down to June.
   Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY . Q.  Was the prisoner secretary to this society? A.  Yes, and he was also a subscriber, and had been so for four years and upwards—there were quarrels and misunderstandings between the members—in the course of these quarrels some of the books were taken from the prisoner—I do not remember what books they were—I know that he had received directions to pay over money to the auditors, from time to time—the auditors were Parker, McBride, Sprey, and Rowe—I have heard that the prisoner paid money to those men in May—the opposite side of this account shows that the auditors received certain sums—the prisoner had authority from the executive council to pay those auditors—Mr. Williamson is chairman of the auditors.
   MR. GIFFARD . Q.  Had he any authority to pay cheques to the auditors? A.  No—I was not at all aware that he had received those cheques—he went to Dartford for one—I discovered that some time after—he did not tell me—I had no communication with him.
   MR. LILLEY . Q.  IS this book in the writing of the prisoner? A.  Yes—I have every reason to believe that this entry, where the leaf is turned down, is his writing.
   MR. GIFFARD . Q.  Do you know anything about this book? A.  No, I never saw it before. JOHN HOPKINSON. I am an engineer, and a member and subscriber to this society—on 26th May, I paid the prisoner a check for 6 l . 10 s .—it has been returned to me from my bankers as paid. EDWARD HALL. I am an engineer, and live at Dartford. I am a sub scriber to the Engineers' Benefit Society—on 28th May, I paid the prisoner a cheque of 6 l . 6 s . for the benefit of the society—it has been returned to me from Messrs. Lubbock and Co. HENRY ROSE. I am a clerk in the London and County Bank—Messrs. Hopkinson keep an account at that bank—on 26th May I paid cash for this cheque over the counter—I do not know who presented it. THOMAS JAMES BRAY. I am a clerk in the Bank of Messrs. Lubbock. Mr. Hall has an account at our bank—on 29th May I gave cash for this cheque—it is dated the 28th—it has not passed through the London and Westminster Bank—it was paid over the counter.
