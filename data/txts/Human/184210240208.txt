   MR. DOANE Q.  What were the rumours about this shop? A.  I heard they had been obtaining goods under false pretences, which I be lieve was current in the newspapers—there was no regular female servant—the whole of the house was not furnished, only the sitting-room at the back of the shop, and two rooms up stairs—each had a bed in them—there was no servant beside me—I was there day by day, from the 11th of June till the Wednesday in August when the shop was closed—I was paid in the evening for the day.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILDE Q.  Do you remember when Gosbell came? A.  No, I found him there on the Saturday I went there—he was there but very little the last three weeks—I think he was there the night before the shop closed. WILLIAM MORRIS( City police-constable. ) I found Shackleford in custody of Mr. Mumford, in a tobacconist's in Fenchurch-street—I took him to the station, and found on him a plate and some cards engraved, which are "F. Shackleford, wine and spirit-merchant, Brighton, Dealer in cigars of the first quality"—I have looked after Charles Sidney Smith, Bachelard, and the two Beaumonts, without success. JAMES BRANNAN( police-sergeant G  20.) I have had a warrant against Charles Sidney Smith, Bachelard, and the two Beaumonts, and have looked diligently for them, in company with inspector Shackle night after night. JOSEPH SHACKLE. I am a police-inspector. I have searched with Brannan, and in vain.
   JURY to WILLIAM KIMBRELL . Q.  You saw some parcel opened; did you see whether it was directed at all? A.  No, I saw some were directed, but I did not see this one.
