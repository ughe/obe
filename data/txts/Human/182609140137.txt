I found a phosphorus-box and nine matches on him, a chisel, and nine duplicates; I went to look for the weapon the next morning, and found this stick.
   WILLIAM BALE. I was there and heard the cry; I saw the gentleman staggering about, and bleeding very profusely; I conveyed him up to the gate.
 COURT to PROSECUTOR. Q. Did you see any person near you that you spoke to about relieving themselves? A. No.
   GEORGE KENFIELD. I was going to take a boat from the water, and heard the cry of murder; I saw the gentleman, and I thought he had been cutting his own throat; I went and inquired which was the man; I collared the prisoner; he said he begged my pardon, and was sorry for what he had done; he was in great distress; I said, "What did you do it with an instrument?" he said,"No, a stick which I took up in the gardens."
 Prisoner's Defence. I merely said, when he came and said the gentleman had his clothes all over blood, and he supposed it was done with a sharp instrument, that I had seen it done with a stick, but I had not done it; when I knelt down and begged the gentleman's pardon, I was treated shamefully.
 Two witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1642.  WILLIAM HEAGREN was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of September , 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Philip Burgess , from his person.
 PHILIP BURGESS. On the 5th of September I was in  Piccadilly; my handkerchief was in my right-hand surtoutcoat pocket; I felt a pull, and turned round immediately; I found it in the prisoner's hand; I had used it a moment before, and put it safe into my pocket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up, and gave it to the gentleman.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1643.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of July , 1 shoe, value 3s., the goods of Francis Lloyd , from his person.
 FRANCIS LLOYD. On the 11th of July I was at the Haymarket Theatre, with my wife - the prisoner, as I believe, came up and requested me to let him get a coach, as is was raining; I would not; he then followed me up the  Haymarket, and there were twenty or thirty persons; some one threw a stone, which staggered me a little; the prisoner then came and stood on my heel; I turned and said, "Is there not room enough on the flag-stones?" - I then put my hand to my pocket, but I had lost nothing - the prisoner then appeared to be inebriated, but I afterwards saw him follow some other persons up the Hay-market, and I said to the watchman, "There is a thief, I believe," and I was going away; but when I had got about thirty doors, Mrs. Lloyd put her hand to my pocket, and found that one shoe was gone from it; I put her into a shop, and went with the watchman; we took the prisoner with the shoe in his hand.
 COURT. Q. Why did not you give charge of him at once? A. I did not know that I had lost any thing; when he was taken he was speaking some flash words to a woman, who was very angry with him for not taking both shoes - one expression which I remarked was,"What a d-d fumbler he was not to get the two - one was of no use."
   MICHAEL QUIN. I am a watchman of Coventry-street. I went with Lloyd, and took the prisoner with the shoe; he pretended to be drunk, but when he got to the watch-house he was perfectly sober.
 Prisoner to FRANCIS LLOYD. Q. Can you swear I took it out of your pocket at the time I trod on your heel? A. I can swear he got it out of my pocket, but I cannot say when.
   GEORGE SMITH. I was night constable. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house with this shoe.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going along the Haymarket - a shoe was thrown at me, and I took it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1644.  WILLIAM KILBY was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 4 sovereigns, 2 half-crowns, 2 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the monies of Joseph Labovitch from his person.
 JOSEPH LABOVITCH. (Through an interpreter.) I live in Brown's-buildings, and am a  licensed-hawker and pedlar. On the 14th of August, about four o'clock in the afternoon, I was in a brick-field, with a box, selling rhubarb; I had a little box, containing four sovereigns, some half-crowns, shillings, and sixpences. The prisoner came out of the field, and cheapened a piece of rhubarb - he told me to wait while he went into the field; I waited on the opposite side of the field, and there was a brook - the prisoner called me, and he laid down some bricks for me to get over, but I went to another place, a little higher, which I could step over; the prisoner then came again, and he pulled the box, which I had across my shoulder - the strap broke, and when the box was on the ground he put his hand in, and took the money out; he ran away: I made a terrible noise, till some persons came to my assistance; I left my box on the ground, and when I came back it was safe, but I have never got my money; I did not see him again till the officer took me to a public-house, where I saw him with many others, and distinguished him - I swear he is the man.
   THOMAS COPE. I am a constable. On the 14th of August, at a quarter before four o'clock, I met the prosecutor in Lower-road, Islington - he appeared much agitated, and put his hand to his pockets; a gentleman said he had been robbed by the brick-makers; I went to the field, and saw the stool where the prisoner had worked was wet, and a boy there called out, "Whahaak," which is a signal for a person who has done wrong to keep away; I then went to the Green Man public-house with the prosecutor, who put his hand on the prisoner's shoulder, and pointed him out as the man.
