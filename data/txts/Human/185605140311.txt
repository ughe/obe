   COURT . Q.  From the course of the conversation, did you ascertain from him that Charnell made his proposal to Martin to write at the time he gave information of the robbery? A.  Yes; and Martin refused, saying that he would have nothing to do with writing, but thai he might write it himself if he chose, there were writing materials there; that Charnell wrote a note, wished Martin to carry it for him, but he objected, telling him that he was tired, and that he had better put it in his pocket, and the delay would not be much longer—I think that was about the whole of the conversation.
   Cross-examined by MR. MAC ENTEE . Q.  You say that some plate was found in the large chest? A.  Yes; that was covered by a slide there were marks of violence on the lid of the small chest, if it had been broken open certainly—there were marks on the pantry, door—the closet door had not been tampered with, at all—the proper key was in it when I arrived—I know nothing about it being taken out of the press—the area keys were ordinary ones.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAREY . Q.  When you saw these parties, did they give the elaborate account you have described in an unembrrassed manner? A.  I thought so—the Monday after the robbery was, I believe, the first time I saw Sale—Fells saw them before me—I did not go by appointment, they did not expect me—Sale did not live there—the inter view lasted half or three quarters of an hour—I saw them three or four times, still pursuing my inquiries to detect the perpetrators or the robbery—I took Sale on the evening of the 24th by the direction of Mr. Fisher, he insisted on it—I did not suggest to him that any of them should be taken, I merely stated the facts—it is my duty to take persons in custody, against whom there is a case—it is my duty to take a party who I consider criminal—I took Sale a fortnight afterwards, on the 24th, at his own lodging—I knew the address of all of them, and they knew that I was pursuing these inquiries—Sale is a butler out of place—Martin is a butler—Peshell is a foreigner; he told me that he expected a situation at the Lord Warden hotel at Dover, and gave me that address—I saw him again, and he told me that he had got it—I took Sale on the morning of the 25th April, about half past 12 o'clock—I also took Martin on the 25th at his lodging, about midnight—I waited for him to come home—Peshell was apprehended at the Lord Warden hotel—all that was done; by Mr. Fisher's instructions, he was determined that they should be taken in custody—I told Sale that I was sorry I was obliged to apprehend him—I searched their lodgings—I made certain inquiries of William Ault, a butler out of place, and mentioned the result to Mr. Fisher—these inquiries were made in consequence of the very minute information that Martin and these three parties gave me as to their movements on that evening—I know Brewer-street, Golden-square—I know a place called Willis's-rooms there, they are sometimes taken by one individual, who arranges for a ball to take place at intervals of several weeks—I know Evans's in Covent-garden.
