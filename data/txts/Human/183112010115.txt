 271.  JOHN FREEGROVE was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of December , 1 waistcoat, value 4s., the goods of Mary Ann Ridley.
   MARY ANN RIDLEY. I am a widow, and live in Little George-street. The prisoner came this day fortnight to purchase a waistcoat, and I showed him several - he came again on the 2nd of December with a young man; they asked for waistcoats, and I gave each one to try on - my daughter was in the shop; I went out, and when I returned she said to me, "Mother, they have put something into their hats;" I said, "Young men, if you are honest, you will pull off your hats;" the other one ran away, but the prisoner was detained - when the Policeman came he threw down his hat in the shop, and in it was this waistcoat - I believe the other man took one, but I would not swear it.
   EPHRAIM PITT. I am a Police-constable. I was sent for, and found the prisoner; he had thrown down his hat before I got there.(Property produced and sworn to)
   LOUISA RIDLEY. I have heard what my mother has stated, and it is correct - I saw the waistcoat in the prisoner's hat - he said if I got it out he would kick me, which he did.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, declaring his innocence.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined One Year.
 272.  JOHN CRAWLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of November , 1 bullock's head, value 2s. 6d., and 1 iron hook, value 1s., the goods of Joseph Harradence.
   JOHN SLATER. I am a Police-constable. I met the prisoner at half-past six o'clock, on the 21st of November, in South-street, carrying a bullock's head on his shoulder- I said, "What have you got there?" he said he had bought it, but he would not tell me where; I took him to the station-house - a boy brought in this iron hook, and said he had dropped it.
   JOSEPH HARRADENCE. I am a butcher, and live in Chiswell-street. I saw the head at the station-house, and knew it to be mine; I had missed that and the hook from my shop - I know nothing of the prisoner; he was rather intoxicated.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. - Confined Three Months.
 273.  ANN CLAPTON was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of November ; 1 necklace, value 10s., the goods of Edward Nelson.
   EDWARD NELSON. I live in West-row, Carnaby-market- the prisoner has lodged in the house for eight or nine months. My wife was ill, and the prisoner tendered her services to her; I believe her husband is a carpenter- she was in my wife's room about an hour; she was the first person that came out of the room - I had seen this necklace in the room just before, and was informed it was gone - I then went in, and missed it; it had hung on an image, over the mantel-piece.
   JOSEPH SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. I received this necklace from the prisoner on the 29th of November.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 20.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 274.  MARY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of December , 5 sovereigns, 2 half-crowns, and 3 sixpences, the monies of Matthew Rutherford , from his person.
   MATTHEW RUTHERFORD. I am a seaman; I had been to the Commons, and received my wages on the 2nd of December, and met the prisoner in a coffee-shop in Ratcliff-highway, between one and two o'clock the next morning: I had then fourteen sovereigns, and I changed one - I was not sober when I got to the coffee-shop; I found the prisoner there: she accosted me - she asked for something to drink - I gave her half a crown: she got some rum, and brought me three sixpences in change - I then went with her to her own house: I was there a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes; I counted my money in her house, but I did not give her any thing: I had nothing to do with her - she put her hands into my pocket, when I sat down on the chair; I said, "This will not do for me;" I got up and counted my money, I found I had still my thirteen sovereigns, and I put them all safe into my left-hand breeches pocket; I bade her good night, and was going down - as soon as I got to the stair-head, she came and put her hand into my pocket; I seized her hand, and got eight sovereigns out of it - she got off with the rest and went out; I went out of the house in about five minutes, and found her in custody; I had not given her any money.
   JAMES GRAHAM. I am a Police-constable. I heard a noise, and hastened to the place; I heard the prisoner run up some steps a little way from the house - she said, "Oh God! I have lost my shoe;" she came running towards me; I had a lamp in front of me - I turned the light on, and saw one of her hands grasped; I caught at it, and, to the best of my belief, I heard three sovereigns fall - I then searched her, and found two half-crowns and three sixpences in her bosom; I searched the place and so did others, but we found no sovereigns; the prosecutor said,"That is the girl that robbed me of five sovereigns, two half-crowns, and three sixpences."
 Prisoner. Q. Did I not tell you, that he gave me two half-crowns and three sixpences? A. No, you never said any such thing; another officer found 8 1/2d. in her pocket.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the prosecutor had given her the money.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
