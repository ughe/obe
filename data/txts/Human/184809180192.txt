JOHN WILLIAM BROWN. I am clerk to Messrs. Steinback and Son, they are brokers to the ship Wallace , Captain Robert Morton—she sailed on the 19th Aug.—I have a list of the seaman, returned to us by the captain from the Downs—we chartered the vessel—the name of Reed or Reburn does not appear in this list—we should not pay any money on this paper—I do not know Mr. Robert Forbes—I have not made inquiry for him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  May there not be another ship called Wallace ? A.  There may, I believe there are many—I dare say there are many Mortons—men sometimes join the ship at Gravesend after it is cleared out—this paper is brought up by the pilot who leaves the ship in the Downs—if a man joins the ship after the pilot leaves, we should not have the account in this paper, but if the man has an advance-note we should have an account from the cap tain.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  When did the ship sail? A.  She cleared out at the Customs on 19th Aug., and went down to Gravesend the same day. JOSEPH DATE. I am a clerk in the Register Office for Seamen. I pro duce the official list of the crew on board the Wallace  of Glasgow, which sailed for Quebec—this list is deposited by the captain, and signed by him on the completion of his crew—this is signed just before the vessel goes—it is made evidence by the Act of Parliament—the name of Reid or Reburn is not here—there may be many vessels named Wallace , but they would not agree with this in the tonnage, the day of sailing, and the captain's name—I have made inquiries respecting these facts from public books, and from those in quiries I can undertake to say that there was no other Wallace , Captain Mor ton, sailed for Quebec on that day—this note bears the name of Reburn—I had an official counterpart of the same—it bears a number which is supposed to be the number of the prisoner's register-ticket—it is 74360—I have ascertained that the name of the person to whom No. 74360 is applicable, is Thorns Lacey.
   EVAN-JONES ( policeman, M  250). I took the prisoner—I told him the charge—he said nothing then—he afterwards said we ought to take the other man he received that note from—I took from him this seaman's ticket, with the name of Reburn on it—I read the name of Charles Reburn—he said, "That is my name; that is my ticket." GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined One Year .
