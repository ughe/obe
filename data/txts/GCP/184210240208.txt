MR. DOANE. Q. What were the rumours about this shop? 1.1
Ricard they had been obtaining goods under false pretences, which I be-

June till the Wednesday in August when the shop was closed — I was paid
in the evening for the day.
Cross-examined by MR. WILDE. Q. Do you remember when Gosbell
came? A. No, I found him there on the Saturday I went there-he was
there but very little the last three weeks- I think he was there the night
before the shop closed.
WILLIAM MORRIS (City police-constable.) I found Shackleford in
custody of Mr. Mumford, in a tobacconist's in Fenchurch-street-
I took him to the station, and found on him a plate and some card:
engraved, which are “F. Shackleford, wine and spirit-merchant, Brighton,
Dealer in cigars of the first quality" - I have looked after Charles Sidney
Smith, Bachelard, and the two Beaumonts, without success.
JAMES BRANNAN (police-sergeant G 20.) I have had a warrant against
Charles Sidney Smith, Bachelard, and the two Beaumonts, and have
looked diligently for them, in company with inspector Shackle night after
night.
JOSEPH SHACKLE. I am a police-inspector. I have searched with
Brannan, and in vain.
JURY to WILLIAM KIMBRELL. Q. You saw some parcel opened; did
you see whether it was directed at all? A. No. I saw some were directed,
but I did not see this one.

a good character.)
GOSBELL-GUILTY. Aged 29.
SMITH-GUILTY. Aged 45.
Judgment Respited.
SHACKLEFORD-GUILTY. Aged 36.
