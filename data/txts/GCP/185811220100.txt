Cross-examined by Mr. RIBTON. Q. Have you heard anything about your
note since? A. No-I drew 351. that morning, and I left my ship with 171
in my pocket-I lost 91.--I had laid out the other 81. for the ship's use -- Mr.
Waters got the 5l. note from me to pay his bill-I did not owe him a bill -
I gave him an order for meat that day to send to the ship, and left the 51.
note with him because I had too much in my pocket-i bave got it from
him since ; it was sent to me by him-he did not stop something for the
ment-I never got the meat ; I had to go away-I recollected having left
the 51. note with him before he sent it to me; I applied to him for it three
days after I did not leave money with any one else that night-I did not
leave a watch and two 51. notes with the landlord of one of the public-
houses -I say that I did not leave them because I never left the public-house
till I got them-I gave them to the landlord to take care of because I did
not want the 51. notes at the time—they were part of the 171. I brought
ont that morning-it was one of those notes that the butcher got-I asked
the landlord for them, it might be half-an-hour afterwards- I was sitting
inside his parlour having some drink-he refused to give them up to me till
Mr. Waters came, and then he gave them to me-he did not say in my
hearing that I was so drunk that he would not give them to me- I was not
drunk-I do not know the landlord's name-I changed a 5l. note at that
public-house : I cannot exactly say what for-I had three bl. notes and two
sovereigns-that was about 12 o'clock, and I was robbed at about a quarter
past 12 at night I was with my brother-in-law during those twelve hours,
and I was at Mr. Waters' some time, and at Mr. Dower's, a grocer-I do
not know how many public houses I was in I had never seen either of the
prisoners before.
MR. AUSTIN. Q. Then you had five glasses of grog between 2 in the
day and 12 at night. A. Yes--I was sober when I saw the prisoners-
I swear they are the men I had 178. 6d. left in another pooket after the
robbery. .

between 12 and 1 o'clock, and the prosecutor onme to me near the Thames
Tunnel : bis waistcoat (produced) was loose and the pocket torn; his watch-
guard was swinging abont-in consequence of what he said I went with him
towards King-street, and saw Kaley run across the road-the prose-
cutor said, “That is one of the men that robbed me"-I ran after him and
brought him back, and the prosecutor identified him-I searched him on
the spot, and found in his pocket nearly half of a brick; he said, “I have
done nothing , Charley; that is for Bill Shannon, we have had a row"-
took him in custody.
