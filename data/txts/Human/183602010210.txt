 587. ALICE DANDY was indicted for stealing, on the   8th of January , 9 1/2 yards of printed cotton, value 8 s  ., the goods of Francis William Vant . FRANCIS WILLIAM VANT. I am a linen-draper, at Woolwich. On the 8th of January, the prisoner came into the shop for a few patterns of print—my young man attended to her—I went to speak to a customer, and when I came back, I said to my young man, "That young woman, I am convinced, has got something; follow her out"—she did not hear that—she went out, and my apprentice followed her. WILLIAM RICHARDS. I am the prosecutor's apprentice—I recollect the prisoner coming to the shop—I was not the person who served her—I fol lowed her by my master's direction, to a pawnbroker's, who produced the property to me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Who served her? A.  Jaber Garrett—he is not here—I cannot swear he did not sell her the cotton. THOMAS BURT. I am pawnbroker, at Woolwich. The prisoner came to my shop, and offered to pawn this print—I asked how many yards there was of it—she said she could not tell—she said she brought it from Miss Grief—while I was questioning her, Mr. Vant's apprentice came in, and claimed it. FRANCIS WILLIAM VANT re-examined . I and convinced it was not sold, for I was standing by the prisoner when she left the shop, and she had no thing but a few patterns of prints.
   Cross-examined. Q.  If so, she had not the print? A.  That was all the young man gave her—I was not present all the time she was there, but I saw the apprentice give her some patterns.
   JURY . Q.  You cannot swear your shopman did not sell it? A.  Yes, I can.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You were away from him part of the time? A.  Yes, NOT GUILTY.
