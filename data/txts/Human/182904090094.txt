what are you going to do? are you going to kill me, I am only an old man?" the prisoner had got the wood in his hand - he said, "You old b-r, I am;" he then took the wood in his hand - it was a thick piece of wood, with a sharp edge; he struck him on the side of his head, and made his head bleed - he fell directly from the blow, and the prisoner went down stairs; he did not strike him a second time -I am quite sure the prisoner picked up the wood after I had put the razor in my bosom: I am sure he must have seen me put it in my bosom - his mother was in the room at the time; he was sober enough to hear me ask my father for the razor - I went down after him, and said,"You wretch, you have knocked my father down, why don't you pick him up;" I did not stop to pick him up, but ran down is a passion after the prisoner - he said it served him right. he had no business to come up with a razor; I staid below about five minutes, and when I got up my father had walked down stairs, and laid on the bed on the first floor - this happened on the second; the prisoner's mother was sitting by his side on the bed, with an oil bottle, which she put to his head - the prisoner went and looked at my father's head; I said, "Is his head hurt" - he pushed me away, and said No, and told me not to frighten myself, he was not hurt; his mother said it was only grazed - I asked if oil was good to put to it; she said Yes, it was good for a graze; I left her with my father, and we all went to bed, and next morning, between eight and nine o'clock, the little boy was sent to tell me Mrs. Hale could not awake my father - I went into his room; he seemed as if he was asleep - this was Wednesday; he died the next day, but never opened either his eyes or mouth - he never spoke after Tuesday night; I fetched two doctors, and he was taken to the hospital, where he died.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Your father was quite sober? A. Yes, and so was I; the prisoner was not quite sober - he and his mother had been out drinking since the morning; I had been to the King and Queen public-house, Norton-falgate, and the Fleur-de-lis public-house, Magpie-alley, with the prisoner, his mother and my father - we went to the King and Queen about one or two o'clock, and staid till between six and seven; we drank gin and beer, but very little, for there was very little money - several of us were drinking together; the prisoner and my father went out to pledge some tools of my father's - he wanted 1s. to get some victuals with; we then all went to the Fleur-de-lis, and had a pot of beer and a quartern of gin - my father left his flute at the King and Queen for a quartern and a half of gin; I cohabited with the prisoner, and my father with his mother- I drank nothing after I got home; the prisoner fetched some gin for my father after he had the blow - he and his mother drank it. I did not tell the doctor my father bad fallen against the fire-place, and cut his head - I said he had been struck with a piece of wood.
 Q. Have you not said, that at the time the prisoner gave the blow your father had the open razor in his hand, and was going to cut his mother's throat? A. No; I know Boston - he is the prisoner's brother-in-law; I never told him so, nor did I tell him to tell the prisoner not to come home to sleep for fear of a row - my father did not put the razor to his mother's throat; it was the noise and vaxation that made my father talk of cutting his throat - he was a quiet man; I was sober - I did take half a glass of gin.
   SARAH HALES. I am the prisoner's mother.
