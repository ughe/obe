ALFRED HEWENS. I am an ironmonger at Ealing—on 26th May I drew, this cheque for 3 l . 10 s . 5 d . in favour of T. Turner and Co., to order, and crossed—I enclosed it with a statement of account in a letter addressed to T, Turner, Suffolk Works, Sheffield—I posted the letter myself at the Uxbridge head post-office about half-past 7 in the evening—I subsequently received a communication from Turner and Co., and they communicated with the post-office. JOHN WOOD. I am manager of T. Turner and Co.—this cheque never reached our firm—the endorsement is not that of any one belonging to us, it is a forgery. THOMAS BRLANT. I am a bicycle maker at Uxbridge-Road, Ealing—the prisoner presented this cheque for 3 l . 10 s . 5 d . to me in payment for a bicycle, the bicycle was afterwards shown to me by the police—I gave the prisoner 1 s . 5 d ., the difference between the bicycle and the cheque—the endorsement was on the cheque when presented—he gave the name of Turner. PHILIP BIGG. I am a constable attached to the Post-office—after the prisoner had been taken in custody, I searched his place, 4, Elm Cottage—I found there the two bicycles.
   The prisoner in his defence alleged that the cheques and orders were given to him at different times by a stranger to cash, who paid him for his trouble. GUILTY. — Judgment Respited.
