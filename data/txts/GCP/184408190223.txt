2196. WILLIAM EDWARD BROWN was again indicted for stealing
1 shilling, the monies of James Dobson, his master.
JAMES Dobson. I missed one of the marked shillings, in the till, and the
policeman found a marked shilling on the prisoner, at six o'clock in the even-
ing-I know nothing further-we did not count the money in the till all
day-if people came and wanted change, the money was used-there were
249. in the till in the morning-we did not count it again in the evening--the
money was paid out in the day, and it might be brought back again I was
dvised, at the station, to give the prisoner into custody that was the reason
why I did not charge him at the moment-one marked shilling was found
on him in the evening, which was one I had given to Mrs. Bell-silver had
gone out, perhaps twenty times in the day, in the course of business-we do
not keep check-books--Mrs. Bell was not before the Magistrate-she was
unwell at the time, and she was unwilling to attend.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. O. Upon your oath, did you not say
to the prisoner that you had kept Mrs. Bell back? A. No.
KATE PANCHIN BELL. Mr. Dobson brought me some money, which he
told me was marked-I was to lay it out at his shop-I laid out 24s. about
ten o'clock in the morning.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you know the prisoner? A. No-he is not an
acquaintance of mine-I was not well at the prisoner's first examination-
do not know why Mr. Dobson did not produce me at the second examination
-when I received this money of Mr. Dobson, I left it where anybody might
have access to it-I and my husband understood Mr. Dobson to say that he
went round to the till and took out a shilling to give to the prisoner to get
some laces.
ELIZABETH Dobson. I do not know anything about the shilling that was
found in the prisoner's pocket.
Cross-examined. Q. You and your husband have had some misunderstand-
ings and quarrels? A. Sometimes we have he is not more violent than
any other gentleman, I believe-he never charged me with having taken any-
thing-1 left him, and went to Hampstead, because I wanted to go into the
country a little while-he did not bring us back undressed in the middle of
the night-we were dressed, except the infant, who was undressed, as infants
are.

2197. GEORGE GRAY was indicted for stealing, at Lambeth, 2 coats,
value 25s.; 1 pair of trowsers, 108.; 2 waistcoats, ll. ; 1 pair of boots, 48.;
i handkerchief, 28. ; 3 shirts, 108.; 1 watch, 1l. ; 1 half-sovereign, 1 six-
pence, and 6 halfpence, the property of Richard Kingsmill Greenfield : and
1 waistcoat, 10s., the goods of William Hole, in his dwelling-house; and
that he had been before convicted of felony
RICHARD KINGSMILL GREENFIELD. I am & carpenter, and live at Green-
wich. I was at work at Mr. Hole's, in Waterloo-place, Surrey. On the 14th
of March I got up about five o'clock in the morning, and left the prisoner,
who was a lodger, in bed - I left all the articles stated, in a box in the room
-I returned at eight o'clock in the evening--they were all gone, and my
Watch and money-the prisoner had left an old coat, a pair of trowsers, and a
Waistcoat, and had taken mine-he promised to come back that evening to
pay his lodging, but he did not come.
Prisoner. Q. When did you miss your things ? A. When I came home
your things were destroyed about a week before you were taken.
