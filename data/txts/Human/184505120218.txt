   Cross-examined. Q.  What is your husband carrying on now? A.  The business of a broker—he has been doing so only a few months—before that he kept a shop in the general line where we now live—we dealt in vinegar at that time, and had given the prisoner one or two orders—my husband has been living in that neighbourhood about twelve years, and twelve months in our present house—Mr. Collingridge, the prosecutor, came to me three or four times—he first came to know something about us, but I cannot tell what.
   Q.  Did he say he did not care about the money for the vinegar; if it cost 300 l . he did not care, all he wanted was to get a conviction against Provo? A.  He never made use of the word "conviction"—he said it was not the money he cared for, if it was 300 l . he would rather lose it than not have the case against him—I think they were the words—I never had any charge against myself—I am not a particular friend of the prisoner's—I do not think I saw him above half a dozen of times—I have no family.
   COURT . Q.  You had had dealings with him before? A.  We had two casks, whether they were together I do not know—the prisoner knew my husband. GEORGE HALLETT. I live in Dove-row, and am a fishmonger. I have a horse and cart, which I generally go with myself—I never knew the prisoner before this—he came to me, and hired my cart for a little job—I cannot say the time exactly—I went myself with it—he told me to go to East's, in Goldsmith's-row, which is four or five yards from my place—it was about one o'clock—he was to give me 1 s . 6 d . for the job—I was to take the cart to Spitalfields church, and meet him there—I saw Mrs. East at her own place—I took the half-hogshead from East's to Spitalfields church—the prisoner was not there, but he came soon afterwards—he said, "Follow me," and he took me to Widegate-alley, and there, by his direc tion, I rolled out the half-hogshead to Mr. Sandifer's shop—it remained outside the door—the prisoner told me to go back to Mr. East's, where he would meet me and pay me—I went there—he did not come, and I new saw him till he was in custody—I saw Mr. Sandifer at the door of his shop—I did not speak to him. ROBERT SANDIFER. I am an oilman, and live in Artillery-passage, Wide gate-alley.
