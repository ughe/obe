was outside-if he was inside they were always taken in to him-I have
often known him open letters before I made any entry in the book-I did
not give the postman the additional shilling-I mentioned it to him on the

clerk there-- there were three, Smith, Allen, and Hickie-I cannot say
whether Allen was there when the man came in the cab-Smith and.

Mr. Hartley's absence, and it was usual to take them into the inner

was the only opportunity I had of seeing him-there was nothing very
singular about him-I gave a description of him-I did not describe
any singularity that I am aware of—at that time he had large whis-
kers, and stooped as he walked-they were dark whiskers, black-
I do not know whether they came towards the mouth, but they were
large and black, that was all the description I gave-nothing else-I
believe he got into the cab and went away, and Lewin Caspar re-
turned into the office-I said the man was between fifty and sixty
years of age-I did not say he was about five feet seven inches in
height- I did not hear Mitchell give a description of him I have heard
him give a description of the man, and it was very nearly the same as
mine-that he was a man with large whiskers, from five to six feet high,
and between fifty and sixty years of age- I believe Mitchell said he was
about sixty-Lewin Caspar returned into the back office after the person
left-I cannot say whether it was before the cab drove away-I remained in
the office-I saw the cab when I went out to see Moss in the cab when be
first called Mr. Smith, the clerk, got the manifest of these goods-he
generally keeps the manifests in his desk- I am not aware that any per-
son went to the box where the ship's papers were kept-it is generally
my habit to go there-consignees' letters, papers, and some parcels are
kept there-I went to the box about nine o'clock-I did not see any
body go to the ship's box-they are locked up, and the keys are hung
up near my desk-any clerk in the office could get at them-I generally
go to the box and take out the papers. at nine o'clock in the morning
the letters which are going to other parties are put in the post, the parcels
sent to the wharf, and the bills of lading, and the rest of the papers, laid on
Mr. Caspar's desk-I had laid the papers on his desk that morning, and
the letters for Mr. Hartley--that was before Lewin Caspar came into
the house.
Court. Q. You say the person you have seen this morning is the
man you saw in the cab? A. Yes-he has very small whiskers now-I
can take on myself to say he is the man.
Mr. CHAMBERS. Q. How often do the ships arrive generally? A.
Every Sunday and Wednesday morning-the ships' boxes come with the
ships--the arrival of ships depends on the state of the weather.
