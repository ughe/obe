Calcutt has said something about standing dozens and dozens of cham-
pagne. There is no truth in that in my time. It is not true that he was
the only person wbo stood champagne, The others were Loftus, Gilson,
Kemp, Haseltine, Cordey, and Lloyd. One would stand one bottle, and
one another-it went round in finger-glasses.
Cross-examined. Champagne was not always going round on contract
nights. I only remember two occasions. I knew contract nights from
other people saying "Those people there are the Guardians having a drink
on contract nights." I think Stainmers stood champagne, and Mr. New-
port, Mr. Lloyd, Kemp, Loftus, and Hirst himself stood six. There
were about 13 or 14 bottles stood that night. The drinking was kept up
about two hours. It would be about two years ago, about April I should
think. From 1903 to 1905 there were similar transactions going on.
April, 1904, was the other occasion. I know George Barker who was a
great friend of Hirst's. I have seen him in the house. I have not seen
young Barker with the Guardians on those nights, but I have seen him
with the Guardians at other times. The three bottles of champagne on
January 18, 1904, would be to take away, Haseltine was a greengrocer. I do
not think he is a Mile End man. I think I have supplied Lloyd with cham-
pagne, which was consumed outside. The handwriting in the corner of
that pags of the ledger is Mr. Hirst's. It is before my time. I went to
the "Freemasons' as licensee. The manager formerly was Harris.
There was no tenant. Harris was the leaseholder. Young Barker was
the tenant immediately before me. When I was at the Freemasons'
Arma "I got into some trouble. I had a conviction for drunkenness on
the premises and had to give up the license.
