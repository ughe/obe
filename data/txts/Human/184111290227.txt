   Cross-examined. Q.  You saw nothing happen to the prisoner's wife? A.  Not any thing—she was let go quietly along—nobody molested her in the slightest degree—Lartin was by my side when the flint stone was thrown—I saw it quite plain, and saw who threw.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Did you see the prisoner throw a flint stone? A.  Yes, before the brick was thrown. JOHN THORN. I live in James-place, near John-street; I am a stranger to all these parties. On the evening in question I was in John-street—I heard the cry of "Murder," and ran to see what was the matter—I saw the prosecutrix and her sister in the fore-court—I stopped some minutes, and saw the prisoner come out of his own house and throw the brick violently—he merely crossed the fore-court, and threw it—he did not aim at either party that I know of—the two sisters were standing together, and it struck the prosecutrix—he threw it towards them—he was five or six yards from them—I did not see him throw any thing else.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you ever said you saw him coming out and throw a large flint stone before he threw the brick? A.  No, I said a brick—I saw a flint—I did not see it thrown—they told me it was thrown—I did not tell that gentleman (Mr. Crisp) that I saw the prisoner throw a large flint stone at the persons in the court. JOHN SHEREDAN. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into cus tody, and took him to Union-hall—the prosecutrix was unable to attend—I produce a flint and brick, which I got from Margaret Mahoney. MARGARET MAHONEY re-examined . This is the flint stone which was thrown—I gave that and the brick to the officer—I picked them up before my sister was picked up. JAMES VAUGHAN. I am a surgeon at St. Thomas's Hospital. On Sun day night, the 12th of September, the prosecutrix was brought there—I examined her head, and found a scalp wound in the forehead, on the right side, extending about an inch or more—it exposed the covering of the bone—she was insensible, and was about three weeks under my care—it was a dangerous wound.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Dangerous, because it might produce erysipelas, I suppose? A.  Yes.
 (  Witnesses for the Defence .) ANTHONY BENNETT. I have been nine years a labourer in Mr. Watkins's manufactory in the Commercial-road. I was in company with the prisoner, his wife, and another young woman, on the night in question—we were standing at the corner between John-street and Bond-street—Lartin came over and said to Conlon, "What have to say to these young women, my friends?"—Conlon had not at that time said any thing to either of them—he turned round and said, "Nothing"—the prosecutrix came over and swore she would knock the prisoner's wife's brains out—she collared her by the throat, and said she would have her brains out—her mother ran over after her, and Lartin came over—I tried all I could to make peace between them—I took no part in the affray—Lartin came and struck him—I heard a cry of "Murder," ran over to the corner of John-street, and picked the prisoner up.
