2182. JOHN RILEY was indicted for stealing 38 gallons of brandy,
valde 77. 128.; the goods of the East and West India Dock Company, in a
port of entry and discharge.
SE 70 Y 1
Messrs. CLARKSON and BODKIn conducted the Prosecution.
William Owen Biggs. I am foreman of the vaults of the East and West
India Docks Company, at the Limehouse entrance. I was at my post, on
Monday, the 5th of Aug., at a quarter past six o'clock in the morning the
prisoner is a lighterman--I saw him coming out of the dock in his lug-boat,
the Orton, No. 774-my gate was the last he would have to pass before he
got into the river-he ought to have produced a pass before he passed me-I
asked him for one--he said he had not got one-his boat appeared empty-I
said he must get a pass, or be could not go out-he came on shore, and went
to get a pass-he would have to go to Anderson, the officer, at the south gate,
forit-when he had gone I went into his boat, and, on lifting up the head-sheet
boards, I found a number of bladders of brandy stowed away between the
timbers of the boat, and a basket containing a gimlet, a cock, and centre-bit
be did not return to the boat, there is no business done in the dock on
Sunday– I attend tide every day I had seen the prisoner in the dock on
Saturday evening, about a quarter to seven o'clock-that was past the time
when, according to the regulation, any boat can go in or out-he had not his
boat with him then—I had seen him on the Monday morning, before he came
with his lug-boat-he was waiting till I opened the inner gates-he could
not, according to the regulations, have had an opportunity of getting in at
that early hour--my place opens at six-the Dock Company officers took
possession of the brandy-Anderson came up, and assisted in searching the
Cross-examined by Mr. Doane. Q. When you saw him on Saturday
evening, did you see his boat? A. No-he ought to have gone to the right
to get out of the dock, but he went to the left, towards the Honduras, which
was on the north side of the north quay-she was not the only vessel that
laid there--I do not know whether this brandy has paid duty-I was not put
into prison for smuggling-I have been in the Dock Company's service seven-
teen years, and have risen to what I am from a labourer-I was in prison
falsely for a very short time—they found that out afterwards—it was not for
smuggling-it had nothing to do with brandy-I am not aware that I ever
tasted brandy which had not paid the duty, I might have done it.
MR. BODKIN. Q. What were you charged with ? A. I cannot tellit
is twenty years ago-I was admitted to bail.
JAMES ANDERSON. I am watchman to the East and West India Dock
Company. On Monday morning, the 5th of Aug., a few minutes before six
o clock, I saw the prisoner on the bridge leading over the lock to go to the
Export Dock, and then I saw him in his lug-boat coming out of the Export
Dock into the basin-I was at the gate-he asked me for a pass--I told him
I would attend to him directly-he passed on to the outer lock, so that he
got no pass-he went on towards the water-gate-he came back in a few
minutes, and said, “I am come for my pass"-he was on land, without his
boat-I said, " Come with me, and get it," it is my duty to examine the boat
before I give the pass- I was going towards the water-gate for that purpose
-he was with me--he turned his eyes towards the water-gate when he was
on the import bridge-I looked the same way, and saw Biggs in the prisoner's
