MARY ANN COWARD was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of Jan ., 1 scarf, value 2 s  .; 1 shawl, 10 s  .; 1 gown, 10 s  .; 1 bonnet, 3 s  .; 1 purse, 3 d  .; 1 cross, 1 s  .; 1 neck-chain, 2 s  .; 2 pairs of boots, 1 l  .; 1 coat, 8 s  .; 1 watch-chain, 2 l  .; 2 seals, 10 s  .; 1 watch-key, 5 s  .; 1 ring, 5 s  .; 1 brooch, 1 l  .; 2 handkerchiefs, 5 s  .; 1 half-crown, I shilling, 2 groats, and 2 pence; the property of Stephen William New ; and that she had been before con victed of felony. STEPHEN WILLIAM NEW. I am a musician, and live in Upper Mary-le bone-street—the prisoner it the daughter of my laundress—I misted a number of articles from my sitting-room, part of them on Sunday evening, the 1st of Jan., and part on the Monday—I missed a gold watch-chain, seals, key, and ring from the corner of the piano, in the sitting-room—I afterwards missed the pearl cross, the scarf, and other things stated—this is the chain, scarf, cross, purse, gown, shawl, and bonnet—they are my wife's, and were in my sitting-room. HENRY FOWLER( police-constable E  111.) I took the prisoner on the 3rd of Jan., in Tottenham-court-road—she had this scarf, shawl, gown, and bonnet on, and this chain and cross was round her neck—I took her to the station—she said, "Let me go, and take off the things, as I have got my own things under them"—they were taken off, and she had her other things under them.
   Prisoner.  They belong to Mrs. Hollis. STEPHEN WILLIAM NEW re-examined.  I kept this chain and cross in a drawer at the top of a cash-box—it was not locked—these are my wife's pro perty, who is here—her name was Hollis before she was married. ROBERT ADAMS( police-constable  109 E. ) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Transported for Seven Years .
   NEW COURT , Monday, February  6 th , 1843.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.

 759. ELIZA MATENOT and MARY ANN JOHNSON were indicted for stealing, on the   28th of Jan ., 2 hams, value 15 s  ., the goods of John Taylor ; and that they had been before convicted of felony. JOHN TAYLOR. I am an oilman, and live in Jermyn-street. At half-past eight on Saturday night the prisoners came into my shop—Matenot asked for an ounce of pepper—she gave me a fourpenny-piece—I gave change—I sus pected them—I watched, and saw a shuffling between their shawls the moment I served them—I came round the counter and missed two hams—I pursued them—they had turned up a passage, where I found them, and said, "You have got my hams; you had better give them up"—then one dropped, and then another dropped—a light was got in the house they ran into, and the two hams were found in the dark passage—I cannot say from which they fell. THOMAS MARCHANT( police-constable C  49.) I was sent for, took the prisoners, and found the hams in the passage.
