 1348. JAMES SAMS, was indicted for embezzlement. ELIZABETH SADLER. I am the wife of George Sadler, of Queen's-mews, Pimlico. On the 18th of March the prisoner came with some beef—I paid him 6 s . 5 d .—I do not know who for—I had never seen his master.
   Cross-examined by MR. WYLDE . Q.  Have you a book in which the items are marked down? A.  No—I had a ticket, but no receipt. JAMES COWELL. I am a butcher. The prisoner was in my service, and it was his duty to pay me the same day as he received money—I sent him with some meat for Mr. Sadler, on the 18th of March, which came to 6 s . 5 d .—he did not bring back the money—it was his duty to do so.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not he in the habit of taking out meat to sell to customers, which he himself only knew? A.  No—he ordered meat, and booked it to Mrs. Sadler—I did not know her then—he was not in the habit of taking out meat, selling it, and giving part of the profits to me—my wife received 2 l . odd from him on the 22nd of March—previous to this I had no customers in the Palace till he got them—I knew that he had one there of the name of Bloss—he used to receive the money from them, and account to me for it. JANE COWELL. I am the wife of James Cowell. I received 2 l . 5 s . from the prisoner, for a bill he had taken in, but not from Mrs. Sadler.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is that book in your hand? A.  The book in which I book the meat to my customers—here is the account of 2 l . 5 s .—the prisoner was not in the habit of taking out meat and selling it on our account until he got the order—he did not bring us many customers—on the 18th he said that he should leave us—I had had no settlement with him after the 18th—I asked him who was to pay the remainder of the money which he took out meat for—it amounted to 2 l . 13 s . 3 d .—he put it down to Mr. Sadler, and others. GUILTY. Aged 26.— Confined Three Months .
