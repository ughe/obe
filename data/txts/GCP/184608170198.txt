ESSEX CASES.
Before Mr. Common Sergeant,
1702. THOMAS CHURCH and JOHN BROWN were indicted for steal
ing I watch, value 11., the goods of Sarah Smith.
SARAH SMITH. I am a widow, and live at Leytonstone. On the 11th of
July I left my watch in the parlour about one or two o'clock-I was absent
from the room about five minutes-when I came back the watch was gone-
the window was open-this is my watch.
WILLIAM FRAZER. On the 11th of July, between one and two o'clock, I
saw the two prisoners near the chapel pump, at Leytonstone--I saw Brown
come out of Mrs. Smith's gate-Church was on the other side of the road
he could see Mrs. Smith's house-Brown took something out of his pocket,
and showed it to Church, and then they went away--I described the boys.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Had Brown any poultry with him?
A. No-he had no basket-you go through a gate to the house.
SAMUEL BUTLER BAKER. I am an engineer. I received information of
this loss, and a description of the boys from Frazer— I went across the forest,
and saw the prisoners- I followed them a short distance-I went and found
this watch in a spot near to where I saw the prisoners.
RICHARD LAMB. I saw the two prisoners together after this loss, they
were sitting down at a corner near where the watch was found--I saw them
about an hour and a half after the robbery, and the watch was found within
two hours.
CHURCH-Guilty. Aged 17.1
BROWN-GUILTY.
Confined Three Months.

1704. HARRIET GRIFFIN was indicted for stealing 4lbs. weight of
pork, value 28., the goods of John Barnes.
JOHN BARNES. I live at Stratford, in Essex, and sell pork. On Satur
day, the 18th of July, I saw the prisoner inside my shop about nine o'clock
at night-she inquired the price of pork-she looked out a piece, as I sup
posed, with the intention of having it weighed-she did not buy anything, to
my knowledge-I made a motion to my lad to look after her-she left the
shop, and in consequence of information I sent for a policeman-she wa
brought back to my house, and the policeman produced the pork-I knew
by some discolouration on the top of the rind it was the outside piece, and
had got stained- I told the prisoner she had got into a bad mess, and
offered to pay me for it.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. What was the weight of it!
A. I do not know exactly-it was worth more than 28.- I have known the
prisoner two years by coming to my shop-- I frequently suspected her-she
has bought things, and paid for them- there were a good many customers
this was the piece of pork the prisoner was inquiring about.
