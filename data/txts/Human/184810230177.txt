I know the prisoner—I was at his house on Sunday, 17th Sept., between two and three o'clock in the afternoon—he went to the house with me—while we were there, Mr. Caspar came—he asked the prisoner for some money—the prisoner said he had none—he offered him some German clothes instead of money—Mr. Caspar looked at them, and said they did not suit him—some further conversation took place—they walked into the next room, and had a cigar together.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL . Q.  Had you ever seen Mr. Caspar before? A.  Yes, at different places—I had seen him in Gravel-lane, coming past—I cannot say the day of the month—there was no one with me—there was a gentleman with him; I cannot say that I knew him—I did not speak to him—I know Mr. Caspar, and I knew that it was him, because one or two told me—I cannot tell you who they were that told me—Mr. Caspar did not name the sum that he came for on the 17th—there was a young woman there, who opened the door—there was me, and the prisoner, and another gentleman—I went there with the prisoner from my house in Gravel-lane,-Houndsditch—I went to see a coat at his house—I am a cigar-maker—it was not one of my cigars they had, it was one of Mr. Krakauer's—I went to see a coat for him to sell me—I went to buy a coat for myself—I did not make any agree ment what I was to give for the coat—I did not buy the coat; it did not suit me—it was a red coat.
   Jury. We have heard enough . NOT GUILTY.
