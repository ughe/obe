GEORGE HARVEY (Police Sergeant M). I accompanied Inspector Fox
and Sergernt Walsh on the night of 31st October to 42, Red Cross Street,
Borough, and saw the two prisoners there--I searched the place-amongst
other things I found two duplicates in a pocket-book on a shelf in the shop
wbere the male prisoner was sitting ; one of them relates to the opera
glasses pledged at Mr. Holman's, and the other to the sugar tongs and spoons
pledged at Mr. Layman's—in a cupboard upstairs this goblet was found in
the room where the prisoners slept.
Cross-examined. There was no obstacle to our searching the house-it
was impossible for you to throw away any; we were too strong-I did not
see you give up anything; what we have here we found on searching the
house.
MATTHEW Fox (Police Inspector M). In consequence of information
which I received from Mr. Winch, the pawnbroker's assistant, I went to
two addresses that he gave me; 128, Suffolk Street, which was a warehouse,
and to another address that was also false-I ultimately went to 42, Red Cross
Street with Walsh and Harvey I saw the male prisoner and another man
in the shop; I asked the prisoner if his name was John White-he said
sometimes he went by the name of John White, and sometimes Edward
White-I told them that I was a police officer, and I was informed that
he had been pledging stolen goods, and I had come to search his house, and
if he had any stolen property in the house it would be better for him if he
wonld tell me where it was, as I intended to search the house thoroughly
he said there was some upstairs I went upstairs with him--at the top
of the stairs he called out loudly to his wife " Police inspector and others
are come to search the house for stolen property "—he asked me to wait a
moment while his wife got out of bed, and I saw her a moment after coming
from the bed; she hurried past me, and went downstairs -- the male prisoner
then said “I made a mistake; there is no property in the house "-I took
him downstairs, and saw Harvey take the property from the woman-I
searched the house with the others, and between us we found 136 daplicates
relating to property-I saw this goblet found in a cupboard upstairs --I
took the prisoners to the station, and charged them I took the other man
at the same time, but I found no evidence to connect him with it, and he
was discharged.
Cro88-examined by Edward White. You told me of a certain person who
you said gave you the things, but what you told me was of no use I only
went into the tickets relating to three burglaries; the Magistrate would
not take any more-you showed me nothing ; you misled me throughout
or thought to do so.
Edward White's Defence. I have been in the habit of pledging for the
trade for years. I have attended sales and bought things, and all this
property I bought of dealers and one and another that I pawned for.
I had no knowlege of its being stolen property. I pawned the things
within five minutes walk of where I lived, which I should not have
done if I had a guilty knowledge of their being stolen. I should
have gone miles away, and pawned them in another name. I don't even
know where the places are that I am accused of. I was never a house-
breaker. I was once in business in India in a large way. This is the first
