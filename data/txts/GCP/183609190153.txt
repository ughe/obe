August, 4 sheets, value 21.; 2 sheets, value 11. ; 1 pair of drawers, value
30; 1 pair of trowsers, value 38. ; 4 pairs of stockings, value 4.; 4 hand-
kerchiefs, value 123.; 2 pillow-eases, value 38.; 6 towels, value 5s.; 1
table-cloth, value 128. ; 1 sheet, value 18. 1 apron, value 6d. 9 caps,
value 28.; 1 habit-shirt, value 1s. ; 4 yards of calico, value 18.; and 1
basket, value 9d. ; the goods of Mary Martin.
MARY MARTIN. I am a widow. On the 24th of August, 1835, I lived
in Sidney-street, Somers Town, and hired the prisoner to go to St. Martid's-
lane, to bring home a basket of linen-he had done so before he went
away with the linen-We found the greater part of it in pledges there
were all the things stated, in the basket--they were retumed to the owner.
JAMES Wilson. I am a shopman to Mr. Walter, a pawnbroker, in
St. James's-street. I took in some sheets, some drawers, and some other
things, in August, 1835-they have been delivered up.
CHARLES GRINHAM (police-constable 8 155.) I apprehended the pri-
soder in Wilsted-street, Somers Town, on Saturday last-I asked if his name
vus Thomas M'Carty; he said it was I told him he was charged with

any thing- I took him to the station-he said he certainly was the per-
son; if and he got over this, he should lead a different life, that bad com-
pany had brought him into it.
