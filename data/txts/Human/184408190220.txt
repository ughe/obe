I was in my garden, and heard the cry of "Stop thief", I saw the two prisoners running in a direction from the prosecutor's house, in a private road, and, whilst they were running, Dawson threw a yellow parcel away—after the prisoners were taken I went back to see what the parcel was—I was looking about for it for some time, and Henderson said, "You b—y snot  what are you looking for?"—I found three pieces of leather, and these spoons, and other plate, in the hedge—I took it up to Mr. White's.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  What are you? A.  I am out of business, and have been a publican—when Henderson spoke to me, he and Dawson had been brought back in custody of a brick-maker to where they had thrown the plate away—I gave them into the custody of a policeman at Peckham—they were stopped about forty yards from where I found the yellow parcel—I was in my garden when I first heard the cry, and got over the hurdles. JAMES BANK. I live in North-street, Lock's-fields. I saw the prisoners together against Mr. White's garden-gate, and when I got round the water, which is opposite the door, I saw Dawson go in at the window, and come out—Henderson was standing outside the gate, and could see if anybody came—they then went away together.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you a dealer in hearth-stones? A.  Yes—the garden is not very large—I saw Dawson go up to the house.

 2192. JOSEPH SINGLETON was indicted for stealing 1 gelding price 5 l  .;, the property of James Elliott . JAMES HOLMES. I am a carman, and live in Camden-street, Newington. The prisoner came to me on the 18th of July—he said he wanted a horse to draw a carriage from Parliament-street to Mr. Herriot's—my man harnessed the horse, and went through to get the whip, and when he came back the prisoner and the horse were gone—I had not let it to the prisoner, because the man went through to get the whip—it was about twelve o'clock in the day, and at three the next day I found the horse in Smithfield—it was a gelding, and was the property of Mr. James Elliott—he sent it to my stable, as he had no work for it—the prisoner might have had it if he had asked for it—he said he was to have 6 s . for the job—he was to have 2 s . of it, and 14 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Was it a man of the name of Lane the prisoner was to have it for? A.  No, he did not mention the name of Lane as the person, to my recollection—I did not put him on the horse my self—I said he might do without a whip. GEORGE BLUNT. I am a greengrocer, and live in Hill-place, Ray-street.
