and another one likewise—I was half-a-sovereign in debt to the parties I was play ing with—I asked the prisoner to let me have another half-sovereign—he said, "Yes," and he took out 30 s ., and put it on the table, and said, "Take that, you will win your money back"—he did not bring change for the check—he said he could not see his friend—he brought the check back—he did not give it to me—I asked him for it back—I asked him if he would take my watch and let me have the check—I had my watch—he was to take that as security for 2 l .—he said, "No, I do not like watches"—he said the check was as safe with him as it was with me, and he told me to meet him in the morning at the bankers—I went on playing till I lost 4 l . or 5 l .—the prisoner still lent the money—as soon as I had lost that, I said I should leave—the prisoner persuaded me to go on further, and ultimately he joined in the game himself, to induce me to play on—I sometimes won a little, and sometimes I lost—I went on till I lost as much as 10 l ., for which sum I was indebted to the prisoner—I staid at the house till about eight or nine o'clock—I was to meet him next morning at the bankers, at half-past nine o'clock—I was there by a quarter-past nine o'clock—the prisoner did not come—I waited till about ten o'clock—I then went to his house—he was not at home—I sat down in the bar, and the parties who were there, said he was gone to meet me at the bank—I said, "It is no use my going, he will miss me; I will wait"—I waited about three-quarters of an hour—he came home and told me he had lost his cash-box, with my check in it, and 15 l . or 20 l . of his own money—he did not say anything about the money I owed him—I went to the bankers—I got some of my money back again in the evening of the 27th of April—I had given the prisoner in charge that morning—I got back 191 l . from Leary and Wyatt.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you recollect that evening, that you gave the prisoner the check for the purpose of being changed? A.  Yes, that was the object for which I gave it him—he did not tell me when he came back, that he had been to the bank, and was too late—I parted with the check for the purpose of getting it changed into money—I should say I was sober throughout—I am a Suffolk man—I have been in London a few times—I have not been in the habit of going to public-houses—I never played at skittles, or tossed up  before in my life—on the following morning, when the prisoner told me he had been robbed of his cash-box, with my check and money of his own, he wanted to go to the Police-station to give information—I did not ask him, before he said anything to me, if he could get the check changed—I only said I had one—I should have left the house—he put that forward that he would get it changed for me—I am not positive whether he produced the check when he returned—this is the watch which I wanted to deposit—it is silver—I went on to borrow a sum of 10 l . in the whole—I afterwards borrowed 10 s ., and left the house.
