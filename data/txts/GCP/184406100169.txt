1754. WILLIAM WHEELER was indicted for stealing 4 candlesticks,
value 17s. ; 2 decanters, 108.; and 2 pairs of shoes, 48.; the goods of Thomas
Teasdale.
CHRISTIANA TEASDALE. I am the wife of Thomas Teasdale, of Lisson-grove.
The prisoner called several times-he said he was a dealer in different articles,
and he came on the 1st of June and wished me to let him have some shawls
to show to some customer-I went out with him to show these shawls to his
customer-he wished me to go on and said he would overtake me—he told
me to go next door to Mr. Batton's, a pawnbroker, in John-street, Edgeware-
road—I went, and found it was a rag and bottle-shop on one side, and a
chemist's on the other-he did not overtake me, but I went and inquired if
any one there wanted any shawls, and there was no one wanted any-I went
back to my shop and missed these articles—I did not see the prisoner again
till he was at the Police Court, but I had seen him several times before that
day.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Had he bought any articles of
you? A. Yes, twice.
MARY TEASDALE. I am daughter of Christiana Teasdale. On the 1st of
June the prisoner and my mother left the shop together—in about two
minutes he came back-he said, "O, they want to see the candlesticks and
the decanters”—he took one pair of candlesticks and the decanters—I handed
him another pair of candlesticks—he went down the opposite street-in about
two minutes he came back again, and said, “There is one thing I have forgot-
ten, and that is the shoes”—he took one pair, I handed him another pair,
and he went away down the same street-I did not see him again.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you see the candlesticks again ? A. On
the Wednesday after, the prisoner had often been to my mother's shop, but
he had only purchased things twice.
Williau Ross (police-constable D 157.) I went to No. 7, Brownlow.
street, Lock's Fields, Walworth-I saw the prisoner there, and said I wanted
him for obtaining two pairs of plated candlesticks, a pair of cut decanters, and
two pairs of men's shoes, at Mrs. Teasdale's, at Lisson-grove-he said he had
them, but he intended to pay for them—another officer, who was with me,
found 105 duplicates at his lodging.
WILLIAM BRYAN. I live with Mr. Attenborough, a pawnbroker. The
prisoner pawned the candlesticks first, be returned in about half-an-hour,
and asked if we took in decanters-I told him we did, and I am not sure
whether he went away or took them out of his pocket-he pawned them in
the name of John Smith, on Saturday night, the 1st of June.
CHRISTIANA Teasdale. These are mine--the decanters were in my house
that morning, and the candlesticks were there some days before—I did not
send the prisoner back after I first left my shop.
IL SOTT
(The prisoner received a good character.)
