CORNELIUS METCALF STUART BABINGTON. I am house-surgeon at 8
George's Hospital. I saw the deceased there on the 19th of September
he was admitted on the 17th-he had a fracture of both bones of the right
leg—the larger bone had protruded through the skin--there was a woundd
about an inch in extent on the fore part of the leg, from the protrusion of the
bone-I attended him till the 11th of October, when he died in consequence
of the accident-I made a post-mortem examination, and found he died
with abscesses on the liver and lungs, arising from the fracture of the leg
-I judge of that from many cases I have seen before of the same kind, i
which the matter is taken up from the leg to the liver and lungs--I har
no doubt whatever that the injury of the leg was the original caused
death-the fracture might have been produced by many causes-
there was no disease of the bone to cause it to give way suddenly,
only the bones of old persons give way more easily than others--there
was a good deal of external bruising around the fracture, about the middle
of the leg.
Cross-examined. Q. Might the fracture be occasioned by a sudden til
on the floor, without any thing else ? A. Yes, the deceased's constitution
was very much broken-if he had been of an unbroken constitution I think
he would have recovered.
