OLD COURT.-Thursday, March 9th, 1899.
Before Mr. Justice Bigham.
232. GEORGE VINCE was charged, on the Coroner's inquisition
only, with the manslaughter of Violet Vince.
Mr. Avory, for the prosecution, offered no evidence. Not Guilty.
233. JAMES TURNBULL (23), Feloniously killing and slaying
William John Elvin.
MR. DRAKE Prosecuted, and Mr. Beard Defended.
The Jury, under the direction of the learned Judge, stopped the case, and
returned a verdict of Nor Guilty.
234. FLORRIE PERRY (19) and CHARLES WARD (21),
Causing to be received by Frank Howes a letter demanding money with
menaces.
Mr. Boyd Prosecuted. Mr. A. HOTTON defended Ward.
FRANK Howes. I am a clerk of 57, Shakespeare Road, Acton-the
girl Perry came to my house on February 6th, and remained there til
February 13th as a lodger-she left on the morning of February 13th-
she was turned out by my wife because she did not pay her way-she did
not pay what she owed when she left-on the evening of the 13th my
wife handed me an unopened letter in a envelope which I cannot find ; it
was addressed to me and I think marked “Private "-I found this letter
inside, “Sir, I was very much surprised to hear of your extremely dis-
gra eful conduct towards Miss F. Perry, a great friend of mine. What is
the meaning of it! You are probably aware that a term of imprisonment
is the penalty for a criminal offence or even the attempting of such an
uffence. But before taking any action in the matter I think it only right
that you should have an opportunity of settling the matter privately, and
you ought at least to pay the girl a sum of money, say £2 or so. I must
add that if you don't agree to settle the matter in this way I
shall certainly call and see you (or your wife), and if I do you
may rely upon my doing my utmost to make matters 'horribly
unpleasant' for you. Lest you should think I am writing this merely
to frighten you, I must add that I have several friends (solicitors
by profession) who would advise me what steps to take on behalf of
Miss Perry, and I have also the necessary capital, and in the event of your
not coming to some terms privately with the girl you will have cause to
regret it. --Yours faithfully, A FRIEND”-on the evening of the next day
I saw Perry at the door of another house in the street-I went down and
said to her, "I want to know who wrote that letter"-she refused to tell
me-I said, "There are ways and means of making you tell ”-a police-
sergant came up, and I asked him if he would wait and I would show him
a le ter which this girl had caused to be written -the girl was present
then I went indoors and got the letter, which I showed to the policeman,
who then took her into custody-I never went into the girl's bedroom
when she was in my house - I was never guilty of any impropriety- I did
not speak ten words to her.
Cross-examined by Perry. I did not ask you to go out with me- I did
not go into your room.
