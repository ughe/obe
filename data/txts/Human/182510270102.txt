MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you look for Hughes in the prison before you went? A. No.
 HUGHES. Q. Did you not see me come to the office the next morning early for my day-rule? A. Yes.
 COURT. Q. You say you did not find Mr. Hughes at Speldhurst-street? A. I should think we got to Speldburst-street in three-quarters of an hour - we did not search the house, nor look into the rooms up stairs - we did not let them know what our business was - I will not swear that he was not there.
 ROBERT WARD. I am a turnkey of the King's Bench. I went with the last witness and Moody to Speldhurst-street - I saw Watts there - the house is situated at the bottom of the street, and one door one way, and the other the other, so that I did not hear what passed between them - I do not know whether Hughes was there - we went into the shop and the back-parlour, but did not attempt to look whether he was in any other part of the house - it would take Moody perhaps an hour and a half to go from there to the King's Bench and back again.
   ISABELLA HURST. I recollect Moody coming to our lockup house in custody - I do not recollect the day, but it was in Hillary Term. I recollect Mr. Gilman, or Lucas, or Copling, coming to bail him - I do not know for what sum he was in - it was at the suit of Hughes; I do not know who paid his expenses - nor whether Gilman, Lucas, or Copling, gave him any money to pay them; I do not remember Moody stating they would bail him to the extent of 1000 l. - he said they would bail him, but I do not know to what amount. I did not hear him say Gilman or Copling must bail him, as they could do nothing against Hughes without him.
   JOHN HAYES. I was assistant-collector of taxes to Skinner's estate. I now live at No. 16, Wellington-square, near the Foundling. I am a broker; I distrained for rent in January last, on Mr. Hughes. I know Mr. Miller and his wife - it was on account of some information given by them that I distrained; I distrained on the goods of Mrs. Miller, in the name of Turner, at No. 3, Speldhurst-street, at the suit of Mrs. Hughes. I have heard Mrs. Miller say, in consequence of his distraining on them, she would not leave the house, but stay, and do him all the injury she could - she stated she had been to several of his creditors, and she would go to them all as fast as she could find them out, and state he was deceiving them, and did not mean to pay for the goods he had of them - she caused the tax-collector to be informed, that unless he distrained for taxes, he could not get them - he is now dead. I cannot state any of her particular words, but there were many others to the same effect, that she would do him what injury she could.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long have you ceased to be tax-collector? A. I was only deputy while the collector was ill, about six months ago, for the paving rate on Skinner's estate; I distrained on this woman for, I believe, seven or eight weeks - I believe it was 3 l. or 4 l.; she was not at all out of humour with me - she was out of humour with Mr. Hughes a little; I think she was rather offended, and when I told her she had better leave the house, she said she would stay, and do him what injury she could, and go to the creditors, and give them some information; according to the receipt, this seems to have been on the 24th of January; she produced a receipt for a quarter's rent at the time, and said she was very ill used in being taken for weekly rent before the quarter came round.
   JAMES BACON. I live in the Commercial-road, Lambeth. I have known Colyer sixteen or seventeen months. In April last I bought some carcases of houses of him - the roofs were on two of them, but not the slates; I paid him 215 l., by bills at different dates; I have paid him 75 l. of it; I think he had them about September or October, 1824, I saw them carcased; I know they were his in the month of November, when they were in the same state.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Where were they? A. In North Wharf-road - Mr. Colyer lived within one or two houses of them.
 COURT. Q. How many bills were there? A. There was one for 15 l.; two for 50 l.; and one for 10 l.; the whole money was not to go to Colyer; there was a ground landlord, and a Mrs. Garratt had a lien on them; Colyer was to have about 15 l., which was the first bill and I have paid it - it was a bill at two months from the 26th of April, and I have paid it.
 AMBROSE PARISH re-examined. Q. Was John Colyer in your custody? A. Yes, in January.
 COLYER to MR. BACON. Q. Do you recollect my building a large public-house in the North-road? A. I recollect the house, but I do not know that you built it.
 Q. Do you recollect my having two houses adjoining that house which you bought of me? A. I know nothing about them, they were built before I left the place.

 SAMUEL HAY -    GUILTY. Confined Two Years.
 WATTS - GUILTY.

 SARAH HAY -    NOT GUILTY.
 BREWER -    NOT GUILTY.

 MR. ANDREWS conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM WELLS. I am an officer of the Common Pleas. I have an affidavit made by a Mr. Leslie in February, 1825.
   JOSHUA PLATT. I attend the Lord Chief Justice in the Common Pleas. I saw a person sign this affidavit, and I have witnessed it. I have no recollection of the person.
   WILLIAM ROBINSON. I know Mr. Leslie. I have seen him write, but I do not know his writing - I know this signature to be Mr. Leslie's.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Have you ever seen him write? A. Yes, once or twice. I will not swear to this writing.
