Was in order to see whether the profits had been sufficient to enable them
to declare an interim dividend I am not clear that the statement as
prepared by me was in the hands of the board, and considered by them
for some timo-I remember Mr. Wain and I and Mr. Beall going through
the account afterwards--you may call it a cimmittee from the board-it
was not considered by the full board the committee of the board met, I
suppose, before we came to a final settlement of the account-I was
brought into the board-room, and certain suggestions were made, and
they were carried out-the official half-year ended on September 13th, 80
that I had from then to Cctober 3rd to prepare this account and adjust
it-I was instructed to take certain items out, and to spread them over
- that is to say, items such as those I have given you-it was considered
unfair to charge the fir-t half-year's working with these items-the
decision they arrived at was that the whole of the preliminary expenses
could not possibly be debited against the trading for six months-right
through to the end you will find the secretary presented a statement";
that is simply a minute in the book; he did not do so; I simply handed it
in ; I was not there when it was presented--"The secretary presented
a statement for the financial half-year ending September 13th, showing
gross profits £2,785 48. 1d., and net profits £1,269 13s. 4d."-I wrote
that minute from the agenda, of course, not that day, the minutes were
always written a week afterwards, not on the day, but at the time I
entered this up I had no reason to doubt that it was right-the figures
were arrived at after the board had dissected the accounts-1 never
presented any such account with those figures--there is no account made
up after the figures were dissected by the board the figures were added
and the additions were made afterwards, and it was left in the board.
room-I did not make the additions-I did not make the alterations-
the statement was simply altered and retained by the board-it was in
the possession of the board, I know I saw it afterwards, certainly, but
only the totals from the agenda paper, which was always signed by the
chairman, and from that I entered up the minute-if the chairman was
not there on the day, the chairman who officiated for the day would sign
it-it never came back into the outer office-I did not make the altera-
tions-I made the minute from what I found on the agenda-in February
of 1895 I was examined before Lord Stormonth Darling in Scotland in the
liquidation proceedings of the London and Seottish Bank (Exhibit258, read)-
I was being asked about the statement which I hid referred to as having
been presented according to this minute, showing a profit of £1,269 13s. 4d.;
it is the same statement in an altered form--my answer then was: "It was
in the hands of the board; it was presented duly to the board "-I say
still, undoubtedly, but in an altered form, it was not altered outside at
all; it was altered in the board-room—the minutes were altogether wrong
when they say that the secretary signed it-I say I presented a statement;
it was altered by the directors at the board-room, and I never saw it-
