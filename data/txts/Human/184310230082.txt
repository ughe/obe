   Cross-examined. Q.  The name of the firm was Felgate and Wood? A.  Yes—I was employed by the prisoner, not by Felgate and Wood—from July the business was carried on in the name of Wood and Co.—no such person as Felgate was ever there while I was there—I came at the latter end of July—I have never seen any person named Felgate—I never heard that such a person had been there.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Was this man that was called Wood paid as servant? A.  I never saw him paid. CHARLES WHITE. I am a coachmaker, and live in the Westminster-road—I know the prisoner—I saw him in March, 1842—I knew him as John Charles Ridgway—I never knew him by the name of Felgate or Wood.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You knew him some years ago I suppose? A.  I knew him these two years. THOMAS SMITH, jun. ( re-examined. ) When I went to the shop, I think they were about opening it, in the name of Felgate and Wood—I can not say whether that name was on the door—I have seen it on the door—I went there perhaps two or three times after this—when I first went, they was putting the stock in the shop—I then went to get the bill accepted—that was somewhere about the time the bill was drawn—I think the shop had not opened at that time—there were two or three bedsteads put up, and upholstery—I do sot know that there was any very great quantity.
