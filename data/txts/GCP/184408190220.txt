51., the property of James Elliott.
JAMES HOLMEs. I am a earman, and live in Camden-street, Newingtoi.
The prisoner came to me on the 18th of July-he said he wanted a horse to
draw a carriage from Parliament-street to Mr. Herriot's my man barnessed
the horse, and went through to get the whip, and when he came back the
prisoner and the horse were gone-I had not let it to the prisoner, because
the man went through to get the whip-it was about twelve o'clock in the
day, and at three the next day I found the horse in Smithfield-it was a
gelding, and was the property of Mr. James Elliott-he sent it to my stable,
as he had no work for it--the prisoner might have had it if he had asked for
it-he said he was to have 68. for the job-he was to have 2s. of it, and I 46.
Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK. Q. Was it a man of the name of Lane
the prisoner was to have it for ? A. No, he did not mention the name of
Lane as the person, to my recollection- I did not put him on the horse my
self-I said he might do without a whip.
