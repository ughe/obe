GEORGE HOLMES (Sub-Inspector of Royal Irish Constabulary, Tralee).
At the end of December I received a communication about a cask that was
expected to arrive there on January 5 I saw a cask addressed to J. S.
Egan at the station, and on the 9th I took possession of it; it was opened
in my presence-it contained nine rifles and bayonets, and 180 rounds of
ball ammunition-I took it to the barracks, and brought it over here.

GARRETT REDMOND (Re-examined). On December 30, between 8 and 9
at night, I was at the Great Western Station, Paddington-Gallagher
pointed out to me a case addressed "M. Glin, Esq., Kilrush, County Clare” |
-it was moved into the office and opened-it contained 15 rifles, with
bayonets, and 28 packages of 10 rounds each for Sniders—the stocks were
cut and tied up, and marked with a shamrock-I took out one rifle and
four packages of ammunition; the rest were re-packed in the case to be sent
on-I travelled in the same train, and saw the same case put on board the
boat for Waterford, and again at Waterford, where I pointed it out to
Keaveny-I produce the rifle and packet that I took out-I marked the
others, and they were brought to London.
THOMAS KEAVENY. On December 30 I went to Waterford, and on
January 2 saw on the quay a case addressed “M. Glin, Esq., Kilrush;" and
two hours afterwards I saw it again at the railway station it was put into
a truck to be forwarded to Limerick, where I saw it again, and told a con-
stable to look after it-Next morning I saw it alongside the wharf, and
went by the steamer to Kilrush, where I saw it put on the quay, and
pointed it out to a constable there.
HENRY GEORGE SUPPLE I am local manager for the Lower Shanonn
