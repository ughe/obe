   Cross-examined. Q.  Where have you come from? A.  From Newgate—I have been there a little more than a week—I have not been visited by any body—I once lived next door to the prisoner—he is a housekeeper—he bore a good character—other boys played at this dolly as well as me, and they won sometimes—it is all by chance—when Mr. Fuller said if I would tell him the truth he would do what he could to get me off, I then told him the truth—I do not know that if I get the prisoner convicted that I am to walk home—I have no expectation about that—my master said if I told him the truth he would get me off—I was with Mr. Robinson, a shoemaker, for two years—I left him because I did not like the trade—I live with my mother, in Webb-street—she has not been to see me in Newgate—Mr. Fuller came for me to go before the Grand Jury—the prisoner gave me 1 s ., but he did not tell me to bring him a shilling's worth of this particular stuff—I told him I was at Mr. Fuller's—I know the prisoner uses things of this sort—he did not say, "As you are coming by my stall, bring me so much"—he told me to bring some, and he would pay me—I do not know what this oil of lavander was worth—I do not serve in the shop more than a pennyworth of anything—I did not tell him that this was damaged oil.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Was the 1 s . the prisoner gave you for that laven der alone? A.  No, for the three articles that I took him, 6 d . on Christmas-day and 6 d . on boxing-day—nothing had been said to me about the price I was to have. WILLIAM ADSHEAD. I live with my father, in Prince's-row, Walworth. I have been in the prisoner's shop before and since Christmas—I remember seeing Weatherhead there one Sunday, about Christmas, and the prisoner passed 6 d . to him—I have seen Weatherhead play with the dolly there several times. JOHN ADSHEAD. I am Mr. Adshead's brother. I know the prisoner—I have seen Weatherhead at his house three or four times. GEORGE BALLARD. I live in Portland-street, Walworth. I have seen Weatherhead at the prisoner's house, both before and since Christmas—I have seen him playing at the dolly, and have seen him take parcels there, but I do not know what was in them.
