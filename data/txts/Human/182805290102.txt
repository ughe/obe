 1317.  SOPHIA FITZPATRICK was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of May , 3 sovereigns, the monies of John Butcher , from his person.
 JOHN BUTCHER. I am a  watchman. On Thursday last I was in Bagnigge-wells-Road, between five and six o'clock in the afternoon; the prisoner came up to me, took hold of my arm, put her hand in my coat pocket and took out three sovereigns, rolled up in a dirty piece of cloth - I felt her hand in my pocket - I put my hand into my pocket and missed my money - I followed her into the Wells, but she had turned a corner and I missed her; I came out, went round and went in again; I then met her and charged her with robbing me of three sovereigns; I gave her in charge of Barnes, who took one sovereign from her hand; an officer took her into a room and found two more sovereigns on her; she had laid hold of my arm - my money was safe just before I met her.
   JOSEPH HENRY BARNES. I am an appraiser and was at Bagnigge-wells. Butcher said he had lost a sovereign; I took hold of the prisoner's hand; some persons came up and wanted to let her go; I saw her hand behind her, took hold of it, and took the sovereign out of it; the officer came up and found the other two sovereigns and a piece of rag; she said "It makes no odds to me whether he gave me a sovereign or a shilling." I said "Where are the other two?" She said she had no more.
   AUSTIN HARLE. I am an officer. I was sent for - the prisoner said nothing to the charge; I found two sovereigns and a dirty rag on her.
 JOHN BUTCHER. This is like my piece of rag.
 Prisoners' Defence. He asked me to accompany him to the gardens, and when there he asked me to go with him, and he would give me three shillings; he gave me this and I found it was three sovereigns.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Life.
 1318.  EDWARD COOKE was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of May , 9 lbs. weight of horse-hair, value 35s.; 1 coat, value 10s.; 2 pairs of pantaloons, value 17s.; 1 waistcoat, value 18d.; and 1 handkerchief, value 1d. the goods of John Salter.
   JOSEPH LLOYD. I am a watchman. I met the prisoner on Sunday evening, the 4th of May, about ten o'clock, at the top of East-street, Hoxton, about two hundred yards from Mr. Salter's with a bundle - I asked what was in it, he said some washing; I took it from under his arm, and seeing some hair and the rest of these articles in it, I took him to the watch-house.
 JOHN SALTER. I am a  horse-hair manufacturer, and live at Hoxton. On Sunday, the 4th of May, I went out at four o'clock in the afternoon, leaving my door on the single lock; I came home about eleven o'clock at night - I had drunk rather more than had done me good; the door was locked - I went in and went up to bed - my wife perceived the things disturbed; we had left nobody at home; we looked and missed these articles from the first-floor front room.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Had you been drinking before you left home? A. I had taken a little - I know I pulled the door too.
   CHARLES CONSTABLE. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house; I searched him and found some picklock keys, a crow-bar, and phosphorus box on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been drinking - I heard a cry, ran out to see what it was, and these things lay in the road - I took them up.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
