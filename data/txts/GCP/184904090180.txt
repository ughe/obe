CHRISTIAN Riger. I live at 3, London-buildings, Whitecross-street, on
the first floor. The street door is usually left open--the landlord does not
live there on 10th April I went out with my wife, about half-past seven
o'clock--she fastened the door-we returned about twelve, and found the
door broken open, and missed these pictures and other things they are mine.
Esther RIGER. I went out with my husband, and fastened the door with
a key-I came back, and missed these things. ita
CATHERINE PEARCE. I live in this house, on the ground-floor. On the
night of 10th April, between eight and nine, I saw two boys come out of
Mr. Riger's passage--one escaped-I laid hold of the prisoner, and said,
"You have been robbing the place" he said, “Oh, Mrs. Pearce, let me
go, I will bring the things back I gave him to my husband.
JOHN PEDRO. I am an iron-dealer, of 5, London-buildings. I took
the prisoner from Mr. Pearce--he asked to be let go, and he would show
where the things were I accompanied him to 4, Broad-yard, and he pro-
duced these things.

MICHAEL HAYDON (City-policeman, 21). I saw the prisoners together for
a quarter of an hour previous-I saw March put his hand into Mr. Cotterill's
pocket, draw something out, and put it into his left trowsers pocket-I laid
hold of his hand; he took it out, and I took this handkerchief from it-Smart
stood so as to conceal him from the view of persons passing-they had made
several attempts alternately before that. to
JOHN SPITTLE (City-paliceman, 9). I was with Haydon, and saw what he
describes--both prisoners were trying to pick pockets.
Smart's Defence. March came running up to me, and said he had found a
handkerchief, and put it into his pocket.
