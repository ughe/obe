MATTHEW KIGHT. I keep the British coffee-house, Blackfriars-road. On Tuesday, 25th Sept., a young man of respectable appearance brought a desk to my house, and asked me if I would oblige him by letting it rem in till the afternoon—I afterwards saw that young man speaking to Lawes at the police-court—I gave the desk to Russell. GEORGE RUSSELL( City-policeman , 34). On Tuesday afternoon, 25th Sept., I went to Kight's coffee-house—I received this desk ( produced )—Kight was present—I found these papers and books in it ( produced ). ARTHUR GEORGE HALL. I am a clerk in the Commercial Bank of London. The offices are now in Lothbury—they were removed there in 1840 or 1841, I believe, but I have only been there three or four years—I know Lawes as a former customer—the last transaction he had with us was on 13th April, 1848, as far as my memory serves—this check-book ( produced; found in the desk ) is one of ours—no doubt he had a check-book when he had an account with us—I have seen the writing on the counterfoils in this check-book—I believe it to be Lawes' to the best of my belief—this memo randum book ( produced; also from the desk ) is the one that was issued to Lawes when he was a customer, and this pass-book ( also from the desk ) was issued to him—the entries in this memorandum-book appear to be in Lawes' writing—this check was presented for payment on 4th Sept., and was refused—we have no customer named Charles Fraser.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have change'd the colouring and lithographing of your check-books since my client had the book? A.  Yes; these two checks are totally different, but still they belong to the same bank—I do not suppose they came out of this book—we sometimes pay post-dated checks by accident—I can say I did not pay a check of 12 l . 10 s . for Mr. Lawes' on 4th May, 1849—I inspected the ledger this morning, and Lawes' account seemed to be almost closed—the account was overdrawn—I do not recollect how much—he began with 200 l . JOHN HARVEY( policeman, G  14). I apprehended Lawes in Milk-street, on 22d Sept.—I afterwards took Fraser, searched him, and found these papers on him ( marked A and B. ) HENRY FLIGHT. I am a share-broker. I know Fraser, and have seen two or three letters from him—the writing in these memorandum-books, "A" and" B," I believe to be his.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you ever seen Fraser write? A.  No, I merely judge from three or four letters of inquiry I have received, purporting to come from Fraser—I have not acted upon those letters—I talked to Fraser about them.
