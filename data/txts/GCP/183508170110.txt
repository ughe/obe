COURT. Q. When did you first see this bill? A. To-day-I came at
the prisoner's request to see the bill, to speak to his handwriting it was
never shown to me.
SAMUEL WHITE. I am agent in London for the prosecutors- I recollect
Ashworth coming to my warchouse before the prisoner's committal-he told
me he had no doubt the prisoner had forged the acceptance in question.
and gave as his reason that he had had to take up an acceptance for 201.,
forged for him about a month before the conversation--we were speaking
of the acceptances of George Ormerod and David Lord-he came into our
warehouse, and said I had sent for him ; but I had not-Thomas M-Mahon
was in the warehouse at the time-he said he had not the least doubt that
the acceptance was forged; for he knew that the prisoner had taken up a
201. forged bill lying in other parties' hands in the country; and that was
about a month before.
MR. KEWNEY re-examined. I received the bill in their warehouse in
the City.

1789. RICHARD NEWCOMB, CHARLES STOKEY, EDWARD
HOOKER, and GEORGE GOODWIN were indicted for stealing, on the
4th of July, at St. Marylebone, 2 pelisses, value 17. 58. ; 4 frocks, value
1l.; 1 shawl, value 11. 155. ; 8 yards of silk, value 1l. 10s. ; 1 apron,
value 38.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 58.; of a yard of crape, value 18d.;
1 yard of bombazin, value 38.; and 1 pair of shoes, value 2s. ; the goods
of George Roberts, in his dwelling-house.
GEORGE ROBERTS. I keep the Portland Arms, in Wilson-street, Dorset-
square. On Saturday, the 4th of July, between five and six o'clock in the
evening, the four prisoners came to my house, with another young man not
present; they went into the back parlour-two young men, (Cope and
Hurd) had been in that room that day, besides my own family; they were
in the room when the prisoners entered-nobody else had been in the room that
day-they had a pot of half-and-half, and, I beliere, a pint of half-and-half
-Hurd and Cope came out of the room soon after the prisoners went in
Newcomb afterwards came out, and went away-after that Stokey, and
the man who is not here, came out and went away: and after that Hooker
and Goodwin came out, and drank a pint of half-and-half at the bar; but
before they came out, they rang the bell, and I answered it, I went into
the room, and they ordered a pint of half-and-half-I did not immediately
leave the room to get it; and they said “A pint of half-and-half two or three
times, in a hurried manner-I left the room to get it, and they followed me
to the bar, and drank it-I observed nothing particular when I went into
the room—they went away immediately I went into the parlour the mo-
ment they left, having suspicion, and observed a small piece of bombazin
and a piece of crape on the floor, and a piece of silk hanging out of the
drawer-I could not have seen the drawer from the part of the room I was
in when I answered the bell ; and I cannot say whether these things were
there, as the drawers were on the other side of the bagatelle-board--I could
not see any thing if it had been on the floor, where I was- I went to the
bar to Mrs. Roberts, and informed her; and then we went together to the
parlour-I found all the drawers locked except one, and the key was not
there I believe it had been left in the drawer that morning, I sent for a
locksmith; he opened one drawer, and Mrs. Roberts missed several things
