Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. It was not usual to account
for the sale of particular portions of liquor? A. Certainly not-I er.
pected, if anything was sold, that the money should be put into the till
there generally is enough money in the till to give change-I had told him
there was a great deficiency in beer and gin--the policeman saw him at
balf-past eleven-I had seen the till three or four times in the course of
the half hour, for I was putting marked money in- I cannot tell how
much was in the till or on the hob at half-past eleven-I was clearing the
till as fast as possible.
MR. Espinasse. Q. Can you say, on the 8th of October, whether
there was sufficient in the till or on the hob to give change for the sove-
reign? A. I should say there was.
Edward WANDERER Townson (police-constable K 143.) I was at Mr.
Gray's at half-past eleven in the morning-I saw the prisoner serving at the
bar-I saw Mrs. Thompson go in-she called for a pint of gin---the pri-
soner served her-she gave him a sovereign-he put it into his waistcoat-
pocket, took some change from his pocket, and gave her-- I watched him
for twenty minutes after the woman went away, to see if he appropriated
it to the till-he did not-Thompson came back, and he gave her a 6d,
which he took from the till-I took him into custody, and cautioned him
against saying anything I asked where he got the money-he said, from
Mr. Gray-Mr. Gray gave him the shilling-he did not say anything
about this-another officer found a sovereign.
ANN THOMPSON. On the 8th of October I went to Mr. Gray's for
a pint of gin-it came to 1s. 4d.--the prisoner served me-I gave him a
sovereign-he put it into his left hand trowsers' pocket-he gave me one
half sovereign, and the rest in silver, out of his pocket-he gave me
6d. short at first, and then he gave it me out of the till.
Henry JOHN PARKER (police-constable K 10.) I asked the prisoner
what money he had about him-he said "A sovereign, two shillings, and
two sixpences."
GUILTY. Aged 18.-Confined Nine Months.
(There were two other indictments against the prisoner.)

3022. ELIZA WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of
October, 2lbs. 10oz. weight of ham, value 18. 6d., the goods of John
Loveridge.
ELIZABETH JEFFRIES. I live in Shouldham-street. On Wednesday
afternoon, the 19th of October, I saw the prisoner take a piece of ham
from the prosecutor's shop.
CHARLOTTE GILES. The prisoner came to my room that day, and
asked me to let her stop for a few minutes, she would make me a recom-
pense-I pushed her down stairs, and the prosecutor stood there with
ham in his hand.
RICHARD LOVERIDGE. I am the son of John Loveridge. I followed
the prisoner up to this place, and saw her throw this ham away-1 stood
till she came out, and I took her-it was my father's.
Prisoner's Defence. Distress caused me to do it.
GUILTY. Aged 40.-Confined Three Months.
