1208. JAMES M`NAIR, stealing 25 cigars and 4 oz. weight of tobacco.
value 4s. 2d. ; 8 half-crowns, 60 pence, and 120 halfpence; the property of
John Hippisley, in his dwelling-house, and afterwards burglariously breaking
out of the said dwelling-house. MR. BIRNIE conducted the Prosecution.
ROBERT RATLEY. I was formerly in the V. division of police. About
three o'clock in the morning of 18th April, I was on duty in Southampton-
street, Lambeth, and heard some one walking in the yard at the back of the
Southampton Arms public-house-I pulled myself up to the top of a side
gate, looked over, and saw the prisoner coming down the stone-steps leading
from the house into the yard - I could see him plainly; I have no doubt
about him-I have known him for years, he is the son of the person who used
to keep the house-I got down from the gate and went back for three or four
yards, and he came and put his hands on the top of the gate, and his feet on
the top of the bar, and got up-I asked what he did there-he said he was
waiting for some Norman cows that were coming up by the railroad-I told
him he had no business there, and he instantly went back-I went and spoke
to Howard, another constable, and in two or three minutes I went in by ano-
ther gate, leaving Howard there, I found the door at the top of the steps
where I had seen the prisoner, wide open- I went in and called up the land-
lord, and we searched the place all over, but could not find the prisoner.
Prisoner. Q. You say you saw me quite plain? A. Yes, I was about
three yards from you-I have known you three or four years, and am certain
you are the man-I should know you from a thousand- I did not see you
that night at the corner of Nine-Elms-lane, nor did you then tell me about
the cows.
John HIPPISLEY. I keep the Southampton Arms, Lambeth; I have had
the house twelve months; I succeeded the prisoner's father when he left the
house. On 18th April, about three o'clock in the morning, I was called up
by the policeman, and found the front and back-doors open-(I had gone to
bed about half-past eleven overnight, and left all the doors bolted)-I missed
two papers containing copper money, eight half-crowns, and some cigars and
tobacco-I had left them in a drawer-I know the prisoner quite well— I saw
him in our house about seven o'clock the previous evening, the 17th-I have
here a paper similar to that in which the tobacco I lost was wrapped.
David PEGRAM (policeman, L 161). On the night of 18th April the
prisoner was brought to the station on a stretcher, from the effects of swal.
lowing a screw of tobacco-he was brought in for dead, insensible--I sent for
a medical man, who recovered him, and he was taken home-after he was
gone, in consequence of information, I went af:er him, and found him at
55, Mount-street, Westminster-road, a night-house-- I told him I had received
information from the B division station that he was wanted, for a robbery at
the Southampton Arms-he made use of some words to his companion, wbich
I could not understand, owing to the noise in the house- searched the pri-
soner, and found on him two half-crowns, two sixpences, 5 d. in copper, two
screws of tobacco, and one paper which had had tobacco in- I have also
produced a paper which was given me by Mr. Hippisley, and three others
which I got as samples from the prosecutor's house.
