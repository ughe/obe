HENRY DAVILEN. I am in the Artillery, at Woolwich. On Sunday,
the 9th of May, about two o'clock in the afternoon, I met the prisoner in
Woolwich I went into a house with her-she sat me down on a seat, put
her head through the window, and called in two girls-just as they came in
she struck me between the shoulders with a poker, and knocked me down
the other girls got a broomstick, and the prisoner laid on me with a poker,
and a broomstick-she put her hand into my pocket, and took out my purse
---this is it-there were two half-crowns and ten shillings in small silver in
it-1 was sober-I had only drunk a pint of beer.
Prisoner. Q. Did not you knock me on the floor, and say you would
have what you wanted, that you had only four pennyworth of copper,
and you would give me half-a-erown next day? A. No-none of that is
true I did not take a purse out of your bosom--the girls did not come to
your assistance, but to assist you to abuse me.
JOHN WALKER (police-constable K 298.) On this Sunday evening I
heard a call, went to the house, and saw Davilen there I saw the prisoner
in sight of the door- I did not go into the house-she was given in charge
-she gave me the purse, and said it belonged to one of the gunners of
the Artillery, and not to the prosecutor.
Prisoner. Q. How much was in it? A. Half-a-crown and Is. when I
took it.

1519. WILLIAM DYBLE was indicted for unlawfully assaulting Henry
Wadlow, a peace-officer, in the execution of his duty.
MR. CLERKE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM THORN, I have charge of the Crown and Anchor public house,
at Woolwich. I act as landlord-On the 9th of April, at eleven o'clock in the
evening, the defendant came there with a man named Glover-they wanted
drink I did not serve them, as they were in liquor they had been there
twice before that day-they were not making & noise, but I did not want
them in the house they wanted to go into the tap-room-I would not let
them. I called a policeman, who came to the front door--he did not come
in, but in consequence of what he said I tried to get the men to leave my
house-I went to Glover, and desired him to walk out he took me by the
collar before I had done anything to him, and knocked me up against the
wall, and tried to throw me on the floor-Wadlow, the policeman, was at the
door, in such a position as he could see it--he came in to assist me, and took
Glover from me-Dyble then took me by the collar and threw me over the
table-the policeman got Glover out of the house--Dyble followed of
himself.
