444. JEREMIAH GOLDING(80), and FRANCIS CHARLESGOLDING
(49), Feloniously forging and uttering a lease, with intent to defraud.
MR. M. WILLIAMS and MR. CHARLES MATHEWS, conducted the Prosecution,
JAMES Briggs. I am an undertaker, of 146, Kingsland Road--on 23rd
April, 1877, I saw the prisoner Francis Charles Golding, who was a boot and
shoe maker, at 5, 6, and 7, Bethnal Green Road-I had been acquainted
with him a short time previously-he asked me to lend him 500L on this
lease from father to son, and I did so under the belief that it was a good
lease—I received as security this indenture of 15th February, 1873-I say
him again in July, 1874, when he asked for a further advance of 5001, on
this original lease. This was dated 31 May, 1831, from the freeholder,
Thomas Yarrow, fand purporting to be for ninety years.) He said that it was for
90 years, that it was the original leare which his father received, and that it
had 45 years to run-I believed that and advanced the money in consequence
- I wrote out this document for the prisoners, and the prisoners both
signed it. (This stated that in consideration of the sun 1,0001, they
deposited the lease of the premises as collateral security until the reo
payment of the amount, and 81. per cent. per annum interest. Signed
Jeremiah Golding, and Francis Charles Golding.) In the early part
of 1876, Francis Charles Golding filed a partition for liquidation, and
I attended a meeting of his oreditors and gave value of my claim
-I subsequently saw this other lease. (This was dated August 17th, 1858,

property--that was the first time I knew that a new lease had been granted
-I believed that I was holding the genuine lease-I took it to a solicitor
