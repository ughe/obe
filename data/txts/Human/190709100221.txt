Wado led the horses to the station, which took, I think, about three-quarters of an hour, and they were put in a truck and sent to Dorking. I sent a telegram to Luck saying that the horses were coming and met him at Dorking Station. Luck asked the price and I told him £30. "Well," he said, "they look cheap horses. Are they all right?" I replied, "As far as I know." They were led round to Luck's place in Pixham Lane and tried in the van. Luck gave me £9 for them and two ponies, which I handed over to Wado. I thought the horses were straightforward and not stolen horses. As to the witness Goldton saying that he saw me at Chingford on July 11, I can assure you I was on the flat of my back on the 10th and 11th, and only got up on July 12, I am perfectly innocent of having stolen the horses.
 Cross-examined. Wado has been up and down Old Ford for about three months as near as I can remember. If I said I did not know saying about horses I should be a liar. I know a little. I asked wado where he got the horses form and he told me he was working for his governor. I know Chingford, but I was not there on the 11th as Godlonton said. I was suffering from a ricked neck and horrible pains in the head and had been in and out of London infirmary. I was, however, well enough to take a trip to Dorking on the 12th. The horses were led along burdett Road, Stepney, into Cablee Street and over Tower Bridge into Tooley Street. We had no trouble in getting over the bridge. I have seen wado once since when he told me he had sold the two ponies, one for £15 and one for £7. I have not tried to find him. I thought they were straight forwared horses.
 The following evidence was given with reference to other stolen property traced to Luck's possession. FREDERICK HENDERSON, florist, 694, High Road, Tottenham. On March 21, I missed a pony and trap and two sets of harness from my stables in Reform Road. The trap, which was of mahogany colour, was found at "The Gun" beerhouse at Dorking, and restored to me by the detectives in August. I valued the pony and trap at £35. WILLIAM LLEWELLYN SAUNDERS, "The Gun" beerhouse, North Street. Dorking. I purchased the pony and trap in question of the pri soner Luck for £16, a mare and two pigs. The mare was worth £7, and the two pigs I gave 14s. each for. I kept the trap till the detec tives told me it was stolen property; Luck told me his wife was sorry he had sold the pony and trap, and he wanted to buy it back from me at a slight premium. I had, of course, no idea that the cart and pony were stolen. I took a fancy to the turnout, as I thought I could take my wife out with it.
 Cross-examined. I have known Luck 11 years as a well-known master baker in Dorking. He has always borne the character of an honest and respectable man.
