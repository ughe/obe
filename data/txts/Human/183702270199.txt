GEORGE LANGHAM was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of February , 50lbs. weight of lead, value 10 s  . the goods of Benjamin Joy , his master: and MARTHA EVANS for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. BENJAMIN JOY. I am a plumber, residing in Westminster-bridge-road, Lambeth. Langham was in my employ—he had no right to sell any of my lead—I cannot swear to this lead, but to the best of my belief it is mine—I bad lead of this description in my shop.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  There is no mark on it? A.  No—I cannot tell one person's lead from another. THOMAS HORN( police-constable L  65.) On the 23rd of February I was on duty in Westminster-bridge-road, and was called into the prosecutor's shop to take Langham for selling a silver spoon—on my way to the station-house he said he was not so guilty as his mate, Richard Clark; that he and his mate had been robbing their employer for some time, and they had taken that day eighteen pounds of lead, and sold it to a woman named Hunt, in Granby-street—I went to the house of Evans, who goes by the name of Hunt, and I asked her for the lead she had bought that day of a boy—she produced this—I took her to the house, and she identified Clark as the boy the bought it of—Langham then said she had got half a hundred-weight of lead at her house, and I went and found seventeen pounds pounds of new lead under the counter—she was then ordered to appear at—Union-hall, and the Magistrate committed her for trial—she was asked if she had any new lead at her house, and she said she had not.
   Crosss-examined. Q.  Did she not go voluntarily to Union-hall? A.  Yes. BENJAMIN JOY. I believe this other lead is mine. RICHARD CLARK. On the 23rd of February this piece of lead was cut off in the shop by Langham, and the pipe was taken out in the morning at breakfast time—this piece of short lead was taken out at dinner time—I took out the piece of pipe by Langham's persuasion, and he took the other out to Mrs. Hunt's shop—I never saw any thing of this other lead that I know of, but Langham told me this piece of pipe came from the Harrow-road—I went to Mrs. Hunt's—she asked my name, and where I lived, and I told her—Langham sold the lead—I cannot say for how much; but after he got the money he asked her what she would have to drink, but she did not have any thing—that was at dinner time.
