JOHN REYMAN was indicted for burglarioulsy breaking and entering the dwelling-house of James Reyman , about the hour of ten in the night of the   10th of January , at   St. Mary, Lambeth , with intent to steal, and stealing therein 2 ounces weight of tea, value 7 d  .; 1 lob  .; weight of butter, value 6 d  .; 1 lb  . wight of sugar, value 6 d  .; 2 lbs  . weight of mutton, value 9 d  .; 1 loaf of bread, value 3 d  .; 1 1/2 lbs  . weight of candles, value 3 d  .; the goods of the said James Reyman ; and 1 shirt, value 1 s  .; 1 pair of gloves, value 6 d  .; and 1 basket, value 1 s  .; the goods of Jeremiah Reyman . REBECCA REYMAN. I am the wife of James Reyman, and live in Bond-place, Bond-street, Commercial-road, in the parish of Lambeth. I am the prisoner's mother—he is a slater's labourer. On Sunday night, the 10th of January, I fastened the house up, at about half-past eight o'clock—I locked the door—there is no back door—the window was fastened with a catch, and a nail put into the frame to keep—it together—I went to bed about half-past eight o'clock—there was nobody in the house, but my husband and myself—we both went to bed at the same time—say husband came down first in the morning, but he is not here—he is very ill—he went down about half-past six o'clock, and called me down—I came down directly after him, and found the street door partly open—the lock was not forced—on examining I found the window had been broken—the middle pane of the upper frame had been put in, and three pieces in the bottom pane were broken out to admit an arm to unfasten the window—it was perfectly safe when I went to bed—the window was shut down again, and the door open—before I went to bed I had put my son Jeremiah's shirt, which I had washed for him, on the back of a chair, towards the fire to air it—he does not sleep in the house—it was gone, and the provisions which I had laid in for the week were gone—there were two ounces, of tea, one pound of sugar, very nearly one pound of butter the best part of a half-quartern loaf, and about one pound and a half or two pounds of pork—they were taken from the cupboard, which was not locked I am sure they were they when I went to bed—I do not know where the prisoner lodged—he was sometimes at home, and sometimes not—a
