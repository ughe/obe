   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH . I don't understand much about the wax trade—I don't think I understand enough to make a bargain—I don't know that Mr. Field has sworn that the wax found at my premises was worth over 2 s . a pound—I kept the whole of it in my shop—I swear that—I small portion of it was found in my bedroom, which I removed in conse quence of an alteration on my premises—that was only about 2 cwt.—I had in stock at that time, I think, 2 cwt., and likewise 2 cwt. of white in the warehouse—all the rest was in the bedroom—the transactions began before November, 1872—I have been to Jewell's shop—he deals in rags and bones, and metal—I sold that wax to Gregory's at 2 s . 1 d . a pound—I paid 1 s . 6 d . for it—he had an invoice either from me or my son, with my name and address on it—I asked Jewell where he got the wax—I thought it a funny thing that a rag and bone dealer should be dealing in wax—he said he had it from a firm who had an account with a wax house—that was sufficient information for me, and I bought with confidence.
   Cross-examined by MR. M. WILLIAMS . I thought I was giving a fair price for it—it is on my invoice—I am in a large way of business—I do about 9,000 l . a year—I was satisfied with Jewell's representation—I had known him sometime, and knew his father-in-law, Mr. Davis, of London Road—I did not think it extraordinary that he should be dealing in wax—I deal in wax—I did not know that it was in an unfinished state when I bought it.
   Re-examined.  Seventeen hundredweight in five or six months is not 8 large dealing for an oil and colour man—I should be satisfied to buy double the quantity—I hear that Jewell has got out of the way—I only know it from hearsay; I have not taken any trouble to inquire. GEORGE JOHNSON. I am a candle-maker, of 175, Bishopsgate Street Without—I received about half a dozen blocks of yellow wax from Mr. King in exchange for candles.
   Cross-examined by MR. M. WILLIAMS . The bargain was at 1 s . 9 d . per lb., but it was not paid for—I believe it to be a fair price—I chiefly trade in tallow, but I buy wax—I do not know much about it, or whether it was in an unfinished state. JOHN EDWARDS. I am a wax-doll manufacturer, of Waterloo Road—I bought wax of Mr. King, and showed a sample of it to Mr. Field.
   Cross-examined by MR. M. WILLIAMS . I paid 1 s . 8 d . or 1 s . 10 d . per lb. for it, and I gave a fair price for it.
