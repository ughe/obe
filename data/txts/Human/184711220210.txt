MR. HUDDLESTON. Q.  If a delivery-order was made out to you for the delivery of certain hops, and you afterwards transferred it to another parts, would you write your name afterwards? A.  I should write it as this is written, on the reverse side—this is not in the writing of any one in my firm. THOMAS HINES GOBEL. I am a hop-factor, in High-street, Borough—I saw the prisoner on or about 8th Oct.—he asked, if he sent in seven pockets of hops, whether I would advance him 5 l . or 6 l .—I told him I would—he then said, "But I can sell them myself"—I said, "I will not take them in, and send them out without a commission"—he agreed to allow me a commission of 4 s . a pocket—on 12th Oct., about one or two o'clock, he called at my office and said, "The hops are in your warehouse, but there are but five pockets; I should like to have the money," and at the same time he said he had sold six pockets of new hops, marked "Pearson," if I could take 67 s . for them—I had lent him a sample of them at 70 s .—I told him I would ascertain it from the men when they came from dinner—I did so—I saw him again on 22nd, and he wanted two of the five pockets marked "Andrews"—he had two pockets away on 22nd, the other three remained. GEORGE WILD( policeman M  94.) I took the prisoner into custody on 31st Oct.—I told him he was charged with stealing the hops from Mr. Collins—he said Mr. Collins gave him the order in his own handwriting, to obtain them from Messrs. Nash and Hicks, of Pickle-herring wharf—he said, "I sold them to Messrs. Haynes and Son, of Paddington"—on Monday, 1st Nov. I got three pockets of hops from Mr. Gobel—I have not got the undertaking here—I cannot exactly say whether I have list it or whether it has been mis laid—I received it from Mr. Collins—I have searched for it and cannot find it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where have you searched for it? A.  In my pocket-book—I cannot say exactly when I saw it last, but I had it here when we came to give instructions for the indictment—I recollect having it last Thursday—I and the solicitor had it—I cannot recollect whether I put it into my pocket-book again—I have been a policeman nine years—we sometimes ask a prisoner what he has to say to a charge—they sometimes admit it themselves without being asked—I do not try to screw it out of them—the prisoner said Mr. Collins knew all about it. CHARLES WELBORN. I am the solicitor for the prosecution—I saw the undertaking, and made a copy of it, which I compared with the original, very particularly—the last time I saw the original was in the office here, on 17th—the policeman then had it in his hand—I had it in my hand and gave it back to him, because I was not concerned for Mr. Collins, only for Messrs. Nash—I kept the delivery-order, and produce it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Then Messrs. Nash and Hicks are the prosecutors of this indictment? A.  They were ordered by the Magistrate to prosecute—( the order was here read, the endorsement was as follows, "Deliver to bearer seven pockets of hops, as per order other side, Haynes and Son, Oct. 9 , 1847." The copy of the undertaking being read, was as follows:—"I undertake to pay Mr. R. Collins of seven pockets of hops, marked, 'Andrews, of Brook-street, Kent, 1846,' provided Messrs. Haynes and Son do not pay the same on or before Saturday next. J. Chittenden, Oct. 8, 1847."
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY.  Aged 42.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Six Months .
 ( There was another indictment against the prisoner, charging a larceny of the goods in question, on which Mr. HUDDLESTON  offered no evidence.)
