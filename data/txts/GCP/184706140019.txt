Cross-examined. Q. In what coin was the good money? 4. Five shil-

THOMAS WELLS (policeman C 1.) I went with Russell and Isaacson to the
station ---I saw the prisoner feeling under her shawl the whole way I asked
her what she had in her left hand-she said, “Nothing ' I asked her what she
had under her shawl; she said, “Nothing"-I found eight counterfeit half-
crowns, wrapped in paper, in her shawl-one of them was bent.
Cross-examined. Q. Was the bent half-crown apart from the others ? A.
Yes, wrapped in paper.
SUSANNAH BUTLER re-examined. This is the bent half-crown, as near
as I can say-I saw the marks at the time-the champagne nips, which
Mrs. Caunt took to mark it witb, would make such marks as these there is
a "W" on it-I do not know that it was bent more than this is, but it
was straightened the next day-the person who bent it is not here.
MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint--these coins
are all counterfeit, six of them are cast in the same mould-these two
marked ones are similar to five of the others.
Cross-examined. Q. Are they good counterfeits ? 4. Pretty good.
GUILTY. Aged 20.-Confined Six Months.

The prisoner came there about nine o'clock in the evening, on the 14th of
May, asked for a half-quartern loaf, some butter, and some other things-
they came to 11 d., be paid me with a five shilling piece - I gave him
change, and put the five shilling piece into my till-there was no other five

my husband took the five shilling piece out of the till to give change-I had
