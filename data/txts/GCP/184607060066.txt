JAMES BALLANTINE re-examined. I sign the receipts when I send for the
money, to save time.
GEORGE HORNBLOW. I was in the habit of receiving newspapers from
Mr. Ballantine-on the 26th of May I paid the prisoner 28. 6d. for Mr.
Ballantine, and have the receipt.
CHARLES ANSELL. I deal with Mr. Ballantine-I paid the prisoner
3s. 6d. for him on the 27th of May.
William POILE ( policeman.) I took the prisoner, and told him he was
charged on suspicion of robbing Mr. Ballantine-he said, “Very well; are
you going to take me to the shop ?"
JAMES BALLANTINE re-examined. I was absent from business daily-I
bave not been absent from town since June, 1845-I am able to say I had a
settlement with the prisoner on the 25th, 26th, and 27th of May-he paid
me money on each day, but neither of these three sums-I settled with him
on the 27th, at night, to the best of my knowledge—the books were made
up to these several nights, and the money paid to me which I entered-
my wife has accounted to me for money received from the prisoner, not
entered in this book.
Not Guilty.

1483. JOHN COSTER was charged, on the Coroner's inquisition only,
with feloniously killing and slaying Richard Hutchinson.
Mr. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
MARY Coster. I am wife of William Coster, and live in Lambeth-walk,
the prisoner is my brother-in-law. I knew Richard Hutchinson when he was
alive-he lived at No. 1, White's-alley, Bankside, with his father-on San-
day, the 21st of June, at nine o'clock in the evening, I and my husband were
at Mr. Willis's house, No. 12, Whitehorse-street, Lambeth-1 had an
appointment to meet the deceased at the Pear Tree-my husband did not
know of it-the deceased came to the top of Whitehorse-street, and I accom-
panied him there-my husband came into the Pear Tree about nine o'clock-
we went from there together to Mr. Willis's my husband did not object to
something which he thought had happened between me and the deceased
-Willis's is a private house--the deceased was intoxicated when I met
him there was a chair at Willis's, which my husband gave the deceased
to sit down in, outside the door-he sat down in it, and fell out of the
chair from the effects of liquor-he fell on his head-he had a straw
hat on-it fell off, and he fell upon the hat he did not fall violently
my husband picked him up-I asked my husband to use him kindly-upon
that my husband struck me-the deceased said he did not like to see that;
he would not have me struck-the prisoner was leaning on his arm on the
window-ledge of Willis's house, and challenged the deceased to fight-the
deceased accepted the challenge, and stripped to fight I did not see the
prisoner strip-they fought-I saw the prisoner give the deceased one blow
-I do not know where he hit him, for my husband struck me at the
same time, but from the position he stood I should say it was on the
head-my husband was sober, and so was the prisoner-I did not see that
the deceased struck the prisoner-I endeavoured to separate them, and got
another blow from my husband, and fainted-when I came to myself Willis
was assisting the deceased to put his clothes on-I then walked with the
deceased to the Marsh-gate- I went into a doctor's shop to have my bruises
