 494.  THOMAS FRANCIS was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of February , 1 bridle-bit, value 15s., the goods of Samuel Snape.
   SAMUEL SNAPE. On the 8th of February the prisoner came home to my house - I put the bridle-bit on the table and told my wife to wipe it and put it by while I went to Drury-lane - when I came back it was gone - I have known the prisoner seven or eight years; his father and mother make list shoes.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you give me the bit to take to the corner of Newton-street, and wait till you come? A. No; I did not.
   ELIZABETH SNAPE. I am the prosecutor's wife - my husband came home with the prisoner - my husband told me to wipe the bit; I did so, and put it on a chair - the prisoner took it up and said he would meet Snape at the corner of Newton-street with it - he took it away rolled up in paper - I had not heard my husband tell him to take it to Newton-street.
   SAMUEL SNAPE. When I heard that he was gone to Newton-street, I went there, but did not see him till Tuesday, when I heard he was taken.
   RICHARD CHAPMAN(police-constable F 18). I took the prisoner - he said he knew nothing of the bit.
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor gave me the bit to take to the corner of Newton-street and wait till he came; I took it, and a man came to me and said he came from Mr. Rowley's; I said I came from one of his workmen - he took the bit and gave me 6d. - this woman keeps a bad house.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 495.  JOHN EDMONDS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 1 brass fender, value 1l., the goods of John James Rippon.
   FRANCIS WILLIAM STEBBINGS. I am shopman to Mr. John James Rippon, of Castle-street, Oxford-street- on the 17th of February I was at home in the evening - I received information, went out, and saw the prisoner walking away with the fender on his shoulder - this is it; it is my employer's - I had seen it safe ten minutes before in the shop- I took him with it.
 Prisoner. I was standing talking to a young man; and when I left him a man asked me to carry the fender - he took it from the door and gave it to me.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 495.  JOHN GROVE was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of February , 30 fathoms of rope, value 30s., the goods of George Turner.
   JOSEPH BAUM. I am horse-keeper to Mr. George Turner - on the 3d of February I was at his house, and saw the prisoner pulling some rope towards him, to get it on his back - he had no business to be there - I asked what he wanted with the rope - he said, "To go down with the barge" - I let him go out, thinking it was all right.
   CHARLES GOLDEN. I am a carman - I met the prisoner in the stable with some rope on his back - I said nothing to him, nor he to me - we had no barge going down that day - he was taken the same night, quite intoxicated - he had some cash in his pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a man who told me to go and get a coil of rope - he said Joseph Baum was there, and he would give it to me - I went - he was there with a light - I took the rope to the bargeman, and he gave me a pot of beer.
     NOT GUILTY.
 496.  ELIZA GLASGOW was indicted for stealing, on the 8th February , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of Mary Finley , she having been before convicted of felony.
   MARY FINLEY. I keep a haberdasher's shop in Stepney- on the 8th of February, at half-past two o'clock, the prisoner came into my shop and asked to look at some silk handkerchiefs - I showed her one out of the window - she said it was too thin - I then turned some out of a box - a man came in without shoes, begging - I said I had nothing to give him - he snatched the handkerchief, which I was showing to the prisoner, and ran away - I had seen him standing on the left of the window when the prisoner came in - I ran to the door and cried "Stop thief!" but not knowing which way the man run, I returned to the shop, recollecting that I had left the handkerchiefs on the counter - I then pushed the prisoner to the door, but Mrs. Freeman called to me to detain her, as she belonged to the man - I then told her, if she would tell me where the man was, that I might get the handkerchief, she might go - but she would not.
   SARAH FREEMAN. I live opposite Mrs. Finley - I was standing at my window about half-past two o'clock, and saw the prisoner come up the street, and stop at the window - a young man was with her without shoes and stockings - they were conversing together, and stopped at her window - they were talking together - the prisoner went into the shop - the young man waited by the window - I saw Mrs. Finley show the prisoner some handkerchiefs, and the man went in and snatched the handkerchief, and came out - I saw it was a red handkerchief - he folded it up and put it in his bosom - I had seen them about three minutes, and looking in at the window.
