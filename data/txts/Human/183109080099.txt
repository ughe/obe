 1745.  WILLIAM THEED was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 1 watch, value 50s.; 1 chain, value 5s.; 2 seals, value 5s., and 1 key, value 6d., the goods of Thomas Fuller.
   THOMAS FULLER. I am servant to Sir William Young. On the 12th of August my watch hung on a nail in the hall about a quarter before eight o'clock in the morning - the hall door was left ajar by a person who had gone out; I saw the prisoner about the middle of the hall - he had no business there; he had just come from the peg - I immediately missed the watch, and followed him - I called to him to stop; he made no answer - I followed him up the area steps; I kept close to him till he was taken - he crossed the street opposite Lady Fitzberbert's, and the watch was found down her area - I did not see it thrown down.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q.Did you keep in sight of him? A. Yes, all the way - I was eight or twelve yards from him - he might throw it down without my seeing it.
   WILLIAM WOOD. I saw Fuller following the prisoner, who I saw throw the watch down Lady Fitzherbert's area- it was picked up, and given to the Policeman.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen him before? A. No - I was about three yards from him.
   PATRICK CONNOLLY. I am a Policeman. I received the prisoner in charge, and went to Lady Fitzherbert's; her servant gave me the watch from the area.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.

 1746.  JOHN FRENCH was indicted for manslaughter.
   GEORGE HAZLEWOOD. I am a patrol. On the 23rd of July, about a quarter-past six o'clock in the morning, I was crossing from the south side of Cheapside to this side of Gutter-lane - I heard a cry, and on looking round saw Hill, who has been killed, close to his cab, about a yard from it, on the Foster-lane side of the street; he had his arms up, as if he had received a violent blow - he immediately fell: I saw that he was very near the wheel of a dray, which was going on towards the Mansion-house; I saw the wheel of the dray come on his body - there were casks on it: I cannot say whether they were full or empty - I did not see any body attending to the horses of the dray; if there had been I think I must have seen; the wheel rested on his body, but did not pass over him - I did not see the driver at all; the dray was backed off by somebody, and I and my brother patrol assisted the deceased to the hospital.
 Q. Was there room for the dray to pass at the time? A. I consider that there was not sufficient, as it was between a coach-stand and a coal-waggon, which was next to the footpath, and there did not seem sufficient room for the dray to pass between them; the horse of the cab was not injured, but the shaft was broken, I suppose by the shaft of the dray - the dray was drawn by two horses; I thought the deceased was dead, when we were opposite the Bull and Mouth, but the surgeon at the hospital said he was not- I did not see the prisoner till before the inquest.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You did not see the beginning of this? A. No, the cry first alarmed me; I only saw one dray - Bates and a butcher helped me to put the deceased into a cab - nobody else assisted; I did not see the prisoner, and do not think that he assisted; I do not swear there was not room to pass between the dray and the coach-stand - there did not appear so to me: I am not accustomed to driving - I did not see  Frederick Edwards there; people gathered round after the man was taken up; I think there were others on the spot - whether they were there before I cannot tell; I might be thirty or forty steps from the deceased when I heard the cry; I was in the middle of the road at the time.
   JOSEPH BATES. I am a patrol, and live in Warwick-lane. I was on duty in Cheapside, on the pavement of the south side, going towards Bow church; I heard somebody scream, and saw the deceased under the wheel of a dray, which I think had four puncheons on it; it was loaded with beer, for I sounded one cask - the off wheel was pressing the deceased along the ground, dragging him along; I could not see the driver - my attention was directed to the man; some man pulled him from the wheel - I came up directly, and the wheel rested on the body, and went back again, not over him; I did not see who drew him from under the wheel - I lifted him up, and took him to the hospital, in his own cab - Hazlewood was so frightened I could scarcely get him to my assistance; I had repeatedly seen the man as a cab-driver; his cab was facing towards St. Paul's - I saw him again between eleven and twelve o'clock, at the hospital, dead; the shaft of the cab was broken on the side that the man had stood with his horse; I took the number of the dray after the deceased was extricated, and then saw a man, whom I believe to be the prisoner.
