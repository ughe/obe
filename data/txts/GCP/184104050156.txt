1188. JOHN TISLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April, 1
hat, value 38., the goods of John Welchman.
ROBERT PARKER. I am shopman to Mr. John Welchman, a hatter, in
Hackney-road. On the 5th of April, about a quarter after three o'clock
in the afternoon, Penry brought the prisoner into my shop, and accused
bim of stealing & hat which had been taken from the door-post-this is it.
CHARLES PENRY. I live a few doors from the prosecutor's. I saw the
prisoner run across the road with the bat in his hand—I ran after him-be
threw it down-I caught him without losing sight of him.
SAMUEL M'COLE. I am a policeman. I was called and took the pri-
soner.
(The prisoner received a good character, and a witness undertook to
employ him.)
GUILTY. Aged 15.-Strongly recommended to mercy.-Whipped and
Discharged.
1189. JAMES NORMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of
April, 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Patrick Joseph O'Doherty,
from his person.
PATRICK JOSEPH O'DOHERTY. On the 7th of April, between twelve
and one o'clock, I was near the middle entrance of the London Dock-1
put my hand into my pocket and missed my handkerchief-I turned sud-
denly round and saw two persons a few yards behind me--the prisoner was
one--I saw part of my handkerchief hanging ont of his trowsers' pocket-
I collared him, and took it from him-he said it was the other gentleman
that prigged it, not him--the other boy went quietly on-I gave the pri-
soner into custody.
Thomas Cook (police-constable K 174.) I received the prisoner into
custody from the prosecutor, with the handkerchief-on our way to the sta-
tion he said it was the stout man who ran away that did it, not him-be
cried a great deal, and said he had never been in trouble before, the pro-
secutor must be mistaken.
Prisoner's Defence. I was going for my mother's washing, and picked
the handkerchief up-I never said any such word as prigged-I saw the
other one running across the bridge, but no one stopped him.
GUILTY. Aged 18.-Confined Three Months.
