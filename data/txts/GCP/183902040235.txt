DINAH SAWKINS. I am servant to John Walker, of High-street, Tot-
tenham. On the 28th of January, the prisoner came in between two and
four o'clock-I purchased a quarter and 9lbs. weight of iron of him for
1s., at the rate of 38, a cwt., as cast-iron-he was in the habit of bringing
bones and things there before-these two articles were among the iron.
NELSON SMITH. (police-constable of Edmonton, No. 5.) I went with
the prosecutor to Walker's shop, and found this mould and ground-plate
there-I fitted them to the prosecutor's plough, and they agreed.
TroMAS WOODROFFE CRAIG. I am a policeman. I apprehended the
prisoner-I told him the charge--he said he found the iron in the marshes,
and knew nothing about stealing it.
Prisoner's Defence. I picked up these things in a ditch in the marshes
-I gather old iron, bones, and things.
GUILTY. Aged 18.--Confined Nine Months.
825. WILLIAM HILL, alias James Hawkes, was indicted for stealing,
on the 18th of January, 3 sheets, value 68. ; 3 aprons, value Is. 6d. ; 3
window blinds, value 18. 6d.: 3 handkerchiefs, value 1s. 60. ; 1 shift,
value 2s. ; 2 printed books, value 6s. ; 2 snuff-boxes, value 28.; 1 tea-
caddy, value 58.; and 1 work-box, value 4s. ; the goods of Richard
Stroud.
RICHARD STROUD. I live at Ilford, in Essex. A man named Hawkes
lodged in my house, but I do not know that it was the prisoner-I never
saw him myself till he was at Worship-street-on the 19th of January,
between five and six o'clock, I lost the articles stated, and saw them at
Spitalfields station-house on the 21st.
GEORGE TREw (police-constable H 125.) On the 19th of January,
about four o'clock in the morning, I met the prisoner in Lamb-street,
Spitalfields, with a bag- I asked what he had got-he said some linen and
other articles-I asked where he brought them from he said from
Ulford, in Essex, and was going to sell them; that he was out of work and
wanted victuals-I asked if the linen was marked-be said, "No"-I
looked at the linen, and found it was marked "M. S."
Richard STROUD re-examined. These articles are mine“MS" are
my wife's initials-her shift is marked "MS"--my sheets are marked
URS."
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Where had you left the things?
4. In the sitting-room, in different places I saw them safe about eight
o'clock in the evening, when we went to bed- I cannot tell how they were
taken-I believe the prisoner came home about twelve o'clock that night
-bis wife struck a light, and let him in our room has no fastening to it
-I came down just as he came in, and heard him talking to his wife, but
I had never seen him myself I had only been in the house five days I
know he was in distress, for when I went into their room, on missing the
things, I saw nothing but a few turnips cut up, and there was no fire to
cook them the children had been crying for victuals for some days-I
would employ him if the Court would allow him to go home.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 32.- Recommended to mercy.--Confined Fourteen Days.
