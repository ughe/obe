MESSRS. PAYNE and LUCAS conducted the Prosecution. LOUISA KING. I am the wife of George King, an oilman, in Westmin ster-road. On the 5th of February William Williams came for a quarter of an ounce of tobacco—he gave me a sixpence—I sent the boy for change, having no coppers in the till—he came back and said it was bad—he brought it back bent—I told the prisoner it was bad—he said if it was bad I did not take it of him—I said I did—he said I could not tell, be cause I had put it into the till—I am not certain whether I had put it into the till or not—I had given him some tobacco in a piece of paper, which I twisted up—it was turned up at the corner—when the officer brought it to me I knew it to be the tobacco, by the paper—I dare say it is not different to other paper, but I am certain it is it—I know it by the twist. JANE ELIZABETH CURTIS. I am the daughter of William Curtis, of Princes-row, Kennington-cross. On the 5th of February William Wil liams came for a quarter of an ounce of tobacco—I gave it him—he gave me a sixpence—I bit it, and found it was bad—I gave it him again. JAMES BROOK( police-constable L  118.) On the 5th of February, be tween six and seven o'clock in the evening, I saw both prisoners in com pany together near Westminster-bridge—I watched them, and when they were near the Obelisk, in Westminster-road, I saw Charles Williams give something to William Williams, who went near a shop window, and rubbed it with his finger—he then crossed the road, and went into Mrs. King's oil-shop—Charles remained on the opposite side of the road—William remained in the shop two or three minutes—he then came out, and joined Charles—they went down the London-road, and up Kennington-road—I then saw William rubbing something under a lamp opposite Mr. Bright's shop—he then went into the shop, and came out again—Charles was standing across the road, under the lamp—when William came out of Mr. Bright's shop he went on, and went into Mr. Curtis's—Charles was stand ing across the road—when William came out of Curtis's he joined Charles again, and they went on to Lambeth-walk—I followed them, and laid hold of Charles by the throat—he struggled, and we both fell to the ground—he spat out five sixpences, which I produce—I searched him, found on him 5 1/2 d . in coppers, some tobacco, and some stuff they call slum,  which is used for rubbing sixpences—it was quite damp when I found it in his pocket—I showed the tobacco I found to Mrs. King, and she stated she believed it to be her tobacco, from the manner in which it was wrapped up.
   MRS. KING re-examined.  It was between six and seven o'clock that William Williams came to me. MR. JOHN FIELD. These five sixpences are all counterfeit—three are cast in one mould, and two in another—the effect of rubbing the coin with dirt, or some mixture, would be to make it appear dark, as if it had been in a dirty or greasy pocket, or in circulation.
   William Williams's Defence.  They unwrapped the tobacco at the sta tion, and did it up again.
   WILLIAM WILLIAMS— GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Nine Months.
