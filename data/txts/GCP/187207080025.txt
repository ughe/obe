525. NIBLO CLARK (28), Burglariously breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of Henry Flaws, and stealing therein two coats, two blankets,
and other articles, his property.
MR. HARRIS conducted the Prosecution. Sob
HENRY FLAws. I am a gold-lace maker, of 48, Brewer Street, Golden
Square-about 3.15 on the morning of 19th June, I heard a noise in my
back shop, and found a quantity of coats, trousers, and boots lying on the
floor-I went into the shop, saw the prisoner, and said “Halloa I what are
you doing here drop that bundle" -he had a bundle containing a lot of
my clothes--he made no answer-I said "I will have you" he turned
round, looked me full in the face, and jumped through the ground glass
fan-light like a Harlequin, saying, “No you won't ”LI informed the police,
and saw him at the station a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes afterwards,
with two of my coats on--he must have dressed himself in them just out-
side my bedroom door, as two coats and a waistooat were left there which
were not mine I had fastened up the place, and seen the things safe
between 9 and 10 o'clock the night before the sky-light was shut down,
but not bolted the entrance was obtained by the sky-light, as none of the
doors were broken-my house is in the parish of St. James, Westminster.
Prisoner. Q. Was it day-light? 4. Yes, it was 3.80-I described one
of my coats to the sergeant before I saw you-it had a pocket-book in the
breast pocket, which I lost-before you were captured, I described you as
having a dark moustache, sallow features, and short, with a long great coat
on I heard that you had a brother; you said "Don't proseoute me; if
you like to go to my brother, he will give you some money, and make a
man of you, and set you up in business " the coat you are wearing is vot
mine--the shop which was broken into is under the same roof as my
dwelling-house there was no breaking to get in, as the sky-light was lifted
up on its hinges, but there was breaking in getting away-you had to get

Prisoner.Q. Which way did I run! A. Up Oxford Street, away from
the prosecutor's premises.
HENRY WALLER (Policeman C 181). On 19th June, about 3.30 a.m., I
saw the prisoner running about 150 yards from the prosecutor's house, and
away from it-I followed him to the corner of Edward Street, where he
turned a corner when I was nearly overtaking him, and I lost sight of him
for about five minutes-I saw him again in the closet of a yard at No. 14,
