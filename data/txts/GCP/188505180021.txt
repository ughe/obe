ISABELLA Cox. I am the wife of John Cox; we live at Market Street,
Warwick-on the morning of 28th Feb. I sent three postal orders for 20s.,
31., and 38., from the Warwick Post-office, in an envelope with an
account to H. O. Wilkinson and Co., 135, Regent Street, London-I
fastened up and posted the letter myself at a quarter to 9 a.m. -Mr.
Roberts was the post-office assistant who issued the orders to me-I
shortly afterwards communicated with the post-office about that letter.
CHARLES JAMES ROBERTS, I am employed as an assistant at the War
wick Post-office-on 28th February I issued three postal orders to Mrs.
Cox, entering the numbers in a book which I have here-these three for
208., 86., and 38., correspond to the numbers I took down-those were the
only three orders I issued that morning-a letter addressed and posted
before 9 o'clock would be dispatched from Warwick shortly afterwards
st 9.5 to London.
JOSEPH FRAYER. I am overseer to the Inland Branch of the post-office
--the 9.5 a.m. mail bags from Warwick arrive at the chief office at
11.27 p.m., and a letter such as that described by Mrs. Cox would be
transferred at 2.9 to the E.O. Office, and afterwards to the W. Dis
trict in Vere Street, which it would reach 25 minutes after 2.9—allowing
for stoppages, it would reach Vere Street before 3.2.

it at 8 o'clock on Saturday morning I should not fetch it till 8 o'clock on
Mooday morning on Monday, 2nd March, I fetched it and handed it to
Mr. Rollison, Messrs. Wilkinson's manager.
