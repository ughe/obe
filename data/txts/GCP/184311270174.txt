John DUNKIN. I am the witness's father, and live with him at Dartford
-I attended at the Court of Requests on the 6th of Oct.--the prisoner pro-
duced the bill and receipt there that day-the receipt is not my writing, nor
the writing of any of my household I said it was a forgery immediately
I saw it-my son, wife, daughter, and myself are the only persons who
attend in the shop-it is not the handwriting of either of them-the prisoner
never paid me this amount, nor anybody for him.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you and the prisoner ever quarrelled. A. No-
I applied to him for the money and he abused me, which I understand is his
constant practice to every body who does apply-he was not tipsy to my
knowledge-I was going by in my chaise, and called for the money-that
must be two years ago.
JOHN CAMPION. I am beadle to the Commissioners to the Court of Re-
quests at Woolwich. I attended the court on the 6th of Oct., and remember
the prisoner producing this receipt.
Cross-examined. Had you told him before the 3rd of Nov, that you
heard there was to be an officer to take him if he came there with that receipt
again? A. I had the receipt in my possession all the time I had told him
that when I went to serve the order of nisi, I told him a policeman had been
waiting the whole day to take him I was present before the Commissioners
on the 6th of Oct. and 3rd of Nov.-several Commissioners said they con-
sidered the debt barred by the statute of limitations-he said he would not
take that objection, but insisted on proving his receipt-I think the pris
soner made that observation on both occasions-it was at the first occasion
he said he should not take advantage of it-Mr. Dunkin applied to have the
receipt impounded at the first and last hearing--the Commissioners said
they thought it was the property of the prisoner, and they must give it up-
the prisoner said he was quite willing it should remain in the hands of the
chairman-I have lived fifty years in the neighbourhood-I have known the
prisoner twelve or fourteen years- I never heard anything against him-
knew him to be a poor man, but never heard anything against his honesty.
MR. WILKINS. Q. In point of fact do you know anything of his character!
4. I do not-I have had process against him-Mr. Dunkin argued the statute
before the Commissioners.
Q. Was it not after the argument he said he would not avail himself of
the statute, but of his receipt ? A, I think it was on the first and last och
casion, both-1 should positively say so-- he did not want the receipt--there
was a dispute between the Commissioners who should have it, and a Com
missioner said, “If you are an honest man, you will leave it where it is," and
he said he had no objection to leave it in the hands of the chairman-that
was on the last occasion.
