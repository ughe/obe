away and went into our own room, and then downstairs and saw Mrs. Oliver the landlady—I then went up to Mrs. Barth's room, and the prisoner—was sitting on a chair—I said "Mrs. Oliver is coming to ask you for the rent" and Mrs. Oliver walked in behind me. and asked him for the rent—this was about 11 o'clock—he said "I will give you a fortnight next Monday"—she said "Very well," and left the room—I asked him why he did not give Mrs. Oliver the rent—he said "Before I give her the rent her house will be in the  Police News"  —he did not take in the Police News , but I think they sold it, or had it at the shop—I said "We should make two nice pictures for the Police News"  —a short time after that I asked him for a penny to get half a pint of beer—he said that I need not trouble myself, I should not want any more pennies for beer out of him—I went and got half a pint of beer with a penny of my own, and brought it back—he drank a very little of it, and I finished it—when he was drinking it he said "My love to you, and I hope you will be better tempered the next time we have a drop more together"—I said "I have no doubt we shall," or something to that effect—I then noticed that the child was in a fit, and went across to it, and as I did so I said to the prisoner "You had better go out for a walk, or else I will"—he said "Next time you go out it will be in a cab to the hospital"—I cannot remember what I said, and I think he went into his own room—I took the child downstairs, and brought it up again, and laid it down—the prisoner was then there, and I said "I wish you would keep in your own room"—I do not think he made any answer—I was sitting at the side of the bed looking at the baby, who was in a fit, and the prisoner shut the door and the window, and came towards me—I said "Bill, look at this child"—he made no more to do, but hit me on the back of my head deliberately with a chopper, which I first saw when it was lifted in his hand to strike—it was Mrs. Barth's chopper, and had been in a corner of that room—I said "Bill, before you hit me again think what you are doing it for, and what excuse you will make"—he said "I will soon find some excuse"—he kept on repeating the blows, and I fell off the side of the bed, and saw him change the chopper into his left hand—I said "Bill, if you hit me once more you will kill me"—he said "That is what I want to do"—how I got up I do not know, but I got to the door—I kept continually screaming, I think, till I lost my senses—just as I got to the top of the steps a person who lives next door came to my assistance—I saw her on the stairs.
