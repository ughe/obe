JOHN PASSMORE SMITH. I am a tea-dealer, in Coventry-street. The pri-
soner was my carman in Oct. and part of Nov.—he never accounted to me
for the receipt of 15'. 4d. from Mr. Almond, 78. 3d. from Mrs. Parker, or
21. 28. 6d. from Mrs. Hedger.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you no partner? A. No; I have eight or
ten persons in my employ—it was not the prisoner's duty to account to any
person for money, but to me or Mr. Johnson—I will not swear he has
accounted to other persons; if he had, there would have been credit in our
books for it—he was discharged on Saturday, 10th Nov., I suppose about
eleven o'clock at night—he bad been with me about eight months—he gene.
rally got home about eight at night—I had a character from a man, saying,
he had a high opinion Of him, but not from an employer—I heard be was in
the employ of Ridgway and Co.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you hear how he came to leave there? A. We
hear it was from the same cause as that with which we now charge him.
HENRY BERESFORD. I am superintendent of the C division of police. I
received information on 21st Nov., and a description of the prisoner. I
looking for him till 1 Ith Dec., when I took him.
MB. WOOLLETTtO MR. SMITH. Q. On 10th Nov. was there not some arrange-
mentas to the prisoner's the outstanding debts? A. No ; there were
two instances in which I asked him for the addresses, and he said it was not
vrey likely he should give me them—he took a list away from our house, Whid
Mr. Johnson made out by my desire, but it was not my desire that it should be
given to the prisoner—it was not for him to collect, it was to ask him if thæ
debts were accounted for—it was only the items of those persons

MR. CLARKSON. Q. The Object of the list was, to ask him which
