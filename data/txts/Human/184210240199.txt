   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  Is it not common in trade to address a person who is a buyer for a mercantile gentleman, by the name of his employer, when you do not happen to know his name? A.  Yes—but I addressed him in the name of the card he gave me—I presumed him to be Mr. Beaumont—the goods were destined for Maidstone, but were to be left at the Swan-with-two-Necks.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  YOU took, him for Beaumont, because he so re presented himself? A.  Yes—the goods were not to be taken to the coach office, but to be delivered at the bar of the Swan-with-two-Necks—whether they got to Maidstone I do not know—we have received no money.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILDE . Q.  Have you attended to this prosecution? A.  Yes—I have not attended any meeting of the creditors—I was present at the'first inquiry before the Magistrates—I think I saw Gosbell there then—I cannot say where he stood—he was passing in and out repeatedly—he was not in any particular part—I did not see him in communication with Mr. Goddard.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  When you were firef before the Magistrate did you know what share Gosbell had been supposed to have taken? A.  No. JENKIN JENKINS. I am porter to Messrs. Smarts'—I delivered the goods at the bar of the Swan-with-two-Necks, for Mr. Beaumont, of Maidstone.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  What goods were they? A.  Silks and velvets—I carried one parcel—I read the direction on it, "R. W. Beau mont, Week-street, Maidstone"—I delivered it to the barman. WILLIAM PATET. I am a perfumer, and live in Lombard-street. On the 25th of May, Shackleford came to my shop—he said be wanted some of our goods, if our prices and terms suited him—I said, "Very well, I dare say they will"—he gave me a card, "B. W. Beaumont, Waterloo bouse, Maidstone," and on the back he wrote two references, Mr. Bache lard and a Mr. Yapp, I think, but I did not see him—I went to Mr. Batchelard's, but I saw it was only an office—I did not ring the bell—I returned, and said it was not a business reference, I wanted a business reference—he said, "I can satisfy you on that point," and he gave me two other names, one of which was Mr. Ingram, and said be would call in an hour—I went and saw Mr. Ingram, and the reference to him was such that I went home, and told my shopman to take Mr. Beaumont's order when he came—I supplied him with goods to the amount of 25 l . or 26 l .;—there was one item of 1 l ., which he took with him the first day; and the others were sent to Week-street, Maidstone—he ordered a third parcel, but by some good luck we got it back again—the amount of our order, which is unpaid, is 26 s . or 27 l ., I forget which—I have never got any thing for it—I certainly never knew him by any other name than Beaumont.
