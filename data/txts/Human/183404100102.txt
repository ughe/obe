720. THOMAS SMITH, WILLIAM HINCHLEY, RICHARD HIBBERT, and JAMES LUTMAN were indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April , three sacks, value 3s.; and 12 bushels of wheat, value 4l.; the goods of William Burr , the master of the said Thomas Smith, William Hinchley, and Richard Hibbert.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
   CHARLES BURR. I am in the employ of William Burr; he is a miller, and lives at Hillingdon. The prisoners Smith, Hinchley, and Hibbert were in his employ: on the 5th of April, I was in the upper floor of the mill - I could see the wheat bin, and the wheat was running out - I called George Clarke, and we went down the ladder to the lower floor - I saw Hinchley and Smith taking the wheat down - Hibbert had got hold of a truck; they put the sacks of wheat on the truck, and took it to Lutman's cart, which was backed up against the door - we could not see the cart from there, but we went to another place, five or six feet off - we then saw them putting the wheat into Lutman's cart - they put in three sacks of wheat, and a sack of bran, Lutman was with the cart - my master had left about eleven o'clock that day, to go to Windsor - Lutman left with the cart a little before one o'clock - I went to dinner, and passed by the Fox, at Cowley, where I saw Lutman's cart standing with the four sacks in it.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How did you see them? A. Through a hole in the next floor, about as large as a pane of glass - the bin was on the side of the floor - we were in the floor over it - we kneeled down - we knew they were stealing it - the bin holds about four loads - my master does not sell wheat - the mill feeds from that bin - I was not there in the old foreman's time - Mr. Clarke is foreman now - I do not know of any dislike between Mr. Clarke and the prisoners - we could not see the bin, and the proceedings at the cart at the same time - I ran to another hole - I was on the second floor from the ground - Hinchley was holding a sack, Smith was holding a bag, and Hibbert wheeled it to the cart - Lutman was against the door - there were three holes; through one hole I could see the wheat running: through another hole I could see the cart - I went to one hole, and then to another.
 Q. Recollect yourself, you say you went to a hole in the loft, and saw from that into the yard, and saw Lutman and his cart, how did you do that? A. I ran to another hole, and then saw them putting it into the cart - we looked through a board - Mr. Clarke has not been tutoring me what to say - he spoke to me about this on the Monday morning, before I had been to the Justice, and the day before yesterday he did not tell me what I was to say - he asked me whether it was the truth, that I did see them, and I said yes.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you say correctly at first, that after you looked through the first floor, you descended a ladder, and looked through another hole? A. Yes; the bin is very large, the hopper is in the middle - the bag is at one corner - the wheat was not running from the middle where the hopper is, but from the corner where the bag is.
 GEORGE CLARKE. I am the son of  John Clarke, he is foreman of the prosecutor's mill. I was in the mill on the 5th of April, with Charles Burr, between twelve and one o'clock - we were at the top of the mill - there is a hole there - I looked through, and saw some wheat running out from the bin, from one corner where there was a bag - we then went down to the next floor, and looked through a hole, and saw the wheat run down into a sack; and the three prisoners who are employed in the mill, were there - we saw three sacks, which were put into Lutman's cart - we could see the door from the hole we looked through the second time - Hibbert wheeled the sack to Lutman's cart, and put them in - I told my father when he came home at night.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Why did not you tell your father when he came home to dinner? A. I do not know - I have not talked with my father above once or twice about them - he and the prisoners have had some quarrels - I told my father as I went home at night, and again the next day - when we first saw the prisoners, we were at the top of the mill - the hole was about as large as a square of glass - we looked through to the floor under us, and saw wheat running - the top of the bin is not inclosed by a sack.
 Q. Was the hole in the under floor immediately under the hole in the top floor? A. Yes; there was nothing to prevent our seeing through the two floors at once - I saw through the first hole the hopper, and then I came down to see through the other hole - the hole in the room where the bin was, is about five yards from the bin - I know the difference between a foot and a yard - it was about five yards.
 Q. Have you not been talking about five somethings, and forgotten whether it was five feet or five yards? A. No; it was five yards - there was no other cart in the road at that time - I know Beech - he came with his cart at the time Lutman went away - my father talked with Charles Burr about this business the next day before we went before the magistrate - my father talked to me once after this about it, but not in Burr's presence - he told me he had talked with Burr - I am sure of that - I do not know what quarrel my father has had with these prisoners - they have been disputing - my father has rather less wages than some of them - he is not jealous of them - they said something to offend him about three weeks before this, and they had quarrelled about a month before - my father is rather angry with them - I was at Clerkenwell with my father before the Grand Jury - we talked on the road from Uxbridge - he has not talked over and over again with me about it.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Had you quarrelled with the prisoners? A. No; the bran is not kept in the place where the wheat is.
 JOHN CLARKE. I am foreman to Mr. Burr. On the 5th April I was at the Fox public-house - I saw Lutman there with his cart - I do not know what was in it - my son gave me some information when I went home at night - I examined the corn bin - two or three sacks of corn were gone - I know the hole the boy showed me in the floor - I could see what they described.
