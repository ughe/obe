   WILLIAM WILSON. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and went with a boy to her lodgings - I found there a bundle, containing this bed-quilt and a duplicate of a pair of sheets; I found a few bits of gold on the prisoner.
   ROBERT BERSENT. I live in King's Head-court. The prisoner took my parlour about six weeks before the officer came with my apprentice, whom I had sent to the prisoner; I saw the articles found, which her niece had brought in a basket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. On the Sunday night I was sent for some rum - when I came back, my niece asked where Mrs. Ricketts was; I said, "In the bed-room, looking over the linen."
     GUILTY. Aged 44. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1886.  FRANCIS TAVERNER was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 1 hand-saw, value 3s., the goods of George Porter; and that he had before been convicted of felony.
   GEORGE PORTER. I am a journeyman carpenter. I lost my saw from a house near the Royal Oak, at Bromley, Middlesex, while I was at breakfast - the door had been secured by a prop against it inside; there could be no admittance but at the sash - I had been about five minutes in at breakfast, when a young man said he saw a man going out of the house, who came and inquired where we were -I went out, and took the prisoner about a quarter of a mile from there with my saw; I told him to give up the saw quietly, but he struck me, and was very violent - this is it.
   JAMES FITZPATRICK. I took the prisoner about eight years back, for stealing two saws and a rule - I had a certificate of his conviction, but have not brought it.
     GUILTY of stealing only. Aged 46. Transported for Seven Years.
 1887.  HENRY MACKELLOW was indicted for embezzlement.
   GEORGE HARRIS. I am a baker, and live in Berkeley-street, Clerkenwell. The prisoner had been in my employ sixteen months, and had to receive money - when he books his bread he gives me the account of the money he has received; he gave his account two or three times a week; he ought to have done it every day, but sometimes he was out.
   MARY MILLER. I dealt with the prosecutor for bread. Some time in June I paid the prisoner 1l., but he has made a memorandum of it and put it down the 13th of May - here is the bill in his writing; I have known him two years, and dealt with him when he was a master.
   CATHERINE KOHNLA. I live in Orchard-street. I paid the prisoner 2s. 101/2d. for his master the beginning of August, on a Wednesday or Thursday.
   RICHARD HARRIS. The prisoner never accounted to me for these sums in any way - I did not know they had been paid till after I had discharged him; I then went to him, and told him he was charged with embezzling my money; he said I was liar - he was confused; I gave charge of him- the officer afterwards brought me a bill of sale, saying he would pay by instalments.
 Cross-examined by MR. BALL. Q. Did not he give you notice to quit? A.No, Sir; I discharged him for spoiling the bread - I had lent him some money, and took 1s. or 2s. a week of him, as he could spare it; the security he offered was not for that money - he said it was for the money he had wronged me of; I charged him with embezzling 6l. -I know this bill; (looking at one) it is a bill of last January - my books are not confused; I am the only person to whom the prisoner accounted for money which he received - the prisoner left a sovereign with my wife and one with her mother, but I do not call that accounting to them; I have never said I would give 500l. to send him abroad.
 The prisoner received a good character, and a master promised to employ him. GUILTY. Aged 47.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 1888.  ANN WILLS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 3 spoons, value 1l. 15s., the goods of Thomas Harmston.
   EMMA HARMSTON. I am the daughter of Thomas Harmston, a grocer, of George-street, Portman-square - I have known the prisoner two or three years. On the 25th of August, about twelve o'clock, she came to borrow three table-spoons - I lent them to her; she said we were to have them back by three o'clock; that she had the care of a house in King-street, and the family were coming in.
   WILLIAM FOWLE. I am a pawnbroker. I have three spoons, pawned by the prisoner about twelve o'clock on the 25th of August.
   EDWARD BURRIDGE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - she said her mistress had sent the spoons to pawn; I said I would go to her mistress, and she said her mistress told her not to say where she lived.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 38.
 1889.  ANN WILLS was again indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 1 spoon, value 2s., the goods of Silas Boreham.
   SILAS BOREHAM. I live in South-street, Manchester-square, and am a butcher- the prisoner was in the habit of keeping houses for gentlemen. On the 25th of August she came to me, and said Mrs. Allen had got a few friends at No. 45, Manchester-street, and asked me to lend her a table-spoon - I said I had no table-spoon, I had an odd teaspoon, which she might have; she took it, and said she would return it in the evening - it was then about half-past eleven o'clock.
   WILLIAM FOWLE. This spoon was pawned with the others.(Property produced and sworn to.)
