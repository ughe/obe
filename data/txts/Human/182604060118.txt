WILLIAM GOODMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 3 pieces of paper, each being stamped with a stamp, denoting the payment of a duty of 1s. 9d., value 1s. 9d. each, and 21 other pieces of stamped paper, value 1s. 3d. each, the goods of Charles Cooper, John Henry Cooper, and William Cooper.

 888.  CHARLES MORRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of March , 1 iron bound cask, value 7s. 6d., 10 gallons and 1 quart of noyeau, value 5l. 12s. 6d., the goods of Francis Pathonier and Maria Weller.
   WILLIAM FAIRBAIRN. I am clerk to Francis Pathonier and Maria Weller, distillers, of Silver-street, Wood-street. I know this cask to be their property - there were ten gallons and a quart of noyeau in it.
   THOMAS STYLES. I am carman to the prosecutors. On the 3d of March I left the cart at the corner of Angel-street- when I came back this cask was gone; I had left it on the cart about three minutes and a half before; there had been seven in the cart, and this laid behind, on the near side, by the tail board.
   THOMAS HUGHES. I was at the corner of St. Ana's-lane, where I saw a cart, with the name of Pathonier and Weller on it - the carman was gone up the street, and I saw a man get into the cart, and deliver this cask out to the prisoner - the other weat towards Newgate-street; the prisoner went towards Bull and Mouth-street. I and several others stopped him, and asked where he was going with it - he said a man had ordered him to take it to a place just by; he then put down the cask, but we made him bring it back. I went and got the officer.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not see the man come and ask me if I would carry a load for him? A. No. - there were five of you together.
   GEORGE THOMPSON. I am a constable. I took the prisoner and cask to Pathonier and Wellers.
 Prisoner's Defence. A man came and asked me to carry this load; I said Yes, and he got into the cart.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 889.  HENRY CADE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Edward Lodge Ogle , from his person.
 EDWARD LODGE OGLE. On the 5th of April I was in  Gracechurch-street; I felt my pocket touched, turned round, and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand - I saw him drop it. I took him to the Mansion House. I had it in my pocket an instant before.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going along Gracechurch-street - there was a sailor and another young man behind this gentleman, and directly he turned round they turned, and dropped the handkerchief - I took it up, and was going to put it into my pocket, when he took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Transported for Seven Years.
 890.  WILLIAM DADE and JOHN GOODHALL were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of March , 42lbs. of solder, value 20s.; 1 sucker for a pump, value 6d., and 7 wall-hooks, value 6d., the goods of Robert Hardcastle and Sarah Dunn.
   JOHN DUNN. My mother, Sarah Dunn, is in partnership with Robert Harcastle. The prisoners had been in their employ seven or eight months; between five and seven o'clock on the evening of the 4th of March, I gave Goodhall the key of the solder cupboard - I then took it away, and said I had not time to lock to it, but in ten minutes after I gave him the key again to go down to the solder cupboard - he went down, with Dade and Hedley - Goodhall was below, and Dade at the top, and Hedley was handing the solder to the counting-house - I stood over the cellar looking at them - I waited for the key; when they had got it all up, I said, "Is it all up?" they said,"Yes;" I then hung up the key and went to my tea - on my return I found all the men waiting, except those three - I went after them, and found them still in the cellar - I jumped down, then Dade ran from them and I called him, but he would not come - I called Goodhall to give me a light, and I held it to Dade, and said, "Is that you?" I took him into the counting-house - he said "It is my first offence;" I said, "You have been found guilty before, but forgiven;" I sent Goodhall for an officer - he was gone some time, and in the mean time the officer came; they were taken to the watch-house - I was present when they were searched; this new sucker was found on Dade, which I believe to be my mother's - and here are seven wall-books, which I cannot swear to - but we had articles of this description; here is half of a sucker, which was found on Goodhall; this solder was found on Dade, under his coat, when I went into the cellar - it is of the same description as that they handed out of the cellar - these four pieces were secreted away, and found the following morning.
