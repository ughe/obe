I had money at home and it—would be all right—I was told the horse lost the race on which the 10 l . was put on for me—I didn't know anything about it—I was told I had 15 l . on Lost Sheep—I had no ticket given me for it—Grant did not put it down on the race card at that time—it was put down, but I could not understand how much I had lost and won—I was told that the race was lost by Lost Sheep—I said I was a "lost sheep" when I was leaving the course—I was told I had won 40 l . at one time—I did not say that I objected to winning 40 l .—I had shrewd suspicions at the time that I had been swindled—I had repudiated the bets all along—I did not repudiate tie bet when I was told I had won 40 l .—we all left—Grant left the cab and went away for the purpose of getting some money—the bookmaker was not with us in the cab—Grant came back and said he had not sufficient money, but I cannot remember the amount he said—he did not say he was willing to hand over his 14 l . to Cannon—I swear that" he handed over some money, but I cannot say how much—he had what appeared to be paper money—I believe he said something to Cannon about it being all he had got, and that he would make it up the nest morning—I recollect the next morning was the 25th August—I recollect the bookmaker said at the Cannon Street Hotel that he should be there in the morning, but at all events he should be on the race course—a part of the next morning was wet—it was raining in the morning when I went to Cannon Street Hotel; not pouring in torrents—it was not a day I should enjoy being on Croydon race-course—I went to Croydon to see if Grant or Cannon were there—I said that at the police-court—Cannon said when I met him that I had not kept my appointment—he did not say I ought—to have waited till ten—I left Charing Cross for the race-course on the 25th by about one o'clock. JOHN BUSHELL( Policeman  294 E ). I was on duty on the 27th August outside Charing Cross station when the last witness pointed out to me a man named Draper outside the post-office and gave him into my custody—I took him to the station and saw him searched—he had on him 5 l ." in gold, 2 s . in silver, and 7 d . in bronze, jockey passes for the grand stand, a duplicate for a gold watch pawned for 5 l ., a dummy watch and a metal chain—he was bailed out subsequently.
