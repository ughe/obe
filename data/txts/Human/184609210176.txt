WILLIAM CHURCHYARD. I am a tailor, and live at Clapham. About eleven or twelve o'clock, on the 17th of Sept., I left my shop, and when I re turned I missed a waistcoat—I went out and met the prisoner—I asked if he had been at Clapham that morning—he said, "Yes"—I asked him what he had got in his pocket—he said, "A rule"—I found this black waistcoat in his pocket—it is mine.
   COURT . Q.  When you came home, did you see anybody leave your shop? A.  Yes—I found the prisoner with the waistcoat, in about an hour afterward, and he was dressed as the person was who left the shop—I said to him, "What have you got?"—he said he had nothing but a rule, and afterwards he said he had the waistcoat.
   Prisoner's Defence . The gentleman said he thought it was me. I bought it of a young man with a fustian coat; I do not know him; he came and asked me if I wanted to buy a waistcoat; I gave him 6 s . for it; when this gentleman came, he asked me if I had got a waistcoat? I said, "Yes, a double—breasted waistcoat." GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Six Months .

 1969. CHARLES SMITH was indicted for stealing 1 blanket, value 3 s  . and 1 counterpane, 1 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Thomas Eley . THOMAS ELEY. I am a tailor, and live in Granby—street, Waterloo—road—in the middle of Aug. the prisoner came tomy house to live with a woman, and agreed to pay me the rent by instalments, half on Wednesday and half on Saturday, which they did the first week—the second week went on till the Thursday—I named it, and he said, "You shall have it by—and—bye"—it went on till the Saturday, the middle of the day—I asked him, and he said, "You need not keep asking about your rent; if you do, I shall pay you and go"—I said, I do not care if you do"—I went into their room on the Sunday—I found they were both out, and missed a blanket—while I was there the prisoner came and said, "Did you think I was gone?"—I said, "Itlooked very much like it; you have shut the place up, and gone out"—he said, "You are not a fool"—I said, "No, not quite; but where is the blanket?"—he said, "I do not know"—I said, "Well, I shall keep you here till the policeman comes"—he went to the tea—caddy and gave me the duplicate of the blanket—he said, "Here is the ticket; will you forgive me, if I give you one shilling"—(it was pawned for one shilling)—I said, "No"—I took the shilling—he said, "You" are not going to turn me out"—I said, "No, but you must go down in the kitchen, and I lock this room up"—on the Monday my wife came home, and said, "They have got the counterpane."
   Prisoner.  She keeps a house of ill—fame. Witness . It is a lodging—house.
   COURT . Q.  Is it not a house where men and women come to sleep? A.  one lodger has been in the house six years and another seven.
   Prisoner . He has three females, two walk the Strand every night, iw another walks Pall—mall; the woman I was with used to go out, and brought in several men, and I went out. Witness . That is false. ELIZABETH ELEY. I missed a counterpane—the duplicate of it has not been found—the counterpane was lost while they occupied my woman took the apartment, but the prisoner lived with her.
