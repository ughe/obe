I took the prisoner up in company with Coleman, on suspicion of this robbery - Thompson was with me at the time; she said,"I know nothing at all about it" - I said "What made you run up Cannon-street so?" she said "I did not run, that I know of" - I was then going to take her to her lodging in Bluegate-fields, but she was going past, and said, she did not lodge there then - I said, "I know where you lodge," and took her to her room; I asked the landlady, if that was her room; she said it was - I then asked her what money she had got; she said all the money she had was one farthing, which was on the mantel-piece, and which I found - I then searched, and in a box under the bed I found this purse, and six sovereigns in it, which she said a seaman had left with her, when he went to sea.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not she know you were an officer? A. Yes, she did; she did not tell me where she lived, but was going past the place.
   JOHN THOMPSON. This is not my purse. I know nothing of it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am innocent. A young man left me 4l. when he went to sea, and has sent me 2l. since.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1746.  ROBERT TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , 4 pairs of boots, value 4s.; 5 pairs of shoes, value 15s.; 5 bed-gowns, value 10s.; 9 caps, value 15s.; 1 watch, value 1l., and 1 coat, value 1l., the goods of Evan Thomas.
   LOUISA THOMAS. I am the wife of Evan Thomas, tailor, of Old Tothill-street. On the 7th of August, the watch was in the drawer, the coat hung on the back of a chair, and the other articles were in the shop - the prisoner is a neighbour's son, and lives just by - I had seen these articles safe when I put up the shutters in the evening, and missed them about seven o'clock the next morning. The house had been entered by the back premises, through a hole, which had been forced in the partition - these shoes, and this bed-gown are mine.
   THOMAS PACE. I am an officer. The two houses were formerly one - there is a partition now between them. I went to the prisoner's father's house, on the 8th of August, with a search-warrant - in one corner of the shop next to the prosecutor's premises, I found a hole, and a bag hanging over it; I lifted up the bag, and found this blue nightgown, and this pair of shoes; and on the other side of the partition, I found this cap, which the prosecutrix identified. I was looking after the prisoner the whole of the day, but the prosecutrix met him, and took him to the watch-house. I saw him there, and asked him what he had done with the things - he said he had given them to his brother, and knew no more about them. His brother, I am sorry to say, is a very bad lad. He was taken up, but not committed.
   ROBERT GREENHILL. I am an officer, and received the prisoner at the watch-house. I found on him this key of the prosecutor's street-door, which hung up behind the door on the Monday, and was missed on the night of the robbery.
   WILLIAM HALL. I am an officer. I went with Pace, and saw him find those things. I went round, and found where the hole came through, into a very dark place, under the prosecutor's staircase.   GUILTY. Aged 12. Transported for Seven Years.
 1747.  ABRAHAM USILL was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 1 fixture. (i.e.) 1 iron stove, value 10s., belonging to Richard Pemberton , and fixed to a certain building of his.
   JAMES DENTON. I am clerk to Mr. Pemberton - he has a house in Wapping-street, which the prisoner agreed to take for 40l. a year. There was an agreement, which was signed by both parties, and the prisoner was to have the use of the fixtures - there were stoves fixed in the house at the time he entered, on the 28th of April. He said that he was in the iron way - he occupied the house, but never resided there; his lad and one of his men were there. On the 16th of July I went there, and missed a stove from the room, on the second floor - the prisoner was not there. This is the stove, which I have no doubt was in that room.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. You saw the house before it was let, and you saw this particular stove there? A. Yes; he was to be a yearly tenant; I had received the rent for it before then - the stove was fixed.
   WILLIAM CRUTCHLEY. I was in the employ of the prisoner. I cannot say exactly when he went to this house, which is 265, Wapping-street - he did not carry on any business there. On the Saturday before I was taken up, he sent me to a green-grocer's, to say that he had a stove for sale - the green-grocer was not then at home, but he came afterwards and had the stove, which came, I think, from the first floor in that house - I had helped the prisoner to take it down the night before.
 Cross-examined. Q. When was this? A. About seven o'clock one Saturday night, but I cannot tell what month. It was the week before he was taken up - he told me the things were his property, and I was not to take any notice.
   SAMUEL GODDARD. I am a green-grocer, and live in Gravel-lane, The last witness came to me with a message one Saturday night, the 14th of July, I went with him to 265, Wapping-street - I there bought this stove of the prisoner - he asked me 16s., but I got it for 4s. 6d.
 Cross-examined. Q. I suppose you gave the worth of it? A. I gave as much as it was worth to me - I might have kept it seven years; I gave a fair market price.
   BENJAMIN BLABY. I am an officer. I took up the prisoner on a charge of taking away fixtures, out of this house at Wapping, and selling them - he said he thought they were his own - he had taken the house, and had a right to the fixtures.
 Prisoner Defence. I took the house and warehouse, to carry on the ship-chandlery business; I undertook to put the house in repair; and this grate I was going to replace with a new one, which would have been three times its value. I had goods ordered, and I expected them in the day after I was taken, but I was obliged to countermand them to save my credit; I had not run away from the house - I was there two or three times a day.
