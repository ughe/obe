MR. PAYNE. Q. You know that he was tried and convicted here in Feb.
1846, for sending a threatening letter to you, for Elizabeth Spriggs ? A. I do
-I believe my case did not come before the Court on that occasion-it wa
some other case upon which he was convicted-he was not tried upon mine.
The letter was here read, as follows-"Mrs. Spriggs, No. 45, White-
street, Borough-London, Aug. 12th, 1847.-Mrs. Spriggs, -As I have before
told you, I am happy to inform you that I know the residence of your daugh-
ter Elizabeth, and I can safely promise you, that before another two months
has passed away, that your daughter will be in her grave; for as true as there
is a God in heaven, I do intend to murder her, and before that time has
passed away too, be the consequences what they may. Yours truly, Henry
J. Killerby."
Donald MURRAY ( policeman, M 119.) I apprehended the prisoner, on
Saturday night, the 14th, on a warrant-I read it to him-it stated that be
was to be taken for sending a threatening letter to Elizabeth Spriggs-he
said he was very sorry for it, but he had no meaning in it.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you hold him out any inducement to say that?
A. No.
MR. BRIARLY called
HARRIET ELIZABETH KILLERBY. I am the prisoner's sister-he will be
nineteen in Dec.-- he is a very quiet inoffensive boy-he is fond of writing
and reading-he reads novels-if he had wished to injure Elizabeth he could
have done so very well, for she has been in the habit of coming and sitting in
our room with us, and playing with me of an evening, up to the time she went
to school she bas not been in our house since my brother came home in Feb.
-before he went to prison, she and her sister used to be always with me
and he used to come and sit down and read, and take no notice of any one-
she has been about our place, but he never spoke to her he used to read
Lloyd's Miscellany, and those sort of books-he has two large volumes of it,
full of such nonsense as this-I have looked into them many times- there
are tales about secret conspirators, persons that have made a great noise in
the world, love tales, and tales about carrying off young ladies into caverns,
but he was quite inoffensive notwithstanding this-he never intended it-
juring any one.
MR. PAYNE. Q. He was a mischievous kind of fellow, was he not? 4.
I do not know what you mean-he sent poison in three letters to three
different young ladies, and was convicted in one case.
MR. BRIARLY. Q. Was it ascertained that it was poison that was sent
A. No, I do not know that it was-it was thrown in the fire-it was sent to
a Miss Ward.
EDWARD KENDALL (re-examined.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's
former conviction-(read-Convicted 23rd Feb., 1846, for attempting to ad-
minister poison to Elizabeth Clouter, and confined one year)-I was present at
the trial--the prisoner is the person who was so convicted.
Guilty. Aged 19.--Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutrix.-Confined
One Year, and once publicly Whipped.
