William RICHARDSON. I am a police inspector. I was on duty at
Ilford station about half past seven o'clock in the morning on the 12th of
February, and received information from Mr. Black-I went up the road, and
saw a cart driven by the prisoner-I passed it at Stratford, and got a small
distance in front of it I desired the sergeant to keep in the rear of it-I
proceeded to the Bell-and-Mackerel public-bouse-I saw the cart driven to
the door-a man came to the prisoner, and he stopped about a quarter of
an hour-the prisoner then drove on, accompanied by the other man, who
had fed the horses, into Ducket's field, which is out of the main road-I
then saw the man who had fed the horses get into the cart-I saw him take
two empty sacks out, and place them on the hind ladder--the sacks were
the wrong side outwards, and had oats hanging to them I then desired
the officer to take the prisoner, and to take care of the sacks--the sacks
were the prosecutor's--the oats have never been found the prisoner had
got three miles and a half from his master's before I overtook him.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he draw up at the Three Pigeons? 4. No,
he did not stop there--from what I had heard I had reason to believe the
property would be left at the Bell and Mackerel -I kept in front of him
about two miles-when I first saw the sacks they were empty, and they
were making preparations to fill the cart with dung-the sacks were merely
moved to prepare to load the dung.
