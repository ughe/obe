   WILLIAM HENRY MCQUEEN. I live at No. 72, Newman-street. Henry Carman is in my service.
   HENRY CARMAN. I live in Foley-street, and am errand-boy to the prosecutor - he is a copper-plate printer. About six o'clock in the evening, on the 4th of Decemher, he gave me a book, containing 1500 letter-press sheets, to take to Mr. Jewel, a hot-presser, in Peterborough-court, Fleet-street; in going along I set my books down at the corner of Southampton-street, Holborn- I turned to look at a person, and when I turned again, I saw the prisoner running away with my books; he had them on his shoulder - I ran after him, and took them from him; I did after that lose sight of him for a moment, but when I saw him again, he was still running - he ran down to Princes-street, where I lost sight of him for about two minutes; I had before that cried Stop thief! and he was stopped by a Policeman, who was but a short distance when the books were taken - I at once knew him to be the same person; they were worth 50s.
 Prisoner. You said 5l. at Bow-street. Witness. No, I said I did not know, and master said 50s.
   FRANCIS REYNOLDS. I am a Policeman. About seven o'clock on the night of the 4th of December, I heard Stop thief! called, and saw the prisoner running - a man put his foot out and threw him down, and I took him; he had been running very fast - he begged very hard to be let go; I took him above two hundred yards from where the books were lost.
 COURT to  HENRY CARMAN. Q. How many yards do you think it was before you saw him again after you lost sight of him? A. About a hundred.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been out of work eight months, was walking along Holborn, and this paper stood at the corner of Princes-street - a gentleman respectably dressed asked me to carry it to Southampton-street, he gave me 6d. and I showed it to the Magistrate.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.
 THE KING v.  JOSEPH MATHER.
 14.  JOSEPH MATHER, who was convicted in July Session of uttering a forged acceptance, (see Crowder, Mayor, page 568) being called to the bar, Mr. Justice Littledale proceeded to deliver the decision of the Judges upon his case, as follows: - "This was an indictment against Joseph Mather for forging an acceptance to a bill of exchange. The prisoner was convicted in July Session, before Mr. Justice James Parke, on an indictment, charging him with uttering and publishing as true, &c.; the indictment was founded on 7 Geo. 2, c. 22, sec. 1 - it was stated that at the last Spring assizes at York, Mr. Baron Hullock had held that this Statute was in this respect repealed by 45 Geo. 3, c. 89, sec. 1, and that the proper form of indictment was on the latter Statute, which makes it an offence to "offer, dispose of, and put away," in consequence of that decision Mr. Justice James Parke directed the judgment to be respited, and reserved the question for the consideration of the Judges: Mr. Justice James Parke has since found that in Fauntleroy's case (2 Buigh, page 413) the verdict was entered on the Counts, charging the prisoner with "uttering and publishing as true," a forged deed, and no objection was taken on this ground. The case has been submitted to the consideration of several of the judges, who unanimously resolved that the conviction was proper; and Mr. Justice Bosanquet informed the Court that the point had been decided by three Judges at the Old Bailey.
