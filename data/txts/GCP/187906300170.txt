GEORGE BRODIE WHEELER. I am a grocer at Richmond-on 7th March,
in consequence of orders given, I sent some goods to 2, Mayfield-in
consequence of something I heard I went there on 18th March-I saw
two of Mr. Weston's vans at the door-I saw the prisoner and said “My
name is Wheeler, ma'am ; I have called for my account"-she said "Come
in ; I'll pay you”—when I got inside she asked me if I had a stamp-I
said "No, I can soon procure one"--I left and came back with one-I re-
ceipted and stamped the bill and she gave me 21. 28. from a purse in her
pocket--the amount being 21. 28. 6d. ; I said " It is sixpence more, ma'am"
- she went into the front room and came back and gave me sixpence-I
did not see where she got it from-her manner seemed rather flurried.
Cross-examined. That was the first time I had seen her-I was standing
in the hall when she took out the amount, sixpence short---she went into the
front sitting room and came out with the requisite change--I do not know
whether Church was tehre.
