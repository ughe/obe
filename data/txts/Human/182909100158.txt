   Robert Carter's Defence. My wife has been in the habit, for twenty years, of keeping the cash; and when I wanted money to pay away, I always sent to her for it.
 Robert Carter, Jun.'s Defence. When I was taken into custody. I was at work in Stacey-street; my father came and said I was wanted, and I went with him to Queen-square - I have been in the habit of receiving notes for my father, and giving them to my mother; and receiving them from her, when I wanted them to pay away.
 Elizabeth Carter delivered in a long written defence, stating that she was on the most familiar terms with the deceased, who usually called her "Dear Carter, &c.;" and felt unhappy if any one but herself attended to her; and that, a few days previous to her death, she told her to send her daughter out of the room, and then gave the notes into her hand, telling her to keep them if she died, and telling her to take every thing in the place for herself, and had often promised to make her and family comfortable. That the papers in question, must have got into the desk when Miss Springweiler was rummaging it.
   HARRIET DOBBINS. I have lodged in Carter's house for sixteen months; I was called down the morning Mrs. Keefe died, to assist Mrs. Carter - it was when she was dying - I had seen her the Friday before her death, and at other times; I have heard her say Mrs. Carter behaved with the greatest kindness to her - on the Friday before she died, my sister and I were in the room helping her into bed; she was much troubled with dropsy - I asked if her doctor was aware of her being in that situation; she said, she believed so - that Mr. Farre was her doctor, and he was her nephew, but he was gone to Brighton, for the benefit of his health - she told me Mrs. Carter was to have all her property when she died, and that she had already made her a very handsome compliment - she was very infirm, and required great attention; Mrs. Carter paid her every attention - she slept in the room over Mrs. Carter, and had a stick to thump the boards to call her - I have heard Mrs. Carter say she went to a physician, in consequence of hurting herself by lifting her out of bed, as she was pregnant - I was taken to the Police-office on the part of the prosecution but not examined.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How often in the course of your being there did you see Mrs. Keefe? A. I should think four or five times - I was not at all intimate with her; every time I went into the room she talked about her property that she had given it to Mrs. Carter; I only saw her four times - the first time might have been five or six weeks before her death; she told me about the handsome compliment on the Friday - she said there was a time-piece on the mantel-shelf which had been given to her by an old sweetheart, and she had given that to Mrs. Carter; I heard nothing of Carter wanting 1s. more to attend her - I was not with her more than ten minutes when she died; she was sitting on the nightchair, but I assisted Mrs. Carter to put her into bed - that was at near ten o'clock in the morning; I never told the officer of this - I was at the office on the day Mrs. Carter went to prison.
   CATHERINE WILLIAMS. I sat up with Mrs. Keefe  the night before she died - I heard Mrs. Brown ask Mrs. Keefe if Miss Springweiler had been to see her; she said she had nothing to do with her - she owed her nothing, and did not want her; a little before that Mrs. Carter was assisting her, and she said, "Carter, my dear Carter, my love! I want no one here but you, you are the first here" - Catherine Carter, the daughter, slept in the room before Mrs. Keefe died, and had done so for some time; on the Sunday night Mrs. Carter asked if she wanted Miss Springweiler - she said she did not want her there; she owed her nothing - "I want none here but you, Carter;" I remember her once sending for Robert Carter, Jun. - she gave him a glass of wine, and said,"My boy, I respect you, you are a nice youth - Will you have a commission in the army or navy? I will buy it you, and make a gentleman of you;" the boy thanked her, and said he did not want either - I remember, when Mrs. Keefe lodged in Carey-street, Mrs. Carter sent me to see her there three times; she desired me to give her kind respects to her, and begged she would move, as she was so poorly - she thought she had better not move back to her house; this was about a fortnight before she did move - Mrs. Keefe's answer was, that so Dr. Farre said, but that did not matter; that her rent where she was, was only 12s. a week, and Mrs. Carter's 13s. - I said I thought she paid 15s. there; she said she did not, but that did not matter - she would go back to Carter's, and if she was to die she would die there; I remember Carter burting herself by lifting her out of bed - she was often in the habit of doing so.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you very intimate with Mrs. Keefe? A. Yes - I washed for her three years ago; I have seen her several times as a friend - I used to go and work in her room; she was very stingy indeed - it surprised me to see her give young Carter wine.
   JANE TAYLOR. I am a widow, and live in Stanhope-street, Clare-market. Mrs. Keefe came from Carter's to lodge with me, in October, 1824 - she has spoken to me very handsomely of Mrs. Carter, and often wished to go back there again; I know Mrs. Carter was pregnant in May, or the beginning of June.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long did Mrs. Keefe lodge at your house? A. Six or seven months - she then moved over the way; I never visited her at Carter's - she never sent for me to go and see her at Carter's.
