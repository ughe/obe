289. JOHN KNIGHT (24), was indicted for breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of Thomas Horne, and sterling a watch and coat and other
goods. (See page 303)
Mr. Frith conducted the Prosecution.
Thomas HORNE. I live at 50, Desborough Street, Pimlico - on the night
of 6th October, I went to bed about 11 o'clock--I was the last person up;
the house was locked up and secured-about 7 o'clock next morning I found
the house in confusion--the drawers were broken open, in the dining-room
especially, and papers strewed about in all directions-I missed a great
oont, a walking stiok, a scarf, & cigar case, two purses which contained a
small amount of silver, and a watoh was taken from my daughter's room-
these (produced) are my articles-1 found this watch at the pawn shop-the
house had been entered from the balcony by breaking & pane of glass in
the drawing-room window, and I found the doors open, and the broken
glass on the carpet--there were marks along the balcony.
JAMES COLES. I am assistant to Messrs. Lloyd & Co., Pawnbrokers-this
walking stick was pawned with us on the 8th October, for 1s. 6d., in the
name of James Pearson, by the prisoner.
WILLIAM LILLYSTONE (Dectective K.) The prisoner was brought to the
station on 15th October last, on another charge-I searched him and found
this purse and cigar case, and a pawn ticket for & walking stick, pledged on
8th October-he was wearing this scarf round his neck.
SQUIRA White (Detective Sergeant B.) The last witness gave me this
pawn ticket, and I went to Messrs. Lloyd's with the prosecutor-he identified
the stick-I examined the house on the 8th, and found the window broken,
and the house in great confusion--I have received a letter since the prisoner
has been in prison--after he was committed for trial he called me to the
cells at the Westminster Police Court and said " It will do you some good,
I will tell you where the property is of which I have had anything to do"
- he then told me he had pawned the watch of Mr. Horne's at a pawn-
broker's, opposite Victoria Station, on the day after the robbery for 21., or
21. 108., in the name of Pearson--he said he had sold the ticket for 58, to
a coffee-house keeper-he also said " It is a good job Miss Horne did not
see me, she looked very nice-" he made a laugh of it-I afterwards went
and found the watch and the ticket as he had stated-he told me that the
coats and the other property that was missing were pledged by the other
two at the bottom of the Ranelagh Road, either for 108., or 158.
The prisoner in his defence stated that the things were given to him by a
shipmate of his, and that he had nothing to do with the burglary. Guilty of
receiving. He was also charged with having been before convicted.
JOSEPH SMITH. I produce a certificate of conviction of John Edward
White on 9th January, 1871, of burglary, sentence eighteen months—I
was in court when he was tried, and saw him convicted - the prisoner is the
same person-I have not the least doubt about him.
GUILTY **-Ten Years' Penal Servitude.
