MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was it not proposed on the part of Mr. Solomons's
solicitor, or himself, that he should refund such of the property as he had
in his possession, and be admitted for the Crown? A. Yes, this was
stated openly and publicly before the Magistrate in the police office, and
his consent requested before it was done the two Magistrates, on the re-
presentation of the counsel for the prosecution, gave their full consent to it
---at that time Moss was a prisoner, and had not been examined as a wit-
ness-it was stated to the Magistrate that the best test that Mr.
Solomon was about to tell the truth, was his delivering up the property,
so that they could depend upon what he said it was also stated by the
counsel for the prosecution that it was utterly impossible they could make
out any case without making some of the prisoners witnesses, with
Q. And was it not under a full conviction on all your parts that Moss
was not to be depended on, having given such contradictory statements,
that Solomon was admitted as a witness? A. Yes, but nothing whatever
was done until every thing had been stated to the Magistrates, and their
consent asked and given, in the presence of the prisoners the value of the
property given up by Solomon is about 24001.—I am quite satisfied we
could not have gone to trial on the evidence of Moss, unless we had strong
corroborative testimony-I think it ought to be strong. I

--that was on the 15th of December last-it was a consignment from Fal-
month I was in London then, and he went with it to the Bank-Allen
was our collecting clerk-he had access to the different offices we trusted
