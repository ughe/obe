   Cross-examined. Q.  Was the prisoner trying to get from her? A.  It appeared so to me; she was calling for her to loose her—they were down together for three or four minutes, the deceased clenching her tight with both hands—she was drunk, and always has been drunk ever since she lived in my room—she was very troublesome, particularly to the prisoner—I believe she was endeavouring to get her into her room on this occasion—she was kind to her mother—the mother was always calling her bad names, and accusing her of robbing her of 10 l .—the prisoner is married—no one attended to the deceased but her. THOMAS NORMAN( policeman, L  23). I was sent for, and saw the prisoner and deceased—they were standing in the passage when I got in—blood was running from the back part of deceased's head, and her face was beaten all across—she gave the prisoner in charge for robbing her of 10 l ., and for assaulting her—as I was taking her to the station, she said, "Ah the d----old wretch, I will have her life now before I have done with her." SARAH HEMMINGS. On 30th Aug. I went into the passage, hearing a noise, and saw the deceased lying on the floor with her head on the step, and having hold of the prisoner's hair—they remained in that position five minutes—I said to the prisoner, "Shall I send for a policeman, or come to your assist ance?"—she said, "Pray send for a policeman"—I went to look for one, and when I came back they were up—the deceased's face was very much swollen, but that it always was—it was more swollen than usual, and a little black.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe she was constantly drunk? A.  I never saw her sober; she fell about very much—she had a very awkward fall a week before—she was drunk at nine o'clock this very morning—I heard her accuse the prisoner of robbing her of 10 l .—I never saw the prisoner drunk—she was always kind and attentive to her mother. SARAH CRESS. I live at No. 12. On 30th Aug. I came through the passage, and saw the prisoner and her mother there—the prisoner asked her for the key of her room—the deceased said, "No, I will not give it you, you w----, you have robbed me enough"—she was drunk—the prisoner then pushed her down—she feil back, and her head came against the step—the prisoner said, "Now will you give me the key?"—she said, "No," and called her a w----again several times—the prisoner then stooped down, and beat her across the face and eyes with her double fist—Mr. and Mrs. Hemmings were in and out of the passage at the time, but they did not stop long—a, young man who stood there, said, "Don't beat your mother any more "—the prisoner said, "Shall I strangle her, and rid the world of such a monster as she is?"—she was standing at that time, and her mother was lying on her back—she had her bonnet on, and when she got up I observed blood on it—her face was very much beaten and bruised, and her eyes were black a quarter of an hour afterwards.
