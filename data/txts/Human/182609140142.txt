 1658.  WILLIAM SEDGWICK was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July , 1 coat, value 4s., the goods of Elizabeth Stewart, widow.
 SECOND COURT, stating it to belong to a person unknown.
   ROBERT CARTER. I live with Mr. Gulston, at the Feathers public-house, at Milbank. On the 10th of July I saw some persons in a boat; some of whom fell over and were drowned; the others were brought on shore by the watermen; they were very wet, and were taken to Mr. Gulston's; there was a coat left in the boat; I saw the prisoner step to the boat, and take it out, and walk with it towards our skittle-ground, and then through the half-hatch; I had seen him before, and knew him. The waterman said he could not stop, he must go with his fare - the next night, when I was out with my beer, he met me, and said, if I said any thing about it, he would knock my bl-y thundering head off.
   WILLIAM BLUNDLE. I was in the boat on the 10th of July, with my brother-in-law, named Stewart; he was drowned; his coat was left in the boat; he had pulled it off to row; there was no other coat in the boat; it has not been found.
   JOHN WARREN. I took up the prisoner; he said nothing to me; I could not find the waterman.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw the men fall out of the boat; I handed the coat up to one of the gentlemen who was leading that witness up to the house; I never went away till the man was found who had been drowned.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1660.  GEORGE SMITH and THOMAS DOWSETT were indicted for stealing, on the 28th of June , 2 hams, value 28s., the goods of Samuel Brown.
 SAMUEL BROWN. I am a  cheesemonger, and live in Whitechapel. I lost two hams from my shop-window while I was absent.
   WILLIAM MACKEY. I live nearly opposite to Mr. Brown. On the 28th of June I saw Smith and another person, who is not here, go up to the shop; Smith took a ham from the window, - they walked up Angel-alley - Smith then came again with Dowsett, who took another ham; they walked off; I spoke to a person, who went and told Mr. Brown's shopman of it - next morning they passed my house, and crossed over; the officer was there, and took them; I knew both their persons well.
   CHARLES READ. Mackey told me the hams were stolen, and I saw the prisoners again in the road; I ran to the shop and asked if they were the two persons, and I took them.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 DOWSETT -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1661.  JOSEPH SHENSTON and ROBERT FITZGERALD were indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , 2lbs. and 6ozs. weight of tea, value 3s., the goods of the United Company of Merchants, trading to the East Indies.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND and LAW conducted the prosecution.
   SAMUEL BALL. I am assistant elder at the East India-house. On the 19th of July, in consequence of information, I directed Gale to watch in a warehouse.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Is it not customary to search every body who goes out? A. Yes; but they might conceal tea, as they are not rubbed down indecently.
   GEORGE WARREN GALE. I am a labourer to the East India Company; the prisoners are also labourer s. On the 19th of July Ball desired me to watch; I examined the contents of a handkerchief and bag, which were on a pile of chest tops; they both contained black tea - at twenty minutes past ten o'clock I heard two persons in the passage, between the chests, and in an instant I saw Shenston come to this place; he took the handkerchief of tea, and put it into his hat; he went to the top of the alley, and said to Fitzgerald "All right" - Fitzgerald then came and took the bag; I went away, and informed Mr. Ball, and a constable was sent for.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you mark the bag in which you say the tea was? A. No; three men have been dismissed from the warehouse.
   JOHN GIBBS. I am an officer. I took the prisoners into custody; I found Shenston at work in the third floor, mending a chest - I went down to take Fitzgerald, but he was gone to the privy - I saw frost search Shenston; he put his hat down, and I found this handkerchief in it, with this 2lbs. 6ozs. of tea in it - he said, it was his hat and handkerchief; he wanted the handkerchief back - I then fetched Fitzgerald from the privy, and found in his pocket this bag, with a few grains of tea in it.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ALLEY. Q. Was not the part of the warehouse in which you searched him, in the City? A. No.
   WILLIAM FROST. I saw this tea found in Shenston's hat; I took these two small bags out of Shenston's trousers pocket, and I saw another bag taken from him by Gibbs, with a few grains of tea in it.
