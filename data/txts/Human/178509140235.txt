 (Here the learned Judge repeated the evidence as read by the Short-hand Writer.)
 Now, it is manifest, that the effect of this evidence at the time was to shew a consciousness of guilt in Newberry, and to confirm the testimony of Lockell, for the sum of the evidence of Harvey was, that when he went in quest of Newberry, Newberry slipt out of the public house when he saw the prosecutor come in, and that he actually ran away from him, and escaped, and that he actually took him at some small distance from his lodgings; the effect of that evidence was to shew, that Newberry fled from justice, which is always considered as a circumstance of guilt; then the indictment charges that the evidence was false; that he did not run after him at all, nor did he run away; that he did not keep running, or go into a little alley; and that he did not take Peter about ten yards from his lodgings, but, in fact, he did take him in custody immediately on his coming into his lodgings; now, the evidence given by Harvey on the trial being material in the light I have stated to you, if you believe it to be false, it is difficult to suppose that he could have been mistaken, for it was a fact within his own knowledge, and which it was his particular duty as a constable to attend to, therefore if it was false, and if, instead of pursuing him, and taking him in the street, he never, in fact, pursued him, but took him in his lodgings; Harvey must have known it was false, and if he did, I believe you will not find much difficulty in concluding, that a person, who knowingly gives false evidence against a prisoner, must necessarily do it with an evil and corrupt intention: the principal question therefore for you to decide in this case seems to be, whether the evidence given by Harvey upon this trial was true or false; for if false, the nature of it seems to leave very little room to doubt tha t Harvey must know it, and give it with a corrupt intention; but if it was true, he is an innocent man: the principal question is therefore this, and that upon which the present trial turns, whether you believe, on the whole of the evidence, that he took him in the street, or that he took him in his lodgings? for the story told on the part of the present prosecution certainly does, instead of shewing the consciousness of guilt in Newberry, shew the direct contrary; that Harvey being searching for him at his lodgings, he went home to those lodgings voluntarily, into the very hands of the people that were in quest of him; and that, in that very house, Harvey being in quest of him, took him; a great number of witnesses has been called, most of whom have positively swore to the fact, that Harvey did take Newberry in his lodgings, and not elsewhere; but in swearing to that fact, they have been called upon to state the circumstances; and it is one of the principal advantages in the mode of administering justice in this country, that the examinations of the witnesses are publicly conducted in the presence of the parties themselves, their counsel and agents, as well as the courts of justice, so than an opportunity is given of not resting satisfied with swearing merely to a fact, which, if the depositions were taken in writing, he might do, but there is an opportunity of enquiring into all the cases attending that fact, in order both to judge of the probability and consistency, and to compare that testimony with those circumstances, and with the evidence of other witnesses examined to the same fact;
