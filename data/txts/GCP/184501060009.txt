DENNIS CARR. I am in the service of Mr. James Brown, a builder, of
College-hill. I know the prisoner-he is a carman to a person named
Cherry, who keeps carts-Cherry was employed to remove bricks for Mr.
Brown I saw the prisoner with his master's cart at Maidstone-wharf-he
took three loads of bricks away, 500 each time-all the brieks belonged
to Mr. Brown--they were carted out of Mr. Brown's barge.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You delivered three loads
to the prisoner, by order of your master? 4. Yes--I saw them counted
out of the barge-I know nothing about them after they were taken. de
WILLIAM PARSLOW. I was present when the prisoner removed a load
of bricks-I do not know where he was to take them to it was about
a quarter past six, or from that to half-past six o'clock.d e
THOMAS COFFER. I live in Moor-lane, Cripplegate. On Saturday, the
list of Dec., I was sent by Mr. Brown, my employer, to unload the bricks
brought by the prisoner-Mr. Brown lives in Staining-lane he brought
two loads, not three was sent there to receive them received one at
twelve, and the other about five.
Hoog HIGGINS. I am in the employment of Mr. Brown, of Maiden-
head-court, Moor-lane. On the 21st of Dee., about five o'clock, I was in
Staining-lane, and saw the prisoner unload some bricks-I asked him if
that was to be the last load-he said that would be the last load-about
seven o'clock I met him in Jewin-street driving a cart, which contained,
apparently, a load of bricks, which is 500-I asked him where he got those
bricks-I followed him into Old-street-he was then in the Pitt's Head
I staid by the horses till he came out-1 told him to turn those bricks back,
they were Mr. Brown's bricks-he made me no answer-he stopped for a
few minutes, and said, “What had I best do ?" said, “You bad better
turn the bricks back to Staining-lane, and I will help you to upload them"
-- he did turn back-I asked how he got the bricks into his cart-he said
he loaded them himself after six o'clock-Old-street is not in the direction
to Staining-lane.
Cross-examined. Q. How far is Old-street from Staining-lanet 4.1
do not know-I do not think it is a mile-I could walk it in five minutes
-Jewin-street is not in the way from the wharf to Staining-lane.
JAMES JANSON (City police-constable, No. 497.) I met the prisoner on
the 21st of Dec. in Little Trinity-lane, Upper Thames-street, about a
quarter to six o'clock in the eveningI asked him if he was waiting
for Mr. Brown's man Jem-he said, "Yes"-I asked if I should tell
him-- he said, "No "-he was standing at the end of the lane watching
two of Mr. Brown's carts being unloaded-bis cart and horse was out of
right at the time, up the lane-I should say it was between thirty and
forty yards from the wharf-it was empty.
JAMES BROWN. I live at No. 24, College-street, Thames-street-my
wharf is at Maidstone-wharf, Queenhithe. These bricks were to be uploaded
In Staining-lane, Wood-street, where I am building a sewer the direct
line there from the wharf would be either down St. Martin's-le-grand or
down Wood-street-Jewin-street or Old-street would be quite out of the
way-when we want hired carts I always direct my people to employ
Cherry's carts-my men leave work when they finish the barge, about
half-past five or six o'clock-none of them were at work as late as six-
was not there myself, but they had finished their job at that time, and I was
the counting-house paying for the uploading the prisoner had no autho-
nity to remove bricks from my wharf in the absence of my people.
