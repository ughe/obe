E. J. Chetwynd—she engaged a room and asked me to board her, and
said that she came from Brighton to see her legal and medical advisers!
-I boarded her for three or four days I told her it was the custom to
have references, and she mentioned Dr. Forbes Winslow, Dr. Tibbetts
and her brother-in-law, Sir George Ohetwynd, 5s, Cork Street--as it
was only for a short time, and as the names were very good, I did not
refer to them-after she had been in the house a week I sent in my
account, amounting to about 21.; she said that her solicitor, Mr. Van
Tromp, was out of town, and she would pay it as soon as he returned-I
believed that Sir George Chetwynd was her brother-in-law-another
week passed, and I delivered her another account; she said that her
solicitor was out of town, and she would settle it directly he came back,
and I was not to trouble about the money-I said that she had better
leave unless she could pay it-she said "I have an income of 7001. &
year and have several houses in London," and showed me a bundle of
Jeases-she said that she was the owner of a large house next to the
blind school in Oxford Street, which she had let to a sewing machine
company on a long lease for 2501. a year, and a very large house at
Highgate, Byfield or Burfield House, which she showed me a sketch of,
similar to this produced)--she said that it was left to her by her mother,
and had 80 rooms, and it stood on 24 acres of land, that it was to let,
and a policeman and his wife were in charge of it at 11. a week, and she
had let the stable for 11. a week-I believed those statements, and gave
her board and lodging for 10 weeks, which came to about 571.-she left
on 18th October without paying-I told her I should immediately tako
proceedings against her, as I had made inquiries and found that she was
a fraud and a swindler-she said that she would pay me that day, as she
would go and see her brother-in-law, Sir George Chetwynd, at 5A, Cork
Street on the evening of 17th October she banded me this letter, which
she said she brought from her brother-in-law: Sir,--Mrs. E. J.
Chetwynd has asked me to write to you saying that she is all she repre-
sents herself to be. In a few days she will settle your claim, so I trust
you will be lenient.--Yours truly, Geo. Chetwynd.” I told her next
morning that I did not believe what she said ; she said that if I waited
till 6 that evening Sir George Chetwynd would pay me-I went and saw
Bir George Chetwynd on the 18th, and I never saw her again till I saw
her at the police-court-I made inquiries at 417, Oxford Street, and also
at Higbgate, and in consequence took proceedings against her.
