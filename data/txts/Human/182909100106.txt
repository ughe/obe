 COURT. Q. The moment they came to the door they asked if Newitt was at home? A. Yes - I said I believed he was; they asked me to call him - I went up and told him two men at the door wanted to speak to him; he came, opened his door, and told me to tell them he would be down directly - I told them so, and did not see any more of them; I heard him come down stairs directly -I did not hear him open the door or speak to them; I heard him come up again - I heard him moving about in his room over head, and go down in about two minutes and a half, and heard the front door shut; I had not heard it opened - I think it was possible for him to dress and come down in the two minutes and a half he was up stairs.
 JURY. Q. Did you know the two prisoners before? A. Yes - I had seen them four or five different times, and have spoken to them in the street.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. When had you last a job of painting? A. The last job I did was for Mr. Leggatt, the witness, at a house in West-street, Walworth - he paid me for the job; I began it on the 5th of August - it took six days; I came here to day with Leggatt - we did not go before the Magistrate together; I was summoned there - I had told Bonington, the landlord, and Leggatt, what I knew; Mrs. Bonington told Limbrick I was the person who answered them from the window.
   WILLIAM GORDEN. The layers at Islington are about two miles from the middle of Drury-lane.
   GEORGE GRIFFITH HOWELL. I am keeper of the watch-house in Albany-street, Regent's-park. On the 3rd of August, between eleven and twelve o'clock at night, a man, who is not here, brought in a sheep skin, and about five next morning, a watchman, who is not here, brought in another - I kept them both, and went on the Tuesday morning, between seven and eight o'clock, to take a walk by the Regent's-canal, and found a parcel of sheep skins in a heap: I was with my horsepatrol, and in the evening be fetched them to the watch-house - there were twenty-five; there was one black skin among them - I gave the twenty-seven skins to William Gordon; Clare-market is about two miles from that spot - none of the skins were entirely in the water, but on the bank; one of them hung on the butment of the bridge - they appeared to have been thrown over carelessly.
   JOHN HOPKINS. I am a horse-patrol. On the morning of the 4th of August, I was with Howell, when the skins were found in the canal; I took possession of them in the afternoon - there were twenty-five; I delivered them at the green-yard belonging to the watch-house -Howell was there; I did not find them exactly in the same situation as in the morning - the day-watchman had turned them over; I knew they were the same - I just turned them over in the morning; there was one black one.
   JOHN SWINGLER. I am in the employ of Mr. Neal. On Sunday night, the 3rd of August I took a flock of seventy-one sheep, by his direction, to Mr. Thompson's layer - they were Leicestershire breed; I noticed the marks on the skins - I saw six skins in Limbrick's possession at the office, and can take upon myself to say they are the skins of six of these sheep - there was a black one in the flock, and a black skin among those.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. It was on Sunday night; do you remember whether it was a rainy night? A. I think not; it would be neither light nor dark at three o'clock in the morning - it would be break of day.
   JOHN LIMBRICK. I am an officer of Hatton-garden. I apprehended Williams in Drury-lane, on Saturday the 29th of August - Hall was with me; I received twenty-seven sheep skins from Gordon, and have preserved six of them; I showed them to Swingler - I have been to Thompson's layers - I suppose it is full a mile or a mile and a quarter from the Coal-yard - it may be two miles.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Do you recollect what weather it was about the beginning of August? A. I cannot say; I do not suppose it would be quite dark at three o'clock.
   WILLIAM BOND. I am a constable. On Friday, the 28th of August, I apprehended Newitt at the Wellington, Wellington-street, Pentonville; he said he was not sorry he was apprehended; he intended to give himself up - this was nearly a month after the robbery - Pentonville joins Islington.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you ask why he was about to give himself up? A. No.
 Q. Did he not tell you that whatever he knew of the business, he was not concerned in stealing the sheep? A. I cannot say that he did - I will not swear he did not - he had no conversation with Limbrick that I know of; he appeared very infirm, and swollen with dropsy much more than he is now - I should think it would be difficult for him to walk five miles together.
 MR. PHILLIPS to  JOHN LIMBRICK. Q. I believe you saw Newitt some time after he was apprehended; did he not appear in such a state of bodily infirmity that any great exertion would be fatal? A. I conceive so.
   JOHN BASHAM. I am a butcher. On the 3rd of August I was going down Holborn, towards Newgate-market, and saw Newitt - I had had some dealings with him about sheep - he accompanied me down Holborn towards Newgate-market, and I saw him several times in the market; he was walking about there, attending to his business.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you observe the state in which his body was? A. Yes, I observed it very big; he walked feeble and infirmly.
