JAMES DARVILL. I am a constable of Uxbridge. On the night of the 9th
of March I heard a row at the station-house-I found about a hundred peo
ple collected there-after Cooper was wounded I saw Pearce, Latham, and
Clements, and several others make an attack upon the station-house gate-
I took hold of Pearce, but at that time I did not know that the policeman
had been stabbed-I told him he had better go home, if he did not I should
put him into the cage-he said, he would not be taken: they had done for
one, and would do for all the policemen and constables in Uxbridge before
he would be taken.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you take him afterwards ? A. No; Mum
did-he was outside the station-house gate when this conversation passed
-I persuaded him to go home he was very tipsy.
CHARLES PATTEN. I am a surgeon at Uxbridge. About ten o'clock

wound in the leg--there was a smaller wound in the leg--one wound w
