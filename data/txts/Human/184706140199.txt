DAVID DAVIS. I am in the employ of Courage and Donaldson. I heard the cry of "Police" that night—I saw the prisoner and Bennett—the pri soner walked first, with a hat in his hand and a sack under his arm—Davis caught hold of Bennett, and the prisoner went off when they hallooed out for a policeman, and left his bag behind him.
   Prisoner. Q.  Which arm did I have the sack under? A.  I do not know—you had it under your arm, and dropped it—I let you pass me near the Rose and Crown. GEORGE TERRY. I am in the employ of Mr. Henry Dunkin, a corn dealer and lightman. He had some barges at Davis's-wharf, Horsleydown—there were eighty sacks of oats in one barge—at nine o'clock that night I received information, and missed one sack of oats—this is it—it had the same kind of oats in it that was in the barge; I had left the barge about half an hour.
   Prisoner's Defence  I had nothing to do with it; I was at Horsleydown, and came up the stairs; I heard Davis speak; I turned to see what it was, and he had hold of Bennett; I walked back; a man came and said, "Cook, I must have you;" I said, "I have done nothing;" I went away. NOT GUILTY.
 1529. JAMES HARLEY was indicted for stealing 1 bag, value 1 d  .; 1 half-sovereign, 1 sixpence, and 3 pence; the property of William Payne ; from his person. WILLIAM PAYNE. I am lodging at Mr. Harris', in Point-street, Ports mouth—on the 12th of May I was on Wimbledon Common, on my way to Portsmouth—I was near some trees—the prisoner came up to me with ano ther man, who asked me if I was on the road—I said "No," I was standing there because it was raining—the prisoner then asked me if I wanted to buy a razor—I said no, I did not know that I did, what sort was it—he took it out of his pocket and showed it to me—I was going to buy it—I took a small bag out of my pocket which contained a half sovereign, a sixpence, and three pence in copper—I was going to get the threepence out of the bag, and the prisoner caught the bag out of my hand—I was going to catch him, and the other man said if I touched him he would give me some cold steel—they went off for thirty or forty yards, then ran as hard as they could—I stood under the trees till they were out of sight, and then pulled off my smock-frock and followed them—I got sight of them a long way a-head, and followed them for five or six miles—when the prisoner got to Kingston he went into a baker's shop—I followed behind a cart and passed the shop—I then saw him come out of the shop and come up towards London again—I gave information to an officer, who took him—I can swear he is the man that took my bag. THOMAS HOLLOWAY( police-constable V  226.) On the 12th of May Payne gave me information—I proceeded in pursuit of the prisoner, and saw him at the corner of Coomb's-lane—I asked him whether he had not offered a razor for sale, and while I was speaking to him the prosecutor came up and said, "That is the man that robbed me"—the prisoner said, "I have not got the money, the other man has got it"—I took the prisoner to the station—I found nothing on him—this is the razor Payne gave me, which he said he had of the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 28.— Confined Twelve Months .
