Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
257. FREDERICK CLEMENT (40), and FREDERICK HENRY
PALMER (38), Unlawfully conspiring, with other persons unknown, to
obtain two sheep and 21. 88. 9d. by false pretences.
MR. MBAD conducted the Prosecution; and MESSRS. W. SLEIGH and GILL
appeared for Clements, and MESSRS. Lyon and PUROELL for Palmer.
George MORTLOOK. I am a clerk to King, Covell, & Co., meat salesmen,
of the Central Meat Market, Smithfield-on 27th February last, Palmer
called there and bought two sheep at 7fd. lb., which came to 31. 11s. 3d-
he offered me in payment this cheque (produced) for 71., on the London
Discount Alliance, drawn by Frederick Clement in favour of Palmer-I gave
him 31. 88. 9d. change, and he or his porter took the sheep away- I paid
the cheque into the bank the same day, and it was returned marked "1.8."
-I then wrote to Palmer but received no answer-I parted with the cash
simply because I had a reference to Davis in the market-he said that he
had given him cheques, and if Mr. Davis had taken his cheques there was
no reason why I should not--if I had thought the cheque was bad I should
not have been satisfied with Davis' reference.
Cross-examined by MR. W.SLEIGH. I did not recognise the name of Clement
as the drawer of the cheque-I knew there was such a bank as the London
Discount Alliance, but I did not know its character-unless I had had s
reference from Mr. Davis, or some person who I knew, I should have
hesitated in parting with my money-it was Mr. Davis making that com
munication to me which induced me to part with the meat, otherwise I
should not have done it.
Cross-examined by Mr. Lyon. I did not sell the sheep, I only took the
money and gave him the change and a ticket to take away the meat-I did
not see Mr. Davis about the cheque, I sent one of the men to see him.
JOHN HARMAN. I am an undertaker, of Brixton Hill-my mother wished
to dispose of two cows, and on 27th March the two prisoners called-I
asked them 291. for the cows-Palmer spoke first; Clement stood a little
distance away-they came again next day, and Palmer asked if I was going
to take the money he had offered, which was 271.- I said that I could not, but
I would take 281.-Clement called out from a distance "Can you make a
deal, governor -I said “No, he is too hard "-they went away together
in a cart, land came again on Friday afternoon together, and I sold them the
cows for 271. 108.- Palmer paid me 11. deposit, and on the Monday evening
they called for the cows and took them away-about two months afterwards
I saw Palmer in Camberwell New Road, and said "How about this chequo
-be said “Has not Mr. Clement paid it " I said“ No, he has not-he said
“If you will let it rest I will speak to Clement about it"-that was all that
passed-I have never got the money.
Cross-examined by MR. W. SLEIGH. Mr. Chivers was present on 27th March
-I had asked him to find & purchaser-there were three persons besides
me-I do not think Clement spoke at all on the first day; that was when I
asked Palmer 291, and he offered 271.-Palmer went away dissatisfied
Clement might on that day have been an occasional companion of Palmers
-when they came the next day Palmer began the conversation alone and
Clement stood some way off-Palmer asked me, without speaking to Clement
at all, whether I would reduce the price to 271.-Clement took no part in
the bargain, and did not seem in any hurry—that was all he had to do with
