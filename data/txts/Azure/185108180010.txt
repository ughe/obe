USGROVE, Mayor.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How long had you known him!
Fourteen or fifteen months—we worked together at ballast-burning—I
no beer except what I paid for—the prisoner used to bring beer to the work,
every day in buckets, and sell it to the men in pints—a man named Bloom.
field, not the prisoner, was a sort of sub-contractor—I worked under him—
the prisoner did not ask me to put my name to a bill for him ; he nevernamd
such a thing—I did not tell him he might write my name, as I could not.
DANIEL HASTINGS. I am a sub-contractor, and live in Blackstock.lane,
Highbury. On 15th Nov. last, I was going to begin a job With the men in
the station-yard at Maiden-lane, and we went to the Swan beer-shop thereto
have some beer—the prisoner was there—I always understood his name to
William Smith; I never heard of him by the name of Bloomfield—a penoa
of the name of William Chandler was also there—he is a labouring man—I
heard the prisoner ask Chandler to go and write or fill up a bill for him—I
went into the Swan a few minutes after them, and saw them writing that bill
—Smith wrote one part, and Chandler another—Smith wrote the body, and
Chandler the acceptance and address—I heard the prisoner tell Chandler to
write the name of James Clark and the address, because Clark could
write—I have not seen Chandler for some time.
Cross-examined. Q. You and the prisoner are not very good friends, are
you? A. For all I know—I do not owe him anything—be has sued me in
the County Court, but he did not appear—I do not know that he was ia
custody on this charge—be was taken for passing bad money—I was the sub-
contractor, and Bloomfield was an assistant to me—I had nothing to do with
the beer—the prisoner brought the beer in buckets, and sold it to the men—
Bloomfield had a beer-shop, and sold beer outside his premises—Clark
not present when the bill was written.
