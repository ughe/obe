CAROLINE BUTLER. I knew the prisoner between three and four months before I was married to—him, which was on the 11th of July last, at Hackney church—I was single—he represented himself as a single man—he said he never went inside of a church before with any woman to be married—my mother and father were present at the wedding. JOHN SUTHERLAND. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner—he was charged by Butler with bigamy—a woman came with me to the station—I should know her again if I saw her—she stated" she was his lawful wife—I asked him if she was his wife—he said yes—I have not seen her since—he said he married her seven years ago, at Shoreditch church—I said, surely he knew he was doing wrong in marrying a second wife—he said no, as he caught her in bed with another man—that he went to her, and told her that he was to be married, and she said he might do as he pleased.
   Prisoner . I never stated any such thing; I never said I was married to her seven years ago. Witness . He said he was married to her seven years ago, at Shoreditch church. WILLIAM YEOMAN. I am a policeman. I have a copy of a marriage register from St. John's, at Hackney—I got it from the minister of the parish—I compared it with the book—it is signed—( read )—"1842, mar riage solemnised at St. John's, Hackney, Middlesex, July 11th. Samuel Taylor, bachelor, and Caroline Butler, spinster, were married in this church, after banns, by me, JOHN GRAHAM." JANE BUTLER. I am Caroline Butler's mother, and was present at her marriage to the prisoner.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not pay for the wedding? A.  Yes, because you said you had no money.
   Prisoner's Defence . I fell in with her when she was four months in the family way; she asked me to marry her; I told her no; I said I had not got the means, and she said, In my situation, if my father could get any body to marry me he would pay all the expenses"—they wish to send me away, because the father of the child is out of the country; she was mar ried to him; the child belongs to him; I never wanted the woman. CAROLINE BUTLER re-examined . It is false—on the 20th of July his wife came, and turned me out of doors, and took possession of the place the prisoner was there. GUILTY. Aged 34.— Confined Three Months.

ELIZABETH M'GILLIVRAY was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of August , 1 brooch, value 7 s  .; 1 pair of earrings, value 5 s  .; and, 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  .; the goods of Edmund Stansbury Brotherton ; and 3 sovereigns, and 1 half-sovereign, the property of Ann Lake , her mistress. ANN LAKE. I am a widow, and live in Thavies-inn. The prisoner was in my service—on the 2nd of August I was at the Isle of Wight—Mrs. Brother son, my daughter, was in charge of my house. JANE PETERS BROTHERSON. I am the wife of Edmund Stansbury Brotherson. I missed two sovereigns and four shillings out of my work table drawer, on the 2nd of August, and in consequence of suspicions, I discharged the prisoner—I accused her of it—she said she had taken it out of the drawer—after she was gone I made up my accounts, and missed 3 l . 10 s . from the scrutoire-drawer in the bed-room—I have since missed a brooch, some earrings, and a handkerchief—these are them.
