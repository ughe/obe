   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  You did not know any thing of Gorken before? A.  Yes, I knew him—he is a driver or jobber, or something—he sells for others, and gets a commission—he has sold for us—it was a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes after he went, that Saps ford came into the public-house—this was on the 20th of December—I went before the Grand Jury on the 2nd of February—we went before the Magistrate the next day, and were ordered to come over to the Old Bailey—the Grand Jury had just broken up—I did not go to Sapsford's house—I do not know that Sapsford ever served us with grains—I always went to the brewhouse for them—I never paid for them—Sapsford's name was mentioned when Gorken and I were driving the cow, at six o'clock—Sapsford's yard was not mentioned, nor any thing about grains, to my knowledge, but the man at the turnpike said, "Is that cow for Sapsford?"—Gorken answered, "No, it is not"—I said nothing—Gorken then said, "Sapsford is a shabby sort of a fellow—my boy drove a cow for him from Smithfield, he was to have 6 d . for it, and he never paid him"—there was in account of 2 l . or 3 l . owing to Sapsford, from my father—I suppose it most be for grains.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Did you go to Horsemonger-lane to prefer a bill? A.  Yes; they would not entertain it there—I am quite sure that nothing was said about Sapsford when the bargain was made for the cow—Gorken said it was not for Sapsford, when the turnpike-man asked about it. SAMUEL JEFFERSON. I am an officer of Union Hall. I apprehended both the prisoners on a certificate of the indictment—I took one in the Borough and the other at Brixton—I had been looking for them about ten days.
   Eldridge's Defence.  I was engaged by Sapsford to go and buy two cows for him—Gorken came to my house with Sapsford, and asked at what time I would go with him to Newman's to look at the cow—I said, "About ten o'clock in the morning"—I met Gorken, and went to Newman's—I handled several cows—I found this cow suitable for my master, who had eleven or twelve cows in full milk—I agreed to give 11 l . 10 s ., and told him to send it to the Elephant and Castle, Vauxhall-gate, that I should be there, and would go with him with the cow; instead of which it never came home till eleven o'clock at night to my master's place—I said to Newman, "I thought you were not going to send it"—he said, "I could not find the place "—it was then tied up—I told him to go to the Elephant and Castle, I would come to him, and to call for a pint of beer—he says he could see the gate from the door; but there is a turning off about fifty yards directly away from the cow-shed—he could not see it fifty yards from the public-house door—when master came to him, he said, "You know there is a little difference between your father and me, and here is the difference out of the account," and tendered him the money—he said he would go and ask his mother—he said nothing to me about taking the cow with him; but
