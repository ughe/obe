JOHNSON, Mayor.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Did the prisoner ever have any
beer in your house on the undertaking of Mr. Dyer? 4. He had half a pint
of beer, and said Mr. Dyer would settle with me, which he did, and the very
morning he had this money I let him have two half pints of beer-I have
ascertained since he was in very great trouble-he has a wife and three
children.
JAMES Davis, Jun. I am the son of James Davis. My father gave me
a letter and two half-crowns, to go with the prisoner-I was to give the
money to Mr. Dyer--when we got to the Gun-makers' Arms, the prisoner took
the note, tore it up, and he took the money from me-he said, "You must
not go in, give me the money"-I said, "No," but he took it out of my
hand-I afterwards saw Mr. Dyer-the prisoner said, “Mr. Dyer, I have
brought you 5s."--Mr. Dyer said he would not be answerable--the prisoner
produced the money to him.
Not Guilty.

1459. CHARLES M'CANNON was indicted for stealing 1 screw, value
18. 60.; the goods of John Jones, in a vessel in a port of entry and dis-
charge.
JOHN SAYRE. I am an officer of the London Docks. On the 2nd of
July I saw the prisoner coming out, and thought it necessary to search bim-
I found this screw between the waistband of his trowsers and his body-
asked where he had been employed-he said on board the St. George, as a
rigger, and had picked up this screw in the dust-bin-I said it was the same
'as picking it from the ship, the Dock Company being answerable,
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. You had not seen this screw before?
4. No-it does not appear as if it had been lately used.
William Rose. I belong to the St. George, in the London Dock-this
screw belongs to the tank of our ship-it is the property of John Jones.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you see it last? A. I think six months
ago-it has not been used for some time-I do not think it is of much use.
Nor GUILTY.
