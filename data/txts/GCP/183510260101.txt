2237. THOMAS NUNN was indicted for a misdemeanor.
MR. Scarlett conducted the Prosecution.
ROBERT DUKE. I went to the George public-house on the 19th of
September. I saw this prisoner at the corner of a settle as I entered
the room, I observed him fumbling about his pocket-I did not see
what he did; but as soon as I had picked up the bag of the other prisoner,
I made this prisoner get up, and on the seat I found this bag, containing
seven counterfeit crowns, nineteen half-crowns, and sixty-five shillings
- he did not give any account of them.
Prisoner. Q. Did not I get up when you told me! 4. Yes; they
were close to you-you must have been sitting on them.
WILLIAM BAKER ASHTON (police-constable G 11). I was present with
Duke-I had two prisoners in custody- I saw Duke pull the prisoner
from the settle, and take something up.
Joun Field. These are all counterfeit coin, and fresh- they have never
been in circulation.
Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing about them--they were close by
me, but another man put them there.
GUILTY. Aged 24.-Confined Three Years.

2238. JOHN SPRING was indicted for a misdemeanor.
DANIEL SMITH. I keep a chandler's-shop in Thames-street. On the
29th of September, in the evening, the prisoner came and asked for half
an ounce of tobacco, and gave me a bad shilling-I bent it and cut it
asunder-I gave him one piece, and kept the small piece myself the
tobacco came to 14d.-I followed him down the street, and asked him if he
had not been to my shop-he said, “No"-I took him to Queenhithe
watch-house, and he was searched no money was found on him, but a
little tobacco-he was taken to Guildhall, but acquitted--I am confident
he is the man.
ELIZABETH SANSOM. I am the wife of George Sansom, and keep a
milk-shop in Titchfield-street. On the 7th of October, the prisoner came
for half a quartern of shilling butter, which came to 14d.-he tendered 4
shilling-I saw it was bad- I said I wondered he should come into my
shop so many times as he had been in- I desired him not to come any
more-he went out, my husband followed, and told the policeman,
who took him he had been at my house about half-past nine o'clock.
JEMIMA TAYLOR. My husband is a stationer, and lives in Newman-
street. On the 7th of October, the prisoner came, about twenty minutes
before ten o'clock at night, for a penny pencil--he tendered a bad shilling
-I said I had no change, as I had taken the money out of my tillhe
told me he had no other money-he took the shilling up, and was going
out, when the officer and Mr. Sansom came in, and took him.
DAVID WILLIAMS (police-constable E 143.) On the 7th of October, Mr.
Sansom applied to me. I took the prisoner in Mr. Taylor's shop, and
found this shilling in his hand,
JOHN FIELD. This shilling is counterfeit, and this is a small part of
another counterfeit shilling.
Prisoner's Defence. I had just come out of the country, and was
inquiring my way, when a man came and put me in the watch-house.
GUILTY. Aged 21.-Confined One Year.
