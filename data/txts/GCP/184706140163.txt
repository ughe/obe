THOMAS BROWNE (police-constable.) I took the prisoner in charge from
the prosecutor -- he had been drinking, but knew perfectly well what he was
about-the place is no thoroughfare-I got a lamp, made a search, but
found nothing-next morning, between three and four o'clock, I searched
again --I saw two half-crowns in a piece of rag, pulled out from behind an
old board, ten or twelve yards from the end of the street.
MARY ANN BARBER. I am the prosecutor's wife-as soon as it was light
in the morning I went with the constable, made a search, put my haud in
between the gutter and boards, and pulled out the rap-it is an old white
pocket-handkerchief.
ELIZA JOHNson. I search females at the Westminster Police-station. On
Saturday night, about eleven o'clock, I searched the prisoner, and found this
coin in a pair of mittens which she had on, and a shilling, a sixpence, and a
halfpenny in her pocket-after I undressed her completely, I said, "What
have you got in your hand" --she said “Only an old dirty glove"-I do not
know where she took it from, but I think it was from her bosom-I found
this half-crown in it.
Prisoner. Q. Did not I put it on the table ? A. No.
Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor had a hole in his waistcoat pocket; at
the public-house there was a large nail hanging out ; a farthing dropped out;
he said I might keep it for a keepsake.
Thomas PRONGER policeman.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's
former conviction from Westminster-(read-Convicted August 1842, and con-
fined three months)—I was present at the trial- the prisoner is the person.
Guilty.** Aged 39.-Transported for Seven Years.
1494. SUTHERLAND MARSHALL was indicted for stealing 14lbs.
weight of copper, value 8s.; the goods of Edward French, in a vessel in a
port of entry and discharge.
JAMES COLLINS, I am a labourer in the London Dock. On the morning
of the 16th of June, I was at work on board the ship Symmetry-I saw the
prisoner on board - I saw him lift the lid of a chest up, take out some copper,
put it into a piece of gunnebag, and hand it over the ship's side to another
man, who took it from him I called to the mate, who went after bim.
Prisoner. Q. Did you see me take the copper from the man again? A. No.
WILLIAM JAMES. I was second mate of the Symmetry, and have since been
discharged. In consequence of what Collins told me I went after the pri-
soner-the policeman brought him back to me-he had the copper under his
arm-he said it was given to him-I knew it directly-it belonged to the
ship-the master is Edward French.
ROBERT MANNING (police-constable.) I took the prisoner in charge-I
found this copper under his arm, wrapped up in a piece of gunnebag-I asked
what he had got, he said he did not know-I asked how he came by it, he
said a person gave it him to carry.
Prisoner's Defence. A man asked me to carry a parcel-into the East India
Docks, and wait till he came: I did not know what I had.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 30. Recommended to mercy.-Confined Four Months.
