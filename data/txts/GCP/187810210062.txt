Twelfth Session, 1877–78.
MARY ANN GOLDSBORO. I live at 7, Twyford Buildings, Great Queen
Stret-Laffar lives on the same floor--a man named Dell used to come
there, but is not living there—I did see the book at the house where
Laffer was living I do not know where Dell lives--I believe he is Laffer's
Another-in-law-he does not squint-I delivered the book to the police pit
was given to me by two respectably-dressed men, about 11.45, to give to
I. Dell when he came there--I had never seen them before--I and Laffer
living on the same floor, the same knock applies for me as for hím--two
toeks came and I went down to the door the same morning that Laffer was
taken to Bow Street his sister and father had then gone to Bow Street
Laffer was taken about half-past eight or a quarter to nine, as near as I
remember-I took the book down to the police-station-I believe Dell is a
billiard-marker.
JOHN LEADER. 1 live at 8, White Hart Street, and am a carman-I have
known Laffer some years-on 5th September, about 11.45 p.m., he came to
me with another--we went into a public-house--he said he wanted me to do a
job for the other party in the morning-I supposed it was to move some
fumiture; I did not ask him who the job was for I met him next
morning; he introduced me to the same party I saw the night before I
went to where he was working at 6.50 in the morning-I had no
anversation with him then as to where I was to go-I went to Bennett,
hired his van, and then went to Endell Street, at the request of the man who
had hired the van-we went to Broad Street first, then we were ordered to
po to Dun's Passage, and there received four springs and an axletree-then
we went to Broad Street and received three bundles of steel; we were then
andered to drive over the water the people for whom we got the goods ordered
me--we went into the London Road and several other places they seemed
to be trying to sell the goods on the way in the London Road they
brought a party to look at them who seemed to have nothing to do with
them and went away again--the first I saw of Cook was in the Kent Road
- he was brought by another party-I now know him by the name of
Garrard-Cook came and looked at the goods and went away we had
orders after half an hour to take them round to his place --we took them
round, and I saw them carried in and delivered at his place.
Cross-examined by Mr. Fulton. Cook went away then; afterwards
Garrard went, and when he returned he said “ Just take them round here."
-when Cook came they seemed to be talking together; dealing ; trying to
arrange what money should be paid for the springs-Cook went away in
cinsequence, as I supposed, of hís not being able to come to terms with the
man-Cook. went away; some time elapsed before the other man went after
him; it might have been half an hour-during the half-hour they were not
still hawking the goods about-at the end of the half-hour the other man
Miched Cook and they came back again then there was a conversation,
and at the close of it I was told to take them round to Cook's, and I took
them.
