MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. Q. On 29th December, how much money
appears to have been paid in A. 1691. 78. lld each item is given in
detail there the cheque for 721. 98. does not appear in that on 31st
December the gross amount appears to be 3521. 198. 2d. I received a list
of what that sum consisted, that is the lump sum I find in this book.
COURT. Q. You stated that the lump sum was 1406. 128. 8d. A. Yes.
MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. Q. Is there any other lump sum, except
that 1402. A. No, there is not, it is a most unusual thing; I have looked
through the book, and do not remember to have seen any other instance of
it; I will look ; yes, there is another, on 4th February, 4821. 108. 2d. the
lump sum of the cheques—there is 1441. 108. 6d. and I see there is another
on 1st April, where the obeques amount to 1391. 48. 1d. I have looked
on to the end of the book and there is nothing more, there are some at the
beginning; in the course of business, if the prisoner received money on the
Saturday, it would be his duty to pay it in on the Monday.
MR. RIBTON. Q. Look back to the beginning of the book, and tell me
how many lump sums appear there ! A. I will look them through and
tell you presently.
COURT. Q. Does the stamp of your department specify the date ? A.
It does not, so that that would be no guide at all as to the time when the
money would have been paid in-one of these cheques bears the stamp of
Williams, Deacon, & Co, showing that it has passed through the bankers
the other is crossed to Williams & Co, but there is no mark showing it to
have been there.
