JOHN ADAMSON( police-sergeant R  1.) On the 27th of April I went to Mr. Carter, a pawnbroker, and found these spoons there—I saw Greeny at Mrs. Bradley's in the afternoon—I asked her if she knew any thing about any silver spoons—she said no she did not—I asked her if she had any young man came to see her—she said yes, a young man named Piears, who lived at Chelsea, but she could not tell where—I apprehended him in the Magistrates' room while the case was proceeding against Greeny.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he come to the room? A.  No; he was brought by another constable. EDWARD CARTER. I am a pawnbroker at Greenwich—I produce these table-spoons, which were pawned with me on the 20th of April for 1 l . by the prisoner Piears—I asked him whose they were—he said his father's.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you know him before? A.  No—there were several other persons in the shop, perhaps half a dozen—it was not par ticularly busy day—there was no other shopman there—there might be 150 persons come to the shop in the course of the day—I suppose there were not half a dozen of them strangers—I asked the prisoner the neces sary questions, and he gave his name as Richard Grant, George-street. ELIZA ALLEN re-examined.  There were two young ladies left in the house with Greeny when I and my mistress went to church—I did not see Piears at all that day. NOT GUILTY.

I am a clerk in the Government Life Annuity Office—on the 20th of April I was at Greenwich fair—I felt a tug at my pocket, turned, and saw my handkerchief in the prisoner's hand—the officer took it from him. JAMES ANDREWS( police-constable M  45.) I was in the fair about one o'clock, and saw the prisoner following the prosecutor very closely—I watched him for a minute or two—when they got opposite Richardson show I saw him take the handkerchief, and collared him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to the fair to try and get work—I was getting through a crowd when the officer took me. GUILTY.* Aged 17.— Confined Six Months.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
