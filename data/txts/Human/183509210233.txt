   MR. PAYNE . Q.  After Bray's omnibuses ceased to run in Trevett's time, who did they run against? A.  They ran in Trevett's time for three or four days, and about three days after that they ran in Mr. Bolton's time, for three or four days.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was it found that these regulations were calcu lated to promote the convenience of the public? A.  Not a doubt of it, Sir, we should do nothing without it—we should be all in conflusion.
   COURT . Q.  Did Mr. Bray communicate with you on the subject? A.  No.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Were you not a police-officer, and sworn in for the purpose of keeping these regulations in force? A.  Yes; I was—I was sworn in before Sir Peter Laurie, Lord Mayor—I have the date of the month here—we have had oppositions from Mr. Shillibeer and Newman, and then Mr. Bray came—I have only been there two years—one of Bray's omnibuses ran against Bolton—he commenced by running against Trevett, and then against Bolton, and then against Richard Bird and Johnson—I never saw Mr. Bray till that day—I receive 25 s . a week by the hands of Nathaniel Colaon—he is the relieving time-keeper—he is a peace officer—I have observed the omnibuses belonging to the two Bird's, Cow deroy, and Johnson—I have seen Bray's omnibuses drive very steadily from the bank—I am stationed at the Bank—three minutes is the regula tion time—they do not stop a longer period.
   COURT . Q.  Suppose a driver came and wanted to take refresh ment, might he not do that before he comes on the stand? A.  Yes.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was not three minutes the time? A.  Yes; I have summoned only our own omnibuses, and they were fined 10 s ., because they would not go when I have told them.
   Q.  Mr. Bray ran his omnibuses without regard to time, just as he pleased? A.  Yes; there was no regular time—he came when he thought proper—he has run against them all nearly.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BUMPUS . Q.  You are paid by the proprietors, were you named originally by the proprietors? A.  I know nothing of the proprietors—I was engaged by Mr. Corney, and paid since by the proprietors, by the hand of Mr. Colson—I am the regular time-keeper at the Bank—Mr. Colson relieves me—when I am not there, he is—when Bray came, on the very first time, on the Monday morning, he tried to take his time at the Bank—he said he should have time—he went up Cornhill—he did not come back in Cowderoy's time that day—I cannot recollect whethere he did the next day—he came once or twice in Bird's time; I think, not so soon as that week; it was about nine days after—he might have come on in their time that week, while I was relieved, for two hours—I remember the "Emperor" and the "Albion" being put on—they came and took time—I have seen the bus  with "Sylvester" on it
