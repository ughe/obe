Cross-examined by Mr. TINDAL ATKINSON. Q. I believe you engaged him
to go round London for the purpose of selling your goods ? A. Yes I deal in
clothing of all descriptions--the agreement was that he was to pay our firm
for the samples he took out and he did so for the first two samples -- after
that he did not pay for them-we did not debit him with them in our books,
but we had a memorandum of it-he has taken out what is called a debit of
it, but not in our regular account-I know nothing about his finding a bag
containing 501, in gold and notes and taking it to Mr. Alderman Mechi,
who was sitting at Guildhall, and receiving a public compliment for it-I
recollect his getting an order for a Mr. Garden-I do not know that he went
to considerable expense to endeavour to obtain the money for me-he gave
that as a reason, but I was not bound to believe it-I made out a list against
him for 101. 18. for these samples; these six shirts were in it-be wrote me
8 note to meet him at the Cathedral Coffee-house-I had sent in no account
to him he had had a list of what samples he had had it was understood
that I claimed the samples I saw him at the Cathedral Coffee-house en
officer was there, but did not go with me I did not present an account to
the prisoner and say, "If you do not pay this, I shall give you into custody
for embezzlement " and nothing to that effect-I said, "I want those samples,
on you give them to me directly ?” he said, "I can produce them at
6 o'clock -I said nothing about the money ; I said, “No; give them to
me now, or I will give you in custody"_ he said, "Come over to my place
and I will settle it ; I dare say I can find the money I said, "I want the
samples; I have no objection to go over to your place to see what you can
do but I said nothing about the money—I showed him a memorandum
naming our prices for the goods it was not an invoice-I went over to the
Borough with him-I said, " Can you produce any of those goods ?” I said
nothing about the money—I had got orders from the firm to give him in
custody, but as a member of the firm it would have satisfied me, perhaps, if
he had given me the money-I never asked him for the money, I said, "I
will see what you can do."
Mr. LLOYD. Q. When he got the shirts, did you debit bim with them
A. No; we put them to Messrs. Brooks and Co., but subsequently we
transferred them to the prisoner, when we found that Messrs. Brooks had
not ordered them-my orders were to give him in custody.
THOMAS SMART (City-policeman. I took the prisoner on 5th September,
and told him he was charged with fraudulently obtaining a quantity of
shirts of Messrs. Hallett and Co. of Cheapside-he said to Messrs. Hallett,
"I hope you will not give me in custody - I told him that my object in
searching him was to ascertain if he had got any pawnbroker's duplicates -
he said, “I have them all in my pocket," and produced twenty-one dupli-
cates, among which was one for the six shirts, and he afterwards handed me
two others for property of Mr. Hallett's
