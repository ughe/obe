JOHN STINGIMORE. I am a police-constable of Dorking—on the night of the 8th of September I was sent to the Three Tuns Inn about 11 o'clock, and saw the prisoner Gilroy there in bed—I said "I want you on a charge of uttering counterfeit coin"—he said "I know nothing about it"—I asked him to get up—he put on his things and went with me to the station—I searched him both there and at the station, and found 3 l . 14 s . 8 d .—there was 1 sovereign, 2 half sovereigns, 2 florins, 13 shillings, 36 sixpences, 1 threepenny piece, and 4 s . 5 d . in bronze, all good money—the public-houses close at 11 o'clock.
   Cross-examined.  I took Gilroy to Mrs. Butler at the Bull's Head to be identified—I asked her if she knew him—there are about 9,000 people in Dorking and there is one superintendent and four policemen to look after the 9,000—these depositions were taken at the Dorking Police-court. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am one of the inspectors of coin to Her Majesty's Hint—these coins are all bad and from the same mould.
   By the JURY . I arrive at that conclusion because these counterfeit pieces are cast in a plaster-of-Paris mould from a good coin, which is termed the pattern piece, and all its imperfections are reproduced in the copies—they are of course all of the same date—I am not bound to point out in open Court where the imperfections are, but I can see them with my naked eye.
   Cross-examined.  You will find the date on the obverse side. GUILTY.
 99. EDWARD GILROY  (38) was again  indicted, with HENRY SEABROOK  (23) , for unlawfully uttering a counterfeit florin to Mary Ann Butler , another to Ann Newman , and another to Harriet Booking . MR. BAGGALLAY Prosecuted; MR. THORNS COLE , who defended Gilroy, withdrew from the case in consequence of the former verdict. WILLIAM WALTON. The evidence given by this witness in the last case was read over to. him from the short-hand writer's notes, to which he assented.
   Cross-examined by Seabrook.  I can swear that you were with your fellow-prisoner. HARRIET BOOKING. The evidence of this witness was read over to her, to which she assented.
   Cross-examined by Seabrook.  It was nearly half past nine when you called at our house but not not quite—I did not find that the coin was bad till Superintendent Lambert came in, and asked if I had taken any bad coin—that was at 10 o'clock or shortly after—no one was with you but Gilroy. MARY ANN BUTLER, The evidence of this witness was read over to her, to which she assented, and added:  I gave Gilroy If. 9 d . change—when I handed Seabrook the florin I said, "It is bad money, give me a good one "—I didnot notice that he made any reply, but he handed me a good shilling end I gave him tenpence change.
   By the COURT . I know the coin was bad because I felt it very greasy in my fingers and I did not put it in the till, till I had said to a person in the bar "This is a bad one, is it not?" and he said "A very bad one" in the prisoner's presence—I did not ring it, but he threw it on the counter himself—it did not sound much because it is a pewter counter.
   Cross-examined by Seabrook.  When I was asked if I could recognise you I did not Say that I could not swear to you.
