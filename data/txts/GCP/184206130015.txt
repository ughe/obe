GEORGE TOWNSEND (police-constable H 194.). About twelve o'clock
in the morning of the 22nd of December last I was in Church-street, Shore-
ditch, and heard the springing of a rattle, which took me to Nicol's-court
-when I got there I saw my brother officer, James Carroll, H 160—he
bad hold of a man, who was struggling to get away-I went to assist
Carroll—there were other persons around them-I and Carroll succeeded
in getting the man about thirty or forty yards along—whilst we were doing
that, the persons around were hallooing, and closed in on us, trying to get
our prisoner away from us--when we got near the Knave of Clubs I saw
a person named Thomas Smedley-he knocked Carroll down, struck me,
and rescued our prisoner from us—I told Carroll if we could not have
the prisoner, we could have the man that rescued him from us--we followed
Smedley, and suoceeded in getting him to the station - he was committed,
