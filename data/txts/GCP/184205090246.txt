Cross-examined. Q. Do you speak with any degree of positiveness
as to the appearance on the axe? A. I think it is decidedly blood, from
the appearance it had then it is not so bright now-it was much more
plain--the appearance of blood on the knife was very slight--I should be
sorry to swear it was blood-I saw the axe standing in the corner that
night, but did not examine it-the inspector said he would put it on one
side till daylight-I saw it at the inquest on Friday-it was then in the
inspector's possession-I think he showed it to me before the Coroner
-the marks are decidedly not so plain now as then-they have been rub-
bed off, or gone off by carriage, or something, from both sides-it is cer-
tainly not so bright as it was-the blood was redder, more distinct.
Q. From what you saw of it at the inquest do you think the appear-
ances might be very visible on Wednesday by the aid of the policeman's
lamp? 4. They would have been visible, but not so visible as by day-
light-there was sufficient to enable a person to see the appearances on
Wednesday, I think-this axe is a very likely instrument to have separated
the thigh bones—there was a clean cut all the way round the neck-it has
the appearance of having been done by one stroke--I think it is be-
tween the second and third vertebræ, but without dissecting, I cannot be
certain-the blood vessels were very empty-there must have been a
very large quantity of blood effused-it was the trunk of a full grown
female.
Court. Q. Could you form an opinion of her age? A. I should say
she was upwards of thirty-there was very little to judge by--the skin
had a very fair appearance--that might have been from the draining of blood
-I should think she had been dead four or five days--the body was plump
-I should think she was about a middle size, and rather a fine made woman
--the bosom was rather plump and full-I should think she had not borne
a child, from the appearance of the breast- should think her rather of a
medium complexion-the skin appeared faif by that light, and very clear
-I think that she had been dead four days, from the general appearance
of the remains the rigidity of the skin was over, and its flexibility come
back-I judge the incisions were made after death, from their clean ap-
pearance--if made on a living person the skin retracts-I judge it had
lost its elasticity at the time the cut was given.

roner's warrant on the 7th to examine the body of a person in Putney
Park-lane-I examined it on Thursday and Friday-I examined it first in
the stable, but did not make a very particular examination then, as the con-
stable would not allow me, on account of the body being so dirty, being in the
stall--on the Friday it was washed, and I made a particular examination
- there were no marks of disease whatever about it-I should say that it
had been dead from three to four days, I could not judge within twenty.
four hours, but about four days from the time I first saw it, which was
Thursday.
