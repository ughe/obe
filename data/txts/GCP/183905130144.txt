1662. GEORGE ROBERTS was again indicted for stealing, on the
20th of January, 3 coats, value 31., the goods of John Holstead.
John HOLSTEAD. I am a tailor-the prisoner used to work for me out
of doors in 1836. On the 20th of January, 1837, he came and stated
his brother wanted some clothes to look at to purchase-I allowed him to
take a great coat and two dress coats, with the express understanding that
he was to bring them back in the course of the week, or the money--bis
brother was to take two coats out of the three-he went away, and I did
not see him again till he was at the office last Monday-1 lost my coats
altogether.
Prisoner. Q. What was I to give for the coats? A. 31. 8s. for the
three.
HENRY HOLSTEAD. I am the prosecutor's brother. I was present
when the prisoner came for the three coats, which he was allowed to take
away-he was to bring one coat back, and the money for the other two in
the course of the week.
Prisoner's Defence. I purchased them in the way of business--I paid
him one sovereign the day I had them, and was to pay for them when I
sold them.
NOT GUILTY.

1663. JOHN MEADER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of
May, 7lbs. weight of horse-bair, value 108. 9d., the goods of Thomas
Peters.
THOMAS PETERS. I am a coach-builder, and live in Park-street
Grosvenor-square. On the 15th of May, I sent 48lbs. weight of horse-
hair in a bag to St. George's poorhouse, by one of my men--the prisoner
was intrusted to bring it home to me after it was picked-he brought it
back the same day, and was followed by a policeman, who asked if I knew
any thing about that hair-I said yes, it was all right I believed the
prisoner was one of the paupers of the parish--the policeman said he be-
lieved it was not right, for he had been selling some of it I told him to
follow the prisoner up stairs, and I followed him-I asked how it was he
had been taking out some hair-he said he was very sorry, but he had got
a wife and family, and he had sold a little to get some tea and sugar--there
was 7lbs. weight of hair missing-it cost me 18. 6d. a pound, and a half-
penny a pound I pay for picking.
JOHN PAYNE. I am a pauper in St. George's workhouse. I weighed
out the hair to the prisoner, and gave it him to take to Mr. Peters-I
gave him rather better than 47lbs.-- it had been picked.
ANN EGGLENTON. I was at Mr. Spencer's, in East-street, Manchester-
square, assisting in his business, as he was ill-the prisoner came and said
he had some hair to sell he put some in the scale, it weighed 71bs.-I
said, “Sixpence a pound"-he said, “Very well"-I said I had not got
the money to pay for it-I sent the hair to sell immediately, and got 8d. &
pound for it- I did not ask the prisoner where he got it.
Prisoner. Q. Did I receive the money at all? A. No, you were to
call again for it, and before you called I understood it was not right.
Prisoner's Defence. I am very sorry, I have a wife and family in the
workhouse.
GUILTY. Aged 36.--Recommended to mercy.-Confined Three Months.
