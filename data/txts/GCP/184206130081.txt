RICHARD WILLIAM SANNEMAN. I am a surgeon. I was sent for, and
saw the nature of the wound-he was wounded on the right side, below
the ribs of the abdomen-I should say a knife most probably did it-it
was done by a cutting instrument--when I went into the room I found
him lying on the bed in his clothes, which were saturated with blood,
but the bleeding had then ceased- I closed the wound, dressed it, and
went home I was called between five and six o'clock the next morning,
to say the bleeding had burst out again- I was obliged to open the wound,
and apply different remedies-in the afternoon I gave the case up to his
club doctor-1 dared not attempt to ascertain the depth of the wound-
wound in that part is certainly dangerous—he was in imminent danger, and
must bave continued so for some time-there were also two small wounds
on the back besides—they were slight wounds, only skin deep-they were
recent wounds, and, I suspect, stabs.
