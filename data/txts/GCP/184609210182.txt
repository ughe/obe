THOMAS KENNARD. I am clerk to the prosecutors. I have seen these three
sacks, two of them are marked with our foreman's name-the other is not-I
am sure these two are ours, and the other I have little doubt of - Palmer was
our second horse-keeper, and slept on the premises-King was horse-keeper
to Simpson.
(Witness for the Defence.)
RICHARD PAYNE. I am a labouring man. In the beginning of Aug. I wa
in Simpson's service-I remember his having a job at Richmond-I had to
go about it-to cart 160 tons of coals--we ran short of sacks-he borrowed
four corn-sacks of Jacobs, because he had not enough-We executed the order
--we went there on the 5th of Aug., and came home on the Sunday after-
wards, and went back on Monday to finish I had the misfortune to lose one
of the four sacks-when the job was finished I tied up the three sacks, and
deposited them in Mr. Simpson's loft- I did not go to them till the Friday
previous to Mr. Simpson being taken on the Saturday morning-I had i
smock-frock with them-I had torn it, and wanted to take it home to my
wife, and I went to the sacks on the Friday, and untied them to get the
garter off, that I had tied them with-that was on the day before Mr. Simpson
was taken-I found the sacks just as I had left them-one of the sacks had
a hole in it-these sacks are a great deal like them--they are similar-they
were old sacks, had been used some time, and looked to be half-worn out-
they were such as these I should not like to swear to them.
MR. BALDWIN. Q. Was it on the Monday you put these sacks into
Simpson's loft ? A. Yes, on the 9th, when we came home-Mr. Simpson
went to borrow the sacks-I was standing, looking at him-I saw him borrow
them-I saw them lent to him-I was waiting twenty or thirty yards off-be
brought four sacks to me-we borrowed four corn-sacks to take food for our
horses --Mr. Simpson had some sacks-we took thirteen altogether I got
the smock-frock out from the sacks on the Friday.
Court. Q. Do you know King? A. I know him by working for Mr.
Simpson-he was in his stable, looking after bis horses--I saw Simpson
cleaning horses the last time I saw him.
(Simpson received a good character.)

1975. THOMAS WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing 1 flute, value
2s.6d., the goods of James Edward Lawrence, and that he had been before
convicted of felony.
EDWARD DWYER. I am in the employ of Mr. James Edward Lawrence, a
New Cut, Lambeth. On the 29th of Aug. I was in his shop, and noticed the
prisoner leaning over the goods- I watched him I went to the door, and pe
went away-I followed him-he crossed the road-1 had suspicion, and
stopped him-I found this flute in his pocket-it is my master's.
Cross-examined by Mr. HORRY. Q. This flute was exposed ? A. Yes
there is no window to the shop-the goods are exposed for sale.
