 2039. GEORGE PEARSON , for feloaiously being at large before the ex piration of the term for which he had been transported. JOHN WADE. I am steward at the convict establishment at Portsmouth—I know the prisoner, and recollect his being on board the hulks there—he escaped on 3rd April—he had been sentenced to twenty years transportation, from this Court. THOMAS WATKINS( policeman, M  18.) I was present in this Court in Nov., 1846, when the prisoner was sentenced to be transported for twenty years—I am quite sure he is the person.
   Prisoner. Q . Did you have me? A.  Yes, it was for robbing a lady of 1 l . 18 s . WILLIAM DERRIG( policeman ). I took the prisoner in July, in a public-house, in Mint-street—he resisted most violently with an open clasp-knife—I charged him with being a returned transport—he said, he was transported innocent, and it was his intention to do for one of us—another officer was assisting me. GUILTY. Aged 28.— Transported for Life.
 2040. CHARLES M'PHERSON , feloniously wounding Henry Decimus Ilderton , with intent to do him grievous bodily harm.
   MR. METCALFE conducted the Prosecution. HENRY DECIMUS ILDERTON. I am a solicitor, and live at Grove-cottage, St. James's-street, Old Kent-road. On 15th Aug. I was angling in the Surrey Canal—the prisoner and three or four other boys were on the canal in boats—he was alone in a boat, and was splashing about the water with his oars, evidently to annoy those who were fishing—we asked him and the others to go away—after some time they did so, but came back again—the prisoner broke the top of my fishing-rod—I jumped into his boat and gave him a slap over the head—he jumped ashore and said, "I will serve you out for this"—he got hold of the rope and began dragging the boat along the canal wilh me in it—he picked up a stone—I jumped out of the boat and ran after him to make him give it up, and just as I got up to him his father got out of a barge and came up to me with his fists up to strike me—the prisoner stumbled over a dog and fell—the father came up in an attitude to strike me, and just at that moment I received a severe blow from a stone on the right temple—it must have come from the prisoner who was on my right side—I bled very much—my hat was cut through the brimlining, and all—with the assistance of one of the bargemen I went to a surgeon, and was under his care for a week.
   Prisoner.  He knocked me down and took away the sculls from me. Witness. A . I did not—I merely gave him a slight cuff over the head, such as you would give a schoolboy. EDWARD JACKMAN. I was anglingin the canal—the boys were annoying me by frightening away the fish—I did not see Mr. Ilderton get into the boat, but hearing a laugh among the men I turned round, and saw him in it, and the prisoner pulling him along—I saw him pick up part of a brick—Mr. Ilderton ran after him—the father ran up to strike him—the boy fell, got up again, and after dodging about two or three times I saw him throw a brick, which struck Mr. Ilderton on the temple—he was only about four yards off at the time. RICHARD WILLIAM KEY( policeman, R  339). I took the prisoner, and told him it was for throwing a stone at a gentleman—he said he had nothing to say to it—he could not deny it.
