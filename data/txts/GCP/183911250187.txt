Holborn. On the 10th of September the two prisoners came there in a
carriage-Smith remained in the carriage-Brown came in and selected
some knives and forks, two candle-lamps, and a study lamp the goods
came to 6l. 12s.-he asked if I could send them down to his chambers, and
gave me his card, "13, East India Chambers"-I said I could not, as we
had no one in the way-he said he wanted them very particularly, as be
had a dinner party that day at Hampstead, and if I would get them ready
his servant should call for them in the course of an hour--he then left-I
was to get the knives and forks ready, and the other things were to be got
ready in a day or two-in about an hour Smith came again in the same
carriage-I delivered the knives and forks to him, and I asked him where
his master, or where Mr. Brown, resided at Hampstead he said he did
not recollect exactly the address, but it was near the Beau de Ville I
then asked how long his master had resided there-he said, ever since he
had been with him.

GEORGE DAVIS. I am a sadler, and live in the Borough. I saw both
the prisoners at my shop in September--Brown came in, while Smith sat
in the phaeton-Brown asked me the price of chaise and carriage harness,
but more particularly of carriage harness--I agreed to make him a
set of carriage harness to order for 211.--the chaise harness was not
determined about he said he resided at Blackheath, and had Cham-
bers at the East India Chambers—the harness was to be made and
sent by that day fortnight-he said the horse he had at Blackheath
was of the same size as the one at the door, and one collar would
fit either-after we had agreed about the harness he said he wanted a
saddle and bridle immediately, to ride the next day, being Sunday--we
looked one out-he said his servant, meaning Smith, would call for it
in half an hour think he was to pay five guineas for that Smith
called, and took away the saddle and bridle-I have never been paid for
either.) 1

BLACKWELL. I live in Oxford-street, and am a sadler. Brown
came to me in a phaeton, and agreed to purchase a carriage barness, for
which he was to pay twenty-five guineas--it was to be made for him, and
paid for before delivery--he gave me a card with "East India Chambers
on it, and said he lived at Blackheath-a saddle and bridle were to be sent
by the carrier to Blackheath, as he said he wanted to ride on the Sunday
Smith was sitting in the phaeton as a servant-Brown went to the carriage
again, and said he was going to dine at Connaught-square, and his servant
should call for the saddle and bridle as he returned I said it should be
ready, and asked for a reference- he looked at my book, which was on
the table, and said he knew several of my customers, and said they were
friends of his-Smith came about half an hour after, and took away the
saddle and bridle-I asked him where his master resided at Blackheath-
he said, “Very near the Hare and Billet public-house"-I said, I never
was there but once, and did he know Admiral Oliver's-he said, “Yes, "it
was a very little way from there-he took away the saddle and bridle, and
I heard no more of them till they were at Union-ball.
