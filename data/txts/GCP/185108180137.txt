1718. JOHN HODGE was indicted for embezzlement,
MR. PARRY conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM CLAYTON POPE. I am a tea-dealer, and live on the Pavement.
The prisoner was my town-traveller-he was to collect moneys for me, and
take orders three days in a week-the orders were made up, and a bill of
parcels, and it was his duty to take them to the customers the next morning
--if he received money it was his duty to bring it home and enter it in the
cash-book- I have the book here--no one in particular kept it-it is the
book to which I used to refer to see what money had been paid if the
prisoner received 158. d. of Mr. Denyer on the 7th May he has not accounted
for it, or 4s. 1 d. from Mr. Brant-I believe the prisoner left on 30th July,
but I was ill.

Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Did he bring back 41.? A. He
did not give it to me I was told about it-Mr. Fryer engaged the prisoner,
and made the arrangement for his wages-I know by my cash-book that he
had 255. & week, and after he had been with me six months, 20s., and there
was an agreement to pay him a commission-I have paid him 8l. commission
- there is 131. due to him-he went out with a van three times a week-be
might go a few miles round the suburbs I think his commission was paid
him in April-he had it when be asked for it, but he came home intoxicated
at half-past 11 o'clock one night and wanted a sovereign; he was very noisy
and troublesome, and I would not let him have it-that was the only time he
was refused-that was in May-I have not paid him the money since, because
he never asked for it-I have never made him pay for bad debts to my recol-
lection - I see by my ledger an account of 61.-worth of goods sold to Osborn,
and on the other side is “Cash, 61."--I have heard it said that the prisoner
paid that out of his own pocket-I do not think he did.
