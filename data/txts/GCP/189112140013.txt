CHARLES COGLAN (Inspector). I was in charge of the station when the
prisoner was brought there on the night of October 8th-when the
charge was read he was crying very much, and he said, “I did not
steal anything”-I told him he was not charged with stealing, and read
the charge again to him and explained it-he said, “I am innocent; I
have been led into this;" he was crying and sobbing very bitterly.
EDWARD DREW (Re-examined). The prisoner had £2 odd, and I gave
that to him; he had also some keys, no pocket-book.
MR. FULTON submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury. With
regard to the counts charging the inciting the witness Dash to commit larcony,
if the facts did not disclose that there could be any felony in Dash for disposing
of his mastor's goods, there could not be a misdemeanour in the prisoner for
inciting him to commit that offence. If Dash had sold the goods for less than
their proper price, that would be a breach of his duty; but he could not be
indicted for stealing, as he would not have appropriated the goods to his own
use, but only sold them for his master, deriving for himself somo pocuniary
benefit by the transaction. As to the counts charging an inoitement to conspiro
to cheat and defrand the prosecutors of their goods and moneys, there could be
no such conspiraoy, because no moneys had passed into the prosecutor's possession,
and a person could not be defrauded of what he had not got, and no goods would
have passed into the possession of Dash. It was no more than a corrupt
arrangoment to let the prisoner have a good bargain. The last count charged
incitement to conspire to steal, and the same arguments would apply. An incite-
ment to commit something which did not amount to a misdemeanour oould not
itself bo a misdemeanour. Upon the whole, therefore, the counts, although good
in form, failed from want of evidence. “MR. MATHEWS contended that the
incitement to commit a misdemeanour charged in the fourth count was itself a
misdemeanour, and that upon the other counts it was a question for the Jury
whether the evidence had not established an incitoment to steal, and an inoita-
ment to conspire to steal. If Dash had fallen in with the suggestion made by
the prisoner, and had allowed the goods to leave the premises on payment of a
less price than should have been paid, a joint felony toould have been committed
by them.
THE RECORDER ruled that the case must go to the JURY, but that if necessary
he toould reserve the point. The prisoner received a good character.
GUILTY.-Judgmont reserved.
