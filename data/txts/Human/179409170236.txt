566. MARY GATHRIE was indicted for that she, on the 16th of August , a piece of false and counterfeit money to the likeness of a good shilling, unlawfully did utter to Richard Finch , knowing it to be counterfiet.(The indictment opened by-, and the case by Mr. Cullen.)
   RICHARD FINCH sworn.
 I am a  fish salesman; on the 16th of August this Mary Gathrie came to me at Billingsgate to purchase some mackeral, between seven and eight o'clock in the morning; she was standing some time before me; she was waiting an opportunity to see when they sell cheap, when there are a great many on hand, and I was selling them at twenty-four shillings a hundred, which was three shillings for fifteen, she gave me two shillings and two six-pences, and endeavoured to set the money into my hand as fast as she could; the first I looked at I told her here is a bad shilling, before I looked any further, then I returned it to her, and she gave me another.
 Q. Was that a good shilling? - No; but in the mean time I looked at the other that was in my hand with the two six-pences, and I said here is another as bad. The constable Peat was standing by me at the same time I held the two shillings in my hand; I never gave her them back; the six-pences are one good and one bad; I kept the two shillings and two six-pences in this state, and have had them in my possession ever since.
 Prisoner. He knows me these four years, and he knows that I have a family of children? - I know nothing of the woman otherwise than by her coming to market buying fish; I know nothing of her otherwise than being informed of her being a common utterer of bad money, and I was surprised at her giving it me.
   THOMAS PEATE sworn.
 I was standing at the corner by the side of Mr. Finch; he says to this woman this is a bad shilling; she gave him another which was equally as bad. I then desired Mr. Finch to keep what he had got in his possession; I went to her, and from her left hand she attempted to drop something, but I prevented her by seizing her hand, and I found two shillings and a six-pence in it, one six pence was a bad one and the other was a good one, and the six-pence was good. I have kept the bad ever since, but the good was given her before the Lord Mayor; I took her up stairs and searched her afterwards, and found two good shillings, two bad ones and a six-pence. These three bad shillings I now produce, one was taken from her hand and the other two taken from her pocket. I cannot tell which is which.
 Q. What was done with the two shillings and two six-pences that were taken by Finch? - That is produced by him.
   WILLIAM PARKER sworn.
 The two shillings and two six-pences produced by the prosecutor are all bad, except one six-pence is doubtful, but I believe it to be bad. These three shillings are likewise bad.
 Prisoner. I took them for my fish, I don't know of whom; I am a widow woman, with three small children.
     GUILTY. Imprisoned six months in Newgate, and to find security for twelve months.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
