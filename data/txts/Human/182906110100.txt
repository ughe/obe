 1229.  WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of May , 1 hat, value 10s., the goods of Marshall Hall.
   BENJAMIN SAGE. I am in the service of Dr. Marshall Hall, of Keppell-street, Russell-square. On the 26th of May I saw this hat safe on the table in the hall, about eight o'clock in the morning, and it was gone in about half an hour - I know nothing of the prisoner.
   JAMES WHITE. I saw the prisoner take the hat from Dr. Hall's; he went in without one, and came out with a hat in his hand, in about three minutes - I told the cook, and in half an hour I saw an officer, and told him.
   WILLIAM CORDWELL. I am a pawnbroker. This hat was pawned at my house by the prisoner, on the 26th of May - I gave this duplicate for it.
 ALPHONSO DALLERY. I am an officer. I received information, and took the prisoner in the skittle-ground of the Brittania public-house, Battle-bridge; I found this duplicate in his fob pocket.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 21.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1230.  ELIZABETH WHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of May , 1 tea-caddy, value 8s., and 16 spoons, value 6s. 6d., the goods of John Perkins.
   JOHN PERKINS. I am a baker, and live in Chequer-alley, Bunhill-row. On the 2d of May, about five o'clock, I lost a tea-caddy from my bed-room, and six spoons; I had seen them safe about three o'clock; I did not see the prisoner - this is my caddy and spoons.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you know a woman named Smith, who came to your house to see some one? A. Yes; I have made inquiries about the prisoner, and I have heard a very good character of her, and that her state of mind is such as to render her a very easy dupe to any person.
   WILLIAM HUMPHRIES. I am shopman to Mr. Clark, a pawnbroker. On Saturday evening, the 2d of May, the prisoner pawned this caddy, and then the spoons.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you observe any woman outside? A. No; she came with the caddy first, and asked 8s. - then brought the spoons, and asked for as much as I could give her, as she wanted to make up her rent; she gave her name and address - I knew her before.
   JOHN CLARK. I took the prisoner up, and found this duplicate on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to Mr. Cotterell's, at the top of that house, for an umbrella - I stopped some time with him, and when I came down I saw a woman in the passage; she followed me out, and asked if I lodged at Mr. Burton's - I said No: she asked me to go and get 20s. for these spoons - I went and asked them, and they said they would chop them up before my face - she then asked me to go to another shop, and I went to Mr. Clark's, where I was known; I pawned the caddy first, and then the spoons.
 JOHN CLARK. Mr. Brooks told me that a woman had been there to pawn some spoons, on which she asked 20s., and she would be there again to take something out.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1231.  WILLIAM PAGE and JOHN PAGE were indicted for that they, on the 8th of June , 49 handkerchiefs, value 5l., the goods of William Venables and Thomas Venables , feloniously did receive, of a certain evil-disposed person, which had been lately before stolen, they well knowing the same; against the Statute, &c.
   RICHARD VENABLES. I am brother to William and Thomas Venables, who are in partnership; Thomas Page, the prisoners' brother, was in their service - he pleaded guilty this morning to stealing these things. On the morning he was committed, I applied to Sir George Farrant for a warrant to search the prisoners' house, No. 2, Windsorplace, and in a hat-box, which John Page acknowledged to be his. I found twenty-six silk handkerchiefs, and seventeen in William Page's box; John said they had been given to him by Thomas, to keep and to sell - William was out at the time; when he came home we took him: he said he had bought them of his brother Thomas; he had given 1s. a piece for some, 1s. 6d. for others, and 2s. was the utmost price he had given; he said he had been in the silk business, and he considered he had given the utmost price - they have our marks on them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. For ought you know, Thomas may have brought them there for them to sell? A.Certainly; I got all the handkerchiefs I could find.
   GEORGE AVIS. I am an officer. I went to the prisoners' residence, and found John at home; I found the handkerchiefs in the boxes; some of them John said had been given to them to sell, and some they had bought: when William came home he said he gave 1s. 6d. or 2s. a piece for them - they live in Hoxton; Messrs. Venables' shop is in Oxford-street; William keeps a butcher's-shop, and I believe John is his servant.
   WILLIAM PAGE'S Defence. My brother brought the handkerchiefs to me, and said he bought them of Mr. Venables - that they were selling off a bankrupt's stock at one third of its value; he said he would bring a few more if I wished it: I said I had no objection, and he brought some more.
   RICHARD VENABLES. We were selling off cheap, but not at the price he speaks of.
 WILLIAM PAGE -    GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.
   JOHN PAGE-   NOT GUILTY

   JAMES ANDERSON. I am a furnishing ironmonger, and live in Wardour-street. I lost an iron pot on the 1st of May, from inside my shop; the prisoner was brought in with it.
   CHARLES WILLIAM GREEN. I saw the prisoner walking about the prosecutor's premises; I went into my own house, and soon after saw him coming up the street with this pot - I stopped him, and took it from him; he said it was his own - he had another person with him.
