CHARLES GIBSON. I am a sawyer, and live at 3, New-court. On 27th
Feb. I came home drunk- I saw the prisoner that evening-we were very
good friends together-when the fight was over I was coming in-doors again,
and directly I got against my own door I found my throat was cut, and I
tumbled down-1 cannot say whether it was the prisoner did it, but there was
nobody in the court but her and me, and she was crying-I was taken to the
prisoner's room and laid upon the bed-I heard her say, "I shall never rest
upon my bed any more, for, poor fellow, look how he bleeds ! look how he
bleeds!"-she said, “Dark Sally has cut poor Charley's throat with a knife,
and chucked it up-stairs again"-she afterwards said, "I have cut his throat
with the washhand basin; I did not intend to do it" -- I swear she mentioned
the word " washband basin"-she said, “I shall never forgive myself any
more"-when the basin was thrown up it did strike me--the instant it was
thrown I found my throat was cut-I do not know what it was cut it-I
was very tipsy-I was in the hospital a month, and have not been able to
work since.
MARY DAY. I was in the yard with Gibson, and saw the prisoner draw
the knife across his throat-he fell down about two minutes afterwards the
basin was thrown up to the window, and it came down on Gibson's head-
did not break before it hit his head-it was meant for Sarah Morris.
WILLIAM WATKINS Edwards. I am house-surgeon at the London Hos.
pital. Gibson was brought there on the morning of 27th Feb.-he had an
incised wound across his throat-I should say such a knife as this produced
would cause such a wound-I should say it was impossible for it to have been
done by a basin being thrown up and falling upon him as he lay upon the
ground--the wound was about two inches long between the chin and apple
of the throat, and about half an inch deep-it wounded one of the branches
of the carotid artery-he had lost a good deal of blood when he was brought
- he would be very likely to fall instantly-be was under my care for a month
Prisoner. Gibson told me the doctor had taken several pieces of china
out of the poultice applied to his throat, and he had several pieces remaining
under his chin. Witness. I have not seen any-I never heard of it till this
moment-no complaint was made of the man's head-I noticed no abrasion
of the skin.
WILLIAM BROWN (policeman, H 111). I went into Morris's room about
half-past five, and found this knife upon the floor, in the same state as it is
now-I found a cloth in the prisoner's room with marks of blood upon it
such as the wiping of a knife would produce. I believe there was blood
upon the prisoner's apron-it was taken from her when she was taken
into custody.
ROBERT GIFFORD (policeman, H 89). I took the prisoner the same night,
several hours after-she was in bed, and when she dressed herself I took this
apron from her produced)-it is stained with blood in two or three parts.
Prisoner's Defence. I saw Sarah Morris pass me as Gibson stood at my
left; I turned round and said, “Oh my God! dark Sally has cut his throat,
and I said no more till he was carried into my room and laid upon my bed.
GUILTY of a most aggravated Assault. Aged 25.-Confined Two Years.
