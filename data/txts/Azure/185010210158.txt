1911. ROBERT OXLADE, stealing bushels of oats, value
goods of William Keen Sedgwick, and another : his masters.
WILLIAM KEEN SEDGWICK. I am in partnership with Henry Green—the
prisoner was our carman—l cut some pieces of paper, and my partner in my
presence put them with some oats in sacks in our granary on Thursday
17th Oct.—next day, in consequence of what Thornton told me, I went
to the police court, and found these oats in a sack bare
examined it, and found some of the papers I had put in.
STEPHEN THORNTON (policeman, A 26). In consequence of information
on the Friday morning. went to the prosecutor's premises in Uppr
Thames-street and watched—and about five minutes after seven o'clock I
saw the prisoner come out with his horse and cart, and the cart had a
number of sacks in it which turned out to be corn—I and Smith followed
him through various street to the Vauxhall-road, where he stopped and got
into the cart—I was some distance behind it, and it was a very foggy morning,
Smith was very close to him—I came up to him near tbe Ship, and saw
him open one sack, take corn from it, and put it into one. on his left, Which
is the one produced—be continued to take com from his right side, aud
putting it into the other sack till he came near the Horns at Kennington,
where there were two constables near him—I then followed him to the White
Horse in the Brixton-road, where he stopped, spoke to a man there, went
round the cart twice, and went into the stable—we followed him, told him
we were offcers, and asked what he had done with the sack—he said it vas
in the stable, he had left it there till he came back to bait his horses with—
I brought the corn to the station, and showed it to Mr. Sedgwick—there
was only one horse to the cart.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Then there was a little taken out
of each sack and put into this sack? A. Yes.
HENRY SMITH (policeman A 28). I was with last witness—I saw what
he says he saw, and the prisoner did what he says was doge.
( The prisoner received an excellent character.)
