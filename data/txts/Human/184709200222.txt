 2260. THOMAS NURSE was indicted for stealing 1 half-crown, the moneys of William Rowe , from his person. WILLIAM ROWE. I live in King-street, East-lane, Walworth. On the afternoon of the 19th of Sept. I was in Battersea-fields, in company with George Double and Thomas Ball—I found the prisoner there with a donkey—I bargained with him for a ride for a penny—I had a halfpenny and a half-crown—I gave him the halfpenny into his hand for the half-crown—I took it out of his hand, and gave him the half-crown—he said, "Go on, you shall have your change when you come back, "and he struck the donkey before I got my legs across—he did not follow the donkey—I went a little way down—when I came back, the prisoner was not there, but another man took the donkey—I afterwards saw the prisoner amongst some men and some donkeys—I asked him for my change—he said, "Go on, you d—d liar, you are to have no change; you gave me two halfpence." GEORGE DOUBLE. I live in Eagle-street—I was with Rowe in Batterses fields—I saw him give the prisoner a halfpenny first, and he said, "The won't do, "and he gave him a half-crown, and the halfpenny fell out of his hand again—the prisoner struck the donkey before he got his leg across, and he said, "Go on, you shall have your change when you come back"—we found the prisoner a few minutes afterwards, and he said that Rowe had only given him two halfpence, and one was a large halfpenny, the other a small one. THOMAS BELL. I live in Bedford-row—I was with Rowe and Double—Rowe gave the prisoner a halfpenny—he then gave him the half-crown, and the halfpenny fell into his hand again—he said, "Give me my change?"—he said, "Go on, you shall have it when you come back"—he strack the donkey, and it ran away with the boy half-way down the field. WILLIAM WALKER( policeman, L  98.) I was on duty last Sunday evening in Battersea-fields—I apprehended the prisoner—I found on him two six-pences, which he gave to another man to get food for his donkey.
   Prisoner.  I had two sixpences in my pocket; I told the policeman that was all I had got; this boy had given me two halfpence, and I gave them for change for a sixpence.
   COURT to WILLIAM ROWE. Q.  Since this occurred, has your master made a charge against you? A.  Yes, about receiving 1 l . 14 s .—I was examined before the Magistrate about it—this half-crown has nothing to do with that—I meant to let the prisoner have the half-crown, and to give me 2 s . 5 d . change, but the halfpenny fell into his hand—I expected he would give me 2 s . 5 1/2 d . NOT GUILTY .
