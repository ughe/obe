Cross-examined by MR. CAARTEEN. Q.  Who came first into the shop? A.  Hearn—I do not knew where the other two prisoners were at the time Hearn took what I considered to be solder—Hearn then left, and came again, and beat up something—I cannot say whether it was piping or sheet lead—neither of the other prisoners were there then—Hearn went away, and came again the third time, and beat up some sheet lead—while he was there Taylor and Allen came—the place is about ten feet long, and eight feet, wide—the man had done work at that time—the prisoners were as near together as they could be—the sheet lead is on the side of the workshop, they were all three at that lead. JAMES LOMAS. I am in Mr. Herring's service. On Friday afternoon, the 10th of Sept., Mr. Baker told me to watch—I watched, and saw Hearn take two pieces of leaden pipe, about nine inches long—he placed them about his body, and went away—he came back again, and took a piece of sheet lead, he beat it together, and put that about his body, under his waistcoat—he went out, and came in the third time, took some more lead, beat it together, and placed that about his body—Taylor then came in, beat up three pieces of lead, and placed it about his body—I did not see Allen come in, for I left Hearn and Taylor in the room when I went up into the warehouse to give Information to a man to go for a policemen—I then went and watched again—I saw the three prisoners together in the room—they were standing together, and feeling one another down, to see whether they would be noticed in going out—they than came up stairs, and went out—I followed them to the bottom of Trinity-street—they crossed, and Taylor turned towards St. George's Church—I saw Baker run after him—I followed him—he turned down a stable-yard, and there threw away some lead. CHARLES WALKER( police-sergeant, M  18.) On Friday, the 10th of Sept, Taylor was given into my charge, and this lead was brought to the station by Mr. Baker—Taylor said the foremen give him the lead to take to the shop. JOSHUA GOLDIN( policeman M  87.) I took Hearn that evening in Bedford bury—I told him what it was for; he said he knew nothing at all of it—I told him I took on a charge of stealing lead from Mr. Herring's premises, where he had been working that day—he said he knew nothing about stealing lead, but he knew there was something up when he saw them following them.
 (The prisoners received good characters.)
   TAYLOR  — GUILTY . Aged 23.— Confined Three Months .
   HEARN  — GUILTY . Aged 27.
   ALLEN  — GUILTY . Aged 29. Confined One Month .
