   Cross-examined by Barnard.  Although you did not write to me posi tively to come over, the meaning of your letter was to that effect, and I believed I was to come because you said you would put me into direct communication with the place—I received no letter saying that if I was wanted they would write to me to come over—I never asked you to repay me the 10 francs—I saw you in Dompe's cellar, where I went to taste some wines; I cannot say if once or several times.
   Cross-examined by Launet.  You did not say you were Barnard, you always spoke and acted as if you were; you took no further part in my affairs—it was Linden with whom I had to do.
   Cross-examined by Linden.  I thought "direct communication" meant face to face—I received from you the address of a Mr. Beck with regard to being a professor at his place, but there also 5,000 francs was required—you never took me to him—I gave the 10 francs to cover small expenses—you spent a little money with me in drinks, not 10 francs; I spent a good deal more—you lent me 1 l . 7 s . (I did not ask you to) when you found I was about to telegraph for some money—I wrote, and an answer by return of post enclosing 200 francs came to my name at Barnard's address—the prisoners opened the letter, but the money was by draft and they could not get it; they gave me the letter opened—I returned the 1 l . 7 s .—it is possible that at the police-court I did not mention the sum of 10,000 francs which I was asked at Dompe's—I never said Dompe told me he did not want any employe—I did not know he was in want of a confidential person to take an interest in the business and deposit 3,000 francs—I should have had one or another place if I had deposited a lot of money—Barnard's firm have not fulfilled their obligations because their letters never mentioned anything about a deposit—it is true your letter mentioned simply the places and nothing about the terms.
