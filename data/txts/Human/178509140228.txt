Then, gentlemen, permit me to ask you, what could have induced Harvey to go a little beyond the truth in his evidence on this prosecution? I will tell you, because it is the only evidence that can be suggested, in hopes of gaining a part of the reward on their conviction; now, this is the circumstance they are to make out, that he, being apprised of the effect of a flight being given in evidence, had confined his perjury to that idea; trusting to that idea having the effect of convicting the two men at the bar. Gentlemen, that you may clearly understand the nature of this case, let me tell you the story of Mr. Harvey; he was the constable of Limehouse, and the evening, when this robbery was committed, about seven in the month of January, it was dark, he was at that time at home in his house, he was called out by the alarm of a robbery that had been committed; the person supposed to be robbed was very near at hand, and he saw him instantly: upon his coming up to him he told him that he knew the men that had perpetrated the crime, that one was Peter, and the other was Will; that he knew where Peter lodged, and in consequence of this information Harvey went to the house of Peter; now, part of this evidence, that you have heard in support of this prosecution is true, for it hardly ever will be the case, that witnesses will come before a court with a story that will be false throughout; Harvey went with this man to the house of Peter, he knocked; he was answered by a woman from above, and he got into the house; Peter was not to be found there; then Harvey went away; he went to a public house; he had not an idea of missing in this public house the person he went to look for, but as he was drinking a glass of liquor at the bar, Lockell, the prosecutor, from without, called out to him, constable, constable, there goes Peter; there had been a man that left the house merely on the arrival of Harvey, whether it was or was not Peter, I am not able to produce to you, more than the assertion of Harvey; Harvey thinks that man was the person; Lockell calling out to him, he necessarily followed; a chace took place; some times he had him in view, and some times he lost sight of him, but as the man was going towards the house of Goodman, Harvey took hold of him; Harvey's evidence say this, that he took the prisoner Newberry within ten yards of his door, but the evidence of the witnesses in opposition to this say, that Mr. Harvey did not take Newberry within ten yards of the door, but in the very house; now, how could it have entered into the mind of the man before you, that supposing there was a difference, supposing the fact to be, (which I deny) that he was taken in the house, and that Harvey had said he took him within ten yards of the door, could it have suggested itself to a mind like his, that any materiality would have depended on that part of his testimony? Does it strike you so?
