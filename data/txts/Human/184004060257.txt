I am assistant to Mr. Philpot, a surgeon near Praed-street, Paddington. I was sent for to Mrs. Stevens's on Tuesday evening, the 31st of March—I got there about five o'clock, or it might be a quarter before—I saw an infant there—I examined it—it was perfectly insen sible—I smelt its breath, and could perceive the odour of laudanum—this bottle was shown to me—I examined it—there were a few drops more than there is in it now—it is pure laudanum I should say, from the smell and taste of it, and from what I see here—it is such as they sell in the shops for laudanum—the state of the child was such as might be produced by taking laudanum.
   Q.  What quantity of laudanum administered in that state would produce the effect you found? A.  One drop has been known to do it—it is never safe to administer one drop to a child—one drop would not produce the effect I found, but one drop has been known to poison a child—a tea-spoonful would—about sixty drops is an ordinary teaspoonful—that if rather a large dose—this bottle would contain about an ounce—half of it would be about half an ounce—a teaspoonful administered to an infant nine months old, such a baby as I saw, would be certain to destroy life—I gave it some medicine—castor oil would not be likely to do harm nor good unless it acted as an emetic—if it had, it might have done good—mustard and water would have been better—it appeared a hopeless case from my first seeing it—I first saw the prisoner on Wednesday morning—she came to Mr. Philpot when I was there, and said, "My poor little one is dead"—she asked if I was the gentleman who had seen the child—I told her yes—she said she was sorry to lose the child when it had attained that age—she then asked if there was likely to be an inquest—I said in all proba bility there would be an inquest, and asked her whether she had inquired into the mistake—(I had heard people talking about one medicine having been given for another)—she said yes, she had been to Dr. Reid's, and that Dr. Reid had told her it was his young man's mistake—she said the young man had given it in mistake for another mixture off the counter—I then left her.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is the bottle precisely in the same stole in which it was handed to you? A.  Yes—it has rather evaporated—there might have been two or three more drops than there is now—there is now very likely one drop—there was about three times as much as there is now—the Coroner tested it—it has been very trifingly diminished by him.
   Q.  Are you able clearly to say that the mixture which is in this bottle has not been adulterated? A.  I cannot say that for certain—if adultera ted by water, whatever quantity was given would certainly produce less effect.
