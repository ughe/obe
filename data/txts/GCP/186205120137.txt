WILLIAM WARD. I am a carman, of 5, James-street, Bethnal-green-I
let vans on hire on Saturday, 28th December, the prisoner came about 2
o'clock, and said he should want a yan in the evening, to remove some rags from
Old-street-I asked him 58. for it-he said that be thought he was enough,
as it was a light load, and we agreed on 46.-I did not see it leave in the
evening, as I was at Chelsea, but when I came home I found that the van
had done its job I only sent out one van that evening. if sosed but
Cross-examined. Q. Has the prisoner bired vans of you before A. No;
but his brother has the prisoner has not hired & van of me, or my son,
during the last twelve months, according to this book, and we generally put
everything down.
& ROBERT KNIGHT. I am a carman, in Mr. Ward's employ, and have been
80 for three years on Saturday, 28th December, between 5 and 6 o'clook,
or it might be later, I was waiting in Mr. Ward's yard, with my van,
a young man, with a moustache, and a little bit of hair on his chin, who Í
do not know, came up to me, and in consequence of what he said, I got up
in the van and drove away with him to Kingsland-road, where another man
joined us, and from there we went to Mr. Brown's--they told me to stop the
van at a publie-house, just round the corner, when they gave me some beer
-the one who came to me first, left me there with the second man, and when
he came back, in consequence of what he said, we all three went to Mr. Brown's
Red Lion-yard--they told me to turn round, and put the van under the
Joop-hole they then chucked some bags in: one man, who was in the loft,
shoved the bags out, the other man was in the van-there were about 6 bags
altogether-1 cannot say exactly I did not see what was in them we were
there about ten minutes-we went straight from there to Mr. Green's
Church-street, close to Brick-lane, where we left the rags, and then went to
the Lamb public-house, where we got some beer, and the man who first
came to me, paid me 46 for my master, and 6d. for myself—I do not know
whether the prisoner is the man or not he wore a round hat, something
like mine.

WILLIAM GRIMES (Policeman, K 291). I took the prisoner, on 17th April,
at & marine store shop, in High-street, Poplar, where he was employed-I
told him he was charged with stealing a load of rage from Aldersgate-street
-Mr. Brown was standing on the opposite side of the way, and I said, " From
his place ?"—the prisoner said, "That is right, I will go with you "I took
him to Smithfield police-station-on his way there, be was about making a
statement-I told him that what he said I should have to repeat to the
Magistrate-he said that all he had to say, was that somebody else ought to
be there as well as him.
Cross-examined. O. Will you swear he did not say "Somebody else
