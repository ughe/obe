DEFENCE OF JOHN BAIBD.
JOHs BAIRD (prisoner, on oath). I was appointed to the infirmary
in July, 1902. I first met Bond about Christmas, 1903. In 1905 I
got to be rather intimate with him; he was a director, and I was on
the committee of the Leyton Football Club. I flatly deny Bond's
account of a conversation between him and me early in 1903; I never
had such a conversation at all; I did not, in fact, pay for my own
position I would not have paid anything; I did not say that as long
as he did not overdo it I would accept his terms. His account of the
next conversation at which he says he offered me 2d. a ton if I would
allow him to deliver coal in bulk is likewise untrue, I did challenge
him about his delivery in bulk; I asked him who gave him permission
and he said that his contraet said nothing one way or the other, but
left it to himself. I never received the monthly payments Bond
speaks of. At Christmas, 1904, he same to me and said, It is usual
at this festive season to give little recognitions, I have never seen
you out," and he gave me E2 10s., saying, 4 That will buy you a bottle
of champagne" he also gave me a box of cigars. At Christmas,
1905, he gave me E2. There was never any suggestion that these
payments were by way of bargaining or bribe. Apart rom these
sums I have never received a penny from Bond. I deny his story of
a conversation in July, 1906, at which he arranged with me that he
should send one third English coal in the Welsh coal; I never had
such a conversation. Early in the currency of the contrast for Welsh
coal the Guardians, Mr. Paul among others, came and looked at the
coal. I never concealed the faet that there was English coal amongst
the Welsh. I first knew of that in July. I made repeated complaints
to Bond, and to the steward, and to the clerk. In October
I took a sample and complained to Lewis Hill. The new contrast
started October 1. After three or four weeks there WaS a prolonged
diseussion before the Guardians about the coal. I complained in
January, 1904, of the coal that came in on Christmas Eve, 1903. That
was when I received my first Christmas box from Bond of E2 10s. In
July, 1904, I reported to the Infirmary Committee about the supply
ef steam coal. Mr. Paul and two other Guardians came. Mr. Paul
pointed out to me the amount of dust in the stoke hole, and said,
You ought to report this to the committee." I said, What I have
been instructed is to report to the steward." He told me to put in a
report to the committee, and say he had told me to do 80. I did,
and the report is in my book. I know now that the steward dealt
with my complaint himself without going before the committee. I
learned that at the Local Government Board inquiry. I made a report
upon MacKenzie's complaint, which appears in my report book. I
never showed that report to Bond. It was written the same day as
it went before the committee. (To the Judge.) I never
showed Bond the report in my book partly covered with blotting
paper. This beek did not come into existence until February 16.
