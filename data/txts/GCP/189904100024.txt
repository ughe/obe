MR. SHERWOOD Prosecuted, and MR. HUTTON Defended.
AMY SATCHELL. I am the wife of Frederick William Satchell, land-
lord of the Apollo public house, at the corner of East Street and
Paddington Street, Marylebone—the prisoner and a man named Haynes
were in the bar on Easter Monday afternoon—the deceased and his
brother George White came in-they began to jangle, and I went round
and told White and his brother to leave the house_they went into
another bar-I told them to leave that-James was making the most
noise—the two Whites went out-I said to the others that I was sur-
prised that Haynes, with a horse and cab, should want to quarrel-none
of them appeared the worse for drink.
Cross-examined. I knew the deceased; he had used the house for
years—be was not a quarrelsome man-he started it first on this day-I
did not hear the prisoner say, “It is holiday time; do not let there be
any rowing."
