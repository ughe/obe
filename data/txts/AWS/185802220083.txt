SAMUEL WILLOUGHBY. I live in New Buildings, Red Cross Street. On
3rd Feb., I left home to go to Walworth, about 8 elock in the evening-
had 10s. 7d. in silver in my pocket. On returning from Walworth, I a
wert into the Sugar Loaf public house-I Was drinking there-I found
sailor there, and was drinking with him some time-it might be an hourit a
might be about 10 o'clock when I got to the Sugar Loafwher I came out I
might go with the sailor towards London Bridge; I would not sayowe came
out of the Sugar Loaf together-I do not recollect going anywhere else-I
had I some money when I left the Sugar Loaf-I cannot tell to what amount
was drunk-j am not aware that I went to High Street, and from there to
Union Street-there was something happened to me; it was in Union Street
the policeman told me-I remember nothing of seeing the prisoners till I saw
them before the Magistratein the morning I had no money, it was all
gone, and my clothes were dirty-I had my 108. 7d. in coming up from
Walworth.
Cross-examined by MR. LILLY. Q. That was about 8 o'clock A. No,
nearly nine in fact, I got drunk, and forgot all about it.
THOMAs HALTON (Policeman, M 251). I was on duty in High Street,
Borough, at a quarter past 1 o'clock, on 4th Feb.] saw the two prisoners
and the proseeutor standing together-Hands had got her hand in his pocket,
and, as soon as she pulled it out, they both laid hold of him by the shoulders,
threw him on the pavement, and ran away-when Hands drew her hand out
of his poeket, her hand was shut-I pursued and overtook them about twenty
yards off-I caught them both round the neck; another constable coming up,
I let him take hold of Welsh, and I said, 46 You have robbed that man"
Hands said, 44 I never touched the man" laid hold of her hand, and took
six shillings, and 6d. in copper, out of it-it was her right hand, which she
carried under her cloak in running.
Cross-eacamined. Q. In what part of High Street was this A. About
twenty yards from Union Street, towards the Town Hall-I was on the same
side of the way as the prisoners, about ten yards from them; there was no
one else there-I never heard the ery of 44 Police from any one-there was
no one passing, and no cab-they ran towards Union Street] followed them
about twenty yards--the prosecutor was lying on his back I sent another
constable to take him up-he was taken to the station-he did not recover
himself-I saw him the next morning.
