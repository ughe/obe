1295. CATHERINE BROOKS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of April , 1 pair of trousers, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Stafford and John Stafford.
   JAMES TRAIL. I am in the employ of Thomas and John Stafford, pawnbrokers, St. John-street. On the evening of the 12th of April, the prisoner came into the shop with another woman to pawn a shawl - while this was going on, the prisoner went to the door, and looked at a pair of trousers which were tied to an iron bar, and I suppose she untied them - she then came back to the counter - went out, and came in again - then went out and took the trousers; I went and found her with them two doors off; I think she was intoxicated.
   WILLIAM CLEMENTS(Police-constable G 82). I took the prisoner; she said the woman who was with her took them down, gave them to her, and desired her to run off with them. GUILTY. Aged 37.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1296.  GEORGE BRIANT was indicted for stealing' on the 29th of February , 1 saw, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Keen ; 1 saw, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Clapton ; and 1 apron, value 6d., the goods of James Harwin.
   THOMAS JOHNSON(Police-constable G 81). I took the prisoner and another man into custody on a charge of common assault; I found on the prisoner twenty-four duplicates, in a purse - some of them relate to the tools in question; I have found three persons who claim some of them - this apron I found on him also.
   JOHN DAVIS. I am servant to Mr. Cordwell, a pawnbroker, in Exmouth-street. I have three saws, two ploughs, and other articles, pawned at his shop - these are the duplicates of them.
   GEORGE FRY. I am in the service of Mr. Lorton, a pawnbroker. I have two saws, pawned on the 29th of April - this is the duplicate, but I did not take them in.
   THOMAS KEEN. This saw is mine - I lost it on the 29th of February, and this other saw is Thomas Clapton's - I went with the officer to take the prisoner, and found on him this apron, which belongs to another workman; there were several other saws and tools lost.
   JAMES HARWIN. This is my apron, and was in the same shop as the tools were.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought the apron in Oxford-street - they have all sworn false against me; I found the duplicates - I put them into a cupboard in my room.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Transported for Seven Years
 1297.  JOHN BALDWIN was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of April , 1 waistcoat, value 14s., the goods of John Foster.
   JOHN FOSTER. I live in Queen-street, Edgware-road. I had seen the prisoner at my son-in-law's, and asked him to assist me to move a few things - I then missed my waistcoat.
   HENRY RIDER. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Chelsea. I took in this waistcoat from the prisoner on the 2nd of April, and lent 3s. on it; I am certain of his person.
   RICHARD BART(Police-constable T 75). The prosecutor came to my house, and said he had lost a waistcoat; I took the prisoner.
   JOHN FOSTER. This is my waistcoat - I lost this and a great many other things.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 15.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury - Confined 14 Days.
 1298.  BENJAMIN BREWER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 2 trusses of hay, value 5s., and 3 bushels of oats and chaff, mixed together, value 4s., the goods of Thomas Cobley, the younger.
   THOMAS COBLEY, JUN. I am a carman, and live in Essex-street, Whitechapel. I had a cart in my yard, loaded with dung, on the 1st of May; it was just going to start -I received information, and examined it; I found under the tilt two trusses of hay, similar to that which I had in my loft - it was the prisoner's business to drive that cart; I spoke to him about it - he said he got it on the road, and then said he did not know how it came there - I sent for an officer, and took him; after he was gone to the station a sack of oats and chaff was found in the cart.
   JOHN KEMBER. I am foreman to the prosecutor. On the 1st of May the cart of dung was there, and two trusses of hay and a sack of oats and chaff mixed together, were found in it; I had been in the loft, and counted the trusses of clover hay - I then went to breakfast; the prisoner was loading the dung - when I came back he was still doing the same; I went into the loft and counted the hay again, and missed two trusses - I know he had not hay in his cart when he came to the yard; the two trusses were found on the copse in front of the cart - they were of the same sort as those in the loft, and made up the forty-four which I had at first counted - he first said he found it on the road, and then that he did not know how it got there; we had some mixed oats and chaff in the loft, but we could not miss any of them - that found in his cart corresponds with it exactly, and the sack, in which they were found seemed to be one which he had brought empty.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to get a pint of beer; I do not know who put them there. GUILTY. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
