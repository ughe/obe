 568.  JOHN STAPLETON was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of March , one watch, value 20 s., and one handkerchief value 4 s., the goods of Isaac Medell.
   ANN MEDELL. I am the wife of Isaac Medell, we live in Mourning-lane, Hackney. On the 15th of March, between eleven and one o'clock, the watch was safe in a drawer in my room. I was out at work, returned about nine o'clock, and it was gone. I saw it afterwards at a pawnbroker's in Barbican.
   ROBERT HUXSON. I am a pawnbroker and live in Barbican. On the 15th of March, in the afternoon, the prisoner pledged the watch with me for 14 s. Mrs. Medell claimed it three or four days afterwards.
   JOHN GARVA. I am a constable of Hackney. I apprehended the prisoner on the 25th of March; I told him it was for stealing the watch, he made no reply.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 569.  SAMUEL SAUNDERS and JOHN STONE were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of March , one 1 l. Bank note and 14 s. in monies numbered, the monies of Charles Louis Francis Vercolme.
 C. L. F. VERCOLME. I am  servant to the Rev. Mr. Lambert, of King-street, Portman-square. On the 7th of March, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, I had been in the City and was going home - it was quite dark. I went into a public-house in Barrett's-court, Edward-street, Portman-square- I had then four 1 l. notes in a purse in my side-pocket, I changed one of the notes there. Saunders came and sat in the same box with me, I treated him with liquor - I do not know Stone. After I had changed and had some liquor I fell asleep; Saunders was sitting close by me. I do not know whether my coat was buttoned or not. When I awoke he was standing by the fire, which was some distance from the box. It is a small room - I did not see Stone. I called for some liquor, Ann Hooper said,
 "You have no money." I was surprised, and said,
 "I have no money!" She said,
 "Go and ask the man with the broken nose (Saunders) for the money." I immediately went to him, and asked him for my money, he struck me in the mouth; I then told the landlady that I had been robbed, and called the watchman. Prendergrast came and took charge of Saunders. He was searched, he had 34 s. in silver and a 1 l. note - I did not mark the notes. He told Prendergrast that he had no money at all. I was quite sober.
   ANTHONY CHAPMAN. I live in Cumberland-street, Middlesex Hospital, and am a shoemaker. I was drinking in the house. I was not there when the prisoner came in - the prosecutor was not sitting by him. When I came in he appeared quite sober, and I saw Saunders drinking with him - I drank likewise with him, and we had not more than a pot of beer each. I was there when he lost his money, and was on the opposite side of the box. Saunders was the only one that sat on the same side with him, no other person could set next to him on the other side. Saunders was on the left side of Vercolme. I observed Vercolme get change at the bar, and did not see Stone; I must have seen him if he had been there. Ann Hooper was on the same side as me. I never saw Saunders doing anything with Mr. Vercolme's pocket. He slept about three-quarters of an hour. When he awoke, he said he would have some beer; Hooper said,
 "you have no money." He said,
 "No money! (feeling in his pocket,) who has got my money?" Hooper said that man with a broken nose had got his money. I did not hear Saunders say anything. I went away before the constable arrived. I did not hear anything said to Saunders repecting his having any money. There was no blow struck while I was there.
   SAMUEL PRENDERGRAST. I am a constable. I heard the cry of Watch! and went into the public-house. The prosecutor said (in the hearing of Saunders), he had been robbed; he said nothing to it. I asked him if he had any money, or had robbed the prosecutor? he said, No. Saunders was very much in liquor - he made some resistance, and denied robbing him. I found 34 s. in silver, and a 1 l. note in his fob, and 3 d. in copper. Stone was not in the house at the time to my knowledge.
 Q. During the time was there any blow struck - A. The prosecutor's mouth was very much swollen and bleeding: and he accused the prisoner with giving him the blow.  Ann Hooper has since absconded and cannot be found. I took up Stone in the same house in the evening, about half an hour after, and found nothing on him.
   GEORGE DORRELL. I am a brick-maker. I went with Mr. Pendergrast, but I did not hear what was said by Ann Hooper. The prosecutor accused Saunders of striking him, but he made no answer - he accused him of robbing him, and he denied it. Pendergrast asked him if he had any money, and he said No.
   ANN PIERPOINT. I keep the house. I saw the prosecutor come in about three o'clock, and he appeared to be intoxicated. I saw Ann Hooper and Saunders there in the course of the day. Vercolme got change at the bar when he came in - no one could see him from the taproom. About two hours afterwards, I heard he had lost his money - Saunders had been absent about an hour during the time.
 SAUNDERS'S Defence. The prosecutor came in and was in liquor - he asked me if I would drink with him, and called for some gin. He went out with a woman of the town, and the man she lives with tumbled them out of the house. When he returned he called for another pot of beer, and laid his head down and went to sleep, and I never left the house more than five minutes. I had no money in my pocket but my own.
 C. I. F. VERCOLME re-examined. Q. From the time you first came into the house did you go out with a woman - A. Yes. I did for a little while, and had my money after that.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
