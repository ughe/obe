Q. What I want to know is this, whether you have continued these
three first omnibuses at these times, up to within a fortnight? 4. Yes:
we have-my father has always run an omnibus at those times-it has not
always run in his name-one has been running latterly with Corbett's
name on it.
Q. Whenever Bray set out, at whatever time, nine, ten, or eleven o'clock
did not that omnibus with Corbett's name on it set out, whenever Brays
out? A. Yes.
Court. Q. And did it not confine itself to those times? 4. At first it

MR. SERGEANT Bompas. Q. Your om nibus always went with one of
his? A. Bray's went with one or other of our omnibuses-both Brays
went with one of ours from Whit-Monday to about Wednesday, with the
exception of one journey-after that there was only one of Bray's with oun
---the first day his started with the second omnibus we had that morning.
Q. When you say that Bray ran with one of your omnibuses, wher
ever it went, was not that the one that had Corbett's name on it? 4. No;
Corbett's name was put on about a week after Whit-Monday-Bray's co-
tinued to run against one of my father's which had not Corbett's name ot-
I have seen it at different times of the day-one day at one time--some
times at another-sometimes he stopped at the same time we did, and some
times he went on, just as it suited his own fancy-he did that from the very
beginning-those which I term the regular buses kept on at the regular
time-the others stopped with him.
Q. Did you ever send your horses down to Holborn, to change the
horses in that one that ran against Bray's? A. Yes, occasionally; only to
that one, that was the "Albion," No. 177, I believe, but we have two
or three " Albions"- I do not know that that "Albion" ever stopped fix
Bray's omnibus, when it was in Bray's yard- I have not the slightest no-
tion that our omnibus ever stopped in Holborn or Southampton-street, while
Bray's was in the yard it was sent to Holborn, because Bray's men said
they would not give them time to change, they would make that pair of
horses work all day long, when Bray's bus was working against our regt-
lar one-if they saw the bus we put on to protect our regular ones, they
would turn down some of the back streets, and if we stopped he would pop
out of some of the streets.
Court. Q. What did they do? A. They turned down the streets to
lose the bus we put on to protect our regular ones, and would wait, and
watch till they saw our regular buses coming, and then go on in front a
them, to pick up the passengers which they would have, and we had nothing
to protect ourselves with, if this other bus had not kept with them.
