SAMUEL MASCALL. On Saturday evening, the 24th of June, I was in
company with Craske- returned with him to his house, from a quarter to
half-past two o'clock-I saw his wife and Hannah Carr at the front win
dow-they called to us before that, Craske had made a communication to
policeman-I stopped at his door-he came back to me, after speaking
to the policeman-1 continued at his door-he stopped at it till I saw a young
man come over the wall, and then he ran- I believe it was the prisoner Wilks
-I asked Craske if it was not J. Wilks--I had every reason to believe it
was- I heard some words pass between him and Craske- I cannot say what,
as I was not near enough-after that he ran towards the Adam and Eve, away
from Printers'-buildings-Wilks lives in the Cholera-houses: that is on the
other side of the way from Printers'-houses- I know Barker and Brockwell
-I cannot say which houses they occupy.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You were locked up on this case?
4. Yes, from Sunday morning to Monday morning-the prisoner said some
thing to Craske like this, "You b y fool, let me go; don't you see it is

that Wilks had any thing to do with it.
