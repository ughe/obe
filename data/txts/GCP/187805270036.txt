FREDERICK FINBOW. I am a licensed victualler, of the Coopers' Arms,
Golden Lane-in September, 1876, I was looking for a publio-house-I
saw an advertisement, "apply to Alfred Harvey, 63, Dorset Street," in
consequende of which I went to that address the name was in the win
dow, * Alfred Harvey and Co., Auctioneers and Trade Valuers "-I saw
a clerk-on the 27th September I saw the prisoner-I told him I had
come about the advertisement of a house to sell_he said yes, he had a
good thing for me, and he sent me to the Shepherd and Shepherdes
public-house, in Old Gravel Lane-he named 5001. as the price I went
and inspected it, and came back and agreed to purchase it-my brother
paid the prisoner a deposit of 101. on my behalf-this is the receipt-
we were to meet at Reid's brewery to complete-I kept the appointment,
when I was told there was no promise made to sell the house, though a
man named Mason had been there bothering about it-I told the pri-
Buner-he said, "Oh, I can work that all right for you, he has made a
mistake"-I received a number of letters from the prisoner, but I never
got the house nor the money returned-I paid my money believing the
prisoner would get me the loans and carry out the purchase.
Cross-examined. You represented yourself as Mr. Harvey-you intro
duoed me to the manager of the house-I did not know that you paid
101. to Mr. Carter-you said you were not Mr. Carter's agent-you in.
troduced me to Mr. Oarter as the owner of the property-Oarter wrote
to me afterwards to say you were a swindler-I have not the letter-I
went to him because you ran away-I authorised you to get the loans for
me - I do not know whether you tried - I gave you references -I
tried to meet you at several places, but you always ran away-I paid
cabs-I was going to pay you for your trouble after completing.
