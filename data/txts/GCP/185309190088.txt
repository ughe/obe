1029. EDWARD BUTLER, feloniously casting and throwing John
Thomas out of a certain steam vessel into the Thames, with intent to
murder him. Messrs. ROBINSON and LILLEY conducted the Prosecution.
John Thomas. I am acting mate to a steam boat which plys between
Woolwich and the North Woolwich Station. On Saturday night, 2016
Aug., we left Woolwich on our last journey, at 11 o'clock, and got over
about 10 minutes past 11 o'clock-the passengers had all gone ashore
except about half a dozen, when I observed the prisoner leaning on the aft-
companion went up to him, and asked him to go ashore ; be turned
round, and said he was not going-1 told him he must go, for we had done,
and were not going back any more-I left him standing there-Captain
Allen gave me orders to moor the vessel, and I went to throw the stern rope
out to do so-while I was in the act of mooring the vessel, the prisoner came
behind me, and put me right over the vessel-he chucked me over the rail
at the stern of the vessel ; as I fell, I caught hold of the lower rail with my
left hand, and so saved myself from falling into the water-the prisoner then
knocked my fingers with his fist, and tried to make me let go, and tried to
lift my fingers up-I called out to Captain Allen for assistance ; he came,
and took hold of my legs, and said, "All right! I have got hold of your
legg" - I could not have held on any longer if Allen had not come-the
water is about twenty feet deep there-it was nearly high water-Allen
pelled me on board, and the prisoner was given in charge-he had been
drinking, but he knew perfectly well what he was about - I went to the
station with him-before he was taken, he caught hold of Allen by the hair
of his head, and said, “You b r , I should like to drown you for saving
the other"-the stern of the vessel from which I was thrown was from ters
to fifteen feet from the pier.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'Brien. Q. The prisoner was a perfect stranger
to you till that night, was he not? A. Yes; we never had any dispute or
quarrel—the side of the vessel I was at was next to the pier--my feet touched
the water--they were wet-Captain Allen stooped down, leant over, and took
hold of my legs-he was on the pier when it happened, and when he heard
me cry out he ran on board—there was no one on board but the prisoner-
do not know how many there were on the pier- I was making the rope fast,
and did not notice.
Jury. Q. You say you asked the prisoner to go ashore ; what did you
say to him? A. I tapped bim on the shoulder, and said, "Now if you
please for the shore "-it was done in a civil manner-he answered, "I am
b- d if I am going ashore."
