Ransom's bank—I said, "I shall give you in charge on a case of forgery on Messrs. Ransom's bank"—he said to the officer, "It is all right, where am I to go to?"—his companion walked off towards the Edgware-road—the prisoner did not say anything till he was taken to Marylebone-lane station—he there abused me very much, called me a scoundrel and a vagabond, and made use of all sorts of abusive names—when he was taken he said he did not know where the bank was himself, and he did not know where Stanhope-street was—he was taken by the constable, in my company, to No. 16, Stanhope-street, the same evening by my desire—I had a very slight doubt about his being the person at the time when I took him—when we got to No. 16, Stanhope-street, we first saw the servant who had previously opened the door to me—the policeman asked if Mrs. Hall was at home—I think that was the name; and the prisoner said to the servant, before he allowed the police man to speak, "Am I the man that took your lodging?"—she said, "If you are not him you are his brother"—I cannot say what observation he made in reply to that, but he called me a d—vagabond, and held up his stick to me—the policeman said he must call in another constable to assist if he did not be quiet—the servant girl then feinted away—in looking at the prisoner now I. have not the least doubt that he is the person who handed me the cheque.
