HORACK CHARLES WARREN (prisoner, not on oath) addressed the jury
at length, saying that he started this business by getting winners for
Pringle from a young man he knew, and one evening Pringle asked
him to give him a letter back which he had posted to Fensom, but be
(Warren) would not do it. That was at the beginning of the flat-racing
season. He then related how he stamped the letter and put it on the
stamping table on June 3. He did not stamp the letter on May 20,
nor copy any horses from the newspapers, only from slips that went
handed to him.
Verdict, Guilty.
The manager of Lloyds Bank at Enfield said that he had know
Pringle for about 10 years, and he had been employed as cleaner &
the bank for 17 years; he had found him most exemplary in character.
Ho received 10s. a week from the bank.
Sentence: Each prisoner, nine months' hard labour.
The Recorder said he thought that some steps should be taken by
the General Post Office to acquaint bookmakers with the rule against
betting in the postal servioe, because the experience in that Coun
was that most of the cases from the Post Office arose through diff-
culties men got into over betting, rather than drinking.
WILLIAMS, George (55, coachman); MACKRILL, Alfred (27,
labourer); SMITH, William (26, labourer); BROOKS, Charles (22,
labourer); Smith and Brooks being found by night having in their
possession, without lawful excuse, certain implements of house-break-
ing; Smith and Williams burglary in the dwelling-house of John
Compston and stealing therein two overcoats and other articles, the
property of the said John Compston; Mackrill feloniously receiving
one of the said overcoats well knowing it to have been stolen. Smith
pleaded guilty of the burglary; Brooks of being in possession of
housebreaking implements.
Mr. H. F. Cornes prosecuted.

Streatham, said wat on the night of June 23 she locked up, and next
morning found the morning room had been broken into through the
window and two overcoats stolen (produced). A ladder was left out-
side the window. Some silver articles were also taken, value £17 10..
