WILLIAM WEBSTER. This is a bad shilling-a bad shilling is gritty
when put between the teeth, on account of the tin which is mixed with
it-you could not make a sufficient impression with your teeth on a good
one to make any sound-it would be much easier to cut a bad shilling in
a machine like that referred to than a good one-it depends what force
is used-you might out a good one right through if you used consider.
able force--if it were cut easily, without force, I should say it was
counterfeit.
