Mrs. Dryden then went with me on the Wednesday to Mrs. Lindfield, to Prior-place, East-street, Walworth, a herb shop. Mrs. Dryden said, 'I have got a friend of mine come to see you.' Mrs. Lindfield, without asking any questions, said, 'I will take her up-stairs directly,' and shortly after did so, and told me to lay on the bed on my left side; she then performed an operation by some instrument, which had some holes at the end. I went again on the 9th, she used the instru ment again, saying, 'It does not come so freely from you.' I slept at Mrs. Dryden's that night. I paid her, Mrs. Lindfield, 2 l . 10 s ., the first day I went, and gave Mrs. Dryden a few shillings for her trouble in taking me to Mrs. Lindfield. I went to Mrs. Lindfield again on the following Monday, the 11th, when she again used the instrument, when I came home and became ill. I told Mrs. Lindfield my father thought I was ill, and she said, 'Nonsense, you are all right.' Richard Orpin promised to pay the 2 l . 10 s ., but has not done so. JAMES BENSON WILSON. I am the father of the deceased; her name was Eliza—she lived with me at Norwood—she was single, and nearly thirty-two years old—she was ill, and died early in the morning of 22d Sept.—I saw her after death—I had seen her alive on the Thursday night—she first became ill on 12th Sept.—previous to that she had tolerably good health—about a week before she was taken ill she went out, and was out one night and part of two days—I do not know where she went—when she became ill, Mr. Chapman, a surgeon, was sent for, and he attended her till her death. JAMES WILSON. I am brother of deceased and son of the last witness, and live with him at Norwood.
