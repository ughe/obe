3419. THOMAS COOPER was indicted for a misdemeanour.
Messrs. SCARLETT and Ellis conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM HOWARD Lono. I am the son of James Long, and drive a
cab for him. On the 20th of September I was in Tottenham-court-road
--the prisoner and one Owen came to me and asked what I would
take them to the Angel at Islington for-I told him for 1s.--they then
got in us we went along, the prisoner asked if I had got change for
half-a-crown-I said no he then said, "What will you take me to the
City-road-bridge for?” I said "181." he said, “Then give me
1.," which I did, and put the half-crown into my pocket-I drove
to the Angel, and Owen got out-I drove the prisoner on to the City-rond-
bridge-he got out, walked a little way, and then ran, which made me look
at the half-crown-I thought it was bad- I had no other-I bit it, took
it home, and put it on the mantel-shell-next morning when I came home,
my mother said it was a bad one, and gave it to the officer.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How long elapsed between
your receiving the half-crown and producing it to any one! 4. Two days
-I had been out with my cab in that time, but I did not take the
half-crown with me I had it in my pocket the day after I bad six fares
That day, but I am sure I did not take a half-crown-it is very seldom I
take one-I said before the Justice that I did not exactly know Cooper,
but I thought he was the man I had never seen the prisoner before.
MR. ELLIS, Q. When did you mark the half-crown with your teeth? 14.
The same night, before I took the other fares- it was taken to a butcher's
shop, and marked after it came from there I had marked it before it went.
ELIZABETH LONG. I am the mother of the last witness-be came home,
on the 20th of September, with a bad half-crown, and laid it on the mantel-
shelf—it laid there till next morning, when I sent my eldest son to fetch me
In some errands-he came back immediately with it-it was cut on the
side when it came back I saw no mark on it before it went-I did not
examine it--I laid it on the mantel-shelt again till dinner-time, when I
gave it to my son William, and told him it was bad.
