 Welch's Defence. I can prove I was in bed at the time; when I was taken, the boy said he did not know me, except seeing me in the highway with two girls, on Sunday morning - Prendergass took him aside, then came to me, and said, "You are identified; the boy says you had top boots on, a long jacket, and was carrying four cheeses;" the boy could not be off knowing me, for I have stood in Princes-street four years - Prendergass said he was satisfied it was not me, and if he could have seen Gipsy, he should not have taken me.
 Lewis and Walker both stated they could prove an alibi.
 ANN MARTIN. My husband is a labourer at the London-docks. On the Sunday before Christmas-day, at five o'clock in the morning, Walker called on his uncle, who lodges with me, in Wells-place, Goodman's-fields; he wanted to go to Woolwich, to see his aunt, and they went out together in five minutes, to the Windmill public-house; I went to the Windmill at seven o'clock, and saw them there - it is about a quarter of mile from our house; I staid there with them till half-past eight - he then came home with us, and stopped at my house till twelve o'clock, and then went home.
 COURT. Q.What enables you to say it was the 20th of December? A. I had a little boy lying ill, and have the doctor's bill to prove it.
 JOHN MARTIN. Walker's uncle lodges with me. On the Sunday before Christmas-day, I and his uncle went with him to the Windmill, about twenty minutes after five o'clock, and staid there till about half-past eight - he then went to my house; he has an aunt and uncle at Woolwich, named Haines.
 COURT. Q. What took you to a public-house at five o'clock on Sunday morning? A. We went to have something to drink - one of our young ones was ill, and we got up early - they did not go to Woolwich, as it was too late, and they had rather too much to drink; they returned to my house at half-past eight o'clock, and staid there till nearly two o'clock, as Walker was rather in liquor.
 Q.When did you attend here for the first time? A. This is the first time; I was was not desired to come before- nobody has asked me to come now; this is the first time his uncle or my wife have come - I do not know who desired us to come; I heard he was in prison a day or two after he was taken - I have not seen him in prison; I heard somebody say he was going to be tried to-day.
   SIMEON COATES. Walker is my nephew; I live at Martin's. On the Sunday before Christmas day, Walker came there about five o'clock in the morning, with the intention of going to Woolwich - I went to the Windmill with him and Martin, and staid there from half-past five o'clock till nine o'clock, I suppose; I do not wear a watch - we then returned to Martin's - there was a child ill there; I believe Walker did not go to Woolwich.
 COURT. Q. Were you going to Woolwich? A. We intended to go that morning; I am a journeyman bricklayer - his uncle Harris married my sister; we sat drinking till nine o'clock - I do not know the landlord's name, it had been shut up before he took it; we got rather in liquor, and went home - Walker staid at Martin's till about twelve o'clock; I then went with him to his own place, helives in Albion-street - I have been here every day since Thursday last; when I heard he was in custody, I said to Martin, "Why he was with me at the house, and it cannot be him" - Martin and his wife came here with me, on Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and yesterday.
 ELIZABETH MORRIS. I am single, and live in Thomas-street New-road, St. George's in the East; my father is a copper-smith - Welch lodges with us; I let him in on the Saturday night before Christmas-day, between half-past eleven and twelve o'clock; he slept at home - I heard him come down about a quarter-past nine in the morning, when I was in the back-room down stairs - I knew him by a particular cough which he has; I saw him about ten o'clock, in the New-road.
 COURT. Q. When he came down, he did not come into your room? A. No; our house is about five minutes walk from Brown's - nobody could come in or out without my hearing them; I got up about seven o'clock - my father got up about six o'clock, and went out; the prisoner is always at home on Sunday morning - I did not notice this more than any other Sunday, but I heard his father talking to him; my father is ill, and not able to attend - my sisters were up, but neither of them are here.
 THOMAS WELCH. The prisoner Welch is my son, and lodged at the house of Morris - he slept with me the Saturday night before Christmas; he was in bed all night, got up at half-past eight o'clock, and left the house at a quarter-past nine.
 COURT. Q.Where did he breakfast that morning? A. I believe at Allen's, in the highway - he is a carman; he came home at half-past eleven o'clock on Saturday night - I was in bed then; I have been here every day since the Session began.
 ISABELLA WHITE. My husband is a coal-whipper, we live at Shadwell - I know Lewis. On Saturday night, the 19th of December, at twelve o'clock, he came up with John, (our landlord's boy) to bed, at the house I lodge at - he lodges at the same house, on the same stairhead; I asked John if I should take down some things which I had hung in his room? He said No, it was only George Lewis; Lewis said, "Don't go away in the morning without me, I am going down the river with you in the boat" - I saw him again on the Sunday morning, at two minutes past seven o'clock, and went to call the boy up to open the shutters; I did not see him after that.
 COURT. Q.How was Lewis dressed that morning? A. In a black coat.
   EDWARD WILMOT. I live in Back-lane, St. George's in the East. On the Sunday morning in question, about half-past nine o'clock, Welch came to my father's, and staid there till I got up; we then went and breakfasted at the Albion, in Denmark-street.
 COURT. Q. When did you first come here? A. Yesterday; Welch's father asked me to come.
