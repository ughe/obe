FRIEND  , John (34, labourer), and LUCK  , Thomas (42, baker and confectioner); both stealing a mare, the goods of Frank William Brown , and feloniously receiving same; both stealing a cart, the goods of John Dunn Halliday , and feloniously receiving same; both stealing a pony, a cart, and two sets of harness, the goods of Frederick Henderson , and feloniously receiving same; both stealing a gelding, the goods of Richard James Keen , and feloniously receiv ing same.
 Mr. J. Wells Thatcher and Mr. P. B. Petrides prosecuted; Mr. Forrest Fulton defended Luck. FRANK WILLIAM BROWN, Folly Farm, Folly Lane, Chingford. I had a bay mare at grass on July 11 in a field opposite my house. On the morning of the 12th, at 4.30, the mare was not there. It was daylight then. I hunted all round the field, and not finding it reported it missing. A chestnut gelding belonging to Mr. Richard Keen was also missing. On August 3 I went to Dorking, where I saw the chestnut gelding in Luck's van. Luck himself was driving the horse. I waited about two hours to see if I could see the other one. I afterwards spoke to Luck as he was coming out of a police-house, and asked him where the mare was. I said to him, "You had two horses down here on July 12?" He said, "Yes." I said, "I have seen one of the horses in your van this afternoon. Can you tell me where the other one is?" He wanted to know who I was and I told him that the two horses which came down on the 12th were stolen, and I was the owner. After I had satisfied him as to who I was he told me he had sold it to a baker company. I asked him if it was anywhere in the town, and he said, "No; it is at Bromley, in Kent." He told me he had sold the mare for £35, and that a man named Smith had sold the animals to him. He said he did not know Smith's address just then, but he could find him, and that he had given £9 for the mare and gelding and two ponies in exchange. On August 4 I went to Bromley with Detective-sergeant Phipps and Mr. Keen and identified the mare. I had no doubt about her whatever. After that we went to Dorking, where Mr. Keen identified his horse.
 Cross-examined. Reen's was a striking-looking horse, easily re  cognisable. When I first saw it at Dorking it was being driven in a baker's van with Luck's name upon it. The police had ascertained that two horses were consigned from London Bridge to Dorking on July 12. Luck made no concealment about it. The first I knew of the whereabouts of my horse was from information given me by Luck himself. I do not suggest that he refused to give me Smith's address. Luck offered to go to the police station with me. There was nothing the matter with the horse when I lost it. RICHARD JAMES KEEN, Cherry Farm, Chingford. I put the gold ing out to grass on July 10, and it was missing on the morning the 12th. I next saw it on August 4 at Luck's stables. I value it at £35.
