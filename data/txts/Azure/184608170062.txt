WILLIAM HOGAN. I am a carman, carrying on business in Foster-street,
Sun-street, Bishopsgate. I was in company with Shrimpton on the 1st of
Aug.—we called at Mr. Deal's, in Camomile-street—we remained there ten
minutes or rather better—I left first—I left Shrimpton there—we met a little
before three, and went to Camomile-street.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Where did you meet? A. In Bishopsgate-street,
little before three o'clock—I did not look at the clock, or at my watch—I
know it was a little before three, because I was going on a little business, to
receive money—I am a carman to anybody—I consider Mr. Shrimpton a
very honest man—I have been in his debt very largely, and he bas been kind
to me.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Do you keep a cart and horse? A. Yes—I have
two carts and a van—I have carried on the business fifteen years, but not in'
the same place—I lived with Mr. Ashby, in London-wall, several years—r
have known Shrimpton, and his father before him.
THOMAS SHEIMPTON. I am the prisoner's brother—we carry on business
together at No. 35, Curtain-road, as coach and cart wheelwrights—the busi-
ness used to be my father's—l have carried on business there for thirty yean
—my brother has lived at home all that time, ever since he left school—he
assisted in his father's business, and has since become partner with me—our
business is a pretty good one—the house is our freehold—my father died
three years ago—the keys whieh have 'been produced came from a country
house at Wanstead, where my father used to live, and where he died—I was
in company with my brother on the 1st of Aug.—I did not go out with him
—I met him—I went with him to a public-house about five Or six minutes
before three—we left about ten minutes or a quarter after three—my brother
left me—I do not know which way he went—I heard on the Sunday mom.'
ing that he was given into custody—I and all the witnesses attended at the
Mansion-house, and told this story to the Magistrate—sometimes my brother
went out céllecting as well as myself, and when he did not go out collecting,
he was always at work in his business.
MR. PAYNE. Q. How long before that were you without speaking. to
him? A. We had been friendly for three or four weeks—he had not done
me out of 301.—what money he received was as much his as mine—he had
received a sum of money, which caused us not to be friendly—I cannot tell
whether it was 301.—he had not robbed me of anything—it was receiving
money for a new cart—it might have been more than 301.—we had not
