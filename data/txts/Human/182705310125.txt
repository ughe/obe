 EIGHTH DAY. FRIDAY, JUNE 8.
 Third London Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1343.  ELIZABETH JORDAN was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of June , 1 watch, value 2l. 10s.; 1 seal, value 1l. 1s.; 1 watch-key, value 10s.; 1 gold ring, value 10s.; and 1 watch-ribbon, value 1d., the goods of Charles Wales , from his person.
 CHARLES WALES. I am a  painter, and live with my father, in Gravel-lane. About two o'clock in the morning of the 2d of June, I was returning from the theatre - I had had some refreshment, but I was sober; the prisoner accosted me on Ludgate-hill and asked me to treat her; I asked what she meant, she said, "Give me something to drink," I walked to the corner of Bridge-street, and stood talking with her for two or three minutes. I saw her with my watch in her hand, and she put it into her bosom. I accused her of it - the watchman came up and took her to the watch-house.
   WILLIAM SINGLETON. I am a watchman. I saw the prisoner and prosecutor together, talking, at the corner of Bridge-street, the prosecutor had hold of her, and asked for his watch. I took her to the watch-house; the officer found it in her bosom.
   JOSEPH STRIBLEHILL. I was officer of the night - The prisoner was brought to the watch-house in custody; the prosecutor appeared sober; I searched the prisoner - this watch was in her bosom.
   HENRY BOLTON. I was watch-house-keeper. I saw the prisoner take this watch from her bosom; she had been charged with stealing it, and denied it.
 Prisoner's Defence. He went home with me, and afterwards to several public-houses - he gave me his watch as a pledge; I refused to return it, and he called the watchman.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Life.
 1344.  WILLIAM PAINTER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of Richard Tooley Chapman , from his person.
 RICHARD TOOLEY CHAPMAN. I am a  surgeon, and live in Bedford-place, Commercial-road. I was in the Justice-room at the Mansion-house on business, on the 15th of May, about half-past one o'clock, and felt a hand coming round to my watch. I put my hand to protect it, and in a short time I felt a hand at my pocket. I put my hand down and missed my handkerchief. I turned round and saw the prisoner putting his hat on his head, in which I saw a handkerchief. I said "You have my handkerchief;" he said "I have not." I said "I know you have, and I insist upon seeing it, if not, I will call the Marshalman." I put my hand to his hat, and he showed me a silk hand - kerchief, and said "Is this yours." I said No - mine is a cotton one." I called the Marshalman, and the case was heard - this is the handkerchief - it was in his hat.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. On what business had you gone there? A. To make an affidavit; the prisoner was behind me; I felt a hand at my pocket, but I cannot tell what hand it was; there was a crowd of people, some before and some behind me; I turned round, and the prisoner was putting his hat upon his head; no person could have stooped behind me; there was not room - it was not a pressure behind me, it was a fumbling; it might have been done by a person raising his hand up from the ground, but the prisoner was upright when I saw him; he might have stopped and raised a hand from the ground, but I should think he could not.
 JURY. Q. Did he deny having it? A. Yes; and he told the Lord Mayor that I said the silk handkerchief was mine - I said I did not; the cotton one was mine - he told the Lord Mayor he picked it up, but he could not, there was not room.
 MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. But how do you known he could not have stooped? A. Because the people were so pressed together; he had a silk handkerchief of his own and mine in the bottom of his hat; his handkerchief covered mine; I took mine out of his hat; it has never happened to me to feel any thing under my feet, and to mistake another handkerchief for my own; he, I understand, had been a person in a respectable situation in life, but has left his family.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I found this handkerchief on the floor, and took it up; the prosecutor turned round and took hold of my handkerchief, and said, "This is mine" - I said, "It is not" - he then took hold of the one I had found, and said, "This is mine" - he then gave me in charge.
 GUILTY. Aged 32. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury, having received an excellent character. Confined Eighteen Months.
 1345.  JOHN MITCHELL was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of Richard Hodgson Galloway , from his person.
 RICHARD HODGSON GALLOWAY. I am an  engineer, and live in West-street, Smithfield. I was in Field-lane, a quarter before seven o'clock in the evening of the 19th of May; I felt something all at once pulling at my pocket - I turned round and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand, and he shuffled it up his coat - I pushed him against a shop, and accused him of it; he said he knew nothing of it, but he dropped it in two or three minutes, and I collared him, and took him to the watch-house; he had not run; I saw the handkerchief in his hand, and saw him drop it.
 Prisoner. Q. Was there not another gentleman with you? A. Yes, my brother - and there was a boy with the prisoner, and I saw the handkerchief shifting from that boy to the prisoner: my belief is, that the other picked my pocket and passed it to the prisoner - I seized the prisoner, and put him up against a shutter, that in case he dropped it, I might see it - I believe I did state at Guildhall, that the boy handed the handkerchief to him; the other was nearer to me; I know the boy's face; I have seen him there.
