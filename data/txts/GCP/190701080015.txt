OLD COURT; Wednesday, January 9.
(Before the Recorder.)
JOEL, Maurice (22, porter); stealing & post letter containing
match-box, the property of the Postmaster-General, he being em-
ployed under the Post Office.
SO
Mr. Forster Boulton, M.P., prosecuted; Mr. C. 8. Green defended.
Elste TAPPER, housemaid to Dr. Newbold, Child Oakford, Bland-
ford, Dorset. I bought the metal match-box (produced) at Westcotts,
5, Market Place, Blandford, on December 19, 1906, for 3s., in a card-
board box packed in red wadding, and directed it to Walter Ord,
private soldier at Gibraltar, with brown paper wrapped round it. I
recognise the match-box and the fragments of the box and the brown
paper wrapping (produced), which contain my writing on both. It
was tied with string, and had the label (produced) attached to it, and
I delivered same to Davies, gardener to Dr. Newbold, to be posted.
Cross-examined. I saw the part of the box and the label op Do-
cember 24, and the metal match-box yesterday when I came to London.
It was empty when sent off. I chose it at Westcotts from a number
of other match-boxes of similar design the others were not quite the
same. The one I sent was exactly like that (produced)—I recognise
it by the leaves on it. I had never bought any other match-boxes.
JAMES DAVIES, gardener to Dr. Newbold. The last witness cave
me a postal packet done up in brown: paper on December 19, 1906,
which I posted a little before six p.m., the post leaving at six p.m.
GEORGE WALDON, inspector, Foreign Section, G.P.O. Prisoner was
employed as auxiliary porter generally in the collection of corre-
spondence already sorted at St. Martin's-le-Grand, and on Decem-
ber 20 came on duty at 4.30 a.m. A letter posted at Blandford be-
fore six p.m. would generally reach the General Post Office between
five and 5.30 a.m. the next day. On that morning it arrived at 4.54
8.m. I was watching the prisoner, and at seven a.m. saw him throw the
parcel tab or label (produced) down on to the table. I was about
four yards from him. I picked it up. The postmark and date are
not legible. If a broken parcel comes into the office it is the duty
of the official who finds it to put it in a box specially provided for
the purpose.
