WILLIAM JOSEPH JONES. I am counting-house superintendent to Mr.
Whiteley, and have the general supervision of orders given as to credit
and so on-the assistants come to me-the goods in question were
supplied by Mr. Whiteley's firm to Mrs. Oopsey--the principal part of
the goods came from the department where Mr. Palmer was engaged
he is not in our service now-we first had a communication about these
goods in March, and I saw Mrs. Copsey a few days after, when she came
and said she wanted some furniture, and gave a reference to Messrs.
Micklethwaite and Co., her solicitors, in Long Acre she came again,
and Mr. Palmer told her the reference was not satisfactory--the arrange-
ment was for a cash transaction, and she paid 1007. on account--a cash
transaction with us means that the money has to be paid when the
goods are delivered, or as soon after as possible very shortly after she
was to pay another 501.- the 1001. was paid on account of goods already
delivered and in course of manufacture she paid the other 507. on
15th May from that time we received no further payment about
10th June we received a notice of a petition having been filed.bi olan
Cross-examined. There was no one with Mrs. Oopsey when I saw her
the first time, I am certain I did not make any inquiry about Messrs.
Micklethwaite and 00.- I did not ask her whether she was married
-We should ask that question if it were a credit transaction, but it was
understood this was to be cash-she was to make payment on account
from time to time I call it a cash transaction--we should take 100%.
down to supply goods to the amount of 6001, if we got another 507. and
a promise of further substantial payment--we took her promise-Palmer
told me she was married-she gave the address 16, Brammerton Street
-I saw her daughter once when Mrs. Oopsey paid the 501., and I have
heard of Miss Copsey-I knew she was an actress I found it out about
a month after-Copsey is not a common name-invoices would be sent
with the majority of things--they were made out to Mrs. Oopsey-the
1001. down impressed us, and the promise of substantial payment.
