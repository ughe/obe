The prisoner and the other man came and looked over the things - the other man said, "Put them into your bag, and go;" the prisoner did so, the other pulled off his own coat, and fitted on another - he then said he had no money; I said I could not let the clothes go without - I pushed past the other man to go after the prisoner; the other prevented me, and held the gate to - I snatched it out of his hand, and ran to the top of the street; the prisoner set off running -I called Stop thief! he was pursued, and stopped - I never lost sight of him; I asked him to give me the clothes, and after some persuasion he gave me them, and followed me back to the house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Then he might have ran away? A. No; the officer was close behind him - I was not present at the first bargain about them; I had not made a new bargain with the other man to let him have them for 25s., nor consented that the prisoner should have one of the articles for the 5s.; he was taken to the office - he was not allowed to offer the 25s. to me.
   JABEZ WOOD. I saw the witness running after the prisoner, and I stopped him - he did not say any thing.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined Three Months.
 943.  WILLIAM COOKE was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 pocket-book, value 5s., the goods of Myer Nathan , from his person.
   MYER NATHAN. I was in Hart-street, Covent-garden, on the 15th of February, about four o'clock in the afternoon, and my pocket-book was extracted from my pocket- I turned round, and saw the prisoner running; I ran, and overtook him without losing sight of him - my pocketbook was handed to me from a cellar.
   JOHN WILLIAM LLOYD. I was going through Covent-garden market. I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner walking very close behind him - I heard the prosecutor cry Stop thief! I turned round, and the prisoner passed me - I made an attempt to catch him, but missed him; I saw the pocket-book then in his hand - he stooped down, and threw it into a cellar; I saw it handed up very shortly afterwards, and the prosecutor claimed it.
 Prisoner. I leave it to the Jury.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 First Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 944.  HENRY WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of February , 25 handkerchiefs, value 7l., 3 pairs of socks, value 2s., and 3 slippers, value 3s., the goods of Margaret Dixon and another, in the dwelling-house of Mary Clark and another.
 ELIZABETH CLARK. I am Mary Clark's sister-in-law This happened at a public-house; the landlord died on the 5th of January -  Mary Clark and his brother then had charge of the house, which is at Knightsbridge; Mary Clark's husband's name is Thomas - this property belonged to a person who had come in; I did not know it was stolen till afterwards.
   JOHN ROYLE. I am porter to Margaret Dixon, who lives in Cockspur-street. I had this property in a parcel to take to Sloane-street, on the 20th of February - I went into the Queen's Head, at Knightsbridge, about two o'clock; I sat in the tap-room, nearly three quarters of an hour, with the parcel in my possession; I then went to the bar to speak to Clarke; I returned in two minutes, and the parcel was gone from the table where I had left it; I could hear nothing of it in the house - I cannot swear the prisoner was there; this was Saturday, and on the Tuesday I found it at a pawnbroker's.
   WILLIAM CHURCH. I am an officer. On the 22nd, in consequence of information, I went to the prisoner's lodgings, and found this duplicate under a chimney ornament, on the mantel-piece - he was then in custody; I knew he lodged there, for I saw him there on Sunday, the 21st, about another case - he wasthen with me.
   JOHN KILLINGWORTH. I am servant to Mr. Perkins, pawnbroker, of King's-road, Chelsea. I have a piece of silk pawned for 12s. on the 20th of February - I have a recollection of having seen the prisoner before, but am not quite positive he is the man; this duplicate is what I gave the person.
   JOHN BUCKERIDGE. I am in the employ of Bell and Co., silk-manufacturers. I know this silk to be their make, and believe it to be part of the goods sent to Dixon and Evans from our house; this invoice is what I sent with the goods.
   JOHN ROYLE. I have every reason to believe this to be part of the contents of the parcel; it had come for Bell and Co. - I lost the parcel about a quarter to three o'clock.
   WILLIAM JAMES WILSON. On Saturday, the 20th of February, the prisoner came to my master's shop in Piccadilly, for some black varnish - I am a brass-finisher; he took off his hat and took a piece of black silk from it- I asked him what it was for; he said for a handkerchief, and said I should have one for 5s. - I said I did not want it; he put it into his hat again - I knew him before; he was groom to Mr. Murray; it was about four o'clock.
 Prisoner's Defence. Mr. Murray pays us at this public-house, and we run a weekly score there; I had a post of half-and-half there that day from our foreman - I had a black silk handkerchief in my hut, with two 1d. loaves and a piece of cheese; my hat fell off in the street - I picked up the handkerchief at the time. GUILTY (of stealing only.) Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
