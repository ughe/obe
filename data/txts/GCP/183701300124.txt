613. WILLIAM NEWTON was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of
January, 2 shillings, the monies of Charles Milledge, from the person of
Susannah Milledge.
EMMA MILLEDGE. I am the wife of Charles Milledge. I sent my
daughter, on the 23rd of January, to pledge a silk handkerchief for 21. a
Mr. Harris's I waited-she did not return, and I found the 2s. were gone.
SUSANNAX MILLEDOR. I took the silk handkerchief to the pows
broker's, and pawned it for 2s.--I was coming out, and met the prisoner-
I did not know him before-there was another boy with bim-he asked if
I would buy any pictures--I said, "No"-he offered to give me one, and
asked if I had not pawned a silk handkerchief for 23.-I said, "Yes"-he
said, "Let me look if the money is good" he took it out of my hand, and
then sounded it on the steps, and sounded it on a board inside a shop,
and then he said he had lost one-he seemed to look for it, and then said
he had lost them both, he then told the other little boy to go home,
and he went-a lady held him till the policeman came, but they did not
find the 2s.
JAMES GREEN (police-constable N 214.) I was called to take the pri-
soner-I searched him at the station-house, and found on him a lot of printe
he told me they belonged to the other boy, Bill Lloyd-he said they had
both run away from Perry's workhouse that morning.
GUILTY. Aged 12.-Confined Three Weeks; One Week Solitary.

614. FREDERICK MEASURE was indicted for stealing, on the 18th
of January, 2 flutes, value 41., the goods of William Bong and another.
JAMES HALL. I am employed by the executor of the late Mr. Chapman
-We deal in musical instruments. On the 30th of January the prisoner
came to the shop-he wanted a trumpet, and wanted to exchange a fute
for it-I did not like that, and he produced a second flute I saw the
name of Boag" on them I made an excuse, and he left them till the
morning, and in the mean time I let Mr. Boag know-when he came
the next morning I said the flutes were stolen from Mr. Boag, and called an
officer-I gave the flutes to Mr. Bong-these are them.
GEORGE COLLIER (police-constable E 38.) I took the prisoner--these
flutes I received from Mr. Boag.
WILLIAM BOAG. I deal in musical instruments. Both these flutes are
mine-they were safe about a fortnight ago and; last Monday Hall gave
me notice, I missed them then-the prisoner has been apprenticed to.
neighbour of mine, next door but one, for seven yearshe was out of
time this day fortnight, I think-he has only had access to my premises by
coming with messages occasionally.
