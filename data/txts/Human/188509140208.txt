The conduct of this case was placed in my hands, in consequence of which I went on 13th August to 21, Noel Street, with Detective Howell, about 3 p.m.—I saw Barnard and Launet—I said "We are police officers, I am to arrest Barnard on a warrant for having defrauded Mr. Taicht"—I said to Mr. Taicht, who was with us, "Point out to me Barnard," and he pointed to Launet—I said to Launet "Are you Barnard?" he said "No, my name is Launet"—Barnard then said "I am Barnard"—I then said "I hold a warrant against you"—I read the warrant to him in French—he said "We did not write these letters to the people to tell them to come over to London, they ought to have remained in France"—MM. Sindoux Dutap, and Saumi came in and said "We have come for our situation, we sent you the 10 francs, and wish to enter it"—I asked them to point out Barnard to me: they pointed to Launet—I said to Launet "I shall take you also in custody for conspiring with Barnard to defraud M. Taicht out of 10 francs"—I then took them to Marlborough Street Police-station and charged them—I fouod upon Barnard a pocket-book containing a postal order for 10 francs, addressed to 21A, Noel Street—at 52, Great New man Street I found several letters addressed to Barnard; also bill-heads of Bourg and Co., 222, Lancaster Road—we then returned to Noel Street and searched, and found 136 certificates, over 2,000 letters, a quantity of bill-heads in the name of Barnard, Charles Linden, and Durant—a number of the bill-heads had on them a printed stereotyped letter, and there were various forms addressed to ladies and to gentle men, one to a lady in the name of Beaurant and Co.—I also found some ( account ) books, a printing press, and rather new type—from about 9.30 to 10 I went with Howell to 58, Radnor Street, Chelsea—in answer to inquiries a woman replied, and we had to wait a considerable time—when Linden arrived I said "What is your name, Linden?"—he said "Yes;" I said "We are police officers, and are going to arrest you for conspiring with Barnard and Launet to defraud M. Taicht out of 10 francs"—he said "None of these persons can produce a document that we asked them to come to London"—I said "Is not your real name Brillon?" he denied it, hesitated, and then said "Yes"—I said "Under what name are you going now?" he said "Linden, of 59, 60, and 61, Hatton Garden; here are my keys, if you go there you will find a clerk to-morrow morning"—I went and found a portfolio containing a foreign newspaper with an advertisement in it, also a quantity of papers addressed to Barnard and Co., also these two books, in which are entries of receipts for post-office orders, all for 7 s . 8 d ., or 10 francs, and "Sindoux 7 s . 8 d., " and "Dutap" and "Dubourg" for 7 s . 6 d ., "Van den Dries" for 7 s . 8 d ., and "Meurant" for 7 s . 8 d.;  and in the other book "Taicht 10 francs;" I also found this sheet of post-office orders, marked by the prisoners, 65 altogether—I counted between 20th June and 18th July, 125 post-office orders—it was a large office in one house—there was a desk, table, and three chairs; no business books beyond these two—we found a great many letters—from August 14th to 17th they received 230 letters, con taining 13 post-office orders, addressed to Barnard and Linden—we found at Noel Street a book with entries in different places; also Mr. Hind let's name as proprietor of and successor to the business at 31, Old Comp ton
