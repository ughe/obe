1651. GEORGE GOODHALL was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of
May, 82lbs. weight of pork, value 11. 13s. 9d., the goods of Ann Lee.- 2nd
Count, stating it to be the carcass of a pig.-3rd Count, stating it to be the
goods of Henry Daniel Hockley.
Mr. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE LEE. I am the son of Ann Lee, of Newgate-market. On Saturday
morning, the 6th of May, I saw the prisoner go out of the shop with a pig on his
shoulder-I had sold thirteen pigs and three quarters to Mr. Hockley, and the
pig the prisoner had, was one of them--in five or ten minutes I went to make
inquiries I did not know but that the prisoner belonged to Mr. Hockley-1
went to Mr. Taylor's shop, and saw the prisoner there-I said, “What have
you done with the pig?"-he said, "What pig?"-I said, “ The pig you took
from our shop"-he said, "I took it to Mr. Hockley's"-I took the prisoner
to Mr. Hockley's, and asked if he had brought a pig-he said he had not--1
afterwards went to Mr. Silverside's he told me where to find the pig-I
found it, and it was my mother's.
Cross-examined by MR. WYLDE. Q. Was this at six or seven o'clock in
the morning? 4. It was between seven and eight, I believe the prisoner
has never done business at Mrs. Lee's I knew him by seeing him about the
market-I positively swear that when I asked him at Taylor's what he
had done with the pig that he took from our shop, that the words he used
were, "I have taken it to Mr. Hockley's"--I believe Mrs. Taylor was there,
but I do not know that she heard the conversation-I believe there was no
one else there-I do not know whether the prisoner was there when Payne
was there was no cart outside to take these pigs--they were to be taken
to Mr. Hockley's shop in the market-he sells them there I was outside
our shop-I did not go in to see whether there was any one inside.
THOMAS GODSALYE. I am scaleman to Mrs. Lee. On the 6th of May I
weighed thirteen pigs and three quarters, for Mr.Hockley—I saw some of
them fetched away by a man and a boy of Mr. Hockley's- I had called over
the weights correctly, and Mr. Greenwood put them down I have since seen
a pig produced from Mr. Horsefall-I had noticed the pigs which were sold
to Mr. Hockley, sufficiently to say that that was one of them.
Cross-examined. Q. Can you tell the score of all those pigs?. A. We
know them from the dressing-we have a great many from different people,
and know the difference of them-they keep them in cloths.
ROBERT GREENWOOD. I am in Mr. Lee's employ. On the 6th of May
I put down the weight of thirteen pigs and three quarters, from the calling of
Godsalve-here is the weights of them which I gave to Payne.
HENRY DANIEL HOCKLEY. I purchased thirteen pigs and three quarters
at Mrs. Lee's—Payne, who was my servant, brought some pigs home-
made inquiries of him-he produced this list of the weights to me, I found
there was one pig less than there ought to have been, and the weight of it
