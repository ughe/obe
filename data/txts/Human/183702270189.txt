   Corss-examined. Q.  How far was he from the prosecutor's? A.  About 100 yards—he was not running—he walked under a gas light—I went with Warden to his house—when I asked him where he got the copper from, he said he received it from Mr. Moore in lieu of money that he owed him when he left Woolwich, three months before—he was not drunk—I will not say he was sober—you might consider him sober, or not quite sober—he walked beside the constable, and I carried the copper—the constable had not hold of him—I did not see what he was carrying upon his shoulder—I was about ten yards from him—I am certain it was not a bundle of clothes—whether it was a copper or tub I could not tell—he said we should be troubled to prove that he stole the copper from Mr. Lee's. CHARLES GOULD. I went into the wash-house about twenty minutes before nine o'clock—the copper was safe then—I missed it at twenty minutes past ten o'clock—I saw the prisoner there at half-past nine o'clock.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know the difference between a drunken man and a sober one? A.  He rolled about as if he had been intoxicated—at half past nine o'clock he came up to me with his fists, and I said, "Don't come your larks with me, as you are not drunk," and be went into the back yard—I went in for cinders, and saw the copper safe at twenty minutes before nine o'clock—I put the candle on the copper—the lead was sheeted round the copper—I saw the whole of the copper. GUILTY.* Aged 35.— Confined Three Months; Three Weeks Solitary.

 886. HENRY HOLDS WORTH was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of February , 1 shirt, value 6 s  ., the goods of Henry Trowse Stevenson . HENRY TROWSE STEVENSON. I am a labourer in the St. Katharine's Docks, and live in Pell-street, St. George's. The prisoner was brought to sleep with a fellow-lodger, in another bed in the same room—I missed my shirt on Tuesday night, the 7th of February. JAMES MACKSEY. I am a stone-mason. The prisoner slept with me on the night the shirt was lost—the prosecutor slept in another bed—he and I got up, and went to work about half-past six o'clock, and left the prisoner in bed. WIILIAM FORD. I am a private in the Marines at Woolwich—the prisoner is the same. On the 10th of February, he came into the room where I was stopping, and asked who slept in the bed by the side of the cupboard—I said, "No one"—he said, "There is a shirt at that bed; I want you to stow it away in your bag"—I said I would—when he was gone, I told the corporal I believed it was stolen. THOMAS TAYLOR. I am a colour-sergeant in the Marines. I took the shirt from the bed—I represented this to the officer, and went before the Magistrate. WILLIAM DAVIES. I am a constable. I took the prisoner, and accused him of robbing a person of a shirt—he said he knew nothing about it—I fond 5 d . on him. HENRY TROWSE STEVENSON re-examined.  I lost this shirt off a shelf—there was no other left in the room—the prisoner never undressed himself at all—he slept with his clothes on.
