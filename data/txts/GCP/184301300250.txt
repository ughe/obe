that there is justly due from the estate of my late father to the estate of the

Court to Miss CONSTABLE. Q. Take that paper into your hand, and
carefully read the memorandum at the bottom, above the signature ? A. I
read it before the signature is decidedly not my handwriting-if any one
has sworn that he saw me write it at Camberwell, he has sworn falsely—the
words beginning "I have carefully compared," are not my handwriting, nor
any part of it-I never saw the paper before this evening.
Q. Look at the very beginning, and explain what those items mean? A.
(Reading)—"Mount Pleasant House, Lewisham.-Richard Constable, Dr.
to Joseph Stanley. 1838, Feb. 1st, paid for carpets, as per annexed, 302."
- I suppose that item was obtained from Stanley's memory, or perhaps a
bill delivered to him-I should think it was taken from an account that
Stanley kept-he and I bought the carpets together--we went together 10
choose them, and he had 30l. given to him to pay for them, by my father
there was a trifting balance, which he never accounted for the next item is,
"Paid acceptance, 25l., for furniture"- that was paid by a bill, and I have
the acceptance, but not with me it was to a man named Rushton, in the
Kent-road, accepted by my father-"Mr. Buffin, 201., stoves as per bill
301."—they did not cost anything like 301.-he had the money to pay for
them.
Q. Had he any funds of his own, by which he could have paid for these
several things ? A. I have ascertained since, that he had not 18.-he had
nothing—"Radburn, for painting house, 1151."-I paid that man myself
in the house, and Stanley was not present-he bronght the gentleman down,
and gave him a promissory note for the amount, and I paid him in his
presence-I do not know the gentleman's name-- he lived somewhere at
Rotherhithe, and this gentleman was his landlord-"Collett, 401. "-he
was Mr. Abraham Constable's coachman, and received it from my father-
"Hind's bill of costs, 221. 198."-Mr. Hind was the coroner of Sitting-
bourne, and these were the costs accruing from Mr. C. Pratt signing the deed
of reversion, in conjunction with the Thompsons--my father paid that I
was not present-he supplied Stanley with the money to go he could not
have gone without it-“Smith's bill, for bell-banging, 441."- that was paid
by an acceptance of Mr. Davies, the wax-cbandler, in St. Martin's-lane
“Paid an account of furniture bought at Shooter's-hill, 7. 128. 3d."
Mr. Langdon bought the furniture, I believe, with a larger sum than this
we bought a cow and calf, but the sum was 91.-- he has doubled it "Ditto,
dtto, 191."-it was rather an aged cow, and we had it for 91.-"A red cow,
131. 58."-I think we gave about 101. or 111. for it, but Mr. Fluker, I be-
lieve, has the items- By cash, 201., by ditto, 802.," but then it was cash
given to Mr. Stanley not as given my father-"Expenses to Wales, on ac-
count of Richard Constable's affairs, 05.1"' I have the numbers of all the
notes-he received a sum of 300.l., and I gave it him myself_"Sept. 23,
paid an acceptance, on account of Foakes, 311. 78. 6d."---that was an accept-
ance that I went and paid Foakes myself, at the Greyhound, at Croydon, on
Saturday, the day he always calls to collect his rents--he is the land-steward
of Captain Cater, of Tulse-hill-"Mr. Gibbons, carpenter, balance of ac-
