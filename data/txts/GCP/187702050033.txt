Fourth Session, 1876–77.
FOURTH COURT.-Wednesday, February 7th, 1877.
Before Mr. Recorder.
238. EDWARD MAHONEY (21), Robbery with violence on John Franks,
and stealing 71. 108., his monies.
MR. A. B. Kellt conducted the Prosecution
JOHN FRANKS. I live at Chorlotte Street, Fitzroy Square--about 1
o'clock, on the morning of 10th January, I was going home and was near
Charlotte Street, when I saw three men, the prisoner was one ; he bit me
in the back, the second one put bis hand into my mouth, and against my
nose, the third took my purse and went away first, the one who held my
mouth ran away next, and the prisoner last; I fell on the ground I after
wards ran after them calling Police " _I caught the prisoner by the coat,
and gave him in charge there was 71. 108. in my purse.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I was not drunk; I had one glass of ele.
Thomas CRANFIELD (Policeman E 188). I was on duty and heard cries
of " Police "--I ran to the corner of Charlotte Street, and saw the prosecu-
tor holding the prisoner by the arm-he gave him into my custody for
stealing his purse and 71. 105.--I took the prisoner to the station where he
was obarged—the prosecutor was sober.
Cross-examined. I did not hear the proseoutor call you a convict-he
said you tripped him up.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate was that he was coming home
from a music hall, and seeing five or six men round the prosecutor went to
look, and the prosecutor charged him with the theft and with tripping ham up,
and that the prosecutor was very drunk.
Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor said, if he could not get the right
thief he would get one, and at the station he said that I was a regular
convict in order to press the charge. My witness is not here.
Guttry-Nine Months' Imprisonment.
239. ERNEST HARRISON (28), Stealing two sacks of corn, and fifteen
sacks, the goods of John Winn.
MR. C. MATEEB conducted the Prosecution; MR. M. WILLIAMS with M
COOPER WILD the Defence.
THOMAS HARRISON, I am a fire wood cutter, of 11, Romney Street-Iam
the prisoner's father and work for him--a few days before Christmas last,
between 6 and 7 o'clock p.m., two sacks of oorn were brought in to my sons
premises in a little van I did not notice what name was on the
van, but & Mr. Gibson who hawks coals, was driving i. & Mr. Carter,
who works for Mr. Winn, & carman, carried the sacks into an office
where we keep the horses' forage, near the gate of the yard-Gibson
stayed in the van-Carter then asked for 266.-he did not mention my son's
name- I went to my son's wife and got it-she acts as cashier-I gave it
to Carter, and they went away-I said to my son the next day " Have you
ordered any corn p—he said "No; who brought it "-I told him Gibson
and Carter-- he said “They had no business to bring it" -- he also said
"They ought to have taken the sacks away ; I won't touch them"-the
police afterwards took them.
Cross-eramined by MR. WILLIAMS. My son was out when the corn came
-the sacks were not touched till the police came.
