COURT . Q.  Where was she when you first saw her? A.  By the side of the second bridge in the fields—I had helped my young man to take her out—I first saw her in the water. THOMAS TRUNCHEON. On Sunday night, the 2nd of April, at a quarter to eleven o'clock, I found an umbrella and bag, containing two letters, about three yards from where the body was found, about three yards from the canal side—I delivered them to Day, the policeman—I saw them in Day's hands the next morning, and saw him give them to Taylor. GEORGE TAYLOR re-examined.  I have the umbrella and the bag, with the two letters in it—I believe they are the prisoner's handwriting; but can only swear to my belief—I have seen her write many times—the last time was three or four yean ago—I never saw her sign her name.
   COURT . Q.  Have you looked at the letters? A.  Yes; there is some re semblance to her handwriting, but I cannot say. ELIZA RUMBALL. I am the prisoner's sister—this is her bag—the letters are like her writing—I should not like to swear they are her's—I believe them to be so from the appearance—I have seen her write, and believe they are her's.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When did you last see her write? A.  Since she has been in Newgate, some writing that came from her—I believe it was her writing—I have never seen much of her writing—I should not like to sweat that they are her's—I believe it to be her's—( read. )

 "Stamford-hill. Dear mother and sisters,—I own I am very much in  debted to you. I have brought on you an expense which is growing heavy on you. You were kind enough to say you would take him home; I have been thinking there will be much jealousy in your doing so, so I think of ending our lives in the canal, which I hope if George will not see us when alive, he will see us when dead, and hope you will bury us both together, me and my darling boy. I hope you and all my friends will pardon my sins. I am praying, and trying to seek repose in the bosom of God, which I have in vain sought for on earth; my own folly has brought me to this unhappy death. I hope you will think no more of me, but pray that my sins may be forgiven me, and that we may meet again in the next world. I hope you will forgive me. I hope you will all live happy together, my dear sister, you and George. Your dutiful daughter— REBECCA RUMBALL ." ELIZA RUMBALL Cross-examined. Q.  Who is meant by George? A.  My brother-in-law—he had not refused to set her—the prisoner was very fond of the child—it is three years old, and is very fond of her—the father is dead—she has not had much trouble—she did what she could to support the child with her wages.
