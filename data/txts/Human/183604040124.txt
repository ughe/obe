EDWARD M'CARTHY re-examined . When I took the prisoner, he was up-stairs in a bed-room, along with a lot of children, in the adjoining house—I did not notice that he had received any blows at all. GEORGE TEAKLE re-examined . The prisoner had no apprearance of any blows at all—there was never a word said about this before. SARAH RUTTER re-examined . I do not think the prisoner got away blows; for, from what I could hear, he opened the door, gave the prosecutor a knock, and shut the door again immediately. SAMUEL RUTTER. I am the last witness's husband, and am a painter and glazier. I was at home in bed and asleep, and heard my next door neighbour's wife, Mrs. Emmet, I believe—she awoke me—I then heard the prosecutor say, "I am come home in right trim; come out, you b—and I am ready for you"—then I heard a violent knocking at the door—then the door was shut and opened again—I think then the prosecutor said the prisoner had struck him with a hammer—he repeated that over and over twenty times at least—soon after I heard his wife come out—there was a violent knocking at the door, and then the police was called—the prosecutor's wife went and knocked at the prisoner's door, and told him to come out, that she would have his life, and a great deal of that—she was ready for him—they then broke the shutters open at the next door neigh bour's, and took the prisoner out of their house—when he came out he had no shoes on, and he asked the policeman to let him go in and put his shoes on, but they would not let him—then the policeman took him away—he said his shoes were under the children's bed—I believe this is one of the policemen—I was in bed all the time—I did not see any thing—I did not get out of bed, for there had been disturbance before—I said it was a shame we should be disturbed by these people—I think the prosecutor did very wrong—he had no business at the prisoner's door.
   JURY . Q.  Being in bed, could you tell whether it was the prisoner at the prosecutor's door, or the prosecutor at the prisoner's door? A.  I could swear to that man's voice from a thousand—the prisoner lives nearly opposite, and the prosecutor lives next door to me—the street in seven or eight yards wide—I thought the prosecutor was in liquor by his tongue that night—the prisoner is not a quarrelsome character. I think, when sober. NOT GUILTY.
