   Q.  Where were you between nine and ten o'clock on Friday? A.  At my own house—I cannot answer where I was on Thursday—I do not now where I was on Wednesday, but at home, I dare say—my husband not in London—I knew nothing of this till the night before I started—I started on Thursday—no one applied to me to come up here—my aunt, and my husband, and I were talking, and I said I remembered the circum tance—Mr. Harding's clerk gave me a notice last Thursday night to come—I was wrong when I said I left on Thursday—I left Newcastle on Friday morning, and arrived here on Friday evening—I came by the railway—I remained at Newcastle till after the election—I was not present at the nomination—I went to hear Mr. Badnall, at the Roebuck, because I wished to hear him—I cannot say how long I stopped listening to him, a considerable time—I had not heard Mr. De Horsey make a speech, during the election—I know Mr. Badnall—my husband is a voter—he voted for Mr. Badnall and Mr. De Horsey—the first time I spoke to Mr. Badnall was last Saturday at the inn, across the way—I do not know the name of is. FREDERICK THOMAS APPLEBT. I am an apprentice to Mr. Josiah Roby, a tea-dealer, at Newcastle-under-Lyne; his shop is right opposite the Castle hotel. I remember the evening of the nomination of the last election—I saw Mr. Miller that evening, at the Castle hotel, between the hours of nine and ten o'clock—he was speaking to the crowd from the window of the Castle hotel—I heard him speaking—he said he had a suc cessful canvass, and he hoped they would be early at the poll on Monday morning.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did you heat all he said? A.  Yes—I cannot remember what else he said—he did not speak any thing like half an hour—about a couple of minutes—he had not his hat on—he put his head out of the window, and spoke about two minutes—I was about three yards from the house—I cannot recollect any thing else he said—I believe he did say something else, but I cannot remember what—I went for the purpose of hearing the speeches—I went across when they were cheering him—I did not wait to hear if any body else spoke—there were other people about there at the window—I did not know the per sons of the gentlemen who were at the window with him—I cannot remem ber that I had seen them before—no one spoke beside Mr. Miller, as I heard—I was alone—I did not take notice of the people—I came to Lon don on Friday night—I was first asked to come on Thursday—several gentlemen desired me to come up—Mr. was one—I was not before the Committee of the House of Commons.
   Q.  Have you seen Mr. Badnall since you were in town? A.  Yes, passing up the street—I stopped and spoke to him—I did not walk with him—this was on Saturday—I have been in no house with him—Mr. Beardmore came to me at Newcastle—I have no vote—my master has—he gave it to Mr. Badnall.
