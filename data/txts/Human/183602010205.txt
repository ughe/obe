   Prisoner. Q.  Did you open the cases to see if there were any brushes to both cases or not? A.  No; I knew by the weight.
   Prisoner . I cannot say how they got into my pocket—my coat hung on 1 peg, and I am out several times in the day, and there is nothing going on but larking among them. Witness . I had missed property some time, and had tole my clerk to take particular notice to find it out—the prisoner worked for me nearly twenty years ago, for a year or two—I have lost sight of him for a number of years—he said he did not put the brushes into his coat. JAMES MEEK. I am clerk to the prosecutor. On the early part of Thursday the brushes were taken out of the case by somebody—I immediately looked into the drawers adjoining to where the prisoner works, and found these four brushes, with four others, placed at the back of the drawer—no one else sits there—it was between three and four o'clock—about five o'clock, when he went to tea, I went to see if the brushes were in the name place, and found that four of them were removed into a drawer, to be put into the case, were put into a third drawer, under some carpet—the prisoner went out after that, about seven o'clock—I then went to the drawer, and found one set of brushes was gone—I communicated it to my employer—other persons had an opportunity of going to the drawers besides the prisoner, but we were never in the habit of putting brushes into them—he never went out of the shop, to go out of the house, without his great coat on—they must have been put in between five and seven o'clock—at the time I suppose the brushes were put there, I looked under his board, and found these three pieces of leather, and having a red-ink pen in my hand. I marked them—these three pieces were found on the prisoner, when he was taken to the station-house. WILLIAM CLARK. I am a policeman. I found this leather in the prisoner's right-hand trowsers pocket, and also a quantity of leather in his apartment.
 (Thomas Chandler, and Robert Herring, of Vauxhall-road, and Damon Diamond gave the prisoner a good character.) GUILTY  of stealing the leather. Aged 39.—Recommended to mercy. Confined One Month.
 582. CHARLES FORSTER was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of February , 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  ., the goods of John Brooks , from his person. JOHN BROOKS. I live in Wenworth-buildings, East-road. About a quarter before six o'clock in the evening of the 4th of February, I was passing along Finsbury-pavement, Moorfields—I put my hand to my pocket and missed my handkerchief—I looked round and saw the prisoner six or seven yards off, in the road, drawing it under his coat—it blew into the road—this is it—I collared him and gave him into custody.
   Prisoner . It was not in my possession at all.
   Witness . I saw it in his possession—I did not see him drop it—he was drawing it under his coat, and looking at me at the same time—there were two others found on him.
