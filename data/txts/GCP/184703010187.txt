WILLIAM WREFORD (City police-constable No. 54.) I was called by
Margaret Malone to a house in Red Lion-court, Fleet-street-I there re-
ceived the prisoner in charge--I heard the prosecutrix ask him where the
ring was-be said it was lost.
JONATHAN Roots. I am the parish clerk of Spitalfields. I register the
hanng of marriage at that Church-no banns have been put up between the
prisoner and Catherine Malone.
--BIRKET. I register the banns of marriage at the parish Church of
St. Giles, Cripplegate-no banns have been put up there for a marriage be-
tween the prisoner and Catherine Malone,
Cross-examined. Q. How do you know that? 4. I have referred to the
book, and have no such name for three months back.
JAMES MARTIN Gowrie. I am parish clerk of St. Mary's, Lambeth. We
have no register of the banns of the prisoner and Catherine Malone-we have
no such names for the last three months,
MR. ROBINSON called
CATHERINE SWAINE. I am a dress-maker. I was present at the public-
house when these parties were going to be married it was at the King's Arms,
in Aldersgate-street-I saw some money pass between them-I sat directly
opposite Catherine and John Clarke- I saw the prosecutrix count money into his
hand-he sat on the left side of her-her pocket was on the right-I made her
dress-I saw her take the money out of her pocket and count it into his hand
--that was not many minutes before we left-we got up directly and proceeded
to Lambeth-Catherine proposed having a cab, and two females sat inside.
Court. Q. What time did you get to the King's Arms? 4. I think it
must have been about eleven o'clock-they started from my house when
they got to the King's Arms they had a knuckle of ham or bacon-Catherine
went outside, and paid for what we had-when she came back sbe sat down,
and the prisoner sat on the left-hand side of her--they were talking, but I
was not close enough to hear their conversation-I was opposite them, but
the table was between us, and it was lengthways--I saw her put her hand
into her pocket, take out the money, and count it into his hand—I do not
know what money she counted-her friend was sitting by my side, addressing
herself to me, which took my attention-I saw her count it into his hand-I.
know there were half-crowns--I saw that-this was done voluntarily; in fact
she was smiling the whole time-she never demanded it back-I believe the
word did pass that he would wear the breeches-she made no effort to get the
money back-she gave it him deliberately-he and her were talking at the
bar afterwards, and I thought he had given her the money back-I do not
know what he did with it I did not see him put it into his pocket-we all
got up-I beard Catherine say, “Come, John, we sball be too late."
MR. ROBINSON. Q. Did you see them speak? A. Yes-they were talk-
ing the whole time after they had eaten-I did not hear them talk loud in a
rational manner-Elizabeth Sperring was talking to me-I could not listen to
both at once-Margaret Malone was standing in the middle of the floor
did not feel much interest in their conversation.
