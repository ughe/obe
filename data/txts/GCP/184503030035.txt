THOMAS Dixon. I manage the business of my father, George Dixon,
who keeps the Worcester Arms public-house, in George-street, Portman-
square; I know the prisoner--he is a tailor, his mother keeps a lodging-
house on the opposite side of the street. On the 24th of Jan. I saw the
prisoner at the bar of my father's house he had a pint of half-and-half,
and paid for it with three penny pieces--before he paid for it he produced
this check, and asked for change for it-I gave him change, seven sove-
reigns and a half-I presented it next day at Sir Clande Scott's it was
not paid--they wrote on it, "No account."
Q. How long had you known the prisoner? 4. Not more than
two or three monthshe lived opposite, with his mother he was
apprenticed away from his mother, to a Mr. Cherry's-I only saw him
casually- I did not see him after I had presented the check, till he was in
custody.

Green-street, on the 30th of Jan., and gave him into custody during the
