EDWARD FUNNELL (Detective Sergeant). I arrested the defendant at 41,
Haymarket - I told him I had a warrant to apprehend him-he asked me
to step into a room, which I did -he then read the warrant, and said that
it was perjury, and they had taken a false oath-I took him to Bow Lane
Station, searched him, and found 7/d., & watch-chain, knife, pencil-case,
pocket-book, purse, and a few memorandums, which have always been open
to the inspection of his solicitor—I have been looking after Teylor, but
have not been able to find him.

Cross-examined by Mr. SERJBANT PARRY. l. Did you take possession of
all his books ? A. Yes, I have a list of them; there are thirty-six- they
were examined by Mr. Lewis, the attorney-I cannot give you the names
of them--they were removed to the chief office, 26, Old Jewry, and have
been in the custody of the police ever since these (produced) are the whole
of them-I cleared the office of all that were laid out, I do not know what
were locked up-I took all I could, and all the papers as well, and a few
cancelled cheques of the Consolidated Bank-I took no blank cheques—one
or two of these have not been untied—these three cheques were looked at
at the Mansion House I have the articles in my pooket which I took
from him, the 7 d. was spent by him--this is his railway season-ticket
from Twickenham-I also took these three private letters from the office,
one is from Mr. Taylor, and one has not been opened--the prisoner was
mensured under a standard by the station-sergeant.
