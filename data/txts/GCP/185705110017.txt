574. FRANCIS JOHN MAYBURY (22), Stealing 627 pairs of boots,
value 1961. ; the goods of Louis Isenberg.
MESSRS. BODKIN and Lewis conducted the Proscoution. de
FREDERICK FULLER. In March, 1856, I was a packer in the service of
Mr. Isenberg, of No. 121, Leadenhall Street. I and Morris, together,
pucked six cases with boots; I have in my hand a list of the contents of
those cases, it was copied from the packing book by the clerk; the packing!
book is not here it was directed to be sent for) the journal is here; the
entries are not in my writing-I packed the cases I looked at the packing
book at the time so as to know what was put in the cases I did not look
at the book before me, that has nothing at all to do with me, that is the
clerk's book-I recollect what I put into the boxes- there were 597 pairs
of boots--the marks of the packages were "G." over 40. O. and O..the
Dumbers were from 1088, I think, to 1093_.the vessel by which they were
to be shipped was the Narrahyarraon 27th March, those packages were
delivered to the prisoner, to be delivered at the St. Katherine's Dooksho
was carman, and of course it was his duty to deliver those trunks at the
Docks--I had nothing at all to de with his duty_I produce a receipt for
those packages in the paroel book-it is the signature of the prisoner those
packages were consigned to Adelaide (The entry of 27th March where
read : « 27th of March; G. over 0. O. and Co.: 1088 to 1093 : St.
Katherine's Docks : six trunks from L Isenberg, Signed, F.J. Maybury
-these packages were delivered between 13 and 1 o'clock in the day I do
not know the prisoner's handwriting.
Cross-examined by Mr. GIFFARD. 0. How were the cases packed
They were packed in trunks three feet six long, what they term anses
they were covered with tarpaulin, and afterwards canvassed and sewn the
marks were on the ends of the trunks, on both ends, outside on the canvas
- the wood was not marked at all-Nicholson marked them.
John NICHOLSON. In March, 1856, I was in the employment of the
prosecutor, Mr. Isenberg. I recollect six cases or trunks, containing boots,
being sent to St. Katherine's Docks, on the 27th-they were delivered to
the prisoner for the purpose of being taken-his father was the carman
employed occasionally by Mr. Isenberg, and his son assisted is the best
ness-he drove a carthe ought to have carried them away in the course of
the afternoon; they ought to have gone straight from the warehouse to the
Docks-in the course of our business it was the duty of the carman to carry
goods of this sort direct to the Docks-we always send them to one parti-
cular Dock, and he had no other place to call at for us he would not be
employed for other goods—there were no other goods in the cart; he was
hired for that particular purpose.
