JOSEPH PEMBERTON (a prisoner.) I have been an errand-boy, and
lodged with my mother, in East-street, Manchester-square. I have known
Broad a little while—I bare only seen Holton twice or three times at most
—he lives in Tom's-court, Thomas-street, Oxford-street—I know a per-
son named Elwood. On Wednesday week, the 30th of January, between
six and seven o'clock, Broad and Elwood came to my house, and wanted
me to come to Gilbert-street, Oxford-street, to get some lead—Mrs. Parry's
stables are at the back of Gilbert-street—we went to the stable in Cock-
yard—Elwood and Broad lifted me up on the wall—I had no knife—I
pulled the lead off with my hands, and chucked it down, and gave it
to Elwood and Broad—there were two pieces—Elwood took the lead
into the mews, left it there, and went and borrowed a sack of Holton
—I got down from the wall into Gilbert-street, and went with Elwood
into Holton's shop, with a piece of lead, for which he received 48.
—he was to receive 48. 3d., but the dd. was not paid—Holton put the
lead underneath the counter—this was between six and seven o'clock—I
afterwards met Broad in Duke-street, and be told me he had got 38. 6d.
for the other piece of lead—Elwood gave me half-a-crown—I did not see
Mott at all that night—we went again the following night, and that night I
saw bim in Tom's-court—Broad was with me—Mott saw us together—
nothing was said when we took the lead into Holton's the night before—
Broad produced the lead—Holton said nothing, but put it into the scale
and weighed it—he did not say what it weighed, but said it came to 78. 3d.
the two pieces—both the pieces were there—Broad took one, and Elwood
the other, into the shop—when I went in with Elwood, his piece came to
48.3d.—Holton paid him 4s.—I was not there when Broad took in the
second piece—I did not hear Holton say it came to 78. 3d., but that was
what it did came to—he asked no questions where it came from, but put
it into the scale.
