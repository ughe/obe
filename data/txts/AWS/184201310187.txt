ROBERT CoLe. I am a watchman in the County jail, at Horsemonger-
lane. I remember the prisoner being brought to the jail, in 1828, with
John Wigg-I cannot exactly say the date-I remember the Assizes being
held at Kingstonit was before the Assizes that he was brought-I saw
him go down to the Assizes, and I saw him after the Assizes- took him
on board the Dolphin bulk, at Chatham, with other convictsI was one
of the guards-the prisoner is the man.
Cross-eramined. Q. You recollect him perfectly, do you A. Very
well-he looks rather darker, and older than he did in 1828, but there is
no difference in his features-] went before the Magistrate on this charge,
and stated that I knew him-I did not express the least hesitation, or say
that I should not have known him- knew him him directly I saw
him.
RICHARD MYERs. In 1828 I was a constable. I was at the Assizes
at Kingston in December that yearI took the prisoner down in a van
among a number of others-I had known him for a number of years be-
fore-] was present at his trial, and brought him from the Sessions-house
to the jail-I have no doubt in the world that he is the man.
Cross-eramined. Q. When did you cease to be an officer A. When
the new police came in force, that was the reason- sent my warrant in
-I was a parish-constable-I used not to be employed by the Mint-1
used to go there occasionally-the was no investigation about my conduct
-J was in business as a tobacconist, but have retired these two years-]
have lived in the same house thirty years.
