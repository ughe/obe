Cross-examined. Q.How long has she lodged with you? A.About eight months; she had not told me she had received the 2l. 12s.; we were not particularly intimate - I had seen her that morning, she came down stairs first -Brown followed her; she did not speak to me, but went directly to the dark place; the rubbish was swept up behind the door - a person must go in and shut the door to get at it.
   WILLIAM BILEY. I am a constable. On the 26th of November, between eight and nine o'clock at night, Johnson made this charge to me, and I took the prisoner Brown- (having been to his house, and brought him to the watch-house,) the girl stated the charge in his presence; he said he had paid the money to a gentleman at the Spread Eagle; I said "Then perhaps Brown you can point out the person at the inn who called the gentleman" - he said he could; Harper and I went with him to the Spread Eagle; he said it was a waiter at the tap that called the gentleman - we inquired at the tap, and found they kept no waiter - he made some excuse, and we went over to the house to find the gentleman there, but we could find no gentleman - a female at the tap said, in his hearing, he had made an application there that morning, and he was in company with a woman when he made the application, and that was at twenty-five minutes past nine o'clock: he said at the watch-house that the gentleman he paid the money to went off by the nine o'clock coach; I do not recollect that he made any answer to the female: we went to the tavern, and made inquiry, but could find no gentleman there who had been robbed; Brown was with us all the time; we then returned to the watch-house: he said he had a receipt which the gentleman gave him; the ward-beadle told him if he had the receipt, he should not be locked up that night; he said he would produce it to-morrow before the Magistrate, but would not produce it then; he was locked up in an hour or two, and remained in the watch-house till then.
 Cross-examined. Q. You took him as soon as the charge was made? Yes - there are a great many cuds about the inns there.
 Q. Do you expect a gentleman who had been robbed at a house of ill-fame would have told every body of the circumstance? A. No.
   JOHN HARPER. I am the ward-beadle. I was with Riley at the watch-house, and the Spread Eagle, and corroborate his statement; I met Brown on the Wednesday morning, about ten o'clock, in Broad-street, and said "This is a very black job," and told him the circumstances of the case; the prosecutrix having been to the watch-house on the previous evening, and given information; he said "I have been down to Gracechurch-street, seen the gentleman, and made it all right;" that he had given the gentleman two sovereigns, and he allowed him to keep the 12s. for his trouble; I told him I hoped it was right, but if not, he would lose his situation.
   SARAH ABRAMS. I live at No. 6, Cock-hill. Johnson was in the habit of coming to my house at times; she came there on Monday night, the 24th of November, at a quarter to eleven o'clock, with a gentleman; I was not at home, but my servant informed me so - I did not see her; she was brought there, between nine and ten next morning, by Brown and Reeves, and said she had been at my house the night before; Brown had knocked at my door, at a quarter to five o'clock that morning; I asked who was there - he said a gentleman in the first-floor had called to him, and said he was robbed; I said "If you will wait a moment, I will take a light of you;" I did so, went up stairs, and asked a gentleman in the bed-room what was the matter - he said he had been robbed of 4l. 9s.; I told him not to make an alarm; I knew the person who had been there well, but I could not tell what time she had been away; he dressed, and went down; I then let Brown and Reeves in; Brown said he was not satisfied but what the woman was in the house; I told him he was welcome to search it - he did so, and found nobody - Brown, Reeves, and the gentletleman went away together.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you hear the gentleman say he lived in the county? A. No; I was out when they came in - I came home at two o'clock in the morning - my servant is not here; my house is shut up at eleven; I described the prosecutrix to Brown, and told him where she lived.
 Prisoner's Defence. Riley and Harper have an old grudge against me, and have taken this opportunity of using this against me, or they never would take notice of what an old prostitute, and well-known thief, have said - they have been trying for some time to spit a spite against me; the gentleman told me he did not wish to be exposed.
   WILLIAM RILEY. I have no grudge against him.
   JOHN HARPER. I have no grudge against him.
   GEORGE REEVES. I am a watchman, on the adjoining beat to Brown. Mrs. Abrams made a communication to me respecting a robbery; I went with Brown to the prosecutrix - when Brown told her the charge, she ran down stairs into the back place - I got into the dark place two or three seconds after Brown, and I found her in a stooping situation, by the side of some dirt; the prisoner asked her where the money was - she denied knowing any thing about it; he called for a light, and said he considered the money was deposited in the dirt; I saw him find the money in the heap of dirt - it was covered all over with the dirt; there was not a piece of it to be seen before the dirt was raked over - it could not have been placed there by accident; she was close to the dirt when she was stooping; I was with Brown when the gentleman came out of the house; he said he was going sixty miles on the following day; when the money was taken out of the dirt, she asked him what he had got; he said 2l. 12s. - she then exclaimed that was all she had robbed the man of.
 COURT. Q. What were her words? A. She said"That is all I took from the gentleman;" she said nothing, in my hearing, about the landlady of the house; I did not hear the gentleman call out of the window - I was not near enough; Brown called me - he has been a patrol about two years.
     NOT GUILTY.
