CHARLES HATFIELD. I was against my father's door, and saw the prisoner go into the prosecutor's shop—he came out again and spoke to a man who was about a dozen yards outside—he then returned into the shop, reached over the board, and laid hold of the cigars—I went in and collared him—Mr. Groom came—he struck me in the face, and struck Mr. Groom—knocked us both off and ran away—there was a cry of "Stop thief!" and he was pursued.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you see me move any bundle? A.  Yes—I laid hold of you when you laid hold of the cigars.
   THOMAS WITHERS ( policeman ). I saw the prisoner about fifty yards from the prosecutor's—he was running, and several persons tried to stop him—he ran into a public-house—I went in—he said, "I have done nothing."
   Prisoners Defence.  I went to purchase a cigar—the bundles were all laid in the window—I had to reach to pick out one—I might have knocked down this bundle, but as for taking it, or havingit in my hand, I deny it. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .

 240. HENRY CLEMENTS was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of Nov ., 7dwts. of gold, value 18 s  .; the goods of Joseph Poole Matthews , his master. JOSEPH POOLE MATTHEWS, goldsmith, Gerrard-street, Soho.  The prisoner was my errand boy—about the 21st of Nov. I missed some gold—we caught the prisoner going from my house—I had him watched to Mr. Rewell's, in Broad-street—he was taken with the gold on him—I do not swear to this piece of gold—I had such gold in my place. JONATHAN WHICHER( police-constable A  77.) I followed the prisoner to a refiner's shop, and took this gold, which the man at the shop was weigh ing—the prisoner said it was his brother's, and he gave it him to sell—I took him to his master's—he went down oo his knees, begged for forgiveness, and said it was his master's gold, and it was his first offence.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 18.— Judgment Respited.
 241. GEORGE GOLLIGHER was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of Nov ., 4 loaves of bread, Value 2 s  . 2 d  .; the goods of Ann Seager , his mis tress. GEORGE GREGG. Mr. Seager serves me with bread; I produce a book, with the number of loaves we had. On the 6th of Nov. the prisoner ought to have delivered five loaves—he did deliver them, and on the 7th he altered the 5 into 9 in my book. SOPHIA GREGG. I live with my brother—I took in five loaves of the pri soner, on the 6th of Nov., and only five. ANN SEAGER. I am a widow, the prisoner was in my employ eighteen months. On the 6th of Nov. I gave him nine loaves for Mr. Gregg.
