at the first public-house-we sat down in the parlour-to the best of my
recollection there was no one else in the room--I did not talk to him, as
that would have prevented his taking the numbers of the notes---I cannot say
how long he was about that I do not recollect taking an omnibus going
to Mr. Cafe's-I did not take one returning, and to the best of my recol-
lection I did not going, but I am not quite certain I do not know that
I hailed an omnibus going along I did not feel inclined to ride in returning
I dined before I left home-I do not know whether I drank any thing
after dinner-I might-I dine at one o'clock-I drank nothing at tea time
but a glass of beer-I cannot tell the distance between the two public
houses I went to-I cannot say how long I staid at the second-I
do not know whether I was there five minutes I sat down in
the parlour-I think I offered 1s. for the beer he had, but they would not
receive it-I handed the notes to the prisoner to take the numbers--I have
been in the habit of going out with the prisoner-I never employed him to
count money for me-he once offered to go and cash a bill for 291. for me,
but I would not let him have the bill to the best of my recollection, we
only had one pint of ale at the first public-house, but I will not swear it-
I will swear we had not three-I did not take any thing at the second
house, to the best of my recollection-I know Mr. Austins, a silversmith, in
Oxford-street-we did not stop at any silversmith's shop on the road-I am
quite sure I did not, to the best of my recollection-I had no business at
a silversmith's--if he took me unawares I do not know-I was perfectly
sober, but I suffered a good deal from my leg- I was walking in pain
half a mile is too much for me to walk-I will swear I did not want to sell
my watch-I had it not about me-it was a rainy evening- I do not
remember going through any square-I did not go through Connaught-
square, to my recollection—when the prisoner asked me to wait, he made
a sign that he wanted to ease himself-that was at the time he left me
I remained there a couple of minutes—there are railings before the houses
there-I did not lean against them-he kept me on the other side I fell
when he pushed me down I did not fall again after I got up I know he
gave me his arm when we came out of the public-house, and if I fell I do
not remember, because I was very weak-I do not remember that he fell
down with me--I do not remember any one speaking to me while I was
there-I do not remember any one calling out to me to keep my body up-
I cannot swear theydid not-I do not remember sitting down on the step of
a door-I did not, to the best of my recollection--the weather had cleared
up, at the time he pushed me down, but the ground was wet- I did not
send for him on Sunday morning, or look after him-I thought it would be
useless-I believe he came to me by himself on Sunday-he did not oppose
my shutting the door-I had three loaded pistols on my bureau, arid I
hrst put those into my pocket for fear he should use them, as they were
quite near his elbow-I locked the door after putting them into my pocket
---my wife took them out of my pocket.
