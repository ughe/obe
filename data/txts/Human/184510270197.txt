CHARLES HEMMINGS. I am shopman to the prosecutor. I saw the pri soner come into the shop—I did not hear the bargain about the clothes—I was sent home with the clothes—I was to take them home, and not leave them without the money—when I got to Dorey-street I did not find the prisoner—I was told to wait—I found the prisoner did live there—I waited about twenty minutes—it was about five o'clock when I got there—the prisoner came in, and asked if I came from Mr. Edmiston—I said I did—he said, "You have brought my clothes, have you?"—I said, "Yes"—he said he would go up stairs and put them on—he took them from me and went up stairs—he came down, and had them on—he asked me if there could be any alteration made in the breast of the coat—I said it could, but it was not usual in that kind of coat—he then looked down, and said they would do very well—he said, "I have just come from Mr. Ed miston and have settled for them, and he sent word you are to leave them"—he said he had made an arrangement if there was any alteration wanted to have it done on Tuesday—I said I had got the bill and was told to bring back the money, I did not know what to do—I said, "Have you any thing to show?"—he said it was all right, and he would call and let Mr. Edmiston see them on.
   cross-examined. Q.  When you got home whom did you see? A.  Mr. Hobday and Mr. Edmiston were in the shop—Mr. Edmiston was very angry with me for having left the clothes—he did not swear at me—he did not tell me he should not keep me in his service—he did not give me warning—he exclaimed he was robbed—he and Mr. Hobday were very angry—I am still in the place, and have had had no warning to quit—I do not know that I have any reason to expect to be turned away—Mrs. Matthews was not present at the time I had the conversation with the pri soner about the clothes—there was no woman at all in the room—I could not tell whether Mrs. Mathews wai in the adjoining room—I can tell every word that the prisoner said when he came down stairs—I know what he said, and the place in wnich every word stood, without the alteration of a word. HENRY NOLAN( police-constable L  129.) The prisoner was given into my custody on the 6th of Oct. by Hemmings—the prisoner wanted him to go into the City with him, and said be would settle with him—he would not—he said he was afraid he would make his escape.
