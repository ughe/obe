Before Robert Maloolm Korr, Esg.
764. ERNEST CALLENDAR (30), Stealing a pooket-book, certain
pieces of paper, and 201., the goods of Joseph Richard Chamberlain.
MR. WARBURTON Proseouted.
JOSEPH RICHARD CHAMBERLAIN. I am a physician, of 107, Lavender
Street, Battersea-on Friday, 21st May, I was in the post-office on St.
John's Hill, and took out my pocket-book to get some postal orders
cashed—I don't remember what I did with my book, but when I got
home it was gone-I had received five bank-notes in a registered cover-
I identify these by the numbers.
Cro88-examined. I can't say if I lost the notes or if anybody stole them
from me-I did not say I lost them by the Windsor Castle, Old Wands-
worth.
Ro-examined. The post-office is at Commerce Place, 400 or 500 yards
from where I live.
WILLIAM EDWARDS. I am a dairyman, of 125, St. John's Hill-I know
the prisoner, he works for a master sweep named Brazier about 5 or 6
o'clock on 21st May he came and paid me some rent with a 51. note-I
noticed the number on it-I returned it to the police.
WENSLEY POTIER. I manage the Freemasons' Hotel, New Wands-
worth, for my father-about 1 or 2 o'clock on the 21st the prisoner gave
me a 51. note to change-I took it to my father--the prisoner wrote his
name at the back, “ Ernest Callendar"-I gave it to the police.
HENRY GEORGE (Police Sergeant V). On Saturday night, 22nd May,
about 8 o'clock, I went to 6, Plough Hill, where I saw the prisoner-I
said "I have come to make inquiries about a pocket-book containing
four 51. notes and some memoranda "-he said “My wife told me you
were here this morning ”-I said "What have you done with them?”
- he said "I picked up a pocket-book outside the Windsor Castle public-
house; one I changed at Mr. Edwards's, St. John's Hill, and paid my
rent, one I changed at 20, Overstein Road, one at Mr. Butler's, St. John's
Hil, and one at the Freemasons'"-I said “What have you done with
the pocket-book?"-he said "I took it home and tore it up, and it
appears some of the children burnt it”-I said "I shall take you into
custody"-he said "I am very sorry, but I will pay the money back"-
in the house I found a bank-book showing a deposit of 81. on that date-
I found 11. 88. in silver, and 4d.--I have made inquiries, and find that
81. was deposited in the bank after I was there making inquiries in the
morning-I had then said who I was.
BERNARD BUTLER. I am a grocer, of 64, St. John's Hill-I know the
prisoner as a customer-on 21st May I cushed a note for him; I paid it
into the London and South-Western Bank.
ALFRED BEATTIE. I live at 29, Lavender Street, Clapham Common,
and am & clerk in the London and South-Western Bank-I produce this
note.
WALTER KING. I am a traveller of 20, Overstein Road-I cashed a
51. note for Callendar, whom I met in a public-house on 21st or 22nd
The prisoner in his defence stated that another man pioked up the pooket-book
and notes, and that he had changed them for him; that he was a master noep
and could not road nor write, and that he did not know he was doing wrong.
