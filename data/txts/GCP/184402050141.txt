679. ELIZABETH ANN CLARKE was indicted for stealing 4 yards of
merino, value 123.; 5 handkerchiefs, 88. ; 2 scarfs, 6s. ; 1 veil, 2s. ; 5 bonnet
covers, 28. ; 2 window curtains, 4s. ; 8 yards of silk, 28.; 16 yards of
ribbon, 28.; and one box, 28., the goods of William Clarke.

MR. HORRY conducted the Prosecution 200
SARAH CLARKE. I am the wife of William Clarke, dyer, King-street,
Hammersmith. The prisoner is my daughter-in-law-between four and fire,
on Wednesday, the 17th of Jan., T went out, leaving her at home-I re-
turned at nine and received this letter from the prisoner's grandmother-it is
in the prisoner's handwriting-she did not return that night-the next morn-
ing I missed a box, and I afterwards missed some handkerchiefs, scarfs, a piece
of ribbon, some merino, and a veil—these tiow produced are them--they are
my husband's—I saw the merino safe the evening before-I did not give any
of them to the prisoner-I have been married to her father twelve years, and
she has lived with me ever since-she had not said any thing to me about
going to Chiswick-she had no occasion to seek a situation-(lelter read)-
“Hammersmith, Jan. 9, 1844. My Dear Father, -I am sorry to leave you,
but I have got a situation, and, as I heard of one, I took the first opportunity
of going; it is at Chiswick. I shall write soon."
WILLIAM CLARKE. I am the prisoner's father. I did not at any
time give any of these things to the prisoner-I did not hear at any time of
her getting a situation at Chiswick-she had no occasion to get one.
ELIZA DAY, dressmaker. George-street. On Monday or Tuesday, the 15th
or 16th of Jan., the prisoner called at my father's house and asked if she could
sleep with me I said I could not accommodate her-she asked me to mind
a box for her she said she had a situation at Chiswick, but was not going to
it for a month, but her mother so ill-used her she could not stay-on Wednes-
day night she called and said she had left the box at Mr. Finch's at the bottom
of our street, she would go and fetch it she was gone about five minutes,
and then brought it and left it with me.I do not know where she went that
night-the box was unlocked-she showed me some of the things in it-these
are some of them-she showed me a piece of brown silk, and asked if I would
make a bonnet for her-I agreed to do it-I asked how she came by them-
she said her father had given them her to make up for herself, as they had been
in the house so long and the owners had never come for them she said her
mother gave her the bit of French merino when she came away to make up for
berself-the constable came the next morning and took the box and all the
things in it-1 had gone to school with the prisoner.
