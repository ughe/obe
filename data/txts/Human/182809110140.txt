 Q. Company or not, had you not seen him? A. Not till after the robbery; I saw him after the robbery a few days; it may be a week or two.
 Q. You have seen him several times since? A. Yes; he did not say he was the person who had robbed him.
 Q. Did not he tell you he was the man who had been with him? A. Yes; he mentioned that.
 Q. Then you knew he stated that the prisoner was the man who robbed him - did you tell him you had seen the prisoner repeatedly? A. Yes, I did.
 Q. Why did not you take him up? A. He gave me no order to take him up.
 Q. Did you not know the prisoner before? A. I had seen him once or twice before.

 1806.  WILLIAM BUTLER was indicted for feloniously assaulting Francis Welch , on the King's highway, on the 3d of August , at St. Pancras, putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, five half-crowns, 17 shillings, and 11 sixpences, his monies.
 FRANCIS WELCH. I live in Fitzroy-market. On the morning of the 3d of August, I was in  Tottenham-court-road, about one o'clock; I met the prisoner and two more young lads, at the corner of an archway - the prisoner came up, put out his leg, and threw me down; I am sure he is the person - the others came to his assistance, while he put his hand into my pocket; while I was down I caught hold of his collar and called for the watchman; we had a tussle - he handed the money out of my pocket to one of the lads, who ran away with it, but I detained the prisoner till the watchman came; I saw him in the watch-house the next day, and I told him if he handed the money over to me I would do no more to him - he said he had not got the money, but if I went to his father, he would see what he could do for me.
 Prisoner. He said I had robbed him of a sovereign, and he was in liquor: I told him I had no money, and when at the office he said I took one sovereign and 15s. Witness. No - I said 1l. 15s.; it was five half-crowns, seventeen shillings, and eleven sixpences.
   WILLIAM EMERY. I am a watchman of St. Pancras. On the morning of the 3d of August, I was in Tottenham-court-road, about one o'clock; I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner entangled together - there were two persons standing not far off, who made off; I took the prisoner and prosecutor both to the watch-house, and the prosecutor charged him with robbing him of 1l. 15s. - he did not say it was a sovereign; he had been drinking, but he seemed sensible enough to know what he was about.
 One witness gave the prisoner a good character.
 GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury on account of his youth.
 1807.  JEREMIAH LEARY was indicted for feloniously assaulting Jacob Woolf , on the King's-highway, on the 3d of July , at St. George, putting him in fear, taking from his person. and against his will, 1 pocket-book, value 2s., and 1 hat, value 15s, his goods.
 JACOB WOOLF. On the 3d of July, between six and seven o'clock in the afternoon, this man went past my shop,  No. 33, Ratcliff-highway; he broke a pane of glass - my housekeeper called to me "Mr. Woolf, the man has broken a glass" - I went out and said he must pay for it; he said "Don't be frightened, I will pay for the glass - I have got no money, go with me home;" I am a foreigner - I asked my neighbours, "Shall I go home with him or not" and they said Yes; I asked where be lived - he said"Old Gravel-lane;" he took me to a very narrow street, and said "Here, I will pay for the glass" I was astonished - he knocked me on the ground; I cried Murder! he put his hand to my mouth and took my pocket-book - he took my hat up and ran away; there were three or four hundred people there, all Irishmen - I was glad to save my life and get away; the prisoner went away; I am quite sure he is the man - he was taken up in eight or nine weeks - I did not see him from the 3d of July till he was taken.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were there not any Englishmen? A. No, all Irishmen; there came two Englishmen, and took me away; it was not another man who said he would pay me for the glass - the prisoner told me he would pay for it; I went out - the prisoner was there alone; there was no other person; I mean to say it was the prisoner who struck me - no one saw him strike me; there were three or four hundred people there.
   REBECCA COHEN. I am housekeeper to Jacob Woolf. On the 3d of July I was outside the window, and the prisoner was just by the window, a man on the other side was talking to him; the prisoner broke the glass, and I told Mr. Woolf of it; I called him - he came out; I shewed him that was the man - he went away with him; when he came back his face was all bloody and scratched - he came back in an hour's time; I heard the prisoner say, "Come with me, I have not got the money in my pocket" - I am certain he is the man.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it this man who broke the glass? A. Yes - it was not a friend of his who said he would pay for the glass, it was this man himself.
 Prisoner's Defence. There are plenty of witnesses to prove that I had no more to do with his pocket-book than a child unborn.
   JOHN BRIAN. I am a coal-whipper. I was going along the highway, and saw a crowd round the window: the prosecutor had hold of the prisoner by the collar, and said he broke the window - the prisoner did not say Yes, nor No; I spoke to Woolf, and said, "You had better go with the man to his house, and perhaps you will get paid"- I followed them, and they went on; I went into my own house and came out again, and saw the prisoner and prosecutor on the ground; Woolf was on the prisoner, and had hold of his handkerchief, and a crowd of women were about them - a number of them separated them, and the minute Woolf got up he pointed to a man to whose shoulder the prisoner does not reach, and ran into houses and houses after him; the prisoner went away without his hat; he did not run - he was not able to run.
