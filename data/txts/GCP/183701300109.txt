FREDERICK FRANCIS WALKER. I am an engraver, and live in Upper Seyal
mour street, Euston-square. I occupy all the bouse except the first floor, which
is let out in lodgings-on Tuesday morning the 17th of January, I got up
about half-past nine o'clock, and on going into the parlour I missed twelve
volumes of Shakespeare off the sideboard - I had seen them safe about
seven o'clock the evening before on going into the passage to get my hat,
I found it gone, and an old one left on the same peg I missed a large
cloth cloak with a fur collar and cape off a peg-on the Thursday morning
following I saw my hat at the station-house in Phænix-street- have
never found my Shakespeare or cloak-I saw the prisoner in my house
about a fortnight before the robbery, in the kitchen with my servant-
never saw him except on that occasion I saw him after the robbery
at the station-house, and said to him, “You are the person I saw in my
kitchen a fortnight ago," he said, “I was"-I bad gone to bed on the
Monday night about twenty minutes to eleven o'clock-I had seen my
hat and cloak a minute or two before I hung them up after half-past ten
o'clock.

HENRY PHILLIPS. I am a sword cutler, and lodge at Mr. Walker's.
On the 17th of January I went to bed about eleven o'clock-about ten
minutes after seven o'clock next morning, I heard the bolts of the street
door pulled back, and the lock turned, but I neither heard the door open
or shut-I came down about twenty minutes before eight o'clock, and found
the door unfastened, which I never found so before-I go out the first every
morning, and always have to unfasten it myself,
AG
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. You were dressing yourself perhaps
at ten minutes after seven o'clock when you heard this ? A. Yes-I
always rise at seven o'clock it was twilight-I could not have seen a
person's face in my room at that time I had no light to dress by-there
might be sufficient light out in the street to see the face of a person. se

following night I knew him before, and knew where he lived-I went
to his father's house No. 11, Compton-place, Brunswick-square-I knocked
at the door, and his father let me in I found him in bed in the same
Toom with his father and mother he was awake- I told him I took him
od suspicion of felony-he inade no answer, but got up directly--I saw
this hat on the table in the room, and brought it away-I asked him if it
was his hat-he made no answer-I took him to Phenix-street station-
house on the 26th of December, I remember having seen him in an
archway in Compton-street-he then had on such a hat as has been pro-
