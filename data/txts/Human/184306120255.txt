JAMES WALDERS was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the warehouse of Joseph Mahon , on the   25th of April , at   St. George the Martyr, Southwark , and stealing therein 2 tons weight of paper, value 57 l  .; 1 sack, 6 d  .; and 1 pair of scales and beam, 2 l  .; his property; and DANIEL REARDON for feloniously inciting him to commit the said felony; and also for receiving the said goods, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. GRIFFITH EDWARDS. I am a green-grocer, and live at No. 43, Feather-stone-street, St. Luke's—I keep a van and two horses—I know the two pri soners. On Tuesday, the 25th of April, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, Reardon came to me and asked if my van and two horses were at home—I said, "Yes"—he said, "Put them to, for my master wants you"—I said, "Where?"—he said, "On Saffron-hill, Mr. Cohen's"—I immedi ately put the horses to, and away we rode together to Cohen's house—I stopped there about ten minutes—Cohen said, "Go along with these three men"—I saw Walders there, and two other men, who I do not know—I have not seen them since—Reardon staid behind at his master's, Cohen's house—I asked them where they were going—they said, "To Great Suffolk-street, overthe water"—I said, "I don't know Suffolk-street"—they said, "If you will go on, we will tell you which way to go"—Walders jumped on the top of my pony, and rode all the way over till we came to Suffolk-street, nearly to the bottom—when we came to the yard, they told me to stop—Walders told me to go and get something to drink, and they would pay for it—I did not go to the public-house, but stopped to look at a picture shop close by—I did not go into the yard then, but stopped outside in Great Suffolk-street—while looking at the pictures, Walders came and called me, and told me to bring the van up the yard, and the two horses—I did not see any difficulty in getting into the yard at first—it was open—there were no gates to it that I saw—I took the van and horses into the yard, and backed the van close to the warehouse ladder, by Walders' direction—I have since learnt that ware-house to be Mr. Mahon's—when I backed the van I saw the two men up stairs in the loft of the warehouse, chucking bundles of paper down to Wal ders, who helped to load the van—when I thought they had nearly enough for my two horses to draw, they put a bag of paper, tied up in small parcels, about the width of my hand, on the tail of the van—the other paper was se cured in bundles about a quarter of a hundred weight each—I should judge by my springs that there was near upon two
