2345. EDWARD POUND was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of
October, 4 bushels of oats, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Souter, his
master.
Mr. BOP is conducted the Prosecution. com
JOSEPH CHANCE. I was a horsekeeper in the service of Thomas Souter,
- brickmaker, in Ball's Pond-he has also a wharf at the basin in the City-
mond-the prisoner was his carter. On the morning of the 14th, about six
s'dock, he came to me for some hay and straw, and said he was to take it
to his master's, at Wenlock Wharf-I always gave him hay and straw-
be also asked me to let him have some oats--I gave him some in a small
nck-I did not measure them--there was a busbel, or it might be two
bushels--the sack was not full-I was accustomed to give him oats the
sack was dirty, and tied round with a string-it had no mark outside it-
he put that in the cart, and said he was going to take it to the wharf for his
master's horse for the night--that was the reason I let him have it.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNs. Q. Have you never given a different
account of the quantity of oats? 4. No-I never gave the particular
quantity-I did not say it was from three to four bushels-I said I did not
know how many-I was in the loft when I gave it to him.
WILLIAM FEATHERSTONE. I am a groom in Mr. Souter's service. On
Friday, the 14th, the prisoner brought some hay and straw from Ball's
Pond, about twenty minutes to eight o'clock in the morning-he brought
no oats, nor any sack--I had not desired him to get any oats--I desired him
to get hay and straw-we did not have oats from Ball's Pond at any time-
I ordered some next day at the corn-chandler's.
Cross-examined. Q. How far is Ball's Pond from where you were ? .
I cannot say the distance exactly-I had given the horses their breakfast
before he started with them, at seven o'clock in the morning-he had been
there before-he had a one-horse cart-1 do not know whether that horse
had breakfasted.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Would a horse eat two bushels of onts for breakfast?
4. No.
SAMUEL SOUTER. I am the prosecutor's brother. I was out on Friday
morning, the 14th, about seven o'clock, passing down Atfield-street, St.
Luke's, and on a waste piece of ground, by Cherry's house, I saw the pro-
secutor's cart, with a truss of hay, and another truss of hay and two trusses
of straw on the ground-at the same time I saw Cherry taking a sack of
something from the cart into his own premises--the prisoner was there
Cherry was taking it from him-I could not judge what the sack contained
-it appeared to me to be quite full, and to be a good-sized sack-I did
not notice whether it was clean or dirty-Atfield-street is a mile or a mile
and a quarter out of his way to the City-road.
