THOMAS BROOKSON. I keep this baker's shop—my wife gave me two
bad half-crowns-I took them to Southwark Station and gave them to the
inspector—this is the one uttered on March 6th–I know it because the
other has a thinner edge-the inspector marked it.
WILLIAM JAMES HUNTLEY (Police Inspector). On the evening of March
6th Brookson brought two half-crowns to the station-I marked them
both, and handed the one produced to-day to Butler.
HENRY FULLER. I keep the William the Fourth-on March 7th I was
standing by my wife when she served the prisoner with 1d. worth of
tobacco and a glass of ale, which came to 24d.-he put down a half-crown
-she tested it, broke it in two pieces, and handed it to me I jumped
over the counter and detained him, and gave him into custody, with the
broken half-crown-he said that he was a hardworking man, and had
taken it.
MARTIN BURGIN (Policeman ). On March 7th I was called to the
William the Fourth, and the prisoner was given into my custody-he said
nothing-I found 3d. on him-he gave his name John Bracher-Fuller
gave me these two pieces of a half-crown.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. These are pieces of a bad half-crown-the
other half-crown is bad also.
Prisonor's Defence. I am innocent of the last two cases.
