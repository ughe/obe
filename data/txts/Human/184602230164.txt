   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  The misunderstanding between you and the prisoner resulted in his summoning you before the Magistrate? A.  No, the misunderstanding was caused through my taking my sister's part—I never was before the Magistrate—I had been summoned before this mat ter took place—the summons was to have been heard two or three days afterwards, on the Tuesday afterwards—this occurred on the Sunday—I am quite sure I did hear what I have stated—he wanted her to take a false oath against me, to swear I had committed an assault on him, which I had not done—the summons was for an assault—she said she could not take a false oath against her brother, and then he said if she did not he would do for her—I heard that distinctly—there were other wrangling words—afterwards I heard him say, "I will take a warrant out against you, I will transport you for seven years, if you do not take a false oath against your brother"—hesaid that several times, for I listened for two hours—he was intimidating her—they were quarrelling for upwards of two hours that night—he said he would transport her if she did not take a false oath against me—he said that first when I knocked at the door, "If you don't take a false oath against your brother I will do for you"—I stated that before the Magistrates—I lived in the house with him—he was the landlord—my mother lived there too—I have had frequent quarrels with him, but not with my sister, merely as you would say tiffs—when she had come up into my room I have ordered her out—I have ordered her out of the room, and just put my arm to put her out—I have not shoved  her down stairs—Ihave not been threatened by the prisoner that he would have me up before a Magistrate for assaulting his wife—never for anything of the kind, not for assaulting his wife, only for assaulting him—he never attempted to assist her—he never complained of my assaulting her, or pushing her out of the room—my mother was not, as  I am aware of, fond of interfering in their domestic arrangements—there were not constant complaints of her conduct made to me—I have never heard the prisoner say that he had no peace through his wife's mother—I have not heard him complain of her interference, nor of my sister's—my mother had not complained to me about it, nor has asked me to interfere—the prisoner and my sister occupied the back parlour, bed-room, and kitchen, and middle room on the first floor—I remained there after this matter took place up till the 16th of Feb.—I was there when the surgeon came—I did not, to my knowledge, see my sister shake hands with the pri soner—I cannot charge my memory that I ever saw her do so on that morning—I do not know whether it might have occurred, and I forget it—I would rather swear it did not occur in my presence, but I would rather give him the benefit of the doubt—I cannot recollect it—it was nothing very particular to observe—I did not hear him say anything to his wife—I did not hear him say to his wife, "How did this happen?" or, "What did it?"—I only heard him repeat that once—I cannot recollect whether she did or did not hold out her hands and shake hands with him—when he said "Who did it?" she was lying on the floor, and she took no notice—that was the only time I heard him—I did not hear him say, "Who did it?"—I was not present then—Jo nathan Cook was there—I fetched him—I was in and out of the parlour because I fetched Dr. Otway some water and some sponge—I could not have been there the whole time—I said at once that I thought he had cut her throat, from the threatening language—I did not say that he had done it—I might have said so, but I cannot recollect whether I did or did not.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Did this assault which has been spoken of at all arise from any misunderstanding of that kind, about taking your sister's part? A.  It arose out of this, one Wednesday morning she came up into my room and said, "My life is in danger," I thought it very strange for her to male that observation, and as I did not wish to interfere between man and wife I said, "Go out of my room," I looked over the bannisters and saw him going into the parlour, and I called him a cowardly villain—he took out a sum mons against me on the 21st, and it was served on me on the 22nd—I was in and out during the time Cook was there—the shaking hands might have taken place without my seeing it.
