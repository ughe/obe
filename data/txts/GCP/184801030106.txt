518. HENRY FIELD, stealing 2 balf-crowns, the moneys of Thomas
Nicholls.
Thomas Nicholls. I am a baker, at High-street, Stratford. On Satur-
day night, 1st Jan., about half-past eleven o'cluck, I was in my shop-parlour-a
neighbour called out-I ran into the shop, and found the prisoner a little dis-
tance from the counter-Mr. Upton was at the door-he said, in the prisoner's
presence, " This man has been at your till”-I laid hold of the prisoner,
heard something drop, and saw a ball-crown on the floor- I did not see him
drop it-1 pushed him back into the shop, and saw a half-crown slip from
his sleeve- he said he merely came in for a halfpenny loaf - I found the till
open to the full extent-I had been to it ten minutes or a quarter of an hour
before--it was not locked-the key was in it-there might be five or six
pounds of silver in it.
Thomas Rose. I am in the service of Mr. Upton, a butcher, of Cha-
pel-place, Stratford. On Saturday night, Ist Jan., about half-past ten
o'clock, I was passing Mr. Nicholls's window, and saw the prisoner with his
hand in the till-I saw silver in his hand - I told my master-he went to
Mr. Nicholls.
CHARLES ROWLING ( policeman.) I took the prisoner--he said he did not
care, he bad got no money on him-I searched him, and found a halfpenny.
Prisoner's Defence. I went in for a halfpenny louf; the butcher called
out that I was at the till, but it is false ; I stood in the middle of the shop.
Guilty.* Aged 18.-Confined Six Months.

George Ekins Crane. (See also Vol. 26, page 1203.)

making lime and hair for plasterers- I do not know anything of him.
