   Cross-examined. Q.  Had he not been to you between 21st Sept. and 2nd Oct., and asked you for the luggage to send to Captain Dodsworth? A.  He had been previous to the time he got it, but I do not know the date—I refused to give it, unless he could produce satisfactory authority— he said he had received a letter from Captain Dodsworth, but he did not show it to me—I was afterwards ordered to give it up to him, by my com manding-officer; not by Captain Cannon—I do not recollect whether he told Captain Cannon that the light van was procured in Woolwich to take the baggage to the water. EDWARD JAMES LEVI( police-inspector, R ). On 23rd Oct., in conse quence of information from Captain Dodsworth I caused Hart to be apprehended—I saw him when he was brought to the station, and the constable who brought him said, "I bring this man here, on suspicion of stealing a quantity of luggage belonging to Captain Dodsworth"—I asked what he had to say respecting the charge—he told me he had given Captain Dodsworth's luggage to a man who came with a horse and cart with a note purporting to come from Captain Dodsworth—I asked him the descrip tion of the man—he said he was between thirty or forty years of age—he said the note was lost or mislaid at the time of the removal of the luggage—I detained him—I have made inquiry at the railway, and endeavoured to trace the luggage but have not been able.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you been an inspector? A.  Five years—I do not consider that I cross-examined Hart—I considered I was doing my duty in putting the questions. LEWIS ROWE.
