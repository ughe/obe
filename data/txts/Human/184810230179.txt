FREDERICK BLOGG. I have my book here, which is sometimes made up by me, and sometimes by my clerk—it is a true copy of the prisoner's book, which he says he has lost—this entry of 5th Oct. is not my writing—I have never received the 9 s .—the clerk always enters it—I examine it sometimes and take the money, and sometimes he does—I did not see the prisoner after 6th Oct. or the book—I saw it on the morning before he left—he returned in three days, and said he had lost his book and money.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  He told you he came to settle with you? A.  No—he said, "Here I am with all my faults"—he may have said he was prepared to go into the account—he did not call on me for that purpose. FREDERICK SHEDELL. I am Mr. Blogg's clerk. I copied the prisoner's sales from his diary in this book—it is a true copy—this 5th Oct. is my entry—here is no entry of money received from Mr. or Mrs. Hill—I cannot say whether I or Mr. Blogg took his money that evening—the prisoner used to come home, go to the counting-house, make up his book, and pay the money before he left—he has never paid me this 9 s .—he never paid me anything that was not entered—I was watching him with his dray—I do not know whether he saw me, but his brother did, and told him—he went on to the next customer, and left two casks—I went in and questioned the customer, and then went home, and afterwards heard that the horse and cart were in the Greenyard. NOT GUILTY.

   Before Mr. Common Serjeant .
 2416. JOHN DRAKE , stealing 2 bottles and 6 quarts of gin, value 16 s  .; the goods of Edwin Stokes , his master. EDWIN STOKES. I keep the White Hart, Woodford—the prisoner was in my employ, to attend the yard and clean knives, and so on. On 10th Oct. I went with the policeman to the coach-house, and found the prisoner and another man, a stone bottle, and a glass one, and about six quarts of gin—they had no business there—I saw the stone bottle last in the loft—the pri soner was not sober. THOMAS DE LABERTOUCHE( policeman, R  146). I found the prisoner in the coach-house—he said the bottle was Mr. Stokes', and the gin came from London—on the way to the station he said he was tipsy, and took it from Mr. Stokes' cellar.
   Prisoner . I had been drinking all day; I saw the bottle, and took it. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Six Months .
 2417. JAMES WILEY was indicted for embezzlement. JOSEPH WRIGHT. The prisoner was my servant—if Mr. Spurgin paid him 1 l . on 5th Sept., he ought to have given it to me on the same day—he has not done so. MARIA SPURGIN. I paid the prisoner a sovereign for his master, on 5th Sept.—I am sure of that.
   Prisoner . I lost it. THOMAS ADDINGTON( policeman, K  189). I took the prisoner at his father's house, at Leytonstone, and told him the charge—he said he should not have kept the sovereign only he was hard up . GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .
