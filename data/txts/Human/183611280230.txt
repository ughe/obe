Mrs. Mann called me up on the morning in question, and I went out and knocked at the door, and I saw two youths come out—I could not positively swear to them—one was considerably shorter than the other—the tallest had a small bundle under his arm, and the short one a set of fire-irons and a hearth-rug—the prisoners' dress and height correspond with the boys who came of the house, but I had no oppor tunity of seeing their features. PRICE JONES BULLOCK. I am shopman to Mr. Jones, a pawnbroker in Tothill-street. On Monday morning, the 17th of October, I took in a set of fire-irons, decanters, and shawls from the prisoner Hardin, between eleven and twelve o'clock, in the name of "Ann Jones, No.14, Strutton-ground." MARY ATTREE. I am the wife of Charles Attree, a shoemaker. The prisoner Hardin lodged in my house—she came in January last, and re mained till the officers came after her, when she ran away—I have seen Brown and Williams repeatedly at my house with her—I told her I wished her not to allow them to come—she said they were her nephews, and where she was they should come.
   Barnes's Defence.  On Friday, the 17th of October, I was at work with my father, from half-past nine o'clock in the morning till nine o'clock at night, at Billingsgate-market.
 (The prisoner, Hardin, put in a written Defence, stating that she had taken Brown to lodge with her, and positively denying having pawned the property, and that before the Magistrate the pawnbroker had not identified her.) PRICE JONES BULLOCK re-examined.  I did express a little doubt about Hardin at Queen-square—I am sure she is the person—I have very little doubt indeed—I cannot positively swear to her. ANN JANE TEASDALL. I am a nurse. I know that the prisoner Har din was ill, and not out of her house from the 14th to the 21st of October—she was not confined to her bed, but to the house.
   BARNES— GUILTY.
