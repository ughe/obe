1748. JAMES SCOTCHMORE, was again indicted for bigamy.
Mr. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution.
LYDIA READER. I am the sister of Elizabeth Williams. The prisoner
was married to her on 5th May, 1828-she was alive last night-she was
living with the prisoner up to the time of bis leaving her-she has not been
living with Nash more than five years I cannot say how long the prisoner
lived with her.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. Did they live together more than
two or three years? A. I should think about seven years they parted
through his ill-usage- I did not live with them-I say it was through his
ill-usage, by my sister's black eyes and bruised face--she said he did it-I
did not see any bruises about the prisoner-be used to beat her she was in
ill-health--the prisoner was then a hair-dresser—when he left her he left a
shop in Ratcliffe-highway-he left her in a state of destitution.
ANN CORDELL. I was a widow-I married the prisoner on 22nd Sept.,
1840, at St. Savicur, Southwark-I was then a licensed victualler-I had
a public-house and business-after marrying him, he brought everything to
ruin very quickly-he lived with me between two and three years-my
furniture and everything was seized for rent-I had no property but my
business, which was worth 4001.--that is all gone-when my house was gone
the prisoner went too-he left me and my two children by my former bus-
band, in an empty room.
