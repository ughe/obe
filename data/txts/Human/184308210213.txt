 2537. THOMAS BENNETT was indicted for embezzlement.
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS BROWN. I am an earthenware and glass-dealer, in Rosemary-lane and at Walworth. The prisoner was in my service for four years and a half—it was his duty to account to me for the money he received immediately he received it, if I was at home, if not to my son, or a young man who had the management of the books—on the 6th of June he did not account to me for 5 s . received from Westbrook—I cannot call to mind whether I was at home on that day, but I am always there at seven o'clock in the morning, and am generally the last there at night—on the 11th he did not account for 2 s . 6 d . received from Dowling—on the 21st he did not pay me 10 s . from Smith—I recollect that day, and recollect his being on my premises after he had been his journey.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Will you swear you were not out all day? A.  No—I was there in the morning, and in the evening—he had a private book of his own in which he used to make entries—he made no entry in my books—he called his account from his own book, and the clerk generally made the entry in my book—my books are not here—he has come in some times and laid down the money, and been told to go into the cellar to get something.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  What was the reason for his going into the cellar? A.  If he was accounting to me for money—a person came and wanted a brown pan—I should say, "Go and get one," but that would not interrupt his accounting—he would go on with his accounts afterwards—I will swear he did not pay me this money on the 21st of June.
