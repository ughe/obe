That was on account of the small-pox epidemic. I have no recollection of all this now. Of course, you cannot go from the minutes; they must be correct. For the last twelve months my memory has been absolutely a blank. I do not think I was appointed on a deputation which might have to wait on the Local Government Board. I do not recollect Draper again moving on May 7, 1905, that nothing be done in connection with the Nurses' Homes. I was first mortgagee of the Freemasons' Hotel at Woolwich, and the man that was in possession had to get out at very short notice, and I wanted someone to temporarily hold the license for me. I met young Barker, and I took him over to the Bench at Woolwich, and told the Bench the case, and they temporarily granted the license to Barker. I think I can find the evidence for that. Young Barker did it merely to oblige me. This temporary transfer I suppose lasted six or seven months until I found a tenant. It was a regular transfer, but I explained it to the Bench because they tem  porarily transferred it to him. I did not think it was as long as from June, 1905, to November, 1907, that he held the license. I am in a fear ful nervous state. I do not know what I am saying. I do not deny it, but it does not seem possible. I think Long succeeded him. I let it to him on a tenancy agreement. He was originally one of my managers. If Barker had any business he could carry it on just the same. He used to go over to the public-house now and again, perhaps once a week. I can not tell you what other business he did. I do not know that he never did a single job. The nurses were provided with accommodation of a sort. I never went over it. I did not know that the cost to the Guardians after Barker's contract was knocked on the head was £1, 000. A pair of sleeve links was presented to me, and I put my initials on them. During the Inquiry I brought the sleeve links out and showed them to Mr. Robinson and one or two others at the saloon, and Harris the jeweller was in the bar at the time. He said "Shall I have these cleaned up for you and have your initials put on them?" I asked him to do so. That was only quite recently. I never used them. I thought the pin was worth about 30s. I put it in my coat for about three months. I put it in the safe. I never had a gold penknife. I gave a gold penknife to Calcutt to be repaired. That is in Court now. My son has it. It was bought in Paris at the Exhibition. Calcutt happened to be there, and he told me he knew some one who could repair it. I paid him 2s. 6d. for having it repaired. I believe it has got the French stamp on it. There was a whip in the trap when I bought it—when I bought the horse. I think it was a whalebone whip. That was part of the trap. My brother Bob works for the Stepney Borough Council in the engineer's department and does some general work in the office. He is a plumber by trade. He introduced Calcutt to me. I think that was about 1900 or 1901. I have no recollection of the contract for £594. I never advised Calcutt to go in for it. I never spoke to him; he had not been to my house to my knowledge.
