JOHN EDWARD KExP (prisoner, on oath) I am 33 years old,
married man with two children. I beeame a Guardian in April, 1904.
My father had been on the Board for many years before-also my
uncle and another uncle before him. My family have been connected
with the Vestry for over 100 years. My father and unele were on the
Board when I joined. My father died on July 13, 1906, and my
unele on March 25, 1906. They remained members till their death.
Before I became a Guardian I got to know Caleutt through his coming
to the offiee of my father and unele, who earry on business together,
I worked for them. They worked on building ovens and altering beer
houses. Caleutt worked for them from about 1902 or 1903. I used
to go to Caleutt's house with my father before I was a Guardian-
not after. I went to his office three or four times. I have never
agreed to act in concert with any other Guardian to defraud the poor
of Mile End, nor have I received money from Caleutt. I was not
supplied with any earriages by Caleutt on my election in Mareh,
1904. I stood for a different ward in 1907. I was not then supplied
with carriages by Caleutt. I may have said to him, after my elee-
tion, All right, Caleute; anything I can do for you I will," but
never with a corrupt intention. He would come to my house when
there was anything on for him to ask me to vote for him if I were in
town. My work took me away a great deal. I thought there Was
nothing improper in his coming to me. He never gave me E10 on
those occasions except when he refunded for the horse. It is an
absolute lie. The only present I had from him was a turkey and
box of cigars. I think I put in the lowest number of attendances
at the Board of anybody. I voted on several occasions for contracts
going to Caleutt. I voted against him once when Franks and Simons
got the eontraet, and on several other occasions When I have voted

father and unele. I voted for Caleutt's tender for the Workhouse
ceiling (297) when the lowest was E45, as I believe he had already
done part of the work before it was advertised. I voted against the
Barker contraet for the Nurses' Homes altogether, as I did not think
