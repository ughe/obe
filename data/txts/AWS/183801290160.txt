February, 2foz. weight of silk, value 5s.; the goods of Phillip James
Chabot, his master.
SAMUEL ELvIN. I am in the service of Philip James Chabot he is
silk and worsted dyer-the prisoner worked there-on the 2nd of February
he came into the room where I was, saying he wanted some tobacco|
went to his coat, and took something out of his trowsers pocket, which I
supposed to be a handkerchief-i was something yellow-he looked at
and put it into the coat pocket-I took an opportunity of seeing what it Wa,
and found it was 2yoz. of silk-this was about twenty minutes to eight
o'clock-I told my master, and about twenty minutes to nine o'clock be
was going to breakfast-my master told me to follow him, which I did,
from the dye-house in Fashion-street, as far as Spitalfields-church, wheo
took him-the officer took the silk from him.
Prisoner. It was impossible for you to see me take this out of my
pocket-it was hanging to my button when I went into the room-I
not want any one to see it, and I was determined to hide it a moment til
I got an opportunity of getting to the parcel which it belonged to, and
had no opportunity of doing so-when I went to my breakfast I though
to get back in time to put it with the silk-I had no occasion to takeit out
of my trowsers pocket, which I must have undone my apron to do, and puti

coat pocket for a bit of tobacco-I would have returned it as soon as I
could. Witness. I saw him roll it up in his handkerchief, and put itinto
his coat-pocket.
