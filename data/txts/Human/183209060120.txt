LEWIS TREBOUT the younger, was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 50 pieces of brass gas fittings, value 10s.; 50 brass tops, value 10s.; 50 plumbing blocks, value 10s.; 50 pieces of brass pipe, value 10s., and 50 pieces of copper pipe, value 10s., the goods of Henry Houldsworth and another.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating them to belong to  Lewis Trebout, the elder, and other persons.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   EDWARD WARNER FIDLER. I was agent to Mr. Lewis Trebout, and was taking care of his premises at Tottenham; there were a number of brass fittings there - the greater part of them were loose on the floor, the others were, I believe, fixed to the shaft; the prisoner is Mr. Trebout's son; the building was not used at all in August; in that month I missed a number of these things - the prisoner lived at Scotland-green, Tottenham; he had no authority, that I know of, to take any of this property - I do not know how much has been taken away.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is the signature to this letter Mr. Trebout's writing? A. Yes; I have been dismissed from the service since this transaction, by Mr. Brett, who has been on bad terms with me.
   NATHANIEL CHURCHILL. I have been a gentleman's servant, but am now out of place - I live at Tottenham. I applied to the prisoner for employ, and he employed me to work in the garden adjoining the factory - on the 26th of July I was working there; he came to me in the evening, and told me to meet him at the gate, at half-past eight o'clock - I met him; he had a sack under his arm - he unlocked the back part of the factory, took me up stairs into the factory, and he put one end of some gas-pipe into the sack, and told me to put the other end in - I do not remember any being put into a basket; he came to me again on the 7th of August, about half-past nine o'clock at night, and told me to take a sack to Mr. Glover, the horsekeeper at the Red Lion, and to tell him that he should be there in half an hour - he told me the horse-keeper was to empty the sack, and I was to bring the sack back; I do not know what was in it - it was some sort of metal, and not very light; I saw the horse-keeper turn it out - it was some brass pipe and some gas-pipe; when I went back he sent me with another parcel, and when I got there with that, he came and ordered the horse-keeper to pack it up in a sack - the prisoner wrote a direction, and gave it to the horse-keeper, who gave it to me to tie on the sack - I cannot read, and do not know what it was; I tied it on the sack - the horse-keeper told me to wait till the errandcart came, and when it came I brought the sack out, and put it on the shafts of the cart; the prisoner was not there then, but he ordered the horse-keeper to put it on the errand-cart.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you known him long? A. No, not till I went to work in his garden - I never saw his father; the pipes were not fixed, but laying loose about on his floor.
   BENJAMIN ROCHESTER. I drive a cart from Enfield to London. I received a sack, with some metal in it, at the Red Lion, on the 8th of August, between ten and eleven o'clock - it was directed to Mr. King, No. 13, Mark-street, Paul-street, Finsbury - I carried it there; Mr. King took it in; Churchill and the horse-keeper gave it to me.
   JOHN FOWLER. I am a constable of Tottenham. I went with Forster to Mr. King's, No. 13, Mark-street, Finsbury, on the 13th of August - I saw a hamper in the front room, but it was empty; I went into the back room, and King's wife followed me; I there found some brass, which I produce - the prisoner was in the front room, at tea, with King and his family.
   NATHANIEL CHURCHILL re-examined. Q. Are these such things as were put into the sack on the 7th of August? A. No, they are not.
 JOSEPH FORSTER. I am a constable of Tottenham. I found, up stairs at King's house, a number of brass tops belonging to gas-fittings, and other brass articles.
 NATHANIEL CHURCHILL. Some of these are a great deal like the ends of the pipes that were taken.
   JOSEPH FORSTER. I found some pipes in the factory - I have fitted some of these ends to them, and they fit exactly.
 MR. ADOLPHUS to NATHANIEL CHURCHILL. Q. Were all the things you put into the sack, pipes with ends, or some ends without pipes? A. Some ends without pipes.
 Cross-examined. Q. Could the ends, which this gentleman says he fitted to other pipes, have belonged to the pipes you took away? A. I do not know; the chief of the things I took were pipes with ends to them; I cannot swear that these are any of the ends I took away.
