   Cross-examined. Q.  How many of you were there came out of the tap-room together? A.  Mr. and Mrs. Shepherd, Pizey, the prisoner, my husband, and myself—Mr. and Mrs. Shepherd went home—they lodge at our house—we all went home together, only they got home first—I have always said I knew which way the prisoner went—I have not said, I did not recollect which way he went. JAMES MILLER. I am an inspector of the N division of police. I appre hended the prisoner at Allen's cottage, on Tuesday night, the 17th of March, at a quarter after eleven o'clock—I found him in the bed-room, in bed with two children—I said, "Gould, Gould"—he appeared to arouse from sleep, and said, "What, what?"—I said nothing more at that moment—a light was procured, and I again roused him, and said, "Gould, get up and dress your self—he appeared still drowsy—I told him to get up—he then got out of bed, and on getting out of bed, he said, "Where is your warrant?" I re plied, "I don't need a warrant"—he then said, "What do you want me for then?"—I said, "I suppose you have beard of the murder?—it is that I want you for"—he smiled, and said, "If I was as innocent of every thing as I am of that, I should not have much to fear"—I do not recollect his saying any thing more then—as he was packing up his clothes to dress himself, I found 9 s . in his trowsers' pockets, all in shillings—while I was counting it, I asked what money he had, and he said 9 s .—in his waist-coat-pocket I found seven lucifer matches, and two duplicates for a pair of trowsers and a jacket, dated, "22nd February, 1340, 2 s . 6 d .;" and "25th February, 1840, 35."—one in the name of Richard Gould, and the other in the name of Ann Jarvis—on the way to the station he said that a pot-boy at the William public-house had taken a warrant out against him for an assault, and it was that he supposed I was after him for—I had missed him for some time, and asked where he had been—he said, "To Norwich"—I searched him at the station-house, and took off his feet a pair of new shoes, or ankle-boots, which I produce—I asked him where he bought them—he said, in Kent-street—a constable who was standing by, said, "The Borough?"—the prisoner said, "Yes"—I then asked him what he had done with his old ones—he replied, "Thrown them away"—shortly after that, I minutely examined his clothes, and took from him this waist-coat, which he was wearing at the time—he had three waistcoats on—two besides this—this was the middle one—this waistcoat I noticed at the time was stained with blood, and it is also burnt in one particular spot, as if a cinder or a pipe had fallen on it—on my saying that the waistcoat
