MARY DORRELL. I live in Wade's-place. Cullender left the sack of soot with me—the prisoner came up to take it away—I said, "That does not belong to you"—he said, "It does"—I said, "It does not"—he said, "It does, and if you don't let me go I will give you something for yourself"—he took it, and when the prosecutor came up I told him which way he went, and he brought him back with him—he was very tipsy. JOHN PIERCE( police-constable K  178.) I took the prisoner—he said he had not taken it, but had been at work at Richmond's brewery.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to Mr. Richmond's, and did a job; the men paid me, and I went straight up to Back-lane, and stopped two hours; I was in liquor; I had not been that way. GUILTY. * Aged 33.— Confined Two Months .
 1921. JOHN SAVAGE was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of June , handkerchief, value 3 s  ., the goods of Samuel Thomas Sutton , from his person. SAMUEL THOMAS SUTTON. On Sunday, the 4th of June, I was passing down Shadwell—the prisoner followed me—I felt my pocket move—I turned, and collared the prisoner—he had my handkerchief in his right hand, and was putting it into his pocket—directly I took him, his friends got up, and get him from me—he went round the corner—the policeman seized him, and he was taken—I had laid hold of him—my handkerchief was hardly out of my pocket—he begged of me to let him go—he put it up to his left-breast pocket—I pulled it from his hand.
   Prisoner.  I had no pocket, I could not put it in. JOHN COOPER. I am a fisherman, and live at Barking. I was with Sutton—I saw him collar the prisoner, and take the handkerchief out of his hand. GEORGE PAVITT( police-constable K  260.) I heard an alarm of "Stop thief"—I saw the prisoner running, and caught him.
   Prisoner.  I saw it on the ground, and took it up—the prosecutor turned—I said, "Here is your handkerchief"—I said, "There he goes"—I pointed to a man running. GUILTY. ** Aged 21.— Transported for Ten Years .
 1922. WILLIAM VICE was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 4 s  ., the goods of Henry Hammond Ogden , from his person. HENRY HAMMOND OGDEN. I am a master mariner, and live in White Horse-street, Stepney. On the 6th of June I passed through Stepney fair, between six and seven o'clock—I felt my handkerchief leave my pocket—I turned round, and collared the prisoner—I commenced feeling about his body to see if he had it concealed—I was told it was under his feet—I picked it up—I went with him till I found an officer, and handed him the handkerchief.
   Prisoner.  It was two or three yards from me when you took it up. Witness.  It was immediately under his feet. JOHN KINGSTON. I was in the fair, and curiosity led me to observe the prisoner—he was rolling a red coloured handkerchief under his waistcoat, and casting his eye down to the ground—the prosecutor came up presently, and attempted to collar him—the prisoner cast his eye round, drew the handker chief from his person, turned round, and faced the prosecutor.
   Prisoner.  When the prosecutor came and tapped me on the shoulder I was in a crowd of people, and this witness was four or five yards off. Witness.  There was a crowd, but I was close to him as I am now. JAMES PARSONS( police-constable K  179.) I took the prisoner, and have the handkerchief—he said he did not take it—it was on the ground.
