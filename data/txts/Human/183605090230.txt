WILLIAM GARRETT. I live in Church-street, Greenwich, and keep po tatoes in a bury in Coom-farm-lane. I missed a great many, and em ployed a man to watch them—I know my potatoes—these are them. WILLIAM WEBLING. I am a labourer. On the 19th of April I went is watch the potatoes—I saw the prisoner and three more come down to the bury—I went up, and caught the prisoner picking the potatoes out of the bury into her lap—I told the Magistrate that—I said I saw her sitting on the side of the bury—that I went there between seven and eight o'clock, and between eight and nine o'clock I saw the prisoner, two others, and a lad, or a man, come down to the bury—I waited a little while, and then went down, and saw two girls in the straw, and the prisoner sitting on the side of the bury, taking the potatoes into her apron—I told her to come to my master, and he would forgive her—he gave her into custody. ROBERT LEE. I am a police-constable. I took the prisoner, and have the potatoes.
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 42.—Recommeded to mercy.— Confined Four Days.

 1350. WILLIAM LONDON was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of May , 1 pair of boots, value, 6 s  ., the goods of Daniel Bain . DANIEL BAIN. I am a gunner in the Royal Artillery. The prisoner was my comrade—I went out on the 7th of May, at a quarter before nine o'clock, to drill—my boots were standing by my bed-side, in the barrack-room, No. 82—the prisoner is married. and is out of the barracks alto gether—I missed my boots at half-past twelve o'clock, when the man was going to clear out the room, and reported the circumstance—the boots were brought back at a quarter past four o'clock, and laid on my bed by some body—I did not see them brought. MAREBROTHER FLEMING. I am sergeant-major of the 9th Battalion. On Saturday, the 7th of May, it was reported to me by Corporal Watkins, that Bain had lost his boots—I called the cook of the room, and in conse quence of what he said, I spoke to the prisoner, pointed out the situation in which he stood, and told him it was a pity an old soldier like him should commit himself, and I was bound to make him a prisoner—he said he would much sooner give up his own regimental boots, than have any dis grace—I said there was no occasion for that, if he was innocent—he said he would sooner do it, then have his name brought in question—I asked if his son had been about the barracks—he said, "Yes"—he was sent to the guard-room—I after wards sent two officers with him to his own house, where they saw his son and his wife—his son was examined before the commanding officer, on Sunday, and said that he himself watched his opper tunity, and went into the room and stole the boots, while the cook was gone out with the ashes—the son was taken into custody, but there was no bill found against him. HENRY WATKINS. I am the corporal. On Saturday, the 7th of May, Bail told me the boots were gone—I reported it to the sergeant-major—he told me to take the prisoner into custody—I did so—the prisoner said, if his boy had been and taken them, if he was allowed a quarter of an hour, he would go and get them out of him—I was afterwards sent to the pri soner's house, and his wife and son were there—they denied all knowledge
