 739. EDWARD MURPHY , stealing orders for the payment of money, 1 concertina and other goods, and 2 l  . in money; the property of Charles Everett . CHARLES EVERETT. I am a newspaper agent in Old Broad-street I locked up my office about 9 o'clock in the evening on 13th July—the prisoner was in my service two years and a half ago—when I got to the office about half-past 10 o'clock the next morning, I discovered the drawers had been broken open—I had left my cash box the night before in my desk, which was locked—there was about 2 l . in cash in the cash box, and two post office orders for 1 l . 16 s . and 3 l . 12 s .—these are them ( produced )—there was a gold chain, a gold pencil case, and a gold key—they were all missing the next day, and this scarf, this waistcoat, and this concertina—they were all safe when I left the night before. GEORGE MARTIN. I am clerk to the last witness. I went to the office on the morning of 14th July—I found the back door of the office had been broken open, and these different things were missing. RICHARD SMITH. I am a tobacconist, and live in Middleton-street—the prisoner lodged with me—he left a portmanteau in my premises—about 15th July I saw him take this waistcoat and this scarf out of the port manteau and put them on—I produced the same portmanteau to the policeman. GEORGE LOMBARD. I live in Francis-court, Clerkenwell. I purchased this concertina of the prisoner on the 14th July, about 9 o'clock in the evening—I gave him half a sovereign for it. GEORGE ROBINSON. I am an engraver. I received this post office order 1 l ., 16 s . from the prisoner on the 23rd July—I lent him 7 s . 6 d . on it—I saw he had a gold pin—this is it. JOHN BAMFORD. I am a solicitor's clerk. I received this post office order from the prisoner on 26th July—I got the cash for it at the London Spa.
   Prisoner's Defence . I had it given me to dispose of. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Twelve Months .
