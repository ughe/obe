GEORGE WILLIAM EVANS. I am a potatoe-dealer, and live in Spitalfields. On the 17th of October I was in a public-house in Barbican; I had a horse there - the prisoner asked me to sell it; another person wanted me to sell it, and take a bill of him, which I refused; I at last agreed to sell it to the prisoner for 4l. 10s. - he threw down this cheque for eight guineas; I said I had no change - he said he would go home with me for it; I said I had no money, and would go home with him for it; some persons in the room, who appeared then to be strangers to him, proposed that I should take the cheque, and present it next morning, and he call on me for the change; he objected to that - I wished to have nothing to do with it; I waited, as he said if he could sell a poney, he could give me the money; I at last said, if he wished the horse, he was to call on me in the morning, but I did not care about selling it; the others interfered, and wished him to give me the cheque; I got up to go away - he then said, "Give me your direction, and what money you have got - I'll call to-morrow for the rest;" he then gave me the cheque; I gave him the horse and 19s. - he did not call in the morning; Newland, who was with me, said he had known him a long time, and it was all right; the prisoner gave me his direction, No. 3, Fan-street; next morning I went with the cheque, and found the bankers had failed ten months ago - he never called for the change.
   ROBERT BOOTH. I was clerk to Sir Peter Pole and Co. for many years. There was one Edward Smith kept cash there, but he died eight or nine years ago; we had no other customer of that name.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are your books here? A. No. I speak from my knowledge; the books are at the Bankrupt-office.
     NOT GUILTY.
 SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, FEBRUARY 21.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Recorder.
 744.  MARY SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 3 pewter pots, value 3s., the goods of John Mullett.
   FREDERICK MULLETT. I am the son of John Mullett, who keeps the King's Head public-house, Museum-street, Bloomsbury. Last Saturday I saw the prisoner coming out of a stable-yard, in Duke-street, about nine o'clock in the evening, with something under her apron; I asked what she had got - she gave me no answer; I heard the pots rattle, and took hold of her; I took her into our house, and she put these three pots down in the middle of the yard - they have my father's name and sign on them.
   TIMOTHY RYAN. I am a watchman. I received her in charge with the pots; she said she did not care what was done to her, as she was in distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 59. Confined Nine Months.
 745.  GEORGE HUMPHREYS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of January , 1 coat, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Charles James Miles.
 CHARLES JAMES MILES. I live at the Tavistock hotel. On the 16th of January this coat was stolen out of my gig, which stood in  Brydges-street, opposite Drury-lane Theatre.
   JOHN ALFRED CHOWNE. I was returning from the theatre, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, and saw Mr. Miles driving his gig; three men were in the street; the prisoner, who was one of them, drew this coat from the gig, and was walking off with it; I ran and collared him with it on his arm; I gave him in charge - his two companions got away.
   JOHN HICKS. I am a constable. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house.
   JOHN FITZGERALD. I am a watchman. I heard an alarm, and took the prisoner and coat from Chowne.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was talking to a coachman, and this coat was put on my arm by some one.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 746.  ROBERT WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of February , 2 deal-boards, value 6s., the goods of William Hall.
 WILLIAM HALL. I am a  builder, and live in Groveend-road, Mary-le-bone. These boards were in an unfinished house in Alpha-road- the prisoner is a stranger.
   WILLIAM PRIEST. I am a carpenter. I was leaving work about seven o'clock last Wednesday, and in Groveend-road saw the prisoner with two deal boards on his back, about one hundred and fifty yards from Hall's house; he said he had brought them from Kilburn, where he had been working for Mr. Pitts, and had come by them honestly - he went on; a person was with me; we followed, and asked where he lived; he said in Boston-street. Dorset-square; I told a watchman, and we followed him - he went to Boston-place instead of Boston-street; we stopped him at the door, and marked the boards - he gave me Pitts' direction; I went, and could find no such person; I returned, and told the watchman; we then went to his house, and took him and the boards; he then said the man he had them of lived in Brownlow-street, Seven-dials. I went there, and found one Pitt.
   THOMAS GRANT. I am a watchman, and took the prisoner and the deals. I had put a broad-arrow on them.
   JAMES REDWOOD. I am in Mr. Hall's employ. I missed these deals from the passage of this house; I nailed the door up the Monday before; I found it forced open; these are the boards.
   HUMPHREY ROBERTS. I went to the prisoner's house with the witnesses, and found the deals in the passage - he said they belonged to him - that he had them from Mr. Pitts, of Kilburn, and afterwards said Brownlow-street - I am certain of it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I buy boards of different persons.
   RICHARD PITT. I live in Brownlow-street, and am a metal dealer, and deal in wood; I did not sell the prisoner this deal; I have a carpenter on my premises in Portland town - I cannot say that he did not sell them; the prisoner is a tinman, but he is builing a house; my carpenter should tell me when he sells wood.
 WM. PRIEST. The prisoner was a quarter of a mile beyond Mr. Pitts' premises, coming towards them.
 GUILTY. Aged 38. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
