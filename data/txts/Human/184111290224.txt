 281. JAMES HOLLANDS was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of No vember , 1 jacket, value 6 s  .; and 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  .; the goods of Robert Bennington , in a vessel in a port of entry and discharge. ROBERT BENNINGTON. I am a sailor on board the brig Durdon , which was lying in the Commercial Dock at Rotherhithe. On the 4th of No vember, about a quarter-past nine o'clock, I missed my jacket from the forecastle, where I had seen it five minutes before. CAROLINE CAVENDON. On the 4th of November, about half-past eleven o'clock, the prisoner came to my lodging at Deptford, and asked whether the landlord belonging to the house was in—the mistress said he was not, did he want him?—he said, "Not particularly"—after he had been there two or three minutes, he asked me if I would go on an errand for him—I said "Yes"—he took off his short frock which he wore, took off this jacket, and asked me to go and pledge it for him, which I did, at Mr. Tighe's, in the Broadway, for 2 s ., which I gave to the prisoner—our house is about two miles and a half from the Commercial Dock. JOHN TIGHE. I am a pawnbroker, and live in the Broadway, Deptford. I produce a jacket which was pawned with me by Cavendon, on the 4th of November, for 2 s ., in the name of "James Holland"—she told me she was pawning it for a man of that name—it was before dinner-time. WILLIAM GEORGE OSBORN. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into custody on the 4th of November—a man named Cobham was also taken—the prisoner said he knew more about the jacket—that the other man knew nothing at all about it.
 (  Property produced and sworn to .)
   Prisoner's Defence . I met a man by the Surrey Canal, who asked if I wanted to buy a jacket; I said "Yes;" he wanted 3 s .; I had only 2 s ., which I gave him, and said, if I saw him again I would give him the other shilling. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months.
   Before Lord Chief Justice Denman .
