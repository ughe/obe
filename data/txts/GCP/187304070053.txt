Rose GLAVE. I am daughter of Mr. Henry Glave, draper, 535, New
Oxford Street-up to the 12th February the prisoner was in our service as
an apprentice-we also had a porter named Ashwood --we sent our money
to the bank every two or three days, and it was generally the prisoner's
business to take it--whoever went à porter would always follow them--the
amount paid in was entered on a slip-—the counterfoil we kept, and the
slip was sent to the bank-I produce the counterfoil of the paying slip of
12th February-on that day the prisoner took the sum of 2511. 86. 10d. to
the bank, made up in this way cheque, 81. 36. 6d."; bank notes, all fives,
452 ; gold, sovereigns, 1401. ; half sovereigns, 501. ; cheques, 62. 5. Bd.
Scotch note, 19s. 11d.--the prisoner had been with us about three years--
he took the money to the bank with Ashwood, the porter-he did not
return and I did not see him again till 28th February, when he was in
custody at Bow Street-Ashwood has never been heard of-it would take
about a quarter of an hour to go to the bank and back-the porter had
only been in the service about three months—the prisoner had been in the
habit of going to the bank with large sums—the porter was paid B&. a
week, with board and lodging, and the prisoner had 101. given him that
year.
Cross-examined. It was the last year of his apprenticeship-I have been
cashier during the whole time he has been there—he has taken cash to the
bank for the last two years-he has taken as much as sixty thousand
pounds to the bank during that time--we had the greatest confidence in
hin-I know that Ashwood was & sailor-I believe we had a character
with him; from Gilbey's, I believe-there were other apprentices in our
employment, and they went occasionally-Ashwood was the porter, who
was paid least of all--the others had 11. a week, with board, but not
lodging.
