 1665. WILLIAM ANDERSON was indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of June , 1 handkerchief, value 3 s  ., the good of Stephen Corcellis Marsh from his person. STEPHEN CORCELLIS MARSH. I live in Fleet-street. At half-past eleven o'clock at night, on the 22nd of June, I was walking with my wife near St. Bride's church—I felt a twitch—I turned and saw the prisoner—I said, "You have taken my handkerchief"—he said, "You may search me, I have not got it"—I said, "I have no occasion to search you, I saw you throw it down that area"—I had said to my wife, "That is the boy," which he heard, and then he dropped it down the area—I gave him to the officer—he was close to me, and stood against the house, and dropped if. JOHN KEEN YOULDON. I took the prisoner, went to the area which the prosecutor described, and found the handkerchief.
   Prisoner , I know nothing about it. GUILTY † Aged 16.— Confined Six Months.
 1666. JEREMIAH HOROGAN was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of June , 1 waistcoat, value 8 s  .; and 1 yard of silk, value 5 s  .; the goods of Henry Wakefield . HENRY WAKEFIELD. I am a tailor and hair-dresser, and live in Anchor and Hope-alley. On the 26th of June, the prisoner came to see my shop boy, and they went out together—they came home, and the prisoner went away alone—soon afterwards I missed this waistcoat and this piece of silk, which I had seen safe about half an hour before he went away the second time.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  HOW long were they absent? A.  About three hours—I have discharged my young man—his name was Richard Young. WILLIAM ANDERTON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce the waistcoat pledged with me about four o'clock that afternoon, by Jane Middleton. JANE MIDDLETON. I pledged this waistcoat—the prisoner gave it to me to pawn.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have known him some time? A.  Yes, he is a respectable man.
