   Q.  What were you quarrelling about when the glass was smashed? A.  He wanted to go out, and wanted me to give him a few shillings, and I would not—I am forty-three years old, and he is twenty-three—I am po sitive I never charged him with attempting to break open the drawer, and take the money—nor ever quarrelled with him about it in the hearing of the lodgers—we have had words the same as man and wife would have—when I counted my money on the 14th of November, I missed 30 l  —not more—80 l . in notes, and 38 l . in gold, was left—that would make 148 l . in all.
   Q.  I thought you said just now that from the time you counted it on the 31st of October till you missed this none had been taken out, is that so? A.  Yes.
   COURT . Q.  You said you had 160 l . how do you account for the dif ference? A.  When we went to the house we paid 9 l . or 10 l . away in bills, which I can produce—that was out of the 160 l . I had no other money—I say when I counted my money on the 29th of October there was 160 l ., and there was the same when I counted it again on the 31st—it was after that I paid some away.
   MR. RYLAND . Q.  Have you not stated that between the 31st of October and the 14th of November you had never been to the bag at all? A.  I bad not been to the bag at all, only in the presence of the prisoner's mother—I was never out of the room—no money had been taken out of the bag between the 31st of October and the 14th of November—on the Tuesday after I met with the accident I gave my husband five sovereigns to pay for painting the bar, a sovereign to buy him a pair of boots, and a sovereign to pay for moving the goods—I had some medicine for myself, and a lotion for my knee, and a bit of calico, and 1 paid the prisoner's mother 1 l . 4 s . for rent—I calculate that I spent about 10 l . altogether for every thing—that 10 l . was part of the 160 l ., and came out of the bag after the 31st of October, and after the 6th of November—I did not count my money on the 6th of November—my hus band was at home at the time I sent the prisoner up stairs for the money.
   Q.  Why not send him to fetch it? A.  He was not in the room at the time—he was up stairs in the room where the money was—I was in bed, and not able to go up stairs myself, and I asked the prisoner as a favour to go and get it for me—I did not think it necessary to ask her to give my husband the key to get it—I placed implicit confidence in her: not more I think than I did in him—I sent for it because I wanted some money, and when the prisoner's mother brought it down I took out half-a-sovereign—I did not count the remainder.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was anybody ever present when you counted your money? A.  The prisoner and her mother were on the morning of the 29th of October—I counted it in the parlour before them—between the 6th and the 14th of November I went to the bag and took a half-sovereign out, but did not count the money—I did not disturb it.
   COURT . Q.  Do you mean to say that during the whole time your hus band never had access to your money-bag? A.  No, what I thought proper he should have I gave him—I had the keys of the drawer where it was, in my possession—I always kept the key about me, and when I went to bed I put it in my pocket under my head.
