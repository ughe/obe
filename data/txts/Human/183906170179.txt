I am wharf-clerk to Mr. Hartley, at Iron-gate-wharf—I remember, on the morning of the 25th of March, Lewin Caspar coming—he came into the counting-house, took off his hat, and threw a letter on the desk—this is the letter—(No. 3)—he told me it was a letter he had received from Messrs. Came, to deliver two boxes, B C 18 and 19, and that he had given an order to a gentleman, who had produced a letter at the office, for me to deliver the two boxes—I was copying the manifest at the time—this is the manifest—(No. 2)—be directed me to deliver them on the production of that order—I asked him if die gentleman would be there shortly—he said, he thought he would have been there before he had reached the wharf; upon which I wished him to stay and see the delivery of them—he said be would do so. and waited for a short time, but left about half an hour before the party came—Henry Moss then came in a cab  —( looking at Moss )—that is the man—when I was called out to the cab,  he had his hat on—( Moss, at the witness's request, put his hat on )—yet, that is toe man—he brought this letter (No. 4) with him, which has an order on it written by Lewin Caspar for me to deliver—I delivered to Moss two boxes, B C 18 and 19—after I gave him the boxes be wrote upon the order "Received the above, J. H. Dean, per Marsh, Esq." Most wrote the whole of that on the order.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS , Q.  You saw the man, whoever he was, write that? A.  I did, I am certain of that—I did not ask him who J. H. Dean was—I have not found out who Dean is—I have not beard he is a clerk of Solomon's—Moss's appearance if not materially changed since I saw him in the cab  —I think there it t slight change in his whiskers, they appear as if some bad been shaved off—I cannot speak positively, but they appear to have been shaved off—I observed that they were black, I should not say they were sandy—I think they have been in some degree altered—I should say they had been shaved off since—if I had never seen him before to-day, I should not say there was any thing remarkable in his whiskers—I should not think of describing him, as I see him to-day, as a man with large black whiskers, nor as having sandy whiskers—I have been about four years in Mr. Hartley's employ—I think Lewin Caspar has been there about two years—he sometimes came early and sometimes late—I was not in the same office as him—I have seen him there at nine o'clock, and I have seen him there at eight o'clock, just as business or caprice might suit—I might have seen him before eight o'clock—the general hour was nine o'clock.
