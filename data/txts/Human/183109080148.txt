 1880.  JOHN MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on 10th of September , 4 sheets, value 10s., the goods of Richard James.
 RICHARD JAMES. I  keep  the Coach and Horses publichouse, at Tottenham. The prisoner and another man came there on the night of the 9th of September, and slept at the house.
   ELIZABETH GREEN. I am servant to the prosecutor. - I saw the prisoner coming down with a bundle, and while I went to tell my master, this bundle was thrown on the stairs, but I do not know whether it was by the prisoner or the other man.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1881.  MARY GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , 1 pair of tongs, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Lewis.
   THOMAS LEWIS. I keep  the Nag's Head, in Whitechapel. On the 5th of August, between twelve and one o'clock, I was called into the lobby, and saw the prisoner with something under her clothes - I sent for the officer - it proved to be this pair of tongs.
   WILLIAM GARRETT. I am an officer. I found these tongs under the prisoner;s clothes - she said she was in distress, and appeared to be so.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Confined Six Weeks.

 1883.  JAMES HALL was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of July , 24 yards of printed cotton, value 20s., and 4 pairs of stockings, value 6s., the goods of Henry Trendall, his master.
   JAMES PEACOCK. I live in Shoredith, and am a headborough - the prisoner was servant to Mr. Trendall. On the 23rd of July I met the prisoner in Shoreditch; he gave me this parcel - it is directed "Mr. Hall, passenger to Reading, Sunday, July 24;" I had some suspicion, and gave information to the prosecutor.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Were you employed by the prosecutor to carry parcels? A. Yes, occasionally; I was not intimate with the prisoner - I made a box for him, for which he pair me, and he lent me 2s. about a month ago, since I had this parcel from him; the prosecutor had not forbidden me to come to his house -I have carried parcels for him for years.
   HENRY TRENDALL. I am a linen-draper, in Shoreditch; the prisoner was in my service rather more than twelve months. On the 22nd of July, I took in a letter by the Twopenny-post, and handed it to him; he read it, and said he must go out of town, on account of the illness of his sister - I think he said to Reading, in Berkshire. On the day following, I went to Peacock's house - this parcel was there - I opened it, and these articles were in it; I can swear to the print, from its matching, where it is cut, with the piece left; the prisoner has taken goods, and charged himself with them, but I had told him not to do so.
 Cross-examined. Q.But he has charged himself with goods? A. Yes, he had done it more than once; I have employed Peacock as a porter, and about the shop, but he had not so much access to it as the prisoner -I had told the prisoner not to encourage Peacock there, on account of an opposite gin-shop - the prisoner remained in my house an hour of two after I discovered these goods.
