 199. HENRY GARDINER , THOMAS HOLLIDAY , JAMES LOUP, and JOSEPH SEAGER, (indicted with Herman Cusell, not in custody.) for conspiring, with others, to defraud Henry Trimby Gray , and other, of their goods.—Other COUNTS,  for obtaining goods by false pretences.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and HUDDLESTON conducted the Prosecution. SAMUEL STUCKLEY BENSON. I am a jeweller, in partnership with Mr. Thoms, at 43, Ludgate-hill. In Jan. last, the prisoners Gardiner and Holiday came to our shop—Holliday said he wished to look at a liqueur-frame and a cruet-frame, that he had a commission for Hamburgh, and if they suited him he would purchase them—he said they were to be sent to Theberton-street, Islington, to be enclosed with other things that were to be sent to Hamburgh—I showed him some articles, and he selected a plated liqueur and cruet-frame, worth 9 l . 15 s . the two—the two prisoners conferred together, and it seemed to me as though Holliday required the approbation of Gardiner before he concluded the purchase—he gave me this card—( read  —"Thomas Holliday and Co., Commission and General Merchants, London, 6, Pinner's-hall, Old Broad-street")—I packed up the things, and sent them to Theberton-street, by Paton—next day Holliday called again alone, and said that he had a further commission for a gold-watch and gold-guard—he said that the gentle man who was with him when he purchased the frames was connected with one of the wealthiest firms on the continent, and that the frames were for that firm, which was Schulkhauser, Brothers, and Co.—he showed me this card—( read  —"Schulkhauser, Brothers, and Co., Merchants, London, 38, Moorgate-street")—he said that that firm owed him a large sum of money, and that if I would allow him to have the gold-watch and guard he would obtain a bill from that firm for the payment of it, including the payment for the frames purchased on the previous day—I said that would be a matter for consideration, in the first place I must make inquiries respecting this firm, which I did, and ascertained that that firm, as well as Holliday and Co., were swindlers—I think this was about 7th Jan.—Holliday came again on that day to inquire if I was satisfied—I said I had not been able to ascertain com pletely, to my own satisfaction, who that firm was—the result was, that I did
