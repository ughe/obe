   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you known him before? A.  Yes, for some years—I think we had some refreshment together after we got to Richmond. I think I had taken refreshment with him once or twice before; I think not oftener—he paid the fare to Richmond, and I paid it afterwards—I had never paid it before—I think this time it was 1 s . 2 d . down and 1 s . 4 d . back, because we came from Twickenham—I paid the fare both ways—I think I rode in the same carriage with him down, but not up. I gave the young ladies money to pay their passage—I have known them about eight months I do not keep company with them—I have never walked out with them in an evening—they might have been at places where I have been, but I have not seen them—what I did, I did by order of my superiors. CHARLES STANLEY. I am a private watchman, in the goods' warehouse of the London and South-Western Railway—I was directed to go down to Richmond on 2d Sept.—I did not take a—ticket when I got to Richmond I paid the prisoner 2 s . for myself and a female who was with me—I was not known to the prisoner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What train was this you went by? A.  Ten minutes past four; a special train—it was on a Sunday—I know the Miss Harveys—I may have known them six months—I am not in the habit of keeping company with them—I was once at a theatre with them JAMES GILL HORSFORD. I am a ticket-collector at the Richmond station—the prisoner was associated with me as collector—he and I were the only collectors there for the last eight or nine months—each of us put down in the excess fare-book what we receive—this is the book—on the 25th Aug. here are some returns under the thirty-five minutes past four-o'clock-train—they are not my writing—I believe them to be in the prisoner's writing—here is no return of 1 s . 4 d . as excess fare by that train—on the 2d Sept. there is no entry by the prisoner of 2 s . or 1 s . 2 d . by the ten minutes past four train—it would be his duty to enter them if he received the money—that it our instructions.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is this the excess fare-book? A.  Yes, it is the book that I and the prisoner jointly had at the Richmond station to enter the excess fares in—we each enter our own—this "2 s . 4 d ." is in my hand writing—this "1 s . 6 d ." and "1 s . 2 d ." are not—there are no entries of mine on 2d Sept.—I believe the entries to be the prisoner's writing—I could not swear to his writing.
   Q.  Suppose you receive from one person 1 s . 4 d ., from another 8 d ., and from another 4 d ., might you not put them down in one sum, 2 s . 4 d .? A.  No; we put them down separately—when the line was first opened, they might have been put down together—there has been a case where we have been too late for the clerk, and we have paid the next morning—we put down each separate sum as far as lies in our power—there are no other money-takers but ourselves—we receive on both sides, both from the up nod down trains, and account afterwards to the clerk—there is a good deal of hurry sometimes—we give change out of our own money—we seldom have ten or twelve excess fares; we have two or three—it frequently has occurred that the moment we let out passengers from one train we have to run the other to take them—we sometimes do not know the times of the special trains; we do sometimes—I have made mistakes.
