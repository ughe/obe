 SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, JUNE 4.
 Second London Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1336.  CORNELIUS MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Charles Harrington Twight , from his person.
 CHARLES HARRINGTON TWIGHT. On the 16th of April, between seven and eight in the evening, I was in  Smithfield inquiring my way and felt my handkerchief drawn from my pocket; I turned round, and a woman said"A man has drawn your handkerchief from your pocket;" I saw the prisoner running; I pursued and cried Stop thief! the patrol stopped him - I had not lost sight of him; he was about five or six yards from me when I first saw him; there were some persons by his side, but none between us - he ran straight forward; a little boy picked up the handkerchief - it is the one I lost.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you able to describe the handkerchief at the watch-house? A. Yes; I was not positive whether there were any initials on it - I said it was like one I had at home.
   WILLIAM SPENCER. I am a patrol. I saw the prisoner stopped - the prosecutor was running after him; a lad gave me the handkerchief.
   JOHN SPRATMAN. I saw the prisoner running and saw him drop the handkerchief; I took it up and gave it to the officer.
   GEORGE GODFREY. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house - the prosecutor said he thought there was some yellow in the colour of the handkerchief.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1337.  JAMES MORLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of May , 1 coat, value 3l.; 1 waistcoat, value 20s.; 1 petticoat, value 6s.; 2 frocks, value 6s.; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 other petticoat, value 1s., and 1 shawl, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Jennings.
   ANN SOPHIA JENNINGS. I am the wife of Thomas Jennings. On the 6th of May I saw this property safe in a box in my room about eight o'clock in the morning; I saw the prisoner at the bottom of the court at ten - I saw him again in my room at half-past eleven o'clock, getting out of my window; I was going into my room, and he pushed against the door - I waited some time, and saw him go from behind the door and stand in the middle of the room- I cried out, "Lord have mercy upon me;" Stephens came from the water-closet, and the prisoner made his way out at the window; I went in and shut the window, put the fastenings on it, and sent Stephens to tell my husband; I went and fetched Mr. Worrall to go into my room with me; I found my trunk broken open, and the articles stated taken from it; I saw the prisoner in about three hours.
   JOHN STEVENS. I was at Mr. Jennings'; I saw the prisoner climbing up at the window; I did not see whether he got out - I went to call Mr. Jennings.
   GEORGE HAZLEWOOD WORRALL. I am an officer. - I went to the house, and missed the things from the trunk in the lower room; I found this chisel just inside the door - we looked round, and found all the articles in this sack behind the door.
   GEORGE GODFREY. I heard of the robbery, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was an out-patient of St. Bartholomew-hospital; I went and got some pills there the day I was taken; I then went to Mrs. Elbury's and staid there from eleven o'clock in the morning till two in the afternoon; I then went to a public-house, and had a pint of half and half - Godfrey came in and said I was his prisoner - I went to Guildhall, and the woman first swore to  David Sullivan; she then described me, and swore to me.
 G. H. WORRALL. She said there was another person with him named Sullivan.
 JOHN STEVENS. I did not see any other person.
   MARIA ELBURY. I keep a green-grocer's shop at No. 15, Peter-street. I have known the prisoner about eight months - he lived in an up stairs room of mine about eight months ago; he has borne a good character. On the day he was taken up, he came to my house to ask me if the landlord had a room to let; I said Yes, and he said he would wait for the landlord; he waited from ten minutes past eleven o'clock till near two, and then went away, and I never saw him any more; I do not know what day of the week it was, but it was the beginning of May, about the 5th or 6th - I am quite sure it was the day he was taken up, because his father came the day after, to ask me if he had taken a room in the house - I am quite certain he did not move from the step of my door till two o'clock that day.
 JURY to A. S. JENNINGS. Q. What time did you see him in the court where you live? A. From ten o'clock till past eleven; I keep a stand in the court.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.

 WILLIAM TAYLOR. On Thursday last, about one o'clock in the day, I was near  Holborn-hill; I felt some one pulling at my pocket - I turned round, but did not observe anybody: a little boy told me that the prisoner had taken my handkerchief; he was then playing on the pavement with money; I challenged him with it - he denied it, and said if I would let go of his collar he would walk with me; I let him go; he ran from me - a gentleman stopped him.

 WILLIAM TAYLOR. My handkerchief was yellow, with white birds' eyes on it; I have not seen it since; this little boy seemed very much frightened.
