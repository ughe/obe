Evidence for the Defence.
Annie SOLOMONS. I live at Brushfield Street, Spitalfields, with my
father, the prisoner he can neither read nor write, but oan only sign
his name -he managed the manufacturing and the buying, and I looked
after the books and everything else--I did all the selling-if I was out
my father would do it-most of it was done at Brushfield Street--the
books kept were the sold day book, the sold ledger, a rough book, and a
bought ledger, but that was never written up-they were all imperfectly
kept-I was responsible for it--we did not keep any clerks-1 had so
much to do that I had not time to keep the books properly--we had a good
many cash transactions - if I took any money it was necessary to keep
it, because we had so many payments to make -I kept it in the unlocked
drawer of a desk-sometimes I had £100 or £80-workmen's wages were
paid out of that on Wednesdays we used to allow the men part of their
wages--we sometimes paid sixty or seventy men on Wednesdays-they
were gonerally poor--the money we received did not always go into the
bank-this (Produoed) is a requisition of the trustee-these are questions
asked with regard to goods supplied to certain firms I find no request
with regard to Mrs. Margolis, or Smith, or Barney Solomons-he is my
father's cousin - there is nothing about Mr. Cohen here-if those names
had been mentioned with the others, particulars would have been given;
being omitted in the requisition, they were overlooked-there was a
loan of £700 to my mother in respect of some houses by my father
she promised to pay him back-it was paid into the bank, and paid to the
creditors in due course --my mother was examined with regard to that
by the Official Receiver-no proceedings were taken in respect to it-I
answered the queries in the requisitions as well as I could we have been
dealing with Margolis for three or four years--they are boot and shoe
manufacturers-they have a factory--we have dealt both with Mr. and
Mrs. Margolis- they have only the one business, but we sold to both-
the business is hers- I think he is her manager- she is no relation to us
--their transactions were always for cash-We sold them unmanufactured
goods, not boots and shoes we sold them pulp board, which is used
instead of coardboard--this card (Produced). Wholesale boot and shoe
manufacturer and leather merchant," properly describes our business-
we do not keep separate books for the sale of leather-I had so many
people coming in and buying that I did not know who they were-my
father has sold leather for twenty years at a profit - we never sold under
oost price-I can remember selling leather for five years-we sold it to
the Avon Boot Company. G. B. Håll, of Colchester. Gluckstein. Sampson
& Company, and others. I remember selling some skin to Mrs. Margolis
about July to September, 1902-these (Prodwend) are original documents,
and show those sales: "Bought of Solomons, skins, £112, July 19th, 1902"
-the next one is July 26th, "Blue backs, £26, August ist, shoulders,
£29 88. 7d."- that is for the soles of boots--this (Produced) is a receipted
invoice of August 30th, for leather boards, which is pulp, £20 108.,
discount of 108. made it £20-September 18th, £84 Ds., with discount,
