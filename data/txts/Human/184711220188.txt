Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  Had you been introduced to him by a gentleman named Reynolds? A.  I had—he did not know anything about the quality or price of the rope—I had seen it when I offered the price for it—that was on the occasion when it was brought to my premises—my house adjoins St. Andrew's wharf—I have been in that business eighteen or nineteen years—I gave what I considered a fair price for the roe, and in deed, I refused a third lot, not considering it worth the money I had given—as far as I was concerned, this was a perfectly straightforward, honest transaction.
   MR. BODKIN. Q.  I believe the value of rope depends very much upon the confidence you can place in the maker of it? A.  Yes; I said 36 s . was a fair price, because I did not know the maker of it—Loup said he did not know the maker—that lessens the price until it is tested—I have since tested this rope, and consider it 35 per cent. below that which we sell—Lodge Brothers and Co. make our rope—that is 35 per cent. stronger, and was sold at 44 s . per cwt. at that time—it is difficult to tell what description of hemp this is made of, I should say the inside is little more than the bandages of bales of hemp—it is doubtful whether it is Petersburgh hemp, but it is im possible to say.
