1785. GEORGE CLARK, ANDREW MURPHY, CHARLES SAUS-
DERS, and EMMA FARMER, were indicted for burglariously breaking
and entering the dwelling-house of Robert Taylor, at St. Mary, Newington,
about 2 0'clock in the night of the 10th of May, with intent to steal, and
1 neck-chain, 88. ; 1 cushion, dd. ;
stealing therein, 2 bottles, value ll. 58. ;
17 tin checks, 4d.; 14 groats, 47 pence, 169 halfpence, and 6 farthings,
his property : 2nd COUNT, for burglariously breaking out of the said dwelling-
house; and that Clark and Farmer had been before convicted of felony.
ROBERT TAYLOR. I keep the Black-horse, Kent-street,St.Mary, Newington.
On the night of the 10th of May I went to bed about five minutes or a quarter
to one—I left the house fast—I cannot swear that the window was fast, but the
other part was—the policeman called me about four o'clock—I came down and
found parties had got in at the back window of the club-room,. come down,
opened the bar door, unlocked it, and gone out the back way—the articles
stated were taken—there was 138. in copper, 58. in sneer, a gilt chain, an
emery cushion, and 17 tin tickets or checks with my initials R. T." on them
—the window was on one pair of stairs, it looks into a yard-—I know the pri-
soner Saunders—he has used my house for the last four or five months.
Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q. You do not know whether the vin-
dow was open or shut? A. I know it was shut, but I do not know whe-
ther it was fastened—the catch will not fasten—I went and looked at it that
night.
BENJAMIN PARSONS (police-constable M-107.) On the morning of Satur-
day, the 1 Ith of May, about aquarter-past two, I was on duty in
and saw the prisoner Farmer in Black-horse-court, against Mr. Taylor's prea
mises—directly she saw me, she turned back and gave Clark a signal"
I could not understand what it was—I was not near enough—I went' up
to Clark, and saw Murphy come over the wall of Mr. Tailor's pre-
mises, on to Clark's shoulder in Black-horse-court, close against Farmer—I
took Clark into custody—Murphy made his escape into the Dover-road—I took
Clark with me, and saw a brother constable in the Dover-road—4 sung but,
Stop him," and he immediately laid hold of Murphy—we took them to the sta-
tion-—on Clark I found 88. 6d. in copper, fourteen check tickets, and one emery
cushion—which I produce—Farmer followed us to the station-house—when
we got there we took her—she was searched, and nothing found on her.
Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q. How long was it previous to seeing
Murphy coming down out of the yard that you saw Farmer? A. Just befor€
she went and gave Clark the signal—it was a signal they have got among
themselves—I was not near enough to tell what it was—I heard her say some-
thing, but could not understand what it was exactly—I could not understand
whether it was G' a crush" or 'c a bobby"—l cannot swear that I heard any words
Of that kind—I heard her say something—it was a signal to let them know a
policeman wag coming—I *ill undertake to swear Farmer had seen me-—she
was meeting me just as I was against the corner of the court—after she turned
back from me she made the signal—she was nine or ten yards off, not half a
stone's throw from the other prisoners—she was close against Clark, close
enough for him to hear what she said—she was very near the top of the court
when she saw me, and then she returned and gave Clark the signal.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Black-horse-court is a public tho:
roughfare, is it not ? A. Yes—one end is in Dover-road and the other in Kent.
—there five or six houses in the court—I do not know the exact length of it.
