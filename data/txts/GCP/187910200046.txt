EDWARD LATTER (City Detective). I served the summons on Bassil on
4th February, 1879, at Willesden Railway Junction-he raid "I am
innocent; I know nothing at all about it"-after a true bill was returned
by the Grand Jury I took Colliver on & warrant at Staines Railway
Station on 24th April, and took him before an Alderman at the Mansion
House next day-I found on him among other papers this letter from Mr.
Chambers to him, of July 26, and these two loose pieces of paper and this
letter from Mr. Johnson, of April 22, 1879; also this draft of a letter to Mr.
Chambers; I don't know whose writing it is I was here on 2nd May.
1879, when the trial was postponed, and Colliver was admitted to buil-I
attended here again on 30th June, and no trial took place-the first day of
the August Session Colliver did not surrender, and I took him in eastody
on 28th August at Staines Railway Station-Bassil did not appear, and I
was not able to find him, but he surrendered last Session-I found this
letter on Colliver. (This was dated August 2, and signed B., telling
Colliver that he had better be at the Court at the Session commencing ob
Tuesday, August 5).
Cross-examined by MR. KISCH. I knew Bassil very well-I looked for

know that he had an intimation that the case was not to be taken till
Thursday- I did not know that his place of business Was 8, Warwick
Court-I served him at Willesden Railway Junction one morning at 9.30.
