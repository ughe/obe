drop of gin-I refused-she called two girls, and they dragged me to the

GEORGE LOCKETT. I am a shoemaker, living at No. 4, John's place. I
was at the Victoria Theatre on the night in question-I saw the prisoner
pick up half a crown-I saw the last witness dragged into the theatre I
know nothing of the persons who did it—there were two or three girls
the prisoner was standing up against the side of the gallery-I saw her take
up the money she went towards the Victoria Tavern-I saw her after
wards taken into custody.

JAMES BRADLEY (policeman, L 93). I took the prisoner into custody
-she was not sober-she had been about the Victoria Theatre for an hour
and a half previous to that. .38. Witness for the Defence
CATHERINE CONWAY. I was at the Victoria Theatre on the night in
question, with my basket-I saw Mrs. Bassett come, and she asked me, did
I see her old man she was running up stairs, hallooing out for her hus-
banda policeman went up stairs, and she came down with him, and he
told her to go outside the door-two girls came up to this young girl (the
prosecutrix), and asked her if she would stand a quartern of gin, she said
no, she had no money—she was fumbling with the money, and she dropped
the half crown down on the floor-she made a reach to pick it up, and she
could not ; one of the girls, with a cloak on, came and shoved her, and this
young man (Lockett) came along with her, and picked it up-I said,
“Why do you not give her in charge !”—the prisoner went outside the
door she was given in charge the young man made a grab at the mo-
ney, and said, "If I was her I should give you in charge "-he said, “I
thought I could get it to give it to the owner " the prisoner was out
side the gallery door when the other two girls shoved the prosecutrix-
she did not take any part in pulling her into the gallery door-it was the
girl that had the cloak on that picked up the half crown.
GEORGE LOCKETT re-examined. I saw the half crown down-I did not
try to pick it up-I was there accidentally-I know none of the parties
who were there I am quite sure the prisoner is the woman who picked it
up she went away towards the Victoria Taverna policeman came up, and
she was given in charge that is what I saw.
SUSAN ABRAHAM re-examined. I saw the prisoner pick up the half crown
-it was knocked out of my hand she was one of them that did it- she
said "Give me a penny, I have got a penny, to get a half quartern of gin"
it is quite false what the witness Conway has said, because it is quite
round the corner where she was-I dropped the money inside the stairs.
