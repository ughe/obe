report of the state of the bank instead of a false one-have not looked at
the ledger, but as a general rule, I should say yes.
Q. I find on 17th November, 1856, " The accounts of liabilities and assets
for October and November at the head office were examined with the
general ledger; Present," among others, “Mr. Eyre," and on that occasion
you were in the chair; do you mean, upon the face of that entry, to swear
that you never saw the general ledger? A. Certainly not; I made a
mistake-having seen that entry, I have no doubt that the general ledger
was brought forward, but cannot say whether I examined it; it is nine
years ago I have not the slightest recollection of the fact I do not know
anything about seeing the general ledger-it is obvious that a new bank is
not expected to obtain all first-rate customers, and therefore we always went
through the securities for loans, and investigated the amounts of the loans-
the bills were brought before and after they had been discounted—when the
bad debts were brought before us, they were taken into consideration-
there was a weekly statement of the affairs of the bank-I took one of
them home with me from the Mansion House, but gave it back upon
application--that would, I consider, have given me full information upon
the affairs of the bank if I had looked into it-I do not know whether it
contained all the affairs of the bank, or whether, with its assistance, we
should have been able to send out a report free from misrepresentation-it
was a general statement, and there were different items--if we had gone
minutely into the statement of our secretary, no doubt it would have given
us the fullest information--though willing to take the word of our sub-
ordinates, I wished the weekly statement to be produced as a matter of
