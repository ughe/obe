575. GEORGE MASON was indicted for stealing 8 shirts, value 188.;
3 shifts, 148.; 4 gowns, 138. 6d.; 4 petticoats, 108.; 5 handkerchiefs,
10.s.; 2 blankets, 88.; 1 table-cloth, 78. ; 4 pairs of stockings, 16s.;
4 towels, 6s. ; 2 aprons, 68. ; 3 pinafores, 68.; 2 sheets, 48. ; 2 caps, 2s.
1 pillow-case, 18. ; 1 pair of drawers, 28.; and 1 basket, 28.; the goods
of Susannah Low, his mistress.
Susannah Low. I am single, and live in Gouldham-place, Chester-
street, Keunington. I am a laundress-on the 9th of Nov. I employed
the prisoner to carry a basket of linen to Mr. Pearce's, in St. Martin's.
Lane I had employed him on two or three previous occasions there were
in the basket the shirts, shifts, and other things stated they were worth
57. 108.--I have recovered about 21. worth of them--the articles were
under my care-I sent my mother with the prisoner-she returned with-
out him I did not see him again till the 4th of Jan. I had been paying
29. a week for the things from the time of the loss till the prisoner was
taken-I have seen some of them in Cox's hands.
SARAH Low. I live with my daughter. I went with the prisoner on
the evening of the 9th of Nov., about five o'clock, to take home a basket
of linen to Mr. Pearce's-when we got near the Female Orphan Asylum a
coach was coming by so fast that I could not keep up with him-I was
nearly run over-he went down Mount-row-I just caught a glimpse of
him, and then lost sight of him-I weņt on to Mr. Pearce's, as I thought
he might have been there, but he had not.

GEORGE SHARP. I am journeyman to Mr. Brown, a pawnbroker I
have some shirts, shifts, a child's blanket, and an apron, pawned on the
Ilth of Nov. by a woman, but I do not know who.
JAMES BROOK (police-constable L 118.) I took the prisoner in Chelsea-
workhouse-- I told him it was for stealing a basket of linen, belonging to a
laundress in Gouldham-place-be said he did not know any person living
there, and had stolen no linen-I produce some things, which I received
from two pawnbrokers, who gave them up to me.
SARAH Cox. I am single, and was in Lambeth-workhouse. On the 9th
of Nov. I slept at a lodging-house in King-street-the prisoner was there
that night-he had some linen, and asked me to pledge it-I pawned three
bedgowns, four shirts, and three pinafores for him--these are the articles
which the officer has produced-on the Monday following I pawned two
white petticoats for him-I went with another young woman, who pawned
the articles produced by Mr. Sharp-the prisoner said he got them from
Gouldham-place, Chester-street, Kennington.
Prisoner. I received the things, and went on to Westminster-bridge;
Cox and another woman stopped me, and got talking to me; we went into
a house, and had some beer; I got up, and in the meantime Cox and the
other woman came out with the basket; I came out, and asked them for
it; they said, no, they would carry it a bit further; they went away, and
I went to the lodging; they came home, and I asked Cox where the things
Were; she told me not to make a piece of work, she had pawned them.
