WILLIAM GILPIN. I am brother-in-law to James Bone. On 25th April,
I had occasion to leave the bar for a short time between twelve and one-I
shut the door after me I was absent two or three minutes-while I was away
I heard the door shut-1 looked through the glass and saw Hurson going
from the till out at the door-I went after him; he ran a short distance: I
overtook him-I saw Douglas in the middle of the road, more towards the
side of the house, ten or twelve yards off-Hurson called out some name to
him, and he advanced towards me-my impression was that Hurson called
him to strike me. I called for Davey, and he took Hurson into the house.
and I saw six shillings and seven sixpences come from his mouth-I had been
at the till two or three minutes before, and there was about that amount in it
I found it empty.

goods of Paul Margitson and others, his masters.
Mr. Parry conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE GOODWIN. I am foreman to Paul Margitson and Co., leather-
manufacturers, Weston-street, Bermondsey. The prisoner was employed
there as a servant in the warehouse for two or three years, and the last twelve
months working in the shed on 28th Feb. I received information from Stubbs,
and about seven in the morning went to search a small shop which is parted
off in the shed over the warehouse--the prisoner was down-stairs in the yard
when I began to search-he had come to work at half-past six ; his time was
six-I told him he had not come to bis time, and he must go back till after
breakfast - be came up to the workshop and saw me searching there and ab
sconded-I never saw him till his father brought him back, about 16th or 17th
April-I found a dozen French boot-fronts concealed in the workshop-these
are them--they are worth about 148. wholesale price.
