I am a private in the Artillery—I have written letters to the prisoner from his wife—I know this piece of a letter—I wrote it to the prisoner—I was at the bathing place that day—the prisoner wanted to leave the ranks—the bombardier would not let him go, he asked two or three times—we marched up to the front of the barracks, and stopped a quarter-of-an-hour or twenty minutes and a non-commissioned officer came and told the corporal we were to be detained—we were marched into the rear—the pri soner saw his comrades getting searched, and put something into his nouth—he said it was tobacco, but I can swear it was not—he left the ranks without being searched, and went out of the back-yard. MICHAEL GARVEY( policeman R  87.) I took the prisoner—I found on him this gold ring, one penny, one half-penny, a snuff-box, two purses, and an envelope box.
   The prisoner put in a written defence, declaring his innocence, and stating that part of the money was given him by his cousin, and part he had earned in his trade as a shoemaker; and that, being in liquor, he left it at the public-house for safety; that he afterwards took part of it out, and bought a ring and purse with it.
