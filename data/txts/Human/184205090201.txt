Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 1665. WATSON STAFFORD was indicted for obtaining 4 half-crowns, by false pretences; to which he pleaded GUILTY.* Aged.— Confined Twelve Months.
   Before Mr. Common Sergeant
 1666. SARAH DRAKE was indicted for a misdemeanor. MART ANN WILLIAMS. I am laundry-maid in the service of Mr. Catley, it Leyton, Essex. The prisoner was cook—I saw her about eight o'clock in the morning of the 6th of April in the kitchen—the appeared to be very ill—I saw her next day ill in bed—I examined her clothes, and I never saw clothes in such a state, unless a woman had been recently confined; and on the 18th my suspicions were confirmed, by seeing discharges of milk on her under-linen—I was satisfied there had been a birth.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Was the linen you saw on the 18th on the prisoner's person? A.  It came with her dirty clothes—they were brought to me by Clark—I am single. MARY CHAPMAN. I am the wife of George Chapman, of Leyton. The prisoner's linen was given me to wash on the 12th of April, by Clark—it had the appearance of the linen of a woman who had been confined. MAHALA CLARK. I received the linen from the prisoner in the kitchen—she was confined to her bed on the 6th, 7th, and 8th of April—I lent her a box on the 13th of April—I saw it afterwards tied up, and sealed with red—I cannot tell what was in It—it was heavier than when I lent it to her—it is here.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were her fellow-servant? A.  Yes—she had been in the service three months—I never said any thing to her about her being in the family way—I did not observe that she was—I heard no talk of it among the servants—I said nothing to her about her having a child when I saw the clothes—the linen was folded up when she gave it to me, and asked me to take it into the laundry—I did not see it at that time—there are three other female servants besides those who are here—I have heard the prisoner speak of her friends living at Nottingham. JOHN SLADE. I assisted the prisoner to tie up a box—I sealed it, and directed it to "Mr. Tipley, Knutsford Union, Cheshire"—I brought it to London, and sent it by the Birmingham Railway—I do not know what it contained—to the best of my belief, the box now produced is it JAMES M'CULLOCH. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into cus tody—she said, "What will become of me!"—she afterwards said that she had given birth to a child, but that it was dead born, and the reason she sent it into the country was because she did not know what to do with it. WILLIAM HARPER. I am a constable of Cheshire. In consequence of information, I went to the house of Mr. Catley, at Leyton, by order of the Coroner of Knutsford division, and searched the prisoner's room there—I saw some paper there, which corresponds with some which I got from Tipley, with part of a shawl, and a piece of fine linen—I brought the box from Cheshire. JOSEPH TIPLEY. I am porter at the Union Workhouse, Knutsford, Cheshire. I received this box—it contained the body of a dead child, wrapped up in this rag, part of a shawl, and paper. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined Six Months.
