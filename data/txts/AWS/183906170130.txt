1918. JOHN CASEY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of May,
coat, value 2s. 6d., the goods of John Boswell and JAMES SULLIVAN,
for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen
against the Statute, &e.
JANs BoswELLe. I am the wife of John Boswell, of Plumtree-court,
Shoe-lane. This is a morning dress-coat that I had to cut up for my chil
dren's things-I had a great coat and trowsers stolen out of the same place
but those we could not find-the coat was safe on the Thursday, about the
30th of May-Casey is my brother, and I have seen Sullivan with him.
Cross-examined by Ms. PAYNE. Q. You did not see your brother give
any thing to Sullivan A. No, but I missed my brother on the Thursday,
MARY BIRD. I live in Plumtree-court. About six or seven o'clock in
the evening of that Thursday, I was in Cow Cross, about ten minutes
walk from the prosecutor's, and saw the two prisoners together-Sullivat
had a bundle under his arm-I cannot tell what it contained.
JUDITH IsAACs. I live in Harrow-alley, Houndsditch. About five or
six o'clock that evening, the prisoners came along the Fair-Sullivan asked
me to buy this coat-heasked 1s. 6d. for it-I offered him 6d., and bought
it-Casey was with him.
Cross-examined. Q. How much is it worth A. Sixpence- gave
the full value-I sold it to a woman for 6d.
Casey's Defence. This man is innocent-I took it-it was lying on the
ground- sold it to the woman.
CASEY-GUILTY. Aged 16.-Confined Three Months.
SULLIVAN-NOT GUILTY.
