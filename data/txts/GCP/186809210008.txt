MESSRS. COLLINS and Grain conducted the Prosecution.
ALPRED COLE. I keep the Prince Regent public-house, in Seymon
Place, Bryanston Square-on 3rd September, about 11 o'clock at night, the
prisoner came to my house with a companion--I served him with a pint d
half-and-half, which came to 2d.-he tendered me a shilling- I gave him
the change, and put the shilling in the till where there was no other moner
but a sixpence-my niece, who was serving in the bar, afterwards gave me
bad shilling, and said in the prisoner's presence that he had given it to her
-I found it was bad the prisoner ran out of the house, I ran after him
brought him back, and gave him in charge, and handed the shilling to the
police.
ELLEN ELIZA BALLARD. I am the niece of last witness-on 3rd Sep
tember, I was serving in the bar-I saw the prisoner come in, and saw my
uncle serve him, and saw him put the shilling in the till—some little while
after, as my uncle was attending to someone else, the prisoner called for a
screw of tobacco, and gave me a bad shilling-I bent it and told him it was
bad-he said he did not know it was bad-while I was talking to my uncle
about it, the prisoner went out-my uncle followed and took him into
custody.
JAMES SAGAN (Policeman D 63). I received two shillings from Mr. Cole,
which I produce-I found two screws of tobacco on the prisoner.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am Inspector of Coin to the Mint-these shillings
are bad. GUILTY.-Nine Months' Imprisonment.

779. MARY ANN FINNIGAN (19), was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS. COLLINS and Grais conducted the Prosecution.
ALBERT LAYTON. I am shopman to Partridge & Co., stationers, of
Chancery Lane-on 10th July there the prisoner came for a half-quire of note
paper, and gave me a bad 28. piece-I called a constable and gave her into
Custody, and handed the florin to the policeman she was taken to the
Police Court, remanded, and discharged.
JAMES Mason. I am assistant at a Post Office in New Bond Street-on
Monday, 31st August, between 3 and 4 o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner
came for 58. worth of postage stamps-she put down a half-crown, two
shillings, and six pennyworth of coppers, in payment-she covered the silver
with the coppers- I found the half-crown was bad-I broke it, and told her
it was bad she said she was not aware of it-she said she had come for the
stamps for her mother-I gave her the half-crown back-she then said she
would only have 48. worth, and she gave me a 28. piece in exchange-I.
followed her to the door, and saw a man opposite, who I thought was con
nected with her-I gave her into custody, and she was brought back.
Prisoner. He mixed my half-crown with a lot more silver. Witness 1
did not; the half-erown never left my hand.
John MORGAN (Police Inspector C). I took the prisoner in charge-
asked her for the half crown, she gave it me at once it was broken.
GEORGE FLETCHER (City Policeman 476). On 10th July, I was called to

forin.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This florin and half-crown are bad.
