1117. ELLEN WALSH, stealing 4 pairs of trowsers, 1 scarf, and 8 pairs
of boots, value 51; the goods of James Wray, her master.
JAMES Wray. I live with my brother at 6, Lyon's-inn--the prisoner is
my laundress, and was employed occasionally in the rooms-I missed some
trowsers, a scarf, and some boots -- these are them (produced)--they are mine.
JOSEPH ELLIOTT. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a pair of trowsers
pledged by the prisoner on 12th April. TORO
SAMUEL HENRY FREEMAN, I am a pawnbroker, I produce two pairs of
boots, and two pairs of trowsers, and the duplicates of them.
WILLIAM West (policeman). I produce the corresponding duplicates-I
found them on the prisoner.
Prisoner's Defence. My master owed me 185.; I intended to get them
back when he paid me.
JAMES WRAY re-examined. She might have had money whenever she
asked for it-my brother used to pay her.
Guilty. Aged 21.-Confined Six Months.

1118. JAMES CANNON was indicted for embezzlement.
MR. Snow conducted the Prosecution.
Thomas COLLEY. I am a grocer, of Westminster; the prisoner was in my
employ. On 11th April, at half-past two in the afternoon, I gave him a
check, drawn by Mr. Watts, for 401. 138. ld., to take to the London and
Westminster Bank as soon as he could, and bring me the money in sove-
reigns-I did not hear of him till the 26th, when I received this letter from
him (produced)—it is in his writing, in pencil— I went to the bankers at four,
and found it had been paid-(the letter being read, stated that two men, who
had got change for a 51.-note at the bankers when the prisoner was there, met him
in the park by Spring-gardens, close to the cannon, and asked him for a piece
of writing-paper, and on his unbuttoning his pocket seized him and took the
money from him, and nearly strangled him; that he was afraid to return, and
was almost driven to self-destruction).
WILLIAM M'KENZIE BATH. I am a clerk in the London and Westminster
Bank. I produce a check for 401. 13s. 1d., drawn by Mr. Watts-I paid it
in gold on 11th April - I do not know to whom.
JOSEPH ROBERT PALMER.' On 11th April I was sentry at the cannon
in St. James's-park from two o'clock to four-I saw no struggle and heard
no cries-it could not have happened without my seeing it-it is part of my
duty to protect the cannon.
Prisoner. Q. Is there a sentry-box near the cannon? 4. Yes.
Guilty. Aged 18.--Confined One Year.
