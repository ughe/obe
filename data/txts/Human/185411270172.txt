Cross-examined. Q.  Did he say they were shipmates together? A.  He did; he said what he had done, he had done in his own defence; but he did not know whether he had stabbed him or not. JOHN FLINT SOUTH. I am senior surgeon of St. Thomas's Hospital. The deceased was brought there, between 4 and 5 o'clock, on 30th Oct.—he was then very drunk—I examined him, and found a wound on the left side of the nape of the neck, about one inch long; a wound on the back of the blade bone, on the left side of the spine, which was the important wound; a deep wound in the left loin; a superficial skin wound on the outside of the left knee, and two other superficial wounds—he died from wound of the lung—the wound between the spine and blade bone penetrated the lung—I found that afterwards, on a post mortem  examination—I have no doubt that was the cause of death—it is very probable that that wound might have been inflicted by a person lying below him, by striking round him over the shoulder—this dagger might produce such a wound.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The other wounds were not necessarily mortal? A.  No—the penetration of the lung, produced collapse of the lung, and caused—death. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury, an account of the great provocation he received.— Confined One Month Solitary .
