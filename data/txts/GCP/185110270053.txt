JAMES MITCHELL. I am cashier at Glyn's. I have not seen the prisoner
write, but I have received checks from him and paid them-I cannot say in
whose handwriting either of these three entries of the bills of 1131. 158., 1181.,
and 1001. are-I am more particularly acquainted with the signature" Josiah
Westley," and there does not appear to be a signature here I cannot speak
to any of the three entries.
JAMES Cooper re-examined I received this bill-book as clerk to Mr.
Groom, the official assignee, from the messenger, Johnson--his officer is
Cartwright, who brought it to me.
WILLIAM CARTWRIGHT. I am a messenger, in the Bankruptey Court. I
eave this bill-book to Mr. Cooper-I got it from the prisoner's premises I
went as the messenger of the Bankruptcy Court, and brought the prisoner's
books, and amongst them was this bill-book--this “ Josiah Westley," in
pencil, is our mark. (Mr. HUDDLESTON proposed to read the entries in the
bill-book, to which Messrs. CLARKSON and BALLANTINE objected, the entries
not being proved to be in the prisoner's writing that, as he must have been
examined at the Bankruptcy Court as to his books, the best evidence would be the
account he then gave on the subject, if he made any admission. MR. HUDDLE-
STON contended that the prisoner, having been a bankrupt, the books were found
by the regular oficer at the place where the prisoner carried on his business,
with such other books as a person would keep; and that, therefore, the prisoner
must be aware of the entries made in it. The Court did not consider that there
was evidence at present of the book being used by the prisoner in his own business.)
JAMES MITCHELL re-exmained. I believe these three papers to be memo-
randums of bills brought to be discounted-I should say they are in the
prisoner's writing, the first column and the names; not the outer column.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Am I to understand you to
say you believe those to be in the prisoner's writing? A. Yes; the name
"Josiah Westley," and, I should say, the column with the name of the bills
-I will undertake to pledge my belief on the subject.
Owen Thomas WILLIAMS re-examined. According to the business of our
house, when a parcel of bills are brought in for discounting we supply them with
foros-these are the three forms (those produced), which came in on the days
they bear date-(one of these was dated Feb. 28th, 1851; headed " Bills left
for discounting, by Josiah Westley," among which was one on Marler, for
1131. 158. The second was dated April 21st; a list of several bills, among which
was, "Gilbert, 1001." The third was dated 19th May, and among other names
was, "Marler, 115l." He requested that those bills might be placed to his credit,
by this letter-(read: “ Playhouse-yard, 19th May. Dear Sir, I shall feel
obliged by your placing the inclosed bills to my account, less discount. I hope to
have an interviero with you on Thursday. Yours sincerely, Josian WBSTLEY.")
