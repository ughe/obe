   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not go with me to Mr. Breton's when we drew two bills on him, I put my name first? he asked you whether it was true that I was partner with you, and you said, "Yes;" there is Mr. Breton in Court now; you signed your name under mine? A.  It is totally false—when the prisoner came to me he said he had not had a meal for two days, nor a lodg ing for two nights—I lodged and boarded him two days, and two nights, and on the third day he asked me to give him a job—we gave him a job. CHARLES ALLEN. I went with Mr. Price and the prisoner to the shop in the Westminster-bridge-road—we then went to a coffee shop, and the prisoner said, "You must go at seven o'clock for your money, I sold them for 3 l .;"—I have worked for Mr. Price five months—I never heard of the prisoner being a partner—I have seen him paid his wages 5 s . a week—he worked for 5 s . a week, and I have seen him paid his wages. JOHN REED. I am shopman to Mr. James Bedell. On the 28th of Oct., about five o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came with three washhand-stands—I went and spoke to Mr. Bedell about them—I bought them for 2 l .; 10 s .—I paid the prisoner, and have the receipt in his writing. HENRY TURPIN( police-constable G  119.) I took the prisoner—I told him it was for running away with the stands of Mr. Price—he said, "That is all right; it is three months ago."
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to work for Mr. Price, and after some time my father was going to the Cape of Good Hope, and Price, not knowing the cabinet business, told me if I would take 5 s . a week, and board, lodging, and washing, I should share in the profits, as it was all my connexion; I said, "Very well;" if Mr. Stallybrass is here, he knows the bills were made out in my name; we drew two separate bills on a customer, and there is my name first on the bills; I paid them both to Mr. Stallybrass, and the bills were, "To Messrs. Griffiths, and L. and It. Price;" this was the only card I could get from a friend; the other cards were taken away, and the invoices and bills with my name on them. ROBERT FICKLING. I am a cabinet-maker, and have lived upwards of two years at No. 36, Brown's-lane, Spitalfields. I was employed by the prisoner—I was not then aware it was a partnership, but I understood three or four days afterwards that it was a partnership—the prisoner employed me—I worked piece work—I was employed in making washhand-standa I believe—I am sure I was, and I made dressing-tables—the same person employed me to do them—no other person employed me—the prisoner was the active man for the partnership concern as I understood—I was paid sometimes by Price, and sometimes by the prisoner—Robert Price said, "You must not suppose this is all Mr. Griffith's concern; I am the principal partner."
   COURT . Q.  Are you sure he said these words, "I am the partner?" A.  No, his elder brother, Mr. Lemuel Price is the elder partner—he said, "It is ours"—his elder brother and his—I always understood the prisoner was partner.
