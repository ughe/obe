a day or two at Albany-street, before they went to market.
Q. Was there any reason to kill them and send them to market except
as a matter of sale? A. I should think not-I was present when a lot of
pigs were bought and sent to Hatfield-I cannot say when that was-1
believe it was before the bankruptcy-I do not know how many pigs there
were-there might be about six or eight--they were purchased in Smith-
field, put into the cart, and directed to be taken to Hatfield--I was not
present at Pinner when a very large pig was killed- I know Winton's, the
Fitzroy Arms, in Cleveland-street- very large pig was taken from there
to Pinner in 1835-the prisoner was present at the time he had a farm
at Pinner---there was a farm-yard, but no grazing stock - there were seven
acres of meadow land I know the pig was killed because I had part of it
myself of the prisoner he was in the habit of buying grains and pollard
for pigs-those things are used to fatten pigs for market I was present
when he bought two or three hundred weight of grains in Drury-lane-I
cannot say that that was in 1835—it was before he was declared a bank.
rupt-I fetched a cart load of grains from Satchell's brewery, in Winslow-
street--I suppose the pigs he bought were for profit--I should say I have
known him buy from twenty to thirty pigs altogether, from ten to twelve
times--I have frequently heard him talk of his knowledge of buying pigs
I never heard him say he got any profit by them--I remember when the
fiat was issued against him in December, 1835-he was about town for two
months before that, to keep out of the way to avoid being arrested-he bas
