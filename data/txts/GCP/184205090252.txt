ROBERT PAYLING (police-constable V 248). I was on duty in the Rich-
mond-road on Sunday, the 3rd of April, and saw the prisoner about twenty
minutes or half-past six o'clock in the evening, near Priest-bridge, about
half a mile from Putney Park-lane-I knew him before as Mr. Shiell's
coachman-he was going towards Putney Park-lane-there was a female
with him-I did not know her-she had a grey bonnet on like the one
produced, and a dark coloured gown similar to this-they were walking
arm-in-arm, and the woman was conversing in a very loud tone, loud
enough to make me suppose they were not on good terms- I could hear
them a great many rods after I passed them.
Cross-examined. Q. She was speaking loudly to him? 4. Yes, they
were apparently quarrelling-he did not speak so loud as her—they ap-
peared having words with each other by her discourse--as far as I thought
it was her finding fault with him.
FANNY HESTER re-examined. I live about a mile from Roehampton,
close to Priest-bridge-they left me about half-past five or near six.
GEORGE ROYAL. I keep the King's Head, Roehampton, a mile and s
half from Priest-bridge, and about three quarters of a mile from Mr.
Shiell's house--a person going from Priest-bridge to Mr. Shiell's, would
not pass my house-I know the prisoner-I saw him on Sunday afternoon,
the 3rd of April, at my house about seven o'clock-it might be a little
before seven-I cannot speak to half an hour, there was a female with
him who I had never seen before she had a light bonnet on, that is all I
can say of her dress they had two glasses of gin and water--the woman
drank-the prisoner pressed her to drink several times, and she seemed to
drink with reluctance, as if she did not wish to take it, as she put it away
from her when he offered it, more than once--they staid at my house about
an hour, and left together about eight-I did not see in what direction
they went.
Cross-examined. Q. Was you in their company all the time they were
there? A. I was in and out of the room-I noticed the female--she was
sitting opposite as I entered the room-she did not as par dejected, that
I observed, but reluctant to drink.
