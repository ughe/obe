JAMES HAYWARD. I am a pawnbroker. I produce an opera glass
pawned on the 6th of April, for 3s., in the name of John Maddocks-it
was a butcher-boy, and I believe it was the prisoner-here is the counter-
part of my duplicate.
THOMAS JAMES CAVANNAH. I am shopman to Mr. Cole, pawnbroker,
Waterloo-road, Lambeth. I produce a metal watch pawned on the 6th of
April, in the name of James Maddocks, by a butcher-boy, who I believe
to be the prisoner, from his dress, size, and complexion--I have no recol-
lection of his features-here is the counterpart of my duplicate.
John FALLIS (police-constable M 122.) I took the prisoner on Monday,
the 13th of May, and found six duplicates on him, one for the watch, one for
the opera-glass--I found four keys, a ring, and one half-crown, five shillings,
and one sixpence, all bad money-one of the keys unlocked the drawers-
I compared the screw-driver with the marks on the desk-they appeared
to have been done by that.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined Six Months.
Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
1707. WILLIAM RICHARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th
of May, 14lb. weight of tobacco, value 5s., the goods of Charles Davies,
his master.
JAMES STEELE. I am in the employ of Mr. Charles Davies, a tobacco
manufacturer, of Coles-street, Dover-road. The prisoner was in the em-
ploy of Mr. Farebrother, who is in Mr. Davies' service-it is Mr. Davies'
practice to rub persons down as they leave work-on the evening of the
7th of May, on rubbing down the prisoner he discovered a piece of cigar-
he then ordered me to search him, and I took this roll of tobacco from him
-it was concealed in his trowsers--here is 14b. weight-it is worth 58.
WILLIAM STOTTER (police-constable M 136.) I took the prisoner-I
found a quantity of loose leaf tobacco in his cap-this is it.
JAMES STEEL. We had tobacco like this in the loft.
Prisoner. I did not have it at all.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
