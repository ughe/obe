1 pair of trowsers, value 78., the goods of Robert Watts. ne t
JAMES HARMAN. I am servant to Robert Watts, a pawnbroker, in Ex-
mouth street. About four o'clock in the afternoon, on the 7th of Novem-
ber, the prisoner was in my shop, she asked for a child's froek while she was
talking about it, I saw her take a pair of trowsers, fold them up, and put them
under her shawl-I went in to tell the young man, and when I came out
the prisoner was running up the street-I stopped her, and gave her in
cbarge-I found the trowsers on her they are my master's tot 02
Cross-examined by Mr. PanLLIPS. Q. How near were you to the shop
when the policeman came up ? A. About 100 yards I had the trowsers
at that time they had been banging on a railshe had no child with her,
she gave me the trowsers after my inspecting them—she told the policeman
she did not mean to steal them.
(MR. PHILLIPS stated that the prisoner had only taken up the frowsers
to look at. She received a good character.)
