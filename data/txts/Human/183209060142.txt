 2058.  JACOB ATTRELL was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , 160lbs. of sugar, value 4l., the goods of John Kerrison , his master.
   WILLIAM KERRISON. I live in the Commercial-road; I manage the business for my father, John Kerrison- he is a carman; the prisoner was in his service. On the 11th of August he lost 1 cwt. 2 qrs. 10 lbs. of sugar; I heard that evening that the prisoner was in the station - the prisoner had had a good character.
   JOSEPH SALMON. I am a gate-keeper at the Leith and Berwick-wharf, Irongate. On the 11th of August I had information that there was something amiss in Mr. Kerrison's waggon, which was coming with a tierce - I struck the tierce from the waggon; I put my hand into the waggon, and found under the tilt a bag of sugar; the prisoner was driving the waggon - I called the officer, and gave charge of him; there was 1 cwt. 2 qrs. 10 lbs. of sugar in the bag, which is about 160 lbs.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you in the employ of the prosecutor? A. No; the tierce was in his waggon, and was going to Scotland.
   CHARLES SKEATE. I am foreman to Mr. Kerrison. I sent the prisoner with the sugar to the Leith and Berwick-wharf, in Mr. Kerrison's waggon; I do not know the weight of it - there was no tilt in the waggon.
   GEORGE MOSS. I am clerk at the Leith and Berwick-wharf. I examined the cask when the prisoner brought it; I found the tin which covers the samplehole had been partly removed - I had the cask weighed, and it was 9 cwt. 2 qrs. 10 lbs.; I had no weight delivered to me but the import weight on the cask, and supposing that to be correct, the deficiency would be 1 cwt. 2 qrs. 15 lbs., including the weight of the cask, which might be 20 lbs. or more - I took a sample of sugar from the cask, and compared it at the Thames Police-office, with that found in the bag - they corresponded and I believe it was of the same bulk.
 Cross-examined. Q. It was brown sugar, I suppose? A. Yes.
   ROBERT LUSBY. I am a delivery foreman at the West India-docks. I delivered the cask on the 11th of August into Mr. Kerrison's waggon; it was about three o'clock - not to the prisoner, but to another carman; the weight at which it came into the dock was 11 cwt. 25 lbs., and I delivered it at 11 cwt. 20 lbs., a deficiency of 5 lbs, for drainage - I was close to the man who weighed it - I saw it weighed again at the Leith and Berwick-wharf, and it weighed 9 cwt. 2qrs. 10 lbs., which was a deficiency of 1 cwt. 2 qrs. 10 lbs., or 178 lb.
 Cross-examined. Q. Whether any sugar was taken from the cask before the prisoner had it, you cannot tell? A. No.
 THOMAS BOWLES. I live in Bedford-street. I brought the tierce from the dock to Leman-street, Goodman's-fields; it was then unbroken, and there was no sugar in a bag in the cart - the prisoner took it from me at Leman-street, to go to the Leith and Berwick-wharf,
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you remain with it while the waggon was in the street? A. I took out my horses, went away; and the prisoner came; it was then about five o'clock in the evening.
 ROBERT  HENRY BURGESS. I am a street-keeper. I was near the Leith and Berwick-wharf, and was called in and took the prisoner - I took the waggon and horses home to Mr. Kerrison's; the foreman put his hand on the tilt, and said, "This does not belong to us" - he opened it, and found on it the name of W. Jury, Curtain-road.
