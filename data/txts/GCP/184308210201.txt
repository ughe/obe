9511. ANN HEWITT and SARAH BENNETT were indicted for steal-
ine on the 24th of Aug., 1 petticoat, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Robert
Watt: and that Hewitt had been before convicted of felony: to which
BENNETT pleaded Guilty.-Transported for Seven Years.
MARIA STEVENS. I live in Clerkenwell. On the 24th of Aug. I was
rassing the prosecutor's in Exmouth-street, and I saw the prisoners together
I saw Hewitt take a petticoat from the side of the rail at the side of the
shop, and put it under her shawl-they both walked away-I gave informa-
tion--they were taken, and the petticoat found on them.
Hewitt. Q. Did you not say at first it was Bennett took it, and afterwards
say it was me? A. No.
JAMES HARMAN. I live with Robert Watt, in Exmouth-street. I received
information, and ran after the prisoners--when I got close to them Hewitt
took the petticoat from under her shawl, and threw it in at a door way-this
is my master's.
RICHARD BYRNE (police-constable E 116.) I produce a certificate of Hew-
itt's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office-(read)-the pri-
soner is the person.
HEWITT-Guilty.--Transported for Seven Years.
2512. RICHARD WEST was indieted for stealing, on the 28th of Feb.,
7 pairs of trowsers, value 158.; and 2 waistcoats, 58.; the goods of Fanny
Payne.
FANNY PAYNE. I am a widow, and live in John's-row, St. Luke's. About
one o'clock, on the 28th of Feb., I gave the prisoner seven pairs of trowsers
and two waistcoats to pawn for me-he did not come back--I have never seen
either them, or the money since.
Prisoner's Defence. I got a drop of drink and lost them.
GUILTY. Aged 66.-Confined Nine Months.
2513. HENRY DETMAR was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of Aug.,
1 waistcoat, value 98., the goods of George Stewart ; also, on the 23rd of
Aug., 2 shirts, 68., the goods of Lanceman Webster; to both which he pleaded
Guilty.-Confined Three Months.
2514. ELIZA NIGHTINGALE BROWN was indicted for stealing, on
the 10th of June, 4 blankets, value 21.; 2 sheets, 148.; 1 snuff-box, 6d.; and
printed book, 1d.; the goods of John Forrester, her master : and ELIZA
BASSETT BROWN, for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them
to have been stolen.
JOHN FORRESTER. I live in Compton-street. The prisoner Nightingale
worked for me I missed a sheet from the bed on which she slept, and the
blanket from the bed underneath-I asked her where they were she made
no reply-I pressed for an answer, and then she said she took them to her.
mother's to wash.
Cross-examined by MR. DOWLING. Q. Did you say it would be better to
tell the truth ? A. No-she was never required to wash the bedding of her
own bed-this snuff-box and book are mine-they are worth about 7d.
THOMAS HOOKER. I am a pawnbroker- produce a sheet and blanket-
I took in the blanket of Bassett Brown.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. She had pawned a great number
of things, and taken them out ? A. Yes I have known her about twelve
months.
