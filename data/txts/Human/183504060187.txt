WILLIAM SAVAGE. I am a market-gardener, and live in Lower-road, Charlton. I have a sample of weeds here—I got it from 'Squire Bedford's hay-stack, "yesterday morning—it is a common weed, which will grow any where—I can pick it out of my own garden—it will grow in any soil—I have known the prisoner five years—he always bore a good character.
   Q.  Did you ever go to the 'Squire's hay-stack before? A.  Never; I went because the prisoner asked me for a character—I put my hand in the stack and pulled the weed out.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Had you heard that Mr. Strother had sworn to the hay, by a particular kind of weed? A.  No, I had not; I brought the weed here to show it.
   JURY . Q.  What made you take that weed out the rick? A.  The same sort of weed was produced to the Court, and Mr. Strother said it grew on his marshes. JOSEPH BLAND. I am a labourer. I remember meeting young Tay lor in the Kent-road, on the 17th of March, between the Dun Cow, and the Little Castle, close by the turnpike—the World turned upside down, is nearer London—there is a trough for horses to drink there—he had some hay in his cart—seven or eight trusses—I did not count them—I have known the prisoner four years—he is a hard-working, industrious man.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Were you in any employment at the time? A.  I was going to Church-street, Spitalfields, with a load of lime, for Mr. Glin ton—I have met the prisoner's son with his fruit-cart—I do not know that I ever met him with hay above twice—this was on the 17th of March, St. Patrick's Day—I was in town the day before—I come up almost every day in the week—he spoke to me as he passed—I think there was a load of straw in his cart besides—I cannot say whether there was any turnips or not.
   COURT . Q.  Has any body been talking to you about the World turned upside down, before you came into Court? A.  No, nobody—I met him about eleven o'clock, as near as I can say—Charlton is between five and six miles from London.
