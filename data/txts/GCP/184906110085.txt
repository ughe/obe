1321. JOHN FROST, stealing 1 pair of boots, value 38.; the goods of
the directors of the poor of the parish of St. Pancras : and MARY ANN
WARD, feloniously receiving the same.
SARAH ANTHONY. I am single. On 7th May, between four and five
o'clock in the evening, I was in St. Giles's burial-ground--a portion of
St. Pancras' workhouse looks into the burial-ground-I saw Frost at the
workhouse window-he whistled, which attracted Ward's attention, and she
came up-Frost got out of the window on the leads, and I saw him with a
pair of boots in his band-he threw them into the burial-ground, and Ward
took them up some conversation passed between them-I did not hear what
it was-they conversed about ten minutes- I left the ground in Ward's com-
pany-I saw her take the boots away-she said she was going to take them
home--I did not see Frost afterwards.
WILLIAM M'Lachlin. I am a pawnbroker. I produce these boots, which
I took in from Ward.
GEORGE JOHN FARRANT. I am master of the stone-yard at St. Pancras'
workhouse. I know these boots, they are the property of the directors of
the poor of that parish-on 7th May, Frost was an inmate there-Ward had
been in the house, but she was then sleeping in the casual ward - I afterwards
took her at No. 10, Phillips'-buildings--I told her she was charged with
receiving a pair of boots of Frost-her mother gave me the duplicate of them.
WILLIAM JOHN BROWN (policeman, S 67). I took Frost at the workhouse
-I asked if he knew what he was charged with-he said he did; he took the
boots for the purpose of wearing them when he came out.
Frost, Q. Did I not say that the boots were given to me? 4. You did
say so afterwards, but not then.
Prost's Defence. On 24th April I was admitted into the infirmary for an
affection of the lungs, which I have been suffering from yearly, and which
incapacitates me for labour; I applied to Mr. Eaton for a pair of shoes; I
received these now produced from Mr. Dalton; on the Wednesday following
Ward called to see me, and was refused admittance; I saw her at the window,
and she was telling me of her distress; I gave her the shoes, telling ber they were
mine, and as such I deemed them, baring had another pair given me which I
could wear; if I have done wrong I am sorry for it.
STEPHEN BULMER Dalton. I did not give him any shoes at all.
Frost. I applied to Mr. Eaton for them; bis attention was called away by
the baker, and a gentleman said, "Frost wants a pair of boots," and you took
these and gave them to me.
