   Cross-examined.  I know Guest's house—I have been in there occasionally—I was not in there with my master that very afternoon—I deny that I have had drink with the prisoner—I have seen him, I think, for the last four or five months standing about—I have been in the prosecutor's employ about six months—the prisoner has not been employed by him to my knowledge. GEORGE CORNISH( Sergeant L ) I arrested the prisoner on December 28th on a charge of highway robbery with violence—he said, "All right" when I told him the charge—on the way to the station he said, "I did. punch him in the nose, but I did not steal his money"—at the station he said, "I was the worse for drink or I should not have done it."
   Cross-examined.  I have heard the prosecutor has been convicted of assault—I do not know the circumstances.'
   The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he had done odd jobs for the prosecutor, getting  1 s. or  1 s . 6 d. a time; that he had had drink with him and Saunders in Quest's; that on the day in question he had two drinks with the prosecutor in the Horn and Groom; that a female came in there and the prosecutor asked her to have a drink, but the barman refused to serve it and they all then left; that they got to Chester Street, when the prosecutor said, "Are you going to pay?" that he replied that he had no money, when the prosecutor and the carman both struck him; and that he (the prisoner) them went away and was at home before  7 o'clock.
   Evidence for the Defence. CHARLES SHADBOLT. I know nothing about the actual fight—on December 23rd I was at the prisoner's place, waiting for him at 7 o'clock, when he came in—we had to meet a mend at 7.30.
   Cross-examined.  I saw the prisoner that night between a minute or two off 7 o'clock—our appointment was fifteen minutes' walk from there—it would have taken half an hour to walk from Chester Street. JAMES HENRY SMITH. On the Saturday before Christmas I was assis tant potman at the Horse and Groom, Newington Butts—I saw the prisoner there with the prosecutor and two others and a woman—I cannot call to mind Saunders being there—it was about 4 p.m.—they were all drinking together—they were refused further drink owing to a woman being served—the prosecutor then made a remark that he had seen me previously at Dartmoor.
   By the COURT . The parties here are all strangers to me—that was the first time I had seen any of them—I am quite sure that the prisoner was one of the men and the prosecutor another, and that they were drinking together.
   Cross-examined.  I have only seen the prosecutor once; I do not think I am mistaken.
   Re-examined.  Why I remember him is the remark he made to me—I have never been at Dartmoor.
