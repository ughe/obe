953. CHARLES ROBERTS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of February , 1 jacket, value 5s., and 1 pair of trousers, value 6s., the goods of William Hall , from the person of Henry Hall.
   DAVID DILLON. I am six years and a half old - I go to church sometimes; I live with my father, in Norwich-court, Fetter-lane. I remember playing with Henry Hall at the corner of Graystoke-place - the prisoner came, and told Henry Hall to go and find his donkey; he went, and the prisoner went after him - when they got to the top of Fetter-lane, I saw the prisoner take Henry Hall on his shoulder, and he went away; Hall had his clothes on then, his pinafore, shoes and stockings - I saw him in the evening when he came home; he had only his shirt, shoes and pinafore, and his sister's pelisse on - the prisoner had a black coat on at first; when I saw him again he had a white coat.
   WILLIAM HALL. I live at No. 2, Norwich-court, and am an ornamental painter- my son Henry in six years old. I saw him on the 14th of February; he had on his stockings, his boots, a shirt, a suit of cloth clothes, a pinafore over them, and a cap; I lost sight of him about two o'clock when I went to my business, and did not return till five -I saw my son again about six o'clock: he then had on his stockings, his boots, a pinafore, and his sister's pelisse, which was sent to fetch him home in; his suit of clothes were gone.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you ever see me? A. Not to my knowledge.
   GEORGE HALL. I saw my brother at dinner time, with a dark suit of clothes on - he was playing in the court at one o'clock; I afterwards went to the White Horse, in Twister's-alley, Whitecross-street - I saw him there in the kitchen, without jacket or trousers on; I brought him home.
   SUSAN SEVERIDGE. I am the wife of Anthony Severidge, of Graham's-building, Blue Anchor-alley, Bunhill-row. I found Henry Hall on the Monday, in the privy of the yard belonging to the apartment - he had only his shirt, shoes, and stockings on, and his cap and pinafore in his hand; I took him in doors, and he told me where his parents lived - this was about four o'clock, and I suppose a mile and a half from his parents.
     NOT GUILTY. (See page 416.)
 954.  ANN PRITCHARD was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 1 bonnet, value 5s., the goods of Edward Barringer.
   EDWARD BARRINGER. I live in Dean-street, Westminster- my wife keeps a bonnet-shop. On the afternoon of the 24th of February I missed a bonnet, between three and four o'clock, which I had seen safe at two; I went to Orchard-street, and saw the prisoner with the bonnet on.
   WILLIAM SCHOFIELD. I am a plumber, and live in Dean-street. On the 24th of February I saw the prisoner pass my shop, and go into the prosecutor's; she came out with such a bonnet as this - I believe it to be the same.
   THOMAS CREA. I am a Police-constable. About three o'clock, on the 24th of February, the prisoner was given to me by the prosecutor.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she was intoxicated, and had found the bonnet on the floor.
     GUILTY. Aged 57. - Confined One Month.
 955.  RICHARD TRINDELL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of March , 1 jacket, value 2s., and 1 pair of trousers, value 2s., the goods of John Sedwell.
   JOHN SEDWELL. I am a gunner in the Royal Artillery; I occupy the adjoining room to Doubleday (see the next case.)I left it between two and three o'clock, and my box was safe - I returned at eight in the evening, and missed my stable-dress, a pair of trousers, a jacket, and waistcoat.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I am a Police-constable. I found the jacket and trousers on the prisoner at the New - way watch-house on the 31st of March.(Property produced and sworn 10.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought these things of a man for 3s, as I was going to Greenwich, at half-past seven or near eight o'clock on Sunday night.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven years.
 956.  JAMES TURNEY was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of March , 1 watch, value 2l., and 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of Henry Doubleday.
   HENRY DOUBLEADAY. I am a private in the Royal Artillery; the prisoner belonged to the same corps, and lodged in the same room at Woolwich. On the 13th of March I went out, and left a silver watch, wrapped up in a white shirt, in my box- I returned about eight o'clock, and missed my watch and 5s.6d. in money; the prisoner was than missing - I saw him in about four days afterwards, at Woolwich; I lost two double handkerchiefs - I had left the money in my knapsack; no one was present when I put it away.
   THOMAS RIDPATH. I am a pawnbroker, in Upper Seymour-street, Euston-square. The watch was pawned with me on the 19th of March, by Thomas Hintridge for 17s.
   THOMAS HINTRIDGE. I live at the Southampton Arms, Camden-town. The prisoner came into the room, and had a pot of porter - he offered a watch for sale, and asked 25s. for it; I offered him 1s - he said he would not take 1l., he could pawn it for that; he went out, and the next day he returned, produced a duplicate, and offered it me for half a crown - I gave him 1s. 6d.for it; I sent Richard Griffin for the watch.
   RICHARD GRIFFIN. I received the duplicate - I went to Battle-bridge, and took the watch out of pawn, for a guinea and 6d; I gave it to Hintridge.
   THOMAS HINTRIDGE. I kept it two days, and then pawned it for 17s. at Mr. Ridpath's.
   HENRY DOUBLEDAY. I can swear to this handkerchief by the pattern and colour - I know the watch by two cracks on the face; I have worn it some years.
 Q. When did you see it again? A. on Wednesday last, and I lost it a month ago last Sunday - it has two seals and a key, which I know.
     GUILTY. Aged 23 - Transported for Seven years.
