To Mr. Marshall Hall. I have looked through the letter book 13,
one of the many letter books found at Tokenhouse Buildings, and
there is not any letter there in my handwriting. I have also looked
through all the 10 or 12 letter books, and there is no letter in my
handwriting. There are a great many accounts that are not in my
handwriting. Those that are in my handwriting are the ones that
I dissected from the accounts received from stockbrokers when I
dissected the amount. In taking out the amount I took out Yor
Smith the other items that appeared; they are protected accounts
that had been opened with stockbrokers.
To Mr. Justice Lawrence. On pay day there were two or three
brokers' accounts, which bad to be dissected to see what my share
was, and also in those brokers' accounts there are coverings for some
of these clients, which necessitated an account being made out iden-
tical with the statement in the stockbrokers' accounts; so when I
took out my portion of the account I took out certain other accounts
there for the purpose of sending to Herbert Smith's olients. The ac-
counts that I have seen in this book are ones that were coverings
and appear in the accounts that the stockbrokers tendered to Herbert
Smith.
To Mr. Marshall Hall. As to those that I have called out to the
Judge, where it says "Paid," I have traced cheques out of Herbert
Smith's account; they total to between £900 and £1,000, in this book,
paid out to clients between October 9 and December 11, 1903. With
regard to Sun Street, the only letters that went from there appear to
be letters of April 5 and 10. My connection with Bleach was in
this manner. I suppose that 20 years ago I knew Bleach's father
and elder brother, who was foreman printer at the printing works
where my paper was published. He died, I think, about 1892 or
1893, and then his brother, the witness Bleach, took his place, and
after the Financial Observer” and “Mining Herald” was stopped
Bleach has only had from me company work or my own private work.
He was an old servant, and he had gone out of his way to get him
work. Since he had had no regular income from his (witness's) paper
he had been very poor. Witness had continually lent him money.
With regard to the 125 cheques, as to which he originally
swore were paid to him, that is not so, because all the
earlier ones were paid to his father or his brother, because
they were matters connected with the printing of the "Financial
Observer." Bleach said on the second examination that they
amounted in all to about £300, of which £60 or £70 were
cheques he had from me to change for himself, and the balance, £230,
had to be accounted as money lent. (To the Judge.) With regard
