 475.  DAVID REES was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of December , 1 canvas bag, value 6d.; 4 sovereigns, 2 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the property of John Jones , from his person.
 JOHN JONES. On the 14th of December, about half-past six o'clock in the evening, I was at the door of the  Lock and Key public-house, in Smithfield; my purse, containing this money, was tied up, and in my breeches pocket - the prisoner came up, and said, "John Jones, how are you this long time?" I said middling; he asked if I would stand treat; I said No; but I had a pot of beer, and he was welcome to that; he asked me to lend him a shilling- I took out the purse - he snatched it, and ran away - he was not stopped.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you mean to say you knew the prisoner before? A. Yes; I was quite sober; I ran as far as Long-lane, and then missed him; I did not call Stop thief! the Magistrate did not ask if I made an alarm, nor did I tell him - I thought it was done in fun, and expected he would come back; the prisoner had high shoes on - they were half-boots.
   DAVID EVANS. I saw Jones at the Lock and Key on the 15th; he described the prisoner to me, and I took him- he tried to get from me - I took Jones to the prisoner's house; I asked the prisoner if he knew Jones; he said No; I called the prosecutor, who said to him, "Give me the money, that is all I want;" he said he would swear he was the man; I found nothing on him; he said he never saw Jones in his life; but afterwards said he had known him twenty-seven years ago.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you and the prisoner ever quarrelled? A. Yes, once - I had him once before a Magistrate.
   ABRAHAM GRIFFITHS. I went with Evans to apprehend the prisoner on the 15th of December - Evans asked if he knew Coran, I think - it was a Welch name, which I forget - it was not Jones.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he make any resistance? A. Yes, when we got to Smithfield he did.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   THOMAS GODDARD. I am a green-grocer, and live at No. 23, Woodbridge-street. I have known the prisoner thirteen or fourteen years. On the evening of the 14th of December, at a quarter to five o'clock I went to the prisoner's house, and asked him what time it was; he told me about five; I went in, drew the clock up, came out again to smoke my pipe, and the parish clock struck five; I walked up stairs, and saw the prisoner looking out of his window, with a night-cap on; I called him; he came down; we stood looking at the men killing sheep, and were together till a quarter or half-past seven o'clock.
 COURT. Q. Can you be sure you saw him from five to seven o'clock? A. Yes. He was taken up the next day.
   FRANCIS ROBINSON. I am a glass-cutter, and live in White-cross-street. I have known the prisoner six or seven years. On Friday the 14th of December I took him a letter from his sister, who lives in my house, at a quarter to four o'clock, and staid with him and Goddard till ten minutes past seven.
     NOT GUILTY.
 476.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of December , 1 seal, value 1s.; 1 watch-key, value 5s. 6d.; and part of a watch-chain, value 6d., the goods of Richard Sellers Yarnall , from his person.
 RICHARD SELLERS YARNALL. On the evening of the 23d of December I was at the corner of  Short-street, Moorfields- a man, in the habit of a sailor, who I believe was the prisoner, came up, made a snatch at my watch, and got part of the chain, seal, and key - I had a heavy bundle, and could not pursue very quick; I lost sight of him, but my son and other persons pursued, and in about three minutes the prisoner was brought back.
   WILLIAM SEAL YARNALL. I am the prosecutor's son. I saw the prisoner snatch at my father's watch - he ran away; I pursued him down a street and down a court, but lost sight of him; I saw him stopped by an officer, whom I had seen before I lost sight of the prisoner.
 Prisoner. Q. When the Alderman asked how you knew me, what did you say? A. I said I knew you by your clothes, but not by your face.
   JOHN ATTERWELL. I am an officer. I was in Little Moorfields. I heard a cry of Stop thief! a moment after I passed Buckley's-alley - I turned and pursued the prisoner from the alley to Crow-court, and there took him; a man came up, and said, "That is not the man," but I knew he was, for I had never lost sight of him - I heard the cry, saw him crossing, and go down Buckley's-alley - I secured him in less than three minutes.
   JOHN WEATHERALL. I am a patrol. I was with Alterwell; we just passed the end of Short-street, and heard the cry of Stop thief! I saw a man run down Buckley's-alley, I pursued, and saw Atterwell with the prisoner - he was dressed as a sailor, and no one else was there.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been at the Red Lion public-house, and as I returned home saw several persons together; there was an alarm of Stop thief! I pursued, and was taken; several persons came up, and said I was not the man; the officer said, "I think it is him."
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Fourteen Years.
