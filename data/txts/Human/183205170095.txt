 1305.  WILLIAM CROSSLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of April , 1 pair of shoes, value 3s., the goods of George Henry Hinkins.
   GEORGE HENRY HINKINS. I live in Golden-lane, and deal in new and second-hand shoes. On the 27th of April the Policeman came and asked me if I had not lost some shoes; I then missed a pair from the step of the door - I went with him, and saw them at the pawnbroker's.
   THOMAS KIRKWOOD. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Brick-lane, Old-street. I took in these shoes from the prisoner on the evening of the 26th of April.
   WILLIAM PURCELL(Police-constable C 194). I took the prisoner from information, and found the shoes.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not take them - it was another boy.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1306.  JOSEPH EMMINGTON was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of March , 3 pieces of wood, value 2s., the goods of Charles Hamilton.
 WILLIAM HUMPHREVS. I was at work at Mr. Charles Hamilton's park, about a mile from  Harrow- I missed several pieces of wood, but I cannot tell on what day - it was about the 17th or 18th of March; I lost a great deal: I found these three pieces at the prisoner's, on the 4th of April, about three hundred yards from where I was at work; he is a labourer, but was not at work there - we put our tools into the house he lodged in, and when we were fetching them I saw this wood; we called his wife, and asked if it was here - she said, "You touch it if you dare;" I sent for the officer, and took it - I know this is part of the wood I lost, by my own work on it.
   HENRY CORBY. I am a constable. I took the wood.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had found the wood in question by the road side.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1307.  HENRY FAULKNER was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 5 lbs. of pork, value 1s. 8d., the goods of George Milton Southcombe.
   JOHN BINDEN. I am in the service of George Milton Southcombe, a cheesemonger, of Shoreditch. On the 14th of April we had three legs of pork hanging in the shop - about half-past ten o'clock in the evening I went for half a pint of beer; when I had drank my beer I missed one leg - I followed the prisoner, who was eight or ten yards off, and took it from his basket; he gave it up willingly; I had not sold it - I cannot swear that it was ours, but I missed one like it.
   WILLIAM TREMAIN BRAND(Police-constable H 35). I took the prisoner and this pork.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going by with the basket, and a person said there was some meat dropped - I took this piece of pork up, and was looking at it at the next window; the shopman asked what I was going to do with it - I said, "Is it yours?" he said Yes, and took it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1308.  CHARLES FRIBURG was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of April , 1 picture and frame, value 2s., the goods of John Whitehouse.
   WILLIAM BONEVAL CHURCH(Police-serjeant T 2). On the 22nd of April I fell in with the prisoner, as I was going my rounds at Hammersmith, at a quarter-past twelve o'clock at night - I saw he had something under his arm, and as he passed me, he brought it round to his breast; I followed, and asked what he had got; he said he had it from his father's - I said I would go there with him; when we got near his house, he said his father did not give it to him, but some person whom he did not know; I took him to the station-house, and in going along he called on some men to rescue him, but I prevented that, and took him to the station; I found this picture on him, which I recollected having seen at Whitehouse's.
   JOHN WHITEHOUSE. I am a publican, and live at Hammersmith. This picture was taken from my parlour; it is the Spanish Patriot - the prisoner had been there that Saturday evening.
 Three witnesses gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 26.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1309.  CHARLES GOODALL was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of May , 2 live tame rabbits, price 6s., the property of John Lief : 1 saw, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Richard West ; and 1 pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of William Sweeting.
   JOHN BANKS(Police-constable N 62). On the 2nd of May, at half-past two o'clock in the morning, I was in Bacchuswalk, Hoxton, and saw the prisoner lurking about; he ran off and I pursued; I took him and found on him this property.
   RICHARD LIEF. I live in Turner's-square. On this morning I lost two rabbits from my hutches - I had fastened them up the night before at half-past six o'clock, they were then alive; they were dead when the officer found them on the prisoner; these are their skins - he must have got over two walls to get them; mine is the sixth house.
   RICHARD WEST. This saw, which the officer found on the prisoner, is mine; it was in my back premises in a little cupboard, fastened with a button; in the morning I found the cupboard open, and missed the saw; I live next door to Mr. Lief.
   MARY SWEETING. I am the wife of William Sweeting. Our yard joins the other, I lost this pot from our back yard, and these two cocks belong to the landlord of the house.
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
