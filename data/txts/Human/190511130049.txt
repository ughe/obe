CLEMENT BROWN. I am a clerk in the Hammersmith Broadway Post Office—on October 3rd the prisoner came and asked for a telegram in the name of Hyde—I asked if he was Mr. Hyde, and where the telegram would be from—on his giving the name and the town it came from as Shefford he was handed the telegram—there was some conversation, as the surname on the telegram was "Hind" instead of "Hyde"—he was under my observation not more than a minute or two—he took the tele gram away.
   Cross-examined.  I have not seen you at the Post Office at any other time—with your distinctive features I have no possible doubt—I saw you at the Police Court about October 10th—I would serve a post card—this is a fair plan of the Post Office. The prisoner said he bought a post card on September  27 th and that was the only time he was there. DANIEL CHRISTMAS. I am counter clerk at the Hammersmith Broad way Post Office—on October 3rd I handed the prisoner a reply-paid telegram about 2.30 p.m.—I had previously seen him talking to the last I witness with reference to a difference in the name in the telegram, and I saw him hand something to him, when he went to the back of the office and apparently wrote a telegram in one of the boxes with his back to me—then he handed me this form, "Unable to meet. Send money. Will come home. Joe."
   Cross-examined.  I had not seen you previously at the Post Office—having seen you once, it is not possible to mistake you—you were dressed in a blackish coat with a wrapper round your neck.
