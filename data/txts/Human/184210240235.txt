JAMES LINFIELD. I live at No. 8, Tibberton-street, Newington Cause way.  3063. JOHN HESKETT was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying Thomas Wellings . SARAH SHERWOOD. I am the wife of William Sherwood, a wire-worker, of No.5, York-street, London-road. Thomas Wellings was my nephew, and was a wire-worker—on Monday night he was up in my room doing a little work for me—his wife called him down—I went down about ten minutes afterwards, and saw him and Heskett fighting in the middle of York-street—a great many people were standing about—I saw them strike each other—they were perfectly sober—they continued fighting twenty minutes or half an hour—Wellings fell down nearly every time—the pri soner fell down sometimes—they kept on fighting till they fell—there was a number of falls, I cannot say how many—they fell from the blows—Wellings was the last that fell—he fell with the back of his head against the curb-stone—he then said, "I will have no more," and I never heard him speak afterwards—I was not with him when he went home—I went up stairs, and they took him home as well us they could—I was with him when he died—his nose and eye bled—he lived a very little way from me—I sat up with him all night—he never spoke, moved hand or foot, or opened his eyes—I and his wife went for a doctor about eight o'clock—we got Mr. Gale—he came with us—the deceased was about thirty years old—the prisoner had married Wellings' sister—they were on friendly terms before—the prisoner shook hands with him after he fell the last time, when he said he would have no more.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  When he fell the last time did not they both fall together and the deceased undermost? A.  They did not fall together—I was standing nearly close to them. MARY RUSSELL. I lived with Wellings as his wife—he was with Sherwood, and I called him to his tea—he came down—the prisoner was in the street—they had a few words—they began abusing one an other and then fought—I do not know which struck first—they both fell—the last time Wellings fell on the curb, and cut his head, and it bled very much—they both had hold of one another and both went down together, Wellings undermost—they were always very good friends—they shook hands first.
