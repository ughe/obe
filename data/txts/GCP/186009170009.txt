746. ALFRED HOARE (24), Feloniously breaking and entering the
warehonse of Zacharias Finlay and another, and stealing 113 pieces of
ribbon, value 2681., 880 ostrich feathers, and other articles, their property.
Second Count, receiving the same
wat
MR. DICKIE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM ARTHUR EADE (City-policeman, 146). On 24th August I saw
the prisoner wheeling a truck with a box and a bag on it, from Monkwell-
street into Cripplegate-buildings--I shortly afterwards stopped him and
asked him where he was going ; be told me to the Eastern Counties Railway
-I asked him where he brought it from, he said “ 26, Wood-street," that was
not the way from Wood-street-I took him to the station and employed a
man to take the truck and box-I searched him at the station and found &
ootton handkerchief upon bim-I afterwards broke the chest open in his
presence, and found this jemmy at the top and 130 rolls of ribbon, 15 head-
dresses, 2 pieces of crape, 30 yards, 14 bottles of wine, 24 ostrich feathers,
9 packages of silk, from 8 to 112 yards in each packet, and 7 remnants of silk,
average 12 yards each.
Cross-examined by Me. RIBTON. . What street was he in when you saw
him A. In Hart-street, which is quite a different way from Wood-street-
I have not had any money from the prosecution ; I will swear I have not
been promised any money-he said he had been employed by a gentleman at
26, Wood-street- this is the bag (produced) that he had in the truck; the
box is not here--it was nailed down and corded with small cord the bag
was under the box.
ZACHARIAS FINLAY. I am a manufacturer, of 35, Monkwell-street, close
to Hart-street-I left the premises about half-past seven on the 23d, the night
of the robbery, quite safe ; all the doors were shut-I was the last person on
the premises that night, I returned next morning at about half-past 8-I
tried to open the door, and after a great deal of difficulty I succeeded I found
the inside door of the warehouse forced open, and the boxes which contained
these feathers and the papers containing these silks, lying open ; the boxes
which contained the ribbons were lying about on the floor-I found three
empty bottles which had contained wine the previous night I found the
counting-house door broken open and the desk, and 41. Îls. 3d. and four-
pence taken out of the desk—the papers were not thrown about much
new pair of boots were also taken, and an old pair left in their stead-the
boxes contained feathers, a great portion of them-two drawers upstairs
were also broken open, on the second and third floor, and some tiles on the roof
of the house1 have samples of the property here; the value altogether of
the property lost was about 5001–I can swear to these goods; they have
all my private mark on them I had not sold them.
WILLIAM FENNING (City-policeman 88). I went to the premises of Mr.
Finley in Hart-street, and compared the jemmy; it corresponded exactly
with the marks on the warehouse door, and also on the desks in the counting-
house.
The Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate was here read as follows:
"I do not wish to say anything. I was asked by a man in Wood-street to
take it to the Eastern Counties Railway, and he said he would give me a
shilling."
Guilty of receiving.--Confined Twelve Months.
