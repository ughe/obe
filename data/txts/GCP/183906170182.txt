WILLIAM MARCH. I am secretary to the Brazilian Mining Company
Mr. George Haythorn is one of the proprietors of that company--there are
a great many proprietors. On the 20th of March I received advices from
Messrs. Carne, of Falmouth, of the arrival of two boxes of gold dust,
marked “B C 18 and 19"- the letter of advice does not mention the
"BC" but the numbers--they were to come by the Sea Gull—a subse-
quent. advice mentioned the letters "BC" as well as the numbers-it
would be known among the shipping people in London that very morning,
the 20th of March, that the Sea Gull had arrived on the 25th I received
further advices from Messrs. Carne, informing me of the transmission of
the boxes of gold dust by the City of Limerick-I never authorised Moss,
or any body else, to go to the Dublin Steam Wharf, and make use of my
name, to receive them I did not see this letter (No. 4) before the boxes
were gone the signature to this letter bears a similarity to Mr. Carne's the
hand-writing of the body does not appear to me to be the same, and it strikes
me as a very informal, unmercantile sort of letter, which it is very unlikely
any merchant should sign-there is nothing else which strikes me-(looking
at No. 3)—I do not think the whole of this is one hand-writing, and it is a
letter very unlikely to be signed by Messrs. Carne-my name is wrongly
spelt, which Messrs. Carne are perfectly acquainted with, as they are con-
stantly in the habit of writing to me, and it designates me as the agent
to the Brazilian Mining Company, which is not correct-my name is not
correctly spelt in No. 4--my correct designation is Secretary to the Com
pany.
Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. The signature to No. 4 you say
is different from the body? A. It appears so, and it is a very unlikely
letter for a commercial man to write--my own name is also spelt wrong
the signature of Messrs. Carne in No. 3 is different from the body-I am not
aware that I can make any other remark as to the hand-writing. I
