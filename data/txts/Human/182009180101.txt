1178. ELIZA PICKETT was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of September , one coat, value 25 s., the goods of James Robert Cassell.
 JOHN AARON. I am a servant to Mr.  James Robert Cassell, a pawnbroker, who lives in Old-street, I saw the prisoner take this coat down, and put it in her basket. I secured her ten yards off with it.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went there to redeem things, somebody must have put it into my basket to injure me.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Fined One Shilling, and Discharged.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.

 1180.  DANIEL MACK was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , one watch, value 3 l.; one seal, value 6 d.; and one chain, value 1 d., the goods of John Mills , from his person.
   JOHN MILLS. I live in Cornwell-street, St. George's in the East. On the 16th of August, between five and six o'clock in the evening, I was in Cornwell-street, returning from a walk, somebody came against me, snatched my watch from my fob, and went off with it. It was produced in three minutes.
   TIMOTHY CALLAMAN. I live in Cow-lane, Shadwell. I was coming out of the Middlesex Society-school, and heard Mills calling Stop thief! my school-fellows and I followed the prisoner who was near Mr. Mills. I saw him throw the watch into a gutter, I picked it up and gave it to Mills.
   WILLIAM HENRY HUNT. I am a constable. I secured the prisoner. Callaman said he was the man who threw the watch away.
 (Property produced, and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Life.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1181.  CHARLES CALLAGHAN was indicted for stealling, on the 4th of September , one watch, value 2 l.; one seal, value 5 s.; one chain, value 5 s., and one key, value 1 s., the goods of Robert Newby , from his person.
   ROBERT NEWBY. I am a sawyer, and live in Richmond-place, Old-street. On the 4th of September, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon I was in Ball-yard, Golden-lane. I went there to call on a mate named Evans, who owed me some money; while I was disputing with him, he knocked me down. I was picked up by several people who stood round, and as they took me up, they took my watch from my fob. A little boy came up and said,
 "You have lost your watch; I saw  Jack Harris take it." He ran away. I have not recovered it.
   THOMAS HILL. I keep a chandler's-shop in Little Arthur-street. I saw a crowd in Ball-yard as I stood at my shop, the prisoner and Harris were there, they ran out of the crowd together, and came up towards me. I knew them both perfectly well. In five or ten minutes, I heard the prosecutor had lost his watch. As they came towards me, opposite the Crown and Sceptre door, I saw Harris give the prisoner something which appeared to be a watch. They were about ten yards from me. I informed the constable.
   JOSEPH PRINCE. I am an officer. I heard of the robbery the same day, and Hill gave me the account he has now. Tweedy and I went to the prisoner's lodgings, he was denied to us, we went up stairs, and found him concealed under the bed clothes, with all his clothes but his coat on, it was in the afternoon. I told him what Hill said, he said he knew nothing of it, he did not take it.
   JOHN TWEEDY. I saw the prisoner and apprehended him. I knew him and Harris before, I have seen them in company.
   RICHARD STEVENS. I am a shoemaker, and live in Ball-yard. I saw Harris very busy in picking the prosecutor up, and saw him draw the watch from his pocket, he would the chain round his fingers, and ran off. My lad ran after him. I have not seen him since, and did not see the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was not under the clothes.
     GUILTY. Aged. 19. Transported for Life.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1182.  JOSEPH BARNETT, HENRY BADMAN, and EDWARD WILLIAMS, were indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , one snuff-box, value 20 s., the goods of William Smith , from his person.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I keep the Stone Kitchen, public-house in the Tower. On the 11th of September, between four and five o'clock, I was on the Tower-wharf, there was a rowing-match, which brought a crowd round, and my snuffbox was stolen.
   WALTER STONE. I am a constable of Tower Ward. I was on the wharf between four and five o'clock in the afternoon, and observed Barnet and Williams particularly active in looking at different peoples' pockets. Badman was with him, but I did not observe him do any thing. Just as the boats started there was a sudden rush. I observed four or five bad characters, and told a person to tell me if the prisoners left the crowd. In a few minutes I was informed they had left. I came up with them on the bridge which leads to the lawn. Barnett was nearest to me, I took him, and Lewis took the other two, who were further off. We took them to the Guard-house. Just as we entered I heard something drop, at that time Barnett and I were behind, the others had entered the guard-house. I found the snuff-box laying between Barnett and me, he said
 "You know I did not drop it." I took him to the guard-house. Nobody was in custody but the prisoners.
   JOHN LEWIS. I saw Badman and Williams on the wharf for a considerable time. I afterwards had information, and saw the prisoners together. I took Badman and Williams to the guard-house. I was before Stone, and was searching them at the time the snuff-box fell. Stone had not entered with Barnett.
