MR. SERJEANT SLEIGH subnutted that there was no case to go to the Jury.
Mr. METCALPE did not rely upon the counts for false pretences, but contended
that there was evidence for the Jury that the defendant obtained the goods,
not intending to treat them in the ordinary course of business, and not de
tending to pay for them. The COMMON SERJBANT could not say that there
was no evidence at all, but was of opinion that there was really none that
the defendant obtained the goods under a false pretence of carrying on business
in the ordinary course of trade; but the case should proceed if the Jury
desired it. The Jury, however, stated they had made up their minds, and
found the prisoner
Not Guilty.
