135. RICHARD GOWER and JOHN DAVIS were indieted for

half-sovereigns, 16 crowns, 273 half-erowns, 1269 shillings, 780 six-
pences, and 42 fourpenny pieces; the property of Thomas Woodward and
another; and that Gower had been before convicted of felony.
Mr. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecutton.
THOMAs WOODWARD. I am a miller and flour-factor. I live in St.
Martin's-le-Grand, and am in partnership with my son-on the 16th of
November, I was out with my gig colleeting money-I put the money I
collected into a bag under the seat of my gigabout half-past twelve
o'clock at noon, I was stopping at a baker' shop at Hoxton-the cushion
was on the seat, and the apron over it-I left no one in the gig-I was
not in the shop a minute and a half, in fact, I was not in the shop, but
on the step--I was told that I had lost my bag, and I then missed it-I
think it contained 1331. 58. 6d. in sovereigns, half-sovereigns, crowns,
half-crowns, shillings, sixpences, and fourpenny pieces-ir consequence
of what a gentleman told me, I ran down a court close by, and before I
got to the bottom of the court, I met the two prisoners coming upI
laid hold of Gower-h asked me what I laid hold of him forI said I
suspeeted he had been robbing me-he said, 46 I have got nothing of yours,
I am only a working mechanic going home to my dinner"- went by
me, and I called out Stop thief"-I saw him stopped by a baker's man,
at the top of the court-I still kept hold of Gower, and when I got him to
the top of the court, he said, 46 For God's sake don't collar me as I go
across the street; your money is safe at the baker's shop'-I had not then
said any thing to him about the baker's shop, or where I had lost my
money-after they were in custody, the bag and its contents were shown
to me-the bag is here, and it contained the money stated.
