   ROBERT GOOSE. I am a Policeman. I apprehended Richards, and on his neck found a silk handkerchief, which the prosecutrix claimed.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Wilson's Defence. On the 25th of June I left my father's house - I called on a young man to go to bathe; I saw the handkerchief laying on his chair - I asked him to lend me something to wipe myself with, and when I came out of the water he lent me this handkerchief; I was going to return it when the Policeman took me - as soon as the young man heard I was in custody he absconded, and the woman who lived with him.
 Mullins' Defence. The room did not belong to me; I went up when the Policeman was there, and seeing him taking the young man's things, I said, "Don't take my property away;" I did not know but it was the young man's till I got to the watch-house - I do not live with him.
 Richards' Defence. I was in Tothill-fields, and met a man, who asked me to lend him 2d. - I pulled out a sixpence to get change; he said he would sell me the cravat for 6d., as he wanted 6d. to get his breakfast.
 MULLINS -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 RICHARDS -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 WILSON -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
 1511.  JANE JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of May , 1 diamond pin, value 8l., the goods of John Maccallum , from his person.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN MACCALLUM. I am an insurance-broker, of Bucklersbury, and reside in Grafton-street, Fitzroy-square. On the 26th of May, having dined with a friend, I was on my road home - about one o'clock in the morning, as I proceeded up Rathbone-place, I saw the prisoner before me and a man walking along with her; I overtook and passed them - they stopped at the corner of Rathboneplace, by the Percy hotel; as I passed the prisoner she looked very hard at me - I crossed the road, and she followed me; I requested her not to go with me, for she could get no advantage at all by my company, and requested her to return to her friend - she said he was no friend of hers, that she had never seen him before, and did not like him for he was putting a great many curious questions to her, and she did not wish to have any thing to say to him - she continued this sort of conversation till I got to the other side of the street; that was in crossing Charlotte-street - she wished me to go to a house with her, which I declined; she then asked me for money to carry her home in a coach - I asked where she lived; she said in Burton-crescent - I said the distance was very short, it was a fine clear night, and she had no occasion for a coach - she then became rather abusive in her language, and said I had deprived her of a friend; I reminded her that I had wished her to keep by that friend - I could not get rid of her; I cannot repeat all the conversation - she was very importunate, and to get rid of her I turned away from her, and at the moment of turning away, another female, who is here, happened to be passing; I joined her on purpose to ged rid of the prisoner, and walked a few paces with her - when the prisoner saw me join that female she ran after me, laid hold of the skirts of my coat, apparently to stop me; I put my hand round to take care of my pockets or my coat, and at that moment (I was walking alongside the other female) the prisoner put her hand over my shoulder, towards my breast - I moved my right hand to keep her away, but she repeated these efforts so frequently that I did all I could to push her away from me - I had not used sufficient force to keep her away, and therefore I laid hold of both her hands, folded them on her bosom, and gently pushed her into the street; I did it so gently as I could; I left her standing in the street, and came back to the pavement on the left side, upon which she instantly rushed towards me, and made a grasp at my breast - I had a diamond pin in my shirt, which was conspicuous; the moment she made the grasp, she withdrew a step or two -I put my hand to my breast, and found the pin was gone; I immediately charged her with robbery, and called Police! Police! a Policeman came up; I laid hold of her left hand myself immediately, and told him to lay hold of the right, which he did, and she was taken to the station-house - I did not feel satisfied with the Policeman's conduct; at the station I made a general complaint that I was robbed; Pattisson went out; the prisoner was on the point of being searched when Pattisson came in with the pin - my shirt was torn with her taking it - I had taken no description of liberty with her whatever.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You have stated very fairly that you took her by the wrist, I am sure you would not use unnecessary violence, but with as much force as you thought necessary, you removed her? A. Precisely so; she certainly appeared angry at my not acceding to her wishes - I was going home, and did not want to be troubled with any body; the last grasp she made was after I had pushed her away - I have never said I might be mistaken as to her intention; I said to Mr. Harmer that I did not wish to press hard upon her, for there was no anger in my feelings - she denied that she intended to steal the pin.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you use more violence than to keep her from your person? A. I did not.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q.What did you say to the other girl? A. I believe I asked where she lived, and where she was going; that was not in the prisoner's hearing - she was ten or twelve paces off: it was a still night - my object was only to get rid of the prisoner.
   FRANCES DUFFIELD. I am unfortunate, but do not get my living by walking the streets. I was returning home, and heard the prosecutor and prisoner talking rather loud; I heard something about a coach, but cannot exactly recollect what - I saw the prisoner snatch at Mr. Maccallum's cravat; he pushed her away - she made a second snatch, and then he called the Policeman, who came up and took her; the Policeman told me I must go too - I heard something drop, but did not think any thing about the pin, and never saw it - they took me to the station; Pattisson afterwards called me out - I described the spot to him; he went away, and returned in about ten minutes with the pin.
