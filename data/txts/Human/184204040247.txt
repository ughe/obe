I remember, on the 12th of February, her putting a purse into a letter directed to Miss Cohen—I bought the clasp of it in Westminster road, and my mother sewed it on—my sister worked the purse—I saw the same purse put into the letter—I believe the purse produced to be the same—I have no doubt of it, and I be lieve the clasp to be the one I bought—I cannot swear to my mother's stitching—the purse, in its colour and every particular, has the appearance of the one my sister made—after it was put into the letter I took it down stairs myself from my sister, and delivered it to Susan Walker, the servant, to carry to the post—the purse was enclosed in a piece of blue paper, just like the blue paper produced—the post-office is immediately opposite our house, at No. 5, East-place—Mrs. Weatherhead, the prisoner's mother, keeps it—the prisoner lives there with her—I know her to be the daughter, and have seen her there, but it is a long time ago.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When were yon at the post-office kept by Mrs. Weatherhead? A.  I do not know—some time last year—I have been there two or three times I believe—I only know Mrs. Weatherhead by going into the shop—I was not acquainted with the prisoner—I mean to state positively this is the purse my sister worked—I swear it—I am sure of it—I know it by the look of it—these purses are worked from patterns—this was worked from a pattern—I suppose the patterns are to be bought—I do not know—there is no mark on the clasp but these little cuts on it—there are many hundreds of them—it is similar to the one I saw—that is the reason 1 swear to it.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Did you see your sister when she made this purse have any pattern by her? A.  Oh, no, she had no pattern by her—I do not know that she worked it from a pattern—she has worked one of the same pattern before—seeing the clasp and purse together I believe it to be the same.
