 192.  GEORGE KING was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of December , 1 cheese, value 10s., the goods of William Dicken.
   JOHN LONDON. I met the prisoner on the 2nd of December, carrying this cheese; I asked where he got it - he said a man gave it to him to carry to the other side of Brentford-bridge; he had no settled place to take it to, but the man was to meet him.
 WILLIAM DICKEN. I  keep a cheesemonger's-shop. The officer brought this cheese, which I knew to be mine - it had been one foot within my door; I had not missed it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going up the street; a man asked me if I would earn 6d. - I took the cheese, and the man followed me as far as the Half-acre, when the officer stopped me.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Three Months.
 193.  SAMUEL HARGRAVE was indicted for stealing on the 2nd of December , 1 pair of shoes, value 3s., the goods of John Dawson.
   FRANCES DAWSON. I am the wife of John Dawson- we live in Monmouth-street. On the 2nd of December I saw the prisoner, and watched him for some time; he was then alone; I then saw him with another bigger boy- he passed our door, but I did not see him take the shoes; he passed again, and I saw he had them under his arm; I ran, and cried Stop thief! he threw down the shoes, and the officer took them up; they are my husband's property.
   WILLIAM ARNEY. I saw the prisoner running, and the prosecutor following him; I took the shoes up.
 Prisoner's Defence. The other boy gave them to me.
     GUILTY. Aged 9. - Transported for Seven Years.
 194.  JOHN HATCH was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of December , 1 purse, value 1s., 4 sovereigns, and 1 half-sovereign, the monies of Samuel Hodges , from his person.
 SAMUEL HODGES. I am a  veterinary surgeon, and reside at the Veterinary-college, Camden-town. At two o'clock in the morning of the 5th of December I was returning home from Drury-lane theatre; I was sober, and had got nearly as far as Little Turnstile, when the prisoner met me, and without any provocation he struck me in the breast, and knocked me down; as I was attempting to rise he drew the sheath from a sword-stick which I had - he put it to his knee, and broke it, threw one part of it away, and was coming at me again with the other part, with an intent of striking me - I gave up the sword-stick to a person who was near, closed with the prisoner, and we both fell, I was undermost - I then felt the prisoner at my right-hand side, attempting to take my watch, and trying to break the guard; the Policeman came up at that instant - I lost four sovereigns and a half from my trousers pocket, and my trousers were very much torn on the left side, where my purse had been; I have no doubt whatever but that he took my money - I have never seen it since: it was in a green purse altogether; the Policeman took him into custody; the purse and all were taken.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. How many person were about at the time? A. I did not see any person except the one to whom I gave the sword-stick - I believe when I got up there were two or three persons; none of them are here - in two or three minutes, I suppose, there were a dozen, but no one was near me till the Policeman came up; my friend is not here - the Policeman took the sword-stick from the man I gave it to: I believe he was a bricklayer - I believe one of the Policemen saw the beginning of the transaction; I was sober - I suppose the theatre closed about a quarter-past twelve o'clock; I then went to an oyster-shop, but had nothing to drink there - I do not know the name of the street; I am a stranger in town - I suppose I staid there an hour; I have the watch-guard on now which he was trying to break - the prisoner appeared to be drunk when he was taken; I did not make an observation about his being drunk before he struck me - no one was with him; I have not seen the purse since; I did not see any one near him at the time - there were plenty there in two or three minutes; I charged him with attempting to take my watch and purse before I got to the watch-house; I had seen my purse safe about two minutes before, as I had met a Policeman, and asked him to direct me to Somer's-town, and he did - I took my purse out to give him some silver, but I had none; I then put it in again safe.
   JOHN WESTCOMBE. I am a Police-constable, and was on duty near Turnstile. I heard the cry of Police! went to the spot, and found Mr. Hodges rising from the ground- I saw the prisoner draw the sheath from his stick; he broke it, threw one piece away, and approached him with the other piece - they fell, and I came up and took the prisoner, while he lay on Mr. Hodges; I took charge of him- the prosecutor did not say the attempt at that moment: I took the prisoner to the watch-house, but found no money; a great many people had collected, and I gave him to my brother officer - in going along Great Queen-street; the prosecutor said he had robbed him of his purse.
