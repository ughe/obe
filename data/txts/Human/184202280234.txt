GEORGE WILD( police-constable M  94.) I was on duty between eight and nine o'clock on the 5th of February, and saw a wagon in Black man-street, Borough, laden with oats—it drew up at the entrance of Lant's-yard, in Blackman-street—Weller was with the wagon—"John Newell, Shad Thames," was on the wagon—I saw Weller put two sacks of oats from the wagon, and take one of them to Gale's stables, adjoining, and another man took the other—I went to the stable in Lant's-yard, and found Wright there with two sacks of black and one of white oats, and two of beans—I asked Wright where the oats were that were brought in—he pointed them out immediately—I asked if he was aware they were coming—he said he was not, but they were brought for the name of "Gale," that was his master—I made a communication to Mr. Newell, in conseqnence of which I then went down to the stable, took Wright, and the two sacks of oats. JAMES WOODLEY. I keep a wood-yard in Swan-street, Dover-road—Wright was ostler at Gale's. On the 5th of February I saw a wagon drawn there—I saw Wild when he came to detain the oats—I had sees Weller bring three sacks of oats from the wagon, and put them in the stable before you come to Mr. Gale's—they were afterwards taken into Gale's stables—Weller then went away with the wagon. JAMES TIDMARSH( police-constable M  51.) I went to King-street, Portman-square, and saw Weller—he had a wagon with thirty-eight sacks of black oats—I asked him if he had been to the Borough that morning—he said he had not—I took him in charge—Mr. Newell was with me at the time. EDWARD WILSON( police-constable M  35.) I was placed by Wild to watch the stable—I remained till he returned—no one went in. WILLIAM HOPKINS. I am foreman to Mr. Newell. Weller was a car man in our service—I delivered him forty sacks in the wagon that day, to go to Mr. Hanson, in King-street, Portman-square, and I sent twenty sacks of white oats to another gentleman—Weller had charge of the wagon—he had no oats to deliver in the Borough—these two sacks of black oats are the same kind as my master's, which I sent that day.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You do not mean to swear to them? A.  No. JOHN NEWELL. I have no partner—I was at Hanson's when the thirty-eight sacks were delivered—they were ordered for the "Patent Safety Cab  Company."
 (Weller received a good character.)
   WELLER— GUILTY. Aged 30.— Confined Six Months.
   WRIGHT— NOT GUILTY.
