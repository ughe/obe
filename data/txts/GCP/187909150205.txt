FREDERICK PRICE. I manage the post-office, Lower Norwood-in July
last Hill handed me a bad florin and asked me in the prisoner's presence
whether it was bad-I asked him where he got it-he said that he must
have got it by accident—he gave me good money, and I sent somebody to
follow him-I described him to Inspector Glover next day, and afterwards
picked him out from 10 or 12 others.
Cross-examined. I recognised him by his personal appearance and his
dothes-the other men were of different ages and wore different kinds of
suits, some with whiskers and some without-I slightly bent the coin and
gave it back to the prisoner.
FLORENCE HILL I am employed at the post-office, Lower Norwood on
11th August the prisoner came in for five shillings' worth of stamps, and
gave me two floring, a sixpence, and some coppers-I gave him back one of
the florins and said that it was bad-he gave me a good half-crown, I gave
him sixpence change, and he left-I spoke to Mr. Corney-I saw the
prisoner in custody at the station the same evening.
JONATHAN CORNEY. I am assistant to Roland Ellis, of Norwood-no
lith August, & little after 4 o'clock, the prisoner came in for 58. worth of
stamps---Hill spoke to me as he left, and I went out and spoke to Sergeant
Lather, and he was taken in custody.
Cross-examined. I gave him in charge at that moment he did not run
away.
ELLEN WALLACE. I live at the Bricklayers' Arms, Lower Norwood-on
11th August the prisoner tendered a florin for a glass of alo-I took it to
my mother, and went back and told him it was bad he gave me a half-
crown, I gave him the change, and he left-I picked him out on 13th
August from 12 other people by his appearance,
CHARLOTTE WALLACE. My husband keeps the Bricklayers' Arms, Lower
Norwood-my daughter brought me a florin-on this afternoon-I bent it
easily.
JAMES GLOVER (Police Inspector P). On 11th August, about 4.15, the
prisoner was pointed out to me-I followed him about a mile I was in
uniform-I said that he was the young man I wanted-he said, "What for?".
- I said, “For uttering a counterfeit two-shilling piece in the post-office in High
Street”-he said, “Yes, I was there, and gave the young lady a two-shilling
piece, which was bad, but I did not know it was bad " I asked him where
it was, he said that he bent it and threw it away--I asked him where he
lived-he said, “In London " I said, “It is strange you should come here
to buy postage stamps" he said that he thought they would be easier to
take home than the money I found on him a 6d., 58. worth of stamps, and
some bronze-he said that he was going to send the stamps home-he.
gave his address, 74, Shepherdess Road, Wandsworth-I went there and
there was no 74.
Cross-examined. Corney pointed him out--no coins were found on him.
Guilty-Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
