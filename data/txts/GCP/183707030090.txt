ABRAHAM SOESMAN. I am a paper-merchant, and live at No. 228,
Hoxton. On the 1st or 2nd of June I saw the prisoner in Red Lion-court,
Fleet-street-he said he had some waste-paper to dispose of-I went into
the office of “The Guide "-he had the key of it-he showed me a sample
of waste-paper-what he showed me was in sheets-I ultimately purchased
on the 7th about 6cwt. I paid nim sl., and have got the receipt-I sold
54cwt. to Mr. Bliss, in Barbican, for 101.- I kept some quires of it at
home, which I gave up to the officer after the second transaction-- I went
to Webber-street, Surrey, on the 7th, where I made the purchase, and the
work was there I was introduced to the parlour, and in the back room was
this paper-this was part of the work of 1832, 1833, 1834, 1835, and 1836.
EDWIN BLISs. On the 22nd of June I bought 5cwt. of Pinnock's
Guide to Knowledge of Mr. Soesman, but a clerk of mine bought some
about the 10th-he is not here—I sold the paper to Mr. Chidley.
Jonn CHIDLEY. I am a bookseller, and dealer in waste-paper. I pur-
chased some paper of Mr. Bliss, at 488. a cwt., and paid him 121.- I have
got it at home now-the officer has seen it, and brought a sheet away.
WILLIAM Simons (police-sergeant L 3.) I took the prisoner into
custody-I have made inquiries about this paper-I have a sample,
which I bought from Soesman-I got some of Mr. Chidley-the
greater part of it is unstitched-here is one number stitched- I have
brought 104lbs. from one, 50lbs. from another, and 14cwt. from Mr.
Crosby.
MR. WILSON. These are sheets of the work in question, which the
prisoner was engaged about.
