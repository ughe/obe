 2447.  JOSEPH LEARY was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of September , 1 piece of iron, called a poppet-head, value 13s.; 1 collar and mandle, value 13s.; 2 rest-screws, value 10s.; 2 tops and boxes, value 5s., and 1 iron cramp, value 12s., the goods of William Bradick.
 WILLIAM BRADICK. I am a  turner and bedsteadmaker; the prisoner was eighteen months in my employ -I was from home when these things were taken.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you pay me any wages on Saturday night? A. Every Saturday; I have his signature in my book up to the last fortnight that he worked for me - when I was absent my wife has overpaid him; I never knew him pawn my property.
   JAMES ALDOUS. I am a pawnbroker, in Berwick-street. On the 7th of September the prisoner pawned a cramp, for 4s.
   JOHN NEEDS. I am shopman to Mr. Chaffers, of Greek-street, Soho. I have a turner's rest and screw, pawned on the 18th of September, in the name of John Williams; I believe by the prisoner.
   JOHN REARDON. I live in High-street, Bloomsbury. I have a box and tap, pawned on the 4th of April, but not in my presence.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am shopman to Mr. Jones, High Holborn. I have a collar and mandle, pawned for 4s. - the prisoner does not appear to be the person.
   GEORGE HOBBS. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 5th of October, at work at another shop - I asked where he kept his duplicates; he said he had but one, and that he gave to the prosecutor's wife - it was for the collar and mandle.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. On the day he got my signature to the book, he got a stranger to witness it; after I signed it I brought home work, which he cannot deny; he said,"I have your signature, and you are paid;" I said, "I am not paid for these draw-knots;" he said I should be paid another time - I often had goods given me to pledge, to raise money to pay the men.
 WILLIAM BRADICK. I never let him go without the whole of his money; if I was short, I let it go, perhaps, a week - I was in trouble about an action, and the person I left in charge of my business, took his signature; I never asked him for it myself - he did not turn sixteen dozen of draw-knots for me.
   JOHN HILL. I am a turner, and live in New Compton-street. The prisoner occasionally worked for me; I lent Bradick a rest, which I never could get back, and this is it - it has been in my uncle's possession fifty years; and, to tell you the truth, all these tools are mine, for I gave Bradick the money to pay for them - I only claim the rest- I consider he will take a false oath.
   CHARLES APPLEYARD. I am a porter. I have been in Bradick's employ nine months; he authorized the prisoner to pawn the tools on account of the non-payment of his money, and he owes me money for labour - I have heard him tell the prisoner to pledge his tools, and offer him money afterwards to redeem them; he was taken to the Marshalsea-prison, and owes me 12s.; he sent the prisoner to pawn a top and box-rests, and things- I never heard him authorize the prisoner to pledge, but he knew it was done, and gave him money to redeem them.
 - FORD. I am a bedstead-maker. The prosecutor called at my shop between nine and ten months ago, and asked if I had seen Leary, as he had pawned some of his tools, and he wanted him to get them out again.
 JOHN HILL re-examined. I know the prosecutor has authorized the prisoner to pawn his own apparel, but I cannot speak about these articles - when he could not get his pay he has made a piece of work, and the prosecutor has offered him his apparel to pledge.
     NOT GUILTY.

 2449.  CHARES JOHN FOX was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of September , 1 pair of spectacles, value 15s., the goods of David Child.
   DAVID CHILD. I live in Berkley-court, Clerkenwell. I met the prisoner at a public-house - I had never seen him before; we went home together, and had a game of cards; after he was gone my wife missed her spectacles; the prisoner called the next morning to borrow a pair of spectacles of our lodger, and then my wife asked for hers- he said he had not got them.
   WILLIAM HENRY BAYFIELD. I am a pawnbroker. I have this pair of spectacles, pawned by the prisoner on the 25th of September.
   MARY CHILD. I am the prosecutor's wife. The prisoner came to our house, and I missed my spectacles when he was gone - these are mine; I had had them ten years.
 Prisoner's Defence. I acknowledge pawning the spectacles now produced; but these are my own, and I had had them ever since 1818 - they were made from a fine Brazilian pebble; the pawnbroker knows that I have pawned them before.
 WILLIAM HENRY BAYFIELD. I have seen him with a pair - I do not know whether these were them or not.
     NOT GUILTY.
