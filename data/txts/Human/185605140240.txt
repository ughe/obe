498. JOHN HENNESSY , burglary in the dwelling house of Alexander John Kerr , and stealing 1 dressing case, the goods of Samuel Goodfellow ; and 1 key and 1 work box; the goods of Mary Wilkinson . MARY WILKINSON. I am in the service of Mr. Alexander John Kerr, No. 67, Grosvenor-street, Grosvenor-square. On Sunday evening, 13th April, I was in my mistress's room on the second floor, at a quarter past 9 o'clock—I heard footsteps in the room above—I took the candle, and ran up stairs to the attic, and as I entered the door I could see the prisoner get ting out of the window—I saw his face, and he spoke to me—he got out of the window down into the gutter—I believe there is a communication with the next house by means of that gutter—the same person came in again—my candle was gone out then—he came in half way, and took a work box of my own—when my candle was gone out there was merely the light of the window—it was moonlight—the prisoner was brought into the house again in about ten minutes—the window was quite shut down about 2 o'clock, it was not fastened.
   Prisoner.  When she was before the Magistrate, she said she could not swear to my features. Witness.  I said so, but I could to his voice, and to his general appearance.
   COURT . Q.  Could you hear this sound above before you went up at a quarter past 9 o'clock—I heard footsteps before I went into my mistreat room—the persons might have got in before 9 o'clock. HENRY MORRIS. I am in the service of Mr. Colster. I was in Grosve nor-street on Sunday evening, 13th April; I heard an alarm—I saw the prisoner coming down a ladder, from the top of a house—I believe it was No. 64, three doors from the prosecutor's—I laid hold of him—he said, "For God's sake let me go"—I gave him in charge—he said he had done nothing. JOHN SHEPHERD. ( policeman, C  116). I heard a cry of "Police!" about a quarter before 10 o'clock that night—I ran to No. 67, Grosvenor-street—the prisoner was given into my custody—I found on him a piece of candle, some lucifers, a knife, and two keys—I went up Mr. Kerr's house, and found a bar had been removed from the attic window. MARY WILKINSON. re-examined.  One of these keys found on the prisoner is my mistress's—it was safe in the room that afternoon.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was coming down Grosvenor-street; I heard screams of "Murder!" I saw some person on the top of the house; I went up the ladder to the top; when I got a little way I could not get any far ther, I picked up the matches and keys, and came down, and this man stopped me; this woman said two or three different things, first, she could not swear to me, and then she could; I hope you will look into the case; no pro perty was found on me; I was seen on a house three doors off. GUILTY.
   The prisoner was further charged with having been before convicted. WALTER HOLMES. ( policeman, F  50). I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction—( read: "Westminster Sessions, Dec. , 1854; George Wiltiams, convicted of larceny, after a previous conviction, and confined six months" )—the prisoner is the person.
   GUILTY .** Aged 17.— Four Years Penal Servitude.
