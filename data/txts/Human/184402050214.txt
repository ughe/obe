   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did all the conversation about the horses take place in the yard? A.  Yes—I began it by asking why they brought them there—Winkley stood very near, and heard the best partof the conversation—the prisoners seemed willing to go to Mitcham.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Was not all Smith said, that he met him and helped him? A.  Yes.
   MR. HORRY . Q.  Did the horses look fit for slaughter? A.  Yes—they were worn out, and one had the mange. WILLIAM PERSHOUSE. I live with my father, William Pershouse, a farmer, at Greenwich. He had the mare and gelding turned out on the Charlton marshes all the summer—I saw them there three days or a week before they were taken away—the two were worth 5 l .;
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Does your father own the vans be tween Greenwich and Woolwich? A.  Yes—he has not disposed of his stock, I swear these two horses are his—I saw them in the marshes night and day—they belonged to the farm—I know them by being both blind in the right eye—the gelding knuckles over the hind joint, which had been sprained—I do not think either of them had the mange—I will not swear it, not having been close to them—they were in rather low condition—they might look fit for the knackers, but answered our purpose.
   MR. HORRY . Q.  Have you seen the mare and gelding? A.  Yes, the day before yesterday, in the upper field, at Charlton. SAMUEL WEBB.
