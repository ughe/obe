1673. JOHN HARVEY was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of August , 2 pieces of leather, value 1s. 4d., the goods of William Thomas Binckers.
 MR.  WILLIAM THOMAS BINCKERS. I am a leather-cutter, and live at No. 52, Compton-street. On the 10th of August I saw the prisoner - I have known him for the last twelve years; he came into the shop and took a piece of leather out of the basket, looked at it, and asked for some others. I had some suspicion, and took notice of what I put before him. He looked at them and asked for some others; and while I was getting them, he reached over, and slipped down two pieces by the side of the counter, and caught them with his knee. He then bought a pair of me for 71/2d., which he paid for, and went out; I pursued him, and met the constable in the street - we went to his lodgings. He had been home, and was gone out again; we wanted to search, but his wife would not let us, unless we had a warrant - we went to the office, and they told us to go back and break the door open. We went back and found these two pieces of leather in a pan of water, which they had not been in above four or five minutes. I can swear to them - we found the prisoner in Newport-market.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. But he has bought a great number of pieces of leather of you, and whether these were not some of them you cannot tell? A. Yes; I can - I had bought this one pair that day, and they have particular veins on them; being in water so short a time will not alter them.
   CHARLES BOWLER. I am a constable. I went with the witness and found these pieces of leather in a pan.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought a pair of soles for 71/2d.; for which I paid - and if he had any suspicion he naturally would have given an alarm, it being in the middle of the day; I went straight home to my dwelling - I left the inner soles, and went towards the Strand - when I was going down Newport-market I met a man I knew, and while talking to him the prosecutor and the constable came up and charged me with the robbery. They took these two pairs of soles from my apartment, where there were several pairs, all with the prosecutor's mark - they had all been bought at his shop.
   THOMAS LINDEMAN. I live at No. 30, Little New-street, and am a shoemaker. Leather being steeped in water, makes it browner - this part appears to have been in water as high as this mark; being in water will alter the appearance so materially as to make it difficult for a person to identify it.
   THOMAS CLEMENTS. I am a bootmaker, and live at No. 33, St. Martin's-street, Leicester-square. The effect of steeping leather in water will alter the colour, so that no man can identify it. There is nothing at all uncommon in the appearances of these two pieces of leather.
     GUILTY. Aged 52. Confined Twelve Months.
 1738.  JULIA SHEAN was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of September , 1 watch value 2l., the goods of Mark Allen.
   ANN ALLEN. I am the wife of Mark Allen- he is a seafaring man- I live at Poplar. On the 5th of September I left a watch on the mantel-piece - when I went out a little before nine o'clock in the morning; I came back a few minutes after ten - the prisoner had left her key in my place before I went out, and when I returned it was gone, and the watch likewise - I then went after her and took her to one of our officers who found the duplicate in a box with three half-crowns.
   WILLIAM BROWN. I was sent for to take the prisoner. I searched her and found this box, which contained one sovereign, three half-crowns, and this duplicate of a watch; she had before declared she had not seen it.
   THOMAS NEAT. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned with me, between eleven and twelve o'clock on the 5th of September, by the prisoner, for 30s.; I gave her a sovereign and three half-crowns.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. This woman keeps a bad house. She has two or three husbands alive; on the night before she picked up a man and brought him home; they had breakfast and then went out; the man came back and asked me to pawn this watch - I said "Where is the landlady?" he said, "She has got a drop." I came back, and they were not at home; the door was shut - I then opened the window and took my key.
   WILLIAM BROWN re-examined. Q. When you went there, did she say anything to you about it? A. She said all the property she had about her was a few halfpence - we took her in a back room; she threw out those few halfpence - we then searched, and found the box and duplicate: she at first said, before the Magistrate, that Mrs. Allen gave her the watch, and 1s. to pawn it; she afterwards said a young woman gave it to her - in coming home, she said she was a poor unfortunate creature and was sorry she had done the crime.
     GUILTY. Aged 47. Confined Two Months.
 1739.  THOMAS KING was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 wheel-barrow, value 14s., and 1 washing-tub, value 5s., the goods of John Shann.
   JOHN SHANN. I am a confectioner, and live at Twickenham. I lost a wheel-barrow and tub on the 30th of July, from the gate of Hanworth-park, in the high road - I had been taking ice out of a well in the park - the prisoner lived in my service about three years ago.
   THOMAS MATHER. The prosecutor came to me about a wheel-barrow and tub. I went the next morning to Uxbridge fair, where I met the prisoner - I asked him what he had done with the wheel-barrow and tub; he told me where they were - I found them at Hounslow.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating, that the barrow stood in the middle of a road, three-quarters of a mile from any house, and by direction of one Maunder, who had employed him, he took it to his, (Maunder's stables), and immediately he was taken he told the officer it was there.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1740.  WILLIAM ATKINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of August , 1 watch, value 2l., the goods of John Leckenby. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
