 2231. WILLIAM PEARCE and ELIZABETH REVELL were in dicted for stealing, on the   19th of September , 3 bottles, value 9 d  .; and 1 1/2 pint of wine, value 2 s  .; the goods of John Gregory , their master.
   JOHN GREGORY . I live in Newington-causeway, and am an oilman. The prisoners were my servants—Pearce was my porter, and Revell a ser vant in the house—I have lost some bottles, and a pint and a half of wine—this is the wine and bottles—I sell British wines—the value of this is 2 s ., bottles and all—they were found on the prisoner Pearce, in my pre sence—from what he told me, I questioned Revell when she came in—she, after some time acknowledged that she ran down and drew the wine the moment I sat down to dinner, brought it up in a jug, and asked Pearce to get the bottles, which he did, and she filled them with wine, for him to take it home—she said she had never done so before—but in a short time she said she did it once before, when I was bottling a cask of wine, Pearce told her it was very good, and she said she had got him some—this led to other things—I had her sister's house searched, and some of my property was found, which was given up before the Magistrate—I will take Pearce into my service again. WILLIAM MORRIS( police-constable M  93.) On Monday last I was on duty, and saw Pearce putting some bottles into his pocket—I took him with these—I took him to his master, who identified the property.
   Revell's Defence.  Pearce came to me, and said his master was bottling some wine, and he should like to taste some—I went down and drew some—I brought it up and tasted it, it was very good—he said I durst not drink—I said, "Get a bottle;" and it would not hold all—he fetched another, and that was filled, and then the third.
   Pearce.  She gave it to me to take home to my sister, who was very ill.
   PEARCE— GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Five Days.
   REVELL— GUILTY. Aged 35.— Confined Three Months.

MARY NEWLAND was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of September , 1 work-box, value 2 l  . 10 s  .; 2 pairs of spectacles, value 5 s  . 1 spectacle-case, value 6 d  .; 1 knitting-roll, value 2 s  .; 1 fruit-knife, value 3 s  . 1/2 yard of silk, value 2 s  .; 3 yards of lace, value 5 s  .; three yards of trimming, value 1 s  .; and two half-crowns, the goods and monies of James Strong Probert , her master. JAMES STRONG PROBERT. I live in Oxley-place, Lambeth, and am a cabinet-maker. The prisoner was my servant—on the 15th of September she went away, leaving my door open—I missed a box, which was made in my own shop—this is it, and all the contents are mine—the whole is worth about 3 l . 5 s .—the box had not been opened, I believe, as the key was in my possession.
