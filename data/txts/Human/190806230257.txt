I said, "Why not build one, you have got some ground there?" He said he should not build on that be  cause he did not like the position, and he asked me if I could sell him a plot to build a house on. I said, "Yes, where would you like it, Mr. Calcutt?" He said, "Somewhere near your farmhouse." We walked down there, and I showed him a piece of ground that I had, and he said he would like to have the corner plot. I said, "Well, you can have that corner site at £1 per foot, and I should think you would want about 30 ft. to build a decent house—a cottage." He said he would talk the matter over with his wife and let me know about it in a few days. About a fortnight later I saw him in my office in Mile End Road, and he asked me what I thought it would cost him to build. I said, "I think you ought to be the best judge of what you want." He said he thought he ought to have a six-roomed villa for something like £300 to £350. He came again in two or three days and said he had been talking it over with his brother-in-law, Mr. Smith' and his wife, and they had come to the conclusion that, if possible, they would like to have a pair of villas. His brother-in-law, Smith, was to have one and his wife the other, as Mrs. Calcutt thought it would be lonely being there alone. He then asked me if I would sell him another 30 ft. of ground and I said, "Yes." I showed him the plan in the office, and he agreed to buy 60 ft. of the ground for £55. I told him I would get a price from Budd for the villas, and the next time I went to Herne Bay I asked Budd to get me out a rough plan for a pair of villas. He sent me a pencil sketch which gave the particulars, to many thousand bricks, etc., and a rough estimate of about £580. I showed that to Calcutt, who approved. I then wrote to Budd. and he commenced, I think, in December, 1905. Calcutt used to call about every fortnight or three weeks and give me what he liked on account of that building. When we had completed the houses, we had spent £564 in wages and material. Then Calcutt wanted some iron railings put in front of each of the houses and a long wall down the side of the road with a pair of carriage gates, and a swing and one or two other things for the children. That extra work cost £56, which was added to the £584. The whole of the money that Calcutt paid me was paid away on the houses. When he paid me the last cheque for £56 4s. 9d. he said, "I am more than pleased with my houses; I certainly think we ought to make Mr. Budd a present," and he said he would write to Mr. Cordys and Mr. Lloyd, who had the houses built adjoining. He said he thought they ought to give Budd a present of £20, out of which he said, "I shall be quite pleased to give £10 myself, and I think Mr. Lloyd and Mr. Cordys would be also willing to give £5 each." I said if they gave him a present of something he could look at, it would be more pleasant than money, and Calcutt said he did not care which way it was, so I suggested a watch, and the watch was bought and presented to him.
