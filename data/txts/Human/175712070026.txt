I ask'd a friend I saw there if he knew that poulterer, who said yes; I said, if you'll inquire of him where that turkey goes to, you will oblige me. He said he would, and he would leave a line with his mother, where I might call and receive the paper. The two prisoners went round the market two or three times more, and at the upper end of the market they talked a quarter of an hour. Then he went towards the Exchange. I thought the woman was my mark, so I followed her down Bishopsgate street; she said about a quarter of an hour in a tobacconist's shop, and look'd suspiciously about her, from thence she went to another shop, and cross'd the way; from thence to Shoreditch Church, and led me round to Charles Square, Hoxton. She knock'd at the door as if she was mistress of the house, and was let in. I took notice of the house. When I came back to Leaden Hall Market, that person had left a direction with his mother, that the turkey was gone to Mr. Wood's, in Charles Square, Hoxton. The next day, being Sunday, I and Mr Fretwel well went to inquire after them, and at that same house we saw the two prisoners. Wood was in his night gown, and she was behind him looking out at the window. Then we came back and inform'd Mr. Chamberlayne of it. That evening Mr. Fretwell and I had some intelligence of their using the Star in Aldersgate Street. We went there, and got in company with White. After that we went to justice Fielding, on the 6th of October, that remarkable dark Thursday, and then we went with Mr. Chamberlayne, and some proper assistance, to the house, and up stairs. We ask'd the maid where Mr. Wood lay, she said on the right hand side; we went and knock'd at the door, no body would answer; the door was immediately broke open, bat there was no body there. Then we went to the door on the left hand and knock'd there, Mrs Wilcox said no body was there; we said we wanted Mr. Wood, and burst open the door; one of Mr. Fielding's men went down into the yard, and saw Mr. Wood thro' a back window in his shirt; we in the room rummaged their pockets, and took some gold out of each of their pockets; I think we returned Wilcox all hers again. We took five guineas and a half from her, and five guineas from Wood, out of which we returned him two.
 Q. Why did you give her her money again ?
 Kemp. Mr. Fretwell look'd over it, and supposed it to be good, so deliver'd it her again; we kept two guineas and two half guineas of Mr. Wood's, which we supposed to be light.
 Q. Did you keep their money separately ?
 Kemp. No, I am uncertain who the light money was taken from, for we put it all together. (Two guineas and two half guineas produced in court.)
 Q. Were these weigh'd ?
 Kemp. They were. Here is one, a queen Ann's guinea, wants ten grains, one of George the first, ten grains, that is 1 s. and 8 d. each. The two half guineas want eleven grains each, that is 1 s. and 9 d. each.
 Q. Do they appear to have been fresh diminished?
 Kemp. Mr. Fretwell is the best judge of that, he said they did appear so to him.
 Q. to Fretwell. What is your opinion?
 Fretwell. I thought then they were lately done, (be takes them in his hand ) and I think so now.
 Q. Give your reason for your belief?
 Fretwell. They seem to have a shining upon them, as if they had not been negotiated; they have a wax, with which they rub them over to dull the shining. These seem to have been fresh and recently done.
 Council for prisoners. By recently do you understand they have never been in any other hands at all, will not that freshness remain upon them after having past thro' two or three hands?
 Fretwell. I can't say really. (The jury look at them. )
 Council for prisoners. Do you speak of some or all?
 Fretwell. I speak of them all.
 Q. to Kemp. Where did you take this money from?
 Kemp. From out of the room on the right hand. After that we open'd the chest of drawers, and found two pair of shears, and a three corner'd file, ( produced in court with gold on the file.)
 Q. Was that drawer lock'd?
 Kemp. It was.
 Q. Where had you the key ?
 Kemp. I had it of Mrs. Wilcox, I ask'd her for it.
 Q. Are these instruments fit for the purpose of diminishing?
 Kemp. They are. We proceeded then to the next room, that is, the left hand chamber, supposed to be Wilcox's room.
