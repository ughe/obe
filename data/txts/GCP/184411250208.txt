Q. Supposing you were fifty yards from an object perfectly stationary,
and you going at full speed, should you be able to stop your vessel before
coming up to that object ? A. You may easily stop the engine, but the
vessel would not stop in the time-the engine might be stopped-if we were
fifty yards from an object, and going with the tide, we could not prevent
our steamer from going over that object-if we backed our engines, and
starboarded our helm, that would not avoid it with the tide in our favour
-We had a man at the helm-if the object, instead of lying still, were
making to the right, we should be able to avoid it by starboarding our helm,
which would throw our head away—that would take very little time--the
boat came to us faster than our helm would act.
COURT. Q. You say he called out,“ Starboard" just before "Ease her,
stop her!” 4. Yes, we were then so close to the boat that by the time
I got up the boat-hook she was striking against us, at the time the helm
was starboarded the boat was about her own length from us, about twenty-
three feet-the object of starboarding the helm was to clear the boat if
possible--if that had been done when we were fifty yards off, we might
have got further out of her way, her head could have been thrown
more off.
