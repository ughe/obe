   JOHN ROBSON. I am a Policeman. About a quarter-past twelve o'clock in the morning of the 30th of January, I saw the prisoner, and persuaded him to go home - his mother was there, and his father might be there, but I did not see him; he had no knife in his hand then - he was a little in liquor, but not drunk; I saw him home, and about a quarter of an hour afterwards I saw him going along about half double, into a green-grocer's shop; I could not conceive what made him go in that way - I had no reason to suppose any mischief had been done to Shannon before I saw him home the first time; when I saw him going into the green-grocer's, the landlord shoved him out of the shop - as he came up he held the knife up to me, and said, "This is the knife," holding it out to me - it was a pocket-knife; I took it out of his hand - I did not see any blood on it; I put it into my pocket, and said, "Come away home;" he used a vulgar expression, and said, "The b - r was going to murder me, but I have murdered him;" I took him about half-way home again, and in about ten minutes three of my brother officers came up with Flannigan- I took them to the prisoner's house, and took him; I cannot tell at what time Shannon received his injury - the prisoner was apprehended about five minutes past one o'clock.
 JOHN FLANNIGAN. I went to apprehend the prisoner about an hour after the transaction, to the best of my knowledge.
   GEORGE HAYWARD. I am a student at the London-hospital. I saw Shannon on the Sunday, and found a wound on the back part of his leg; there is every reason to expect he will have the use of his leg again, and that he will not be disabled, except by lameness, for a short time; it is not a serious wound, nor in a dangerous part at all, even if it had extended farther than it did - it was about half an inch deep, and near an inch and a half long.
   JAMES MORGAN. I am a Policeman. I should think that it must be a quarter or twenty minutes before one o'clock when Shannon met with this accident - I sent a party of men to disperse a party who were kicking up a row, soon after twelve; I was not present when Shannon was wounded - I saw Barrett come out of Anchor and Hope-alley, swearing murder, but what he meant I do not know; Shannon was brought to the station rather before one - I sent him to the doctor's instantly; I cannot say how long before he had been wounded; I had seen the prisoner twenty minutes or half an hour before that; about ten minutes or a quarter of an hour before Shannon was brought in, I saw the prisoner coming out of the passage where he lives, with a knife in his hand - he appeared in a passion, and used the word Murder! with an oath.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was the row you saw at the Rose and Crown? A. Very near to it; there were ten or fifteen persons there the first time I saw them - that was twenty minutes or half an hour before Shannon was brought in.
   JOHN FLANNINGAN re-examined. As soon as Shannon met with the injury, he was taken home, and in about ten minutes I took him to the station - he was not taken to the doctor's first.
 Prisoner's Defence. My father sent me out for half a gallon of beer for supper; a gentleman gave me a shilling to get it - I had a piece of bread and meat in my hand; I met Shannon, Flannigan, and a man named Wilkes at the top of the alley - they said, "Here comes the b - r," and ran after me; I ran along - I came back, and caught hold of a post; they caught hold of me; I got away - they caught me, put me against a door, and threw me down; Flannigan jumped on me, also Shannon and Wilkes - I called Murder! they tried to choke me; I still called Murder! Cotter came - my belly was burst I thought; Maloney came, and after Cotter opened his door, I crept into his house on my hands and face - I got just by the door; Flannigan and Shannon pulled me out by the legs, with my face and hands on the ground, into the middle of the road.
   MICHAEL COTTER. I keep a green-grocer's shop. On the night in question I heard a row in the street; I heard the prisoner call out to me for protection before I saw him - I went as far as the door, and saw him; Flannigan came up, caught hold of him by the throat, and threw him down right facing my door; he made an attempt to get up, and Flannigan kicked him on the hip - I went and caught hold of Flannigan; another man came up named Quirk (I think) and made an attempt to kick him, but whether he hit or missed I do not know, and the third man who came up was Shannon; he laid over him, and I having hold of Flannigan, my hat fell off; a man named Maloney, who had been in my shop, came over to me, and lifted Shannon off the prisoner - what the prisoner might have done to Shannon I cannot tell - I said, "Barrett, come into my house;" he said, "My belly is broken, I cannot go" - this was between twelve and one o'clock.
   PATRICK MALONEY. On the night this happened I was just outside Cutter's door - Barrett ran up the footpath along the street as hard as he could, and asked Cotter to let him search his place; a man named Flannigan came up, and caught him by the collar; he jostled him down, made a hit at him when he was down, and I believe a kick too - Quirk came up, and made a kick at him while he was down; Shannon came up in a little time; I saved the prisoner as well as I could - I turned back on Shannon, and stopped him from meddling with him; I afterwards found Shannon had received a hurt on his leg - there was no appearance of his being wounded when I first saw him - I assisted in taking him to the doctor's about half an hour after.
 COURT. Q. Did you help to take Shannon home? A. I took him on my back from the watch-house to the doctor's - I have no reason to suppose he was hurt till after he followed the prisoner to Cotter's; he was about three perches behind the others - I did not notice that he was lame.
 Q. You must have noticed if he was lame at that time? A. I dare say I might - he could hardly walk when at the watch-house.
     NOT GUILTY.
