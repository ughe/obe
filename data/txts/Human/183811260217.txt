 226. EDWARD DONALD was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of Octo ber , 1 coat, value 10 s  ., and 1 pair of gloves, value 1 s  ., the goods of John Hop-wood . JOHN HOPWOOD. I am porter to Mr. Taylor, an upholsterer in Dover-road. On the 26th of October, in the afternoon, I hung my coat across the horse in my master's van—I was taking a sofa into Mr. Holt's house, and when I came out my coat was gone off the horse—the gloves were in the pocket. CHARLES NICHOLLS( police-constable R  164.) On the 26th of October I was on duty about half-past six o'clock, and was informed that a coat was concealed under some leaves on the top of, Croom's-hill—I concealed myself in a garden, and about twenty minutes before eight o'clock the prisoner came in company with another one—he came direct to the spot, turned the leaves off, laid hold of the coat, and threw it over his arm—I jumped out and collared him, and the moment I collared him a pair of high shoes fell from him—I asked how he knew the coat was there—he said he did not know it was there, he saw it as he was passing, but he could not have seen it, it was quite covered with leaves—I took up the shoes—the other man made his escape—there was a donkey just by.
   Prisoner . I was taken a quarter of an hour before the boots were produced—I know nothing of it whatever—I was walking across the park, there was a donkey before me and a young man—he said, "Look at that donkey"—I passed it, and it stumbled over the coat and turned the tail up—the constable came up and charged me with stealing it—he went there afterwards and found the boots.
   Witness . The donkey did not disturb the leaves—I was concealed there an hour before he came—there was six inches of leaves over the coat, and it was three yards out of the path—the donkey did not tread near the leaves.
 (  Property produced and sworn to .) GUILTY.—Aged 14.
 227. EDWARD DONALD was again  indicted for stealing, on the   26th of October , 2 pairs of shoes, value 16 s  .; and 1 shoe, value 2 s  .; the goods of William Newsom . WILLIAM NEWSOM. I lost some shoes and boots out of my shop on the 26th of October, about half-past seven o'clock—my shop is about a quarter of a mile from Croom's-hill—I ran down the street on missing them, and went to the station-house—somebody must have got into my shop to get them—I was in the shop—they must have opened the door and crawled in. CHARLES NICHOLLS( police-constable R  164.) I saw the prisoner near the spot where the coat was concealed, about half-past seven o'clock or twenty minutes to eight—I jumped up and laid hold of him, and a pair of high shoes fell from him—I saw his companion go twenty or thirty yards from the spot, and go round the corner of a wall and stoop—I afterwards received information that these shoes were stolen—the prisoner denied that the shoes fell from him, but I am certain they did—I found the others be hind the wall where I saw his companion stoop.
