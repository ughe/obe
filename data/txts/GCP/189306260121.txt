ARTHUR SEWELL (Detective W). On 7th May I was called to and I
examined the Wurtemberg Arms-entry had been effected by breaking
the back-parlour window and moving the latch, and opening the window
-on one of the curtains I found blood as though from & cut handon

cupboard a quantity of farthings tied up in a handkerchief-the prisoner
was then at home, and he had two sons and a young lad (Milne) sleeping
in the wood-shed.

WILLIAM NEIGHBOUR. On 25th May I charged the prisoner at his
house-he said, "All right; I will go with you, but I know nothing about
it "-I found on him this fourpenny piece.
Cross-examined. I went to the place to which you asked me to
go and saw the baker and his wife, and she told me your wife had been to
make inquiries, and she said she told her that she had not the slightest ro-
collection of having received a fourpenny piece from you or your family,
and if she had she would have recollected it.
