MOSES GIDDINS. On the night of the 20th of september I was at the Water loo Arms public-house—I cannot say the time, but I do not think it had gone twelve o'clock—I am a milkman, and live at Camberwell—I had 2 l . 4 s . 6 d . in one pocket when I was in the Waterloo Arms, and 3 s . 6 d . left in another pocket when I came out—there were two sovereigns, four shillings, and a sixpence—Ellis was with me at the Waterloo Arms—I had not pot there, which was all I paid for—I had not been drinking any where else—I paid for one pot on my own account—I had drank with others—I do not know how much I drank—I was sober when I left the Waterloo Arms—I found myself in Waterloo-street and the three prisoners came up with several others—I am sure of the prisoners—when I got opposite them, Mills sung  out, " Whitey! " (I had a white coat on)—I was going to make some observa tion; but before I could get the wards out of my mouth, I was knocked down by Mills—he hit me in the mouth with his fist—I had given him no provocation—when I was down I found his hand in my left hand waistcoat pocket; but I cannot swear he took my money—It had been in my left hand waistcoat pocket—Mills had his hand in my pocket, and Cop per came and pitched  down on my head—all his body came down on my head—he fell right across my face when I was down—I sung  out for my brother to come and assist me—he was in the Waterloo Arms, and Ellis the gardener was round the corner—he heard me halloo out, and came be fore my brother did—Ellis came and pulled Copper off me; and when I got off, Simpkins was at me—he hit at me, but I do not hink he struck me—he hit at me in the row  —that was after I called out—all three of them stood in front of me when I got up—when Ellis pulled Copper off my body, all three of them stood before me—Simpkins was with the other two when I was knocked down, but I did not see him do any thing—when I got up, they all hit at me—they did not strike me—when I got out of the crowd I pulled my jacket off, they were all three at me—Copper and Mills hit me—I never swore I knocked two of them down, and afterwards ran away—I could not—I shoved them off the gardener—what I stated before the Magistrate was read over to me, and I put my mark to it—I did not knock two of them down, that I know of—I shoved them down, and they were on the ground, but not with a blow, with a snove—I shoved them with my double fist—there is a difference between striking a man and shoving him—I pushed against them with my clenched fist—as soon as I missed my money I cut  away to the station-house—I missed my money after they got off the ground—I put my hand into my pocket, and they were gone—that was the same pocket into which Mills had introduced his hand—I took Mills into custody—the policeman took him in bed—he told me he had not had my money—Mills said he would give me 1 l . on Monday morning, if I would let him go that night—he did not use the word "for give. "
   Q.  If you have beer taken down to say that he said if you would for give him, it is not true? A.  If I would forgive him and let him go—If he had given me 1 l . that night, I should have forgiven him.
