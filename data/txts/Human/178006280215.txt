I observed the prisoner among the people within the circle, appearing to me to incite and instigate that part of the mob, to the best of my recollection, it might then be about half an hour after seven o'clock, which was about half an hour after the mob assembled. I observed him several times changing his situation, and lastly, I think about nine o'clock, I saw some of the keys, which were said to be the keys of Newgate, in the hands of a person, but I cannot identify the person who had the keys; there were four or five together who were genteelly dressed; the prisoner was amongst them; the keys were held up, but by whom I cannot say; but amongst those persons was the prisoner; the keys were carried through the crowd by those people who appeared to draw off towards Smithfield, among whom was the unfortunate black (Bowsey) whom I considered to be the servant of one of those people, and the prisoner; and then I saw no more of it.
 What time was that? - I think about nine, rather before than after.
 Did you observe in what clothes the prisoner was dressed? - A dark coloured drab, as it appeared to me.
 The same he has on now? - They appeared darker I think.
 Cross Examination.
 Where did those keys come from which were in the hands of some persons? - They seemed to come from towards the felons door.
 Was the prisoner doing any thing? - No.
 Can you take upon you to swear that you can so draw the line between the guilty and the innocent spectators as to say who were guilty and who were innocent? - I can in this way, the persons who are innocent stood inactive the others were in action.
 Where those keys came from you cannot say? - No.
 The prisoner was not a mischievous spectator? - No; I did not see him do any thing.
 You did not see him take up the keys? - No, he was in company with one of the persons who had the keys.
 You did not see him do any mischief? - No.
   ANN WOOD sworn.
 Was you in the Old Bailey at the time Mr. Akerman's house was destroyed? - Yes. I was at the dining-room window all the time.
 Did you observe any person with large keys? - Yes, I did; I saw a man held up some keys in one hand and his hat in the other huzzaing.
 Did you see that person do any thing else? - No; before I saw him with the keys I saw that person, whom I take to be the prisoner, poking something to the fire with a stick, but I could not see what it was.
 Did you observe how the person was dressed? - In a lightish brown coat, a cocked up hat with a blue cockade in it.
 Look at the prisoner do you believe that to be the person? - I saw the prisoner come with the mob when it first came; and when I saw the man hold up the keys at the fire and poke something with his stick I thought it was the same man, but I am not certain; I am certain the prisoner is the person whom I saw come with the mob.
 Court. Are you sure you saw the prisoner come with the mob at first? - Yes.
 Did you see the person who had the keys go into the jail? - I saw a man go into the jail at break of day; I believe it was the person who had the keys, but I am not certain that it was the same man.
 Cross Examination.
 You cannot positively swear to the prisoner? - No.
 The man you saw with the keys go into the prison at break or day you believe to be the prisoner? - Yes.
 Every body had cockades almost? - Yes.
 The idle, the busy, the wicked, and innocent, all had cockades? - Yes.
 You saw a great many persons there who were not doing mischief? - I did.
 What sort of were they? - One was a large br key, brighter than the rest.
