1761. WILLIAM EDWARDS and ELIZABETH EDWARDS were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August 17 lbs. of feathers, value 25s., the goods of George Armstrong.
   SARAH ARMSTRONG. I am the wife of George Armstrong- we live in Ironmonger-row, St. Luke's. The two prisoners took our back parlour, furnished, on the 2nd of August - they left on the 20th, and the next day I found the bed had been opened; a week before they left the female borrowed a needle of me, and returned it with a piece of black thread in it - I found the bed had been sewn up with the name kind of thread; we had weighed the bed when they came - there were then 50 lbs. of feathers, and when they were gone it weighed 17 lbs. less - when the female was taken she told the Policeman we could not do any thing with her, because we did not see her take them; she was taken on a charge of stealing feathers - they owed us 5s. when they went away.
 CHARLES SCOTCRMER. I am an officer. On the 21st of August I took the female prisoner, and told her it was for stealing feathers - she said they could not hurt her, for they did not see her do it - on the 23rd I took her husband - he said he thought it not worth his while to stay away any longer, that he heard she was remanded for a week, and he therefore gave himself up.
 E. EDWARDS -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Six Months.
 W. EDWARDS -    NOT GUILTY.
 1762.  ELIZABETH GRIFFEN was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , 10 handkerchiefs, value 35s., the goods of John Veall.
   GEORGE LEWIS HOWES. I am in the employ of Mr. John Veall, linen-draper, of Islington. On the 12th of July the prisoner came to the shop, and asked to look at some silk handkerchiefs; she appeared very respectable, and we showed her some - she had six cut off; she selected articles which came to about 4l., which she desired might be sent to Goulden-terrace, at half past two o'clock, and said she would leave 8s. deposit; she had a boy with her, and I suspected she had some handkerchiefs - I followed her about ten yards, and asked her what she had in the bag; she said I should not see; I was taking her back, and she attempted to run away - I brought her back, and the officer took her; these ten handkerchiefs were found on her - I asked her reason for doing so, she said, "Bad luck."(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner handed in a petition, for a lenient sentence. She received an excellent character. GUILTY Aged 23.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor. Confined Six Months.
 1763.  MARIA HEMLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of September , 1 shawl, value 8s., the goods of Grace Roberts.
 GRACE ROBERTS. I  keep a coffee-shop, in Widegate-street, Bishopsgate. On the 1st of September the prisoner came and I trusted her with bread, butter, and coffee, which came to 6d.; she came again on the 2nd, and asked me to trust her again which I did - and when she was gone I missed my shawl; she was taken at twelve o'clock the same night.
 CHARLES STILL. I am in the employ of Mr. Wood, a pawnbroker, in Union-street East, Spitalfields. I produce a shawl, which I took in of the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been drinking and did not know what I was doing; I pawned the shawl to get me a lodging, thinking it was my own.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. Transported for Seven Years.
 1764.  JAMES FORD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 30 penny-pieces, and 60 half-pence, the monies of Henry Pye.
   HENRY PYE. I keep the sign of  the St. Luke's Church, Wenlock-street, St. Luke's; the prisoner has lodged with me for the last ten or twelve months. On the 19th of August I remember he went out about eleven o'clock in the evening, to get a little money owing to him - I expected him back that evening, but he had no key to let himself in - he used to get over the wall sometimes, and get in at his own bed-room window; the Police-officer called me up about two o'clock, and said somebody was in the house - I was going down stairs, and saw the prisoner on the stairs; I said, "Jem, how can you be so foolish, why did you not call me up, without knocking at the door, and making a bother with the Policemen?" I let the Policemen in at the back door - they said somebody had been in the bar - I opened the bar, and missed a 5s. paper of halfpence; it was safe about half an hour before I went to bed - I was in the tap-room when the prisoner was searched, but I cannot say, from the fright I was in, whether I saw the Police-officers take a bundle from him, but I heard it smack down on the table; I have no doubt it came out of the prisoner's pocket - I believe it is what I had taken for 5s.; I had locked the hat, but there is a sort of half-door, which a person might lift up and unbolt one holt, and any little thing would unbolt the other; the Policemen found this wire in the tap-room, it had come from a window of the stairs, where we keep empty cans; we found a candle in a gin measure; it was not there when I went to bed.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe the prisoner apprised you of his going out that night? A.
