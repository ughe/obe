 MR. CLARKSON. Q.When the woman was brought in, did you examine your stock of chrystals? A. No, but I did when the male prisoner ran away, and missed a considerable number.
 COURT. Q. I see part of this is clear, and the other part is not? A. Yes, I knocked a piece off, to see if it was clear, to go to work.
   WILLIAM FORTY. I am a Thames Police-surveyor. On Saturday morning, the 26th of September, I was in Craven-street, and saw the female prisoner with this basket of chrystals; I asked how she came by it - she said she brought it from home, and was to have met her husband at Charing-cross, but, as he had not come, she was going back with it; I left her in charge of an officer, made inquiries, and took her to Mr. Barnett's house - in consequence of what I said, the male prisoner came down; I asked if he had given his wife any chrystal - he said Yes, he had half an hour before, and he would put on his coat and explain it to me; he went up stairs - I followed him a few steps, and he said," Come on;" I thought it was all right, and waited for him - he made his escape at the top of the house; I went up to the top of the house, and saw some saw-dust, and a foot-mark in the gutter - a person might get from the workshop window, to the window of the room where the chrystal is kept; the rooms are on the same floor.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q.What time did you meet her? A.Between seven and eight o'clock in the morning; I do not know that the prisoner was afraid of a civil process for debt.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q.When he talked about his coat he did not say any thing about a civil process? A. No.
   ESTHER BARNETT. I am the prosecutor's wife. I know these two pieces of chrystal - I know this one by its having a rough coat, and by its form; it is my husband's - they were among our stock of chrystals; I had seen them on the Friday.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. I suppose if you had seen them in Whitechapel, or any where, you would have known them again? A. Yes, they have been so often in my hands; I do not know that there ever were such pieces any where else - (looking at some other pieces produced by the prisoners' Counsel) I know some of these to be ours; this one by a flaw, and this by its being partly coloured.
   HANNAH BARNETT. I am the prosecutor's daughter. I know these two pieces, and know this piece; I had seen it on the Friday morning, and washed it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you know this piece? A. Yes - these two pieces I had given to the prisoner; this other I know by a piece being knocked off by a chisel.
   THOMAS DOWN. I was in the prosecutor's service. Hamilton has told me he could see what o'clock it was on the leads - I have seen him get out of the window; St. Martin's clock cannot be seen from the front room - it lays at the back of the house; I have seen him get up at the window about twice a day.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How long did he stay? A.Perhaps two minutes, or a minute - he was in my sight while he was in the room.
   THOMAS FOGG. I am an officer. I took the male prisoner on the 28th of September, on Tower-hill - I told him it was on suspicion of a felony at Mr. Barnett's; he said he was going to Mr. Barnett's.
 Thomas Hamilton's Defence. No person can go from the top of the house to the back premises - his daughter has sent me out to see the time; Down stood by me all day at work - chrystal is a thing no man can swear to; I escaped because I was in debt.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I am a lapidary, and live in Goswell-street. I have been in the business fifty years - I know the nature of chrystal; it is not uncommon for it to have a rough coat - I could not swear to chrystal, nor can any man; I gave half a dozen pieces of chrystal to this gentleman, but I could not identify them after they are out of my sight; I had these pieces ever since May last.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Had you ever bought chrystal of the prisoner? A. I bought some in a worked state eight or nine months ago, which he told me he bought of Mr. Cashmore.
 COURT. Q. Did you know that at that time he was in the employ of a dealer in chrystals? A. He told me that Mr. Barnett had not employ for him, and he had bought some chrystals.
   JAMES COX. I am an optician, and live in Barbican. I have dealt in chrystals all my life - it is impossible to swear to them without having a private mark; I have bought chrystals of the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.

 2038.  JANE BAIN was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of September , 49 prints, value 40s., the goods of Henry Triggs; and ROBERT SIMPSON was indicted for feloniously receiving part of the said prints, well knowing them to have been stolen.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating them to be the goods of  Thomas Norton Longman and other persons.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND and CRESWELL conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY TRIGGS. I am a copper-plate printer, employed by Messrs. Longman, Rees and Co., Westley and Co., and Mr. Marshall, to take impressions from steel plates for the forthcoming annuals, the Friendship's Offering, the Gem, the Literary Souvenir, and the Juvenile Forget Me Not; I was employed first about June last - the respective proprietors find paper for those plates: I had struck off impressions from them all, previous to the 19th of September, but never gave any of them out of my custody. The female prisoner was in my employ, as servant, and had been so about six weeks previous to her apprehension - I gave her a positive injunction when she came into my service not to touch the prints; on the 18th or 19th of September she was taken into custody - it was on a Thursday; I accused her of stealing the prints - she said she was very sorry, that she had given them to her mother for her youngest brother, and her mother was a very wicked bad woman to sell them - I do not recollect her saying how many she had taken.
