Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing of the first charge; I was at home,

559. HARRIET SMITH and ELIZABETH HARRIS were indicted
for a like offence. MEssRs. ELLIS and POLAND conducted the Prosecution.
MaRY SPRATT. I am staying at the Black Cap, in High-street, Camden
Town. On Saturday, 2nd April, I was in the bar about 9 o'clock at night
-the two prisoners came in together-Smith asked for half a quartern of gin
and peppermint-she gave me in payment a sixpence-] gave ber a 4d.-piece
in change-it was a black-looking one, and she refused to take it-I gave
her another- laid the sixpence down on a little counter a little below the
other counter-the prisoners went away together-I left the sixpence on the
counter, and our barinan saw it-I saw him take it up, and he said it was a
bad one-I saw him take it from the place where I put it-the prisoner
same back in about two bours, about 11 o'clock-I did not see them come
in, but I came into the bar, and saw them there-they had half a quartern of
gin and peppermint again-they paid for that in copper money-while they
were drinking it I came in, and they put the measure towards me, and asked
me for half a quartern of peppermint, and I think Harris gave me a sixpence
for that-I tried it, and it bent-I went to the barman, and I said, Here
are those two women who have uttered another bad sixpence". gave it
to the barman, and he gave the prisoners into custody.
Smith. The first time was in your house was 20 minutes before 11
o'clock. Witness. No; that was the second time-you had been before, and
refused a 4d.-piece that I gave you.
Harris. Q. Did you say that I gave you a sixpence A. I think it was,
but I could not be positive-yo were both together.
FREDERICK SHELLEY. I am barman at the Black Cap. On 2nd April I

46 Give me half a quartern of gin and peppermint," in great haste-they had

Smith. Q. You said you marked the sixpence when you took it, but you
