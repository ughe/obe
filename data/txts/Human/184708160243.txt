On the 6th of July, about twenty-five minutes past ten at night, I saw her in the yard—she had taken a drop, but was not too far gone then—she was able to walk—she had the child in her arms, holding it round, close to her—I took it out of her arms, and said, "Take care of your child, and yourself too"—she did not seem satisfied to part with it, and I gave it her back again, settled it to her in her arms, and closed the shawl up—she was not holding it in a dangerous way, but the shawl was hanging down, and I settled it about the baby—she then went towards the Bull public-house, and I lost sight of her—I saw her again in about five minutes, going by the thoroughfare that there was—there is a drain there, she missed her footing, and fell down with the baby in her arms—the baby happened to come on its head; it fell under her—I went towards her, she picked herself up imme diately, and went through the thoroughfare—I heard the child groan after she rose—I did not see her again till the Tuesday morning following.
   COURT Q.  Was she behaving kindly towards the child at the time you settled it to her, as you say? A.  Yes—what I did to the child was out of kindness, and she took it as such—she took the child kindly to her bosom, then walked away, and fell by accident—a sober person might have had just such a fall in the same place, there is no light there—it is an open drain, which a sober person might have fallen over.  1991. JAMES BRUCE was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying Mary Allsworth ; he was also charged on the Coroner's Inquisition with the like manslaughter.
   MR. PLATT conducted the prosecution. ANN DIGWEED. I am the wife of William Digweed, a grocer, of High-street, Lambeth. On Saturday night, the 10th of July, about half-past ten, I was behind the counter—the prisoner came in very tipsy, and staggering—he asked how I did, then turned round from me to the boy Blake, who was sitting on a sugar-tub by the door, and began pulling him about, roughing up his hair on each side, and saying, "You are the chap that serves out the coffee"—he pulled him off the tub, and pushed him against the street-door, he then dragged him back to the counter, then took him round the head with his arm, and the boy being shorter than himself, he had that power over him that he reeled him round and round, and I thought both would have fallen into the street—he took the boy off his feet—they both went out into the street together, and as they went out, they met an old lady, who was stepping on the edge of the path with her supper-beer in her hand, and with one blow they went against her, and knocked her backwards—they were both together at the time the blow was struck—the prisoner had the boy round as he went out of the shop, and went against the old lady—it is a small shop, and the counter is near the door—I could see pretty plainly, there was a lamp alight at a public-house opposite—the old lady turned out to be Mrs. Allsworth, who I knew very well as living four doors off—I saw her fall, with her jug in her hand, as if she had fallen from some high place, she fell direct on the back of her head—I ran out to her, and heard her say, "Oh my head; this will be my death-blow"—I lifted her up, and she was taken home—the pri soner
