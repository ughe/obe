 1662.  JAMES MALONEY was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 1 shirt, value 2s.; 1 hat, value 3s.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d.; 1 shawl, value 2s.; 1 sheet, value 3s., and 1 pair of trousers, value 2s., the goods of Cornelius Gore.
 CORNELIUS GORE. I live in  Cato-street. I missed this property on Monday week last; and on the following morning, at nine o'clock, I met the prisoner in Langham-place, and found it all in his possession - I asked how he came by them; he made no answer; I took him to the watch-house.
   ELEANOR GORE. I am the prosecutor's wife - the prisoner slept at our house on this night; the things hung on a line in his room, at six o'clock that evening - the hat hung in the same room - at seven o'clock in the morning he was gone, and I missed them.
   ROBERT WILLIAMS. I am an officer. I took him into custody at a cook's-shop.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 34. Transported for Seven Years.
 1663.  MARY WEST was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of June , 1 gown, value 6s., and 2 caps, value 4s., the goods of James Carrol.
   ESTHER CARROL. I am the wife of James Carrol - we live in Vine-street, Westminster. On the 28th of June I went out at twelve o'clock, and told Webber, who lives opposite, to watch my room - the door was not locked - I fastened the latch with a nail - when I returned, at one o'clock, I saw the prisoner coming out of the street-door with a bundle; I knew her before I found my door ajar, and missed these things.
   SARAH WEBBER. I was left in charge of the room, and went out at half-past twelve o'clock - next morning I met the prisoner in the Broad-way, and followed her to Harris' the pawnbroker; I went in and had her detained.
   JOHN JACKSON. I am shopman to Harris. About four o'clock this gown was pledged by a female, about the prisoner's size.
   JAMES PATMORE. I took her in charge, and found the cap on her head.
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 16.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1664.  MATHEW NORMAN, PHILIP OLIVER and PATRICK BRYAN, were indicted for stealing on the 24th of August , 1 wrapper, value 6d., and 2 table-cloths, value 16s., the goods of John Connor , from the person of Eleanor, his wife.
   ELEANOR CONNOR. I am the wife of John Connor. On the 24th of August, at night, I was in Buckridge-street, and had two table-cloths in a wrapper; they were snatched from me - I thought the boys who did it were taller than the prisoners - I have bad eyes - I think the person who snatched them threw them down a cellar - I think there was a man, who received them - I believe they ran away, but I did not see them.
 Q. Did you see them thrown down the cellar? A. I think they were, but it was very dark - the persons got away - I have not found the property.
   WILLIAM BISHOP. I heard of the prosecutrix being robbed - and on the 25th of the watchman brought Bryan to me, with the prosecutrix - she said in his presence, "This is one of the boys who robbed me last night of the tablecloths" - I said, "Are you sure of that?" - she said, Yes, and she knew the others, and mentioned their names - I then asked, if he was the boy who took it out of her hand - she said, "No, but he ran away with it." - I did not lock him up, but sent her to get the others; and in the morning, from information Bryan gave me, I went and took Norman and Oliver out of bed - Bryan was with me, and said, in their presence, "It was Norman took the bundle from Mrs. Connor, and threw it down the tinker's cellar" - Oliver and Norman said Bryan took it - Connor said, in their presence, that they were the boys who took it, and they did not deny it, but said the tinker had the property; I did not see the prosecutrix on the night of the robbery - her husband told me, the boys' parents had been and made it up.
   CATHERINE McGRATH. This business was settled in my apartment, and the prosecutrix promised not to come here. I know the prisoners and their parents.
 The prisoners put in a joint written Defence, stating, that the prosecutrix had offered Norman 1s. to lay the charge upon the other prisoners, since which she had been paid for the property by their friends.
 NORMAN -    GUILTY. Aged 11.
 OLIVER -    GUILTY. Aged 13.
 BRYAN -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1667  JOHN BUDD TARRELL was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , 1 coat, value 10s., and 1 waistcoat, value 5s., the goods of John Peters.
   JAMES WADE. I am a fireman at the London-docks. On the 16th of August, about ten o'clock, I saw the prisoner go on board the Susannah, where I was at work;(John Peters is the mate) he came away in ten minutes; I called to Peters, and asked if he knew him - he said, No; we followed him - he saw us, and ran off - we overtook him, and found he had Peters' coat and waistcoat on, under his own; he said he took them from the cabin; Peters claimed them - he went down in the cabin and pulled them off - but not in my presence.
   THOMAS BOLTEN. I am an officer. Peters gave me the clothes.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. Confined Three Months.
