ROBBRT LARKIN, sen. I am the witness's father. On the 5th of Aug., about six o'clock in the afternoon, I had been to the police-court, and Mrs. Traebody was bound to keep the peace—when I went to Stamford-place, Mn. Hart abused me—my son went for a policeman, and when he returned with one, the prisoner and Finn came up—Finn struck my son and then the prisoner struck him—I saw them struggling together afterwards, and there was fighting and quarrelling going on with the others—I was assaulted but not by the prisoner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  you had a stick? A.  Yes—I shook the stick at Mrs. Hart when she had scratched my daughter-in-law and pulled her hair, and she served me so—I did not give her a gentle tap with the stick—I dare say it was two feet from her. GEORGE KIDNEY( police-constable L  179.) I was on duty on the 5th of Ang.—Robert Larkin fetched me—I went with him—when I got to the corner of Stamford-place, Finn and the prisoner came running round the corner—they came up to Larkin, and both of them pitched into  him—I was standing there—they both struck me—Finn struck me first, and I instantly laid hold of Finn—I saw Parsons—I called him to assist in holding Finn, and Parsons was thrown down by Finn, I believe—when I took hold of Finn, I said to Larkin, "Get hold of the other,"or something to that effect-1 did not see the knife used at all—there was fighting going on—I was bit in the arm by one of the women.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not the first thing you saw Mrs. Hart running away? A.  I saw her running from the place where old Mr. Larkin was—she seemed to be going to fetch somebody to her assistance—she went in (he direction in which I afterwards saw Finn and the prisoner come.
