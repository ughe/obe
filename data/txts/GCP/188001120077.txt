SYDNEY LEAROYD. I have the misfortune to be a large loser in this
concern-I put in 2,0001. to begin with, 4,2001. altogether, and have lost it-
I never had any active connection with this firm-I first put in capital
about March, and then some in May-I went to Paris for the first time in
May with my brother; they had then established the Paris house - I don't
know whether the rent of it was paid in advance; there was an office and
& salesman there-Dunderdale had then joined the firm as a buyer after
my return from Paris I saw him and told him I thought the business in
Paris looked very nice, and I thought they were doing a good business I
was only there for a week--I did not go round with the traveller-I saw
people coming in and out-I went to Paris on pleasure; I went to Paris
aguin a few weeks afterwards, and went to the business premises --Wilton
was with me—we looked at the stock, and he telegraphed to Dunderdale to
come over, which he did he looked at the stock, and went round with
the salesman to see the customers—that was about the latter end of May or
the beginning of June—Dunderdale remained in Paris a week or 10 days—
he went out very nearly every day to the business premises I said he
looked very busy, walking about all day long I cannot tell whether it was
after I had been to Paris on the second occasion that I put more money
into the business; it may have been after-I was first acquainted with the
fact that the firm was insolvent on 27th September-I got a telegram-I
then went to Mr. Samuel Learoyd at Huddersfield, who is the solicitor con-
ducting this prosecution-I explained to him the nature of my transactions
in the matter-I have not taken any active part in the business--I have
no idea what Dunderdale received; I knew nothing of any money being
paid to him--I knew that he was agent to two large firms in the North
before he entered the service--I do not know at whose instance he left.!
Cross-examined. I was a member of this firm, but not a willing one
-I first put in the 2,0002. because they wanted money to meet some bills
--they looked so busy in Paris that I was quite taken in--my second loan
was 1,0001.; they told me if I did not let them have the money they
would go to smash; that was in March-my 2,0002 set them on their legs
again--then I let them have 2,2002. mare-I unfortunately signed my
name to a paper as a partner, so they took the rest; they wanted a bank
as a reference, so I signed my name to a paper to say that they had
80 much money of mine in the firm in order to give the bank a s
reference to manufacturers my second visit to Paris was at the latter
end of May or June, I cannot recollect the date_I was scarcely ever in
London, I have always been on pleasure I know nothing of business I
heard Dunderdale and some other member of the firm talking of buying
goods on credit - I could not swear it was Dunderdale-I have sworn it
once, but it was a mistake, I could not swear it now-I know nothing of
goods being sold below cost prioe and the rest being pledged and never
redeemed I did not know that when I put in my 2,2002., if I had I don't
think I should have done it.
Re-examined. I was examined at Huddersfield for the prosecution and
cross-examined on behalf of Dunderdale I then said the conversation
between Wilton and Dunderdale did not alarm me I was then speaking
of about three months ago, but I was not certain about the conversation.
By the Jury. I received money from the firm-I was spending money
I always lived as a gentleman.
