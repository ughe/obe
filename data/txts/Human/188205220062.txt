On Sunday afternoon, 7th May, I was on duty outside Chingford Railway Station; I heard some thing, and went and saw the defendant's horse being held—Mr. King showed me a cut on his head, and from what he said I took the prisoner in custody—he was charged with riding furiously alongthe highway, to the danger of persons passing along; I have not got the charge-sheet here.
   Cross-examined.  That is the ordinary charge under the Police Act for the 40 s . penalty—it was the only charge against him at that time; it was not turned into maliciously wounding when Mr. Felton came down the same night—Mr. King did not say that he should not have said any-thing about it but for the prisoner trying to get away—I did not see a gentleman leading a horse—the prisoner gave his name and address, and was allowed to go.
