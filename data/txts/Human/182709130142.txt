MATILDA WILLIAMS. On the 15th of July, between twelve and one o'clock in the morning, I was going along Long-lane; the prisoner passed me, and said, "Are you going home, Mrs. Williams?" he knew me; he is a drover, I believe - he said, "Shall I go home with you?" - I said, "No, I thank you," - I had not gone many steps before he tripped me up, and struck me on the left eye; he tore my shawl off, then tore my gown open, and took my handkerchief off my neck, and my cap off; he then took out a knife, and cut off my pockets, which contained my money - I screamed out, and he struck me over the head, with a stick, and got away.
   SARAH CLINTON. On the morning of the 15th of July, about four o'clock, I saw the prisoner in Fleet-market; he said to me, "Will you buy a shawl?" and showed it to me - I said, "That is Mrs. Williams';" he said, "It is, but you need not mention that, and you shall have it for 1s." - I said I would not have it on any account, and went away.
   DAVID EVANS. I am a watchman. I took up the prisoner on Monday morning, the 16th of July, between twelve and one o'clock - I had received information of this robbery from one Benning - I called the prisoner out of a public-house; he objected to come - I then called my fellow watchman, and we took him to the watch-house; we searched, but found nothing on him; he said he did not know any thing about Mrs. Williams, but afterwards confessed that he saw her part of the way home.
   WILLIAM BUSHNELL. I am a watchman. I went with Evans to the Bull's Head public-house, and told the prisoner what he was charged with; he denied it, but afterwards said he had taken the prosecutrix home to where she lodged, and the landlord refused to take her in.
 MATILDA WILLIAMS re-examined. I raised an alarm, but it is a bye place, near the pig-market; my forehead was bleeding, and my left eye.
 Prisoner's Defence (written). I assure your Honour, that I am perfectly innocent, not withstanding the false allegations made to prove my guilt, of the charge, and which evidence, were I sufficiently competent, or enabled to employ counsel, I feel confident, would clearly be made to appear, in the course of the investigation, as completely false; and a case made up between the parties against me, who are of the most abandoned character, and whose manner of obtaining a livelihood, is by the most base means; as to the above-named person (Williams) I know her to keep a house of the most abandoned character, it being a common receptacle for prostitutes; and the witness, that she has caused to appear against me, I am totally unacquainted with; but I have no doubt, if questioned, that she would appear to be one connected with the prosecutrix in those, her abandoned pursuits, by which means I have, in the present instance, tallen an unfortunate victim. I, however, trust that by the following statement, you will see the case against me is altogether a false charge:- In the month of July, at about twelve o'clock at night, I had (on Saturday) been spending the evening with some of my workmates, at the sign of the Horse and Groom public-house, Cow-cross, and in leaving the company, and passing through Smithfield, on my way home, I met the prosecutrix, in company with a watchman, who I asked what he was going to do with her; he said he was going to take her to the watch-house, she being then in a state of intoxication - I told the watchman I would see her home, if he would allow me; and accordingly, knowing where she lived, from being in the habit of passing and repassing her house, I proceeded to take her home, where, after a deal of trouble, I arrived, and the woman belonging to the house came to the door, who, on telling her I had brought home on of her lodgers, denied that she (Williams) was a lodger of hers, and refused to admit her - with this I left her at the door, and went home.
   SARAH CLINTON re-examined. I had been carrying out things for washer-women, but was out of place at that time - I knew Mrs. Williams by working at different laundresses.
   DAVID EVANS. The prosecutrix lives at a Mr. Webb's- she had marks of violence on her.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 19.

THOMAS SMITH and WILLIAM BUTLER were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , 1 basket, called a flat, value 1s.; 1 gown, value 2s. 6d.; 1 handkerchief, value 6d.; 3 dead pigeons, value 1s. 6d.; 2 dead fish, value 1s.; 2lbs. weight of pork, value 1s. 6d.; 4 quarts of currants, value 1s. 4d.; 20 eggs, value 1s. 6d., and 4lbs. weight of potatoes, value 4d., the goods of William Barker.
 SECOND COUNT, stating them to be the property of  John Reed.
 WILLIAM BARKER. I am  carter to Thomas Wright, of Sawbridgworth. On the 16th of July I came to Smithfield with some lambs in my cart - there was a flat on the top of the cart, and as I crossed the market, with a load under my arm, I saw Smith leading my horse - I called to him to stop - he did so; I then missed the flat from the cart - it contained several things; I had received it from John Reed, to leave at the Ram Inn, Smithfield; Smith brought the flat back - I had not seen him take it.
   GEORGE BUSH. I am a watchman. On the 16th of July, about a quarter to four o'clock in the morning, I stood about forty yards from the cart, and saw Smith take the flat off at the corner of King-street - I ran down West-street, and saw Butler standing near the work-house in the street; Smith went up and spoke to him, and gave it to him: Smith saw me, and came up towards me; we came up to Smithfield, and he gave the flat up.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Who is Butler? A. His father keeps a public-house in Smithfield; I do not know that the house is usually opened early; he had not got hold of Smith; he said, when I was taking them to the watch-house, that he had taken it from Smith, having stopped him with it - that was within a minute or two of the time. I ate nothing that was in the basket.
   SUSAN REED. I live at Harlow, and am John Reed's brother. I know this gown and handkerchief; the other things have not been saved.
   ABRAHAM GRIFFITH. I am a constable. I received charge of the prisoners. This gown and handkerchief were in the basket when it was opended at the office.
 SMITH's Defence. Butler knew nothing about it.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 BUTLER -    NOT GUILTY.
