Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
404. JOHN HENRY CALCOTT (32) PLEADED Guilty to unlawfully
publishing false and defamatory libels of and concerning Albert Henry
Wood. Discharged on recognisances.-(405) ERNEST BLONDELİ.
(26), to stealing two boxes of cigars, a box of cigarettes, two bottles of
whisky, and 24s., the property of William Henry Lunn. Three months'
hard labour.-(406) EDWARD MORGAN (44), to wilfully, and with
intent to defraud, falsifying a certain book of account belonging to E. D.

407. ALBERT JEFFERY (23), Feloniously uttering false and counterfeit
coin twice on the same day, knowing the same to be false and counterfeit.
MR. WILKINSON Prosecuted.
NELLIE MACKLEY, I am a barmaid at the Crown and Cushion, West-
minster Bridge Road; Mr. Norman is the landlord-I was serving in the
bar on April 10th-the prisoner came in about 9.40 p.m. and asked for a
"pony " bitter, the price of which was one penny-he tendered in
payment a half crown, which I examined and took to Mr. Norman, who
said it was bad-I told the prisoner so, and he produced another half crown,
which was also found to be bad,
JOSEPH HENRY NORMAN. I manage the Crown and Cushion-I was
in the bar on April 10th about 9.40 p.m., when Mackley showed me a coin
which appeared to be a half crown-I felt it and found it greasy, and
took it in my teeth and bent it-Mackley took it back to the prisoner,
and then brought me another one, which I also found to be bad-I tried
it in a similar way to the other-I do not think the prisoner could see
how I tried the coins I said to him, "What shall I do with you!"-
be made no reply-I sent for a policeman and gave him in charge-the
constable asked him how he obtained the coins-he said he got them
by selling flowers-he had no more money when searched.
JOSEPH FROST (363 L.) About 10.20 p.m. on April 10th I was called
to the Crown and Cushion, and found the prisoner detained there—Mr.
Norman said to me, “This man has tendered these," producing these
two half crowns I said to the prisoner, "How do you account for their
possession !"-he said, "I took them selling flowers last Saturday "-
I searched him, but found nothing on him whatever I took him into
custody-whilst on the way to the station he said, “This is all right;
someone has bunged them into me"-he made no reply when charged.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am an inspector of coins in His Majesty's
Mint-these half crowds are both counterfeit, and from the same mould.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : "I did not know the
money was bad, or else I should never have gone in there and attempted
to pase them; I would sooner have chucked them away than have got
myself into trouble. I call no witness here."
