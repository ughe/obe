   Cross-examined. Q.  I suppose Mr. Collins has not paid you any part of it? A.  I did not know Mr. Collins—I never saw him till the present transaction. WILLIAM HUXLEY re-examined , I got this check for 6 l . 15 s . from Mr. Mills, got it cashed at a public-house opposite our hotel, and gave the amount to Mr. Smith.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When Mr. Smith gave you the first check to take to Mr. Mills, did he not say it was post-dated? A.  If he did I did not hear him—he was talking something, but I was ordered off in a hurry to Barnet-fair, and did not notice what he said—I could not swear he did not tell me it was post-dated—I do not know what he said. MATTHEW KIGHT. I keep the British Coffee-house in Blackfriars-road. On Tuesday, 25th Sept., I received this desk from a young man, who I after wards saw speaking to Lawes at the police-court—I gave it up to Russell that same afternoon, as I received it. GEORGE RUSSELL( City-policeman , 34). On 25th Sept., I went to Kight and received from him this desk—it then contained the papers and documents that are here now. ARTHUR GEORGE HALL. I have been, since Jan. 1845, a clerk in the Commercial Bank of London. On 4th Sept., this check was paid to us by the London and Westminster Bank—it was first paid by us to the London and Westminster Bank, but we found out our error and had it back—on searching I found that Charles Fraser, the drawer, had no account at our bank—we do business for the Gravesend Bank—I believe this check has come out of this check-book ( produced from the desk )—I cannot point out from what place, but the numbers agree—the counterfoils all bear the same number, 5676—the number of this check is 567—the last figure 6 has been removed—I know Lawes; he once kept an account at our bank—this check-book was issued by us to him—I believe the writing on the counterfoils to be his—the body of this check I also believe to be his—I mean the words that are written "Sept 11, '49. J. F. Collins, Esq., 7 l . 10 s . on account of the Gravesend Bank," and the figures" 7 l . 10 s ." all the writing part, except Fraser's signature—this was our pass-book with Lawes—I believe the entries in this memorandum-book to be Lawes's writing—the last entry is "1849, Aug. 28. Cash per Smith, 2 l . 10 s ."—I cannot say when Lawes's account with us ceased—the last transaction I am aware of was on 13th April.
   Cross-examined Q.  Have all these books the same number of checks in them? A.  No; they begin at 25 and go up to 500—I have not counted the checks in this book to see whether any are missing—I feel no difficulty in speaking to the body of this check being in Lawes's writing, from the know ledge I have of it—I should say it is the same character of writing as that on the counterfoils—in my opinion all the body of this check, except the signature, is in one handwriting—there is a slight difference in the colour of the ink with which" Pay to F. Collins" is written. JOSEPH BEADLE . I am a clerk in the Commercial Bank of London.
