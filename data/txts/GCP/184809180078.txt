Cross-eramined by MR. MELLER. Q. Are there policemen about there!
A. Yes; I think there are a dozen or two on the station, which is about
thirty-seven acres--they do not patrol about-some are in the gates,
others at points-they cannot have a view of all the premises-I should
think there were any within forty yards of the place that was broken-
spot is not lighted up with gas-business is carried on all night in different
parts, but not near there,
THOMAS CHESTON Mills. I am goods manager at the Camden-ton
station. This office was appropriated to me-on 4th Aug. I left between
five and six o'clock in the afternoon, leaving 141. 28. 1d. in my table-de
--there was one 51. note, and I believe a second, but I am not certain ; ali
gold and silver-I went next morning about nine-the money was all gone
but one sovereign, which I found amongst the papers on the floor-Edward
was the Company's servant fifteen or eighteen months, from the commence
ment of 1847- he knew my office-I always kept a small sum of money
there for the payment of petty disbursements.

GEORGE LE PLA. I live at 8, Wellesley-street, City-road. I am out of
employ-on 4th Aug., I saw Edwards and Norman against the Stanhope
Arms, Camden-town, about a hundred yards from the goods-station, about
eleven in the morning--they were talking- I knew Edwards by the named
Hocker-1 beard him say to Norman, “ Tom, I know where we can get some
money to-night, if you have a mind to come"-Norman said, "Whereabou
is it?"- Edwards said, “In the Company's office, at Camden-town; at the
station"--Norman said, "Very well"-Edwards said, “Tom, you have got
some tools at home"-Norman said, "I have"-Edwards left bim, and said
he would see him about eight, down at the Oxford Arms; that is about two
hundred yards from the Stanhope Arms—they parted— I was at the Oxfond
Arms about eight that night, and saw them again-Edwards asked Normas
for the tools-Norman said, “Here they are," and gave him an old chisel and
a razor-Edwards said, "I will see you up at the Stanhope Arms bye-and-bye"
- Norman said, "Very well"--I remained till about half-past ten ; I then
went up to the Stanbope Arms-I saw Norman come out of the gates of the
Camden-station--he asked me if I had seen Hocker- I said, "No" -I then
went into the Hampstead-road, and saw Edwards coming over the bridge
Norman met him there I went with them down the Hampstead-road-
met three girls, and all went to a public-bouse on the left-hand side, and had
some beer-Edwards paid for one pot with a sovereign which he got changed
-Norman then called for a second pot, and paid for it with a sovereign whid
he got changed-we came out, and Norman said to Edwards, "Hocker, you
had better give me them notes, and I will put them away for you"-Edward
refused-1 had seen the notes-just as we came over the bridge Edwards
showed them to Norman and to me, they were two 51.-notes-Edwards said
to the girls, "Do you know where we can put these notes away ?"-one
them said she would put them away for him to-morrow morning-we then al
went to a house in the New-road, and remained there all night-next morning
we went to a coffee-shop down a turning on the left-hand side, in Tottenham
Court-road, and had breakfast-after remaining there about half an hour,
Edwards came out and said, “Come, let us go and get rid of these notes
