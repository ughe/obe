GEORGE FREDERICK LEONARD MULLINEAUX (City policeman, 293). I took
Phillips on 14th April, at a beer shop at the corner of Orchard-street, London
and Hurst were with him-I told him I was a policeman ; that whatever be
said to me might be used in evidence against him; and that if he wished to
say anything about the robbery, he could say what he thought proper-he
said, "Well, I will tell you the truth; I have had two desks, and London
has had two; I do not know how many Hurst had, and do not know what
he did with them "-the other prisoners could hear what he said, and they
told him to hold his tongue; and said that if it had not been for Eldridge,
they should have known nothing at all about it; that he put them up to it
- I searched Phillips, and found on him 30s, and fd.
Cross-examined by Me, Payne. Q. You only found a door key on
Hurst? A. That is all.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'Brien, Q. There was another policeman with
you? 4. Two more ; one of them (Moss) remained while I was searching
- he is not here, he heard what occurred-he was at Guildhall, but was not
examined-I made no memorandum of the conversation-I did not see Mr.
Wooster's servant girl till after the first examination before the Magistrate
she was there at the second examination, which was about a week afterwards
-I did not bring her to the police office, I believe Mr. Wooster brought her
--she did come to the station on the night we apprehended the prisoners --
two desks were not seen on the 14th by the servant girl, it was one of the
work girls, Clara Tyler.
