 214. SARAH BAILEY and ANN WATERMAN , stealing 28 yards of Orleans cloth, value 1 l  ., the goods of John Orrick Kennard . WILLIAM KENNARD. I am in the service of John Orrick Kennard, a linen-draper, of the Broadway, Deptford. On 15th Nov., about a quarter before five, Waterman came for a pennyworth of thread, and Bailey took a piece of Orleans cloth off the board—this is the cloth—here are twenty-eight yards of it—it is my master's. JOHN WHITE( policeman, R  180.) On 15th Nov. I was the Broad way—I saw the prisoner in company for a quarter of an hour—I knew them, and watched them—I saw them look into Mr. Kennard's window—they both left the window and went to the shop door—Waterman went in, and Bailey put her cloak over this cloth, which was on a stool, and walked off with it—I crossed the road and took her, and took the cloth from under her arm—she said, "Don't make a noise about it"—I sent her to the station, and took Waterman as she came out of the shop door—I told her she was charged with stealing this cloth with Bailey—she said she knew nothing about it, but she certainly knew Bailey.
   BAILEY  — GUILTY . Aged 27.— Confined Six Months .
   WATERMAN  — NOT GUILTY .
 215. ANN WATERMAN , again  indicted for stealing 36 yards of plaid, value 2 l  . 14 s  ., the goods of Isaac Watts and another. JABEZ WHITEHEAD. I am in the service of Isaac and William Watts, of Deptford. On 20th Oct. I saw thirty-six yards of plaid safe—I missed it in the evening—I believe this is it—it is my master's.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  You are not sure about it? A.  It corresponds in every respect with what we had—there is no mark on it—I know it by the pattern—I knew by the book, the quantity there was of it before it was lost—I had not measured it myself—there are six persons in our shop—it was in my department—I had handled it and placed it at the door in the morning—I bought it, and know the length of it from the manufac turer's mark, but the mark is not on this piece—it is much wider than plaids generally are.
