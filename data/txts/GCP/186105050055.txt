MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. Q. How were they sent? A. By post-I
have seen them made out-I never delivered them myself.
MR. GIFFARD. Q. As long as the prisoner has been in your employment,
has it been the course of business for persons to make out those forms, for the
purpose of being sent to the prisoner ? A. Yes; I had no reason whatever to
doubt that from February, 1846, down to the time that he ceased to be in our
employment, they were sent—the prisoner would have no means of knowing
what customers to go to, or what sums to ask for, except by these accounts
furnished to him-I did not furnish him with receipts personally, nor did I
superintend their posting this is one of the statements of account sent to
us; it is in the prisoner's writing.
MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. Q. Do you know that yourself? 4. Yes
I know his writing, by seeing it every day—I do not know that I have ever

having seen him write, to say that this is his writing-I will swear to it,
from having seen him write-I speak from having the correspondence; I
wrote to him and he wrote to me.
MR. GIFFARD. Q. For fifteen years you have communicated with him,

threatened to send for a policeman.
