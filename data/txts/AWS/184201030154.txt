FANNY FINcH. I am the wife of William Finch, who keeps the Cock
public-house, at Clapham. About a quarter to twelve o'clock, on the
night of the 29th of October, I left a cash-box containing 15 sovereigns,
and about 51. in silver, on a chair in the tap-room-w were about putting
the persons out of the house-] was the last person up, and shut the people
out- did not let any one in-I went up stairs without returning to the
tap-room- missed the cash-box at seven the next morning-th prisoner
Cooke slept in the house that night-he was not a regular servant-
gave him no wages-he did odd jobs about the house-he was discharged
next day, but not on account of this-he came back to the house after.
wards, but was not in anv employ-I have known Hardman some time, and
always understood him to be honest till this time.
JAMES KNowLes. I am a labour and live at Clapham. I was em-
ployed in the cow-house at the prosecutor's premises on the 18th of No-
vember-] found this cash-box in the cow-house-it had been covered
with dirt but a dog which was with me pawed the dirt off it-it was
empty, and half open-l delivered it up to Mr Finch in the same state in
which I found it.
ROBERT EMERSON (police-constable 9.) I was on duty at the police
station at Clapham, on the 20th of November, when Cooke was there-
man named Laing came in-there is a wooden partition in the station
-I was on one side the partition, and Cooke and Laing on the other
-I could hear what passed between them-I heard Laing ask him if he
did not know who committed the robbery-he said at first he did not-
Laing said, 64 Did not you take the money and the cash-box, and you and
Hardman divide it between you?"-Cooke replied that he did, that he went
into the tap-room about seven o'clock in the morning, and saw the cash-
box on the chair, that he took it away up the yard, broke it open, and
took the money out of it-he said he kept 6l. himself, and gave Hardman
5l., 4l. he hid in a place where they cleaned the knives, that he fetched
the 4l. away another day, and he gave his sister a part of it--he said be had
the cash-box in the cowshed, under the straw-after I heard that I sent
for Mr. Finch, who came and charged him with stealing it-I believe
Laing is a labourer, a cousin to Cooke-he lives at Clapham-there had
been some inquiry about some new clothes which the prisoners had.
WILLIAM EDMEAD (police-constable V 179.) I took Cooke into cus-
tody- received this cash-box at the station-Laing is not here-I did
not know he was wanted.
CHARLES EDMONDS. I was pot-boy to Mr. Finch-I know Hardman-he
visited Cooke there sometimes two or three times a day. On the morning
the 30th of October, I heard that the cash-box was missed-] saw Hardman
about the premises between seven and eight o'clock-Cook was in the bouse
several times all day long-] saw Hardman in the morning part of the day
in the yard, and in the afternoon in the house-he was with Cooke about four
o'clock in the afternoon-he was by himself when I first saw him-I did
not see whether they were talking to each other-one was coming out of
the yard, and the other coming the other way, but I cannot say whether
they were speaking, I was such a distance from them.
