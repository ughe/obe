 1485.  THOMAS FURLONGER was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , a watch, value 20 s., and a chain, value 1 s., the goods of Margaret Furlonger, widow.
 MARGARET FURLONGER. I am a widow. The prisoner is my nephew, and visited me. On the 14th of July, a little girl came to me with a note, and I gave her this watch to deliver to my daughter; I found it in possession of a pawnbroker; the chain was not on it then. I live in Great New-street. I believe the letter to be the prisoner's writing.
 Cross-examined. Q. When you read it you thought it your daughter's writing? A. Yes; I have received letters from the prisoner; I did not suspect it when I read it, but it is spelt the same as letters he has written to me. I apprehended him next day, and he confessed writing it.
   ANN FURLONGER. I am the prosecutrix's daughter. This letter was never written by my authority. I never received the watch or chain, and did not expect it.
   ELIZABETH CAPPER(aged nine years, appeared perfectly to understand the obligation of on oath). I know the prisoner; my father used to bring him to our house - he gave me this letter, and told me to say it had come from Mrs. Furlonger, at the White Swan; and told me to take it to Mrs. Furlonger, of Great New-street - which I did; she gave me the watch - he stopped at a public-house, near the house, and I gave him the watch - he gave me nothing - next day I was asked about it, and told how I got the note.
 (Letter read.)
 "DEAR MOTHER, - I should be obliged if you will send Furlonger's watch; he has sent to me to get it repaired against he comes home; there is something the matter with yours - it don't go, but he will have it repaired before he brings it to you. He is very well, and sends his love - hopes this will find you well, as it leaves us at present. Please to send the watch by bearer.
 ANN FURLONGER.
 MRS. FURLONGER. The watch was my son's - he had mine, and I had his - he was gone to France, and we had changed watches. I thought by this letter that he wanted it back.
   JAMES CLIFFORD. I apprehended the prisoner on the 14th or 15th of July - a duplicate of the watch was left at my house.
   JAMES WALKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Aldersgate-street. I have a watch pawned on the 14th of July, in the name of Mary Furlonger, by Mary Copping, who is not here - she is ill.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Six Months and Whipped.

 Middlesex Cases, Fourth Jury, Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1486.  MICHAEL CLANCY was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of July , a handkerchief, value 5 s., the goods of John Tilley , from his person.
 JOHN TILLEY. On the 11th of July, between four and five o'clock, I was in  Arlington-street- I felt a pulling behind me, turned round, and saw the prisoner, and two others, near me; but the prisoner was nearest. I took hold of him, and found my handkerchief in his hand - he was in the act of giving it to another boy.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw two young men following this gentleman - his handkerchief was half out of his pocket; I saw them in the act of picking his pocket; I called out to the gentleman, and they threw the handkerchief down - the gentleman turned round and took me.
 JOHN TILLEY re-examined. Q. Did he say any thing of this at the time? A. No, but when I took him to the watch-house, he begged forgiveness, it being his first offence.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Transported for Fourteen Years.

 1488.  WILLIAM CRICK was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , a whip, value 3 s. 6 d., the goods of James Tweedy.
 JAMES TWEEDY. I am  coachman to Mr. Brodie. The prisoner was helper in the stable, in New Burlington-mews- he was discharged just before the 27th of June; he called about that time - I went with the carriage, and left him there, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening - there was another helper in the stable; when I came back the prisoner was just gone, and I missed a whip and a hand piece. I found the whip about a week ago.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN BATHS. I live with Mr. Keats, in Albemarle-street. The prisoner lived with me as groom about four months - he sold me this whip; he said he bought it in Mary-le-bone, for 7 s.; I said it was not worth 2 s. 6 d.; I gave him 3 s. 6 d. for it.
   GEORGE AVIS. I took the prisoner into custody; he said he had sold the whip, and hand pieces to Bates.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Whipped and Discharged.
