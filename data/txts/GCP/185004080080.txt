WILLIAM HOLLAND. On 26th Feb., I was in the service of Mr. Dale
Between eight and nine o'clock that evening I came out of the tap-room, and
saw the prisoner at the bar with a young woman-she asked me to go and
get change for a 51.-note-my master gave me the note- I took it to Mr.
Beaumont's, and asked Weller, the barman, to change it-he put the note an
the desk, and wrote master's name on it-I did not see what he wrote, but I
saw him writing-he put it into the desk-a young woman who came out of
the parlour gave me the change, four sovereigns, and the rest in silver-1
brought it back, and placed it on the counter before the prisoner and the
young woman I did not see what became of it.
Cross-eramined. Q. When you went to the House of Detention, did you
pass by the prisoner without recognising him? A. Yes, at first; my master
said, "Look well at him "-the first time I went by him very quick-I looked
at him again, and knew him I looked at him two or three minutes the second
time-I looked at his cell the same as I did at the others, but my attention
was not attracted till my master said that to me.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did your master say that to you at any other cell
you passed by ? A. Yes; he said a good many times, "Look well at them."
John WELLER. I am barman to Mr. Beaumont, of the Red Lion, Guild-
ford-street. On the evening of 26th Feb., Holland came for change for a
51.-note, which he banded to me-my master's sister gave him four sove-
reigns, and 11.-worth of silver-I wrote on the note, "Mr. Dale, Marchmont-
street"-this is it-master was about to pay it away on 6th March, and the
clerk detected it to be a forgery.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you changed any other 51.-note that day!
A. I do not know; we change them very frequently-we always put on them
the name of the person we take them from-I changed no other for Mr.
Dale that night. Guilty. Aged 18.-Transported for Fifteen Years.

768. RICHARD JOSEPH GEAREY, feloniousy cutting and wounding
Samuel Edward Parkes on his left eye and the left side of his head, with
intent to maim and disable him.
MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.

standing in the yard, with a ladder in his hand-Parkes, who was employed
as a carpenter, said to him, “If you don't put that ladder away, I will come
and take it from you"-the prisoner had no business with it-he replied,
"I'm d d if I do," and threw it at Parkes with all his force, with his two

him at the time-Parkes then went into his workshop, and came out again,
with the lath of a bedstead in his hand-the prisoner then attempted to run
away-he and the other boys dodged Parkes about the yard, hooting and
