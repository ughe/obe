1665. MARY CAVANAGH was indicted for stealing 1 spoon, value 28.;
2 sheets, 68. ; 2 napkins, 18. ; 2 pillow-cases, 18. ; 1 table-cloth, 6s. ; 1 pair
of trowsers, 6s.; and 6 towels, 48.; the goods of James Martin, her master.
ELIZABETH MARTIN. I am the wife of James Martin, of King-street, St.
James's square. The prisoner was in my service-I missed these articles,
and gave her in charge-this table spoon, sheets, napkins, and pillow-case
are all my husband's, and were in the house at the time the prisoner was
there-these trowsers belonged to a gentleman who lived in the house-I
received a good character with ber.
ROBERT WHITTON. I am in the service of Mr. Luxman, a pawnbroker, of
St. Martin's-lane. This shirt, two pillow-cases, and two napkins were
pledged at our shop by the prisoner-I am sure she is the person.
THOMAS MANBY DICKER. I am in the employ of Mr. Martin, a pawn-
broker, in Piccadilly-I do not know who pledged this shirt, but I gave the
counterpart of this duplicate to the person.
ARTHUR JOHN NORTH. I am in the employ of Mr. Norton, a pawn-
broker, of Green-street, Leicester-square. I have a tea-spoon pawned by the
prisoner, in the name of White.
NICHOLAS BUTLER (police-constable C 175.) I took the prisoner into
custody-she gave me three duplicates-one of them is the counterpart of
one which has been produced.
Prisoner. Q. Did not my mistress tell you I was in distress when I took
them? A. She said she thought you were, and she would forgive you
you would produce all the property, but you did not give it all up.
(The prisoner pleaded distress.)
Guilty. Aged 29.-Confined Six Months.
