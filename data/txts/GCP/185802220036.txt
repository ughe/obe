Cross-examined by Mr. SLEIGH. Q. The men were all coming out of the
building in a body, when you stopped them? A. Yes—I cannot say how
many came out; perhaps a hundred or a hundred and twenty-I should say
there were more than a hundred altogether-I took Field and came back-
I searched Ginger and Shore in the building the doorway is but a short
distance from the gate- I searched the men nearer to the gates than the
doorway.
Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. Q. The piece of lead you found on
Ginger was very small? A. Yes—it did not weigh half a pound.
JOSEPH TAYLOR. I carry on the business of a plumber in Clements Lane.
I am engaged in doing the plumber work at Clothworker's Hall—the four
prisoners were in my employ as labourers—they all receive the same wages
-in consequence of information, I went with two police officers, on Monday,
15th Feb., to the building-I went about 3 o'clock, and put myself in an
office which overlooked the building--the men were at their work-about 4
o'clock, I saw Jones take his position on a part of a scaffolding at the back
part of the building, and, a few minutes afterwards, Shore came forward with
a quantity of lead, and handed it to Jones—about a minute afterwards,
Field came forwards and he got underneath where Jones was sitting-he
brought some lead, and put it on the scaffold where Jones was sitting
Ginger then came and brought some lead, and threw it on to the scaffold -
Jones had a brick in his hand, and he was knocking up the lead that was
brought to him the men who brought the lead could see what he was doing;
they were standing within a yard of him-he handed several pieces, nine
or ten, underneath to Field-I could see Field distinctly, and could see what
he did with the lead; he lapped it round his body-the lead that Jones did
not give to Field, he put some into his pocket, and fastened a portion under-
neath his stomach I have seen the lead that was found I have not any
doubt that it is my lead the prisoners had not any right to take it.
