 2029.  WILLIAM EMMERSON was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of July , 1 coat, value 2l.; 1 waistcoat, value 5s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 7s.; 1 pair of gloves, value 1s., and 5 sovereigns, the property of Alexander Hirons ; and 1 coat, value 5s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 3s.; 1 pair of boots, value 2s.; 1 sheet, value 4s.; 3 handkerchiefs, value 3s.; 1 waistcoat, value 1s., and 1 pair of gloves, value 6d., the goods of David Hirons , in the dwelling-house of Alexander Hirons.
   ALEXANDER HIRONS. I live in Foundry-place, Shoreditch- the prisoner is a tailor, and lodged on my first floor since Easter. On the 23rd of July some property was missing, and he absconded without notice - I had seen him in the house after two o'clock that afternoon.
   ANN HIRONS. I am the wife of Alexander Hirons. On the 23rd of July I missed five sovereigns from a box up stairs; it was locked, and I found it still locked, but the key was in the room; I had not seen the money for ten days - my husband's clothes were all right in the morning - I went out at two o'clock, returned at four, and the things were gone - the prisoner, whom I had left in the house, was also gone.
   WILLIAM ATTFIELD. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner in Langston-place, Hampstead-road, on the 23rd, about nine or ten o'clock, and charged him with this robbery - he strongly denied it at first, but I found a pair of gloves, which the prosecutor claimed, and 1l. in silver; and in a box, which he said was his, I found all the property belonging to the prosecutor's son; he then said he was guilty, and that he had sold a coat at Benjamin's shop - we went, and took Benjamin that night, and found the coat and other things hanging in his shop.
   JOHN BENJAMIN. I bought the coat and things of the prisoner, between five and six o'clock that afternoon, for 28s.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. She said, at the office, it was a 5l. note she lost - I can prove I had no money for a week before.
 MRS. HIRONS. I never said any thing about a 5l. note; this handkerchief was in the same box as the sovereigns. GUILTY of stealing to the value of 99s. only. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 2030.  WILLIAM PUTNAM was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , 1 ewe sheep, price 18s., the property of Thomas Goddard.
   THOMAS GODDARD. I am a farmer, and live at Ealing. On the 27th of August I had ninety-three ewes in a field, at Harrow, and next day, about three o'clock in the afternoon, I found the prisoner dragging one of them along the field by its legs; he drew it to the hedge, threw it into the ditch, then turned, saw me, and ran away; the field was enclosed - he had no right there; he ran very fast, but I ran round another way, and secured him.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. What condition was the sheep in? A. It was healthy; they were there to graze - there is a footpath through the field; it was a fine day - he was not near the footpath; I do not think it was shady under the hedge - when I took him I asked what he was going to do with the sheep; he said, "What do you think?" I said, "I think you were going to kill it;" he said, "No, I had nothing to kill it with, if I had wanted;" the ditch was dry, and about three feet deep.
   JOHN PASCALL. I am an officer. I received him in charge, and found a knife in his pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. The sheep was laying down, and not well; I took it up, and put it under the hedge; seeing Goddard (having heard his character) I ran away.
   WILLIAM COOMBS. I am a farmer, and have known the prisoner all his life; he bore a good character. I was passing through this field a few days before, and a sheep laid exactly in the footpath - I have no doubt it would have died if I had not taken it up; all the sheep there were ill.
 - MILLER. I am a farmer. I have been obliged to move the prosecutor's sheep; they were so rotten that they were ready to drop as I moved them, and several have died since.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Baron Gurney.

 1032.  WILLIAM DIGNUM was indicted for feloniously assaulting Henry Andrews , and with a certain sharp instrument, cutting and wounding him in and upon his neck and throat, with intent to kill and murder him.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating his intent to be to disable him - or do him some grievous bodily harm.
   HENRY ANDREWS. I am a clerk to the London United Copper Mine Company. On the 24th of August, at half-past eight o'clock, I was going over Haggerstone-fields, alone, and was stopped by two men; one inquired the way to Shoreditch, while the other knocked me down and robbed me - one knelt on me, while the other robbed me; I was going to cry out, when they swore they would throw me into the canal, if I made any noise - I was going to call out Police! when the man who knelt on me, drew a knife from his pocket, cut my throat, and then they ran away; they had taken 2l. 10s. from my pocket, and my watch - it was not very dark; the sun had set, I should think, two or three hours, but I did not take particular notice; it was not very dark - I saw the face of the man who used the knife to me - it was the prisoner; I had a good opportunity of seeing his face - I had never seen him before; I got up, and went to the man at the lock, and he went home with me; I had three cuts on one side of my throat, and one on the other; they bled, and I was attended by a surgeon - I saw the prisoner at the Crown, Church-street, Bethnal-green, a fortnight after; I was taken there by two Policeman; he was in company with others, and was not pointed out to me; I pointed him out; I should not like to swear positively that he is one of the persons, in consequence of an alibi set up by three witnesses - I am fully convinced he is the person, but do not swear positively to him.
