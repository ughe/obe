MARY SHARP. I am a widow, of 94, East Street, Marylebone-on 31st
August, just after midnight, I was sitting down in Thayer Street tying
up 38. in a handkerchief; the prisoners came up to me, and one of them
snatched my handkerchief-there was a lamp near-one held me
down while the other searched my pockets; they hurt my chest and
arms very much I called for the police; the prisoners were stopped, and
I went to the station, and charged them-I had seen Coleman before;
he made a snatch at me on the Saturday night previous, and I knew
him again.
Cross-examined by Coleman. I can't say whether there were two or
three young men-you ran away when you saw the policeman coming,
or I should have been very much hurt; I was not able to go to work all
that week.
Cro88-examined by Collins. I was not the worse for drink-I lost a
purse, but I did not value that at anything. b u hang
CHARLES NIGHTINGALE (Policeman D 261). I was in Marylebone Lane,
and saw Coleman kneeling on the prosecutrix on the pavement - Collins,
who was standing about 10 yards off, shouted out “Come on, Jack," and
Coleman got off her; went up to Collins, and they both ran away she
shouted "You villains, you have stolen my money”-I gave chase
through Thayer Street, Marylebone Street, William Street, Hinde Street,
into Mandeville Place, where I caught Coleman; he said "I did not
touch the old woman " - I had not spoken to him then, being out of breath
from running-I took him back to the prosecutrix in Thayer Street, and
another constable brought Collins-she charged them with stealing 38.;
they said that they did not know one another they were taken to the
station, and I found on Collins ls. in his pocket, 18. in his boot, 18. in
the lining of his trousers, and a knife; and on Coleman a Hanoverian
medal, a loz. weight, & duplicate, a knife, and a small looking-glass-
there was a lamp at the corner outside the Angel public-house.
Cross-examined by Coleman. I am sure I saw you kneeling on her-you
are the only one I chased-I had to take three or four turnings.
MARY SHARP (Ke-examined). It was three shillings I lost-not smaller
money.
THOMAS MOCARTHY (Policeman D 148). I heard Mrs. Sharp calling
"Stop thief," and saw the prisoners running-I followed Collins, and
took him in Wigmore Street, 300 yards off-he was taken to the station,
and I saw 38. found on him-he said "I know nothing of Jack
Coleman."
Cross-examined by Coleman. I did not say “Come on, Jack, we have
got you at last.”
The Prisoners' Statements before the Magistrato. Coleman says: "Late in
the evening I met a couple of friends, and drank with them; I was
drunk. I was going home. When a constable came up in Mandeville
Place and took me I was surprised.” Collins says : "I was not in Cole-
man's company at all.” They repeated the same statements in their defence.
CHARLES NIGHTINGALE (Re-examined). Coleman was sober.
GUILTY. OOLEMAN was further chargod with a conviation at Clerken-
woll in February, 1881.
