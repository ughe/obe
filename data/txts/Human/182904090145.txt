The van was very nearly loaded when he was taken; whether any thing was put into it after he was taken I cannot say - it was never wholly filled; Pettet said he had proper authority, and if I interrupted him, he should give me into custody - I saw nothing put into the van after he was taken.
   JOSEPH BENNET. I am a mail-coachman, and lodge in the first floor of this house. On Friday, the 3d of April, I went out about ten o'clock in the morning; I padlocked my door - I returned about six in the evening, and found my room door broken open, and some of my things in the passage; there was a large portmanteau trunk, and part of my bedstead - I had left my keys in my trunks, and they had locked the trunks and took the keys with them; my bedstead was all in pieces, and had not been unscrewed, but strained out from one to the other, and pulled to pieces - my keys were returned to me next morning; on going into my room I found all my things thrown about; I had a table in the room with a drawer in it, and I had put ten sovereigns and twenty shillings in that drawer that morning - I did not lock the drawer; the money was there when I left the room, and when I returned it was gone - I missed several articles out of the room; two glasses and other things; the bedding was all tied up.
 Cross-examined. Q. You have no means of ascertaining who took the money? A. No.
   THOMAS BUTLER. I live at No. 92, Mary-street, Hampstead-road; I am employed by Mr. Ray to collect his rents, and have been employed so about ten years - among others I collected from Miss Howorth; I abstained, by direction of Mr. Ray, from pressing her for rent - she owed between four and five years' rent; on Friday, the 3d of April, I went to her house, and saw a great number of persons at the door, and also a van partly laden with household furniture, as it appeared to me - I went into the house, and saw Pettet and Hillick nearly at the bottom of the stairs; Hillick was carrying a table, and Pettet behind him - I insisted on their not taking it out of the house -I stopped it, as the passage was narrow, and desired them not to remove any thing out of the house; Hillick stopped directly, and Pettet said, if any body interrupted him in what he was doing, he would send for a constable and give them in charge - I asked him to show me his authority for making the distress; he told me he should not show it me at all - I insisted on seeing it, and then we went into a back room; he produced a paper, which he held with both his hands, leaving only a part of it to be seen - I read part of it, and asked him who put in the distress, and he said William Chapman, whose name was on the paper; I asked him his name, and he said it was John Smith, and I saw that on the paper also - this is the paper (looking at it); in consequence of this, I sent for Mr. Minshull, clerk to Messrs. Freshfield, who are Mr. Ray's solicitors - I saw White and Martin at the house; White was just at the entrance of the garden door - I did not see him do any thing; he said he was employed by Smith - mentioning the name of Smith; a constable was sent for.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. He held the authority in both hands, did you get it into your hands? A. Never. I have collected the rent about ten years; I believe a claim has been set up to the premises by a person of the name of Young; nobody else that I know of- there is, I believe, an action in the Court of King's Bench which is not yet decided.
 MR. BOLLAND. Q. The action is by Mr. Young? A. Yes. I never heard the name of Chapman or Pettet; I have received the rent for all these houses ever since I have collected for Mr. Ray.
 MR. LEE. Q. Would you necessarily be in possession of the circumstance if any claim was set up? A. It might have been without my knowledge. White and Hillick came to the office twice voluntarily.
   HENRY KEIBLE. I am in the employ of William Gardner. On the 3d of April I went to the street-door, as Mr. Gardner sent me; I heard a knock at the door - I opened it, and Pettet. White, and a man in top-boots came in; they came to Mr. Gardner's room door; I saw the men afterwards putting the things into the van - Pettet was taking them out of the house.
   JOHN MINSHULL. I am clerk to Messrs. Freshfield and Son, who are solicitors to Mr. Ray. On the 3d of April, being sent for, I went down to this house, and saw a number of people at the door; a van was there, partly laden with goods - I went in, and saw Pettet; I did not see Martin - I told Pettet I came from Messrs, Freshfield and Son, solicitors to Mr. Ray and Mr. Oliver, and requested to know by what authority he distrained; he said, "By authority of Mr. Chapman;" I asked him to produce his authority - he refused, and was taken away in custody; I saw White near Gardner's parlour door - I asked him what he was doing there; he said he was the man in possession - I desired him to go out; he said he would, and he did; I directed the van to be unloaded - I saw Pettet afterwards at a public-house by the Police-office, and asked him where Chapman lived; he said he did not know - I said, "Has he any house in London?" he said he could not tell, he did not know that he had - I asked if he knew where to find him; he said he did not - he believed he had gone to Manchester; I then asked if he could find him at Manchester; he said he could not - I then asked him where the warrant was signed; he said at a public-house in Goswell-street; I then asked him his name and address - he said, "John Smith, North side, Bethnal-green;" I asked him if he was a broker - he said he was; I asked if he kept a shop or a close house - he said it was a house, he kept no shop; I afterwards attended before the Magistrate - (looking at some papers) - these are what the prisoner put in before the Magistrate.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Hillick was bailed before the Magistrate? A. Yes, and has surrendered to-day; I had no opportunity of seeing who loaded and unloaded the van.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Are you sure Pettet did not say he acted as agent for Smith, who lived in Bethnal-green? A. No, he did not; I am quite positive; he said he met him at a public-house in Goswell-street - not at the Horns, in Smithfield.
