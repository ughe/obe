JAMES CUDDY (police-constable L 20.) I took the prisoner into cus
tody, between three and four o'clock, on the 21st of January, in a privy-
I called Hodgson, and asked if he was the person who committed the assault
- he said, "Yes"-the prisoner said nothing-as we were going to the
station-house, I said he was a foolish man to get himself into that trouble
- he said it was a foolish thing, but in the irritation of the moment, seeing
his children crying round him, and he having not a mouthful of victuals to
eat, it so irritated him, that he did not think for the moment that the knife
touched the man's arm, and it must have been in the scuffle.
JOHN STADDON. I am a surgeon. I was requested by Mr. Jeremy the
Magistrate to examine Hodgson's arm, between four and five o'clock on the
afternoon of the 21st of January-I discovered a wound near the elbow
joint of the left arm, produced by some sharp instrument-it appeared to
have been a stab or thrust-I did not examine the depth of it then, but
after it became necessary to lay the wound open, it was discovered to be
three inches deep, or more-it was above the elbow, and had passed be-
tween the integuments up towards the shoulder-I think it was such a
wound as unight have been made with a shoemaker's knife--it is getting
well now.

ELLEN SULLIVAN. I am the wife of a carver and gilder. I was
in the prisoner's room when Hodgson came in the prisoner was at work
at his bench at the time-directly Hodgson came in he seized the cradle
the prisoner jumped up at the moinent, with the knife he was at work
with in one hand, and a boot in the other, and seized hold of the cradle
did not observe whether he dropped the boot-I saw them struggle for the
cradle-I think, from what I saw, that the blow was quite accidental--the
prisoner has four children-there were three there they were crying-I
have known the prisoner eight or nine years-he has always borne the
character of an honest, peaceable, unoffending man-he has been a long
time out of work.
