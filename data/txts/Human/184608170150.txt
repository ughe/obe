   Prisoner.  I have a witness to prove it is not my coat. Witness.  The witness you intend to call is waiting his trial at Clerkenwell for pot-stealing. JAMES ANDREWS( policeman. ) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction—( read—Convicted March , 1845, and confined nx months )—he is the person tried and convicted—I have given evidence against him four times besides, and he has been twice summarily convicted.  1644. ROBERT GRIFFIN was indicted for uttering a forged warrant for payment of 10 s  ., well knowing it to be forged, with intent to defraud Elias Moses and another:—2nd COUNT  , stating it to be a certain forged order for 10 s. LEWIS ADOLPHUS. I am in the service of Mr. Moses, of the Minories. The prisoner was one of our tailors—when the workmen bring in the whole of their work they receive a ticket, which is taken to the clerk, and he pays them—this ticket is an order for money, and purports to be written by Mr. Pengelly—it is forged.
   Prisoner. Q.  Have you any of the tickets which I have received on former occasions of Mr. Pengelly's writing? A.  No. JAMES PANCOMBE PENGELLY. This ticket is like my handwriting, but it is not mine—it is a forged order.
   Prisoner. Q.  Have you not given persons tickets twice in the week? A.  Never; I have never been mistaken and given a person a ticket twice. CHARLOTTE ORANGE. I work for Mr. Fredericks, who is an assistant of the prisoner. On Saturday night the prisoner sent me with this ticket—I went, and Mr. Forster gave me the 10 s . JOHN FORSTER. I am pay-clerk to Mr. Moses. I paid the 10 s . to the girl, believing this order to be written by Pengelly.
   Prisoner. Q.  Had you any suspicion of it not being Mr. Pengelly writing? A.  Not the slightest, but I have now—it is not such a free hand.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I have received nothing but what was owing to me; I received the ticket from Mr. Pengelly on Saturday night, and kept it to provide for myself, as we are so often kept without our money. JOHN FORSTER re-examined.  I did not owe him any money—by receifinf the 10. he has overdrawn his account—the ticket merely says, "pay"—"already entered in the book How much is to be paid—the prisoner was called up—I looked at the book, and found I had paid him—he said I had not and, to make my statement good I searched and found the ticket, which exonerated me—if I had not found it I should have had to pay him again—I had go "paid" in the book. GUILTY. Aged 26.— Confined Six Months
