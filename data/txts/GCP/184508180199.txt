CHARLES ADAMS. I am a cabinet-maker. I saw the policemen endea-
vour to persuade Hare to go to the station, telling him the inspector
would not take the charge.
Court. Q. Was there any violence on the part of the police before
they were struck by Hare and Payne ? A. No.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Did not Payne say, “ If you will
let him go he will go home quietly with me?" A. Yes, a great number
of times- I found Hare in custody - there was a bit of a struggle after
Payne laid hold of Hare's braces-Hare lay on his back, and folded his
arms, and said they should carry him if they wanted him, or words to that
effect one of the officers went for a stretcher-Hare was on the ground
several times, and Wigzell had his knee on his chest one time--they threw
Hare down, and one of the policemen struck him, and some of them called
out, " You bad better kill the man at once"- they called them butchers
-Hare had nothing in his hand- I cannot tell whether he was drunk--I
never saw him before-when he was down, and Wigzell had got his knee
upon him, a woman came up.
CHARLES BARDONS (police-sergeant P 25.) I took Hare-I told him
what for-he said he was not there--I said I should certainly take him,
and he certainly was there-he said, “Well, to tell you the truth, I was
there, but I was drunk."
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did you take him on the 24th of
June? 4. Yes, at half-past three o'clock in the morning, in bed-he was
very violent.
John Wills (police-constable P 187.) I took Mannix-he asked what
charge I had against him-I told him there was a warrant for his appre-
hension for an assault on Wigzell, and a riot-he said, “You d -d
rascal, you have picked me out at last, have you?"-I was taking him
to the station - he said he had been out of the way for some time, and he
would have been out of the way then if he had known-just before I got
bim to the station, he said I had got the wrong man, that his name was
not Cornelius, but Michael-I had got the warrant, and I called him Cor-
nelius.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHARNOCK. Q. He was drunk while you had
this conversation ? A. Yes, his brother was not taken then he has been
since I took the prisoner on the 20th of July-he said, the next day, "I
was drunk, and I dare say I did say what you represent, but I said it out
of bravado."
JOHN JAMES STEADMAN (police-constable P 151.) I took Sbae-he
was drunk -- he denied being there at the time of the disturbance, and said
he was miles away.
John Swan Flower. I am surgeon to the P division of police-I.
was called on the 23rd of June to attend Wigzell-he was labouring under
excessive pain in his chest and abdomen-he spat blood-be was in great
danger, and he is still- he will be quite incapable of performing his duty
-his bladder is injured-he is forced to get out of bed every five or ten
minutes-it will be a long time, if he ever does recover-he is in such
pain he is glad to get into any position for relief.
