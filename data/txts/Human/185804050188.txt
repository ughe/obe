 490. DANIEL HAYES  (28) was indicted for a like offence.
   MR. THOMPSON conducted the Prosecution. SUSAN DOUTHWAL. On Thursday, 11th March, the prisoner came into my mother's shop, between 3 aod 4 o'clock in the evening, and had some bread and butter, tea and eggs; he gave me a good shilling, and I gave him the change—he came again in about half an hour, and had some cofee and bread and butter, he gave me a florin; I gave him 1 s . 6 1/2 d ., and put the florin in my pocket, where I had no silver—the prisoner went out, and after he had gone I went to give change, and discovered that it was bad—he came in again between 3 and 4 o'clock in the morning, for a cup of coffee, and gave me a shilling, it was bad, and I gave it back to him (I had not then discovered that the florin was bad)—he said, "I will give you in charge, for you gave it to me"—he walked across the road wilh me, and said that he would rather be the looser of the shilling than be given in charge—I gave him in charge with the florin, and he gave the inspector the shilling; I marked them both, these are them.
   Prisoner. Q.  Where did you put the shilling? A.  In my pocket, and When I went into the room I gave it to my aunt—you came in with some female. JAMES M'CARTHY( Policeman, M  134). On 11th March, the last witness gave the prisoner into my custody—he had a shilling in his hand, he attempted to run away, but I caught him; he resisted, very much—I asked him for the shilling at the station, he would not give it to me, but afterwards gave it up to the inspector in my presence—I receieed this florin from the prosecutrifx; she said, "He has got the counterfeit shilling in his hand."
   Prisoner. Q.  Was I going toward the station, or from it? A.  I cannot say; I met you half way down Suffolk Street—you did not say that you were going to the station to get satisfaction for a bad shilling. WILLIAM WEBSTER. This florin and shilling are both, counterfeit.
   Prisoner's Defence.  Does it stand to, reason that I would take a had shilling to the station to get myself into trouble?
   The Prisoner called MARY ANN SWEENEY. I went with the prisoner twice to the coffee house in Suffolk Street; first at half past 12 o'clock at night, and again between 3 and 4 o'clock—he asked me what I would have; I said, "A cup of tea and a slice of bread and butter," and he called for a cup of coffee—he asked her whether she had a room; she said, "Yes"—he asked the price, and she said 1 s . 6 d .—heoffered 1 s ., and she said, "As we are not busy, you shall have it for 1 s .;" she tried the shilling twice before putting it into her pocket, and then went into the kitchen to get a candle—I said that I would not go up stairs till I had had a cup of tea, and he said then be would not stop at all—he asked for his money back; she turned to a person in the kitchen, and said, "Shall I give it to him back?"—she said, "Do not have any dispute about it,"when she gave it to him, he said, "This is a bad shilling"—she said, "It is the one you gave me"—he said, "No; I gave you a new, Victoria shilling"—shesaid that she had no other—only one young chap was present, sitting at the bar—I had no refreshment at all—two men called for something when we went in first, and gave her a 2 s . piece. SUSAN DOUTHWAL re-examined.  There is not one word of that true. GUILTY.*— Confined Two Years .
