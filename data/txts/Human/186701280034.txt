   Cross-examined. Q.  What is his business? A.  A dealer in jewellery—he has told me he attends Debenham's pawnbrokers' sales, and I have seen catalogues come from there—I know that his business is to purchase things and sell them as well as he can—I pledged some of the things in my mis tress's name—he did not tell me to keep it secret.
   MR. BESLEY . Q.  Do you know anything about his occupation except what he has told you himself? A.  No—I did not think it mattered which name I gave to the pawnbrokers.

   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know the prisoner? A.    MR. BESLEY . Q.  You say you know the prisoner has been in the habit of pawning for some considerable time? A.  I know he does pawn—we do not like doing business with him—I have known him five or six years.
   COURT . Q.  How lately have you done any business with him? A.  As far as I know, I never took anything of him—he has brought us goods to show us, which I have not taken—if he pledges he always wants a profit, so I think it best not to have any dealings—I think the person who brought the moire wanted more than I was willing to advance—I fancy she asked 3 l . 10 s .—I offered 3 l . and lent three guineas—It did not look so well by candlelight as it does by daylight. JOHN FOY.
