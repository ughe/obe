52. WILLIAM EDWIN LEWIS was indicted for bigamy.
M R. METCALFE conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAS PALMER. I am a sawyer, and live at Millwall, Poplar. I was
present at the Registrar's office, at Bridgewater, in Sept., 1851, when tbe pri-
soner was married to Elizabeth Cook—they lived together for perhaps four
months after—the wife is in Court now.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you know the wife at all ?
A. Yes; I haye known her about fifteen years—I believe she is about
twenty-three years old—I saw ber at her house after her marriage—I have
not been out with her—I do not know a person named Edward or Edwin
Gooding; I know some persons named Gooding, but do not know their
Christian names—the wife conducted herself properly after her marriage,
1 have heard so—I do not know whether she js prosecuting this case—her
father is here.
SARAH HALLE. I live at 3, Steven's-street, Tottenbam-court-road. I
was married to the prisoner at St. James's, Westminster, on 5th Sept., 1852,
and afterwards lived with him as his wife—he represented himself to be
a single man.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you prosecuting this case? No—I said
when I first heard of it that I would forgive him, if it was true, and give him
up to his wife, and they showed me the certificate, and I saw be was a
married man—I do not wish to press the matter; I freely forgive him the
injury he has done me.
MR. METCALFE. Q. Did not your brother give him into custody ?
A. Yes.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Was it by your authority? No.
