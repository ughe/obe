866. TERALASCO LEONILDA (22), Feloniously wounding James
Miller, with intent to do grievous bodily harm.
LILLEY Prosecuted ; A. B. KELLY Defended.
JAMES MILLER. I am a labourer, and live in the Lower Road, Deptford
-on 9th August, about 12 o'clock, I was in Napier Terrace near the Plough
Road in company with my young woman's mother, Mary Ann Preskland;
I had just parted with my young woman I heard a scream at the top of
the road, I ran up to see what was the matter and saw two men catching
hold of Franklin's young woman, Miss Mayne, and pulling her into the road,
they seemed to be foreigners; when I got up they ran away-my young
woman came out of doors and the four of us went on to the bridge, that
is my young woman and Franklin and his young woman-We were turning
round to oome back home and we met three other men, foreigners—they
caught hold of my young woman round the neck and pulled her off the
path; I went up to the prisoner who was one of them, and pushed him
away; he then struck me in the left hand and arm--one of his comrades made
a run towards me and the prisoner struck him in the side-the man called
out, “Oh John, no," or something like that, put his hand to his side and
ran away over the bridge-I felt my hand numbed and my finger doubled
up-I went back to Napier Terrace and my hand was bathed, blood was
running down my arm, my coat was cut through-I was taken to the
police surgeon about an hour after, and he dressed my wound-I first went
to Dr. Kelly, of Plough Road, he would not look at my arm-I had given
no provocation to the prisoner, only pushed him away-there was sufficient
light to enable me to see him ; I have no doubt about his being the man
that strnck me.
Cross-examined. I saw no knife in anybody's hands--I saw that my
hand was covered with blood- I did not see a man named Adolphe there,
I saw him afterwards at the police-court; I did not recognise him as one of
the three foreigners; I could not say whether he was the man the prisoner
struck, it was one of the two-he was coming towards me in a fighting
attitude when the prisoner struck him.
MARY ANN PRESSLAND. I am single and live in Napier Terrace,
Plough Road, Rotherhithe-on the night of 8th August, a little after 12
o'clock, I was in company with James Miller; he left me indoors and went
up the street with my mother-I heard & man scream and ran up to the top
of the street and saw five foreigners pulling Franklin's young woman in the
road-Miller went up and the men ran over the bridge-Franklin's young
woman ran down home with mother, and Franklin, me, and Miller went up
on the bridge--we met three foreigners; the prisoner put his arms round
my neck and Miller pushed him away and the prisoner pulled out his knife
and stabbed him twide-I saw it glitter, I was close to him then Adolphe,
one of his friends, ran in between Miller, and he caught a blow in his side
from the prisoner-I then returned with Miller to Napier Terrace; Franklin
came home too, but he did not stop indoors-Miller's arm was all open and
the joints of the fingers of his left hand were cut and bleeding-his wounds
were bathed-he had done nothing to the prisoner but push him away when
he had his arm round my neck.

the prisoner; I don't know his name ; he was discharged--the prisoner
put his arm round my neck in a friendly way; my young man was
