1402. GEORGE BELL was indicted for stealing 3 door-locks, value
26.; 2 keys, value 6d. ; 1 brass cap, 2d. ; 360 iron screws, 88. ; 18 brass
knobs, 18. 6d. ; 33 brass scutcheons, 18. ; 7 iron buttons, 6d. ; 2 pails,
48.; and 168lbs. weight of nails, 11. 108.; the goods of George Cole
and another, his masters.
RECEU
Thomas Cook. I am foreman to Mr. George Cole and his brother,
they are building in Clarence-street, Greenwich; the prisoner was employed
there by them. On the 17th of May I was in the store-room-I missed
nothing then, but on the Monday following I missed knobs and screws,
and other things-1 believe the whole of the property produced is my
masters', but I can swear to these two locks.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY, Q. You superintend the building of
these houses ? A. Yes—these locks had been on the shelves for six
weeks, and one was without a key-I am sure these two locks were
on the shelves.
DOO
WILLIAM TOOkey. I am a marine-store dealer—the prisoner brought
all this property to me--he wished me to look over the things, while he
got his tea, and let him know what they were worth-I called the police-
man in-he was there when the prisoner came in, and took him-he
had been to me about a week before with similar property, and I refused
to purchase it-he said he had bought these things for his brother, they
were all the wrong sort, and he wanted to sell them.
WILLIAM STANFORD. I know these pails--I made them for Mr. Cook
between the 8th and 12th of November last.
Cross-examined. Q. How many pails have you made since ? A. None
- I can swear to my own work.
