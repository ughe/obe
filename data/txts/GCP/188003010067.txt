JAMES CHARLES COVELL. I am one of the firm of Lee and Covell, carrying
on business as meat salesmen at 130, Metropolitan Meat Market on the
16th January I received this letter. (Daled 15th January, from E. Hunt, of
Zinzan Street, Reading, consigning pigs to Messrs. Covell)—we received two
pigs, which came by passenger train-we sold them-after deducting our
commission the price realised was 31. 08. 8d. -I then drew this cheque on
Messrs. Barclay, Bevan, and Co.'s Bank-it has been altered to 8901.-I
sent the cheque to 42, Zinzan Street, Reading, the address mentioned in the
letter on 17th January--that is the only transaction I had with Mr. Hunt
-I knew nothing of him-I gave no one authority to interfere with my
cheque in any way.
GEORGE BURROWS. I live at Caversham, near Reading-I am in the service
of Mr. Thomas Day, pork butcher, of 24, Pratt Street, Reading on Saturday,
17th January, between 11 and 12 a.m., Wordley and another man came-
the other man purchased some pigs—the price was 61. 68.-Wordley went
to get a wrapper; he came back with a wrapper, needle, and string--they
wrapped up the pigs, and I assisted them to lift the pigs into my master's
cart-I was told to take them to the Great Western Railway-station at
Reading I did so they did not go with me I saw them at the station
they helped me to get them out of the cart--they said they could manage it
then, and I left the pigs in their possession I had not seen Wordley before
-I saw him the Monday afterwards coming down the King's Road, Reading
I did not speak to him--Wordley gave me no name and address as it was
a ready-money transaction--I went to Newgate on the 3rd of February, and
recognised Wordley at once.
THOMAS GAMBLE. I am employed at the Great Western Railway station
at Reading-on Saturday, 17th January, I was doing porter's work there
on that morning some pigs were brought wrapped up in canvas- I weighed
them—the carriage came to 48. 3d.--I have not seen the person who brought
them-I made out the way-bill, and filled in the address from that on the
cloth-I sent them by the 12.50 passenger train.
FREDERICK GEORGE WYLDE. My father, Thomas Wylde, carries on busi-
ness at the Metropolitan Meat Market-on Saturday, 17th January, I received
two pigs, accompanied by this letter: “Sir,--Please find two pige, kindly
sell them at best price, and return account to above address " I sold the
pigs-after deducting my commission they realised 41. 168. 100.- I drew a
cheque dated 19th January for that amount, payable to E. Hunt-my father
signed it in my presence---it was sent to Mr. Hunt, of 42, Zinzan Street,
Reading—this is the cheque-it has been altered to 1,4001.- I have the
counterfoil—that was my only transaction with Mr. Hunt. (The cheque was
endorsed " Edward Hunt.")
