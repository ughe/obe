1339. THOMAS SHEPPARD was indicted for stealing, on the 20th
of April, 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of William Rhodes, from his
person.
WILLIAM RHODES. I live upon my property, in Claremont-place, Old
Kent-road. I was at Greenwich fair on the 20th of April-I went into a
booth, but did not miss my handkerchief till I was told of it--this is it.
JAMES WILD. I am a policeman. I was at the fair, and saw the pri-
soner sounding several gentlemen's pockets, about twelve o'clock at night-
at last he went behind the prosecutor, who was listening to a song- I saw
him walk suddenly away-I went and asked the prosecutor if he had lost
any thing he said, yes, his handkerchief-I told him to go with me, and
we found the prisoner blowing his nose with the handkerchief-I took
him, and found on him these snuff-boxes, this other handkerchief, these
cigars, and cases.
Prisoner. I had not the handkerchief-it was at my feet-I had been
in a booth-this man came and took me, and took the handkerchief up
from my feet.
GUILTY. Aged 21.-Transported for Seven Years.
1340. MARY ANN ASHDOWN was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of
April, 2 coats, value 21. ; 1 pair of breeches, value 108.; and 1 pair of
gaiters, value 58.; the goods of George Gayton Palmer.
GEORGE GAYTON PALMER. I am a captain in the Royal Artillery, at
Woolwich. I have a servant named Joseph Ashdown, who is the husband
of the prisoner-1 had a pair of breeches, some gaiters, and two coats,
which he wore--I cannot say when I had seen him wearing them, or
whether he gave them to the prisoner-he went into the hospital on the
31st of March, and on the Sunday following I received information, and
the prisoner left my kitchen, where she had been allowed to live with her
husband-I saw her at my quarters between the 31st and the Sunday-
I cannot say whether, during that time, she resided in the kitchen-she
might have done so if she pleased.
LEWIS DAVIS. I am a pawnbroker. I have one coat which I took in
of the prisoner, in the name of Mary Wright, on the 2nd of April.
THOMAS PACEY Burts. I am a pawnbroker. I have a coat, a pair of
breeches, and a waistcoat, pawned by a person in the name of "Ann Pol-
lock, for William Smith, it was not the prisoner."
MARY WRIGHT. I am the wife of Benjamin Wright. I was sent for
by the prisoner on the 4th of April, to Captain Palmer's quarters-she
asked me to take this suit of clothes to the pawnbroker's, told her I
never made a practice of taking things there-these are the clothes, or like
them-a young woman named Ann Pollock took them, and got 308. on
them.
Prisoner. I did not ask her to pawn them, but another person did I was
in distress, and pawned them thinking they were my husband's, and that
he would get them out again.
NOT GUILTY.
