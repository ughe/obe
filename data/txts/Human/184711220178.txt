known the defendant about two years—when I first knew him he was clerk to Mr. Pellem, a solicitor, in Piccadilly—he came to me about three months ago, and said he had left Mr. Pellem, and was living with Mr. George, a solicitor, and that he himself recovered debts in the New County Courts, and Mr. George had granted him letters to enable him to sue persons in the County Courts, for unless a clerk had a lawyer's letter, he could not appear there—I employed him to get some debts for me—he was to take out a summons against Mr. Purvis, who owed me 2  l . 14 s . 6 d .—I gave him 5 s . 3 d . to take out the summons in the Bloomsburry Court—I saw him a few days afterwards—he told me he had taken that summons out, and it would be heard on 29th Sept.—on 1st Oct. I received this letter from him by post—I know it to be his hand-writing—I saw him next evening, he told me the cause came on on the 29th, that he had attended, that the cause was un defended, and the Court granted Mr. Purvis six weeks to pay the debt in—he said the money was to be paid into Court, and he had paid the ex penses of the hearing himself, which was 5 s . or 6 s .—he asked me if I would pay him that, or give him some money—I gave him 2 s . on account—I gave him that 2 s . on the faith that the cause had been heard, and that he had paid the money out of his pocket—this was on 2nd Oct.—I have never received the money from Mr. Purvis, or the prisoner—I saw the prisoner again about three weeks afterwards, at the latter end of Oct., in Albary-street, and gave him into custody—he said he did not think I should have been so hard with him. that I should give him time—he said he was coming into some money, and he would pay it.
   Cross-examination by MR. PARRY. Q.  He was rather surprised when you gave him into custody? A.  He was very much agitated—I did not give him a list of debts to collect—I gave him the names of three persons to take out summonses for—if he had brought me the money the next morning I should not have been angry with him, but he had no business to have gone to the parties at all—all I wanted was to get my debts in in the most expe ditious way—this conversation on 2nd Oct. was not about other cases—Car ter's business had been settled before—his debt was to be paid into Court—I had given the defendant his commission on that—he was not entitled to any thing on Purvis's debt till I had received it—he would then have been entitled to half-a-crown in the pound—I gave him the 2 s . on the faith of his having paid for the hearing—he told me the cause had been heard—I said to him, "By your letter, I perceive Purvis's cause has been heard"—he told me it had—I said, "Did Purvis appear?"—he said, "Yes, the cause was not defended, all that he did was to ask for time, and the Court granted him six weeks to pay the money into Court"—I do not recollect anything else that he said—he had not paid for Knight's summons—he had the money.
   MR. PARNELL. Q.  Was he to get the money through the Court? A.  Yes—he had no authority from me to go to any of the parties themselves—the 2 s . I paid him was not for the commission—it was on account of the money that he said he had paid out of his pocket for the hearing—he said he had paid either 5 s . or 6 d .—he told me he was very badly off—I gave him 2 s ., and he was to have the rest when he brought the plaint note up. JOHN PURVIS. I have not been summoned to the Bloomsbury County Court, at the suit of Mr. Squire—I know the prisoner by his calling on me—he never served me with a summons—I was not at the Bloomsbury Court on 29th Sept.—the Court never allowed me six weeks to pay.
