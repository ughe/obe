NEW COURT.-Monday, June 15th, 1846.
Fifth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq.
1218. GEORGE PAINE was indicted for embezzlement.
JOHN HUGRES. I am a baker, and live in Richard-street, Brook's-market-
the prisoner was in my service for about two months the last time-he had to
deliver bread, and if he received money to bring it to me the same day when
he brought me money I always entered it in the cash-book-be brought me
63. from Mrs. Brown on the 27th of April, and I entered it he did not say
anything about any more.
Cross-examined by Mr. Baldwin. Q. I suppose he accounted to your
wife as well as to you? 2. No, she might receive money in my absence-I
charged him and his brother with stealing 51. on the same day I took bím for
this--they were discharged by the Magistrate - I took him twice about this
51., and he was discharged both times.
MARY ANN Brown. I deal with Mr. Hughes for bread, which the pri-
soner used to bring, and receive the money on the 27th of April he brought
me a bill, and I paid him 8s. for his master-I saw him every day-I spoke to
him the next week about the bill-he said he would take back the bill and
get it rectified, it must have been his master's mistake-be did not bring the
bill again- I asked him about it-he said it was his mistress's mistake, and
she hoped I would say nothing about it, as it would make words with bis
master.
Cross-examined. Q. He said it was a mistake of his master? A. Yes,
first, and then he said it was a mistake of his mistress the bill was 11. 136.
-I paid him 88., and he brought another bill of 11. 78.-of course I saw it
was a mistake-he took both the bills back-the bills were in the same bend-
writing as I always had.
ELIZABETH HUGHES. I am the prosecutor's wife. The prisoner did not
bring me any money of Mrs. Brown's on the 27th of April, or after that-
he did not speak to me about any mistake.
Cross-examined. Q. Nothing of the kind ? A. No-he was not in the
habit of paying me--I swear he did not pay me any money on this occasion.
