   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN . Q.  Have you fitted the stove to the place it was taken from? A.  No; there is no fitting for it—the smoke goes through a funnel—part of the funnel was taken away—it was broken.
   COURT . Q.  Have you any doubt it is the stove? A.  Not the least; it had been broken and repaired. JOHN GILES. I am engine driver to Mr. Lewis, at Bow. I belong to the ballast engine—this stove came up in Mr. Mackintosh's barge, the Richard  —my steamer laid about two miles from the yacht—they brought the barge up; and they asked my leave to put the stove on board till the barge came back from the barge builders—that was about the 24th of February.
   Q.  Who do you mean by they? A. Bill  Short; and George Everest—they brought the barge up in the morning—it was day-light when they took the stove out—they brought the barge alongside me—they have been in the habit of bringing barges alongside for two years—I cannot tell whe ther it was morning, but it was day-light—they put it on board—I have asked them several times since to take it away—I give any of the lighter men leave to put things on board—Evans took the same stove from my barge.
   Cross-examined. Q.  It was broad day-light? A.  Yes; there was no concealment—I knew them well before—the barge belongs to Mr. Fuller—they did not mention his name—I know it belonged to him. HENRY NASH. I was sleeping forward on board the Maria , and was awoke by a great noise—I thought it was the police-boat coming on board, and I was afraid to get up, but it was Joseph Everest—he had come in a boat, and somebody else was in the boat—I knew Joe  only by his voice—I said, "Who is that?"—he said, "Bring a lantern"—I said, "It is you, Joe , you have come to frighten me"—I laid hold of his tarpauling hat, and said, "Joe, is it you? you have come to frighten me?—he jumped out of a sailing boat—I did not see him take any thing; but next morning the stove was missed from the cabin—it was safe overnight.
   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN . Q.  Are you what they call a mud-lark? A.  No; I sometimes pick up things in the mud, when I can get nothing else to do—I do not not pick up coals—I pick up iron, bones, and rope—I was afraid the Thames police were coming to take me away to the Thames police-boat—I have never been there; I was sleeping on board that night, because I had no where else to sleep—I knew Joe  by his voice—he said, "Bring the lantern"—I took hold of his hat: I then knew him—I knew him by his voice before—the foreman knew I slept on board this boat—I have slept there two or three months—I knew the stove was there, because I had seen it in the day-time—the cabin doors were open in the day-time—it was about ten o'clock when I went to sleep—I did not see it at night—the foreman missed it next running, or about the middle of the day—I had gone away at day-light—it had not been missed then—I did not know it was gone, when I went away in the morning—I saw the man step out of the boat into a skiff—I did not see any one with him.
