WILLIAM GOLDSMITH (Policeman, S 57). On 8th July I went to the
Greyhound-the last witness gave me this shilling-I bent it, and have
kept it ever since-from a description I received from the witnesses I
apprehended the prisoner in the Edgware-rond, about three quarters of a
mile from this house - I stopped him-he said, "What do you want with
me!"-I said, "To see what you have about you"-I found on him six
sixpences, two fourpenny-pieces, and 28. 21d. in copper, good money-I said
to him, " You are charged with uttering counterfeit coin-he said he never
had any, and did not want it-I took him to the station I received this
shilling from Mr. Hocking.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coiu to the Mint-these shillings
are both bad, and from the same monld.
Prisoner's Defence. I had these coppers for the purpose of giving change
to my customers; if I have not change they will not buy of me.
GUILTY.-Confined Nine Months.

738. JOHN HAMMOND ARMSTRONG (17), WILLIAM HARRISON
(16), and ALFRED LEWIN (16), were indicted for a like offence; to which
ARMSTRONG-PLEADED GUILTY.Confined Nine Month
MERS. COLERIDGE and LUMLEY conducted the Prosecution. al
ALFRED GREEN. I am a butcher, living at Hendon-on 7th August
about balf-past 4, the three prisoners came, and Armstrong asked for a
pound of steak-I served him, and he gave me a crown-the steak was 9d.
-my wife gave them the change-I went to Mr. Hocking's, who keeps the
Rose and Crown, about half past 7 as near as I can say, and saw them there
--they were served with a pint of six ale-Harrison paid for it with a half-
crown--they all drank of the beer–Fr. Hocking took the half-crown on
one side, bent it, brought it back, and said, “Have you any more like this ;
where did you get it from !”– Harrison said, “I brought both from home
this morning" he had another good one--- Mr. Hocking said, “I have a
good mind to look you up"—the prisoners said they were very sorry, they
did not know it-Mr. Hoeking went out at the side door and came in again
directly with a policeman and the prisoners—I am quite sure they are the
men who were in my shop.
MATILDA GREBN. I am the wife of the last witness on this Monday
evening I saw the prisoners in our shop,they asked for a pound of steak
-I saw Armstrong put a five-shilling piece on the board—I took it up and
gave him change, a balf-crown, a shilling, a sixpence, and three-pennyworth
of halfpence- I placed the five-shilling piece in a desk in the parlour-there
were no other coins in the desk--I shut the desk up--later in the evening,
I found the crown was bad, and gave it to the sergeant.
BENJAMIN HOOKING. I am the landlord of the Rose and Crown beer-shop
at Hendon-a little before 8 on 7th August, the prisoners came-Harrison
called for a pint of six ale, and gave me a bad half-crown-I tried it, bent
it, laid it on the counter, and asked him if he had any more, and where he
got that-be said he bad brought two from home and that was one of them,
and he did not know that they were bad-he then produced a good half-
crown, and I gave him change- I gave the half-crown to Sergeant Hughes-
