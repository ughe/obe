SAMUEL WEBB. I am churchwarden of St. Ann's, Westminster several
weeks before September 22nd I opened the money-boxes in the church
and found some sticky stuff where the money is put in, and the slits were
very much marked-there was also a good deal of sticky stuff adhering
to the bottoms of the boxes-here is a specimen of it produced) on the
morning of the 22nd I went with the policeman and unlocked all the
boxes the prisoner had then been taken to the station.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. It is impossible to say how much money
bad been in the boxes.
Prisoner's Defence. I was not coming out of the church–I was on the
steps, and I passed the policeman-he examined the church and found
the door open-I had no keys and no tools to break in or break out-the
doors must have been left open—they were both open right and left.
BAMUEL WEBB (re-examined). This was Saturday morning—unfor-
tunately the church had been left open—there had been a choir practice
over night at 10 or 10.30, and whether the prisoner got in then and con-
cealed himself I cannot say, but it was palpably plain that the boxes had
been tampered with.
GUILTY-Six Months' Imprisonment.
798. THOMAS SMITH (24), Burglariously breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of William Walker, and stealing therein two sheets and
two pillow-cases, his property.
MR. PUROELL conducted the Prosecution.
HENRY FEAT (Policeman G 325). On 9th October, shortly before twelve
o'clock. I saw two men in Compton Street loitering by a low wall-I
crossed the road and watched them, and saw a bundle fall over the wall,
and a man came down and ran away, and then the prisoner came over the
· wall-I stopped him and took him to a house which is at the end of the
wall, and some persons came out and said that they had seen them
loitering about– I took the prisoner in custody-he struggled—the bundle
contained two sheets and two pillow-cases.
ALFRED GILLHAM (Policeman G 11). I examined this wall—it is seven
feet high-there were marks where a person had got over there is a closet
at the back of a public-house which joins & window, one of the panes of
which was broken, and the sash was hanging by the cord-it is an empty
beerhouse-I went upstairs and saw two small ladders, one on the ceiling
and one on the roof-I got on the roof and saw that some person had
gone over several tiles and slid down-the outer skylight had been taken
off and the inner one broken, and a trap-door in the ceiling removed, so
that access could be got to the landing.
HARRIET WALKER. I live with my husband at 57, Northampton
Street, Clerkenwell-on 9th October, a little after ten p.m., I heard glass
break and called my husband I went downstairs I did not feel satisfied,
but I went to bed about eleven o'clock-I was afterwards called by the
police, and heard people on the roof calling out “Hold him tight”-I
found the trap-door open-I had left these sheets hanging on the landing.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. I was coming down
Compton Street, and saw the things lying, and was going to pick them
up when the constable seized me I was never on the house or wall, or
in the beershop-I have been in it when it was open, but not since.
