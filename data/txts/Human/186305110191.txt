   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you quite certain it was Bell who took the house? A.  I have not the least doubt of him—I was satisfied with his re ferences—he called on me one day, and wanted to come into the house the following day, as he had got a fortnight's leave—I saw him, very respectably dressed, doing up the front garden, a fortnight after he entered into occupa tion, and did not see him again till he was in the police-court. WILLIAM CHILD. I am assistant to a Sheriff's officer—for certain reasons I watched No. 16, Cottage Grove, Peckham, for about a week before 23d April—I saw Bell go in three times, and French three or four times—I also saw Lansdale go in with French several times—the house was closed on Thursday morning, the 23d at 6 o'clock.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you A.  Assistant to Mr. Jackson, the Sheriff of London—I have been with him about two months before that—I was an officer at Red Lion Wharf for about sixteen months, and before that I was an officer of the City of London—I was beadle of Trinity-square twenty years—I have never been charged at the Mansion-house with perjury, or anything else—I was watching this house for the Loan Society people, Lisson Grove—I made no communication to Messrs. Levaux—I happened to be at the Court, and saw the prisoner brought up. ROBERT MARTIN. I am landlord of the Duke of Sussex, Cumberland-road, Peckham—I saw French at the police-station, and fancied I had seen him in the house one day—Lonsdale came to my house two or three times a week—he brought me twenty-three bottles of port and sherry to sell; it was then that 1 fancy I saw French there, but I did not speak to him—I do not know that I saw Bell; but there were several in the bar, as I have an ordinary at 1 o'clock—the wine was on the kitchen table when I came in—I bought twenty-one bottles, and afterwards sold one bottle of the port to Broderick, the constable—two bottles were opened as samples, and drank by Lonsdale, myself, and two friends. JOHN BRODERICK( Policeman, M  210). I produce a bottle of wine which I bought of Martin—I showed it to Mr. Vickers—I took French on 1st May—he' was with Lonsdale, in Brunswick-street, Blackfriars-road—he at tempted to run away, but I overtook him—I told him the charge was conspiring to defraud Messrs. Vickers & Co. of 25 l . worth of wine, and asked if his name was French—he said, "No; I do not know anybody of that name"—I took him as far as Lonsdale's shop—he wanted to go in there, but I would not allow him—I took him to the station—the inspector asked him his name—he said, "French"—I took Bell about a quarter-past 9, on 2 d . May, in Lucretia-street, Lambet—I told him the charge, and asked him if his name was Bell—he said, "No"—he asked two or three times what the charge was; and I said, "Do you know a man named French?"—he said, "Your duty is to caution me that what I say will appear in evidence against me"—I said, "You are very right"—he begged to be treated as a gentleman, as he was a solicitor—when we got to Blackman-street, Borough, he said, "If I know French, I will eat a b—y lamp-post"—the inspector at the station asked him his name—he said, "Bell"—he was dressed in a reversible coat; he looks cleaner now than he did then. GUILTY.*— Confined Twelve Months each .
