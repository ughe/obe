On the second interview, I waited on him with Miss Perrott, and requested him to pay her the wages which she claimed; and also, to deliver the property in her chest of drawers; and I said I thought, as the parties had complained against each other, the best way would be for him to give up the property she claimed, and then I should advise her to have the agreement given up, and it should be cancelled. He said she had already broken the agreement. I said I would not discuss that subject then. He said he would do nothing without consulting his solicitor; I then left him, requesting him to name an early day to settle the business; he named the next day, at four o'clock, but I did not go, in consequence of some intimation from Madamoiselle Perrot, but I went on the following Saturday alone. I had met him in the morning in the City, and it was agreed, that I should wait upon him at four o'clock. I requested that she should go with me to identify her property; he strongly objected to that; I urged it several times without effect; but he said, if she would write an order for him to deliver the property to me, and give me the key, it should be delivered to me. I am quite sure he mentioned the delivery of the key. I went at four o'clock, and took an order which I had written, and Miss Perrot had signed. I had shown him the inventory on the first occasion. At the third interview I was shown into the room, but just as I had opened the drawer, he went away. I believe there were two ladies and a Gentleman in the room. I did not find the goods corresponding with the inventory, but I found some of them, and I made an inventory of what I did find. Here is the inventory I made. I found a pair of diamond ear-rings, which are these, but no other articles of jewellery. I found an inventory in the drawers, in French: here it is. I did not search the drawers for any other articles than what were in the inventory she had given me. I could find no Bank Bills or the confidential letter she described.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. When did you see Mr. Askew first on this business - A. On the 4th of September; I had two interviews with him before I saw Etherington and Gregory. I did not leave the inventory or a copy of it with him, nor a demand in writing, but read the articles from the inventory. I said, I came from Miss Perrot, and the Consul had written me a letter on the subject. I did not say, I came from the French Consul. He afterwards showed me the letter; it was the third interview, I had the key, and went into room, but at the the first interview, he produced the letter. When I demanded the goods, at the second time, I proposed that they should give up the agreements, and they should be cancelled. If Miss Perrot had received her property and wages, we should have had no ground of complaint. I read the letter twice over: it was such a one as, perhaps, a Lady would not like to have made public; I should think it was of a nature to irritate the mind of Miss Perrot; she has been very much vexed at its being in Mr. Askew's possession; she has not expressed to me that it has been shown to other persons. I went before a Justice, about the beginning of September. I cannot recollect the date when I was requested to attend at Marlborough-street, by Miss Perrot. I saw Mr. Conant on the subject; it was before the goods were delivered up to me, I believe, but I cannot swear it.

 Middlesex Cases, fourth Jury.
 Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1512.  GEORGE SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , at St. James, Westminster , a watch, value 37 s.; a seal, value 15 s.; a watch-key, value 10 s.; and a watch-chain, value 1 s. the goods of John Clarke , in his dwelling house.
   HANNAH CLARKE. My husband's name is John Clarke; we live at No. 5, Silver-street, Clerkenwell Green; we have two shops next to each other. On the 6th of August my husband's watch was in the back parlour of one of the shops. I had left that shop a few minutes to go to the other, and on returning I saw the prisoner coming out of the back parlour; he came into the shop and asked for something, but what he I said don't know. I enquired what he wanted there; he said,
 "Nothing, mistress; I have not touched any thing." I caught hold of him, he struggled, and I began to scream out. He took this watch from his pocket or his bosom, I cannot say which, and laid it in the window.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   REUBEN RICE. I am an officer. I went on the 6th of August and took the prisoner; the last witness had detained him. He said he hoped we would excuse him; he had nothing about him.
 The Prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he had no idea of the watch being taken by any one; there were other persons in the shop at the time, and it must have been deposited by some one else in the place where it was found; he had nothing about him.
 HANNAH CLARKE re-examined. Q. Are you quite sure the prisoner took the watch from some part his person - A. Yes.
 One witness gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 16.
 1531.  THOMAS JAMES was indicted for the wilful murder of Thomas Smith.
 EDMUND SMITH. I am the brother of  Thomas Smith, the deceased. I was present on the 12th of July, in a field near Copenhagen-house; the prisoner was there: he was what they call bottle holder. My brother fought with one Harry Bostock, for two hours and seventeen minutes: I was in the field, but not with my brother. On the Thursday previous I went into my father's house, and then heard that my brother was to fight on the Monday. I never saw Bostock before the fight, nor since; my brother had lived fellow servant with him, and when he left they were always in opposition; I do not know that my brother challenged him to fight: but when I got to the field I went to my brother, and tried to persuade him not to fight; it was then about a quarter past eight o'clock; he said he was not going to fight, for the other party had not come, and they had forfeited the money - the others then came; my brother said they had forfeited the money; they replied that he was afraid to lose, and then they fought.
