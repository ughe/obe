Witnesses for the Defence.
WILLIAM BROWNING. I know the prisoner-I saw him on 15th April,
the County Court day, in Southwark-I saw him as to making me some
clothes-he measured me as far as he could-he was not capable of reasur-
ing; he was intoxicated—I should consider he was drunk-he had been
drinking for a week-he could not measure me as he ought-I should think
that was about 10 o'clock in the morning.
Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. Where did you see him A. At
the “Earl Grey"- I was not in the County Court-I am certain it was on
15th April– I know it, because it is my pension day-I received it the
same day-I receive a pension from the police-I go to Scotland-yard for it
- I went about 2 o'clock-1 happened to go to my brother the same day,
and on my way, close to the house, I was given to understand he was in
custody; and that was the same morning that he measured me-he tried
to measure me—I do not consider he did the thing that was right, and
therefore I sent for my cloth and took it back again-that was the same
evening--I heard he was in trouble, and I thought I would get him off as
far as I could I went to the County Court and offered bail for him he is
not a relation of mine I have known him for two years and a half-I have
met him at my brother's very often-I never stopped late- I have met him
in the morning as well as the evening.
MR. BEST. Q. Have you seen him since he was taken into custody? A.
I have, in Horsemonger-lane gaol—he has been in gaol the whole time.
BENJAMIN BROWN. Q. I am a tinman, and reside in Oakley-street, Lam-
beth-I know the prisoner-I saw him on 15th April-I cannot exactly
say what time, but somewhere about 10 or 11 o'clock-he was then in liquor
- I had seen him the day before-he was in liquor then-he was not
excited, but quietly drunk.
Cross-examined. Q. How long were you with him? A. Perhaps five or
ten minutes-we were talking-I met him in the street on the day before;
at the time I am speaking of, it was in the Earl Grey-I heard of this the
day he was locked up.
Court. Q. You say he was quiet ; could he walk? A. Yes I know he
was in liquor, because he spoke in that way-when a man is in liquor, he
cannot speak so plain as when sober-I knew he had been drinking for two
or three days previously-I was talking to him about going to work-he
said he was going on the following day- I believe he understood what I
said this was on the day previous, when I spoke to him about going to
work ; pot the morning he was locked up-I was talking to him about ten
minutes on the same morning he was locked up, I was not talking to him
then about going to work-I dare say he understood what I said I know
he was in liquor very much. GUILTY.—Confined Six Months.
