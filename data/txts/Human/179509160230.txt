I believe it is a malicious prosecution; had I paid the prosecutor what he wished to demand of me it would not have been, but he would not name and sum, and there was a runner there that wished to be paid, and when I found that he wanted to make a property of me, I said that I had rather be committed to prison, and trust the event.
   HUGH FERGUSON sworn.
 Q. I believe you are one of the inspectors of the letter carriers in the post office. Will you be so kind as to tell us what is the practice of the office, with respect to letters paid money with? - The general rule is, when a man takes a letter that money is paid with, to have it entered in the money book.
 Q. In the hurry of business, does it, or does it not, frequently happen that that is omitted? - It does; the post master's permanment order is, that those that neglect that duty, shall pay the loss, besides a suspension.
 Q. How long has this man been in the employ of this office? - Two years.
 Q. During that time, what has been his character and behaviour in the office, good or bad? - To the best of my opinion as honest a man as ever was.
 Q. How many hands does a letter pass through between here and Bath? - I cannot say, a great number.
 Q. Was he in the service of the office when apprehended? Supposing him acquitted, would he be taken into the office again? - I have not a doubt of it.
 Q. With regard to his settling of his accounts at the post office, has that man been regular and punctual, or not? -Always regular and punctual.
 Court. Do they turn them out of their office if they refuse to pay the money? - There never has been such an instance, but I am very confident they would be dismissed if they were guilty of such a thing as that.
 Q. This letter was put into the post office on Friday, the 21st of August; do you remember whether at that time there was any robbery of the mail, or any loss of letters complained of? - Not that I know of.
 Q. Supposing it not being entered in the money book, might it not go to Bath? - Yes, but a letter may go to Salisbury or Bristol and we never hear of it again.
 Q. But in general do not they come back to the office when they go wrong? - Yes, but sometimes they do not.
 - SPARKES sworn.
 Q. Are you employed by the post office? - Yes, a deputy inspector; the prisoner was letter carrier, I have known him about two years, he made his payments always regular.
 Q. You have been in court when Mr. Ferguson was examined, he has spoken to the practice of this entering in the money book? - That is the practice.
 Q. Does it frequently happen that that practice is omitted? - We have frequently instances of it, from the hurry of business.
 Q. Supposing the letter to be lost, the party can be no gainer by it? - He is liable to make it good, or forefeit his place.
 Q. What do you think the place of a letter carrier is worth to a man? - Fourteen or sixteen shillings a week.
 Court. When there is any money in it, it is entered in the money book? - Yes, and they pay as a double letter.
   JOHN WILSON sworn.
