ber 27 I went to 12, Chapel Street with Inspector Martin. No. 12
is a clothier's shop. The cellar we found to be fixed up with orange
boxes, with boards laid across so as to form tables, and 6,632 copies
of pirated music, representing 71 different sorts, arranged alphabeti-
cally on a table near the window. We also found a quantity of blot-
ting paper and what appeared to be some burnt notes in the fire
place, also a quantity of labels similar to those on the parcels I have
mentioned. There were also some sacks. Afterwards we went to 2,
Chapel Place, where prisoner, Robert Kidd, lives. We found eight
genuine songs. Looking at a copy of "My Ain Folk," I notice the
peouliarity that the composer's signature has been torn off. Ten
pirated copies of that song were found amongst the 6,632 copies
found at i2, Chapel Street. Another genuine song found at 2,
Chapel Place was, "It's a Different Girl Again," of which 237 pirated
copies were found at 12, Ohapel Street. Of another genuine song
By the side of the Zuyder Zee," 246 pirated copies were found
at 12, Chapel Street. No pirated copies of the other
genuine songs found in Chapel Place were found at No. 12,
Chapel Street. The genuine songs "It's a Different Girl Again" and
"By the side of the Zuyder Zee" show marks as of drawing pins in
the corners, which suggests that tracing paper has been put over the
originals with a view to taking copies from them. The pirated copies
of the three genuine songs are all lithographed. The method of re-
production is to place specially prepared transfer paper over the
original copy, pin it down, and, by means of specially prepared ink,
make a traoing of the print, and then transfer the tracing to a stone
or & photo-zinco block.
Cross-examined by Mr. Black.. I have not seen the process de-
scribed. I do not suggest the litho reproduction was done at the
house. Linford was supplied with funds by the Musical Copyright
Association. I agree that the labels found are of the commonest,
kind, but they correspond with the labels found on the parcels.
WALTER STEPHEN LINTORD. I formerly dealt in pirated music. On
July 31 I was instructed by Mr. Preston to go and buy some. I
went to Chapel Street and saw Kidd and asked him if he had any
music. He said, "Wait a minute; I will see Mike' and
let you know." "Mike" (Walsh) came up and wrote out for me
list of music. I got from Walsh a parcel of music. I cannot now!
remember how much I paid him. The prioes of the music ranged from
48. to 12s. per 100. Exhibit No. 2 is the parcel I got from Walsh of
July 31, which I took to Mr. Preston at Queen's Hall. On the same
day I saw M.Carthy. He was standing just by the corner of Chapel
Place. I asked him if he had any "Merry Widow Waltz." I had
been told he had by a person whom I knew as Jones in Wellington
Road. McCarthy said "Yes." I did not get any from him that
day, but next morning he gave me 12 copies of the "Merry Widow"
waltz, and I took them to Mr. Preston at Queen's Hall. I do not re-
