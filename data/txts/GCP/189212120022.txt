JOHN MODARRELL ( Detective 0). On November 21st, about 9.30 p.m.,
I was with Sergeant Boulter in Goodge Street, and saw the two prisoners
examining something like silver-Hickman rubbed a coin between his
fingers and handed it to Hamilton--they walked together to Wigmore
Street, where I stopped them, and said, "You answer the descriptions
of two men wanted for uttering counterfeit money"-Hamilton said,
"I never uttered counterfeit money in my life "- they were taken to the
station, and on Hamilton I found seven counterfeit half-crowns, one
counterfeit florin, and 48. 6d. in good money-I said, “How did you
come into possession of these counterfeit coins?"-he said, "I met a
woman in Goodge Street, and gave her 38. Afd. for the load"-a load is
twenty-I have frequently seen them together before in Lisson Grove
and Tottenham Court Road.
Cro88-examined. I did not say at the Police-court that I saw Hamilton
pass more than one coin to Hickman-Hamilton gave his address at his
mother's, at No. 9; I went there, and she said he lived at No. 7; that
is what I mean by his giving a false address-his mother did not tell me
that he was living with her-the prisoners have done no work fora consider-
able time-Hamilton was working for the Marylebone Vestry three months
ago—they are labourers, I believe-Hickman was & porter to Mr.
George, a grocer, up to last April—there was no third man- I do not
know Bill Stephens.
Re-examinod. I have found out that they both live in one house, 7,

back; and there are two women there-Hickman did not mention the
name of Mr. Smith.

-he handed Hickman what appeared to be a coin; he rubbed

-I found four good shillings on him.
