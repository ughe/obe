   Q.  Since you have been at the police-office, have you conversed with your husband about the time he came home? A.  My husband said, when he came home, "Mary, Richard did not come home till morning"—I be lieve my husband did not speak when the prisoner came in, because I spoke—I did not ask my husband whether he was awake, nor did I speak—I was out of bed three times to nurse my child—the prisoner got up between seven and eight o'clock next morning, and went through the wash-house to the privy—there is a catch to the privy-door—it is what I term a night-bolt—it shuts and bolts—there is another entrance to it from the garden—if the door is shut you must go through the wash-house—if the door is open you can come in that way—the bolt is in the wash-house—it is the wash-house-door bolt—the privy-door was off its hinges at that time—the privy joins the wash-house, and when in the privy you can go out of the privy into the garden—you cannot get into the wash-house without the door is left open—you can get to the privy, through the wash-house, without going into the garden—the privy has two doors—the door leading to the garden was off its hinges at that time—I heard of Mr. Templeman's death, I think, at twelve o'clock in the day—I had been out—my husband went up, and he told me all the particulars when he came home, of the old gen tleman being murdered—I cannot remember whether be told me any thing about the state he was found in on the floor—I knew all about it, and be gan to talk to the prisoner about it before my husband came in—when he came in he broke upon the conversation.
   Q.  Was his going for the bacon and beer the only occasion when your husband went out that night? A.  Yes—with the exception of that inter val he heard every thing that took place—when my husband said Richard had got a new pair of shoes, I said, "I think not, I think he has blacked and shined his old ones"—he said so to me the moment Richard walked across the room, while he was gone to the privy—when he came back from the privy he asked my husband to fetch him a drop of beer, and I believe he went out to fetch it—I think he was eating his supper.
   COURT . Q.  When did your husband remark about his having new shoes? A.  When he came in, he walked through the room, and went into the privy before he sat down, when he came in in the evening.
   Q.  I thought your husband was out when he came in? A.  My hus band was out, but he had stood talking to me by the table, and had not sat down when my husband came in.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Was it in consequence of your husband saying he had new shoes that he said he got them from his cousin? A.  I do not know, but he said that—I did not ask him about the shoes—my husband looked at them, and so did I—I never asked where he got them—he said he had been very lucky that day.
   Q.  Was it not after that, he talked so despondingly as you say about being such an unlucky man? A.  It was during the conversation.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You did not unrol the stockings by the bed side? A.  No, I merely took them in my hand—when they brought me the stocking in, I said, "There was a pair of stockings on the box on Mon day"—I am quite sure that was the outside stocking the money was tied in, and I am quite sure, by the feel of the stockings, that there was more than one.
