   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Let us understand you rightly, you had two shops at the time of the transaction, one in the Strand and one in Coventry-street—the shop in the Strand is where the fire is ready, and the crucibles prepared? A.  Where the gold was melted—it is the place of business—the crucible was ready, unfortunately, at that moment.
   Q.  It was merely accidental that the crucible was ready, it was never so before? A.  Never, to my knowledge—I have been twenty-five years in business—Moses came to me in the morning, and said he had gold dust to sell—I said I would not receive more than thirty ounces—I had not dealt with Moses before for gold—I had dealt with him for spirits—I did not ask where he got the gold—I got 3 l . 17 s . 9 d . an ounce for the gold—that is standard—it contained silver and sulphur at first—I cannot tell what I got for it, because it was mixed with other gold—the gold contains silver and sulphur.
   Q.  Is not the gold dust coming from the Brazils in the highest state of perfection? A.  No, not in the highest perfection, it requires a process to make it pure—I do not know whether it is in the purest state when it comes from abroad—it comes in different prices—it varies in price, and cannot be quite pure—alloy is used to it afterwards to make it workable—I had beard there was gold dust lost before Moses came to me—I heard that it had been stolen—I asked no questions of Moses—I was astonished when Mrs. Abrahams came with the 110 ounces instead of thirty, and I was sorry I had got into it—I had melted it and weighed it before J discovered there was 110 ounces—I could tell, without melting it, that there was more than thirty, ounces, because I made the observation that she had brought more than her father intended—I did not say, "Take it away," nor did I say, "Come again with as much as you please."
   Q.  Pray did either of your sons see any thing of the transaction of that day, when Mrs. Abrahams was there? A.  My son Isaac saw her come in, to the best of my recollection, in the morning, the first time she came with the gold—he did not go with her into the back shop, he remained in the front shop—I do not recollect whether any body else was in the front shop—when Mr. Moses came in I was attending to two or three persons—I do not know who they were—in a public shop I cannot tell people—none of them are here—I received the summons to attend before the Ma gistrate on the Friday night, three days after I bought the gold—I at tended, and was examined—what I then swore was false—I was not thinking at the time, and did it to screen myself—I knew it was wrong—I signed my name to it—I did not consider it willful and corrupt perjury—I did not swear to injure any body, but to screen myself—I was not think ing at the moment—I am very sorry for it—I feel great remorse and sorrow for it—I am satisfied I swore wrong, but I did not think it was wilful corrupt perjury at the time—I regret it very much—my conscience has told me I ought to regret it—I saw Mrs. Abrahams at the office, while I was at the bar in custody—I am sure I do not know How far I was from her when she was being examined—I should think I was as far as I am from the foreman of the Jury now—but I cannot remember such trifling things—I was as far from her as I am now.
