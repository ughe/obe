CHARLES Woon. I am a professor of music, of 14, Great Quebec Street
-I married the prisoner on 20th May, 1871, at Marylebone Church, but
did not cohabit with her--we made appointments to meet each other-
15th or 16th May, 1872, she came across from the Metropolitan station in
great excitement and said "That fellow Newton is following me, I cannot
stay"-I said “You mentioned the name of Newton, that is your own
name, and I understood you were a widow"-she said "I considered myself
a widow, but I have had an interview with Newton and he tells me he was
married before he married me "-I then ceased to meet her-I endeavoured
to save money to get & divorce, but found it impossible, and
detective came to me with the certificate-I made no communication to
the police--the prisoner represented herself to me as a widow, and to
friend of mine also.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. You wrote me letters importuning me to

JOHN H. BEECHEY. I am an attendant at the Registrar-General's Office,

Prisoner's Defence. Before I married this gentleman I explained very
clearly what position I was in. He said that he had a wife living, but he
was not going to criminate bimself. I do not think Mr. Wood is the actual
prosecutor, he always said that he would never appear against me. I did
not take his name or make the marriage known.
