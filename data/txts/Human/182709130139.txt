On the 7th of September, at a quarter to seven o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoners in company together, in Castle-street, Leicester-fields; they appeared bulky in front; I met Murray, we followed them into a plumber's shop in Gray's-inn-lane - I went in and seized Cross, and said, "I must have what you have got here;" I unbuttoned his breeches, and suspended to a strap, was this piece of lead; the buckle of the strap was fixed to the center brace - I opened the lead and found this solder inside- the lead was nailed round it; I asked where he got it, he said he found it near the Diorama, Regent's-park, and they were both out of employ.
   BENJAMIN MURRAY. I joined Boston, and followed the prisoners into the shop. I found six bars of solder under Collins' clothes, nailed in a piece of lead - here is a hole in it which fastened on his braces in front; he said he found it in some rubbish in Regent's-park.
   JOHN GROOM. I am a constable. On the 7th of September I saw the prisoners coming down Pall-mall, towards Cockspur-street; about twenty minutes to six o'clock; they had something bulky in front; but I could not stop them, as I was going on business.
   WILLIAM ROLES. I am clerk of the works at Buckingham-palace; the prisoners have been employed there some time, as plumbers' labourers, they leave work about half-past five o'clock; there is such lead and solder as this at the Palace, it is new - they might have access to it with the assistance of the plumber's foreman, who absoconded on the following morning. Here is scratched on the lead, "Centre building," and on the solder, here is "North;" which I presume, means the north wing.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Who wrote that you cannot tell? A. No; the prisoners were employed there that day; the solder and lead is delivered to the foreman. I cannot swear it ever was at the Palace; nothing was allowed to be taken away.
   THOMAS JOHNSON. I am a labourer in trust. I was at work on the centre building of Buckingham-place; the prisoners were labourers there - the men are all told they are to take nothing away. I saw Collins on the roof of the centre building at five o'clock - the foreman delivers out the solder.
 CROSSY -    GUILTY. Aged 28.
 COLLINS -    GUILTY. Aged 32. Transported for Seven Years.

 1927.  ANN FIELD was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 1 blanket, value 2s.; 2 sheets, value 4s.; 2 bolsters, value 12s.; 1 tea-kettle, value 1s. 9d.; 1 saucepan, value 2s.; 1 pail, value 1s.; 1 fender, value 6d.; 20lbs. weight of feathers and small pieces of flannel, mixed together, value 10s.; 1 quilt, value 4s.; 1 window curtain, value 1s., and 1 set of bed curtains, value 3s., the goods of William Summer.
 WILLIAM SUMNER. I am a  printer, and live in Fenchurch-street. I let the prisoner a room in a furnished house, in Grub-street- I had suspicion; in consequence of information, I went and broke her room open, and missed the articles stated; I have found nothing but the flocks and feathers.
   MARY EARL. On the 24th of July the prisoner come and asked me to take her as a lodger - I said I would in a few days, but I was going to make a bed for a woman, and was deficient in a pillow - she brought some flocks and feathers in her apron next day, and on the day after brought some more, and slept at my house. I delivered the same feathers to the officer.
   JAMES PEACHY. I produce the flocks and feathers.
 WILLIAM SUMNER. I believe this to be mine.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1928.  ELIZABETH NEALE and ELIZABETH JOHNSON were indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 1 hat, value 6s.; 1 looking-glass, value 1s.; 4 cups, value 6d.; 4 saucers, value 6d.; 2 sheets, value 4s.; 2 waistcoats, value 6d.; 1 gown, value 1s. 6d.; 2 shirts, value 2s.; 1 yard of calico, value 6d.; 2 pairs of stockings, value 2s.; 2 quilts, value 1s.; 1 frock, value 2d.; 1 table-cloth, value 2d., and 1 apron, value 2d., the goods of James Furrein.
 JAMES FURRIEN. I am a  coal carman, and live in High Timber-street. On the 8th of August I left home at five o'clock in the morning - these things were then all safe; I received information and came home between eleven and twelve, and missed them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   SARAH BOSWELL. I live in the same house, on the ground floor. On the 8th of August, between eleven and twelve o'clock, I saw Neale go up the stairs which lead to Furrein's room; in a few minutes I went and peeped into the room, and saw her take the sheets from the bed, and twist them round her, and in a few minutes I saw both the prisoners come down stairs with bundles - we stopped them with the property.
   MARY SKIPP. I was called, and stopped the prisoners in the street - Neale said she had got some dirty linen to wash.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I took them in charge. I found on Neal four keys, one of which opens Furrien's door.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 NEAL -    GUILTY. Aged 42.
 JOHNSON -    GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 1930.  JOHN CRAMER was indicted for embezzlement. MAURICE EVAN EVANS. I am a carpet dealer, and the prisoner was occasionally in my employ; he used to receive money, which he should have brought me immediately; he was in my employ on the 3d of September, but did not account to me for any money that day; when he went out, I gave him change to receive Mr. Well's account; he went out, and I did not see him again till the Friday night following; he came voluntarily; his wife had been to say he would come: I asked him for the money he had received for me; he said he had not got it, but if I would let him go, he would find it by twelve o'clock next day.
 Prisoner. Q. What did you give me in the morning? A. The difference of 10l. - 3l. 9s. 4d.; I have no other complaint against him; he took my truck out; that was brought home by another person.
