   ANN DEARY. I was married to the prisoner on the 19th of November, 1826, by the name of George Foyles, at St. James, Westminster; I had known him eight or nine months - I was at that time a widow; I had a house and let it furnished - I had a little boy: the prisoner represented himself to me as a shoemaker, and said his father kept the Jolly Hatters public-house, at Stockport, in Cheshire; I lived with him but six weeks, and then left him in consequence of his ill usage - he told me about a month after we were married that he had been married, and I then heard it from a young man who came from the country; I wrote to his wife who answered my letter, and then I would not live with him; he had pawned some of my things and I have worked very hard to get them out - I had to maintain him; he might do a little work, but what he got he spent; my former husband was a saddler, and we lived in Riley-street, Chelsea.
 Prisoner. I never struck the woman in my life; but I could not bring home five or six guineas a week - and nothing would do; I gave her a pound or two when I could - she was jealous of another woman. Witness. It was not without reason; he cohabited with a married woman who lived close by.
 Prisoner. I gave her all the money I could; I gave her my pension when I received it, and I said, "There is three pounds for you" - the day she left me, she robbed me of every thing. Witness. I left him furniture enough for a room; I took the other things away.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Transported for Seven Years.

 1059.  CHARLES LEDGERWOOD and WILLIAM WHEELER were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of February , 3 cast-iron ornaments, value 9s., the goods of Henry Dartnell.
   JAMES CHETTLE GOMME. I had the care of a house at Hammersmith, which belonged to Mr. Dartnell; a great many ornaments had been taken from the top of the iron palisades, in front of the house - I missed some on the 20th of February; there was a man in the house to take care of it, who gave me information.
   RICHARD READING. I have the care of that house; it belongs to Mr. Henry Dartnell - Mr. Gomme had the care of it under him; on the 18th of February I got up to go to work at six o'clock in the morning - I missed some knobs; I came home at six in the evening, and watched from six till twelve - I then went to bed, and in the morning I missed fourteen more; I told Mr. Gomme of it, and the constable found some of them at a shop at Hammersmith - I gave him one to find the other; I have seen then fitted, and they match exactly - I have no doubt they were taken from there; I know the prisoners do not live far off.
   MARY MARTIN. I keep an iron-shop at Hammersmith. On the 20th of February, the prisoners came together to my shop, and brought three knobs of iron; I asked how they came by them - they said, they came from near the new bridge, that the barges were fastened to them, and had loosened them; I cannot tell whether they said they got them out of the Thames, or out of the sewers - I put them among my iron, and gave them to the officer; I gave a farthing per lb. for them.
   MARY ANN MARTIN. I live with my mother; the prisoners came to my mother about one o'clock on the 20th of February, with three knobs, which they said they got out of the sewer.
   EDWARD EDGSON. I am a constable; I took these knobs from Martin's - I showed then to the witness; I took the prisoner, who said, at first, they had not taken any; but afterwards they said, they had taken them out of the Thames all over clay.
 LEDGERWOOD's Defence. We never said any such thing; but because we were taken up, that woman and her daughter came and swore this against us; I know nothing about it.
 LEDGERWOOD -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 WHEELER -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined One Month.
