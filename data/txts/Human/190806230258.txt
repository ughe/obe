At about this time I bought "The Bank of Friendship" public-house in Mile End. It was in the hands of Receivers when I bought it and the occupier had some furniture. I said to him, "Well, I shall not want this furniture." Then the thought struck me that Calcutt, having to furnish his house at Herne Bay, it would be useful to him. I sent across for Calcutt and showed him the furniture in the room with the manager's figures; I think the manager agreed to sell the whole of the furniture in that room for £34, or £39, I am not sure which. I paid for it and Calcutt repaid the same amount to me. I put no sort of pressure on him to buy it and made no profit on it. I did not at any time know that Calcutt had hired carriages for the use of my canvassers at the election. I know he had none of his own which he could lend out. I recollect the witness Da Costa; I saw him on the morning of the polling day in 1904. He either told me that he was going to vote or had voted for me. I saw him in the evening at the "Three Crowns" with Calcutt and one or two others. I had no conversation with him and never promised him a "spread." I did not know that he had been going about Mile End in a carriage for me that day. I never gave 5s. to Calcutt to give to the driven of the hired carriages, telling him they were not to know where it came from. The truth about that matter is this. It was not on the polling day, but either before or since; I had been to the West End one evening and I came up to Mile End Road. I wanted to pay the cab  man. The fare was either four or five shillings, and Calcutt happen ing to be there I said, "You might pay the cabman," and I handed him those two blackened half-crowns. I said, "Don't give him those two half-crowns, but you might give him 5s. of your own money." With regard to the meeting at the "Three Crowns," the Board meet ings are on Thursday nights and there are Committees on Tuesday nights; on those occasions a good many of the Guardians would drop in after the meetings. That made no difference to the time I went home. I would sometimes come out of my office into the saloon bar and see them there, but I never heard any talking about how contracts should be dealt with. I never saw dozens and dozens of bottles of champagne being drunk and never saw Calcutt standing champagne round. On one night in March, 1906, there were several of the contractors there, and I did see several bottles of champagne, but I do not think one was paid for by Calcutt. There were several of the Guardians, and many friends of the various contractors. I left at 9.30; I do not know how long they stayed. With regard to the conversation with Calcutt and Haseltine in 1904, I deny the whole conversation, and with regard to the whitewashing of the Infirmary in 1903, when Calcutt says that he and Knight discussed their rival claims before me, that conversation never took place, and I did not say to Calcutt, "Look here, what a fool you were not to stick out; you would have got another £30."
