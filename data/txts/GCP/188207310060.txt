754. JOHN MCCLURE (30) and WILLIAM JENKINS (21), Robbery
with violence on Charles Rayment, and stealing a hat, a watch, and 128.
his property.
MR. CRANSTOUN Prosecuted; MESSRS. KEITH FRITH, and Lever Defended.
CHARLES RAYMENT. I live at 4, Vivian Road, Old Ford-I am an
omnibus conductor-on Friday, 30th July, I was at the Alexandra Park
Races— I left about 7 p.m.- I was going home, and I went into a publio-
house opposite the entrance to the Palace I saw the prisoners and others
-I remained there about 10 minutes-I had two glasses of ale-I saw
the prisoners in other houses afterwards-I went into another house at
the bottom of Hornsey Lane-I drank ale-the last house I went in was
the Junction Arms-I saw the prisoners there; I did not speak to them
-I left about 12.15–I was not drunk; I knew what was taking place
the prisoners followed me 100 yards from the house, where there is a
spring gate to a carriage drive-McClure pushed me into the gateway,
struck me with his fist and knocked me down, and took £12 out of my
right-hand trousers pocket, unbuttoned my coat, and took my watch-
this rag is the lining of my hat which I lost-Jenkins and others were
helping McClure, pnshing me and knocking me down-I was stunned-
the men ran away-I went to the Kentish Town Police-station and gave
information-I have not since seen my hat, my watch, or my money-I
identified the prisoners the following Monday-I saw Jenkins at Maryle-
bone on the Tuesday.
Cross-examined. I had been at the races since 2 o'clock-I had not been
betting-I had had a holiday-I had more than usual to drink-I treated
ope, Webster, who gave the orders for drink to the others, and I paid-
there were five or six altogether—the same thing went on at all the
publics—I had 131. in the morning, and I spent il.--that is how I fix
ihe 121.-I felt them take my watch—I had seen it about an hour before
-I was going towards Highgate Road; that was the way home I fell,
and thoy fell on me-the others must lave held my arms.
Re-examined. I saw my watch taken and a large sum of money.
CHARLES MILLER (Police Sergeant Y). On Saturday, 1st July, I received
information from Rayment, made inquiries, and on Monday, 3rd July, I
went to a public-house in the Kentish Town Road, where Rayment pointed
out McClure as one of the men who knocked him down--I said, “You
will be charged with others with stealing a watch and about 121. in
money on the morning of the Ist”-he said, “I had been with him, but
I was not there at the finish”-the next morning at 11.15 in the lobby
at the Marylebone Police-court Rayment pointed out Jenkins--I arrested
him, and told him he would be charged with McClure and others-he
said, “You have made a mistake, I was home before that time."
Cross-eramined. From 14 to 20 people were in the bar when Rayment
said, “That man standing there is known as Scotty, and is one of them,"
not "I think that is one of them."
