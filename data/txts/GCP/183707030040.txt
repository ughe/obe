Before Mr. Justice Vaughan.
1669. EDWARD WELCH was indicted for killing and slaying Joht
Goulding.
GEORGE Wilston. I am a house painter, and live in Buckeridge-street,
St. Giles's. I knew the deceased, John Goulding; he was a bricklayer,
and lodged in the same house with me. On the 30th of last September the
deceased was in the house, and the prisoner came in between eight and
nine o'clock in the evening-there were several persons in the house-the
deceased came to the fire-place to light his pipe, and the prisoner took off
his own bat and struck the deceased on the head with it-nothing had
passed between them previous to that-Goulding had a hat on-he said,
* If you do that again I will knock you down"-the prisoner directly
struck him again with his bat, and the deceased made a blow at Welch,
but it did not hit him-he then seized the tea-pot, which stood on the hob,
and threatened to throw it at the prisoner-a man named Smith, who it
belonged to, came and took it out of his hand-Welch came from behind
the stool, where he stood, and took hold of the deceased-he laid hold of
him with both hands by the collar and threw him down on the floor-
Goulding was intoxicated at the time--I had not known much of him
before-I have seen him several times- I have seen him very often tipsy
--I should think he was between forty and fifty years old-he was not a
large, powerful man, but a small man-when he fell I observed his head
come in contact with a wooden form-he never spoke or moved after he
fell—the prisoner lifted him up into a sitting position on the floor, and by
his orders some water was brought in, and he (the prisoner) bathed the
man's temples-several persons said a doctor ought to be sent for, as he
seemed in a dying state-one was sent for, but did not come-the person
is not in court who went for the doctor-the prisoner lifted the deceased
off the floor and put him on a seat-he was obliged to be held up by the
prisoner and another person-he appeared quite senseless from the moment
he fell on the floor-he could not sit without being assisted he was quite
helpless-the prisoner then said, “Some person come and hold him, and I
will go for a doctor" --somebody held him, and the prisoner went out and
never returned at all afterwards--I went to the station-house and got a
policeman, who took the deceased to a doctor's, in Oxford-street--the
