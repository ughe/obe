THOMAS MATHER re-examined . to the best of my belief the building he describes is the house I have been receiving rent for, for the last twenty years, but there are a great many houses in the same square with only one room—whether the lead came from there I cannot say.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are there in that street other places with only one room? A.  Certainly—there is no back-door to this place, either blocked up or open—there never was a back-door to that house—never at all—I have known it ever since it was built, and saw it building—I swear it never had a back-door, or I must have seen it—this place is covered with tiles—there were no slates on it to my knowledge—if the policeman says that the house from which the lead was taken had a back-door blocked up, it certainly cannot be Mr. Goles's house.
   COURT . Q.  Have you been to this house since the charge has been made against the prisoner? A.  Certainly—I went by myself—I found a hole in the roof. HENRY THOMAS MALLEY re-examined. Q.  Had the house the appear ance of having a door out behind? A.  Yes the appearance of a way out, blocked up with boards—there are boards at the back more than twice my height, and all the way up is boards—I thought there was a door-way, and laid hold of a place—I could not get to the back, it was so blocked up—it is only one room—there are no windows in it, but I think four shutten at the front part—there is one door on the right-hand side as you go out of the alley—you go past the first shop at the corner—it is the second door from the alley, on the right hand.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did you not say corner there was a back-door blocked up? A.  Yes. THOMAS MATHER. That is the house I receive the rent of; but there never was a back-door—the house is built with those boards at the back. HENRY THOMAS MALLEY.
