GEORGE BEAZLEY. I am a dry salter, and live in Spicer-street, Brick-
lane. On the 20th of Feb. Smith came to my house about four o'clock in
the afternoon-he asked if we were buyers of garblings, which are the sift-
ings of cochineal—I looked at the sample which he produced-it was not
garblings, it was granilla-I said I did not think it would snit us, but he
had better leave the sample, and call again in half an hour-he left it, and
came again - we asked what he wanted for it-he said ls. 6d. a pound, and he
had got about lcwt. of it-(the market price was about 2s. 6d. a pound) --we
agreed to give him the price he asked, in order to get the goods--he went
away, and came back in about half an hour with Maynard, who had the bag
on his back containing the granilla-he put it down, and I told them they
had better place it in the scale, and we would see the weight of it-they did
80-the mouth was open, and I saw it contained granilla the same as the
