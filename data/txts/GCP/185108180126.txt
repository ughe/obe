MUSGROVE, Mayor.
MR. PAYNE called
MARY ANN HALL. I know the prisoner; I lived in his house-- his wife
is here-I only know the prosecutor by being up in his room for a fortnight
before I went to the prisoner's ; about half-past 12 o'clock at night I was to
dressing myself, and the prosecutor came into the room and asked whetber
the serang (who is the head of the ship, and who used to come there) was in
the room-the prisoner said he was in his, the prosecutor's, room-he said
he was not; and said, "What have you been saying about my Emma!"
--that is a young woman he lived with then, but she has left him dow---the
prisoner said he had not been saying anything about Emma-he said, "Yes
you have; and although I am in your room, I am not afeard of you; I will
break your bones before I leave you"-he spoke in English; they generally
do so, unless there is anybody there who they do not want to hear--with that
he drew his stick and struck the prisoner, and struck his wife on the forehead
--the prisoner caught hold of the wooden rolling-pin which was on the man-
tel-shelf, and struck the prosecutor across his forehead with it, where he had
a scar before-the prosecutor's wife, as they call ber, came in-that'is, Emma;
and the serang came in and helped to turn him out-Emma caught hold of
the prisoner's wife by the hair, and they were all fighting-1 left the room-
Smith was not in the room at the beginning of the transaction-he was not
there when the conversation took place about Emma, nor when the prosecutor
was struck with the rolling-pin.
Cross-examined by MR. WOOLLETT. Q. Did you go before the Magistrate!
A. I was there, but was not called - I spoke to the policeman, and he said
we could give our evidence op here-I sleep in the same room with the pri-
Boner and his wife-I get my living on the streets--there is a fireplace in the
room-there is a poker, birt Mary Ann Hayes bad taken it out that day to
open a grating, and next day, about 11 o'clock, she found it in the yard-I
saw the policeman searching in the room for a poker-I did not point out the
rolling-pin to him ; it was on the floor- I did not say it was not the poker,
but the rolling-pin, because I was undressed, or just upon it-the prisoner
only occupies one room-I left the room when they began fighting-when
Emma came in, I saw her catch hold of the prisoner's wife's hair-I saw DO
poker there, I swear that all I have told to-day was said in English-I did
not tell the policeman it was the prosecutor who struck the first blow.
MARY ANN HAYES. I live with the prisoner and his wife. On 9th July,
about 3 o'clock in the afternoon, I took the poker to open a drain in the yard
to let the water run free-I forgot the poker, and left it there till next mort.
ing, when I found it where I had left it, and brought it in when I came to
the prisoner's house next morning, about half-past 6, I saw marks of blood
on the floor, and the rolling-pin was on the floor with blood on it, but not
much I washed it off-the prisoner is a peaceable man; I never heard abge
thing against him before.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you wash it off? A. This day week-
am an unfortunate girl-I slept in the prisoner's house with bis wife-Mary
Ann Hall did sleep in that room then, but not now-John Dexter is the land
lord, and the prisoner is one of the tenants- I left about half-past 11 o'clock
that night, and slept at John Dexter's lodging-house twenty or twenty-bre
yards off-I left all peaceable and quiet when I went away, and left the
rolling-pin on the mantel-piece-Hodge is not here; his ship has gone.
WILLIAM HORRELL PEARCE re-examined. I searched among the ashes,
but found no poker-I searched all over the room - there was no rolling pin
there at that time.
