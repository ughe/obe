825. JOHN ANDERSON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of Feb-
ruary, 4 gowns, value 14s. ; 1 shawl, value 28. ; 1 sheet, value 28; 1
handkerchief value 1s. ; 1 cloak, value 58.; 1 pair of shoes, value 2;
and 1 breast pin, value 28., the goods of Susanna Johnson.
SUSANNA JOHNSON. I am a widow, and live with my son, in Cheyne-
place, St. Pancras. The prisoner is my grandson-and lived with me
these articles are mine; I missed them at different times.
WILLIAM BARRANGER (police-constable E 94.) I was sent for, and
took the prisoner-I shut him up in a room, and observed him take two
duplicates of two gowns from the chimney-these are them.
JAMES TRAIL. I am in the service of Mr. Crouch, a pawnbroker.
have a cloak, a gown, and three pairs of shoes, pawned in the name of John
Anderson by a boy, but I do not think the prisoner was the boy-he was
a head taller than the prisoner-he stated that it was for his mother-
duplicates correspond with these.
EDMUND Brown. I am in the service of Mr. George Lowther, a pawi.
broker of Tottenham-court-road. I have a gown, a handkerchief, and
pin, pledged in the name of John Anderson, for his mother-I know the
prisoner by sight, but I cannot say whether he pawned these-his mother
has used our shop for some years.
HORACE PeckOVER. I am in the service of a pawnbroker. I have i
sheet and shawl pawned in the name of John Anderson by the prisoner,
believe; but an older one was with him-he stated he brought them for
his mother--we are not in the habit of taking things from children, but I
asked if he brought it from his mother-he said, “Yes."
