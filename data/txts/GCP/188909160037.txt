THOMAS BANNISTER (Police Inspector 8). On 28th July I was called to
Evison Road, about three-I saw blood on the pavement-I afterwards
went to St. Mary's Hospital, and there saw the prisoner; he was in a
state of semi-unconsciousness-on 8th August I again went there, and
saw him-he was then in a condition to understand what I said I told
him he would be removed next day, and charged with attempting to
murder his wife and her mother, and attempted suicide - he said nothing
referring to the charge; he simply asked if he could take some clean
linen with him-he was afterwards brought to the Court, and charged
next day the charge was read over to him; he made no reply-I searched
his room at 26, Evison Road, on the Sunday, the day of the occurrence
-I there found the paper marked A; the papers B and were found by
the sergeant.
EDGAR CULLAM (Police Sergeant). On 28th July I searched the
prisoner's room, and in the table drawer I found the letters B and C,
also some cartridges in a hat-box.
The letter marked A was put in and read it was dated 22nd July, 1889,
addressed to the Editor of the " Kilburn Times," and proposed to give a history
of his married life, which he described as an undoubted failure, and ho complained
of the moanness of his father-in-law and the interference and unkindness of the
mother, which caused unhappiness and disagreement between his wife and him-
self, and led to her taking proceedings for a judicial separation. The letter B
was dated July 23rd, addressed to his uncle, stating that his life had been moreoked
by a wicked man and woman. C was without date; at gave the address of his
father and of his wife, adding," May God forgive them for their
unkindness."
