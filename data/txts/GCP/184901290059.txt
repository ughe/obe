SAMUEL TAYLOR, I am an instrument-maker. On 19th Dec, I had an
accordion to sell - I lost it, and believe this to be it.
JAMES BATCOCK. I live at Queen-street, Chelsea. I left this accordion
with Taylor to sell--it has my mark on it.
CHARLES RICHARDSON. I bought this accordion of William Fenton, for
28., on the day it was lost--the other prisoner was with him, and said, "You
may as well buy it," and he took the money.
William Penton. You knew it was stolen ; it was a boy named Barrett
thieved it. Witness. There was a boy with you.
· TIMOTHY WELLS (policeman, V 83). I took the prisoners--they said they
knew nothing about it-afterwards they said, " Are not you going to take
the others?"
JOSEPH Forster. I saw the prisoners and two others playing this accordion.
William Fenton's Defence. I did not sell it, or have the money : I saw it sold.
Thomas GLYNN (policeman, V 91). I produce a certificate of William
Fenton's conviction (read— Convicted Feb., 1848, confined six months)-1
was present-he is the person.
WILLIAM FENTON-GUILTY. Aged 19.-Transported for Seven Years.
ROBERT JOHN FENTON-GUILTY. Aged 15.-Confined Three Months.
629. EMMA SCOTT and MARY BEANEY, stealing 1 boot, value 78.5
the goods of William Daniels.
WILLIAM DANIELS. I am bootmaker of Water-lane. Blackfriars. I
missed this boot (produced)-it is new.
WILLIAM KNIGHT. I am waiter at the Glazier's Arms. I was at my
door, and saw Scott take a boot and walk away, arm-in-arm, with Beaney.
with it under her shawl-I told Mr. Daniels-we pursued and overtook the
prisoners in Printing House-square-Seott had the boot on her.
SCOTT-GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Three Months.
BEANEY-GUILTY. Aged 16.
(There was another indictment against the prisoners.)
530. HENRY JOHN DONNELLY and FREDERICK STANTON,
stealing 1 wheelbarrow, value 108.; the goods of Harriet Smith; Donnelly
having been before convicted.
John SMITH. I live with my mother, Harriet Smith-we let out wheel-
barrows. On 18th Jan. I let out this barrow to Donnelly-he never
brought it back.
WILLIAM REARDON. On 18th Jan., about three o'clock, the prisoners
brought me this barrow, and asked if I would buy it-they asked 9s, for it-
I offered 58.- they would not take it, and left-Stanton came back, and
asked 6s.--I said, "No"-he went back to Donnelly, and then came back,
and let me have it-I knew him before, and knowing that he was hard set
I bought it.
JOSEPH HANSON (policeman). I took Donnelly at Chelsea-workhouse,
and told him the charge-he said he borrowed it to fetch some linen for
Miss Williams, of 22, Lower Store-street-I said I must see her he said.
"It is no use going, she don't live there now"-I said, "Which way did you
go with it?"-be said, “By the Edgeware-road, and while we were in the
public-house having a pint of beer the barrow was stolen"-I afterwards took
Stanton-he said the barrow was stolen from them in a public-house in the
Edgeware-road-he bad not heard Donnelly tell me so.
