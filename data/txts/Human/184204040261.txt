On Friday evening, the 10th of March, about five minutes past ten o'clock, the prosecutor came up to the railway to go down by the train, but he was late, and the gates were shut—Dust was on the stand—he asked the prosecutor where he wanted to go, that he would take him as cheap as anybody—they made an agreement, and the prosecutor said, "Come over to the public-house, and we will have some  thing to drink, I have got a friend or two there"—they went in, and I stop ped by the cab-door  —in about a quarter of an hour I saw the women come out of the public-house with the prosecutor—one of them wanted to get into the cab  —I said, "No, no, you had better not"—the prosecutor said, "Let her come in, she is a friend of mine"—I would not let her in, but kept my hand on the door—I did not think them proper persons to get in with him—he was intoxicated—I never saw any of them before.
   Q.  How should you know they did not belong to him? A.  Why, I am a pretty good judge of that sort—I am an old hackney-coachman, and have seen a good many transactions—I expected they were women of the town—I swear I saw them come out of the public-house with him—I think it was Fraser wanted to get into the cab  —the women went away, and I asked the prosecutor if he had got a penny for me.

 1415. CHARLES GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of February , 1 pencil-case, value 4 s  ., the goods of Jabez Newsome . JABEZ NEWSOME. I am a greengrocer, and live in Dover-place West, Kent-road. The prisoner occasionally lodged with me—sometime ago a customer of mine left a pencil-case in my shop, which I put on my sitting-room mantel-piece—I missed it—I said nothing to the prisoner about it—I never gave it to him, or authorized him to take it—this now produced is it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Whose pencil-case was this? A.  It belonged to a gentleman who left it at my shop—I do not know his name—I have some idea who he is, but am not positive—I think it is Mr. John Hearn's, who came to me on business—he is the only person I can suspect of having brought it—several others had been in the shop the same evening—he lives at No. 19, Trinity-street—I did not write to ask him whether he had left it—I did not think of that—I thought the next time he called I might show it to him—I missed it on the 11th of February, about three days after I had found it, which was about three months ago—as it was missing, it was no use applying to Mr. Hearn—it was found in Feb ruary—I never sent to him, because it was missing, and if I had it not to produce how could I ask him if it was his?—he has called at my house many times since—I did not tell him I had found a pencil-case, which I thought was his, because it was missing, and I could not produce it—I have never told him up to this moment—I gave the prisoner into custody last Thursday night—I have not seen Mr. Hearn since the pencil-case has been recovered—a letter would reach him certainly—I gave the prisoner in charge first, on suspicion of stealing a coat and umbrella—the coat was the first charge—the coat alone, without the umbrella—he was discharged
