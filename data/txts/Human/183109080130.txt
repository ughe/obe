 1821.  SARAH BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of August , 1 sheet, value 7s., the goods of Isaac Williams.
   ISAAC WILLIAMS. I keep a lodging-house in French Horn-yard. On the 29th of August the prisoner came to lodge there, but I did not see her - I went to the room when she was gone, and missed a sheet.
 Prisoner. I left at half-past eleven o'clock, leaving the man I had been with to sleep there, and next morning he gave me the sheet to pawn. Witness. There might be a man with her; we let rooms for an hour.
   WILLIAM BURD. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. -This sheet was pawned by a woman, who I do not know.
   JAMES FORD. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner, and found the duplicate on her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1822.  HENRY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , 4 frocks, value 4s.; 5 aprons, value 3s.; 2 pillow-cases, value 1s.; 3 shirts, value 2s.; 4 handkerchiefs, value 1s. 6d.; 1 cap, value 1s.; 1 collar, value 6d.; 1 table-cloth, value 1s., and 4 window-blinds, value 6d., the goods of James Grant.
   ELIZABETH GRANT. I am the wife of James Grant; we live in King-street, Camden-town. On the night of the 7th of July this linen was in the back garden, and at half-past one o'clock in the morning the Policeman alarmed me.
   JAMES CARTER. I am a Policeman. About one o'clock in the morning I was on duty in King-street, and saw a bundle thrown over the wall; I saw the prisoner getting over the wall - I asked what he was about; he said he intended to have slept there - I took him in charge, and saw him drop this frock; I got over the wall - nobody was there.
   WILLIAM LODWICK. I am a Policeman. I took up the bundle, which contained some of the things - this handkerchief was found round the prisoner's body, with other things, and another article in his hat.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had had no food all day; the things laid under the wall, and I took them up.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1823.  SARAH BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , 1 watch, value 20s., the goods of William Barber , from his person.
 WILLIAM BARBER. I live at Chelsea. I was in a public-house in the Haymarket, about two o'clock in the morning of Sunday, the 27th of June, and went to  Whitcombe-street with the prisoner, whom I was talking to for some time; she wished me to go home with her - I refused- she turned round, and said, "Here comes the Policeman;" I turned to look at him, and she ran away - I immediately missed my watch; I followed, and lost sight of her, but saw her in custody the same day.
   WILLIAM ABBOTT CREWS. I am a Policeman. On the 27th of June, about half-past two o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner going down Parliament-street with a soldier, who was having words with her; she had some thing in her hand - the soldier said, "You have something there, I will give you in charge;" I walked over, and saw her put her hand to her bosom - she turned a corner, and began to run; I followed her, and put my hand on her shoulder - she then took this watch from her bosom, and threw it away; I asked what it was - she said she had no watch; I took it up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to a public-house with him; he was intoxicated - he went to a room with me, and gave me the watch to keep till Monday; we then came out, and he said I had robbed him.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1824.  JOHN BURTON was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of July , 1 pocket-book, value 10d.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s., and 1 glove, value 6d., the goods of John David Barnes , and 1 handkerchief, value 2s. 6d., the goods of John Cross; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   JOHN DAVID BARNES. I lived in Church-street, Marylebone. I went to a skittle-ground in Bridge-row, Bishopsfields, on the 7th of July; the prisoner was setting up the skittles - I took off my coat, and hung it on the rails, and on going to it missed my pocket-book, handkerchief, and gloves; I went and took him - he said he had got them, and gave them to me.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. What time did you go there? A. About twelve o'clock; I missed the property about half-past four - there were five or six people there; there was a bustle there, but my coat was not thrown about - I found it where I hung it; the property could not fall out - the prisoner had left the ground, and as a man lost his handkerchief, I looked and missed my things, and went after him; I was sober.
   JOHN CROSS. I was in the skittle-ground. My handkerchief was taken from my hat, on the bench; Barnes stopped the prisoner in a garden, and my handkerchief was found at his feet.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where is the garden? A. About twenty yards from the skittle-ground; we were not tipsy- the prisoner offered to fight me; I did not see any other scuffling - my handkerchief was safe after the scuffle; my coat was thrown about.
