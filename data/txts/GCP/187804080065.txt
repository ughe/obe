CHARLES HENRY GROVE. I am assistant to Grant and Gask, of Oxford
Street-on 5th February Wright came there and wanted to look at a skirti
which a lady had seen a few days previously--we had not got it then, and
I showed him some others-he selected a black silk costume at six and a
half guineas, and asked for it to be sent as he had not sufficient to pay for
it-he gave his address “George Lewis, solicitor, 4, Camden Avenue,
Peckham," and then said "If you can change a cheque I will pay you
for it now," producing this cheque : (Read: “Feb. 4, 1878. Birkbeok
Bank. Pay Mr. Smith or bearer 161. 108. Thos. Pitman." Endorsed
"Wm. Smith, Geo. Lewis.") I took it to the manager and came back
and said that we would change it-he turned it over, pointed to H Geo.
Lewis," and said “That is my name'- I took the six and a half guineas
out of it and gave him 91. 138. 6d. in cash and this receipted invoice he
said that he would call for the dress in half an hour a boy was with him
who came back in about half an hour, and I gave it to him I parted
with the dress and the cash, believing him to be George Lewis and a
solicitor-I thought the cheque would be honoured. TU SON Y
Cross-examined by Mr. Glyn. I went to Wright's house on another day
and detained him till we got a warrant-he and I had dinner together at
Grant and Gask's I found him in Southwark Bridge Road and waited till
he came out-our firm found out a few days afterwards that his name was
not Lewis-he said that a lady had seen a dress trimmed with white,
which we never had, and then he said “Let me see some dresses."
Cross-examined by MR. PURCELL. The dress was afterwards pawned; I.
do not know in what name.
