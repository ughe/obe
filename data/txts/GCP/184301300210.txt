814. FREDERICK CHARLES BURNHAM BLACKWELL, alias
Frederick Burnham Blackwell, was indicted for feloniously forging and utter-
ing, at Camberwell, on the 30th of Dec., a certain deed, purporting to be an
indenture, made in the year 1840, between one Richard Constable, Eliza-
beth Hollis Constable, and Joseph Stanley, with intent to defraud the said
Elizabeth Hollis Constable.-4 other Counts, varying the manner of laying
the charge.-2 other Counts, stating his intent to be to defraud Eliza
Watts.--2 other Counts, stating his intent to be to defraud Richard Con-
stable.-6 other Counts, for forging and uttering a certain written instru-
ment, purporting to be a deed, with the like intents.
Messrs. CLARKSON and BODkin conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD Bircu. I am a solicitor ; until lately I was in partnership with
Mr. Sutcliffe; we carried on business in New Bridge-street, Blackfriars-we
were professionally concerned for the late Richard Constable-I believe our
engagements with him commenced in May, 1838; he was at that time residing
in Manor-street, Kennington-be had a life-interest in a place called Mount
Pleasant, at Lewisham, at that time-I prepared his will - I cannot say when
-he died on the 31st of December, 1840--this is the probate of the will-
(looking at it)-after the execution of his will I prepared a deed of settlement,
by way of conveyance of his property; that must have been about Feb., 1839–
-I prepared the deeds on behalf of Mr. Constable—these are the deeds- I pre-
pared this deed of the 1st Feb., 1839, and here is another of the 4th and 5th-
they were prepared at the same time- attended the execution of them-
all these instruments assume to dispose of his real property-Mr. Constable
was introduced to us as a client, by a Mr. Joseph Stanley-I do not know when
Stanley died-from time to time, during Mr. Constable's lifetime, Stanley
saw me on the part of Mr. Constable, about his affairs-Mr. Constable was
very old and infirm-I have no doubt that Stanley came to me in July, 1840
-he came, I think, in Aug., 1840, and gave me instructions about preparing
a deed-I prepared the deed, in pursuance of written directions, which I
believed were given by Mr. Constable- I had a letter, which I believed, at
that time, to have been written by Mr. Constable--that letter was handed,
with all Mr. Constable's other papers, to Messrs. Hale, Boys, and Austen,
who succeeded us in the business-I am not attesting witness to all these
instruments, only to these two of the 1st of Feb., and 4th and 5th of Feb.-
I think I am'acquainted with the handwriting of Mr. Constable-I have seen
him write, and have had frequent letters from him I also attested his will
in January, 1839, and a mortgage-deed, in 1838, the transfer of a mortgage
-- this is the letter wbich Stanley brought to me in August, as from Mr.
Constable (marked A)-in consequence of that letter I prepared the draft of
a deed--Stanley brought me back the draft-it remained in my office till
Dec.- I got it engrossed by Stanley's direction-this is the deed--(looking
at the deed charged to be forged)-it was engrossed on the 30th of Dec.,
1840, and handed to Stanley on the 31st-I am quite sure of that-
I cannot form any idea as to what time of the day Stanley had the deed
from us-it was the last day in the year, and we closed our office
rather earlier than usual that evening, but I cannot remember the time-
our office opened at ten, or a quarter to ten o'clock, and closed, I should
think, at four-at the time it was given to Stanley, it was not exe-
cuted by any body-it bad just come from the stationer's- I cannot re-
member how soon I saw the deed again-it must have been a few days-it
was brought to me by Stanley, and I rather think Stanley signed the deed
in my presence, but I am not sure when he brought it, it was executed as
