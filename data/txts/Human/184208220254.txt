No—I was never up in the room—I have heard that you ill-treated her very much before I went there—I never saw or heard you ill-use her—I never heard any quarrelling. THOMAS OAK MITCHELL. I am a surgeon, and live at Greenwich. I knew the deceased before this occurred—I was called to see her after her death—the prisoner applied to me to give a certificate of the cause of death—I had attended the woman twelve months prior, for a rheumatic affection—at the time the prisoner called on me he stated that she had been in a very infirm state of health a long time—I left the room, to consult a medical report book—on my return, I found Mrs. Vincent there, and in consequence of hearing the word "ill-treatment" made use of, I refused the certificate—I afterwards, by the order of the Coroner, examined the body externally—it presented appearances of bruises, extending from the left shoulder, over the left side, nearly to the pelvis, and also marks of bruises on the right and left arm—four ribs were fractured, two of which were found penetrating a portion of the left lung, which, in my judgment, was the immediate cause of death—the most prominent symptom was the difficulty of breathing—a blow or a kick would most probably produce such an injury—it must have been very violent—I should say she had been suffering from the injury three or four days.
   Prisoner . She had been under him once before with a very bad cough, and was ailing; she took liquor, and has fallen down stairs; it is not the first time I have taken her up stairs, bruised, and complaining of her side. Witness . I never attended her before with any complaint of that description—she was subject to rheumatic affection in the winter—the body was in other respects perfectly healthy—the fracture of the ribs was the cause of death, by producing inflammation of the lungs—I doubt whether, if it had been seen to in the first instance, that life could have been saved, from the position in which the ribs were, and the lung being lacerated—it might have given her a chance. JOSEPH BERRIDGE. I am a surgeon. I assisted Mr. Mitchell in the post mortem  examination—he has correctly stated the appearances of the body—I agree with him entirely in his opinion as to the cause of death. JEREMIAH COOLAN.
