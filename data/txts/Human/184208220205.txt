JOSEPH BURGOYNE. I am assistant to Richard Thornton Perkins, a pawnbroker, at Chelsea. On the 6th of August, about five minutes be fore one o'clock, I saw the prisoner at the counter—I asked what she wanted—she said, Nothing, she was only standing out of the rain—I said, "Very well, we don't charge anything for that"—I walked to the end of the shop—I returned, and missed her, and the shawl which had been hanging about three yards from the door, inside the shop—it had done raining when I first spoke to her—she was only in the shop two minutes after I spoke to her—I went after her—she went into a public-house eight or ten doors off—I followed her—she turned rather pale—I said to the bar-man, "Will you detain this woman a minute?"—she ran out of the other door—I saw the shawl concealed between herself and her child—she ran round Sloane-square—this is the shawl. CHARLES WRIGHT. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner, and took the shawl from between her person and the child—she said what a fool the was that she did not give it up.
   Prisoner's Defence . I went into the shop, and the shawl was down by the side of me; a woman came in, and I thought she might have dropped it; I took it, and walked out with it; I was very much in distress at the time. GEORGE BEST. I am a policeman. I produce a certificate of the pri sonar's former conviction, which I got from the office of the clerk of the peace at Westminster—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Four Months.
 2493. RICHARD SAUNDERS was indicted for embezzlement. EDWARD BAXTER. I am a grocer, in Cromer-street—the prisoner was my porter, to take out things, and receive-money—on Friday the 22nd of July, I sent him with some goods to Mr. Howard, of Little Windmill-street—the bill was 2 l . 14 s . 6 3/4 d .—I sent it with them, and he was to re ceive the money—he ought to have accounted to me for it when he came back, but he never did—I spoke to him about it on the Saturday week fol lowing, (he never said anything to me in the interim)—he then said he was very sorry, and he would make it up—since he has been committed, his father has paid a portion of the money, which I understand I should not have taken—before his father called, I did not treat it as a debt—it was nine days before the father called on me.
   Prisoner. Q.  Have you not received twice 5 s .? A.  Yes, I have—this is my handwriting. EDWARD HOWARD. I live in Windmill-street, Golden-square. On the. 22nd of July the prisoner brought me some goods from the prosecutor, and this bill with them—I paid him 2 l . 13 s . on his master's account. JOHN MURRAY. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner—he said, "He promised to make it all square with Mr. Baxter." GUILTY  . Aged 23.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined Four Months.
 2494. SAMUEL TAYLOR was indicted for bigamy. GEORGE YARROW. I am parish-clerk of St. Leonard, Shoreditch. I produce the register of marriages at that church—I find an entry of a mar riage—( reads)—"  Samuel Taylor, of this parish, bachelor, and Mary Ann Weaver, of this parish, married by banns, this 5th of July, 1835, by me, Thomas Williams"—I was the attesting witness, but I cannot say who were the parties.
