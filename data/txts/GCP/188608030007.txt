Tenth Session, 1885–86.
315
Street, Clerkenwell-on 28th June, about 11.30 p.m., Hartley and Neill
came in-Hartley called for half-a-pint of ale and two of port wine, which
came to 31d., and handed me a bad shilling-I bent it with my teeth
and told him it was bad and I could not take it-he asked me to give it back
to him and he would give me a good one, and said he got it at the Bell-I
gave it back to him and he paid me with a good florin-Neal went out at
the door twice, and while he was away I asked Hartley why he gave me
the bad shilling-he said that the other young man Neill had given it to
him-after Neill came back the second time, and while they were being
searched, Gregory came into the opposite compartment and then into the
one next to them, and put his hand up and made signs to Neill, and I
heard Neill ask Gregory to bring him some breakfast in the morning-
the police took Hartley and Neill-Gregory went out, but the police
brought him back and I recognised him—this (produced ) is the shilling,
I know it by the way I bent it and by my teeth marks.
ALEXANDER TUPPER. I keep the Prince of Wales public-house I saw
my wife serve Hartley and Neill --she spoke to me and I watched them
and saw Neill go to the door twice-he went half-way out the first time
and passed a little paper parcel to Gregory, who was standing outside-I
asked Hartley and Neal what they meant by trying to pass bad money-
Neill had got it between his teeth and said, “It is a good one," and
asked me what I meant-I got over the counter, shut the door, and sent
for the police-I saw Gregory inside the house while they were being
searched, after Neill had gone to the door a second time-he did not go
into the same compartment as Neill and Hartley-I got a constable and
gave them in charge--Hartley had been a customer there for two or
three weeks.
Cross-examined by Hartloy. I never saw you pass any bad coin before.
Re-examined. I have never seen Neill or Gregory in the house before,
and never saw Hartley with them.
EDWARD MOYES (Poliooman G 169). I was called and Hartley and
Neill were given into my custody-I found on him a bad shilling, a
shilling, & sixpence, threepence, and some tissue paper, not wrapped
round anything-Hartley said, “Neill gave me the shilling to pass, and
he said he would stand two drinks if I could pass it"-Neill made no
answer; he refused to be searched, but another constable came, and then
he allowed us to search him-while doing so I saw Gregory in another
compartment making motions to Neill with his hand-Hartley said at
the station that it was all & hoax.
Cross-examined by Hartley. I found on you a bad shilling, two six-
pences, and bd.
SAMUEL PULLEN (Policoman G 100). I was on duty near Mr. Tupper's
house, and after Hartley and Neill were taken in custody and were
being taken away in the street I saw Gregory speak to Neill—I asked
Gregory what he was doing there-he said, "It is only an old pal of
mine; I am going to bring him some breakfast in the morning"-I took
Gregory to Mr. Tupper's house, who identified him as the man to whom
Neill had handed a parcel-he said he knew nothing about it-I found
two good florins on Neill and some bronze; he said that he knew
nothing whatever about it.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. This shilling and florin are bad-abad
coin put into a fire would melt and run through at once.
