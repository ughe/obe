 1948.  THOMAS KNIGHT was indicted for stealing' on the 11th of September , 2 horse-shoes, value 1s.; 1 curry-comb, value 6d., and 1 brush, value 2s., the goods of Joseph Palmer, his master.
   JOSEPH PALMER. I am a farrier- the prisoner worked occasionally for me. On the 11th of September I had information, and followed him - this curry-comb and brush were buttoned up in his jacket.
   WILLIAM MALINGS. I work for the prosecutor. I went into his shop on the Sunday, and found the prisoner with a pair of horse-shoes buttoned up in his waistcoat - I said, "What are you going to do with them?" he said, "I don't know" - he had no business in the shop that day.
   CHARLES McCARTHY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - I found on him this property, and some horsenails.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not take the shoes out of the shop.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Three Months.
 1949.  JOSEPH MAWSON was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , 1 sieve, value 1s. 6d., and 3 pecks of apples, value 6s., the goods of William Smith and another.
   HENRY COLLINS. Mr. Smith keeps a shop in Coventgarden. On the 13th of September he sent me out, and I saw the prisoner going away with a half-sieve of apples - I told my master, who called to him; he said he had left another man to pay for them; he had got about fifty yards off - he had not asked the price.
   THOMAS POWELL. I am an officer of the market. I took the prisoner.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I have one partner. Collins gave an alarm, and I missed a sieve of apples; I saw the prisoner sitting on the sieve under the Piazzas - I called to him; he said he had left another man to pay for them - I was about to give charge of him, when a man stepped up and said, "Here is half-a-crown in part payment;" they had not been off the porter's head two minutes - they were not unpacked, nor fit for sale.
 Prisoner. I had dealt with Mr. Essex, Mr. Smith's partner, for them.
     GUILTY. Aged 37. - Confined Three Months.
 1950.  ELIZABETH MOSELEY was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of August , 1 table-cloth, value 9d.; 1 sheet, value 2s.; 2 pillow-cases, value 6d.; 1 flat-iron, value 4d.; 1 blanket, value 9d.; 1 bolster, value 2s., and 1 sheet, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Rebecca Robinson.
   REBECCA ROBINSON. I am a widow, and live in Palmer-street, Shadwell. On the 18th of June I let the prisoner my back-parlour, furnished, for 4s. a week - she paid one week; I go out nursing, and had been out two months; I returned on the 22nd of August, went to her room with an officer, and missed all the furniture but the bed.
   WILLIAM SCOTT. I am an officer. I went with the prosecutrix, and took the prisoner; she said I should find the duplicates in Philip-street - I did so.
   DANIEL KENNEDY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, two pillow-cases, and two other articles; three of them pawned in the name of Chant - I cannot say by whom.
   JOSEPH MICKLEFIELD. I am a pawnbroker. I have an iron, a sheet, a bolster, and a blanket - these are the duplicates of them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. My husband came home from sea - the prosecutor came into the room; he said he was sorry he could not get any half-pay for me, as it was so short a voyage; she told him not to make himself uneasy about that, as I might have anything I wanted - I pawned some things, but I got work, and redeemed some again.
 REBECCA ROBINSON. I told her husband not to make himself uneasy about the rent; but I never allowed her to pawn the things. GUILTY. Aged 20.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Four Months.
 1951.  EDWARD PAYNE was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 1 kettle, value 2s., the goods of James Davey.
 JAMES DAVEY. I live in  Whitecross-street. On the 17th of March, I had this kettle to repair; it was taken from my celler-window.
   EDWARD HANDS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on the 22nd of March; the prosecutor said he had lost a tea-kettle, and found the person who had bought it. I locked the prisoner up, and the next day he made his escape - I did not catch him till Monday week last.
   BRIDGET HOWLEY. I bought this tea-kettle of the prisoner for 1s. - the prosecutor came, and I said I had bought it a good bargain of his brother, which I took the prisoner to be.
 Prisoner. Q.Were there not two more lads with em? A. There were two outside.
   PATRICK CORCORAN. I am an officer. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was returning from the doctor's, and saw two lads with the kettle; they asked if I could tell them where to sell it; I went to the witness; some person there said I had stolen it; she said, "It don't much matter if it don't leak; I will give you 1s. for it."
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
