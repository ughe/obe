THOMAS WINDSOR ALLEN. I am a pawnbroker. On the evening of
the 11th of Feb., about seven o'clock, Emery pawned this watch with me
for 89., in the name of Peach-there was a female with her, whom I do
not remember.
James Bisden (police-constable L 133.) I took Carter on the 11th of
Feb. for stealing this watch, in Lowman's-pond.
Joseph HODGES (City police-constable, No. 31.) I took Britton on that
Tuesday evening, about eight o'clock-he had this tobacco-box in his hand
-I let him carry it some distance-I then took it, and asked what was
in it-he said, "Only a few buttons"--I found in it the duplicate of this
watch.
Carter's Defence. Morgan brought the watch into my place, and asked
me to buy it; a young man went out for some errand, and Morgan
showed it to him, and asked him to buy it; Morgan offered it to me for
10s.; I said I had not got the money, but I would go and pawn it for him ;
he said, “It won't do to go and pawn it, we shall get found out; we made
it last night of a bug ;" I said, " What do you call a bug" he said, " That
is what we call a drunken man, in our way;" he said before the Magi-
strate that he stole it out of a lark; when he said that in the room I
dropped the watch, and walked out to see for a policeman ; I went back,
went out again, and he gave me in charge for stealing it; I never saw
it after he took it out of my hand; I don't believe either of them know
the title of an oath.
GEORGE MORGAN re-examined. I know what an oath is; if I do not
tell the truth I shall not go to heaven; I did not want to sell the watch;
I did not steal it; my brother gave it to me.
Britton's Defence. I never put a finger on the watch; I picked up the
duplicate.

781. ROBERT HOSIER was indicted for stealing 1 pump, value
151., the goods of Daniel Watney, in a barge on the navigable river
Thames, and that he had been before convicted of felony, in June, 1844.
MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES HEATH SAUNDERS. I am a millwright, in the service of
Daniel Watney, who keeps the Wandsworth Distillery. He has what
called a wash-barge-there was a pump on board of it, used for pumping
wash into butts-it was worth 15l.
John WILLIAMS. I am a lighterman, in the service of Mr. Daniel
Watney. He had a barge in which there was a copper pump-1 saw the
barge safe on Sunday evening, the 2nd of Feb., moored off Mr. Watney!
premises, on the Thames, at Wandsworth - I saw the pump in it then!
