 972. MARY ALLEN was indicted for uttering counterfeit coin. JOHN WILLIAM WORLEY. I am servant to Mr. Stannard, who keeps the York Hotel, in Covent-garden. On the 18th of Feb., I saw the prisoner—she gave me a counterfeit crown-piece—I did not discover whether it was bad or not—I immediately gave it to Mr. Stannard—on the 22nd, she came again, and gave me a counterfeit crown—I gave it to Mr. Stannard—on the 28th, she came again, and threw down a crown-piece on the counter—Mr. Stannard took it up, looked at it, and told her it was bad, and that she had brought two before—she repeated the word "bad?" five times over—Mr. Stannard then asked where she brought it from—she said from across the Park, and then said a gentleman in the Strand gave it her. WILLIAM STANNARD. I received a crown-piece on the 18th, 22nd, and 28th of Feb.—the two first I received from Worley—the last I took from the prisoner myself—I did not discover that the first was bad till I had paid it away, and it was brought back to me after I had received the second one—I then compared them, and found they were precisely the same—I wrapped them up in paper, and put them in a small drawer—I gave all three to Walter. CHARLES WALTER( police-constable F  138.) On the 28th of Feb., 1 re ceived a crown-piece from Mr. Stannard—I marked it, and then he gave me these other two. MR. JOHN FIELD. These are all counterfeit, and I believe were all cast in one mould. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Six Months .
 973. JOHN GROVES was indicted for uttering counterfeit coin. JOHN M'CORMICK. I live on Ludgate-hill. About one o'clock on the 2nd of Feb., the prisoner came to my shop and tendered me a good half-crown—I gave him a pencil he had asked for, and two shillings in change—without looking at the pencil, he said that was not the sort he wanted, and described one that I never saw—I said I had not one, and he returned me the pencil, and two shillings—I saw that one of them was a bad one—he ran out but the pavement being slippery he fell down—I overtook and brought him back—he struggled violently to get away—I had one shilling in each hand—he strained both the backs of my hands, and I saw he swallowed something—my wife came to assist me, and he bit the back of her hand—I gave the bad shilling which the prisoner returned to me to the officer—I swear it was not one of those I gave him, as I had my money spread out on a green cloth under the sky-light, as I was going to send for the beer for dinner, and I can swear in the most positive manner that all the silver I had was good—I had four shillings, and one half-crown.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you inspect them at the time you gave them to me? A.  I did not—I called after you as you were going out—my shop is long—you might be six yards from the door when I served you—I called instantly after you—I think you heard me, and the attempt you made to go out proved it—you endeavoured to snatch the two shillings from my hands—I returned you the half-crown.
   COURT . Q.  Do you think he swallowed the half-crown? A.  No—I hink he swallowed the good shilling I gave him.
