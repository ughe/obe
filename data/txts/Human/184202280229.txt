JAMES SHOWELL. I am in the service of Mr. Spink, of Gracechnrch-street, a pawnbroker. I took in this pearl ring on the 18th of October, 1841, and advanced 5 s . on it—this is the duplicate I gave with it—to the best of my belief the prisoner is the man who pawned it—I could not swear positively to him, from the lapse of time, and not having seen the person before. ALICE WOODLEY. I live with my aunt, at No. 9, Newton-terrace, Ken mington-green. The prisoner took two rooms in my aunt's house in Oc tober last—he left about the 3rd of December—he gave a notice of his intention to leave, but he went off without our knowing it—in conse quence of some suspicion, I examined a pocket-book that was in his bed room, I think about a fortnight before he left, I cannot be sure—I took a copy of some duplicates that were in it—this is a copy of one of them—( reads )—"D. E. Spink, 2, Gracechurch-street, 18th of October, 1841; I ring, 5 s . Williams, Kennington-green."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was his property left in your house? A.  No; he took it with him—I do not know whose house he went to when he left us—I did not know that he went to Mrs. French's—he went to Mrs. French's for fourteen or fifteen days while he was living with us, and it was after he came back from there, that he left our house—he owed us 2 l . 16 s . for rent when he left, and for some paint—his rent was 12 s . a-week—fifteen days of the last month he was at Mrs. French's—the former month's rent was paid—he took it by the month, but to pay Weekly—he came in on the 6th of October—he did not pay up to the 9th of November—there were about 4 s . left, I think—at the end of the first month her removed into another room—I was once charged falsely with pawning other people's property, but I did not do it—the Magistrate convicted me—I do not know of any particular visit paid by Miss French to the prisoner—she was there very often—she used to come sometimes with the family—I do not know whether she was oftener there without her mother than with her—she has been there without her mother. CHARLES LANGLEY. I am uncle-in-law to Alice Woodley. I remem ber the day on which she examined the duplicates, I examined them—this is the paper which contains a copy of them—she wrote it by my direc tion—I saw her do it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.
