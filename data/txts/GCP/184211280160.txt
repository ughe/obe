WILLIAM ACKLAND. I live in Phoenix-street, Somers-town--my wife
keeps a haberdasher's shop-up to the 23rd of Nov. I had been living a
assistant to Mr. Foxwell, a haberdasher, in Judd-street--the prisoner used to
come there to sell artificial flowers-about a month before he was taken be
offered me this piece of ribbon for sale--I purchased it of him--he told me
he took it in exchange for flowers of his brother, who lived in Chiswell-street
-I said I did not want it-he said he would sell it to me cheap-I asked the
price of it-he said 6d. or 7d.- I cannot say which-I ultimately bought it
at 5d. a-yard- there were sixteen yards, for which I paid bim 6s. 8d.it was
the same as it is now, unblocked, and the wrong side ontwards-I afterwards
offered the same ribbon for sale to Mr. Lawrence.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean you were unable to judge of the value!
A. Now I have bought ribbons at warehouses for 4d. a-yard, and they were
worth 18.-I did not entertain any suspicion-I have been dismissed from my
situation in consequence of this.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you ever raise a word about the prisoner getting
it from his brother-in-law till this time, or is it since you have been dis-
charged that you have thought of that? A. No-I gave the same deposi
tion before the Magistrate when we buy ribbons at 4d., which cost 9d., it
is what we call job goods--this is not a new style of ribbon.
JOHN LAWRENCE. I live in Chapel-street, Somers-town. On Wednes
day, the 16th of Nov., Ackland came to me and brought this ribbon-he left it
for 98. 6.- I went to Mr. Gouger's warehouse, and saw the same pattern-
I said I had got half a piece, and gave it up.

the trial, to get parties who saw me take the ribbon in exchange for flowers.
