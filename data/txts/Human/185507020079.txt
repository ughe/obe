I live at No. 2, Lansdown-place, Black Horse-yard—in April, I was out of work—on the evening of Friday, 27th April, I was down Black Horse-yard, between 5 and 6 o'clock; the last witness was there—we went up to Chapel-place for the keys—we came back, and opened the stable door—I said, "What a smell there is!"—we began to look about in the stable to see if there was anything that did smell—we went up in the loft, and saw a lot of bags; pockets of hops—they were like these—they were marked, "C. & F. Leny, Wateringbury, Kent"—we came down—Smith began to clean the harness—I was standing in the yard—Charles Stafford came down the yard, and Smith said to him, "Charles, there was such a smell when I opened the door; it almost made me boozey "—Charles Stafford said, "What was it?" he said, "A lot of bags," and Charles called Mm into the stable; I do not know what he said to him; he whispered something—Smith then finished cleaning the cab , and took it away—on the next day, Saturday, I was in the stable, and saw the hope in the loft—on the Monday, I was in the yard about 4 o'clock, and William Stafford came down the yard with a rough cap and a hairy dog—he said to me, "Do you know where young Shakespeare is; the chap  that does  Charles Stafford off' ?"—I said, "I think he is gone to get his tea"—I came into the yard again between 7 and 6 o'clock—I heard a noise in the loft; a shifting about—I saw Charles Stafford again—Smith was then cleaning the harness—I went into the yard, and I heard Charles Stafford tell Smith to sell some bags—I did not hear him say where he was to sell them—I was going that evening with Smith down Goswell-road with the bags—we met Charles Fox—we brought the bags back to the stable, and pat them under the manger—I went with Smith to the police station, and told them something.
