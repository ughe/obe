I am single, and live in Rope-yard-rails, Woolwich—I was in the Duke on Horseback on the evening of the 4th of Aug.—I saw the prosecutor and the two prisoners—they all came in together—a pot of beer was called for, and the two prisoners shoved the prosecutor against the fire-place—they came away from him directly, and his right-hand pocket was tamed inside out—Pitcher then went back again to him and took bis watch-guard off his neck, and his watch out of his pocket and put into his own—the two prisoners were then going out, and they said to another marine who wat there," Come on"—he said,"Indeed I won't"—the prisoners went out, and I never saw them again till they were at the station—the prosecutor stopped till Mr. Harris came and fetched him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How came you to be there? A.  I went to have a pint of beer, the same as anybody else might—both the prisoners put the pro secutor to the fire-place—it was Pitcher took the watch—they were all drunk—the pot-boy was there, but he did not see what I did—I beard Pitcher tell the Magistrate that the pot-boy said when he went out, "Well, Pitcher, I hope you will remember an old friend when you come back"—the pot-boy was examined—he is not here. JOHN MARSH. I am waiter at the Duke on Horseback. On Tuesday, the 4th of Aug., I saw the prosecutor there—Pitcher and two or three more were there—I do not recollect seeing Graham—I went into the tap-room when a pot of beer was called for by the prosecutor—I took it in, and was waiting for the money—Pitcher said the prosecutor would pay me, and if he did not he would—I went out for another party, and when I returned I found the prosecutor on his back, on the floor against the door—Pitcher was coming out of the door—I said to him, " Bill , don't go away, pay me for the pot of beer; you said you would"—he said, "I will pay you for the pot of beer, Jack "and he gave me 4 d . worth of halfpence—when I went in with the pot of beer the prosecutor had some seals hanging down in front of him, and a chain round his neck—when I went in with a pint of beer afterwards I missed the chain and seals—I told my master.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe you let them all go before you went for the policeman? A.  Yes—the prosecutor appeared to me to be mad—he did not draw out his watch, and smash it on the table in my presence. THOMAS WILLIAM GILLING. I am shopman to a pawnbroker at Green wich. On the 4th of Aug., about a quarter past seven o'clock in the evening, Graham pawned this watch—there was a chain to it—I gave him the chain back, as it was of no value—about five minutes after I went to the door, and saw Graham go and join Pitcher, and they went away together—Graham told me he had had a fight, and the watch had got damaged, and it was his own.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you often take such things of drunken people? A.  He was not drunk—he knew what he was about—I cannot say he was sober—the glass of this watch is broken, and it is battered.
