pairs of drawers, 1s. 2 shirt-sleeves, 18. 3 napkins, 4d.; 3 stockings, 4d.
and 1 gown, 4d. the goods of James Huntley: and that he had been pre-
vionsly convicted of felony.
MaRy HUNTLEY. My husband is a cloth-worker. On the 19th of Noy.,
about five o'clock in the afternoon, I went into my kitchen, and found this
bundle at the foot of the stairs-the things in it are my husband's- called
for a young man who lodges in the house- found the prisoner at the back
area window-he caught me by the throat-I struggled with him, and got
away-I went up stairs into the passage, out of the house, and called the
police, who came and took himall these things are my husband's-- had
left them hanging wet in the kitehen- do not know the prisoner.
WILLIAM MONTGOMERY (policeman.) I was called, and took the prisoner
-I found an old knife on him-these articles were brought to me by a young
man.
Prisoner's Defence. I was passing the house, and asked a young woman
who came out, if I might go in to ease myself; as soon as I got in I met the
prosecutrix, who gave me into custody.
JO8EPH WaRD (policeman.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former
conviction-(read Convicted May, 1845, and confined three months.)- was
present at the trial-he is the person.
