356. ROBERT PERROTT (42), was indicted for a like offence..
MESSRS. POLAND and BESLEY conducted the Prosecution.
ALBERT COURT. I serve at the Anchor and Hope beer-shop, Star-corner,
Bermondsey-ou Thursday, 16th February, the prisoner came for a glass of
inild ale-he gave me a shilling-I put it in the till-there was no other
shilling there-I gave him his change, and he left-in a quarter of an hour,
my sister showed me the shilling-I found it was bad, and put it away by
itself.
ANNIE COURT. I am the wife of Edward Court, who keeps the Anchor
and Hope-I was present when the prisoner was served -I afterwards went
to the till, took ont the shilling, and put it by itself-I had been present in
the bar from the time he was served - I put no other shilling into the till
no one else had gone to the till in the meantime-the prisoner came again
on the 18th, and I recognized him-he asked for a glass of ale, and gave me
a bad shilling-I took it, and sent for a constable—I then charged him with
having been there on the Thursday before, and gave the shillings to the
constable.
JOSEPH DOUBUN (Policeman, M 266). I took the prisoner on 18th
February, and received these two bad shillings (produced) --I searched him,
and found one florin, five shillings, and a half-crown, all good.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These two shillings are bad.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrale : "I am innocent of the charge.
I was not there on Thursday; I can prove it by a witness."
Prisoner's Defence. I have respectable friends here to prove that I was
not out on Thursday, and likewise to my former good character.
The prisoner received a good character.-Guilty."-Confined Two Years.
357. THOMAS WILLIAMS (25), was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS. POLAND and ORRIDGE conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAS WALLACE I am a draper, of 183, High-street, Newington-on
14th January, the prisoner came for & scarf, and gave me what seemed to be
a half-crown-I tried it in the detector, and broke it in half-he said that
he did not know it was bad-I sent for a constable, and the prisoner run
away--the policeman canght him, and I gave him in custody, with the
half-crown.
GEORGE MILLER (Policeman, L 20). On 14th January, I saw the prisoner
running--I ran after him, and took him-he said his name was Richard
Thompson-he was taken before the Magistrate, and remanded-he gave a
false address, for I went there-he way discharged on the 18th-I received
this portion of a bad half-crown from Mr. Wallace.
JULIA LONGHURST. I was on a visit at the Cheshire Cheese, Fleet-street
---the prisoner came to the bar on 22 January for two-pennyworth of rum
-he offered me a five-shilling piece-I broke it, and told him it was bad-
a constable was sent for, and Mr. Moore gave the prisoner in custody, with
the crown.
THOMAS GOODMAN (City policeman, 315). The prisoner was given into my
custody at the Cheshire Cheese, with this crown-he gave his name, Arthur
Pope-- he was taken before the Magistrate at Guildhall, remanded till the
25th, and then discharged.
