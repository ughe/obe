 2057. RICHARD MULLETT was indicted for steeling, on the   7th of April , 1 hat, value 1 l  .; 1 pair of boots, 1 l  .; and 1 coat, 10 s  .; the goods of William Shurr . JANE SHURR. I am the wife of William Shurr, of Fraucis-place, South-wark. On the 4th of April the prisoner took my lodgings, and he left at half-past ten o'clock on Friday, the 7th—I went to the room a very few mi nutes after, and missed the boots from under the bed, the coat from between the mattress and the bed, and the hat from a box—no one else was in that room—the prisoner's old hat was put into the box instead of ours—I had it in my hand the night before, and looked at it—none of the articles are found.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Have you any other lodger? A.  A young man and woman in the parlour—he came in at half-past nine o'clock, and the things were then safe—I have not stated that I did not know when the things were safe—the coat was safe when I made the bed between the lights—there was no key in the door. THOMAS LYNE( police-constable L  137.) I took the prisoner—I asked if he knew Mr. Shurr—he said he should answer no questions. NOT GUILTY.
 2058. JOHN SHANNON was indicted for stealing, on the   14th of May , 3lbs. weight of kitchen stuff, value 6 d  .; and 1 cask, 9 d  .; the goods of Har rison Underwood , in a vessel on a navigable river called the   Thames . JOHN CANNON. I am mate of the schooner Limerick Lass , which laid in Mill tier, off Bermondsey, in the river Thames. On the 14th of May, about a quarter before three o'clock in the morning, I heard a foot coming on deck—I came on deck, and saw the prisoner with the sludge cask and three or four pounds of sludge in it—he was carrying it from the starboard side of the windlass—I asked what brought him there, and he said he was taking two sailors from the other side of the water, and he had put one on board a vessel lying astern—the other man he had put on our vessel, and he had got over the ship's side and made off—he was taking this sludge as payment for his water job, as the sailor had run away—no sailors had come to our ship—the cask that the sludge was in belonged to the ship—the owner is John Russell, of Limerick, and the captain, Harrison Underwood—I saw a boat on the lar board side of the fore chains—the prisoner left the cask on the deck, and made a spring, but I laid hold of him—a young man who was in the boat let go the painter, and pulled the boat in amongst the tier.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  I see you call this kitchen stuff, to whom did it belong? A.  To the cook—it was his perquisite—the cask was the ship's property—I am certain of that—it was a butter firkin.
