Cross-examined. Q.  Did your uncle walk from the house? A.  Yes, and I went with him as far as Clapham—I cannot tell where the Jew lives.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Was the Jew taken up stairs? A.  No, he was not beyond the lobby—I have seen him since at Clapham, but not to speak to him—he is always to be found—there were chisels in a drawer in the library, with other tools, belonging to the late captain—that drawer was always open—I have not been up stairs to examine the marks on the drawer—I was never up stairs after the 30th of June—the prosecutrix occupied the garret, and the prisoner the adjoining room.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was she permitted to go out in the day time, al though in charge of the house? A.  She was to go out occasionally—she told Mrs. Alsager that it might be about three days a week—she always slept there, except for three days, when she went to see her father, or to bury him. SAMUEL LONGBOTTOM re-examined . I saw the chisels fitted to the marks on the drawers—they appeared to have been made by the chisels—I mentioned this to Mr. Painter the Magistrate.
   MRS. GRIMMETT ( looking at the fifteen sovereigns .) Here is one which is the colour of one of mine—I cannot say any thing about the others—I had noticed two of my sovereigns being of the present Queen's reign, and there are only two among them of this reign—one was a brighter colour than the other, and here is a little difference in the colour of one of these.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had one of them, to the best of your judgment, never been in circulation? A.  Yes, Captain Alsager had brought some new sovereigns home from the Bank, and mistress gave me one. SAMUEL LONGBOTTOM. Directly I found the sovereigns I looked over them, having been told there were two particular ones—the rest are all of the reign of George IV., except one, which is William. HANNAH WILLIS re-examined . When my aunt borrowed the caddy of me it used to stand on the top of a bed in a front room at her house—she bor rowed it when I first took it home, two years ago, and had the use of it all along, and. the key—it was in her exclusive possession—I had not the key at all.
   MRS. GRIMMET re-examined . I received thirteen of the sovereigns last Christmas—I have had one of the sovereigns by me some years—I kept them altogether in my drawer, not in my pocket, so that they could not get soiled. HANNAH WILLIS re-examined . The key of the drawer my aunt used was in the servant's room, which my aunt slept in, next to the cook's room—it was left in the drawer, so that any one could get at it for three months, but after the footman left my aunt locked the drawer, and took the key with her, and as she cleaned all the rooms she locked them, and took the keys. NOT GUILTY.
