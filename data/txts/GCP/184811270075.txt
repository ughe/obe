118. RICHARD CALVERT, stealing 1 pair of boots, value 58.; the
goods of Lewis Jacobs ; having been before convicted.
LEWIS JACOBs. I am a clothes dealer. On 8th Nov. I gave Ann Mad-
don a pair of boots to clean-this boot (produced) is one of them.
Anx MADDON. I am in Mr. Jacobs service; he gave me the boots to
clean-I put them on a step, turned round, and missed them-I saw the
prisoner and another looking in at the window - I called “Stop thief!” and
they ran.
JOSEPH MADDON. I am no relation of the last witness I was on Saffron-
hill, heard a cry of, “Stop thief!" and saw the prisoner running, and he
dropped something I picked up this boot-I am sure he dropped it.
ALEXANDER TURNER (policeman, G 56). I saw the prisoner running in
Saffron-hill, with a pair of boots in his hand-I followed him-he was stopped
-I asked him where the boots were-he said he did not know anything
about them; it was not him that took them.
WILLIAM WALLIS. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former con-
viction (read-Convicted Sept. 1847; and confined three months)-he is the
person. GUILTY. AGED 21.-Confined Twelve Months.
119. SAMUEL JAMES HAYLAND, stealing 2 boxes, 12 spoons, 8
forks, and other articles, value 21.; the goods of Eliza Carter.
Eliza CARTER. I am single. On 16th June I went in an oronibus to
King's-cross. I left four boxes at the toll-bouse there--I employed the
prisoner as a porter-be took two with me to my lodgings-I sent him back
for the other two, and never saw him again or the boxes.
John Kern. I was at the toll-house, and Miss Carter left her boxes
there, returned, and employed the prisoner-I saw her go away with him with
two boxes-he came back and took the other two away.
