On the 25th of March I was in the counting-house when the General-post letters were delivered—Lewin Caspar was there also—it was about half-past nine o'clock—I took the letters from Mitchell's hands, to whom the postman delivered them—it is my practice to enter them in a book which I have here—I did not take any account of them before I gave them to Lewin Caspar—he took them from me—there is an entry made by me that morn ing of the letters that came—( reads ) "1 Falmouth, 2 Plymouth, 4 Dublin"—they came to 7 s . 10 d .—Lewin Caspar took the letters from me, and returned them to me afterwards—some of them he opened—when he returned them to me there were two Falmouth letters—1 s . is the postage of a Falmouth letter—it was the custom of the General-postman to put the amount of all the letters on one of them, and he had done so that morning—I ascertained the amount myself—the amount, when I put it down, did not agree with the postman's—I put down the 7 s . 10 d . from one Falmouth letter, two Plymouth, and four Dublin, but there were two Fal mouth letters, after taking them from Lewin Caspar—I at first ascertained there was but one, and put it down so—after Caspar returned them to me, I found two, and altered the 7 s . 10 d . to 8 s . 10 d  —the entry of the postman on the letter was 7 s . 10 d .—we settled with the General Postman once a week—when I paid him that week I only paid him for one Fal mouth letter—after that the letters were taken into the office—Lewin Caspar took them in—about eleven o'clock that morning I was called out to see a person who came in a cab  —( the witness Moss was here called in )—that is the person—he asked for some one in the office—I went out to him, and he asked me if Mr. Hartley was in town—I told him he was not, but that Mr. Hartley's agent would see him—I went in and called Lewin Caspar—he went out to the cab,  and they both returned into the office together—they remained there a very few minutes, and came out together—they were in the back office—before they came out, Lewin Caspar came into the room where I was, and asked me if there were two boxes of gold dust, marked "BC 18—19" consigned to the Brazilian Com pany—I told him the manifest had gone to the wharf, but I would refer to the book—I did so, found the entry, and told him—when they left the office Lewin Caspar opened the door, and let Moss out—he went away, and Lewin Caspar returned into the office—Moss was not present when Lewin Caspar asked me about the gold dust.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS .— Q.  Was it Lewin Caspar's duty to open letters when they came? A.  It was—Mitchell, our porter, gave the letters to me, and went away directly—Lewin Caspar had arrived then—I made the entry the moment the letters arrived—this book is kept by me alone—the postman and I have differed before.
