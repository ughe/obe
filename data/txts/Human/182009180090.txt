 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1125.  JOHN MANNEY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , five pair of shoes, value 30 s., the goods of John Chalmers.
   THOMAS DUNKLEY. I am a shoemaker, and live in Middle-row, Broad-street, On the 25th of July I bought a pair of shoes of one Summer, in Rag Fair, he is not here, and on the 27th I bought three other pairs of him; I also bought another pair of Bumpus, whom I saw buy them of Summer.
   JOHN FORRESTER. I apprehended the prisoner, and found two pair of shoes at his lodgings.
   JOHN CHALMERS. I am a shoemaker, and live in Holborn, the prisoner was my servant. The two pair of shoes are mine; I do not know when I lost them. I believe the prisoner never bought them. My son has superintended my business for seven years. They are of a particular make, but have no shop-mark on them.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Where were they made - A. At Northampton. The same person makes for other people.
 Q. You had notice to produce your books here - A. Yes, but I have not brought them, as they would prove nothing.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1126.  NATHANIEL BRIGGS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , one piece of nankeen, value 1 s., the goods of William Luke.
   WILLIAM LINDSAY. I am chief officer of the brig Britannia, the prisoner was a labourer in the East India Docks, where the brig laid. I thought he appeared bulky, had him searched, and the nankeen was found on him, which had been stolen from the brig- William Luke is the owner.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined Three Months.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1127.  WILLIAM M'VILLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , one hat, value 22 s.; one brush, value 9 d., and 17 s. 3 d. in monies numbered, the property of James Pitt.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to belong to  William Yate.
   WILLIAM YATES. I manage the business of Mr. James Pitt, who has a hatter's shop in Oxford-street. On the 12th of August the prisoner ordered a hat to be sent to Mr. Bond, No. 12, Colvill-court, Charlotte-street, Rathbone-place - the hat came to 22 s., and a brush to 9 d. I sent them by Wood, and gave him 3 d. for change. He brought them back, and asked for change of a 2 l. note. I gave him 17 s. in addition to the 3 d.; he returned with two 1 l. notes of the Milston-street Bath Bank, made payable at No. 11, Crutched Friars. I sent them for payment two or three days after, he brought them back. I had ordered Wood not to leave the goods without the money. On the 14th I went to Colville-court, and found no Mr. Bond lived there.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. Did you receive the order from the prisoner - A. Yes, he wrote his name as Bond - he tried three hats on, and said it was for his brother at Romford. I am not in partnership with Mr. Pitt, but merely his agent.
   THOMAS WOOD. I am errand-boy to Mr. Pitt; Yates sent me with a hat and box to Mr. Bond, No. 12, Colville-court - he gave me 3 d., and told me not to leave them without the money. I went to Colville-court, and met the prisoner at the door - I did not knock; I am certain it was him. He said,
 "My little man, is that hat for me?" I said,
 "Yes, Sir." He asked if I had change for 2 l.? I said I had only 3 d. He said,
 "Run back, you may take the hat with you or leave it here; you have no need to be afraid, I will not run away - make haste, as I am in a hurry." I ran back with the hat, got 17 s., and found him at the corner of the court. He said,
 "You have made haste, here is 3 d for you." I gave him 17 s. and he gave me two notes - I brought Mr. Yates the same notes, he did not approve of them. I went a day or two after to No. 11, Crutched Friars; I found no such persons as Sampson and Brothers, nor had they lived there for ten years.
 Cross-examined. Q. They never sent you to Bath - A. No. There was a man tried at Clerkenwell for an offence at this house.
