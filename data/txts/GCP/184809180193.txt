John SHILLITO. I am a tailor. I was at the Crown and Anchor on 5th
Sept., between ten and eleven o'clock-I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner
in the tap-room-they were laughing, joking, and drinking together-there
was no foul language while I was there, till they came out-Jones came out
first, and Sutton after him-Jones called Sutton a parish-bred Sutton
went up to him, and asked him if he called himself a man to call bim that
Jones then went and took the scraper, and knocked him down with it-Jones
ran away, and I ran and overtook him-Sutton was not at all offensive to
Jones while I was there.

John Pearce. I am a surgeon, of Larkhall-lane. On the night of 5th
Sept. Sutton was brought to me, suffering from a severe contused wound,
close on the skull, as deep as it could go--I saw him the next day-there
was a great deal of constitutional disturbance, which might have arisen from
an old wound on the hand-it might have been the wound, or it might have
been from drink-it might have happened without the wound I saw him on
the Thursday following-he was very ill, and suffering a great deal from the
head-I was obliged to bleed him this wound was not dangerous, because
there was no fracture-his arm is bound up from a constitutional disturbance
-he may be subject to fits from the effects of this wound.
