 JOHN ROBERTS. I am a  dealer in potatoes, and live in Upper East Smithfield. My coat was on a shelf behind the counter; the pocket-book was in the pocket; I had seen them safe about two o'clock on Friday, the 14th of July; I missed them about five o'clock; I did not see either, of the prisoners in my shop - I had gone up stairs to be down.
   THOMAS GRAY. I am an officer. On the 14th of July I was in White's-yard, Whitechapel, a little after five o'clock in the evening, about two hundred yards from Mr. Roberts' house - I stopped both the prisoners, in company, and in Surry's right hand I saw a black pocket-book, which he was looking at - and in his left-hand a bundle of papers; I asked where he got the book - he said he bought it of a hawker for 1s. 6d.; I asked if he knew the hawker - he said, No; I took him to the office; Marston took the papers from his right hand.
   ROBERT MARSTON. I am an officer, and was with Thomas Gray. I took Gillett, who had this coat on over his own - he said he bought it at a shop in Whitechapel; I found a letter in the pocket, which he said came from his brother in the country; I found it related to the prosecutor, and found him out.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 SURRY'S Defence. I bought the pocket-book of the same person he bought the coat of - he was a hawker, who exposed braces, and such like things, for sale; he found the papers in the pocket, and gave them to me to read them.
 GILLETT'S Defence. I gave 9s. for the coat, to a hawker near Petticoat-lane.
 SURRY -    GUILTY. Aged 23.
 GILLETT -    GUILTY. Aged 23. Transported for Seven Years.
 1528.  ELEANOR STREET was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of June , 2 silver tea-spoons, value 6s.; 1 shawl, value 5s.; 1 gown, value 4s.. 1 pair of shoes, value 2s., and 1 silver table-spoon, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Murray.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I am a constable - the prisoner was taken up for disorderly conduct on the morning of the 20th of June; at three o'clock, she was brought to Lime-house watch-house, and I found on her two silver teaspoons; she had a gown and a pair of shoes on, but I knew nothing about them; I detained the spoons, and took her to Union Hall - she had three examinations, to give us an opportunity to discover the owner; she was committed to Brixton, and on the day she was discharged I endeavoured to follow her home, but she avoided me, and was given in change to another officer.
 THOMAS MURRAY. I live in  Goswell-street, and am in the jewellery line. The prisoner is my grand-daughter, and apprentice; she has been with me four years; her father and mother deserted her, and I took her from the workhouse; she left me on the 19th of June, about two o'clock - I had sent her to Love-lane, with a message, and desired her to make what haste she could, on account of her grand-mother being on a sick bed - but she did not come - and at five o'clock, on looking round for the tea-things, I missed the tea-spoons, and then the table-spoon, in which we gave the medicine to her grand-mother; I never heard of her again till she was discharged from the office, when she was brought home with her grand-mother's gown and shawl on - she never ran away before; I never permitted her to take these things.
   ANDREW LLOYD. I am an officer - I have a gown, a shawl, and a pair of shoes, which I took off the prisoner, when she was delivered to my care.
   EDWARD COLVER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Newgate-street. I have a table-spoon pawned, I believe, by the prisoner on the 19th of June. GUILTY. Aged 15.
 Recommended to Mercy. Transported for Seven Years.
 1629.  THOMAS RUBY and EDWARD SPENCER were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 looking-glass, value 15s., the goods of Samuel Moore.
   EDWARD WILLIAM NEWMAN. I am an apprentice to Mr. Ashcroft, of Covent-garden. Mr. Moore is a broker, and lives in Russell-street. On the 29th of August, between twelve and one o'clock, I saw Ruby in his shop, and Spencer standing right opposite; they were looking at each other - a third person stood against the post, at the corner of Drury-lane, four doors from Moore's. I saw Ruby come out of the shop, and Spencer crossed to him - the third one came half way to meet them; Ruby made a motion with his mouth, and they went away - one back to the post, and Spencer stopped in the road; they then all went to the post together; I went down Drury-lane, to a broker's-shop, and continued to watch them; I saw Ruby and Spencer go away - I then saw them come back, and give the other a looking-glass; he went on the other side of the way, and they kept on this side - I ran and told Moore, and we both went after them; a boy told us they were gone down Orange-court; I then saw Spencer, and then Ruby, came up. I had not seen them bring the glass out of the shop - it was a square glass.
 SAMUEL MOORE. I keep this shop. On the 29th of August I lost a looking-glass, which stood about the middle of the shop, on a table, to which it was tied. I have never seen it since.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1530.  FREDERICK SERGEANT was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of June , 1 pair of gloves, value 10s., the goods of George Strange.
 GEORGE STRANGE. I live at  Hounslow, and am a shoemaker. On the 30th of June, between eight and twelve o'clock, I lost a pair of shoes from inside my doorpost; a man came, gave me information, and the prisoner was taken; I cannot swear I had seen him at my shop.
   JAMES JORDAN. I am a gardener. I bought this pair of shoes of the prisoner on Monday, the 3d of July, for 6s., and paid a score of 2s. at the public-house for him - he said he found them on the bank of the river. I sold them again for 10s.
   EDWARD GROSSE. I bought this pair of shoes of James Jordan.(Property produced and sworn to).
