JOHN SPELLANE. I am an invalid soldier, returned from India. The
prisoner came home in the same ship-he slept by himself, in a room
where all our boxes were, at a lodging-house-I had a watch-guard, with
a silver cross to it-I took it off on Monday night, the 25th of July, and
put it into my box, which I locked, but the staple was not down into the
lock-the next morning I observed my chest-I then locked it-I returned
in the evening, and my chest was apparently as I had left it-I missed
my chain on the Saturday following--this is my chain- I have not seen
the crucifix since.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHARNOCK. Q. How long had you known the
prisoner ? A. Only from the time we sailed from Calcutta--this guard is
a mixture of silver and metal - I gave about 8s. for it-I know it by a
dent in the turn, where the watch goes on, which was done by a watch-
maker on board the ship-I have seen plenty of these chains in Madras,
but not in Bengal-I arrived at Mr. Barron's on the 25th-I never saw
the prisoner wear a similar chain to this on board ship-a whole week ex-
pired before I missed it-I had been to the box between the times, but I
had different departments in the box-the prisoner slept in the lower room
on the ground floor-he always kept the door shut-Mr. and Mrs. Barron,
and their family, lived in the house-there is a servant in the house.
Court. Q. How long had you had this? 4. I bought it in February
last, before I left Calcutta.
ROBERT BARRON. My wife is too ill to be here-the prosecutor called
me into the room on Saturday morning, where the prisoner was lying on
the bed, and asked me whether I had seen his watch-guard-I then asked
my wife if the prosecutor had given it her-she said he had not, but she
had got a guard and watch which the prisoner had given her, and told her
not to let any one see it-I then asked the prosecutor if he would know
the chain-he said, "Yes"-my wife then got it, and showed it him-he
said it was his--the prisoner denied it, and said it was his own-after that,
he agreed to give the prosecutor up his chain, and say no more about it;
and then he agreed to give him the watch and chain, and counted out 8,
to give him for the crucifix-I would not allow him to do it-he said he
had not seen the crucifix, and offered to pay him rather than have a piece
of work.
Cross-examined. Q. Will you swear your wife did not say it was on
Monday, when they came, that the prisoner gave it her? A. No-I was
at home on Monday when they came the prisoner was in bed most of
the time-he went down on his knees, and pressed me not to let the pro-
secutor prosecute him, and he would do any thing-he said it was his own
chain.
WILLIAM SLADDEN (police-constable K 50.) The prisoner was given
into my charge-he said the guard was his own, that he bought it at
Benares-after that he said he bought it of an invalid.
Cross-examined. Q. You searched the prisoner and his room? 4.
Yes, and found 78. 4d. in silver-the watch was delivered to me by Mrs
Barron-it is worth 158. or 11.- I did not find any crucifix-he insisted
that the guard was his.
