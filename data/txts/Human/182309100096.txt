 1213.  JOSEPH HEWLING was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , a watch, value 50 s., the goods of Robert Brain.
   ROBERT BRAIN. On the 27th of August, I was at the Red Cross, public-house, Barbican, the prisoner came in, I told him I had broken my watch - he said the main-spring was broken, and if so, he would put it in for 3 s. 6 d., and if not, he would do it for 6 d; that he would go home to my house and take it to pieces, which he did - and said, he had no tools to put it together, and would take it home and bring it in the morning - but I saw no more of him till he was taken. I knew where he lived.
   LEONARD MATTHEWS. I am a pawnbroker. On the 28th of August, the prisoner pawned this watch.
 The prisoner in his defence said, he had to go into Berkshire, and took the liberty to pawn it, intending to redeem it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 THIRTEENTH DAY. WEDNSDAY, SEPTEMBER. 23.
 Middlesex Cases, Fourth Jury, Before Mr. Arabin.
 1214.  JOHN PICKERING was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , two live tame pidgeons, price 15 s., the property of Robert Coe.
   ROBERT COE. I live in Church-street, Bethnal-green, and am a weaver. I borrowed a pair of pidgeons of Mr. Meux for breeding, and lost them on the 21st of July. I had one of them three weeks, and knew them well; they were carriers - I kept them in my garret - I missed them about six o'clock in the morning, of the 21st of July; they were safe the night before - they were loose in the garret. I found the tiles broken sufficient for a hand to be put in and undo the trap-door. I found one the same day at Baxter's, and the other next morning at Turner's, and knew them well.
 Cross-examined by MR. WALFORD. Q. I am told there is a practice of trapping pigeons in the neighbourhood - A. Yes; I do not keep a trap - they call me the cat-killer. My pidgeons could not get out - the prisoner is a tradesman's son in the neighbourhood - he does not keep pidgeons.
   MARY BAXTER. I live in Osborne-street, Whitechapel, and keep a bird-shop. The prisoner brought me a white pidgeon; I gave him 1 s. for it - Coe afterwards claimed it.
 Q. Are they not worth 15 s. a pair - A. They may be; but my husband was not at home - I did not know the value.
   MARY TURNER. I keep a bird-shop in Commercial-road. I bought a carrier pidgeon of the prisoner for 1 s. 6 d.
   JOSEPH BROOK. I am an officer. I apprehended him; he said he had sold a pidgeon to Baxter, and no more.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     NOT GUILTY.
 1215.  RICHARD TIBBLE was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , a watch, value 10 s., the goods of Richard Baxter.
   MARY BAXTER. I am the wife of Richard Baxter; we live in Osborne-street, Whitechapel; the prisoner came to my shop two or three days running; being out of employ I set him him to feed my birds. On Saturday evening, before he went away, I sent him up with a parrot into the room where this watch was. I missed it next morning; it hung by the fire-place. I saw it at the office on Monday.
   WILLIAM BELCHER. I am servant to Price, a pawnbroker, of Wentworth-street. On Saturday evening, about eight o'clock, the prisoner came to pawn this watch for 12 s., and said he came from his mother; I said he must send his mother, and he said she was ill. I sent him to fetch his father, and detained the watch, he did not return.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 11. Whipped and Discharged.
 1216.  WILLIAM WHEATLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of June , 100 lbs. of potatoes, value 3 s., and a bushel of oats, value 2 s., the goods of Thomas Life.
   THOMAS LIFE. I am a farmer, and live at West Ham; the prisoner was in my service as waggoner. On the 26th of June, I sent him to town with a load of potatoes, about 2 1/2 ton to be sold, to make up money for rent.
   GEORGE ELLIS. I am an officer. On the 26th of June, between one and two o'clock in the morning, I was on duty at Mile-end, and heard a noise by the Bell and Mackarel, public-house, and saw the prisoner coming from that direction; we went up to the palings; the prisoner was on this side of the pales - I did not see him do anything myself. I went after him in consequence of what my partner said, and asked him what he had thrown over the pales; he said, it was corn he was allowed by his master to feed his horses. I asked if there was anything else in the sack - he said,
 "No;" I said,
 "Well, we will go and see what it contains," and as we went along, he said, there was a few potatoes which he dug out of his own garden, and had promised them to the hostler; he said afterwards, that he picked the sack up in the road. I found it contained three pecks of oats, and 50 or 60 lbs. of potatoes.
   DANIEL HALEY. I am a Bow-street patrol. I saw the prisoner coming from the paling of the public-house. got over, and found the sack of potatoes and oats. Ellis brought him back. He said it was allowed him by his master for the horses, and he was going to leave it there till he returned, as he generally fed there, and he brought the potatoes for the hostler, as he had done him several favours.
