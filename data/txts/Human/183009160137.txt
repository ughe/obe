 1733.  SUSAN GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July , 1 purse, value 1d., and 2 half-sovereigns, the monies of William Rumball , from his person.
 WILLIAM RUMBALL. On the 10th of July I met the Prisoner at  Enfield-highway; I walked four or five poles with her - I saw no one near me but her; I did not go out of the road - I looked at my purse, saw the two half-sovereigns, and put it into my pocket; when she was gone I felt and missed it - I followed, and caught her in the road- I asked for my money; she said she had not got it - I held her, and sent for a constable: I have never seen my money since - it was about twelve o'clock at night.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Were you quite sober? A. Yes - I did stop with her for about ten minutes; I had put my money into my right-hand pocket - the constable took her on the Monday morning following; I sent the man who came up for a constable, and up came five or six men, who released her from me, and knocked me down - she got away.
 COURT. Q. Did you go up a lane with her? A. No; I stopped, but did not loosen any part of my dress - I had been drinking with my partners, where we were paid for our work.
   JOHN WILSON. I took the prisoner on Monday morning; I found nothing on her - she said she had not had it, nor seen the man; when I got up on the Saturday night there were four or five men round the prosecutor - he was laying down, and when he got up he was all over blood.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you find the prisoner? A. At her mother's; I went there on Saturday night, but could not find her - I found her in bed on Monday morning.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never saw any thing of the kind - he knocked me down on the road, and would pull my right-hand pocket out; there were 3s. in it - nobody touched him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1799.  WILLIAM TAYLOR was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the shop of William Fowler, on the 10th of September, and stealing therein 2 sets of harness, value 9l.; 3 bridles, value 14s.; 1 chain bridle, not quite complete, value 14s.; 1 pair of traces, value 1l.; 1 back-band, value 5s.; 7 halters, value 17s.; 1 tug and trace, value 18s.; 1 pair of shaft-tugs, value 10s.; 1 cart back-band, value 5s. 6d.; 1 pair of winkers, value 14s.; 1 chain trace, value 7s.; 9 bridle-bits, value 2l. 10s.; 13 brushes, value 30s., and 4 curry-combs, value 5s., the goods of the said William Fowler.
   HENRY MASKELL. I am apprentice to Mr. William Fowler, a saddler- his shop is in the New-road. on the evening of the 9th of September I left it safe, and went to High-street, Hampstead, where my master lives - about six or seven o'clock next morning a Policeman came, and I went to the shop; I found the sets of harness and other articles in two sacks - I had left them hanging on pegs; they are all my master's.
   JOHN LAUDON. I am a watchman. On Friday night, the 10th of September, between twelve and one o'clock. I was going round my beat, from Tottenham-court-road to the New-road - I tried the doors and windows, and found the door of this shop open; this padlock was hanging to the door- it had been picked; I pushed it open, and saw the prisoner standing inside the shop; I asked him what brought him there - he said he was Mr. Fowler, from Hampstead, and he was going to shut up his shop - I said he was not Mr. Fowler, for I knew him; I met the policeman, and gave him into custody - another Policeman then came with a light, and we found these two sacks of harness; the prisoner said he was going to Hampstead, and if I would go with him he could prove he was Mr. Fowler; at the station he gave his name as William Taylor, St. John-street-road, Clerkenwell.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you find on him any one thing that could pick a lock? A. No - I did not ask him if he was Mr. Fowler; he said if I would go to Hampstead I should find it was all right - I told the Magistrate that, and I suppose it was taken down- he was sober.
