To Mr. Justice Lawrence. The reason my paying-in book was at 43, Moorgate Street was in case anything happened to be paid in. I kept it there so that if any money was owing to me I might know it was paid in and have a record. It was generally kept at home. When Herbert Smith owed me money—it might be lord several different things—I arranged with him that it was paid into my account as soon as he got it. I knew it came from him at the time; I do not know now. Perhaps he would any so, or one of his darks, or they would give me the book. The identification is obvious. If it came from Smith I knew it at the time, because I should be wanting it and asking for it, and they would take care to say that it had been paid in. That is my knowledge that the money had been paid in. Numerous amounts and names of payments in were put to witness, amounting to £959 15s., from October to December, 1902, the great majority of which were said by witness to be in Harris's writing, the exceptions being in that of a clerk.)
 To Mr. Muir. If £959 went into my account from Herbert Smith, and £125 went to his account, it simply showed that he owed me the money. There was £900 paid in apparently in the last three weeks of Herbert Smith's life; that was money he owed me. If he did not, it is a matter for argument whether the money ought to have gone to his widow; it ought, I think, to have gone to hit creditors. The widow had every opportunity of seeing whether Smith owed me the money; the books were there. I offered that she should take the whole business if she liked at the time of Smith's death. Harris had opened a fresh account at the London Joint Stock Bank. There was no obligation to give Mrs. Smith an opportunity of testing the rights of the thing. We have not put in the Stock Exchange accounts yet, which will go to explain a great deal of this. Harris carried on busi  ness in the name of Smith up to June 50; it was stopped because Mrs. Smith came up and requested it. The name remained up at 43, Moorgate Street, for the purpose of winding up the business. I do not know whether it was wound up in February, 1905. I was con sulted about putting up Smith's name at 27, Sun Street, that was for the purpose of continuing the winding up. There was no need for it to be continued at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings. It was proper to have an address, it did not matter where, really. Bleach says he got all the books in March to destroy them. I do not know why they should have been destroyed. I accept no responsibility for that My books are in the possession of the police. I did not have my own books of account; I had the fortnightly account only. I have not kept books for 10 years, and have not got any receipts from Smith; there were none required. I paid the notes in and traced them. I never gave any receipts to Smith; no necessity. I have heard of actions brought against Herbert Smith and of actions by him. There was Leslie v. Herbert Smith; another one of Smith against Foster.
