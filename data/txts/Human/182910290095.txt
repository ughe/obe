2091. ALFRED WILKS was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of October , 2 carpenter's rules, value 3s., the goods of James Butler.
 JAMES BUTLER. I am a  hardwareman. I lost two rules from my shop window in Tottenham-court-road, on the 29th of October.
 DANIEL FOX. I saw the prisoner take something from the prosecutor's window - he put it into his pocket, and ran away; I told the prosecutor, saw the prisoner taken, and the rules were found on the pavement - the sash was thrown up, he reached over, and took them.
 FRANCIS WARWICK. I took the prisoner, and have the rules.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Three Months.

 2094.  ABEL PIERS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of September , 1 silver tea-pot, value 4l., the goods of William Cathcart Boyd.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to be the property of  Louisa Elizabeth Boyd.
   DANIEL FORRESTER. On the 25th of September I saw the prisoner and another turn out of Moorfields-square and go, one to South-place and the other to South-street; I went down Cross-street and came up South street - I missed the prisoner, but saw the other man; I went on to Wilson-street, saw the prisoner come up to Wilson-street, and then he ran - I went and took him, and found this tea-pot in his apron.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did you see the two persons together? A. Yes, walking within a yard of each other; I did not observe any conversation - I think they were not walking in the usual way; the prisoner did not come to me and ask what I wanted - I took hold of his collar; he said he had been to Blackfriars, and found the tea-pot - the other man did not join him afterwards; I took him about thirty yards from the prosecutor's.
 CATHERINE JONES. I live servant with Mrs. Louisa Elizabeth Boyd, at No. 2,  South-street. This tea-pot is her's, and was either on the kitchen table, or in the pantry on the 25th of September; the person who took it must have come down the area steps.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you put it at night? A. I used to take it to my bed-room - I am sure I had it in my bed-room the night before, and brought it down that morning; the Lion on the top of it turns round, and I have it in use every day.
   ELIZA MILLER. I am a niece of Mrs. Boyd's; she is married to William Cathcart Boyd.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been over the water to receive orders, and coming down Moorfields I saw a handkerchief on the step of a door - I took it up and put it into my apron - I had not got more than twenty yards, when the officer said, "What have you there?" I went back, and said, "A tea-pot;" and gave it him.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 2095.  CHARLES O'CONNER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of November , 1 hat, value 20s., the goods of Samuel Adam.
   SAMUEL ADAM. I am a stone-mason. I was at the Marquis of Granby, Drury-lane, last Sunday morning, about ten o'clock - my hat was on a cask; I had a pint of beer, and when I looked for my hat it was gone; I had seen the prisoner there - he was gone when I missed my hat; there was another hat left.
 WILLIAM REYNOLDS. I went to the prisoner's lodgings in King-street, Drury-lane, about three o'clock on Sunday; he was not there - I went again at two in the morning; he was in bed; he said he had it, but it was a mistake.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took it by mistake.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2096.  ANN COLLINS, MARY TEWSBURY, and JOHN NEAL, were indicted for stealing, on the 31st of October , 1 purse, value 9d.; 3 half-crowns, 4 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the property of William Rickarby , from his person.
 COLLINS pleaded GUILTY. Transported for Life.
 WILLIAM RICKARBY. I am a  linen-draper, and live in Vine-street, Hutton-wall. About eight o'clock last Saturday evening I was going down Holborn, towards home - I had had too much in the course of the afternoon; I met some women, but I have but a very faint recollection of them; I had had a purse with some silver, but I do not know whether I had it at that time.
 WILLIAM JEFFREYS. I saw the prosecutor and the three prisoners come into the  Castle tavern, Holborn- they called for half a pint of liquor - the prosecutor took out his purse and paid for it - he had change for half a crown, and put 1s. 6d. into his purse again; his purse had some other silver which looked like half-crowns in it - he put it into his left-hand pocket - I saw Collins put her hand into his pocket and take out his purse; the other two prisoners were sitting opposite her, they saw it, and winked at her - I told the officer, who took Collins, and found the purse on her - they were all taken before they left the box.
 -. I am an officer. I took this purse from Collins.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 TEWSBURY -    NOT GUILTY.
 NEAL -    NOT GUILTY.
