We walked very softly together, with her arm under mine; we went on till we came to Abington Buildings, on the other side of the Abbey; she then asked me what o'clock it was; I pulled my watch out, there were two lamps at a door, but I could not see exactly; but said, I believed it was much about nine: I put my watch in my pocket again. I was not got three doors from that place, but she took me round the waste, and said, sir, you shall not go any farther, I am going to College-Court; and she kist me, and I her. I just went from her, a very moment, five or six steps homewards, but happening to put my hand to my breeches I mist my watch; I turned round and ran after her to the end of College-Street, but could not see her; I ran down College-Court, but saw nothing of her. I then went past my own door and through the park to the house of Mr. Troy: I said to him, for God's sake tell me where this gentlewoman lives, for she has got my watch; he said, he did not know where she lodged, but he would see where she lodged; he said, there was one person went home with her he believed could tell me where she lived, his name is Coningham, in Long-ditch; we then went with his son-in-law through the park: when Coningham came down stairs he was to shew us the way to where she lived; and I got a warrant and constable, and we all went to her lodgings. If she had gone there at first, I must have heard her knock at the door, for I went by it after I had lost my watch. We knockt at the door, and it was opened; we asked for her, but was told she was gone to bed; the woman went to shut the door, but I clapt my foot to it and insisted on seeing her; she came out then, and said, Mr. Adams what is the matter? I said to her, you have got my watch; she denied it three or four times; I then served the warrant on her, upon which she burst into tears, and said, don't expose me, and asked me the price of the watch; I said it cost me 7 l. but now it was not worth high so much; she said, I will give you any thing for it. The gentleman who goes for her husband came in, in a great flurry, and calling me aside, asked me what it was worth; he proffered me money; I told him I could not take money. She was taken to the constable's house, the justice being gone to bed.
 Cross Examined.
 Q. Where had you been that day?
 Adams. I had been with Mr. Troy doing business for him that day, we dined in St. Martin's-lane; he took me to appraise some goods for him.
 Q. Was you sober?
 Adams. I drank only two glasses of wine at Mr. Troy's, that is all, at seven at night.
 Q. Do not you know the prisoner is the wife of an officer in the army?
 Adams. I never saw her before; when I went to have the indictment drawn up, I was asked her husband's name; I said, I did not know that; he said, then I will put her down widow.
 Q. You know since that she is wife of an officer in the army, don't you?
 Adams. I have been told so.
 Q. Could you see what it was o'clock, when you pulled out your watch?
 Adams. I could not.
 Q. Why do you think she had your watch ?
 Adams. When we parted she put her hands round my waste.
 Q. Was it possible for this woman to take the watch out at that time?
 Adams. I don't know how it was; I mist it presently, I don't know how she did it?
 Q. Did you perceive her hand at your fob?
 Adams. I did not.
