On the 23rd of June I took a parcel to the George, in the Borough, addressed to Mr. Beaumont, of Maid-stone—I left it there. HENRY WILLIAM INGRAM. I am a woollen warehouseman in Addle-street. On the 13th of May Shackleford came to my warehouse—he pre sented a card with a reference to Mr. Bachelard, in George-street, Man sion-house; "R. W. Beaumont, of Week-street, Maidstone," was on the card—he said he wished to purchase goods of me—he said he was about opening a shop at Maidstone—he said the shop was taken, and he wanted some goods—I did not see him write the name of the referee—I promised to go to Bachelard—I went there that day—I saw him, and he said he knew Beaumont, that he had about 600 l . or 800 l . in the business, and his wife had some property in houses in Sloane-street—I was satisfied of the solvency of Beaumont, from the account be gave me—Shackleford looked out goods that day, part woollen goods, and part trowsers—they amounted to 50 l . 5 s .—they were sent to the Swan-with-two-Necks, by his direction—my porter, James Steel, took them—I will not say how they were ad dressed—on the day after, there was another parcel to the amount of 56 l . 14 s . 6 d .—he paid my salesman 20 l . on the 13th, and in all he paid 80 l .—106 l . 3 s . 6 d . is the balance remaining due—the witness came to me from Mumford's and Patey's for a reference—I was induced to give a satisfactory account of Beaumont, by having a reference myself, and being paid part of my account.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  You had no intention of assisting the person who applied to you, to obtain these goods? A.  Not in the least—I gave the character which I thought he had, honestly and fairly—others may have done the same thing—I gave my opinion, and thought they might do as they liked—I was examined last sessions on Shackleford's trial.
