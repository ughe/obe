 1825.  REBECCA ALDRIDGE was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 2 gowns, value 5s.; 2 aprens, value 2s.; 1 shift, value 1s.; 9 caps, value 5s.; 3 towels, value 2s.; 2 napkins, value 1s.; 3 petticoats, value 2s.; 3 night-gowns, value 2s.; 1 hair-brush, value 6d.; 4 combs, value 2s., and 2 tooth-brushes, value 6d., the goods of Mary Bland.
   MARY BLAND. I left my trunk, containing the articles stated, at Mr. McGowan's, on the 27th of July, when I went to Marylebone workhouse to be confined - when I got out I went for my box, and the landlady told me the prisoner had been and taken my box and her own; I had never allowed her to take the box or the articles; she had left there- I never heard of her till within three weeks, when she was in the workhouse; I went there with an officer - I found her with my cap on her head.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Have you any other name but Mary? A. My name is Mary Ann, but I always went by the name of Mary; I have never been called Mary Ann since I was a child.
   ISABELLA BURGOYNE. The prosecutrix left a trunk at my house, it was locked; the prisoner came, said she came for her own box and for Mary's, and asked what there was to pay - I said nothing at all, and asked what she was going to do with Mary's; she said, to take it to the workhouse - I only knew the prosecutrix by the name of Mary.
   ANN FOY. The prisoner became a lodger where I was- I pawned two or three articles by her orders.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you receive the articles from her? A. At a house where I worked; I did not assist in opening the box - I was on the opposite side of the kitchen when it was opened; the prisoner said Mary had got the key in a mistake, and she must have it opened, as she wanted something.
 COURT. Q. Did Mary Bland authorize you to take any of the things? A. No.
   HENRY HAYES. I am a shopman to Mr. Baylis, a pawnbroker. Ann Foy pawned a petticoat for 1s., a gown for 2s., and some other things with me.
   JOHN ANDREWS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and produce these articles, which the prosecutrix identified- I took this cap off the prisoner's head.
 MARY BLAND. These are all mine.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not break the box.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1826.  WILLIAM ALLSOP was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of July , one 10l. Bank note, the property of Henry Pickford.
 ELIZABETH PICKFORD. I am the wife of  Henry Pickford; he is in the 3rd regiment of Foot Guards. On the 12th of July, the prisoner was pot-boy at the Canteen, in Portman-street - my husband is pay-serjeant of his company; the prisoner came that evening to know if I wanted any beer - I told him what beer to bring, and told him to ask his master to give me change for a 10l. note; he returned with the beer, and said I could not have the change that evening, but as early as six o'clock in the morning I might; I said I did not want it so soon, when my husband came off duty would be time enough - he came the next morning, and asked if I had the paper; I then gave him the note to get the change, and never saw him afterwards till he was apprehended on the 17th of August - I never got the note or the money.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Did not you say you gave him the note that evening? A. No, in the morning; I asked if I could have the change - I never saw him after I gave it into his hand - I positively swear that; I will swear he did not come back, say his master could not give me change, and I tell him to get change elsewhere - I saw him go towards his master's with the note; I believe my husband had leave from Mr. Conant to compromise the matter.
   HENRY PEARSON. I am the publican. The prisoner came to me with the note, and asked if I could give change - I said I could not; he went away, and came back in ten minutes and asked again - I said I could not; he said he must go and get change; he went out, and did not return.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had he the note with him? A. Yes, the second time, but not the first - he asked for change for Mrs. Pickford.
   DANIEL CHAPMAN. I was sent for to apprehend the prisoner on the 17th of August - I asked if he had had the note; he said Yes, and he had spent it - that as soon as he found his master could not give him change, he saw some soldiers, and said to them, "Come along with me, and have a spree, I have plenty of money now."
   JOB JEFFRIES. I am a corporal in the 3rd regiment of Guards. The prisoner came to me on the morning of the 13th of July, a little before ten o'clock; he had some money in his hand, and asked me to give him some more to get a pot of beer - I said I would not; in about ten minutes he came again, and said he had a 10l. note.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you attend the first examination? A. No, but the serjeant asked me about it one morning - I attended on the last examination; this was on Tuesday morning, the 13th of July - I remember it very well; I will swear it was Tuesday, the 13th of July.
 GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to Mercy- Confined Six Months.
