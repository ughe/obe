182. WILLIAM RANDALL was again indicted with JAMES JESSOP
(45) for stealing a pair of boots, the property of Richard Coleman.
RANDALL PLEADED Guilty.
MR. BEARD Prosecuted: MR. PURCELL defended Randall.
ALFRED COLLINS (Detective V.) On December 14, at 8 p.m., I saw the
prisoners in High Street, Tooting-Jessop was carrying a pair of boots,
partly exposed, under his arm-I asked him where he got them from,
and he said, "Randall gave them to me "-Randall said that he had
bought them in Battersea - I took both prisoners into custody-1 subse
quently found that the boots belonged to Mr. Coleman, of Wimbledon-
I have seen the prisoners frequently together.
RICHARD COLEMAN. I am a boot maker, of 34, High Street, Merton-
on December 14th, at five o'clock, I missed a pair of boots--these boots
(Produced) were subsequently shown to me I knew they were mine,
because I have a pair exactly like them.
Jessop, in his defence, said that he was not guilty of stealing the boots ;
that Randall had told him that they were his, and that he was going to pass
them for him, and that he was drunk at the time.
JESSOP-Gunty, RANDALL then pleaded Gunty*** to a conviction
of felony on September 3rd, 1888, and another conviction was proved against
him. Several convictions were proved against JESSOP but not since
1899**+. To enter into their own recognisances in £10 each.
