MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did you tell him yon could not take him on that charge? A.  No; I was willing to take him, but he seemed to hesitate about charging him—the carman fetched me, I do not know his name—he told me to come to Ann's-place to take a man into custody—he said his master wanted to charge a man; I asked what for, and he said he did not know—I swear he said that—I went with him, and waited there a few moments—there was some conversation—the foreman first said he would charge him, and then he said he would not—I said, If you charge him, you take the responsibility on yourself"—he said he wished to have it settled some other way, he wished to have his money—they had some conversation about the goods—I did not hear exactly what they said—upon my solemn oath I took no gin-and-water.

   Cross-examined. Q.  What about the bill? A.  I met Mr. Edwards that morning, and at his request I went to the prisoner about it—I am a commer cial agent—I have no office—I have no place of business—I do not practise on 'Change—I am a general agent—I have lived at 4, Ely-court, a few weeks—I merely occupy the bedroom there on the ground-floor—I never saw the prisoner till 1st January—about three weeks afterwards I had to go to Whitecross-street prison and I saw him there—I have lived three weeks where I do now—I lived before that at Camden-town—I was coming to town every day—I have the use of part of an office at Mr. Jones's, a wine-merchant, 58, Greek-street, Soho—I am allowed to have my letters addressed there, and to come and write my letters there—before I went to Camden town I think I lived (upon my word, I almost forget) at 2, Pemberton-row, Gough-square—before that at 21, Tottenham-court, New-road—I was in a situation there, at a wine-merchant's, twelve months.
