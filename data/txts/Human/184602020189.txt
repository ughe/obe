HENRY BULLEN. I am in the employ of Mr. James Gardner, at Camber well—on the 10th of Jan., about four o'clock, I saw his five ducks safe in a pen—I went the next morning a little after seven o'clock, and there was only one duck—I observed some footmarks close up to the place where the ducks were, and in the pen—some shoes were examined in my presence by putting the shoes in the tracks in a new piece of ground—I looked at the tracks first and at the shoes, and they corresponded exactly—nothing could be more exact—these are the two pairs of shoes—we compared them three days afterwards, but there had been no wet—the marks were quite fresh.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  You were with the policeman when you examined the tracks? A.  Yes—I am perfectly sure about the footmarks—there were a few others beside those, but not on the bed where these were—these were in the upper garden—there were the same marks in the pen, but we did not try them—we only tried them about 100 yards from the pen, and that was three days after this occurrence.
   COURT . Q.  Were there traces from the place where you tried the marks up to the pen? A.  Yes, and they appeared exactly the same. GEORGE ROSSEN( police-sergeant P  29.) I got these two pairs of shoes from the two prisoners on the morning of the 15th of Jan.—I compared them with the marks in Mr. Gardner's garden—I examined, as far as my eye could, the traces of the foot-marks from the pen to the garden, and to the best of my belief they were the same.
   Cross-examined. Q.  In every mark that you examined did you put the shoe down? A.  No—in everyone that I thought was the same mark I put the shoe down. NOT GUILTY.
 658. MATTHEW GIBSON and RICHARD BOXALL were indicted for stealing 4 tame fowls, value 10 s  ., the goods of Joseph Kimpton ; and that Gibson had been before convicted of felony. JOSEPH KIMPTON. I live in Sussex-place, Old Kent-road. I had four fowls—I shut them up between two and three o'clock in the afternoon, on the 30th of Jan.—the next morning they were gone, between eight and nine o'clock—the policeman found them—he had the four which I had lost at the police-court.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  Have you any doubt of them? A.  Not the least. WILLIAM MORTON( police-constable P  289.) About eight o'clock on the night of the 30th of Jan. I met the prisoners in the Old Kent-road—I had been in search of them for a week on another charge—I took Boxall, but did not get more than twenty yards before he got away—I took him again, and took him across the road to a cheesemonger's shop—he had a basket on his head—I threw it off his head, and my wife, who was close behind, called out, "Here are some fowls in the basket"—I told her to take care of them—Gibson was brought over to me by another officer—I handcuffed the prisoners together, and asked them where they had got the fowls—Boxall said "You would b—y well like us to tell you, should not you?"—two fowls were found in Gibson's pocket—the fowls were shown to the prosecutor—he identified them—they were just killed, and warm—the prisoners had got about 300 yards from the prosecutor's.
