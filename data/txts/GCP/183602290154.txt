candlesticks, value 168. ; 5 yards of carpeting, value 10s. ; 1 pair of
81 affers and tray, value 4s.; and 1 decanter, value 58.; the goods of Georde
Selby, her master.
Mr. Clarkson conducted the prosecution. One
GEORGE SELBY. I am a solicitor, and live in Sergeant's Inn, Fleet
street. The prisoner was in my service last May-I missed a variety d
articles & girl was in the habit of coming backwards and forwards to
assist the prisoner-in consequence of my loss, I mentioned the circumstane
to the prisoner about a month ago she said she dare say that the girl had
put them away, or taken them home to clean-from that time I desired
the girl should come no more_last Sunday week I spoke to the prisoner
again- I said the things were not produced I insisted upon having them
produced, and said it was all nonsense about their being put away the
then said they were pawned, and produced ten or a dozen duplicates, wed
said the others were at Mr. Cottrell's I took these into my possession
I took her into custody last Monday- I went to Cottrell's, and one of his
young men delivered up about thirty things before Sir Chapman Marshall
at Guildhall, I recognised them as what I had lost.
Prisoner. I did not take them with the intention of stealing them-I mean
to take them out again as soon as possible, I did not know that there w
any thing lost--you said, if I gave up the tickets, and you found the
things were not lost, you would forgive me. Witness. No; I said it
would be better for her to give the whole history of what she had pawned
-she had 301. a year, coals and candles--her husband is a respectable
man.
Tuomas WILLIAM GRATTAN. I am servant to Mr. Cottrell, a paw
broker, in Shoe-lane. I have a piece of carpet, a pair of candlesticks and
snuffers and tray, some spoons and other things, pawned by the prisoners
different times-on the 31st of October, the 2nd of December, and at other
times -I knew the prisonershe pawned them in her own name, as living
in Water-lane I took in most of these things I enquired if they were
her own property-she said they were her own, several times she de
scribed her husband as keeping a house in Water-lane-I considered the
kept a lodging-house-I have a great many other things.
Prisoner. I was never asked whose property they were.
THOMAS CALVER. I am assistant to Mr. Beeston, a pawnbroker, in the
Strand. I produce a decanter, pawned by the prisoner for 85. on the 17th
of February, in the name of Mary Nurse she said it was her husband's
property, and she kept a house in Water-lane.
Prisoner. I should have had them all back in the course of a few
weeks.
