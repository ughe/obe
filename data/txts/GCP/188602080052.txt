ERNEST RICHARD CLIFF. I am a money lender and bill discounter of
43, Cheapside-I have known the prisoner some time-on March 7th he
brought me a bill of exchange for 501. dated 24th February, payable by
Mrs. and Mr. De Belen on 27th May-previously to that he had
asked me to lend him some money; I said I could not do so on his own
seonrity, but if he could get anybody as security for him I would, and I
understood this bill was security for a loan to him-he said nothing about
handing the proceeds to Mr. and Mrs. De Belen-on 7th March I gave
him 127., deducting 21. 23. 6d. for interest, 92. 178, 6d.-on 14th March I
gave him 91. 178. 6d. again, on 27th April 91. 178. 6d.; altogether I gave
him 387. 108. on the whole bill in three instalments-I took no receipta
--my cheques are receipts.
Cross-examined. I gave him 21. in cash in March, that hardly belonged
to this particular amount-I lent him 21. as he was going away into the
country; I don't think it would come into this amount-he might not
have had so much as 387. on this bill; the payments were spread over
from 7th March to 27th April, and he never had a larger sum than
107, 108, at a time; he had it in driblets I went to see the prosecuton
with reference to the note before I advanced the money; I saw Mrs. De
Belen, who said her husband had known the prisoner in India, I think,
and that Halyett would meet the bill ---she said they had made the nota
for the prisoner to get it discounted and he was to let them have some
thing out of the proceeds, that she trusted Mr. Campbell as her husband
had known him, and that he had promised to lend them something out
of it.
Re-examined. She did not tell me that the prisoner was going to give
them the proceeds and that they were going to let him have something
out of it, or I should not have let him have it-I cannot remember the
exact words, but she said he was going to let them have some of it.
By MR. MOYSES. I realised nothing from the plaintiffs-I believe
the prisoner has been a captain in the army.
CARLES BRYAN (City Detective). On 23rd January I saw the prisoner
in the Waterloo Road-I said to him "Excuse me, sir, are you Mr. Camp.
bell? he said "No"-I said "What ir your name, please ?"-he said
"My name is Sinclair ” I said "Where do you live?-he said " Round
the corner"-I said "I believe you are Mr. Campbell, and I shall arrest you
on a warrant for stealing 201.; unless you can show me that you are not,
you will have to come with me to the station "-he said "I will be honest
with you, I am Mr. Campbell "-he said on the way "It was merely s
money transaction, they wanted 1007."
Cross-examined. Directly I told him I was a detective, he told me he
was Mr. Campbell.
FREDERICK DOWNES (City Detective). On the 23rd I read the warrant to
the prisoner-he said he was very foolish not to have appeared to the
summons, but they had been trying to settle the matter-he heard there
was a warrant, and he intended to appear when he had sufficient means,
but he did not see how Mr. De Belen could prosecute him for felony as
he was a bankrupt.
