MAJOR BUSKIN. I am a hackney-coachman. I was on the stand opposite Adam-street, about two months ago- a youth came and engaged my coach to go to Whitecross-street; I went there with two youths and two pieces of cloth - I cannot swear either of the prisoners were the persons; I took them to the corner of a court - my coach is No. 1200.
   JAMES SIVIANE. I am a butcher, and live at No. 192, Whitecross-street. On the 12th of July I saw the coach No. 1200 drawn up to the corner of a court; Collis took out a large roll of cloth, and a small one attached to it - I did not see any one with him; I followed him, and saw him take them to the second house on the left-hand, where fresh people had come; I went, listened, and heard a conversation in the room - I heard Nathan say, "What do you ask for the cloth?" I had known Nathan before - he had lived up the court; the reply was 40l.: I heard him say he would give 10l. for it - I came out, and met the coachman, who asked me where the lad had gone; I showed him, and then went to my own shop - I then saw Farrell join Collis; they stood opposite my shop, and looked at me - they then went together down Playhouse yard, and the coach went away; I afterwards saw the prisoners return in half an hour or three quarters of an hour, with each of them a bundle in their hands, and new hats on their heads - they went down the court, and came out again, with each a new suit of clothes on.
   THOMAS VANN. I am an officer. I went with Waters to Shrewsbury-court, Whitecross-street, on the 25th of August; we found Farrell there.
   THOMAS WATERS. On the 25th of August I went to the court, and found Farrell; I knocked at the door - his mother asked who was there; I told her, and she said, "I will make you wait till I dress myself;" I went up stairs, and asked if her son was at home - she said No, he slept there sometimes, but was not there then; I found a bed there which was warm; I looked, and found Farrell under his mother's bed, naked.
 Two witnesses gave Collis a good character.

 1736.  HENRY PECK was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 4 sovereigns, the monies of Isaac Burrows; and HENRY TILSON was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   ISAAC BURROWS. I am a broker, and live in Upper Cleveland-street. On the 17th of July I hired a boat, from four o'clock in the morning till eight; I hired it at Westminster-bridge - Thomas Mellows and his son were with me; I saw a person laying drunk - I cannot say that it was Tilson - I hired the boat of Peck, and was to pay half a crown for it; he asked for security for it, and I gave him four sovereigns - he said his name was Cook; I said the boat had the name of Sawyer on it - he said it was his, and he had bought it of Sawyer a few days before, and that he would be there to meet us when we came back; I went out, and the boat began to leak - to save myself I took hold of one of the barges, and the boat went off; a waterman came and took me in - my two friends caught another barge, and they were brought back; when we came to the stairs I did not see Peck - I came again at eight o'clock, and staid till nine; he did not come: I gave the boat to the Thames Police, and mentioned the circumstance.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was Peck intoxicated? A. I cannot say - I am positive I was sober- there was a lad named Watts; I do not know whether he was sober - I do not know that I saw Tilson then; Peck was sober enough to stand, and to take my money - he did not deny having taken the sovereigns; I did not nearly upset the boat - we had had a pot of stout and two pots of ale, at Vauxhall, among four of us.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was Tilson the man laying asleep in the boat? A. No; I cannot say whether he was the man laying on the stairs - the man had his face down on his knees; I have not been trying to make any money of any friend of Tilson's - I have received 8l., but I hope I shall be allowed to explain it; since the prisoners were indicted a gentleman called in my neighbourhood, and made inquiries of my neighbours as to my circumstances, and left a card, inviting me to an inn in the City - his name was Waters; I went and saw him, and we got into conversation on this affair, how near I was being drowned - he said he supposed if he were to make me some recompence I should not be above receiving it; I said No, and he said, "Here are 8l.;" since then it has been said it was intended that I should not appear.
 COURT. Q. What did you receive the 8l. for? A. I took it to be from some charitable fund - he did not tell me where it came from.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What were they to give it you for? A. It might be partly for my fright - I did not take it for what I said I had lost, and in lieu of my loss of time. Mr. Mellows was with me.
   THOMAS MELLOWS. I was with the witness, and not in the boat; Peck is the man we had it of - he gave his name Cook; I was on the top of the stairs: my son came up, and said, "This man wants 4l. as security for the boat;" I said, "It is as much as the boat is worth;" I said to Peck, "What is your name?" he said Henry Cook - I said, "How comes the name of Sawyer to be on the boat?" he said, "It is all right - I bought it a few days ago;" when we got to Waterloo-bridge it began to fill with water - we got back; I waited from eight o'clock till twelve, but Peck did not come.
