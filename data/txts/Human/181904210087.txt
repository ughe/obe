 745.  JOHN MALBOROUGH was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of March , two coats, value 4 s., and one spencer, value 6 d., the goods of John Butterworth ; and one coat, value 5 s., the goods of William Hayman.
   ANN BUTTERWORTH. I am the wife of John Butterworth, who is a weigher in the Custom House. On the 16th of March, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, the coats were in the butler's pantry, at a gentleman's house which I have the care of, in Marlborough-street. My little girl called me; I ran and saw the prisoner in the passage with tracts in his hand, which he wanted to sell. I sent him away, and told him he should have knocked at the door; I bolted the door, and saw him with the coats, on the area steps. I pursued him, and secured him in the street with them - one belongs to William Hayman, and the other is my husband's.
   MARY ANN GOSS. I heard the last witness call out Stop thief! and assisted in securing the prisoner.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 746.  THOMAS POWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of March , two spoons, value 4 s. 9 d., and 12 s. in copper monies numbered, the property of Michael Brown.
   MICHAEL BROWN. I am a tailor, and live in James-street. On the 14th of March, about four o'clock, I went out with my wife - I returned about five, found my parlour window open, and the canary bird taken away, with two spoons, and 12 s. in copper. I saw the prisoner in the passage calling to the lodgers - he was apprehended next morning. I found my spoons at a silversmith's in Cheapside - I knew the prisoner before.
   THOMAS THOMPSON. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner at a public-house on Saffron-hill. I told him I took him for stealing the spoons - he said he sold them in Cheapside, and told me where to find the bird.
   JAMES STEVENS. I am clerk to Mr. Bull, who is a silversmith, and lives in Cheapside. I bought two spoons of a boy about the age of the prisoner, for 4 s. 9 d. I did not ask him any questions.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Six Months.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 747.  JOHN THOMAS was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of April , 60 lbs. of lead, value 20 s., the goods of William Marshall.
   WILLIAM MARSHALL. I am a plumber. On the 15th of April this lead stood at my door. I went to dinner about one o'clock, returned at two, and saw the prisoner walking by the door with the lead on his shoulder. I followed him, he threw it down, and I secured him without losing sight of him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM WIBER. I am a constable. I assisted in securing the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 748.  CHARLES THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of April , 56 lbs. of mutton, value 30 s., the property of William Robbins.
   WILLIAM ROBBINS. I am a butcher, and live in High-street, Shadwell. On the 24th of April, about a quarter before five o'clock in the morning, a young man, named James Stevens, knocked at my door, and informed me that my place was broken open. I ran down, found the door open, and missed a carcase of mutton - it was double-jointed all round, which was very remarkable - I never saw one like it before. I afterwards found the whole carcase cut up very badly, and can swear to it. I fitted the joints to it.
   ROBERT HICKS. I am a patrol of Old Gravel-lane. On the 24th of April, about a quarter after four o'clock in the morning, I found the prisoner laying down in a twine-shed, with his head on a bag. I asked him what he was doing there? he said he had laid down to sleep. I asked him what he had got in his bag? he said it was some rags. I found there were two fore-quarters and four other pieces of mutton - he said he found it in the West India Docks.
   JOHN PATERSON. I am a watchman. About a quarter after four o'clock in the morning of the 24th of April, Hicks brought the prisoner to me. I went into the shed, and found the hind part of the mutton hanging up about four yards from where he laid.
   WILLIAM STEVENS. On the 24th of April, about a quarter before five o'clock in the morning, I was looking through the window, and saw the prosecutor's door open; I went down, found the staple had been drawn, and gave the alarm.
   WILLIAM SUMMERS. I am a constable. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house. I found two kidnies and a large spike nail on him, and two knives, one in each stocking - I also found some fat in a bag in his hat. The prosecutor saw the mutton, and compared it with one of the knuckles - it corresponded exactly; it was double-jointed.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
