Twelfth Session, 1849-50.
721
NEW COURT.-Monday, October 21st, 1850.
PRESENT-Sir CHAPMAN Marshall, Knt., Ald.; Mr. Ald. WILLIAM
HUNTER; and Mr. COMMON SERJEANT.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant and the Fifth Jury.
1697. MARIA MARKS and JOHN MARKS, unlawfully conspiring to
defraud Henry Israel.
(MR. SERJEANT JONES offered no evidence.)
Not GUILTY.
1698. FRANCIS MATTHEWS, unlawfully attempting to defraud John
Hooker Williamson.
(MB, WOOLLETT offered no evidence.)
Not Guilty.
1699. WILLIAM THOMPSON, stealing 1 handkerchief, value 8s.; the
goods of Henry Knapp, from his person : to which he pleaded
Guilty.* Aged 18.-Confined Six Months.
1700. ALFRED JACKSON and THOMAS JONES, stealing 1 handker-
chief, value 2s. ; the goods of Thomas Ayerst, from his person. 2nd Count,
charging JACKSON with receiving.
EDWARD FUNNELL (City policeman, 32). On 19th Sept., about half-past
four o'clock in the afternoon, I was in Seething-lane-I saw the two
prisoners speak together, and walk into the crowd, and out again-I saw
Jones put his hand through his velvet jacket pocket-hole-he was then close
behind a gentleman--the gentleman moved away, and I saw his handkerchief
half out of his pocket-Jones looked round to Jackson, and they went to
another gentleman; and Jones again put his hand through his jacket pocket,
and then turned and gave something to Jackson, who put it into his trowsers
pocket, and came out of the crowd, and Jones directly followed him-I spoke
to the gentleman, and then followed Jackson-I found this handkerchief
(produced) in bis trowsers pocket.
THOMAS AYERst. I live in Surrey-square, Kent-road. I was in Seething-
lane on 19th Sept.--the officer came up, and I missed my handkerchief
this is it.
JACKSON*_GUILTY of Receiving. Aged 40.--Confined Six Months.
JONES-GUILTY of Stealing. Aged 48.-Confined Four Months.
1701. MARY AGAN, stealing a brooch, value 30l; the goods of Ben-
janin Louis Meyer Rothschild, her master, in his dwelling-house and
BARNEY AGAN feloniously receiving the same: to which
MARY AGAN pleaded GUILTY. Aged 21.-Confined Twelve Months.
John BANYARD (City-policeman, 562). On 20th Sept. I was sent for
to Mr. Annis, a pawnbroker-I took Barney Agan-I asked him how he
came in possession of this brooch-he said he had received it from his
daughter, who was in service-I asked him if he had seen it in her possession
before she went to the situation-be said he had-I asked him if he knew
how she came in possession of it-he said he never asked her that question
took him to the station--he went very readily-the inspector asked him
how he came in possession of the brooch, and he told him the same that he
had told me-he was asked where his daughter lived, and he said he did not
know the name of the street, nor the number of the house, but it was near
Flisbury-square, and he could find it.
