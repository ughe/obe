MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution. JAMES GRIFFITHS. I am a brewer, and live at Islington. The prisoner was engaged by me as a collecting clerk, at 1 l . a week, and 1 s . for each new customer he brought—these terms were afterwards changed to 30 s . a week—he was to receive the money for the ale and beer delivered to the customers, and to pay me that money on the same night, when he returned, and was to enter in a book what he had received in the day—I have that book here, in his own hand-writing—on the 15th of November here is no sum entered by him as received of Mr. Leach, of Broad-street; nor did he on that day pay me 5 s . as so received; nor has he at any time paid it me—he entered other sums as received that day, and paid them to me, but not that—it was his duty to take out, for the purpose of giving receipts to the customers, a little book, containing blank forms of receipts—he ought not to receive money without giving one of these forms, or a bill with a printed head—he is not my partner.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  How long had he been in your ser vice? A.  About three months—he received 5 l . or 6 l . or 8 l . or 10 l . a day—there are several entries by him on the 15th of November—I think he left me on the 24th of January—I am not aware that any customers left me when he did—his brother came to me shortly after he left—I think I found out about this 5 s . at out three weeks after he left—his brother did not complain that I had been spreading reports against the prisoner's honesty—he said, if there was any thing wrong he would make it right—another bro ther of the prisoner told me where I could find him—when his brother called on me I knew of one charge, but not of this. ROBERT LEACH. I am a plumber, and live in Broad-street. On the 15th of November I paid the prisoner five shillings on account of his master, Mr. Griffiths—I have his receipt here—I saw him write it—I knew it was the custom to give printed receipts—I looked at this receipt when he gave it me—I forget the words I made use of, but I know the prisoner said, "I have given you this on a blank piece of paper, as I have not any printed receipts with me," and he said, "I am a partner with Mr. Griffiths." NOT GUILTY.
 810. JOHN SINFIELD was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of feb ruary , 1 metal cap, value 3 s  ., the goods of the London Dock Company .
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. HUGH SMITH. I am assistant-foreman at the London Docks. On the 5th of February I was going towards the North-quay, and saw the prisoner, who was a stranger—I made some inquiries of him, and he walked away—I followed him, and asked what he wanted—he said, "Nothing"—he had a blue coat on, over a smock frock—I asked what he had got under his coat—he said, "Nothing"—I put my hand under it, and found this piece of brass—I saw a belt under his frock, his frock was turned in, and that supported the piece of metal—I asked where he got it—he said he picked it up, and pointed to a place where it came from—he then began to cry, and begged my pardon. DANIEL RUDKINS. I am a constable of the London Docks. On the 5th of February I took the prisoner from the witness, with this metal cap—the prisoner said he picked it up—I went to examine the jiggers and crabs, and I found a deficiency, and this piece fitted exactly—the nuts and staples were left in the crab, and this had been taken away—he was not in the employ of the Docks, and had no right there.
