HENRY BALSER. I am a dock labourer, but at present I look after a
cab rank at Drury Lane-on June 6th, about 1 o'clock at night I was in
Osborne Street, Whitechapel, on my way home a woman asked me for
twopence for a night's lodging, and I gave it to her-I walked on a little
further in the middle of the road, when the prisoner and four other men
came up to me--the prisoner got hold of my arms and pulled them over
my head, and another put his hand over my mouth while another took
8fd. from my waistcoat pocket-I shouted, a policeman came up, and I
gave the prisoner into custody-I was perfectly sober.
Cross-examined by the prisoner. There was no woman in the street
when you set upon me-the woman I gave twopence to was at the corner
of the Whitechapel Road-you hit me twice under the jaw-you did not
rob me, but you held me up while the others did.
Isaac MALLETT (135 H.) At 1 a.m. on June 6th I was on duty in White-
chapel Road-I heard shouts of “ Police,” I ran into Osborne Street, and
saw the prosecutor and the prisoner-the prosecutor said, “This man has
knocked me down and four others have rifled my pockets and taken 8/d.
from my waistcoat pocket"-I asked him if he was sure that Jacobs
was one of the men, and he said yes--I took the prisoner to the station,
and in reply to the charge he said, "A woman asked me to take this man
&way from her as he was going to kill her "-he was sober--no money was
found on him-the prosecutor was sober.
Cross-examined. I never saw any woman about when I came up.
Prisoner's defence. “I know nothing about the robbery or the violence;
I am quite innocent of it.”
