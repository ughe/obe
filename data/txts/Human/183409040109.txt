 1408.  JOHN HANCOCK was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of August , 1 counterpane, value 4s.; 2 blankets, value 6s.; and 1 gown, value 1s.; the goods of Thomas Carter.
 THOMAS CARTER. I am  coachman to a lady, in Old Burleigh-street. I occupy a room over a stable in Brooke's-mews- I went out of town on the 23rd of August, and returned on the 27th - I then missed these articles - they were all there when I left - I had known the prisoner eighteen months.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Had he lodged with you? A. No; I gave him leave to sleep in the spare stall in the stable - I did not tell him he should not sleep there any more - I did not put his things away from the premises, but they were not in any room - the place where his things were, was open to half a dozen people - they were in an open space belonging to all persons - it was not locked up.
   GEORGE ORPWOOD. I am a coachman, and live in this mews - on the 23rd of August, about twelve o'clock at night, I was awoke - I heard somebody lift the latch - I heard it a second time - I got up and heard the prisoner's voice, asking if any body was at home - he then went towards Carter's room, and unlocked the door or something like it- he remained there some time - he then came out, and returned a second time to Carter's room - he remained there some time with a woman, and then went down - I looked and saw the woman go away first, and he after her - I got up at half-past five o'clock - I saw him coming up the mews, and he opened his trunk, and put something in.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you know that he had been in the habit of being there? A. Yes; in the stable - he had nothing when he went out the second time.
   JAMES NEWMAN(police-constable C 109). I was on duty in South Moulton-street - I met the prisoner, and after he had passed me, I picked up a duplicate - I had not seen it there before - I went to the place it referred to, and found these articles.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long was it since you had been at that place? A. About ten minutes - there were not many persons then passing - it was half-past five o'clock- it had not been there all night, for it was quite light, and I must have seen it.
   GEORGE FARMER. I am a pawnbroker. I took in these two blankets and counterpane of a man, but I cannot say who.
   THOMAS JOSEPH WEST(police-serjeant C 15). I took the prisoner - in going down the street I saw his hand was in his pocket - I said, "Keep your hand out of your pocket," and within a few yards the officer found the duplicate - this was a week after the robbery.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Confined for Six Months.
 1409.  TIMOTHY KAYLOR and BENJAMIN BROMMAGE were indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 6d., the goods of Saint John Mason , from his person.
 2nd COUNT. For stealing from a person unknown.
   GEORGE ANTHONY. I live in Park-street, Camden-town. On the 24th of July, a few minutes past ten o'clock at night, I was at the top of Tottenham-court-road- I saw the two prisoners and another attempting to pick pockets - I then saw Kaylor go and pick Mr. Mason's pocket of his handkerchief - I had watched them for half an hour - I called the policeman, and went and told the gentleman he had been robbed - I then crossed and took the prisoners - this handkerchief was found on Kaylor.
   JOHN WILLIAMS(police-constable E 99). I had been watching the prisoners for some time - Anthony spoke to me - I took Kaylor, and found this handkerchief in his right hand trowsers pocket.
 (  Henry Tapp, No. 2, Cromer-place; and Celia Barton, of No. 9, Spa-place, gave Brommage a good character.)
 KAYLOR -    GUILTY. Aged 14.
 BROMMAGE -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
