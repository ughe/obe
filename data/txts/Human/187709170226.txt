I am the wife of Frederick Taylor Oram, a clerk and commisson agent, of 101, St. John Street Road, Clerkenwell—Allen and his wife in May occupied the breakfast parlour facing the street—I heard of his being taken—shortly before that I saw some silk in his room—I saw it taken away in a horse cloth by two men, one was named Johnson; I saw the rep lying in the same breakfast parlour on a couch—Allen went with the men.
   Cross-examined by MR. STRAIGHT . All this was done openly—I saw the silk, I believe, on 5th June—I cannot say when it came—it might have been in the house a week—it was a pure white silk—I did not closely examine it—it was not similar to this produced, but softer—I could not tell you how much there was; I did not count the pieces, because it was usual for Allen to buy things to sell—I was not astonished to see it there.
   Witnesses for Harris. LEWIS HARRIS. I live with my son Morris Harris, at 8, Great Prescott Street, where he carries on business as a shirt manufacturer and warehouseman—my age is seventy-five; my son keeps me and I help him in the business—I know Johnson—he is a sailors' outfitter in Shadwell, and has dealt with my son—in May he owed my son 20 l . odd, I called for it scores of times, twice or three times a day—on 4th June, Johnson brought me some samples of silk, and I told my son I could not get Johnson to pay my money, and that he would sell it in part payment of his debt—it was about 1,000 yards—I Johnson afterwards called on my son and asked if the silks would suit him as he would take it off his debt and added, "And the balance you can pay me in goods or cheque or both"—he asked 1 s . a yard for it, and then 10 d . as my son had had so much trouble—my son said he would see it and give him an answer—he saw it next morning and said he would take it at 10 d . in the afternoon—Johnson came again in the afternoon, the account was gone into and my son signed this cheque for 22 l ., and this receipt for 48 l . was given him by Johnson, my son giving Johnson 6 l . in goods, and the cheque for 22 l . to make up the balance, the debt being 20 l .—about two days after I went to Messrs. Spiers with a sample of the silk—my son did not know of my going; he does not attend to this part of the business—I went back to Prescott Street, and told my son of an appointment which had been made for between 10 and 11 o'clock on the morrow when Mr. Spiers foreman was coming down, and the foreman did come down, and I was there when the silk was shown—I had left the price 1 s . 2 d . a yard at Mr. Spiers' warehouse, which is a fair price for such a house, as they often want discount; I gave Johnson's receipt to the officer—it was among the invoices which my son had prepared to post at the end of the month.
