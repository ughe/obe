an hour after, and went up the Colne Road and met Mr. Frederick Mussett,

can I see you home?' I said Oh, no, I do not want anyone to see me
home. I afterwards went into the George public-house with him, and we
had a glass of ale. We then left, and he said he would see me safe home.

would before he went much further. He afterwards had connection with
me. It was about 5.30 in the evening. He then offered me a sovereign,
I refused to take it. He then said have not got any more change only
38. 6d., but I have got a bank note, which he took out of his purse and
gave it to me, and I took it, also the 3s. 6d. I said to him 'What am I to

you came by it, tell them you have lived with me three or four years, and
that is some of your wages. I then went about half a mile further with him,
and then left him. It was near Chappell Railway Station. I did not know
