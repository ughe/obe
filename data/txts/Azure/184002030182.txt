MARY FEBOVSON. I am the wife of Charles Ferguson, who keeps that
shop. I came into the shop while the prisoner was there—it was quite
light—it was a little after three o'clock—I received the crown-piece, and
gave the change—he left the shop directly, and immediately he left I dis-
covered that the 58. piece was bad—I put it into my apron pocket, and kept
it separate from all other money—I gave it to Emmerson the policeman
on that day week—it was marked in my presence—I am certain it was the
one I received from the prisoner—I had no other.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen him before A. No—ehe looked
down on the floor—he did not look us in the face—he had bought the
valentine before I came in—he had a hat on, and was dressed in a flannel
jacket— I saw him again on the following week—I had given a description
of him as having a downcast look, and I described his dress.
ELIZABETH MILLARD re-examined. I told the policeman he was dressed
in a flannel jacket, and had an apron round his waist—I did not say any
thing about his having a downcast look.
THOMAS EMMERSON (police-sergeantV16.) Between three and four o'clock
in the afternoon, on the 23rd of January, I was in Lark Hall-lane, and saw
the prisoner, and a female with him, going towards London, away from
the prosecutor's shop—on the 30th of January I Gas near the Vaux-
hall gate, and saw the prisoner and two more persons in custody, one of
whom was the female I had seen the prisoner with on the 23rd—I went
with the prisoner to Mr. Ferguson's shop—I saw Mrs. Ferguson—she gave
me this crown-piece—I went with the prisoner to the station-house at
Clapham, and found on him Is. 6d., and a new pair of braces were found in
his hat, which are worth about a shilling.
Cross-examined. Q. Had Mrs. Ferguson previously given you a de-
scription of the man? A. She sent the description by another sergeant to
the station-house.
COURT. Q. What description had been left? A young man, with a
flannel jacket, and an apron, and a pencil sticking out of his pocket—the
prisoner was so dressed when I saw him, and also when he was taken, and
he had a pencil in his pocket, and part of it stuck out.
