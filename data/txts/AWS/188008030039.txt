Cross-examined. Mr. Barker is not hereI have invoiced goods direct
to Pretty and Morton, but 44 Stock" has always been written on the invoices
-I never made an cut sale to them-they never made a purchase of me
which was charged against the commission aecount-they have not made
direct purchases from me-I never consigned goods to any place in England
for sale by auetion, nor were any sold by auction at iny direction- have sent
goods abroad for sale, but do not know how they were sold-umbrellas do not
depreciate in value for a year-Mr Pretty retired in 1878, and Morton
went on; besides being our agents they were independent merchants,
excepting in umbrellas-they could not retain our agency and have another
-they could sell to wholesale houses only-I cannot say what discretion
they had-you will find the terms of the contract there--the prisoner
frankly told me what he had done, but I do not think I made any remark,
I only asked why he could dispose of goods in that way-I was on very
good terms with him-I went and had some refreshment with him-I said
that he could not starve though he had committed a breach of morals and
a breach of contract--I had a statement with me, which I correeted by his
duplicate books, showing the account between him and the firn-I did not
take stock, I assume that he had disposed of 4001. oddthere is no ledger
account-I did not draw up the account debiting him with the value of the
goods which he has not accounted for by regular sales-Mr. Barker has
only been up to town once since Mr. Pretty retired.
Re-examined. After the prisoner gave me the pawn tickets I told him
that I must communicate with Mr. Barker, which I did that day or the
next-I put myself in the hands of the solicitors, and took what course
they thought right-the prisoner was not authorised to pledge goods in
any way.
ROBERT OUTRAM (Detective Oficer). A warrant was placed in my hands
on 10th May, upon which I took the prisoner on 23rd July, about 11 p.m.,
at 8, Brunswick Road, Tottenham.
UITY.-Telve Months' Imprisonment.
