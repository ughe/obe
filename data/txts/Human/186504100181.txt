—I saw some bonds, called Micklam bonds, in Mr. Tayloe's hands—there is a bill now pending in the House of Lords which will make them good or bad, according to how the House of Lords decide on a question of Scotch law—they have been treated by other clients of mine at valuable securities, and dealt with as such—they are chargeable on estates in Scotland—Bunyard gave them to the bank in October—I did not go to Mr. Terry for the execution of the trust deed; I think Mr. Terry might have considered that he was right—I considered that it was the best way of dealing with the estate.
   MR. GIFPARD . Q.  The best thing for Mr. Bunyard? A.  For the creditors generally; it was supposed that a large surplus would have been realized, which would have paid the debts in full; at present those securities are not realized—there has been no dividend made yet—besides those bonds, there is security to the amount of 9,000 l ., upon which they have a claim of 6,000 l .—the creditors are "nursing" the securities—there was also a sum of 200 l . in the Bankruptcy Court, which was swallowed up in costs—un less the House of Lords reverse the latest decision, the bonds are worth nothing—Mr. Micklam has since gone into the Bankruptcy Court, but that does not affect them, because they are real estates, and 16,000 l . has been voted to carry the question through the House of Lords—Mr. Chidley is the solicitor to the appeal—we thought it more prudent to have a composition deed to carry the matter out of the Bankruptcy Court.
