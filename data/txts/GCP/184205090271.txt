1717. LUCY COLLARD was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
March, 1 shirt, value 10s. ; 1 sheet, value 98.; and 2 handkerchiefs, value
58.; the goods of William Hayden, her master.
WILLIAM HAYDEN. I am a haberdasher-the prisoner was my servant
of all-work, and had 8l. a year. On Sunday, the 24th of April I missed
some things, which made me look to see if any thing else was gone, and
I missed a sheet- I told her I would not be satisfied till I had got the sheet
--in consequence of what she said to me I found the shirt, sheet, and two
handkerchiefs at Mr. Turner's, a pawnbroker, in Walworth-road-I went
there in consequence of seeing the duplicate in her hand-she said she had
pawned the sheet- she was not willing for me to get it out, she wished to
get it out herself-I said, "You have no money to get it out"-she said
she would pawn some of her own things to get it.
Q. How came you to know she had the duplicate ? 4. We said no-
thing about the sheet till Monday morning-I then pressed her to tell me
where it was-she said she knew nothing about it-I said nobody had ac-
cess to it but herself-then she said she had pawned it, and produced the
duplicate-she said there was only the sheet and her own shawl-I went
to Turner's, and found the articles-had seen the shirt safe about a fort-
night before-the handkerchiefs were in the back-warehouse-I knew the
papers they were in were there, but I did not look into them--the police-
man found a pair of gloves, which I kept in the same chest of drawers up
stairs with the shirt—the drawer was found broken open on the Sunday
night-I had seen it safe that afternoon-I always kept it locked-I do
not think I had opened it that day-I gave her into custody on the 25th
of April.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. How long had she been in your
service ? A. Since the 21st of February-she came as a single woman,
I have since found she is married-she told me after a long time that she
had pawned the sheet and produced the duplicate, and said she had
pawned a shawl of her own at the same time, but that did not prove true
---I am a widower--my family consists of a servant and four children-
the prisoner did not say she had pledged the things for the purpose of send-
ing money down to her son at Bristol-1 let her have a sovereign on the
15th of March, and afterwards 58. on the 26th, to send her son-I saw
Turner's name on the duplicate-I think I can swear to the bandkerchiefs
when I see them the prisoner said she had brought them from Bristol with
her after we found them-she did not deny the shirt and sheet being mine.
CHRISTOPHER THOMAS GREAVES. I am apprentice to Turner, a pawn-
broker. I have a shirt, sheet, and two silk bandkerchiefs I wrote the
duplicate produced for it on the 5th of March, for 88.- they were pledged
by the woman in the name of Ann Hemmings--I cannot speak to the
prisoner.
MR. HAYDEN re-examined. This shirt, sheet, and handkerchiefs I can
swear to-the shirt has my name on it-I know the handkerchiefs, in
consequence of their being in a damp place they got spotted, and I altered
the price of them all, and puta fresh mark on them--the mark is not here-
produce one of the same quality with the same sort of spots on it-I also
know the gloves.
