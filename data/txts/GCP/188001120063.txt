JOSEPH MARKS, I was an office-boy in the employ of Messrs. Learoyd
and Wilton - I went there at the beginning of January, and remained
until the failure-I recollect Mr. Dunderdale first coming there he came
to the office every day when he was in London, and had an opportunity of
seeing what took place --I have seen Mr. Cauchai at the office and Mr.
Allen, not Dunckley or Faulkner-when Caucbai or Allen called they
wed to go into a private office-Mr. Wilton was there and sometimes
Mr. Dunderdale--they called from about March up to the time of the
failure-I have overheard conversa ions between Wilton and Dunderdale
respecting pledging woollen goods and getting advances on them from
Cauchai, Allen, Dunckley, and Faulkner-I have very often heard that
conversation they were speaking of the quality of the goods and what
would be advanced upon them if Mr. Wilton was not there when Cauchsi
and Allen called Dunderdale saw them--when Wilton has been leaving the
office during the day I have heard bim say that he was going to Faulkner's
or Dunckley's I know that woollen goods were being bought by the firm
and they were pledged—I did not know of any -ale of goods whilst I was
there-there was no salesman employed-no patterns were sent for the pur-
pose of sale about London, only to Paris and Genoa— the invoices which the
manufacturers sent were first copied by Griffiths and kept in the outer
office, and the originals were given back to Mr. Wilton-I recollect transfer
orders being written ; I copied them into the private letter-hook--Mr.
Wilton gave them me to copy, and sometimes Mr. Dunderdale-after
copying them I gave them back to Mr. Wilton- there were two such
books; one was a continuation of the other I last saw them about a
fortnight before the failure there was no other book to my knowledge
which contained any record of those transfer orders they referred to
woollen goods, and were transferred into the names of Allen, Cauchai,
Faulkner, and Dunckley.
