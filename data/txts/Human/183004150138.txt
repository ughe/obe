989. MARY ANN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March, 1 watch, value 1l.; 1 seal, value 3s.; 2 watch-keys, value 2s.; 1 ring, value 2d., and 1 watch-ribbon, value 1d., the goods of Thomas Walker , from his person.
   JAMES DITTUM. I was going to my work at half-past one o'clock in the morning, on the 13th of March, and met the prisoner - the prosecutor was following her, crying Stop thief! and she ran towards me; I went to stop her, she turned back; I ran and took her - she fell down, and threw a watch from her; I held her down till the prosecutor came up, and took her - I saw the constable take up the watch, and I took up the case; the prosecutor charged her with stealing it, in my presence - she said it was not her, it was another girl with her.
   WILLIAM EVERETT. I am an officer. I heard Stop thief! called, but did not see the prisoner taken; on going down Gold's-hill I saw the prosecutor, who said she had got his watch from him; she said she had not taken it, but another girl had - she ran, and was stopped; I asked where the other girl threw the watch - she pointed out the place; I took it up, and the witness took the case - the prosecutor gave his name as Thomas Walker - he is master of a vessel.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I not say I was not the person? A. Yes; I saw the prosecutor and her stand at the end of Gold's-hill. as I was going on my beat, five minutes before the alarm; there was another girl at the other corner - the prosecutor said it was his watch; he is gone to sea.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the man, who asked me to go home with him; I said I would not - I saw him talking to another woman; I went down Three Cups-alley - he came and took hold of me, and I said, "Let me go - I will walk with you;" we went on to Gold's-hill, and this witness came, and took me. GUILTY (of stealing, but not from the person.) Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 990.  JOHN MASON was indicted for embezzlement.
   THOMAS EVANS. I am a milkman. The prisoner was in my employ, and was to receive money, which he was to pay as soon as he came home.
   FRANCIS DAVISON. I paid the prisoner 11d. on the 14th of March, about eight o'clock in the morning, for his master.
   ELIZABETH FOSTER. I am in Evans' employ. I never received the 11d. - he says he put it on the table; my master was very sleepy, and we think he took it up.
   ELIZABETH DAVIS. I was in the prosecutor's employ. The prisoner never brought his account right; he never paid me the 11d.
   JOHN EDDS. I met the prisoner on the 16th; I took him up for a waistcoat which he had on; the prosecutor swore to that at first, but denied it afterwards.
     NOT GUILTY.
 991.  GEORGE HOWSE was indicted for embezzlement.
   JOHN MARRIOTT. I am a carman. The prisoner was in my employ on the 25th of February; he sometimes received money for me, and was to bring it me on that day - he received 5s. from Mr. Gray, which he never paid me; he did not return.
   GEORGE GRAY. I paid the prisoner 5s. on the 25th of February, to take to Mr. Marriott, for him to buy a sack of potatoes.
 Prisoner. He said I was to bring them - he did not say I was to give it to Mr. Marriott. Witness. I meant it for Mr. Marriott; he had his horse and cart with him.
   THOMAS FARRANT. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on the 26th of February; I charged him with stealing this - he said he was very sorry, but he had the misfortune to lose it.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Confined Three Months.
 992.  THOMAS HAY was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of April , 1 watch, value 30s.; 1 watch chain, value 3s., and 2 seals, value 3s., the goods of James Dingwell , from his person.
 JAMES DINGWELL. I am a  carpenter, and live in Edward-street, Battle-bridge. On the 13th of April I was drinking at the Maidenhead public-house - I came out for a short time, and was sick; I sat down, and saw the prisoner there - he offered me help, and advised me to give him my watch to take into the bar, or perhaps I should be robbed of it; I did not give it him - he unbuttoned my waistcoat, and said by getting the air I should be relieved: he then unbuttoned the band of my trousers: I looked down, and saw my watch hanging, (I believe.) by a button; I grasped at it, but he was too active - he got it, and handed it to another person, who got off; I seized the prisoner, took him into the house, and charged him with stealing it - he opened his waistcoat, and pleaded innocence; he said he was not the man - he then attempted to run away, but he did not escape my notice; I saw an officer, and gave him in charge.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. When was this? A. On Tuesday afternoon - I am sure it was not Monday; I believe I had drank a small glass of gin at another public-house in the morning - I might have some porter at eleven o'clock; we had some ale at the Maidenhead - this happened between four and five o'clock; the ale had not impaired my intellects - I went into a corner which was parted off; I did not sit down in the fifth, there is a stone on which I sat - I was almost asleep when he aroused me; I looked at my watch after he came up - I was not in such a state that I did not know whether I had my watch; he came to me two or three times - I was not asleep, I was heavy.
   WILLIAM FOSTER. I saw the prosecutor on the 13th of April, against the Maidenhead - I saw the prisoner take something out of his pocket and give it to another, but what I cannot say; the other ran away - the prosecutor seized the prisoner, and gave him in charge.
 Cross-examined. Q. What did you do? A. Nothing -I did not go after the young man who ran off; he turned too quick for me - I did not see the prisoner put his hand into the prosecutor's pocket, but I saw it against the band of his breeches; I think he was unbuttoning it.
   SAMUEL PRIME. I know the prosecutor - he lodged with me; I saw his watch about two o'clock that afternoon.
   THOMAS WOFFINDEN. I am a Police-officer. I was going round Battle-bridge - the prosecutor had the prisoner by the collar, and charged him with taking his watch; he said he did not take it.
