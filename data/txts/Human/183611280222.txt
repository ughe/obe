   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you say a word of this conversation before the Magistrates? A.  No—I could not, he was committed at the time—I went into the wood from half-past four to five o'clock, and stayed there till a quarter past ten o'clock—that was the time the men first came into the wood—it had gone ten o'clock—I am certain that is the right hour—I got to the cot tage after the man ran away—I met the prisoner in the road before I saw him in the cottage—when I met him it was, I suppose, very near 11 o'clock: be cause when I came back, Mr. Watson, of the White Horse, was about to put his shutters up; that was above half an hour after I saw him in the wood—it was near 11 o'clock when I met him in the road—I wished the tall one good night, because the prisoner crossed the road—I passed them both—I was in plain clothes—the tall one had seen me, I know, because I spoke to him when he came out of the cottage—I do not say that he knew me to be a policeman—I put the shoe alongside of the mark, and it was the same length—many working people's shoes are made very much alike, but some are longer than others—there was no occasion for any pressure—when I asked him about the shoes, he said they were not his, then he hesitated, and then picked them out again—I saw him at the foot of the ladder—he had shoes on then.
