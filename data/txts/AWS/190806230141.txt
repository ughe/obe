JOHN DUNLOP, Hove, near Brighton, colliery agent. In 1899 I was
agent for Messrs. Evans and Company, of Cardiff, colliery owners,
who were supplying the coal known as Nixon's Merthyr, I supplied
prisoner with that coal in 1899 and 1900, and up. to Lady Day, 1901.
Between January 6, 1899, and October 23, 1899, the quantity I
supplied him with was 930 tons 2 ewt.. the prices being 11s. 11s. 3d..
11s. 6d., and 12s. coal and waggon. Then there would be 7s. 10d. to
be added for the rilway, so that the price in London varied between
18s. 10d. and 19s. 10d. On July 6, 1900, I entered into a contract
with Mr. Cade to supply Nixon's Merthyr, and that contrast ran till
Mareh 31, 1901. The coal had risen considerably and was 19s. per
ton coal And waggon and 7s. 10d. for the railway, making 26s. 10d.
I had 8 later contract with prisoner in October, 1900, for the supply
of Nixon's Merthyr, under which I supplied 522 tons 2 owt. The
seam was not worked out in 1901. Messrs. Evans and Company's
lease ran out, I think, in March, 1903. The colliery belongs to the
Marquis of Bute.
Cross-examined. There was not stoppage between March and Sep-
tember, 1901. The pits were going all the time. I have known Mr.
Cade close upon 20 years on the Coal Exehange in the way of business,
not as a personal friend. His reputation on the Exchange is very
good one for fair dealing and honesty, and he is respeeted on the
market. I never heard anyone say a word against him. I do not know
what average per ton the coal merehant has to pay before he can make
profit. As an agent that does not interest me. The price of Nixon's
Navigation would be about 1s. to 1s. 3d. above that of Nixon's Mer-
thyr The latter is a very good coal for stationary boilers, like that
of the Guardians, but not a first-class coal.
