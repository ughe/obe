Victoria Street-in July or August last Crighton came and wanted to
obtain some goods on credit-no particular amount was mentioned-I said
it was our custom to have cash or good references he gave the name of
Mrs. Roberts as a reference; he said she was his landlady-I communicated
with Mrs. Roberts and she came to our place with Crighton and signed this
guarantee (this was dated 9th August, 1877, and was to the amount of 502)
she said she had known Crighton for some years as a very respectable man,
and was anxious to help him as much as she possibly could-in the mean-
time I had sent up my man to the neighbourhood-goods were supplied to the
amount of 201—about 2nd Nov. Crightop was indebted to me about 401.-I
afterwards sted Roberts on the guarantee, but never got any money.
Cross-examined by Roberts. I don't recollect that Crighton gave any other
reference than to you—he ordered 201. worth of goods—these (produred)
are two of the receipts I gave, Aug. 9 and Oct. 10—we had 201. on account
-you called on me afterwards to obtain more goods to carry on the business,
and paid for what you had—that was after the credit was stopped-you said
if I gave you time the balance that was due should be paid.

John JARVIS. I am accountant and manager to Henry Archer and Sons,
171, Strand, tobacco manufacturers about the latter end of 1877 or early
in 1878 I saw Mrs. Roberts several times in company with Crighton-she
gave the name of Crighton; I believed them to be husband and wife
she bought goods, and for about two months paid cash, ultimately she got
credit, and it ended in owing our firm over 401. in about three months the
last goods were supplied on 27th April, 1878 I went several times to
Belgrave Street and saw both the prisoners I did what I could to get
money, but never got ene penny.
