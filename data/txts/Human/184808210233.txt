JOHN PAYNE( policeman, T  252). About four o'clock, on the afternoon of 18th July, I was on duty at Old Brentford, which is about three miles from Richmond and saw Brown—I had received information, and asked her what she had got in her bundle—she said, "Two shirts"—I asked whether they were her own—she said, no, they were her brother's—I asked where her brother was—she said, at the public-house (pointing to the One Tun); that she was going to sell the shirts for her brother—I went to the One Tun, and found no one there—I asked what the shirts were marked with—she said, "Red"—I asked what the initials were—she said she could not tell me—I afterwards showed them to Mrs. Eden—she identified them—these are them ( produced ). EMANUEL HARRIS( policeman, V  184). In consequence of information, I went to a pawnbroker's, at Brentford, and found some boots—I heard Brown say at the station-house that the shirts and boots were her brother's, and that she had pawned them—she said her brother was outside—I went outside, found Smith there, and brought him in—I asked Brown if that was the person she wanted—she said, "Yes"—I asked him where he got the boots—he said they were his own, he had them made at Northampton—I asked him if he sent her to pledge them—he said he did, he gave 14 s . for them—I asked him what stockings he had on—he pulled up his trowsers, and I found these ( produced ), which Mrs. Eden identifies—I asked him where he got them—he made no answer—I had seen him about 100 yards from the house about twelve o'clock on the night of the 17th—I do not know whether he was alone or not—he is a stranger to me. WILLIAM LINEY( policeman, T  5). The male prisoner told me the two shirts were his, and that he had them made in Hampshire—I said, "Are they marked?"—he said, "Yes"—I asked how—he said he did not know—I got the shirts from Brown. ABRAHAM REED. I am assistant to Mr. Reed, a pawnbroker, at Brentford. I produce a pair of boots and a shirt, pledged on 18th July, by Brown—the ticket has "Ann Brown" on it ( produced ). JOHN EDEN. These are my boots—I know them by the wear—they were safe that night. ELIZABETH EDEN re-examined.  These shirts and stockings are my hus band's property—here is the mark.
   Smith's Defence.  I found the bundle in a hedge.
   Brown's Defence.  I met this prisoner; he said he had had no breakfast; I said I had had none, and I pledged the things to get some money; he is not my brother.
   SMITH  — GUILTY of Stealing  . Aged 17.
   BROWN  — GUILTY of Receiving  . Aged 72. Confined Twelve Months

 2047. JOHN SAUNDERS and GEORGE JONES , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and DAWSON conducted the Prosecution. MARY SYEDS. My husband is a tobacconist, in Rotherhithe Wall. On 15th Aug., Saunders came for half an ounce of tobacco—he paid me with a very new shilling of the Queen's reign—I gave him change for it, and put it into my pocket—I had two old shillings there—a policeman came—I took the three shillings out of my pocket, and at once recognized this one pro duced, which I had taken of Saunders—the other two were King William's—I gave it to the policeman—this is like the paper of tobacco I sold to Saunders. ROBERT WILLIAM ANDERSON. I am a fancy soap-maker, of Deptford.
