(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
1949. WILLIAM TURNER, breaking and entering the shop of Charles
Slinn, and stealing 23 pairs of boots, 3 calf-skins, and other articles, value 201.;
his goods.
MR. CAARTEEN conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN SHIRLEY. I am errand-boy, in the employ of Mr. Slinn, boot
and shoemaker, 103, High Holborn. On Monday, 15th Oct., about nine
o'clock at night, I left my master's shop to take out some work-I left the
door quite secure and locked-left no one in the shop-I returned about &
quarter to ten, and found the door open-I pushed it wide open, and two
young men came out-the prisoner is one-he said, “Is your master at
home !"-I said, “No"-be up with his hand, hit me a blow in the chest,
and ran up Hatton-garden--the other man ran down Holborn-hill-I ran
after the prisoner towards Hatton-garden, crying out, "Stop thiefP" and
Williams came up and stopped him about 100 yards from my master's shop
I then went back to the shop with Cagford, and found this black bag (pro-
duced), with boots in it, just inside the shop doorI then went to the station,
returned again, and found this white bag (produced) very nearly under the
window, inside the shop—the bags were opened at the station-the black one
contained twelve pairs of boots and one odd one, eight pairs of shoes and one
odd one; and in the white bag there were eleven pairs of boots and one odd
one-they all belong to the master-when I left the shop the window was fall
of boots, and when I came back it was all cleared out-1 found a parcel on the
counter containing leather, of the same kind as my master had, and which was
hanging on the nails when I went out--I had no light-there are lights in
the street, and next door there is a public-house where there are two lamps.
John WILLIAMS, I am a labourer. On 15th Oct. I was four or five yards
from Mr. Slinn's shop, and saw the two men come out of the shop-I am sure the
prisoner is one of them I did not see him strike Shirley-be rap up Hatton-
garden-I ran after him, stopped him, and Adams came up immediately.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. How far up Hatton-garden was it?
A. It might have been twenty-five yards up-I did no lose sight of him from
the time he came out of the house.

Singing out “Stop thief!"-I saw Williarns stop the prisoner--I then went
