746. WILLIAM JOHNSON PLEADED GUILTY** to possessing counter-
feit coin, after a conviction in September, 1885, of feloniously uttering
counterfeit coin.-Five Years' Ponal Servitudo.
747. ELLEN WILMOTT (30), Unlawfully uttering a counterfeit
crown.
MR. PARTRIDGE Prosecuted.
WILLIAM WARRINER. I am a clerk in the Post Office, Porchester
Road, Bayswater-at 6.15 on Wednesday, 8th June, the prisoner asked
for a 58. postal-order and six stampe, tendering this counterfeit Jubilee
58. piece and seven coppers-I gave her the order and stamps, put the
coppers in the till, and tested the crown, but not sufficiently--the pri-
soner went out, and I ran out and went to a large drapery firm-I after
wards spent about a quarter of an hour looking for her-I went out of
the post office not three seconds after her, and did not see her the fol-
lowing evening I took the crown to the police-station and gave it to
Detective Fuller, with a description of the person-I afterwards saw her
at the station, and identified her from eleven or twelve other women.
ROBERT FULLER (Detective F). On 9th June Warriner gave me this
58. piece about eight p.m.--I have kept it ever since.
DAVID Cox (Detective Hl). On 24th June I went with Camber to 100,
Lancaster Street, Borough, and arrested the prisoner on the first floor
back room on another charge-I took her to the station-100, Lancaster
Street, is a private house, where the prisoner and Johnson were living
together; the landlord pointed out a room to me.
JOHN SHELTON. I am a pensioner from the Metropolitan Police Force,
and am landlord of 100, Lancaster Street, Borough-the prisoner and
Johnson took & room there on 1st June, as Mr. and Mrs. Wilmott,
and lived there as man and wife till 24th June-on the Wednesday, 23rd,
before she was taken on the Friday, Johnson said he thought the woman
was going wrong, and he was going to leave her; she said he was going
wrong, and she should have to leave him--they did not leave my house
till they were taken; the man was taken first.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to H.M. Mint-this
is a counterfeit crown of the Jubilee type, very well made.
The prisoner, in her statement before the Magistrato, and in her defonce, said
that she did not know the coin was bad,
