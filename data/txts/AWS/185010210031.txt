1727. EDWARD COOTE, stealing, whilst employed in the Post-office,
a
letter cuntaining a half-sovereign; the moneys of her Majesty's Postmaster-
General.
MEssrs. CLARKSON and BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
WALTER ROBERTSON SCULTHORPE. I am one of the presidents of the
London district offices of the General Post-office. On Saturday, 28th Sept.,
for reasons I had, E made up this letter (produced),. and put into it a half-
sovereign-I sealed it as it is sealed now, and saw it addressed to Miss
Mary Ann Ingram, Cliff-terrace, near Clifton-bars, Margate, Kent" then
gave it to Peak, the officer, and directed him to post it at the Tottenham
North-office on Monday morning-if it was posted at half-past seven on
Monday morning, it would be forwarded by the mail-cart to the principal
office at Tottenham, where it would arrive about ten o'clockit would then
be placed in the London bag, and forwarded to the chief office in London-on
Mooday morning, 30th, I proceeded to Stamford-hill- got there about ten
o'clock-] there met the mail-cart that was bringing the bag from Tottenbam
-I took possession of the bag, and opened itit did not contain this letter
-I then went with Peak and Mr. Cole, one of the inspectors of letter-
carriers, to the principal office at Tottenham, at which the prisoner was
employed as a letter-carrier-I arrived there about half-past ten-I saw the
prisoner leave the office, and when he had got about fifty yards I stopped
him, and asked whether he had cut open the Tottenbam North-office bag that
morning- meant merely opened it in the usual course-the said, "No, Bowers
did"-(be is another letter-carrier employed in the same office)- asked
whether he had sorted any of the letters---he said he bad-I told him there
was a money-letter missing-be made no answer- then directed Peak to
search him, and I saw him take this letter out of the inner pocket of his coat
-it was in the same state as it is now, unopenedml then said to the
prisoner, How do you account for having this letter in your pocket "whe
said, I did not know it was there"- then gave him into custody--the
letter bears the stamp of the North Tottenham-office-ther is no date to it
(the toitness here opened the letter, and it contained a halfsovereigw)-thi is
my half-sovereign- put some mark on it-the prisoner would in sorting the
letters have the opportunity of seeing the letter, and taking it if so minded-
it has not been opened till the present moment.
