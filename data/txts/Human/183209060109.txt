 Cross-examined. Q. Is it your custom to hold conversations with prisoners, and afterwards give evidence of it? A. No - I told her what I took her for, and she talked to me; I do not think I had any motive in telling her I had found the probate - it was after she had told me all about it that I said so.
   JOHN STYLES. This is the probate she produced.
   SAMUEL MAYNARD. This is the probate, which I took out, on the oath of the prisoner.
   MARY ANN PRINGLE. I know the prisoner; about the beginning of March this year I went to the Cock, in Leadenhall-street, and saw Thomas Fallen, Andrew Morgan, and John Dalton there; I went out with Dalton, and when I came back the prisoner was in the tap-room -Dalton and Fallen said to me, "How did you get on?" and I told them what Norrington's clothes sold for; I said his clothes sold for 7l. odd, and that his wages were 7l. odd - I said nothing more in the prisoner's presence, and do not think she said any thing; we drank together, and I left them; nothing more was said while I was there- I did not stay two minutes; none of them made any answer when I stated what the clothes sold for; Morgan and Fallen lived in the same house as I did - the ycame to me one evening, but the prisoner was not there; next morning Dalton and she were at Fallen's house, where I lived - they worked there; all that was said before the prisoner was, that the will was made out, and they were to give me a sovereign, and 5s. for my trouble in going up; I was to have that if she got the money - this was said on Saturday night; it was for going to the owners about the money, which I had done - I was to do nothing more for the sovereign; the prisoner said she did not mind; they seemed all agreeable to it; a few days after that I asked her how she got on with Benjamin Norrington's will; she said she was obliged to take it to a lawyer, as she could not get it, for the ship was put into the Commons', and she did not think she should get it at all; but Morgan was to pay 1l. to fetch the will away from the lawyer who she had employed to try to get the money - I afterwards asked her how she got on; she said she did not think she should get the money, for there was another will made on board the ship - I do not recollect ever seeing this paper before (looking at one.)
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you ever married? A. Yes; my husband's name was Thomas Pringle - I have not lived with him for several years - I do not know whether he is alive; my maiden name was Pettit - I have gone by the name of Francis, who I lived with; and by the name of Carnel, who I lived with nine years, and buried - I swore by the name of Mary Elliot, at Doctor'scommons, by Mrs. Dalton's request, as she had a will at Mr. Clarkson's, and could not go up herself; I was in the House of Correction for a month, for unlawfully pawning a cloak which I had borrowed - I forget whether I ever swore in this Court that I was single; I did not swear I could not read - I said I could not read writing well - I do not recollect mentioning I could not read at all; I said I could not write the alphabet - I can read a little - I do not recollect whether I swore I could not read; I can write the different names I have gone by, but nothing else.
   JOHN CLIFFORD PETRIE. The witness never came to my house with the prisoner - I never saw her there to my knowledge; I know her; Norrington's effects sold for 7l. 9s. 6d., and his wages were 7l. 7s.
 WILLIAM THOMAS. I am master of the Baretto Junior. She sailed from Portsmouth on the 20th of February; she left London about a week before - she arrived again on the 4th of March, 1832 -  Benjamin Norrington was a sailor on board, and died about the 14th or 15th of August.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long before she sailed had you joined her? A. On the 7th of February, in the London-docks - she sailed from London about a week before the 20th; sailors who write a middling or bad hand, sometimes make their mark instead of their names- I have known that done two or three dozen times.
 COURT. Q. Look at this; has that been the case with people who could write as well as this? A. Yes; men often say their hands are unsteady.
   JOHN FRAZER. I was Dalton's landlord. The prisoner called on me with a letter from Dalton, who was then a prisoner in Newgate; it was two or three days after he was committed, and about two months ago she wished to remove some clothes, but did not have them.
 The five letters found in the prisoner's pocket were all directed to Mrs. James, or Phoebe James; four of them were signed Andrew Morgan, and one John Dalton.
