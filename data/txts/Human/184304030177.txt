JAMES COOK. I am the prosecutor's shopman—on the 22nd of March I weighed three hundred-weight of coffee to be roasted—it was in three bags—there was a delivery note with it—I desired Wilson to bring it back with the coffee. MARY BECK. I live in Harrold's-place, John's-row, St. Luke's, which is next door to Wilson's stable—between nine and ten o'clock on Friday morn ing, the 24th of March, I saw Wilson go into the stable—shortly after I saw Singleton—I afterwards saw a cart and horse go out with some bags—Single ton was driving—Wilson had gone on a few minutes before.
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  What was in these bags you can not say? A.  No. THOMAS WELLER . I live in New-street, Borough-road—I let a cart to Singleton on Thursday, the 9th of March—he returned it on the 24th of March, from a quarter to ten minutes to one—my cart bad my address on it—I did not let him a horse—he took the horse away with him.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  When did he pay you? A.  He gave me 3 s . when he hired it, and 3 s . more on the 22nd. JOHN HALES. I am collector of the City tolls at Moorgate—on the 24th of March, about a quarter past eleven o'clock, there was a horse and cart driven through my gate by Wilson—I gave him a ticket that was marked "Moorgate; City tolls, 243"—I did not take any notice of the load. GEORGE EDINGTON. I am collector of the City tolls in Jewry-street, Mino ries—about ten minutes to twelve on the 24th of March, a cart and horse passed through my gate—a ticket was produced, with "Moorgate, City toll" on it—the cart appeared to be laden at the top with coffee—I did not see how many bags—I cannot say who was driving.
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Did you see any coffee? A.  No, I could not see what it was—I know by the smell it was coffee—I stopped the cart promiscuously and demanded the toll—I did not notice the cart nor horse, or the man—I jumped up behind and felt the bag—it felt like coffee.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Are you able to say it was coffee? A.  I think it was—there was a scent of coffee in the cart at the time.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  You have been examined about this before? A.  Yes—I know Wilson was not the man who was with the cart. ELIZABETH FULLERTON. I am landlady of the house No. 14, Pearl-row, Blackfriars-road. I know Singleton by sight—between twelve and one o'clock, on Friday, the 24th of March, I saw him come with a horse and cart to my door, to Aburrow, who has my front room—he carried up something, I do not know what—he came down, and drove off—soon after he came back with the horse, took off the harness, and went up stairs—it would take him five or ten minutes to go to the Borough-road.
