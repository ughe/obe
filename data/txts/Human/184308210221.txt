   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How do you know the other house you went to was Barker's house? A.  Barker was not there at the time, but I saw his wife there—she told me she was his wife. JOHN TUCKER. I own Printers'-buildings—it is also called Tucker's-build ings—Barker lived at No. 27, at the latter end of June, and Brockwell lived at No. 28.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know Wilks? A.  He was in my employ—he was taken from his business to be apprehended on this charge—he had been in my service ten years and a half—I can say no thing against his character.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How long has Barker been in your employ? A.  About the same time—they both served their apprenticeship—I kept Barker for about eighteen months afterwards—he was at work for me in the latter end of June—he and Brockwell worked two days after the robbery, and then absconded.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What days were they? A.  Monday and Tuesday—they left work unfinished—they gave no notice of their intention to go.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDEROAST . Q.  How long has Brockwell been with you? A.  Four or five years, perhaps—I am a silk-printer, and have from 600 to 700 men in my employ. JOHN ANDERSON. I am governor of Ilford jail. On Monday, the 10th of July, I was in the yard in which Barker was confined—I said, "So, I see, you are remanded again"—he said, "Yes, because they say we have sent a lad, or a boy, out of the way, and told him where some of the things were put; we did not say that we put them there ourselves, but that some one else might have put them there."
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Were you examined before the Ma gistrate? A.  No, but I mentioned this to the Magistrate.
   MR. VOOGHT re-examined. Q.  Were these the sort of bottles in which your wine was? A.  Yes—these two contained soar beer—there was a seal on this, with the word "marsala" on it—this looks like the seal on the bottles in my cellar—I cut this off one of the bottles found in the orchard—my beer-bottles were fastened like these—I have got two bottles similarly secured—this stone jar is a similar one to that which contained the spirits.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  I believe Wilks was discharged on bail before the Magistrate? A.  Yes—my sister keeps the house, and the property is hers. RALPH HINDS. I am servant to Charles Woolley, a wine-merchant, About the 15th of June I delivered some Marsala wine, some rum, and gin, at Mr. Vooght's house—the top of this cork is some of my work—it is my master's seal—the corks of the marsala were sealed with it, and all of this colour—this appears to be the same—this is a similar jar to one in which I delivered the spirits.
