 Cross-examined by MR.BODKIN. Q. Did you know of any gambling going forward in your house? A. Yes, bagatelle; I did not state before the Magistrate there was no gambling: in my house that night - I said ther were no cards; I will swear I told the Magistrate I did not see any gambling: I am not in the habit of frequenting that room- I was told of it by my servant, the day following; I never said" You should not have paid for what you can't pay for," to the best of my knowledge: I did not know there had been any gambling till the next day - I do not believe I said that.
 MR. SMITH. Q. When he came to the bar, and paid you for one pint, and left one pint unpaid, what did you say? A. I said I was not in the habit of giving credit, and I would not, and he said he would return and pay me.
   JOHN COMPTON. I keep the Three Jolly Butchers public-house, in Brookes-market. On Tuesday, the 17th of March, about half-past eleven o'clock at night, Hawkings and John Ballard came to my house; I have no doubt they are the persons - I knew Hawkins before; he said he had come to pay me what he owed me, as he had been up to the neighbourhood of Clare-market, and had won 25s. at cards; he gave me a crown-piece, which was not a good one, and I told him it would not do - he said to Ballard, "John, you must lend me 5s.;" Ballard put down two half - crowns, and a penny, and I took it up - they then went into the tap-room, and had half a gallon of ale.
   THOMAS JEFFREYS. I keep the George public - house, in Brooke-street. On the 17th of March, about a quarter before twelve o'clock at night, Hawkins, John Ballard, and another person, whose name I have heard is Riley, came into my house; they called for three glasses of wine, and one of them, which I believe was Hawkins, put down a crown-piece, which I refused, as I was convinced it was not a good one, and they gave me a shilling - the crown-piece had rather a deep cut on the edge, rather wider than a piece would be cut, to ascertain whether it was good or bed.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. You will not swear who it was? A. I think it was Hawkins; I have a good deal of bad money offered me - some people do cut the edge of money, to ascertain whether it is good, but that. is not my way; this was very bad - it was white metal; I looked at it for some time because I happened to have one before.
 MR. SMITH. Q. Did it appear to be cut for ascertaining whether it was good or bad? A. Yes, but more cut than is usual for that purpose.
   WILLIAM JOSCELYNE. I am a butcher, and live in Brooke-street, Holborn. At Seven o'clock in the morning, on Wednesday, the 18th of March, I saw Haswkins, John Ballard, and Riley come from under a gateway; there is a grating opposite my master's shop, leading to the common sewer - I saw Hawkins throw something down the grating, it appeared to me to be a crown-piece; they went on: I told my master, and we went to look, but could not find it.
 Cross-examined by MR.BODKIN. Q. You thought there was something to be got? A. No, the grating was taken up, but we found no crown-piece.
 COURT. Q. Do you think he did throw any thing down? A. yes, and I think it went into the sewer.
   GEORGE BEAN. I am a tripeman, and live in Beauchamp-street. Brookes-market. On Wednesday, the 18th of March, I saw Hawkins and Riley in West-street, Smithfield; there were several young men with them, and they were quarrelling - I saw Hawkins put his hand into his pocket, and pull out some sovereings: he said to another young mans that he would fight him - I think here were ten or twelve sovereigns; I cannot exactly say how many there were - Hawkins was not so drunk as Riley; Riley was very drunk.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Have you been much in Hawkins's company? A. No, never.
   WILLIAM WINGFIELD HAWKINS. I am ten years of age; I go to church, and say my prayers: if I tell lies I shall go to everlasting torments; I have taken an oath to tell the truth. I remember my brother, Thomas George Hawkins, coming home to my mother's house one Wednesday - he said he had won 5l. at cards the night before; he sent me to fetch his clothes from the pawnbroker's, and gave me 6l.1s.
   JOHN HOARE. I am pot- boy at the Crown public-house, in West Smithfield. I remember Hawkins and Riley coming to that house, but I cannot tell the day - I think it was in March; Hawkins paid me and my master's niece for three pints of ale, and one bottle of port wine; I saw some sovereigns and some silver in his hand- he then went out; Riley and another young man fought; I saw Hawkins pull out a sovereign, and ask who had a sovereign to lay on the fight.
   JOHN BARNLEY. I am a constable. On the 18th of March, in the forenoon, I apprehended John Ballard, in company with Riley, who was very drunk: I took them both, but Riley was discharged; I searched John Ballard in the lock-up yard at Hatton-garden - I found in his waistcoat pocket 2s., and in his right shoe five sovereigns; I told him I apprehended him on suspicion of a robbery - he said he had found the sovereigns down Bagniggewells-road that morning, as he was taking a walk, and he knew nothing of the robbery; I took Hawkins about an hour afterwards, at the Globe public-house, in Hatton-garden, at the corner of Charles-street - he was very much in liquor, and attempted to pay for a glass of liquor with a sovereign, which I took from him, and in his pocket I found 11s. in silver; I took George Ballard at his won house, on the Friday following - I took the mother in Holborn some short time afterwards, but I found nothing on them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You searched the place, I suppose? A. Yes; I found no bills of exchange, or any thing of that sort; I produce the chisel.
