Tuomas JAMES BARRETT. I am a surgeon, of Stratford-I examined the
child externally; I have heard Mr. Moore's evidence and agree with it-I
was not present at the post-mortem examination, but I saw the body two
days after and found it a fearful object, mere skin and bone-I weighed it,
it only weighed 84 lbs., and it was 26 inches long; it was a large framed
child, and at that age it ought to have weighed about 20 lbs. -I could come
to no other conclusion than that it died from starvation and neglect.
BY THE COURT. From the state of extreme emaciation in which it was
I should say that it had frequently been without food, because children's
stomachs are very elastic, but it was gorged with lumps of bread and
starchy matter given to it shortly before death-I cannot help thinking
that that food was put into it four days before its death, and now it
appears that the stomach could not digest it, and therefore that food was
improper food; but I do not consider that the food was the onuse of its
death so much as the prolonged starvation had been.
