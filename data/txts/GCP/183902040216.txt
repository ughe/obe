Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. When did you see the bill
of lading first? A. At the time I was taking in the butters-the butters
were landed from the Regina-I got the bill from the counting-house-it
is the original-I first saw it in the office at the time of the delivery-
had to go into the counting-house to see it.
THOMAS MURPHY. I am warehouseman to Mr. Barber, of Brewer's
quay. On Tuesday, the 4th of December, I delivered twenty casks of
butter to Sullivan, marked P E, and I MR branded—it was about twelve
o'clock in the day, I should say-I received this order from Sullivan's
hand-I heard him speak to a person named Dowsett, who is in our em-
ploy—there was a conversation between them, and Dowsett pointed to me
-I went up immediately, and asked Sullivan what he wanted-he said,
« Twenty casks of butter, "-I said, “For whom?" -he said, "For Rice,"
and with that produced the order-I gave the order to Dowsett to go to the
principal warehouseman-after he returned I delivered the twenty casks
of butter to Sullivan, and gave him a cart note for Rice and Son.
Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Was this note delivered to you
open as it is now, or folded up? A. I cannot remember-I am quite sure
that Sullivan asked for twenty casks of butter for Rice.
Cross-eramined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You are the warehouseman!
A. Yes, occasionally-the delivery was made in the due course of my
duty.
JAMES Dowsett. I was, at the time in question, a labourer in Mr.
