WILLIAM CHAMBERLAIN (Police Inspector E). On 20th February I went
to 3, Queen Street, Soho, and saw the prisoner in his room-I had previously
made enquiries at 23, King Street-I told him we were two police officers,
and asked him if he had changed a 51. note at Mr. Butler's on the 5th-he
said "No"--I said " I believe you did; I was told so; and that you bought
a pair of uppers "-he said “Yes, I did buy a pair of uppers ; and I remem-
ber now, I did change a 51. note; a man gave it to me to buy some leather
to make a girl a pair of boots "-I said "I think you also changed another
51. note the same morning, in Goodge Street, Tottenham Court Road "-he
said that he did not-I said that I had every reason to believe he had done
so, and that the two notes he had changed had been stolen from 5, Broad
Street, there having been a burglary there the night before--I said " You
will have to come with me," or "You will have to account for them somehow
or other"-- he said that he remembered he did cash one at Gordon's publio-
house the same day, but what ocourred after he hardly knew, for he was
drunk-I said "Can't you find the man whom you made the boots for 1-
he said "No"-I said I don't mind stopping with you all day if you can
point them out to me"-he said “No, I don't know where they live; I
cannot say; and if they are stolen I must suffer for it_I received this
knife from Mr. Steddal, at his office, on the morning of the burglary; I
