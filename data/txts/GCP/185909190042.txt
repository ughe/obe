BENJAMIN WHITMORE (Policeman, G 238). About 12 o'clock on the night
of 13th September, I was in York-street, City-road-1 heard a cry of
“Stop thief," and I saw two or three persons go up Rahere-street-I went
up President-street and met the prisoner- I caught him, and said to him,
"Did you holloa ?"-he said, “ Yes." -- asked him what was the matter
he gave me no answer-I took him back a few yards and met the prose-
cutor-he was bleeding very much from the head-he said, “That is he
that struck me and stole my watch," - the prisoner said, “You are mistaken;
it was not me"- I took him to the station.
COURT. Q. When you first saw the prisoner, was he standing, or walking,
or running! A. He was running, and away from his own house.
MR. COOPER. Q. Did you find anything about 6 o'clock that morning!
4. Yes: this ring of a watch, on the spot where the prosecutor said he had
been attacked.
Cross-examined. Q. How far was the prosecutor from the prisoner when
you stopped him? A. About twenty yards-- I took him back and met the
prosecutor.
JOHN HENRY SQUIRE MAY. I am one of the house-surgeons at St. Bar
tholomew's Hospital—the prosecutor came on the night of the 13th
September-I examined him, and found a contused wound on the upper
part of the left side of his head.
Cross-examined. Q. I suppose a fall might have occasioned it ? A. If
his head had come in contact with the kerb-stone, it might-I don't think
a rail would have done it; it was near the upper part of the head.
