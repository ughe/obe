1954. MARIA SCOTT was again indicted for stealing 18 pairs of gloves, value 15 s  .; 1 shawl, 8 s  .; 1 parasol, 2 s  . and 2 bonnet-falls, 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of James Stephenson , her master.
   MR. WILDE conducted the Prosecution. JAMES STEPHENSON. The prisoner was in my employ—in consequence of the other robberies, I was induced to search her box—I found all these articles( produced ).
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  The two servants slept in the same room? A.  Yes—the box in which these things were found was open—there were two beds in that room—I found some of these things marked with William's initials—the prisoner has been in my employ three years, and was always honest—I will take her again. NOT GUILTY .
 1955. GEORGE JOHN NAYLOR was indicted for embezzling 40 l  . 10 s  .; the monies of Anthony William Clark , his master.—2nd COUNT  , for stealing 1 10 l  . bank note, 11 sovereigns, and 1 half-sovereign; the monies of Anthony William Clark, his master. ANTHONY WILLIAM CLARK. I am a surgeon, and live in Cheapside—the prisoner was my assistant. On the 19th of July, a bill of 40 l . 7 s . 6 d . became due—the acceptor of the bill called on me that day, and I being out he wrote a note, which enclosed a check for 19 l . and a sovereign, and begged I would get the difference to meet the bill, naming a gentleman who would lend it me—he left the note on my table, informing the prisoner or what he had done—the prisoner went out afterwards to pay the bill—I never saw the check—there was no other person there who could have taken the check—I received the note about half-past four o'clock—the check was then gone—it has been changed—the prisoner told me he had got it, and I sent him out to take the bill up with it—I have never seen the check since—it was on the London and Westminster Bank—I do not know the names of the parties, it is a com pany—the prisoner had the 10 l . note and the ten sovereigns of Mr. Richards, who is present—he is the person who I was to borrow the money of—the prisoner told me he had the check, and Mr. Richards was to give him the money, and he was to go and take the bill up—he did not do so—the bill has been dishonoured and returned—I am the drawer of it—this produced is it.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALE. Q.  Where did you find this letter? A.  On my desk—it was not sealed—the prisoner told me he had got the check and the money—Mr. Richards had already given him the money, and asked me if he was right in so doing—I said, "Yes," and told the prisoner to go and take the bill up with it—the prisoner had authority to receive money for me—he was in the habit of doing so—if anybody called to pay him money he would have received it—he was authorized to receive it for such a purpose as this—I had implicit confidence in him. JOHN THOMAS KING. I wrote this check for 19 l .—I enclosed it in a letter to Mr. Clark—I made the check payable to the prisoner, for the purpose of making a payment at the bank—it was enclosed in a note addressed to his master—it has been paid, and returned to me—I had requested Mr. Clark to let Naylor go and take this check—the prisoner being before me, it was quite immaterial to me who I wrote it for—it happened to be so.
   COURT. Q.  Though you wrote it for Mr. Naylor, who was it for? A.  For Mr. Clark.
