Fifth Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 2189.  JOHN EDWARDS was indicted for embezzlement.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   CATHERINE EDWARDS. I am a baker; my husband left me eleven years ago - I do not know whether he is dead or alive. The prisoner is my son - he is twenty years of age; he was employed as my servant in business - I had a customer named James Hook, who owed me 1l. 3s. 4d.; the prisoner used to serve him - he accounted to me only for 13s. 4d. received from Mr. Hook, and said he had spent 10s. at skittles; he said it was a good thing he had saved that.
   JOHN HOOK. On the 17th of September the prisoner called on me, and I paid him 1l. 3s. 4d. on account of his mother.
   WILLIAM HALL. I took the prisoner; he was quite intoxicated - in taking him to the office he said he did not know what I took him for, unless it was disorderly conduct; I said it was for receiving 10s. - I said, "I don't wish to know any thing about it, but it is right you should know before you go before the Magistrate."
 Prisoner's Defence (written).
 On the 1st of last month I received warning to quit my mother's house, but owing to my not being able to get employment, she consented to let me stop until I could get into a situation. On the following Wednesday I went to the house of Mr. Hook with bread, who (without being asked), paid me 1l. 3s. 4d. as part of his bill; and on my leaving his house I met with some friends, and having no money of my own, I spent 10s., and of course gave my mother but 13s. 4d., she agreeing that she would stop it out of my week's wages; but on Monday she paid me my full money, telling me that she would place it to an account she had against me of monies paid by her on my account.
 COURT to CATHERINE EDWARDS. Q. Why did you not charge him with this till October? A. Because I wanted to keep him in my house, but his conduct was such I could not endure it; I never gave him permission to take out money, but he has done it; I would have placed this to his account, but his conduct was such; he has put me in fear of my life - I have been forced to have an officer in the house to keep me from blows and insults - I did say I would place it to account that night, but my little son told me that he was just as usual, very drunk - I have devised every means to reclaim him. GUILTY. Aged 20.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined Six Weeks.
 2190.  GEORGE BRISCALL and FREDERICK CRAWKSHAW were indicted for stealing, on the 15th of October , 9 lbs. weight of beef, value 2s., the goods of John Day.
 JOHN DAY. I am a  butcher, and live at Fulham. On the 15th of October I was out; when I came home this beef was missing - I firmly believe it to have been mine.
   JOHN TOUSE. I am a gardener. On the 15th of October I saw Crawkshaw take a piece of beef from the prosecutor's shop-board, and put it into Briscall's apron.
   ELISHA GIBBS. I was going down a lane which leads to Shepherd's-bush; I saw the prisoners - one of them had a piece of beef in his apron; I ran up to them - they dropped the beef, and I pursued and took them; I showed the same beef to the prosecutor.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I took the prisoners, and found where the beef had been in their apron.
 CRAWKSHAW'S Defence. When he heard the cry of Stop thief! he took Briscall, and said, "Throw it over the pales," but he would not - he then went on, but he ran and took him, when he heard there was 5s. reward offered.
 ELISHA GIBBS. I knew of no reward at that time.
 BRISCALL -    GUILTY. Aged 18.
 CRAWKSHAW -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months.
 2198.  ROBERT BUTLER was indicted for stealing, on the 16 of October , 1 fixture, (i.e.) 1 copper, value 10s., the goods of Francis Whit , and fixed to a certain building; 1 blanket, value 1s.; 1 apron, value 2d., and 1 knife, value 6d., the goods of Hannah Roe ; 1 bonnet value 6d.; 1 piece of crape, value 2d.; 1 handkerchief value 2d.; 1 apron, value 2d.; 1 pair of gloves, value 6d., and 2 caps, value 6d., the goods of Jane Nash.
 HANNAH ROE. I had the care of a house at the corner of  St. Martin's-lane and Newport-street; I left it on the 16th of October, at a quarter-past five o'clock in the evening; the property was then all safe - the prisoner was taken about half-past seven o'clock: I did not hear of it till the following morning, when I went to the house, and the things were gone; the blanket, apron, and knife belonged to me, and had been in the back parlour - there were some other articles of Jane Nash's; these are the articles - I know nothing of the prisoner.
 JANE NASH. There were some things of mine in the house that evening, but I had not seen them for three weeks or a month - these are them.
   GEORGE BOWER. I saw the prisoner on the 16th of October, with a copper on his head, in Gray's Inn-lane - I asked where he got it; he said from No. 12, Tottenham-street - I was not satisfied, and left him with my brother officer; I went to the place he stated, and found it was not true; my brother officer found the other things on him; I fitted the copper in the house - it fitted exactly.
   GEORGE FOSTER. I was with Bower. I found these other articles on the prisoner, four picklock-keys, a phosphorus-box, and a screw-driver - they were all in his pockets.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am porter to Mr. Way - he is a grocer. I open and shut the house in question; I went on the morning of the 16th of October, and found the door half open; I went in and found no one - I went and told Mrs. Roe - we went in, and missed the articles.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met a person in Rupert-street, who asked me to carry this copper over the water for him- the other things he said he brought from a lodging in Tottenham-street; I do not know the number - I thought it was 12.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
