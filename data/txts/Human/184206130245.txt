   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  How long have you known the prisoner? A Some two or three years—I knew him in Mr. Wilson's service—I was also in Mr. Wilson's service—he has always borne the character of a respectable man, and been a first-rate man in his business—he was in Mr. Wilson's service when I went, and I left him there—he had the management of their cap business—he at that time carried on busi ness for himself in the London-road—he worked on Muiler and Simpson's premises—I never recollect his bringing his own materials to make up—I remember some braid being fetched from his premises, which was made up for Muiler and Simpson, and we had some old hat-boxes of the prisoner—I have sold the prisoner an article we call" threshers," what the country farmer boys wear—I do not recollect any thing else—there was a cap or dered of me—I ordered it of Simpson and Muiler, and the person did not call for it—I could not afford to lose it, and the prisoner took it of me—I was examined at the police-office—I swear I never sold, the prisoner any thing else—he never supplied the prosecutors with any buttons—I know that he employed some of the same workwomen as the prosecutors did—I swear positively I have never seen him giving them work to do for himself on the prosecutors' premises—there is a boy named William Trimm in the prosecutors' employ—he might look out hats for the prisoner, not caps and velvet—I think the prosecutors bought the chain velvet at Man chester—I do not recollect whether they deal with any one in the Old Change—I do not know the value of the velvet—it is my duty to enter articles which I sell of the prosecutors—Messrs. Muiler and Simpson sometimes make entries of the articles they sell—the prosecutors are not in the habit of selling. materials to their workpeople—we have never done so—I told the prosecutors of the cap the prisoner ordered, on the day of his commitment—I told Mrs. Harris, the forewoman, I wanted a cap of a certain size, and she got it made for me—I paid the prosecutors for it—the prisoner made some allusion to a cap, and I said the only cap I sold him was the one I had entered—I entered it at the time I bought it, before I had it away from the premises, after I had ordered it—I left Mr. Wilson's employ about eighteen months ago—I had been in his service three years—I collected orders for him, and occasionally received cash—I left him suddenly—I was out of employ six months before I got to the prosecu tors'—Mr. Gibbs, Mr. Wilson's partner, gave me a character to the prose cuton—I swear I referred to them for a character—I had a dispute with Mr. Wilson before I left, about money matters—he was my partner at that time—I swear that Mr. Gibbs was not partner with me, but he was with Mr. Wilson—I was in business in Castle-street, Southwark, as a hat-manu facturer, and Mr. Wilson was my partner there—"Edwin Hunt" was over the door—Mr. Wilson's name did not appear—his partnership with Mr. Gibbs was not made public, and mine with Mr. Wilson was not made pub lic—I was in partnership with Wilson—our term was seven years—the business did not answer—Mr. Wilson got the money—there were deeds and an agreement—I left Mr. Wilson because he charged me with misappro priating his money—I am not aware of the amount—it might be 200 l .—I left him the next day—I had been two years in his employ, receiving no salary—I was his partner and his servant—I have not taken any proceed ings against Mr. Wilson to enforce my partnership agreement—I at one
