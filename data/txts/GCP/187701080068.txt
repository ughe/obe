Before Mr. Recorder.
191. WILLIAM WOOD (32), was again indicted (see page 326) with
GEORGE BOWYER (24) for unlawfully conspiring to obtain a situation for
the said George Bowyer, as barman.
MESSRS. BESLEY, J. P. GRAIN, and CHILD conducted the Prosecution ; MR. M.
WILLIAMS appeared for Wood, and MR. STRAIGHT for Bowyer.
BRUCE GOLDIE. I am manager and cashier to Messrs. Matthews & Can-
ning—they had an interest in the Conquering Hero public-house at Peck-
ham-Wood went into that house on 8th November, 1875, and left on 26th
April, 1876.
JAMES MILES. I keep the White Lion Hotel, Beccles-up to 21st June
I was landlord of the White Horse, Long Acre, and I think that was the day
of its transfer to the prisoner.
RICHARD WINTER. I live in Lime Street Square, and am some connection
of the man who calls himself Gibson--his right name is George Bullimore--
that is the prisoner Bowyer.
Cross-examined by MR. STRAIGHT. His mother was my late wife's first
cousin- I had not seen him for six months previous to 19th December!
never knew him by the name of Bowyer till this transaction--he followed
the occupation of a barman for some time.
Re-examined. This certificate is correct—his father and mother were
George and Lucy Bullimore. (This certified the baptism, at West Lynn, Nor
folk, in January, 1851, of George, the son of George and Lucy Bullimore).
Cross-examined by MR. M. WILLIAMS. I do not know every situation
that Gibson bad- I did not know him in Wood's employ at Camden Town-
I never heard Wood's name till this transaction.
