   LEWIS MYERSON. I am a constable. I went to the house and saw the prisoners - I heard Harris say before they came down, "I may as well be sent, out of the country as be starving here."
   JOSEPH PAGOLIN. I am a bricklayer and live next door to this house. I was ill in the night, and got up; I heard a noise; I went up to bed again, and said to my father, "I think there are thieves next door." My father got up, and I followed him, he went to the back, and I to the front; one of the men then looked over the parapet, but I cannot tell which it was; my father called up some bricklayers, and I went to the watchman; when I returned my father had raised a ladder; we could not find any one there, but the lead, coat, hat, and shoes, were in the middle house.
   THOMAS WALKER. I am a constable, I fitted the lead to the place.
 The prisoners put in a written Defence, stating that they had gone to the premises to sleep, and declaring their innocence. Coltney received a good character.

 1872.  JOHN TOOLE was indicted for stealing on the 10th of August 1 handkerchief value 4s., the property of David Barber , from his person.
 DAVID BARBER. On the 10th of August I was in  Little Earl-street Seven-dials; I felt a twitch at my pocket, and missed my handkerchief; I looked round and saw the prisoner with it in his hand - I called to him to stop; he put it under his coat, and ran down a court; I pursued him, and saw him take the handkerchief; it was taken from him at the watch-house; this is it; it is mine.
   THOMAS WILSON. I am an officer; I took the prisoner and found this handkerchief on him.
 Prisoner. I throw myself on the mercy of the Court.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Two Months.
 1873.  ROBERT STOCKS was indicted for stealing on the 12th of July , 2 pairs of stockings, value 2s., the goods of Robert Romanis.
 ROBERT ROMANIS. I am a  hosier, and live in Norton Falgate; the prisoner had been my shop-boy for twelve months, and before he was my father's: he had a good character, but on the 12th of July I found these two pairs of stockings secreted in his box - he said he brought them from home.
   JOSEPH FRYER. I took the prisoner.
 Property prodnced and sworn to.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he received no salary, but was allowed 7 1/2d. a day for dinner and 2d. for supper; that his sister had wished him to purchase her some stockings, and he laid these aside for her.
   GEORGE SMITH. I live with the prosecutor - I sold the prisoner two pairs of stockings about six weeks ago - I cannot say whether these are them.
   ROBERT STOCKS. I am the prisoner's father. On the Sunday before he was taken I heard his sister tell him she wanted a pair of stockings.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1874.  SUSAN KING was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 1 watch, value 1l.; 1 watch-chain, value 1d., and 1 seal, value 6d., the goods of Edward Croft , from his person.
 EDWARD CROFT. I am a  labourer. I came from Bedfordshire on the 16th of July, late at night, and could not get a lodging - I was walking in the street, and met the prisoner between three and four o'clock in the morning of the 17th - she asked me to give her something to drink; I said it was late, and we could not find a place; she said she would find a place, and I went and gave her a glass of rum, and took one myself: we came out, and she wanted me to go home with her, which I refused, but I went with her into some alley, and she clasped her hands round me, and asked me to go home with her - I still refused; she drew her hand from me - I felt my fob, and my watch was gone - she then ran off; I pursued, but lost sight of her - I met Lewis, who asked me what was the matter - I told him; he took her- I am sure she is the person; I was a little the worse for liquor, but knew what I was about; I did not give her the watch, nor promise her any money.
   JAMES LEWIS. I met Croft in the street, on the morning of the 17th of July - he gave me information, and I went and took the prisoner in about five minutes in Whitecross-street; she wanted to know what she was taken for - I took her to the watch-house, and said she had robbed a man of his watch; she said "Well, what have you to do with it"- I said I had, and I took her along - she seized a knocker of a door, and said "I will be d - d if I will go any further till you show your authority;" I said "Anybody has a right to take a felon:" I saw Adams on the other side of the street - I called him to me, and said "I will thank you to ring that bell," which he did; Mr. Richardson, the officer, came down, and we took her.
   CHARLES RICHARDSON. I am an officer. I came down, and took the prisoner to the watch-house - she produced this watch from her pocket, and said it was given to her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the prosecutor had taken her to several public-houses, and got intoxicated, and had given her the watch to pledge.
