RICHARD THOMPSON. I am an attorney at law. In 1856, I was acting
s attorney for the prisoner he had not consulted me in July last-I spoke
to him-I was written to by Mr. Robertson to see him I did see him on
some business on 8th July last, I went to the Queen's Prison, with the
prisoner and Mr. Gower I had spoken to De Salvi as to an arrangement
being made for the discharge of Mr. Robertson, who was at that time
prisoner in the Queen's Prison-De Salvi was one of his detaining creditors,
Mr. Gower was another, and there were others-it must have been about
2 o'clock when we went to the prison--we went to Mr. Robertson's room-
he was not there at the time, he came in afterwards there was Mr. Roberto
son, the prisoner, Mr. Gower, and myself in the room-ra conversation took
place between the different parties Mr. Robertson took his seat near the
vindow, I was on his left hand side, the prisoner was standing about four
or five, or six feet in front of Mr. Robertson, and Mr. Gower was standing
a little further off I spoke to Mr. Robertson as to his making some proposal
to the prisoner and Mr. Gower for his discharge - Mr. Robertson, addressing
bimself to Mr. Gower, made an explanation as to a debt of 2001. to De
Salvi, he said, “The 2001. is owing by a Mr. Brown, it is not my debt
on that the prisoner said, "I let you have my money, you owe me the
money-Robertson said, “ No, Brown had it "be denied it, and then there
was an angry feeling between the prisoner-he seemed yexed at his
denying that he had received his 2004—the angry feeling was expressed by
the prisoner-the said, “ You had my money"-I cannot recollect exactly
the words used by him, but I saw that he was angry with him for denying
owing him the 2001.- he repeated the denial more than once after he had
so repeated it, De Salvi got nearer to him, and I saw him strike Robertson
with his right hand, placing his left hand on his shoulder, and striking with
the right Robertson was sitting in his chair at the time--he struck him
on the right side, in the neck-I should think there were four or five blows
given-I could not see anything in the prisoner's hand after the blows
were struck, Mr. Robertson got forward about four feet, and went to the
ground-I immediately jumped up from my seat De Salvi left the room,
and I saw that Robertson was bleeding from a cut in the temple, and I im-
mediately took hold of him, and supported him on the ground-De Salvi
left the room immediately after the blows were struck, and Mr. Gower
