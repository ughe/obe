   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  I believe you had all three been in the employment of Mr. Clarkson, a builder? A.  Yes, for several months—I had been at two public-houses with the prisoners before in the space of twelve months—the last time was about a week before, and a week before that—I think I was drinking with them at the Railway tavern on the 24th of March—Wright pawned his own shovel, as he told me, to pay for beer—he went home to my lodging that night, and staid all night—I did not awake rather thirsty next morning, and want some beer—I did not send Wright to sell a piece of lead for 3 d . to get beer—I gave him some lead to get something to eat for himself, not to get beer—we went to the Roebuck public-house—I gave him a stock and bit to pawn that morning, as he said he was very badly off, and had nothing to eat—that was at Deptford, not in the public-house, it was the same day that we went to the Lord Duncan—I was going to Sydney, and they talked about going also—I left them to go to the Victualling-office, and agreed to meet at the turn-pike to go home together—I live at Greenwich, and they at Deptford—I had told them if I could do any thing for them, I would tell them when I met them. HENRY THURLOW. I am a servant at the Lord Duncan public-house, On the afternoon of the 25th of March the prisoners were there, between three and four o'clock, I was rung for to fetch up the basket of tools from the cellar—I brought them into the tap-room, and delivered them to the prisoners. JOHN EVANS( police-constable R  190.) On the night of the 25th of March I stopped the prisoners on the Greenwich-road—Lake had a basket—I asked what he had got in it—he said a few tools belonging to his mate—I found it contained two mallets, two chisels, two gauges, one oil-stone, one gimlet, one brad-awl, one hammer, and a file—I asked Wright if the tools belonged to him—he made no reply—I asked him several times—at last he said, "Yes, they are mine, and what more do you want?"—I afterwards showed them to White, who claimed them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not Wright tell you they belonged to York, and tell you where he lived, and that turned out to be White? A.  Yes—the prisoners had been drinking. NICHOLAS MULLINS. I am a policeman. I took Wright into custody—I was with Evans, and found some duplicates on Wright—five are for the tools which have been pawned. JOHN LOVICK. I am shopman to Mr. Nash, a pawnbroker, in London-street, Greenwich. I produce two planes, a saw, an axe, and three chisels, pawned on the 25th of March, about a quarter to nine o'clock in the even ing, by a young man named "George Smith, for Thomas White, No. 2, Bridge-street "—the prosecutor claimed them.
