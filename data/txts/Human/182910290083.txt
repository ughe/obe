 2055.  WILLIAM WALTHAM and JOSEPH PEACH were indicated for stealing, on the 25th of September , 5 bushels of wheat, value 35s., and 2 sacks, value 3s., the goods of Edward Whalley; and ELIZABETH PIGGOTT was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, knowing them to have been stolen.
 MR. BODKIN, who conducted the prosecution, declined offering any evidence against Elizabeth Piggott, who was here    acquitted.
 EDWARD WHALLEY. I am a  farmer, and live at South Mimms. On the 25th of September I had fifteen loads of wheat in my barn, which had been dressed for market - there were some sacks of mine there; I saw the place secure at five o'clock - it was locked; I have seen some corn and sacks in the officer's hands; the sacks have my mark on them.
   JOHN SIMKINS. I am in Mr. Whalley's employ. I went to the barn on the 26th, in the morning - the door was open, and between five and six bushels of corn taken away, and two sacks; I have seen the sacks since, and knew them, and some corn which seemed to be the same.
   BENJAMIN LAMBERT. I am a smith. I was at South Mimms on Saturday, the 26th of September; I saw Waltham, Propstring, the officer, and Mr. Watch, at the Cross Keys - they then went to the cage. and left Waltham there for breaking Mr. Alderman Heygate's pales; I afterwards let Waltham out, and went with him to the house where Elizabeth Piggott and he lived together - Peach was there and Thomas, who is gone away; we had some gin, and all drank - then Peach and Thomas said they would go away; they packed up their clothes and went - I was coming out, and Waltham asked me to stop; I being tipsy did not stop - he said, "I have some wheat I want to move that came from Mr. Whalley's on Friday night;" and by his persuasion I helped to move it in a sack, that Piggott had in the house with some gleaning corn in it - I heard nothing about any other sacks; they said Peach and Thomas had brought it in two sacks - we took it across the road, and put it into Whittington's pond: the sack was taken back to the house - Piggott said they did it for fear they should get into trouble.
 Prisoner Waltham. Q. Did not you come to the cage to me, and ask if I would come out? A.No.
   WILLIAM HIGGS. I am an officer. I went to Whittington's pond on the 27th of September; we found a great deal of wheat on the bridge and fence, and some in the pond - I found these two sacks under an arch, about a mile and a half from South Mimms; Waltham was in the cage, and the lock was broken: but he came back into the cage himself, as I was told - I went to him; he was quite tipsy - I asked who broke the lock of the cage; he said he could not tell - I went and took Peach at Biggleswade, at work at a blacksmith's shop; I said, "You are my prisoner" - he said, "Don't say any thing about it before these people; say it is for a little one;" and I said so - when we got out I told him it was for taking the wheat from Mr. Whalley's, and told him it would be better to speak the truth.
   ELIZABETH PIGGOTT. I was living at South Mimms. There was something brought to my house, but I cannot say what it was, nor who brought it - I was not at home.
 COURT. Q. Was there a sack brought in? A. Yes, two - Peach and Thomas were there, but I cannot say whether they came before or afterwards, nor what time of night it was.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2056.  DAVID LYONS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of October , 6 knives, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Saunders.
   THOMAS SAUNDERS. I am a watchmaker, and keep a shop in Whitechapel-road. On the 6th of October, between seven and eight o'clock in the morning, I was standin the parlour, and saw the prisoner with his hand in a little glass-case in the shop - he took out six knives; he came in from the street - I did not know him before; I ran to the door - he was taken by an officer, and brought back; the tray was deficient of six knives.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Will you take upon yourself to swear the prisoner's arm was in the glass-case? A. Yes; I was six or eight feet from the place -I saw the prisoner in the shop, and he had his arms in the glass-case; he saw me, drew his arm back, and ran away - the prisoner is the person who took the knives - there were only this half dozen pen-knives; they were in the glass the night before, and the shop had not been opened, only the door - they were dropped at my door, and brought into my shop immediately; I never said the arm I saw in the glass-case I thought was my son's - I said I thought my son was in the shop, as I had just opened my street door, and then went into the parlour; at first I thought it was my son, but when I went to the door I saw she prisoner running - I was as quick in getting to the door as he was; it is a wide road.
   ROBERT ROBERTS. I was in Whitechapel-road on the 6th of October, early in the morning; I saw the prisoner running, and some persons after him - I saw Mr. Thompson stop him; I saw some knives in the road a minute or two after I saw the prisoner in the - they were place over which he had run - I did not take them up till he was stopped.
