ROBERT RICHARDS, ESQ. I am a barrister—on the 19th of July I paid the prisoner a 10 l . note, ten sovereigns, and a half-sovereign—I should say I paid it to the credit of both the parties—Mr. Clark had not authorized me to pay any—I was aware of the purport of the letter which Mr. King had written—I went in about three o'clock, and found Mr. King writing the letter—I ad vised him to finish his letter, and let it go forward—I paid the money—I was sure that Mr. Clark would recognize it the next day—I was aware of the letter—I would have acted upon it.
   COURT to MR. CLARK. Q.  Whose check was this? A.  I am the drawer of the bill—I presume the check was my own—the prisoner told me he had got the check—I told him to go and take up the bill—the 20 l . 10 s . I pre sume was also paid to me—I sent the prisoner with 40 l . 10 s . to take up the bill with the check, and 20 l . 10 s . which I had got of Mr. Richards—he told me he had paid it for me.
   MR. METCALF. Q.  What were the duties of the prisoner to you? A.  To make up medicines and any drugs that might be made up for sale; to go out for me, in fact to do anything and everything I ordered him—if he had refused to take up this bill, or to do any other menial work for me, I should have compelled him.
   COURT. Q.  If he had not done this, should you have discharged him? A.  Yes—he had done it for me often before.
   MR. METCALF. Q.  He was taken by you to assist in you professional duties? A.  Yes—I should have discharged him if he had not done a duty of this sort, because I considered it was what he ought to have done for me—I should not have conceived he was bound to go and take up a bill—he might have said, "That is not physic"—but no master I think would retain his servant an hour if he had refused.
   COURT. Q.  Did you employ him as your servant, and did he take the bill as such? A.  Yes.
   MR. METCALF. Q.  Did you employ him as a servant, or as in assistant in your medical profession? A.  As my servant—I think had he in any way refused to do this, and I had dismissed him, I should have had to pay his month's salary—this was one of those sort of things that no medical man could have kept an assistant half an hour after, if he had not done it.  1956. MARTHA SEERS was indicted for stealing 8 cigars, value 9 d  .; the goods of Rachel Dallen , her mistress. WILLIAM DALLEN. I am a licensed victualler. I act for my mother Rachel Dallen, who lives at Chelsea—the prisoner was her servant—on the 12th of Aug., I missed some cigars from the cigar-box—I found them amongst the ginger-beer bottles, at nine o'clock at night—I afterwards gave the prisoner into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  How long has the prisone lived with your mother, is it fifteen or sixteen years? A.  I do not know—I believe my sister is here to give her a character—my sister is married, she has not been living under the same roof with me, and I should be sorry for her to do so. GEORGE LINDON. I am pot-boy to Mrs. Dallen. On the 12th of Aug., the prisoner came to me, and said she ad got eight cigars concealed amongst the ginger-beer bottles, and if I would accept of them she would be much obliged to me—I told my master—she said she could not get out at the back door, as it was locked—she often goes through the stable.
