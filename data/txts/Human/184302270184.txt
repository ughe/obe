MARY HOLLAND was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of Feb , 1 kettle, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 7 wine glasses, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 decanter, 1 s  .; 1 pair of salt holders, 2 s  .; 6 cups, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 6 saucers, 1 s  .; 1 milk-pot, 6 d  .; 3 orna ments, 1 s  .; 2 jugs, 6 d  .; 1 sugar-glass, 2 s  .; 2 pictures, framed and glazed, 4 s  . 1 teapot, 1 s  .; 9 yards of printed cotton, 3 s  .; 1 cape, 2 s  .; 10 towels, 10 s  .; 5 petticoats, 6 s  .; 6 gowns, 10 s  .; 6 bed-gowns, 5 s  .; 3 frocks, 2 s  .; 1 pair of stays, 6 d  .; 1 razor, 6 d  .; 3 pillow-cases, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 table-cloth, 1 s  .; 1 pair of trowsers, 3 s  .; 3 shawls, 7 s  .; 1 coat, 4 s  . 6 d  .; 1 cloak, 1 s  .; 1 handkerchief, 2 s  .; 2 boxes, 9 d  .; 1 bonnet, 2 s  .; 1 knife, 6 d  .; 1 fork, 6 d  .; 2 blankets, 8 d  .; and 2 pain of sugar-tongs, 15 s  .; the goods of Barnet Abrahams. PRISCILLA ABRAHAMS. I am the wife of Barnet Abrahams, a general dealer. We live in Castle-place, Castle-street, Whitechapel—the prisoner was my servant—she had 1 s . a week, and her board and lodging. On the 15th of Feb. I left her in charge of my child and the place—I returned about half-past five o'clock in the afternoon, found the outer door shut, and the child in the cradle—the prisoner was gone, and the place was stripped—the drawen had been broken open—the pictures taken from the wall—the china from the cupboard, and the sugar-tongs and wearing-apparel gone—the things were worth rather more than 5 l . altogether—these now produced are the articles.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When was she taken? A.  The same evening, about seven. MARY HARVY. I live in Wentworth-street. The prisoner came to me on the 15th of Feb. with a large bundle, which she said was her clothes, that she was going to leave her situation, and asked if she might leave them till seven the next morning, as she was going off very early in the morning—she said her father was dead, and she was going to his funeral—I found the articles she left were not clothes, and sent for the policeman. PHŒBE SEATHARD. I am the wife of Jeremiah Thomas Seathard. The prisoner brought a quantity of things to my place on the 15th of Feb., and inquired for a lodging—the policeman and the prosecutor came afterwards, and the things were given up. WILLIAM FREDERICK SMALLSHAW. I am assistant to Mr. Fleming, a pawnbroker, in Whitechapel. I produce a pair of silver sugar-tongs, pawned on the 15th of Feb., by a female, who I believe was the prisoner. MILES DOWLING( police-constable H  135.) I live in Crown-court, Little Pearl-street. I was called to the prosecutor's, and examined the place—I found this instrument on the drawers, which appeared to have been broken open with it—the marks correspond with it.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined One Year .
