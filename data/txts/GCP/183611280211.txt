entering the dwelling-honse of William Brittain, on the 7th of November,

11. 58. ; 1 watch-key, value 1d.; 1 shirt-front, value 18. 6d. ; 1 coat,
value 12 108.; 1 pair of trowsers, valge 11.; 1 necklace, value 4s. ;
1 watch-guard, value 108.; 1 ring, value 2s. ; 1 shawl, value 408.;
1 shirt, value 28.; and 8 waistcoats, value 158.; his goods; and that
he had been before convicted of felony.
Rostka BRITTAIN. I am the wife of William Brittain, a bricklayer, at
Maidstone-hill, in the parish of Greenwich. On the 7th of November 1
left the house about five minutes before twelve o'clock, and left the door
on the spring-lock-the windows were safe-I left a watch-ribbon and key
in the house, and the other things stated in the indictment-I am sure they
were all safe before I left-I missed them when I returned--we have
moved since.
FRANCES BRITTAIN. I am the prosecuteix's mother-in-law. I went to
the house on Maidstone-hill on the 9th of October -I received the key
from my daughter-in-law's little girl, who I met on London-bridge-I
opened the door with it, and in about ten minutes after I got in I perceived
there was something not right, and missed the metal watch, and a suit of
my son's clothes I saw my son next morning, and informed the police
19 soon as I got to town.
