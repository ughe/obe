411. DANIEL FARRELL (10), Stealing 1 purse, and 21. 178 6d. the pro
perty of George Woods, from the person of Mary Ann Woods.
MR. PALMER conducted the Prosecution.
Mary Ann Woods. I live at 2, Wardrobe terrace-on 11th March, I was
on Ludgate-hill, looking into a shop-window-the prisoner stood at my right
side, and I felt a sudden jerk at my pocket-I missed my purse, and said
to the prisoner, “Little boy, you have got my purse "-he said, “What
purse?"-I said, "The one you have taken from my pocket"-he took the
purse out of his left trouser's pocket, and ran up Pilgrim-street with it in his
hand-he threw it down on Ludgate-hill, and a gentleman stopped him in
my sight, and gave him in charge with the purse-this is my purse—it con-
tained 21. 178 6d.
Prisoner's Defence. I did not do it; three boys were standing in a row,
and took it out of her pocket, and ran up the street; she took hold of me:
they all clustered together, and one of them threw it over their heads into
the road, and the gentleman caught hold of me.
Guilty.*+-Judgment Respited.
412. JOHN COLEMAN ( ), (a sailor). Robbery on Hannah Beering
and stealing 1 coat, her property, MR. PALMER conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN BROOKS (Policeman, D 50). On 14th March, about half-past 8 in
tbe evening, the prisoner was given into my custody for stealing & coat,
and assaulting the prosecutrix, wbo stated in his presence that he came
into her shop to be fitted with & coat, that she showed him one which
he said was too small, and another which was too large; that there were
two other young men with him, one of whom ran away with the prisoner,
and the other remained the prisoner denied it-he was the worse for
liquor.
Cross-examined by MR. SHARPE. Q. Is the owner of the shop & woman!
A. Yes; the prisoner said that he was not the man.
HANNAH BEERING I live at 19, Exeter-street, Lisson-grove-on 14th
March, a young man came in and asked for a coat-I went round to
serve him he said that it was not for him, but for a sailor outside- be
went to the door and called the prisoner in, who put the coat on, and
the one who asked for it said that it fitted bim-the prisoner said that
it did not fit-he said, “There is a sailor outside who is worth hundreds"
- he came in, and the other man said that he did not approve of the
coat-the prisoner asked for another-I said, "I have only one, which is
too large"—the prisoner then up with his fist, and knocked me backwards
-I fell, and when I arose he was gone-presently he came up to the
door without the coat, and said, "I am not the man who struck you"
said, “You are, and you took my coat-one sailor remained behind-he
spoke to me, and onlled some one to fetch a constable I have never seen
the coat since-the prisoner smashed all my teeth--I have no doubt he is
the man-I gave him in charge five or ten minutes afterwards,
Cross-examined by MR. SHARPE. Q. Will you swear he is the man .
Yes—the policeman was behind him when he came to the door, and I said,
“Policeman, that is the man that struck me"-the prisoner said, "I am not
the man"-the first man who came in was not dressed as a sailor--the
prisoner was dressed as he is now the whole transaction only took four or
five minutes.
