HERBERT GREY (Police-Sergeant). I was at Tottenham Court Road police
station on March 17th, when the prisoner was placed among six foreigners
and two Englishmen-he was immediately picked out by Visconti and
Trench-I explained the charge to him in French—he said in French
thathe knew it was stolen, and, “I never signed the signature"-I showed
him the endorsement.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : "I reserve my defence."
The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he was employed for seven
months by a Mr. Gillia, where he met a man, Leon Nepell, who had no pro-
Jession, but was well known to Visconti, and who had on previous occasions
antrusted him with cheques to change, that Leon asked him to change the cheque
for £14 15s.6d, with Visconti ; that Leon said it came from clients of his,
but he (the prisoner) had suspicions that it must be stolen, as he knew Leon
did not work.
CAMILLO VISCONTI (Re-examined.) There were also cheques for £2 5s.
and £4 10s. I went to the owners of the £2 58. cheque and they told
me the endorsement was forged--the £4 10s. cheque went through and the
prisoner had the money-I do not know if it was forged or not.
GUILTY of uttering. It was stated that Leon was an associate of the pris.
oner's, and was wanted by the police. Fifteen months' hard labour.
