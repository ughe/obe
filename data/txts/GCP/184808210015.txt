John Heart. I am a grocer, in Crowo-street, Soho. On 28th June, the
prisoner came for half an ounce of tea, and a quarter of a pound of sugar,
which came to 2}d., and put down a shilling-I gave her the change, and she
left-in half a second I perceived the shilling was bad-I leaped over the
counter, but the prisoner was out of sight-I put the shilling in paper, and
locked it in my cash-box-the prisoner came again on the evening of 5th
July, for half an ounce of tea, and a quarter of a pound of sugar-she gave
me a shilling, I told her it was bad, and that she had passed one the week
before-I sent for an officer, and gave her into custody-she said, "Oh do
let me go, I will do anything if you will let me go," and she put a good shil-
ling on the counter - I gave both the bad shillings to the policeman.
WILLIAM GUERY (policeman, P 30). I took the prisoner into custody-
she said to the prosecutor, “For God's sake don't do anything with me, I
will give you another shilling, let me go"-I have the two shillings.
CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint
these shillings are both counterfeit.
