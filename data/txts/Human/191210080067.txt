Cross-examined. He said they were always annoying him. I asked him who he mean it by "they," and he did not know. He said he heard voices in the other room and in the street trying to get at his wife. He said his wife used to signal to these voices by moving her leg as she lay in bed. Prisoner was in a very low state of health. He looks very much better now than he did when I first saw him.
 Sergeant  LANG , G Division. On September 21 I saw prisoner de tained at the police station. I told him he would be charged with wounding his wife with a knife with intent to do her grievous bodily harm. He said, "Yes." When the charge was read over to him he made no reply. He was calm, but he kept making mumbling state ments.
 Prisoner is a man of good character, a good husband and father, and a man of sober habits.
 (Defence.) SIDNEY REGINALD DYKE, medical officer, Brixton Prison. Prisoner has been under my observation since September 21. I have had con versation with him, and I have formed the opinion that at the time this offence was committed he was suffering from delusions and hallu cinations, which would prevent him knowing the nature and quality of the act.
 Crow-examined. Under the rest and treatment in prison the delu  sions have cleared up, but there is a certain amount of melancholia left, and he requires a prolonged mental rest. He was suffering from a nervous breakdown when he came in.
 Verdict, Guilty of unlawful wounding, but insane at the time so as not to be responsible according to law. Prisoner was ordered to be detained during His Majesty's pleasure.
