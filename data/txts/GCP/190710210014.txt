Upon the indictment for larceny the Grand Jury returned a true
bill.
(Thursday, October 24.)
Prisoners were brought before a fresh jury, the evidence being the
same. Joseph again pleaded guilty.
FREDERICK LUCCIONI (prisoner, on oath) stated that be was out a
that early hour with his brother going to Rotherhithe to seek employ.
ment. In passing No. 49 Joseph observed that the fanlight was open,
and said, "I am hungry; I have got nothing to eat; I am going in
here to see if I can get something." Then he climbed up the door
and witness walked away quickly because he was frightened and
wanted to have nothing to do with it. He was followed, and brought
back by the constable as described.
Verdict, Guilty.
Police-constable Smoru, 577 N, described prisoners as street ven-
dors, and said the elder had been an artist's model. In 1903 they were
charged as suspected persons. Since then they had been hawking.
On that occasion he had them under observation from half past 10
at night till quarter to two in the morning, and during the whole of
that time they were trying locked up shops. The elder prisoner had
bound round his waist two long leather straps 7 ft. long and a holder
in his pocket. They were discharged with & caution.
Inspector Lyons said that since their release on the occasion to
ferred to prisoners had been employed by Messrs. Locke, Lancaster
and Co., white lead merchants, Millwall, for over 12 months. The
elder prisoner was discharged for laziness and the younger on account
of ill-health. In 1905 Joseph was employed by the Central Catering
Company, but only for a few months, as the firm went out of exist-
ence. Frederick was employed from August last year till September
this year by one of his own countrymen, but left in consequence of a
dispute about money matters. The elder prisoner had been in Eng-
land 12 years and the younger 10 years.
