FREDERICK BOCK. I am a journeyman cooper, in the employ of Mr. George Blackman. I know the prisoner by sight—he lives in Gower-walk—about half-past six o'clock in the morning of the 22nd of July, I was piling staves in the yard—I saw the prisoner go across, and stoop down by the side of a beer-cooler that was in the road—he put his arm under and drew out three saws, placed them under his coat, and walked sharply away—I and Phillips followed—he got to Leman-street—I hailed him, and he came back—I asked him whose saws they were—he said his own—I asked where he was going to take them to—he said home, and that he had found them—I said, "They belong to some of Mr. Collier's men, I believe"—I said "I saw you take them from under the cooler"—I called a policeman, and gave him in charge. WILLIAM PHILLIPS. I am a labourer. I was coming out of Mr. Black man's yard—I saw the prisoner stoop down, put his arm under the cooler, draw out these three saws, and walk away with them—I went after him with Bock—I called him, and he came back—my mate asked him what he was going to do with them; but whether he said he was going to take them or not, I cannot say—my mate said he thought they belonged to some of Mr. Collier's men—he said, "If you think so, take them there and see"—he said he was going by and saw the handles of the saws sticking out, and picked them up. WILLIAM HOWE( police-constable H  168.) About half-past six o'clock I saw the prisoner and the two witnesses in Leman-street—Bock told me he saw the prisoner take up the three saws from under the cooler in Rupert-street—the prisoner said he found them, that he saw some chickens picking about there, which made him look under, and he was going to take them to Mr. Collier, perhaps they might belong to them—he gave his name Henry Jacobs, No. 2, Gower-walk—that is his name and address. ALFRED COHEN. I am a pencil-maker. I have a workshop in Gower-walk—I know all these saws—two of them belong to me, and one to a person named Pantland, who was employed on the premises—on the 22nd of July I was at work—I had fastened the gate up the night before—I do not recollect where the saws were—I know they were there on the Saturday afternoon as late as four o'clock, under the work-bench—on Monday morning they were gone—I afterwards heard that they were taken to the station. JOHN PANTLAND. I am a bricklayer. I have been employed the last three or four weeks in setting the steam-boiler at Mr. Cohen's—I went on Monday, the 22nd, and found the string which fastened the gate had been cut—this is my uncle's saw—his name is Robert Pantland. DAVID HATTERSLEY( police-constable H  90.) On Tuesday after this hap pened I heard something about this—I knew where the prisoner lived, and took him on the 3rd of Aug. in Church-lane, Whitechapel—I said, "You are the person who found these three saws?"—he said, "No, you are mistaken"—I took him to the station, and fetched Howe to identify him—the prisoner said it was not him, it was his brother—Howe fetched Phillips, and he still denied it—when asked by the sergeant on duty why he denied it, he said he had been in trouble before.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was going to work and saw some chickens; I looked and saw these three saws; I took them up, and the witnesses came and asked where I got them. CORNELIUS FOAY( police-constable H  98.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY.
