MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
JOSEPH HILL GREGORY. I am gardener to Mr. James Farquhar of
Charlton. On his premises there is a tool-house, where apples and things
of that sort are kept-it is within the fence that encloses the ground-yon
can go to it from the house without going outside the fence-on Saturday
the 26th of November, I fastened it myself, about five o'clock in the evening
-the applea, walnuts, aud baskets were all safe I went the following
morning about ten o'clock, and found some persons had entered by taking
a pane of glass out of the casement, that would enable them to force buck
the lock of the door--it appeared that that had been done-I missed from
seven to nine bushels of apples, two sacks, some seed peas, some walnuts, and
a gathering basket-these apples are the same kind that were lost-I know
this sack by the particular way in which the mouth is torn-I had used its
day or two before-here is the other sack-I know it by being a very large
one-I am certain it is the same the apples correspond with what they
left--I have got a sample of them.

JAMES WILD (police-constable R 141.) I went to the prosecutor's pre-
mises, and I saw the way the tool-house had been entered-I observed foot
marks, and covered them over-they proceeded towards the place that
had been entered, and towards a wood they were plainly visible,
and I covered them over--I afterwards went to the wood in company
with the gardener, and found these two sacks of apples they were
concealed in the wood, and covered over with the branches-there was a
gathering-basket close to the sacks--I remained there from half-past five
o'clock till about a quarter-past ten o'clock at night, when the prisoner and
another man came it was a very moonlight night-I had an opportunity
of seeing the men-I am certain the prisoner is one I and the other
officer laid down, and the two men took each a sack on their backs. We
got up, they ran away, and threw down their sacks of apples--we heard
them fall--they made their escape-I took the basket, and left the other
officer in charge of the apples- I went along the Woolwich-road towards the
turnpike, and coming back I met the other man who had been with the priso-
ber, coming out of a cottage where the prisoner lived--in consequence of
what he said I went towards the White Horse, Charlton-I followed hm,
and just as we got between there and Lord Howick's I saw the prisoner and
the other man coming along the foot-path together that man went
