HERBERT HAMLEY. I am warehouseman at Murdoch and Co.'s, 91 and 93, Farringdon Road—on 4th November, 1884, Mountford came to pur chase a pier-glass for his son, whose name he gave as Isett—he wanted it on a hiring agreement—he gave the address 10, Churton Place, Vaux hall Bridge Road, and said that his son was engaged at the Civil Service Stores—I sent the glass next day with this agreement. ( This was signed Isett, Civil Service Stores, Westminster, and was an undertaking to keep the glass in his own custody, and not sell it till it was paid for . ) He paid me 10 s .—he never paid any more—we sent our collector, and they were then gone—I saw the glass at the police-court in the custody of the police. SARAH WAITE. I live at 110, Churton Place, Pimlico✗—Isett and his wife took lodgings with us about the middle of October last, they occupied the back parlour as Mr. and Mrs. Isett—two or three days afterwards Mountford came, but I never heard his name, they called him "Uncle," he was there every day except Sundays—I remember the pier-glass coming; I missed it a few days afterwards, I don't know how it went—they were there four weeks, they paid me 11 s . and still owe me 13 s .—after they left, Mountford called and asked for a letter for Isett—I spoke to him about some of my property which had been taken away, and he brought back a flat iron, but I did not get the rest—I went to 51, Southam Street, but could not make anybody hear—I called again and saw Mountford, who said if I would not call for the money he would bring me 5 s . at a time till it was paid, but I never had any. THOMAS GAMMON. I am a pawn-broker, of 44, Wilton Road—on 7th November, 1884, this pier-glass was pawned with me in the name of J. Mountford, 10, Charlwood Place, and on 21st November this carpet for 3 l . in the same name, the owner has identified it.
   Cross-examined by MR. DILL . I did not take in the carpet, I took in the pier-glass, but can't recollect who pawned it. RICHARD HANCOCK. I am employed by Enoch Thomas, of Charles Street, Fitzroy Square—Isett came there on 15th November, 1884, and selected a carpet, value 5 l . 8 s ., on the hire system—he described himself as of Suffolk Street, Pimlico, and that he had been an assistant at the Army and Navy Stores 12 months—he had not got any money then, but he afterwards paid 10 s . deposit—the agreement was to be signed when the goods went home—this is it ( produced ), it is dated November 20th—Cramer brought the deposit to the shop the day before—I have identified the carpet, it is here, the pawnbroker showed it to me.
