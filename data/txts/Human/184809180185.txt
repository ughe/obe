 2218. WILLIAM JOHNSON , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. CLERK conducted the Prosecution. SARAH SWIXDELL. I am the wife of James Swindell, a general-dealer in Wellington-street. On 14th Sept., the prisoner came for a quarter of an ounce of tobacco, and gave me a shilling—I gave him 11 1/4 d . change, and he left—I looked at the shilling, it was one of the Queen's reign—I tried to bend it—I then put it into the till—there were three other shillings there—an officer came in in a few minutes—I had not taken any other money—I then took the money from the till—there were four shillings altogether, one a Queen's, and the other three King's. ANN DIXON. I assist Mr. Turner, a general-dealer, of Broad Wall. On 14th Sept., the prisoner came for half an ounce of tobacco—he gave me a shilling—I tried it with my teeth—it was hard, and I did not think it was bad—an officer came in, and I gave him the shilling. THOMAS WEST( policeman, M  249). On 14th Sept., I was with Whit lamb—I saw the prisoner in Friar-street with another man—we followed them, and saw the prisoner go into Mrs. Swindell's shop—after he came out, I went in, and received this shilling from Mrs. Swindell—I came out, and saw the prisoner again, in company with the same man—they went on to Broad Wall, and the prisoner left the other man, and went into Mr. Turner's—I went in as soon as I heard the money go on the counter, took the prisoner by the collar, and told him I wanted him for passing bad money—I saw Whitlamb take the shilling. JOHN WHITLAMB( policeman, M  89). I took the shilling—the prisoner became violent—the other man got away. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint—these shillings are both counterfeit. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Six Months .
 2219. WILLIAM WESSON , stealing 1 sack, value 1 s  .; and 1 1/2 bushel of beans, value 3 s  .; the goods of James Page , his master. JAMES PAGE. I am a farmer at Merton. The prisoner was my carter—it was his duty to feed the horses—I went to his house, and found these beans—they are mine, and the sack also—he said it was his custom to take the horses food to his house—he had no business to do so, there is a proper bin in the stable for it.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  How far is his house from the stable? A.  About fifty yards; I think I found these beans under the bed—they are inferior—he has been with me five or six years—he said he had taken them to feed his horses—he treated his horses well, and liked to see them fat—they work the better for it. NOT GUILTY.
