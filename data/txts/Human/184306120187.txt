Mrs. Taylor is my mother—I did not tell the prisoner to pawn this jacket—I did not know it was pawned—I ex pected to have it on when I awoke—I live close to the prisoner. ROBERT FROST SMITH( police-constable K  277.) I took the prisoner, and asked where the jacket was—she said she had pawned it, and he could not hurt her, as she had only taken 1 s . 3 1/2 d . out of his pocket.
   Prisoner.  He acknowledged that he gave me 1 s . 3 1/2 d ., and the next morn ing he looked through the window, and there were other individuals who had parted with their jackets in the same way; he laughed and said he had done the same as others did. JOHN GEARY. I am a general dealer, and live at No. 3, Bluegate-place. I was up next morning between five and six o'clock, and saw the prisoner and the prosecutor looking out of window and laughing at persons who had gone by without their jackets—it is a common thing in that neighbourhood if a sailor does not pay the female, that she takes his jacket and pledges it till the next morning. MARGARET GEARY. I am the wife of the last witness. We live in the house the prisoner does.—on the 3rd of June she came in with a young man who was in liquor; he sat down on a chair; she asked if he would give her a pint of ale, he said yes—he gave me 1 s .—I brought in a supper and a pot of ale—they then went up stairs, and she called me up to help to undress him—I untied the handkerchief from his neck—his jacket was off at that time—about half-past two o'clock the prosecutor came down, accusing her of robbing him of 2 sovereigns—he went out, and could not find an officer—in the morning he got up about half-past seven, and he said "Will you give me a drop of grog?"—she said, "Not till I have pawned the jacket"—he said, "Then I will pin you"—my husband was out at the door.—I saw three or four men without jackets, and the prosecutor looked out at the window and said he had lost his jacket. JACOB SANTNER re-examined.  I did not go down and accuse her of robbing me of two sovereigns—I had no gold. NOT GUILTY.
 1945. CATHERINE BEEBY was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of June , 1 bonnet, value 6 s  .; and 1 petticoat, 1 s  .; the goods of Thomas Wood field . ELIZABETH WOODFIELD. I am the wife of Thomas Woodfield, of Church-passage, Stepney. I took the prisoner from Wapping workhouse—she be haved rather oddly, and left—I took her back again—on the 7th of June, which was Stepney fair, she went out, and took my bonnet and petticoat, but I really do not think she intended to steal them.—I will give her one more trial. WILLIAM SAWYER( police-sergant K  24.) I took the prisoner—she said we had the bonnet on her head. JANE SCOTT. I found this petticoat on the prisoner. NOT GUILTY.
