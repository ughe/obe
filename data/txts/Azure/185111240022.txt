13. JOHN WHITE, stealing 1 watch, value 21. ; the property of Isador
Abrahams, from his person.
MR. conducted the Prosecution.
I am a native of Holland, and am a ship agent,
ISADOR ABRAHAMS.
living in Scarborough-street East, Goodman's-fields. On Monday, 10th
Nov., between 11 and 12 0'clock in the morning, I was in Princes-street,
near the Bank, during the procession of the Lord Mayor's show—there was
a very large crowd—the prisoner stood next to me—I felt something in my
pocket, and felt something go from it, and I saw the prisoner go with my
watch from the place : it had been in my right hand waistcoat pocket—I am
quite sure I saw it in the prisoner's hand—he ran into tbe crowd—I ran after
him—he stopped, and turned back of his own accord, and I asked him for my
watch—be said, Oh, I have not got your watch ! "—he called out several
names, Bill, Tom, and so on and several people came up, and said, it the
man has got your watch, why don't you search him? "—and they eame
between us, and stood in front of me—they said to the prisoner, 'by don't
you run ran away, and one person, a little fellow, came in front
of me, to give the prisoner the opportunity of running away—I ran after him,
and called "Stop thief! "—he was seized by a policeman—I did not lose
sight of him—he made a very pitiful face. and said, I never took your
watch ; and coming from the station to Guildhall, he said, If you will go
with me to my house, I will give you 21., and you must drop the matter "—I
gave more than 21. for my watch ; it was a silver one; it bas not been found
—this ring ( produced) was found on the ground—the prisoner found it about
three yards from the place where he took my watch—he said, "Why don't
you look round, may be you will find your watch? and before we moved
from the place, he said, There is your ring on the ground "—I picked it up
—he saw it—it was not possible for bim to have seen it on the ground, be-
cause I stood before him.
Cross-examined by MB. COOPER. Q. I suppose the crowd WAS very thick
there? A. Yes; the people were standing as close as they could—the
goner was not standing by me a minute before he ran away—he was about a
yard from me at the time 1 108t my watch—I went up to him, and said, J
want my watch "—he directly ran through the crowd—I ran after him, and
he came back again—he could have run away—some person said, be is not
the man that atole your watch, another man did it—I should recollect that
man if I were to see him—I called out that I had lost my watch—the people
about heard me, but they took no interest in my loss—I saw 7d. and a little
comb found on the prisoner.
MB. BIRNIE. Q. Was it one of the persons who answered to his eall of Bill
and Jack, that said he was not the man that took your watch ? A. Yes ;
I am quite sure of that.
ROBERT JOHNSON (City-policeman, 812). On Monday, 10th Nov., I saw
the prosecutor in Princes-street running after the prisoner, calling out Stop
thief! "—I took the prisoner to the station, searched him, and found 6d.
in silver, lid. in copper, and a comb—in coming from the station to Guild—
hall, he said to the prosecutor, I will give you 21. if you will say no more
about it."
Cross-emmined. Q. Was the prisoner walking when you took him ? A.
He was running from the prosecutor—there was a great crowd.
GUILTY." Aged 23.—Transportedfor Seven Years.
