GUILTY. Aged 28.-Confined Nine Months.
Before Mr. Recorder
1067. CHARLES CHAMBERS, feloniously cutting and wounding
Matthew Jones on his head, with intent to kill and murder him.--2nd
COUNT, to do him grievous bodily harm.
MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution
WILLIAM CHURCH. I am taskmaster at the workhouse of St. Mary,
Lambeth. I know the prisoner perfectly well—he has been in and out of
the house during the time I have been there, about nine months on the
morning of 27th Sept. I went to the place where the prisoner slept about
half past 5 o'clock-he was in bed I told him to get up-he said he should
not get up then-I visited him again about half an hour afterwards I
again found him in bed, and told him to get up-he said he would see me
first-I visited him a third time, about 8 o'clock, I think he still
persisted that he would not get up, and made use of bad language I
told him I should call the attention of the master to it, which I did-Mr.
Jones, the assistant master, and myself then went to him, and we pulled the
clothes off him-with that he got on to the ground with his feet-he was
sitting, and he rose up and struck Mr. Jones on the chest with his foot-he
made use of bad language and swearing—I do not recollect what he
ordered Mr. Jones out of the room--Mr. Jones refused to go, and he said,
"You if you don't go, I have got something here will make you"
-with that he went to a bed in the further end of the room, and drew
from under the bed this bar of iron, and he came back, and gave a slashing
out with it towards Mr. Jones's head-it was a violent blow, and I saw the
blood flow immediately afterwards-Mr. Jones did not strike him from first
to last, but after he struck, Mr. Jones closed in with him, and I took the
bar from him--this is a protection bar of the window guard - I had seen it
in its proper place about 7 o'clock the evening before it was attached to
the window-it had been fastened with screws, and it had been unscrewed
--this is only half the bar the other half was under the bed when I saw
it the evening before, it was one long bar attached to the window-I cannot
say that I had ever heard the prisoner use any threats towards Mr. Jones
he was a man that used very violent language-he was placed in that
Ward by order of the guardians, the night before, for bis misconduct it is
not a worse ward than the others, only they do more labour in it it is a
Ward for casual paupers the whole of the inmates of the workhouse got up
at half past 5 o'clock at that time Mr. Jones did not strike the prisoner
from first to last till he had been struck by the bar, he did not lay hand
on him-I did not touch him till I took the iron: out of his hand neither
Mr. Jones nor I had any bludgeon or staff, or anything in our hands-
neither of us threatened to strike him.
