165. MARY OSBORN was indicted for stealing 1 cloak, value 10s. ; 3
handkerchiefs, 3s. and 1 cap, 1s. ; the goods of George Gardner.
ANN GARDNER. I am the wife of George Gardner, of Kentish-town-the
prisoner lived in the same house with me-I missed a eloak, a cap, and some
handkerchiefs ; this is my cloak-it hung on a nail behind the door-I saw
it safe on the Wednesday night, and missed it on the Saturday morning after
-the handkerchiefs and cap were wrapped up in a bundle, to go to the wash
think I saw them safe in the bundle on the Friday.
SARAH MAYNARD. I live in Crown-place, Kentish-town, with my mother.
On Saturday, the 11th of Nov., I gaw the prisoner in Streatly-terrace, Cam-
den-town-she asked me to take a cloak into a pawnbroker's, which I did-
she had nothing beside the cloak.
NOT GUTrY.
166. MARY OSBORN was again indicted for stealing 4 shirts, value 8s.
1 counterpane, 78. 4 pillow cases, 48. 2 table covers, 4s. ; 3 shifts, 6s. ; 5
petticoats, 108. 1 bed-gown, 2s. 2 table cloths, 58.| 2 napkins, 38. and
11 yards of lawn, 2s. ; the goods of George Gardner.
ANN GARDNER. I had some shirts of my husband's, George Gardner, also
a counterpane, some pillow cases, table covers, shifts, a petticoat, table cloths,
and napkins- missed them on Saturday, the 11th of Nov.-they had been
in a large box in my room-I had not seen them for some time, but I went
to the box, which was at my bedside, on the Thursday, for a bed-gown and a
shift-] could not find them-] had no light with me- did not go to the
box again till the Saturday morning, and I could not find these things--that
was the place where I usually kept them-it was in general kept unlocked-
I did not lock it on the Thursday evening-I saw these things again at the
pawnbroker's-they are my husband's.
CHARLEs KEMP (police-constable 81.) On Monday, the 18th of Nov.,
I received information, and made inquiries-] found all these things at the
pawnbroker's, after I got the duplicates from the prisoner, when she was
searched at the station.
AMELtA BARRETT. I am employed to searcb females. I searched the
prisoner, and found ten duplicates down her bosom in a little purse-I deli-
vered them to sergeant Porter, and he gave them to Kemp.
RICHARD DANIBLS (policeman.) I was called to Mrs. Gardner's on the
14th of Nov.-the prisoner lives with her mother in the same house-I
searched the room (the prisoner was in custody at the time)-I found four
shirts, three handkerchiefs, and some stockings in the room-Mrs. Gardner
has owned the whole of them.
JAMEs JoNes, shopman to Mr. Trail, pawnbroker. I produce a sheet, a
shirt, and some other things-] know the prisoner by sight, and have seen
her in our shop, but I cannot recollect whether she pawned any of these
things-these duplicates which were found on her were given to the person
who pawned these articles.
Prisoner's Defence. I am very sorry for it.
