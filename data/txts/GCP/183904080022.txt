1182. WILLIAM MOODY and MARY MOREY were indicted for
stealing, on the 2nd of April, 1 shift, value 38.; 6 pictures framed and
glazed, value 23.; 2 shirts, value 88.; 1 counterpane, value 88.; 8 petti.
coats, value 5s.6d. ; 1 pair of stockings, value 2s. ; 2 pillow-cases, value
28. ; 1 bolster-case, value 2s.; and 1 nightcap, value 1s. 60.; the goods
of Aaron Ensor.
ELLEN Ensor. I am the wife of Aaron Ensor, and live in Strutton-
ground, Westminster. On the 1st of April the prisoners took my two-pair
front room, at 45. 6d. a week, as man and wife--they took possession that
evening, and next day, about four o'clock, they left without notice-they
had locked the door, and taken the key-I went in, and missed the articles
stated--the apparel was wet linen, which hung in the adjoining room, which
was locked- I found the lock forced—they were apprehended next day.
BENJAMIN RICHARD MURRAY. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Union-
street, Borough. I have a petticoat which was pawned by a female, on the
2nd of April-here is the duplicate I gave for it.
Mark NOBLE. I am a policeman. I took the prisoners into custody-
the male prisoner gave me seven duplicates, four of which relate to the stolen
property- I have a shift and six pictures, which I found at a house in
Duke-court, Borough, where they had taken a lodging-I found the female
prisoner there.
Peter Henry PIDGEON. I am servant to Mr. Powell, a pawnbroker.
I have a shirt which was pawned by the male prisoner.
SAMUEL SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. I have a shirt and counterpune
--the female prisoner pawned the counterpane-I do not know about the
shirt.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
Moody's Defence. I went out at twelve o'clock, and had nothing in my
hand-I came in again, and went out at two o'clock-the witness says I
left at four o'clock-I never saw the room myself.
Morey's Defence. Distress was the cause of it-he was out of employ-
ment- it was my fault, and not his.
MOODY-GUILTY. Aged 27.
MOREY-GUILTY. Aged 34: Transported for Seven Years.
1183. ELIZABETH KINSELLA was indicted for stealing, on the 10th
of March, 1 watch, value 8l.; 1 guard-chain, value 11.; 1 seal, value 6d.
1 watch-key, value 4d. ; 1 ring, value 2d. ; 1 breast-pin, value 48.; 1
handkerchief, value 4s.; 1 pair of gloves, value 1s. 6d.; and 4 sovereignd;
the goods of John William Parlour.
John William PARLOUR. I am an accountant, and collect tradesmen
bills-I live in York-street, City-road. On the 10th of March I was getting
out of a cab, and being rather the worse for liquor I made a false step, and
injured my face and legs-the prisoner came up and condoled with me, and
induced me to go with her she was a stranger-we went to No. 1,
Woodstock-street, Oxford-street, to lodge for the night-I had in my waist-
coat pocket five sovereigns I sent out a sovereign for liquor, which we
had in the house-I had a watch, a chain, a brooch, a pair of gloves, a hand-
kerchief, and a scarf-I went to bed before eleven o'clock-she did not g)
to bed with me-when I awoke in the morning I found I was robbed of
four sovereigns, and all my property-this is my watch and chain-looking
at them)--) did not go with her for any improper purpose.
