JAMES FROST. I am a bricklayer. On 15th Oct. I was in Mr. Fawcett's house—he requested me to take care of the prisoner while he went to fetch a policeman—while he was gone, the prisoner asked me if the note was a forged one—I said I believed so, he would hear further of it—I asked him if he had been building any houses in the neighbourhood—he said "Yes"—I asked where—he said, "Against the Elephant and Castle"—I asked him what street there—he said he did not exactly know the street—he said, "Then it is a forged note"—I said, "Yes, have you got the money with you to pay for itr—he said no, he had not, but no doubt he could get it if I went with him—I asked if he knew where he got the note—he said, yes, he took it of a gentleman—I said, "Did the gentleman make you a present of it f—he said, no, but he paid it to him for work he had done for him as a plasterer.
   Prisoner. Q.  You asked me what I was by trade; I told you I was a bricklayer and plasterer, did I not? A.  No; I asked if you had been building houses in the neighbourhood, and you said, "Yes"—you did not say you had been plastering the houses there—you said you could no doubt find the man that gave you the note, if I came with you—I said no, I would give you in charge. JAMES THOMPSON. ( policernan, M  204). I took the prisoner into custody—I charged him with uttering a forged note on the 9th—he said, "I did not know it was forged till now; I stood on Newington-causeway one day last week; some gentleman asked if I wanted any work; I said I did; he gave me the 5 l . note to get some laths, and as I knew Mr. Fawcett, I went to him for the laths"—he said the gentleman told him that his name was Clark, and that he lived at 31 or 32, Westmoreland-road, Newington—(there is no such number there)—he said the gentleman had told him he would remain there till he came back with the change, and when he left Mr. Fawcett's, he returned and gave the gentleman the change—on the way to the police court next morning, he pulled his trowsers down and said, "Here, I have got my working trowsers on now"—I said, "Yes, it is a strange thing you did not say so last night"—he said, "Oh, it don't matter, for I have nothing on I care about"—I did not search him. ROBERT CLARK. I am a carpenter and joiner, at No. 20, Westmoreland-road, Walworth. I know nothing of the prisoner—I never saw him that I know of—I never gave him a 5 l . note, or sent him to buy laths at Mr. Fawcett's—I do not know whether there is any other person of my names living in Westmoreland-road. JOSEPH BUMSTEAD. I am an inspector of notes at the Bank of England, This note is a forgery.
