received: a confession must flow voluntarily from the mind of the person

sible if made under the impression that he might suffer if he did not make it;
there were authorities to sustain both propositions, and, after alluding to the cir.
cumstances of the present case, he referred to Reg. V. Williams, 2 Denison's
Croun Cases, p. 433 Reg. V. Warringham, 15 Jurist, 318 and Reg. V. Gar-
her, 1 Denison, 329. Mr. STRAIGHT (on same side) referred to Reg: V. Shep-
herd, 7 Carrington and Payne, 579, and urged that, if the acts and circum-
8tances of the case were taken in connescion with the observations, there was

fession. MR. GIFFARD in contending that the evidence toas admissible, stated
that the authorities referred to, were overruled by later decisions, Reg, V. Baldry,
2 Denison, 430; Reg. V. Sleeman, Dearsley, 249 Reg. V. Parker, Lee and
Cave, 42; and Reg. V. Zeigert, Sessions Papers, Vol. 66, P. 163: the principle
upon which these confessions were excluded, was, if any tcorldly advantage toas
