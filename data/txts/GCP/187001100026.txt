NEW COURT.-Tuesday, January 11th, 1870.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
160. JOHN ROBINSON (28), and EDWARD PORT (21), Robbery
on Thomas Leaver, and stealing from his person 7 lbs. of bacon, 1 lb. of sugar,
and 1 oz. of tea, his property.
MR. LEIGH conducted the Prosecution ; Mr. M. WILLIAMS appeared for
Robinson, and MR. METCALFE for Port.
Thomas LEAVER.On Saturday night, 11th December, I was going from
Edgware across the fields to a place called The Hall, in the parish of
Hendon--I had to pass a reservoir of the Great Northern Railway Company
--two men overtook me I cannot swear to the tall one, but the short one
was the prisoner Port, who caught hold of my arm, hit me on the mouth,
and knocked me down--I got up again, and he knocked me down again,
and fell on me the tall one kicked me on the ribs-I halloaed, and they
said if I halloaed they would serve me worse--they took my basket, which
contained bacon, sugar, and tea, and threw the basket down-they stopped
behind, and I got up and went away-I afterwards picked out the prisoners
at the station, as having been in the White Lion public-house with me, and
I am able to swear to Port--we were together about an hour and a half-
when I left the White Lion they had left about five minutes—I was sober.
Cross-examined by MR. WILLIAMS. Q. Can you swear that the taller
man was Robinson A. No-I know Frederick Wilds—I did not charge
him--I said nothing about him and the robbery to anybody.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALYE. Q. You picked out Robinson as well
as Port! 4. Yes—I was two hours at the public-house, drinking-a number
of other people were there I was playing a concertina there-the people
were not dancing—they did not give me beer for playing—I played for
amusement-I left my concertina behind me, because I had to carry my
basket, not because I could not pay for my beer-I did pay for it, but I
did not have much-the reservoir is not half a mile from the public-house
it is across two little meadows.
DAVID ABRAHAMS. I am landlord of the White Lion-on Saturday, 11th
December, Leaver was there, from 8 or 9 o'clock to 11.30—the prisoners
were there also, and left about a quarter of an hour after him, as near as I
can say-Leaver had been playing his concertina, and left it with me-I
cannot say he was exactly sober when he left, but he was not very drunk.

I told them -- Thomas Smith was one of them-he left, I believe, with the
