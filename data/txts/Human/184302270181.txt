Prisoner's Defence.  I had been to the docks, and could not get any em ployment; I met with two men, we went to this house, and played at bagatelle; I had occasion to go into the yard; Beard came before me, and walked through the house, he was talking to the landlady, and I looked up to see what time it was; if I had been guilty there was plenty of time to give me in charge. THOMAS JONES. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person—I was at that time an officer—I took him, and he had been convicted before that. GUILTY. Aged 29.— Transported for Seven Years .
 1002. GEORGE MEAD and ABEL ALLEN were indicted for stealing, on the   13th of February , 1 truss of hay, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Robert Hodson , the master of Mead. ROBERT HODSON. I am a farmer, and live at Willesden; Mead was my carter. On the morning of the 13th of Feb. I delivered him half a load of hay and half a load of straw, to take to Mr. Hoare's stable, in Great York mews, and about the middle of the day I received a letter.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How many trusses were there? A.  Eighteen of each—I am not the proprietor of the farm, but I transact the business of it for my uncle, who is aged—his name is also Robert Hodson—Mead has been in my service about eight years—he has three children. WILLIAM MITCHELL. I live in Earl-street, and am jailor of Marylebone Police-court. On the morning of the 13th of Feb. I was coming along the New-road from Edgeware-road—I saw Mead driving the bone and cart—Allen was walking and talking with him by the side of the cart—I saw the cart stop facing Homer-row, New-road—Mead loosened the ropes—as I passed I heard Mead say to Allen, "Carry it on your back"—I turned my head, and saw Allen take a truss of hay from the load, and go down Homer row with it—I then met Thompson, and gave him information.
   Allen. Q.  Did you see me take it off the cart? A.  No; but I saw it on your shoulder, and you were going from the cart. THOMAS HENRY THOMPSON( police-sergeant D  4.) I received informa tion from Mitchell, and met Allen in the New-road—he had nothing then—I said, "What have you done with the truss of hay? you have taken it to Atkins's, you must go and show me where it is"—he went and pointed it out to me—I asked him to carry it away—he refused—I said, "I shall take you into custody for stealing this"—he said, "I have not stolen it, I was ordered to carry it by Mead"—I asked if he received any money—he said no—I took him to the station—he said Mead was in the habit of leaving hay—I returned, and took Mead at the corner of Stingo-lane—I said I wanted him for stealing a truss of hay—he made no answer, and I took him to the station, and there he said he was allowed to sell it by the coachman and groom of Mr. Hoare, on account of their not being able to give him anything for the delivery—I saw the vacancy of a truss of hay on the cart—this is the truss.
   MR. HODSON . This truss is like my hay, but I would rather not swear to it—here is a remarkable brown vein in it which went round the rick—Mead was not authorized to sell it.
   Allen's Defence.  I was out of employ, and Mead asked me to take the truss to such a place, and to say he would call as he came back. NOT GUILTY.
