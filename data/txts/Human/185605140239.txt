Cross-examined. Q.  Mrs. Eagle is a very old person? A.  Yes—my husband does not manage the business, he is away altogether; I do what I can, as I always did—Crampton had been in the service I dare say tea years—my husband had nothing to do with the business—he was out of employ at that time, but since that he has got some employment at the wharves in East Smithfield—he went on errands for my mother, but he had nothing to do in the yard—I know Crampton cannot write—I have paid him one week under another, and sometimes my mother has. THOMAS HORNER. I am potman at the Three Colts, in Grove-street, Hackney, close by Mrs. Eagle's. I saw Mr. Strong's cart on 19th March, and Neale with it—he asked me if I had seen Crampton—I said, "Yes"—he told me to tell him he wanted to see him—I went and delivered the message to Crampton—he said he could not come down, he had better see the old lady—he gave me this ticket to give to Neale—I gave it to Neale—the cart was at that time at the public house with grains in it—after I gave Neale the ticket, he took the cart down to the yard, and delivered the grains—after that Neale came back with the cart which had some grains in it, and he told me to take a pot of beer round to Crampton, which I did—I did not take it the usual way, but another way, a back way, because of Mr. O'Brien—Crampton told me that when his gave me the ticket—that was on the 19th, the time before the last that the cart came—I saw Neale with the cart, and he and Crampton came into the tap room after the grains had been delivered—that was four or five days before the 19th—they used to settle for the beer: they had—sometimes it would, be St.;, Neale paid for it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Some directions were given to you by Crampton on 19th? A.  Yea—I never mentioned before about his telling me to go another way—I was not asked that before. MARY O'BRIEN. re-examined.  I never saw Crampton read writing—I asked him how many grains had been left on the 14th, and oa the 19th also. SAMUEL NEALE. ( the prisoner ). I was carman to Mr. Strong; I forget whether I took any grains to Mrs. Eagle's on 14th. March—it is a long time ago—I remember the two last times I went there with grains—I quite entirely forget how many grains I took there the last tine but one—I got five quarters from the distiller's—they wen put into the cart—they were not measured—there was a board dividing them—there were three quarters at the bottom, and two at the top—I delivered those on the top of the board to Mrs. Eagle—no more, as far as I can recollectr—this seems like, the bill I gave Mrs. O'Brien on the last occasion, but I cannot read it—the pot man gave me one on the last occasion, and I gave it to Mrs. O'Brien—I received for three quarters 19 s . 3 d .—Crampton did not, say anything to me about receiving for three quarters at that time—he has sometimes said so—I forget now what it was that Crampton said to ma—after I had paid 12 s . 6 d . to Mr. Strong, I believe the rest of the money was nearly all spent in beer—it might not be all spent that day—I do not recollect that I paid any money to Crampton—no, I did not—I did not, pay him any on the 14th, or on the last day—I am sure I do not know how I came to spend it all in beer with Crampton—I quite forget now what he said to me about the three quarters. NOT GUILTY.
