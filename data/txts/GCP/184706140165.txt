1497. MARY ANN HOY was indicted for stealing 1 shawl, value 30s.;
1 bonnet, 108. ; 2 petticoats, 28.; 2 pairs of drawers, 1s. 6d. ; and I pair of
boots, 2s.; the goods of Esther Cohen: 1 shawl, 308.; 1 gown, 48. ; and
1 handkerchief, 6d. ; the goods of Charlotte Cohen, her mistress.
CHARLOTTE Cohen. I am single, and live in New Gravel-lane. The
prisoner was my servant-on the 28th of May I told her to get up and open
The shop-in about half an hour my sister came down, and told me some-
thing--I found that the prisoner was gone- had not told her to go-my
boxes were broken open--they had been shut-one was locked the night
before-all the things were lying about the floor-I missed two shawls, a
dress, two petticoats, and a silk handkerchief-one shawl belonged to Esther
Cohen, my sister-she lived with me--this sbawl produced is hers--the other
things are mine, I have had this dress three years- I had only bought the
shawls on Monday-I made these two petticoats-I had not had them a
week in the house they had been safe in the boxes--we went after the pri-
soner about eight o'clock in the morning, and caught her at Barking about
five o'clock in the evening.
THOMAS JOHN BLAKE (police-constable K 137.) I went with the pro-
secutrix to Barking, took the prisoner, and told her I wanted her, for stealing
things at Cohen's, in New Gravel-lane-she said, "Yes, I stole the things; I
will take you where they are"- she took me to her house, in Barking-I
found some there she put her hand into her pocket, and gave this duplicate
for some of the things.
Prisoner. I have lived with those girls seven months, and kept them by
my prostitution.
CHARLOTTE COHEN re-examined. It is not so, on my oath.
Guilty. Aged 17.-Confined Six Months.
1498. SELINA ALLEN was indicted for stealing 2 coats, value 16. 58;
1 set of curtains, 88. ; 8 yards of silk, 78. ; seven knives, 18d. ; 7 forks, 1s.
2 pairs of shoes, 11. 88.; 1 waistcoat, 28. ; 4 blinds, 168. and I bruisb; ls.
the goods of George Macnalley, her master.
GEORGE MACNALLEY. I live in Park-street, Camden-town. The prisoner
was in my employ six years-about a month ago I missed a pair of boots
they were my son's, George Robert Macnalley's-I asked the prisoner where
my son's boots were--she said they were sent over to the shoemaker's to be
repaired-I also missed my own boots-she told me she knew nothing of
them-I told her if she did not tell me where they were I would call a police-
man and take her up.
BENJAMIN BADDELEY (police-constable S 248.). On the 4th of June I
took the prisoner in charge at Mr. Macnalley's- I told her what it was for
she wished to go to the water-closet-I would not allow her-I followed her
into a back room--the prosecutor said he should press the charge-I took the
prisoner to the station-she was there searched by Mrs. Doss, who gave me
these duplicates.
ANN AMELIA Doss. I live at the Police-station, in Albany-street-I search
female prisoners. I searched the prisoner, and found forty-five duplicates in
a bag, between her petticoat and her dress-she said she had pledged some,
and another woman some I gave them to Baddeley.
JAMES MARLOW. I live with Mr. Castle, a pawnbroker, in High-street,
Camden-town. I produce a brush, pawned in Dec. last- this is the ticket of
t-I produce a coat and some white window-curtains-I find duplicates of
them.
