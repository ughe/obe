I live in Long-alley, Finsbury, and am a hatter—some weeks before the 9th of Oct. I saw a van at the Swan, in the Dover-road—Bashford was the driver, and there was a little boy on it—I saw Doughton riding on the box with Bashford—Bashford asked Doughton to gave him a sheet from the top of the van, under the tarpaulin, and Doughton let it down from the top into a sack—Bashford took it up the yard by the said of the public-house—I saw him come back without it—on the 7th of Oct. I was at the George-tap, Aldermanbury—I saw Bashford conme in, and Doughton asked him if he had got rid of the sheet—he said no, he should like to do so—Doughton said he thought he could get him a customer—a sovereign was mentioned as the price of it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.  I am not at present in any situation—I had two days' work last week in George-street, Blackfriar-road—Doughton formerly lived with Mr. Sweeting, in Cheapside—he drove his cart—I was not in the habit of driving in his cart—I have a brother who lives up the George-yard. and it was him who lost the harness—I never heard that Doughton was accused of having stolen it—I go to the George-yard perhaps on an evening—I had seen the harness—I dare say I might have seen it in company with Doughton—I frequently go in the stable—I only have Bashford by seeing him once or twice—when I saw Douhgton hand this tarpaulin to Bashford, I made no remark—I did not know but he might be obliged to leave it there—hearing that Doughton was seen with the policeman, I mentioned the tarpaulin to my brother—we were merely talking about the circumstance, and I happened to say that I saw it, and my brother brought me up to the policeman. ROBEART PARKMAN( City policeman  133.) On the 9th of Oct., I received in formation from Doughton. I went to the Swan, in the Dover-road—I gave Doughton a marked sovereign—I followed him to the stable, and saw Wealfare—he was bringing a sack from one of the stalls in the stables—he looked at me, and said, "You have got a good bargain'—I said, "Is it full size?"—he said, "Yes, it is fifteen feet long"—the sack was them put on Doughton's shoulder, and taken out—I asked Wealfare what he sold it for—he said, "1 l ."—I said, "Was it your property?"—He said, "No"—I said, "How do you account for the possession of it?"—he said, "I know nothing of it, I was only told to sell it"—I said I was an officer, and asked him to be so kind as to hand me the sovereign—he put his pocket, and gave me the marked sovereign out.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When had you known Doughton before? A.  About three weeks before—there was some harness stolen from the same yard where he lived—I cannot say that I suspected him more than any other person—there was inquiry made about the yard—I asked Doughton to accompany me to Smithfield, to see if we could discover it—I never charged him with being the thief—I never suspected him the least in the world.
