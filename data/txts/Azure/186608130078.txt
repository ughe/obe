FOURTH COURT.—Thunday, 16th, 1866.
Before Robert Malcolm Kerr,
725. FREDERICK HIBBERT (20), Burglary in the dwellinghouse of
Charles Brown on the night of 2nd July, and stealing eight coats and two
pair of trousers, value 51., his property.
MR. HARRIS conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES BROWN. I am a clothes salesman, and live at 45, Prince's
Road, Notting Hill—on Monday night, 2nd July, gbout half-past nine, I
locked up my shop—all my premises were then secure—the shop was full
of clothes—I returned about half-past eleven to twelve on the same night
—I found the candle that I had placed on the counter, moved, and a ticket
was on the counter that had been on a coat—the coat was missing—I an
quite certain it was there when I went out—I found a shelf ou which some
clothes were kept was empty, and thé clothes gone—an entrance had been
effected from the back window—that was fastened with a catch—some
flowers on tho ledge had beeu lifted down, aud the catch forced backwards
—the lock upon the door leading to the room had been forced back—the
door was open—it was shut when I went away—I found a screwdriver
in the shop—the constable compared it with the marks on the door—the
prisoner lodged in the kitchen under my shop—he bad done so about two
months—I didn't see him on the night of the robbery, but I know he left
the premises on the Monday morning—I heard him come upstairs and go
out—I lost eight coats and some trousers—they have not been found.
ELIZABETH GANT. I am the wife of Joseph Gant, and live in the
front kitchen under the prosecutor's premises—the prisoner is my brother
—he was not there on the night of the robbery—he has not been there
since the Monday night the robbery was committed—I saw him go out
that night—I spoke to him—I asked him when he was coming in—he
said, Not just yet "—l know this screwdriver—it is mine.
