   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was there any dispute with James Bayley? A.  No, the prisoner said that what he said was right—James said he had paid him 3 s . and some odd pence—I had no knowledge of the robbery till the bills were out. WILLIAM CRAWLEY( police-constable R  53.) About half past seven o'clock on the night of the 16th of November I was in the watch-house at Greenwich, where the prisoner was confined—he said, "The lead which I sold to Mr. Bayley was some lead I took from the back of the cottage, situ ated on the premises of Mr. Oliver"—I went to those premises the fol lowing morning, and saw some lead had been taken that appeared to corre spond with one of the pieces—I had said nothing to him to induce him to make the confession.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he tell it to you without asking him any questions? A.  Yes, quite of his own accord—Bayley, the constable, was present, and Kent the inspector of the R division, and Sizer a policeman, and several more—I had no conversation with Mr. Bayley before that, that I am aware of—I heard of the reward—I cannot say who told me—Bayley never told me, to my recollection—I my have mentioned about the re ward to the young Bayleys—I heard of this robbery on the night of the 16th—it was from one of the Bayleys.
   COURT . Q.  Do you know what officers were examining the premises before that? A.  No, I never heard of any officer being on the premises before the 16th. JAMES PARRY( police-sergeant R  8.) I went to Mr. Oliver's and com pared the lead, and about half past eleven o'clock on the night of the 16th I went to the cage, and asked the prisoner, "Whose garden do you mean the lead was in that you left?"—he said "At Mr. Oliver's, at the back of the cottage, where I got this," and he said the remainder of the lead was in his garden—I went to the prisoner's house, and could not find it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You asked him whose garden the lead was in that he left? A.  Yes, I believe they were the words—I do not think I told the Magistrate I used the word "buried"—I know the Bayley family well—I know of the reward—I have spoken to them about it two or three times—I was before the Magistrate—Thomas Bayley was there—there was not a word said by him before the Magistrate about his having entered any lead in any book.
   MR. PHILLIPS called JOHN DRAKE FINCH. I am clerk to the Magistrates of Woolwich. I attended the examination of these men, and took the depositions—Thomas Bayley did not say one word about his having entered in the book the account of the lead—there was never a word said about a book, I am quite certain—I believe the uncle was present—there was no dispute about the price of the lead. NOT GUILTY.
