69. FRANZ FELIX STUMM (34), Feloniously forging and uttering
a deed purporting to be between Urban Napoleon Stanger and another,
with intent to defraud. Other counts for forging and uttering a receipt
for 6501.
Messrs. POLAND, SNAGG, and LITTLETON, Proseouted; MESSRS. MONTAGU
WILLIAMS and FILLAN Defended.
CHRISTIAN ZEUTHER. I am a German, I can speak English _I live nt
the Black Bull in Old Montague Street, Whitechapel-1 was formerly
journeyman to Mr. Stanger, at 136, Lever Street, St. Luke's I was there
as a journeyman before Mr. Stanger purchased the business in 1879-up
to that time it had been carried on by Jacob Kents--Mr. Stanger carried
on the business continuously until November last year on Saturday
evening, 12th November, I saw him at 5 or 10 minutes to 12 o'clock, at
midnight-- there were three other master bakers along with him, the
prisoner, Mr. Kramer, and Mr. Lentz-Mr. Kramer has since died
have seen Mr. Lentz since-the three did not come into the house - I
heard him say "Good night" and the three went home, and he same
into the shop-I did not see him again, I went to bed-I did not see him
go to bed-my bedroom is on the second floor, at the top of the house
Mr. Stanger's was on the first floor-Mrs. Stanger was at home when he
came in-besides Mr. and Mrs. Stanger only me and another chap named
Peter slept in the house-Peter works for Mr. Lentz now-next morning
I had a conversation with Mrs. Stanger, in consequence of which I went
to the prisoner's house in St. John Street Road- he was a master baker
there, and lived there with his wife-I first saw the prisoner's wife, and
afterwards saw the prisotier-I told him that Mrs. Stanger wanted to see
him, and to come down directly-he said, "I will be there directly”_
I then went back, and shortly after the prisoner came, about 9.30 or
10 o'clock in the morning-I saw him there till 2 о'elock, and then from
7 o'clock in the evening till 12 o'clock-he stopped there that night-I
did not see Mr. Stanger that day, I have never seen him since on the
Monday morning I saw the prisoner there, he was baking the broad.
attending to the business-about half past 4 o'clock I said to him, "How
is the master he said, "Very ill he did not say where he was
the prisoner helped in the business from that day till I left in May this
year-I afterwards went back again he did not live in the house at first
he was always there in the night for the first few days he went home in
the afternoon-he slept there at night, in the parlour of course he had
to help at night in the bakehouse - Mrs. Stanger continued in the house
---the shop was painted and the name changed from Statiger to Stumm
I think that was in April-I remember the prisoner being taken into
custody- I was in Lever Street that evening, working there-Elizabeth
Harvey, the servant, was there we called her Alice-I saw her in the
