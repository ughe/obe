On the 17th of July I saw the chests in my master's van, directed to Carter, of Croydon, in Wilson's writing—I followed the van—I examined the carman's book which was in the care of Wilson, except when the carman had it—I went to the Talbot, and saw the two chests delivered into the yard—I sent for a police man—I saw the directions after they were delivered into the yard—they were delivered at Mr. Moore's offices—I afterwards saw Savage at the police-station—he handed me this bill—it was Wilson's duty to enter in the order-book goods that were sent out.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was Wilson's situation inferior to yours in point of emolument? A.  Yes—it was my duty to examine the delivery-book from time to time, but perhaps I have neglected it now and then—I do not know Carter, of Croydon—Wilson and I were good friends—it was his duty to attend to the books when Mr. Molyneux was absent—I was on the premises all day—if a customer came, it was Wilson's duty to send for me—I have looked through the books—there is no such name as Mr. Carter, of Scotland. JOHN WESTMORLAND( police-constable M  233.) I went to the Talbot-inn on the 17th of July—I saw Mr. Riding there, and he pointed out two chests to me—they were directed to Mr. Carter, of Croydon—I saw the prisoner Dyke come to Mr. Moore's office where the two chests were left—he looked at them, and removed them from Mr. Moore's office to Mr. Stanbury's office—Dyke then tore off the directions which were on the chests, and put them into his pocket—he went away—he came back within an hour, and spoke to Mr. Stanbury, who allowed one of his porters to take one chest away along with Dyke—I followed them to the prisoner Savage's, No, 17, Mint-street—the chest went in there, and I remained outside—I then saw Dyke and Savage come out together—I followed them to a spirit shop at the top of the Mint—I took Savage, and called Moy to take Dyke—I found 1 l .; 18 s . 3 d . on Dyke at the station. JOHN MOY( police-constable M  207.) 1 assisted Westmorland in taking Dyke—he asked what authority I could produce for taking him—I told him he could learn the charge at the station—he immediately ran away—I pur sued, and took him—this money was found on him—I found in his pocket-book book these two directions, "Mr. Carter, Croydon, to be left at the Talbot-inn, Borough." BENJAMIN STANBURG. I keep a booking-office in the Talbot-inn yard. I know the prisoner Dyke—he went to me by the name of Carter—I remem ber two chests being at my office on the 17th of July—Dyke called on me, and requested that one might be taken away with him, and the other left—he signed my book in the name of Carter as a receipt for the one chest.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  How long before had Dyke repre sented himself as Carter? A.  He called once before for a chest that was left in the name of Carter.
   WILSON  — GUILTY. Aged 31.
   DYKE  — GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined One Year .
   SAVAGE  — NOT GUILTY.
