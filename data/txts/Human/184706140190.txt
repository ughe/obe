   MR. PARNELL . Q.  Did you see the note? A.  Yes; this is it ( looking at the one produced. ) PHILIP KESTER. I am housekeeper at the Royal Hotel. There are two houses, Nos. 6 and 2—I am housekeeper at No. 2—the prisoner staid there some time, and then went into lodgings—I recollect a fire taking place near her lodging—I went to her shortly afterwards—I look some keys with me, and tried to open a trunk—she had lost her own keys—I cannot recollect when the fire took place—it was some days before the Saturday on which this matter happened—I recollect sending my wife to the prisoner on the Saturday evening with a chain—she had told me four days before, that she would go to Bristol—she did not know what day she should go—she told me on the Wednesday to send the chain, and she said something about it on the Saturday morning between eleven and twelve o'clock.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Where did you take the chain from? A.  Oct of pawn between seven and nine o'clock in the evening—I paid the money from my own pocket, 2 l . 16 s . 3 d .—I did not owe the prisoner any money—I lent her the money because I read her letters she received from Germany, and I took her for a respectable lady—I thought I should get my money, with interest—I did not take a receipt from her—the pawnbroker's is in Fleet-street—I do not know the No.—I took the chain to the hotel, and gave it to my wife to take to the prisoner—I was afraid to take it to her myself—I must not go out of the house without permission, because my wife cannot speak English. CONSTANCE KEYSER. I am proprietor of the Royal Hotel. There are two houses, No. 2, and No. 6; one of them is kept by Philip Kester—I recollect the prisoner coming there in March—she came recommended—I do not admit customers generally without they are recommended—while she was there, she was in the habit of dining at the table d' hote  almost every day, and she continued to do so four or five times a week after she left—she conducted herself very respectably indeed—these articles of jewellery are hers—she was always sit ting beside me, and I recollect them all very well—I recollect Mr. Bloom of Bristol—he is her uncle—she was preparing to leave my house on the 5th of June to go Bristol.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You would have lent her this money? A.  Yes; of course, and a deal more—it was not very strange that she should apply to a waiter—my waiters are in the habit of lending money sometimes, and I am myself—the prisoner's name is Fredericka Asge in my book—I always called her "Madam"—I considered her to be married.
