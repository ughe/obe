 1431. RICHARD FOWLER was indicted for feloniously assaulting James. Davidson , on the   17th of March , putting him, in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 purse, value 3 d  .; 1 crown, 3 half-crowns, 1 shilling, and 7 halfpence; his property. JAMES DAVIDSON. I am a shipwright, and live at Deptford. On Sun day morning, the 17th of March, I was at the Crown and Mason's Arms, Woolwich, having a glass of rum and milk—the prisoner was there—he was a stranger to me—I asked him to drink with me—he said he did not like rum and milk, he would sooner have beer—I called for a pot of beer, and he drank with me—we then went to the Crown and Cushion—I got some steaks, as he said he was hungry, and he partook of them—there was another man with him—we walked up the town, and came back to the house we had first been to, I think and had a pot of beer or half-and-half there—the other man did not go there with us-every thing was had at my expense—I had come over to Woolwich to see a friend-while at the public-house I had occasion to go backwards, and while in the act of looking at my money in the privy, the prisoner came in and snatched my purse out of money in the privy, the prisoner came in and snatched my ling, and three pence in copper—I said, "You are not going to rob me don't take my money"—he laughed at me, put his hand into the purse, took what he chose, returned me the purse with half-a-crown and one shilling, and went away—he had seen me with the money before I left the room—a woman came in, saying the constable was after him about some robbery, and to get out of the way—he turned out of the house-the constable came, and I gave him in charge—I we not at all tipsy—I did not give him the money to take care of—he asked me to lend him a shilling before I went into the yard, and I said I thought he had had quite sufficient without my lending him any money.
