CHARLES Howell. I am a surgeon, and live at Stratford. I saw the de-
ceased about half-past ten o'clock on the 16th of Aug.-- he was quite insen-
sible, and breathing with great difficulty-I bled him, but it did not relieve
him-he died about three quarters of an hour after-his death was caused by
extravasated blood on the brain-I opened the head thirty-six hours after the
death-there was a blow externally on the left side of the head-it seemed a
violent blow, and in my judgment was the cause of his death.
James Dyson. I saw the fight between seven and eight o'clock in the
evening. The prisoner was taking part in it; at least I think it was him;
but there are two brothers, so alike, that I could not swear it was him-the
parties were quite agreeable to fight, and there was nothing unfair in it-
did not last longer than ten or fifteen minutes—I think the injury the de-
ceased received was principally through falls--there were not many very
severe blows he had a very severe fall or two backwards, in the struggle, and
he fell undermost.
