FREDERICK WALLACE. I am a general practitioner and have the licence
of the College of Physicians-on 2nd April, about 5.30, I was called
to the prisoner's house and found the deceased sitting on the threshod
of the passage, supported by two men he was then dead-I had
him lifted into the passage and afterwards into the parlour, and
saw a bullet mark on the left side of his face, just below the
cheek-bone--the man's height was about 5 ft. 9 in., and it was four or
five inches from the top of his head—I did not take his measurement-he
had an ordinary forehead-I afterwards made a post-mortem examination by
order of the Coroner-I opened the head, the bullet had entered exactly
below the cheek-bone, passed through the upper jaw-bone, going straight
backwards; it then entered the skull in front of the upper part of the spinal
cord, I might say the most important part of the spinal cord, com-
pletely smashing it ; it then went transversely along the base of the skull;

skull on the right side, the lowest part of the occipital bone, which it
fractured, and I suppose not having sufficient force to get out through the
