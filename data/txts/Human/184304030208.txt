   Cross-examined. Q.  When did the prisoner come into your service? A.  At the end of 1839—he came on trial first, and then he became my ap prentice—I had a partner named Thomas Hutchinson—we have dissolved partnership—he is alive and well, I believe—we ceased to be partners on the 19th of last April—I have understood that would be an end of the prisoner's apprenticeship.
   Q.  Did the prisoner talk of leaving you after he was free? A.  I believe I first gave him warning about a fortnight prior to his giving me warning to leave, which was in Feb.—I did not induce him to stay by offering to raise his wages—I will not swear I did not—he may have made two journies in a week, and sometimes one, after these customers—the number of distinct per sons he would have to call on would depend on the number we have—he would have to call on forty, fifty, or sixty in a day—he might probably on a Monday have to call on two hundred, to collect these sums of 1 s ., 1 s . 6 d ., or 6 d ., or half-a-crown.
   Q.  Has it not happened sometimes that the prisoner has paid an excess of money from what he has received? A.  Not on these days—it is very pro bable that sometimes he might have paid more than he received—such a thing might happen as that, out of these two or three hundred items—he has omitted to put down the 1 s . or 6 d . in one column, but has put it to another person, and that would account for the excess—the columns in this book represent customers—this book lasts six months—here are different columns, in which the entries ought to be made—such a thing might have happened that the entries have been made the next day, but it was unknown to me—I do not recollect your putting to me at the office an enormous number that had got under the wrong head—I had not time to examine this book every night—it has been left to my clerk—the prisoner might have paid me sixpence on the 26th of May—I will not venture to swear ha did not pay me the sixpence—I generally received the money at night; if not, the first thing in the morning—I have an account of the money I received—I do not enter every night myself what money I receive from each; but if not, I do it in the morn ing—sometimes it is done by the clerk—there were three examinations of this case before Mr. Trail, the Magistrate.
   Q.  Did he tell you his opinion, when you had gone throngh the whole case the last time, that it was a matter of mistake? A.  No sir, I do not re collect his expressing that as his opinion—I do not recollect that he gave me his opinion—he took a great deal of trouble to look over the book with me—I think I gave the prisoner warning about Feb., 1843—he left me about the middle of the month.
   Q.  Did you detain his boxes? A.
