About six o'clock—I did not search Payne's house; Wilde searched it—it was rather an oversight of mine that I did not search the house at six o'clock—I have been about three years in the police—when he offered me 10 s . to compromise a felony, I neither searched his house nor took him into custody—there is a marine-store dealer's next to Payne's; I went in there first—I did not address myself to that store-dealer, I came out again—there is a gateway next to Payne's, it dose not project at all; the front of it is in the same line with the front of his house—I received information, and went to Payne's—the person who gave me the information is not here—he was sum moned before the Magistrate, but was not called.
   MR. ROBINSON. Q.  From the time Salmon left the cart till he came back, could he have gone to his own house? A.  No, it was impossible—he could have gone to Payne's after he had left me—he was moving the lead from the front of the cart to the back—I did not search Payne's house, but he admitted the lead was there—he said Salmon had left lead there, but he had not paid him for it—that was said before the half-sovereign was offered. THOMAS ROGERS. I keep a shop in the archway across Bermondsey-street, On the 31st of Aug. I saw a cart in Bermondsey-street, opposite Payne's door—I saw Salmon take something out of the cart into Payne's marine store-shop—I could not tell what it was, it was rather heavy—he carried it in his arms, open—I was about thirty yards from it—he took it from the hind part of the cart.
   COURT. Q.  Was it open? A.  Yes—I could not tell what it was—it was a light colour—it appeared to be heavy—this is my writing to this deposition—I sthink it might be wrapped up in something—I think it was wrapped up with something, because it was of a light appearance.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  Was the railway between you and the cart? A.  I was right under the railway—from the way in which he carried it, I should say it was heavy—he carried it openly in his arms—I was examined three or four days afterwards—I can recollect every circumstance for a month as well as for a week—it was between five and six o'clock—it was not raining—I live under the arch—I was in front of my own house—Payne lives on the opposite side to me—the cart was between us—I could see him leave the cart and go into the house—I had never seen the cart before that day—I did not see it pass down with some children in it—I keep a general shop.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q.  How long elapsed between the time you saw this and the first examination before the Magistrate? A.  It hap pened on Tuesday, and I think on the Monday following I went to the office—I was up twice—I was not examined till the 14th of Sept.—a fortnight elapsed before I was examined, but I was at the office four days before the police man Adams came to me.
   MR. ROBINSON. Q.  When did you first mention this circumstance? A  The next day—when I heard what had happened, I told the policeman—I saw the there—I saw Salmon sideways as he went in. GEORGE NICHOL( policeman M  128.) I was on duty in Snows'-fields on the 31st of Aug.—I saw Adams talking to Payne—I might be two or three yards from them—I saw Payne put his hand into his pocket, take it out, and hold it towards Adams—I heard Adams say, "No, I will not take it"—I heard Payne say, "I hope you will not say anything about it then."
