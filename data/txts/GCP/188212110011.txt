ELIZABETH PICKLES. I am the female searcher at the Albany Street
Polioe-station-I searched Ward and found a key and pawn-ticket on
her and no money I searched King on the same day, and found three
two-shilling pieces wrapped up in tissue-paper in part of a kid glove-I
gave them to Gregory-when she came in the room she put down a
sixpence and said “This is all I have got "--while she was undressing I
saw her two fingers like that; I said "But you have got something in
your hand, haven't you?”I don': know where she could have put
them, but she put her hands like that, and said "No, I have not"-then
I saw her with her two fingers like that again-I said "You have got
something in your hand," and I went behind her and opened her hand,
and took them from her-she said "Oh, my G_ I'm a done woman;
for G 's sake don't show them, for there is five years stares me in the
face.”
Cross-examined by Mr. FRITH. I have not a note of that statement; I
am not required to make notes of statements--I am sure those were her
words.

WILLIAM WEBSTER. This florin uttered by Ward is bad, and from the
same mould as these other four.
Cross-examined by Mr. FRITH. They are very bad imitations, they
outwardly bear a very good resemblance to persons who know nothing
at all about coins.
Ward in her defence denied changing the two-shilling piece at the linen-
draper's, and said she had the stockings in her hand when arrested, and that sho
had no bad money about her.
WARD and KING-GUILTY. WARD PLEADED GUILTY to a previous
conviction of uttering counterfeit coin on 28th June, 1880.
KING was further charged with having been convicted on 10th January,
1876, in the name of Amelia Aulden, of having counterfeit coin in her
possession.
