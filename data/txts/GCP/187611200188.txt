104. GEORGE PEARSON (38), was indicted for feloniously killing and
slaying Charles Upsdell; he was also charged on the Coroner's Inquisition
with the like offence.
MR. BEslay and MR. HORACE AVORY conducted the Prosecution; and Me.
WILLOUGHBY the Defence.
ALICE SPARSHOTT. I am the wife of Frederick Sparshott, and live at
Hunston, in Sussex, next door to the deceased Charles Upsdell, his garden
adjoining ours--it is separated by a high wall of Mr. Pearson's, and by
quick set hedge of Mr. Upsdell's, you can see through the hedge, I could
not see into Pearson's garden-on Thursday afternoon, 26th October, I was
in my garden and heard Pearson in Upsdell's garden--their gardens adjoined
they run at right angles to mine, Upsdell's garden is on the right and Pearson's
on the left-there is a high wall between then I heard both Pearson's and
Upsdell's voices; I heard Pearson telling Mr. Upsdell *Your cows are in
my garden"-Upsdell used bad language and said "I will do for you, and,
I will see it out Pearson - I heard no more words, I heard blows, I could
not see them, the wall separated us--the blows were in Pearson's garden-
I saw them in Upsdell's garden and go towards Pearson's, and it was after
that I heard the blows; I could not tell how many blows; there were more
than two; they were heavy blows I heard no voioes at the time I heard
the blows--I heard no more expressions after they left Upsdell's garden-
the next thing I saw was Mr. Upsdell return to his own house by the hedge,
the way he went; that was about a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes
after-he appeared to walk very weak, and he was covered with blood
-I did not notice his head, he had no hat or cap- he was not doing anything
with his hands, only walking to his own door.
Cross-examined. I had known both the men for a considerable time
the deceased was a man of very violent temper at times I could not say
whether or not he was sober that day, he was in the habit of getting drunk
at times, and he was then disagreeable and violent-he was a big stont man,
a powerful man, he always walked in a stooping position, with his head &
good deal forward-he was older than the prisoner, the prisoner is somewhat
exciteable at times-some little time ago he was pitched out of a carriage
on, his head; he recovered damages for that accident, he was on one occasion
struck on the side of the head with a hammer by a Mr. Duke I did not see
