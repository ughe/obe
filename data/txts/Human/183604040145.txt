ELLEN FORD was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of March , 7 stockings, value 3 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of James Child ; and Sarah Mil ler, for feloniously receiving 5 of the said stockings, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. ELIZABETH CHILD. I am the wife of James Child, of King's-place, Commercial-road. I know Miller by coming backwards and forwards to my husband's shop, he is a baker—on the 28th of March, I lost some stockings from the drying ground at the back of Mr. Salmon's, where we had apartments till we collide get a house—I saw the stocking I lost, at the Thames Police-office—both get a prisoners were then in custody. MARY SALMON. These stockings were hanging at the back of my pre mises at twelve o'clock, and were missed between twelve and two o'clock—I knew Miller a long time. JOHN MURRAY( police-constable K  173.) On Tuesday, the 22nd, I re ceived information from Mrs. Salmon, that some stockings had been stolen—I made inquiries, and found Ford—she said her name was Shannon, and her father kept a tailor's shop in Cross-street, which I knew to be false—I then said I should take her for stealing some stocking—she said they were at an old woman's at the top of Bluegate-fields, where she had sold them for 4 d .—I asked if she would show me, and she said, "Yes"—I went to the house and saw Mrs. Miller—I asked her if she had bought any stockings of the girl—she said, "Yes, but I do not know where they are"—I asked the girl how many stockings there were—she said she did not know, but she had given her 4 d . for them—Miller then went into a little room, and stopped seven or eight minutes, she then came out, and I said if she did not produce the stockings. I should search the house—she went into the room the third time, and when she came out, she called Wilson, who was present, to give them to me—she gave me five stockings, which I pro duce—I then asked Ford what she had done with the other pair—she said she had pawned them at Mr. Hawes' for 3 d ., and a gown for 4 d .—I asked her again whether she knew any thing about Mrs. Dudley's table-cloth—she said she had pawned that for 1 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  What time of the night was it you went to Mrs. Miller? A.  I think about a quarter-past ten o'clock—I do not know whether she was partially undressed—she had her upper clothes on—the stockings were behind the counter, lying on the floor, and some bagging alongside—Miller went to the station-house, and was allowed to go back to come in the morning, through my saying, "Let her go till the morning, and come down to the office"—these stockings are darned at the toes and heels—Miller's husband works at the Docks, I believe, and is a respectable man, and she is a respectable woman.
