 1791. ANN HEAD was indicted for stealing 1 ring, value 6 s  ., the goods of Moses Marks : 1 watch, value 1 l  .; 1 watch-guard, 12 s  .; 1 key, 4 s  .; and 8 sovereigns; the property of Martha Levy , her mistress, in her dwelling-house. MARY LEVY. I keep a broker's-shop in Prospect-place, St. George's. This ring was in my room; it belongs to my daughter's husband, Moses Marks, and this watch, guard, and eight sovereigns were safe in a cupboard in my bed-room on the evening of Sunday week—I missed them on Monday morn ing, and the prisoner too—she was my servant—I did not see her again till Tuesday morning, when her mother fetched me—I said, "You hussy, what have you done with my things?"—she began crying—there was my watch, guard, ring, and four sovereigns, and 10 d . on the table—she said she had bought a gown, a bonnet, and shawl with the remainder of the money. ALFRED WINGATE( police-constable L  117.) I produce these things.
   Prisoner.  There was not so much money as she says. GUILTY  . Aged 16.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined Fourteen Days .
 1792. WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing 11b. weight of jam, value 1 s  . 3 d  .; 1 jar, 3 d  .; 11oz. of mixed spice, 2 s  .; 1 bottle, 2 d  .; 30lbs. weight of sugar, 19 s  .; 3 1/2 lbs. weight of currants, 1 s  . 6 d  .; loz. weight of mace, 6 d  .; and 1 1/2 oz. weight of cassia, 2 d  .; the goods of Frederick Hills , his master. FREDERICK HILLS, confectioner, Kennington.  The prisoner was my porter—I missed these things—I went with the officer to the prisoner's house, and found them—the prisoner had no business with them.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST , Q.  Did you ask him to show you his lodging? A.  We did—we spoke to him first in the bakehouse, and he said he would show us his lodging—I can swear to the jam; I made it—the prisoner said it was mine on the evening we took him, and he said the other articles were not mine—he did not say why he had taken the jam—I found it on his shelf in the room his mother occupied—I found all these articles in that room—the jam is not very stale—he did not deal with me be fore he came to live with me—I said before the Magistrate that he said the jam was mine—what I said was taken down in writing—I do not find here that he said the jam was mine—( looking at his depositions )—it is an oversight—he did not say the pot was mine—I took up the jam-pot and said, "This is mine"—at that time he said it was mine—he did not say so as we were going to the station—he did not say that the pot was mine and the jam was his, of his own making—I am not aware that he had been in the habit of selling buns—he had been in my employ before. HENRY LUPTON( police-constable L  7.)
