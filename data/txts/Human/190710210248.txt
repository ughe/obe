To Mr. Orr. I cautioned Harris after he had pretended to make a search at the office for the books. I went to 2, Tokenhouse Build  ings and said to Harris. "Tyler is in custody; he has been arrested upon a charge of obtaining money by false pretences in connection with the business at Pinner's Hall. I understand that you. have some books belonging to him here." I then produced Tyler's letter. Harris said, "I do not think that there are any here." He then went into the private office, came out almost immediately, and looked in a cup board, and said, "There does not appear to be any here." I said, 'I am not satisfied with the way in which you have searched, you have books here, and I want them before I leave." He them went to the cupboard and produced letter book of Tyler and Co. I said, "How is it that you have got Tyler and Co.'s letter book here." He said, "Tyler brought it up to ask a question on a point of law" I said, "Who is the tenant of these offices?" Harris said, "I do not know, but I think George Martin." He brought four press copy letter books of the "Financial Guardian "out of the cupboard. I knew him as Harris. I then returned to the police office with Tyler's letter book and told Tyler that this was the only book I had obtained from Harris and that I did not recognise the writing a a his or his lady clerk's. I asked him whose writing it was. He said, "I do not know." I said, "It is strange you do not know whose writing this is if this is your business."
 To Mr. F. E. Smith. I now recollect that I cautioned Tyler be  fore I went to 2, Tokenhouse Buildings.
 To Mr. Bodkin. On May 28 Tyler was brought up before the Alder  man, the hearing was adjourned, and by June 11 I received warrant for the arrest of Harris. I found a number of documents at Tyler's house, including those from Smiles. On June 11 I arrested Harris in Lothbury.
 To Mr. Orr. I cautioned Harris at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings, when I made inquiries as to the books of Tyler and Co. I did so because I was satisfied he was not speaking the truth.
 To Mr. Bodkin. I read the warrant to him. He said, "You have made a great mistake." Later I told him he had been identified as the person who cashed a cheque for £350 on April 24 on the London Joint Stock Bank, Pall Mall, which had been sent to Tyler and Co. He said, "Tyler asked me to cash the cheque, and I afterwards paid him the money." I then went to 2, Tokenhouse Buildings and found a number of books, papers relating to William Tyler and Co., Herbert Smith, Chalmers and Cox, and the "Financial Guardian," and to another bucket shop, "A. M. Noble." On the door was painted "Rand Roodeport Mining Company" and "Gold Coast Property Owners, Limited," and on a piece of paper stuck up at the side of the door was written, "The Financial Guardian and Mining Market Mirror." On the same landing was a room with "C. W. P." on the door, of which the key was in the other office. I found it empty except for a rolled top desk which belonged to the landlord; the place was covered with dust and apparently unused; there were no books.
