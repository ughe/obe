 1813.  JOHN OWEN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 2 shovels, value 5s., the goods of the   trustees of St. Leonard's, Shoreditch.
   ROBERT LANDER. I am a Police-man. On the 27th of August, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, I was in Kingsland-road, and saw the prisoner standing by the gates of the parish stone-yard, in Fleming-street- he turned round, and went towards the wall next the gates, where two shovels were handed over the wall to him - he went about forty yards with them under his arm; I stopped him; he said he had picked them up by the side of the gate, and would take them back.
   JOHN WHITTY. I hold a situation under the trustees of the poor. These shovels are their property; I left them locked up in the yard on the Saturday night.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had occasion to stop for a moment, and on turning my head I saw the shovels lay there; I carried them about forty yards, and then thought I would have no more to do with them - as I turned back, the Police-man came up.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1814.  JOHN OSBORNE was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of August , 1 pair of boots, value 2s., the goods of Samuel Lea.
   EDWARD MARRYATT. I am a servant to Samuel Lea, a shoemaker, in Peter-street, Saffron-hill. On the 22nd of August I heard somebody in the shop, turned round, and saw the prisoner just turning the door-post, going out: I missed these boots, which I had seen safe on the counter a quarter of an hour before; I went after him - he dropped them at my feet, and I secured him.
   JOHN CLARK. I am a constable. I received the prisoner in charge - he said nothing.(Property produced and sworn to)
 GUILTY. Aged 14. -    Judgment Respited.
 1815.  DENNIS MCDONALD and JOHN DONOGHUE were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 4 coach-springs, value 40s., the goods of John Robson and others.
 WILLIAM FACHE. I am in the employ of  John Robson and three others, who are assignees to the estate of Robert George, Parker-street, Drury-lane- I was in care of the whole property. On the 15th of July I saw these coach-springs in the loft, and missed them next day - the prisoners were strangers; on the morning of the 28th of July I saw Donoghue leaning on a post, opposite the gate- I told him to move, to make way for a carriage which was coming in; he was taken that afternoon - four coachsprings were stolen.
   WILLIAM LASSITER. I am porter to Mr. Leslie, an auctioneer, of Charles-street, Middlesex-hospital. Master and I took sixteen springs to the yard, on account of the assignees, to be entered on the catalogue, and on the 16th four were missing.
   JAMES ELLIS. I am a coach-spring maker, and made these springs on Mr. George's premises - I saw them there about three weeks before they were lost, and on the 29th I saw them in the officer's possession; I know McDonald by sight - he is a stranger to the premises.
 ROBERT HUSON. I am a Policeman. On the 28th of July, about ten o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoners together at Mr. Jones', a pawnbroker, High Holborn- they were both leaning over the counter on which the springs lay, offering them in pawn; they came out together; I took McDonold, and in about an hour and a half, in consequence of information, I charged him with stealing the springs - he said he would fetch the master who gave them to him in half an hour; I trusted him to go, but he never brought any body to me to own them -I found him in the shop of Mr. Washbrook, a springmaker, for whom he worked; I took Donoghue that evening - he said he had nothing to do with the springs, he merely carried them there for a man: I brought the springs from the pawnbroker's, and have had them ever since.
 McDonald. I came to the station of my own accord. Witness. I told his master if he came in to detain him, but I met his master bringing him to the station.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 McDonald's Defence. Seven weeks last Thursday, as I was going to breakfast, I met this man and another opposite St. Giles' church; being in the habit of pawning for my master, to get the work on, this man asked me to show him where I pawned, and as it was in my way home, I did so.
 Donoghue's Defence. On Sunday evening I stood at the corner of Drury-lane; a man came up in his shirtsleeves and a hairy cap; he asked me to carry a parcel, and if I had a basket - I said No; I borrowed one - the man took me into a stable in Parker-street; he told a man to put the things into my basket, and told me to meet him at eight o'clock on Monday morning; I went, and he brought out the springs and asked me to pawn them; this man being in the habit of pawning, I asked him where to go.
 DONAGHUE -    GUILTY. Aged 39. Confined Six Months.
 McDONALD -    NOT GUILTY.
