Detective ALEXANDER MACINNES, F Division. On June 25, 1909. I
saw prisoner at the Moor Lane Police Station, and read the warrant
to her charging her on Mrs. Sargent's case with obtaining credit for
£1 10s. She said, "It cannot be £1 10s. I was only there four
days." Iconveyed her to Notting Hill Police Station. On the
way she said, "I wrote her a nice letter when I left her: I did not
mean to defraud her." When charged, she said, "It cannot be
£1 10s."
A long written statement of the prisoner was read stating that
she had been suffering from & weak heart, that she did not intend
to defraud, and that she would make arrangements to pay the
indebtedness, that ber father was & colonel in the army, and that
after his death her health had broken down in endeavouring to sup
port herself.
Verdict, Guilty.
Dr. FULLERTON, deputy medical officer. Holloway Prison, stated
that the prisoner was suffering from anæmia, dropsy of the legs,
and varicose veins, and that she was eccentric but not insane.
Convictions proved : Marylebone, September 19, 1900, six weeks
hard labour for stealing & diamond ring; March 31, 1904, North
London Sessions, three months' for obtaining food and lodgings by
fraud; in 1908 she was charged with unlawful possession of
lady's watch. There were seven additional similar charges to the
present.
Sentence, 12 months' imprisonment.

STANTON, Robert; attempting to commit suicide.
• Mr. Waroburton prosecuted.
Police-constable JOHN SHEPHERD, 706 City. On July 8, at 1.25
p.m., I was on duty at Fresh Wharf, when I saw prisoner deliberately
jump off the parapet of London Bridge, falling about 30 ft. into
deep water. I ran to Old Swan Pier, when I found that James
Windsor (who is not here) had clutched the prisoner as he floated
by. Windsor said in the hearing of the prisoner that he struggled
a lot and seemed to try to get out of his clutch as he was getting him
on to the pier. Prisoner said his head was bad he did not know
what he was doing. He was taken to Guy's Hospital, where he to
mained until July 12, when I arrested him on a warrant. He said,
"I jumped off the bridge because my head was bad."
Prisoner stated that he did not intend to take his life; he jumped
off because he could not control himself his head was very bad
the time; he was very much better now and he would promise never
to do such a thing again.

officer, Brixton Prison, was sworn. I have had prisoner and
special observation since July 12. There is a history of insanity,
his family. Prisoner is a chair maker and is subject to fits of
