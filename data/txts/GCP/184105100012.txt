WILLIAM POWERACRE. I travel in the tea line at present--some time
since I was in Whitecross-street for more than two years I had a situa-
tion there the greater part of the time as crier and messenger-I remember
Antrobus being there I had some conversation with him--he said if
Norris would satisfy him he would go out of the way, and the prosecutor
should not find him-he said he would certainly prosecute and transport him
if he could, that he had suffered the punishment himself, and if he would
pay him he would be off out of the country, and the prosecutor should
not find him-he afterwards said that the prosecutor had been there, and
offered to pay his debt and liberate bim from prison if he would give cer-
tain evidence, for he was determined to get Norris transported if he could
-I saw Antrobus a few days before he left-he said he had made ar-
rangements with the prosecutor, and it was too late now for Norris to say
any thing-I cannot say that he told me he had given his evidence on his
return from the Insolvent Court.
