OLD COURT.--Tuesday, January 29th, 1895.
Before Mr. Recorder
191. EMILY ROSS (19), PLEADED GUILTY to unlawfully obtaining
by false pretences from Harrods Stores, goods to the amount of
£10 38.7d. - Judgment respited. And 192. JAMES GEORGE
MARYON (40), to forging and uttering the endorsement on a cheque for
the payment of £2 58.; also to obtaining by false pretences from John
Cropper Denew £2 5s., with intent to defraud. --Eight Months' Hard
Labour.
193. GEORGE TOWNSEND, Unlawfully forging and uttering two
writings purporting to be tickets issued by the British Tea Table
Company for food obtained from them.
MR. A. GILL Prosecuted.
FLORENCE PORTER. I am an attendant in the employment of the
British Tea Table Company-on or about December 28th the prisoner
came into our Aldersgate Street depot-I had seen him before—I served
him with things of the value of 11 d., and gave him this ticket on which
I wrote 11 d.-after that a customer sitting at the same table as the
prisoner was at spoke to me, and I, in consequence, spoke to Miss Carter,
the cashier-she took this ticket off the file and showed it to me; the
prisoner had then just only got outside-and saw that 14d. was written
on the ticket- I looked out for the prisoner after that I next saw him
on the day he was given into custody-t recognised him when he came
in, and I spoke to Mrs. Barrett, the manageress—the lfd.on this ticket is
not part of what I wrote: it is re-written.
EMILY HESTER. I am an attendant in the employ of this company-on
January 12th I was serving at the Aldersgate Street depot-I served the
prisoner with refreshments of the value of 7 d., and gave him this ticket
with 5 d. and 2d, on it-this “G” is my signature; we all have a letter
-the "G" on this is as I wrote it after the prisoner was charged I
was shown this ticket and I found it had been altered—I had been told
something previously which made me careful to remember what ticket I
gave him-I knew the man's appearance, and was on the look out-I had
good reason for remembering the exact amount I wrote on the ticket.
FLORENCE PORTER (Re-examined). The “B” on this ticket is my
signature.
ROBERT WINNOP. I am a counter hand in the employ of this company
--on 12th January Mrs. Barrett spoke to me-I saw the prisoner sitting
at the table--I stood outside the shop in case the prisoner came out, and
I looked through the window into the shop, and saw the prisoner sitting
at the table with a ticket in front of him in the attitude of either writing
or rubbing out.

company-I know the prisoner by sight-shortly after Christmas he

spoke to me, and I then took this ticket off the file, and I have carefully
kept it ever since-on 12th January the prisoner came in again, and I
