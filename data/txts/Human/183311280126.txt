 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 180.  JAMES CHANDLER was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November , 4 shifts, value 10s.; the goods of Thomas Smith.
   MARIA SMITH. I am the wife of Thomas Smith, we live at Parson's-green, I take in washing. On the 27th of November, I hung out some linen to dry at four o'clock; I missed four shifts from the drying-ground at the back of my house about six o'clock; the prisoner is a gardener, and lives next door; these are my property.
   GEORGE FENNELL. I live about two hundred yards from the prosecutor; I was at a house where they sell greens, and the prisoner came in and brought these shifts; he said, he had been into Mrs. Smith's yard, and taken them off a line; he gave me two of them to pawn; I took them to Mr. Salter, but they would not take them in.
   EDWARD SOMERS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Paradise-row Chelsea. The last witness brought me two shifts, which were rough dried, and I would not take them in; as soon as he was gone Joseph Chandler brought these other two shifts which had been ironed, and I took them of him - I did not know they belonged to the same person.
   JOHN HOWLET(police-sergeant V 20). I know nothing of the robbery, but I found these shifts at the pawnbroker.
   JOSEPH CHANDLER. I am brother of the prisoner. I was sitting with George Fennell in Mrs. Lugden's room, when my brother came in with a bundle of four shifts; he showed them to me, but did not say where he got them; the next day he sent me to pawn two of them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming home about seven o'clock at night, and picked these four shifts up in the Kingsland-road; I took them to Mrs. Lugdue's, and said,"Perhaps Mrs. Smith has lost them;" I kept them till the next night, but there was no inquiry made for them, and I sent my brother to pawn two of them.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Six Months.
