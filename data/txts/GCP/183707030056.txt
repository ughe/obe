Prisoner's Defence. Mr. Brandon being of the Jewish persuasion, does
no business on Saturday-either he or Abraham Peres put shoes into my
bag on Friday, and having taken these out, I had a friend who was to have
half a dozen pairs, and I knew I should not get the money of him, because

a dozen pairs at once, but I kept one pair at one time and one pair at
another, and accounted for them; my friend came to town, and left without
my seeing him I took these to the witness, and she had the one pair
the prosecutor acknowledges he let me have, and the other two pairs I let
her have at 18. a pair-I took them out of the bag, which I accounted for.
ABRAHAM BRANDON. He never had authority to sell the samples at all.
Prisoner. Very often a person will say, "I will take this pair, and
bring me some more equal to them," and they have kept them I had no
doubt but Mr. Brandon would have acknowledged that he had put them
into my bag-I have kept them one pair at a time, and given the money
-I was twelve years with Mr. Hook, in Bond-street, and have lived
in several respectable situations.
JURY to MR. BRANDON. Q. Did you never authorise the prisoner to
sell shoes ? A. No; only morning slippers.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY.-Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor.
Confined One Year.

1691. JOSEPH COPCUT and WILLIAM THROWDEN were in-
dicted for stealing, on the 17th of June, 30lbs. weight of lead, value 58.,
the goods of William Brumley; and fixed to a certain building; against
the Statute, &c.
Peter HAWKINS. I am servant to Mr. William Brumley, a solicitor,
living at No. 8, Avenue-road. He had five unfinished houses there-I miss-
ed a great deal of lead-on Saturday evening, the 17th, I met the prisoner
Throwden with lead under his arm, coming out of one of these houses-
I went and asked him how he came there-he said he got over the wall
I said, “What have you got under your arm?"-he said, "Nothing"-I
found it was lead I said, “ Have you got any body with you?"-he said
he had a partner in the house-- I kept him in tow till I had got a police-
man to take him, I then went into the house, and found the other
prisoner quite at the back of the house. out of the building altogether-he
had nothing with him-I have fitted the lead that I found on Throwden
to the house-it corresponds, nails and all-after I took Copcut, he took
the policeman to where he had taken his lead to.
HENRY COONEY (police-constable S 112.) I took charge of Throwden,
and have fitted this lead-it fits the left-hand corner of the house - I heard
Copcut say he had been three times on the roof, and had lead from there.
Copcut. Q. Did I tell you that I had been there before? A. Yes;
to get the lead.
