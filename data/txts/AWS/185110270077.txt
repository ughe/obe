JOHN OveR. I am a earpenter and builder, of 3, Eastling-place, Harrow-
road. On Saturday evening, 18th Sept., I was at the Ben Jonson, and saw
the prisoner there in the parlour-| went there shortly after 9 o'elock, and
found him there-he was endeayouring to kick up a disturbance with twe or
three people-two of them were brothers, named Robinson-be appeared very
quarrelsome, and challenged one of them to fight--in about three-quarters of
an hour the landlord came in and by dint of perseverance of the company
to make the prisoner quiet, he was quiet-the persons removed to the other
side of the room out of his way, the two Robinsons, and two gentlemen
named Holmes und Rogers-after a short time the prisoner followed them-
they would not make room on the seat; and he stood in front of the seat, and
threatened and challenged them to fightthe landlord requested the prisoner
to go out, took his glass from his hand, and took it to the bar expeeting him
to follow it, but he did not; and the landlord came in again, put his band
gently on his shoulder, and put bim out of the room--the landlord was absent
about a minute--on his return he was moving the table to get past a gentle-
man, when the prisoner rushed in with the glass in his hand, and struck the
landlord with it on the forehead with all his might, and cut his forehead very
much, and bis own band in three or four places--the glass was smashed all
to pieces, except the bottom part-it was a large grog-glass-the landlord fell
on the table, and was assisted by the companyiI secured the prisoner till a
policeman came-he said he did not intend to do it, and wished me to let him
go-the blow appeared to me to be done on purpose, deeidedly-he said be
did not intend to do anything, and if he had done anything he was sorry.
Cross-eramined. Q. You say the Messrs. Robinson were the persons he
was chiefly annoying A. He was endeavouring to quarrel with them prin-
cipally-they were not before the Magistrate--I believe one of them is here
-there was a person named Parnell there- did not hear the prisoner express
any anxiety to get into his service-he is a master builder, and employs men
-the prisoner is a journeyman plasterer- heard no language used to the
prisoner of an insulting or annoying kind-1 heard him say be went there that
evening to receive money for a job-the Robinsons said nothing about the
way he did bis work, or that be did not know how to plaster, and laid it on
with an American shovel-the prisoner had a glass of gin and water when E
went in, and I think he had it filled once after-I was drinking porters-the
blow was struck with the glass in the left-band--I saw the pieces on the
ground.
