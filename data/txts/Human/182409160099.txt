RICHARD TAYLOR, TIMOTHY TAYLOR, and MARY CLIFFORD, were indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of James Perry , about twelve o'clock in the night of the 6th of August , at St. James, Clerkenwell , with intent to steal and burglariously stealing therein, fourteen sheets, value 40 s.; four shifts, value 10 s.; a pair of sheets, value 12 s.; four table-cloths, value 20 s.; fifteen caps, value 15 s.; a frill, value 6 d.; seven handkerchiefs, value 12 s.; a cushion cover, value 1 s.; nine pinafores, value 5 s.; three pillowcases, value 3 s.; five petticoats, value 4 s.; seven gowns, value 20 s.; a habit-shirt, value 1 s.; four aprons, value 2 s.; three pairs of drawers, value 1 s., and a pair of stays, value 1 s., his property.
   ELIZABETH PERRY. I am the wife of James Perry; we live in Union-place, Bagnigge Wells-road, in the parish of St. James's, Clerkenwell, and rent the house - nobody else lives in it. On the 6th of August I got up about ten minutes before six o'clock in the morning, and went into the wash-house, which is in the yard, and is not more than a yard from the house; the yard belongs exclusively to us, I was in the wash-house the night before at eleven, and left it locked; the window was put down - I left a basket of clothes there, ready to iron: I found the window open, and the towels which I had left on the top of the basket were thrown all over the wash-house, and the basket emptied, except a very few articles. I looked about, but found none of them - the house door was locked; I missed the articles stated in the indictment - (enumerating them) - they were all in the basket the night before; they were my husband's property, and worth 8 l. or 10 l. at least.
   WILLIAM COLTON. I am constable of Islington. On the 6th of August I was applied to, and went with the watchman in search of the prisoners. I found Richard and Timothy Taylor on Saffron-hill about six o'clock in the evening, and took them both to Tunbridge-street watch-house, New-road; and at the corner of Tunbridge-street I observed Timothy Taylor throw something from his pocket into a garden. I took him to the watch-house, locked him up, and brought Richard back with me to this garden, and then told him to go on, and stop at my house till I came there, which he did; he went alone, and waited till I came. I desired the watchman to go and pick something out of the garden, which I pointed out, and it proved to be six duplicates, five of which were for property pawned on the 6th of August; I thought one of them related to the property lost, and went over to Button's the pawnbroker's, to enquire about it. Richard Taylor was with me; the pawnbroker said that Richard Taylor had pawned two whittles that morning - I asked him whose they were; he said they belonged to his landlady - I said I must go and enquire, and took him with me; and as we went along, he said he would tell me something - that he had bought some property at five o'clock that morning of a man (who stands indicted, but is not in custody - he has never been home since.) I found at Taylor's lodging thirty different articles of wearing apparel, which I now produce: I saw the watchman pick up the duplicates.
 Prisoner  RICHARD TAYLOR. Q. Have I not been in employ up to the time I was taken - A. To the best of my knowledge he has: he works close to me.
   SAMUEL GAIGER. I am a watchman, and know Richard and Timothy Taylor. On the 6th of August, about half-past four o'clock in the morning, I saw Timothy Taylor standing near Battle-bridge turnpike: nothing passed between us except saying that it was a fine morning; it was day-light. I am in the habit of seeing him about that time; I was with Colton  that evening, and apprehended Richard and Timothy Taylor; and at the corner of Tunbridge-street, Richard Taylor, who was with me, crossed over; Colton was on the other side with Timothy Taylor, who was put into the watch-house, and Richard ordered to go to Colton's house. As we returned, Colton sent me into the garden to pick up a paper which he pointed out; I found it was duplicates, and gave them to him: we then went to Button's the pawnbroker, of Battle-bridge, with Richard, and then to Richard's lodging; he said something to Colton about buying something, and in his room the bundle of property was found.
   EDWARD BULLSWORTHY. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Aylesbury-street, Clerkenwell. On the 6th of August, the prisoner Clifford came to my shop, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, and pawned two table-cloths, and two shifts, for 5 s. - she wanted 9 s. - they are worth 7 s. I asked if they were her own - she said they were. I have had them ever since.
   JOHN BUTTON. I am apprentice to Daniel Button, a pawnbroker, of Albion-place, Battle-bridge. On the 6th of August, about two or three o'clock in the afternoon Timothy Taylor pawned a silk handkerchief for 1 s. 6 d. it is worth 3 s.
   GEORGE HARVEY. I am servant to Mr. Cross, pawnbroker, Turnmill-street, Clerkenwell. On Friday afternoon, the 6th of August, between three and six o'clock, Clifford came to my shop, and pawned two shirts for 4 s., and a shawl and shift for 3 s. 6 d. I asked if they were washing things; she said No, they were her own - she came twice within those house; the value of the whole is about 10 s. 6 d.
   ALEXANDER MILLS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Greek-street, Soho. On the 6th August two gowns were pawned, which are worth 6 s. - it was by neither of prisoners.
   WILLIAM JORDAN. I am constable of Clerkenwell. On the 13th of August, at ten o'clock at night, I apprehended Clifford at Bagnigge Wells, and asked if she knew any thing of the robbery right opposite - she denied it. I took her to the watch-house, but found nothing on her, and next morning, as I was taking her before the Magistrate, I neither threatened or promised her; she confessed that she had pawned a number of articles which the Taylors had given her, at Cross and Bullworthy's, and declared that she did not know how they came by them - she did not say what they were.
