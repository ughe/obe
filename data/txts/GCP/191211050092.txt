CHARLES BAXTER.On October 14 I was on board the "Viscount"
following a sculling match. I was standing just behind Charles and
Henry Gobbett: I know Charles. Prisoner was standing on a seat just
behind me, shouting the odds. I did not see Hy. Gobbott make a bet.
Just as the steamer was rounding at the finish of the race I beard
somebody shout, "Hullo, he is over." I looked round and saw some
body leaving the launch and getting into a skiff ; I am sure it was pri-
soner. There was a hue and cry for the police. About 20 men
threatened to throw the Gobbetts overboard if they called for the
palica. Prisoner had a badge with Williams, of Lambeth," upon it;
he was wearing brown boots and blue melton overcoat. I pointed
him out to Detective Miller at 11.30 on Ootober 25 at Waterloo Station
as the man I had seen leaving the launch; he was in a train going to
Sandown Park Races.
Cross-examined. I did not attend at the police court on October 15
or 23. I knew from the first that Gobbett had been "welshed, but
I did not hear till the 24th that anybody had been charged with it.
Prisoner was a stranger to me on the 14th and I did not see him again
till the 25th. It would be wrong to say that he was wearing a light
mackintosh on the 14th.
Police-constable PLINXER, who was recalled at Mr. St. John
Hutchinson's request, stated that prisoner, when he arrested him, was
wearing a buff mackintosh and brown boots.

CHARLES HENRY SEABROOK, bookmaker's assistant, in the employ of
Mr. Clinch, of Clinch and Wilson, 28, Hogarth Buildings, Vauxhall
Bridge Road. I am a man of excellent character. On October 14 1
went with a man named Hayes to Putney, arriving there at 1.30. A
Mr. Winter asked us to go for a row to see the race. The three of us
rowed in a skiff to the "Bell," Hammersmith Bridge, where we had a
drink. We then rowed to Barnes; this was about 2.20. I saw In-
spector Bullivant, whom I know by sight, on a police launch, with
three more constables, and I saluted him. We went as far as Morf-
