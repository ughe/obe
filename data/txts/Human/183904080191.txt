I was sent for—when I got to the shop, the prosecutor was holding the prisoner by the arm—he said he had taken a piece of cloth—Mr. Daniels went behind the counter and picked up the cloth—I searched the prisoner at the station-house, and found no money on him, but a pocket-book, and a flash note.

   Prisoner's Defence.  I had a little portion of a tunic coat in the bundle, and I wanted to make a pair of towsers to match it for my own child, and to match some waistcoats—he said he had not got it—my little coat was then lying on the pieces of cloth. I took the coat and tied it up in my bundle—he was looking at me all the time—it is not likely I should take any thing else—I had not stepped beyond the threshold of the door—he took me and told a young man to fetch a policeman—I said, "For what?"—he said. "If you don't know what is your own, I will tell you"—I said, "I have nothing but what I paid for"—I did not say, "I will pay you"—he se lected this from a quantity of pieces, and I said, "If you are so inclined, why not take the first piece that comes to hand?"—that note was put into my hand one day in the street.

 1396. WILLIAM HODGES was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of March , 1 saddle, value 5 s  ., the goods of Moses Jacobs , his master.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. STEPHEN SLAUGHTER. I am in the employ of Moses Jacobs, a glass-merchant, in Berwick-street, Oxford-street. The prisoner was occasionally employed as a labourer—my master rents some premises in Rupert-street, where the horse and cart are usually put up—on the 16th of March, the prisoner was employed to move the cart and some harness from one stable to another, in Rupert-street, Haymarket—I saw a gig saddle and other things safe in the stable, between six and seven o'clock, when I went to do up my horse—I went there again the next morning, and they were gone—on the 22nd I came through Smithfield, and saw a lad with my master's saddle for sale—I knew it, and gave the lad into custody—this is the saddle—I never authorised any body to take it.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  What name is over Mr. Jacobs' door? A.  Jacobs only—his son's name David Jacobs, it is on the cart, but it is his father's business. DANIEL SUTHERWOOD. I am a costermonger. I bought this saddle of the prisoner on Saturday night, three or four weeks back, for 1 s . 6 d .—I sent it to Smithfield on the Friday following, to be sold by a boy—it would not fit my donkey—it has been a pony-saddle—he said he had a collar and harness to sell.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is the boy here? A.  No—I know it is the saddle I bought—I gave it to a boy named Johnny  —he works for me.
