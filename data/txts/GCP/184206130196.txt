1928. ELIZA ANN CRAWLEY was indicted for stealing, on
30th of May, 2 sheets, value 48., and 1 pillow, value 38., the goods
Benjamin Levy.
Mary Levy. I am the wife of Benjamin Levy; we live in Albica
place, Clerkenwell. The prisoner took a furnished apartment of mine
there was a man with her whom she represented as her husband, but he
afterwards said he was not--that man has been acquitted-as well as
remember, the prisoner was at my house on the night of the 29th of Mar
but she did not sleep there every night-when I let my room to the
these articles were in it-the prisoner left on the 5th of June, and I missed
a pair of sheets that day-I had not seen them from the time I let the room
which was on the 30th of April, I did not see the prisoner again tillate
was in custody, but she had called at my house in my absence.
Prisoner. You gave me the pillow about a fortnight after I was in the
room. Witness. I believe it was in the room when you took it.
MICHAEL PARKER MALONEY. I am in the service of Mr. Rawlins, 1
pawnbroker. I produce these two sheets, which were pawned by the pri
soner, I believe, in the name of Ann Smith, on the 30th of May-I gave
her this duplicate- looking at one).
BENJAMIN MECHAM. I am in the service of a pawnbroker. I have 1
pillow, pawned on the 30th of May, I believe by the prisoner, in the name
of Ann Smith-I gave her this duplicate for it.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
ANDREW TWEEDALE (police-constable C 222.) The prisoner was
given to me by the prosecutor-she said something about being forgiven
-I took four duplicates from the prisoner-these two belong to these
things.
Prisoner's Defence. I am ignorant of the things she charges me with.
GUILTY. Aged 34.- Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Months.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)

1929. JANE ELLIS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June, 1
half-sovereign, value 10s., the money of John Brady, from his person.
John BRADY. I live in George-yard, Lombard-street, and am a boot-
maker. I was in the City-road about half-past eleven o'clock at night,
on the 6th of June, walking home--I had been supping with a friend
the prisoner came up and laid hold of my arm, and asked how I did-she
said she was very drunk, and would I see her home-I said, "No," and
persuaded her to go home-she still asked me to go with her-I said I
had no money, and could not-she then put her arm round my waist, and
in fact all over me, and appeared in a druken state-I then found I had
lost a half-sovereign out of my waistcoat pocket-she had not hold of
me then-I had had my fingers in my waistcoat pocket three minutes
before, and felt it safe-I charged her with robbing me-she said, "What?
let us go and have something to drink"-l still refused, and said, "Here
is a policeman coming towards us, if you will not give me back the hall-
sovereign I will give you in charge "-I was quite sober, and the prisoner
appeared more sober then I gave her in charge I do not know what
became of my half-sovereign.
