   Cross-examined by MR. WOOLLETT . Q.  Where had you been? A.  To see a friend part of the way home, it being late—it was a female friend who had been spending the evening at my house—she resided somewhere in the Waterloo-road, I do not know where—I had left her and was returning home—I called the female who was walking with me Mrs. De Coster—I did not say she was my wife—I will not swear 1 did not—that was not the same lady I had been to see home, but another—the prisoner was sober—I could not see a constable all the way he followed me, or I should have given him in charge—I did not meet any person—I did not strike him—I laid hold of him when he laid hold of me, or I should have fallen—the female cried out for police—after he got to the station he threatened to do for me after he got his liberty. EDWARD FISHER( policeman, M  272). In consequence of hearing a cry I went into Great Union-street, and found the prisoner and prosecutor there—the prosecutor gave him in charge for assaulting him, and demanding money of him—I took him to the station—he said to the prosecutor, "I know where you live, and I will mark you when I come out."
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Nine Months .

 1210. WILLIAM GROVES , burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of James Linfield , with intent to steal.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. ELIZABETH SARAH LINFIELD. I am the wife of James Linfield, a grocer and broom-dealer, at 54, Tiverton-street, Newington. On Wednesday morning, 22nd May, I was awoke, about a quarter or twenty minutes past three o'clock, by the noise of the shop shutters breaking—I looked out of the bedroom window and saw a hat go in at the shop window—I got a light, and in about five minutes went down-stairs into the shop—I found the window and shutters open, which I had left safe about eleven over night, and saw the prisoner lying down behind the counter—I said, "Man, what do you do here?"—he made no answer, and never moved—he was very tipsy indeed—I called my husband, who got a policeman, and gave him into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. BIRNIE . Q.  Did you miss anything? A.  No; I had 30 s . in the till—not a thing was disturbed—I put my foot against him when I asked what he did there—he was led to the station by my husband and a policeman—he seemed to have no legs to walk upon—I had never seen him before—the shutters were very old and rotten, the least touch might have broken them. THOMAS GRANVILLE( policeman, M  251). I was called, and received the prisoner in charge—he was very tipsy—I could make nothing of him—he was not able to walk very well.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) NOT GUILTY.
