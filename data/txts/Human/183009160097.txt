 1674.  ELIZABETH COLLINS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 1 watch, value 4l.; 1 seal, value 2l.; 1 key, value 6d.; 1 purse, value 2s.; 1 sovereign and 14 shillings, the property of James Watson Bell , from his person.
 MR. CHURCHILL conducted the prosecution.
 JAMES WATSON BELL. I am  captain of a vessel. I had been spending the evening out, on the 14th of July, with some friends near town, and coming to my lodging, about twelve o'clock, I met the prisoner who asked for something to drink - I refused some time, but at last I said, "If you can find a house open I will give you some" - we went to a house; she had three glasses of spirits, and I had some porter - we came out, and I went on to Wellington-square; I was very unwell, and missed my watch - I went to the house we had been at: the landlady got a Policeman; I have no doubt the prisoner is the person, but I could not swear to her - this is my watch; I am certain I had it in the public-house.
   CHARLES WILLIAMSON. I am a pawnbroker. I received this watch from the prisoner - the prosecutor came two days afterwards; when the prisoner brought it she said it was her own, and she said so at the office till the prosecutor came, and then she said the prosecutor gave it to her to make money of; she said it had been her son's for seven or eight years, and she had a youth with her whom she called her son - she came to my shop two days afterwards, and was detained.
   GEORGE PHILLIPS. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner - she said it was her husband's watch before she knew him, and she had known him fourteen or fifteen years - she said her son was going to sea, and as he had no money, she pawned the watch for 10s. and gave him 5s. - she said at the office that Bell gave it her.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she had known the prosecutor for four years, and that he had given her the watch to pledge.
 MR. BELL. I never saw her before, on my oath.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1675.  SARAH CRELTINGTON was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of August , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s. 6d., the goods of John Thomas.
   ANN THOMAS. I am the wife of John Thomas. On the 23rd of August I was in our parlour, and saw the prisoner come in, take these shoes from the shop-window, and put them on her feet - I came into the shop to serve her, but she was going out, without paying for them or asking the price; I said, "I think they are about your size;" she did not say a word, but was going out - I would not let her go; she insisted upon my not stopping her - she then said she had made a mistake and came out without money; she insisted upon my not taking them off.
   EDWARD MARKS. I took the prisoner, and the prosecutrix, with the assistance of her apprentice, and took the shoes off her feet; I asked if she had paid for them; she said, "Yes, 2s." I said I thought she had made a mistake - she looked into her pocket, and had not one farthing.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1676.  MARY CAIN was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , 1 gown, value 3s., the goods of John Wells.
   GEORGE MACKAY. I am in the employ of Mr. John Wells, a pawnbroker, of Broad-street, St. Giles'. I saw the prisoner take this gown from the door on the 31st of August - she rolled it up, and put it under her shawl; I went and pushed her further into the shop - she dropped it from under her shawl, and pretended to ask the price of it; it had been hanging on two nails - she took it off one and then off the other, and pretended to be looking at a spencer.
   JAMES GUY. I was sent for and took the prisoner - she wanted to go on her knees to beg pardon, and said she did it from want.
 Prisoner. I asked the price before the shopman came round.
 GEORGE MACKAY. No, she did not till I seized her; it was close under her shawl.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the gown had fallen down at her feet, and she had merely taken it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Confined Two Months.
 1677.  WILLIAM BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of August , 1 book, value 10s., the goods of William Hill.
 WILLIAM HILL. I am a  bookseller, and live in Princes-street, Drury-lane. On the 21st of August I was called out, about nine o'clock in the evening, by my son, who was minding the shop - I came and saw the prisoner, who had asked the price of a set of books; I went out and saw a space on the board - I took the prisoner, and found the book under his flap; he had not come into the shop, but asked the price of a set of books next to this one; I asked him if he had this book; he said No; I said, "Why do you keep your hand behind you?" he then shifted it, but on my asking, he gave it up.
   THOMAS POCOCK. I took the prisoner; this is the book - there is a shelf of books outside the window.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had taken it to read, not to go away with.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. - Confined Fifteen Days.
