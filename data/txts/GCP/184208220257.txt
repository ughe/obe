Edward DOWLING. I am surgeon to the police force at Greenwich.
On the morning of the 11th of August, I examined the constable Cannon
he had a contused wound on the left side of the head, two on the
right side, a contusion on the right temple, a wound on the band, and other
marks of violence on his body-I considered the wound on the hand very
serious, there was a great deal of inflammation about the arm, it had
wounded the ligaments, and appeared to be done with great force-the
prisoner had a severe contused laceration, such as a policeman's staff would
make--the policeman's wounds were such as the iron end of the instru-
ment would make--I think his head and hands were struck with that end.
Prisoner's Defence. Coming out in the morning about four o'clock,
over the gate of the place, the constable came up and said, "Where are
you going ?" I said, “What is that to you ?" he said, “You must
give an account of yourself; I said I was on a bit of a spree all
night; he said, “Well, I have been an old soldier myself, and have had
many a spree;" I laid hold of the handle of the pump; he laid hold of my
hand, and said, “You must give up the stick; I said, “Why ?” he said,
"Come with me to the station;" I would not; be then up with his staff,
and struck me; I said, “What would it be for a soldier to come along
with a policeman ?" and he then struck me before I struck him; he tore
the breast of my shirt off ; I said, “I am a soldier, and will go to my bar-
racks ;" he then struck me down; his wounds are not so much as they
appear with that plaster over them; I picked the instrument up in the
road.
