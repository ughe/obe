2175. WILLIAM BAINES and WILLIAM REYNOLDS were in-
dicted for a like offence.
Louisa JULIAN. I keep a tobacconist's shop. On the 22nd of Sept.
Baines came in for a pennyworth of tobacco-I served him with it—he
paid me with a 4d. piece I did not look particularly at it-I had looked
at the till shortly before I had no silver but a shilling in it-as Baines
stepped out the policeman stepped in, and in a hurried manner said,
“What did that man pay in ?"-I said, "A groat" - my son went and
took it out of the till, bit it, and gave it to him.
Cross-examined by Mr. Robinson. Q. How did you put the 4d. piece
in the till? A. I opened the till and put it in- I saw there was only 1s.
in it-1 examined it in one minute afterwards.
John JULIAN. I am the son of Louisa Julian. I came into the shop
directly Baines went out-the officer came in- I took the 4d. piece out of
the till and bit it, and handed it to him—there was only 1s. in the till
besides.
Sarah Aitkin. I am the wife of Charles Aitkin. We live in Great
Suffolk-street, in the Borough-on the 22nd of Sept. Reynolds came into
our shop for a 1d. saveloy-he paid me with a 4d. piece-I put it into the
till-the officer came in-I took it out and gave it bim-I had no
other silver in the till.
Reynolds. Q. Did you give me the change? A. I laid it on the
counter-I do not know whether you had it.
John WARD. I am barman at the Red Lion publie-house at the corner
of Great Sutton-street, Southwark. On the 22nd of Sept., about eight
o'clock, Baines came in for half a pint of porter-he laid down a bad 4d.
piece-I bit it double, and very nearly broke it into two pieces- I returned
it to him-he walked out with it, and left the beer on the counter.
Cross-examined. Q. How long was he in the shop? 4. A micute or
two-there were two or three other customers.
John WHITLAM ( police-constable M 89.) I was on duty in the Old
Kent-road in plain clothes with West-I saw the prisoners in the act of
leaving the Kent-road and proceeding towards the Dover-road with ano-
ther person-I saw Reynolds go to a grocer's shop-I followed them
through the Dover-road to the Red Lion-Baines went in there-I saw
him come out and join Reynolds and the other-they proceeded along
Suffolk-street to the Windsor Castle-Baines went into Julian's shop,
and Reynolds into the Windsor Castle-he came out in about a minute-
Baines came out of Julian's shop, and I saw him put his right hand up to
his hat twice, which I considered was a signal - they then joined com-
pany, and all proceeded to Aitkin's shop-Reynolds went in there-Baines
and the other went on, and stood conversing at the corner of a dark turn-
ing-Baines then left the other, and passed and repassed Aitkin's shop--I
