Q. Did you ever write to your father, or see him in the whole of
this time? A. I wrote to him once-I do not recollect the time- it was
before he was examined-it was before my statement was taken, or any
thing occurred-I do not know whether my brother, or Dean, wrote to
him-I never went to see him, or wrote to him by Mr. Abrahams, nor did
any of us to my knowledge-I am now living in Coventry-street with my
brother--I believe my brother, John Dean, and Mr. Abrahams, were pre-
sent when my statement was taken--whether he examined me for the
purpose of getting my father admitted as a witness I do not know-he told
me he had hopes of my father being admitted as a witness.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Now, before you went into the Magistrate's pri-
vate room, had you stated to your attorney, or your father's attorney, all
that you knew ? A. All except my going to Moses the last day, on Mon.
day evening, when Mrs. Abrahams spoke to me about the bars - he took
down from my mouth what I had to say-he told me to state all that I
knew, for he had hopes that my father would be admitted as a witness,
if all he had told was fully corroborated.
Q. At that time, had you seen or heard the statement that your father
had either made or was to make ? A. Never-I never had any commu-
nication directly or indirectly with my father on the subject-I detailed to
Mr. Abrahams all the facts and circumstances that I then recollected-
did not at that time recollect the fact of my going on the last Monday to
Moses's house-Mr. Abrahams read over the statement to me which he
had taken down, before he took it away-when I went before the Magi-
strate I was not accompanied by my attorney--Mr. Abrahams was in the
Magistrate's room with me I am not sure that I saw him hand my state-
ment to the Magistrate--I suppose it was the clerk read the statement to
me--when I was examined publicly in the office it was by questions put
to me by you--my examination was taken down by the clerk at the desk
-after I had finished my examination, it was read over to me by the
clerk, and I signed it.

Friday, June 27th, 1839.
JOHN DEAN. Easter Tuesday last I was in the service of Henry Solo-
mons, of the Strand. I remember going on that day for my master to
Messrs. Cock and Johnson's-I returned about one o'clock, as nearly
as I can recollect--when I returned, my master was in the workshop-the
prisoner Mrs. Abrahams was with him--there were two furnaces alight,
and a crucible in each-I saw Mrs. Abrahams give him some lumps of
gold-he put them into a crucible-when it was melted, Mrs. Abrahams
went out into the front shop-I did not observe any thing done between her
and my master in the front shop, if any thing was done I do not know how
long she staid in the front shop before she left-she came back in about half
or three-quarters of an hour, and brought more gold, which was melted in
the same way as the other-Mrs. Abrahams was present in the workshop at
