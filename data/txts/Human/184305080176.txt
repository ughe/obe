ESSEX CASES .
   Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
 1663. GEORGE SIMMONDS was indicted for stealing, on the   13th of April , 10 1/2 lbs., weight of lead, value 2 s  ., the goods of William Nyren and others, his masters; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 31.— Confined Four Months .
 1664. MAJOR BURTON was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of April , 13lbs. weight of lead, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of James Patrick Murphy , and fixed to a certain building; against the Statute, &c. WILLIAM SHEEN( police-constable K  275.) I received information about some lead, and I went and apprehended the prisoner at his father's house—before I spoke to the prisoner about lead, he began to cry, and said he had stolen some lead, and he had cut it off, with another boy, from Mr. Murphy's premises, in North Gate-lane—this lead had been sent by a marine-store dealer to the station—I have fitted it to the premises—it fits exactly. RACHEL RAYNEK. I am the wife of John Rayner, a marine-store dealer, at Stratford. The prisoner came and offered this lead for sale—I had some suspicion, and informed the police. JOSEPH LINNEY. I live with James Patrick Murphy, at Marsh-gate, West Ham. The policeman showed me this lead—I fitted it on the engine-house—it fitted exactly.
   Prisoner.  A boy told me to get up and cut two pieces off, and then he got up and cut some off himself. GUILTY. * Aged 11.— Confined Ten Days , and Whipped.
 1665. ELLEN BARRY was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of April , 1 shawl, value 6 s  ., the goods of Mary Kentain . MARY KENTAIN. I lived at Mr. Scott's, in High-street, Bromley. The prisoner lodged at the same place—on the 26th of April I missed a shawl, which I had laid on the scale at the corner of the counter in Mr. Scott's shop—I told the prisoner—she said she was sorry for it, and persuaded me to go to the pawnbroker's to look for it—I went with her to Mr. Crawley's, the pawnbroker, and I inquired, while she was with me, for the shawl—they said they did not recollect taking one in—I afterwards went to Mr. Isaacs, a pawn broker—I found it there—this is it.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What is it worth? A.  About 5 s .—it was pawned for 1 s . 6 d . ELIZABETH NICHOLLS. I live at the Bow station. I found this duplicate for this shawl on the prisoner. JSOSEPHS BENNETT. I am apprentice to Mr. Isaac, a pawnbroker. This shawl was pawned by the prisoner—I gave this duplicate for it. PETER M'CARDELL( police-sergeant K  4.) I took the prisoner.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Four Months.
