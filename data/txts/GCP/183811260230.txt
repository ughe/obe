JONATHAN Peacock. I am a commission-traveller, and live at No. &
Nelson-place, Nelson-square, Blackfriars-road. On the night in question
I was at Mr. Jones's, who keeps the Coach and Horses, and about ten or
eleven o'clock I saw the two vans there as I came out, I heard the per-
sons standing about, say to Ellis, “Why don't you strike him again, he
has struck you first, and abused you grossly ?"--Berry then came, and at-
tempted to strike him again, and Ellis, to defend himself, struck him and
knocked him down-I imagined it was a mere drunken quarrel between the
two carriers- I considered they were both drunk-I did not help Berry into
the van-I saw an elderly person in a dark dress, who I do not know, do so
I did not see three men step back on the pavement—there were ten or
twelve persons about when I came out, but I was not there at the first of
it-the prisoner afterwards called on me, and I went with him to the pro-
secutor to see if the matter could not be arranged without any further de
lay or expense, as I was daily expecting to go down to Hull on business
of importance--we went to a house in the Borough-market, and sent for
the prosecutor-he came, and I said it was a very unfortunate thing, could
not the matter be settled-he said he did not care much about it, only he
had lost his watch, which I was not aware of-I said, "Do you accuse
Ellis of stealing it?"-he said, “No, I do not accuse him, but it was
through him I lost it; I can't do any thing in it, you must consult some
other party, for we are determined to drive him off the road, and then the
rest of his family will be of no use"-that was in Ellis's hearing-finding
that to be his determination, the matter ended he took a glass or two of
ale with me, and went about his business.
