JOHN JAMES GRIFFIN. My servant Tyler gave me a shilling on the 29th of Feb.—I told him it was bad, detained it a little while, then gave it to the policeman. HANNAH APPELBEE. My husband is a baker. On the 29th of Feb. the prisoner came to our shop for 1 d . worth of bread, or a 1 l ., loaf—he gave me a good half-crown—I gave him 2 s . 5 d .—I noticed the two shillings which I gave him—one was old, and one new, but both were good—he turned and said, "You have given me a bad shilling"—I said, "No, I have not, I am too good a judge to take bad money"—I took the 1 s . from him, and took it into the parlour to Mr. Appelbee. WILLIAM JOSHUA APPELBEE. My wife gave me a shilling—I took it, and went into the shop to the prisoner—I said, "Did my mistress give you this shilling?"—he said "Yes"—I said, "I am sure she did not, I will have you searched before you go out"—he mumbled, and said, "I suppose I must be at the loss of it"—I went to shut the door, and he ran away—I caught him—I kept the shilling till it was cut, and then gave it to the policeman. EDWIN POFFLEY( police-constable V  302.) I took the prisoner, and found on him five penny-pieces—I produce this shilling, which I got from Mr. Griffin. WILLIAM TOMPKINS( police-constable V  119.) I assisted in taking the prisoner—I received this shilling from Mr. Appelbee. MR. JOHN FIELD. These are all counterfeit, and the two first were cast in the same mould.

 1346. JOHN JENKINS was indicted for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin. SARAH GREEN SCRIVENER. My husband keeps a tobacconist's shop in Blackfriars-road. On the 12th of March the prisoner came for half an ounce of tobacco—he gave me a shilling, which I had in the till—there was no other money in the till, in silver—I did not perceive at the time that it was bad; but my husband came in. and found it was—I had not taken any other money. RICHARD CAMPION SCRIVENER. On the 12th of March I recollect taking the shilling from the till—there was no other there—I kept it in my pocket, and gave it to the policeman the next morning. SARAH WHITTAKER. I am the wife of Joseph Whittaker, and live in Mark-street, St. Saviour's. On the 12th of March the prisoner came for six pennyworth of gin—there were three men with him—the prisoner gave me a bad half-crown—I bit it, and gave it to my husband, and the prisoner was taken. JOSEPH WHITTAKER. I received the half-crown from my wife—I gave it to the policeman. WILLIAM POOLE( police constable M  33.) I received this shilling from Mr. Scrivener—I took the prisoner at Mr. Whittaker's, at the Noah's Ark—I received this half-crown from Mr. Whittaker—as I was taking the prisoner to the station, a female followed him—he put his hand to his mouth, and said, "I have coped  you." MR. JOHN FIELD. These are both counterfeit. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Six Months,
