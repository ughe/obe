702. JOHN MCARTNEY (24), Unlawfully wounding John Savage,
with intent to do grievous bodily harm.
MESSRS. SLEIGH and HOLDSWORTH conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN SAVAGE. I am a smith at the Royal Arsenal, Woolwich-between
19 and 1 o'clock on Tuesday morning, I was at Mr. George's oyster shop-
the prisoner, whom I had never seen before to my knowledge, come in and
after annoying the customers he was turned out by the man who minds the
shop-after ten minutes he came back, rushed and struck at me I pre
vented him and went outside the shop --he followed and struck me again,
and I struck him, and then he stabbed me I went into the shop and found
I was stabbed in three places; the blood was running down on my waist-
coat, which was cut in three places in front-I did not strike him until he
had repeatedly struck me I did then in self-defence my hand is still very
bad- I was obliged to have surgical assistance.
Court. Q. Where were the stabs? A. On the back of the neck, on the
chin, and on my hand-my waistcoat was cut here, and my coat at the
back
Prisoner. Q. Did I strike you before you struck me? A. You struck
at me and I prevented you twice-you used insulting language to all the
company in the shop-you did not say anything to me, but pushed against
me, and I told you to keep your company to yourself-I spoke to Mr.
George and you were turned out, and then came back again I did not feel
the effect of any of the wounds while I was fighting-1 was sober—I knew
I was wounded by seeing the blood drop on to my waistcoat-I did not see
any knife in your hand I heard some one say they saw something in your
hand, and so I left off fighting and went into the shop on my onth, I did
not say at the police-court that I knocked you down three times in the
street-I fell the last time I cannot say how often I fell.
