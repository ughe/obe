1018. GEORGE FOGG was indicted for embezzlement. SARAH FARRELL. I deal with Mr. Thomas Yates. On the 28th of March I paid the prisoner 3 s . 6 d . for his master, for meat which I had had at different times. ANN EVANS. I paid the prisoner, on the 29th of March, 2 l . 4 s . 9 1/2 d . for his master, on account of my mistress—I am quite sure of it. THOMAS YATES. I am the prisoner's master. On the 28th of March I did not receive 3 s . 6 d . from Sarah Farrell, nor on the 29th of March, 2 l . 4 s . 9 1/2 from Ann Evans—I am quite clear I did not receive either of them—he ought to pay me every day.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  How long had he been in your service? A.  About a year and a half—he has sometimes paid money to my wife, when I was out of the way—he was taken upon the 2nd of April.
   COURT . Q.  Have you demanded this money of him? A.  No, not till he was given into custody—on the Wednesday morning my little boy brought me a book, which was to be made up and returned—the prisoner told me he had received the money—the book and money had been sent by him on the 29th—this is the book which I sent as a bill—the book was to be returned immediately, and he kept it till the Wednesday morning. NOT GUILTY.
 1019. GEORGE FOGG was again  indicted for embezzlement. JANE TRACEY. I paid the prisoner 10 s . on the 31st of March, for his master for meat. THOMAS YATES. The prisoner was my servant—I did not receive 10 s . from him on the 31st of March—I never received 10 s . from him on account of Mrs. Tracey—my wife is in the habit of receiving money from the pri soner—on Tuesday morning, the 1st of April, I said to the prisoner, "Ask Mrs. Tracey for money," and he said, "Very well"—my wife is not here—she is not able to attend—no conversation has passed between her and me in the prisoner's presence about this money. NOT GUILTY.
 1020. CATHERINE HALL was indicted for stealing 1 pair of boots, value 4 s  ., the goods of George Ashwood , from the person of Sophia Esther Ashwood ; and that she had been before convicted of felony. SOPHIA ASHWOOD. I am the wife of George Ashwood—I have a little girl four years old, named Sophia Esther Ashwood—I sent her out on the 4th of April with a pair of boots on—she came back without them—I gave information, and the officer brought the prisoner—the child said it was her that took her boots—the prisoner said, "I never saw you, I could not take your boots," and then she said, "Yes, I did take them, and sold them to a woman at the foot of Westminster-bridge, for 4 d ." THOMAS BALDWIN( police-sergeant L  13.) I heard of this, and saw the prisoner standing against the station door—I asked her name—she said Catherine Hall—I compared her with the description in the book, and took her to Mrs. Ashwood, in Lambeth-walk—she said her mother had turned her out of doors, and she had taken the boots, and sold them to a woman. JAMES PORTER( police-constable D  55.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's conviction—( read. ) GUILTY. Aged 14.— Confined Six Months .
