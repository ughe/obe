 To the Recorder. A shed is about 100 yards long and there was one man there to watch. Prisoner had not time to draw out the rum while I was watching him and it must have been all ready beforehand. Detective-constable  WALTER LILLEY , Victoria Docks. On August 10, at 8 a.m., I went to "D" jetty. There were Rome casks of rum stored in "D "shed, about 200 puncheons. I examined the casks and found that one had had the tin removed from the shive and the shive had been taken out and so replaced that you could take it out with your finger and thumb. The shive is a kind of wooden bung; we have to use wood on account of the spirit. With the shive in that position the rum could easily be removed from the cask and transferred to a bottle. Behind the cask there is a gangway. I there found two empty bottles, which had contained beer. A good number of people have access to that shed and anybody knowing the condition of the casks and wishing to abstract the spirit would have the opportunity of filling up those bottles. I pointed out the puncheon to the Custom house officer on the station, and in my presence he drew a sample from the cask. SAMUEL HECK, Custom House officer. I examined the cask men tioned by last witness. Its full contents should have been 101 gallons I found a deficiency of six and a half gallons, but do not assume that six and a half gallons were taken out, it being customary to find even in a cask which has not been tampered with a vacuity representing a deficiency of three gallons or four gallons. The deficiency in this case was an unusual one, and I estimate that 2 1/2 gallons had been removed. The strength of the sample I took was 40. 4 deg. over proof. The spirit is from Demurrer a, and the average strength would be 36 deg or 40 deg. over proof. I found two of the bottles of rum produced by the police to be 39 deg. over proof and have no doubt the spirit came from the cask I examined. The value of the spirit in the seven bottles is 10s. 10d., and the duty upon it is £12s. 4d. £112s. 5d. HARRY BOWMAN, assistant superintendent, Royal Mail Steam Packet Company. On August 4 we were discharging rum from the Catalonia at "D" jetty. Prisoner was not working for the company and bad no right to enter the premises or take rum. The rum in these bottles is the property of the company, who are the only people that discharge rum in the dock. The rum on being discharged would remain on the jetty a short time before it goes into bond.
 Police-constable  WILLIAM WALLER , 218, K. On August 9, about 7.30 p.m., I was on duty at the Victoria Dock and saw prisoner. In formation had previously been received that a man named Compton was wanted for being in unlawful possession of bottles of rum I took him into custody; he said, "All right."
