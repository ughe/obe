I live at 79, High Street, Whitechapel—on 14th February I was walking up Commercial Street with the last witness—I saw the prisoner standing at the corner—he crossed over with the young lady—I saw him take hold of her bag—she held it so tight that he threw her down and ran away with it—I ran after him half way down the street—he went into the lodging house or the next house, I am not sure which, and I did not follow him any further—I next saw him at the station on 10th March with 12 or 14 others—I picked him out—I have no doubt he is the man.
   Cross-examined.  I live in the same house as the last witness—I am a general servant, she is an assistant in the drapery business—the prisoner wore a peaked cap. WILLIAM THICK( Police Sergeant H ). On 10th March I took the prisoner into custody—I told him I should apprehend him on suspicion of violently assaulting and robbing a young lady on 14th February in Commercial Street—he said "I was at Birmingham when it was done"—I took him to the station—he was placed with eight others, and was identified by the witnesses—I found on him 12 s . 6 d . in silver and 9 1/2 d . in bronze.
   Cross-examined.  The ring has not been traced—the poisons he was placed with were obtained out of the street as they were passing—they were asked to step in, young and old—the prisoner had on a round hard felt hat. GUILTY.*— Four Months' Hard Labour.
