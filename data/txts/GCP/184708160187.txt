1922. WILLIAM BASTERFIELD and WILLIAM DAVEY were in-
dicted for stealing 4 bushels of mixed oats and chaff, value 78. ; and 1 sack,
6d.; the goods of Samuel Pocock, and another, the masters of Basterfield ;
to which
BASTERFIELD pleaded Guilty. Aged 49.-Confined Six Months.
EDWARD HENRY CHAMBERS. I am horse-keeper to Messrs. Samuel and
Lewis Pocock, of Phænix-wharf, White Friars. Basterfield was their carman
- he went out with a team on the 20th of July-I delivered him two sacks,
one contained about four bushels of horse-feed, consisting of clover, chaff,
and oats, which he was to give to his horses-the property produced is my
masters', and is worth 6s. or 78.
CHARLES RANDELL (policeman N 172.) I saw Basterfield come out of the
field, and I watched him to Davey's shop-Davey came out and spoke to him,
he then went on, and Davey ran after him, and overtook him-Basterfield
then lifted a sack out of the cart-Davey took it on his back, and went to-
wards his own stable with it-I stopped him:
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Davey went away with it? A.
Yes, he hurried away-I told the clerk twice that he hurried away—I do not
know wherber is is written down that he walked-he went in the direction of
his own stable-I asked him where he got it-he said he bought it of a man
-I asked him which man--he pointed to Basterfield, and my brother con-
stable took him into custody.

* 1923. JANE CLINE was indicted for stealing 2 sheets, value 5s. ; 2 pet-
ticoats, 88. ; 2 handkerchiefs, 3s. ; 2 bed-gowns, 38.; and 1 sheet, 5s."; the
goods of Henry William Byrn, her master; and that she had been before con-
victed of felony.
WILLIAM AYRES. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a sheet pawned by a
woman--the duplicates which are produced by the officer correspond with
the property produced, and with the duplicates which I have.
WILLIAM THOMAS (policeman T 60.) I went to the prisoner's house-I
found there a towel, a handkerchief, several pieces of lace, and twenty-six
duplicates, some of which Mr. Ayres has identified.
MARY BYRN. I am the wife of Henry William Byrn, and am a laundress.
The prisoner washed for me I lost all this property-some of it is mine, and
some is my employers'.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Are you able to identify these
articles ? A. Yes, many of them, the names have been taken out of some
-the prisoner is married, and has six children--I have heard that her husband
does not allow her much, and I felt for her.
John Jones (police-sergeant T 31.) I produce a certificate of the pri-
soner's former conviction- (read-Convicted 5th of April, 1841, and confined
four months.)-she is the person.
