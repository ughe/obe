471. THOMAS MAISEY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of December , 1 bed, value 20s.; 1 carpet, value 3l. 10s.; 4 sheets, value 18s.; 2 curtains, value 2s.; 2 tablecloths, value 8s.; 6 napkins, value 5s.; 4 coats, value 12s., and 3 blankets, value 13s., the goods of Emanuel Moses.
 EMANUEL MOSES. The prisoner was employed by me at times, and on the 27th of December I sent him to Edwards' auction-room, Vere-street, Clare-market, to fetch these goods, which I had bought on the 26th; I waited at the auction-room for him, but he did not come; I wrapped them in a carpet, and left word for them to deliver them to him if he came - I saw him afterwards, and he said he had not been able to go; I told him to go next day.
   WILLIAM HENRY SLADE. I am a coachman. On the 27th of December the prisoner hired me at the corner of Dean-street, Tooley-street; he put these things into the coach at the King's Head public-house, to go to Petticoat-lane - when we got there he said he did not know where to go; I thought he was to meet a person there to buy the things - he then told me to drive to Tooley-street again, and when I was in Whitechapel it was between eleven and twelve o'clock at night; I did not know the turning, and asked the watchman the way - the prisoenr told me to go on to Cornhill, and there a man spoke to the prisoner, and got in - they ordered me back to Aldgate again - they got out there, and told me to wait; and in a few minutes a man came, and told me the goods were stolen; the prisoner came up - I asked him about them - he said they were his master's; I told an officer, who took us all to the watch-house.
   MICHAEL MEREDITH. I attend the sale-room in Vere-street; Moses bought these articles, and told me to deliver them to a person who would mention his name; I delivered all of them to the prisoner on the 27th; he left another bed, as he could not carry it.
   WILLIAM IVEY. I am an apprentice to Mr. Reeve, pawnbroker, of Snowhill. I have two blankets, pawned on the 27th of December, by a woman.
   JOHN ROBERTS. I am an officer. I received information, and detained the coach and goods; I saw the prisoner there; he said they were his sister's, who lived in King's Head-yard, Tooley-street; I asked what he was going to do with them - he did not seem to know; this duplicate of the blankets was found on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I thought I would leave the things at my sister's, and then took them to Petticoat-lane.
 GUILTY. Aged 60. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Months.
 472.  JOHN STANTON and JAMES HAYDON were indicted for stealing, on the 5th of January , 1 pair of boots, value 4s., the goods of George Joseph Ford.
 GEORGE JOSEPH FORD. I live in  Field-lane. On the 5th of January, in the evening, I saw the prisoners pass my window two or three times, and told my shopman and apprentice to look after them; these boots were taken from the board at the window - I went out - found Stanton with them under his arm, and Haydon by his side; they were then going up a court into Holborn; I had seen the boots safe ten minutes before; Stanton said, "Let me go, it will be all right;" I took him, and my shopman took Haydon.
   THOMAS LONG. I am a watchman. I saw the prisoners go up Field-lane twice, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, and the second time I heard an alarm that they had stolen the boots; I assisted in taking them to the watch-house; when I got up, Stanton had dropped one boot, and Mr. Ford was taking it up.
   JOHN EDWARDS. I received the prisoners in charge at the watch-house; they said they were going to the play, but we found only a few halfpence on them.
 STANTON'S Defence. I had been cleaning a chimney which was on fire, and got intoxicated afterwards, and did not know what I was doing.
 HAYDON'S Defence. I know nothing about them.
 STANTON -    GUILTY. Aged 27. Transported for Seven Years.
 HAYDON -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Six Months.
 473.  ROBERT TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of December , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Edward Janson , from his person.
 EDWARD JANSON. On the 31st of December I was in  Whitechapel, about noon - I crossed over at Houndsditch- some persons called to me; I turned round, and saw the officer with my handkerchief in one hand, and the prisoner in the other; I had not noticed him before - my handkerchief was taken from my coat pocket.
   FRANCIS KEYS. I am an officer. I was on duty, and saw the prisoner, and two others make an attempt at the prosecutor's pocket, just opposite Petticoat-lane - they then went on, and closed up to him by Aldgate church, and I saw the prisoner put something into his breast - I crossed and took him with the handkerchief.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you see his hand at the prosecutor's pocket? A. No; but they were all together; I did not know him before.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been after a situation at Mr. Baker's; I picked the handkerchief up.
 MR. JANSON. It was a muddy day - there was no dirt on the handkerchief.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 474.  MARY HALEY was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of December , 2 pairs of boots, value 5s., the goods Robert Langford.
 THOMAS DAVIS. I live with Robert Langford, a  boot and shoe-maker, of Cripplegate. I did not see these boots taken, but I had seen them two minutes before, on a horse in the shop - the prisoner came in to look at a pair of boy's boots; I showed her a pair - she did not like them; she offered 3s. 6d. for another pair, which I could not take - she left, and came back, and asked for a pair of boy's spencers, which were in a case, and while I got them, she must have put these boots into her apron: Sarah Davis came up, and asked what was in her apron; she said nothing - but these boots were found in it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was she not talking to you about boots at this very time? A. No.
   SARAH DAVIS. I was coming down stairs, and saw the prisoner take two pairs of boots off the horse, and put them into her apron; I asked what she had there; she said nothing - but I took them from her apron.
