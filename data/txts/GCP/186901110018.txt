WILLIAM JAMES. I am manager of the Windmill public house, within
four doors of Mr. Gare's on 4th December, I saw the prisoner served with
a glass of aleI heard her make no complaint-she tendered a good half-
crown-I had not given change for any gold to anybody before that.
ARTHUR FORDHAM. I am a son of Thomas Fordham, a bookbinder, of 85,
City Road- on 24th December, I served the prisoner with a pack of playing
cards, which came to 9d.-she gave me a half-crown-I put it in the till;
there was no other half-crown there—the prisoner was afterwards brought
back by a policeman-my brother took the half-crown out of the till, and
said in her presence that it was bad, and handed it to Mr. Gare's shopman,
who handed it to the constable--the prisoner said nothing.
John DYMOTT (Policeman G 57). "I received information, and followed
the prisoner to Mr. Fordham's shop—when she came out I took her, and
took her back to the shop; Arthur Fordham was there, and Mr. Gare's
shopman, who gave me this half-crown (produced), which Arthur Fordham's
brother took out of the till ; 6s, 5 d. in good money was found on the
prisoner.
Prisoner's Defence. It was the inspector who marked the half-crown. It
is not the one I gave.
GUILTY.
She was further charged with having been before convicted, when she was
sentenced to one year's imprisonment; to this she PLEADED Guilty.**

ISABELLA BURTMART. I keep a tobacconist's shop at Horselydown--on
14th November I served the prisoner with half an ounce of tobacco, and a
pipe, which came to 14d, he gave me a bad shilling-I asked where he got it,
he said "For carrying a load from the City to the railway"-I gave him in
charge with the shilling.
CHARLES SMITH (Policeman M 251). I took the prisoner, he was remanded
and discharged.
