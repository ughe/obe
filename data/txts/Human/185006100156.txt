 1200. THOMAS SABIN , HENRY FETHERSTON , DENNY FETHERSTON, JUN., JEREMIAH REEVES , PETER GOULD , STEPHEN TOMS , SAMUEL TUBBY, and JAMES PEARSON, were in dicted for a forcible entry.
   MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. JACOB SMITH. I am a farm-servant of Mr. Stephen Sabin, of Lewisham, the prosecutor. On 7th May, about a quarter-past six o'clock in the morning, I saw Reeves come over our yard gate, followed by young Fetherstone, Gould, Toms, Tubby, and Pearson, and they went into the stable—my master came out, and ordered them off, telling them they bad no business there—I heard Gould say they were there for 150 l . rent—about half-past six, Sabin came in towards the stable—Gould met him, and spoke to him—he turned back, and I did not see him any more that day—Gould came back again—the elder Fetherston was there next day—they remained in the stable, in and out, till eight in the evening when my master, Mr. Turner, and Wallis went in, and master asked who was the broker's son—the men pointed him out, and master took hold of his collar and turned him out—the other men got between them in a cluster, and one of them knocked master's hat off—I could not see who it was—Tubby held up a stick, and said he would knock the first man down that came near him—one of them, I do not know which, pulled off his gloves and put up his hands, as if he meant to fight—we then left the stable, and left them there all night—next morning, about eight, I heard the elder Fether ston call out at the yard-gate for one of his men—Reeves went, and I saw him take up the iron tyre of a cart-wheel, and knock the padlock off, which was on a chain on the gate leading into the road, and Mr. Fetherston came in—he then came round with a paper in his hand, went into the stable as if looking at the horses—he then came to the barn where I was, looked at the pigs, cows, and heifers, and I heard him say, "There is not sufficient to pay, we will have an entrance into the house"—all the prisoners were present except Sabin—they then went towards the house, and Reeves picked up a crowbar—I went into a cart-shed, right opposite the door, to see whether I could see, and when I got there they had forced an entrance into the house—I did not see them break in—I believe my master and mistress and the female servant were in the house—I have worked for Mr. Stephen Sabin twelve years, and have known him to have possession of this place twenty years, and he remained so till 7th May—I worked for his father—I do not know how young Sabin got in—the gate was locked then.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Young Sabin was not present when Fetherston said he should break in? A.  No; I did not see him in the house.
