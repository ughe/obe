   Cross-examined by MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE . When I first spoke to him he said he would see me damned before we should have a farthing of the money—I think he did not exactly know what he was saying—he did not suggest that I was a thief, but he said he would not touch Mr. Freeth with a barge pole—I went into the inner room because he made so much noise—he followed me, and confessed that he was a party to the robber)', but I do not think he knew what he was saying, he told me so many lies—I have known him many years—I do not think he had the knowledge that it was a robbery—I do not think he looked upon it in that light—hesaid the 8 l . he received was interest—I had no business with the 32 l . 12 s . 8 d ., but I wanted it for the iron delivered short at our works—I do not know whether we received the full amount for which we paid—I only have to do with the City office—I knew there had been a dispute about the amount, and instructions were sent to me to collect it—we went into liquidation two years ago, and Mr. Parsonage bought the works and arranged with us to carry them on and take a salary of 500 l . a year, and according to the letters we were to take it off his salary—his profits were to be 10 per cent on the little money that he had lent us from time to time—he was acting as a dummy to enable us to trade—he purchased three parts of the works—we considered ourselves his servants—we had 10 l . a week regularly, which is entered in the wages book.
   Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY . I was a commission agent before this—I took capital into the concern—no one has spoken to me outside since Freeth was cross-examined, or said anything about the 10 l . a week—not a soul has spoken to me, and the policeman knows it—I did not try to get the 32 l . 14 s . 10 d . out of Porter or Douglas—I did not go after Porter—he came to me—I did demand the money two  or three days after the 1st of May, and I went on demanding it till the 1st of June, when I saw Douglas—I never saw Porter at the Lombard—he has not complained of our weights to my knowledge—some one may have—it is one of the best weighing machines in London—it has been there nine or ten years—I told Porter that there was a warrant out against him—I cannot say when. Porter and Clarke received good characters.
   FENNELL  and CLARKE  — GUILTY  — The Jury and Prosecutor strongly recommended them to mercy— Eighteen Months' Imprisonment each .
   PORTER  — GUILTY — Five Years' Penal Servitude.
   DOUGLAS  — NOT GUILTY.
