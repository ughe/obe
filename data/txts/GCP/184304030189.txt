WILLIAM WOODER. I am the prisoner's brother we live together, at No.
5, King-street, Blackheath. On Monday morning, the 20th of March, he got
up at twenty minutes to six o'clock-I called him-he sleeps in the back-
room, the next room to me- I told him to make haste, and dress himself,
which he did in three or four minutes--we both left the house together,
and went to the same place to work-we got to work a little after six-we
continued at work together all day, till night-Mr. Meriton's house is about
a mile or more from ours, and about a mile from where we work--the pri-
soner had only been employed at the work on the Saturday and Monday-he
ought to be at his work at six o'clock.
COURT. Q. Who saw you at work there that day? A. All the men in
the building—we have two clocks in our house, one up stairs and the other
down-it would be, I should say, a quarter of a mile out of our way to go to
Mr. Meriton's, instead of going direct to our work I know the time we
started I looked at the church clock on Blackheath-hill-it was exactly ten
minutes to six then it is close to our house, on the right coming down the
hill, one side of the clock is towards the road, and one side towards Black-
heath-Mr. Bath is foreman of the bricklayers there, but we were at
work for the plasterers---Mr. Emly is foreman of the plasterers--he is here
my brother had no knife-he borrowed a chisel of the carpenter to cut his
meat with, both on the Saturday and Monday-Mr. Emly and others saw
him using it.
James JAOKMAN, I am a bricklayer's labourer, and live in Essex-place,
Blackheath-hill-I am a neighbour of the prisoner's. On Monday, the 20th
of March, I was going across Blackheath, and saw the prisoner and his
brother going across the heath, about two minutes before six o'clock, near
the Hare and Billett-I knew them both very well for three years and a half
or four years—they are hard working industrious boys-the prisoner has
been out of work a great deal lately, and I have also they were going to-
wards Lee-six o'clock was our time to be at work.
Court. Q. Where were you going to work? A. At Mr. Couchman's,
the upholster, in the village was wheeling a barrow-when they passed
me they were walking in the ordinary manner--they appeared going to their
work- generally saw them going to their work-we often go together-I
kept a particular account of this day, because I was told I should have to be
here--I went before the Magistrate, but was not called.
