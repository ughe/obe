CHACKSFIELD, George Frederick (29, postman); stealing s post-
letter containing two postal orders for 12., the property of the Post-
master-General, he being employed under the Post Office. Sentence,
Nine months' hard labour.
KIRBY, Arthur (51, jeweller), pleaded guilty of stealing and re-
ceiving three silver cups, the goods of Harry Rix and another
(trustees of the Mildmay Radical Club); stealing a plated cake dish
and other articles, the goods of Lewis Lewis; stealing a mandoline
and case, the goods of Emily Clark; stealing pair of salad salvers
and other articles, the goods of Horace Bolton. Two other indict-
ments for larceny were not proceeded with Prisoner confessed to
# conviction at Spalding Sessions on March 4, 1890, of felony.
The police gave prisoner & very bad character as a professional
receiver of stolen goods, the proceeds of burglaries.
Sentence, Three years penal servitude.
An order was made for the restitution to the owners of certain
identified stolen property.
FOWLER, Edward (28, cook), pleaded guilty of feloniously marry
ing Emily Eliza Block, his wife being then alive.
It appeared that it was at Block's request that he had gone through
a form of marriage with her, she being in the family way by him
she stated that he had always treated her with the greatest kindness ;
be bore a very excellent general character.
Prisoner, having been in gaol for seven weeks, was now sentenced
to two days' imprisonment, entitling him to immediate release.

with intent to defraud (a further indictment was not proceeded with)
be confessed to a conviction at Clerkenwell Sessions, on June 6, 1905,
for a similar offence; four other previous convictions were proved and
he was described by the police as an habitual criminal
