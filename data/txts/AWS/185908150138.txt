Mr. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. Q. With regard to the inflammation that
you would expeet to find, supposing arsenic was taken through the throat,
would it depend at all upon the mode in which it was taken and what it
was mixed with. A. Certainly to a certain extent at least: if no arsenic
or antimony was found, but some small quantity in an evacnation, I should
still entertain the opinion that the death was occasioned by an irritant
poison, and, of course, the poison that is detected after death is the most
likely to produce it but poison may be exhibited not only alone, it may be
combined, and combined with other substances which may mask their deteo-
tion-I am unable to give a decided opinion whether that would be the ease
if antimouy or arsenic, or both, were given ab different times or at the same
time- have no experience on that subject.
