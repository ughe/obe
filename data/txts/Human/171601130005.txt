   James Goodman , alias    Footman of the Parish of St. Dunstan at Stepney was indicted for feloniously stealing (together with one John Stephens ) a Bay Gelding, Value 8 l. the property of Philip White, on the 17th of December last. Mr. White depos'd, That on the 9th of October last, between Stratford and Ilford in Essex, he met three Highway-men on Horseback, one of them he believ'd was the Prisoner, who took from him his Horse, his Spurs, and 15 s. in Money; after this, on the 17th of December last, he met the Prisoner upon his Horse at Bow in Middlesex and Stevens with him, upon which he sent his Servant to the Prisoner to demand his Master's Horse ; which being done they set spurs to their Horses and rode away; but being pursued, the Prisoner flash'd a Pistol close at Mr. White, but it did not go off, and then they quitted their Horses, not being able to leap the Ditch, and took to a Field; hereupon Mr. White gave his Servant a small Gun that he had with him, and order'd him to pursue them. The Prisoner fir'd twice at the Servant, who had it in his Power (his Gun being loaded with small Shot) to have made a Cullinder of his Scull; but not knowing the Law, he miss'd him, and kept a little out of his Way; whereupon he heard the Prisoner say to the other, D - n is, we'll kill or be kill'd; we won't be taken alive: Let's turn upon them and fire; our Lives are as good as theirs; which made him charge his Gun again, and put some Pebble Stones into it, with which he hit the Prisoner behind the Head, who immediately fell down and was taken. The other Highway-man was also taken by another Mr.White, no Relation to the former, who was accidentally upon the Road, and being appris'd of the Matter, leap'd over the Ditch and pursued him, with his Hanger drawn: Stephens seeing himself hard put to it, presented two Pistols to him, who thereupon bid him fire, but told him, if he miss'd, he'd cleave his Scull; whereupon, seeing his Companion already taken, he surrendred, and dropt his Pistols. They had two Musquetoons and a Screw Pistol, of a particular Make, and very unfair, one of them being loaded with 3 Bullets, and the others with 2 each. The Prisoner in his Defence said, the Horse was lent him, and to prove it call'd two Persons, one of which swore, Four Men came in November last to his Inn, where they put up their Horses, and one of them (which was that mention'd in the Indictment) being lame with a Cut on the Knee, one Smith came to him, and desir'd that a Farrier might be sent for to cure him; and then they all went away, and left him for that Purpose; and being cur'd, Smith's Brother came and paid for it, and had an Order to deliver the same to the Prisoner; which was confirm'd afterwards by the Farrier, who receiv'd the Money for his Cure. But this making nothing for him, the Court being of the Opinion they were all Accomplices, which the Jury considering, brought him in   Guilty of the Indictment.
 This Desperado, after he was Convicted, was put into the Bail-Dock, it being thought the safest Place for such Persons, since the Murder of Mr. Spurling, in the Hold; but about 7 a-Clock on Saturday-Night, (while the Court and the Keepers were employ'd about another Cause) with the Assistance of some other Criminals in the same Place, he threw himself over the Spikes into the further Passage, tho' fetter'd and made his Escape, before he receiv'd Sentence.
 Stevens was admitted to Bail, and not appearing was not tried this Sessions.
   William Eckersly and William Walton, of the Precinct of St. Katharines, were indicted, Eckersly for feloniously stealing 2 Yards of Shagg, 2 Coats, 1 Wastcoat, several Drawers, Shirts, Handkerchiefs, and other sorts of Slop Goods, from William Jesson, on the 10th Instant; and Walton as Accessory in receiving the same, knowing them to be stole. The Fact was plainly prov'd upon Eckersly, who did not deny it at his Trial. It was also prov'd that Walton did receive the Goods, but he denied he knew them to be stole; which not being prov'd to the Jury's Satisfaction, they   acquitted him, but found Eckersly Guilty to the Value of 39 s.    Deborah Green of the Parish of St. Paul at Shadwel, was indicted for stealing 1 Worsted Camblet Riding-Hood, Value 10 s. and 17 Shillings in Money, the Goods and Money of Thomas Medcalfe, on the 30th of December last. It appear'd to be a very trifling Prosecution, and the Jury   acquitted the Prisoner.
   John Moor, of the Parish of Pancras, was indicted for feloniously stealing 1 Black Gelding, Value 20 s. the Good of Francis Hope, on the 6th Instant. Hope depos'd, That he had turn'd his Horse loose in the Evening aforesaid, that he might go Home as he us'd to do, but finding him not there, he enquir'd after him, and at last heard he was secur'd in Kentish Town. Another Evidence depos'd, The Prisoner came to his House, and knock'd him up, wanting a Lodging; That going down, he wonder'd to see a Man with a Sword by his Side upon a Car-man's Horse with his Harness on, which made him inquire how he came by him; upon which the Prisoner, who was in Drink, and had been thrown by him into a Pond, answer'd, D - n him, he found him in Hampstead Road, but he was quite tir'd of him, and did not care if the Devil had him, so they were parted. The Prisoner, it seems, was a very honest Man, a Trooper, and being in Drink, thought what he did was a very harmless Frolick; upon which the Jury   acquitted him.
   Jane Collins, of the Parish of St. Dunstan in the West, was indicted for feloniously stealing 1 Silver Tea-Spoon, value 5 s. 1 Tobacco-Box, 5 s. a Gold Ring, and 5 l. in Money, the Goods of Humphry Dearn, on the 28th of December last. Mr. Dearn swore, That while he and his Wife were abroad, the Goods above-mention'd were stole; and an ingenious young Woman, who lodg'd in another Room in the same House, depos'd, That the Prisoner brought the Case of Drawers (out of which the Things aforesaid were stole) to her, desiring it might be lodg'd there a small Time, because there was not then Room enough in her Son's Shop, which was over the Way; and that some Writings (of Consequence only to the Prosecutor) was given to her, by a Person in that Shop. The Prisoner called some Witnesses to her Reputation, who giving her a pretty good Character, the Jury   acquitted her.
