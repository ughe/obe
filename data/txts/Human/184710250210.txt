On the 4th of Oct. the prisoner was in my service—Wheeler was not—I had 330 quarter of oats to work out of the Tennant  on the day in question—I directed the prisoner to load the Eclipse , and to put the rest in the Jolon  barge—I gave no direction as to the use of a skiff—the bundle of sacks was to stack the Eclipse  —he was to take the Eclipse  to Whiting's wharf—that was not done—Adcock gave me information—I after wards saw the barge inside the tier—I found the skiff at Prince's stairs—nothing was in it then—it was in my charge, so that the prisoner could get it before twelve o'clock—I crossed the water—I saw a boy who I employed to load the barge, go ashore—the oats were to be delivered to Edward Raper—they were delivered, but not the five quarters—I have been charged for this defi ciency, and had to pay for it—I went to the Thames-police-office, and went with an officer to find the prisoner—I found him in the George public-house—I asked him what had become of the five quarters—he said he knew no thing about them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where was the Tennant  lying? A.  At Prince's stairs—about half a mile from Whiting's—I only employed the prisoner to unload this corn—there was a boy employed to turn the meter—the prisoner had not been long in my employ—he was merely employed by the day—that is the general practice—Wheeler is occasionally employed by persons there—that is the way he gets his living—I gave the order the night before—I have never recorded the five quarters, nor found what was done with them, nor found Wheeler.

 2511. SAMUEL TOOMBS was indicted for bigamy. JOHN STRIDE. I am an engineer, and live in Victory-street, New Town, Deptford. I have know the prisoner about nine years—I was with him in Oct., about nine years ago, at the parish Church of St. George's, Bloomsbury, when he was married to Mary Woolridge—he was then about thirty or thirty-one years of age—I was one of the witnesses to the marriage—I after wards saw them living together as man and wife for three or four years—she is now alive—I saw her five minutes ago—she is about the same age or rather older than the prisoner—I had not been there before the marriage—none of the other witnesses are here—my wife was one—I had known the female rather longer then the prisoner—the prisoner was a carpenter—I do not know the name of the elergyman who performed the ceremony—Mr. Catty was the clerk—he was present.
   Cross-examined by MR. WOLLETT. Q.  Do you know on what terms the prisoner lived with his wife? A.  In my opinion he lived on very good terms—I do not knew of any separation—I have heard that it is three or four years ago since he left her—I then saw them living together very happily, and comfortably—there did not appear much disparity in their ages—I have heard of the prisoners allowing her so much a week.
