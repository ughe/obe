1794. GEORGE HARE, JOHN PAYNE, WILLIAM GOOD-
RIGHT, JAMES GOODRIGHT, HENRY GOODRIGHT, JAMES
FLINN, WILLIAM PASSFIELD, MICHAEL MANNIX, alias Cor-
nelius Mannix, and JOHN SHAE, were indicted for assaulting Thomas
Wigzell, a peace-officer, in the execution of his office.-2nd COUNT, with
intent to resist the lawful apprehension and detainer of the said George
Hare.
Messrs. BODkin and CLARK conducted the Prosecution.
EDWARD KAYSER (police-constable P 121.) On Sunday, the 22nd of
June, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, I was on duty in the
Camberwell-road-I went into Windmill-lane, and saw Hare and Payne,
and a great many other persons-Hare was rather drunk, and was stripped
to fight-I requested him to go away-he said he should when he liked ;
but when he found I was desirous for him to go, he went, and the people
separated after a few minutes-some time afterwards I received information,
and went to Nelson-street, and saw Hare throwing up his hat and jacket,
and there were fifty or more persons about-I went to the Walworth-
road, and got Wigzell-when we got to the bottom of Windmill-lane we
saw Chantler at a distance, and beckoned him-when we got to Nelson-
street I saw Hare in fustian or corded trowsers-- he had no coat or
waistcoat on-his shirt-sleeves were tucked up, and his handkerchief tied
round his waist-he was throwing up his hat and jacket, and challenging
any one to fight-he challenged several in my presence-he did not speak
to one alone- I went up to him with Wigzell, and said he must go to the
station-he wanted to know what for-1 said he would know that when
he got to the station-he went a short distance very quietly, then turned
very restive indeed I requested bim to go quietly, and told him we were
taking him to the station-be requested to know the charge-I said,
"Being drunk and disorderly, and creating a disturbance"-he said he
would not go, life or death first-he laid himself down on the ground seve-
ral times-after we got a short distance Payne came up and caught hold
of Hare by the braces and shirt, and said he would be
d d if he should
go; he should go along with him-I told Payne to take his hands off
him, or I would break his arms with my truncheon-he continued to hold
on to Hare several times after that-Hare laid himself down on the ground,
and said, if we would allow him to button his shirt up, he would go
quietly-I had seen him kick out several times before that-we allowed
him to get up, and he was about buttoning his shirt, when he clenched his
fist, struck me in the face, and made my nose bleed-he struck Wigzell,
who very nearly fell, but he held on by Hare, and kept himself up-I then
drew my truncheon, and struck Hare on the head, who fell to the ground
--when Hare struck me in the face my truncheon was in my pocket-
there was a hooting and shouting of the people, and a singing out to Hare
not to go with us--he got up, and struggled, and the mob all closed in
upon us--we were being overpowered, and I went to the corner of Wind-
mill-lane, to get more assistance-Wigzell and Chantler remained with
Tare-I returned with three other officers in about three minutes-Hare
