 1742.  CATHERINE CALLAGHAN was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , 1 waistcoat, value 4s., the goods of Joseph Cohen.
   ELIZABETH COHEN. I am the wife of Joseph Cohen - we live in Duke-street, Grosvenor-square. The prisoner was in our service nine or ten days previous to the charge - I did not miss anything, but I saw a waistcoat concealed behind a useless water-butt in the back kitchen; on Thursday, the 7th of August, I desired the prisoner to go on an errand, but she did not go; I then sent for a constable - the waistcoat was then gone; when the constable came, he met her at the door, and told her to come in; we asked what she had done with the waistcoat; she said she had none, but we found it under her clothes.
   WILLIAM CRAGG. I apprehended the prisoner, and found the waistcoat tied round her middle.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 GUILTY. Aged 22. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury. Confined One Month.
 1743.  BENJAMIN BULLOCK and ALEXANDER MAYSTON were indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 1 silver pocket communion-service, value 50s., the goods of Mary Gilbert. widow.
   JAMES STAPLES. I am shopman to Mary Gilbert - she did live in Cockspur-street, Charing-cross; she is a widow- she is a goldsmith and jeweller. On the 31st of July I watched the shop window; I saw the two prisoners there: Mayston put his hand through a pane of glass, (which had been previously broken), and took hold of this case of articles; Bullock was close by him, and when he drew them out Bullock took them of him, they immediately parted- I ran and took Mayston, and my fellow-shopman took Bullock, and when he had got hold of him I saw him throw two of these articles into the road; they were taken to St. Martin's watch-house - the articles are worth 50s.
   GEORGE DOGGETT. I am porter to Mary Gilbert. - Staples gave me some information; I ran out and took Bullock; I saw these things fall from him, either at the moment I took hold of him, or just before; it was between eight and nine o'clock in the morning.
   JAMES MERRETT. I was in Cockspur-street, and saw Doggett take hold of Bullock - I saw him throw down these articles, and I took them up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 BULLOCK -    GUILTY. Aged 24.
 MAYSTON -    GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 1744.  MARY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of August , 1 umbrella, value 4s., the goods of John Williams.
   JOSEPH PRICE. I am a patrol of Bow-street. I saw the prisoner, on the 23d of August, take this umbrella from the shutter of a house next door but one to Southampton-street; I saw Mr. Williams at the same time handing his wife from one of the stages - he then turned, and was looking for the umbrella; I followed the prisoner, and took her with it; I took it back - Mr. Williams would not at the time swear to the umbrella; but said he lost it from the spot where I saw it taken.
 COURT. Q. When she took it did she move on with it? A. Yes; it was between twelve and one o'clock.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I lost the umbrella, but cannot speak positively to it.
   SUSANNAH WILLIAMS. I am the wife of John Williams. I saw the umbrella; I know it to be mine - I did not see it taken; I was going from the coach at the time.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took the umbrella, but there was no person near it; I considered some person had left it there, and it was free for any one to take.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1745.  WILLIAM BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , 1 pair of boots, value 12s., and 1 pair of stockings, value 1s., the goods of Thomas Sarjeants.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
 THOMAS SARJEANTS. I am  superintendent of the Sailors' Asylum. I do not recollect that I had ever seen the prisoner, but my wife shewed me a pair of stockings and a pair of boots on the 8th of July, which were mine.
   MARY SARJEANTS. I am the wife of John Sarjeants. On the 8th of July I was in my back room on the first floor, and heard a strange footstep coming down the stairs; I went to the door, and saw the prisoner - I asked what he wanted; he said he had come there for relief: he said money was meant for such as him - he was distressed, and would have it; the office is in the house, but down stairs, and he was coming down from the attic; I told him Mr. Sarjeants was not at home, and he could not have it then; I saw something bulky under his frock - I came down after him to the street door, and said, "You have robbed this house!" he said, "I have not," and he ran about five yards, to the second house, when he took out the boots, and threw them down; I took them up - Mr. Smith ran and took hold of him, and brought him back; there were some other trifling articles found on him, which I had mended up to give to a poor woman.
 COURT. Q. Had he been at your house before to ask for relief? A. I cannot say I recollect him; but I believe he was relieved, in December last; the office was then up two pairs of stairs; these are the boots he dropped - they have my husband's name on them.
   THOMAS HABGOOD. I am a constable. I took the prisoner, and produce the boots; he swore most desperately, and said he would have his revenge on Mrs. Sarjeants.
   GEORGE SMITH. I came to the assistance of Mrs. Sarjeants; I saw the prisoner drop the boots from under his smock-frock, and run off; I chased and caught him - I brought him back; his hat was taken off, and these stocking were found in it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not know what I did; I was intoxicated - they know I was.
