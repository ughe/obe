DANIEL MORGAN (Police Inspector X). I know the prisoner Young by
the name of Thomas Shaw and Thomas Hawes-I know his father; he
has gone by 100 names, but I believe his correct name is James Shaw-I
know Randall; Charles Thomas Randall is his correct name in February,
1876, he was living at 11, Goose Green, Dulwich, with Young and his
father, old Shaw, and the other brother who is now wanted; they were
then in the name of Shaw and Hawes, all living there together---Randall
married Young's elder sister; I have the marriage certificate- I cannot
find old Shaw or the brother; they have absconded.
> Cross-examined by Young. I first knew you swindling with a man
named Hall you were at 37, Charles Street, Hatton Garden, in Feb.
ruary, 1876, when I called there in respect of certain frauds when Frank
Hall was arrested—I swear it was you-I did not take you into custody
then, because you were only 17, and I thought your father was the prin-
cipal person; he was arrested—I have not found you anywhere since.
CHARLES VINEY (Police Sergeant P). I received Young in custody on
this charge-I read the warrant to him-he said "Oh, that is only a
debt”-it was for Mr. Garnham's dog-biscuits-I said "Well, it may
be, if it stood alone; there is the piano at Mr. Walter's, at Camberwell,
& sewing machine in the Borough, and many others "-he made no reply
to that I said " There is the shop in Oglander Road, last year "-he said
"I never was in Oglander Road”-I said "You were, and you had
goods there, and went away, and took the fixtures, and never paid any
rent"-he said "I never was there"-I had seen him there on several
occasions last year, and two or three years ago in & shop in Victoria
Road, Peckham, which was never opened-after Young's arrest I went to

you have made a mistake"-I said “Well, there is very little in names;
