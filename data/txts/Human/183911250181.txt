   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Have these shoes ever been through your shop? A.  Never. GEORGE WARD. I am foreman to Mr. Toby. These shoes are my master's—I prepared them for a foreign order—I cannot say how recently before the prisoner left they were on the premises—when we went to pack up the order, there was nine pairs short—they might have been made five or six weeks before, and were hanging up in the store-room on the first and second floors—the prisoner's sister worked there, and had access to those rooms. JOHN TOBY. These shoes are my manufacture, and were never sold to any one—they were made for the foreign order—I never sold the prisoner any.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who makes the shoes? A.  My men, not my self—it would be impossible for them to make a few pair over—they could not get the materials—I book all the work against them—they could not make any on their own account, I should know it—these have my private mark on them. MART ANN TOBY re-examined.  I know this apron to be mine—it was not an apron when I lost it, but I know the stuff, and the ribbon on it is mine—the thimble is my mother's—she used to work at the top of it, and I at the side—I know it well—two pair of boots and shoes were found in a basket under the bed, covered over with a frock which I knew to be the prisoner's.

 264. CAROLINE FRANCES PAGE was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of November , 33/4 yards of calico, value 2 s  .; 1 handkerchief, va lue 1 s  .; 1 veil, value 2 l  .; and 1 pair of boots, value 5 s  ., the goods of John Toby , her master.
   MR. CHAMBERS conducted the Prosecution. . MARY ANN TOBY. The prisoner worked for us—on Thursday, the 7th of November, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, as she was leaving, I told her I was sorry to put an unpleasant question to her—that I had lost a great many things, but never suspected her till the last time she was there at work, and then I missed several articles, which she must have taken away—she said she had not—I said, "I believe you have articles belonging to us about you now"—she said she had not—I called in a policeman, and found under her clothes, between two pet ticoats, drawn up with a string, 33/4 yards. of long cloth, which I missed from a trunk where she had been sitting at work, and a pocket handkerchief, which I had missed the last time she was at work, was in her pocket—I went with the policeman to search her premises afterwards, and all these other things were found there—in a drawer, of which she had the key, was found a veil, worth 2 l ., this piece of muslin, and this ribbon, which I had missed the last time she was at work.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did she say any thing when you found the long cloth? A.  No, before I found it, she said, if she had any thing, it did not belong to me—I had missed so many things, that I had marked the cloth—I have four pencil marks on it.
