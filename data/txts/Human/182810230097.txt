2162. ELIZA YATES was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 1 shawl, value 5s., the goods of Thomas Barker , from the person of Mary Barker.
 THOMAS BARKER. I am the father of Mary Barker. - On the 14th of August she went out with the child, who had a shawl on; she returned about three o'clock, without the shawl.
 MARY BARKER. I am nine years old. I was coming from my grandmother's, in Whitecross-street; I met a lady, and I met the prisoner at the corner of Banner-street - she asked if I knew the lady, and asked where I was going - I said, home to Old-street: she said, "Don't go up Whitecross-street - there is a mad horse tumbled down;" she took me up Banner-street, and wanted me to go to the field, then took me to the mad-house - she took me up Brick-lane, and said my mother wanted the shawl, but I would not let her have it till I got opposite our door; she then took it, ran over, and pretended to give it to my sister; I went in soon after, and found my mother had not sent for it.
   JOHN FORSYTH. I am a surgeon. On the 9th of October I met Mary Barker, who pointed the prisoner out, with three other girls; she said she was certain she was the girl who took her shawl - I asked the prisoner to go with me to the child's father; she said she had no objection, but went reluctantly.
 MARY BARKER re-examined. I know her by her talk, and her face - I was about an hour and a half with her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2163.  ROBERT ARCHER was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of September , at St. James, Clerkenwell , 1 coat, value 5l.: 1 pelisse, value 3l.; and 1 umbrella, value 8s., the property of John Plumb , in his dwelling-house; and that the said Robert Archer before the said felony was committed, that is to say, at the Delivery of the King's Gaol of Newgate holden for the county of Middlesex, at Justice Hall, in the Old Bailey, on the 3d of June, in the fifth year of the reign of our Lord the King, was convicted of Felony.
   LOUISA PLUMB. I am the wife of John Plumb; we live at No. 27, Chapel-street, in the parish of St. James, Clerkenwell - my husband is a jeweller. On the 24th of September, there was a knock at the door; I went, and saw the prisoner there - he had the appearance of a gentleman; he gave a note into my hand, which was not sealed; I asked who he brought it from - he said from Mr. Oxley, or some such name; I said "I don't think this note is for me" - he said "If you open it you will see;" I took it up stairs, but did not stop hardly a minute - I came down again, and missed a travelling pelisse, a box-coat, and an umbrella, from the passage - the door was wide open, and the prisoner gone; the value of the articles was at least 9l. - one of them was quite new and had not been worn above once or twice in the chaise; I was much alarmed - I went out, and a woman asked me if I had lost any thing, and I told her - I have never got them again.
 Prisoner. Q. What time was it? A. Between seven and eight o'clock in the evening - it was quite a moon-light night; I had not a light; I stated at the office that the person had light trousers, and a blue coat buttoned quite close.
 COURT. Q. Are you sure he is the man? A. Yes - I am quite positive I saw him at the door - I stood talking with him I suppose about five minutes, I had no light, but it was quite light - I could see to read at the door; it was a beautiful night - the 24th of September.
 Q. What did the woman say to you? A. She asked if I had lost a coat; I said I had two; I saw her at Hatton-garden, but she is not here; her name is Shanks - she is the prisoner's mother-in-law, as she stated; this is the note the prisoner brought.
   GEORGE WADDINGTON. I took the prisoner in charge.(Note read.)

 2164.  THOMAS EVANS and JAMES DEANE were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of September , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s., the goods of John Howard.
   JOHN BELSHAM. I am foreman to Mr. John Howard, a shoemaker, St. Martin's-court. On the 16th of September, the prisoner Evans was brought in with a pair of shoes - I took them from him; I have seen him about once or twice, but I did not see Deane that morning.
   WILLIAM BALCH. I am a hatter, and live in St. Martin's-court. On the 16th of September, between ten and eleven o'clockin the morning, I saw Deane take the shoes from Mr. Howard's door, and give them to Evans - I had frequently seen them lurking about the shop.
   JOHN GROOM. I am an officer. I took Deane about four hours after the other; I knew they were in the habit of going about together.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 EVANS -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 DEANE -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
