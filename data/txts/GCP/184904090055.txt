GEORGE KING Giles. I am foremart to Mr. Bourne, a hatter, who has
a shop in Regent-street, which I manage, and another in Lamb's-conduit.
street--the prisoner was the errand-boy and porter about six months
used to send the prisoner every evening to Mr. Bourne, with a statement of
the day's proceedings, the cash taken, and any memorandum I might have to
send he used to carry it in his right-hand trowsers' pocket-on the evening of
23rd Feb. his wages were due-about twelve o'clock in the day he asked me to
advance 1s. for bis dinner; I told him to ask the man in the shop, and he after-
wards told me he bad got it in the evening, at twenty-seven minutes after sit.
I sent him to Mr. Bourne with a bundle of goods and a small parcel, containing
a statement of the day's proceedings, five sovereigns, a 5l.-note, and two notes
addressed to Mr. Bourne-as he stood by me I placed a box on the counter,
and it swept the parcel on to the floor-it fell weighty, as if there were sote-
reigns in it-he heard it fall-I said, “George pick that up, it is the cast and
the day's statement"-he did so I did not see which pocket he put it in, it
was about the size of a card.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. When did he come to you? A.
Last June-I have sent him with goods and money every night during those
eight months--the five sovereigns were wrapped in the note, and the note in
another piece of paper, he did not come back for his wages--I have sent
good deal of money by him from time to time.
EDWARD Cook BOURNE. The prisoner came to me at the shop &
Lamb's-conduit-street at twenty minutes past seven, and laid a bag down
on the counter-he gave me this small piece of paper--(produced)-I said,
"Have you nothing else ?"-he said, “Yes, I have something else," he put
his hand in his back coat pocket, and before he bad time to search it he said,
“But I have lost it"-he said so immediately-I said, "What is it you have
lost ? was it money?" and he said, “No, I think there was no money; I think
it was cards"-I said, “Let me see the hole in your pocket"-he took off his
coat, this is it (produced)- I put my hand into the pocket, and it came out at
the bottom--the pocket has been rent open; it was a new rent-in every other
part the coat was perfectly sound-I said, "Do you mean to say you had a
hole in your pocket like this without knowing it?"-he said he knew he had
hole in his pocket, but he thought it would not fall through-I had never
before seen him take it from any pocket but his right trowsers' pocket, and I
said, "Let me see what you have got in your trowsers' pocket?"- I turned it out,
and it was empty- I looked at the left-hand one, and found 2s. 7d., a knife,
and a button.
GEORGE KING Giles re-examined. This paper is what I gave him on that
evening-it has my initials on it-I gave it bim loose.

a halfpenny, as he said he had not sufficient to get a loaf. Not GUILTY.
