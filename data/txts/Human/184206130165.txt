1890. WILLIAM SHOREY was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of March , 54lbs. weight of  lead-pipe, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 2 bill-hooks, value 2 s  .; 1 hammer, value 6 s  .; 2 bags, value 6 s  .; and 1/2 a pint of peas, value 6 d  .; the goods of John Copeland . THOMAS HICKMAN( police-constable D  204.) Between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, on the 28th of March, I was on duty at Maida-hill, Padding ton—I saw the prisoner with another man, each carrying a sack, which appeared to be weighty—they were making towards Lisson-grove—they saw me, and put the sacks down on a resting-place, and sat on them—I passed them on the opposite side—I saw each of them take up their sacks and go  on the other side—I turned back and watched them for five minutes—I gave an alarm, and they stopped at the corner of the building—they then ran away with their sacks—several persons pursued with me—they went to a hedge—they had their bags when they went to the hedge—when they came back from it, they had not got them—the bags were brought from the hedge before I got up to them, by the parties who pursued—they contained the leaden-pipe, a hammer, and a bill-hook—neither of them were taken at the time—both made their escape—the prisoner was taken by another officer—I am sure he is one of the men—I knew him before. EDWARD FORREST. I am bailiff to Mr. John Copeland, of Sudbury, near Harrow. On Sunday morning, the 27th of March, I went to the tool-house, and missed two bill-hooks, a hammer, two sacks, a quantity of lead, and some peas—I had put the bill-hooks and the hammer in there on the Saturday morning—I had put this piece of lead in the tool-house three days previous—it had a tap in it—I took the tap out, and have it here—this hammer and the bill-hooks are my master's—his dwelling-house was attempted to  be broken open the same night. GEORGE LACEY( police-constable D  283.) On Saturday night, the 26th of March, I was on duty in the Harrow-road, about two miles from Sud bury—I saw the prisoner and another man—I stopped them, and asked where they were going—they said, to a brother in Harrow—they went by me—I was returning about three o'clock in the morning of the 27th, and heard some talking—I stopped and listened—I saw the prisoner and the other man—I went up to them—the other man, who was sitting down. said, "It is a very cold night"—I said, "Yes, it is; have you been sitting here long?"—he said, "No, only just sat down to tie my shoe up"—he got up, and took up a canvas bag that he had with him—it contained peas—I allowed them to go  on.
   Prisoner.  I know nothing about it; I was at work on the Eastern Counties' Railway when this was lost. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Six Months.
