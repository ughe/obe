HEXY DRAPER, boot manufacturer, Milo End. I became a guar.
dan in 1904. I went on to the Finance Committee and attended re-
gularly and also the meetings of the Board. I attended on days
when contracts were given out, but not in 1904. I did not go on
to the Board until after the contracts had been issued. I was also
on the Infirmary Committee and noticed the coal heaps from time
to time. They comprised a quantity of dust and dirt, and complaints
were made about them. I several times had conversation about the
coal with the engineer. With regard to the complaint in November,
1904, I agree in the description that it was "rubbish." I understood
that Nixon's Navigation was the coal we were to have and I know it
w inserted in the contracts from time to time. It was at the meet
ing of April 20, 1905, that I first heard of the suggested arrange-
ment under which other coal was to be substituted for Nixon's Navi-
pation. A discussion arose upon the question. I wanted evidence
of the understanding or arrangement, but no document or minute
of resolution of the Board was ever produced. I was present at the
meeting on May 18 when the report of the Workhouse Visiting Com.
mittee, to whom the matter was referred, was discussed, and voted
for Mr. Kirwin's motion that Messrs. Cory be asked to supply Nixon's
Navigation direct. I remember that we were outvoted on that; it
w such a frequent occurrence. The objection made by certain
fuardians to Messrs. Cory supplying the coal direct was that if we
Ind it through a local man the local man would get the profit. I
objected to that as it would be at the expense of the ratepayers. I
