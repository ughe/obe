210. GEORGE BARNES, JAMES BROWN, and JAMES WIL-
LIAMS were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling
house of Charlotte Luton, on the 17th of October, at St. Mary, Lambeth,
with intent to steal, and stealing therein, 1 pair of decanters, value 11. ;
I set of fire-irons, value 58.; 1 hearth-rug, value 16; 1 table-cover,
value 108. ; 5 spoons, value 1l. ; 1 looking-glass and frame, value 21; 1
umbrella, value 6s.; 3 shawls, valae 31.; 1 pair of ear-rings, value 11.;
4 rings, value 31.; 1 opera-glass, value 58.; and oną card-case, value 2s,
6d.; the goods of the said Charlotte Luton : and MATILDA HARDIN
for feloniously receiving part of the same goods, well knowing them to
have been stolen ; against the Statute, &e.
CHARLOTTE LUTON. I am a widow, and live in Basing-place, Waterloo-
road, in the parish of Lambeth. On Monday night, the 16th of October,
I was the last person up- I went to bed at eleven o'clock-I fastened the
house up entirely- I was alarmed about half-past six o'clock in the morn-
ing by an opposite neighbour knocking at my street door-I came down
stairs, and missed the articles stated, from the parlours- I had seen them
all there the evening before-I found the street door wide open, and also
the staircase window-the persons must have entered by the staircase win-
dow, by getting over the side wall from the street-it is a sash-window-
cannot say whether it was fastened over-night, but it was shut there was no
appearance of violence on it-if it had been left unfastened a person outside
could have thrown it up-I have seen all the articles, and have no doubt
they are my property.
JANE MANN. My husband is an egg-merchant, and lives in Agnes-street,
Waterloo-road. On Monday morning, the 17th of October, at a quarter be-
fore six o'clock, I hear footsteps under my window-I live opposite Mrs.
Luton--the street is about twenty yards wide-I threw my window open,
and saw the prisoner Barnes-it was quite daylight-1 had never seen him
before-he removed from the window to the end of the street looking into
Waterloo-road, and a few minutes after I saw Brown open Mrs. Luton's
street door, and whistle-Barnes then crossed the road, and Brown came
out of the house with two bundles, one was in a red cloth--they looked
up at the window and saw me, and then Barnes ran up the street-Mrs.
Luton's staircase window was open, but the blind was down-Brown
returned into the house-I went down and awoke Mr. Hancock, my land-
lord, suspecting it was a robbery-I then returned to my window again,
and staid there till exactly half-past six o'clock, when I saw Brown and
Williams come out of the house I feel certain of Williams as well
Brown had a small bundle, and Williams a hearth-rug and fire-irons--they
left the door wide open, and I asked Mr. Hancock to knock at the door,
which he didMrs. Luton came down, and in half an hour she fetched me
over to her house, and complained of missing several things-I did not know
her before-l said before the Magistrate I felt certain that these two were
the prisoners, but could not swear to them I think Williams to be the
man I saw with the fire-irons.
