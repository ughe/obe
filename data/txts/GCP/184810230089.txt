ROBERT GEORGE THURSTON. I am warehouseman to James Houghton
and sons, of Bartholomew-close-the prisoner was in their employ-he left
in July, I believe-we keep empty oil-pipes in a yard in Whitecross-street-
the priosner would know that-I saw eight oil-casks at the station--they
were Messrs. Houghtons', and had been in that yard--the prisoner had no
right to remore them—there were eight of them--they were worth about
64s.
John GODFREY. I am a cooper, and have lived in Widegate-street,
Bishopsgate, for forty years. About 15th Sept. a person came into my shop,
in a hurried way, about three or four o'clock in the afternoon-be said he had
got some pipes to sell-I believe it was the prisoner, but cannot swear to him
-I said, "I cannot take them here, they must go to my place in Half Moon-
street" - he said, “I will draw them over"-I said, "Do"- I went in about
ten minutes, and they were out of the van-I said, “You have turned them
out of the van very quickly"-some one said yes, they wanted the van to
do another job-one of them was very inferior-I began to look them over,
expecting the man would be over there who had come to my shop-I then
saw the officer, who told me the pipes had been stolen from Messrs.
Houghtons'- I went to the station, and they locked me up-I had never
asked the price of the casks, nor had anything to do with them-I never
saw the prisoner before, though there had been many of Mr. Houghton's
carmen there to bring loads.
