JOHN FIELD. I am a veterinary surgeon, employed on the premises of William Reid and others, in Liquorpond-street—they had some nails of a particular sort—I lost some of them, to the value of about 40 s .—I beleive these produced are them—they correspond in every respect.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  What nails are they? A.  For horses shoes—I gave the description for the pattern of them—I have never seen any like them—I cannot say when I had last seen any of these—we have missed nails for the last two years; but I cannot say that all these left at one time. LEWIS DURANT. I am foreman to Mr. Branscomb, an ironmonger—we supply the prosecutors with these nails—they are made for no one but them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you not reason to believe that there are other nails of this sort made? A.  I do not think there are—they are drawn from under the head very differently to what the generality of nails are. HENRY REDMAN( police-constable G  140.) I stopped the prisoners with these nails in Goswell-street, on the evening of the 13th of October—Haley had them on his head—I asked Johnson if they belonged to him—he said they did, and that he bdught horse-parings—Haley said he worked for Johnson.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he not say he bought horse-parings all over London? A.  Yes, and that he picked the nails out of the horse-parings—I believe these nails have not been on a horse's foot. JOHN FIELD re-examined.  I believe people in shoeing horses, drop some nails on the ground—a poor man going about buying the horse-parings might get a few nails, but very few, and they would not be in this condition—a few of these are rusty, but not many.
   Haley's Defence.  I am in the employ of Johnson; we travel all over London; I have the sifting of the parings, and I put the new nails on one side, the old ones on the other, and take them to a farrier's shop and sell them. JOSEPH GINGER( police-constable G  137.) I produce a certificate of Haley's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person.
   JOHNSON  — GUILTY. Aged 36.— Confined Six Months .
   HALEY  — GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined One Year .
 3039. JAMES SMITH and THOMAS OWEN were indicted for stealing, on the   16th of Oct ., 1 coat, value 4 s  .; 1 pair of trowsers, 9 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 handkerchief, 6 d  .; the goods of John O'Reilly ; to which
   OWEN  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 37.— Confined Four Months . JOHN O'REILLY. I live in Orchard-street, Lisson-grove. Owen lived in the same room—I left on the morning of the 16th of Oct.—my things were all there—I returned about a quarter to twelve o'clock, and missed the articles stated—these are them—Smith did not lodge there. SAMUEL GREEN. I slept in the same room. I was going home at eight o'clock in the morning, on the 16th of Oct., and met the two prisoners going to the pawnbroker's—Smith had a bundle—he had lodged at the house, but not then. JOSEPH DONALDSON. I am in the employ of Mr. Haywood, a pawnbroker. I took in these articles of Smith, on the 16th.
   Smith's Defence.  When I met Owen I had not the least knowledge that he stole these things; I pawned them by his desire.
   SMITH  — NOT GUILTY.
