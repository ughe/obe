Q. Suppose these letters to have arrived at the office under a mistaken
impression that they should come to the Foreign Office, how should they
be conveyed to the Inland Office ? A. They would go through the tunnel,
according to the usual course of business; but if, instead of sending then
through the tunnel, the President comes and asks for money, I am bound
to give it to him it is not the proper practice for the President to come
himself for the money-he ought to send the letters by a messenger-Mr.
Barnes has done it-the messenger should take the letters to the bag clert
to receive the money, and the money and letters are forwarded immediately
to the Inland Office by the tunnel.
