WILIAM ARCHIBALD CULLEN FERRALL. I live at Canterbury and at
Barrow-in-Furness-on Sunday, 18th November, I got to Trafalgar
Square about 3 o'clock and left shortly after 5-I was standing on the
steps of Morley's Hotel, at the main entrance, at a few minutes past 4,
and saw a slight scrimmage on the pavement, by the cordon of police
which extended round the Square; it was caused by the mounted police
charging down the erowd at that part, and the scrimmage was very
nearly over-I saw the defendants after they were arrested and taken
inside the cordon-] did not see them advance to the police, I was look-
ing at other things in the Square, and if they had been accompanied by
a erowd or were leaving a erowd I should certainly have noticed them-
I was on the top of the steps, so that I was overlooking the corner-I
was struck by the respectability of the crowd it was certainly more
raspectable than I should have expected on the occasion of a London
meeting, and it certainly was not all that was most worthless of the
London lower classes, as the Times called it, very much the reverse-I
did not see confusion and disorder on any occasion, except when the
police charged the crowd-it did not appear to me that the demeanour of
the erowd changed after the defendants' arrest-there was a little dis-
turbanee, but I do not think the majority of the crowd were aware of the
arrest having taken place.
