The prisoner introduced the prosecutrix between eight and nine o'clock, and asked if I had got a bed for the night—I went down and got a light, and took them up to the room—he had been up about half an hour—he then came over to the opposite house to me about ten—he was out about an hour and a half or two hours—he came in again, and was up stairs five minutes, he came down, and asked if I would have something to drink—I said, "No"—he pulled me to go, and take something—I would not—he said it was all right, he was a club-mate of my father's—when I went up with him first I asked him to turn the key of the door in the morning as the land lord did not lodge in the house—he said he should not go till half-past eight—if the prosecutrix had come to the door I should have seen her, because I was opposite—I did not hear of the robbery till the morning, when she came and said, she had been robbed of 32 l . 15 s ., and asked if I should know the man—I said, "Yes, he told me he was a club-mate of my father's"—I gave my father the description of him in the morning, and he told me it was another man. JAMES FROST( policeman, L  59). I took the prisoner in bed the next night at Broadwall, four or five hundred yards from Mayhew's—while I was. searching, the prosecutrix came and wished to compromise the case. NOT GUILTY.

 2460. EDWARD JOHNSON , WILLIAM JONES, and ROBERT HANLON , stealing 1 pocket-book, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of James Saxby Jarvis . from his person; Johnson having been before convicted; to which
   JONES  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined Nine Months . JAMES SAXBY JARVIS. I live at Union-place, Lambeth. On 26th Sept., in the evening, I was in Black friars-road—as I turned the corner of Stam ford-street, I saw the prisoners larking  —a little farther on Johnson came before me—I pushed him on—he kept before me—I shoved  him aside once or twice—Hanlon was on my left—Johnson stopped short, got under my legs and tried to lift me up—I felt something touch my pocket and took hold of Jones's hand with my pocket-book in it—Hanlon was holding my pocket up—he ran away—I found him next morning at the station—I secured Jones, two gentlemen stopped Johnson—his head was tied up then, and I know him well. AMBROSE FURMINGO( policeman, L  162). I was on duty, and saw Jones and Johnson together about an hour before the robbery—I took them from Mr. Jarvis. WILLIAM POUND. I am a shoemaker of Duke-street, Westminster. I was a witness against Johnson by the name of Benjamin Lawson, and produce a certificate of his conviction—( read—Convicted September , 1847, and confined three months )—he is the person.
   JOHNSON  — GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined Nine Months .
   HANLON  — GUILTY, and received a good character .—Aged 16.— Confined Fourteen Days.
