RICHARD RICHARDSON. I am a surveyor, at St. Paul's Lodge, St. Paul's
Road, Camden Town--on 19th July, about 1.30 a.m., while going up High
Street, Bloomsbury, near St. Giles's Church, I felt something like the report
of a pistol in my right ear, but I do not know what it was, and I fell to
the ground-I saw no one-I then saw two men unbuttoning my coat-
have a slight recollection of Riley, but cannot swear to him-I had 51. 104
in my trousers pocket, but missed nothing but an ivory two-foot rule-I
was five or six minutes on the ground-a cabman came up and called
“Stop thief !”-I think the cab had a grey horse.
Cross-exumined by Mr. FULTON. I had been to 21 and 22, St. Martin's
Lane, to make a survey-it was an important matter, and occupied me till
12.55—I saw the rule last while using it there, and have not been there to
inquire for it as I am positive I put it in my pocket-it has not been found
-I had my spectacles on and my rule in my hand, and I put them in my
pocket together after I left St. Martin's Lane.
Cross-examined by MR. PURCELL The sergeant asked me at the police-
station if I had lost anything- I took off my coat and said " Oh, I have not
got my two-foot rule."
JAMES IRVING. I am a leather dresser, at 10, New Street, Cloth Fair-
at about 1.30 a.m. on 19th July, while going down Bloomsbury Street, I
saw the prosecutor lying in the middle of the road with the two prisoners
on top of him the cabman drove up, and the prosecutor called “Cabby,
stop !" and the prisoners got up and ran round several turnings and then
into Bloomsbury Street again I followed all the way, and did not lose
sight of them till the policeman came and I caught hold of Barry.
Cross-examined by MR. FULTON. Several other people, about six, were
running behind the prisoners—there were four or five people about while
the prosecutor was on the ground.
Cross-examined by MR. PURCELL I was coming in the opposite direction
to the cab.
GEORGE MATTHEWS (Policeman E 139). I heard cries of “Stop thief !"
and saw the prisoners running down Denmark Street, and when they saw
me following them they turned up Arthur Street I never lost sight of
them till they were taken- I took Barry.
Cross-examined by Mr. FULTON. Three or four other men were running
after them-I did not find a two-foot rule upon them-I found 38. 6d., 3ch
in bronze, a knife and key.
JOHN FUDGE (Policeman E 116). I saw five persons running after the
prisoners, and heard a cry of “Stop thief!"-I ran after Barry; Irving
caught hold of him, and we took him to Bow Street-I did not lose sight of
him or follow him round any corners.
