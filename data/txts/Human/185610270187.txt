ANN PRITCHARD , burglary in the dwelling house of Elizabeth Skinner , and stealing therein 4 table cloths, 1 sheet, and other articles; her property: and JOHN PRITCHARD , feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen. ELIZABETH SKINNER. I am a widow, and take in mangling, at No. 4, Bridge-road, Battersea, I rent a house, and have some lodgers; Mrs. Alcroft occupies the back parlour adjoining the room where I mangle—I the mangling room fronts the street—the room is entered by a door on the left of the passage from the street door—there is a string through the street door from the latch, so that it can be opened from the outside—on Wed nesday evening, the 15th of this month, I left home a little after 8 o'clock—I locked the mangling room door, and took the key with me—I returned a little before 11 o'clock—I found the street door open—it appeared as though it bad given way to a sudden jerk—I found a night dress under my feet the minute I opened the door—I picked it up, and then looked to the basket, which I left full, and found nearly all the things missing—there were six chemises, four table cloths, a lot of children's things, a night dress, and several other articles—I know the female prisoner—she had been in my mangling room that afternoon—she is not a friend of mine—she came in to know whether a next door neighbour was there; and then the party sitting there asked her to drink out of the pot she was drinking out of-these ( produced ) are a portion of the articles I missed—I know the male prisoner by sight—he and his wife live near me.
   Ann Pritchard.  I followed her to the public house, and cautioned her about leaving the door open. Witness.  About half past 1 o'clock she came in where I was at the beer shop—she said, "Mrs. Skinner, I have been at your house, your door is open, and you will lose your linen—I then went back, and found both the doors were open. CHARLES SIMPKIN. ( policeman , 120.)
