I was passing the skittle-ground, and heard a scuffle - I went in, and took up a coat and hat, which I put on a beer-cask; there was a quarrel among the prisoner and others - Barnes was intoxicated, and was scuffling; clothes and hats laid about in all directions - there might be fifteen or twenty people there, and a great disturbance.

 Q.Did you not know he was convicted here in January last? A. I certainly did.

 1825.  CAROLINE DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of August , 1 watch, value 2l.; 2 sovereigns, and 14 shillings, the property of Henry Carr , from his person.
   HENRY CARR. I am mate of the ship Martha, which lays at Limehouse. On the night of the 23rd of August I went with a girl named Allen, to a house in Union-street ; I had two sovereigns in my left-hand pocket, 14s. 6d. in my right, and a watch in my fob; I did not take off my clothes, as I had to go on board early - I laid on the bed, and went to sleep; I told the prisoner to go away, I did not want her - the Policeman awoke me about half-past two o'clock.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not give me the watch and money to take care of? A. I did not - they were in my pockets when I fell asleep; I did not go to her lodgings at all.
   WILLIAM WHEELER. I am a Policeman. I went in search of the prisoner, and found her at a house in Leming-street, Shakespear's-walk; she said if I would go down the yard she would give me the two sovereigns and watch, if I would keep the other girls from coming down - I went, and she gave me them in a piece of flannel; she took them from the privy - I said, "Are these what you took from the man?" she said Yes.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the prosecutor had gone to her lodging with her, and given her the property to take care of.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1826.  WILLIAM GARDNER was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 1 half-crown, and 1 shilling, the monies of John Peters, his master.
   JOHN PETERS. I live in Warner-place, Hackney-road, and am a braid-manufacturer. The prisoner had been two or three times in my employ - on the 1st of July I sent him to Bethnal-green-road, with a half-crown and a shilling, to match some worsted; I expected him to return, but I did not see him till the next morning, when his father brought him.
   GEORGE TEAKLE. I am a Police-officer. I received the prisoner in charge - I asked what he had done with the money, and he said he had spent it.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1727.  WILLIAM GLEESON was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 1 watch, value 5l.; 1 hat, value 10s.; 1 handkerchief, value 2s.; 5 half-crowns, 4 shillings, and 3 sixpences, the property of George Horn.
   GEORGE HORN. On the morning of the 14th of July, before day-break, I changed a sovereign at a confectioner's shop in the Strand; I received chiefly half-crowns in change, and some shillings and sixpences - I was rather the worse for liquor; a man spoke to me, and followed me from the Strand to Waterloo-bridge, where I sat down, and fell asleep; I had then a hat, a handkerchief, a watch, and my money - I was awoke by the Policeman, and missed them.
   JOHN CRISPIN RAWLEY. I am a Police-officer. On the morning of the 14th of July, I was on duty in the Strand, about two o'clock, and saw the prosecutor and the prisoner - the prosecutor seemed to wish to get rid of him- I told the prisoner I did not believe he had any knowledge of the prosecutor; he said he had known him for four or five years; I soon afterwards saw the prosecutor asleep on the bridge - he had no hat on - I awoke him, and he complained of his loss; I took the prisoner about three o'clock the same morning - I took from him this hat, five half-crowns, three sixpences, and two shillings, but no watch.
   GEORGE HORN. These are my property.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. You were in liquor? A. Yes; I had been to the Theatre, and might have been in two or three houses - I know my hat was not changed at any of these houses.
   JOHN RATTRAY. I am an officer. I saw the prosecutor and prisoner walking towards Waterloo-bridge - one of them had his handkerchief hanging out, and I told him of it; I saw them turn, and both fall down - I assisted to help the prosecutor up - he then walked towards Waterloobridge; he appeared to have been drinking - the prisoner said, "He is an acquaintance of mine," and walked after him.
 Cross-examined. Q. When they fell down were their hats off? A. Yes, and they might be changed at that time.
 J. C. RAWLEY. The prisoner had only this one hat when I took him - I recollect seeing their hats fall off, and I particularly noticed the prisoner's hat; he put his own hat on his head, not this one - his hat was a very indifferent one.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is not this such a hat as the little boys in the street follow, crying "Shocking had hat?" A. I should think not - I went to the hatter, and found the prosecutor by that means; I took the prisoner opposite Wellington-street.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am innocent. I was very much intoxicated, and cannot say whether that was the gentleman I was with or not.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Life.
