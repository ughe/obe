885. MICHAEL DRUITT was indicted for that he, on the 20th day of April last, feloniously and falsly did make, forge, and counterfeit, and cause and procure to be falsly made, forged, and counterfeited, and willingly aid, and assist in the false making, forging, and counterfeiting a certain order for payment of money, dated 18th April, 1785, with the name J. Wood thereto subscribed, purporting to be signed by Joseph Wood , directed to Thomas Hankey , Joseph Chaplin Hankey , Stephen Hall, Robert Hankey , and Richard Hankey , Esquires, by the name and description of Messrs. Hankey and Co. for the payment of 17 l. 12 s. 6 d. and which said false, forged, and counterfeited order for the payment of money, was in the words and figures following, that is to say,
 "Messers. Hankey and Co. pay Mr. Barnes or bearer 17 l. 12 s. 6 d. J. Wood, April, 13th 1785." with intention to defraud the said  Thomas Hankey, Joseph Chaplin Hankey, Stephen Hall, Robert Hankey and Richard Hankey.
 A second Count, for uttering the same, with the like intention.
 A third and fourth Counts, The same as the first and the second, with intention to defraud  Joseph Wood.
 (The witnesses examined apart at the request of Mr. Garrow and Mr. James, Prisoner's Counsel.
 (The Case was opened by Mr. Fielding as follows.)
 Gentlemen of the Jury, your attention is now to be directed to the investigation of a charge, which as it may be of the last consequence to the unhappy young man at the bar, so is the punishment of the crime imputed to him, of the utmost importance to this town: I shall first suggest to you, the parties in this transaction, and the manner in which the prisoner was apprehended. Mr.  Joseph Wood, whose draught has been forged, was in habits of intimacy with the brother of the prisoner, during their intercourse Mr. Wood had frequently supplied him with sums of money, and those supplies were by way of draught, whether the prisoner took opportunity from these draughts to commit the crime in question, I know not: although there are several more indictments here remaining against the prisoner, I do not think myself at liberty to go into any of the facts concerning them at present, but I shall confine myself to the present transaction. On the 20th of April last, a Mr. Tait, who is a clerk of Mr. Hankey's, was in the office for the purpose of paying the draughts that were brought, and then it was that the draught, the object of the present enquiry, was brought by the prisoner; Mr. Tait, upon looking at the draught, had some suspicion, he thought there was an evident dissimilarity in the writing, he went to a drawer where there were two other draughts which we say are forgeries, and which had been presented a little time before, but on comparing this note with those, and their appearing alike, he was induced to pay it; however, the circumstance itself caused him to suspect the prisoner, and made him take a more than ordinary notice of him: Gentlemen, undoubtedly the evidence of identity will be always received with considerable caution: probable it is, very probable, that persons may be deceived with respect to likeness, and with respect to recollection; but where there are circumstances that aid the memory, and induce at the moment particular observation, people in cases like this, may be enabled to speak with as much certainty as any human creature can do from memory; and you well know, many of the transactions in life require we should rely on memory, for seldom do we come to demonstration; if Mr. Tait therefore speaks certainly to the man, there will remain nothing but for you to go into the other circumstances of the forgery, which I shall then proceed to lay before you, and if they are proved to your satisfaction, it will then become your duty to find the prisoner guilty of the charge.
