 MR. BODKIN to MR. SOFTLEY. Q. Did you say to Mr. Haines that it would be a great injury if you did not convict this man? A. No; I said it would be a great injury if I did not appear against him; he wrote to me, wanting me to make a flaw in the indictment.
 COURT. Q. Did you say, that in consequence of some letter written by somebody? A. Yes, because they wanted me not to appear; Mr. Haines came to my place, and began a conversation on the subject of this prosecution - the letter came by post from the prisoner.
 MR. BODKIN to MR. HAINES. Q. Do you remember going to the prosecutor's? A. Yes, and he said to me, that it would be a great injury, if he did not succeed in convicting the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. Are you sure those were the words he used? A. Yes; I went to him and he said, it was his duty to prosecute, but in consequence of his family, I hoped he would be as lenient as he could; I was very cautious in my expression - this was about ten days ago.
     GUILTY. Aged 41. Confined Six Months.
 1943.  MARY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of July , 2 sheets, value 10s.; 1 pair of boots, value 2s.; the goods of Richard Marks - 1 pair of stockings, value 1s.; and 1 handkerchief, value 1s.; the goods of Elizabeth Moorm.
 RICHARD MARKS. I live in Old-street-road; the prisoner was my  servant- I lost two sheets, and a pair of boots, on the 7th of July; my niece, Elizabeth Moorm, brought down the prisoner's bag to me, and I found a number of duplicates in it.
 ELIZABETH MOORM. I lost a pair of stockings and a handkerchief, on the 7th of July; I found the stockings in the prisoner's box in her room, and my handkerchief under her bed - the duplicates were in a blue bag in her box.
   JOHN BURGESS. I am a pawnbroker; I have a sheet pawned on the 10th of June; a young man, who is not now in my service, took it in, and gave this duplicate.
   LEONARD CLARE MATTHEWS. I am a pawnbroker; I have a pair of boots pawned at my shop, but I did not take them in - this is the duplicate.
   THOMAS WATERS. I am an officer; I was sent for, and took the prisoner - I found in her box a sheet cut up in pieces, and these stockings; here is a mark on the sheet - I found those duplicates also.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Six Months.
 1944.  ROBERT DURANT was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 2 sovereigns, the monies of Charles Hogg , from his person.
   JAMES TAYLOR. I am a watchman. On the 9th of July. I was called at a quarter before ten o'clock to take the prisoner at a public-house in Ratcliff-highway; I asked if he had any silver, he said, Yes - I asked if he had any gold, he said, No; I took him to the watch-house, and found two sovereigns on him - a man named Hogg gave charge of him, but I do not know his Christian name; he said, he had lost two sovereigns, and he swore to the piece of paper they were in - he is not here.
   GEORGE DEVERELI. I am an officer. I took the sovereigns from the prisoner in the piece of paper - the prosecutor said he had been out all day, and the prisoner took them from him; the prisoner said they were his own, and he had received them from his master by the Dog and Duck.
 Prisoner's Defence. I came from Sunderland with one sovereign and eight shillings in my pocket, which was to pay the ship; instead of which, I worked my passage; the prosecutor came to my house at Deptford, and asked me to go out drinking with him; we went to several public-houses and drank, and then came to Ratcliff-highway - we went to a public-house, and I went to sleep, and so did Hogg - I was taken sick, and went out and came in again, and laid my head down to go to sleep; when I awoke, my hat was gone, and a handkerchief in it - Hogg then awoke up, and said he had lost two sovereigns; I said, "Charles, I know nothing about it."
     NOT GUILTY.
 1945.  RICHARD DAVIES was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , 3lbs. weight of cheese, value 1s. 6d.; 4lbs. weight of ham, value 2s.; 1 tub, value 6d.; and 4 cloths, value 1s.; the goods of John Green Winter and Robert Winter , to whom he was servant.
 JOHN GREEN WINTER. I am in partnership with my brother Robert: we are  cheesemongers- the prisoner was in our employ; on the 8th of September I sent him behind the counter while I wrote a letter - during that time the till was robbed; when the prisoner came the next day, I accused him of this felony - he was given in charge, and I went to his house, where I found a butter firkin, and four butter cloths, which are my property, and some flats which are not mine.
   MARGARET HEWSON. I am servant to Mr. Winter. On the 15th of August, I was coming down stairs - I looked into the warehouse, and saw the prisoner put some ham and a Dutch cheese in a flat; he brought down the flat, and put it into the cart, and took it to the stable - I came down and asked the prisoner if the meat was bad; he said, "What do you mean" - I said, "Never mind," and I stood there till he took it off
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you a fellow-servant named Betsey? A. Yes, she had a sweetheart, but he could not take the prisoner's place - he is not allowed to come to the house; I saw this on the 15th of August - I did not go and tell my master instantly; I did not mention it till the money was gone from the till - some weeks had passed - my master was at home at the time.
   DANIEL DUTCH. I am an officer. I went with Mr. Winter to the prisoner's lodging, on the 9th of September- I found this butter tub and four cloths; I found no ham or cheese.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you take the poor man into custody? A. No, he was at the watch-house; I did not hear his wife say she had taken away some dirty things in the tub.
