ROBERT GREGORY. I am an emigrant, about to sail in the Sea King for
Australia ; she is now lying in the London Docks." On Thursday, 1st May,
I saw Marshall on board the Sea King-he introduced himself to me as a
gentleman going out by the same ship, and said that he had some machinery
on board-after talking with him on board for some time about the Colonies
and other matters, he got ine to walk off with him as far as the Spread
Eagle, in Gracechurch-street-from thence he proposed going for a stroll to
see the sights of London, and he took me to a public house near West-
minster Bridge, and treated me with a glass of ale and a biscuit while we
were there, Marshall came in, he joined me and Swilson-hº said that he
had just come from America in consequence of an uncle having left him a
great deal of money-he pulled out his money, and showed several notes
and several sovereigns, and seemed greatly rejoiced-he said that he would
treat us with a glass each to drink his health, which he did this was about
3 o'clock in the afternoon Swilson then proposed that we should do to
Cremorne Gardens we all three left the public house together, and walked
about different streets _I am not much acquainted with London--we after-
Wards took a boat we then went to another public house near Cremorne
Gardens. I know it was near Oremorne Gardens, I had beer there
while we were in that public house Swilson gave me a small bag, and
also Marshall, and he also said he would give us an American dollar each,
to keep for his sake__there was nothing in the bag that he gave me
I had at that time in my possession a bag containing about fifty sove-
reigns, among it there were two old guineas there had been 501, sovereigns
and half sovereigns, I had changed one half sovereigniamy bag was within
another bag, about the same size and colour as this one produced
I took my bag out of my pocket, and put it on the table before Swilson-
Swilson took it up, and also this bag, and said he would put it in for me
with the American dollar-he appeared to do so, bag and all, tied it up, and
returned it to me, and I put it into my pocket, thinking that I had got my
money right-shortly after we all three left the house together when we
had got a little way Swilson said, “ I have forgot my pocket handkerchief,
which can run the fastest pr_the other hesitated, and I turned back and
ran to see if there was any pocket handkerchief left behind, but there was
no pocket handkerchief - then returned to the spot where I had left the
prisoners, and they were both gone-I did not look at my money then it
roused my guspicions, and as soon as I pulled it out, I found my money was
gone. I cannot tell at what time that was, it was at some time in that
evening-I believe it was as I was going to pay for my breakfast it must
have been next morning, therefore I slept at some public house at Ken-
sington that night I went to see a cousin, and then to the public house
where I slept--next morning, when I was going to pay for my breakfast,
I took out my bag and opened it, and none of my sovereigns were there,
por the two guineas, nor the American dollar, but & quantity of farthings, I
think more than fifty : part of them are here, the other part I had to pay
for my breakfast with, as I had no more money in my pocket I then
gave information to the police I had never seen the prisoners before
I am perfectly satisfied they are the men.
