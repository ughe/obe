832. FREDERICK CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the I0th of
January, 214lbs. weight of coals, value 28., the goods of Charles Hoppe
and another, in a barge on the navigable river Thames.
HENRY JOHN KINg (Thames police-constable, No. 30.) On the morn-
ing of the 10th of January I saw the prisoner in a boat alongside the Bull
coal-barge, belonging to Mr. Hoppe, which was lying in the Thames by
the Surrey Canal-I saw him take both his arms and sweep a quantity of
coals from the bulk of the barge, into his boat--he then rowed away some
distance down the river, then rowed back again, and threw his drag over-
boatd-I rowed after him, took him into custody, and found the coals in
his boat-he said he had dragged them up-there were 214lbs. weight.
Prisoner. I got the coals by the Surrey-canal, where there were plenty
of men by. I did not take them out of the barge. Witness. I saw him
take them.
JOsEPH PARRY. I am lighterman to Mr. Charles Hoppe and another.
The officer told me of this-I went to the barge, and perceived that a few
coals had been taken out-those produced by the officer correspond with
the coals in the barge.
GUILT'Y. Aged 18.-Confined Three Months.
Before Mr. Baron Alderson.
