CATHERINE CLIFTON. I was eleven years old the first of last month-
lived in the same house with the prisoners, 6, Little Pearl-street-they have
a little boy, named Billy-I am out at service, but I was at home when the
little baby died-I believe it was on a Monday-I saw it alive that day,
between 4 and 5 in the afternoon-I was in the street, and Billy called me
mp; he said, "Kitty, come up and see the baby "-I went up and saw it;
it was covered over with the quilt, and I did not see it at first I said,
Billy, where is the baby," and le pointed to the bed I lifted the clothes
up, and took the baby up; it was alive, it cried and frothed at the mouth,
and I laid it down again, and went down and told my mother, she came up,
and then my father came up, and went and brought a doctor_I did not see
anything more-I went down-Mr. Rawley is my stepfather,
MARY RAWLEY. I live at 6, Little Pearl-street--the prisoners have lived
there for about eight or vine months—they bad one room on the first floor
--the man is a shoemaker–I don't know that the female does anything-I
remember the baby being born-Mr. Staek came to me that morning, and
asked me to come up, and I was up there for about half an hour-I saw
the baby born, and covered it over till the nurse came- I did not go up
again afterwards, until the day it was reported dead that was on Monday,
28th August-my little girl Catherine came down, and said, " Baby ain't
derd mother " I went up to look and-found it alive asked my husband
to come up, and he went for the doctor—the child looked very weak, but
was alive--while my husband was gone for the doutor, Mrs. Stack came to
my window-I said, “Mrs. Stack, the baby ain't dead "she rather took
angty, and I went indoors--I did not understand what she said she does
not speak very plain, but she snapped at me like she had bad something
to drink-she was not very drunk --I am always at work-I have seen Mr.
Stack pass in and out many times, but I have not met Mrs. Stack more
than a time or two-she was sober then.
Cross-examined. Q. How many rooms are there in this house 4. About
sixteen- in some rooms there are more than one family living--the house
was rather in a crowded condition at that time I could not say how many
persons were living there; there was a very large number-I heard that
there were a few cases of fever round the corner; it is rather an udhealthy
neighbourhood; the people in the house all seemed healthy I think it WAS
