   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  This was on Friday evening? A.  Yes—the prisoners were in Stamford-street when I first saw them. CHARLES BURGESS GOFF( police-constable L  31.) On Friday night I was in the York-road, and saw the two prisoners walking together—I saw Hart run away, and I went in pursuit of him—I ran twenty or thirty yards, and secured him—I found nothing on him—I asked him where be brought the soap from—he said, "From home"—I asked where he lived—he said, close by, and he was going to take the soap to that man's (point ing to Killick)—I afterwards went to the address that Hart gave, at No. 5, Angel-place—he and his mother have one room there—I found two pieces of soap in the cupboard. JOHN MEAGER. I am clerk to Benjamin, Thomas, and William Hawes. The prisoners were employed by them as carmen, and when they were not employed in delivering soap, they were engaged on the premises—they leave at seven o'clock in the evening—Killick was carrying out mottled soap on the 1st of April, and on the 2nd they had both been carrying out yellow soap—this mottled soap is my employers', and this yellow also—there is a little imperfection in the machine which cuts it, and one side is thicker than the other—that is the case with these outside bars—Hart was employed occasionally in the warehouse as a packer—here is about 28lbs. of soap, worth about 13 s .
   Cross-examined. Q.  About how much soap in the course of a year is cut by this machine, which is imperfect? A.  Several hundred tons—this machine is always used, and all the soap sold by us has the same mark—the mottled soap sent out by Killick on the Thursday had the same imper fection—he had two small parcels to deliver that day, one at Lambeth and one at the London Hospital—about 1 3/4 cwt. in all—neither of these cus tomers are here to-day—we have had Hart for several years—I believe his father was there for thirty years—the gate-keeper is there till eight o'clock in the evening.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Is there any other circumstance except the out side bars that you can speak to? A.  The mottled soap was not a good boiling—the mottle did not come out well, and the mottle in this soap is not out so well as we could wish—there was soap of this boiling in the warehouse. WILLIAM WATSON. I am groom to one of the Messrs. Hawes. I know this Macintosh—I have seen Killick wear it—he told me he made it himself. JOHN LYNCH. I am gate-keeper at Messrs. Hawes.
