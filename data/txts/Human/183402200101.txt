 490.  JOSEPH MILLARD was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of January , 6lbs. of copper, value 4s. 6d., the goods of John Seaward and others, his masters; and JOHN HOPSON was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen, against the Statute; to which Millard pleaded
 GUILTY. Aged 17. - Recommended to mercy by the prosecutor. - Confined Five Days.
 MESSRS. CLARKSON and PARKER conducted the Prosecution.
   HENRY SCOBEL. I am a foreman of the engineers to Mr. John Seaward and Co. - on Saturday, the 4th of January, I ordered two quarters and eight pounds of copper bolts to be sent to the smiths' forge were Millard worked, to be cut up for melting, and to be returned to the brass shop - I was engaged in my vice shop, turning - about an hour afterwards I saw Millard near the brass shop, with a piece of copper bolt in his hand, which he appeared to be playing with - I followed him to the edge of the wharf, and saw him endeavouring to put something under his apron - I went to the brass shop and weighed the metal - I missed six pounds of copper bolt - I saw Millard leave the premises at one o'clock to go to dinner - I followed him to Salmon-lane, and went into a new beer-shop, opposite Hopson's premises - I saw Millard pass his premises, and then return back - Hopson has two shops, one is a harness shop, and one an iron shop - Hopson was standing at the door of the harness shop - Millard passed him and went into the iron shop - I afterwards went into Hopson's.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How soon did you go? A. I should think in about five minutes - the policeman went in before me, and afterwards beckoned me over - I saw some copper bolt, which the policeman said Hopson took from the scale - when Hopson was told he must go to the station-house he said, "Let us go out at the back door, at all events."
   JAMES CLARK(police-constable).
