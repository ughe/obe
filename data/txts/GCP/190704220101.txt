RALPH RAPHAEL, recalled. On referring to my call book I find I
was in error in stating that I did not see Simeon Sydney Jacobs on
March 4. He did call. (To Sir E. Carson.) He did not say that
Abel had said he was not prepared to pay anything further or * Why
should I pay my brother's creditors I have lost enough already." I
did not say, "Then a prospect of a settlement is hopeless." No such
conversation took place at all. Jacobs said, "I have not been to see
you because I found that what took place between us in confidence
and without prejudice is trying to be used by the Myerses and I
decline to be a party to it." I perfeotly remember that. I have no note
of it. I did not send my clerk Clayton to ask Jacobs to call on me.
Re-examined. At none of the three interviews I had with Jacobs did
the conversation suggested take place.
Rose MAX SDMONDS, clerk to George Butler, 34, The Areh, Great
Suffolk Street, furniture manufacturer. Jopeph Myers was our cus-
tomer, and I produce copy of his account from October 5, 1904, to
July 28, 1905. His indebtedness at the time of his bankruptcy was
£56 8s, 6d., which is unpaid. Three items are for goods supplied to 34,
High Street, Clapham. I produce post-dated cheque of August 21,
1905, for £50, by Joseph, which was dishonoured.
Cross-examined by Sir E. Carson. After April 14 no goods were
supplied to Camden Town in the name of Joseph. (To Mr. Adler.)
The last supply to Clapham was July 28, 1905. (To the Recorder.)
The last three items of the account were supplied to Clapham; all the
rest was to Camden Town.
WILLIAX JOHN PILOHER, manager to Jones Brothers, Oakweed Court.
Kensington. My firm are owners of 61, St. John's Road, Clapham
Junction. On August 11, 1904, lease produced was granted to Joseph
Myers for 21 years at £275. It contains a covenant against sub-let-
ting without the consent in writing of the lessor. David wrote on
August 21, 1905. asking us to transfer the lease to him but that has
| not been done. The letter from David encloses chegue for £72 for a
quarter's rent due in June, and states: "As the lease of 61, St. John's
Road is made out in my brother's name, and as I have always found
the rent, rates, and taxes, and as I have purchased the above pre
mises I would be glad if you would consent to having the lease trans-
ferred to me, as all the firms recognise that the shop is mine." It is
headed “Camden Town Furnishing Company." Receipts were sent to
Joseph Myers. I do not know whether David signed the cheques.
(To the Recorder.) On letting the premises Joseph appeared to be
a man of substance, and his references were satisfactory.
