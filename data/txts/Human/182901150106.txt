 519.  WILLIAM JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of January , 1 jacket, value 30s.; 1 pair of trouseres, value 15s.; 1 waistcoat, value 10s., and 1 shirt, value 8s., the goods of George Downie.
   GEORGE DOWNIE. I am a sailor, and live with Mr. Briant. On the 8th of January I lost these clothes from a chest by my bedside; I put them there at night, and in the morning, when I awoke, they were all gone - I never saw the prisoner; I was asleep before he came in, and when he went out.
   GEORGE BRIANT. I keep the Boy and Helmet public-house, in East Smithfield; Downie lodged with me for about three weeks - on the 8th he went to bed, and the watchman brought the prisoner in quite late, and asked if I could give him a lodging; I led him up to bed - he asked what there was to pay; I said, "You are a poor man, 6d."I went into Downie's room to get a candle: told him to put it out, and bid him good night; the next morning he got up early, knocked at my door, and asked me to let him out - I got up in the dark and let him out: about seven o'clock the prosecutor came down, and said he had lost his clothes - the prisoner was afterwards found on boards a ship, but none of the property on him; no other persons slept in the house.
 Prisoner. I went into the room you shewed me; I put on my own clothes in the morning - I had no bundle or any thing with me. Witness. I let him out with the key, which I always take with me; I did not see him with any bundle, or that he looked more bulky than the night before.
   ROBERT HENRY BURGESS. I took the prisoner. I have two old shirts and a pair of old trousers, which were found in the room which the prisoner slept in that night - a key and a handkerchief were found in the prisoner's bed, which had been in the prosecutor's jacket pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. He took me on board a ship, just as I was coming on board; I never saw these things in my life, which are said to have been found in my bed.
 GEORGE BRIANT. No one else slept in the house that night; they might possibly have been there before.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. Transported for Seven Years.
 520.  JOHN OSBORN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of January , 1 cap, value 3s. the goods of Joel Hotchkiss.
 THOMAS WISEMAN. I keep the  Prince Regent public-house, in Ratcliff-highway; the prosecutor Joel Hotchkiss, who is now gone to sea, and another man, slept in a doublebedded-room, on the 9th of January, in my house; the prosecutor came down and said he had lost his cap - I told him it was not possible, as no one had left the house, and I told him to go and look under the clothes; he did so, but could not find it - on the Saturday following I went to the Anchor and Hope public-house, and found the prisoner there intoxicated, and to my great surprise he had this cap on his head, which I knew to be the prosecutor's, as I had had it in my possession before, and I knew it by this mark.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not 1 lose a pair of trousers that night? A. You said you did, and your waistcoat; I went and got you a pair.
 JAMES FOGG. I took the prisoner, and produce the cap.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought the cap of a man that came from St. Mary-axe, and the publican took it off my head, because he thought I would not pay him a little money.
     GUILTY. Aged 11. Confined Six Weeks.
 521.  JOHN SMITH and JAMES HILBERT were indicted for stealing, on the 11th of January , 4 carriage glasses, value 4l., the goods of Watkin William Martin.
 WILLIAM SUMMERFIELD. I am a watchman of Mary-le-bone. On the 11th of January, at a quarter before seven o'clock in the morning, I was in  Adam-street, Westminster- I watched the premises of Mr. Martin, a stable-keeper, in consequence of information, and saw the two prisoners cross Upper Berkley-street, which is two or three hundred yards from Mr. Martin's - they were in company, and coming towards me; I am positive they are the two men: Smith was carrying something - I went and clapped my hand upon his shoulder, and said,
 "You are the very fellow I want;" I took these eight glasses from under his arm - Hilbert immediately decamped; I called for assistance and gave Smith into custody - Hilbert ran into Upper Seymour-street; I ran and took him as he was stooping as if going to tie his shoe - I am confident he was with Smith.
   WILLIAM GOROON. I am a gas-lighter. On Sunday morning, the 11th of January, at a quarter before seven o'clock, I saw the two prisoners in the news and gave information to the watchman - Smith asked me what o'clock it was.
 WATKINS WILLIAM MARTIN. I am a liverystablekeeper - my stable is in the mews; these four glasses are my property - they were in post-chaises which were standing in the mews; I have no doubt they are mine - one is broken and the piece of it is left behind; they would cost me about 8l.
 SMITH'S Defence. I was passing by the mews, and saw two young men pass with these glasses under their arms- they went to a watch-box, returned and placed them against the side of the mews, and when the watchman came by, they went to a watch-box, returned and placed them against the side of the mews, and when the watchman came by, they decamped; I staid till be passed, and then I must own I was going to take away the glasses.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 23.
 HILBERT -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
