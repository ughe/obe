BEFORE JUDGE RENTOUL
(Wednesday, Mareh 20.)
GRAINGE, Thomas Walter, who was convicted last Session (see
page 634) of stealing a bicyele, lamp, and cape, the goods of Ernest
Saunders was brought up for sentence.
Prisoner was released on his own recognisances in E10 to come up
for judgment if called upon.
DOVE, James, otherwise Wilson (70, tailor), pleaded guilty of
stealing one overcoat, one pair of gloves, and one cigar ease, the
goods of Horace Broughton Nash, and one umbrella, the goods of
George Samuel.
H previous conviction was proved.
Sentence (Mareh 22) Seven days' hard labour, Judge Rentoul re-
marking that he was merely sending prisoner to finish his last sentence.
WILSON, George, otherwise Nolan (27, porter), pleaded guilty
of breaking and entering the shop of George Baxter, with intent to
steal therein being found by night having in his possession without
lawful exeuse, certain implements of housebreaking.
Prisoner confessed to a conviction of felony at this Court on
September 10, 1907.
Sentence Eighteen months' hard labour on each indietment, to run
concurrently.
LAZARUS, Simon (19, dealer), FRANKEL, Reuben (39, tra-
veller), and GOLDSTEIN, Sophie (38), feloniously receiving five
rolls of silk, the goods of George Beckwith, well knowing the same to
have been stolen.
Mr. Jones Lewis prosecuted Mr. Huntly Jenkins defended Lazarus;
Mr. Purcell defended Frankel.
ROBERT JAMes ELVIN. T am warehouseman to prosecutor, whose
warehouse is at 14, Knightrider Street, E.C. The premises were
locked up on February 8 at 7 p.m. with a padlock and a bar. Next
morning I found the door splintered and the lock broken away. The
lock was in the hands of the police. I missed 48 pieces of silk and 68
pieces of cotton goods. I identify the goods produced as belonging
to my master.
