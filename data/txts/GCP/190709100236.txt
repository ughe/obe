September, 1907
BEFORE JUDGE LUMLEY SMITH
Prisoner.
(Wednesday, September 11.)
JOHNSON, Charles (22, engineer), and OWEN, James (21.
labourer), pleaded guilty to burglary in the dwelling-house of John
Cookburd and stealing therein 179 gold rings and other articles,
his property
Mr. Ricardo prosecuted.
Sentence, each prisoner, Two years' hard labour.
Mr. Ricardo asked his lordship if he would mention the condget
of the police.
Judge Lumley Smith objected that there were so many cases now
in which an expression as to the conduct of the police was asked for.
It was becoming a regular business, and he thought was a little over-
done. The police were expected to do their duty, and of course in
this case they were quite right. But he did not know that any
extraordinary ability was required to stop two men at night carrying
a bag on Richmond Green. He should think that was about what
any policeman would do. He had been asked before that day to
pass a commendation on the police, and it was becoming a little too
common; but he always liked to encourage the police, and they had
done very well on this occasion.
