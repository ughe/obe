1106. CORNELIUS STRONG was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd
of February, 1 jacket, value 38., the goods of John Neale.
John NEALE. I live in Blue Gate-fields. On the 22nd of Februar
I was intoxicated the prisoner was drinking with me I went to bed that
night, and I then had my jacket-I missed it about eleven o'clock the
next day-I charged the prisoner with having taken it-he said he knew
nothing about it--this is my jacket.-(looking at it.)
Prisoner. He gave me the jacket that night when he was drunk, and
told me to pawn it-he keeps a brothel-there was a captain robbed in
his house, and he does this to keep me from finding the captain out-
he told my sister, Catherine Page, that if I sent him the jacket back.
he would say no more about it, and told me to keep out of the way
of the police; and if he had not been drunk he would not have put it
into the hands of the police. Witness. My wife does keep a brothel-1
did not give him the jacket to pawn-I agreed to take some other property
which his sister has brought back--if I had given him the jacket, he had
no business to tear up the duplicate; and when I asked him the next day,
he said he had taken no such thing.
THOMAS SQUIRE (police-constable K 282.) I took the prisoner-I
said I wanted him for stealing a jacket-he jumped up from a chair, and
said to a woman, “ You , if you had not given him information,
he would have known nothing about it when he was at the station
house, he said to the prosecutor, “You know you gave me the jaeket."
JANE SOWERBY. I am the wife of William Sowerby, a pawnbroker.
This jacket was taken in of the prisoner, between seven and eight o'clock
in the evening, on the 22nd of February.
WILLIAM SOWERBY. The prisoner redeemed this jacket on the Monday.
Prisoner's Defence. I got it out on the Monday, and sent it back by my
sister.
MARGARET PYBUS. I live in Broad-street, Ratcliff. The prosecutor
told me several times, that this was a drunken frolie entirely, and that be
gave the prisoner the jacket to pawn, to get more liquor-he took the
jacket back of the prisoner's sister, and then he told me he would not hurt
a hair of his head-he said this voluntarily this morning, when I and Mrs.
Page and her husband were drinking with him at the public-house over the
way.
JOHN Neale re-examined. I did not say this morning, that I gavo
him the jacket.
CATHERINE PAGE. I am prisoner's sister. I went to the prosecuto-
he said he and my brotber had been drinking together, and my brother had
pawned his jacket--I said I was very sorry, but if he had pawned any
thing, and I could redeem it, I should be glad to do it-I lent my brother
the money, and got the jacket, and took it back-the prosecutor said, this
morning, that he was sure my brother would not get hurt, and he said he
gave it my brother to get more drink.
NOT GUILTY.
