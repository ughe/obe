ELIZABETH JANE WALES  , alias    Partridge, was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of August , at   Greenwich , 2 sheets, value 10 s  .; 1 blanket, value 4 s  .; and 2 pillow-cases, value 1 s  .; the goods of Thomas Stephens .—2nd COUNT  , stating them to belong to Sarah Stephens . SARAH STEPHENS. I am the wife of Thomas Stephens, a labourer, and live at No. 3, Rome-street, Greenwich. These articles are my husband's—the prisoner came to lodge at my house in July, and staid not quite a fortnight—these articles were let to her with the room—she took the key away when she left—I sent for a constable to open the room, and missed the things—I afterwards received a parcel containing the key and some duplicates, by which I recovered the property—I don't know the hand writing of the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  She shut her door upon me. Witness.  I did so a week before, at half-past eleven o'clock at night, and she never came again that I know of—I don't know that she came that night, as I went to bed. CHARLES NICHOLLS. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 5th of September, in East-lane, Greenwich—I told her it was for robbing her lodgings—she said she thought that was settled before that time—as we came along, she said she had taken the things and pawned them, except the blanket, which she gave a person to pawn for some drink—I found nine duplicates on her, but not of this property. HENRY RYDER. I am shopman to Mr. Seaward, a pawnbroker. I have some sheets and the blanket, which were pledged—I have no knowledge of the prisoner—I took in part of the things—they were pawned by a female—the duplicates of them are in this letter—I have the counterparts.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I had parted from my husband, and was in great distress. I took her room at 3 s . 6 d . a week—on Monday I did not pay my rent—a friend called on Tuesdasy, and she asked him for it—I said she might allow a person to come up stairs without insulting them, and next morning she told me to get another lodging—I told my brother-in-law I had pawned some of the things, and had not the money to redeem them—he came on Saturday, and said he had found me a room at 1 s . 6 d . a week, and he would compromise things with the landlady, as he saw I was sorry for what I had done—he told me on Sunday he had agreed to see it paid—I wrote to her on Tuesday to give her a week's rent, instead of stopping in the room—my brother-in-law said she had a bill against me of 16 s . 8 d .; and he had said he would pay it—the following week he paid her 2 s .—she agreed with him to make it a debt, and take it of him as fast as he could get it—Mr. Marshall, my landlord, offered her 3 s . 6 d .
   MRS. STEPHENS . Her brother-in-law called and paid 2 s . on the rent after she left, and brought me the key and a letter, with the duplicates—he said he would see the rent paid, if God spared his life—he has brought me 1 s . at a time, and he had a misfortune, or I should have received more—he has paid 5 s .—7 s . was due for rent—the things are pawned for 9 s . 6 d .—he never said any thing about paying the money advanced on the things—I don't recollect being offered 3 s . 6 d .—I will not say it was not offered;
