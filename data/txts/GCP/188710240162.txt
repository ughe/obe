JAMES MORRISON. I am a clerk, and live at 58, Gibson Square,
Islington—I bought this gold chain on 5th May, 1885, of Spink and
Sons, and this watch from Roskill, of Liverpool, on 23rd May, 1885, for
251., by letter- I did not go to Liverpool; I sent a cheque for 25l., and
got the watch and the receipt-the number of the watch is 73234— I wore
the watch and chain till June, 1886, when they were stolen from me in
the street in London-Mr. Roskill afterwards wrote to me, and I went to
the police-court in October.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I do not know how I lost them-I sent
my address to Mr. Roskill; this is the letter I wrote (produced).
WILLIAM BARNARD. I am a pawnbroker, of 10, St. George's Circus-
this chain was pawned with me for 61. in the name of George Johnson.
MICHAEL GIBSON ROSKILL. I am a watchmaker, of 21, Church Street,
Liverpool—this watch is my manufacture; I sold it, by letter, to Mr.
Morrisson, of 53, Gibson Square, in May, 1885, for 251.-these are his
letters ordering it, and this is the bill and the receipt dated May 22nd,
1885-it is numbered 73234-I have never seen the prisoner before-I
had an application from the police in May, 1886, for information.
Cro88-ecamined. I refused to give up the customer's name without his
authority– I did not write to the customer; I waited for a communica-
tion from him-I never wrote to the police.
ALFRED WARD (Detective Sergeant L). On 28th August, 1886, I took
the prisoner in Lambath, on another charge-I found this watch on him
and a pawn-ticket for a chain pawned on August 15th, 1886-he said
“The watch is mine; I bought it of the maker, Mr. Roekill, of Liver-
pool, in 1884, the year Busybody won the 1000 Guineas, for 18 guineas"
-I said "How do you account for this ticket, which relates to a valuable
chain ?"-he said "I bought that for 141. at the same time”-he was
taken before a Magistrate, remanded three times, and discharged on
September 20, as we were not able to trace the owner of the watch and
chain, and the Magistrate ordered the police to keep them, and we have
had them ever since-we afterwards found out the oner, and got him to
attend--we communicated with the Liverpool police, but not with Mr.
Roskill.
Cross-examined. The information we got from Liverpool was that the
watch had been sold to a Mr. Morrisson, address unknown.
The prisoner in his statement before the Magistrate and in his defence
said that he bought the watch and chain at Kempton Park Races, on
7th July, of a gentleman who told him he bought it at Roskill's, and there
fore he thought he had better say that he bought it there, than at the races.
