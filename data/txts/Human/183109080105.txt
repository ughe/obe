 1830, January, Cash to pay Halliday and Carter's bill, but returned, 7l. 13s. 4d.
 Prisoner's Defence. When I saw that the cheque of 7l. 13s. was charged to me, I naturally concluded that it had been given to me, and I gave Mr. Macdonald credit for it.

 1752.  THOMAS JAMES was indicted for bigamy.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   JAMES HILL. I am vestry-clerk to St. James, Clerkenwell. I produce an examined copy of the register of the marriage, in the church of St. Philip and St. James, Bristol, and likewise an extract of the register of St. James, Clerkenwell - I have compared them both with the books, and they are correct.
   STEPHEN JONES. I am a shoemaker, and live in Gloucestershire. I know the prisoner; I was at his wedding at the church of St. Philip and St. James, Bristol; I think it will be sixteen years ago in October - he married Martha Short; I knew them living together as husband and wife for two or three years - I believe they had three or four children; I was overseer of the parish at the time - I saw his wife at my house on the 27th of August last.
   MARIA WARBOYS. I married the prisoner at Clerkenwell church on the 4th of October, 1825- he lived with me two years, and it will be four years on the 25th of October next since he was taken by the Bristol officers on account of his first marriage; I did not know at the time of our marriage that he had a wife - the officers told me of it; I then asked him to tell me, and he said it was so.
   JAMES WARBOYS. I am the brother of Maria Warboys; I gave her away on her marriage to the prisoner - he always denied having a wife; my mother told him she had been very nearly bit two or three times, and she hoped she would not again - this was the night before the marriage; he positively denied having been married before.
 Prisoner. I was drinking at a public-house all night -I was taken from there to church; I had not a single farthing - she paid for all. Witness. It is quite false - my sister had not a farthing in her pocket, and I am sure I did not pay.
   SAMUEL MOUNTSTEVENS. I am a beadle of Clerkenwell. I removed the prisoner and his family to Gloucestershire in May last; in going down his wife was troubling him, as she was fearful they would go to Bristol, and find his other wife - he told her to be quiet, she had nothing to fear.(The certificates were put in and read.)
     GUILTY. Aged 45. - Confined Six Months.
 1753.  FREDERICK HILLYER was charged, on the Coroner's Inquisition, with manslaughter.
   DIANA STEED. I live in Salmon-lane, by Limehouse church. On the night of the 8th of September, which was the Coronation day, I was standing at our door, seeing the fire-works - the prisoner lived in my mother's house; I recollect a scuffle for some fire-works - a little boy named Thomas Allen was trying to get some of them; he was knocked down in the crowd, just as the prisoner crossed the road with a gun under his arm; Allen was getting up suddenly - he hit his head against the muzzle of the gun; it went off, and he was shot - he fell dead on the ground; the prisoner was taken into custody - he told the officer he was the man who did it, he was carrying his gun under his arm, with the muzzle pointed to the ground; I had seen him fire the gun in the course of the evening, but upon those occasions he raised the muzzle so that the contents went into the air - he dropped down immediately, and never moved afterwards.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you not been holding a stick, with a catherine-wheel upon it? A. Yes - it fell off, and that occasioned the scramble among the little boys; it appeared to me to be quite an accident: the prisoner called out immediately, "Oh, my G-d! the child is killed!"
   EDWARD ALLEN. I am the father of the deceased - his name was William Edward; he was ten years old last January - I was not there at the time, but I ran to the place, about twenty minutes afterwards, and saw him laying on the floor in a shop, dead.
   WILLIAM SPINK CUMMING. I am a surgeon. I was applied to to examine the child's head, in his father's presence; there were several causes which were sufficient to produce death - the carotid artery and the nerves were divided, and a great quantity of blood effused; that would have occasioned death - I saw him in about a quarter of an hour after the accident, but did not examine his head that night; in my judgment, the division of the carotid artery and the nerves was the immediate cause of his death; the explosion of gunpowder would have occasioned it; but I also found some wadding in the bottom of the wound, which was from one inch and a half to two inches deep.
     NOT GUILTY.
