STAPLES, Mayor.
Heaton--this claim for 37. 98. 2d. for commission on acoount of Ryan and
Rudge is in the prisoner's handwriting-we paid him 21. on account of it
and his receipt appears on the document; the real commission of what
was due on those orders would be 178.-on 30th November we received
an order through the prisoner for Maltini, of Newcastle, for 50.000
handbills, value 61. 178. 6d., and we received this order purporting to be
signed John Reed, “ Please supply on usual terms, 50,000 "-we also
received this document, setting out Maltini 61. 58. and Reed 67. 178., and
claiming commission of 11. 138. 6d., in the prisoner's writing-I sent him
that amount and the receipt is pasted on the document-we got no money
from Maltini-we found him; we evidently instructed our solicitor to sue
him, but from what we heard did not go on with the action-on 17th
December I received this order, marked F, for 100,000 handbills for
Baldwin, of 17, New Bridge-street, Newcastle, 71. 158., from the prisoner
--in consequence of what we heard we did not execute that order-in
those cases where we paid the prisoner the commission we did so because
we believed at the time the orders were bona fide and that commission
was due.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. We did not send you £2 at the end of
the year and leave a balance of £4 88. in your favour--the whole of your
claim has been settled up-each transaction has been complete in itself
and the receipt at the bottom has settled each matter, you had no after
claim to make at all you never wrote to us with respect to making any
claim to commission on bills sent in excess; this is the first I hear of
claiming fancied commission on orders.
By the COURT. He entered our service six months prior to December
-it is unreasonable to expect we can go back over the whole of the trans-
actions, inasmuch as each document closes each transaction-the general
counting-house business does not go through my hands, but I can speak
to these.
JANE ANN CLAKKE. I live at 2, Ingham Place, Newcastle-on 13th
December I kept a milliner's shop at Ridley Street, and on that day
the prisoner called and asked for an order for handbills, and I gave
him an order for 5,000, not for 50,000—I gave him this order (produced)
signed for 5,000 in pencil; that has since been transmogrified into
50,000 in ink-abont a fortnigbt after the time at which the bills
should have been delivered the prisoner called again-I told him I had
not received the bills, and they would be no use to me now as I was
going to leave-they had not then come-I afterwards received a notice
from the Railway Company that the goods were at the station.
THOMAS JAOKSON. I am a clothier, of 40, Grainger Street, Newcastle
-in October last I gave the prisoner an order for 25,000 handbills,
which were supplied -about December the prisoner called on me and I
gave him an order for 25,000 handbills of a peculiar pattern-I received
30,000, and subsequently I received 100,000 which I had not ordered, a
different lot altogether-I wrote to Messrs. Franklin that they were of
no use to me and that I had never ordered them, and I refused to take
them.
Cross-examined. I signed the first order-Franklin sent me 30,000
instead of 25,000-I got no proof of the bill, but 30,000 of them.
