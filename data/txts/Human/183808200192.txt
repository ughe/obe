   COURT . Q.  Had you previously known of his having them? A.  Yes I had given him leave—I did not know who they were for.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Have you never sworn that you never served him but twice, and then as a customer? A.  No; I should say I did not say so—I said, "I let him have two pairs—I served him on two occasions—he told me he knew a friend who would be very glad to purchase them, as they were an exceedingly strong pair, and if I would allow him, he would take them to try—he kept them, saying the party was at Richmond, and he could not get them—he never had boots or shoes from me, except the three pairs I have mentioned, to my recollection.
   Q.  Has he not had goods as many as half a dozen times, and settled with you for them on Saturday night? A.  He has not half a dozen time—I think I am quite safe in swearing it was not five times—it may have been four times—I may have been mistaken in one—I preferred the charge before the Grand Jury—the prisoner has not been committed by any Ma gistrate.
   COURT . Q.  When did you prefer your bill? A.  On the 17th of April, I believe, while the Grand Jury were sitting—I have never had him ap prehended.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  What was the reason you did not take him? A.  I could not find him—I waited outside his mother's house while the officer went in—I did not go to his mother's house for him after that—I told the policeman to keep a look-out—I heard he was driving a cab , and I kept looking at the cabs  —it was after the second hearing that I went to Bow-street—the week before he was taken, he returned me the pair of ladies' shoes that did not fit the customer-his mother did not appear when he was before Mr. Bennett, and bring forward a pair of shoes, that I saw—there was no pair brought forward that had been part of my stock—I swear it was not so—she did not produce a pair of boots—I did not see her within sufficient distance to produce anything—she stated no thing there—I swear that without her being heard Mr. Bennett dismissed the prisoner—he was taken up ultimately on the charge of bigamy—he was remanded twice, I believe—I did not tell my story there before the Magistrate—I handed in the certificate of his being indicted.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  you had got your bill found by the Grand Jury? A.  Yes; that was by the advice of Mr. Twyford-all the settlements I had with the prisoner alluded to the boots and shoes I had sold him—I never allowed him to take boots or shoes out without my consent.
