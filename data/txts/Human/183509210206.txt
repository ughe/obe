there—I believe they had sent for my father over there—I heard Cowderoy say, "That is right,  old one,  start half-a-dozen, it will soon bring it to a close; we are such b----y rum ones,  and such out-and-outers"  —and Mr. Hill said, "The old one  is a right one, he ought to be one, he is an old hackney-coach master"—Robert Travett was present at the time, and he had some conversation with him, but I did not exactly hear what it was—they were each of them drinking a great deal, and got pushing each other about in their chairs—Mr. Trevett said, "Why don't you buy a time?"—my father said, "No, I never intend buying a time"—Mr. Trevett said, "It is no good your trying to beat us, for we are such out-and-outers  you can't beat us"—I did not hear any thing said about 10 s .—Richard Bird has same place that we did—we did—we put up our omnibus at Mr. Davis's—we remained there sometimes half an hour, and sometimes two hours, or more, and the "Emperor" always remained exactly the same time, come after me and found me—when I have wanted to take up passengers, they have tried to get them into their own bus  —I remeber our calling for Mr. Hillhouse, the surgeon, in Oxford-street, and he
