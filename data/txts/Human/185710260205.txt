   Witnesses for the Defence. CHARLES VICKERS. I am a member of this society, and have bean so three years or more. I have known the prisoner all the time acting as general secretary—more than half the time I have been one of the executive council for the Lambeth branch, I believe for two years—during all that time the prisoner has had the character of an honest, upright man; a man more respected I never knew—he gave notice that he would leave the society as secretary, and he was retained again—there have been differences in the society between the members for the last four or five months—I have seen this little book in possession of the prisoner—he told me it belonged to the society—I first saw it in Sept.—I was asking the prisoner to give me a sheet of paper of the expenses of the auditors; he said, "I have not got the list done"—he said he had the account of all, and then I saw this book—he said, "They have taken the books, and this book has got the account"—the prisoner was not in custody at the time I saw this book—I saw it the first week in September—I had not seen it previously to that—I saw the entries which the book contained, because I wanted the account to show to my branch—it is in my knowledge, that in consequence of the differences the prisoner had not access to the whole of the books—I understood this book belonged to the society; it is a cash book-no charge of this kind had been made against the prisoner at the time I saw this book.
   MR. GIFFARD . Q.  Had no charge of embezzlement been made against him at that time? A.  No, I swear there was not—I do not remember the date when it was,—there was a charge made by the auditors that there was something wrong—that was before I saw this book-—it was not in con sequence of that charge that I asked for the book—it was to give information to my branch—I did not look to try to find some accounts of the receipt of the money that Mr. Hall had paid—I was told this was one of the society's books—it was before August that the prisoner was asked to give up the books belonging to the society—I forget the date when this book was bought—it was when he came from Sidney Smith's office—I do not know where it was bought—I believe I saw the account furnished by the prisoner on this register—I did not look if there was any account of these sum it—there were differences in the society for five or six months—that had reference to the disappearance of money; what the auditors called deficiency—they said various sums were deficient one time and various sums at another; but they contradicted themselves several times—they said some times 100 l . and sometimes 40 l .—I was not at the Police Court when the prisoner was under examination.
   MR. LILLEY . Q.  Were you requested to attend? A.  No; the prisoner had not access to the books of the society down to the time of this charge—I cannot recollect the date when he ceased to have access to the books, but I know they were taken away from there in a most abrupt manner, by men who had elected themselves—the prisoner continued to act as secretary down to the time of his apprehension, he continued to act after the books were taken from him—he said he would give up, but they requested him to continue as secretary.
