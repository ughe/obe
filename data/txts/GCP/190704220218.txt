Cross-examined by Mr. Hammond-Chambers. I was chairman
when Hodgkin was appointed master. I have always given a great
deal of trouble and time to the affairs of the workhouse. When
Morgan, the previous master, retired, the workhouse had got into
almost a state of chaos, and we were very anxious to get the best
man we could as master. There were some 200 or 300 candidates
from whom seven were selected. We desired a married man, so that
his wife could be matron. The salary was £300, with an increase up
to £350, and superannuation after 33 years' service of £180. We
had excellent testiminials about Hodgkin, showing that his age wa
thirty-nine. He began with two years as junior clerk at St. Pancra,
five years storekeeper at St. Pancras. Then the husband and wife
were 4 years master and matron at Shardeloe Union, and then master
at Shoreditch for seven years. I accept those particulars from you
I know he has been in Poor Law a great many years. On his taking

audit. so far as I know, he .periormed all his duties satisfactoriis.
