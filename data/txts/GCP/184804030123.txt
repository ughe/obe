Marks Defence. I sent for Mr. Barnett at six o'clock next morning :
that paper is a false paper; I do not know anything about it, except that
before my husband's death, in Nov., Mr. Barnett gave me a paper to put
away, and I put it into my drawer; my husband's name is signed at the
bottom, which will prove it; I cannot read, and do not know what was in
the paper; I never saw any property with the old woman; when she paid
me her rent, 41., she made me give her the receipt before I had the money;
I do not know what became of her money, unless she was robbed when she
had the fall; I have had no money to get a counsellor; she was in the habit
of going out to drink very often, and came home late ; that is not the paper
that was in my drawer; I am a strange woman in a strange country, and
throw myself on the mercy of the Court.
Robinson WEBB re-examined. I took the servant up with me into the
bed-room, unlocked the drawer in her presence, and she saw me take the
paper out-it was not at the top-there were several memorandums and
receipts with it—this paper was at the back part of the drawer, tumbled about
• good deal --I saw no other papers but receipts and memorandums, and
several in Hebrew.
MR. HORRY called
WILLIAM CLARK, I am beadle of Gough-square. I bave known Barnhard
about sixteen months-I always found him a highly respectable man-he paid
his rent very regularly.
Mr. RYLAND. Q. Was he a lodger of yours ? A. Yes; for sixteen
months, down to the time of his apprehension-I have no reason to know
that he was in very straitened circumstances-I know he had very valuable
goods-be paid me 4s. 6d. a week for one unfurnished room on the first floor
-- he is married—he has no family-I believe the reason he lived in that way
was, because his goods has been seized for income-tax-he was sur-
posed to be worth more than he was-be told me so-it was not because ho
was poor that he lived in this way, but he did not think he ought to pay the
income-tax.
