 2149.  EDWARD CROWDER was again indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 1 pair of sheets, value 5s., the goods of Samuel Wood.
   ELIZA WOOD. I am the wife of Samuel Wood. The prisoner hired a lodging at my house, on the 8th of August, at 3s. a week - he slept there that night, and never came again; we missed the sheets off the bed as soon as he left the house - there was no other person there.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do not you know that your husband and he went out at the door together? A. He stood talking with us at the parlour-door for two or three minutes - the sheets have not been found.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years longer.
 2150.  WILLIAM JENNINGS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 12 ozs. weight of leather, value 2s., the goods of Eusebius Smith.
   JOHN LEACH. I live with Eusebius Smith, a leather-seller, in Kingsland-road. On the 20th of August I was in the shop - the prisoner came in; he asked for some half soles; I put the box on the counter - he turned them out, and looked at them; while I was serving another customer I saw him secreting something in a bag, which he had on the counter - I kept my eyes on him, and observed him put some half soles in the bag a second time; he then looked out a pair - I pressed them for him, and he paid me- I then said, "What have you in your bag?" he said Nothing; I said, "You have some half soles;" he then said, "I will pay you for them" - the officer happened to come in; he took him, and found these half soles in his bag.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he must have put the goods into his bag by mistake.
     GUILTY. Aged 49. - Confined Six Weeks.
 2151.  HENRIETTA HANAGAN and MARY CARBEY were indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , 17 yards of printed cotton, value 8s., the goods of William Shearman.
   THOMAS CHURCH. I was passing Mr. Shearman's shop, in Chiswell-street, on the 11th of August, about twelve o'clock, and saw the two prisoners looking at some prints at his door - I watched them, and saw Carbey put her hand under the print, and Hanagan walked round the shop- they then walked up to the print again; Carbey held up her apron, and Hanagan pulled this print down into her apron; Carbey walked off quick with it, and came towards me - the other prisoner was close behind her; I stopped Carbey, and when Hanagan came up I took her also - I took them to the shop.
 WILLIAM SHEARMAN. I am a  linen-draper, in Chiswell-street. This is my print.
 Hanagan's Defence. I was going past the shop, and the print fell down - she took it up to take it to the shop; this witness took her; I went to ask him what for, and he took me.
 HANAGAN -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 CARBEY -    GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.

 2152.  WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of John Smith , from his person.
 JOHN SMITH. I live in the Commercial-road, and am a  captain of a vessel. On the 8th of August, at half-past ten o'clock in the evening; I was walking in Grenada-terrace, Commercial-road- I felt a tug at my pocket, I turned and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand; I said, "You scoundrel what are you about?" he immediately ran away - a young gentleman took him; I went up and found him in possession of the officer: I took up my handkerchief.
   THOMAS SMITHERS(Police-constable K 101). I was on duty, and saw the prisoner running - I took him; this handkerchief was picked up.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I turn the corner with it? A. I did not see you drop it; I saw you run and turn one corner: they were pursuing you - I did not lose sight of you.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 15. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2153.  SAMUEL HILL was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , one 5l. Bank note, the property of John Head , from his person.
 JOHN HEAD. I am a  smith, and live at Maidstone. I was in Robert-street, Grosvenor-square, about the middle of July, 1831; I cannot tell the day - I had seen the prisoner before, when he had called at my shop, and asked if I had a job for him; he was a stone-mason: on the day in question he asked me, if I had a set of iron-work for an oven to sell - I said I had; he said if I would go with him and a gentleman, he thought he could get a customer, and a good job for himself - we went along Piccadilly, and he said, "We will have something to drink;" I said, "No, we will go and have our business done first," but he went into a public-house, and I had a glass of porter - we then went on to Westminster, and he showed me some premises in Tothill-street; I looked at them, and told the person not to have any thing to do with them, without he had 100l. to spare, and 100l. to lay out - we then all went to the Cock, in Tothill-street; the other person spent half a crown in bread and cheese, and beer - the prisoner called for gin and water; I said to him, "Don't call for any thing without you have money to pay for it" - he went to countermand the order, but the landlord brought it, and I said, "It is a shame you should have this, we have had enough, but as it is come, I will pay for it:" I took out a 5l. note, and said to the landlord, "Can you give me change?" he said Yes; I wrote my name on it, and his wife called out at that moment -"You know we can't, we paid the brewer this morning;" the prisoner jumped up, snatched the note from my hand, and said, "I can get it changed:" he went off, leaving his hat, stick, and all behind him - he has been away from his wife and family for twelve months.
