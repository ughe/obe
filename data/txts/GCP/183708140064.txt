MICHAEL JOHN DEMPSEY. I am a policeman. I was called to the
Pavior's Arms, between five and six o'clock in the evening on the 6th of
July, and saw the prosecutor lying on the ground in Chancery-court, all
over blood-I was obliged to lift him off the ground-he was not able to
speak, and could not stand without help-that was from injury, for his face
was quite swollen, bis eye quite black, and his cheek open; and his body
appeared sore, for he could not bear to be touched-in consequence of what
I heard, I went in search of the prisoners, who, I understood, lived in the
court the mob forced the door of a house in, and they had their backs to
the door-when my fellow-constable came up we pushed in the door, and
got the prisoners out--we did not search them, not knowing there had
been any robbery. D
