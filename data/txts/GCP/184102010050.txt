JOAN GOZEE. I am a policeman. I have produced the letter and
cheque, which I received from Mr. Duncan-it is a letter from Miss
Malcom.
MR. DUNCAN re-examined. The prisoner wore mustaches when I
saw him I have not the slightest doubt of him being the man.
SARAH WADHAM re-examined. He wore mustaches at our house,
and a blue coat buttoned up-he looked like a seafaring man-I am sure
he is the man I have no doubt of him.
Cross-examined. Q. Who are you looking at? A. The prisoner-I
did not point to the gentleman under the dock-that is the prisoner-I
might be confused about it-that is the man--he slept in the house several
times.
MARIA PARTRIDGE re-examined. The prisoner is the man-he had
mustaches- I have not a doubt of him, though he has none now.
Miss Malcom re-examined. He wore mustaches when I saw him at
Boulogne.
GUILTY. Aged 23.
648. MARK SALOM was again indicted for stealing, on the 6th of
January, 9 shirts, value 11. 158., and 3 pair of socks, value 2s., the goods
of Charles George Ladensach.
George HANCOCK. I am shopman to Charles George Ladensach, a
linen-draper in the Minories. On Wednesday, the 6th of January,
between eleven and twelve o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came and
asked for some shirts at 48. each, the same he had had some of before--he
was showed some at that price by Reeve, another shopman, which he did
not like he was then shown some at 48. 6d., of which he chose six--they
were put apart for him-he then asked for some fancy shirts at 28. 9d., the
same as he had had before, he said, as they had worn so well, he would give
Mr. Ladensach a turn again-he had not had any before to my knowledge-
some of that sort were shown him-he chose three, which were put apart
for him-he then asked for some fancy socks, and selected three pairs-he
ordered them all to be sent to No. 6, in the Crescent, in the Minories,
the bill to be sent with them, and he would pay for them there-he gave
his name as Porter-Reeve wrote it down Potter, in mistake--the prisoner
said it was of no consequence, they were to be sent within ten minutes or
he should be out-he alluded to a party who we had served in the spring,
who went out on board the Napoleon to Swan river: and he said since then
he (the prisoner) had been to St. Jean d'Acre, and was wounded in three
places-he pointed to three places, and a scar on his chin was one-he has
such a scar-I can recognise it now-he bad not mustaches then-his
bill came to 1l. 178. 750.--I carried the goods home according to his
direction, and took the bill with them-I inquired for Mr. Porter-I saw
the servant, Mary Martin--she showed me into the back parlour, where
I found the prisoner sitting-I laid the goods on the table, and gave him
the bill--he then said he wanted some more of the coloured shirts, but the
price was a long price for them- he would give me 2s.6d. for some more
- I told him he could have some at that price, but not that quality-at
last he said he would take three more at 2s. 9d. each, which he requested
me to add to the bill - I did so, gave him the bill back again, and waited
for the money-he said he would pay me when I brought the others, and I
was to be back in five minutes, or he should be out-I left the goods there,
