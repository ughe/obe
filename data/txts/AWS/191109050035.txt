HENRY R. HARLEY, M.D., Wealdstone, divisional surgeon. I have
attended prisoner for about four years. On August 10 about 6.30
p.m. I went to her house and saw her; she was then partially con-
seious; she was recovering from the effects of coal-gas poisoning.
I saw also the body of Kathleen, and assisted at the efforts to restore
conseiousness, which were unavailing. I subsequently made a post-
mortem examination of the body, which confirmed my opinion that
she died from coal-gas poisoning. I had a conversation with prisoner
and her husband and then went into the soullery. There I noticed a
strong smell of gas, more than in the rest of the house. As I was

Cross-examined. During the four years I attended prisoner she
appeared to be perfectly happy in her home ; she and her husband
were devoted to each other and fond of their children Prisoner did
not quite reeover from the gas poisoning for about 24 hours. She was

done; she complained of pain on the top of her head. I came
to
