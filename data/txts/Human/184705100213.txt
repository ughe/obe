   Q.  What was it the policeman omitted to say? A.  He omitted saying that Anderson said, "Ashlin, I am done for, for Gods sake intercede with the policeman to let me go until the morning"—I said, "You have been an officer, you know better than that; if this man gives you up, he is liable to lose his situation"—I did not want anything set right—I had no object at all—I am nor the prosecutor of this indictment—I am not supplying any of the funds—I do not know who is—I never had a key to this yard.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  Anderson asked you if you could get him some one to remove this iron? A.  Yes, that occurred in the yard where the shaft was—he had previously pointed out the shaft—I did not know exactly on whose premises it was—it might belong to the Railway Com pany—my two men went to help in putting it on the truck—Anderson and I had a drop of gin together—I do not know what Smith had, it was something in a pot—my men are named Jones and Parsons—this happened about one o'clock in the day—Oake is porter to one of the auctioneers who had been selling the property—I was not aware whether Oakes had been helping the iron into the truck—I was not there—the public-house is about fifty yards from the yard—I think the gates were left open—there was not any one in the yard when we were in. JAMES MILBURY. I am in Mr. Nicholls' employ. I was in the stable in Mr. Baker's yard, which adjoins Mr. Nicholls' premises, on that day—I saw a shaft of iron in the yard—I saw five men go and take it out—they were Smith, Parsons, Jones, Oakes, and a boy—they brought it out, put it on the truck, and took it right away—Smith assisted in dragging the truck. JOHN KNOWLDEN. I live with my father, who is an engineer, in Great Guildford-street. On the 16th of April Smith came to me with some old iron, a piece of a shaft, and a rigger—he offered it for sale for 8 s . a cwt.—I offered him 7 s . 6 d ., which he agreed to take—he took some old iron, and paid me the difference—I showed that shaft afterwards to Mr. Nicholls.
