6. ALFRED STARTIN (24), was indicted for a like offence.
Messrs. COLERIDGE and WIGHTMAN conducted the Prosecution.
MATILDA BENNINGFIELD. I am the wife of George Benningfield, tobacconist,
of 60, St. Martin's Le Grand-on Wednesday, 27th October, about 6 p.m.,
the prisoner came in for a three-halfpenny cigar, aud gave me a sixpence-
I gave him change, and be turned to go, but my husband tried the sixpence,
found it was bad, and went after him-I had put it in a drawer where there
were two old sixpences--this was a rfew one, and the old ones were at the
back of the tray-two days afterwards my husband called me into the shop,
and I saw the prisoner and identified him.
George BENNINGFIELD. On 27th October my wife served the prisoner with
a cigar, and he left-shortly afterwards I looked in the till and found a bad
sixpence in front of the tray-I did not notice whether there were others I
went to look after him-on 29th October, about 3 o'clook, I served the
prisoner with a cigar, and he gave me a bad sixpence—I called my wife,
and said, “Do you know this man !" she said, “This is the man who
passed the bad sixpence the other day"-I do not remember whether the
prisoner said anything-I gave him in charge with the two sixpences.
GEORGE WARD (City Policeman 120). The prisoner was given into my
custody on 20th October-I searched him at the station, and found a bad
sixpence in his waistcoat pocket, wrapped up in paper, and 48. 4fd. in good
coin-I received these two sixpences from Mr. Benningfield.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These three sixpences are bad, and the two passed
at Mr. Benningfield's are from the same mould.
Prisoner's Defence. I did tender a sixpence that evening, but whether
that is the one I cannot say. Guilty-Two Years' Imprisonment.
