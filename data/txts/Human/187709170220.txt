   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH . I have known Chappell about twelve years and always thought he was a solicitor—I have not seen him at police-courts—I was cross-examined at the police-court as to the offence for which I was tried—it was thirty years ago since I came out of Brixton for seven days—we were a lot of boys together in a field roasting laters , and twenty years ago 1 got three months for stealing a sack of beans—I have also had six months for gambling in 1866—I have been home thirteen years and nobody can say anything against me—I call it gambling, it was for stealing money from the person—there was not six months for gambling us well—the first statement I made to the Magistrate was that Chappell gave me the card and my wife paid the money—I had to admit in cross-examination that I had the card five days before any money was paid—I cannot read much, nor can my wife—we did not read the card—nobody read it to us till I took it to the solicitor—I saw the prisoner outside the Centurion at Deptford, and told them that I had had a horse killed—I did not say "I have got a bit of a law job on"—I said "I have got a bit of a hard job on"—I said I should have to get somebody to attend to it for me—I did not go for the purpose of getting Chappell to attend to it—they stopped me and said "Halloo, Bailard, how are you getting on?" and I told them, and they said "If you are going to have anybody you might as well put it in our way, we are both solicitors"—when I said I had a bit of a law job on 1 intended to ask Chappell to do it for me, thinking he was a solicitor—I should not have told them anything about it or asked them to attend to it if 1 had not thought they were soli citors—Chappell said that he could not stop to talk the matter over then
