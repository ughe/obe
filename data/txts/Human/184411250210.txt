 182. HENRY BRENTON was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying Mary Tremlett Turner Brenton . He was also charged on the Coroner's Inquisition with the like manslaughter.
   MR. HAKE conducted the Prosecution . HENRY WILLIS LAYER, I am a surgeon, and live at 38, Stamford-street, Lambeth. I knew Mrs. Brenton, the deceased—I was called to attend her about the 17th of Aug., and I attended her in the delivery of female child on the 20th of Aug.—she left her bed after that at about the ordinary period—she had a very good time—it was quite a natural labour—I believe it was not her first child, but I never attended her before—after leaving her bed she was attacked with rheumatism—I think she left her bed about the third week, or probably rather before—I do not know how long after leaving her bed she returned to it—I think several days, but I was not there all the time—I merely called in occasionally to see how she was, after being attacked with rheumatism—my attendance then recommenced—she was then keeping her bed—I left town on the 25th of Sept. I think, and left the case in charge of Mr. Saunders—I returned on the 10th of Oct., and on that evening I saw the deceased with her father, whom I was then introduced to, and Mr. Saunders—she was then in a state of almost insensibility—she was paralyzed in the right arm, and the left leg appeared paralyzed—she had spasms of the muscles of the jaw, producing what is commonly called locked-jaw—she had a very low feeble pulse—she could only be aroused to answer a question or two, not a sequence of questions—I was shown a wound which was then sloughing on her back—it was about two inches long and one wide—it was a slough in the centre with a red margin, and shading off to the natural colour of the skin—it was on the sacrum , over the lower bone of the back, not quite the lowest—Mr. Brenton returned with roe to my house for medicine, and he said, seeing I was returned, and being his wife's medical attendant, he thought it right to state to me what had occurred on that particular even ing in question, that he was desirous of having intercourse with his wife, which she resisted, and thinking it arose from ill temper, or words to that effect, he had persisted, and was exceedingly sorry for the results—I think he remarked that he thought it rather extraordinary the bruise on the back
