   Cross-examined. Q.  You have come here to speak the truth? A.  Yes—I met him first coming from Mr. Brooks's shop with a parcel—he went to his house and I was at home, and then we went away together—I met him first by the Dover-road, near St. George's Church—I know Surrey-square—that is where I went with the prisoner—it may be more than half a mile from the Borough end of the Dover-road—the first time I met him in the Dover-road he had a heavy weight with him—I went with him to the back of Surrey-square—I did not meet him in the square—he left the parcel at the house—the gentleman was not at home—the lady took it in, and she said there was nobody at home—I had left my work in Suffolk-street then—it was about the 15th of September, I think—I cannot tell—I cannot read—I think it was some time about the election—I never told anybody another day on which I pawned this—it was the day I met him—I think it was July—it was either September or July—he told me to pledge it in the name of John Clark, No. 9, somewhere near Suffolk-street—I think I told the pawnbroker High-street—I got 5 s .—it was a false name and address—I have been sorry since I did it—I have been working lately with my father as a shoemaker, since last Saturday—I went down before that to St. Alban's for a fortnight—I am not known there—before that I was lodg ing at the prisoner's mother's, working up Suffolk-street at shoemaking—I was about a month there—I had always been with my father before that—I had been to work at different places, and went home to my father—I have worked next door to my father, No. 3, Newington-grove—I have been in a good many situations in Surrey—I know what an oath is—I have worked in Horsemonger-lane jail, because one day I was ill and was very hungry, there was a pair of gaiters, and I took them and got 2 d . to buy some victuals—I was in Horsemonger-lane jail four days and had a flogging. JOSEPH BURTON. I took in the lawn in the name of" John Clark," on the 17th of July—I cannot say who pawned it—it was a piece of lawn for 5 s .—it was a boy or a man.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You asked him the usual questions? A.  Yes—he said it was for Mr. Clark, who had sent him—he did not say he was sent by his mother, or we should have put it down. THOMAS BROOKS re-examined.  The prisoner was porter to my traveller, and was often left alone in the warehouse where this was.
