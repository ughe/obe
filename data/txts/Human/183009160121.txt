 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you with the prosecutor when he received the eight sovereigns? A. Yes - I do not know what possessed him to think that a charitable fund gave him the money; I understood it was for what he had lost, and the danger he had run.
   JONATHAN SAWYER. I am a boat-builder. I let boats to watermen by the week - I saw this boat at the Thames Police-office; it was not an old boat - I examined it - it appeared to me to have been damaged by being launched; there was a hole which had been patched up with canvas and grease.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Do not watermen lend their boats to one another? A. I believe they do allow others to take fares in them; the damage to the boat had been done in launching, not on the water.
   WILLIAM WATTS. I am a waterman. I saw Peck and the gentleman talking, but I did not hear what they said; Peck came up to Tilson, who was lying on his face, and said, "Tilson, here is money;" but I did not hear what it was - Peck then said, "Row the boat to Waterlon-bridge;" I took them there - Peck then offered me a sovereign; I said I would have nothing to do with it - Tilson said, "Take it, it will do you good;" I said, "I will have nothing to do with it;" they took me to Covent-garden, gave me a cup of coffee, a slice of bread and butter, and a glass of gin; I left them at five o'clock, in Bow-street.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. So a sovereign was offered you? A. Yes; I was not sober - Peck was drunk, but not so much as I and Tilson were; I was on the top of the stairs at Westminster-bridge, lacing up my boots; I never was in trouble myself - my master was taken before the Magistrate about some coals, the sweepings of some barges; I went with him - my master had a month at Kingston; I was sent home - I never was in prison; I was taken on this charge by Mr. Little - he said, "Watts, you must step into our boat;" I was examined before the Magistrate - I told the truth.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was not Tilson exceedingly drunk? A. Yes; I could not say he knew what he was about.
   ALEXANDER MITCHELL. I am a Thames Police-surveyor. I took Peck on the Sunday; I found but 6d. on him; he said he had received the money and spent it, and had but 6d. left - he said Tilson had part of it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was he sober? A. He was half drunk then; I saw Mr. Burrow's about eleven o'clock in the day - he appeared as if he had been either flurried or drinking; I sent Mr. Little after Cook, but he could not find him - Mr. Mellows did not appear drunk.
   JOHN LITTLE. I am a Thames Police-surveyor. I took up Tilson on Sunday, the 18th of July; I said he was wanted for letting out a boat - he said he was asleep on the stairs, that Peck came, awoke him, showed him some money, and gave him part of it, but he was so much in liquor he did not know how much; I asked what became of it - he said they cut his fob, and took 13s. out, which they took to his wife; his wife was present, and she said she had made away with all but two half-crowns.
 Peck's Defence. I was very much in liquor, and do not know what I was about - Watt's took a sovereign, and returned it.
 Tilson's Defence. I had been drinking.
 Peck received a good character.

 TILSON -    NOT GUILTY.
 1737.  WILLIAM WESLEY and THOMAS BROWN were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , 1 purse, value 3d.; 26 yards of ribbon, value 6s.; 5 handkerchiefs, value 9s.; 3 pairs of gloves, value 2s.; 1 dozen of buttons, value 6d.; 1 purse, value 1s.; 2 sovereigns, 1 crown, 5 half-crowns, 1 shilling, 1 sixpence, and one 10l. Bank note, the property of John Jones.
   JOHN JONES. I am a haberdasher, and live in Tottenham-court-road- Wesley lived with me, and Brown lived in Marylebone-street. On the evening of the 7th of August a Police-officer called, and produced three lengths of ribbon which had my mark on them - I knew them, but I cannot say exactly how lately I had seen them - I went to Mrs. Collier's, where Brown lived; we found Brown, and asked him who he had received them from; he said from Wesley, but did not say when or where - we then found on Brown five silk handkerchiefs, which he said he got from Wesley to take care of for him; we took Brown to the Police station; I went home - Wesley was out; we broke open his box, and found gloves and other things which are mine; when he returned we showed him the things, and asked when he stole them - he gave no answer; the officer searched him, and found 1s. 6d. in his waistcoat pocket - and fastened to his braces was a kid purse of mine, in which was the 10l. note, two sovereigns, and a crown-piece - I gave him into custody; the note is one I had received on the 29th of July, and marked it - it had been in a cash-box in the desk, but the key was left in it; we left him in the shop, making up grosses of buttons, when we went to dinner, and he said he went through the room, saw the key in the desk, and took it out.
 Cross-examined by MR. STURGEON. Q. Did you mark the note yourself? A. Yes.
   ELIZABETH MARY COLLIER. Brown was in my employ - Mr. Jones came to my shop about him; some property was found which had been brought to me by a person named Gosling - there were four lengths of black ribbon; I took charge of them for a few minutes, and then sent them out to three different linen-drapers - I asked Brown if they were his - he said Yes, that his mother sent them by mistake with his clothes; I believe these are the ribbons - I gave them to the Policeman, who gave them to Mr. Jones.
