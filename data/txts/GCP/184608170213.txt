MARY Brian. I keep a fruit-stall, and live in Maypole-alley, Southwark.
On the evening of 10th July, the prisoner Jones came to me and asked for
a halfpennyworth of gooseberries, which I gave her she gave me a shilling,
and I gave her elevenpence halfpenny change-Lloyd, I believe, came up
while that was going on, and asked me if I had any gooseberries—I told him
no, the woman had bought the last I had-Jones said to him, "I will sell
you my lot for one penny"-Lloyd said, “You want to gain profit"-Jones
went away immediately, and Lloyd stopped and asked me the question still,
if I had any gooseberries, after I had said I had none-I told him no, I showed
him the shilling, and asked him if it was a good one-he rung it on the stone,
and said it was a good one, and he wished me or him had a good many like
it-he gave it me back-I never lost sight of it-he went away-I don't
know whether he went in the same direction as Jones, because she crossed the
road-I did not see Wilson at all ---Whitlam came up and asked me what had
passed - I went into a public-house with him and gave the shilling to a bar-
man, who bit a piece out of it in my presence, and gave it to Whitlam.
Cross-examined. Q. Directly you gave change to Jones, Lloyd came up ?
4. Yes, I know it was the shilling Lloyd gave me, I had not another-he
could not have changed it, I watched all the time he had it, and all the time
the barman had it-I bit it at the time I took it, before I gave change, because
I wanted to be confident it was a good one I did not suspect it.
JOHN WHITLAM (policeman). On the 10th July I was in company with
West, and followed the prisoners- I saw Wilson go to Mrs. Leary's stall by
St. George's Church--when he left it I followed bim till he joined Jones and
Lloyd, about two hundred or three hundred yards from the stall at the time
Wilson went to the stall, the two others were about ten yards from it-after this,
I saw Jones go to Mrs. Briant's stall—while she was buying the gooseberries
Lloyd stood overlooking her with his hand in his waistcoat-pocket-as
soon as he left the stall, I saw him take the shilling which had been tendered
by Jones, and gingle it on the pavement, and say it was a good one-di-
rectly he left, I went up and asked the woman what she had received--we
went into the public-house, and the barman bit it-I produce it-I have had
it ever since I went up to the stall directly after Jones and Lloyd left it-I
lost sight of Wilson about a minute before they went away-they had been
all three standing together talking-my attention was directed to Jones and
Lloyd going up to the stall-I produce the second shilling. I
