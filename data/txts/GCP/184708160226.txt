1966. MARY CAIN was indicted for stealing a jacket, a pair of trowsers,
4 waistcoats, and other articles, value 51.; the goods of Leonard Hoey.
LEONARD HOEY. I am a seaman, and live at the Cock public-house,
Tothill-street, Westminster. On the 19th of July I had a bag of clothes,
containing the articles stated they were safe that morning, and in the even-
ing they were gone-on the Wednesday week afterwards the prisoner came into
the public-house, and offered the buttons for sale for a pot of beer-she left
them with the landlord's son; they were in a piece of paper in her hand-
they had been in the pocket of the waistcoat I lost-I went to her apartment
and found several duplicates, one of which related to my shirt and waistcoat
- these are them.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'Brien. Q. Who was sleeping in the room
with you? A. They were strangers—there might be half-a-dozen persons
in the public-house when the prisoner came in with the buttons,
WILLIAM MILLERMAN policeman B 95.) I went to the prisoner's house
- she said it was her place- I there found this duplicate of a waistcoat and
shirt, pawned at Mr. Arnold's.
GEORGE Parsons. I am assistant to Mr. Arnold, a pawnbroker. I pro-
duce this shirt and waistcoat, they were pawned by a woman- I have a
duplicate like this produced by the officer; it is dated the 20th of July.
EDWARD Thorn. I keep the public-house--the prisoner was in the habit
of coming there--she left these buttons with my son.
