I will not swear I did not say that he owned it was his first offence, but to the best of my knowledge I did not - it had slipped my memory; the prisoner had no other hat- the gentleman was not sober.
 WILLIAM SMITH. This is my hat
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you sober? A. Pretty well; I was sober enough to know I had been robbed.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the prosecutor with a young man, who asked me to go with him; I got on to Milbank, and he was so very obstinate I was forced to leave him - I went on and found the hat; I took off my cap, and put it on; I met the officer, who said he must detain me - the gentleman then came up without his hat, and said he had no knowledge of me, and I had not robbed him.
   JOHN TUBB. Immediately I brought up the prisoner, the prosecutor said, "That is the fellow (or) the villain who took my hat."
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 20. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 1815.  THOMAS SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , 1 sovereign, and one 5l. Bank note, the property of Robert Mitchell , from his person.
   ROBERT MITCHELL. I am a sailor. On the 15th of September I was lying on a bed in a house in Albion-street, Commercial-road- I had been paid at the India-house, and was rather intoxicated; the prisoner was with me at the India-house - he was not in the room when I lost my property, but was under the same roof; the sovereign was in my right-hand trousers pocket, and the note in the other; I have not seen the note since - I have seen a sovereign, but could not swear to it; there was a girl lying behind me, but not asleep - she awoke me, and told me of it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. What time was this? A. I was a little intoxicated; I think it must have been between seven o'clock and ten - I was not so drunk I could not stand up; the woman was not drunk; I called an officer, and gave her into custody with two men - there was a person named Hardy there; I think I had been in three public-houses after I received my money - I will not swear I had not been in six; I had but one girl with me; there were three other girls in company; I had been twelve or thirteen days living at the prisoner's house, and owed him money, which I intended to pay when I got up; I owed him a bill of 6l. 12s. - it is still due to him; no one was in the room with me but the girl - I found the prisoner in the passage, and Hardy with him; I gave the girl in charge, and then then the two men as fast as I could speak - after the girl was in custody she said the prisoner had it; I do not remember seeing any money fall - I heard a woman say to the girl, "If you have got the money why don't you give it to him?" the girl was crying, and said she had not - she did not say she bad money, but not mine - this was in the room; I remember her going on her knees, and protesting she had not the money.
   HARRIET CLEVER. I was living with the prosecutor at the prisoner's house - he came from the India-house, and said he had a 5l. note and a sovereign; he went on the bed to lay down with me between four and five o'clock; the prisoner asked him to pay him - he said he would as soon as he got sober; the prisoner then left the room, and came in a little afterwards, while the prosecutor was asleep, and felt in his left pocket, took a pocket-book out, and a paper out of it - he then felt in his right-hand pocket, and took out a sovereign.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1816.  DENNIS SULLIVAN and JOHN KIRBY were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 2 iron gratings, value 3l., and 1 iron frame, value 2l., the goods of Christopher Richardson the elder, and others, the   Trustees and Commissioners of a certain Turnpike-road, there called the Commercial-road.
 2nd COUNT, stating the goods to be the property of  Christopher Richardson.
 3rd COUNT, stating the goods to be the conveniences belonging to the said road.
 FIVE OTHER COUNTS, varying the charge.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   JAMES BARNES. I am contractor for excavating the Commercial-road. I saw some grating and frames lying near Green Arbour-square, at six o'clock in the morning, on the 10th of September, that belonged to Mr. Richardson and the other Commissioner s - Sullivan worked for Mr. Rutter.
   JOHN COLLINS. I am a wheelwright, and live in Little Turner-street, in the Commercial-road. Sullivan came to my house last Friday week, about two or three o'clock, and said he wanted a truck to move some sailor's things from the Halfway-house to Church-lane; I let it him - I found it again at the Flying Horse-yard near the office.
   MATTHEW SKIPP. I deal in iron, and live in Church-lane, Commercial-road. Last Friday week I saw the two prisoners between four and five o'clock - they brought two iron gratings and one iron curb in a truck; Sullivan offered it for sale - I asked where they got it; he said he came from Mr. Robertson, the builder, at the back of the hospital - I said, "Why does not Mr. Robertson send a note that I might know who it came from?" Sullivan said I had no occasion to be afraid - I said I would know whose it was before I bought it; Kirby then said, "To tell you the truth, we picked it up in the Commercial-road to sell for some beer;" I said they might put it into the scale, and while they did that I saw the officer, and gave charge of them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you ever bought things of this kind before? A. No.
   THOMAS SHELSWELL. I am an officer. On the 10th of September, I was near Skipp's shop; he took me in - there were these two gratings and frame - I asked the prisoners if they belonged to them; Sullivan said, No; I asked Skipp's men who brought them, and one of them said the two prisoners - I then asked Sullivan where he got the truck; he said, "From Collins, a wheel wright;" I put the prisoners into two different lock-up rooms, then went to Sullivan and said, "I suspect they belong to the Commercial-road Trust;" he said, "You have just guessed it?" I then went to Kirby and asked him where they got it - he said he knew nothing about them - that he was employed by Sullivan.
 Cross-examined. Q. He said, "You have guessed it," not"You have guessed right?" A. No; and Kirby said he had helped the other - I asked them about it to get the truth.
