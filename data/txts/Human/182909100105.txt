 MR. BODKIN. Q. Was when you were before the Magistrate the first time you saw Dennington after that? A. Yes - the Magistrate pointed him out then; I had pointed him out myself before the Magistrate told me his name - the Magistrate then said his name was Dennington - there were four people at the office when I pointed him out; there were no more prisoners there - he stood at the end of the bar; there were other people standing at the end of the bar; I was not told the people who had been at our house were standing there - only one person besides him stood at the end of the bar; nobody had told me he was the man.
 COURT. Q. You opened the door to Dennington - did you continue in the shop, or go up stairs? A. I stood on the stairs, about an hour, while they were driving them in; the door opens into the shop - I could see the shop from the stairs; my uncle and father came into the shop; I called them while he was gone for the sheep - when they were driven in Dennington came into the shop, so did my uncle and father; I saw no other man came in, nor did I see any body outside; I stood on the stairs till they were driven in- I never saw thirty sheep come into my father's at any other time.
   JOHN LEGGATT. I keep the Green Man, Coal-yard, Drury-lane. I know the three prisoners. On Tuesday afternoon, the 4th of August, I saw the three prisoners and Holloway in company at my house; I changed a 5l. note for Newitt - he asked me to take four sovereigns to go out, and return, pretending I had been out to borrow it for him- he gave me no reason for that; he said he did not wish the men to know he had any more money; they were at my house four or five hours together, in company - I did not receive the reckoning, and do not know who paid; he told me to pretend I had borrowed the sovereigns of a man named Bonington, the landlord of the house he lives in; I went into the shop, came out, and carried four sovereigns to him in the tap-room.
 COURT. Q. When he gave you the 5l. note, where was he? A. In front of the bar; Williams and Dennington were in the tap-room. and could not hear or see any thing of it; when I brought the four sovereigns he was in the tap-room, sitting with them, and I gave them to him there- he asked me after I changed the 5l. note if I would take care of a good deal of money for him - I spoke to my wife, and she would have nothing to do with it; he had a good deal of money, and put it into his fob - he had between 100l. and 200l. about him, for he asked me if I would take care of between 100l. and 200l. for him.
   ANN KING. I am Leggatt's niece. On Monday, the 3rd of August, between one and two o'clock, Dennington and Williams came in together, and had beef-steaks at our house; they staid rather better than half an hour; I do not remember who paid - I never saw them before; I saw them again on Tuesday - Newitt and Williams were with them then; I do not know at what time they came on Tuesday.
   JESSY SURBY. I am a painter, and live at Mr. Bonington's Coal-yard, Drury-lane. Newitt lodged in the second floor back room, and I live in the first floor back room. On Monday, the 3rd of August, at three o'clock in the morning, there was a knocking at the door; I looked out of window and saw two persons - they were Williams and Dennington; they asked me if Mr. Newitt was at home - I think it was Williams spoke; I said I believed he was - I went up and called him; he came down, and went out about five minutes after I called him, but I cannot say whether they all went together - I went to hed again - I saw Newitt again, at home, about twelve o'clock that day; I saw him next day at the Green Man, Coal-yard, with Williams, Dennington, and Holloway - they were all drinking together that afternoon; I had seen Dennington and Williams before that morning, and had spoken to them five or six times before.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Newitt lived at the house? A. Yes; he is married. I saw Williams and Dennington go away when I gave them the answer at the window; I did not see Newitt go out - I saw him at his bed-room door when I called him; he appeared as if he had just got of bed - I did not stay at the window above a minute; I only gave the answer to each person - I cannot say whether Dennington and Williams came back and talked with Newitt at the street-door; I have heard Newitt complain of dropsy before this happened - he was very much swollen; I have not observed it affect him so as to prevent him making any great exertion - I heard him go up again, and dress himself afterwards, and go down again in five minutes; he was up stairs long enough to dress, and he went out - I did not see him go out, but I heard the street-door shut directly after he went down; I heard him go down when I called him, heard him go up again, and then down, and never heard him go up again.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. There can be no mistake about time? A. No - I heard the clock strike during the time I was out of bed; I do not suppose I was out of bed three minutes altogether - I do not burn a light; it was not very dark - the moon was -
 Q. Will you swear it was a moonlight morning? A. It was moonlight and daylight together, so that I could distinguish - I will not swear it was moonlight; I cannot say there was a moon - it was not clear daylight at the time.
 Q. Why did you say there was a moon? A. Because I could not recollect whether it was daylight or not; I did not see the moon, and will not swear there was one - it was so far daylight that I can swear to the prisoner's if you put them among 20,000 men; it was both daylight and moonlight together - it was not raining then; I did not look to see if the ground was et - one man stood answering me, and the other a little distance off; I only looked at the mens' faces, their appearance and dress - I took particular notice of them; I do not know whether there was a moon or not - I did not see it; it was between daylight and moonlight, because I know it was about the time the moon was up - my recollection is furnished by a knowledge of the state of the moon and the night; it was the third quarter - I am sure of that; I do not know when the moon was new - I calculate from the last time of the new moon, that it was the third quarter; I do not know when it was new, only from seeing it full on Sunday or Saturday night last - I will not swear it was the third quarter; I swear I have not sworn it was.
