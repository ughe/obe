Q. What were you quarrelling about when the glass was smashed? 4.
He wanted to go out, and wanted me to give him a few shillings, and I
would not-I am forty-three years old, and he is twenty-three-I am po-
sitive I never charged him with attempting to break open the drawer, and
take the money-nor ever quarrelled with him about it in the hearing of
the lodgers--we have had words the same as man and wife would have
when I counted my money on the 14th of November, I missed 301-not
more-801. in notes, and 381. in gold, was left-that would make 1482 in
all.
Q. I thought you said just now that from the time you counted it on
the 31st of October till you missed this none had been taken out, is that
so? A. Yes.
COURT. Q. You said you had 1601. how do you account for the dif-
ference? A. When we went to the house we paid 91, or 10l. away in
bills, which I can produce that was out of the 1601.--I had no other
money-I say when I counted my money on the 29th of October there
was 1601., and there was the same when I counted it again on the 31st-
it was after that I paid some away.
MR. RYLAND. Q. Have you not stated that between the 31st of
October and the 14th of November you had never been to the bag at
all ? A. I had not been to the bag at all, only in the presence of
the prisoner's mother-I was never out of tbe room-no money had
been taken out of the bag between the 31st of October and the 14th
of November-on the Tuesday after I met with the accident I gave
my husband five sovereigns to pay for painting the bar, a sovereign to
buy him a pair of boots, and a sovereign to pay for moving the goods--I had
some medicine for myself, and a lotion for my knee, and a bit of calico,
and I paid the prisoner's mother 11. 4s. for rent-I calculate that I spent
about 101. altogether for every thing—that 101. was part of the 160l.,
and came out of the bag after the 31st of October, and after the 6th of
November-1 did not count my money on the 6th of November--my bus-
band was at home at the time I sent the prisoner up stairs for the money.
Q. Why not send him to fetch it ? A. He was not in the room at the
time-he was up stairs in the room where the money was- I was in bed,
and not able to go up stairs myself, and I asked the prisoner as a favour
to go and get it for me-I did not think it necessary to ask her to give
my husband the key to get it-I placed implicit confidence in her: not
more I think than I did in him-I sent for it because I wanted some
money, and when the prisoner's mother brought it down I took out half-a-
sovereign-I did not count the remainder.
Mr. Phillips. Q. Was anybody ever present when you counted your
money? A. The prisoner and her mother were on the morning of the
29th of October-I counted it in the parlour before them-between the
6th and the 14th of November I went to the bag and took a half-sovereign
out, but did not count the money-I did not disturb it.
COURT. Q. Do you mean to say that during the whole time your hus-
band never had access to your money-bag? A. No, what I thought
proper he should have I gave him-- I had the keys of the drawer where it
was, in my possession--1 always kept the key about me, and wben I went
to bed I put it in my pocket under my head.
