I was not on the Finance Committee. During the three years 1904,
1905, 1906, I was on the same Committees; in 1907 every member
of the Board was on every Committee. As to the indestructible paint,
report was brought up by the Committee, and the chairman, ir
moving the 1s. 6d, a yard, stated that they had had a letter saying
he had to shift the beds, and in consequence of the material being
more than the tender, we thought 1s. 6d. was right. Mr. Frarik Bryan
spoke very strongly as to the unemployed, and I thought I was acting
rightly, and the Clerk did not say we were doing wrong. Bryan took
the view that the ur.employed workmen were expensive workmen.
With regard to the dining hall, the reason I voted for Calcutt was
this: There had been an order given to have the ceiling painted and
Mr. Knight gave the order to Caloutt and allowed him to start; wher.
it came before the Board he said, “Well, gentlemen, Calcutt has
suaried this work, there is a defective cornice, and this work will cos!
over 1€50." I believe he said, "I have got the prices from Caleutt."
and told them that his price was ninety odd pounds. They said, "As
the man has started the work, we will have to let him do it," ard
so he had the order, although it was advertised. I have here a list!
of my attendances when Calcutt's contract came up. Mr. Chapple
and I went down at the time the Inquiry was on to see what we had
been votiug for, and asked for those particulars, and so we are both
mentioned there together. There are 13 attendances on contracts,
and three annual days. The first two years I was on the Board of
Guardiars I was very regular in my attendance; after that, I was
elected on the Stepney Borough Council and was not able to keep
up so good an attendance on the Board of Guardians. It is not true
to say that I always voted for Caleutt. I have voted for others or
several occasions; twice for Jackson, once for Pearce, once or twice for
Hubbard, and for Franks and Simons. Those are all I can remember
at the moment. Mr. Poiteau, who, I think, was elected in 1905,
on my introduction, was one of the members who was active for
reform. On March 15, 1906, he moved that no son or near relative
of the Board should receive a contract. I seconded that, and on Sep-
tember 8, 1904, I moved that no further houses should be purchased
for the Seattered Homes. I have always endeavoured to do my duty
to the ratepayers, and was never aware that there was any corrupt
practice with the Guardians at all. With regard to Caloutt's state-
ment that I used to receive £10 quarterly, I have never received any
such payment; and, with regard to the clock, I was married on Feb-
ruary 24, 1904, and about a fortnight afterwards he gave us this
clook as a wedding present. After I was on the Board of Guardians
I continued to supply Calcutt with clothes; I also made clothes for
his father, his sons, and some of his workmen to the value of about
£78 in four years; I never regarded those transactions as being in
the nature of a bribe, and never charged him a penny more than the
ordinary prices. On one occasion, I think it was October or No-
vember, 1904, he had coat made for Trott. I did not regard that
