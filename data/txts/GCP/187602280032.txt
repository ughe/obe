BARNABAS RILEY. I am a tobacconist, of 278, High Street, Poplar
the 26th February, after 10 o'clock at night, I was at the Iron Bridge
public-house, Barking Road, with a friend—I saw the prisoner there doing
sleight-of-band work and different kinds of tricks-I have known him bi
sight many years; be is what they call a sharper-he is not a particular
friend of mine, but he made himself an acquaintance-I serve thirty &
forty public houses--I had had more drink than was quite right-I went
out of the public-house, leaving him there, but he was very quick after me
-he ran after me as hard as he could with a little man about 5 feet high-
I ran into Mr. Forsyth's shop, and remained there till I got him to go home
with me and bring a little bit of a truncheon-the prisoner had not spoken
to me nor I to him-we had to go 600 or 700 yards to my house-we were
talking, and I did not see the prisoner-Mr. Forsyth left me at the bottom
of Robin Hood Lane, and I went on alone-I opened my side door with my
latch-key, and as soon as I had done so the prisoner said “ Ain't you going
to bid us good-night, old chap?" and he threw me down with one hand and
tok my watch and pocket-book-my wife and my boy spoke to me at my
door, but I did not go in; I ran to the police station with as much strength
as I had left-I could not speak, but I was told something-my pooket
book was returned to me next morning, but I have not seen my watch
again.
