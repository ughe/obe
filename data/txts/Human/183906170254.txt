   MR. BODKIN Q.  You have been asked about the door and its thick ness; How high was it? A.  As near as I can state, from six to seven feet high, and over it were some short bars—there was no window—it was an open space. JOHN HARDWICK, ESQ I am one of the police Magistrates at the office in Lambeth-street. I have been so about seventeen years—the examina tion of the prisoners and the witnesses in this case, was conducted before me—I am the committing Magistrate—I remember some papers being pre sented to me, which I took into my private room—I had had no communi cation with the parties to whom the statement alluded before they were put into my hands—they were put in publicly in the office in the presence of the counsel and attorney—I took these statements into my private room at the request of the counsel, which was made publicly—it was publicly stated to be for the purpose of enabling me to ascertain whether, in my discretion, I should receive the testimony of Solomon as a witness for the Crown—the prisoners were present, and the counsels and attorneys on all sides, and the office full of people—I desired the clerk to accompany me to the pri vate room—the attorney of Solomon was present in my private room—the statements were in the hands of the clerk, and I believe were read by the clerk in the presence of the young Solomon and Solomon's attorney—each of the persons who had made statements were brought into the room to me by themselves, and each of the statements were read to me without the interference of any body—the statements were read to each separately, and the others were not in the room at the time—after that had been done, and I had come to my conclusion as to the course to be taken, the persons left the private room, and were taken into the office, and there a viva voce  examination by counsel took place publicly.
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  You say that these papers were delivered to you publicly in the public office? A.  Yes—I do not know whether any of the prisoners or their counsel or solicitors had the means of ascertaining one word of their contents—all that passed was, the coun sel requested the Magistrates would take certain papers into their hands and retire into their room, and there read them—none of the counsel of any of the prisoners had any means of knowing one syllable that they contained—the counsel and attorneys for the prosecution retired with me—
