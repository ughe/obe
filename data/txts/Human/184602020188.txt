   Cross-examined. Q.  About what time did you see the prisoners first? A.  About a quarter to ten o'clock—I saw them again on my beat at a quarter before twelve, crossing from one side of the road to the other, directly oppo site where the property was found—Mr. Willis's is 800 or 900 yards from where I saw them cross. JOHN WOOD( police-constable P  131.) About twelve o'clock that night I saw Hastings and two other men, but I cannot identify the others—about as hour afterwards I found eight fowls and two geese, a sack, two walking-sticks, and a saw—I pointed out to Hayter where I found them. GEORGE RUSSEN( police-constable P  529.) About nine o'clock in the morning I took the shoes off Hastings at the station, and went to Mr. Willis with them—I traced foot-marks from Mr. Willis's to within three yards of where this property was found—the foot-marks corresponded with Hastings' shoes.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You prove the shoe-marks? A.  Yes, these are Hastings' shoes—I found the shoe-marks from the prosecutor's premises across another gentleman's premises to within three or four yards of where the property was found—I put the shoe in the marks and I found they actu ally corresponded—there was one place where he had trod in some cow dung and here are the remains of the cow-dung left on the shoe—Mr. Hayter was with me. JOHN HAYTER( re-examined. ) I saw the officer examine these marks—they correspond with one place in particular, in the cow-dung—I saw the mark in that cow-dung before the officer put the shoe into the hole, and it corresponded with the cow-dung.
   Cross-examined. Q.  That is, the cow-dung on the ground corresponded with the other cow-dung? A.  Yes, there was the impression of part of the cow-dung on the shoe—previous to the policeman putting the shoe in, I saw the same dung on the shoe—the officer put the shoe into the impression of the cow-dung—I cannot remember whether it was a wet night.
   COURT . Q.  Did you observe this footmark in the dung before the shoe was put into it? A.  Yes, there were marks of the cow-dung removed from the earth, and the dung appeared to be on the same spot on the shoe—it appeared to correspond exactly—the officer called my attention to it.
   JURY . Q.  Were there footmarks of other persons? A.  There were foot marks of more than one—the cow-dung is here on the inside of the shoe—there were other footmarks near to where the property was found, but there was a bank near there, and I could not trace the marks further—the feet had slipped.
   COURT to DAVID WILKES . Q.  Are you able to identify the other two men who were with Hastings; you did not before the Magistrate? A.  Yes, the prisoners are the two men—a female told me their names, and that in duced me to look at their faces. NOT GUILTY.
