EDWARD GODLONTON, 40, Coleridge road, Walthamstone, meter tester, employed by the Gas Light and Coke Company. I have sons allotment ground near Folly Lane, but I cannot see Mr. Brown's field from those allotments, as it runs rather low there. On the even ing of July 11 I was engaged on my allotment from half past six till nine. Three men passed who struck my attention, because every time I looked to they were looking towards me, and they bring strangers I thought it rather suspicions. They were going towards Mr. Brown's farm. One was carrying a braided affair which looked very much like a halter. I watched them till they were out or sight. On the morning of the 14th Mr. Brown came and told me of his loss. On August 9, at Stoke Newington Station, out of about 20 men, I picked out Friend as one of the men I saw pass my ground I have no doubt he is the man. ALBERT ETHERTON, 15, Russell Road, Dorking, railway porter. I remember two horses arriving at Dorking Station in the middle of July, at 11.53 a.m., consigned from London Bridge by train leaving at 10.38. Luck, who had been waiting in front of the station, paid for their carriage. I saw prisoner Friend there. He asked me where Mr. Luck was as I was uncoupling the horse box. I told him he had gone into the booking office to sign for the horses, and as I looked up I saw him coming down the platform. Luck and Friend led the horses away. On August 8 I identified Friend at Dorking Police Station.

I first met Luck in January last in connection with buying a horse which he advertised for sale. On July 19 I re ceived a letter from him offering "A good, useful vanner, quiet in every way, six years old. If your committee is looking for a good, genuine animal they will do well to see this. Price 35 guiness." The matter was placed before the committee of my society, and I was deputed, in conjunction with our horsekeeper, to go and see this horse, and we went to Luck's residence in Pixham Lane. He asked 60 as for two horses, and ultimately I decided to give him £35 for the brown mare and offered him £25 for the gelding. I made out a re ceipt, at Mr. Luck's request, as follows: "Received of Mr. L. W. Fooks the sum of £3 as part payment of bay mare for the Bromley Co-operative Society." I stamped it and got Luck to sign it. Subse quently he was notified to send the other horse on for trial for a few weeks, but he informed us that he had loaned it to his Geomenry officer for a fornight. It did not strike me to ask Luck where he got either of these horses. I was at his place early in January last, and I said to him, "Our society is frequently in the habit of buying horses of good character, and any time you know of a good one let us know."
