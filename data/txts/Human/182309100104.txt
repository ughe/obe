JOHN PORTER. I am servant to Edward Roberts, linen-draper. On the 9th of September, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to my master's shop. A pane of glass was broken, and the flannel drawn through the glass. Lane alarmed me; I ran out, and, at the corner of Prince's-street, found the prisoner in custody.
   JOSEPH LANE. I was near the shop. I live in Queen-street, Oxford-street. I saw the prisoner drawing the flannel through the hole in the window. He ran off. I followed, and saw him stopped; he dropped it immediately.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met a boy in Oxford-street, who stopped at the shop, and gave the flannel into my hand; half of it being out; he then threw it down, and I picked it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Confined Three Months.
 1250.  MARY JOHNSTON was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of September , a chair, value 5 s., the goods of Thomas Ankins.
   THOMAS ANKINS. I am a plaisterer, and live in Red Lion-street, Spitalfields. On the 3d of September, I had five chairs at my door, to be sold. I saw the prisoner. about one hundred yards off, with one of them, trying to hide it with her gown. I went up and took her. She said nothing, but appeared intoxicated.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 35. Confined One Week.
 1251.  FRANCIS TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , a saw, value 5 s., and a rule, value 2 s., the goods of Joseph Gosbell ; and a saw, value 5 s., the goods of John Birchmore.
   WILLIAM TARRANT. I am a carpenter. On the 10th of September, I was in Felton-street, Hoxton, and saw the prisoner in some buildings in Harvey-street. I went to the house, and took him on the staircase, with two saws in his hand. He got up on the floor. I asked what business he had there. He threw the saws down, and jumped out of the first floor window. I ran round and caught him. He denied having anything else, but as we went along he dropped a rule, which I picked up, and delivered to Gosbell.
   JOSEPH GOSBELL. I am a carpenter. I was employed in this house. The rule and one of the saws are mine. I found the saw in the well-hole.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 38. Transported for Seven Years.
 1252.  EDWARD BLUETT was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM WALKER GILBERTSON. I am a baker, and live in Upper George-street, Bryanstone-square. The prisoner was in my service for six weeks, and employed to deliver bread and receive the money. Mr. Sagden owed me 9 s. 3 d., for which the prisoner never accounted. He left me, without notice, on the 13th of August, and was taken ten days after.
   SAMUEL SAGDEN. I owed the prosecutor 9 s. 3 d., which I paid - I cannot say whether I paid the prisoner himself, or sent my servant to do it; but I produce the bill and receipt.
 MR. GILBERTSON. This is in the prisoner's writing.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Six Months.
 1253.  MARY RYELL was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , twenty-three yards of cotton, value 20 s., the goods of John Augustus Brook , privately in his shop.
   PETER KING. I am apprentice to John Augustus Brooks, a linen-draper, of Parliament-street. On the 6th of September, between three or four o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came to the shop, to match a pattern of blue printed cotton - I said we had none, and as she went out, I saw a piece of printed cotton under her arm, which had laid on a pile inside the door. I followed her into another shop, and as she came out, I asked her for the print that was under her arm - she said,
 "Take it, and let me go, see how distressed I am." I gave her in charge.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 32. Confined Three Months.

 1254.  JOHN HANISEL was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , a watch, value 3 l. 10 s., the goods of John James Harris , in his dwelling-house; and JACOB LEVY was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to be stolen.
   JOHN JAMES HARRIS. I rent a house in Upper Nightingale-lane, Bishopsgate without, and am a watchmaker. On the 10th of September, my shopman came to inform me that a watch was stolen - I examined, and missed a customer's watch off my shop board. I gave information the afternoon after it was stolen. Hanisel was brought to my house, about two o'clock - I immediately went in pursuit of Levy; I met him in East Smithfield, collared him, and told him there was a valuable watch stolen from my shop, by Hanisel, his lodger, and he had received it from him, and given him 8 s. for it - he protested solemnly that he never saw it. He was brought before Hanisel, at my house, and said,
 "Oh! you wicked youth, for charging me with the robbery, you know I know nothing of it, I have received no watch from you at all, and did not know you had one." Hanisel answered,
 "Oh! Levy, you know you waited at the window, while I took the watch out, and you gave me 8 s. for it when I brought it out, and said it was an old watch, and worth no more;" he still denied all knowledge of it. The servant came in, and said she saw Levy at the window - he at last said,
 "I wish you would let me speak to Brant;" he went into the kitchen with him; then came out, and said,
