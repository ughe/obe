JOHN BAILEY. I am a warehouseman to S. More and Son, surgical-instru-
ment makers and dealers in druggists' sundries-here are three kinds of
oiled-silk; this is the commonest and cheapest; it is of French manufacture
--the cost of it is 68. 20. a piece, buying twenty-five or thirty pieces at a
time-the French is not so apt to stick together as the English, but it is
very rotten-there is no English here-this green is an unusual thing; it is
French manufacture, and not the ordinary kind--the peculiarity is the
colour; the fabric, which is a fine muslin, is dried before it is oiled- I am
not aware that this green can be bought in the ordinary way; it is about
the same value as the white, which is 98 the piece wholesale--the piece
measures 47 yards.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you not given Mr. Laing more than 98. A.
We might; lut in that case we should only buy a few pieces-I should
expect to pay about 108. then; but we are in the habit of buying it direct
from Paris-118. would not be an unfair price for the green, 68. 2d. for the
common, and 78. 6d. for the orange.
MR. GIFFARD. Q. If compelled to purchase, you would be compelled to
pay what the seller chose to ask A. Yes; but as we could buy it in
several places, we should not think of paying more than 78. 6d. I think,
apart from the colour, the green would be the same value as the white.
MR. METCALFE 0. Have you ever seen any green before? A No-I
think the object of giving it that colonr is to make it resemble some of the
English silk.
MR. GIFFARD. Q. Does it resemble the English in quality ? A. Not at
all; it is in every respect French, in make and material ; the English is
considerably wider and has a green appearance in the quantity-no one who
understood it would mistake this for English.
