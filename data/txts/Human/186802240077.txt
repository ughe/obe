I am inspector of the Surrey Docks—no vessel called the Annie  came into the docks on 3rd February—there is no vessel or barge of that name there at all—I should know if there was—I have got books here to refer to.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know a good deal about the docks? A.  Yes—there are men about who take persons to lodging-houses; they are called crimps .
   Watkins to A. C MILLS . Q.  Was it not 5 s . you paid the men for me? A.  No; 10 s .—there were no clothes in the bag, only rubbish and rags—you asked me for the clothes—I did not say the clothes you had on were too bad, and I would give you some more—you said you wanted to go out for a walk, and asked me to let you have some clothes to go with in the evening. NEHEMIAH SHEARING( Policeman K  179). Watkins was given in my charge by Mr. Mills—he said he did not know the gentleman, and had never seen him before. HERBERT BUTT( Police Sergeant K  62). On 6th February I appre hended Much and Brown—I told them they were charged with being concerned with a man named Watkins, in obtaining money and clothes by false pretences—they said they knew nothing about it, and that would not be enough to keep them there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you say they were concerned with Watkins? A.  With a man named Watkins—they said they did not know who he was.
   WATKINS  — GUILTY.— Six Months' Imprisonment.
