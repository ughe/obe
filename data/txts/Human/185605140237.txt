three times within the last four or five years—some of these characters that you defend say I am a sort of amateur policeman—I never receive any money for it, not for my expenses—I have never pocketed my expenses; I have always given them to the officer in the case—I do it on public grounds—I have never made any mistake—it is not true that a woman was wrongly convicted on my evidence at Manchester, and after wards pardoned—I do not recollect any woman being convicted there of picking pockets on my evidence, and being afterwards pardoned, because it was found out that I was wrong—I never heard of it—I will not swear it is not true—I have not given evidence a dozen times at Manchester; perhaps three or four times; it might be five—I will not swear it was not half a dozen; I will swear it was not a dozen—I have given evidence a great many times at Liverpool, perhaps twenty or thirty—I do not know how many times in London; two or three times, perhaps—you may not see these things; perhaps you have not the observation—I lived at Liverpool seven teen years, and have received the thanks of the Magistrates, and of the Judges—I left Liverpool to suit myself; I prefer London in every way as a place for business—I have been before Mr. Maude, the Magistrate, at Man chester; he knows me very well; it is an infamous fabrication to say that Mr. Maude ever told me that he wished not to see me there again, and that the police were much better without my services than with them—he never said anything of the kind; he has thanked me once or twice from the bench—any expenses I might get I hare always given to the officer employed in the case—I have never received any expenses in London; I have not been a witness here—I do not recollect the names of any officers in Liverpool or Manchester to whom I have handed over my expenses.
