1918. ELIZABETH COX, alias Williams, alias Burrows, was indicted for
bigamy.
CAROLINE Cox. I live in Waterloo-place, Church-street, Chelsea, and am
single. On the 26th of February last, I was present at the parish-church of
St. Luke, Chelsea, when my brother, John Richard Cox, was there married to
the prisoner-he was a single man—the prisoner was married to him by the
name of Elizabeth Burrows—she described herself as single—they were mar-
ried by banns, by Mr. Kingsley tbe clergyman of Chelsea-my brother is now
living—he was at the time he was married a servant in Charles-street, Gros-
venor-square-I had seen the prisoner before that day, but I had no acquaint-
ance with ber—I have heard she had been a nurse-maid at Mrs. Pagh's, in
Regent-street-when they were married they went home to No. 17, Manor-
street, King's-road, Chelsea—I went and remained there that day, but did not
stay that night—they had apartments there—I saw them there the next morn-
ing-my brother was not out of a situation then—he returned to his situation
the next morning, and the prisoner stopped at home at Manor-street-my
brother was porter at a large milliner's—he slept there and had charge of the

PETER SPURR PARKINS. I am deputy-clerk of the parish of Chelsea-I
produce a certificate of the marriage of John Richard Cox to Elizabeth Bar-
rows—I have examined this with the register-book myself—I recollect the
fact of the marriage of the parties perfectly well—I was present—their names
were put up by banns, and published on three consecutive Sundays-(read)
-"John Richard Cox, batchelor, and Elizabeth Burrows, spinster, were
married in the parish church of St. Luke, Chelsea, after banns by me, Charles
Kingsly, on the 26th of February, 1843."
Prisoner. He is not my husband, I never slept with Mr. Cox.
CAROLINE Cox re-examined. They slept together one night I know that
by seeing them there when I went up on the Monday morning between eight
and nine-I had left them together between ten and eleven the night before-
the landlady was in the house also--I found them up and dressed the next
morning-I breakfasted with them-whether they had staid in that house all
night I cannot tell—the prisoner took the lodging-she was living there before
the marriage for a little time I had seen her there occasionally when I came
home–I dined and drank tea with them on the day of the marriage--my
father and mother were there they went away between ten and eleven at
night-the marriage had been talked of in the family for five or six months
