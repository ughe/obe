BOKENHAM, Philip Fleming (82, printer); all conspiring and
agreeing together and with divers other persons to print and cause to
be printed for sale divers sheets of musio in which there was then
subsisting copyright, without the consent of the proprietors thereof,
aud unlawfully conspiring to sell and having in their possession for
sale the said music without such consent, and conspiring together to
defraud and injure the said proprietors and to deprive them of the
profits arising from their property in the said copyright McCarthy,
Kidd, and Walsh were also indicted for larceny.
Mr. R. D. Muir and Mr. Houston appeared for the prosecution;
3r. H. Black and Mr. A. C. Fox Davies defended Kidd, McCarthy,
and Walsh.
(THE CASE AGAINST KIDD AND WALSH TOR LARCENY.)
Mr. Muir submitted for the prosecution that the selling or conversion of the various
pieces of music by Kidd and Walsh amounted to laroeny at common law. The
Musical Copyright Aet of 1906 was passed because the Act of 1902 had been found
ineffectual, and it had been found necessary to prosecute persons acting in combine
tion in the disposal of pirated music, for conspiracy. The offence could only be
proved where a number of persons were found Acting together in a criminal combi
nation. The method of prosecution had been upbeld, both before the learned judge
presiding in this Court and before Mr. Justice Walton at the Leeds Assizes. The
system of fines provided for by the Act of 1906 not having proved effectual recourse
was now bad to the provisions of the Act of 1842, 5 and 6 Victoria, cap. 45. By!
virtue of that Act it was contended that a person dealing with pirated copies, though
the ownership was never vested in the true owners of the copyright, committed
trespass, of which remedy was open by action, and the person committing such a
trespass, if he converted the property to his own use by selling it committed
felonious trespass and a larceny at common law. If, for example, a person accident
