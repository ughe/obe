EDWIN BENJAMIN GAMMON. I am clerk to Mr. Thomas Grissell-the
prisoner was in his employ as carman, to take things to the buildings
on Tuesday, 16th Nov., I received from Attride from fifty to a bundred
small pieces of paper rolled up this is one of them-it has T. G., and a
cross on it, and is Attride's writing-I threw the pieces of paper in the coru-
bin with the mixed food of oats, split beans, and clover-chaff-the beans were
Egyptian beans, and from that bin I saw the prisoner fill his nose-bags,
and then take about a bushel and a half in a sack that was not regular-he
should have gone to the man for that he would have been allowed to have
some for his horses during the day-I have seen the beans produced from the
bin at the Swan--they were Egyptian beans--there was clover-chaff in that,
and a small quantity of meadow-hay, but none in ours when the officer
brought the prisoner to me, he asked if I would allow him to remain at home
that night-I told him I had no authority to do so, as he had been seen to
leave his corn two days-he said he had len a little, but it should not occur
again I told him I was very sorry he had been so foolish, after being with
Us 80 many years.

Cross-examined. Q. Had you been asking him about his leaving coke!
4. I think I also told him he had left coke, and had left bis corn two days,
and then he said he had left some, or left a little-we do not allow our men
to buy and sell coke, it would be contrary to our orders----we allow the
prisoner and other men to take home a sack or two in their cart when they
have done work-I put the papers in the bin about a quarter past six o'clock
in the morning, and about half-past six the corn was taken out-I saw the
prisoner return bome on Wednesday-nothing had been said to him when
he went out on Thursday, he had brought home some feed on Thursday,
but none on Tuesday or Wednesday.
