RICHARD STUBBERFIELD. On the 12th of February, I was in the ser-

ELIZABETH Coombs. I am in the service of Mr. Kirk. On Sunday,
the 13th of February, I was in the kitchen, and saw a hat-box in the
kitchen between twelve and one o'clock-the prisoner took two bottles of
gin, a soda-water and ginger-beer bottle out of this bat-box, and put
them under two rusty old covers on the next shell-1 told my master,
and he desired me to watch him-I did so I afterwards saw him take the
ginger-beer bottle of gin out and put it into his pocket-be went a second
time, and took the soda-water bottle and put it into his pocket-my master
stopped him, and asked him what he had got-he said, " Nothing.".com
SAMUEL WRIGHT (police-constable P 172.) I took the prisoner about
nine o'clock in the evening, on the 14th of February--in going along, he
said this half-a-pint of gin he had, was ordered by a man that lived at
Mr. Nicholls's-he did not tell me his name-he said it was ordered on the
Saturday, but he was too late for it, and he was going to take it up un
the Sunday, that he had bought it of Mrs. Kirk, and paid her for it.
SAMPSON DARKIN CAMPBELL (police-inspector.) The prisoner was
brought before me. I told him what he was taken for-he said he had
purchased the half-pint of gin at the time he had two pots of beer, that
it was for Mr, Nicholls's house, he had been too late to take it on the Satur-
day, and he was going to take it on the Sunday at the station he said it
was not for Mr. Nicholls, but a person in Mr. Nicholls's employ.
