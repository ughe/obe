JOHN CARPENTER (police-constable R 84.) I went to Mr. Tuck about
nine o'clock at night, on the 30th of April-I waited till the prisoner came
home, and Mr. Tuck charged her on suspicion of robbing him-she said,
"Me rob him--what of?” -I asked her to give me the key of her box,
she took it from her pocket and gave it to me-- the box was searched in
her presence, and a great number of things were found in it-amongst the
rest these six towels, this necklace, these duplicates, and other things-I
afterwards went to the house of a person at Lewisham, where a child of the
prisoner's is, and there I found this wine-glass, basin, and egg-cup, and a
handkerchief which Mr. Tuck identifies-after the prisoner had been
searched at the station-house she said, "I am very sorry for what I have
done ; I hope my master will not come against me."

John CARPENTER (police-constable R 84.) I searched the prisoner's
box in her presence at Mr. Tuck's—I found in it one towel, the pillow.
case, right-gown, and some other things which Mrs. Nugent identifies-
at the place where the prisoner's child was, I found this other towel and a

Prisoner. They were all put into my box ; I never put them there.
Guilty. Aged 27.- Transported for Seven Years.
John CARPENTER stated that other property was found in the prisoner's
box belonging to four other persons with whom she had lived, and he had
two certificates of character by which the prisoner obtained situations,
