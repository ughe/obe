2856. EDWARD INDE THURGOOD was indicted for stealing, on the
5th of Oct., 3 post letters, containing two half-sovereigns and 1s., the pro-
perty of her Majesty's Postmaster-general; he being employed under the
Post-office.
MESSRS. SHEPHERD and ADOLPHUS conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM Blott, I am a clerk in the Inland department of the General
Post-office; the prisoner was so also. On Thursday evening, the 5th of Oet
I was employed in the Exeter and Devonport division I went to him, and
said I wanted him in Mr. Bockenham's room-he went there, I followed him
-Mr. Wynne was there, and said to him, “Thurgood, it has been communi
cated to me, that you have been seen to put a letter into your pocket "-he
said he had not done so-Mr. Wynne asked him if he had any objection to
be searched, in order to clear away all suspicion-he said, “None whatever
-Mr. Wynne asked me if an officer was in attendance, I said, "Yes," I let
the room to fetch one, met Peak at the door, and he and I returned together
-Mr. Wynne then said, in the prisoner's presence and hearing, " Blott, he
has thrown the letters into the corner"-he pointed to the corner at the time
I went to the corner, and picked up three letters, crumpled up, and handed
them to Mr. Wynne-these are them-(produced)-1 told the prisoner be
might say what he pleased, but whatever he said might be used against him
- he said nothing, and in the course of a minute fainted away-the letters were
afterwards opened in my presence at Bow-street--they were in the same state
as when I picked them up two of them contained half a sovereign each, and
one a shilling-when I picked them up, I did not feel or discover whether
there was anything in them I handed them immediately to Mr. Wynne-this
letter is addressed to Miss A. Ford-it bears the Fulham post-mark, and the
Inland post-mark of 5th Oct.-the second one, addressed to Mr. Cane, bears the
post-mark of the Great Surrey-street receiving-office, and the Inland office
paid stamp of the evening of the 5th Oct. ---the third bears the St. Alban's
post-mark of the 5th Oct., and the Inland office paid stamp of the same day-
the letters might pass through the prisoner's hands in the course of his duty
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Would they in the ordinary course

maniac" - I have heard that in the Post-office.
