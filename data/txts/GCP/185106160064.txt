1348. ELIZABETH ROCKALL, stealing 3 lbs. of pork, value 1s. 6d.;
the goods of George Hollingdale: having been before convicted.
GEORGE HOLLINGDALE. I live in Red-lion-street, Holborn. On Satur-
day, 7th June, about 12 o'clock at night, the prisoner came to my shop, and
wanted a penny rasher of bacon-I saw ber take something, and said, "What
bave you got under your clothes ?"-she said, "Nothing"-I lifted up her
clothes, and found a hand of pork there, my property-I had seen it just
before in the window; I charged her with stealing it, and she went down on
her knees, and hoped I would forgive her.
WILLIAM Toomes (policeman, E 150). I took the prisoner-on the way
to the station, she said the prosecutor would not prosecute ber.
Prisoner's Defence. I picked it up about half a yard from the door; if I
had stolen it, I should not have gone into the shop.
(The prisoner pleaded guilty to the previous conviction.)
GUILTY. Aged 27.-Confined Twelve Months.
1349. LEONORA WEYMOUTH, was indicted for bigamy.
Mr. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLOTTE BILLINGTON. I live at Friar-street, Horsleydown. I was
present, in March, 1845, when the prisoner was married to Frederick Wey-
mouth-I saw him alive on 6th June.
Cross-examined by Mr. Horry. Q. Did you go to witness the marriage ?
A. Yes; at Mr. Weymouth's request--I did not see him after the marriage
till this affair-I was applied to by Mr. Lewis, or his clerk, to attend before
the Magistrate-he was the solicitor for the prosecution.
FRANCES BOBBIT. I am the wife of James Bobbit, of Bryan-street, Cale-
donian-road. I produce a certificate of Valladier's marriage with the pri-
soner produced)-I got it from a church in the Minories.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known Valladier? Since the
beginning of 1849, when he came to lodge at my house, with the prisoner
they lived there about four months, as man and wife--I had known the pri-
soner about twelve months, and understood she had been married to Mr.
Weymouth; they dined at my house with Mr. Weymouth's father once, as
man and wife-Mr. Weymouth went to live at Dover-street, Kennington-
I never saw Weymouth and Valladier togetber.

Strand. In Nov. 1848, I was married to the prisoner, at Trinity Church in

Viscount Beresford was her guardian, and that she had never been married
I received this letter produced) from her-I know it to be her writing-

twenty months.
