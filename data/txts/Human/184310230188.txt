3069. HENRY JONES was indicted for stealing, on the   25th of Oct ., 2 pewter pots, 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of William Coomber ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. WILLIAM COOMBER. I am a licensed victualler, and live in Dorset-street I swear these two pewter pots are mine—the prisoner had no right to take them. JOHN HADDOCK. I was going down Harley-street last Wednesday even ing—I saw the prisoner stoop down an area and take a quart pot—he put it into his pocket—he went into the Park, took it out, took a knife out, stooped, and appeared to be cutting it—he then returned, and took two pint pots from an area—I gave information to an officer.
   Prisoner. Q.  What are you? A.  A labourer—I have worked for Mr. Grant, a coachmaker in Hatton-garden—I have been convicted of pot stealing myself. WILLIAM JONES( police-constable D  59.) I received information, and took the prisoner—I found on him these three quart pots and these two pints.
   Prisoner.  Haddock was the person who induced me to do anything of the kind; and he was on this very occasion after the same property; if I had not taken it he would. JOHN SPIERS( police-constable D  142.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 48.— Transported for Seven Years .
 (There were two other indictments against the prisoner.)
 3070. MARY RYAN was indicted for stealing, on the   14th of Oct ., 1 box, value 3 s  .; 12 yards of lace, value 1 s  .; 3 collars, 3 s  .; 6 ostrich fea thers, 1 s  .; and 1 tea-caddy, 2 s  .; the goods of Mina Wood . MINA WOOD. I am single, and live in Queen's-place, Great Queen-street; the prisoner was employed by me. On the 14th of Oct. I found her outside my door—I looked at her very hard—she said, "Don't look at me; I have got nothing belonging to you"—I said, "I did not say that you had"—I sent my ser vant out for an errand—she came back and said did I give the woman two boxes—I went after the prisoner, and found her with two boxes under her apron—I asked her what she had got, she said, "Nothing"—these are them—she had no right to them—I had placed them under my bed.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  What was she employed as? A.  A charwoman—she had got about eleven yards from my door—I keep a lodging-house—I do not inquire what my lodgers' friends are—I do not know what my lodgers do—mine is not a house of ill-fame.
   JURY . Q.  Who admitted her into the house? A.  There is a latch and a handle, which must be turned—I am quite sure my door was shut THOMAS TICKNER( police-constable F  11.) I took the prisoner at the prosecutrix's house.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The boxes were in the house? A.  Yes, all the prisoner said was, "You are quite mistaken"—I know nothing about the house, it is not on my beat. MARY HEIFY. I went out on an errand, and met the prisoner coming out with two boxes—I asked her if she had seen my mistress—she said, "Yes, it is all right"—I had cleared the room before, and saw the box under the bed. GUILTY. Aged 40.— Confined Six Months .
