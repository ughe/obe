PATRICK SMITH. I am a ballast-heaver. On the 5th of June, I was standing at Ratcliff-cross, and saw Connor going down the ballast-office passage—I saw a cinder strike him in the head as he was going down—I did not see who threw it—the prisoner was standing outside with his back to the wall—he said, "Connor, come out"—some other words passed between them which I did not hear—I saw Connor turn his head round to wards the prisoner—the prisoner stepped out from the wall, and hit him with his right-hand on the left ear—I did not see Connor strike at the prisoner before the prisoner struck him—Connor fell down, and when he was lying down, the prisoner walked towards where he was lying, changed colour and shed tears, and said he was very sorry for it—I saw no other blow than I have mentioned—Connor appeared in liquor—I did not suppose he was angry when he went up to the prisoner—when he fell his head went on one of the paving stones of the street—he was carried to the doctor's shop. JOHN POWER. I saw part of the transaction—I did not see the blow struck—I was standing talking to Hagerty—I saw no blow struck on either side—I saw Connor fall on his back, and saw blood coming out of his ear. GEORGE ADAMS. I am a surgeon. I attend at the London hospital—the deceased was brought there on Saturday, the 5th of June—he was in a state of insensibility—I examined his head, and found a quantity of blood flowing from his ear—I found no mark of any blow outside—he was in the hospital till the 13th of June, and then died—he continued insensi ble the whole time—I afterwards examined his body—his skull was frac tured, and the brain torn considerably—there was a quantity of blood in the base of the brain—before I opened the head, I examined to see if there were any external marks—I attribute the immediate cause of his death to pressure on the brain from the blood—the appearances I saw might un doubtedly have been occasioned by a blow or fall—it was the lower part of the skull that was fractured, behind the ear—it would very likely be occasioned by the head coming in contact with a stone—I should think it almost impossible it could have been caused by a blow from a man's fist—it was no doubt a recent injury—I do not remember seeing the wit ness Hagerty at the hospital—I saw him before the Coroner when Con nor's body was there—it was the same body I had examined.
