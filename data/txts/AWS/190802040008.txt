Police-constable MARK SIPsoN, 171 S. On December 30 I was
on duty in Avenue Road, Hampstead, and on passing the Chureh of
St. Stephen the Martyr, at about 10.30 p.m., I noticed that the door
was a little bit open. I pushed it open further, when two men rushed
out and knooked against me. I gave chase, and caught prisoner, who
was one of the men, in St. James's Terrace, a distance of about half
a mile. I questioned him, and he said he did not understand Eng:
lish. I took him to the police-station, where I found on him 4s. in
silver and 11d. in bronze. Some of the money was very sticky, and
there was also a stieky substance on the end of prisoner's stick. I
am positive prisoner is the man I pursued from the chureh. I only
lost sight of him momentarily as he was turning the corner.
FRANK HAMPTON, verger. On December 30 I locked up the church
just after seven o'eloek. Just before 11 I was summoned to the
church and found the yestry door had been broken open. On light-
ing up I found the alms box fixed inside the vestibule south door
had been broken open. Underneath the box I found that the grating
over the hot walter pipes had been pulled away, and there was 6d.
in the opening. I imagine that the coin had dropped down and the
grating had been pulled away to facilitate getting at it. There was
money in the box, which had not been opened for five months. The
money was collected for charitable purposes, and distributed by the
churehwardens, Mr. Frederick Morgan and Mr. Frederick Whinney,
Avenue Road, Hamipstead. I found the poor box had also been
tampered with. I got the keys from the churehwardens in the mort-
ing. The other boxes had not been formed, but there was some sticky
substance in the aperture at the top of the boxes. While I was with
the vicar and the detective a jemmy was found in one of the pews,
and the next night, after the wateh night service, I found another
one, which corresponded with some marks on a cupboard in the
vestry, where our registers and some other things are kept. I think
I saw prisoner in company with another man a week previous to the
robbery in the vicinity of the oburch by the west door, which is at
right angles to the south door. I ordered them away, as no one has
any right to be in the Avenue after service hours. I saw prisoner
searehed at the police station. There was sticky stuff on some of
the coins found on him.
