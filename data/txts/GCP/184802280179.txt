MR. BODKIn conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN BRIGHTMAN. I am a lamplighter, in the employ of the South Me-
tropolitan Gas-works Company, Kent-road-I have lived in that neighbour-
hood nine years. On 17th Jan., between five and six o'clock in the evening,
I was in Upper Grange-road, near the yard of the Bricklayers' Arms station
- I saw the prisoner at the corner of Willow-walk, lurking about--I kept
my eye on him some time-I believe his hands were in his pockets-it was
a wet, cold night-- he went down Willow-walk, and then to a recess, which
leads to the company's fence--he went to the fence-I watched him on the
opposite side of the way-there is a little hillock at the bottom of the recess,
and when a man was on that, the fence would be about breast high-I saw
something at the top of the fence-I withdrew, and stood about fifty yards off,
and saw the prisoner come out of the recess with a can in each hand-he
went down Willow-walk to the Grange-road-I followed him about a quarter
of a mile, met a policeman, told him, and he said to the prisoner, What
have you got here?"-he said, "I brought it from home"-I said, "That is
not true" he said, “You are a false man"- the policeman said, "Where
are you going to take it?"-be said, "Home," and said, “Come with me,
and I will make it all right"—the policeman took him-I took him back to
where be brought the property from he denied that he had been there-on
the way to the station he said he came from Clerkenwell, and was going to
take it to a man at Camberwell- he was asked again at the station where he
was going to take it-he said to a man named Oldham, at the Swan, in Old
Kent-road-he said he had got about two quarts of turps--the policeman
said, "Could you swear to these cans if I were to take them from you, and
produce them to-morrow? are there any marks on them?" -he said, "None
whatever."
