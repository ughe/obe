   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was the first bill destroyed when you received the other? A.  The first bill was backed by George Chapman, and ac cepted by Thomas—the day before that bill became due the prisoner came and said to me, "I shall not be able to take up that bill, I am afraid; I have been laying out a great deal of money in building and one thing or other; I don't think I shall be able to do it"—"Well," says I, if you can't pay it, your brother must"—he said, "He will call here pre sentry," and he did—I did not present the bill on its becoming due, as the prisoner called and said to me, "Don't you present that bill, I shall be out of town, and the people at our house know nothing about it," and I did not present it—I waited till ten o'clock at night for both parties to come, as I supposed, to settle—I did not demand the money of George—I went to my attorney, and found that by not presenting it I had taken off the responsibility from George—George is a good man; I should have taken a bill on him much better than this—I should not have taken this bill at all without the backing of George—I have never been able to get the money since. THOMAS JOHN DUBOIS. I am clerk to Mr. Boy dell, a solicitor. I am solicitor to the Southampton estate, and acted as attorney to Mr. Green, on the preparation of the mortgage deed—this is a draft of it—I cannot recollect whether Mr. Green or Mr. Chapman brought the lease for preparing this deed—I believe it was Chapman—our clerk prepared it—I examined it—it was engrossed on paper, and executed at our office by the prisoner—our clerk's name is Charlton—I cannot say whether he or myself was present on the occasion of its execution—either he or myself was the attesting witnesses—no one else was in the room—Mr. Charlton was not there the whole of  the time.
