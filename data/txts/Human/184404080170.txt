 1228. JANE TURNER was indicted for stealing 1 blanket, value 2 s  . 1 shawl, 1 s  .; 1 apron, 4 d  .; 1 pincushion, 2 d  .; 1 handkerchief, 4 d  .; and 1 comb, 2 d  .; the goods of George Pearce . JANE PEARCE. I am the wife of George Pearce, of Chapel-street, New-road, Paddington. I am a laundress—the prisoner is my daughter—I left her at home on the 19th of March to take care of the house—when I returned she had taken all her own clothes and some bedding for herself, and other things, these now produced are them—I have three children by my first husband. CHARLES JOSEPH, pawnbroker.  I have a shawl pawned by a companion of the prisoner, named Mary Nicholls—she is about seventeen—I have known her a long time.
   Prisoner.  I beg my mother's pardon. GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined Ten Days Solitary .
 1229. GEORGE BARRATT was indicted for stealing 1 dead fowl, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the property of William Roberts . LYDIA ALFXANDRINA ROBERTS. I am the wife of William Roberts. I had a dead fowl—I saw it safe about five minutes before the police man arrived.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Was there any body in the shop but you? A.  I was in the parlour—I had six fowls, and the other five were all safe—I could swear to this by that tear—it made the fowl of less value. CHARLES ATKINS( police-constable S  91.) I found the prisoner with the fowl at Kilburn—I asked where he got it—he said he brought it from Willes don—a man gave it to him there—he was about 150 yards from the prosecutor's.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 17.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined One Month .
 1230. WILLIAM COOK and CHARLES LOTTY were indicted for stealing on the   19th of March , 23 telescopes, value 2 l  . 17 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of John Cetta : and THOMAS ROBERT MOORE for feloniously receiving 11 telescopes, part of the same, well knowing them to have been stolen. JOHN CETTA, telescope-maker, Brook-street, Holborn.  I missed these teles copes on the 20th of March—they might have been gone a day or two—these are them.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  What is their value? A.  About half-a-crown a piece—I had every reason to suspect one of my servants at first, and that servant told me on the 20th of March, that Cook and Lotty did it, that he had seen them outside the shop. GEORGE EATON. I am the prosecutor's shop-boy—on the morning of the 19th of March, I had seen Cook and Lotty outside the window for about one minute—I am sure they are the persons—there were three parcels of teles copes there then—they looked all full—I missed them the next morning—there was a pane of glass broken that they were near to.
