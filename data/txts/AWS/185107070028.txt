1461. CHARLES SHORT, stealing 1 watch, value 30s. the goods of
John Beacham, bis master. Mr. ROBINsoN conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN BEACHAM. I keep the Devonshire Arms, at Pimlico. The prisoner
was in my employ between three and four months, up to the 14th June, as
weekly servant-I let him have a watch to hang up in his bedroom, that he
might know the time to get up in the morning-i was one that I had no use
for-it laid in my drawer-I gave it into his hand and never saw it afterwardls
-I gave it him about the beginning of May-there is no pretence for saying
that he bought the watch of me-I never did sell it to him.
Cross-examined by Mr. PARNELL. Q. How long had you had the wateh!
A. About three weeks-I won it at a raffle at my house by some of the
workpeople at Cubitt's-it was a shilling a head-] believe there were fifty
members-it was not for the good of the house-it was to help their fellow.
workmen-perhaps that might have been the second or third raffle I had had
there-I allow no gambling, to my knowledge-I never lent money to any
one to gamble-after I won the watch I think I did not offer it for sale-|
did not offer it immediately for sale for 338.-1 know Mr. Crane, Mr. Dove,
and Mr. Smith-they were all present-I did not offer to sell the watch for
33s. to my recollection-I do not know how long it is ago-I do not keep
account of such things-I do not know whether it was in May or April-|
did not offer to sell the watch that evening for 30s., to my knowledge-Iam
quite sure I did not sell it that evening-I did not give the prisoner 108. in
May to induce him to stay with me- am sure I did not sell the watch to
him-I do not know whether the watch went-I put it into the drawer after
I won it-a waiter of mine, named Alfred, slept in the same room with
the prisoner-Alfr had not a watch, to my knowledge-Crane is a carman
of Messrs. Cubitt's, I believe-I never told Crane that I sold the watch to the
prisoner for 30s.-I do not believe Crane asked me the morning after the
raffle what had become of the watch-I will swear I did not say to him, "I
have sold it to my chap for 30s.". do not know how long it was after I
won it that I let the prisoner have it-I suppose it laid in my drawer a fort-
night or three weeks.
Mr. ROBINSON. Q. What wages had the prisoner? A. Five shilling
a week-there is no pretence for saying that I gave him 108. to stop-
missed it the morning the prisoner absconded-I do not know where he lived.
ELIzABETH BULLeN. I am the wife of James Bullen, and live is

owed me for board and lodging-he said he had no money, and he garen
