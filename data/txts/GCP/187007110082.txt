MR. STRAIGHT. Q. You have been asked a great deal about the employ-
ments you have been in, did you give the names and addresses of your
employers at the Police Court, so that inquiries might be instituted at the
several places? 4. Yes--I left Messrs. Bull & Wilson six months after
this affair-I had three months' notice there was no reason for my being
sent away--merely that there was to be a change in the establishment-
got a character from there to my next situation-I went to the Pavilion to
see what Musgrove meant, and to see what kind of person he was going to
introduce me to-I stayed there because I did not know what to do.
JOSEPH SPARROW. I live at 7, White Hart Street, Drury Lane, and am a
cloth worker and presser-Messrs. Bull & Wilson were customers of min.
Musgrove was in my service for nearly eighteen years, as carmin-he always
conducted himself as an honest and respectable man I placed very great

son, and bring them to my premises--I did not know Isaacs at all I did
not know that he knew Musgrove in the month of September, 1867,
Mr. Coleman sent for me, and I went with Musgrove to have a conversation

head or tail of it-but I can see it better now-I said “Musgrove, you have
been seriously accused, what have you to say "-he said he did not know
