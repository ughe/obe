But I am to call witnesses to you in support of that story that Mr. Harvey has told, and as to the witnesses on the part of the prosecution, I would recount their contradictions, if it was not that the extreme ingenuity of the learned Recorder will, I am sure, amply supply that defect; certain I am, however, that the boy is materially contradicted by the father; certain I am that the boy is materially contradicted by Mrs. Milledge, and it seems to me as if the only evidence I had to apprehend the least possibility of danger from was the evidence of Mrs. Brookes; for, I rely entirely, as to the other witnesses, on the observations which I am sure will be made by the learned Recorder. Mrs. Brookes standing up here, seeing her called on their part, I thought her a decent looking woman, coming with a child in her arms, I thought there would be more regard to truth; and altho' I observed confusion, I thought it did not signify: but, gentlemen, when I call your attention to the circumstance of the case, I am bold to say that her evidence cannot be true, if much of that evidence which has preceded it be true; therefore, wherever there are contradictions as they cannot be both true, the safer way I take it, is to reject such evidence entirely; if the evidence of Goodman be true, the evidence of Brookes is not true; if Brookes's is true, Goodman's is not true; the safer way, and that which has always been taken, as I conceive, by every Jury, is, to reject that testimony entirely; and therefore when I recur to this, that Harvey acted merely in his duty, and if he is here prosecuted without great foundation, deserves the compassion of every man in Court; when I say he has interfered only in his duty, and there cannot be any positive motive assigned to him, except that one of the reward; and when you take into your consideration the probability which yet remains of this robbery's being committed in fact; I say you will attend carefully to the evidence; I say you will dispassionately attend, never presuming guilt, always intending innocence, carefull to give weight to the evidence laid before you, but not to let it hastily involve a man in guilt. On the other side, the evidence I shall lay before you on the part of Harvey will be extremely satisfactory as to the beginning of this business; afterwards I shall lay before you in evidence, a character, which I am sure ought to have, in a case like this, the first weight; not that it will be necessary as I think for your verdict, but that he may return with honour and credit to his neighbourhood. I have the satisfaction of seeing a prosecution like this fairly contradicted and reprobated.
