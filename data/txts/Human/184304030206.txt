1395. JOHN DONOVAN , JOHN CONNOR, and JAMES DAVIS, were indicted for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. DOANE and  SCRIVEN  conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES BERRY( police-constable V  59.) About eight o'clock in the evening, of the 20th of March, I was on duty at Nine Elms—I saw the three prisoners going towards Mr. White's house, and within 300 yards of it—they were in conversation together. JOHN WHITE. On the 20th of March, about nine o'clock in the evening, Donovan came to my house, and asked for a pennyworth of bread—I served him—he gave roe a sixpence, which I immediately saw was bad—I asked where he got it—he said from his brother, in the public-house—I told him to go and fetch his brother—I kept the sixpence, and he went out—I went out, but could not see him—I told the policeman about it, and gave him the six pence after I had marked it. MARIA EGGLETON. My husband is a tobacconist, and lives in Belmont-row, Vauxhall. About half-past nine o'clock, on the 20th of March, Donovan and Connor came to the shop—Donovan asked for a halfpennyworth of tobacco, and Connor said, "We may as well have a pennyworth"—I served them—Donovan gave me a sixpence—I gave the change—they put their hands be hind them, divided the tobacco, and tossed which should have the biggest half—directly they got outside the shop they ran, seeing that I looked at the sixpence, and found it was bad—I gave it to my servant for a short time, and told her to go after them, and get the tobacco back if she could—she returned, and gave me the sixpence—about a quarter of an hour after I saw the pri soners in custody—I gave the sixpence to the officer. ELIZABETH ANN NICHOLAS. I am a servant to Mrs. Eggleton. I re ceived a sixpence from her on the 20th of March, and went after the prisoners I gave the same sixpence back to my mistress. JOHN ANDREWS( police-constable V  46.) A little after nine o'clock on this evening, I saw Davis going towards Mrs. Eggleton's shop—Donovan and Connor were running after him, and one of them called out "Jemmy"  —Davis stopped—I walked 150 yards, and Mr. White gave me this sixpence—Davis stopped, and the two others overtook him, and I did not see any more of them—Mr. White gave me a description corresponding to Donovan—I pointed to the direction the three had gone—White went in that di rection—about a quarter of an hour after I saw Davis and Donovan standing at the corner of the Nine Elms—I watched—they went to the Southampton Arms, and came out with Connor, and all three went together to Vauxhall-gate—they stopped a little way down the road—I still kept sight of them till I saw Bent—I then took Connor and Donovan, and Bent took Davis—as soon as Bent took Davis I saw something drop from his hand—I turned on my light, and saw three sixpences on the ground—I then found another—I took up one of them, and Bent took the others—I then went to Eggleton's—when we got to White's I searched Davis, and found 5 d . in copper on him. THOMAS BENT( police-constable P  95.) I assisted in taking the pri soners—I found 4 d . in copper on Donovan, and 1 s . 1 d . on Connor—I took Davis—he dropped four sixpences—I took up three of them—Mrs. Eggleton gave me this other one. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—these are all counterfeit—three are cast in one mould and three in another.
   Connor's Defence.  I was selling things as I had no work to do, and coming out of the Southampton Arms I met Donovan and Davis.
