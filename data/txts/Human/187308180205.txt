I am a chair-maker—I know Mrs. Castle; not much of her son—about ten months ago she came to my shop—I was not at home, I saw her afterwards—she left some wax, about 12 lbs.—it was of a reddish cast—she asked me 2 s . 1lb. for it—I did not take it because I can buy it 1 s . 4 d . a lb. or 2 oz. at a time for that.
   Cross-examined by MR. LEWIS . I thought she asked too much, and I do not use above a quarter of a pound a week, and that would have lasted me a twelvemonth. HENRY ADDIS. I am in the employ of Williams & Co., 108, Minories—the prisoner Howe has been known to me some little time; he has an oil and colour man's business in Cambridge Heath Road—I have been in the habit of using the Three Colts public-house, a short distance from his house—I had a conversation with his brother about some wax, and afterwards with him—he handed me two pieces of wax, one dark and one light—he said he should have about 2 cwt. of it; this was given me as a sample—he said he wanted a quarter of a pound—I showed the sample to Palmer, and also to Mr. Williams, my employer—I afterwards saw Howe, and told him they would buy half a cwt. at Mr. Williams's, and a cwt. at Mr. Gadsden's.
   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH . I have been in Mr. Williams's employ two and a half years—I was in respectable employment before then—I have known Howe some years; he has borne an excellent character during that time—I have not been paid 1 l . a day for my expenses; I had 1 l . the first day, and a half-crown for a cab home—it was Howe's brother that showed me the samples, not Howe himself—I said at first that it was Howe; but, on considering, I found it was his brother—he gave me the samples in his presence—he went and fetched them from his premises; they are the same premises as the prisoner's—he is a wholesale, as well as a retail, oil and colourman—he keeps a man on the premises to mix wax—after I saw Mr. Field about this, I told Howe that I had heard the wax was stolen, and he had better not have anything to do with it—he said he should not buy any mure of it, and he was much obliged to me for telling him of it.
   Re-examined.  The shop is Howe's, I believe—the brother fetched the samples from the shop, and brought them to me at the public-house and asked if I could-sell them—I said "Yes"—both the brothers were at the public-house—I saw them afterwards several times, both in the shop toge ther—the prisoner sometimes took part in the conversation—this was about Whitsuntide, I think; about the middle of April.
