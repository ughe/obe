than your tavern ? A. I only knew him as an occasional customer at the bar.

find the port, cheese, and other things, on the Thursday? 4. Not thirty-two

-I found the property in an excavation that has lately been opened the

not in the habit of drawing the staple of the cellar flap- there is no staple in

JAMES GIBLING. I live in Victoria-place, Lambeth, and am in the employ of
Captain Abbott. I drive the Gloucester mail carriage, and Culverhouse the De-
vonport one, to the Great Western station--I look after two of Captain Abbott's
horses, kept in Mr. Smith's stable, and Culverhouse has charge of two in the
same stable for his mail-he slept in the same bed with me, over the stable-
I had the key of the stable on the night of the 17th of March-I locked the
-table-door, or he locked it and gave me the key--I had it in my pocket when
I went to bed, about ten minutes to ten o'clock, and in the morning, when I
got up-between twelve and one in the night Culverhouse came and awoke
me-he asked me to give him a match, which I did-be then asked for another,
which I gave him he went away, and returned in an honr, or an hour and a
half-I had been to sleep in the meantime-he awoke me, and said, "Get up,
I have got a swag"-1 did not know what he meant at the moment I asked
what it was, and where he got it-he said out of old Smith's cellar, that it was
brandy, wine, cheese, and pork, and said I was to get up, to put the horses in
his carriage, and take it over to my house, we should not be gone longer than
half or three quarters of an hour, and no notice would be taken I said no,
I would not-(I have a house of my own)-I said I would have nothing at alle
to do with it, and told him to take it back to Smith's again-he went away
