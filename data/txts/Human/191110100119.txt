(Thursday, October 24.) LOUIS LEWIS, labourer. I have done odd jobs for prisoner. I took several parcels of goods like those produced to Fruitenan and Co., shippers, and brought some from Palast's.
 Cross-examined. There was nothing unusual in my taking the goods to Fruitenan's. LILIAN JACOBS. I have worked for prisoner 19 months. On July 17 the place was shut up. The stuff produced looks better than the stuff I used to make up into garments. There were about 16 or 17 other girls at work there. ANNIE BROWN. I worked in defendant's workroom for two years and nine months. I had to look after the girls and their work. We never made up stuff as good as the three parcels produced.
 Cross-examined. There was always plenty of work right up to July 15. ESTHER JACOBS. I am one of defendant's work girls. I never saw stuff made up so good as parcels produced. JULIUS LEVINE. I am employed by Mr. Stalbow. I lodged in prisoner's house, 38, Riversdale Road, Stamford Hill, up to July 28. The prices per yard of the stuff produced range from 9d. to 2s., whereas the prices of the stuff he previously bought from Stalbow ranged from 3s. to 4s. per gross of yards. KATE WELLER, assistant to Michael Jalon, 10, Duke Street, Aid gate, ribbon merchant. On July 12 prisoner ordered goods to value of ₤8 19s. He said he had a big shipping order.
 Cross-examined. He selected special colours and would not take anything I offered him. JOHN RILEY, carman. On Sunday, July 16, I removed goods from prisoner's premises in Aldgate to Sigden Road, Hackney.
 Cross-examined. The goods included fittings and machinery; there was no stock. SIMON OPPENHEIM, lace dealer, 94, Brick Lane. On July 18 I was at Mr. Stalbow's place when prisoner was there. I heard Mr. Stalbow ask him what he had done with the goods he had from him in the last month and his machinery. Lewenstein replied, "You don't take me for such a fool as to tell you where I have put it."
