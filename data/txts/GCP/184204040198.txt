John PALMER. I am a City police-constable. On the 29th of March
about half past eight o'clock in the morning, I was on duty in Hounds.
ditch, and saw a cab stop at the corner of Cutler-street-the prisoner per
out, paid the man his fare, and brought out of the cab this bag, with

him, and both went up Cutler-street-I followed them, and when ther
found I was following them they dropped it and run away- I took it up
cried “Stop thief," and saw the prisoner stopped by a brother constable
I never lost sight of him.

THOMAS Malin (City police-constable, No. 74.) On the 29th of March
I was on duty in Petticoat-lane, beard a cry of “Stop thief," and saw the
prisoner and another run down Harrow-alley from Cutler-street-I stopped
the prisoner-the other made his escape-the officer was behind with the
clock-the prisoner said, "It is not ine, Sir, it is the other one;" and the
other one said, “It is not me, Sir, it is this one."
Cross-examined. Q. But the other did not say so in the prisoner's ptb-
sence? A. He said it in my presence-I had quite sufficient to take this
one---if I had taken him I should have lost the prisoner--the prisoner said
he had met the other man at six o'clock in the morning, at the Cat and
Mutton public-house, Cat and Mutton Fields, that he waited there till the
other fetched a bag, and they went together to a stack of bricks, and there
found the clock.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
