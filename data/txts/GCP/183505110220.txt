WINCHESTER, Mayor.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Was this on Easter Wednes.
day? A. Yes, or Thursday- I went to bed in the middle of the day on
Wednesday-I was not much in liquor on Tuesday night I was a little
intoxicated on Wednesday at one o'clock, which made me go to bed-1
was not at home on Tuesday night I was walking about, not a mile from
home-- I was not at all the worse for liquor-I met the prisoner and
Rogers about eight o'clock, as near as I can say I believe I was drinking
-I was not the worse for liquor when I met them-I was not in their
company on Tuesday- I was at home principally the whole day.
Mrs. Cox. I am the prosecutor's wife. On Wednesday the 22nd
of April my husband came home and went to bed-he remained in bed
the whole day-I shut up, and went to bed between eleven and twelve
o'clock-Jenkins, my boy, left at ten o'clock-before I went to bed I saw
every place secure-I looked at the window-stall shutter--I saw it was
fastened-the window-slides and the shutter-bolts--at one o'clock in the
morning Mr. Cox awoke me, as the dog awoke him-he called out and said
there were thieves in the place-I immediately jumped up, and ran into the
shop-I saw the prisoner--he was the only person I saw in the place-he
went down into the stall, and got out-I saw no more of him-I opened the
street door and hallooed out “Thieves" and "Murder"–the policeman
came very soon after, and he went down-I saw the bolt of the shutter
next morning almost broken in two-the prisoner could get out, as the
stall-shutter was unbolted the street door was quite safe, as I had left it,
bolted with two bolts and locked-he must have come in through the
stall.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you first learn from your husband that
you were to be a witness ? A. I was sent for about an hour and a half
ago I was not before the Magistrate-I can swear to the prisoner-I was
nearer to him than my husband was-my husband was quite sober that
night, but in the middle of the day he was a little drunk ; but not so that
he could not stand, and was obliged to go to bed-I did not charge Ro-
gers with having been on the premises ; but I understand he was accused
I cannot tell where my husband was on Tuesday.
MR. DOANE. Q. Whether Rogers was in the shop before you got
there you cannot tell ? A. No; the dog had seized the prisoner, and that
awoke us-I did not see the dog seize him, but he made a sad noise when
he came towards our bed-room.
JOHN JENKINS. I am in the prosecutor's service. On Wednesday,
the 22nd of April, I went on an errand for my mother, after leaving my
master's to the Monument public-house in Union-street, about twenty yards
from Mr. Cox's premises--I saw the prisoner and Rogers there, and two
girls with them--the prisoner asked me if my master was at home-I said,
“What is that to you? Yes, he is"-Rogers then asked me if my master
was in bed and asleep-I said yes, he was—this was rather before eleven
o'clock at night--I had left my master's at ten o'clock-next day I was
sent by my master down to the New-cut, and on the road I saw Rogers
with two other girls, by the side of Mrs. Slater's shop, where the prisoner
works--I overheard a conversation between Rogers and the girls, and
informed my master-I saw the prisoner in Mrs. Slater's shop on my return.
NOT GUILTY.
