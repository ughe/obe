   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I gave you 6 d . and a fourpenny piece in change, which you put in your pocket. EDITH LEWIS. I am barmaid at the King's Head, Little Queen Street, Holborn—on Saturday night, 29th September, the prisoner came in for. a glass of ale and gave me a bad shilling—I put it in the bar—I am sure the other barmaid took out the same shilling—I saw Julia Wright give it to Mr. Kiel. CHARLES JOHN KIEL. I am the landlord of the King's Head—my attention was called to the bad shilling—the prisoner was then at the bar—I asked him where he got it—he said at his work in a printingoffice—my potman then told the prisoner that he had been trying to pass a bad shilling at the Red Lion—the prisoner said he had not been to the Red Lion—I afterward fetched Glander, the barman—I gave the prisoner in custody with the shilling. CHARLES MARTIN. I live at 7, Eagle Street, High Holborn, and am a builder—I was at the Red Lion when the prisoner came in—I saw the shilling bent—I watched the prisoner—he pretended to be drunk—he walked about five yards and then ran across the road and went to a man with a white apron and a female with a brown dress—I followed them to Little Queen Street, where they parted, and the prisoner went into the King's Head. JAMES HODGSON( Policeman E  475). I was in the King's Head public-house on the night of 29th September, and received a bad shilling from Mr. Kiel—I asked the prisoner where he got it; he said from his work—Glander came from the Red Lion and pointed him out as having been there—I took him to the station. WILLIAM: WEBSTER. This shilling is bad. JAMES HODGSON( re-examined ). I went to the prisoner's place of work—they had employed him fifteen months, but he had not been there more than two days during the last month. GUILTY  — Recommended to mercy by the Jury. Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
