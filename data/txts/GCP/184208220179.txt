2430. GEORGE COOPER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of
August, 1 hand-barrow, value 51. 58.; and 28 loaves of bread, value
118 3d.; the goods of Charles Stephen Moore.
WILLIAM ROBERT BALDRY. I am in the employ of Charles Stephen
Moore, a baker. On the 13th of August I was delivering bread for him
-I had a barrow with me containing twenty-six half-quartern loaves I
went into a house in St. Michael's-place, Brompton-when I came out my
barrow and bread were gone-I found the barrow, about eight o'clock the
same evening, at North-end, but the bread was gone.
JAMES STRATTEN. I keep the Seven Stars public-house.On Satur-
day evening, the 13th of August, I saw the prisoner drinking in the tap-
room--he was offering the barrow, which was at my door, for sale--he
pointed it out, and asked 108. for it-I thought it was worth a great deal
more-I persuaded him to put it in my yard, and come for it on Monday
morning-he said it was his own, and he should sell it-he took it, and
went to the Crown, below me-soon afterwards Mr. Moore came to me
I went and pointed out the barrow to him, which was then standing by the
Crown.
Thomas Edgson. I am a wheelwright, and live at North-end. On
the 13th I saw a baker's barrow at the Seven Stars the prisoner was with
it-he was drinking outside the window-he said he would take 14s. for
it; he then said he would take 108.-he offered to sell it to me- I looked
at it, and then he went to the Crown, and returned and said he would take
188. for it-I said it never was made for 61.-he said I was wrong; that
he had had it for three weeks, and gave 31. 16s. for it-I saw a ledger
green baize in it.
Charles Birch (police-constable V 92.) I took the prisoner in charge.
(The prisoner pleaded distress.)
GUILTY. Aged 39.-Confined Six Months.
2431. WILLIAM FOX was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July,
1 piece of wood, value 28., the goods of John Adam, in a certain barge, on
the navigable river Thames.
FREDERICK WHITE. I am a waterman, and live in Gardiner's-row,
Chelsea. This boat-seat belongs to John Adams, a shoemaker, at Chelsea
- I had charge of his boat- I missed the seat on Friday evening, and it
was found next morning--the boat was lying at Cheyne-wharf, Chelsea.
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. This was on a pier, was it
not? A. Yes- I placed it on the pier for safety-it belongs to the boat-
I missed it from the floating pier-it is an erection on the barges-the
wood was put under the barges' head-persons go over them to get into
the boats.
HENRY KIMBER (police-constable V 20.) On Saturday I received in-
formation that there were some boards taken from the floating pier-I
went to the prisoner's house, and there found this board.
