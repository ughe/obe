145. JOHN HARRIS and GEORGE BROWN, stealing 2 iron plough.
shares, and other articles, value Is. 4d. ; the goods of William Adams.
JOSEPH MILTON. I am bailiff to Mr. William Adams. I found the
prisoners breaking up some rails on 23rd Nov., about twenty yards from my
master's; I took them back to my master's yard—they had a bag between
them—I found in it some iron hoops, which had been broken up—thew
plough-shares and rails are all my master's; I challenged the prisoner*
they said they found them in a ditch.
JAMES BONE. I am in the employ of Mr. Adams. About four o'clock, on
23rd Nov., I saw Harris go into the yard, and go to the cart-shed—when be
came back he had this iron in his band, and he took it out; Brown was
about twenty yards from him—when Harris took the iron out Brown jumpd
upon it, and broke it.
Harris. He has only just come out of Guildford gaol, for stealing apple;
he had one month. Witness. I have been there.
Brown's Defence. I was not near the premises; the things were Of
use to any one.

146. FRANCIS THOMPSON, stealing 8 shillings, 1 sixpence, and 8
pence ; the moneys of John William Ford, from his person.
JOHN WILLIAM FORD. I am a tobacco-pipe manufacturer. On 27th Nov,
I was in the Shakspere's Head, Stratford—I got drunk, and got dancing
a man—I had in my left pocket 9'. 6d. in silver, and 3d. in halfpence—I
lost 88. 6d. and 3d. ; I cannot tell how.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You were tipsy when thb
money was lost? A. Yes, but I was not without other money—my
brought me 38. to the door, from some customer in Chapel-street.
JOHN STACEY. I was in this beer-shop—I saw them dancing—I saw
the prisoner put his hand into my master's, Mr. Ford", left-hand pocket—
I did not see any money come out.
Croas-eramined. Q. What, was your master drunk? A. Yes; be
dancing with the prisoner, and two or three more men—they were not dancing,
they were jumping round the room; they were not all drunk.
NOT GUILTY.
