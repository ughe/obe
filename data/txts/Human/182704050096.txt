 992.  JOHN NEWMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of February , 1 plough, value 10s., the goods of James Johnston ; 1 saw, value 8s., the goods of John Smale ; 1 sash-saw, value 8s. 6d., and 1 stock, value 8s., the goods of Francis Stevenson.
   THOMAS WOOLSTENHOLME. I am a pawnbroker. On the 27th of February, about nine o'clock in the morning, the prisoner pawned two saws, a plough, and a stock with me; he siad they were his own; I asked where the iron was; he said, "In another plough." On the 2d of March he called to have the duplicate altered, and I detained him.
   JAMES JOHNSONTON. This plough is mine - I left it on the 26th of February, on a bench at Mr. Sparks;' I know nothing of the prisoner.
   JOHN SMALL. I lost this saw from Mr. Sparks'.
   FRANCIS STEVENSON. This stock, and sash-saw are mine, and were taken form Mr. Sparks', on the 26th February.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met a man in the Minories, and asked him for a job; he said he he exceptedone, and asked if I would work with him; I met him again on Tuesday with these tools; he said he had got a job to go to in about a week, and asked me to pawn these tools, while he went to speak to his old master, to get me a job - told me to give the name of Dunn, Bow-street, with them - he said the iron was with another plough. I met him on Thursday - he asked me to get the ticket altered, as they had only put "Saw" on it, without saying what saw it was - I told them the man was waiting for me.
   T. WOOLSTENHOLME . Four duplicates were given for them, but only this one was found on him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 991.  CHARLS BALLARD was indicted for stealing, on the 1th of February , 1 ream of coloured paper, value 2. 5s.; 4 reams of other coloured paper, value 6l.; 11/4 ream of other coloured paper, value 2l. 10s., and 8 quires of double-milled blue coloured paper, value 18s., the goods of Edward Jones, his master.
 EDWARD JONES. I am a  stationer, and live in Budgerow - the prisoner was my warehouseman for about sixteen years. This paper is mine.
   JOHN WILLIS. I have bought paper of the prisoners at different times, within the last eighteen months; he sold me all this paper at different times; the last he sold me was this double-milled blue paper - I bought it on the 9th of February - he called for the money on the 10th; I knew him by the name of Wilson, but Mr. Candle was in my shop, and called him Ballard; when he was gone away Candle told me where he lived - he had told me he was a glazier, and took the paper for work. I informed Mr. Jones.
   GEORGE CAUDLE. On the 10th of February I was in Willis' shop, and addressed the prisoner by the name of Ballard; Willis said I was mistaken.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   RICHARD GEORGE STATHAM. I was employed to watch the prisoner, and afterwrds took him.
 The prisoner put in a written defece, pleading distress.
 MR. JONES. He left my service while I was in the country, in consequence of which I discharged him; he petitioned to return, and Isaid I would give him 15s. a week tillhe cold get a better place - since that his wages have been raised, in consequence of his family; he broke his thigh in my employ, and I thought I was in duty bound to keep him.
 GUILTY. Aged 41. Strongly recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Year.
