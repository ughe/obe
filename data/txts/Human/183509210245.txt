I am a wine and spirit merchant, and live at No. 412, Oxford-street, I have observed the omnibuses drive up and down from Paddington to the Bank—I have had frequent opportunities of observing the conduct of the drivers—the conducting of the omnibuses before Bray's came on, was upon a very excellent principle, and since that, they have been a great annoyance to the passengers—I have had occasion to ride down to the City, having a nervous debility, and have been annoyed by Mr. Aaron Bray's omnibus coming close to the one I wished to get into—I think that Bray's omnibuses were the aggressors.
   Cross-examined by MR. WHITEHEAD . Q.  Why do you think they were the aggressors? A.  Because I have seen the others drive to get a-bead get ahead of the others—I have never seen the others drive to get a-head of them. not when I have been fiding in them—I have seen the omnibuses driving past each other—I have seen two or three omnibuses with Bray's—I think I can swear I have not seen so many as five—I will go as far as two or three—I will not go farther—I have not noticed the names of the omnibuses—there are so many passing and re passing; but I noticed Bray's, because there are only two of them, and I suppose there are thirty others—Bray's always seemed to be driving up to the spot where I live—they came on the road, in my opinion, to oppose others—I have noticed them more particularly—they did not drive up to my house, but nearly so; within twenty yards or so—they all stop there.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  When you have seen them, was the conduct you complain of, the conduct of Bray's omnibuses? A.  Yes. JAMES KENDALL. I am a victualler, and live in Rathbone-place. I have noticed the omnibuses that go along Oxford-street repeatedly—I have observed Bray's omnibuses—when I have been riding in another, they have repeatedly crossed the front of the bus  that I was riding on, and pulled up by the side of it or behind it; that has caused a great annoyance, so far, that I felt inclined to get off the one I was on—our omnibus went on the same as usual—I felt annoyed by those buses  con stantly drawing up infront of the bus  I was on—I do not know that the rest of the passengers did—Bray's pulled up in the usual way of pulling up—I did not observe that they did it to take up a passenger—I cannot say what reason they had for pulling up, but they did pull up—I did not see them take up passengers when they pulled up.
