   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  How long was it since you had seen the prisoner before you went to take him? A.  I had not seen him since I let him the rooms—he paid me no rent—I did not apply to him for it—the house was in a dilapidated state—there were no other tenants in the house, of my letting, but I heard there were other persons there—I did not know who was in the house or out of it—I went into these rooms when I let them to the prisoner—I locked the mangle up in the front parlour—I cannot tell whether I went into the room that I had let to the prisoner when I went with the policeman—I went into the room where the mangle was in, directly, because the prisoner was to be secured—there are eight small rooms in the house—to my knowledge people have not gone in and out, paying no rent, but lodging as they had a mind—it is my  house—the mangle had been in the house two or three months before the prisoner went there—it had been in the next house; and having let the next house, I caused it to be removed into this room—the other house was in a dilapidated condition—I had let it, and not applied for the rent—the house in' which the prisoner lived was left me by will, by Mr. Hutton—the glass was all out before the prisoner came into it, and I put the glass all in—after the prisoner was in custody I got some glass from a shop—the prisoner's wife told me there was some glass that she had left there for 5 s .—I have not received any communication about glass from anybody since the prisoner has been there—I never said anything to anybody about not wishing to hurt the prisoner—the solicitor said, "I suppose you have no vindictive feelings?"—I said, "No, but I must put an end to stealing property in this way"—I have distained on the prisoner's wife lately, after first offering her goods if she would leave the place—there was a weekly rent to have been paid by the prisoner—it was not an understanding with the prisoner that I should not be paid any rent because the house was di lapidated—very recently there was a quarrel with the prisoner's wife and a bad character, and they broke sixteen squares of glass—I believe the prisoner is a cutter-up of beef and ham at fairs—the rent was 3 s . 3 d . a week—I did not make a memorandum of it in a book—after he was in custody I told his wife she appeared not to be able to pay so much as the rent of two rooms; if she chose to go into a back room up stairs, she might—I have seen the mangle at Dawson's—I know it from a crack in the iron-work of the box, and from its general appearance.
