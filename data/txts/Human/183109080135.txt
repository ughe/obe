 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q.Was there any thing to have prevented the prisoner from absconding before you got home? A. No; there is a communication to the next house by the back way- she might have come in that way; 3s. 6d. was found on her; a Mrs. Carter might have visited at my house unknown to me - I have since heard she was in the habit of coming; I believe search has been made for her- the prisoner sent for me home, and told me of this; I know the persons who live at the next house - I have visited there, but not played at cards or drank tea there; I never allowed the prisoner to go - there is a kitchen and a wall between the two back doors; I have gone by the name of Thompson, but my name is Vincent.
   GEORGE HARRISON. I am a Police-constable. I was on duty, and went to this house; the prisoner was there, but the prosecutrix had not arrived - the prisoner said her mistress had been robbed of money and jewellery during her absence next door; that the people said there was a double-knock at the door, and as her mistress had desired her not to leave the house, she got over the back wall - when she got to the street door, she found it bolted; she said she fancied all was not right, went up stairs, found the drawers out, and missed the property - she said she fancied that as she got over the wall some one brushed by her, but it being dark she thought it was a cat; but when she found the drawers had been opened, she thought it was a person in a dark coloured coat; she went up again with me, and I saw the doors opened - I went backwards, and scaled several walls, but saw no one; there was nothing found on her.
 Cross-examined. Q. What sort of a neighbourhood is this? A. The square is very respectable; some of the streets adjoining have bad houses in them, but I know of only one bad house in that street, and that is the prosecutrix's.

   JAMES HARRIS. I am a pawnbroker. I have a tablecloth and spoon, pawned on the 8th of August, in the name of Ann Lill, by a woman, for 1s. 6d. - I gave her this duplicate; it was not the prisoner who pawned them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I am innocent; the duplicate was picked up at the step of the door, when a person came to light a candle.

 1840.  THOMAS FORD was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 1 box, value 2s., the goods of John Drinkwater , from his person.
 JOHN DRINKWATER. I was coming, on the 1st of August, from  Hanwell to Brompton; my mother and brother were with me - I was taken unwell, and retired; I fell into a fit, and as I was recovering, I found the prisoner's right hand in my right-hand waistcoat pocket, where I had a box, containing two duplicates - he still staid with me; we then went on; I told my brother and mother of it, but I did not ask the prisoner for the box - we went on to the Seven Stars, and called for a pint of beer; the prisoner followed us in - I sent for the Policeman; my brother knew the prisoner, but I did not.
   WILLIAM PEASGOOD. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner; I said, "I want you, you know what for;" he said, "I shan't come;" the prosecutor then said, "I give him in charge;" the prisoner said, "Oh! about this box," and took it out of his pocket - I said, "Give it to me;" he said, "I shan't," and put it into his pocket again - I took him to the station.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   THOMAS DRINKWATER. I was with my brother - I did not see the prisoner take the box, but I saw him pull it out at the public-house.
 Prisoner's Defence. He said would I give up the box; I said not till I got to the office.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Confined Seven Days.
 1841.  SAMUEL GREENLAND was indicted for embezzlement.
   CHARLES JAMES FOX. I am a trimming-manufacturer. The prisoner had been in my employ about five weeks - he was employed to go out to sell goods, and receive money; he was to account for them every day - I did not keep a book; I looked over his box when he returned.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.Was he your regular  servant? A. Yes - Bell, Bentley, and Co., in Cheapside, became customers of mine a few days after the prisoner came into my service; I charge the prisoner with selling goods to them, and not accounting to me for them - I did not trust him with a very large stock; I never allowed him any commission on what he sold, or agreed to do so - I keep some books, but when he took out goods for sale I merely made a memorandum of them, and looked over the box when he came back; he should account as soon as he came home - I entered the goods that were sold in a day-book; I have not brought that book - these sums I charge him with are entered in the day-book, but not as paid; I have no means of knowing what he sold, except from the day-book, and the bills of parcels.
