 Q. What passed between you and the prisoner respecting these articles? - A. I asked him, from whom he brought them; he said, it was nothing to me, if I did not like to buy them, I might give them him back; I told him, I should not, till I was more satisfied how he came by them; I then asked him, what his name was; he said, his name was Morris; I asked him, where he lived; he said, he lived with a Mr. Dickson, an attorney, in Mincing-lane; I then told him, if he brought a memorandum from Mr. Dickson, or brought Mr. Dickson with him, if our gentlemen were in the way, the property would be delivered up to him; he then went away, leaving the things in my possession, in a kind of huff, because I would not deliver him up the property; when Mr. Murl came back from dinner, the same evening, I informed him of the circumstance.
 What passed between you and Mr. Murl you must not tell us. - A. The prisoner called again upon the 5th of January; the articles had laid upon a desk of mine from the 28th of November till the prisoner called again upon the 5th of January; there were three or four other clerks, besides myself, present; he told me, he had come for those articles he left the 28th of November; I told him, I could not deliver them up, without the authority of my master; if he would call the next morning, about eleven, they would be, one, or both, in the way; he said, he could not call at eleven, he would call between the hours of four and six in the evening, those being the hours of absence from office; I saw Mr. Murl about an hour and a half after this, and I communicated it to Mr. Murl; the next morning, the 6th, I delivered the two articles to Mr. Murl, in the same condition in which I had received them from the prisoner; the next time I saw them was about three or four hours after; when Mr. Murl came back from 'Change, he brought them back with him, to the counting-house, and I gave them, the next morning, to Mr. Cox, to take them to the Mansion-house; I went with him, to the Mansion-house; they were then left in the hands of the constable, who is here, his name is Juson.
 Q. When was he apprehended? - A. On the 7th, by means of Mr. Barnes; I was sent for on the 7th, to attend the Lord Mayor.
 Q. The day before he was not in custody? - A. Not that I know of.
 Q. Who apprehended the prisoner? - A. I don't know.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Knapp. Q. You are clerk to the house of Cox and Murl? - A. Yes.
 Q. That is one of the most respectable houses in the city of London, as refiners? - A. None more so.
 Q. The prisoner at the bar came with these things, and you told him, if he would bring a memorandum from Mr. Dickson, his master, you would purchase them at a future day? - A. Yes; if our gentlemen were satisfied, it was to be delivered up.
 Q. The prisoner, who brought the spoon and the other thing broke, gave his own name? - A. He did.
 Q. The very name by which he is indicted, where he lived, and who was his master? - A. He did.
 Q. And though you had entertained suspicions, and would not deliver up the property, he came again to you? - A. Yes.
 DANIEL JUSON sworn.
 I am a constable of Tower-ward, (produces the property): I received it from one of my Lord Mayor's clerks, at the Mansion-house.
 Q. These are the articles that were produced before the Lord Mayor? - A. Yes.
 Q. (To Bennett). Look at these things, and see if they are the same? - A. I believe they are.
 Q. Have you any doubt about it? - A. I have not; they have been out of my possession a long while.
 Q. But where the articles you gave to Mr. Cox, the same things that were produced before the Lord Mayor? - A. They were.
 Q. How many pieces are there there? - A. The spoon in two, the skewer in three; just as they were when the prisoner brought them to me; there is a mark of W S M upon the spoon, there has been a mark upon the skewer, which is erased out.
 Q. (To Barnes). Look at these articles, and see if you can put them together? - A. They appear as if they belonged to each other; the broken parts fit each other; and it has the cypher upon it, W S M; I had had them five years.
 Q. Can you undertake to swear that they are your's? - A. Yes; here is the fellow spoon to it.(producing it), with the same cypher; I have also the fellow to the skewer, (producing it); the cypher upon it is the same; and upon the broken one it is defaced.
 Jury. The cypher and crest are both nearly out.
 Prisoner. I leave my defence to my counsel.
 For the prisoner.
