 1753. WILLIAM GOOCH , stealing 1 gun, 1 gun-case, and 3 printed books, value 8 l  .; the goods of William Clapperton , his master. WILLIAM CLAPPERTON. I keep the Kenilworth Castle, public-house, New North-road, Islington. The prisoner was my  potboy, and had been so three or four months—I had a gun in a case in the club-room—I saw it there on 15th July, between 10 and 11 o'clock, and missed it about half-past 10 in the evening—I was told something, and went to M'Carthy, and, in conse quence of something he told me, I went to Bermondsey-street, where I saw the prisoner coming out of a public-house with my gun in his hand—he was very tipsy—I gave him in charge—I also lost three books the same evening. WILLIAM M'CARTHY. I live in Shepperton-street, Islington. On 18th July, a little after 10 o'clock in the evening, I was at Mr. Clapperton's—the prisoner asked me to carry a gun out for him—he was in the yard, just out side the door, and said he was going home that evening, over the water—he had got the gun in his hand—I asked if it was his own—he said, "Yes"—he was a little in liquor—I carried the gun about twenty yards outside the door—it was in two pieces, and not in the case—he came out with a little can, and some beer, and brought two or three little books which be  asked me to take care of for him till the morning—I agreed to do so—he took the gun and went away—next morning I gave the books to Mr. Clapperton. DANIEL MILLS( policeman, M  173). On 20th July, I was on duty, and saw the prisoner coming out of the Anchor and Eight Bells, Bermondsey-street, with this gun—it was put together, and the case was swinging on his arm—Mr. Clapperton came up in a cab , and gave him in custody—he was very tipsy—I received these books from M'Carthy next morning. WILLIAM CLAPPERTON re-examined.  This is my gun, and books—I never lent him the gun—it had a lock on it, which is gone now.
   Prisoner.  I was in the habit of  bringing books home, and have got some now; I lent your sister some books, and she lent me some in return, Witness. I was not aware of it—my sister lives in the house—she is unmarried—he never went out shooting, or talked about it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I took the gun with the intention of going out for a day's shooting, and returning on Thursday morning; I asked M'Carthy to take the gun out that it might not be noticed; I intended to take the books back, but being very tipsy was afraid I should lose them.
 (  The prisoner received a good character. ) NOT GUILTY.
