2036. ROBERT PARROTT , stealing one pair of boots, value 25 s  .; the goods of Henry Isaac Brewer . HENRY ISAAC BREWER. I conduct the business of a public-house at Rotherhithe, for my mother-in-law. On 14th Aug. the prisoner was there—I missed these boots from the kitchen, and met him leaving the house—I said, "What have you done with, the boots you took from here?"—he said, "I have no boots "—his apron was pulled down, and the boots were under it—I save him in charge. HENRY NEWBERRY. I saw the prisoner come from the passage with a boot protruding from his jacket—I found a pair of boots under his apron—he said they were given to him—at the station he threatened to serve me out when he got his liberty.
   Prisoner's Defence.  There was a row  there, and a man asked me to take care of them. GUILTY. Aged 37.— Confined Six Months .
 2037. JOHN LAWRENCE, and HENRY CHAPPEL , stealing 100 sovereigns, 80 half-crowns, 500 shillings, 200 sixpences, and 2 bags, value 2 d  .; the goods of Edward Smith , (since deceased,) in his dwelling-house.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. EDWARD SMITH. I live with my mother, at the Bricklayers' Arms, Eccleston-street, Pimlico. My father died last Dec.—he was alive at the time of this robbery, but it hastened his death—on 15th Sept. last, about a quarter-past four o'clock in the afternoon, I was in Eccleston-street, near our house, and the two prisoners came up to me, and asked if I could tell them where the Bricklayers' Arms was—I do not think they mentioned the land lord's name—I directed them—I went home half an hour after, and they were then standing at the bar, drinking gin and water—on 21st Sept., about seven in the evening, I saw both the prisoners again—I saw Lawrence first in company with another man, not Chappel—they were standing at the counter, taking half-and-half—Chappel came in shortly afterwards, and called for a glass of half-and-half—he stood talking to me about a quarter of an hour—after that Lawrence and his companion asked for two glasses of gin—Chappel was not with them—I had to turn my back to serve the gin, and when I turned round again Chappel was gone—I did not see him go—Law rence and his companion had some gin and cigars—they then began to talk about their friend not having come; that they should not stop any longer, and they left shortly after—soon after that, my sister Mary, who was sitting sewing, jumped up, and ran up stairs, as though something had suddenly attracted her attention—she then called out to me that the house had been robbed.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Is yours a corner house? A.  Yes; the wall of the skittle-ground is about ten feet high—there is a back door—there is no field at the back—the skittle-ground is covered in—there is a street at the other side of the wall—a person would have to get over a wall twelve feet high to get in at the back-door—that door is kept locked, and my father and the man were in the skittle-ground, making a new skittle-frame.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  When did you see Chappel again? A.  About six weeks ago, at the Police-court—there were two other people sitting in the tap-room on the 21st; that was all the company in the house at that time—I had been in the house all day—I did not go out till this alarm.
