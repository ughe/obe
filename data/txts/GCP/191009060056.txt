first hearing I asked him if he wished me to make any inquiries con-
cerning him, and he said, "No, I do not want any enquiries. I admit
I have advertised from these places, but there is no fraud." I found
among the papers this small brass plate with “Edwards and Co."
upon it. Over the letter-box at Tachbrook Street there was the name
"The Copying Co.”
Prisoner's statement before the magistrate. “There is no evidence
of any intent to defraud."
FRANCIS WILLIAM RUTTER (prisoner, not on oath). I have simply
got to say that I carried on the business according to the terms of the
circular, and all the drawings you see have been done by the panto
graph. There is no intent to defraud. It was a genuine business.
As regards the two sacks of letters there were no letters left at 54,
Cornwall Road, when I left there. What was there was put there
afterwards.
Verdict, Guilty. It was stated that prisoner refused to give any
information nor could any be obtained as to how he had been getting
a living other than by these means, and that it was believed that he had
been formerly convicted in America.
Sentence, 18 months' hard labour.
