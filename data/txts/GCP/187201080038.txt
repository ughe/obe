ROBERT COLMAN HALL. I am a ten-dealer, and live at Pembroke Lodge,
Brixton - I had a boy in the school at Brixton, under Mr. Watson, and in the
beginning of 1871 I had occasion to call on him respecting my son—I had
known him seven years, and I noticed a very kroat difference in bis demean-
our and manner on that occasion, to what I had noticed before-he seemed
depressed and lost, and said he was about leaving the school, after so many
years--I noticed at the time to my wife that he seemed depressed at the
time--the interview lasted perhaps ten minutes or a quarter of an hour, and
I had ample opportunity of forming a judgment as to whether he was de-
pressed or not.
C1088-examined. I went to him because the gentleman who my son was

COURT. Q. What do you mean by “lost ?" A. He seemed low, and he
hardly knew what he was speaking about at times—he seemed to me as if
he was worried at leaving the school, and he said he had been treated
badly—that is all the explanation I give of the word “lost"-he seemed
remarkably low.

conclusion I came to-1 believe he is suffering from melancholia, the symp-

of melancholia-I found that his age was sixty-seven or sixty-eight, and in a
person of that age melancholia would have greater effect and force than in a
