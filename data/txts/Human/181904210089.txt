 756.  GEORGE SCONE was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of April , one handkerchief, value 1 s., the goods of Henry James Brooks , from his person.
   HENRY JAMES BROOKS. On the 27th of April, about ten o'clock at night, I was in Holborn with John Mullins, who said a person was picking my pocket - I felt, and missed my handkerchief. I turned round, and saw the prisoner throw something white away, but am not certain that it was my handkerchief. I collared him, and called Watch! he struck me three times, and ran off, but was stopped. My handkerchief was not found.
 Cross-examined by MR. ALLEY. Q. Did you not charge him with an assault - A. No.
   JOHN MULLINS. I was with Brooks, and saw the prisoner behind him. I suspected him, and informed the prosecutor - he never collared the prisoner. I went back to look for the handkerchief, but it was gone.
   FRANCIS GAMMAGE. I am a watchman. I heard the alarm, and stopped the prisoner. He said it was only a row.
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor collared me - I pushed him off, he struck me, and I struck him.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 757.  GEORGE M'LEAN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of April , 7 lbs. of pork, value 3 s., the goods of Robert Pilkington.
   SUSAN PILKINGTON. I am the wife of Robert Pilkington, who is a pork-butcher, and lives in Chandos-street. On the 27th of April, between nine and ten o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came into the shop, reached over the counter, and took a leg of pork. I secured him with it.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 58. Confined One Month.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.

 759.  THOMAS HARRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of April , one handkerchief, value 2 s., from the person of a man whose name is unknown.
   THOMAS STEVENS. I am a potatoe salesman in Covent-garden. On the 17th of April, at half-past eleven o'clock at night, I was taking my scales and weights to the public-house, and saw the prisoner and another young man rush out of the door as I went in; as I returned to Covent-garden I saw them again about twenty yards from the door, the prisoner took a handkerchief from a gentleman's pocket, turned back again, and went into the public-house - I followed him, and stood by the door; he came out again with the same young man - they went up the street, and both stood against a railing in the street. I passed them, three gentlemen and a lady came by, returning from the Theatre, they both followed them, and I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief from the gentleman's pocket - it was a blue and white one. I pointed him out to the watchman. I am certain he is the man.
 Q. Why did you not stop the gentleman, and tell him - A. Because I was once robbed myself, and when I took the man I was surrounded by a gang of about twenty, and got almost killed.
   CHARLES HUMPHRIES. On the 17th of April I was with Bond under the Piazza, and met a watchman, who pointed the prisoner out to me in a public-house, I beckoned him out, searched him, and found a blue and white handkerchief in his hat; he said he had had it almost a fortnight - it smelt very strong of perfume; he said it was not marked, but it was. Bond found another handkerchief on him.
 WILLIAM BOND. I searched the prisoner, and found a handkerchief in his side-pocket; he said it was not marked, but it was.
 Prisoner's Defence. They are both my own.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Life.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
