 1345.  THOMAS KEAN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of June , 4 deal boards, value 3s., and 6 ends of joists, value 6s., the goods of Edward Holgate.
   WILLIAM STOTTARD. I am foreman to Mr. Edward Holgate, builder, of Mitchell-street. The prisoner was in Mr. Wing's employ - he was working in Noble-street, where Mr. Holgate was employed; I got these boards out of the cellar of a public-house - I had seen them safe on the premises at half-past twelve o'clock that day.
   GEORGE MIDDLESHIP. I am a waiter at the Mail Coach public-house, Noble-street, Falcon-square. At ten minutes before one o'clock, on the 11th of June, the prisoner brought three baskets of wood for sale - we had bought some old wood of some of the men at the same buildings; I found this was new wood, and I shewed it to Stottard - my employer sent to Mr. Wing.
   HOWELL GODDARD. I went to the building to take the prisoner; he got down a ladder, and ran off, but I took him.
 Prisoner. He told me to run away; if Mr. Wing was here he would know how it was - I never to my knowledge brought any of that wood; some of the working men were there all the time.
 JURY to WILLIAM STOTTARD. Q. Are you sure it is your master's property? A. Yes - I found the fellow pieces on the premises; the men are not allowed to take any wood - they take chips; these are only part of what were gone. GUILTY. Aged 43.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1346.  ANN McCORMACK was indicted for that she, on the 26th of February , feloniously did receive 6 yards of drill, value 12s., and 2 yards of jean, value 2s., the goods of William Palmer , well knowing them to have been stolen.
 MR. RYLAND conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM PALMER. I am a hosier, and live on Ludgate-hill. About two years ago I commenced the business of a tailor, and took the prisoner's brother into my service to measure, and cut out at the end of the shop - there was a partition between us; the prisoner was employed to make clothes - she used continually to come backwards and forwards to receive the clothes and make them up; in about two years I gave up that business, and dismissed her brother - in consequence of suspicion I went to Mr. Gray's, about a month ago, and there found some drill; I went to the prisoner's lodging with a search-warrant - he was searched in my presence; the officer has the duplicates found on her, and some others which were found in the room; I had not before that missed any articles, but I did afterwards - I have seen some articles which were found at the pawnbrokers, and compared them with some pieces of drill and jean which I have.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did the prisoner buy any thing, or merely come for work? A. For work - her brother employed her to work at the business, what he told her I cannot tell - my attention was directed to John Boston, who lived with the prisoner as a husband- I accused  Edward McCormack and Boston, but one was acquitted, and the bill against the other was thrown out.
   WILLIAM MORGAN. I am a Police-officer. I went to the prisoner's premises on the 9th of May; in a box, which was owned by Boston, I found five duplicates, and on her I found two duplicates, but they did not belong to any property which the prosecutor claims.
 Cross-examined. Q. The box was claimed by Boston? A. Yes, but he said he knew nothing about any duplicates, and when Mrs. Boston came in she would tell me; when the prisoner came in I shewed them to her, but she made no observation.
 JOSEPH AVANT. I am in the employ of Mr. Gray, a pawnbroker, of Fleet-street. I took in this drill from the prisoner, in the name of Mary Thompson, and gave her this duplicate; she had been there before.
   JAMES TAGUS SHORT. I am a servant to Mr. Brown, a pawnbroker, in Fetter-lane. I have a piece of jean, pawned on the 28th of February.
   WILLIAM PALMER. These three pieces of drill are mine; I have the pieces here to match them.
 Cross-examined. Q.How do they match? A. In quality, colour, pattern and selvidge; it is impossible to see two of these things alike.
 JURY. Q. Have these original pieces been cut since February? A. I have not sold one yard of them in my life; they were cut up for trousers - there have been a number of trousers cut off, but not since McCormack left me, which was in April.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1347.  SUSAN JAMESON was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of May , 3 half-crowns, the monies of George Abbott , from his person.
   GEORGE ABBOTT. I live in Moorfields, and am a porter. On the 28th of May, at twelve o'clock at night, I shut up my master's shop in Westmoreland-buildings, and was going home to my father's; the prisoner caught hold of me - I told her to let go, as I was going home; she wanted me to go into a house, but I would not - I gave her 2d. to get rid of her; I then missed three half-crowns from my breeches pocket, and told her she had them - she began swearing, and I said she should not go till the watchman came; he came up and took her - I did not go up any alley, or to any house with her; I cannot say what pocket I took the 2d. from - she had been behind me; three half-crowns were found on her.
   EDWARD PAGE. I am a watchman. I was on duty twenty minutes after twelve; the prosecutor cried Watch! I went up, and he said he had lost three half-crowns - I took the prisoner to the watch-house, and the officer found some halfpence on her; she then squatted down on the floor, and put the three half-crowns out of her mouth into her hand.
