   Re-examined.  I gave her the 5 s . partly out of charity and partly because I wanted to discover where my watches were—she did not come back—I had put the matter into the hands of the police before I gave her the 5 s .—I paid her railway fare—I am satisfied as to the prisoner's identity. RICHARD WILDEY( Police Inspector ). From information received I went to Chelmsford on 30th July and took the prisoner into custody there—he knew me before—I said "I shall take you into custody for stealing some watches"—he stopped me and said "He wrote a letter to my wife telling her to come and ask me what had become of the pawn-tickets, and gave her 5 s . to come and see me; and because she told him I knew nothing about them he said he would swear to me"—on the way to the station he said "Where were those watches from, Barking way?"—I said "Bark ing Road"—he said "Yes, my wife told me so"—I had not mentioned Barking Road to him previously—descriptions of the watches have been circulated; they have not been recovered—the prisoner was living in Ocean Street, Stepney, three or four miles from the prosecutor.
   Witnesses for the Defence. LOUISA WALTON. I am a tailoress, of 66, Ocean Street, and am married—three weeks before 3rd August I was lodging in the same house with the prisoner's wife, occupying the front room upstairs—on the Tuesday the prosecutor called to see the prisoner's wife—I sent for her, and then went to my room upstairs, where I could hear what was said in the kitchen—the prosecutor said "Is your husband at home?"—he said "No"—he said "He is locked up"—she said "Yes, I know he is"—he said "I saw him in the Barking Road once, but I let him pass me, because I was not sure he was the man"—he wrote on a piece of paper which I saw afterwards the number of the watches, and his name and address—I know his hand from a letter he sent, which I saw a week afterwards—when he left the paper on the table he said "Be sure you don't show this to any one."
   Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHEGAN . I have been lodging with the prisoner's wife two years, and am doing so still—the prisoner was living with his wife in June; he went out at 7 in the morning and came back at 5—I don't know where he was working, I never asked him—when I first went he worked for his brother—on 15th June he went out and came home about the same time—we have lived at three addresses together during the two years—I said at the police-court that the prosecutor said he was not sure the prisoner was the man—the prisoner's wife gave me no papers to bring here—she had the paper the prosecutor left on the table, and the letter he wrote afterwards—I live in the same house still; they have left—I owe the prisoner's wife no money—we paid 6 s . 3 d . a week for the house; I paid 3 s . for the top room.
