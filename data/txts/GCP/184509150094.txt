THOMAS SMITH (police-constable N 93.) I produce the fowl-I was on
duty at Low Leyton, on the 25th of Aug., at a quarter past six o'clock in
the evening, near Mr. Tyler's farm-I received information that the pri-
soner had got a fowl in a bag- I went after him, and asked what he had
got in the bag -- he told me he had got a fowl, and he bought it of a man
who was hawking it at Wanstead-1 took him to the station-the sergeant
sent me round to inquire, and I could find no one who had seen a man
hawking a fowl- I dare say it was three or four miles from Mr. George's
house where I saw the prisoner.
JAMES WARD (police-constable N 144.) Mr. George's Christian name
is Harvey.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you not sworn it was Robert Harvey
George? A. No- I did not know him till Wednesday; I then talked
to him, and he told me his name was Harvey George.
Not Guilty.

1900. MARY ANN WATSON and CATHERINE COLEMAN
were indicted for stealing, on the 8th of Sept., 1 pair of boots, value 38.;
the goods of Theophila Newton; and that Coleman had been before cor-
victed of felony.
THEOPHILA NEWTON. I am single, and am a milliner, living at No. 104,
Powis-street, Woolwich. On the 8th of Sept. I had a pair of boots hang.
ing on a horse inside my shop-I saw them safe at eight o'clock in the
morning, and missed them at half-past four in the afternoon these produced
are them--the prisoners came to my shop together about three o'clock that
afternoon-I had never seen them before.
GEORGE STEVENS. I am shopman to Mr. Davis, a pawnbroker, at
Woolwich. On the 8th of Sept., about four o'clock, Watson brought
these boots--10 one was with her-she said at first they were her own,
and then that they belonged to Mrs. Smith-I saw nothing of Coleman.
