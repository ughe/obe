 Cross-examined. Q. Is that a convenient spot to get to the prosecutor's house? A.    JOSEPH RYAN. On the morning of the 7th of May I received information, about half-past four o'clock that the prisoner had some property - I saw her carrying a basket, and another woman and two men with her - I met a private watchman, and he joined me in the pursuit; when they got to Bishopsgate-street, they crossed over towards Petticoat lane - when we came up to them they all ran; the prisoner was taken, with this property.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you found out that she is married? A. I have heard so.
   JAMES CASTLE. I am a watchman of Bishopsgate ward. On the morning in question I pursued the prisoner to Bishopsgate-street: there were two men and another woman, who made their escape - the prisoner had this basket and this property on her head.
   JAMES PAYNE. I was on duty at Bishopsgate watch-house. I received the prisoner in charge with this bundle of silk, and the basket.
   MILES HANLEY. I am an officer. I went to the prosecutor's premises, and found they had been opened by means of a hole made with a centre-bit - I found this dark-lantern - this centre-bit, and this piece of wood there.
   JAMES HADFIELD. I know this is Mr. Kent's property- here is a mark on this bundle, in his hand-writing, which I can swear to.
 Prisoner's Defence. My husband told me to carry it- I did not know what it was.

 1769.  WILLIAM TUBBS was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July , 4 yards of silk, value 50s., and 10 yards of silk warp, value 6s., the goods of Samuel Apling.
   DANIEL APLING. I am foreman to my brother Samuel, a silk manufacturer, of Spital-square. On the 18th of July I delivered to Rattery this warp and shoot to work - she worked at the prisoner's house, in Quaker-street; on the 29th of July she came and stated that it was stolen during the night or early in the morning; I went to the prisoner's house that morning, about halfpast eight o'clock, and asked how he supposed it had been stolen; he said the thieves must have broken in at the back door - I found it had been cut out of the loom -I examined the back door; there was not the slighest appearance of violence - I heard Rattery say, in the prisoner's presence, that Woodcock did not sleep in the house that night - he did not contradict her.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe about this time there were a great many depredations committed on looms? A. Not within a year and a quarter - the prisoner was given into custody in about an hour.
   JANE RATTERY. I work at the loom, and live at the prisoner's house - he is my uncle; he is a weaver. Woodcock used to lodge there, but did not sleep there on the 28th or 29th of July; I finished about twenty-four yards of silk, and had about one hundred yards of warp - I left about nine o'clock at night on the 28th, leaving it safe; I slept in Pope's Head court - I went next morning about seven o'clock, and the silk was gone - when I left there was nobody in the house but the prisoner and a strange man - I told the prisoner I was not coming back, and he said he should not go out that night: on my missing the silk I told the prisoner I thought he had done it - he turned round, to a man who sat there, and said, "There is a b-h;' somebody asked where Woodcock was - the prisoner said he did not sleep there that night.
 Cross-examined. Q. At what time did you tell the prisoner that he had done it? A. About eight o'clock - he remained at home till he was taken - I did not sleep there that night, because his wife was absent, and I did not like to be where so many men were constantly coming.
   HARRIET TIMS. I live in this house; the prisoner rents it, and occupies two rooms above me. Before Rattery left, the prisoner came down, and told me he was not well and should go to bed, would I fasten the door; the street door stood open from that time till ten minutes past ten o'clock - I bolted the back door inside; I went to bed between ten and eleven o'clock, and about eleven o'clock the prisoner was taken ill; he came down, and asked me to give him a light - he went into the yard, returned in a few minutes, and I heard him bolt the door after he came in; I heard a noise about two o'clock, or a little after, in the passage - I called out, "Is that Tubbs?" somebody answered, Yes - I cannot say whose voice it was, for I was hardly awake; I said "If it is you, will you fasten the door?" he came about seven o'clock, knocked at my door, and said he had lost his work, and that he had found the back door open.   NOT GUILTY.
