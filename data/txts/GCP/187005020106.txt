EDWIN CORBETT (re-examined by MR. SERJEANT SLEIGH). I have lived either
at my father's or at my own home all my life; that is true I have lived
in a school, not the British School at Lambeth, at the other side of Batter.
sen-I am twenty-seven years of age-I am my father's eldest son, he is a
gasfitter; when I was a lad about eleven or twelve I got into trouble onoe,
and was convicted summarily before a Magistrate for stealing, only once-
I know that gentleman (Capt. Layland) he is the governor of the Reforma
tory--I could not say how long I was in the Reformatory, I was in gaol
three months before I went there ; I was convicted at Lambeth-I dare-
say I might have been something like twelve months in the Reformatory-
I may have told Captain Layland that I had been a very bad boy, and had
stolen many things, such as apples from greengrocers' shops; I have
arrived at manhood now, and have forgotten all those frivolous things-
can satisfy you of my respectability since; I have never been in trouble since
I left the Reformatory.
MR. METCALYE. 0. The Reformatory did its proper work? A. It did,
and I am very thankful that I ever went there the summary conviction
was for taking some milk, or something of that sort. Nor Guilty.
