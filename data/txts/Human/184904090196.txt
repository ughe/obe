 1045. ELIZABETH SMITH and EMMA MARTIN were indicted for a like offence. JAMES CHAPMAN. On 3d March the prisoners came to my tavern at Rotherhithe,"and asked for half-a-quartern of gin—Smith gave me 6 d ., it was a little worn, I put it into a bowl in the till—there was a new sixpence there of the Queen's reign—a policeman came—I took it out, found it was bad, and marked it. THOMAS TOWERSEY( policeman ). I saw the prisoners go into Mr. Chapman's—I went in after they came out, and received sixpence from him; I came out, and saw the prisoners in front of the Black Bull—I took Martin, and told her the charge—she said she knew nothing of it ANN CARPENTER. I keep the Black Bull, at Rotherhithe. On 3d March the prisoners came, Martin asked for half a quartern of gin, and gave me 6 d .—I tried it, and said it was bad—she appeared surprised, and asked Smith If she could give me 2 d ., which she did—I put the sixpence into a bowl in the till—there was nothing there but farthings—a constable came, and I gave it to him. JOHN WHITLAMB( policeman, M  89). I took Smith, and found on her 1 s . 10 1/2 d . and a fourpenny bit  —I produce sixpence, which I received from Mrs. Carpenter. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. These two sixpences are counterfeit, and from the same mould.
   SMITH  — GUILTY. Aged 19.
   MARTIN  — GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Nine Months .
 1046. CHARLES LEE , robbery with violence on Benjamin Hatfield , and stealing from his person 1 half-crown, 2 shillings, 1 sixpence, 9 pence, and 18 halfpence, his moneys; having been before convicted.
   MESSRS'. BODKIN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. BENJAMIN MORRISS. I live in Walworth. On 18th March, 1848, the day of the great meeting on Kennington-common, I saw 300 or 400 persons leave the common, and go  in the direction of Thomas-street, near Mr. Lyon's, the bakers—the prisoner was the ringleader—he stood in front of them with a club, which he waved, and the mob left Mr. Lyon's, and went down Thomas-street after him—I afterwards saw them in Southampton-street, Camberwell—they began smashing Mr. Hatfield's, the tailor, windows—the prisoner was waving his club there in front of them.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Waving his stick is all he seems to have done? A.  Yes—I had not seen him before to know him—there were two or three other dark men there, of course—I was. at the former trial, two black men were tried then—I next saw the prisoner at Maidstone jail—Quinnear showed him to me—I was always positive of him—I am an engineer—I worked at Mr. Carter's, but lost my place through coming here before—I was not very cross about it—it was a very good place—I am not a Chartist—I went to see the meeting—I did not go there housebreaking—I am nineteen years old.
