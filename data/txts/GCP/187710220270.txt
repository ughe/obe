meant-I said that I was there to arrest his brother, and he said “Now,
Thomas, if there is anything you can tell the officers about this business
do so." Thomas said "Well, all I know about it is this: I bid for Penn's
iron, and Porter heard of it. Porter saw me, and said 'What do you
want to bid for Porter's iron for, I have had it so long; let me bave it
this time, I can get some overweight and you shall have part, or half
of what I make,' and he afterwards gave me some money, 8l. or 101, I
don't know which. I do not think there was anything wrong about
taking that money, as I advanced him part of the money to pay for the
iron, and, of course, I ran a certain risk.” He said that he was not fit to
be removed—I saw his medical man, and he was left there with an officer
in charge of him.
Cross-examined by Mr. BESLEY. I understood him to say "I cannot
account for the deficiency in the weight," or it may have been "for any
deficiency”—he did not tell me that he had seen Whitling-I had been
watching outside for a long time-he said that he had gone over the
garden wall to his mother's so that I should not see him, as he did not
want to be locked up that night.
Cross-examined by MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. I believe Douglas is &
wharfinger-I believe other officers have been to bis wharf to make in-
quiries- I only went to his house-he might have learnt that proceedings
were going on-there were four or five remands—I was present at all the
Magistrate did not say on the last renand that there was no case against
Douglas-he did not intimate bis intention to discharge Douglas or ans-
thing to that effect-I think it was on the last hearing that Whitling was
produced—the Magistrate had given an intimation about Douglas's case
on the previous hearing, and then Wickling was produced for the first
time, but not in consequence of what the Magistrate said he was going to
be a witness, that was understood, and he was there at each hearing, but
