Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Was not what you said that they
were three you thought were yours? A. Yes, they looked like mine--it
has turned out since that only two were of the same kind as mine-
they are all three of the same quality-I speak from merely seeing it in
the cart, and then looking at it in the stable.
Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS. Q. I suppose at Whitechapel,
there are public-houses? A. I seldom go into one, except to have a bit
of bread and cheese-I cannot tell whether I did that morning- I cannot
recollect Byers and I going to a public-house--it is not customary to bind
the bargain-I agreed to give six guineas a load.
JOSEPH SEARING. I live at Cheshunt, in Herts. On this Saturday, I
was driving a horse and cart-I saw Woodhouse's wagon with two loads
of hay-I saw Ricketts and the boy on the top, and Skiggs was driving
the wagon-they stopped at the Elephant-Rickett's got off, and went
with Skiggs into the house, and had some beer, Skigge then came
and took the fore horse, and Ricketts drove the hind horse, and turned
down the street by the stable-door-I saw Bennett by the stable- I saw
Skiggs undo the ropes-Ricketts got on the top, and took three trusses
down, the first Bennett took in his arms, the next truss Ricketts set
down on the ground-then he took the other down, and Ricketts spread the
cloth over the remainder of the clover--then Bennett, Ricketts, and the
boy came to the door, and Bennett gave Ricketts some money– I do not
know how much.
Cross examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How many times have you been
charged with felony ? A. Never.
EBENEZER WOODHOUSE. I am nephew of Mr. Woodhouse. I am
twelve years old-I came up with two loads of my uncle's hay on this
occasion-Ricketts was the carman-after that I went with Ricketts to
Mr. Rhodes's-before we got out of Whitechapel, Skiggs came to us-
after he came up, Ricketts got on the top of the wagon, and Skiggs drove-
they stopped at the Elephant, in the Kingsland-road-we stopped there,
and Skiggs took the fore horse, and Ricketts drove the bind horse, and
took the wagon down by the side of the public-house stable --I saw Ben-
nett by the side of the stable-Skiggs undid the ropes, and Ricketts put
the head of the tilt up, and then put three trusses of hay down-Bennett
got them, and put them into the stable--Bennett and Ricketts had some
drink, and Skiggs went on with the wagon-Bennett gave Ricketts
some money, and then I went on after the wagon.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Where were you all the time!
A. On the wagon-Skiggs drove it round into the high road again-1
saw Mr. Searing behind with his cart-I did not tell any body about it.
ALLAN CARSWELL. I am in the service of Messrs. Rhodes--when the
wagon arrived there were sixty-nine trusses-there ought to have been
seventy-two-I remember Mr. Banks returning from the market before
the prisoners left the yard-Skiggs was in the yard at the time.
