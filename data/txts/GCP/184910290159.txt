CHARLES FRASER, unlawfully obtaining 7 soverigns and 1 half-sove-
reign; the moneys of Francis Cuthbertson, by false pretences :2nd COUNT,
for a conspiracy.
MR. M'MAHON conducted the Prosecution.
FRANCIS CUTHBERTSON. I am a bootmaker, and live at 123, Aldersgate-
street, City. On Tuesday, 28th Aug., in consequence of a message which I
received, I went to the Clarence Hotel, and in the coffee-room there I saw
the prisoner, Smith, and a Mr. Collins, in his company--Captain Smith, as
be called himself, stated to me that the landlord had gone out of town, or he
would have given him the money for this check (producing it)-I unfortu-
nately knew Smith for a few weeks before, as a customer-I looked at the
check, and finding it was made payable to Mr. F. Collins, who was in Smith's
company, and his companion, and knowing Mr. Collins to be a man of pro-
perty, and as it was drawn payable to him, I supposed it was a good instru-
ment, and immediately took out my purse and gave Smith 72. 108. for it;
that is the amount the check is drawn for- I did not at that time perceive
that the check was post-dated, the circumstance of its being crossed was the
only thing that induced me to give them the money without its going
through a banker -Smith alladed to its being crossed, and said, as the
check was crossed, would I give him the money for it, as the landlord
was out of town-I did not discover that it was post-dated till next
day, when it was going to be sent to the bankers; Smith never alluded
to it-I afterwards charged him with giving me a post-dated check-he did
not explain the circumstance at all--the only explanation I had was, that
