The Account which this Prifoner gave me, for the Satisfaction of
any one who should be inquifitive about bim, is as follows. He went
very Young he faid (from a Town near Dublin in Ireland where he
was born) to the City of Cork, and was there put Apprentice to a
Cooper, but could not by his Mafter be perfwaded at all to Labour
yet he continued about that Place above four Years, tho' his Mafter
was by no means defirous he fhould there remain and took an uncom-
mon Care of his Houfe, left he should be injur'd in the Night-time by
Angier or his Companions So that at length his Father perceiving, that
the Mafter was wholly refolved to have nothing more to do with bim,
was prevail'd upon to carry him from Ireland to England, and let him
try his Fortune, where he would have. an Opportunity of preferring
himfelf.
After he had wander'd about, and been in feveral Places, he was in
Danger from fome he had wrong'd, and therefore enlifted in the King's
Service, (at Eighteen Years of Age, as he faid) It being the time of the
late Rebellion in Scotland, he was in expetation of being ordered thi-
ther, where he had Hopes of improving his Fortune, at the Expence of
the Country Folks, without much Guilt, as he believed.
After this, being known to one Duce, (who fuffer'd at the laft Exe-
cution) he improv'd in a wrong way from his Converfation. Becom-
ing alfo acquainted with the Sifter of Duce, marryed her, tho' he ac-
knowledg'd he had no vertuous Designs in his Mind at that time, as to
the Courfe of Lifehe intended to lead, together with her; adding, that
he wifh'd he had then dyed, or had had fome good Advice given him.
But he was glad that the Cruelty and Barbarity that was in Butler and
Mead, and others whom he too well knew, in fhooting all, or moft of
thofe they robb'd, was not in his Temper And Mead and Butler, &c.
having told him, that overtaking a young Man, a Shoemaker, in the
Dover Road, who told 'em he was lately marry'd, and was going to his
Friends, thereupon, they ask'd him to go and Icok at a Bird's Neft in the
Hedge, and there bound and gag'd and robb'd him, and afterwards
turn'd back and fhot out his Brains; this Angier affirm'd that he re-
fus'd ever to fit down or drink with Mead after that, and told Butlor
that Cruelty was not Courage.
