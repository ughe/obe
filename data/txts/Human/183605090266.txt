1378. GEORGE STAINER and HENRY JORDAN were indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. CHAMBERS and SHEA conducted the Prosecution . ANNA MARIA COLWELL. I am the wife of a tobacconist, and live at Bridge-house-place, Newington, Surrey. Stainer came to our shop on the 14th of April, for 1/4 oz . of tobacco—it came to 1 d .—he gave me a sixpence—I had not change enough—he said he would have 1/2 oz .—I gave him 4 d ., and took his sixpence, and laid it at the corner of the till—Langley then came in—I took the till out, and showed him the sixpence—it was a bad one.
   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN . Q.  How long elapsed from the time of your taking it and the officer coming in? A.  I should think not a minute—my husband was out at the time—there had been other customers in the shop, but only Stainer brought a sixpence—when my husband went out there was silver in the till; but I put the silver I take in a corner of the till, to show my husband what I have taken in his absence—there is no division in it; but I took the till out, and showed the officer the sixpence in the corner—I had put no mark on it before.
   MR. SHEA . Q.  Did you shut the till hard? A.  I do not remember—I do not think that one sixpence could jump out, and another jump in. MARY EVANS. I live with Mr. Budden, who keeps the Rose. I saw Stainer about five o'clock, on the 14th of April—he offered me a sixpence—I just laid it in the till—there were other sixpences there—the officer came in at one door as the prisoner went out—he asked me for it—I had just laid the sixpence out of my fingers—I gave it him—I am sure it was the same sixpence—it was just in one corner of the till—I had not lost sight of it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is yours a till that draws out? A.  Yes; we put the money through a notch in the counter—when I drew it out I saw a good deal of silver in the till—I had just laid the sixpence out of my hand when the officer came, but not out of my sight—all the silver was in my sight, and that among the rest.
   JURY . Q.  Had you shut the till? A.  No, I had nearly shut it. MARIA LOUIBA JAQUES. I am a tobacconist, and live in Lambeth. On the 14th of April, Stainer came for a 1/4 oz . of tobacco—it came to 1 d .—he gave me a sixpence—I gave him change—I put the sixpence into the till—there was no other sixpence in it—there were shillings and half-crowns—in a short time Langley came in—I showed him the sixpence—it was a bad one—I gave it to him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you always attend to the shop? A.  Yes—I had sold a good many cigars—some are 3 d . each—I dare say I had sold some that afternoon—when I gave the officer the sixpence I looked in the till, and there was no other sixpence there—we must have taken some and passed them again.
