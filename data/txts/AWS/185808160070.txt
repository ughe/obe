CARDEN, Mayor.
789. DANIEL SULLIVAN (19), Stealing 1 purse and 98. of John
Lawrence, from the person of Elizabeth Lawrence.
Mr. CAARTEN conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH LAWRENCE. I am the wife of John Lawrence, who lives in
Princes-street Drury-lane-on Sunday evening, 18th July, I was going
along Fleet-street my husband was in advance of me; I felt a hand being
drawn from my pocket-] immediately laid hold of the hand-I had a
purse with me which contained 98.-I had seen it safe not two minutes
before-when I seized the hand, the man immediately ran-I kept hold of
him-he ran quite as far as to the end of this court-I had an imperfect
view of the man, and to the best of my belief the prisoner is the man he
had a rough coat on-I kept hold of him, and he made a sudden jerk and
left the coat in my hand, and ran awayacross the road and escaped-he got
out of the coat-he made a jerk which nearly dislocated my shoulder, and
left the coat in my hand-I felt the edge of my purse in his hand-] after.
wards missed it.
Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. Q. You ean't swear to the prisoner
being the man A. No-my husband was in advance of me-he 8aW me
running I have no doubt but he saw the man-I was holding the man
and running-my husband did not run to my assistance, he is too old to
run no one else was with me-the man got out of the coat very easily.
JAMES THORNDIKE (City policeman, 286). On 19th July, I went to
Union-court, Holborn-hill, about 9 o'clock in the evening-I found the
prisoner at the Union public-house with two other persons. went to him
and said, I want you for picking a woman's pocket in Fleet-street, last
night" said, 4 I don't know anything at all about "I asked him
where his rough jacket was-he said, I don't know what you mean "I
told him he must go with me to the station-he said, no, he should
not-I took him by the collar, and told him he was in my custody
they commenced fighting and kicking, and one of his companions got both
my arms behind me, and they dragged the prisoner away he made his
eseape-I saw him again on Saturday night, 7th August, in Upper White-
cross-street I followed him-he went into a public-house-I went to him,
and said, I suppose you know what I want with you m"-he said, 4 Yes,
master, I do; I was coming on Monday to give myself up have known
the prisoner about two years-I know this coat (looking at it), I have seen
the prisoner wear it, I might say some hundreds of times-I saw him on
18th July, between 1 and 2 o'clock in the afternoon, and he had this coat
on- saw him again on Monday morning, the 19th, without any coat at
all then it was between 9 and 10 o'elock, before I had received information of
this robbery-when I took him in the evening, he had a smooth cloth coat
-I have not the least doubt that this rough coat is the coat the prisoner
wore-I know it by the colour of it, the cut, and a button off the collar.
Cross-ecamined. Q. You saw him on Monday morning without a coat
A. Yes, in Union-court, between 9 and 10 o'clock-there might have been
some more men at work there without coats I did not take notice of any
one but him-this is not the sort of coat that a man would wear in July,
but if a man has only one coat he must wear it.
