Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How long was the cabman at Ben
Caunt's? A. About three hours, walking backwards and forwards, and now
and then having a drop to drink-the cab was waiting outside I do not know
what fare the cabman asked for-when he was in the river I do not think he
was lying on the bottom-it was high tide, too high for him to be in the mud
- he was 200 yards from the side-it was about a quarter past 12 o'clock on
2nd Jan.-(it was high water at 10).
BENJAMIN THAXTER. I am the captain of the Petrel, and live at Yarmouth.
I trade between anywhere in the north and London--the prisoner was my
mate-on the night of 2nd Jan. I went on board with my wife between 11
and 12 o'clock- I went down into the cabin, ordered the cabman out, and he
went afterwards found he was in the water, and helped to pull him out-
we sailed about half past 3 next morning.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Was a summons sent to you at
Yarmouth ? A. Yes; I told the mate, and arranged that when we came to
London we should go and see about it-we came up again last Tuesday week
- the prisoner's wages were 31. 2s. 6d. per month he is a very steady, kind-
hearted man-he has often persuaded me not to drink, and has refused to
drink when I have offered it to him.
Guilty. Aged 28.-Confined One Month.
