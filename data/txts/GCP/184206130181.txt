1908. HENRY BODMAN was indicted for embezzlement.
MARY SPARROW. I am single. I keep a china and glass shop in Pad.
inston-street-the prisoner was my errand-boy-he came into my service
The 2nd of May-he received some monies for me which be brought to
me on the 3rd of May I sent him out with some china in a basket, to
No. 6, York-buildings, New-road-he was to receive 228.- he did not re-
turn to bring the china or the money-I saw him again on the 2nd of June,
in charge.
EMWA WELLS. I live with Mrs. Eassy, at No. 6, York-buildings. I
honeht a set of china at Mrs. Sparrow's, and the prisoner went with me to
carry it in a basket-I took it out of the basket, and gave him the basket,
with a sovereign, and 2s. in silver.
Prisoner. This witness would not pay me, because I had not got a re-
ceipt. Witness. I gave him the money in the passage.
Thomas Henry THOMPSON (police-sergeant D 4.) On the 2nd of June
the prisoner was given to me by his father, who told me what he was
charged with-I found Is, in one of the legs of his trowsers, and 28. in the
other--as we were going to the office I said, " What have you done with
the basket ?"-he said, "I threw it away in a court in Fleet-street."
Prisoner's Defence. She would not pay me because I had not a receipt.
I was coming back, a young man met me, and took me to the Surrey
Theatre. I threw away the basket in a court in Fleet-street. The young
man kept me about a week, and then gave me 58.
GUILTY. Aged 15.--Transported for Seven Years.-Convict Ship.
1909. EMILY WYATT was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of
April, 1 pair of spectacles, value 20s., the goods of William Hexter.
CHARLOTTE HEXTER. I am the wife of William Hexter, of Adam-street
Past, Manchester-square-the prisoner called on the 14th of April, under
the pretence of telling me of a place for my son-(I had seen her once be-
fore).--I had a pair of silver spectacles on the table--they were safe about
two hours before she came- I missed them next morning-these now
produced are them.
RICHARD KING TAYLOR. I live in St. Martin's-lane, and am a tailor.
The prisoner lodged in my house from the 10th to the 16th of April-on
the 14th of April I saw her wearing a pair of silver spectacles about the
house--they were like these-I never saw her wear spectacles before or
after.
HENRY Hutchins. I am apprentice to Mr. Clark, a pawnbroker, in
Long-acrè. These spectacles were pawned by Miller.
MARY Ann Miller. I live in Parker-street, Drury-lane. I was ser-
want to the prisoner-in April last I remember her bringing home a pair of
silver spectacles, which she said she had found-she gave them to me to
pawn, which I did at Mr. Clark's, for 3s. 60.-I gave her the money.
Prisoner's Defence, I did take them and pawn them, but I meant to
take them back. The prosecutor told me, if I would tell where they were
she would not come against me: I did tell, and then she took me. I am
a foreigner, and did not know the law was so strict,
