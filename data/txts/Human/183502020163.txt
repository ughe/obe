   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  What are you? A.  A bar-man. This was about nine o'clock in the evening—there was a crowd round us before the prisoner came up, but I had dragged the boy who had robbed me, across the street, because the people wanted to get me down a street—there were all sorts of characters round me—when the prisoner came up the mob was pushing me about—I am quite sure they did not push me against the prisoner—I am sure I did not go against him—I had my eye on Brown, but when I found I had tight hold of him, I looked for a officer—I was not very much confused—Brown has been tried here this morning—I am quite sure the prisoner struck me—he might have been a second in front of me before he struck me—he had time to see his compa nion, if he was his companion, but they had not been in company then—this was last Tuesday week—I had a swollen nose. WILLIAM DUNGATE( police-constable F  22.) I was on duty, and saw the mob—I saw the prosecutor detaining a boy, and the prisoner struck him a violent blow on the nose—I did not hear any cry then—I afterwards heard the cry of, "Go it, Dan."
 (—O'Grady, of Charlotte-street, and—Taylor, of Charlotte-street, gave the prisoner a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined One Year.
