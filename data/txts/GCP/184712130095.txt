Second Session, 1847–48.
19. JOHN THOMAS BAGGS, stealing 6 sovereigns and 1 101. note;
he moneys of John Clarke ; from his person,
JOUX CLARKS. I live in Queen's-terrace, St. John's-wood. On 1st of
Deo. I went with the prisoner to the Abbey-tavern-we had some gin-and-
vater had a 101. bank-note in my pocket, and five or six sovereigns-I
stopped there perhaps an hour and a half-the prisoner was with me the
whole time, and he only there was a master-builder in the parlour part of
the time, but we sat in separate chairs-I fell asleep for a short period, and
when I awoke the prisoner was gone, and my money also- the policeman kas
pot the number of the note, and I knew the number-previous to going to
the tavern the prisoner had asked me to advance him sixpence or a shilling
Je said he had not had anything to drink for a day or two--I said I was
Morty, but if bo would accompany me to the Abbey-tavern I would give him
some refreshment
Prisoner, Q. When you called on me was I not getting my breakfast
2. No: there was no food of any description there we had been in a
public-house before we got to the Abbey-tavern- I took nothing there - you
did not pay for some ale in my presence.
JAMES SMITH. I keep the White Lion in Edgware-road-I know the
prisoner - he came to my house on 1st Dec., about five in the evening, and
asked for change for a 101. note-he was told he could not be accommodated
- he left and returned in about an hour and a half-he went into the front
foom, remained some time, and fell asleep, leaving a bundle unprotected-
the waiter saw it and brought it to the bar-I took posession of it--the
prisoner came afterwarde to know if it had been delivered over the bar, and
I was shown him; be took it and returned into the room--he remained a
part of the evening and required a bed he came to the bar to go to bed
larning be bad money I recommended that he should hand me the money
before he went to bed-he deposited nine sovereigns with me he had a new
great coat on.
- -
Prisoner, You bave known me many years, and always knew me to
be a respectable person Witnost. I never heard anything of the like of this
before you are acquainted with the people who use my house.
RICHARD PAINTER, I live with Mr. Grove, an outfitter, in Edgware-road
--the prisoner came about six o'clock in the evening, on 1st Dec. he bought
two jackets and a pair of trowsers, which came to 11. 78. 9d.-he paid with
i 101, note- he said he was in an office in Somerset-house.
CHARLES CALLAGAN ( policeman, D 134.) The prisoner was given into
my ebarge-he said, "What is all this about? what is all this fuss about"
-I found on him a half-sovereign, 88, 6d. in silver, and 6d. in copper-Mr.
Smith delivered to me six sovereigns,
Prisoner's Defence. I had been all the day along with Mr. Clarke; I have
always conducted myself in a respectable way, but I bad too much to drink
with him, which led me to appropriate what did not belong to me; I did not
pick his pocket, but picked up the note from the chair where he was sitting
I admit I changed the note and appropriated part to my own use; I put my
own name on the back of the note; I hope you will be merciful to me; if I
should be released, I will do everything to remunerate him; I declare I
never saw the sovereigns.
GUILTY. Aged 36.- Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury-
Confined Nine Months.
