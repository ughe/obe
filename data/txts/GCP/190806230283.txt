Cross-examined. I make no complaint that I was not honestly
trested in respect of these betting transactions-quite the contrary,
It was not the first time I had made bets. I have made bets both
professionally and unprofessionally. That I did not find myself in a
criminal court may have been due to the fact that I did not make
them in a "place." Before searching prisoner's premises I had been
informed that prisoner had been to the police and said he thought
his house was under observation, and if the police liked to go there
he would point out anything they wanted to know. As to whether
since the case of Stoddart was decided there has been any prosecution
of a bookmaker similar to this, I can only speak with accuracy for
the Metropolitan police, and there has not been, but a prosecution
was launched against a bookmaker at Southport two months ago.
The prosecution were not able to obtain a conviction without testing
the other part of the question. I am aware that it is now a common
practice for bookmakers to have a Dutch or foreign office, but that
has only arisen since the passing of the Street Betting Act. I very
seldom bet, but have sometimes done so when I have been on a
racedourse. During the time I was at prisoner's house he gave me
overy assistance he could. There was no concealment of any kind.
Prisoner told me at the last Sessions that he had abandoned the
foreign office as the legality of the practice was to be tested, but I
have not ascertained it the statement is true.
WILLIAM SYDNEY NASH, clerk in the General Post Office secretary's
office, gave formal evidence with reference to the postal orders.
GEORGE ALFRED DEPRIES, clerk in the Plaistow branch of the
London and Provincial Bank, produced a number of paying in slips
forwarded by Pattisor: to prisoner's account during March and April,
representing £261 12s. 2d.
Verdiet, Guilty.
The Recorder (to prisoner). The sentence is that you pay a fine
of £100 to the King. I pass this sentence because I rather gather
that the object is to try and raise this question. It must be clearly
ur.derstood that if cases of this kind come before me I shall think it
my duty to pass sentence of imprisonment, having regard to the great
mischief which in my opinion arises from the use of houses in this
way. I think that should be krown.
Mr. Ritter. There is a certain amount of money in the hands of
the police which it is not suggested was improperly come by. I sug-
gest that it should be applied to the fine.
The Recorder. I have no power to make an order with regard to
it. I have to doubt the police will do what is right in the matter.
