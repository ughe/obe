   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Supposing goods were bought for shipment, would they remain on your premises? A.  They would go to the East India ware-house, and remain some days before they went—we should take care of their going abroad—by the prisoner representing he wanted these on approbation, he got them off the premises at once—I should not have suffered them to leave the warehouse unless he had represented they were for approbation. MICHAEL WILLS. I am warehouseman to Messrs. Tucker and Co. I packed up these goods and delivered them to Cliff. HENRY CLIFF. I took these goods to Mr. Elkin's, No. 38, Bucklersbury—there was no appearance of shutting up the shop then. JAMES PATTISON. I am an agent for Irish linens—I have an office at No. 2, Church-passage, Guildhall. On the 27th of April I received seventy pieces of Korah silk handkerchiefs from the prisoner—I gave him 21 s . 6 d . a-piece for them—I had dealings with him before—he owed me better than 100 l .—these handkerchiefs went in discharge of the debt—he gave me them, and a check for 22 l .—these goods have been since seized by the Sheriff—I had made a contract with the prisoner about my receiving these goods—about the beginning of April he delivered five pieces as a sample—I expected three on the 27th of April—I meant to buy them of him—I gave him an order for 100 pieces.
   COURT . Q.  Did you mean to export these? A.  yes: I got them for an export house.
   MR. BALLANTINE  here withdrew from the prosecution. NOT GUILTY.
