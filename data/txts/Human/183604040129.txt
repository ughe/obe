MARGARET HUMPHREYS was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of February , 1 locket, value 20 s  .; 1 printed book, value 5 s  .; 4 knives, value 5 s  .; 4 forks, value 5 s  .; 1 shift, value 5 s  .; and 1 petticoat, value 5 s  .; the goods of George Frederick Allcock , her master. GEORGE FREDERICK ALLCOCK. I live in Park-terrace, Brixton. The prisoner lived with me, as cook, for eight days previous to the 12th of Fe bruary—we missed a shift the day after she came into the house—when she left, on the 12th, in the morning, we missed the other things stated in the indictment—it was last in a drawer, that was kept locked, in the bed-room—she had access to the bed-room, but the key of the drawer was in my wife's possession—the prisoner had keys in her possession which opened my drawers—these are all articles.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS Q.  Do you know a woman of the name of Price? A.  Yes, and another of the name of Shaw—they are not ac quaintances of mine—they live in the neighbourhood—I never spoke to them before the transaction—the prisoner came from Wales I believe—she sent a letter by me to town—I do not remember my having a dispute with my wife about it—I never told her that my wife was amazed at her send ing a letter by me, and making me a postman—I never heard my wife say so—she was not jealous about it—she was angry—I had never given a present to this girl—I swear that—not of any kind—I did not speak to her about my wife's anger till she left the house—I saw her at Mr. Shaw's house—I went to his after she had left my service.
   Q.  Did you tell her about your wife's anger? A.  She did not tell me—she made a declaration in the presence of me and Mr. Shaw, but not to me.
   Q.  What brought you there at all? A.  Within an hour after she left the house, she came to my counting-house, to stated that the party whom she had introduced into the house the day before, (who turned out to he Mr. Shaw,) that Mrs. Allcock, when Mr. Shaw went down there, had stated, that she was such an infamous bad character she could not keep her in the house, and she would give him anything to take her away—she came to say she should apply to the police-office for a warrant against my wife—I went round to Shaw immediately, to know what had been stated, and then this declaration was made—I afterwards went to Mr. Shaw, and saw him—he had not been in our service—he was sent for to our house by the prisoner, to see her—I heard that from Shaw, and the prisoner was present at the time—I stayed there about a quarter of an hour—I never saw the prisoner afterwards till she was in custody—I did not give her in charge—she was in charge on another felony—I swear that I missed the things three or four days after she left, and I found her in charge, between a fortnight and three weeks afterward—I did not give her in charge, because she was not to be found—I went to Mr. Shaw's for her—I did not got to her aunt's—I went to a Mr. Williams, in Hatton-garden—no flirtation
