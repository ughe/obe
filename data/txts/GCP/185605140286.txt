MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLOTTE KING. I am an unfortunate woman, and live at No. 11
Wellesley-street, Seymour-street, New-road. On the night this happened, I
went into Smith's coffee shop, Caledonian-road-I had four sovereigns and
38. in a purse--I had some supper, which came to 5d. I know Wren, he
was a waiter there I had seen him very often when I have been there I
gave him a sovereign to pay for what I had, and he brought me the change
half a sovereign, and the rest in silver-I then went outside the door, and
Wren asked me if I would lend -him 58.--I said, “You do not want it"
he said, “I do "-I said, "I will lend you half a crown," and I did so
while I was talking to him a friend of mine, a girl, came up; she went in with
me and I paid for her supper, and a young man's, who was with her—while
I was talking to them Wren came in, and said, “There is a young woman
waiting to speak to you at the door "-I went out, and he went with me; I
said, "Where is she?" he said, "I do not know "-I looked round the
corner to see where she was, and Raymond knocked me down—I have seen
him in the coffee shop, he was not employed there-he struck me on the
back of the neck, and pushed me as well, knocked me down, and took my
purse, which was round my finger-I saw no young woman : Wren stood on
the step of the door looking on, while the other knocked me down, but he
did not interfere at all—I did not see the man before he knocked me down;
he beld me down with one hand, and took my purse with the other-he bit
me very hard, my neck and head were very sore afterwards; he hit and
pushed me both at once-he hit me with his left hand, he had a crutch in
his right, but did not hit me with that, he is lame- screamed when he
pulled my purse off my finger, but Wren did not take any notice; he was
there when I was knocked down and when I got up, but did not help me,
or join in the attack upon me-Raymond walked from me and stood against
the lamp post, and there he stood when I got up-I said to Wren, “You
have been the instigation of my being robbed, and I will not have you locked
up if you will give it to me, and I said the same to Raymond at the time
I was saying it, Jane Hamilton came out at the door; they both denied the
charge, and said that they knew nothing of it.
Court.Q. While you were talking to Wren, Raymond stood near the
lamp post ! A. Yes, he did not run away-when Jane Hamilton came out
I said, “I have lost my money, and this man has robbed me "--I had my hand
on his shoulder, and he went into the coffee shop; I followed him, and told
Mrs. Smith-she said that she knew nothing about it when Jane Hamilton
came, she accused Wren of the robbery, and he said that he knew nothing,
about it-while she was talking to Raymond, Wren went into the coffee
shop—when I went in and spoke to Mrs. Smith, I left Raymond outside,
still talking with my friend, she was accusing bim of the robbery when I
went in.
