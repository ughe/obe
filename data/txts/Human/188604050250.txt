   Re-examined.  The name of the man called Howell is Brocket—the prisoner came into the bagatelle room after I pointed him out to Fry—Bush was present. JAMES FRY( Policeman V  302). I am stationed at West Ham—on 22nd February, about 5.30, the prosecutor made a communication to me—he was rather excited—his trousers were cut at the top—I went to the spot the prosecutor indicated, and observed the ground—he made a statement to me there—on 6th March I was drinking with the prisoner in the Crooked Billet—the prosecutor came in the bar—he asked me if I would have anything to drink—I said, "No, thank you, I have got some"—the prisoner walked past the prosecutor into the back yard, when Sullivan made a communication to me—the prisoner paid for a portion of the drink—I have known him seven years—I spoke to the prosecutor when the prisoner was gone out—I went into the room where Bush and Sullivan were—Sullivan still insisted that that was the man—the prisoner had gone out—I went to the Ham Brewery, and told the prisoner that Sullivan accused him of knocking him down and robbing him—the prisoner had changed his clothes—directly after he was accused he went home—I waited for him, and he "went to Richmond Police-station with me after he had changed his clothes—these are the trowsers he had on before he changed his clothes—he had them on at the Crooked Billet—he said his clothes were dirty, and he did not want to go to Richmond Police-station in them—the prosecutor appeared at the police-station, and formally charged the prisoner with having robbed him—the prisoner had an opportunity of reply—he did not reply—the prosecutor was sober when he made the charge.
   Cross-examined.  I cannot say the prosecutor's excitement was due to drink—at the Brewery the prisoner, when told he was accused of the robbery, said "Never"—after he was charged he said, "I shall be able to show I was at Claridge's on that evening"—I have known him seven years as a blacksmith; I know nothing against him—he was in his working dress at the Crooked Billet—the prosecutor gave a rather rambling description, which I could not make anything of—he said the prisoner had brown trousers; these produced are not brown—I did not see the slightest sign of a scuffle on 22nd February at the spot where the robbery is said to have occurred; it was an open space on the grass; the ground was soft, and you would expect to find marks—there is a urinal in the yard at the Crooked Billet—the prisoner was outside the first door leading to the passage when the prosecutor spoke to me about him—the prisoner could have gained the road and got away from the back yard—he came back and finished his beer on the counter.
