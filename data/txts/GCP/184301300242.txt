COURT. Q. Do you mean that it was both before and after the deed in
blank was executed, advances were made to Mr. Constable ? A. No, to Mr.
Constable, and to Miss Constable after his death-that the claim of Stanley's
executors consisted of advances made both in Mr. Constable's life-time and
after his death I was so told at the time-the last item in this account is Oct.
30, 1838-that was supposed to be after Mr. Constable's death in 1840.
MR. CHAMBERS. Q. At the second interview, had this deed of the 14th of
Dec. 1841, been prepared ? A. No-at the first interview Miss Constable
expressed a wish to convey the surplus of this property to Mr. Blackwell
alone, and it stood over for me to see Miss Blackwell, which I did-they
were both interested under the will of Mr. Stanley, as executors and devisees
-I saw Miss Black well in the mean time; and, at the second interview, re-
ference was made to her being a party to the deed I have got the memoran-
dum of what took place at the second interview, with Miss Constable and the
prisoner-we further discussed the matter, and I took instructions to prepare
the deed-according to Mr. Kemp's, my conveyancer's opinion, in the best
way-allusion was made to the sum which ought to have been inserted in the
deed in blank, because the interests of some of the parties whose names were
in the deed in blank, without sums, had to be attended to in the new deed-
11091. was the sum mentioned to be inserted in the deed as the sum due to
Stanley's executors (referring to the draft)-that was to be taken as the sum
due to his executors, and was to include the sum which ought to have been in
the blank deed.
Court. Q. How could that possibly be, when Stanley did not die till six
months after Mr. Constable? A. As I was instructed, there was a sum due
to Stanley-1 was instructed that there was a debt, part paid in Mr. Constas
ble's life-time, to him, and part to Miss Constable, after bis death -- it was only
part of the sum that ought to have been in the blank deed.
