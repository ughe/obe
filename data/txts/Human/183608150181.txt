   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you say you never saw a woman once that you did not know again? A.  Yes—I took particular notice of her—she had a straw bonnet on, but I did not notice what ribbon—it was white straw and a green stuff gown—I cannot tell what kind of stuff—she had boots, I cannot say what colour—I took no notice—I observed nothing particular about her bonnet—I did not observe a feather, nor a red flower—it was between five and six o'clock on a Wednesday, in July—I had never seen the woman before—I told the policeman she was carrotty-haired woman, in a green gown, dark shawl, and straw bonnet—I did not tell him she had boots—it was a dark shawl, with a large flower at the corner—I did not take notice of the colour, or what kind of flower it was. MARTHA COWDROY. I am a widow, and carry on the business of a pawnbroker in Frederick-place, Borough-road. On the Wednesday even ing, between five and six o'clock, a female with a child in her arms came to pawn this handkerchief—I do not at all recollect the person—this is the handkerchief—she did not stay to take the duplicate—the child was crying very much—she said she would call again. TIMOTHY MABER. I keep a public-house in New Cut, Lambeth. On this Wednesday evening I remember the prisoner coming to my shop with as infant in her arms—she had a pennyworth of gin, which she paid me for, and after waiting some time, she had half a pint of fourpenny ale—I am positive she is the person—it was from a quarter to twenty minutes after six o'clock. WILLIAM TAYLOR( police-constable M  79.) In consequence of a de scription given to me, I went to Market-street, where the prisoner lived, and she opened the door—we told her we came about the child—she said she had heard of it, but knew nothing about it, that she had been out every day from eight o'clock in the morning till night—this ring I got from Mary Jones at the Town-hall.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where did you find this handkerchief? A.  At the pawnbroker's—I had known the prisoner before—I have had no quarrel with her—no more than when she has been in a disturbance—I did not say I would send her over the water if I could—Mrs. Cowdroy described the prisoner to me as a stoutish woman, with carrotty hair—that she had her hair plain, and she had a green gown on and a straw bonnet, and a child in her arms—she gave no other description—I am sure of that—she described no other article of dress—she did not say she had a dark shawl with a flower in the corner—not to my recollection. JOHN RICHARD COWLIN( police-sergeant M  5.) I went with Taylor to take the prisoner.
