ARRAHAM LAZARUS. I am a dealer in unredeemed pledges, and live in
Tenter-street, Spitalfields. About half-past ten o'clock on this night I was
in the Vinegar-ground-I saw a cab driving very furiously-it nearly knocked
me down-Henry Baker drove, and Robert was on the box with him-I said,
"Can't you mind where you are driving ?"-he made some answer, which I
did not hear-he drove on-the cab came up Vincent-street, and turned round
Catherine-street--it stopped there-both the men got down from the box
Henry opened the door, and Robert laid hold of something, which appeared to
me to be a bundle, but which turned out to be the prosecutor-he was bundled
out into the road-Johnson and Williams were in the cab Johnson had a
black bonnet on, and Williams had a straw bonnet on-Johnson was pushing
the prosecutor out-Williams was coming out of the cab, and was shoved
back by Johnson-the cab drove off-I halloed out, " Pick up your bundle"
---they went on-I went up, and found it was the prosecutor in a helpless
state- I gave directions, and the cab was brought back.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How near were you to the cab ?
A. About ten yards—I do not speak to the faces of Johnson or Williams,
only to their bonnets.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Do you buy goods of pawn-
brokers ? A. I do I have never acted as an attorney for prisoners in my
life-I might have given a brief over for prisoners as their friend, not deriving
any profit- I did not know them before--they were no friends of mine-I
did it for nothing I was tried seven years ago for a bedstead which I bought
-to the best of my belief there was three sovereigns and some silver
lying about on the pavement—when the cry was made the neighbours came
out-I am not living in a brothel, nor constantly in brothels—I was looking
for a lady that night.
JAMES Dawson. I am a cabinet-maker, and live in Catherine-street.
About half-past ten o'clock that night I was standing at my door-I saw the
cab stop, and, after a time, drive off again as hard as it could drive-some one
who was with me attracted my attention-I went where the cab stopped, and
found the prosecutor.
JAMES FARRELL (police constable N 145.) About eleven o'clock this night
I saw the cab in the Vinegar-ground-Henry and Robert Baker were on the
box-Johnson and Williams were inside- it was going very fast—we were
called to stop it- I turned round the corner, and saw the prosecutor-I
looked on the ground, and found three sovereigns and a half-crown-Lazarus
called my attention to the money-I got the prosecutor into the cab, and
took charge of Johnson and Williams, who were in the cab-another officer
got on the box-Williams began crying and wringing her hands, and said
several times she knew she should be transported now I was supporting the
prosecutor's head, and noticed Johnson stoop down--her hand slipped along
my leg-when I got to the station I got a light, and found one sovereign,
two half-sovereigns, 23. and one halfpenny in the cab-I searched Robert
Baker, and found 9s. 2d. on him.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You found nothing on Williams?
A. No.
JAMES CAFFRAY (police-constable N 153.) I was in Old-street Road, and
heard the cry of “Policeman, stop the cab"-I looked round, and saw the
cao coming furiously towards me-I ran and stopped it-I took H. and R.
Baker into custody -I went back, and found the prosecutor, took him into
the cab, and then I got on the box-while I was going along I asked Henry
where he got the prosecutor-he said he took him in Aldgate, drove him to
Finsbury, then to the Lock and Key, back to Aldgate, then to the City-road
