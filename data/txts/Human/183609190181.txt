   Prisoner's Defence.  One morning I was going down the street, and met this boy—he said, "Will you do me a favour?"—I said, "I don't mind"—he said, "Here is a jacket and trowsers"—I took them to the pawn-shop, it was not open, I took them to a house, and I took them again, I took half the money with me to sea—his name was Tom Everett—he is gone to sea—I have never seen him since.
   JURY to WILLIAM GEORGE BRACEGIRDLE . Q.  Was your vessel in such a situation that any other person might get on board from the shore? A.  Yes; they might—there is occasionally a person on board to see who comes—from three to four people could have come on board without notice that day—in the morning, when the boy was called to come to his work, he had to send to me for things—during the time of low-water the vessel was dry, and any body could have come on board. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Transported for Seven Years.

 2217. WILLIAM DWIGHT was indicted for embezzlement. SARAH RYDER. I am the wife of William Ryder. The prisoner was in his employ on the 27th of June, to take out tares and sell them in London—he was not to give any credit—he took out 30 bundles, and was to return with 13 s . 4 d .—when he returned I was present, and he brought back only 1 s . 7 d .,—my husband asked him where the remainder of the money was—he said he did not know—my husband said then he ought to know, and he must learn him better—the prisoner said my husband might get the money how he could—we have no witness to show that he sold the tares—my husband saw them loaded on the cart—he used to be trusted to sell them, and bring the money home—he has regular customers to sell them to—they are not here—he never said he sold the tares—he produced 1 s . 7 d ., and said it was all we should have. EDWARD SIMS. I am a constable, and took the prisoner into custody, I apprehended him at Hounslow, and told him he was my prisoner—he said he was glad I had come after him, and he would give himself up, and owned he was wrong, and owned to embezzling the money—he said, "I own I am wrong, and owned to embezzle the money; I am myself in fault"—those were the words—he wished to compromise it; if he could see the prosecutor he would make good the money—I did not ask him if he had sold them—I asked him no questions—I cannot exactly say his words.
   JURY Q.  Did he tell you he had sold the tares? A.  Yes—some he had sold, and some he trusted—he did not say to what amount—he did not tell me he had received 11 s . 9 d . in money—he owned he had done wrong. NOT GUILTY.
