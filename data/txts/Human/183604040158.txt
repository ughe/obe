JAMES HAYES. I am a messenger in the Post Office. I sometimes stamp letters in the Foreign Office—there is a different stamp for every day—I have the night number book—the letter addressed to Mellish, at Halifax, was stamped on the 2d of March—the number of the stamp is entered every morning in the book—this number of this is 166—I do not know whether that indicates that is came into the Foreign Office on the 2nd of March—I suppose it indicates that it should go forwards on that day—I cannot say that it indicates that it came into the office that day.
   COURT . Q.  Here is "F.M.A. 36—166.?" A.  F. means foreign, 36 means 1836. and 166 the number we used on that morning.
   MR. SHEPHERD . Q.  If a letter is sent from the office on the 2nd of March, would it beat any other date on it than the number for the 2nd of March? A.  No; it would bear no other number—after the stamps are used, they are given to the President—I cannot tell whether that letter must have been in the office on the 2nd of March.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS Q.  Do you stamp the letters your self? A.  not always—generally speaking I do—the 2nd of March was a busy day—I stamped some letters that day—I cannot speak to stamping any particular letter—there persons stamp letters besides me—the stamp is a moveable one, fixed into a handle to suit the purpose of the day—I always put the stamp into the handle—I put it in the morning except on busy days—then we may put it in of an afternoon, which is rarely—when I speak of the busy days, that happens on the 1st of the month, which is more busy than any other—it is very rarely indeed that it hap pens, except on the 1st of the month—I have done it in the afternoon on other days—the stamp is kept on the desk—it is a public room—messen gers, and clerks, and various persons, have access to the room in which the stamp is kept.
   COURT . Q.  How does it happen that this book does not proceed regu larly? A.  We take the numbers irregularly out of the drawer—there are various number in the drawer—we pick out what is proper for that day—the 2nd of March comes in the book after the 5th of April—because we put any numbers we like in the stamp—we do not proceed regularly.
