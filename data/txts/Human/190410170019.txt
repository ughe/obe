   Cross-examined by the prisoner.  I turned round because the noise of your running behind aroused my suspicions—the bullet was found outside St. Paul's Hospital, about the second door in Leigh Street—I did not see your brothers, I only saw you running after me.
   Re-examined.  His brother was not facing me with his coat off. GEORGE ALFRED DODD. I am a fire wood dealer, of 28, New North Street, Theobald's Road—I was with Ryder when we saw the prisoner and his friends near the Holborn end of Chancery Lane—there were two other men, a female, and a dog-one man was carrying a half gallon stone jar bottle—I had not seen them before—the dog continually ran up in front of us, but nothing was said—the prisoner and his friends followed us—as the shot was fired the shortest of the men hit me on the back of my neck and knocked me down—he had come up in his shirt sleeves—the prisoner must have been in front of me—I ran for all I was worth into Mr. Steel's, the undertaker, for protection, two or three doors round the corner—it did not matter what door had been open, I would have gone in—the men were strangers.
   Cross-examined. WILLIAM STEEL. I am a carpenter of 50, Eagle Street, Holborn.
