I got up to show them to the door, and going into the passage the prisoner placed himself rather quickly in front of me, and I was between the two men, and just before we got into the passage the prisoner turned round and struck me a violent blow on the nose, which knocked me backwards, and at the same time the other man (who has been convicted) put his left arm across my shoulder, caught me by the throat, bent me backwards, and gave me the "knee" in the back. The result of that was that it broke one rib and, I believe, fractured another. They could not get me on the ground, but they forced me on the stairs. While in that position they tried to tear my right pocket. Finding they could not do that, they tipped the money on the floor. I had £3 or £4, because I had been paid off only the day before, but I had at least £2 5s. They picked the money up, and one of them said, "Run for it," and they ran out of the house. I was taken to the Poplar Hospital the next day by my landlord. I was kept there as an in-patient for a week and afterwards treated as an out-patient. I did not see the pri  soner again until Saturday week in the Stratford Police Court. I was asked to pick out the man who had assaulted and robbed me, and I failed to pick out the prisoner; I picked out another man.
 Cross-examined. I cashed my advance note about three o'clock. I had other money with me. I had not met any friends earlier in the day or had drinks with anybody else. We were about half an hour in the "Peacock" public-house. I had two or three drinks at the outside. I only drank common ale. All this time the prisoner and Maxwell were in the public-house. I invited them into the "Swan." I had two or three drinks there. I quite knew what I was doing. I admit I picked out another man and failed to identify the prisoner. I am positive that prisoner was with us. I recognised him at the West Ham Police court. I had a couple of drinks at the "Swan." When in 39, Milford Road I sent for a gallon of ale. I do not think it was all drunk that night. The passage was not light like an electric car, but light came through the back kitchen door. I could see distinctly who hit me; I swear it was the prisoner. I can  not say exactly what time we started supper.
 (Wednesday, November 27.) ALFRED READ, engineer's labourer, 178, Star Lane. In November, 1905, I was living at 29, Milford Road, and had a lodger named Brown, a seaman. On November 15, about four o'clock in the after noon I went with my wife and Brown to the "Peacock" in Free mason's Road, Custom House, to change an advance note. We saw Mrs. Smith there While we were there prisoner came in, and Maxwell, who has been convicted. Brown had a conversation with the two men and stood drinks; he took out his money and gave the two men 1s. each. I went with Brown and my wife on a tram down the Barking Road. The two men were also on the tram. Brown and I got off the tram at the "Swan," and went in there.
