 882. HANNAH M'MAHON , stealing 2 sheets, 1 thimble, and 1 pair of slippers, value 6 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of William Augustus Byrne , her master. ELIZABETH ANNA MARIA BYRNE. I am wife of William Augustus Byrne, a surgeon, of St. Martin's-lane. The prisoner was in my service, and left on 19th Feb.—I saw her in bed—I do not know whether she was tipsy—her pocket was on the floor—I searched it, and found in it a needle-book, con taining some pawnbroker's tickets, one of which referred to this pair of sheets—it was tied round with black cotton, to appear like a winder—I put it back again—I missed several other things—I found my silver thimble—I had not missed it—these sheets are like mine. WILLIAM SHAW. I am in the service of Mr. Priest, a pawnbroker of Long-acre. I produce two sheets, pledged by the prisoner, in the name of Keeffe—we gave this duplicate—( produced. ) GEORGE WILKINS( policeman, H  183.) I took the prisoner at Mr. Byrne's—I said she was charged with stealing several things of Mr. Byrne's—I asked her about the thimble—she said she had got a brass thimble, but she produced a silver one—I asked her about the duplicate—she said she found it against St. Martin's Church—she was wearing these slippers at the station—( produced. ) CHARLES THORNE. I am apprentice to Mr. Byrne, and live in his house. These slippers are mine—I missed them on Sunday morning, when the pri soner was given in charge.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The slippers are mine; Mrs. Byrne would only let me have one sheet, as she said the servant before me had stolen them.
   MRS. BYRNE re-examined.  I missed the sheets a day or two before she was given in charge, while she was in my service—I do not know how long before they were safe—she had only lived with me five days, but I have no reason to believe they were taken before—I had a good character with her. NOT GUILTY .
