HERBERT NELL. I am a clerk in the Army Pay Office-I issued this
money order on October 3rd-it is payable to G. A. Spooner, and is
addressed to Rowton House--an identity certificato was posted at the
same time in a separate envelope-it was returned the next day through
the post office.
HERBERT JOHN COOPER. I am a clerk in the Parliament Street Post
Office--this money order was presented to me on October 4th-the
identity certificate was presented at the same time--the order is for
£l 48., and is properly signed-I cannot recognise to whom I paid it.
Frank Prs (Detective L.). On October 31st I found the prisoner
detained at Rowton House, Newington Butts-I told him I should
arrest him for forging and uttering a money order for a reservist named
Spooner-he said, "All right,"-I conveyed him to the police station-
in the presence of the Inspector he said; "This is how it happened :
Spooner and I were out for a walk together; outside St. George's
hospital he asked me to wait; a few minutes afterwards a policeman
brought him out, and he was taken to Gerrard Road Police Station ;
the following morning I endorsed the order in his name; he asked me
twice."
The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said the prosecutor had been
about with him at his expense, and had asked him to get the money
and had given him too letters and the identity certificate, and the next
morning after the prosecutor was taken into oustody he cashed the order
at Parliament Street Post Office. Guilty. Six months' hard labour.
