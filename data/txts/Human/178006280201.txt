 When did you see him again? - I did not see any thing more of him till the next day. The strange man who brought in the liquor the last time asked me to drink, for he said he had run great hazards to get it, for the cask was on fire as he  drew the last out.
   MARTHA SINCO sworn.
 I live at No. 29, Hosier-lane. The prisoner lodged with me seven weeks. I saw the cask of liquor brought in; not by the prisoner, but by the person who had been with him all day and was with him all night and all the next day.
 Did the prisoner come in with the person? - The prisoner came in about five minutes after I saw him.
 What time of night was that? - I cannot say; soon after Mr. Langdale's house was set on fire; it was the beginning of the evening, about seven o'clock, but the hour I cannot say exactly. I said to Cubbage, this acquaintance of yours has brought in some liquor, I suppose from the fire; and I desired there might be none of that gentleman's property brought into the house, for we should come into trouble; he smiled and went up to his own appartment. There was the man who brought in the liquor, the woman who lives with him, and the prisoner.
 What was the woman's name? - Cubbage.
 How long did he stay above with his comrades? - He was in and out all night. My husband went to the door, and broke the dining-room windows. The prisoner, d - d his eyes, and said he would fetch the mob, and he swore we were all Romans, and he would have us all down. He went on cursing and swearing in that way all the next day.
 You say he came in five minutes after the man brought the liquor into the house? - Yes. I told him it was brought in and carried up into his room. The man was a countryman and sells eggs. The prisoner, the man, and the woman he lives with, were making a racket in the house all night.
 Did you see the funnel brought in? - No.
 Prisoner. She says I came in five minutes after the man.
 Sinco. The man sleeps in the garret. The neighbours came backwards and forwards into the house and desired we would let him say what he would, for they were afraid he would have the place burnt down.
 For the Prisoner.
   THOMAS CHALLONER sworn.
 I have known the prisoner about nine or ten years; he was always very honest and just to me in his dealings, in paying for what he had.
   JOHN MERRICK sworn.
 I am a coachman. I have known the prisoner about three years. I have dealt with him for eggs, bacon and cheese. I never knew any harm by him; he was always very fair and honest in his dealings with me.
   WILLIAM BAGWELL sworn.
 I am a watch-spring maker. The prisoner lodged in my house; I have known him a dozen years; he was always very honest; I never heard any thing amiss of him.

 388.  SARAH GUY was indicted for stealing a two-quart pewter pot, value 1 s. the property of Thomas Langdale, June 7th.
   JAMES MATTHEWS sworn.
 I am a servant of Mr. Langdale's. At the request of Isaacs, I went up to Justice Blackborough's to look at a two-quart pot; I saw it there; I told the justice I was very confident that was my master's property. I had often used it, and there was T L marked upon it.
 Prisoner. Did you see me at the place? - I never saw him except before the justice.
   THOMAS ISAACS sworn.
 I am a constable of St. James, Clerkenwell. I know the prisoner well, I searched her lodging for stolen goods, and found this two quart pot and a keg; I asked whose property they were? she said her own. I examined it, and found T L upon it, and took her into custody.
