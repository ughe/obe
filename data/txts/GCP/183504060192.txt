1091. JANE JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of
March, 1 Bible, value 28. ; 1 umbrella, value 18.; 1 table-cloth, value 26.
2 napkins, value 18.; 2 pair of stockings, value 38.; 1 petticoat, value
2s.6d.; and 1 shift, value 2s. ; the goods of Ann Parrott.
Ann Parrort. I live in Ship and Billet-row, Greenwich. The pri-
soner and her mother came to lodge at my house on the 18th of December
-on the 17th of March, I went out at half-past nine in the morning, to go
to Bexley--I left Elizabeth M Kenzie and the prisoner in the house
the prisoner's mother had gone away on the Monday before-I had a box
in my house, containing the greater part of the property stolen-I found
on the 18th of March, that it had been opened, and the articles gone; and
a pair of stockings from another box up stairs.
MARY DUNFORD. I am the wife of Henry Dunford, and live at Greet-
wich. On the 17th of March, the prisoner came to our house, and brought
an umbrella, a Bible, a table-cloth, and two napkins-she asked me to
purchase them-I asked if they were her own-she said, "No," but they
belonged to the person who sent her with them I gave her 1s. 4d. for the
Bible and umbrella, and 1s. for the table-cloth and napkin.
Prisoner's Defence. M'Kenzie gave me the things-I did not know
they were stolen.
ANN PARROTT. M'Kenzie went away the same evening, and she did
not return--the prisoner went out, but I went and found her-I could not
find M'Kenzie.
NOT GUILTY.
