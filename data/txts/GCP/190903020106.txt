Mr. Bodkin, Mr. Travers Humphreys, and Mr. H. D. Harben
prosecuted; Mr. Symmons defended.
Mr. Bodkin said the case was an important one, as, if insurances
against sickness or accident or fire, or whatever risk might be assured
against were absolutely worthless, that fact struck at the root of
thrifty habits. Companies hoiuing out inducements to people to be
thrifty should be conducted on solid lines by respectable people, and
above all should be in a position to guarantee their undertakings to
people who entrusted them with premiums. Companies insuring life
were bound to deposit no less a sum than £20,000 with the Board
of Trade, but in the case of companies insuring against accident, ill.
health, or fire no deposit is required, and it would seem to be a most
salutary rule to be adopted, whatever the risks insured against, that
& substantial sum should be deposited by way of guarantee. Within
