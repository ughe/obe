Thomas Hawes WARREN. I keep a chandler's-shop, in Canterbury-
place, Old Kent-road. On the 16th of March, about four o'clock in the
afternoon, the prisoner Hall came into my shop-for one penny-worth of
tobacco-he put down a bad shilling- I tried it, and it was bad-I bit it,
and bent it, and gave it to him back-he went away-I directed two of my
sons to follow him, and tell a policeman.
THOMAS WARREN. I am the prosecutor's son. My father told me to
go after Hall-my brother was with me--when Hall got as far as the
Crown and Anchor public-house, he crossed the road, and then threw a
tobacco pipe at Palmer, and another man, who got away they walked on
some distance, and then the other one went into a baker's shop-I went
into the baker's-I did not see the prisoners taken.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you hear the other's
