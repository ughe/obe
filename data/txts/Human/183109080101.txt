   THOMAS HAYWARD. I am a cab-driver. My cab was on the stand, about eight feet from Hill's - I was behind Hill; I saw a coal-waggon, and two brewers' drays; the first dray passed without anything happening - I saw the second come up to the cab - I did not see the driver there, but he might have been there, as I was looking at the deceased, who was leaning on his shaft, and was struck by the dray; he went to lay hold of the iron part of the dray, missed it, and fell under the wheel; the dray went on, and the wheel kept pushing him on; a mob gathered, and I saw him taken up - I had spoken to him just before; he did not appear the worse for liquor; he might have had a glass; the dray-men did their best when they saw what was done, to stop their horses, and rescue him.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was the man leaning on the shaft and projecting out towards the road? A. He was leaning on it just at the step; the dray made a kind of slough towards the pavement, and hit him; a brick, or a hole, might have caused it to slough; the dray-man assisted to put the man into his cab, and in getting him from under the wheel; they stopped the moment they were called to.
 Q. If the man who drove the second dray was by his first horse, could he see the man who was behind that horse? A. I should think not, unless an alarm was given; there was no alarm till afterwards.
   WILLIAM STEPHEN SMITH. I am shopman to Mr. Lobsey of Cheapside. I was looking out of the second floor window, down towards the cab, which stood nearly opposite our house - I noticed two drays and a coal-waggon coming along; the waggon was first; the first dray passed between the waggon and the cart very close; the waggon was in motion at the time; the second dray had less room to pass, in consequence of the progress of the coal-waggon - I was rather surprised on seeing the second dray come on, that the cab-man, who was leaning on his shaft, did not move, and as it got very near to the cab; the horse of the cab made a little move, which appeared to awaken the man to his danger; he moved from the dray, rather more towards the south side, which gave the drayman room to pass - I thought he had been asleep; he made a sudden start, turned round towards Bow church, and ran, as it appeared, before the wheel of the dray; after going four or five steps I saw his hands extended, and he fell, after which I saw no more - I could not see where the driver of the second dray was, for I was more intent on the deceased.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was your house close to where the accident happened? A. Exactly, so that I had a bird's-eye view of it - I should have thought that the dray made noise enough to awake the man before it got to him; there was plenty of time to have moved; in my judgment, it was pure accident; the man ran into danger certainly.
   GEORGE GODWIN. I live in Stacey-street, Seven-dials. I drove the first dray between the coal-waggon and the coach-stand, and passed without difficulty - I was at the head of my horses, and the prisoner was alongside his horses and never went on the pavement - I looked back before the accident, and saw him at his horse's head.
 Cross-examined. Q. As you drove your dray through, did you see his approach? A. Yes; drays follow each other as close as possible - I looked back, to guide my horses, saw the man under the wheel, and stopped; the prisoner got hold of his horse's head, and stopped as quick as possible; he went up and assisted the man.

 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
