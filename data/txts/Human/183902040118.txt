MATILDA CHEESE. I am the wife of William Cheese, a labourer; we live in Hooper's-lane, Enfield. We had a clamp of potatoes in the garden, and about nine o'clock in the evening, on the 27th of January, I found the prisoner taking some away—I knew him before, as he lives in the neigh bourhood—I called a young man, and he was taken—he had got out about half a peck of potatoes—the clamp had not been opened before. WILLIAM SKEGGS. I lodge with the prosecutor. I saw the prisoner take the potatoes from the clamp—I took him, and he took the potatoes out of the bosom of his shirt. GUILTY.* Aged 17.— Transported for Seven Years.

THOMAS MAPP was indicted for stealing, on the   14th of De cember , 576 bobbins, value 3 l  . 4 s  .; 4 countermarks, value 1 s  .; 1 batten, value 1 s  .; 1 roll, value 8 s  .; 26 slays, value 3 l  .; 1 pair of vices, value 7 s  .; 3 runners, value 4 s  .; and 20lbs. weight of cotton, value 1 l  . 18 s  .; the goods of Samuel Green . SAMUEL GREEN. I am a web and brace manufacturer, and live in Shoe-lane, Holborn. The prisoner took two rooms at my factory, No. 24, Wilks-street, Spitalfields—he came there on the 3rd of December—on the 10th of January, I missed some bobbins, some slays, and some battens—I spoke to the prisoner about it on the evening of the 10th of January—he said he would confess the truth, if I would forgive him—I said it would be better for him—he said he had done it through distress—that he had left some bobbins at Rondean's, and meant to get them again. EDWARD RONDEAN. I am a pawnbroker. I bought these things of the prisoner about Christmas. ROBERT BROOKS. I am a loom-broker. I bought some of these bob bins of the prisoner.
 (The prisoner pleaded poverty, and received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 46.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Six Weeks.
 676. JOHN FREEMAN was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of Ja nuary , 1 cart wheel, value 7 s  . 6 d  . the goods of George Pavitt GEORGE PAVITT. I live in Essex-street, Hoxton. I lost a cart wheel on the 3rd of January out of my yard, which is surrounded with a wall and palings—I saw the wheel safe on the morning of the 3rd, and in the evening it was gone—I know the prisoner by his coming to the yard for his master's horse and cart which were kept there—I lost another wheel afterwards, and I found one of them at Mr. Hood's, in the Curtain-road. THOMAS HOOD. I am a coach-broker. I bought two wheels of the prisoner for 5 s . 6 d .—this is one of them—we had sold the other. JOHN EVANS. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner, and found this one wheel at Hood's. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined Three Months. (There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
