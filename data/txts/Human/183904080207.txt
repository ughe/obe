   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Did Williams, when you took the first piece, say there was 137 yards? A.  He both pointed to the mark and said there was—I have taken in small quantities of flannel at different times—I suppose this to be worth 1 s . 6 d . or 1 s . 10 d . a yard—I consider at our sale it would fetch that—it is a bad time to sell flannel in January, February, or March, and that I took into consideration, as we should have to sell it at a lower rate.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Have you measured it since? A.  Yes, there are 94 yards—there are marks at each end—on the inner end is "1734," and on the top "137." FRANCIS COTTON. I am a pawnbroker. I have one shop in Shoreditch, and one in Hackney-road—on the 7th of January I was in my shop in Hackney-road—about the middle of the day the two prisoners came in and brought a piece of flannel, but I did not take it in—it was in flat folds—I forget the sum they asked for it, but it was 9 l . or 10 l .—I asked what quantity there was, and they said the quantity was marked on it, 137, pointing to a red chalk mark on the outer end—my young man was attending to the prisoners, and I asked him if he had measured it—he said, "No"—I said I could not tell what there was without—they said they would not have it measured on any account, as it would put it out of con dition, and those marks on the flannel were made in the country by author ised registered men—they showed me a ticket which my young man had given them, and that of Mr. Fleming's.
   Williams's Defence.  They were my own goods, and I meant no harm—my step-son merely carried a piece or two—he knows nothing about it. (The prisoners received good characters.)
   WILLIAMS— GUILTY — Confined Three Months.
   BAYNTON— GUILTY — Confined One Month.
