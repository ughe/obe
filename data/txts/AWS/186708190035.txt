eighteen I months it is an association to proteet our interests in the trade-
know what a lock-out means- believe it is where the master tailors dis-
charge the whole body of their workmen, because they have some ground
of grievance against them-I believe they combine-I did so before the
association was formed last year-that was sometimes done with regard to
not having the men in, but not since the association was formed- loek-
out is a strike of masters against the men-I was engaged in striking
against my men before the association was formed last yearI cannot
rightly say how many masters were engaged in that-I was not on the
committee-to the best of my belief I should say there were fifty or
sixty master tailors engaged in it-we did the same believe it was
arranged that we should not employ the men longer I do not know
whether that was by way of form of resolution, because I was not at the

settled, but I was out of town-the lock-out onlv lasted a few days not
a fortnight, certainly, as regards myselfI can scarcely tell you the cause
of dispute-i was a demand of the men for an increase of wages-I think
we conceded them sixteen per cent., and they still wanted more-I then
turned them all away, I believe every one-I think about 1000 or 1200
operatives were looked out at that time.
