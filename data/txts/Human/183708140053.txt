 1826. ANN DAVIS and ANN PAYNE were indicted for stealing, on the   10th of July , 1 pair of shoes, value 7 s  ., the goods of Thomas Double Dutton . THOMAS LENGE. I am foreman to Thomas Double Dutton, of White chapel-road, a shoemaker—this pair of shoes is my master's—I had seen them safe three or four days before they were lost. GEORGE BUTLER( police-constable H  20.) The prisoners were given into my custody at Mr. Cripps's shop—I produce the shoes. RICHARD CRIPPS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Morgan-street, Commercial-road. These shoes were offered to be pledged at our shop by the two prisoners, about four o'clock in the afternoon of the 10th of July. NOT GUILTY.
 1827. ANN DAVIES and ANN PAYNE were again  indicted, for stealing, on the   10th of July , 1 gown, value 5 s  ., the goods of Richard Cripps . MARGARET HAYES. I live in Morgan-street, Commercial-road. On Monday evening, the 10th of July, I saw two women go into the prosecutor's shop, one took down the gown and hid it under some part of her dress, I do not know which—one of them came and told me they would give me six pence to say nothing about it, but I do not know who the women were. RICHARD CRIPPS. I am assistant to my father. I received information from Hayes, and these two prisoners were in the shop—I heard what was said by the witness—they contradicted pulling the gown down, but I found it behind them—the prisoners were attempting to pledge these shoes—I gave them in charge. NOT GUILTY.
   Fifth Jury before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
