Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. How long has the prisoner been
in your master's employ? A. Ever since the winter--the load on each
cart was about 30 cwts.--the prisoner went home after I questioned him,
and returned again he went out with two or three loads in the morning,
and one or two in the afternoon-Barrett has been in my master's service
a long time he is older than the prisoner.
JOHN HOGARTH. I am clerk to Mr. Plumley. On the 4th of May I
asked the prisoner what he had done with one of the loads of iron, which I
understood he had placed under the Greenwich railway-- he admitted it
had been shot there, but said he had not shot it-I said I thought he had,
and ordered one of the men to go immediately for a policeman-I went
with the officer up to the arch-the prisoner said that was the arch
where the iron had been shot, and I saw the dust of old iron on the
ground.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he say who did shoot it? A. I was very
angry, I do not remember whether he did or not-I do not remember
hearing any thing pass between him and Harley-I gave him in charge.
JOHN Higgs (police-constable M 92.) I was sent for, and took the
prisoner for stealing 31cwts. of iron, on Saturday the 4th of May-Mr.
Hogarth wished me to take him under the railway arch where the iron had
been shot-we went there-it was one of the arches near Crucifix-lane-
the prisoner said he was in a public-house drinking a pint of beer, and
some one drove away the horse and cart-he did not know who it was-
he said he did not know what public-house it was, but it was in Tooley-
street, and he found the horse and cart under the railway arch, the iron
shot there, and no one was with it - while at the station-house he sat down,
and said he had been led astray by Jack Barrett; that Jack Barrett had
come to him in Tooley-street, and they both drove the horse and cart down
Dog and Bear vard, and shot the iron under the railway arch, near Cruci-
fix-lane; that Jack Barrett was to look after the iron-he said that Jack
Barrett and hini were colleagued together in stealing the iron, they were
going to sell it, and part the money between themand if I would let
him go that afternoon, he would go to the King and Queen iron-foundry,
where he should find the iron and the parties.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you sure he said Jack Barrett was to look
after the iron ? A. Yes--and he was to go home-I did not tell him
what he was to go to the station-house for- Mr. Hogarth had told him.
