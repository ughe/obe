1722. EDWARD GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of July , 1 pair of trousers, value 13s., the goods of William Austin ; and 1 saw, value 2s., the goods of John Lawson.
   FREDERICK LANCE. I am in the employ of Mr. William Austin, a pawnbroker, in High-street, Shadwell. On the 2nd of July, about five o'clock in the afternoon, a person came into the shop, and told me a pair of trousers had been stolen - I went to the door, and the prisoner was pointed out to me; I went and took these trousers from under his jacket.
   JOHN LAWSON. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Ratcliffe-highway. On the 2nd of July I lost a saw - in consequence of information I went and found the prisoner, and took it from his hand - I had seen it safe a quarter of an hour before.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been drinking very hard, and did not know what I did, until the next morning.
   FREDERICK LANCE. He was drunk, but not so drunk as he pretended to be; he walked very well until I took hold of him, and then he would not stand.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Confined One Month.
 1723.  ANN SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of June , 1 bonnet, value 1s., the goods of Stephen Eastland.
   REBECCA EASTLAND. I am the wife of Stephen Eastland - I live in Green Arbour-court. On the 13th of June I was at home, about nine o'clock in the evening; the prisoner came to my room, and said she wanted to speak to me - she went round by a small leaf-table, and sat down on a chair, by the side of which was a band-box, and a bonnet on it; she put her hand down, took the bonnet, and at the same time she got up and said, "I must go;" I had no candle in the house - I went to get my bonnet to fetch a candle and saw the bonnet in her right hand; I said,"Give me my bonnet, you hussey;" she opened the door, ran down stairs, and I after her - she ran into some alleys, where there are a great many low Irish people.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are not you and the prisoner well acquainted? A. No; I do not think I ever spoke to her above twice before this - I have seen her at the Goat; I gave her part of a pint of beer, which she begged of me the day this happened - I did not promise to lend her my bonnet that night; she was not in the room two minutes - my husband was in the room, but he saw nothing of it.    NOT GUILTY.
 1724.  RICHARD CLAY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of June , 4 jackets, value 18s.; 2 pairs of trousers, value 8s.; 3 shirts, value 6s.; 2 waistcoats, value 4s.; 6 stockings, value 1s. 6d., and 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of James Duggan.
 The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1600.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of July , 2 pairs of shoes, value 4s., and 4 lasts, value 1s., the goods of Richard Lee.
   HANNAH LEE. I am the wife of Richard Lee - he lives in Princes-place, St. George's, and is a shoemaker. On Wednesday afternoon, the 4th of July, I heard a noise in the shop; I looked, and saw the prisoner stooping - I called to him "Halloo! halloo!" he ran out - I pursued him, crying Stop thief! but I did not know that he had stolen the shoes till I got to Mr. Smith's shop - I found these shoes and lasts there; I know they are my husband's- I saw him make them; they are not finished.
   JOHN SMITH. On Wednesday afternoon, the 4th of July I was having my tea in my back room at my house in King-street, Commercial-road; I heard a lumbering noise in front of my shop - I ran, and found these four shoes on the lasts, about one yard in the shop; I stood two or three minutes, and saw a mob - the officer and Mr. Lee came- I gave up the shoes to them.
   WILLIAM BROCK. About half-past five o'clock that evening I was coming by the prosecutor's shop ; I saw the prisoner come out with something under his coat; I heard Mrs. Lee call Stop thief! I pursued him down King-street, and assisted in taking him; I saw him pass Smith's shop, but did not see him throw any thing in there- I had only lost sight of him while he turned the corner.
   WILLIAM REEVES( Police-constable K 152). I heard the alarm as I was at the corner of Grove-street . I followed the prisoner, and saw some shoes under his coat, partly concealed; I pursued him to a street leading to King-street, where I lost sight of him; I got sight of him again in King-street, and told my brother officer to follow him.
   THOMAS OWEN( Police-constable H 176). I saw the prisoner running , and took him, he was very violent , and tried to get me down ; it took three of us to take him .
 Prisoner's Defence . I ran with the mob , and was taken; the officer struck me .
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Seven Years.
