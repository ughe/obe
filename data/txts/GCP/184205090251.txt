John Brown. I keep the Coach and Horses at Barnes—I know the
prisoner-I saw him on Sunday, the 3rd of April--be came to my house
and had a female with him a little before four or about four--they were in
my parlour when I went in-when I went into the parlour he said, "How
do you do, Mr. Brown !"-I said, “How do you do, coachman ?"-he
ordered a glass of gin and water, which I brought him-he introduced the
female as his sister, and said she was a Welsh woman, that she was a dairy-
maid, or lived at a dairy-he did not say where-he said many gentlemen's
families were very partial to Welshwomen as dairy-maids, and that she had
brought him a bottle of cream, some tea, and sugar--I said it was very
good of her--the bottle was like the one produced it was a pint bottle,
not a regular wine bottle-I cannot undertake to say it was a pint bottle
-it was one of this colour-the woman took it out of her pocket, and
Good said she had brought it-I observed white liquor in it-they had two
glasses of wine and water, and were about twenty-five minutes in my house
-she drank as well as him--I knew him before, as coachman to Mr. Shiell,
but never knew his name.

FANNY HESTER. I am the wife of William Hester, who lives at New
York, America—I live at Priest-bridge, near Roehampton--I have known
the prisoner two years- I remember his coming to my house on the even-
ing of Sunday, the 3rd of April, at hall-past four o'clock-a female was
with him-I had never seen her before--she was dressed in a Mazarine-
blue bonnet, dark shawl, with a black ground, very large flowers on it, a
dark cotton dress, and black boots tied on one side--they drank tea with
me.
Q. Did be give a reason for wishing to drink tea with you! 4. He
said the gardener was not agreeable for him to have a fire in the barness-
room--he introduced the woman to me as his sister-in-law, that she came
from London, and was going to return home that evening-while we were
at tea I said the gardener said something very disrespectful respecting
Susan Butcher sleeping in the harness-room--I said this to the female, in
the prisoner's presence, and she seemed very much agitated-she asked
who it was-the prisoner said it was a friend of his-- she said she was quite
a stranger to her--they went away at half-past five, or near six--the pri-
soner said be was in haste, he was going to wait at table-I knew the
little boy, and asked his sister-in-law to bring the boy down, as she was
saying he was in a confined school, I thought it would be a treat to come
into the country--the boy came on the Tuesday with the prisoner – I had
not seen the prisoner between Sunday and Tuesday morning ---when he
