   Elizabeth Price, of the Parish of St. Dunstans in the West, was indicted for breaking the Dwelling-house of John Lang , in the Day time, no Person being therein, on the 7th of October last, about two in the Afternoon, and taking thence a Serge Counterpain, and other Goods of the said Lang. The first Evidence deposed that he had an occasion to go to a Gentlemans Chamber in the Temple, and did mistake the Stair-Case, and going up four pair of Stairs he saw a hole broke at three pair of Stairs, and looking saw a Woman peep, but did not see her Face, and mistrusting her to be a Thief, he went down Stairs, and spoke to a Gentleman in the lower Room, desiring him to stop her if she should come down whilst he went to call the Porter, and in the mean time the Prisoner came down one pair of Stairs, where she was taken; upon which they went up Stairs, and looking in the hole found some Chissels; but opening the Door found several Trunks and Boxes broke open, and the Goods pack'd up ready to be carried away. The Prisoner denied the Fact, saying, she come to enquire for her Counsellor, and was told he lived in that Stair-Case; she went up Stairs and was taken when she came down: All which Matters the Jury having considered, found her Guilty of Felony only, and acquitted her of breaking the House.    Jane Stacy, of the Parish of St. Buttolphs without Bishopsgate, was indicted for feloniously stealing a Kettle, value 3 s. 6 d. from John Belch, on the 8th of October last. The Evidence deposed that as he was looking after the Shop two Women came and cheapen'd a Box Iron; but not agreeing on the price, they went away; but the Kettle being missing, he pursued her, and took it upon her in her Apron. The Jury found her Guilty to the value of 10 d. [Whipping. See summary.]
   Mary Johnson, of the Parish of St. James Westminster, was indicted for feloniously stealing a Trunk, 6 Holland Shirts, 4 pair of Ruffles, 4 Neckcloaths, the Goods of the Honourable Phillip Howard, Esq; on the 16th of September last. But there was no Evidence could prove the Prisoner took it. The Jury   acquitted her.
   Anne Spackman and Elizabeth Messenger, of the Parish of St. Giles in the Fields, were indicted for feloniously stealing 6 Diaper Napkins from John Spackman, on the 7th of September last. There was no Evidence to prove they see them take them; nor nothing being found upon them, and they alledging their Innocenc, the Jury   acquitted them.
   Elizabeth Stephens, of the Parish of St. Martins in the Fields, was indicted for breaking the House of Daniel Saxton in the Day time, about 7 in the Afternoon, on the 14th of July last, no Person being therein, and taking thence a Stuff Wastcoat and a pair of Breeches of Rich. Davis , and a pair of Sheets and Blankets, the Goods of the said Saxton. The Evidence deposed that his Room was robbed and in pursuit he took the Prisoner in the Passage. She denied the Fact, and nothing being found upon her, the Jury   acquitted her.
   Cicilla Gregory, of the Parish of St. Giles in the Fields, was indicted for feloniously stealing two Silver Spoons from Joseph Mathias, on the 10th of August last. The Prosecutor said that she came into his Chamber, and having a Mess with something in it, with a Siver Spoon, she tasted of it; but being called down to the Door in the mean time, she took two Spoons and went her ways; and said that afterwards he took her by Broomly-street in Mans Cloaths. The Prisoner denied the Fact, and there being nothing found upon her, and she calling divers Persons to her Reputation, the Jury   acquitted her.
   Simon Johnson, of the Parish of St. Martins in the Fields, was indicted for Felony, for that he the 11th of June last, at the Fleet, in the Parish of St. Brides, London, did take to Wife one Susanna Cornwell , Spinster , and afterwards, at Knightsbridge, in the Parish of St. Martins in the Fields , did take to Wife one S - S - , Spinster, on the 23d of September following, his former Wife being alive. The first Evidence was the Chaplain of the Fleet, who deposed, that he did marry one Simon Johnson and Susanna Cornwell, and did believe that the Prisoner was the Man; and the Clerk deposed the same. As to the second Marriage, the Prisoner confest the same, saying he would live and die with her; but denied the first Wife, altho' he had owned before that he was married to her; but she having some mistrust that he was going to be married to a second Wife followed him to Knights-bridge in a Coach, where he was married a second time before she came; and the Prisoner looking out of a Window and seeing his first Wife, ran away into Hyde Park, leaving his Hat behind him, never coming near his last Wife after. Another Evidence was the Minister of Knightsbridge, who deposed that the Prisoner and the last Wife came the 23d of September, about eight a Clock in the Morning, with a Licence, upon which he married them. Another Evidence was the Clerk, who deposed that after they were married he earnestly desired him to keep the matter secret. Another Evidence deposed that she heard the Prisoner own when he was in Custody, that he had two Wives, and that the first Wife told her she was with Child by him, and where they were married. Another Evidence deposed, that when he was going to Newgate he did confess that he had two Wives. The Prisoner for himself said, that he was not married to his first Wife. But both Marriages being so plainly proved, the Jury found him   Guilty. [Branding. See summary.]
