Subsequently, on March 1, they dropped to 175 3/4." They did, as a matter of fact, but that was ex dividend. The stock being 174 1/2 ex dividend, left still three points cover. There is no allegation in the correspondence that the cover had run off before March 1. Messrs. Cooper, Walker, and Hall, Mrs. Butcher's solicitors, wrote to Ablett, asking the price at which and the date on which the cover ran off, and Ablett replied on May 3 that "when the quotation for Canadian Pacifics fell to 174 1/2 the deal ran off, as the cover became exhausted, and on March 6 they fell to 173 1/2." Mrs. Butcher offered to take up the stock, but that offer was not accepted. It is clear that on the dates Ablett mentioned the cover had not run off, if credit is taken for the amount of dividend to which Mrs. Butcher was entitled. In the case of Mrs. D'Altera of the purchase of £1,000 (50 shares) Canadian Pacific Stock, at 176 5/8 cum bonus, she re  ceived a letter in precisely the same terms as that addressed to Mrs. Butcher. On March 2 she sent £30 further cover, and from that date onwards was regularly charged contango. When she was asked to produce more cover her cover had not run off. She could have closed at a price which would have left a balance due to her, because she had £60 cover. At the end of May dividend would have been paid. The dividend was paid in April, and allowing two or three weeks for collection, there would have been due to her on my showing a balance of £75. Assuming that they still claimed the right to hold £30 cover, the stock being still open, there would have been a balance due to her then of £45, after charging her with carrying-over. When the new issue was made the shareholders got one share in every five at the price of £160, which was equivalent to a profit of $12 per $100 share. The "Stock Exchange Weekly Official Intelligence" shows the price at which the stock was made up.
