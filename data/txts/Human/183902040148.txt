EDWARD FLOWER( police-constable T  42.) I went to Mrs. Buckingham on the 11th—I asked if she could recollect that Mr. Carpenter's wagon had been there on the 2nd of January—she said she knew nothing about it—I went again, and told her to recollect herself—she then said the wagon had been there—that the wagoner dined there, and he, and the ostler, and Mr. Philp's man were drinking together, and Mr. Philp called Car penter's man out—she said she had her flour of Mr. Kidd and Mr. Andrews. RICHAHD KIDD. I am a miller, and live at Isle worth—there is no firm of Kidd and Andrews in the trade, that I know of—I hare occasionally sold Mrs. Buckingham a bushel of flour at a time—the last bushel was on the 15th of September. CHARLIES MANWELL. I was in the employ of Mr. Andrews, a miller, at Brentford. I used to deliver flour to customers of his—the last flour I left at Mrs. Buckingham's might be about three months ago.

 700. JOHN WYBRO was indicted for stealing, on the   19th of January , at   Hammersmith , I frying-pan, value 1 s  ., the goods of James Pegg . RICHARD HANCOCK( police-sergeant F  10.) I was at Hammersmith on the 19th of January—I saw the prisoner enter the prosecutor's shop, and take the frying-pan—I followed and took him with it. FREDERICK THATCHER. I am in the service of James Pegg. This is his pan.
 (The prisoner pleaded poverty.) GUILTY. Aged 35. Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Eight Days.
 701. GEORGE JAY was indicted for stealing, on the   19th of January , I tea-kettle, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Thomas Joyce and another. WILLIAM WOOD. I am in the service of Thomas Joyce and another, ironmongers, in Whitechapel. On the 19th of January I was coming out of the shop, I saw the prisoner and another boy—I saw one take a tea kettle, and give it to the prisoner, who was about half a yard from the door—I ran past the one that took the kettle, and took the prisoner with it under his coat, and brought him back.
