Mr. Roach proseeuted,
Prisoner Wa8 a driver in the 77th Battery of the Royal Field
Artillery and was recently appointed mess butler to an ofhicers' mess
in Tipperary. He shortly afterwards abseonded, taking with him a
cheque-book. He came to Londan and used the smoking-room of
the Grand Hotel, Strand, representing himself to be an officer in
the regiment. He sent a messenger with a letter to the proseoutor, at
Woolwich, requesting him to change a cheque for E7 10s., which WaS
enclosed in the letter. The cheque purported to be signed by an
offcer. Proseeutor sent prisoner E7 10s.. but the cheque WaS subse-
quently discovered to be a forgery. Prisoner was arrested upon the
charge, and it was ascertained that he had similarly filled up several
other cheques from the cheque-book and changed them in Ireland,
where he obtained 233 in respeet of them. Others were found at his
lodgings in readiness to be cashed. The police stated that prisoner
was an American citizen and a native of Chicago. He appeared to
have got tired of the Army, and he had purchased his discharge
before he abeeonded with the cheque-book. He thought that by
flling up and cashing the cheques he might be able to get money to
enable him to return to Chicago.
Sentence, Nine months' hard labour.
