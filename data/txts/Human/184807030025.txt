1670. HENRY NICHOLLS , stealing 1 pair of sugar-tongs, value 8 s  .; the goods of Robert Woodcock , his master; having been before convicted. ROBERT WOODCOCK. I am a tailor, of High-street, Kensington—the pri soner was my errand-boy. On 14th June I missed a silver fork, and told him to empty his pockets—I there found this pair of sugar-tongs ( produced ), broken into three pieces—he said he had found them in the outhouse—the fork has not been found. MATILDA THIRMAN. I saw the sugar-tongs safe on the kitchen-dresser about one o'clock that day—I did not touch them afterward's—they had been there a month—they laid there to be repaired—there was no one in the house bat me and the housemaid—they could not get to the outhouse.
   Prisoner.  I was told to clean out the outhouse, and found them there. GEORGE DUNBAR( policeman, T  91.) I produce a certificate of the pri soner's former conviction—( read—Convicted Feb . 1846, and confined six months )—he is the man. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Transported for Seven Years .
 1671. JOSEPH WILKINSON , stealing 1 watch, value 5 l  .; the goods of Thomas Doddrell , from his person. THOMAS DODDRELL. I was at Hounslow fair on 20th June—a woman, who is not in custody, came to me, and I missed my watch—this is it—the prisoner was in the skittle-ground, where I was.
   Prisoner. Q.  Can you swear I was there? A.  A man came to me in the skittle-ground—who it was I cannot tell. JOHN SCOTNEY( police-sergeant, T  18.) I know a woman named Harris, and the prisoner—they had been at the public-house the day before, and both left it in a hurried manner, about three o'clock, the morning after the robbery. RICHARD ROADKNIGHT( police-sergeant, T  11.) On 21st June I saw the prisoner offering this watch to pawn, at Uxbridge—he said he bought it of two men—I said, "Your woman stole it from a man at Hounslow"—he said, "She was with a man, and she gave me the watch"—he has lived with her three years—he told me if I went I should find her at Harrow—I went there, but they know me, and she was put out the back way.
   Prisoner.  I want to get rid of that woman—she gave me the watch to pawn—it had no glass or hands.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 25.— Recom  mended to mercy by the Jury.— Confined Three Months .
 1672. THOMAS WILLIAM PARRINGTON , stealing 1 jacket, value 4 s  .; the goods of Eliza Adams ; having been before convicted; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 13.— Transported for Seven Years.
 1673. WILLIAM MOSS , stealing 1 sovereign, the money of William Lawrence . WILLIAM LAWRENCE. I live at Great Stanmore—I employed the pri soner. On 22nd April I wrapped a sovereign in a piece of paper—my wife took it out of my hand, and gave it to the prisoner, to get eighteen gallons of beer, which came to 17 s . 6 d .—he was to bring me the beer, and half-a-crown change—he did not return—I did not see him for several weeks. ANN LAWRENCE. I gave him the sovereign. JOHN MARTIN( policeman, S  363.) I took the prisoner on 22nd June—he said he came on purpose to be taken.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I lost the sovereign, and was afraid to go back. GUILTY  . Aged 34 .—Recommended to mercy by the Jury. — Confined Ten Days .
