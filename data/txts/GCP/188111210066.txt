Walter Tait-in October last he brought me a New Zealand bond for
1,0001.- I don't remember the number of it-it was produced before the

him-he had borrowed money of me before-I lent him the 2001. on it,
and he gave me an I 0. U for the sum, leaving the bond with me in the

repay the loan-he said it was not convenient for him to do so, but he
asked me to give him a letter of introduction to my bankers, the National
Provincial Bank-he said he would open an account there, and get a
further advance upon the bond, and repay me out of it-I gave him that
introduction, and he gave my name as a reference—the manager wrote to
me for a reference, and I answered the letter, and the prisoner opened an
account there-he did not get the advance—he told me they did not care
about the security-I then gave him a reference to the Imperial Bank-
this (produced) is the letter I wrote to the manager, and the prisoner
opened an account there—he called on me and said that he told the
manager I had lent him 5001., instead of 2001., and I think he gave as a
reason, that 2001. seemed rather small on such a large security-he
handed me a cheque for 5001., signed by himself, payable on the delivery
of the bond, which I was to present to the bank the following day- I did
BO, handed the clerk the bond for 1,0001., and he handed me 5001, for
the cheque-next day the prisoner called on me, and I handed him 3001.,
the difference between the 2007. that was due to me and the 5001.-he
paid me interest at the rate of 6 per cent.—when he originally asked me
for the loan on the bond he did not tell me where the bond came from-
he did not say anything about it-he said when he applied for the loan
that the bond was settled on his wife, and that was the reason why he
could not sell it, although he could get an advance on it.

anything-the bond was never in my hands before it was in yours-I got

another loan-I had lent you money upon the security of a Queensland
bond-I cannot give you the number of it I did not take the numbers
