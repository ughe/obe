WILSON, Mayor.
Q. Having heard the injuries were sustained on the 14th of March, can
you be certain they were the cause of the erysipelas ? A. I have no doubt
in my own mind that the erysipelas was the result of the injury, not the
least-I speak of the appearances of erysipelas in all parts of the body, all
arising from the external injury-when it once begins it travels about from
place to place-it very frequently arises without any external injury-
--drunken habits would no doubt cause it, but there being external injury,
I have no doubt that was the cause—had she been more temperate those,
injuries would have produced any thing but erysipelas-they would have
produced a sort of healthy inflammation.
Prisoner. The wound in her leg she had had four or five years,
Witness. I should not think it was a very recent wound-it was muchl onger
than a fortnight-I should think it was not so recent as the wound on the
head-the one on the ancle was the most serious part of the injury-it had
penetrated through the whole of the joint, which must have produced very
great irritation in the systemerysipelas would be most likely to attack
the part which had been previously injured that wound itself would not
have produced erysipelas, it must have been the result of more recent in-
jury--the erysipelas first commenced in the headerysipelas in the leg did
not make its appearance for two or three weeks after I saw her-the wound
in the leg could not have caused the erysipelas in the face.
ISABELLA ATTREE re-examined. The 14th of March was Thursday
I got hot water and washed her face, and put her to bed-I saw
her next morning, and she complained of her head, and of being very
ill indeed-1 saw her every day till her death-her face was in &
most dreadful state-I did not observe that for about a fortnight after
she received the blow, but it was always swollen from the time she
received the blow-I had seen her before she received the blow, three or
four times a day-I saw her that morning before she went out, there
was nothing the matter with her face then there was no breaking out,
or any humour at all-she was about forty years old, I had known
her fourteen years I never saw her in liquor after this, I do not think
she took more than half a pint of beer a day- she was never able to go
about her business afterwards I never saw the prisoner at the house after-
wards--I have seen him go down the street-she expressed a wish to see
him, but he sent word he would not come I know the situation of Cooper's
bed-room window-I have never been in the room-it faces right over the
back-yard I should say a person could see the back-door of Lovett's house
very well from that window I cannot see the window from the passage-
I dare say I could from the back door I was standing at my own door at
first-I then went across, not into the house, but on the pavement opposite
the door, which was open-I looked through the passage, which is a
straight one-what I saw was more in the middle of the passage--the back
door was open, and comes to the foot of the stairs-she had struggled, ap-
parently, into the passage--but he was then trying to get from her, and
dragged her to the front-door,
ELIZABETH COOPER re-examined. I am quite sure I could see the
back-door from my window-I could not see into the house-what I saw
took place just at the step of the back-yard, not in the passage I went
down stairs afterwards, she was then leaning against the window-ledge.
