3050. JOHN WALLACE was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd
October, 1 pewter pot, value 1s., the goods of Henry Edwin Jaggers.
ALEXANDER M‘NOUGHER. I am in the service of Henry Edwin Ja.
gers, who keeps the Artillery Canteen at Woolwich. On the 22nd of
tober I heard a noise, and went into the tap-room, and saw the prisoner
with a pint pot beside him-there was no one else there--when he wa
gone the pot was gone--I told my mistress- I went to the prisoner's house
and asked for the pint pot-he shoved me to the door, and shut it--I said.
" I must have it"-he went back, gave it me up, and said, "For God's
sake don't say anything about it, or you will ruin me."
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. How long was he there? A. Not
two minutes- I knew the prisoner's name- I believe he works for my
father-I do not know his son intimately I have seen him often-I do
not know that he and his father quarrelled-the prisoner lived about
quarter of a mile from my master's house-he has lived there four years-
he is a brush-maker-I went to the prisoner's four minutes after he left-
no one was present when he gave up the pot-I swear he did not say he
was sorry he had kept the pot so long, he hoped there would be no piece
of work about it-he had not had beer in it.
EDWARD PALMER (police-constable R 231.) I took the prisoner, and
told him I wanted him-he said, “What for? the pint pot ?"-I said,
"Yes"-he said, "For God's sake, be as lenient as possible; I never did
such a thing before, and how I came to do it now I don't know."
Cross-examined. Q. Are these your depositions ? A. Yes- men-
tioned about being as lenient as possible before the Magistrate -I heard
him say afterwards he could not believe he could do such a thing,
NOT GUILTY.

3051. THOMAS BEAUMONT was indicted for stealing, on the 16th
of October, 1 purse, value 2d.; 2 half-crowns, 2 shillings, 2 sixpences, 2
pence; 1 five-franc piece, value 4s. 2d.; and 2 francs, value 1s. 8d.; the
property of Angus M'Donald, in a vessel on the navigable river Thames.
ANGUS MÓDONALD. I belong to the Dreadnought Hospital ship. The
prisoner slept next to me in my cabin-in the afternoon of the 15th of
October I had a purse containing two half-crowns, 158., and the French
pieces- I put them into my jacket pocket, under my head, when I went to
bed-at half past seven o'clock next morning I missed them--the boat-
swain found this money on the prisoner.
John Lord. I am boatswain. After this complaint about the money
I spoke to the prisoner--he said the money belonged to him-I found 86
one five-franc piece, two single francs, and some coppers on him-I took
him before the captain, and then he said he picked it up under the hammock.
John Bovis (police-constable R 139.) I took the prisoner-he said, "!
is done, and it can't be helped."
Prisoner's Defence. When I turned out it was under my hammock;
and it is a rule when anything is found to take it to the captain, but we
cannot see him till nine o'clock.
