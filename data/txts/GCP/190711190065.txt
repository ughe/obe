not on that day. I did not tell him that I wanted some carpets, but
he knew I wanted them. When he was at my house he saw I did not
have any. I certainly did buy a carpet the very same evening that I
received the £5. I did not receive £21 from Preston; I have never
had that sum in my possession from August to the present day; not
altogether. The £5 was for nothing. Childs was not present when
£21 was handed to me, because I never received £21, and I have
never seen Childs in my life till I saw him in the dook. I have never
told Preston that I felt so nervous if Greenbaum was caught that he
would tell the police I had lent him Gibson's letter, and never knew
Greenbaum had done anything wrong. Preston did not say, "Well,
you have had your share." I only paid 22s. for the carpet. I owe
my landlord to-day, I suppose, about £21-perhaps £22. In the
middle of July I owed him, I believe, £17. I have paid the rent
since then out of my office, and a little time ago I sold some shooting
for £100, of which I got £10. As regards my personal expenses,
generally have a cup of tea and my railway fare, otherwise I do not
spend any money. I have a wife and child. I never keep books, but
you can see my letters. I can easily prove that I carry on a legiti
mate business. I never received £12 from Preston on August 12. I
know nothing about the cheque for £12 13s. in any way. Redgrave
called in frequently (I cannot tell you a particular date) until
I commenced to come very late to the office and
went away on purpose to avoid meeting him. I asked
him to keep away because he made me nervous. Ho!
was a changed man in a very short space of time; in the beginning
he was a very nice man to talk to, but afterwards he became some-
what unpleasant. I never said to Preston that it was unsafe to have
anything more to do with Gibson's name. I have only received one
£5 from Preston that was a gift. Preston told me it he had any.
thing to do with my office he would not have certain people in it
he objected to Pyke being there and asked what I wanted him for
and he did not like Mr. Chinner coming in my office. I first heard
the name of Childs from the manager of & bank, who called and
asked me if I knew anyone of that name or Watson. I said "No,"
but then said, “One moment. I had a man named Watson in my
employment, but he was not in a position to open a banking account
and his character was an indifferent one." I did not receive a letter
from Barclay's Bank in reference to Childs. I received one from
Rickmansworth, which I handed to Inspector Willis. I did know
man named Day, but did not know that was the alias of Childs.
The Day I knew was an estate agent. The letter produced is the
ordinary bank letter asking for a reference. I never replied to that
because I did not like the bank's coming to me for a reference. I
wrote to the police and asked them to see me to give advice whether I
should answer the letter. I was crossing Shaftesbury Avenue when
I saw Redgrave, and he spoke about the letter. He asked me to give!
him a reference for a bank. Why I wrote to the police was because
the inquiries were in different names-Childs and Day. Redgrave
