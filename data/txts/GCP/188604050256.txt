By the COURT. The independent circulation begins directly the cord is
tied, when the blood does not flow from the mother to the child--if the
child's throat was cut before the cord was tied the mother might bleed to
death as well as the child, that is to say if the connection remained.
By Mr. Avory. An independent circulation is generally set up in the
child in consequence of its breathing, before it is severed from the
mother.
By the COURT. When the cord is tied it sets up the action of making
the child breathe; it breathes of itself, but it does not necessarily follow
that the child has breathed of itself when the cord is tied, it may never
breathe at all the independent circulation becomes established at the
birth-the fact of breathing generally speaking, establishes the circula-
tion---supposing a wound to be inflicted on a child in the act of being
born, I should expect about the same flow of blood from the wound as if
it had been done after it had breathed.
.
By Mr. WARBURTON. With regard to the question of independent
circulation, the blood would cease to flow either from the tying of the
cord or from its being cut; there could be no independent circulation till
one of those things was done; the cord might not be cut for two or three
hours--the breathing and the independent circulation generally act
together-it is almost impossible to define when the breathing would
commence.
By the Court. I think there is a very strong probability that the child
was fully born.
WILLIAM ARTHUR FORD. I am one of the Kent constabulary stationed
at Penshurst-on 25th January I went to Edenbridge, and saw
the prisoner there at her aunt's house, and said to her "Were you at
Penshurst and Leigh on Friday evening ?” she said “Yes”-I then
told her that I should have to charge her with the murder of her child,
and cautioned her in the usual way; I said “Whatever you say will be
given in evidence against you”-she said "If I speak the truth shall I
get off ? ” I said "I don't know ”-she then said "I put the child where
it was found; I done it myself ; I had it in my bedroom at Mrs. Birkin's,
at Redhill, and I stuck it with a table-knife; it lived about 10 minutes"
-I had seen her about a fortnight before, at Christmas time, when she
was at Leigh, and she looked then as if she was in the family way.
Cross-examined. There was a little conversation about family matters,
I being her cousin.
