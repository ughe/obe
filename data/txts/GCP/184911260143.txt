136. ELIZABETH BROUGHTON was again indieted for stealing
1 necklace, 1 umbrella, 1 knife, and 2 soaps, value 108.; the goods of William
Lloyd ; and 1 pair of boots, and other articles, value 58.; the goods of
Charles John Maintree, her master.
CHARLES John MAINTREE. I am a jeweller, the prisoner was in my service.
On the Sunday week, before I went before the Magistrate, I had some
conversation with her, and told her it would be better for her to confess-the
boots produced are mine; they were in my bedroom before I lost them-I
also lost six pieces of ribbon, and some velvet, and other things I have not
seen the velvet since--I have seen an apron, which has been cut up; it
is my property.
WILLIAM LLOYD. I lived at the house at the same time as the prisoner.
I lost an umbrella from the passage, and two necklaces, two gold-snaps, and
a fruit-knife, from the same drawer where the rings were--this knife, neck-
lace, and umbrella (produced) are mine, and the same I lost.
ANN HORTON. The prisoner slept for two or three nights at our house
she brought this umbrella on 6th Oct., and told me she gave 1s. 6d. for it-
she said she found this knife and the rings, with other articles, in a piece of
paper, on Mount-pleasant.
Cross-examined by MR. KETTLE. Q. Did not she sleep at your house on
the Friday night? 4. Not that I recollect-I swear she did not, or on any
other night before the fire-she had the necklace and knife at the same time
as the rings.
WILLIAM HONEYWILL. I am a stoker. The prisoner made my baby a
present of these coral beads on 6th Oct.
Not GUILTY.
137. JOHN GALLEY was indicted (with William Anns, not in custody,)
for unlawfully obtaining money by false pretences.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
Henry Billson. I am assistant to George and Robert Barker, pawn-
brokers, who have a shop at 91, Houndsditch, and another at 17, High-street,
Aldgate-I attend at the Houndsditch shop. On 26th June the prisoner
brought this box, (produced, containing a chronometer,) and asked if we took
in chronometers- I told him “Yes," and asked him what he wanted on it-
he said, “ 201,"--I said I would look at it, but that was more than we were
in the habit of advancing-for any sum advanced above 101. the Act of Par-
liament requires an agreement, and it is called a loan-I asked him who it
was made by-he said, “ Brockbanks"--I said I would take it in-doors, and
show it to our managing man- I came back, and told him we could not
advance more than 101.-he said, “Oh! it is a very valuable article, and
cost a great deal of money" - I went in again, and our managing man asked
the prisoner to step round into a private room, which he did we then looked
at the article again, and asked him about it, I said, “This is a genuine
article, I suppose ?"- he said, “Oh! yes; it was bought at Brockbank's, and
