   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know the name of any of the other persons there? A.  I do not, of my own knowledge—the name of Denton was given, bat his real name is Hunter—I have not seen any thing of him lately.
   MR. JONES . Q.  Had you known Denton before? A.  No, I did not know him of my own knowledge—I have been looking for him since, but I cannot find him—I did not ask the prisoner if he could tell me where to I find him—I had no conversation with him about that person—the pin was pawned on the 13th of July. FREDERICK HAZELL re-examined  I have not seen my pin since I gave it to the prisoner—neither of the handkerchiefs found on the prisoner is mine—I have not seen my handkerchief since—I have looked over these, but it is not among them. DIANA HAYES. I am a widow, and live at No. 43, Little Surrey-street, Blackfriars-road. I was examined at the police-office, and bound over to appear here—the prisoner lodged with me between three and four months—we lived together as man and wife—Page also lodged in the house—he and the prisoner came together about the same time—they had lived opposite, at No. 23, in the same street, and came from there to me—the prisoner assisted my shop while he was with me—he had no other means of getting his living while he was with me—he had been in the tea way—I have heard him say he had been in the country within four months of September last—he did not go into the country at all while he lived with me, which was between three and four months, up to this time
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know a man named Denton? A.  I saw him come backwards and forwards to my house—I heard the prisoner say that Denton owed him money.
   MR. JONES . Q.  Who did Denton come to-tee at your house? A.  The prisoner—Denton is a tall man, not more than thirty years of age, I think; rather stout, and fair—I do not know what he is—I knew nothing of him before the prisoner came to live with me—the last time I saw him was the Saturday the prisoner was taken into custody, to the best of my recollection—I have not seen him since—he lived in Church-street, Blackfriars-road—he seemed an intimate friend of the prisoner's—I have heard the prisoner say he knew where he lived.
