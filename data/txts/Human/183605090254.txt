On the night in question I saw the prisoner, Edward Nunn, in Marshall-street, Borough-road, between twelve and one o'clock, running—he had a hat in his hand—he was coming from the prosecutor—I stopped him, and took him back to where the prosecutor and Cowline were—I stopped him about 150 yards from where they went—I am certain this is the hat I took from him. WILLIAM REED. I am a policeman, I heard a cry of "Murder," and went up to the place—I saw Cowling and David Nunn there, and afterwards saw Edward brought back—I searched him at the station-house, and found the handkerchief in his pocket—David Nunn's waistcoat, was torn—he said, in the prosecutor's hearing, that he had hit them, and had torn his waistcoat, and that he had struck him first—the prosecutor was not at all tipay.
   Edward Nunn's Defence . I called on my brother, at a general meeting that was held of a union to keep up the price of the work—I was drunk, and my brother went to see me home from the Lion, which is not more than eight minutes walk from the house—we were arm, and met the prosecutor on the pavement, by the wall—he requested the wall, and I, being drunk would not give it up—he struck me on the head—I fell, which perhaps, I might not have done, if I had been sober—after that I got up, and hit him again—I believed him to be very drunk, and he fell—but before that, I have my brother call out to chapman to take his friend away—Chapman me after my brother—after that, I picked up the hat, and the handkerchief some little way further on, with the intent to give it to the owner, let it be who it would—I could not tell whether it was my brother's, the prosecutor's, or the witness's—I had no intention of taking it away—I met that policeman, and said, "Here is a hat belonging to some body," and I went back with him without being asked—I gave him the hat.
   David Nunn's Defence . I was at Mr. Hicks's about half-past eleven o'clock, on Monday evening—my brother called for me—I thought he was tipay, and went to see him home—we met the prosecutor and his witness arm in arm—they would take the wall, and my brother would not let them—he struck my brother in the eye—and he fell—my brother struck him again. JOHN BUTT re-examined . I asked the prisoner Edward to let me look at the hat, and he gave it to me. ALEXANDER HICKS. My father keeps the Red Lion public-house is Pearl-row, Blackfriar's-road—I saw both the prisoners there, on Monday night last—David was there all the evening—Edward came in about twenty minutes after eleven o'clock—I saw them leave the house, and wished them "Good night"—Edward was tipsy—they have both borne honest characters. NOT GUILTY
