CHARLES JORDAN. I am a brushmaker] live at No. 8, Union-street,
London-road, and have done So rather better than two years. The prisoner
has lodged there all that time, up to the night of his apprehension he left
my place about eight o'clock that evening-h slept down stairs- and
my wife slept up stairs-his bed was carried up stairs of a morning, and
taken down at night, as we live in the room below in the day time-
know this jacket to belong to the prisoner.
Cross-eamined. Q. Was he in the employ of the West Indian Doeks
at the time of the charge? A. Yesmy house is about four miles from
Homer-street--h was in the habit of going to work at the Docks every
evening-on the Tuesday evening before the robbery the prisoner came
home as near six o'clock as possible| have worked at Mr. Batty's, in
Smithfleld, for ten years, and leave my work at five o'clock-] went to bed
about ten that night-myself, my wife, two children, and the prisoner, were
in the house then-he slept in the lower room-] was in the habit of locking
the door of that room-I generally take the key up of a night, because
when the prisoner awakes in the morning he generally calls up to me to
know the time to get up(I have a little Dutch clock up stairs)- I
awake before him I call out to him the time, and give him the key to let
himself out-the door of the prisoner's-room opens into the street-there
is no passage locked the door that night-it is an invariable rule with
me to lock it gave the key to the prisoner next morning about a quarter
afler six-he called me up about a quarter before sixhe generally washes
himself before he leaves-he has been employed in the Doeks at least
five or six months--I know nothing wrong of him-no one lives in the
house but myself and wife, and him-he went out with my wife that Wed-
nesday morning-we deal in fruit and herrings, and my wife goes to
market every morning-he accompanies her on his way to the Docks-
she some times goes to the Borough market, and some times to Billingsgate
-she went out with him that morning, about a quarter after six.
