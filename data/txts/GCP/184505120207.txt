Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. What are you? A. A tallow-
chandler-I live in Gardener's-lane, Putney. I am in the employ of
Willshire and Son, of Crucifix-lane, Bermondsey--the greater part of my
time I am out getting orders and collecting money-I work in the factory,
and am partly a town traveller-I have been there since the latter end of
Nov.--I have collected outstanding debts for respectable gentlemen, and

Thomas DALEY ( police-constable V 127.) I received information from
Orger- I went to the Plough-I saw the wagon, with “Barclay and Perkins"
on it-I first spoke to Woodland--I asked him what he had done with
the hay which he took off the wagon-he made no answer-I asked him a
second time--he said nothing, but went to go to the public-house-I
stopped him, and said, "I want that hay you took out of the wagon, and
put into the stable"-he opened the stable door, and gave it me out I
then went into the tap-room, and saw Rendall-I asked for Barclay and
Perkins's man-Rendall made no answer, but the landlord pointed bim
out-I then asked what he had done with the hay he had on the wagon
he said, “ I had none on "-I said, "Come out and see "-I brought him
to the wagon, and said, "Do you mean positively to say you had no bay
on this wagon ?"-he said, "I do "-when we were going away I said,
"Don't say anything to each other, for if you do, I am bound to pro-
duce it in evidence - Woodland then said, “Old man, I want a word
with you; you ought not to have taken all the hay, it looks so much."
-I then cautioned him again, and he said, “I will tell the truth ;. I did
take it off the wagon, but it was by order of the wagoner;" pointing to
Rendall, who was in custody of the other officer.

Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON. Q. How many persons were in the
tap-room? A. I think about four-I believe they were drinking and
talking-Fielder and Rendall sat together.
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Do you know how long Woodland
has been ostler there ? A. He has been under-ostler, I think, for nine or
ten years--I have been on duty in that vicinity for fifteen years--I never
heard anything against him.
DAVID FERNIE. I am a haybinder, in Mr. Perkins's service-I have
seen the two trusses of hay which were found at the Plough-they were
my binding, and Mr. Perkins's property-these are the bands they are
my making I do not work for anybody else I can tell my own binding
from any other.
