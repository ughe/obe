RICHARD DAKIN. I am an officer, and live in Grenville-street. Somer's-town. On the morning of the 14th of July I saw five persons together, about twenty minutes before six o'clock, perhaps about one mile and a quarter from the prosecutor's house; two on them attacked me with the butt-end of this gun; I searched the prisoner, who was one of them - the others got away; I found this gown in his hat, and this gun was thrown at my feet.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going to bathe - I met four young men in Maiden-lane - one of them had some birds; they said it was too cold to go into the water, and they asked me to go and catch birds; we went on to Camden-town, and one of them asked me to put the gown in my hat, and said it belonged to his sister; I did so, and when we got on further, the officer took me - the gun was thrown down.
 Three witnesses gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 19. Of stealing only. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1730.  ISAAC WILLIS was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Robert Smith , about three o'clock in the morning of the 18th of August , (no person being therein) and stealing 5 handkerchiefs, value 5s.; 1 coat, value 20s.; 1 pair of breeches, value 18s.; 1 cloak, value 15s.; 2 table clothes, value 3s.; 2 shifs, value 5s., and 1 sheet, value 1s., his property.
   JANE SMITH. I am the wife of Robert Smith, and live in the parish of St. Dunstan, Stepney. On the 18th of August I came home and found two men in my house; when they heard me open the door, they came down stairs and I struggled with them both, but they got from me - I am certain the prisoner was one - he had a crow-bar; nothing had been taken from the house; as they came across the parlour, these handkerchiefs were thrown on the floor; they had been removed from where they were before; I shut the door too, but they got from me one at a time, and got out; I gave an alarm.
   WILLIAM WELCH. I live next door to the witness. On the 18th of August I heard the alarm, and saw two men running from her house; I pursued, and took the prisoner - the other got away.
   WILLIAM DICKINSON. I am an officer. About four o'clock on the 18th of August, I was at the corner of Heath-street, and heard a noise - I saw the prisoner running, and Welch following him - we took him; I found this crow-bar in his right-hand pocket; I had passed the house just before, and heard a noise, but I thought it was a family affair; I asked how he came to break into any person's house; he said he was sorry for it - that he was in distress; there was no marks of violence whatever on the door.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going across Stepney, and as I ran by the prosecutrix's house, she came and cried, Stop thief! I had not been in the house. GUILTY. Aged 36. Of Stealing only- Transported for Seven Years.
 1731.  PHOEBE ALLEN was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of July , 1 cap, value 4d.; 1 collar, value 2s.; 1 veil, value 18d.; 1 gown, value 15s., and two 10l. Bank notes, the property of Susannah Hollis , her mistress, in her dwelling-house.
 SUSANNAH HOLLIS. I am a  widow, and keep a coffee-shop and eating-house, in Crown-court, Westminster, in the parish of St. James'. The prisoner was in my employ three weeks and five days; she left me on the 2d of July, without giving me notice; she asked me for 5s. on the Saturday night, and had 9s. before; it was about eight o'clock in the morning when she went, and at five that afternoon I missed my property from the second-floor; her bed-room was on the first-floor; I missed a silk gown, a lace-cap, a lace veil, a tippet, and two bank notes of 10l. each; I have not seen the notes, but the other articles are here.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Is not that room open to any one who comes to the house? A. No; only to two young men who have slept in that room the last two years; they slept there the night before these things were lost - they are gentlemen's servants, and go out at seven o'clock in the morning - they are not here now - one is gone to Portsmouth with a family; the room was not locked, but the drawers were - a great many people come to the house; but they do not go to the second floor, they go to the first floor.
   CHARLES SAMUEL SMITH. I am a pawnbroker. I have a cap and tippet pawned for 6s., and a veil for 1s. 6d., on Monday, the 3d of July, by a woman (in the name of Phoebe Allen) like the prisoner, but I cannot swear to her.
   WILLIAM ALDER. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Lambeth. I have a gown pawned, on the 3d of July, by the prisoner, in the name of Phoebe Allen, No. 10, John-street; I have known her for years.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is that address correct? A. No; not where she lived last; I could have directed any person to have found her; I knew her name when she first came to the shop.
   DANIEL DORKINS. I am a Bow-street patrol. I found the prisoner in the Strand on the 11th of July - the prosecutrix pointed her out to me - she, at first, denied the charge, but afterwards admitted it, and told me where she had pawned them.
 SUSANNAH HOLLIS re-examined. Q. Did you know what time the two servants went out? A. Yes; at seven o'clock - the drawer was kept locked - I had seen the articles safe at half-past eleven o'clock the night before - I had seen the money safe on the 26th of June, when I took it to pay my rent at the Bank, but the person was not there, and I brought it back - the servants came to breakfast at eight o'clock that morning; they were out all day, and came home at ten o'clock at night; I think it was in August they went into the country - it was the prisoner's duty to to make the bed in that upper room - when I discharged her she went directly out of the house.
 Prisoner's Defence. On the Saturday night there was a little boy there, and when I left the prosecutrix she came for me to come back; I said I could not, but I would come the next day; she cannot say she saw the things safe on the night before - she was very much in liquor - I never had the money.
