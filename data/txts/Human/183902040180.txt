 736. ROBERT SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of January , 1 time-piece, value 2 l  ., the goods of John Westaway . EDWIN WESTAWAY. I live with my father, John Westaway, in Gower-street North—he is a watch-maker. On Thursday night, the 24th of Ja nuary, I was in the parlour, and saw somebody stepping out of the shop—I told my brother, and he went out. HENRY WESTAWAY. I was told of this by my brother, and missed a time-piece from a stand in front of the shop, near the door—I saw the prisoner go off the step of the door, and throw the time-piece against the chapel—I pursued, and took him—I had not lost sight of him at all.
   Prisoner.  When he overtook me in Euston-square, he said, "Tell me where the property is, and I will not prosecute you." Witness.  No, I did not—when I took him, he said, "Pray don't—pray don't"—I said, "Pray don't what?" and the officer came and took him. JOHN WESTAWAY. This time-piece was on a stand in my shop—it had been left me to repair. JOHN ROUSE( police-constable E  24.) I was on duty, and took the pri soner.
   Prisoner.  I hope you will recommend me to mercy. GUILTY.* Aged 22.— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. Transported for Seven Years.

THOMAS MATTHEWS and WILLIAM WALKER were in dicted for stealing, on the   15th of January , 2 locks, value 8 d  ;2 keys, value 4 d  .;1 latch, value 4 d  .;1 ornament, value 4 d  .; and 1 vice, value 3 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of John William Wintle . JOHN WI LLIAM WINTLE. I keep a general shop in Kingsland-road. On the 15th of January I found the prisoners in custody at the station-house—I found these articles in possession of the officer—the vice has not been found—I know this ornament—I had locks and keys like these, but I cannot swear to them.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are you a dealer in marine-stores? A.  No—I never dealt in marine-stores in my life, but I have it written up to save myself from the fangs of informers—I would not swear to this ornament, but I had such a one—it represents the binding of our Saviour. GEORGE KEMP( police-constable N  82.) On the evening of the 15th of January I saw the two prisoners with another, near the prosecutor's shop in Kingsland-road, where there were a great many articles of ironmongery on a board—I watched, and saw them all three pass, and take something off the board—they went past, then turned back, and I saw the prisoners take two locks—I then followed them to a marine-store shop in Essex-street—the prisoners went in to sell the things—I went and took them in the shop—Matthews had a file and this leaden ornament—Walker had these two locks and keys, and latch—the other one escaped—Matthews said another boy gave him this ornament, and then he said he picked it up.
