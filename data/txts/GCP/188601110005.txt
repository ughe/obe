-on 25th September I went to the police-station at Kentish Town and

6th October I went and saw Morris again I asked him who this Smythe
was that had endorsed my cheque, that I might see him-he said he was

lently I said, “You have taken proceedings?"-he said “Yes"_I

that he said he had sworn at the Mansion House, and read it out to me
-on the 8th I went with him to Cannon Street to see Morton--we did
not see him there, and we went to his house, 64, Elm Park Road, South
Kensington-he was denied at first, but after a lot of inquiries he came
out of a room close to my elbow; he could have heard all the conven
sation-I went in and said I had come about the cheque, and asked for
the 51., and Mr. Buzzard's 51. 108., and my expenses that I had paid at
the County Court, 21. 48. 8d.--he promised to pay it if I would give him
two days' grace - I said, "Why do you want two days' grace when you
have had seven months 2"-he said he would procure it by Saturday,
and he gave me a letter to that effect, and he handed me an American
share certificate, but I thought I had already too much of his paper in
my pocket, and I handed it back-he then wrote this memorandum
undertaking to pay on Saturday at 8 p.m.-on Saturday, 10th October,
I went with Bassett and Hicks to 64, Elm Park Road at about 3.25-We
did not find him--the servant said he had waited till half-past 3, and
then went out to keep an appointment-on 12th October I received this
letter in Morton's handwriting. (This stated that he would call on Thursday
at 12.30 to take up the choque. I never saw him afterwards till he was
before the Magistrate-on 27th May I met Morris accidentally in Finsbury
Park-he said, “How about your cheque?” I said, "It is in the hands
of my solicitor"-he said he had nine cheques of Morton's to the amount
of 751., and he had heard nothing about him, and he had indicted him
because he had served him so badly.
