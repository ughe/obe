Thomas Light. I am the father of the last witness. I know the pri-

Light came to me, wishing me to come into the parlour, and desired me to
ask the prisoner what was the meaning of the paper-I did so-he told me
it was a paper he had received from Mr. Alner-I then asked him whether
it was Mr. Alner's handwriting-he said, “Yes, do you think I would be
guilty of any thing of that kind of its not being Mr. Alner's hand-
writing)-- I would sooner have my head off my body"-I then said to my
son," I suppose, as Mr. Alner's name is there, there can be no harm in
your signing it, as he says it is nothing but for Mrs. Cornish to have the
money," and my son signed it I do not recollect any thing more.
COURT. Q. What are you? A. I have been a farmer, but am old now,
and live with my children.
