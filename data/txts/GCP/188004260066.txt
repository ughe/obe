ALFRED WEIGEL. I am a merchant, of 39, Mark Lano-I discounted
this bill of 4331. 128. 6d. on December 31st-I gave Mr. Ross the two
acceptances for 1001. and 831. 128. 60.--I paid them in due course through
my bank-I am still liable to Mr. Ross for 2501.
Cross-examined. The bills were presented on 1st April, and paid on the
12th-I did not pay a shilling on 31st December.
MR. BESLEY submitted that under the statute of 24th and 25th Vic. c. 96.
8. 75, the direction must be given to a person, not a firm, and that there tow
no evidence that the prisoner was an agent, also that the bill of exchange woo
not a valuable security. MR. WILLIAMS contended that the prisoner, having
received the direction, and the proceeds, tous for that purpose the firm ; that
any person who received a direction to pay money was an agent. The COURT
considered that there was evidence to go to the Jury as to whether the prisoner
1008 an agent.
Guilty. Recommended to mercy.-One Month's Imprisonment.

DAVID DUNBAR, Stealing two fans and three trinkets, the goods
of the Rev. Arthur Wolfe Hamilton Gell. Other Counts for stealing

MESSRS. BESLEY and AUSTIN METCALFE Prosecuted; MR. MEAD defended

JAMES BABBINGTON. I am assistant to Mr. Edwin Soames, & pawnbroker,
of Queen's Road, Chelsea-on 29th October Dunbar brought these two china
plates (produced) and pawned them in the name of David Dunbar, 57, Red-
bourne Street-1 gave him 128.--I said, “Who do they belong to ?"-he
said, “Charles Wilson, I have him outside "-I asked him to fetch him in
- he brought in Witton, who said, “They are my own property"-on 5th
December Dunbar brought these three china plates, and pawned them for
118, in the name of Charles Wilson-on 9th December he pawned these
two china plates for 78. for the same person-at the first interview he said
Wilson was a dealer in china-I had known Dunbar many years as a
customer-on 24th December Witton pawned these two plates for 88.-on
10th January Dunbar pawned this clock for 258., and on 15th January these
five plates for 88.-—there is a fancy value attached to the articles-the plates
are mostly odd ones; there are fourteen, two of each set.
Cross-examined by Dunbar. I have known you ten years, and your wife
about the same time, as customers—I distinctly understood they were Wilson's
property-you appeared to be respectable.
ALFRED WILLIAM FITCH. I am assistant to Mr. Millar, a pawnbroker, of
Sloane Square-on 6th November Dunbar brought & grebe muff-I was
present when he pledged it for 58.-I forget what was said-on 3rd Feb-
ruary, 1879, Witton pawned this china card-tray for 31. by special contract
under the Pawnbrokers Act-it was renewed on 26th October, and he paid
78. interest-on 5th August he pawned this gold-bladed knife for 158., on
27th October these six china plates for 258., on 29th October these six china
plates for 61. 108., and on 12th November 12 plates for 11. 108.-a little
child was with him on one occasion-he gave his address 4, Stafford Place,
