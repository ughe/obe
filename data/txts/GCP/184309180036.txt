EDWARD HENRY LOVELOCK. I am clerk to John Chapel and Co. shia
brokers- there is a ship called the Bangalore, which they are brokers for
captain's name is Thomas Smith-she sailed to India about the 25th or 2674
of July from Gravesend I know the captain's signature-I have seen him
write frequently--no part of this note is his handwriting--the signature
not his certainly-it is the usual sort of advance note that is given to an
men--it would be purchased at the stationers', or given to them by these
agents-I can tell by reference to the list of the crew, when they were shined
on board the vessel-I know nothing whatever of it of my own knowledge.
ENEAS M'ALLEN (police-constable K 95.) The prisoner was given into my
charge by the prosecutrix for uttering a forged note- I told her what it wa
for-she said if her husband would not go in the Bangalore he would go i
some other ship, she had not the means of paying the money at that time.
but as soon as she had the money she would pay her-she said that several
times-Mrs. Thomas gave me this note—it has been in my custody ever
since.
Prisoner's Defence. My husband gave me the note; he said he was e-
gaged as steward on board this ship; he asked me to take it to Mr. Thomas,
and ask him to change it ; he bad changed one before; he did change i,
and I offered him 6s. for doing it, and paid him 6s. more; I did not
ask my husband if it was a forged note; I considered it was a true one, as I
have had four true potes before from him and got them changed; when Mn.
Thomas told me about the note I went to the dock; they refused me ad-
mittance, because my husband was not in the ship that I considered he wa
in from the Monday to the Thursday; when Mrs. Thomas spoke to me
about the note, I said I was very sorry indeed, but I did not think it was
forgery, and if my husband did not go in that ship he would go in another,
and then she should have the money; I would take it to the owners when be
got another ship and get the money; I did not intend to defraud ber; I con
sidered it was a true note; my husband was in the house at the time I took
it; he gave it me out of his pocket ten minutes before ; I took the money
back to him when Mrs. Thomas told me it was very bard; I said it was very
hard for me, for I was left with three children wholly unprovided for, but not
to worret about it, for in a few days I should perhaps hear from him; I was
afraid he had fallen down in a fit in the street, as he was subject to fits; they
asked me if the owners were the same as in the last note; I said no, but they
would have no more trouble in getting this note changed than the last; if!
had known it to be a forgery I should not have taken it to a neighbour; !
have lived in the neighbourhood and in the same house before last Christmas
