I took him to my house in Chatham-place, and afterwards to the Mansion House. In consequence of information he gave us we went that evening to Zamira's house, in Duke's-place, but did not go in; we went there again on the evening of the 18th, and part of my stamps were found there. I never sell stamps in any way whatever - here are what I found on the prisoner Meredith when he was taken, and here are those found at Zamira's on the 18th - they are what I had lost on the 13th or 14th.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ALLEY. Q. Then it was not on the evening you took the boy you went to Zamira? A. No; we did not go into the house till the evening of the 18th; I have about six servants; the stamps were not locked up - they were in a private counting-house; Mereditch could have access to it, because he came at eight o'clock in the morning, before the clerks came; we have three porters and two clerks, but the counting-house is never left by myself or Mr. Smith; I think only one clerk or book-keeper knew where the stamps were kept.
   WILLIAM MERCHANT. I am an officer. I went to Zamira's house, at the corner of Bury-street, St. Mary Axe, on the 17th, and on the 18th I went in with the prisoner Meredith, Mr. Shirley, and a gentleman here - we sent Meredith in, who asked if Zamira was at home; they said Yes; as I went in he was coming down stairs; I said to him "Do you know this young man," (pointing to Meredith) who was standing with his back against the door - he said No; there was no other young man in the shop; I asked what he had done with the stamps he bought of Meredith; he said he knew nothing at all about them; I then told him I was an officer; he said "Don't make a noise, I have got my house full of company;" he then spoke to his wife, or an elderly woman there, and, to the best of my recollection, it was to go up stairs, and bring the stamps down; she brought them down and gave them to Zamira - he gave them to Mr. Shirley, who identified them as his property; here are four for 3s. 6d., four for 4s. 6d., and five for 5s.; they are quite clean, and here is a mark on every one of them; we then came down to the Mansion-house; they were then committed - Meredith did not deny it or say any thing about the stamps produced - it is a kind of grocer's and stationer's shop I understand.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BRODRICK. Q. Did he call to this old lady? A. No; she came without being called - he only said "Go and fetch me the stamps;" I heard all he said - he is not deaf that I know of.
   PETER MURPHY. I am an officer. I went to Zamira on the 17th, and again on the 18th, but I did not go in till the stamps were delivered.
   WILLIAM NORWOOD. I am cellarman to Messrs. Smith and Shirley. Persons come there to business at a quarter or half-past seven o'clock in the morning; I did not know before the 18th of March, where the stamps were; I have seen Meredith about the house in a morning - he used to go to the counting-house when his brother was not there; I saw him about a week or ten days before he was apprehended.
   THOMAS SHIRLEY. These are a part of the stamps which I marked on the 13th or 14th of March.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BRODRICK. Q. What is the value of those bits of paper without the stamps? A. A halfpenny or a penny.
 MEREDITH's Defence. Mr. Shirley gave me a promise that if I would tell all I knew he would do nothing to me.
 Nine witnesses gave Zamira an excellent character.
 MEREDITH - GUILTY. Aged 17.
 ZAMIRA - GUILTY. Aged 56. Recommended to Mercy. Confined Three Months.

 883.  ANTHONY DRUMADA was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of March , 1 hat, value 5s., the goods of Robert Hugh Franks.
   JAMES BAYLEY. I am in the employ of Robert Hugh Franks, a hatter, of Red Cross-street, Barbican. On the 18th of March, a witness, named Phelps, told me some person had taken a hat from the door - I went out and received this hat from a young man who had been in the shop buying one. The prisoner was then brought back.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM PHELPS. I am fourteen years of age. I was in Red-cross-street on the 18th of March, about half-past five o'clock - I saw the prisoner take the hat from a peg - he said to me "Do not say any thing - come along with me;" he went a good way off with it - I did not see him stopped - I told the shopman of it.
   WILLIAM PAYNE. I am an officer. I was sent for to take the prisoner into custody - I found him in the shop, and this sack under his arm - the hat was given me by the witness - I took him to the Compter, and found four half-crowns and 4s. 6d. in his pockets.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to buy a hat, and a man gave me a push, and told me to go on; I had the hat, and some men came and took me by the collar; I had the bag to go to Billingsgate to get a job - the hat was outside the door.
 JAMES BAYLEY re-examined. The hat was on a stand inside the door - a young man there saw him take it - he followed and brought him back.
 GUILTY. Aged 55. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined One Month.
 884.  JOHN KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Lowndes , from his person.
 THOMAS LOWNDES. I was in  Fleet-street, near Water-lane, on the 5th of April; I felt something press behind me, and put my hand to my pocket, and my handkerchief was gone; I turned, and the prisoner was as close behind me as he could be; I said he had taken the handkerchief, and I saw it between his feet - there were two youths behind him - he denied having taken it.
 Prisoner. I saw two lads before him, and one just off the curb; he turned round and said to me he had lost his handkerchief; I said I knew nothing about it.
     NOT GUILTY.
