Miller, in his defence, stated that two or three different writings had been
put in as his, but he admitted the order for the coat. He complained that
all the documents in his favour had been held back, valus 4001, though the
others had been produced. He stated that he ordered two tons of coal for
Mrs. Haygarth at her request, but five tons were sent; that he was introduced
to Rogers three years ago as a man of private means and whore wife had a
private income; that Mrs. Haygarth toas a tenant of his, and he never knew
where the soap and sanitary compound came from ; that he never lenero
Moran til he saw him in the dock, and the only men he knew among the
defendants were Rogers and Woolley, whom he had known for 12 years ;
and stated that he had made 6,0001, by speculating in cotton, and that there
was 3,0001. worth of property at his house in Elgin Place. He denied owing
any one a shilling. (Mr. GILL produced all the documents found, which
were handed to Miller.)
Bizton, in his defence, contended that there was no evidence to connect him

Nerberry produced a written defence, stating that he gave a reference to
Rogers because he had sold goods for him on commission, but denied con-
spiring with him or any one else, not then knowing the other prisoners; that
he never received anything in the other transactions, and what he had done
