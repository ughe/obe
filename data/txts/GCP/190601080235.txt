Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
New Court, January 8th, 1906.
190. WILLIAM COCKERTON (40) and LILLIE COOKERTON (38),
Uttering counterfeit coin, well knowing the same to be counterfeit.
WILLIAM COCKERTON PLEADED GUILTY.
Mr. PARTRIDGE Prosecuted ; Mr. MURPHY Defended.
THOMAS COVELL. I am a corn chandler, of 39, Station Road, Manor
Park, which is about two miles from Bow-between 3 and 4 p.m. on
Priday, December 22nd, a female, whom I cannot now identify, came into
my shop for lb. of tapioca, price 14d.--I served her, getting in payment
florin, for which I gave her 1s. 101d, change - I put it on one side, where
there were no other florins-the female left the shop with the tapioca -
she was carrying this basket (Produced)—this bag is very similar to the
one I put the tapioca in (Produced) -my bags have not my name on them-
at 9 p.m., when I came to look at the florin and compare it with some
half-crowns, shillings and sixpences, I could see it was a bad one, and I :
communicated with the police.
Cross-examined. Unfortunately I have not a great deal of business
in my neighbourhood people as a rule buy small quantities; there
was nothing unusual about this purchase-I do not keep & record of
every customer, nor do I keep a descriptive list of the handbags they carry
- this is an unusual sort of bag, because it has got cloth handles and the
straw is open work-this tapioca corresponds with the best tapioca I sell.
HENRIETTA BARTON. I am the wife of William Barton, of 604, Romford
Road, Forest Gate, which is about an hour's walk from Bow about 3 p.m.
on December 22nd the female prisoner came into the shop and asked for a
pennyworth of crumpets and two scones, which would be 2d.-I put them
in a bag and gave them to her, and she laid down a florin on the counter-
I took it up and tried it on a small piece of slate we had on the counter and
I noticed it made a greasy soft mark, by which I came to the conclusion
that it was bad-I called to my husband and showed him the mark on the
slate and said, "This 28. piece is wrong" in the female prisoner's hearing-
on seeing the mark my husband asked her where she got it from and she
Teplied that she did not know-I then marked it across with another coin,
so as to deface it, and my husband then handed it back to her and told her
to take it where she got it from she paid for the articles she had bought,
but I do not remember what with–I have been shown three counterfeit
florins by the police, but this one was not amongst them the female
prisoner left the shop and my husband followed her immediately.
