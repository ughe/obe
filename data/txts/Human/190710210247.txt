 Detective-inspector JOHN PTTAWAY, City, On May 9, 1907, I kept observation on 16, Great St. Helen's, saw Tyler leave, and followed him to W. Tyler and Co.'s office, Pinner's Hall. I went in and saw Claire Latus in the outer office, the door to the inner room being open, I said, "I have called on behalf of Madame Pittet, of Chingford. I want to see one of the principals." She said, "Mr. Tyler is not in." I said, "Can you tell me when he will be in?" She said, "No, he it in and out all day. Madame Pittet had better write." I said, "Madam Pittet is tired of writing, that is the reason I called. I will come back later." I kept observation on the office, and two or three minutes later Tyler came out. On May 27 I received warrant for Tyler's arrest, saw him in Tokenhouse Yard, and told him I held a warrant for his arrest for obtaining money by false pretences. I took him to the Chief Office of Police and read the warrant, which related to £15 obtained from Madame Pittet. He said, "They can charge me if they like, but I do not think they can do it." A little later he said, "I know nothing about the business, although I am nominally responsible." I then searched the office at Pinners Hall; Latus and Reece were there. In the outer office were two fixed desks, a stool, a copying press, two packets of headed notepaper, 92 prints of the Rio Tinto mine, a bundle of folded circulars (produced), many hundreds of addressed envelopes, and several lists of investors. There were no books of account whatever. I then went to the office of Gibbs and Tyler, 16, Great St. Helen's. The names up were "Malayian Property Syndicate" and "Malayian Para Rubber Estates, Limited"—not the name of "Gibbs and Tyler." I found a number of cheque books, mostly used, on the Middlesex Bank, signed "William Tyler and Co."; memorandum book containing pay ments in and out to "George." Pass book of W. Tyler and Co. with the London and Provincial Bank. In the memorandum book was written "7348 Wall," which is the telephone number of the "Financial Guardian." I also found circulars and notepaper of W. Tyler and Co.; press copy letter book of Gibbs and Tyler; cheque for £86 14s. 6d. by W. Tyler and Co. to Perryman. (The witness gave evidence as to the handwriting on a number of cheques.) I then returned to the police-station and saw Tyler. I said, "I have searched your office, but I have failed to find any books of account. I want you to tell me where they are." He said, "If you go to 2, Tokenhouse Buildings and see George Harris he may give you some." I asked for a letter of authority, which he gave me, as follows: "Dear George,—I have been arrested and am going to Moor Lane, Detective Ottaway is coming up to see you."
 To Mr. F. E. Smith. I cannot say whether I cautioned Tyler be  fore this—I certainly cautioned him afterwards.
