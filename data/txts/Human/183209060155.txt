 2102.  LYDIA ANDERSON was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 4 gowns, value 24s.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d.; 1 petticoat, value 2s.; 1 handkerchief, value 3d.; 2 gowns, value 4s., and 4 caps, value 4s., the goods of Hannah Parsons.
   HANNAH PARSONS. I live in North-street, Chelsea. The prisoner lodged in the same room with me for a week; I missed these things from my drawer on the 14th of August, at half-past eleven o'clock - they were safe the night before; the prisoner was absent, and did not return that night - I saw her next morning, and charged her with stealing them; she said, "Pray, don't hurt me, I was going to bring them back" - I found two of my gowns at her mother's; she had one of them on, with a cap and petticoat.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned a gown with me, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, on the 15th of August.
   THOMAS BRANSTON. I am a Policeman. I took her in charge; she had one gown on - I found two gowns at her mother's, in Great Peter-street.(Gowns produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she had been compelled to resort to prostitution; that she had been on terms of intimacy with the prosecutrix, and that they were constantly in the habit of wearing each other's clothes.
   HANNAH PARSONS. I did not give her leave to pawn my clothes; I did not give her leave to wear any of these things - I lent her a shawl; I have not gone by different names.
   WILLIAM DAVIS. I am a jeweller, and live in Crown-street. The prosecutrix lodges in a house of mine; she was brought home tipsy last Saturday night by a Policeman, in a coach, and said she was robbed of half a sovereign, and accused five or six people of it; I think her word ought not to be taken - she told me last Sunday that she was in the habit of lending the prisoner clothes, and gave her leave to pawn them; I have told her to leave my house- she has been there three weeks, and brought home a stranger every night.
   HANNAH PARSONS. I never told him I gave her leave to pawn my clothes; he was always asking me to come and live at his house - he keeps a bad house.
   WILLIAM DAVIS. I let my rooms to men and women, single as well as married; the prosecutrix answers to three different names.
   SARAH KIERNEY. I live at No. 11, Crown-street; the prosecutrix and prisoner formerly lodged there, and were intimate - Parsons used to dress her in the greatest style, and said she need not want clothes while she lived there, she would always lend her them to pledge; and the prisoner used to give her what money she got - they both absconded from my house, taking their clothes out of window.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2103.  MARY ANN AUSTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of August , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., 6d., the goods of Asher Daniel Humphries.
 ELIZABETH HUMPHRIES. I am the wife of  Asher Daniel Humphries, and have not seen him for five years; I live in Ironmonger-row, St. Luke's- I take in washing. The prisoner kept a school in the front-parlour; I saw this handkerchief laying with dirty linen at twelve o'clock, and missed it between three and four - the prisoner had looked at it that morning, and said it was a pretty one; when I told her I missed it, she said it was a very wicked thing to rob the widow and fatherless.
   JAMES COOK. I am a shopman to Mr. Russel, in Fore-street. This handkerchief was pawned on the 22nd of August, by the prisoner, in her own name and address.
   THOMAS DOWLEY. I am a Policeman. I apprehended her, and found eighteen duplicates on her - but none for the handkerchief.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Three Months.
 2104.  GEORGE ANSLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of June , 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence, the monies of John Stevens , his master.
   THOMAS KING. I am servant to John Stevens, a butcher, at Harrow; the prisoner and I were in his service together. The prisoner came in at seven o'clock in the evening, and said, "Go and get a truss of hay for the horses;" he returned and said he had got it, and wanted half a crown to pay for it - I was serving a customer in the shop at the time; he went into the parlour where the money was - there was 3s. 8 1/2d. in the bowl; I saw him at the cupboard; I went into the parlour, and caught him at the cupboard - I said, "You have been robbing your master;" he said he had not, and dropped some money: I sent for the officer, who found 1s. 7d. where he sat - I have not found the other 2s.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. He went to get the money for the hay? A. No - he said he would go and get something to eat; he must have thrown the two shillings out of window, for I did not find them.
   JAMES ROWE. I am a constable. I found 1s. 7d. by the prisoner's chair, and 2 1/2d. in his pocket; he said he did not take the money.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2105.  JOHN ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 1 poker, value 1s.; 1 shovel, value 2s., and 1 pair of tongs, value 5s., the goods of Catherine Whitfield and another.
 CATHERINE WHITFIELD. I am a  broker and live in Old-street. These fire-irons were taken from outside my door; I did not see the prisoner near.
   GEORGE BLACKMAN. I am a tailor, and live two or three doors from the prosecutrix. The prisoner passed with these things in his hand; I followed and took him: he dropped them.
   AUGUSTUS PENMAN. I am a Policeman. I saw the prisoner running, and secured him.(Property produced and sworn to)
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Confined One Month.
