JOHNSON, Mayor.
MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not see some of your ribbons in another
shop window? A. No--I saw the same patterns in one or two win.
dows-I did not go in the prisoner's shop is about two miles from mine
-- he lives in Shoreditch-the ribbons were all lost the same day-most
of them were in the white box, which is not here-here are one or two
pieces in this box which were in it when lost-the others were not found.
WILLIAM HOLLAND re-examined. I took away a large quantity of pro-
perty from the shop in the first instance, which was not claimed - I took
away 580 rolls of ribbon, and left about 2000 rolls behind-those produced
formed a very small portion of the stock--the prisoner and his wife and a
little boy served in the shop- the business was not on a very small scale.
HENRY WILLIAM Dubois re-examined. The prisoner, his wife, and a
boy, were the only persons I saw in the shop- I took all the books away
-I gave two up by application from counsel to the Magistrate-they
were small books--the one I retained is not respecting this case--the
other two were for entering goods, but I could not make out how they
were kept-there was an account of ribbons and various other articles in
them, but they were not regularly kept, and there were no dates.
THOMAS PARKER re-examined. I have now brought the other book-
there is no entry of any later dealing for ribbons—there are other transac-
tions since-the last transaction is on the 11th of May, but then he did
not buy any ribbons-he has not bought any ribbons since 1840--the last
dealing for ribbons was on the 26th of April, 1840--the dealing ceased in
May, 1840-there are no dealings entered in the new book at all-I have
looked, and there are no dealings later than I have stated.
JURY. Q. Do you ever sell ribbons of different lengths and remnants
A. No-we always sell in half pieces.
