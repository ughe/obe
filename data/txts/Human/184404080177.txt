1244. GEORGE BIRCH was indicted for stealing 3 planes, value 6 s  ., and 2 saws, 4 s  .; the goods of Thomas Downes , his master; and that he had been before convicted of felony. THOMAS DOWNES, bedstead-maker, Flower and Dean-street, Spital-fields.  On the 5th of Feb. I missed three planes and two saws from my workshop—I bad seen them safe the day before—the prisoner was at work at my place that morning—I accused him of taking them—he beckoned one of his shop mates out—I did not see him again for two months—I have not seen the tools again. JOSEPH READER. I work for the prosecutor. On the morning of the 5th of Feb., he complained of missing his tools, and accused the prisoner of taking them—the prisoner beckoned me out, and confessed to me that he had taken them for drink, and he wanted me to go to his lodging to show me the tickets of his tools—I would not go—he went away, and did not come back. JOHN SHEARING. I work for the prosecutor. On Monday, 1st of April, I went to the Globe, in the Borough-road, and found the prisoner—he said, "Jack , you are going to take me"—I said, "Yes," and told him to come borne with me to my master—he said, "Yes, I will;" and in coming along, he owned that he took the tools for drink, and he was willing to pay for them, provided his master would take him back to his employ—I gave him in charge on Tower-hill. GEORGE HYDE( City police-constable, No.  516.) The prisoner was given to me—he said it was all through drink, or it would not have happened. GEORGE TEAKLE( police-sergeant H  8.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 26.— Confined Twelve Months .
 1245. PETER WINCEY was indicted for stealing three sheets, value 7 s  .; the goods of Mark Hammond . HANNAH HAMMOND. I am the wife of Mark Hammond, and live in Chapman-street, Commercial-road, East. The prisoner lodged at my house for three weeks and three days—he occupied a furnished room, with his wife and two children—he left on the 23rd of March—I missed three sheets and a table-cloth from the room—I afterwards saw the prisoner—I charged him with having taken them—he said he had taken them and pledged them, and said I should have them returned—I said, "It is impossible I can take your word, I have waited nine days, in consequence of a note you sent me"—he did not let me know where he had gone to—these are the sheets.
   Prisoner. Q.  On the Saturday before I left, did you not tell me if I was in want of money, I might pledge one of these articles? A.  Certainly not—you did not say if I would lend you 1 s . till the following day, you would not part with them—I did not send word if you wanted a loaf of bread you might have it—I found this note down by the door, on the evening they sent me the key—they did not send me any address— note read  —"Mrs. Ham mond, I am sorry to leave you, but as it was your wish for us to do so, we have done so. I am again disappointed in receiving my money, and shall not have it till April. Your sheets and table-cover are at wash; and on Monday evening you shall have them. I will send you the rent with them." WILLIAM STARR( policeman. ) I went with Mrs. Hammond to No. 13, Baker's-row, and found the prisoner—she gave him in charge—I asked where the sheets were—he said they were in the wash, and then be told me they were in pledge—I received some duplicates from his wife. HENRY EDWARD STREET, shopman to Messrs, Walker and Brown, pawn brokers.
