The evidence of Carlo and Giovanni Moriggia in the Bankruptcy Court
put in and partly read. MR. BESLEY then submitted that as neither Pedrolli
nor O'Connor were bankrupts, and as their statements were not voluntary, but
given under pressure, they could not be used against thom. Mr. Grain took
the same objection. MR. COLE contended that the statements were admissible, as
the Act not only compelled the bankrupt to appear and give evidence, but other
parties also, under pain of committal. (See Queen v. Chidley, 8 Cox's Criminal
Cases, p. 365, and Queen v. Robinson, 1 Crown Cases Reserved.) The
COURT considered the evidence admissible, and the notes were put in and rai.
Mr. TICKELL, referring to the decision of Mr. Justice Hawkins in the Albion
case, reported in the Sessions Paper of May, 1878, claimed the right of reply
on behalf of the Attorney-General, although the Defendants called no witnessu.
MR. WILLIAMS contended that this was simply a Bankruptoy and not a State
prosecution. MR. BESLEY submitted that the attention of Mr. Justice Harokiu
had not been called to the Act of Parliament of May, 1865, or he would not
have decided that counsel representing the Crown had a right of reply, that
this prosecution was instituted by a private solicitor, the name of the Oroon no
being on the Indictment. MR. TICKELL replied that the prosecution was insti.
tutod by the Treasury, and was on all fours with the Albion case, in which Mr.
Justice Hawkins had decided that counsel representing the Crown had the right of
reply. The COURT, having looked through the crises, considered that they were all
in favour of the right claimed, except one, in which Mr. Justice Brett had hold
that the right of the Attorney-General was personal to himself. In the present
case, the prosecution being instituted by a public department of Her Majosty's
Government, the counsel for the proseoution had the right of reply, although no
witnesses were called for the defonce. MR. COLE then replied.
