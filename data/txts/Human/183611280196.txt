   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Are you living that house now? A.  I am not—I never had cards played in my house before—I played alto gether against my will—I was against betting at all, and never bet—I played the third game particularly against my will—I played the last game more against my will, because there was the heavy bet of 5 l ., against it.
   Q.  What did you care about the amount, as you were not a party? A.  They misled me—I am thirty-seven years old, and have kept a public-house nearly twelve months—I left it because I am a bankrupt, and these men have been the great cause of it—it cost me about 30 l . to prosecute them—it is a plan put on me, which they do whenever a young man gets into a new house—I did not wish my wife to lend me the 5 l . to bet with—I told the waiter not to go down and get it, for I was sure my wife would not lend me any. ROBERT CAREY. I was waiter to Mr. Tennant. I recollect seeing the prisoner and the others coming—they had a bottle of port wine—when they rang the bell I went up—the prisoner said, "Bring your master up five sovereigns"—master said he did not wish it to be brought up—I went down stairs again—the bell rang shortly afterwards—I went up, and the prisoner said, "Bring me up change for a £5 note"—I went down, and brought up five sovereigns, which I placed on the table, at the right-hand side of my master—the prisoner took it up, and gave it to Anderson—I came out of the room then, and went down stairs—shortly after I went up and there was a dispute in the room—I heard master say he was robber of five sovereigns—he came out, and told me to send for the police, which I did, you they came.
