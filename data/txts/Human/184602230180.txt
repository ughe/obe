 810. JOHN OLPIN was indicted for feloniously uttering a forged acceptance to a bill of exchange for 5 l  . 5 s  ., with intent to defraud John Bartlett .
   MR. WILDE conducted the Prosecution. JOHN BARTLETT. I am a broker, and live in Red-lion-court—I have known the prisoner about four years—on the 16th of July he came and requested of me to procure the sum of 5 l . for him, to enable him to pay his rent, and he gave me the name of Michael Coney—I let him have 2 l . on the 18th of July, for which he gave me an I O U, and the same day I gave him 3 l . 5 s ., which made up 5 l . 5 s ., and he gave me this bill of exchange for 5 l . 5 s ., purport ing to be accepted by Michael Coney, and the ink was not dry when he gave it me—I bud stated I would not advance the 5 l . 5 s . unless I had some security.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  You saw Coney before you lent the money? A.  Yes—he said he had no objection to be bound for Olpin in the sum of 2 l . or 50 s .—he thought I came from a loan society, but I explained that I did not—here is the bill, and a eross under the words accepted M. Coney, and the ink was not dry when I had it for the money in Olpin's house—it purports to bear date 19th of July, and it was given me on the 18th—here is Olpin's indorsement on it—the acceptor cannot write—the ink-stand and pen were on the table in Olpin's house—it was about two o'clock in the afternoon—I stepped in quite accidentally, and he said, "It is all ready," or else I did not expect it till the following day.
   COURT . Q.  You went to the prisoner's house, he produced this cross, which was wet, and which purports to be the cross of Coney? Yes—I in quired if Coney was there, and the prisoner said he was just gone.
   MR. PARRY . Q.  You took the bill away with you? A.  Yes—when it became due the prisoner either called or sent to me to request me to hold it, and I wrote to Coney—I saw Coney about a month after it became due, and two months after it was given to me, and he told me it was a forgery—Olpin then told me it was accepted by Coney's son—I went to his son, and he denied it—when the bill was due I did not present it to the acceptor—I held it over for a month before I saw him—the money I lent belonged to Mr. Palmer, whose business I was transacting, and I communicated the fact to him—I applied to him for permission to lend to Olpin—I did not consider I was the person to prosecute the prisoner—Mr. Palmer gave him into custody.
