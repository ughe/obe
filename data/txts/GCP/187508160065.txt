have been an insurance broker, but that is not called a business I was an
insurance agent, representing a company--they paid me a salary, and I
had private means of my own- I started as a wine merchant with capital-
I bought for ready money and never on credit at that time, but I did after-
wards-I always paid ready money, or I paid when the time arrived, that
payment should take plave by our agreement-I have crrried on business at
the Cour Chapeau Rouge, for two years- I was eighteen months & wine
merobant at Bordeaux, and ten years an insurance agent-I am still
carrying on both-I had another business there which I carried on six
months, and gave it up to go into the town-I had an agent in Paris, M.
Besson-he is not a relation of mine; he is Challier's father-in-law-he
represented my house, selling wines on commission-I did not know him
before--my traveller, M. Doussou, appointed him as my agent in Paris
Doussou, is my cousin - I have known him ever since I was born, but I
have ceased to be on good terms with him since I made the complaint in
this case I was on very intimate terms with him-I never saw him in
Paris--I have seen him in Bordeaux, at my office, not at my house-
he wrote letters for me and assisted me in my business—I hnd great confi-
dence in him-he was my agent for a year-I paid him a salary-I did not
see him for ten years before he was my agent-it was after the introduotion

orders which he had obtained for wine from customers in Paris, and I sent
much wine on his representation-I have been paid for it by bills which
are all paid to the amount of about 12,000 francs--Doussou was in the
habit of making inquiries for me about the solvency of oustomers—Challier
