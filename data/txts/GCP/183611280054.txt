48. JOHN BARNES was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November,
1 handkerchief, value 38., the goods of Edward Williams, from his person.
EDWARD WILLIAMS. I live in Basinghall-street. On the 27th of No-
vember, about half-past nine o'clock, I was in Duke-street, Smithfield-I
felt a slight touch behind-I instantly turned round, and saw the prisoner
running from me- I ran after him about fifty yards, and laid hold of him,
and said, “You have got my handkerchief" -he denied it, I said, "You
must have dropped it," and the constable passing by took hold of him-1
found it on a dust heap in another street-not where it was taken from me,
Prisoner. It was nine or ten yards from where I was standing- I had
not been in that street at all. Witness. It was taken from me in Duke-street
- he ran through Bartholomew-close, and there I found it.
ROBERT TYRRELL. On Sunday night I heard the cry of “Stop thief"-
I was in a house-I ran out, and saw the prosecutor with this lad-I took
him, and found nothing on him-- I told the prosecutor to look about-he
went a few paces, and got on a dust-hill and found it.
Prisoner. I stopped to make water-a man ran past me—this gentleman
came and caught hold of me, and said, "You have got my handkerchief"-I
said, "No I have not " he said, “I am sure it is you" - I said, "I have
not, search me"-he would not-the officer came, and he jumped up a
place and found it-I had not passed that place at all.
EDWARD WILLIAMS. Yes, he had passed close by, and was just by
there when he was taken.
GUILTY. Aged 18 Years.--Transported for Seven Years.
49. JOHN LITTLE, alias John Harris, was indicted for stealing, on the
2nd of November, 20 yards of merino, value 41., the goods of Thomas
Braithwaite.
Thomas BRAITHWAITE. I live in High-street, Camden Town, and am
a linen draper. These twenty yards of merino are mine.
HENRY JOB. I am a watchmaker, and live opposite Mr. Braithwaite's.
About half-past eight o'clock on the evening of the 2nd of November, the
prisoner was pointed out to me--I saw him walk into Mr. Braithwaite's
shop-there was another man on the opposite side of the way, I watched,
and the prisoner came out with the merino under his arm-I ran over, and
got up to him as he got to the curb-he looked me in the face, dropped it,
and directly ran away-I cried, “Stop thief," and followed him as far as
King-street, where he was stopped by some person-I never lost sight of
him-he said, "What do you want with me?" I told him I had been
waiting for him half an hour-I had been watching him that time before he
went into the shop-I have no doubt about his being the man.
Cross-examined by Mr. Bookin. Q. What part of High-street was
this? A. Near Warren-street-it is a very broad road, and it was dark-
he was stopped about a hundred and fifty yards from where he dropped
the merino.
Thomas DURNHAM. I live next door to Mr. Braithwaite's. I saw the
prisoner drop the merino on the pavement- I picked it up.
