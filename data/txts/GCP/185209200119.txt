Q. Did he offer to give you anything? A. Yes; 501. that was between the
last two games, because I did not want to stop any longer- he wanted me to
play, and I said, “I have got no more money"-he said, "I will give you
501. if you want it"--we then played for a bottle of port wine-that bottle
was not finished; when we had done playing, there was some left-we gave it
away to some persons in the ground-I asked the prisoner for the money he had
of me, and he said, “I will give you that when we get home”- then we went
across to my friend's--I asked the prisoner several times for the money-he
said he would give it me wben we got home; and after I had asked him
several times, he said he would not give it me at all the prisoner went with
me to my friend's house, whose name is Innes, and it was there that he first
refused altogether to give me my money-Innes was present there were
two constables afterwards came, and I gave the prisoner in charge-Indes
sent for my sisters, and they sent for the policeman ; at least they sent for my
father, but he was not at home-the prisoner went into the yard at Innes,'
and I saw him try to get over the wall - that was before the constables came
- young man took hold of the prisoner, and prevented his getting over
the wall that young man is not here.
Court. Q. Did the prisoner hear the order given for the policeman
to be sent for? A. I cannot tell; I did not hear it myself-the prisoner
tried to get over the wall before the policemen came, and after they came
be had one leg over the wall.
MR. REED. Q. How was it you played with him after he took the 151,
from you? A. I did not think he meant to rob me; I thought he was a
gentleman-he did not take it from me with my consent-I told him I could
do as I liked with my money.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Your were unwilling he should
take it, but you let him bave it-you did not offer much resistance ? A. I told

market. I do not think I counted the money then, but I took the bag out
