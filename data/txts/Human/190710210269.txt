I not only paid the rent and the rent of the machine, but I paid the rent of Bleach's private bouse when the brokers were share. The total amount during the period we are dealing with, according to Bleach's statement, was £200, but I thought it was very much less. That worked out to somewhere between 15a. and a £1 a week. With reference to Sun Street, as Bleach wanted work, the original idea was that we should restart the "Financial Observer," of which I still own the copyright—not the "Financial Guardian." There was no object in my personally starting any other paper, as I was rather well-known in connection with the "Financial Observer." The next thing was where the offices should be. Bleach said he had had a convenient place offered to him in Sun Street, Finsbury, and he suggested taking it at £30 a year. I talked over the question of plant and machi  nery with Bleach, and he arranged to get both from a large firm who did this kind of business, and to have it on the hire system. I undertook to pay the rent of the offices and Bleach got the machinery and plant in. It was only adapted for newspaper work. Bleach did not get much work in, and went on for about a month or so, and then I abandoned the idea, saying that I did not want to be bothered over the paper any more. I told-Bleach that if he liked to keep on the printing works he could have the benefit of the amount that I had paid on the machinery. Bleach kept them on for a year as he stated. The alleged conversation that Bleach spoke to with re ference to some case pending in the Courts was absolutely untrue. Bleach has never spoken to me at any period anywhere about the matter in question. I was attending the Court on subpoena, and saw Bleach in the Court, but had never spoken to him in the Court or near the Court. I was appearing on the same side that Bleach was. There was no question whether I was Ablett or anybody else. The case was not heard, and there was no occasion for Bleach making the suggestion he did. There was an Ablett. I have seen Ablett. I have never posed as Ablett or represented myself to be Ablett, or been accused by anybody as being Ablett. Bleach has been repeatedly in the company of two policemen. I and my friends have seen him in public-houses. The whole tale is a myth. I have known Parkinson many years. He or his firm did work for me in connection with the "Finan cial Observer." I should call him an unintelligent witness. I think he has made a mistake when he says I have given him orders on ac count of these people. Probably ha is confusing in his mind the circumstance that at some time, for some months, or weeks, he had orders through me for work which has been paid for, and he is simply muddling what the orders were. I knew of the Lombard Financial Corporation, but I have never had the least to do with it, nor with the business of the "Financial Guardian," either under the name of Ablett or Martin or Wood. I had not the least to do with the business of "Tyler and Co." I did not know it existed.
