WILLIAM HOLLOWAY, I carry on business as a mantle manufacturer
under the style of Holloway and Sharpe in St. Paul's Churchyard-
there is one house intervening from Pawson's-Lawton has been in my
employ, I think, 20 years—I was not aware that he had a shop-I have
known Wicks 10 or 15 years, and during the last eight or 10 years I have
had transactions with him-he used to come once a week and clear away
the cuttings, what we call rags-I do not deal in lengths or pieces of
silk, or silk handkerchiefs or gloves-Lawton had no authority to sell or
dispose of any capes or mantles in my possession-I have sold him capes
and silks- I went to Wicks's house with the detective, and found capes,
Ulsters, and other things, and some at the warehouse-I have looked
them through, they belong to me, I never sold them to Lawton or to
any person ; most of them are this season's goods--the value of the
things found at Wicks's is about 441.-I have also seen the dolmans and
capes and other articles found at Lawton's; they are my property-
I cannot say positively whether I sold the capes to him, but the other
things were never sold to him by me or any one in my employ; I have
no entry in any of my books of the sale of these particular things to
Lawton or to Wicks. (MR. WADDY objected to any evidence of goods being
found unless they were found at Wicks's or in his possession, and had been
stolen. MR. WILLIS contended that as Lawton was charged with stealing, the
ciroumstances must be admissible, as it was incumbent on the proseoution to
prove the theft of Lawton before proving the receipt by Wicks with a guilty
knowledge. The RECORDER admitted the evidenca.) I have never sold
goods of this description to Wicks, my transactions with bim have
been confined to the clippings and outtings from the warehouse-
Lawton and Wicks were arrested on the evening of 6th September,
and I received a parcel from Mrs. Wicks the next evening, which I sent
in to Messrs. Pawson.
