1035. JAMES BARNES was indicted for embezzling and stealing, on
the 2nd of March, the sum of 6s. 4d., 4d., and 2s, 6d., which he had re-
ceived by virtue of his employment in his Majesty's Post Office-11
other Counts, varying the manner of laying the charge.
Messrs. SHEPHERD, ADOLPHUS, and SCARLETT conducted the prosecution.
WASTELL ARROWSMITH. I am a clerk in the Foreign Office of the Post
Office ; the prisoner was one of the Presidents of that office, and had the
control over the inferior officers. I received instructions respecting the
prisoner, which made my attention to him more exact, and my memory
more accurate-letters received at the receiving-houses, in different parts
of the town, for inland and foreign delivery, are made up in bags for the
evening, to be delivered at the Post Office--it often happens that Colonial
letters are put into the foreign instead of the inland bags (the etiam
dence given by the witness in the former case, page 1016, as to the routine of
business at the Post Office, was here read to him, which he confirmed)
-When letters come by mistake into the Foreign Office, they are sent
to the Inland Office, by a tunnel, with the money-the money ought
not to come into the hands of the President-he ought to send the
letters by a messenger to the bag-clerk--that is the usual course of
the office -- the prisoner had been in the habit of coming himself, in-
stead of sending a messenger, to get the money - I had been spoken
to to pay particular attention to Mr. Barnes on Wednesday evening,
the 2nd of March, I was on duty as window-clerk I came on duty
between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, and remained on duty all
day till half-past seven the prisoner came to me, about a quarter before
five o'clock, with three letters, which he said were to go into the Inland
Office, and it required 68. 4d. as the postage-he also read out the amounts of
the postage of each letter--one amounted to 1s. 2d. I cannot say where it
was for, but it was very peculiarly marked I san swear to the appears
