HENRY JACOBS. I am in the employ of John Hawkins, a grocer, of Halle
moon-passage, Whitechapel-this package and chest of tea and pepper are his
--they were safe at nine o'clock, when I locked up the warehouse and left it
secure-next morning, about a quarter to eight, I found the skylight open
and propped up-the things must have been taken that way.
Cross-examined. Q. Had your master been there before you? 4. Yes -
he has three or four porters.
JAMES CHAPMAN (policeman). I found a loop-hole open at four o'clock in
the morning, and a piece of rope lying on the stones--I had seen the premises
safe that night.
Cross-examined. Q. Did the prisoner appear to bave been drinking? 4. Yes.
Thomas KYLEY (police sergeant). I found this iron hook, and traced
marks of it all up the house to the skylight-it would fasten to the skylight,
and they could get up.
(The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY of Stealing only.
Aged 25.--Recommended to mercy by the Jury --Confined Twelve Months.
2141. JOSEPH WARD was indicted for embezzlement.
THOMAS TERTIUS FLATHER. I am an oilman, of Queen's-buildings,
Knightsbridge--the prisoner was in my service, to obtain orders and collect
money, which he should enter in a book, and pay to me or my foreman, who
would sign the book for each sum-this “ Joseph Ward" on these bills of Mrs.
Wherry are the prisoner's writing - they are for 11. 168. on 2nd Aug., 11. 178.
on 23rd Aug., and Il. 16s. on 13th Sept.- I received this bill from Mrs.
Mealing for 11. 38. 9fd., on 5th July--this "Received, J. Ward” is
the prisoner's writing there is no receipt of money from Mrs. Mealing
entered in the book that day-he did not pay me that or these of 158. Ojd.
and 88. 9fd. from Mrs. Mealing on the same day-here is in the book 10s.
instead of 188. 10d.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did not you have a very good cha-
racter with him? A. Yes-his brother made me no direct offer to pay the
money.
MARY ANN Wherry. I am the wife of James Wherry-I paid the prisoner
11. 78. 6d. and 17. 168. for Mr. Flather-he gave me this receipt.
MATILDA MEALING. I paid the prisoner 188. 10d. and 11. 3. 94d, for
Mr. Flather-these are the receipts he gave.
William Phelps (policeman, A 242). I took the prisoner, and told him
the charge-he said he was very sorry for it; he hoped Mr. Flather would
forgive him.
Cross-examined. Q. Did not be say he knew he had done wrong! A.
Yes, on the way to the station.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
