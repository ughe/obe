the letters—these other figures, " 48. 4d.," I believe are Mr. Kershaw's.

Cross-examined by Mr. Horry. Q. Can you recollect whether Thacker

JAMES BRODIE. I am a clerk in the Inland-office-I was in Mr. Kelly's
room on the 23rd of June, when Ringrose was brought in with bis letters-1
saw him produce his letters- I told up the postage of them-he placed them
out on one of the tables in Mr. Kelly's room-the total amount of unpaid
letters was 61. 138. 8d.- that was inclusive of newspapers, the total charge
against him-I could not say how many letters there were, without a memo-
randum, which I have not here-I did not take them down in detail-that
was aſterwards done by another officer-I have a memorandum here which
shows that the total number of letters, produced by Ringrose, was fifty-five,
and twenty-six newspapers--a calculation was afterwards made of how many
letters and newspapers would make 41. Os. 9d., and I told them over-I found
that thirty-two letters and twelve newspapers would make 41. 08. 9d. ; but ME.
Barnard made that out, and can speak better to it-the list was not made out
until after the examination at Bow-street.
