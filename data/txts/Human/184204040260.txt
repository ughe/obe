   Fraser. Q.  Did not I tell you, going along, that I had a half-crown tied up in the corner of my apron? and did not you say if I offered to touch it you would knock me on the knuckles? A.  You might have said so; I did not hear you—when I first took you you said you had not got a halfpenny about you but a silver sixpence—I knew, if you had the money, you would soon make away with it. MARY WALKER. I am a searcher at the station. I searched the two female prisoners—I found three half-sovereigns and sixpence in Thorn ton's boot—I found no money on Fraser.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was anybody present besides you? A.  No—I am quite sure I found the half-sovereigns in Thornton's boot—I do not know which boot it was—she undid them herself—she said it was her husband's money, that he was a chair-maker, in Cheshire-street, Beth nal-green, and he had given her the money that day. WILLIAM THORN. I am a policeman. I took Fraser—she was run ning away towards London as fast as she could run—I told her to come back, that she was charged with stealing the money from this old gentle man—she said she had not a farthing of money about her—when we got to the station she gave me half-a-crown and a halfpenny—11 s . 3 d  was found on Dust—the prosecutor was perfectly sober.
   Dust's Defence.  I am a carpenter and undertaker by trade. I have not driven a cab  above a month. I tried to get  into the police once, and but for a cut on my forehead I should have been admitted. The cab  be longs to the Eastern Counties Railway, and I had earned the money found on me that day. I do not ply in the street, but this being after time I did so. I have a witness to prove that the females and the prosecutor were together in the public-house, and that they got into the cab  with him.
   Fraser's Defence.  We were in the public-house by London-bridge about ten minutes to ten o'clock on Friday evening. I had 4 s .; we went and had some bread and cheese, a pint of beer, and a halfpenny orange; the prosecutor and another man was there; he called for a pot of beer, and asked Thornton to drink; she gave me some; he said, "Have you a mind for a ride?" she said to me, "Will you go,  Mary?" I said, "I don't mind;" he said, "Don't go in there, there is a friend of mine there." We got into the cab;  we had not been in five minutes before he said, "I did not know that two of you were coming, two is company, and three none." I got out and rode with the cabman; when we got a little way he knocked at the window, and asked the cabman  to get some thing to drink, and to get what he liked for himself; he went and fetched half-a-pint of rum, and when we got a little further we had a quartern of gin; when we got to New Cross he pulled out 7 s ., and said he wanted some rum and water; he asked me if I had got a penny.
