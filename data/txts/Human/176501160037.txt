I said, had you any engines? he said no, Mrs. Crab and he put it out: but he was surprized how they did it, there being so much of it. I then directed my discourse to Mrs. Crab. I went up into the garret, and found one of the casements open, and wondered what could be the cause of it: I went down again, to assist in putting the things to rights: I found a quantity of chips among the things in the bed: I could not help observing the behaviour of the prisoner, who seemed to behave more by way of diversion rather than otherwise: I took these chips up; some were burnt to cinders, and some half burnt: I began to suspect something wrong. When we had taken up all the pieces of bed furniture, and cleared away the water out of the room, I began to observe the wainscot, and found one part of it exceedingly burnt, more than the rest; I believe it was three parts through; I think I could have pushed my thumb through it: the door was pretty much burnt: Mrs. Crab said the middle of the bedstead turned up against that part that was much burnt, I said it was very odd which way fire could come between the bedstead and the wainscot; I am afraid fire has been put there, on some bad design. The prisoner immediately answered in a very surly manner, No, the middle was not in that place. Observing him so surly, I said no more, but thought to take another opportunity to talk to him, when working by ourselves. Then the next day, I said there was a great deal of mischief done below, it is very odd to me a great deal of smoke did not come up stairs: said he, there was a great deal, and I was obliged to open the casement, to let it out. I said, it is very odd you did not go down stairs: no, said he, I did not; but soon after Mrs. Crab called me down. I said, I would have gone down to have seen what was the matter; for you knew very well, that chimney never used to smoke, and there was but one fire, from whence you must conclude the smoke must come; and you must open the window and stay till you are called: said he, what do you think I would sit here to be stifled with smoke, and not open the window to let it out? there is a person below, able to take care of the house, and I thought I had no business with it. The next day I said, was you down stairs, James, when the fire was, before you was called? he said, yes, I was. What did you go down for? I went down to desire Mrs. Crab to save me a bason of tea, for he knew his master would not be back soon. What, said I, was this silly old woman, Mrs. Crab doing, when you went down stairs? he said, blowing the fire. I said, in what position was the bed? he said, turned up, as usual. How long did you stay below? he said, about four or five minutes, and he left her blowing the fire. How long might it be before you was called down, after that? he said, about twenty minutes. Was there a great deal of smoke came up? yes, a great deal; and the first thing he did when he went down, he said, was, he pulled the bedstead from the wainscot, and got a knife, and cut some of the bed-cords. and pulled the bed on the floor; then he got a pail of water, and threw into the middle of the bed. I asked him some time after, which way he supposed this could happen? his reply was, you are to understand, Mrs. Crab is blind on one eye, and I imagine she had gone too near the bed with a candle, and set the curtain on fire. Then I pressed the matter pretty hard upon him: he at first denied it very strongly, and resented it very much. At last he began to be very silent: then I was so too. On the afternoon, on the Saturday, I began again, in the mildest and softest terms, relative to the heinousness of the crime, and the punishment I thought due to such a person that could be guilty of doing such a thing; and that his master had told me he would have him before a magistrate: he seemed very much concerned, and tears came. I told him there was but one way to escape the danger, and that was to confess the fact, and beg pardon for it; and if he could not speak to his master himself, I would solicit for him. I pressed him with such like arguments, but to no purpose, till about 8 or 9 o'clock: he said, you know we have a strange cat in the house; said I, I know it: said he, that morning, I chanced to go into the back parlour, and there was nobody there: I saw this strange cat sitting under the stove, and I frighted her away, or pulled her away, and she ran into the bed: there was a red hot poker in the fire; I took that, and threw into the bed after her, and that was the way that it happened; and he left the poker there. I asked him the reason for leaving it, and he said he forgot it, and that was the way it happened. But James, said I, this red hot poker could not set the house in such a flame, in such a time; because when you saw the cat run away, you would naturally take the poker out again: he said, no; I did not take it out again.
