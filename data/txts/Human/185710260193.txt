I took the prisoner, took her back to the shop, and received a half crown from Mr. Putley—the 1 s . 4 d . change was found on the prisoner by the female searcher, who is not here. MARY HUNTINGTON. My father is a potato salesman, in the Borough. The prisoner came there on Saturday night, 29th Aug., a few minutes before 12 o'clock, for six pounds of potatoes, which came to 6 d .; she gave me a counterfeit shilling—I said, "Are you aware that this is bad?"—she said "No"—I said, "How many more have you?"—she said, "Very likely fifty"—I bent it in a detector, and put it in the farthing compartment of the till; and kept it there till I gave it to Baldry.
   Prisoner.  I deny all knowledge of it. Witness.  I am certain she is the person—I have never seen her before—there was no one else in the shop—there was a gas light—I called a policeman in, and asked him if he knew her—he said that he did not—I showed him the shilling, but did not wish to be severe, and let her go. JOSEPH BALDRY. ( Policeman, M  71). On 29th Aug., I was called by Miss Huntington, and saw the prisoner in the shop; she said that she was not aware the shilling was bad, and she was allowed to go; I received this shil ling from Miss Huntington on 2nd Sept. WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Mint. This half crown and shilling are both bad.
   Prisoner's Defence.  There was another in the till, and I am positive the gentleman who gave me that would not give me a bad one. NOT GUILTY.
 1131. JOHN WELCH  (35) and JOSEPH SEERS  (29), were indicted for a like offence.
   MESSRS. CLERK . and W. J. PAYNE . conducted the Prosecution. LYDIA EDWARDS. I am barmaid at the Coach and Horses, at Kew. On Friday, 22nd Sept., between 7 and 8 o'clock in the morning, Seers came for a half pint of gin; it came to 8 d .; he gave me a sovereign, which I put into a little box in the bureau drawer, where there was no other money—I gave him change, and he took the gin away in a bottle—Moore was present; about 8 o'clock, or a little after, he came again for a pint of 6 d . ale, and gave me a good sixpence—he was in the habit of using the house; on the day before I had seen him with Welch, they went into the tap room toge ther—they used the house to breakfast, and sometimes they came in the after noon too—on the morning of the 22nd, between 9 and 10 o'clock, Mrs. Huntley came to me with a sovereign in her hand, and in consequence of what she said, I went to a building of Mr. Ridley, a little further up the road, to try to find Seers, but he was not there—in the afternoon I saw him on an omnibus; I am quite sure he paid for that gin with a sovereign, and not with a half crown.
