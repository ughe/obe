   JOHN PETERS sworn.
 I was in the Fox, publick-house, when the evidence came in; the witnesses who have been examined were asking the prosecutor.
 Who asked the prosecutor? - Mr. Edger and Mr. Rodnor. Mr. Edger asked Crossley if he had seen him at the place where the riot was? He said no, nor any one whom he knew.
 Where did Edger mean? - At a place in Ropemakers-alley.
 That is not White's-alley? - White's-alley is a street which turns up in Ropemakers-alley.
 Dillon's house was not in Ropemakers-ally? - I do not know where Dillon's house was; that was the conversation between him and us.
 Do you know the house which is pulled down in White's-alley? - I cannot say I do.
 How came Edger to ask Crossley whether he had seen either of them in Ropemakers-alley? - I cannot say what was the reason of his asking that question; the prosecutor came into the ale-house, the prisoner's mother had sent for him; they were talking of this, how the prosecutor could be so base to take a man up and swear against him without being sure.
 Jury. Are you one of those who called Crossley a Papist? - No.
 Did your hear him abused and called a Papist? - I do not remember his being called a Papist; there were some high words arose but nothing of abuse.

LUKE HAND was indicted for that he, together with forty other persons and more, did, unlawfully, riotously, and tumultuously, assemble, on the 7th of June , to the disturbance of the public peace, and did begin to demolish and pull down the dwelling house of William Gerrard , against the form of the statute, &c.
   ELIZABETH COUCH sworn.
 My husband keeps horses and carts to let out; we live in Pear-Tree-street, in the Parish of  St. Luke's, almost opposite to Mr. Gerrard's. Upon the 7th of June, between ten and eleven o'clock, the mob came by our door; the first person I saw was the prisoner and a man in blue drummer's clothes; the prisoner had an ax over his left shoulder; the mob got very thick to Mr. Gerrard's door. Then the prisoner called shew a book, and a bible was put out; presently after I saw two pannels of the door broke with the thick part of the ax, I believe; I did not see him break any other part of the house; then the mob got in, and the first man I saw at the one-pair-of-stairs window was the prisoner at the bar; he leaned out of the window, waved his hand, and said buzza! all Popery! A very little after that I saw him bring a drawer of linen and books to the window, and he cried again buzza, all Popery. Our house is opposite; our yard adjoins Mr. Gerrard's house; they wanted to burn the things in our yard. I desired they would not, as there were wooden things in the yard. The furniture was thrown out at the windows, the windows were broke, and the wainscoting torn sadly by the side of the windows; and two of the middle pannels were broke. The prisoner, I believe, continued there three quarters of an hour; I did not see him come out but I saw him go down the street with something to burn, but what it was I do not know.
