 2431. MARGARET HUDSON was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of August , 1 earthenware pitcher, value 1 s  ., the goods of Phoebe M'Kenzie . MART BOOTH. I live with Mrs. Phoebe M'Kenzie. On the 7th of August I put a pitcher out, and I lost it. JOHN BAKER( police-constable K  217.) About six o'clock, on the 7th, the prisoner came to the station with this pitcher, and asked if I would take her into custody—she said she had stolen the pitcher that she had in her hand from an earthenware shop in the Mile End-road—I took her, and found it was right. GUILTY  . Aged 28.— Judgment Respited, to be passed to her parish.
 2432. MARY ANN SCULLY was indicted for stealing, on the   13th of July , 1 gown, value 8 s  .; the goods of Charlotte Ann Laughton : 1 handker chief, 1 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 towel, 3 d  .; the goods of John Laughton . CHARLOTTE ANN LAUGHTON. I live with my father, in Caroline-street—the prisoner lodged with us. On the 13th of July I missed a gown at ten minutes past twelve o'clock, and a handkerchief belonging to my father, John Laughton—I spoke to the prisoner—she said, "I am innocent"—I gave her in charge—this is the gown and handkerchief. WILLIAM CARPENTER. I live with my uncle, who is a pawnbroker. This gown and handkerchief were pawned by the prisoner on the 18th of July.
   Prisoner.  I will take my oath I never pledged them. Witness.  I have not the least doubt of her.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The prosecutrix said she had lost her gown, and no one took it but me; I said, if she had any suspicion, to take me; she did not till next morning. I am quite innocent of it. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months .
 2433. GEORGE STUEART was indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of Aug ., 1 half-crown; the money of John Rix and another, his masters. NATHANIEL RIX. I am in partnership with my brother, John Rix—the prisoner was in our service. In consequence of information I said to him, "Deliver up the money you have taken from the till"—he pulled out a half-crown and two shillings, and laid it on the desk, but denied having taken it from the till—I sent for his mother, and spoke to her—I think he was honest when we first had him—I am afraid he has got bad connexions. WILLIAM PARKER. I am in the prosecutor's employ. On the 2nd of Aug. I saw the prisoner walk behind the counter, and commence scraping up the paper—as he had no business there, I watched him narrowly, and saw him draw out the till—he left off scraping up the paper, and walked from behind the counter with his hand under his apron, as if he was putting something into his pocket—I called the attention of the clerk to it.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Had you been to the till? A.  Not for some time before—there had been customers in the shop. GEORGE WILLIAMS. I saw the prisoner open the till, then go a second time and open it, and put his hand in—he then shut it, and went to the other end of the shop.
