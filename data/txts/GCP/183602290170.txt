792. HENRY GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of Fe-
bruary, 1 silver spoon, value 208., the goods of James Johnston.--2nd
COUNT, stating it to be the goods of John Cooper and others.
JAMES JOHNSTON. I live in Raquet-court, and am a paper-hanger.
The prisoner is a stranger-I lost a silver spoon-the last time I sawit
it was on the dining-room table--Mr. John Cooper is trustee of my pro-
perty.
SARAH TUCKER. I am cook to Mr. Johnston. I know this silver
spoon is his--the prisoner is my cousin-I missed the spoon on Sunday:
morning-he had been to visit me on the Saturday.
Cross-examined by MR. PAILLIPS. Q. Had he been in the habit of
visiting you often ? A. Yes; I went out of the kitchen for three quarten
of an hour while he was there there was a great quantity of plate be
might have taken-he was taken up last Saturday night-from the time
he went away till he was taken I had no opportunity of seeing him-
did not see him after he was taken up there was none left about except
what was in the plate-chest-there were eight large table-spoons, eight
tea spoons, eight desert-spoons, and the silver sugar-tongs, all of which he
had an opportunity of taking--I left no one in the kitchen with him.
CHARLES SIMMONDS. I am a goldsmith, and live at No. 3, South
ampton-row, Russell-square. On Saturday evening last, the prisoner
came, and asked if I purchased old silver-I said, "Yes"-he asked what
I gave an ounce for it-I said it depended on what he had-he then said
he only had this spoon-I said it would not suit me, "How was it
broken?" he said it had been trodden on-I said it had been broken on
purposebe then said, could he not have it repaired- I said, "Yes, e
Monday morning"-he said could he not have it sooner-I said, "Yes,
in two hours" he said could it not be done in less I said, "Yes, in an
hour" -- he gave his name "Jones," and said he would call again for it 10
an bour- I then sent for the officer.
