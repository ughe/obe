WILIAM JOHN BATES (policeman, R 154). I was on duty at the Dept-
ford Dock-yard on the morning of 22nd Nov.-about half past 12 o'clock
in the morning I heard a knoeking at the doekyard gateI opened it, and
saw the witness, Mary Brady, thereishe gave me some information about
some men being stabbed-] saw a man standing there, bleeding-he was
about six or seven yards from the doekyard gate--there was a gas lamp
over the gate-it was not a dark night; it was dark in some parts where
there was no light, but there was a very strong light over the gate--in
consequenee of what Mary Brady told me, I ran down Doek-street, and as I
ran I saw some. persons turning the corner of Doek-street-I ran after them
-they were going at a very fast walking pace--I overtook them in Old
King-street-the prisoner was one of them-I have not found out who the
other man was-I took hold of them, and spoke to them-I cannot say
whether they understood me-I came back with two of them in eustody-]
saw three men altogether; one was some distance ahead of the others when
I came up to them-I laid hold of two, and the third, seeing me approach,
ran away-the two men I laid hold of, came back part of the way with me,
up Old King-street, about thirty yards-I was then assaulted by the
prisoner, and they got away-I have not the slightest doubt that the pri-
soner is one of the men I took-I never lost sight of him the whole time
when the two got away from me, one turned down Old King-street, and the
other ran up Doek-street, towards the Dock-yard gate-sergeant Smith was
on duty there, and he came out into the road, on my calling out to stop
him, and he stopped the prisoner in the road-I took him to the station-
