GEORGE ADAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of February , at St. Stephen, Coleman-street , 1 wooden window-sash, framed and glazed with glass, value 5l.; 2 wooden doors, with sashes glazed with glass, value 10s.; 1 wooden pilaster and cap, value 6s.; 9 piece of wooden quartering, value 9s.; 39 pieces of wooden linings. value 40s.; 8 wooden facias, value 4s.; 9 piece of wooden skirtings, value 5s.; 1 wooden door jam, value 1s.; 1 wooden door stile, value 1s.; 1 wooden door ground frame, value 1s.; 4 wooden shutters, value 10s.; 1 wooden partition, of the length of 26 feet, and breadth of 9 feet, value 3l.; 1 other wooden door, value 2s., and 2 other wooden partitions, value 10s., the goods of the Mayor, Commonalty, and Citizens of London , belonging to a certain house of theirs; and 1 leaden cistern, in a wooden frame, value 10s.; 1 copper ball-cock, value 1s.; 1 leaden service-box, value 2s.; 200lbs. weight of leaden pipe, value 20s., and 1 other leaden cistern, of 1000lbs. weight, value 5l., their property, and fixed to the said house; against the Statute,&c.
 2nd COUNT, for ripping, cutting, and breaking the said goods, with intent to steal.
 3rd COUNT. for stealing the said goods, not stating them to have been fixed to the said house.
 MESSRS. ALLEY and GURNEY conducted the prosecution.
   THOMAS HERDSFIELD. I am a City-officer. On Saturday, the 23rd of February, I went to No. 8, Pavement, Moorfields, about eight o'clock in the evening; I saw a van loaded with wainscoating and wood - I saw that the house looked very bare; I unloaded the van, and put the articles back into the house - I have since seen them fitted to various parts of the house; I found in the house Virgo, Jones, Lowe, and young Poole; I live in the Old Bailey - there were no houses pulling down in the Old Bailey at that time.
   EDWARD JONES. I drive a van. On a Thursday I carried some articles from this house, and on this Saturday night I had got some more, and was stopped by the officer - I carried some boards and fixtures; I did not see the prisoner at the Pavement - I had seen the prisoner at Mr. Poole's yard, in Whitecross-street, and he went with me from there to Mr. Wilkinson's, in Golden-lane; this was on Saturday morning - I carried to Mr. Wilkinson's some leaden pipes; I cannot tell who helped me to load the cart, but the prisoner was in the yard at Poole's, and he went with me from there to Wilkinson's; I had before carried those things to Poole's from the Pavement, but I did not see the prisoner there; I also took a cistern to Poole's, and I took that to the Green-yard - I cannot say on what night I took the cistern from the Pavement; I did not see the prisoner at the Pavement.
 COURT. Q. Had he given you any directions before you went to the Pavement? A. No; Virgo told me to take the things from the Pavement.
   PATRICK LOWE. I am a carpenter. I was employed at No. 8, Pavement; I do not know the day - I had been there at work for three or four days before the things were stopped; the prisoner employed me to take down some fixtures there - I took down an iron safe, some partitions, and other things; I took down a cistern, and the prisoner assisted me - I took down some lead pipes, but the prisoner was not there then; I took that down by direction of young Mr. Poole.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was not all this done in open day? A. Yes, there was no disguise about the prisoner; there were four persons at work there; these things were put into a van in the open day - there was no attempt to cover them. I remember the officer coming, and the prisoner demanded to know by what authority he came there - the prisoner said he had a right to the fixtures; he did not give me directions not to hurt the house, but I understood that was what he intended - I acted as I would in any case between landlord and tenant.
