On Monday evening I received information that the horse was stolen, and saw it in a field at Brentford - I saw Ward, and from his information apprehended the prisoner on Monday, at Uxbridge, about three miles from East's; I asked if he had received any money from Cox for a horse on Sunday - he denied it; I took him to Ward, who gave the account he has now, in his hearing.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going by Botwell on Friday, and saw John Smith; he asked me to go to Moore's, the jobber, at Brentford, and ask him if he wanted to buy a horse - I went, and he was not at home; I returned to Smith, and he asked me to come at six o'clock on Sunday morning, and bring the horse to Ward's house; I did so, and told him Smith had a horse to sell - he gave me 5s. for my trouble.
 JAMES COX. I did not see any John Smith about the house.
 JAMES SOUTH. I found 10s. 7d. on the prisoner, and asked what he had done with the rest of the money he had received from Cox - he said, "Go to my wife, and ask her for what I gave her last night;" I went, and she gave me two sovereigns - I said, "Is this all you received from your husband last night?" she said No, and then brought me two sovereigns and a half.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Life.

 1992.  JOHN BRIDLEY was indicted for stealing' on the 15th of August , 1 pair of shoes, value 4s., the goods of George Clarke ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
 GEORGE CLARKE. I am a  shoemaker, on Ludgate-hill. On the 15th of August, about half-past nine o'clock, while my back was turned, these shoes were taken.
   WILLIAM BROWN. I live in Whitmore-place, Hoxton, and am a shoemaker. I was in Mr. Clarke's shop on the 15th of August; he was busy - I saw the prisoner come in, and run off with a pair of shoes, which were two yards within the door; I pursued, and in Stationers'court secured him, and saw him throw them down - I held him, and brought them back.
   SAMUEL COLLIER. I live in Kirby-street, Hatton-garden, and am a professor of music. I was at the top of Stationers'-court; the prisoner ran up the court, and Brown after him - I saw him throw down the shoes, and picked them up; he was taken, and I followed him into the shop with them.
   MICHAEL LYON. The shoes were delivered to me, with the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   LEONARD COX. I am a Policeman. I have a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, and know him to be the man - I was present at the time - (read.)
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Life.
 1993.  JAMES BURKE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 4s. 6d., the goods of Timothy Baly Roe , from his person.
   TIMOTHY BALY ROE. I am clerk to Messrs. Helps and Co., of Cheapside. On the 12th of July, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, I was in Holborn, opposite Brooke-street, and felt something at my pocket - I turned round immediately, and saw my handkerchief under the prisoner's jacket; I saw nobody with him - I attempted to seize him, but missed my hold, and he dropped the handkerchief - he got out of my sight, but the officer pursued him - I took up my handkerchief.
   JAMES SLOGDEN. I am a Policeman. I saw the prisoner's hand in Mr. Roe's pocket - I had seen him attempt a gentleman's pocket before, and watched him; a person came in between us, and I did not see him take Mr. Roe's handkerchief out - Mr. Roe turned round to catch hold of him; he ran away; I pursued, and overtook him in Brooke-street; he said he was going to school, and not to pick pockets; but I had seen him attempt more than one pocket before - I found nothing on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not take it.
 GUILTY. Aged 9.
 Recommended to Mercy. -    Judgment Respited.
 1994.  THOMAS GOODMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of William Aldridge , from his person.
 WILLIAM ALDRIDGE. I am a  coachman, and live in Mount-street. On the 28th of August, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, I was in Long-lane, Smithfield; I did not feel my handkerchief taken till I received information - it was produced to me about two minutes afterwards, when the prisoner was secured.
   WILLIAM COLTON. I am a broker, and was in Longalley. I observed the prisoner and another following Mr. Aldridge, who had a female with him, and near the end of Long-lane I saw the other take the handkerchief out of Aldridge's pocket, and pass it behind to the prisoner, who went into a shop - I stopped Aldridge, and told him; Cadby went in, and took the prisoner; the other ran away - I took Aldridge to the shop where the prisoner was.
 JOSEPH CADBY. I am a constable. I was in Longlane, with Colton; the prisoner, and another followed the prosecutor; they made several attempts; at last the other got the handkerchief out, and gave it to the prisoner, who went into a shop - I went, and took him; I found the handkerchief under his coat; I do not think I should know the other.(Property produced and sworn
 Prisoner. I hope the prosecutor will recommend me to mercy.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Life.
