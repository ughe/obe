   SAMUEL HERCULES TAUNTON. I am an officer of Bow-street. On the 7th of May I apprehended Conway at No. 96.  DEAR GEORGE - I cannot go with you over the water, having work to do, which will detain me all the morning; go to the fakement and obtain the leaf, then it will be my ingenuity that will settle the slum and put the bustle in our clyes. I am, with every feeling of gratitude, your's faithfully, and truly for ever.
   B. M.  CAREW.
 Have pluck; when done bring the leaf to my house - will be at home all the morning. Destroy this. B. W. HORNE.
 To George Coombs, care of Miss Smith.
   FRANCES SMITH. I go by the name of Frances Smith, but it is not my right name; I lived at No. 7, William-street, Waterloo-road, for a fortnight, about three weeks ago - I am acquainted with Coombs; I remember his coming to my lodging one afternoon in company with Martelly and Conway - the prisoners are the persons; they came in a coach - they had a bottle of wine there: Coombs gave me some money - he had a few pounds in sovereigns; I do not remember exactly what he gave me - I was in debt for rent at that time; Coombs paid it in Henry-street- I saw him pay it; it was 2l. 16s. - he paid it when I lived in William-street; he paid it on the Monday - he had come with them on the Saturday; he purchased me a dress on the Monday - I do not recollect how much he gave me on the Saturday; it was silver - I do not know who paid for the wine; I cannot recollect how long they remained at my lodging.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You had never seen the persons who were with Coombs before? A. No; I see a great many young men - I have been intimate with Coombs since last Vauxhall season; he was not particularly a sweetheart - he used to come to see me; I live by receiving visits from young men.
 BENJAMIN WORTHY HORNE. I act as a coach proprietor, at Charing-cross. I keep an account at Drummond's, and draw cheques on them (looking at the cheque) - the signature to this is not my hand-writing, nor is any part of the body of it.
 Q. Here is a letter with "  B. W.  Horne" on it, is that in your hand-writing? A. Not any part of it; the signature certainly bears a great resemblance to my writing, more particularly the one in the cheque.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Is yours a joint account? A. It is a family account; it is the account of my father's executors - I am not an executor, but have the management of the money: it is deposited there in my name alone - I am responsible for it, and draw cheques for it.
 T. A. RUSH. Mr. Hoper, of Old Burlington-street, keeps an account at Drummonds' - I do not remember seeing either of the prisoners before I saw them at Bow-street.
   JAMES WOOD. I am a coachmaker, and live in Long-acre. The endorsement on the back of this cheque is not my hand-writing, nor done by any person authorized by me - I never saw it till I was examined at Bow-street.
   JOHN WILLIAMS, I live in Cornhill. The prisoner, Martelly, was in my employ - I knew him by the name of Edward Gage; he came into my employ in October, 1826, as town traveller - in June 1827 I proposed to add him to several country travellers; it was necessary he should produce me security for 200l. - he produced to me this paper, purporting to be a security; he continued in my employ till the following August - his last letter was dated August, 1827: I did not dismiss him - I heard no more of him after he was at Leeds in August, and saw nothing of him from that time to this; part of his duty was to collect money for me, and for that reason the security was given.(The cheque was here read.)
