Q. That you will swear? A. Yes; Mr. Bailey and Mr. Wilkinson did not indict either of them for robbery- Mr. Wilkinson never applied at all, to my knowledge, for payment for his pianoforte; he, Mr. Bailey, and all came together - there were twelve or fourteen of them and their men, but I was not there; I did not take any steps - Mr. Bailey sent in a bill for 400l.; the painoforte was about 70l. I sent to Mr. Bailey, about three weeks before, that I should give him the security of a gentleman in the Lord Chamberlain's-office - I owed Mr. Plasket, the wine-merchant, 30l., Mr. Goodey, a tailor, 16l., but he owes me a little, I had some child's clothes from Mr. Lock, another tailor; I owe Mr. Whiteley, an ironmonger of Holborn, 7l.; I offered Mr. Bailey security; I told him if he was not satisfied with a gentleman he was acquainted with not to serve me, not to send any thing, and when he sent me about 20l. worth I said, "Go to the Lord Chamberlain's-office, and ask your friend again;" then he sent them - I do not know whether he took the goods away; they are not in the house now - James Rivett is the person in the Chamberlain's-office; he holds a berth there now: he gives his name on the card, and I believe he is there to-day; I do not know that he became security - I owe Mr. Foster, a linen-draper of Oxford-street, 8l. or 9l., and Mr. Blaket, of Regent-street, 8l. or 9l., Mr. Mortlock, a chinaman, of Regent-street, 40l. - I believe Mr. Williams, a silk-mercer, claims 4l.; I was to pay 160 guineas a year rent for the house in Clifford-street, besides taxes; I was to do all the repairs before I had the lease.
 Q. Tell me the name of the attorney you went to after Mr. Richards robbed you? A. I mentioned it to Mr. Reynolds, of Clement-street, Kingsland-road, and to his clerk: I never saw Richard Miller, who is now a Policeman, letter C. No. 50 - I came out of the Marshalsea more than twelve months ago; I do not remember after that being in a public-house in Fleet-lane with  Richard Miller and some women - I might be in Fleet lane; I remember a man, named Biggers, in Fleet-lane; I do not know a woman named Cann - I have heard of a woman named Jenkins; I was many times in Fleet-lane, next door to Biggers', at a public-house.
 Q. Were you drinking in company with two women and a man there, Mrs. Jenkins being one of them? A. Very likely - I am sure I was; I have no doubt but I told a man there that I had been just discharged from the Marshalsea.
 Q. The man I allude to is Richard Miller - did you ever tell him you were acting with others in getting goods and selling them? A. Never - that I am sure of; (looking at Miller) I do not recollect him - I never recollect seeing him; I might be drinking with him at a public-house in Fleet-lane - I was not a gentleman then.
 Q. On your solemn oath, did you never tell a man that you were employed acting with others in getting goods and selling them, and did you not propose to Miller to join you? A. No, I did not; if I did, I must be completely in liquor, and lost - I did not propose that Mrs. Cann's husband should give the references, and that I and Mrs. Jenkins should sell the goods; I swear that positively - nor that we should share the money; it is totally untrue - nothing to that effect passed; if you could bring half a dozen to swear it, I would still deny it - if true, I would admit it.
 Q. What took you to drink with a Policeman? A. If he came into the parlour to ask me a question, I do not know, he might take advantage and ask me a question, for some motive planned by other parties; it was Mrs. Rivett I took to Mr. Richards' - she is the wife of Mr. Rivett, who is in the Lord Chamberlain's office.
 Q. What is he in the Lord Chamberlain's office? is not be a trumpeter? A. Yes, he is, and I will tell you what he is - he attends before His Majesty at the coronation; and he collects the Peers' fees; he lives at No. 12, Belmont-terrace; I believe I took but one lady to Richards' - the other lady whom I believed that I took, lis  Mary Wright; she is a widow - I do not know where she lives; she never went to Richards' with me - I picked her up in the Borough; she sometimes came to my place in Clifford-street - I think I first met her in the London-road. in the street - I had known her a good many years in Bishopsgate-street, at No. 108 or 109; she lived with me there as servant twenty years ago.
 Q. Will you swear you never went with Mary Wright to other tradesmen's houses, disguised as a lady? A. I never did - I never took her to tradesmen's houses, nor any other woman; I have a board up to sell the house in Clifford-street; I am now living in Stingo-lane, if I please - I have an objection to tell where I sleep every night; I slept in Lisson-grove last night - I slept the night before at Mountford-place, Kennington, and I slept there on Sunday, Saturday and Friday, at No. 1; my son keeps the house - my address is in Clifford-street.
     NOT GUILTY.
 NEW COURT. SATURDAY, JANUARY 5.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 501.  WILLIAM JUDE was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of November , 40 lbs. of potatoes, value 18d., the goods of John Walpole.
 JOHN WALPOLE. I live at  Enfield. On the 2nd of November I had a quantity of potatoes laying in one of my fields; I got a warrant to apprehend the prisoner, but could not get him till one day last week.
   THOMAS EDWARDS. I am in the prosecutor's employ. On the 2nd of November he employed me to look after his potatoes; they were harrowed up, and were to be picked up by the women the next morning - I saw the prisoner in the field; he came out about half-past ten o'clock; a person named Walden was with him - the prisoner had a bag on his shoulder; I took Walden at the time, but the prisoner got away - there appeared to be as much in the prisoner's bag as there was in Walden's, and that had a bushel of potatoes in it; the prisoner's bag seemed to have potatoes in it - he went off as soon as he saw me run.
 Prisoner. I did not run. Witness. Yes, he did, directly he saw me.
   WILLIAM ARNOLD, I was in the field to keep the birds off. I saw the prisoner and Walden; the prisoner picked up potatoes, put them into a bag, and put it on his shoulder - he jumped over the hedge when he saw me coming; I told Edwards - he went after him, and the prisoner ran away.
