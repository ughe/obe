OLD COURT.--Monday and Tuesday, April 25th and 26th, 1904.
Before Mr. Justice Darling.
386. ERIC GORDON MAOCRAE SHORT (45) Unlawfully causing
and inducing William Jeffery to affix his name upon a banker's cheque
for the payment of £375, in order that the same might be afterwards
used as a valuable security, with intent to defraud; also obtaining credit
to the extent of £5,000 from Emerson Bainbridge by false pretences.
MR. MUIR and MR. J. H, MURPHY Proseouted.'
FREDERICK WILLIAM Crown. I am an officer in the employment of
the Clerk of Assize for the South Eastern Circuit, and produce from his
custody depositions, exhibits, and other documents relating to this case
--they came into his possession upon a committal from the Hastinga
Petty Sessions.
REGINALD Kelsey. I am a solicitor, and formerly practised at Kast-
bourne for eighteen years and at Bexhill for two or three years first
made the prisoner's acquaintance on May 9th, 1901, when he called at
my office at Bexhill, and said he was in very low water, waiting for some
pupils to come to him, and could I lend him £5, which I did I understood
the pupils were boye-he said he was living in Wilton Road, Bexhill-
after that I several times lent him sums of £0-about the beginning of
June he told me that he was expecting a very large sum of money to be
left him on the death of a Mr. Baring the day that he mentioned that
he got some money from me-he also said that the pupils who had been
coming to him had got measles-he told me that Mr. Baring was a man
of immense property, getting on for £4,000,000; that his wife was to receive
a very large sum, and so was he; that he was Mr. Baring's grand-nephew
and that Mr. Raring's Christian name was George Tasked him if Mr. Baring
was anything to do with Baring Bros., and he said no, he was not a
member of that firm at all, but that his Mr. Baring was half brother to
Lord Revelstoke-he said that owing to his Mr. Baring having drawn
£800,000 from the firm of Baring Bros, it had practically brought about
the smash in that firm-he said his Mr. Baring was then ninety-two years
old ; that he was suffering from canoer in the throat-he said he was
coming into Mr. Baring's money under his will; that one of the trustees
was a Mr. Pollock, and that his wife, Mrs. Short, was the executrix -
the prisoner stated that Mr. Baring's health was so bad, and he was NO
imbecile, that he could not alter his will-he said he had seen Mr. Baring,
but had never spoken to him I do not remember if he said how often
he had seen him-ho said that three firms of solicitors had been down
