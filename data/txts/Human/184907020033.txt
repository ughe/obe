 1453. JAMES BASSETT , stealing 1 tea-pot and other goods, value 25 l  .; the goods of Isaac Jacobson , his master. ISAAC JACOBSON. I live at 322, Oxford-street On 18th June I had a bill up in my window that I wanted a person to attend to my horse—the prisoner presented himself as a person fit for it—I said I thought he was not—he said, "Will you take me on trial one day?"—I did so—a gentleman came, and brought me a tea-pot and a caddy, and another person brought a mustard-pot to be polished—I took them to the silversmith's—next evening I sent the prisoner for them—he brought word that they would be done by ten o'clock in the morning—next morning the prisoner went for them, and did not return.
   Prisoner.  I was stopped in the street by a man, and delivered them up to him. Witness.  I did not authorize him to deliver them to any man in the street. SAMUEL WHITFORD. These articles were sent to me—the prisoner came about eleven o'clock the next morning, and took them away. WILLIAM CAUGHT( policeman, C  140). I took the prisoner—I said I wanted him concerning that silver he stole from Mr. Jacobson—he said, "You have got me to rights  this time." GUILTY  . Aged 16.— Judgment respited.
