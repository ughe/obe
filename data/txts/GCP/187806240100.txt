644. CHARLES MISSELBROOK (41) was indicted for and charged
on the Coroner's Inquisition with the manslaughter of George
Misselbrook.
It appeared from the opening of MR. MEAD for the prosecution that the
Prisoner was one of the "Peculiar People," and the deceased was his child, who
died without any medical aid being afforded during his illness. The evidence
of the surgeon would only go to this extent, that in his opinion if medical assist-
ance had been called in the child might reasonably have been expected to recover.
MR. JUSTICE MANISTY was of opinion that such evidence would be far too
speculative and doubtful to sustain a conviction, and directed the Jury to find a
verdict of

MICHAEL FINN. I live at Rotherhithe-on Friday, 17th May, about
10.30 p.m., I was with other boys near Mill Pond Bridge and saw the
prisoner on the bridge with a baby on her right arm- she took it by the
arms and legs and threw it over into the water the wall is about 4j feet
high-before I could say " Look!” to my mate she fell on the ground
and said " Oh, my child is drowned !” she did not drop it accidentally,
she flung it in- I pulled off my coat and was going to jump over, but
Shepherd rushed into the water and saved it and handed it to Thomas
Smith-the prisoner then ran from the high wall to the low wall and
tried to throw herself in over the wall, but some men pulled her back.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. The child did not fall in as you werd
letting him lean over-you never leaned him on the wall at all.
WILLIAM SHEPHERD. I live at George Row, Bermondsey-on 17th

the wall of the bridge into the water, fetched & child out of the water,
and handed it to Thomas Smith-the water is about 4ft. 6in. deep when
it is full, and the wall is about the same height.
