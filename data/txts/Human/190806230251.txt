STERN. minister of the East London Synagegue, Stepney Green; and Dr. WALTER TURNER, of Bal ham, were called on behalf of Hirst as witnesses to character. JAMES BEARD, recalled. In June, 1906. I was walking down the Mile End Road and saw Calcutt standing outside the "Three Crowns" with a horse and trap. I went up and said to him, "Whose lot is this?" He said "It belongs to me. I have taken it for a debt from a friend at Southend. Will you buy it?" I said, "What do you want for it? He said, "I will take £45 from you." I said, "You know I have just sold mine off that cost me nearly £100 for half the money; I don't want it." Then he said, "Do you think Mr. Hirst would buy it?" I said, "You had better ask him." He said, "Well. I think I ought to get a bit more off of him." I said, "You must do the best you can." That ended the conversation. I was present at the Public Inquiry when Calcutt gave his account of the sale of this pony and governess car to Hirst. I understood Mr. Robb's suggestion to be that Calcutt had bought this horse especially for Hirst, the car in one place, the horse in another, and the harness in another. I got up and said, "That is a lie; Calcutt offered it to me for £45." I re member the election of Guardians in 1904, but I do not remember anything about an election in 1907. It is very likely that I should be in the "Three Crowns" on the night of the election, but I know nothing at all of any conversation between Hirst and Calcutt about giving 5s. to some men.
 Cross-examined. I do not remember everything that takes place at a public-house on the night of an election. I should certainly be working hard for the Association. I go into Hirst's house very often; I should never pass the house without popping in to see him. I was with Hirst when he was met at the station by Calcutt's man and the trap. He had come from Herne Bay. We drove away together to Hirst's house. We went in, then Hirst came out and went off in the trap to Leytonstone. I believe he paid £46 for the car; I should think the harness was worth about £5, and the cob about £15. At the Inquiry Calcutt was screening Hirst, giving evidence in his favour.
