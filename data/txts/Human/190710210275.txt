To the best of my belief the majority of this was Harris's stock. When I took the "C. W. P." offices I was negotiating two companies. This charge has had something to do with their not having been brought out, but the companies also dragged because the markets were bad. Mrs. Strick was my clerk from 1886 to 1896, and left me to get married. An extraordinary thing is that I met her outside policeman Ottaway's premises, in Old Jewry, about a fort  night before the trial began, and she said, "I have been to the police." It is not true that I ever asked her to take in letters in the name of Ablett. Tyler told me he had found suitable offices at 79 1/2, Gracechurch Street, but Smiles who was acting for the landlord, would not let without a guarantee, which I gave. There was some trouble about the superior landlord, and I demanded the guarantee back, but did not get it. After that Tyler went to 16 and 17, Great St. Helens, and was joined by Gibbs. Gibbs had had 30 years ex perience on the Stock Exchange in Industrial shares. Grosvenor, a friend of Gibbs, was going to assist in the business. I had nothing to do with the taking of the offices, and was never there while Gibbs was there. Mr. Muir's statement in his opening speech that I guaranteed the rent is absolutely untrue. In December, 1906, Tyler came to me at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings, and told me Gibbs was leav ing and going back to the Stock Exchange. Tyler also said he had established the business, and thought it would be a prosperous one, and asked if I would help him, He wanted to borrow a little money, and he also wanted shares for the purpose of dealing in. I lent him £30, and subsequently another £30 for his rent. With reference to the circular of Messrs. Pope and Westlake, I then owned, and still own, two founders' shares in the Fullers Earth Union. I knew If there was a willing buyer if I could find a willing seller I could make a profit between the two, and for the purpose of finding a seller I selected Tyler as intermediary, as I thought it was the class of busi ness he required. As a result he purchased Lord Kilmorey's share for £50 and I gave him £55 for it, and resold it to Pope and West-lake by arrangement for £89.
