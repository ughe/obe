I produce the book in which is recorded the check-books issued to customers—I find that the number of the check-book issued to Lawes was 5676—the entry is in my own writing. JAMES BECK. I am a clerk in the Gravesend Bank, and have been so five or six years. During that time no such person as Charles Fraser has kept any account at our bank—there was no money to the credit of such a name OD  4th or 11th Sept. last, nor at any time.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You do receive money paid in by strangers? A.  Yes and pay those sums in London through the Commercial bank. JOHN HARVEY( policeman , G 14). I apprehended Lawes in Milk-street, on 22d Sept., about half-past two o'clock—I told him the charge, and said be must go to Clerkenwell police-court with me about Smith's business respecting a check—he said he would be dif he would—he said be gave
 Smith the check—he then said that Fraser signed the check, and he ought to  go  as well—I asked where Fraser was—he said on the Exchange, and I went with him to the Exchange—I there found Fraser—I told him I wanted him to go to Clerkenwell police-station—he wanted to know what for—I told him about a check—Lawes then said, "You know you signed your name to that check"—he said, "Yes"—I believe Smith's name was mentioned by a person named Hall—Fraser said if I would wait half an hour he would go, when he had finished his business—I then took him and Lawes in a cab  to the station—a person named Hall was also in the cab , he represented himself as being an attorney in Moorgate-street—I did not take him into custody, he went of his own accord—as they were going along Lawes said to Fraser, "Charlie, if the Magistrate should ask you why you gave a check on the Gravesend Bank, tell him you expected the money to be paid in immediately, "and Fraser said" Yes"—I examined Lawes at the station, and found on him this pocket-book, containing these two blank checks (numbered 5676), a 10 l .-note, and a quantity of papers—I also searched Fraser, and found on him the papers marked" A "and" B"—I was before the Magistrate when Collins was being examined ( looking at the deposition )—this is Mr. Combe's writing ( read  —" The prisoner Fraser voluntarily states : The signature to the check is in my handwriting; only the signature is in my handwriting; I do not know any thing about the rest of the handwriting ")—I heard that stated—Lawes after wards made a statement—he said, "I believe all the handwriting on the check to be in Fraser's handwriting; none of it is mine"—Fraser said, "Most probably I signed the check at Garraway's; I have had transactions of this kind before, but such transactions were always met, and if there is anything wrong, come to me on Monday at twelve o'clock on the Exchange and it shall be all right"—Inspector Brannan is not here; he is ill of the cholera.
   Cross-examined. Q.  It was a 10 l . Bank of England note you found on Lawes? A.  Yes; what he said to Fraser in the cab  was nearly at the end of our ride—there had been a good deal of conversation—I think what Lawes said was, "You know you signed your name to that check"—I could not say positively that it was not," Did you sign your name to that check?"—I do not know which it was—both charges were not investigated on the first day—I am speaking of the 22nd—Smith had been taken on the night pre vious—the first charge was Mr. Mills's—the other check was not then produced—they were remanded, and Mr. Cuthbertson's check was brought forward on the second examination.
