 213.  GEORGE BEST was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of October , 2 books, value 14s., the goods of John Pearman.
   JAMES MUNTING. I keep a book-shop in the New-cut. On the 27th of October the prisoner came to sell two books for 2s. 6d.; I asked if they belonged to him - he said Yes, and that he lived at Kennington; I offered him 1s. 6d., and sent Nathan with him, to see if he lived there.
   NATHAN NATHAN. I went with the prisoner, and when we got three quarters of a mile, I asked if he lived at Queen's-row, Kennington; he said he had lived there three months ago - he at last ran away; I overtook him and gave him in custody.
   ALICE STACEY. I am pew-opener at Tavistock chapel, Drury-lane. On the 27th of October I saw a strange man go into Mr. Pearman's pew - it was not the prisoner; but he is something like a man who gave me a note that night for a gentlemen, who, he said, would come to take a sitting.
   JOHN PEARMAN. My family attend Tavistock chapel. I left these books there on the 23rd of October; they are mine.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he was a book-hawker, and had taken the books in exchange for others.
     NOT GUILTY.

 214.  EDWARD ANDERSON was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of October , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of William Rultish Manger , from his pocket.
   WILLIAM RULTISH MANGER. I am a town-traveller, and live in Salter-street, Cannon-street-road. On the 25th of October, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, I was about half-way down Fleet-street, on the pavement, and felt my handkerchief go from my coat pocket; I put my hand down, turned round immediately, and saw it laying on the ground, and the prisoner in the officer's hands - I had used it as I came through Temple-bar.
   THOMAS LIGHTFOOT. I am a street-keeper. I watched the prisoner for some time, going up and down Fleet-street, with another; they followed another gentleman, who went into Serjeant's-inn - they then followed Mr. Manger; I saw the prisoner put his hand into Mr. Manger's pocket, and draw the handkerchief out - I took hold of the end of it in his hand, as he was handing it over to his companion; part of it went on the ground - his companion ran away; I should know him again - he was there ready to receive it; I did not see Mr. Manger at the moment, but I called out; he turned round, and claimed the handkerchief - the other was a youth, stouter and taller than himself; the prisoner said he had no parents, and did it for want - he had no money.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going by, the other boy took it out, and chucked it on my shoulder.
     GUILTY. Aged 17 - Transported for Life.
 215.  JOHN GURREN was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of December , 1 box, value 6d.; 1 shawl, value 6d.; 2 frocks, value 6d.; 4 pinafores, value 4s.; 1 pair of shoes value 2s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s.; 1 pair of stockings, value 6d.; 1 tippet, value 6d., and 1 1/2 yard of ribbon, value 6d., the goods of Edmond Bird.
 EDMOND BIRD. I  keep the George, West-street, Smithfield. Last Thursday night, about six o'clock, a bandbox, containing these articles was put into a butcher's cart at my door, for my nephew to take with my daughter to South-street, Park-lane - the cart had not left the door ten minutes before I heard the box was stolen; my nephew is not here - he lives in the country; the prisoner was brought to my house in about half an hour, with the box, in the same state as before.
   WILLIAM BUSHNELL. I am a basket-maker, and live at the Crown, in West-street, Bird sent for me to mind the cart, which stood at his door; I saw his little girl and the box put in - there was a tail to the cart; it left the door a little after six o'clock - I went into the tap-room to finish a pint of beer, and heard the cart come back very quick Bird's nephew said the box was gone - Bird called me; I went out, and found the prisoner in Black Boy-alley, West-street, with another person, who had got the box; I passed them, and then returned back quick - they both made a full stop together; I said that was the box I was looking for, that it belonged to Mr. Bird - the prisoner then laid hold of it; I stopped and said, "I must have it" - the prisoner said, "No, let it be," and "I will put you in it;" it is a lonely place - I did not like to stop him; I took it from them, and told the prisoner I would not say who had possession of it; he gave it to me with great reluctance - the other never spoke; Bird insisted on knowing who I got it from, and I told him - I knew him before for some years; he jobbed for people.
   THOMAS STALIAN. I am a patrol of St. Sepulchre's. I apprehended the prisoner about half-past seven o'clock on Thursday evening, in King-street, Snow-hill; he had another person with him - I said I had information about him; he said I was deceived in my information - I said if so it would be better for him; I had received information of the cart being robbed, but all I told him was, that I had received information about him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming from my uncle's about half-past six o'clock, and met this young man in the alley, with the box; I knew him, and asked how he did - the witness came up, and took the box out of his hand.
     GUILTY. Aged 21 - Transported for Seven Years.
