 1280. JOHN HARRISON was indicted for a misdemeanor. JONATHAN PEARSON. I am an ironmonger, in partnership with Thomas and John Pearson. I deal with Mr. Griffiths—on the 26th of March the prisoner came and said he wanted one of the best bottle-jacks—I got him one to look at—he asked the price, and said it was for Mr. Griffiths, of No. 2, Burton-crescent—he said he would have that one, and asked to see a lamp which was in the window—he said it was just the sort of article Mr. Griffiths had been speaking about—I wished to send it home, but he said he would take them, and said, "That is an excellent guard you had put up in Mr. Griffiths's winery"—I let him have the articles, believing he came from Mr. Griffiths—I had some recollection of having seen him before.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  What pas the value of the bottle-jack? A.  14 s ., and the lamp 7 s . 6 d . THOMAS PEARSON. On the 26th of March, after the prisoner had been with my brother, he came and said he wanted the lamp he had just looked at—I said, "Which do you mean?"—he pointed to one on the counter, and said it was for Mr. Griffiths—I said, "You had better let us send it"—he said, "No, you had better let me take it," and I gave it to him—between half-past four and five o'clock he came again for a dozen table knives and forks, he said Mr. Griffiths had a party that day, and they were all in a bustle—I asked what sort be would have—he said what Mr. Griffiths had were balance ones—he selected a dozen at 36 s ., and took them away with him—in two hours suspicions were raised, and I went to see Mr. Griffiths—I parted with the goods, believing he came from Mr. Griffiths—he also said to me, "That was an excellent guard you put up a twelve-month ago."
