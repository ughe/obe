MR. WILLIS submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury, repeating
his argument of yesterday. The prisoner had properly paid in the amounts
to the prosecutors' credit at. Martin's, roho had credited them to the prose-
cutors, and the prisoner had not diverted them from their proper course, nor
had he drawn out any specific items answering to the sums in the Indictment.
What he had done was to draro cheques which might not have been honoured
if the prosecutors had known all the facts, but that was not larceny or
embezzlement, and he therefore ought to be acquitted, and not put to the
expense of having a question argued in the Court of Criminal Appeal which
had been already decided there. (See Reg. o. Prince, 1 Luro Reports, p. 150.)
Mr. Grain contended that there was no analogy between this case and that
of Prince ; it was not necessary to prove that the prisoner had stolen the
thole of the money; a man could be indicted for stealing 1507, and convicted
of stealing 11. The prisoner putting his name on the yellow credit slips soas
equivalent to putting his hand into the till, and he therefore committed
larceny by a trick, and the actual taking was when he made the false entries.
MR. WILLIS having been heard in reply, the COMMON SERJEANT considered
that there was no case to go to the Jury.
