MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I do not know whether you know Moss's house?
A. Yes, I should say it was about a mile and a half from the Royal Ex-
change, and perhaps two miles and a half from Charing Cross-it is not a
mile, I think, from Charing Cross to St. Paul's Church-yard-it may be
three miles from Charing Cross to Moss's house--I should say it is a
quarter of a mile beyond Whitechapel church-I should say it is two miles
and a half from Charing Cross to Whitechapel church-that is my calcula-
tion- I will not swear that it is not three miles and a half.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. You speak of distance to the best of your judg.
ment and as to time? A. Yes do not say whether old Caspar got out
at Charing Cross or in the Strand—I should think it would take a cab
about twenty-five minutes to go from Charing Cross to New-street.
Q. Supposing a man to be standing at or near two of the furnaces, could
he see a person standing by the wainscot of the shop? A. Certainly, he
could see into the street- I went on purpose to try if it was possible, as I
thought it would be a necessary question as the plan was first marked you
could not see the furnaces at all from the door, but they can be seen from
the street--the brick work can.
COURT. Q. You could see a man standing at the wainscot from the
furnaces ? A. Yes, certainly.
MARY SOLOMON. I am the daughter of Henry Solomon. I was at home
in the Strand on the morning of Easter Tuesday, the 2nd of April last-
saw the prisoner Moses at my father's house that morning, in the sitting-
room up stairs, where I was sitting my father came there with him, and
I retired from the parlour.
Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS. Q. Did you return into
the room after you retired ? A. No, I did during the day-it was a back-
room- went into the front-room-I cannot exactly remember how long
I staid there-I remained some time I returned into the sitting-room in
the course of the day, but not while my father was there-I do not know
exactly how long-it might be about an hour I did not hear my father
go away from the room, nor any one leave the room- returned as soon
as I thought they had gone away.
