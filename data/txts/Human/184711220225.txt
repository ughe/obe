I went to Hyman's shop on Saturday night, 6th Nov.—I said, "I understand my little boy has brought a copper here; you should not buy anything from these children unless their parents know it; you should inquire of the parents"—he came out from his shop—I said, "This is a very serious thing"—he said, "You keep your boy in away from the other boys, it will soon blow over." JAMES NASH. I am coachman to Mr. John Hough—he has an empty house at 3, Peckham-grove—I saw a copper safe there at five o'clock in the evening of 6th Nov.—I missed it about eight o'clock the same evening. SAMUEL JOSEPH HADLOW. On 8th Nov. I went to Hyman's shop—I said, "Have you had a copper brought here, and other things, "which I described, "for sale"—he said, "No"—I said, "I have had a house broken into, and these things stolen"—he said he knew nothing of them—I described the copper to him—I was standing looking at his stall a few days afterwards, and he said to me, "Have you heard of your copper, Sir?"—I said, "No, I have not indeed"—after a moment's pause, he said, "All these things you know make good for trade."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had he known you before? A.  I had bought a stove of him two or three weeks before—I am not in business now—I was a stockbroker for many years—I dealt in stock about a month ago—I know what they mean by a lame duck  —I believe it was never said of me that I was the most notorious liar in the Stock Exchange—I never heard it.
   MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  Did you ever sell your copper to Mr. Hyman? A.  No—my character is quite different to a most notorious liar—I can bring 100 witnesses to my character. MARY FRY. The boys, Walker and Rice, came to me with a cooper on 6th Nov.—I refused to buy it—my place is about five minutes' walk from Hyam's.
   Cross-examined. Q.  They told you it belonged to their father? A.  Yes.
   MR. O'BRIEN  to HENRY RICE. Q.  What time did you sell the copper to Hyman? A.  We took it between five and six o'clock, took it to Fry's about seven, and sold it to Hyman about eight.
 (Hyman received a good character,)
   HYMAN  — GUILTY.  Aged 27.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury. — Confined One Year .
