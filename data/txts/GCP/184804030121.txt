Ann DONOVAN. I lived in Mrs. Marks' service at the time Mrs. Folks
died, and had done so six months. Mrs..Folks bad lived there all that time
I knew Barnhard by his coming there at different times it was an eating-
house, and he dined there I have seen him and Mrs. Marks together some-
times--they were on good terms-Barnbard used to come there regularly in
Mr. Marks' lifetime-he came regularly afterwards, the same as before I
temember Mrs. Folks being taken ill-I cannot rightly say what day it was
it was about one o'clock the night previous to the nurse being sent for I
asked her that evening if I should go for a doctor for her she always
ohjected to having a doctor-she could speak when she was taken ill-she
always spoke to the last-I saw ber more than once during the night-I
spoke to her every time I saw her, and she answered till between one and
two, when she was taken speechless-she never spoke afterwards, except the
word "tea," to Mrs. Jonas--she had come in on the Monday evening, about
seven or eight, while we were at tea, and appeared tipsy-she complained of
having had a fall in Whitechapel, and that two men had taken her up she
had some tea-she said her arm was a little burt-she had some supper that
night-she complained of not being well at twelve, and my mistress said,
"We will not go to bed; we will stop up, for fear she should dio ; and we
had better send for Mr. Barnett"--we did not stay up in her room-she went
to bed, and I and Mrs. Marks went up to her she was not very bad, but
complaining-I was present when Dr. Brown and Mr. Barnett came and
searched for some money after the house was searched, I remained there
alone for some days I found no money-I did not put anything into any of
the drawers, or take anything out, or open anything.
