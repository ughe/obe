Prisoner's Defence . The object I had in view in keeping half-crowns was this: I had some gold once when I received my wages—I gave away half-a-sovereign for a six pence and I said I would never keep gold any more. JANE WILKINSON. I am the prisoner's sister. I recollect his having 5 l . in sovereigns paid him by his master four or five months ago—he came to me and my mother to h ave them changed for half-crowns, he said they looked so much more than sovereigns.
   MRS. WILKINSONS . I have heard what my daughter has said; it is per fectly correct-within the last two years I have changed as many as ten sovereigns for half-crowns for the prisoner. WM. WILLIAMS. I was fellow-shopman with the prisoner—I know he had a fancy for having money changed for half-crowns—he knew the money in the till was market on the Wednesday previous to his being taken up.
   COURT . Q.  How came he to know it? A.  He told me it was marked and asked the reason why—he has to go to the till to get change—I told him that I thought it was very strange Mr. Needham suspected us—I told my master that the prisoner had noticed that the money was marked. RALPH NEEDHAM re-examined. Q.  Had you marked any money on the Wednesday? A.  That with my initials on it was marked on the Tues day and Wednesday, but that with a cross on Friday the 29th.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  And some with each of the marks were found in the pri soner's possession? A.  Yes, I has marked shillings and sixpences, but no marked shillings were found in his box—I only missed one half-crown on the Friday—it is impossible that he could have substituted any other money for that one as I saw him go to the till, and I came down directly, and there was only 2 l . 17 s . 6 d . there—I had put 3 l . there—he was taken immediately after I had seen him go to the hill—he had not had time to go up to his box.
   JURY. Q . When did you pay him his wages? A.  I have not paid him any thing, he had not been with me more than eight or nine weeks. (Mr. Roberts, a linen-draper, of Oxford-street; and Mr. Kenny, a linen draper, of the Kent-road; gave the prisoner a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 19—Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor. Confined Six Months.
