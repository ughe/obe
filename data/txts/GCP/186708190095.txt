LEWIS CLEMENTS (Police Inspector N). From information I received
on the 14th I went to some premises under one of the arches of the North
London Railway--they are in the occupation of the two prisoners--they
were not there then-1 caused a watch to be placed on those premises, and
about eleven o'clock the prisoners were taken into custody and brought to
the station-I afterwards went back to the premises and found in the stablo
the carcases of three sheep--they were skinned and dressed, and ready to
be taken away—the skins were found at Walthamstow the next morning
-I also found on the premises a horse and light cart, and in a small room
which had been used as a bedroom ten knives, two of which were covered
with blood—the cart that brought the sheep away from the shed on the
Saturday night was Mr. Tyler's.
JOHN GARDNER (Police Sergeant 9 M). From instructions I received I
went to the premises mentioned by the last witness about a quarter-past
eleven on the Saturday night I saw the prisoners in the yard--they went
into the stable, came out, and locked the door-I said to Watson, " Are
you master here?”—he said, “Yes ; don't make a noise out here "-I
went into the yard and said, "Give me the key of the stable ; I want to
look in your stable”-Watson said, “I left it in the lock, outside the
gate "-I said, “I shall break open the door if you don't give me the
key"-I said to both of them, "Have you anything in there, before I go
in ?"-Watson said, “I shall decline to answer any questions at present "
I then broke open the door and found three sheep hanging in the stable-
I asked whether they could give any acecount of the sheep-Watson said,
"I shall decline to answer any further questions at present "-I asked
Johnson, and he said, "No"-İ then told them they would be charged
with stealing Mr. Tyler's sheep, and I should take them into custody, and
they said, "Very well "-I afterwards went to Mr. Tyler, and he identified
the carcases in my presence, and in the presence of other butchers, from
certain marks that were on the inside of the skin-I noticed the marks on
the skins that Mr. Tyler refers to-we compared the insides of the skins
with the carcases, and I can positively swear that they corresponded-I
found 88. ld. on Johnson, and two keys; one of the keys opened the
stable door.
HENRY Nixon (Policeman 379 M). I was with Gardner when the
prisoners were taken into custody-I saw a horse there, and the footprints
of the shoe of that horse correspond with the marks round Mr. Tyler's
field—I discovered in turning over the mould a quantity of putrid blood,
sheeps' feet, wool, and such like things-the horse was shod with a par-
ticular shoe, called the feather-edge.
JOHNSON-Not Guilty. "WATSON was further charged with
having been before convicted, to which he PLEADED Guilty.--Eight Years'
Penal Servitude.
