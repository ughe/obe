   MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Did you ever have that memorandum back? A.  No.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What was on that memorandum? A.  I wrote down the weight and the price, 60 s . or 3 l . an ounce—I also wrote the amount that it came to, and if I recollect right, the monies paid also—I paid her 330 l . in notes and silver—to the best of my recollection that was what I paid her—she went away with an understanding to bring more—she returned again in about a half or three quarters of an hour, and brought some more gold—I melted that also in the same way—after I had done so I put it down on the memorandum paper—I took it into the front shop and weighed it the same as the other, and put it down on the same memorandum paper—I do not remember what the amount of the weight of that was—I gave her a me morandum of it—I did not pay her at that time—I told her I should have to send into the city for some money—she said she might as well go and fetch some more, and she went away—to the best of my recollection after the second melting I re-melted some of the gold and made a bar of it—I re-melted it with some silver-gilt lace—I gave the bar to my son Isaac, and told him to take it to Cock and Johnson's, who are refiners in Hatton-garden—while my son Isaac was gone, Mrs. Abrahams came back with some more gold—I melted that in the same way—I took it into the back shop, melted it, and took it into the front shop, weighed it, and put it down on the same memorandum paper—to the best of my recollection it was on the same paper—she brought gold in this way five or six times, to the best of my recollection, and each time I melted it and put it down on a memorandum paper—she waited for my son Isaac's return from Cock and Johnson's—she waited in the drawing-room, while is the front room on the first floor—not the room I saw Moses in—that was the back sitting-room—while she was waiting in the drawing-room, her mother, Mrs. Moses, came—I was in the drawing room at the time, and she came up stairs where Mrs. Abrahams was—she said her husband had sent her to see why Alice had stopped, and that he was uneasy—I told her I had sent into the city for some money—she seemed satisfied, and went away—my son Isaac brought me 400 l . in bank notes and sovereigns to the best of my recollec tion.
