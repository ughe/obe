Mssrs. Ellis and BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM HARDING. I am a miller, at Acton. On 14th June, about 8
o'clock in the evening, a person, who I believe was the prisotier, came to my
house for three half-penny-worth of oats-be gave me half-a-erown, and I
gave him change-I pat the half-crown into my pocket-I do not think I
had any other there at that time, but I put in two other bad half-crowns
these were the only three I had in my pocket-I took a bad half-crown half-
an-bour afterwards from another man, and that induced me to look at the
three, and I found they were bad-on 18th June the prisoner came again for
a quarter of a peck of peas- I recognised him he gave my wife a bad ball-
crown-I told him I suspected he was the man who came on the Saturday
before ; he denied it—with respect to the half-crown be brought on the 18th,
he first said he had been working in the country, and had brought it up with
him-be then said he brought it from Yorkshire with him; and then he said
some men on the road gave it him to go and get some peas to make some
soup; but who tbey were, or where they lived, he could not tell while I.
was holding him till the policeman came, he said I might as well let the poor
go, fot I could only give him a week for it if I held him I gave the
other man in custody on the 14th, and he was discharged, after being detained
a fortnight, I believe.
