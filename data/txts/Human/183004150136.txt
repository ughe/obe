 981.  FREDERICK BROOKS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 1 blanket, value 9d.; 1 quilt, value 1s.; 2 pillow-cases, value 1s.; and 1 sheet, value 2s., the goods of Samuel Denby.
 SARAH DENBY. I am the wife of Samuel Denby. The prisoner had a furnished room in our house - I missed the articles stated, and had him taken on the 15th of April; I had let these articles to the prisoner.
   JOSEPH BEAL. I am an officer. I was sent for and took the prisoner - I found four duplicates of the articles, which were pawned; they were in a drawer, and he gave them to me.
   HENRY ROBERT REDWOOD. I am in the service of a pawnbroker - I gave these two duplicates to a person who pawned this blanket and pillow; these other two were written by a young man in our service.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. They would have been redeemed on the Saturday following - I had a job, and wanted 5s. worth of varnish.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. - Transported for Seven Years.
 982.  CHARLES DUNN and THOMAS WOODROFFE were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , 1 sack, value 2s., and 280 lbs. weight of flour, value 48s., the goods of Edward Westrup.
   EDWARD WESTRUP. I am a miller, and live at Bromley, in Middlesex. The prisoners were in my employ on the 1st of March; Dunn was a carter, and Woodroffe a miller; Mr. Kingsford went to the mill on the 1st of March, and said something - I sent for Dunn the next day, and asked how he came by a sack of flour which he had sold to Mr. Kingsford; he said he had not sold any, but afterwards he said, to tell the truth, he had it of a baker in Gray's Inn-lane - we immediately sent to Mr. Perry, who returned an answer with which we were satisfied; Dunn was gone then, but his wife sent him over to us on the Wednesday - I think I had some conversation with him, but did not threaten or promise him any thing; nor did Shelswell in my hearing.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. His wife sent him to you - do you not remember your father saying, in your presence, that if he would tell all the particulars nothing would be done to him? A. He did not in my presence - I was present when his wife said she would send him; nothing had passed between me and his wife - I was present when his wife said she would send him; nothing had passed between me and his wife - I do not know what induced her to send him, except she thought we should get him; my father had some conversation with his wife - I did not tell Dunn that if he would tell all the truth I would pardon him.
 Q. Now you see his wife is here - did not your father tell her it would be better if he would come? A. Not in my hearing - my father officiates there, but the business is only mine.
 COURT. Q. Did your father say to Dunn's wife, "If he comes and tells the truth he shall not suffer for it?" A. Not to my knowledge; I did not hear any thing of that kind - when Dunn came he said he had sold a sack to Kingsford for 48s., and that Woodroffe had 15s. of it; Meredith, another carter, had received part of it - he said he had sold various others to the amount of eight sacks altogether; he said he had given Perry a sack of flour to sell - I left Dunn in custody; Shelswell brought Woodroffe.
   THOMAS SHELSWELL. I am an officer. I was present when the prisoners were taken - I told Woodroffe I took him for being connected with others in robbing his employer of some flour; he said if I had a mind to hear what he had to say, he would tell the truth - I said he might do as he chose; he said he was coming to work and met Dunn, who gave him 15s., and told him that was his share of a sack of flour which had been sold, but he was not to say any thing about it; he considered it was a sack belonging to his master.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What flour it was, was not specified? A. No - I did not know who his employer was.
   JOHN KINGSFORD. On the 1st of March Dunn called on me with a sack of flour, and said he had been to Mr. Orton with five sacks, and he had not money enough to pay for more than four, and would I take the other; I said how much was it - he said 50s.; I said I would give him 47s. - he said he should lose 3s. by that; I gave him 48s.
   THOMAS PRENDERGRASS. I was with Shelswell when Dunn was taken - he began talking a great deal about selling one sack here, and another sack there; I said he could expect no mercy, I must take him before the Magistrate - he said he wished to act like an honest man, and that he had taken eight sacks in the whole.
 Dunn received a good character.
 DUNN -    GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined Three Months.
 WOODROFFE -    NOT GUILTY.
