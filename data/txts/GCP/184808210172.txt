(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
1984. ROBERT WILLIAMS, feloniously receiving 7488 cards, value
11. 188. 3d. ; the goods of John Dickenson and another.
Messrs. CLARKSON and BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM PRESTON. I am in the service of Mr. Henry Penny, of the Old
Bailey. I went by his orders on 18th July, to Frederick Edwards' house-
he is a beer-seller-an arrangement took place between us-in consequence
of that I went to St. Mary Axe, about three hours after I had seen Edwards
- there saw the prisoner Williams-he touched me on the shoulder, and
said, "Are you waiting to see a party respecting some cards?"-I told him,
"Yes"-be said, "Edwards is not here, he was drunk last night, he has
made a mull of coming'-I said, I was sorry, for be promised to be there
at half-past one o'clock-he said, "Never mind, come along with me,
I have got them"-we went down Lime-street, and some other streets, and at
last I found myself in the Poultry-market at Leadenhall--we went into the
Poulterer's Arms--I saw him go behind the bar and fetch two parcels, which
were two gross of cards he took them into the tap-room, and put them on
the seat-I untied them, and looked at one parcel-I said they would suit me
very well, and they seemed the same that we were in the habit of using
Dickenson's-I said, "They are Dickenson's, are they not ?"-he said, "Say
no more about that"-I gave him 30s. for them, and he gave me 1s. 6d. -
these are the cards-he gave me this receipt.
Cross-eramined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. There is something by which
some of Dickenson's cards are known ? A. I believe there is, I do not
know-I am in the vellum binding department,I had made arrangements
with Edwards, and saw the prisoner-I paid him the price arranged between
me and Edwards-he did not tell me he was to have five per cent for what
he did.
