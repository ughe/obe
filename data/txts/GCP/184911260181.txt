JOHN Jones. I took three dozen and a half of short boots, and one dozen
and a half of slippers to the prisoner's house, and knocked at the door--I
asked a little boy if Mr. Whale lived there he said, Yes--the prisoner
opened the parlour door, and said, “Bring them in"-he opened them, and
found there were four pairs short, and he found fault with four pairs; he said
to me, "Take these back, and bring four pairs of others"-I said I could
not leave the goods without the money-he said, “You can't take these back;
take these four pairs back"-he insisted on my going back with them, and
said, "Can you come back this evening ?"-I said, “I don't think I can
he said, “Come in the morning by eleven o'clock, I will wait at home for
you"-I went to my master and told him-he gave me four pairs of others
for what I brought back, and the other four which were short-I went back,
and saw the prisoner; he said, "I did not think you would come back to
night "-I said I did not think I could I said I must have the money or
the goods--he said, "I can't give you the others back, I have taken the
trouble to size them and brush them, to send in the morning"-he took those
that I brought, and took the invoice from me he said he could not give me
the goods back-I said I could not leave them-he brought me out a chair
to sit down-he said, “You can sit there as long as you like: I shan't turn
you out"-he said he should not give me the goods back, nor the money,
if I called at eleven in the morning he would pay me I stopped an hour-I
asked him for the invoice to take back with me-he said he should not give it
me back, and I was obliged to leave the house without anything--I went to
my master's, and told him-my father was there ; he wanted to know why I
stopped so long-I asked my father to go with me-my father, my master
and I, all went together-my father knocked at the door the prisoner's wife
came, and said, "Who is there?"-my father said, “Me"- the prisoner came
down, and opened the door-we all went in--the goods were lying on the
banisters-my master said he wanted the goods or the money-the prisoner
said he would not pay to-night, he would call in the morning and pay.
Prisoner. Q. Did not I say you might take the goods back that night if
you liked ? A. No.
GEORGE HORNSBY. I am a pawnbroker-I have some boots I took in of
the prisoner, one pair on the 6th, one pair on 8th, and one pair on 11th Oct.
Mr. Lion re-examined. These are part of what were sent on the occa-
sion, and what the prisoner ordered.
CHARLES BERESPORD YOUNG. I am a pawnbroker-the prisoner pawned
a pair of boots with me on 2nd Aug., another pair on 28th Aug., two pairs
on 29th Oct., and one pair on 8th Nov.-the prosecutor has seen them and
identified them.
EDWIN CHAPMAN. I am a pawnbroker-1 have a pair of bocts, pawned
on 4th Aug.: I do not know by who, in the name of Ann May.
MR. LION re-examined. These are part of the boots the prisoner got.
GEORGE QUINNEAR (police-sergeant, P 1). I took the prisoner on 11th Nov,
on another charge-I searched his house, and found ninety-five duplicates,
and seven on his person-eight, found in his house, relate to those produced
by these different pawnbrokers-fifty-six of them related to boots and shoes.
Prisoner. Most of them were my own manufacture, and some bave been
returned on my hands, and with money that I should have got next month I
should have redeemed them. GUILTY. Aged 31.-Confined Two Years.
(There were two other indictments against the prisoner.)
