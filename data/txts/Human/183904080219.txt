OWEN ROWLAND. I am a porter. On the 2nd of April I was in Greenwich fair—I felt a tug at my right-hand pocket, and missed my handkerchief from it—I saw the prisoner walking away from me, and looking confused—I took hold of him, and said, "I believe you have my handkerchief"—he said he had not—I began to search him—he would not allow me to do so—the policeman came up and found the handkerchief between his legs in his trowsers. JAMES WILD. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner and prosecutor straggling together—I took the handkerchief out of the prisoner's trowsers—he had no pocket.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I picked it off the ground—I did not take it from the gentleman. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Six Months.
 1428. EDWARD POTTER was indicted for embezzlement.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. JOHN KING. I am butler to Mr. Joseph Sladden, of Lea, in Kent. I was in the habit of sending parcels by Mrs. Walker's errand-cart—the prisoner was the servant employed—on the evening of the 26th of February, 1838, or the following morning, I paid him 3 s . 6 d . for parcels on his mistress's account.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are you quite sure you paid it all? A.  Yes, quite; I had a memorandum of it at the time—I cannot find it—it was the last sum I paid him.
 Mr.  CLARKSON . Q.  What was the parcel? A.  A portmanteau, and bag of my young master's. FANNY WALKER. I am a widow, and carry on the business of a carrier at Eltham. In February last year the prisoner was in my service, and drove my errand-cart—He received money for parcels—I keep a book in which I enter the money received—I have it here—he should account to me for what he received on the following morning—he did not account to me for 3 s . 6 d . at any time from Mr. Sladden—I discharged him on the 9th of November—he gave me no account of its being unpaid—after he left my service I sent in a bill to Mr. Sladden, and found it had been paid.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long was the prisoner in your service? A.  Five years—he has been in the service of another carrier since I discharged him, but I have not seen him—he has been in custody about a month—I wanted to see what amount he had robbed me of before I prosecuted—this is my book—( producing it )—the entries are in my own hand-writing, and were made at the time—I never made any mistake, to my knowledge, in the accounts.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You sent in a bill of 25 s . to Mr. Sladden? A.  Yes—I saw King, the butler—he could only recollect paying this 3 s . 6 d ., but he had received altogether from him 10 s . 6 d . JOHN KING re-examined.  The prosecutrix sent me a bill for 25 s .—I had paid 10 s . 6 d . of it altogether—I recollected the 3 s . 6 d . directly, but I could not give a date to the other payments. JAMES ADAMS. I am the superintendent of the Lewisham watch. I took the prisoner into custody on the 9th of March, and told him I wanted him for some money relating to Mrs. Walker's accounts—he said he knew very well what it was—that he owed her about 30 s . GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined One Year.
   Before Mr. Recorder.
