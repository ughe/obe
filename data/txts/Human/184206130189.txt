 MR.  CHARNOCK . Q.  How long have you been a landerer? A.  The last eight years—I never knew the prisoner before I saw him at Wor ship-street—this mangle came from Tatters ell's, at Hyde-park-corner—I bought it in the yard about a fortnight before I sold it to Dawson, for 2 l . 7 s , 6 d .—a man named Loosely was present—I hired a cart of Ha mar, a green-grocer—I gave him 2 s ., and the man half-a-crown, for helping me, and telling me it was to be sold—it was removed at four o'clock in the afternoon—there was no balance in it then—I sold it to Davson for 4 l . 7 s .—I never bought and sold but two before—I never saw Dawson till I had the mangle to sell—I put a ticket in the window, "Baker's patent mangle for sale"—I had a great many applications, but could not get the price—Dawson came to me afterwards, and asked me if I should know it again, I said, "Yes, from a hundred"—I have been with Dawson several times—I came here with him—we have had very little together—on Thursday I brought my own dinner—on Friday we had a little repast, which Dawson paid for, and on Saturday—there were some nails in the mangle I sold—one was a tin tack—the black patch is on the crosspiece which the panel goes in.
   MR. HORRY . Q.  Was it Mr. Dawson came to you about the mangle for the prisoner's friends? A.  Mr. Dawson—I bought it to sell again. JOIIPH LOOSRLBY. I am a carpenter, living in Queen-street, Pimlico—I was present when Webb bought a mangle—I marked it with my pencil on the cross pieces—one of the ears was in two pieces—I knew the mangle for four or five years at Mr. Tatters all's, at Hyde Park-corner.
   MR. CHAENOCK . Q.  How long have you known Webb? A.  Between four and five years—the marks were put on when I took the mangle to pieces, that I might put it up again. THOMAS EDWARDS. I am a carman, living in Queen-street, Hoxton-market—I took this mangle from Mr. Webb to Dawson's—I know it by taking it from Mr. Webb to Mr. Dawson's—I put it up in the cellar, and there it remained, till Mr. White came and took it away—it had an oak bed—Mr. Dawson applied to me to come here—he sent me to Webb's for it.
   MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  How many mangles has Mr. Dawson? A.  Twenty or thirty—there is a great similarity between mangles—these are all put in the lower part of Mr. Dawson's premises for safe custody—I am the man that fetched a mangle from Three Cups-alley, on or about the 28th of April—I was not told that it was not the property of the man that sold it, to my recollection—it is often the case, where I fetch mangles and goods from, there is wrangling and jangling amongst the people—there are often disputes about the property ray master purchases—it makes no impression on me—I was not told it was stolen, and would be claimed by the owners—Mr. White's name was not mentioned—I fetched it about seven o'clock in the morning.
