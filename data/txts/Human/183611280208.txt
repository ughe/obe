JOSEPH ALLCHIN. I am a watchman. On Tuesday night, the 8th of November, I had information given me, and went to the guard-room, with the sergeant—I saw Tyer lying in the guard-room—I searched her, and found nothing on her—I took her to the watch-house and left her—I found this pillow-case, table-cloth, and trowsers in the prosecutor's room—next morning I asked Tyer in what manner she entered Mr. Varlo's quarters—she said, while they were in search after the key she found it on the ledge of the door, and unlocked the door herself—Alexander was not present—I found a shawl in the prosecutor's room, which Tyer claimed CHARLES STEWART. I am superintendent of the parochial✗ police at Woolwich. On the 9th of November I took Alexander into cus tody at her father's—I told her I apprehended her from information I had received, as being in company with Jane Tyer, concerned in the robbery at the quarters of Lieutenant Varlo—she said she was present previous to the robbery in company with Tyer, but she did not commit it—I searched her, and found a prayer-book, a small box containing moon-stones, a ring, and three studs—I had previously searched Tyer at the watch-house, and three pocket handkerchiefs dropped from under her gown—I have these here.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you not forgotten something which Alexan der said to you? A.  I have not forgotten it—she told me before the things were taken she had been with Tyer to Mr. Varlo's quarters, that Mr. Varlo's servant locked the room door, and then she went away, and was to Woolwich Common with another person; that when she returned, she found Tyer lying on the ground, and the linen lying about, and that she picked the things I found off the steps.
