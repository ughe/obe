856. ELIZA GOODRIDGE was again  indicted for stealing 1 blanket, 1 sheet, and 1 table-cloth, value 10 s  .; the goods of Frederick Inglis . MR. THOMPSON conducted the Prosecution, ELLEN INGLIS. I am the wife of Frederick Inglis, of 13, Bull-and—Mouth-street. The prisoner occupied our second-floor front-room—she took it of me—the furniture was mine—the policeman came with the key of the prisoner's lodging; I then missed a blanket, sheet, and table-cloth, worth about 10 s .—I did not give the prisoner any authority to pawn them.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  How long bad she been living with you? A,  A fortnight; I do not know where she had lived before—I do out know that there was an execution in her lodging before—she came tome as a married woman—I am not anxious to press this charge. ELIZA PRICE. I am the wife of William Price. I searched the pri soner the Smith field station—I found on her these four duplicates and 2 s . 9 1/2 d . EDWIN HARRISON. I am assistant to Mr. Baylis, a pawnbroker, in Aldengate-street. I produce this blanket and table-cloth, pawned by the prisoner on 3rd March—this sheet was pawned the same day, but not with me—these are the duplicates of them. ELLEN INGLIS re-examined.  These are my property, and were in the mm the prisoner hired of me.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did the prisoner and a man take the lodging? A.  Yes, they were together; I believe her husband is a bookbinder. GUILTY. Aged 30.— Confined Three Month more .
 857. ANNS AUNDERS , stealing—1 pair of trowsers, 1 pair of breeches, and 1 dress; the goods of William Wheeler . SUSAN WHEELER. I am the wife of William Wheeler, of Enfield. On Sunday, 9th March, I went up-stairs, and opened a box—I missed a dress, and my husband's trowsers and breeches—these are them; I had seen them safe about a fortnight before—on 6th March I had bees at work at Mr. Humble's—I left my house after dinner, a little before one o'clock—the window was shut, and not broken—on my return at night, I found it Woken—a person could then put in their hand and undo a button, which would enable them to open the window, which was large enough to admit a person. WILLIAM WINSBURT( policeman, N  315). In consequence of, informa tion, I went to a pawnbroker's shop at Edmonton on 10th March, and got the trowsers, breeches, and dress. THOMAS WILLIAM KILSBY. I am assistant to a pawnbroker. On 6th March, these articles were pawned with me by a female in the name of Ann Lambert—I have seen the prisoner before, but I should not like to swear she was the person—I may have seen her once or twice.
