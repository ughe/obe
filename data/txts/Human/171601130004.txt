   James Bullock, of the Parish of St. Mary White-Chappel, was indicted for stealing 1 Hen Turkey, value 4 s. 15 Capons, 30 s. and 5 Hens, 7 s. the Goods of Nathan Samuel, a Jewish Poulterer on the 29th of December last. It was plain that Samuel lost the Goods, and that they were found upon the Prisoner; who giving no Account how he came by them, the Jury found him   Guilty.
 He was a second Time indicted, of the Parish of St. Leonard in Shoreditch, for breaking open the House of Robert Tutt , in the Night time, and stealing thence 1 Pewter Dish, value 12 s. 1 Brass Porridge-pot, 6 s. and other Goods, from the same Robert Tutt. It appear'd the Prisoner hir'd a Room in the same Tenement where Mr. Tutt had his; who having some Suspicion of the Prisoner, after he was rob'd, caus'd the Door of it to be broke open, and search'd the Room, and there found some of his Goods, with a great Quantity of all sorts of Poultry, and other Things, that look'd very suspicious upon him. He made no Defence, and was found   Guilty.
 He was a third Time indicted, of the Parish of  St. Dunstan's Stepney, for feloniously stealing 3 Iron Crows, value 18 s. and a Hand-saw, the Goods of William Bruce and Thomas Dun , out of the New Church-yard in Spittle Fields, on the 17th of December last. These Goods were also found in his Possession and the jury found him   Guilty. [Death. See summary.]
   Robert Passmore, and Thomas Pottle, of the Parish of St. Leonard Shoreditch, were indicted, Passmore as Principal, in feloniously stealing 17 Pound Weight of Wool, value 12 s. the Goods of John Lemmet, on the 5th Instant; and Pottle, as Accessory, in receiving the same, knowing them to be stole. It was fully prov'd, that Passmore stole the Goods, and that Pottle received them; but he denied he knew them to be stole; and called some to his Reputation, who spoke very well of him. The Jury considering the Matter, brought Passmore in Guilty to the Value of 10 d. and   acquitted the other. [Whipping. See summary.]
   Katherine Ely, Elizabeth White and Anne Bartley, of the Parish of Stepney, were indicted for an Assault on the Person of William Fife , and robbing him of a Pair of Silver Buckles, Value 12 s. on the 3d Instant. Fife swore he was going over Tower-hill about 6 a Clock at Night, and there met Katherine Ely, who told him it was very cold, and desir'd him to make her drink; which he, not being acquainted with such, sort of Persons, complied with; and she led him to a House in Church Lane by Rag-Fair, where they drank some strong Drink and Geneva. White was in the Company, and ask'd him if he had any Money, and swore she would have it, which made him try to get out, but was prevented by the Prisoner Bartley, who bolted the Door; then they all fell upon him, and White held out a Knife to him, and swore she would cut his Throat if he made any Resistance; and then took his Buckles out of his Shoes, and his Breast-Buttons off his Shirt, and at last turn'd him out of Doors, where meeting with an Officer, he was afraid of being murder'd, supposing him to be one of their Bullies; but the Headborough, who had the same Thoughts of him, hearing him say, For God's Sake, Sir, now you have robb'd me, don't murder me too, was mightily surprised, and ask'd him, Who had injur'd him; and being inform'd of the whole Transaction, bid him take Courage, and he would stand by him; so they forc'd open the Door, and took them all. There were two Witnesses who swore, White offer'd him his Buckles, on Condition he would not prosecute. Thus the Fact was prov'd by the Evidence for the King. As to the Prisoners, they denied it stifly, and brought a most impudent Prostitute, whose extraordinary Progress in all sorts of Wickedness had gain'd her the Name of Pretty Lady, to swear, that Fife chuck'd her under the Chin, as she was standing at her Door, and ask'd her if she had any Fire; to which she replied Yes, and ask'd him to come in. That they went to Bed together, and that he gave Her the Buckles for Satisfaction. Upon the whole the Jury found the Prisoners all   Guilty of Felony; and their Evidence was order'd into Custody by the Court, for her extraordinary Impudence. [Branding. See summary.]
   John Daton, of the Parish of St. Mary in White-chappel, was indicted for breaking the House of Edward Barlow in the Night time, and stealing thence 15 Yards of Muslin, Value 40 s. 6 Gawze Handkerchiefs, 10 s. 6 Silk Ditto, 10 s. and other Goods, the Property of the said Edward Barlow on the 22d of October last.    John Thomas, of the Parish of St. Martin in the Fields, was indicted for feloniously stealing a Flaxen Sheet, Value 12 s. the Goods of Nicholas Clark , out of the House of Sarah Tweedee on the 17th of December last. Tweedee was a Washing-Woman, and another Evidence being in the Room where the Goods were, saw a Hand take it out, which he pursued, and found the Goods under the Prisoner's Arm. The Jury found him Guilty to the Value of 10 d. [Whipping. See summary.]
   Thomas Cane, of the Parish of St. Giles in the Fields, was indicted for feloniously stealing a Looking Glass, Value 10 s. the Goods of Jonathan Downes, on the 16th of December last. Mr. Downes depos'd, he was at work in his Cellar, and heard his Door unlocked, and the Noise of some Person in his House: upon which he went up Stairs, and saw the Prisoner go out with the Glass, which made him go after him and stop him, telling him he had got his Glass. Have I, says he, then take it; which he did, and the Man too. The Prisoner's Defence was very trifling, and the Jury Found him   Guilty, [Branding. See summary.]

   William Veal(a Boy) of the Parish of St. Martin in the Fields, was indicted for feloniously stealing 1 Cloth Coat and Wastcoat, Value 30 s. 1 Handkerchief, 1 Pair of Stockings, 2 Neckcloths, and other Goods from Thomas Brooks, on the 9th of December last. Mr. Brooks swore; That having lost the Goods, he took up the Prisoner upon Suspicion, who confess'd the Fact, and where he had sold the Goods.    William Clark, of the Parish of St. James's West-Minster, was indicted for breaking the House of Jane Bradshaw , in the Night time, and stealing thence 1 Pair of Holland Sheets, value 12 s. 1 Callicoe Quilt, value 4 l. with Napkins, Tablecloths, Aprons, Ec. the Goods of the said Jane Bradshaw, on the 1st Instant. The Fellow was taken with the Goods upon him, bundled up in the Quilt, but the Burglary was not prov'd: The Jury found him Guilty of Felony only. [Branding. See summary.]
