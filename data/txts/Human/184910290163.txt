COURT . Q.  They have not indicted you? A.  They have not. MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Have you got 7 l . 10 s . in the world? A.  I have at Pocklington's and Laceys, in Smithfield, my trustees keep my accounts—I can have money by applying to them for it—I cannot draw any thing out myself.
   COURT . Q.  What has become of the 7 l . 10 s .? A.  God knows; I can repay it at any time—I suppose it has been spent; since that, money has been obtained by another party, and I did not think this would be brought in question—I have paid away the money, and I have had a party receiving that money through some rather indirect transaction—I would rather not mention names; it would not criminate myself, perhaps I might say more than I should wish; there is nothing very particular about it, but perhaps it might go further into private matters than I should like—it might involve me in another transaction; I do not know whether it would involve me in an indictment.
   MR. M'MAHON . Q.  Where did you receive the 7 l . 10 s . from Laws? A.  At the Dolphin, in Milk-street; an officer was with me; Lawes was not in custody—it was on the Saturday we went to the police-court, I forget the day of the month—I went from the court to Lawes, to inquire whether there was anything not fair or fraudulent in the transaction—I told him that Smith was then in custody, and I should like him to come up and see whether anything could be done in it.
   COURT . Q.  Did you give any part of the 20 l . or 30 l ., you received from Lawes to Smith? A.  I lent him some money, Smith bad taken up the trans action on my behalf; at least I let him do the dirty  work, going backwards and forwards—he received it for me one day, and brought it me, and had some of it: we were pals  together—he was not my agent to raise the money, and we were rather more intimate than I liked—I am not yet twenty years old.
   SMITH  — GUILTY on the 1st Count. . Aged 29.— Confined One Year.
   LAWES and FRASER  — NOT GUILTY

   MR. M'MAHON conducted the Prosecution WILLIAM HUXLEY. I am ostler, at the Clarence Hotel. On Monday, 3d Sept., Smith sent me to Mr. Mills, of Goswell-street, to get a dog-cart for two or three days—he gave me this check ( produced ) to pay for the hire of it, and to bring him the difference—I went to Mr. Mills and told him I had come to hire a dog-cart for Mr. Smith, of the Clarence Hotel—I gave him the check, and told him to. take the three days' hire out of it—he gave me the difference, 6 l . 15 s .—I went back and saw Smith in the Clarence-yard, and gave it to him—I saw the dog-cart brought to the yard.
