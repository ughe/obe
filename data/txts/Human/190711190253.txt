ALBERT POTTER. I live at 11, Bath Road, Stratford. I am a clerk in the employ of the Home-Light Oil Company. On Thursday, Oc tober 10, I looked up the premises at 8.20 in the evening; the bicycles were then on the premises. I went the next morning to open the premises. The door was intact, but the box of the look was forced off. I found some things missing; the bicycles were in the superin tendent's private office. WILLIAM WOODS. I keep a marine store dealer's shop at Leyton stone. On October 15 the two bicycles in Court were brought to my place by the prisoner Waker and another man not in custody. Waker asked me to buy the bicycles. I refused. He then asked me if I would lend him something on them. I refused to do that. He then asked if he might leave them there a little while, to which I con sented. The prisoner and the other man led the bicycles into my shop. Prisoner said he would call for them later on, but he did not do so. The same day two police officers called.
 Cross-examined by prisoner Waker. One of the bicycles was not fetched up on the Monday afternoon, and the other one was not fetched up on Tuesday morning. You did not come and say to me, "Are you going to bay the bicycles."
 Detective-sergeant WILLIAM SMITH, Victoria Park. On the even ing of October 15, together with Police-constable Orchard, I saw the prisoner Sickelmore. I spoke to him and arrested him. He made a statement to me, in consequence of which I went to Woods' shop at Leytonstone and recovered the two bicycles. They were given up to me by Woods. Having got the two bicycles, at 9.20 that evening I went to the "Lord Napier" public-house, Hackney Wick. I saw the prisoner Waker in the bagatelle room. I was with Police-con stable Orchard. I said to prisoner, "You know me," and he said, "Yes," When I got him outside I said, "I am going to arrest you for being concerned with three others in stealing on the night of the 10th from the Home-Light Oil Company two bicycles." He said, "I don't think." I subsequently conveyed him to the Victoria Park Police Station. Sickelmore was detained, and they were both charged at the West Ham Police Station. In answer to the charge Walker there said, "I admit being at Woods' wife the bicycles, but I never had a finger on them." The next morning at the West Ham Police Court he said, "I only went with the other man to try and sell them."
 Cross-examined by Waker. You made that statement at West Ham Police Court.
 Police-constable CHARLES OECHARD, 276. Victoria Park. On Octo ber 15 I was present when the prisoner Sickelmore was arrested. I went with Detective-sergeant Smith to Woods' marine store shop and the bicycles were hended to me by Woods' foreman. WILLIAM WOODS, recalled. (To the Judge.) When the prisoner Waker and the other man not in custody came on the Tuesday they both spoke—the other one spoke first and Waker afterwards. Waker took an active part in the conversation with a view to selling the bicycles.
