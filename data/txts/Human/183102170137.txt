681. CHARLES GAST was again indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 5th of January , 3 yards of velveteen, value 9s., the goods of John Clark.
 JOHN CLARK. I am a  tailor, and live in Cirencester-place, Marylebone. On the 29th of January I lost eighteen yards of velvet from my shop, and saw it at the office on the 8th of February.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I found this velvet in a drawer in Gast's shop; I never said any thing to the prisoner about it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 682.  ANN TAYLOR was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 16th of January , 1 medicine-chest, value 2l., the goods of William Martyn , well knowing it to have been stolen.
 WILLIAM MARTYN. I live in the Quadrant, Regent-street, and am a  chemist and druggist. On Sunday, the 16th of January, about nine o'clock in the morning, I missed a medicine-chest from a glass-case opposite my shop door; I think I had seen it the day before - I found it at the office; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I apprehended the prisoner on the 5th of February at Gast's house; I saw her in the shop - I told her I wished to go into her room; she took me up to a second floor room - this medicine-chest stood on the drawers; I put my hand on it, and said, "Is this yours?" she said, "It is mine," and that she had had it a long time; I said, "Where did you get it?" she made some humming and hesitation, which was not an answer, and I took her in charge.
     NOT GUILTY.
 683.  HARRIET DAVIS was indicted for feloniously receiving of a certain evil-disposed person, 1 writing-case, value 16s., and 2 machines for the blind, value 2l., the goods of Ralph Wedgwood , well knowing them to have been stolen.
 RALPH WEDGWOOD. I live in Rathbone-place. I lost this writing-case, with a number of others, the latter end of November or beginning of December - I afterwards found it at the office - it is my own manufacture; I am quite sure of it; I lost three machines for the blind at the same time - I have seen them all since; I think I must have seen them in the course of the day they were missed.
   THOMAS VESPER. I am a pawnbroker. On the 14th of December the prisoner pawned a machine for the blind for 10s. in the name of Eliza Johnson, No.2, Jeffries-buildings, Westminster; I do not recollect what account she gave - I am certain of her person.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Is it uncommon for people to pawn in a wrong name? A. Not at all.
   THOMAS SEAWARD. I am a journeyman to Mr. Harrison, a pawnbroker, of Tottenham-court-road. I produce a portfolio which I took in pawn from the prisoner, in the name of Mrs. Johnson; I have not taken her Christian name - I knew her so well; she said it had been bought at Dover, and cost 24s.
 Cross-examined. Q. You knew her well? A. Yes, by frequenting my shop for some time - she gave her right address, No. 7, John-street; I will not swear she said it was bought at Dover, or that she came from Dover.
   JOHN KEMP. I am in the employ of Mr. Barret, a pawnbroker of Norfolk-street, Middlesex-hospital. I have a machine for the blind which I took in pawn from the prisoner, for 10s., on the 15th of December, in the name of Johnson, of John-street, who I had repeatedly seen before; I asked how she came by it - she said it was her brother's; I knew she lived in John-street, Tottenham-court-road.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is it not common for persons to give erroneous accounts to conceal the fact of their raising money? A. Yes; I had seen her frequently at out shop.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. On the 5th of February I was standing between two doors in Gast's house, when the prisoner came in; she put her shoulder against the partition, and called Harry! Harry! to a boy who was in the shop - he saw her, but would not speak to her; she turned round, and saw me standing at a short distance behind; I said, "What is it you want?" she said she only wanted to speak to Harry - I said, "What do you want with him?" she said she had come to fetch a shirt to wash for him, and that she washed for him generally - I took her into the next room, and asked where she lived; she said in Grosvenor-street - I asked which, and after a little hesitation, she said Lower Grosvenor-street; I said "It is impossible that a washerwoman can live in Lower Grosvenor-street, what is the number?" Ellis was in custody in the shop, and desired her to answer no impertinent questions, upon which Ellis was taken away to the office - I said I should not let her go till she satisfied me where she lived - she afterwards said she lived in Colvell-court, which leads out of Charlotte-street; I sent Goddard there with her - in the trunk, at Mr. Thompson's, No. 11, Tottenham-court-road, I found these three duplicates; I went to the pawnbroker's - they relate to these articles.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did she know you? A. I am satisfied that she did; but the darkness of the place, and the dirty state she was then in, I did not then know her: Ellis was in custody; I cannot say that he lived in that house; for the room pointed out as his, had neither bed nor bedding in it, nor did he live at Thompson's.
   HENRY GODDARD. I am an officer. I received the prisoner from Ballard, and went with her to Colvell-court, Charlotte-street, to a shop there - I asked the landlady, in her hearing, if she lived there; she said Yes, in the first floor back room - I left her there, and from information I afterwards received, I went again, found her in the act of moving away, and took her in charge; the landlord said at the office that Ellis lodged in Colvell-court with her - I do not know whether she heard it.
 Cross-examined. Q. She took you to this place as her residence? A. Ballard sent me there with her.
 MR. WEDGWOOD. This machine for the blind I have no hesitation in swearing to; here is another, and this is a manifold-writer, not fitted up - I am positive they are all three mine.
 Cross-examined. Q. These are part of a number of articles stolen one night in November? A. Or the beginning of December - I sell a great many; these three happen to be the first that I stamped with a tool which I have - I never sold any with the mark of this tool, and these are not fitted up - the prisoner would not know but it was a perfect article.
 Prisoner. I leave my defence to my Counsel.
