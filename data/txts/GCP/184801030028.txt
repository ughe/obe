MATTHEW YOUNG. I am a cow-keeper in Mile-end-road. On the 17th
Dee. I was at the George Inn, West Smithfield-I gave the landlady's son
change for a 51. note- I gave him four sovereigns and two half sovereigns;
they were all good-shortly after, Mrs. Johnson brought four sovereigns and
two half sovereigns back-she asked me for the 51. note, and I gave it her-
the money was laid down on the table-I took it up, and directly saw one
sovereign was bad--that money was by itself-I had not mixed it.
Cross-examined. Q. How much gold had you altogether, before you
changed the note? A. About 8l. or 91., or it might be 101.--I had
taken it from Hill and Sons' banking-house-I put it all in my pocket-I
did not weigh the sovereigns, or do anything to them--Mrs. Johnson put
down the sovereigns she brought in, on the table-she said there was some
dispute, they did not like one of them-I knew I gave four good sovereigns
and two halves-I do not want to lose the sovereigo, and she does not want
to lose it-young Johnson got the money from me, and he ought to have
brought it back to me.
FREDERICK WHITE (police-inspector). I produce this sovereign.
MR. YOUNG re-examined. This is the sovereign.
