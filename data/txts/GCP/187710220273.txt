WILLIAM ANDERSON. I am in Messrs. Penn's employ, and have charge
of Evelyn Wharf, Deptford-in April Fennell told me that Mr. Notley
had given Porter permission to lay a barge alongside the Evelyn Wharf
to load, and the barge was loaded with iron on 17th, 18th, and 19th April I
saw Porter at the wharf two or three times during that time, and I saw
Douglas once--they were on the quay close to the edge of the wharf
while the loading was going on.
Cross-excamined by MR. BESLEY. The iron is weighed in the factory
they sometimes weigh the cart and the iron without the horse, and in a
great many cases they weigh the cart and the iron-I remember the 19th
of April, because on the 18th the horse fell into the bargeI did not
Bee Porter there after that that I remember-he only remained a few
minutes.
SAMUEL FREETH. I was in partnership with Mr. Whitling-I was
manager to the Phønix Iron Works at that time---prior to April I knew
Porter, and had been in the habit of buying scrap iron of him on 21st
April he said he had a cargo coming from Messrs. Penn's, consisting of
lump iron, cast iron, iron borings, and wrought iron turnings on the
evening of 19th April he brought & barge, called the Andrew, alongside
with the metal as I had arranged-we commenced unloading on the 21st
-many men were engaged in it under Verrall's superintendence a man
named Andrews was in Douglas's employ, and was present at the unload-
ing-it was Verrall's duty to see the cargo as it came from the barge and
enter it in the landing-book-I observed a conversation going on between
Verrall and Andrews, and afterwards saw Porter come to the wharf-I
had a conversation with him, and he gave me this invoice. This was
dated 24th April, 1877, charging iron of different kinds and amounts, making
a total of 1041. 168. 11d. less 10 per cent., 1031. 168., credit 901., balance
131. 168. by the barge, Andrew, settled by cash 131. 168. H. Porter and Co.,
1st May, 1877.) I afterwards got the landing-book from Mr. Verrall,
compared the weights, and found that they corresponded-I only paid
901. on account when the invoice was given me, on aceount of my suspicion
about the weights, but he said that those were the weights which he had
already charged-I told him the weight was considerably less than was
in the barge, but he said the full weight was there I said that I should
require to see Penn's invoice before I paid the balance, and he said he
would bring it the next morning, which he did, and turned down a portion
of it where it should be receipted after he left me I went to the Phoenix
Wharf and had the load of iron reweighed, and found out that the weights
came out in correspondence with this paper produced) -I then paid the
balance, and on, I think, the 2nd of May I wrote a letter to Porter-I
wrote three or four letters to him, and about May 7th he called upon me,
and I saw him-I also saw him at the works on 15th May I said there
were 12 tons 18 cwt. short in that barge-he raid “The weight is all
right; you have got the full weight," and I believe he said “You have
made the overweight at Penn's"be then asked if I would take a bill
for the difference, 321. 128. 5d.-I said that I had paid him cash, and he
