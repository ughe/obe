JURY. Q. How long before he was apprehended had you seen him shoot any soot? A. About six months - he was not in my master's employ, but he used to buy soot of the prisoner.
   JOHN BURNESS. I am a private watchman. I was at the corner of Fleur-de-lis-court, on the morning of the 15th of January, and saw two sweeps - one had a sack of soot on his back, and the other was following him; they came as if from Grey Eagle-street, which is about one hundred yards from there - I saw them return twice with empty sacks; I saw them afterwards in Blossom-street - Barnard was following them.
   BENJAMIN BARNARD. I saw two men carrying soot - I believe the prisoner was one, but cannot swear it.

 648.  BENJAMIN JENNINGS was again indicted for stealing, on the 6th of December , 1 watch, value 2l., the goods of John Rose.
   ELIZABETH ROSE. I am the wife of John Rose - we live in Walter-place, School-house-lane, Ratcliff; my husband is a waterman. On the 6th of December, I called in the prisoner to sweep the kitchen chimney, about ten o'clock in the morning - my husband's watch was hanging over the chimney; when the prisoner had got the machine about half-way up, he told me to take down the watch, which I did - I put it into a basin on the dresser, and covered it over with a little silk bag; I went up stairs to get some money, and left my little girl in the room, but she went out to see if the machine was up the chimney - I was away about a minute and a half, and when I came down she was out in the yard; the prisoner soon finished the chimney - he made some difficulty about putting the soot into the bag, and I asked if I should hold it; he said No - I paid him 4d., and he went away; I missed the watch in about ten minutes - no one had been in the house but him, from the time I had put it into the basin; I received information, and went to White's-yard, Rosemary-lane, saw the prisoner, caught him by the jacket, and said,"Where is the watch you took from Walter-place, Schoolhouse-yard, where you swept a chimney this morning?" he said, "You had better come and see;" he struggled, got from me, and ran as hard as he could - I value the watch at two guineas; it cost me four - it was as good for me as when I bought it; my daughter is twelve years old, but she is very ill - I am certain no one was in the house but her and the prisoner.
 Prisoner. Before she saw me she wanted to swear to another man of the name of Fossett. Witness. No - a Policeman took another person, but I said that was not the man. I was informed he either resorted to, or lived in White's-yard - I went there, he came out of a passage, and I caught him.
   DAVID FOSSETT. I live in Essex-street, Commercial-road. I have known the prisoner a great while - two Policemen came, took me to the office, and the prosecutrix was going to swear to me, till I took her under a gaslight, and said, "My good woman, what are you going to swear to?" I took off my cap, and then she said I was not the man - if I had not spoken to her, she would have sworn to me, and I should have been where the prisoner is now; I never knew any harm of the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 649.  SUSANNAH MANLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of November , 1 sheet, value 2s., and 1 blanket, value 7s., the goods of Daniel Kemmish.
   DANIEL KEMMISH. I am a bellows-maker, and live in Tent-street, St. George's; the prisoner's husband keeps the house, and she lives there. About the latter end of November my wife missed a blanket off the bed; we had a box in the room, from which the sheet was taken - I do not know when I had seen that; I cannot say that I ever saw it in the box. On the 25th of January the prisoner and her husband quarrelled, and at her husband's instigation I had her taken up - I have since seen a sheet in the possession of Pool, which I know had been on our bed; the prisoner's husband gave me a duplicate, in her presence, which I gave to the officer.
   DANIEL KEMMISH, JUN. I am thirteen years old. I was left at home by my mother one morning, two or three months ago; the prisoner came into the room we live in, and asked me to go to my aunt's to know if she had mixed the bread up - I went, and as I was coming back I met my little brother, whom she had sent out; she then sent me out again for some flour, and said the bread was too wet - my father was out, and my mother was at Tottenham.
 Prisoner. That was at a different time altogether; it was a fortnight afterwards - I sent him for the flour as his mother had left the bread running all over the place.
   WILLIAM POOL. I am in the employ of a pawnbroker, in Ratcliff-highway. I have a sheet, pawned on the 30th of November, by the prisoner, in the name of Smith - she was in the habit of pawning at our shop by the name of Smith, New Gravel-lane, which is fifty or sixty yards from Tent-street.
   JAMES KENNEDY. I was called into the house in Tent-street, on the 25th of January; I took the prisoner there - her husband was there, and gave Kemmiah a duplicate; he said that was his property, and the prisoner ought to be punished - Kemmish gave me the duplicate, and I took the prisoner to the watch-house; she there said that Kemmish's wife gave the sheet to her to pawn.
   LOUISA KEMMISH. I know this sheet is my husband's; it was kept in a box under the bed - I missed it on the night I came home from Tottenham; I do not know the day - it was in November or December, I think in December; I had never given the prisoner, or any one else, permission to pawn it; she and her husband live on very bad terms.
 COURT. Q. Now, take care what you are saying, it affects the prisoner very much - are you quite sure you had not given her the sheet to pawn? A. Yes; we did owe some rent at that time - her husband has not excused it; we still owe about 12s. 6d.; we have two rooms there - we have never found the blanket; 1s. 6d. was lent on the sheet - when I went into the room to get it out of the box it was gone; I looked well about, and then I went into one of their rooms - I said, "Mrs. Manley, I have lost a sheet;" and she hooted out of another room,"Oh, you will find it again" - but I think she was in liquor; her husband then said, "That is all you get for going into their room when she was ill."
 JURY. Q. Were you in the habit of drinking with her? A. No.
