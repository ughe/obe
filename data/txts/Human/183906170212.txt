   Q.  Do you recollect whether in the course of the day on which you sent the two bars to Messrs. Bult they made any communication to you on the subject of them? A.  Yes, I do—I was not in a condition, after settling with Mrs. Abrahams, to return Messrs. Bult their money.
   COURT . Q.  Do you mean the 1200 l ? A.  Yes.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  At what rate did they buy of you? A.  At the rate of 77 s . 9 d . standard per ounce—I cannot form any judgment what would be the difference with Russian copper and silver—the quantity of copper was put in accidentally—I no doubt made a good profit—Mrs. Abra hams did not call on me for three, four, or five days after I paid her the six £100 notes and one hundred sovereigns—she then said that her father was very anxious for more money—at that time there had been some inquiries made about London—I told her there had been a great stir made about it, and I could not part with any more money or sell another ounce of gold at present—I told her not to call at present for any more money, I could not sell another ounce of gold—I felt very uneasy about it.
   Q.  After your son Isaac had taken what you sent to Cock and Johnson, did you receive a communication also from them? A.  I saw Mr. Cock myself—he made a communication to me about the gold on the Thursday—I afterwards sent my young man (Dean) to them—I think that was on the Friday—I was afterwards summoned to give an account of this matter at Lambeth-street—I received a summons on the Friday night to appear on the Saturday, to the best of my recollection—that was the Friday in Easter-week—I had my attorney with me when I attended—it was Mr. Plumtree on that day—you put some questions to me, and after some answers which I gave I declined answering any more questions—the magistrate let me go—on my receiving the summons on the Friday, before I went to the magistrate, I sent my son Isaac to the prisoner Moses' house—he brought me back a message from Moses—on the Saturday when I went before the Magistrate I gave directions to my son Selim, and Dean, about money, and in consequence of those directions I got back six £100 notes, and 75 l . in £5 notes, of the money I had paid Mrs. Abrahams, the self-same notes—I got them changed for sovereigns—I got rid of them—I paid some in to my bankers, and got change for them as well as I could—I had given my son Isaac directions on Easter Tuesday to get money for me—I sent to Cock and Johnson's the bars—I sent in other directions for money, to more than one, two, or three places—I know Mr. Woolf, a tailor in Regent-street—he is a nephew of my wife's—I sent to him among others—I told my son Isaac to go to him, and he brought me 120 l . in notes and sovereigns.
   Q.  Among the money you paid Mrs. Abrahams did you pay any of the notes your son brought you from Woolf? A.  Yes, no doubt I did, to the best of my recollection—I did not change them after they were brought to me before I gave them to her—it was on Easter Tuesday I sent to Woolf for money—on the following Wednesday, the next day, I returned him in this cheque—( looking at one )—the money I had borrowed.
