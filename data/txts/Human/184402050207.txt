 783. ANN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing 3 lbs.  weight of mutton, value 1 s  ., the goods of William Couldry ; and that she had been before con victed of felony. JOHN JENNINGS. I am in the service of Wm. Couldry, a batcher at Green wich. A little after seven o'clock, on Thursday evening, the 8th of Feb., the prisoner was going by the shop—she stopped at the corner of the board first, then at the middle of the board, took a breast of mutton, and walked away with it—I followed, and saw her cover her shawl over it, and slip it under her arm—it fell on the ground, and I stopped her.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How long have you been in the batcher line? A.  About six months—my master was in the room, having his tea—the prisoner appeared to have been drinking—my master was willing to let her go, and the officer did not like to do that—the mutton was outside the window. JOHN GRIFFIN( police-constable R  229.) I took the prisoner—I saw the mutton on the pavement—I asked how she came to take it—she said she did not care a d—n—she was the worse for liquor. MICHAEL THOMAS CANNON( police-constable R  157.) I produce a certifi cate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY  .    Before Mr. Recorder.
 784. CHARLES ELLIOTT was indicted for stealing 6 cheroots, value 1 s  ., the goods of Mary Phoebe Taylor Williams . MARY PHCEBE TAYLOR WILLIAMS. I keep a tobacconist's-shop in Black friars-road. On Saturday, the 6th of Jan., about seven in the evening, the pri soner came and asked how many cheroots I would let him have for 1 s .—I said six—he asked if I would not let him have seven—I said no—he said he would take a shilling's-worth—I counted them out, and laid them on the counter—he tried to change one, it being chipped—I would not allow that—I laid them on one side, to wrap them up—he snatched them up, threw down a bad shilling, and ran out—I do not give credit—I could not prevent his snatching them up, he did it while I turned—I laid the shilling on the counter while my brother ran after him—it was there about five minutes—my brother looked at it when be returned without the prisoner—I then threw it into a drawer where I had two other bad shillings, two half-crowns, and a sixpence—I gave the policeman all the bad shillings I had from that drawer—I saw it was a bad shilling before he got out, and called out, "This is a bad one," loud enough for him to hear—I cannot swear which of the shillings he gave me, as I threw it with the others, but I gave them all to the policeman—it was about the same brightness as the others.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is there a bell to your shop? A.  Yes—it rings when people come in, but it goes to the back of the house—I have sometimes a hundred customers in a day—I am not positive that I had ever seen the prisoner before, but I think I have—he was a minute and a half, or more, in the shop—I am quite certain of him—I charged him at Union-hall with passing bad money, and robbery.
