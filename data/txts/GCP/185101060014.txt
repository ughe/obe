336. ABRAHAM GRAINGER, unlawfully having counterfeit coin
in his possession.
Messrs. Bodkin and CLERK conducted the Prosecution.
Thomas Murphy. I am a hawker, and live in Wentworth-street. On
22nd Dec. I went with the prisoner to a public-house--at the door he
took a leathern purge out of his pocket, and a shilling dropped down-I
took it up, and offered it to him-he said, "Never mind; that will do "-
I ordered a pot of beer, and tendered that shilling-I was told it was bad.
and it was given back to me- the prisoner had gone out when I put the
shilling on the table-I saw him in about a minute, and I told him he had
no business to give me a bad shilling in that way-he said he did not
know it was bad ; he had taken it in change.
Prisoner. You dropped the shilling out of your own hand. Witness.
No; I picked it up off the grating in the street.
HENRY JACKSON (police-sergeant, K 11). On the evening of 22nd
Dec. I was called to the public-house-the prisoner was just leaving-
asked if he had got any bad money about him, as the landlord had told
me there was a bad shilling passed-he said, "No"-I put my hand into
his left-hand trowsers pocket, and took out three bad shillings; and as we
were going to the station, he dropped these two shillings from his person
-I took them up.
Prisoner. Q. Did I not say I did not know they were bad? A. Not
at that time.
William WEBSTER. These five shillings are all bad, and four of them
are from the same mould.
Prisoner's Defence. This man, Murphy, has been parted from his wife,
and he has been convicted; he asked me to go out with him; he went
into the public-house, and called for the beer, and then he turned round
on me, and told the policeman that I had bad money; I did not know it
was bad; he spends more money than any man in the neighbourhood, and
he has not done an hour's work for some time; he goes about passing bad
money.
Thomas MURPHY re-examined. I am a hawker-I worked in the
London Docks for six years, I have not been charged with any offence,
either here or anywhere else, only with an assault on my wife.
ANN RICHARDSON. I am the bar-maid at the public-house. Murphy
gave me the shilling; it was bad, and I returned it to him when the
prisoner left the house, the landlord followed him.
