   MR. GODSON . Q.  Did you not give the prisoner notice to quit your house because it was being put  in a bad character, by certain women frequenting it? A.  I did not—I was not aware that women frequented it, only as lodgers—I am quite sure the prisoner was my tenant for the shed—it was fastened by a padlock, which he put on about five years ago—I have seen that shed open for days together—the prisoner was employed by Grissel and Peto, doing Government work in the yard. GEORGE KAYSER. I am a smith, and live in Guilford-street. I have known the prisoner eight years—he bore a very good character while he was employed at the Parliament-house, and as a master for himself at Brewer's green, Westminster, where he had a shop to do mason's tools—after that I did not see him for some years.
   COURT . Q.  How long is it since he was at Westminster? A.  Seven or eight years ago—I do not know at what time he went away—the policemen came and asked where he came from—he said he worked at Woolwich Dock-yard, and lived at Woolwich—I am not certain whether he told them at what place—he said he brought these things from his house at Woolwich—he said he had worked in Westminster before, where he was in business, and he had a quantity of steel by him that he wished to dispose of, because he was going to move from his house where he was living, and he wished to give up his shop.
   MR. GODSON . Q.  Have you had any steel with the broad arrow on it? A.  No—if I had seen any I should know it—I did not expect the prisoner—he came accidentally to me that night—I had not seen the metal till the policemen opened it—I never saw the broad arrow on it—I saw them take two or three pieces out of the bag—I did not see any mark on them—if I had bough that metal I should have worked it into mason's tools—it is as good as other steel, it is the same as what we buy—I have seen Mr. Sims now—I did not know him before—I make a great many mason's tools—I buy steel of merchants and other persons that have it to sell—I have been to Woolwich once or twice—I have been in Coleman-street—I have known where the prisoner lived about two or three months—I was down at Wool wich, about a week or nine days before the 24th of April—he had a good deal of steel in his smith's shop—I did not see any copper.
   MR. PARNELL . Q.  You did not think it was your business to rummage the shed and place? A.
