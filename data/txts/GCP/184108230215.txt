Undey appeared to me to have been drinking-he was not actually
drunk. but I am confident he was in liquor-I afterwards saw the body of
my friend he had just been taken out-he had been in the water between
half an hour and three-quarters of an hour, and was quite dead, but he had
been taken out once before that--we did all we could for him.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I suppose you were naturally
very much agitated at this misfortune? 4. Yes--I had not seen Undey
before, that I am aware of I often go to fish there, but I never observed
the steamers-I have always been intent on fishing, so that a steamer
might approach from behind without my seeing it-a steamer makes a
noise, but there are other noises ; so that it might not attract attention, if
intent on fishing--the punt was moored across the river-I did not ob-
serve any other steamer there I did not observe the Vivid- she might
have been within sixty or eighty yards of us, and I not see ber-I did not
see her after the accident, I was so agitated and engaged in looking after
the body, not after steamers-before the accident happened the punt was
moored firm-I saw one steamer, but I believe that was the Richmond,
which caused the accident- I did not see a second-it might have been
there, but I was so agitated at the time.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. What were you sitting in? 4.
An ordinary-sized punt, as long as from me to his Lordship, and about
three or four feet wide-- it was a flat-bottomed one, and I should think
drew no water, but I do not understand it-I have fished there many times
before in the same situation-the punt was moored stem and stern above
the bridge, right athwart the stream-one of the poles to which the punt
was moored had given way, and she turned and lay with the stream; but
the fisherman came up again, and set her exactly in the same place as she
was before, as one of the poles had not moved-it was done by pushing the
pole in the river, and making a piece of rope fast to her there were three
chairs in the punt, there were no sculls or oars in her she was not moved
by sculls, but pushed along, in shallow water, by means of a boat-hook-she
was square stem and stern-I know the place where the Vivid moors,
when she comes up to receive her passengers--I have seen boats lying
there it is about 150 yards from where we were moored-I cannot
say whether there are shallows about there--we had only been fishing
about an hour when the accident happened—we had been fishing before,
but in consequence of the tide not suiting, we were obliged to discontinue
fishing, and then resumed it again at about four o'clock-I do not know
Whether it was about flood-We resumed it when the fisherman told us
We might do 8o-the fisherman put us on board-I cannot say whether
the punt was injured at all-I have not seen it since—if I had had the
curiosity to look, I could not have done it, for I was taken out of the punt
a very few minutes after the accident happened she was filling with
water-she did not go down I cannot say whether she would not go
down-I did not then observe any mark on the punt--the steamer's
slriking her dragged her from her moorings- there may have been some
injury done to her, but I do not know whether there was or not-I have
not looked-the chairs we were sitting on were only a few inches above
the level of the water-I should not think the ripple of a sailing-barge
Would place us in very great jeopardy, sitting in that position--several
mers had passed us in the day-there was a little swell with them,
