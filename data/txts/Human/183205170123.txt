 1398.  THOMAS BENNETT and ALEXANDER PAWNS were indicted for stealing, on the 15th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of William Stephenson , from his person.
 WILLIAM STEPHENSON. I live at Old Brentford. I attended a meeting of the Political Union at Brentford, on the 15th of May; we went to the One Tun - I had a large red silk handkerchief, spotted with white - we went from there to the Red Lion; I did not see the prisoners till I returned to the One Tun.
 THOMAS DUNKLEY. I live at  Old Brentford. I was at the One Tun, and saw the prosecutor; I saw Bennett take a handkerchief from his pocket, and give it to Pawns, who put it into his flap - I told the prosecutor; he said,"I have not lost it," and just at that moment he was called out to take tea, and in going along he felt for his handkerchief, and found, as he says, a book instead - I could not see what sort of handkerchief it was.
 Cross-examined by MR. ROWE. Q. What time was the handkerchief taken? A. About half-past five o'clock; it was a public room; there might be sixty or seventy persons there- I took it to be a Belcher handkerchief, but I only saw the corner of it; the prosecutor did not put his hand into his pocket when I told him of it - he came back in about half an hour, and said he had lost it; I never said it would be more profitable for me to attend the Old Bailey, and get 5s. a day, than to follow my trade.
 MR. ROWE to WILLIAM STEPHENSON. Q. Where did you see Dunkley? A. At the One Tun - I knew him before the gave me notice that I had lost my handkerchief -I felt outside my pocket, and felt a book; I said I had not lost it - I afterwards found it had been stolen; it was a full sized silk handkerchief - I missed it about half an hour after I was told of it.
 Bennett's Defence. I went to the One Tun; I sat there till some person said that those who did not belong to the meeting, were to leave, and I got up and went away.
 GEORGE TAYLOR. I live with my father, who keeps a broker's shop at Old Brentford. I have known Bennett from his infancy; I have heard Dunkley say several times, that he should like to get a good job at the Old Bailey, to get his crown a day; and it would be a d-d sight better for him than to set upon his seat.
 COURT. Q. What is Dunkley? A. He is a kind of a translator of old shoes into new ones; he had made new shoes; I heard him say this two or three months back.
   THOMAS DUNKLEY re-examined. I deny it; there is not a word of truth in it - I never said so.
   WILLIAM STEPHENSON. I had in my pocket a book belonging to the society, and the substance of the book made me think I had not lost my handkerchief; the prisoners were taken on the Tuesday after.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1399.  BRIDGET COSGROVE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 1 gown piece, value 7s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 2s.; 1 cap, value 4s.; 1 shawl, value 13s.; 1 pocket-book, value 1s.; 1 tooth-pick, value 1s., and 2 sovereigns, the property of Mary Ann Sullivan.
   MARY ANN SULLIVAN. I lodge in Moor-street, Bryanstone-square. I have known the prisoner three years; I met her in Oxford-street on the night of the 30th of April, between the hours of eleven and twelve - she said her husband and her had fallen out, and he would not let her in doors that night; I took her home, and she slept in the same bed with me - my shawl was on a line in the room, and my gown-piece on the top of a box; I had two sovereigns in my pocket - I fastened the door with a bolt; I awoke between five and six o'clock in the morning - she was gone; I got up - I missed all these articles.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you ever see me in the streets of London? A. Yes, I have seen you many times; you have spoken to me, and have drank in the Yorkshire Grey.
   WILLIAM TRODD(Police-constable C 84). On the 3rd or 4th of May I went to where the prisoner lodged, in Oxford-buildings; I saw her daughter, who said her mother was out, and she had been out drunk all night - I sent her daughter to tell her I wanted to speak to her; she then absconded - I saw her in the street, but she ran from me; I afterwards took her from a brother-officer, and asked if she knew any thing of this robbery; she said No, but she had been out on the spree.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
 ELIZA KEEFE. I am a servant out of situation - I live in White Lion-street, Soho. I went to the prisoner's house on the last Friday in April, in Oxford-buildings: I slept there four nights - I came home on the 1st of May; I did not lodge with her that night, but I saw her that night when she was going to bed - the next morning, before she was up, I gave her 2s. to get her children something to eat- I do not know the prosecutrix; the prisoner's husband is a pavior, and gone to work at Liverpool; they had no quarrel.
   MARGARET DALEY. I saw the prisoner on the Monday night - she had no victuals; she has eight children, and one in arms - her husband had left her only 2s; I said if she would send one of her little girls with me I would let her have 2s. or 3s. - I took the little girls, gave her 3s., and went to see her home; I saw her and Keefe go to bed -I lodge in White Lion-street; my husband is foreman at the gas-house - I have the prisoner's duplicates, and I find that on the Monday morning, at half-past eight o'clock, she pawned her shoes for 1s. 6d.
   MARY ANN SULLIVAN re-examined. Q. When was it you fell in with the prisoner, on the 30th of April or the 1st of May? A.
