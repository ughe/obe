John UTLEY. I am a publican, and live in Eaton-place, Kent-street.
On Friday night, the 19th of Feb., about a quarter-past eleven o'clock, I saw
Capningham at my house-she had a quartern of the best rum, and paid for
it with a half-sovereign-after I had given her the change she asked if I
would have the goodness to take care of 21. 10s. for her till the morning, as
she was afraid she should lose it-she produced three half-sovereigns and a
sovereign-she said there was one very small one; did I think it was a 78.
piece or a half-sovereign-I told her I thought it was a half-sovereign, and a
good one-I did not notice of what reign it was-it was very small--I have
seen one as small before, and never but one-I put the money on the shelf
behind, and kept it for her-she did not come for it, and I took it to the
Police-court, as I thought it had been stolen-I afterwards came to Newgate
to see Cunningham--she was the woman that left the money with me after
she had left this 21. 108. with me to keep for her, she went out, and returned
in about five minutes, and the prisoner Williams was with her they whis-
pered together for a long time-I could not hear much that they said, they
watched me too well, but I heard Williams distinctly say, "I don't think he
will know me again"- I think they had a pot of beer, and went away about
a quarter before twelve o'clock.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you ever seen either of them before? A. I
had seen Williams many times I knew him well—I do not think that I
had seen Cunningham.
GEORGE RADFORD (police-sergeant M 19.) In consequence of informa-
tion, I went at half-past one o'clock in the night of the 19th of Feb., to No.
55, Ann-street, Henry-street, Kent-road, and found Cunningham in bed with
Williams-I commenced searching the bed -- Cunningham said I need not
search, I should find nothing-Williams asked if I was going to take his girl
from him-he had given her 28. to sleep with him for the night-he said he
met her in White-street, and they went into the Grapes, and bad a quartern
of gin together-I took Cunningham to the station-I said it was on suspi.
cion of robbing a publican--she said she bad not robbed any person, that she
had left the railway about half-past ten o'clock-she said Williams had given
her 28., that she picked him up in White-street, and she would not have taken
him home for 28. if she had had any money-I did not know anything about
a charge against Williams at that time when I took Cunningham, Williams
went away-between that time and half-past four, I received some informa-
tion about Williams- I went to the same house I had taken Cunningham from
-I found the room door shut, and a chair propped against it to prevent its
opening-I got in at the window, and found Williams lying on the bed with
his clothes on-he pretended to be asleep-we shook him, and got him into
the yard, and told him we wanted him for being concerned in the robbery of
