1128. CHARLES HARRIS was indicted for stealing, on the   13th of February , 1/2 a pint of gin, value 10 d  .; and 1 bottle, value 3 d  .; the goods of Thomas Bell Kirk.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS BELL KIRK. I keep the King's Arms tavern, Peckham-rye—the prisoner was my pot-boy for about seven months—it was part of his duty to carry out beer and spirits to my customers—sometimes I supplied him with the goods, sometimes my wife, and sometimes my barmaid, he never was allowed to supply himself—on Sunday, the 13th of February, is consequence of information, I went to the back-kitchen, about one o'clock I saw there a soda-water bottle and a ginger-beer bottle with gin in them, on a shelf, covered over with an old tin dish-cover—I tasted both of them—nobody had any right to have gin there—about eight o'clock in consequence of what Coombes said, when the prisoner came up out of the kitchen, I stopped him, and said to him, "What have you got?" he said, "Nothing"—I said, "What, nothing?" he said, "Yes"—I searched hit, and out of his coat-pocket I took the soda-water bottle containing half-a-pint of gin—he said "That is mine, I have paid for it"—he did not say who he had paid, or when—I said I would take the risk upon myself, and I took it into the bar—he went out with his beer—about nine o'clock I sent for an officer and gave him in charge—I had previously sent for one, but could not get one.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When you found the soda-water bottle, did you not say you would keep it? A.  Yes—he said, "If you do you must pay me for it, as I have paid for it"—I allowed him to do his usual work on Monday—I did not settle his account at night for his Monday's work—he gave me half-a-crown in part of it—he was in custody then, it was about nine o'clock—my wife or the bar-maid are constantly in the bar—it is their duty to give out the goods to the pot-boy—it is not the general practice before we deliver out the goods to receive the money the pot-boy, except what is taken in the tap-room—it is put down against him—he may either pay for it or have it put down—the gin was kept in the cellar—he has access to the beer-cellar, not to where the gin is—I did not persuade him to come into my service—I have a customer of the name of Nicholls, of Peckham-rye—I believe he employs two or three persons—he lives half-a-mile from me. PHILLIPPA KIRK. I am the wife of Thomas Bell Kirk—I am in the ha bit of serving in the bar—on Sunday, the 13th of February, I did not give any gin to the prisoner for a man of Mr. Nicholls, nor for any one—I did not hand over to him any gin for a man named Stebberford, for which he paid me—I did not receive any payment from him on Saturday for any gin—I settle with him every evening—his account on that evening was a small one—I remarked that it was rather small.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you examined before the Magistrate? A.  Yes—it very seldom happens that the prisoner pays me at the time—it does happen so sometimes—if he pays me I do not book it or any ac count of it—I do not recollect handing any gin to him on Saturdays—I will not swear it—we have a very considerable business, and are selling gin great part of the day—he had to take gin out, and to take it into the tap-room—that he pays for at the bar—I have no recollection of serving him with 10 d . worth of gin that day—I did not send him with any gin and change for a sovereign.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did he bring any soda-water bottles to have gin in it? A.  No, nor a ginger-beer bottle.
