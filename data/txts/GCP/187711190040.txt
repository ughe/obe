OWDEN, Mayor.
JAMES WILSON. I am a traveller and collector, employed by Stephen-
son and Son, and live at Waterley. Lane, Birmingham-I remember a
person coming to the office and settling an account with Stephenson with
reference to Edwards-Leach is not he-I heard a conversation between
him and Stephenson, and afterwards saw the person go into Stephenson's
private office-I have not seen him before or since-I saw some cards
there.
Cro88-examined by MR. WADDY. No name passed.
HARRY HERDERT JOHN STEPHENSON. I am the eldest son of the
prisoner Stephenson, and carried on business with my father for
some years, I was 24 last August-my father has been in business
at Dale End, Birmingham, as long as I can remember—we have
shipping and other orders--the turnover is between 40,0001. and 50,0001.
per year-on account of the bankers' charges we are in the habit of pay-
ing away cheques--we deal in general goods, such as grocery provisions
of all kinds, and wines and spirits, and cigars—we generally buy for
cash-I was present when T. Edwards's card was brought-Leach was
not the man who brought it-he brought samples of tea, and father looked
over them-I was not present when the order was given-there was an
invoice the traveller came several times he did not give his own name
these 12 invoices came with the goods-I did not know of the name of
Marshall till my attention was called to it-it is usual to put the carman's
name on the delivery note-each invoice has the usual mem. on it, such as
"The above are duly forwarded by the Midland Railway Company to
your address"-the goods were marked off against the invoices upon
arrival-I was present on the 30th of July when the account was made
up-the pencillings on the invoices were my father's, and the totals
brought forward, and there is the receipt for the payment of that invoice
to the amount of 851. 98. 8d.--I was present when that was paid, not when
the 501. was paid on account-it is dated July 23, that was on a Monday
- both are receipted as cash payments; the 501. is signed " W. S.”-
the pencilling carries forward eight invoices, and leaves a balance of
4161. 98. 5d.-I called over our customer's cheques to my father-I
called over the six cheques-1751. odd was paid in gold and silver-this
(produced) is a counterfoil of the cheque of the 30th July for 1821. 128.
the traveller went away with the money and the cheques--there was a
small allowance made of 8l. for goods sent which were not ordered-
nothing was said about any other goods being at the railway-station-
no invoice was sent to my father in respect of the goods which were
stopped at the railway-station-on 2nd August & gentleman came fruin
London at my father's request-I went to the bank and stopped a cheque
there I was present when my father took Mr. Howard through the
premises, and the private marks of the original dealers were found on
certain tea chests and other things there they were not separated from the
other stock, and my father promised that they should not be disposed of,
and they are now as they were when Mr. Howard saw them-I also
showed them to Mr Spendler und Mr. Hudson, and also the invoices
my father was paid 28. & pound, with the duty, for the Assam tea in
chest No. 1101, and 28. 3d. a pound for the 10 boxes of gunpowder, in
chest No. 1331-I cannot recollect when my father was summoned to the
Mansion House-Mr. Hudson and Mr. Spendler called two or tbree days
after that-my father entered into contract for buildings in the spring of
