EMMA CLEGHORN. I am a milliner in the Burlington Aroade I have
been a customer of Harvey, Nicholls, and Co., for 25 years I did not
authorise my daughter to go there on 27th February and cash a cheque for
me-I have casbed cheques for her in the way of business-she has done
a little business on her own account-I think it is about six months since
I cashed one for her; that was in exchange for some fur.
Cross-examined. She was married about three years ago, when she WAS
16, and was very unfortunate in her married life, and her home was broken
up, and she came to live with me she put herself under training as a pro-
fessional singer, and pursued that calling-up to that time she was a very
good girl she has sung in public and met with great sucoess that took
her a good deal from home-it is only very lately that she has got into
company that she would not have done had she remained at bome--she bas
been very good up to the last month or 80—she remained with me until
last Christmas-if she had asked me to cash a cheque for her I should have
done it-I was at Southport at the time the cheque was presented at
Harvey and Nicholls's—I am very well to do--if my daughter had applied
to me for money she could have had it-I have never refused her money
I knew nothing of her connection with the male prisoner.

ANNIE JACKSON. I am a servant in the employ of Mrs. Nash, of 11,

she was going by the name of Mrs. Lonsdale she stayed nine days
-- one day my mistress asked her for some silver that she had lent

had any—that was the man who was living with her; he came on the
Sunday afternoon she came down and said her friend had not any money

all she owed her she said her friend had 3001, in the bank in Lombard
