WILLIAM HOLLAND. On 26th Feb., I was in the service of Mr. Dale. Between eight and nine o'clock that evening I came out of the tap-room, and saw the prisoner at the bar with a young woman—she asked me to go and get change for a 5 l .-note—my master gave me the note—I took it to Mr. Beaumont's, and asked Weller, the barman, to change it—he put the note on the desk, and wrote master's name on it—I did not see what he wrote, but I saw him writing—he put it into the desk—a young woman who came out of the parlour gave me the change, four sovereigns, and the rest in silver—I brought it back, and placed it on the counter before the prisoner and the young woman—I did not see what became of it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you went to the House of Detention, did you pass by the prisoner without recognising him? A.  Yes, at first; my master said, "Look well at him"—the first time I went by him very quick—I looked at him again, and knew him—I looked at him two or three minutes the second time—I looked at his cell the same as I did at the others, but my attention was not attracted till my master said that to me.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did your master say that to you at any other cell you passed by? A.  Yes; he said a good many times,"Look well at them." JOHN WELLER. I am barman to Mr. Beaumont, of the Red Lion, Guild-ford-street. On the evening of 26th Feb., Holland came for change for a 5 l .-note, which he handed to me—my master's sister gave him four sove reigns, and 1 l .-worth of silver—I wrote on the note,"Mr. Dale, Marchmont-street"—this is it—master was about to pay it away on 6th March, and the clerk detected it to be a forgery.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you changed any other 5 l .-note that day? A.  I do not know; we change them very frequently—we always put on them the name of the person we take them from—I changed no other for Mr. Dale that night. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Transported for Fifteen Years .
