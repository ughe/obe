340. GEORGE POYNDER HALL, stealing 1 trowsen, and
other goods, value 31. 1 Is.; the goods of Charles Samuel and another.
MR. PARRY Conducted the Prosecution.
ROBERT RICHARDS. I am in the employ of Charles and Michael Samuel,
of Ludgate-hill. On 2nd Jan., about seven o'clock in the evening, I wu
employed to take a suit of clothes to Mr. Hall—I rang the bell at the door,
a woman came, and I gave her the clothes—my master bad ordered me not
to deliver them without cash—the woman took them up-stairs. and brought
this note Mr. Hall, 5, Poultry, City. Pay the bearer 31.
for said it would not do, I must either have the clothes or
money (that was the first time I had intimated that)—she said his father
always paid for his clothes, and he was not Of age ; and if I were to be in the
Poultry by eight, I could get the money—that did not satisfy me, and I said
I would take the clothes to his father's, and if he paid, I would bring then
back—the prisoner came down dressed in the clothes, but I did not know
them, as I had not seen them—I said to him, I must have the clothes orthe
money back ' '—he said, You will have neither the clothes nor the money;
you, nor no one else; if you will be at my father's before eight o'clock, you
will have the money," and if I did not get the money in the Poultry, I wu
to go to a tripe-warehouse, 130, Bishopsgate-street—l went with him there—
he said his father was not in the way to speak to him—we then both wentto
my master, at Ludgate-hill—he stayed outside—I went in and told Mr.
Samuels.

street? A. An elderly woman came to the door—the prisoner asked if bil
father was in—she said, Yes ' '—this bill was sent with the clothes; the

COURT to Mr. SAMUEL. Q. Have you applied to the father for this

JOHN SEACOMB. I am in Messrs. Samuel's employ. The prisoner came
