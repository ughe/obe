JOHN THOMAS, and THOMAS BELCHER were indicted for burgla-
riously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Charlton, about
the hour of one in the night of the 17th of March, at Woolwich, with in-
tent to steal, and stealing therein, 1 wateh-chain, value 5l. ; 1 guard-
chain, value 58.; 6 brooches, value 21. 28. ; 1 buekle, value 28. 1 soent-
bottle, value 1s. 6d.; 1 cornelian heart, value 53.; and 1 head ornament,

Joan CHARLTON. I am a jeweller and watchmaker, and live at No. 8,
Wellington-street, Woolwich. On the 17th of March, I went to bed about
eleven o'clock at night, as near as I can judge I was the last person up in
the house--I examined the doors and windows that night--they were all
safe--the fastenings were all on, and the bolts right--the shop window is a
projecting square window, about six feet long, fastened with a bar and two
bolts at the end ; and the door was fastened with two bolts and a lock-I was
awoke about half-past one o'clock in the morning by the cracking of
glass--I immediately got out of bed, and ran to the shop door my
bed-room is on the ground floor, about seven yards from the window
-when I got to the shop door, I heard the glass trickling as if it
was outside the window - I put off the top bolt of the door, and
heard some persons running from the door, from the outside, very quickly
I could not tell whether it was more than one personat that mo-
ment I gave an alarm, as loud as I possibly could, to the family up-
stairs-my father and mother, and brother-in-law-I then unbolted the
lower bolt, and unlocked the door-I went outside, and by the light of
the gas-lamp, which was about thirty yards distant, on the other side of
the way, I distinctly saw a quantity of glass immediately under the bar
of the shop-window-there was a piece of gold among the glass I took
that up, and came inside again, and got a light-I saw nobody--when I
