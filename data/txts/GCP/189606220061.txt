JESSE JAMES Flood. I am assistant to William Manning, trading
as Carpenter and Wesley, opticians, 24, Regent Street on March 31st I
received this letter. (From H. Stracey Phillips, requesting them to send
an artist's pocket diminishing glass.) We sent one valued about 9.-on
April 8th I got this letter. (Prom H. Stracey Phillips, ordering a pocket
magnifier with three lenses, suitable for examining muiniatures, etc., and a
Claude Lorraine mirror, a silver pocket compars, not to exceed 258. or 308.,
and a Urinomater.) We sent those articles, which caine to £4 178, 6d., by
hand, and with an invoice, on which was, “Terms, ready money "that
means we collect at an interval-we have not been paid for those goods-
I did not know, when I parted with those goods, that the prisoner was an
undischarged bankrupt; I had no knowledge of him—we thought we had
had business with him, but we found we had not—if we had known his
real position we should not have sent the goods without payment.
Cross-examined. Our customers take twelve months' credit-the first
application that would have been made in the ordinary course for this.
money would have been in June, at the end of the first quarter. d
