STEVENS TRIPP. I am a solicitor at 12, Burleigh Street, Covent Garden,
and a Commissioner for Oaths in the Supreme Court of Judicature-I
administered the oath to some person calling himself John Claxton, whose
signature was attached to this document on the 8th March, 1878, and the
person producing the same swore that this was his signature, and that the
contents of the affidavit were true.
Cross-examined. I do not know if he was alone, and have no recollection
of the prisoner, I swear so many affidavits.
HENRY SUTTON. I am a jobmaster, of Harley Mews, North Harley
Street, and know the prosecutor and the prisoner-I knew the prisoner
at the end of 1877, and had dealing with him before then-1
recollect his selling me a bay mare for 501., I think, and a black horse
for 401.-I paid him for both of them and took receipts, but I cannot
find the receipts-some time in the same year Mr. Stevens came to me, and I
then had the receipts, and allowed Mr. Stevens to see them, and left them
on my desk for him to copy them-I found him the paper for the pur-
pose—this (produced) is the paper; it had this stamp upon it. (5th
December, 1877, 401. Black horse, October 7, 1877, 587. Bay mare, both
signed" John Claxton")—I have the horse now, but sold the mare because
it went lame-I did not return them to the prisoner on the ground that
they were unsound or obtain the money back from him.
Cross-examined. I have been threatened with proceedings by the prose-
ention after I was summoned at Bow Street-I have purchased a number
of horses from the prisoner - I have known him four or five years, and
never knew anything against him or had any dispute with him-the money
referred to in these receipts was paid by instalments.
Re-examined. I had paid him all the money before Christmas, 1877-I
have a good many transactions in horse-dealing.
