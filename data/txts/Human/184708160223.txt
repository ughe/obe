WILLIAM SMITH. I am in the service of the Dock Company.  1961. ELIJAH KEATES was indicted for stealing 12 ounces of sugar, value 3 d  .; the goods of the London Dock Company .
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. SAMUEL EGERTON( policeman H  193.) On the 1st of Aug. I was on duty in the London Docks—the prisoner was on duty as a constable there—he had been doing duty on the East-quay the night before—he came to the station—I followed him, and saw him go to his cupboard, unlock it, put his hand in, pull something out, put it into his hat, and put his hat on a shelf over the dresser in the kitchen—he then said he would go and buy something for his breakfast—he put on his hat and was going out—we met the inspector in the passage, I told him—he ordered the prisoner to pull his hat off, and in it was this sugar—the inspector asked him where he got it—he said he picked it up the other night as he was coming off duty on the North-quay.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q.  What time was this? A.  Between seven and eight o'clock on Sunday morning—I was about a yard and a half behind him—I should say the cupboard is about a yard wide—he opened the door quite wide—I saw that plainly—there are six samples of sugar—he was in the same division as I am, and was engaged at the same place—his pay as a policeman as 18 s . 7 1/2 d . a week—he had been seven or eight months em ployed in the docks. GEORGE MADDOX. I am an inspector of police. I saw the prisoner's hat taken off, these samples were in it; he said he did not know what it was—I found it was sugar—he said he picked it up in the docks—it was his duty to take care of the property in the docks, and to report anything he found to me.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How are these things got at? A.  These samples are nailed on the outside of each cask, with a nail through them, and are samples of what the sugar is in the cask—if he picked any of these up, it was his duty to bring them to me.
