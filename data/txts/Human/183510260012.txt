On the morning of the 19th of October I saw the prisoner in the Blackfriars'-road with a basket said some things belonging to his mate, and he brought them from Guay'-inn-lane—I asked where he was going to take them—he said to the Cus tom-house—that his mate was a seaman on board the Columbine  steam-ves sel, and that he belonged to the same vessel—I took him to the station-house, and went with him to Gray'n-inn-lane, and there heard of this robbery—I took him to the prosecutor's, and as the prosecutor returned with us to the station-house, I heard the prisoner tell him that there was part of the pane of glass out before, and he took out the remainder—I produce the articles—the Magistrate ordered me to give up two pair of shoes—I have the boots and other things. JOHN SUMPTER re-examined.  These are my boots, and were taken out of my house that night—I had them on that day—these caps are mine—this pair of scissors and this book belong to my sister, whose husband's name is Charles Smith—they were in my house—this razor belongs to Thomas Porter, who lodged in my house—they were all in the room.
