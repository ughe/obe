CHALLIS, Mayor.
GEORGE TESSIER. I am clerk at Smith, Payne, and Smith's. Mr.J.
Dalgleish banks with us-on 22nd Jan. the prisoner brought a check al
2261. odd, to be cashed-I cashed it, and gave him two 1001. notes, and
the rest gold and silver--(referring to his book) the notes were Nos. 94932.
and 94,279, dated 10th May, 1852.
RICHARD ADYE BAILEY. I am clerk in the Issue Department of the
Bank of England. produce the notes Nos. 94932 and 94279 (produced
-on the face of both of them I find "Benazuz, 10, South-place, Finsbury.
EDWARD WEBB re-examined. I believe this name and address of
Benazuz on these notes, to be in the prisoner's writing.
DOUGLAS WORDINGHAM. I am a clerk in the Bank of England. I gare
change for these notes in sovereigns and half-sovereigns-I gave it in gold
because that was asked for.
JOHN CARRICK. I am landlord of the Swan Inn, West Malling, in Kent
The prisoner came there by the 8 o'clock evening coach, on 22nd Jan. -
remained till the 29th, when he was taken into custody-after he had been
there a day or two, he gave the name of Melton-he did not give any other
pame-Wilmot was the name he gave; he did not give both names; the
name he gave was Wilmot-I did not know him by the name of Melton &
all-in consequence of a description I saw in an advertisement in the Timer
I looked into the prisoner's carpet bag- I 'found a bill there; and in conse-
quence, gave information--two or three days after the prisoner came, be
gave me a bag of gold to take care of, supposed to be 2001.-he after-
wards asked me for the bag, and gave it me back again-I delivered it up
before the Magistrate.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe he had been at your house before? 4.
About five weeks before, I knew him.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you know his name? A. No; I never
heard it before.
John YOUNG. I am the constable of West Malling. On Saturday, 29th
Jan., in consequence of information given me by Mr. Carrick, I took the pri-
soner into custody at Addington, which is about two miles from West Malling
- he was in company with two more--I told him from information I had
received, and an advertisement I saw, I should take him into custody- I took
him into custody, and confined him in the lock-up at West Malling-
searched him, and found on him 221. 10s. in gold, and 35s. in silver-I saw
the superintendent of the police at West Malling find fifty-nine sovereigns in
the prisoner's carpet bag, which were delivered to me, and are now in the
hands of Webb, the Mansion-house officer- the bag had been brought by me
from the bedroom at Mr. Carrick's.
JOHN CARRICK re-examined. It was the prisoner's bedroom from which
the bag was taken.
HENRY WEBB (City policeman.) I took possession, amongst other things
of the bankers' ticket for a bill of 661., and also a purse-I am the officer
who has produced to-day such of the articles as have been called for- I took
care of the 1831. odd-the difference between that and 2261. is gone.
MR. DEARSLEY submitted that the evidence disclosed no legal offence; it was
clearly not a case of larceny from Dalgleish, because the prisoner was ordered by
Mr. Dalgleish's clerk, Webb, to take the check to Benazuz, and he delivered ;
nor could it be a larceny from Benazuz, he, after receiving it, having requested in
prisoner to change it, saying it would save him the trouble. He also submitted
that it was not a case of embezzlement, one solitary case not being sufficient to
constitute the prisoner the servant of Benazuz.
