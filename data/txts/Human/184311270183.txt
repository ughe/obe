 267. EDWARD DWYER was indicted for the wilful murder of James Dwyer .
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. JOHN SPELLING. I am a labourer, and live at Green-bank, Tooley-street. On 24th Oct, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, I was at Havill's public-house, the King's Head, Green-bank—I went into the tap-room, and saw several people there—the prisoner was there when I went in, smoking his pipe—he had a pint of ale and a glass before him on the table—I knew him before—he filled me a glass of ale, which I drank—-another working man went in with me—the prisoner drank with us both—I cannot tell how much he drank, but we had three pots of beer between four of us—the wife of the man who went with me had come in before we had done drinking—after being there some time, several people came in—the prisoner's wife came in a little before nine, with a baby in her arms, and her mother came in directly after—when his wife came in she put the baby out of her arms into the prisoner's arms, and blowed  him up—she scolded him—I cannot tell her exact words, but she got hold of the hair of his head behind, and punched him in the face very hard—I cannot tell how many times—I think thirteen or fourteen times in the face—I cannot recollect what she said at the time, but she was crying, and was very much bad tempered with him—I do not recollect her calling him names, but the mother called him a murderer, a Greenacre, a man-eater, and a whoremonger, and, after her calling him these names, he called her a b—y w—c—she then ran and got hold of a quart pot, abused him as much as she could, came towards him, and hit at him with the pot—I got hold of her, and told her neither man or woman in the house should injure him—I prevented her striking him—his wife and mother kicked up a gallows row , and at last the landlord turned them out—the prisoner then had the baby in his arms, and was smoking his pipe—he shed tears, and said, "Tears cannot Help but come down"—after that he went outside the door, as far as the corner of the house, with the baby in his arms—I told him to come into the tap-room again, and we would have another pot of beer, and to bring the baby in, as it was too late at night to have it out—he came in, and when he got in a woman offered to take charge of the child—he gave it her, and she suckled it—she did not keep it above two minutes, I think, because she had a baby of her own crying—when he had the child back, there was a little girl, an aunt of the child, there—we told him to give the child to the aunt, and she would take good care of it—he gave it her—she had it for a few minutes—the aunt's mother came in and blowed  her up—the aunt was the sister of the prisoner's wife—the mother-in-law came in and blew  the aunt up, and hit her on the shoulder—she came in again, and made the girl give the child to the prisoner, which she did—they kicked up a row , and the publican turned the lot of them out—the prisoner shed tears, and said again, "Tears cannot help to come down"—when we had been there some time, he said he wanted to go out—we did not want him to go out, fearing the child, which he still had, would catch cold—he told Havill, the publican, he insisted on going out—Havill told him to give the child to the woman who had suckled it before—he said he would not, and nobody but himself should do for the child, it was his child, and he would do for it himself, and nobody else should—he asked Havill's liberty to go out—Havill spoke to him—I cannot say what he said, and we got up to let him pass
