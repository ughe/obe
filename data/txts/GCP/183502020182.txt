Fourth Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
650. JOHN JUSTICE and WILLIAM PALMER were indicted for
stealing, on the 4th of February, at St. Mary, Newington, 26lbs, weight of
soap, value 8s.; and 21lbs. weight of candles; the goods of Jabez Pike, the
master of John Justice ; and WILLIAM MILLER was indicted for ſelon
niously receiving 12lbs. weight of soap, value 48. 6d.; and 21lbs. weight al
candles, value 8s., part of the said goods, well knowing them to be stolen,
as aforesaid.-2nd Count, for receiving of an evil disposed person.
JABEZ Pixe. I am an oil and colourman. Justice was in my employ,
as porter-1 sent him on errands to deliver goods-Palmer was the servant
of a Mr. Holman, my neighbour my premises open upon Holman's yard
-on the night of the 4th of February, I received information from Holman,
and accompanied him to a cart-shed in his yard; and under the cart, in
box, I saw a quantity of soap, which I had every reason to believe was
mine, from having a similar description of goods on my premises-Holman
went into the loft, and there was a quantity of eandles in a canvass bag-
I have every reason to believe they were mine, having candles of a similar
description on my premises I got a policeman, and went to Holman's
premises again, and in the interval the property had been removed--the
candles from the loft, and the soap from the box-I found near the box fire
bars of soap afterwards in a nose-bag-in consequence of information we
went to a shop in Arbour-street, within fifty yards of my premises, and
there saw Miller-I told him I had come for the soap and candles which
Holman's boy had left a few minutes before his reply was, “I have no
candles"-I said, " It is no use to deny it; we know all about it;" and
then he stooped down, opened a drawer, and produced the soap, and after
wards the candles, in a canvass bag- I believe the bag to be the same asl
had seen in Holman's loft-I got a policeman, and gave Justice and Pal-
mer into custody-I value the candles at 8s., and the soap at 88. or 91
Miller keeps a chandler's shop-Holman is a carter.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I beliere you stated to Miller that
what you wanted was some soap and candles, which Holman's boy had
brought? A. Yes—the prisoners were all before the magistrate Justice and
Palmer were detained-I undertook Miller should come next day, and be
was let go on that-he has been a neighbour of mine many years-he came
according to promise next day-I offered the magistrate to become bail
for him the magistrate said as the Grand Jury were sitting, it was not
worth while I have supplied Miller with goods, but not for months post.
