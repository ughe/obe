CATHERINE WILLIAMS. I keep a stationer's shop at 28, Charles Square,
Hoxton-I know Pulley, he came to my shop in July last and asked to
have letters directed there in the name of Rawlings-he received about
thirty letters, paying a penny a letter-he carried on no business as & money
lender or anything else at my house--my premises were not destroyed by
fire in July.
PRISCILLA PLUMSTEAD. I live at 47, Banner Street, St. Luke's, and
assist my uncle, who keeps a stationer's shop-early in August Pulley
came to my shop, and asked that letters might be taken in there in the
name of Rawlings-letters were taken in for about three weeks or a
month-he carried on no business there we have had no fire there.
HENRY TUOKSWORTH. I am & grocer and provision dealer, of 21,
Henderson Gardens, Moss Side, Manchester-in October last I was living
in Napier Street-on 27th October I saw this advertisement (produced) in
the Manchester Examiner and Times (This stated that 501. to 5001. could be
borrowed at 5 per cent. interest, and smaller sums, not loss than 101., at 6 per
cent., ropayable by yearly instalmente, on note of hand alone, by applying to G.
H. Potter, 3, Orange Street, Rod Lion Square)-I wrote asking for a loan
of 1001--on 31st October I received this printed form, signed G. H.
Potter, Orange Street, asking for the inquiry fee of 2s.6d.-I sent 28. 6d.
in stampe, believing it to be a bond fide loan office, and that the persons were
prepared to lend the money on 22nd October I received a printed letter,
asking me for 6l., the interest in advance, and 18. for the promissory note
-I sent the 61. 18.-then I got an application for 11. 118. 62. for travelling
expenses—I did not send that I believed it was a bona fide office, and
that they could lend the money, when I sent the 6l. 18.-not getting an
answer I came up to look at 3, Orange Street to see what sort of a place
if was—I found it was a small newspaper shop, and gave information to
the police.

stationery business thereon 20th October Pulley came to our place and
bought a newspaper, and then asked me to take in letters for him-he
wrote his name Potter on a piece of paper-he called for letters directed
Potter, and paid d. a letter-he received about 30 altogether I think-
he came to 3rd November I think, on Saturday-on that day Hartley
