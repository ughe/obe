 276.  JOHN WELSH and THOMAS KELLY were indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of December , 3 lbs. of cheese, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Thomas Gardener.
   THOMAS GARDENER. I live in Somer's-town, and am a cheesemonger. I missed a piece of cheese on the 2nd of December, at a quarter-past five o'clock; I looked out, and saw the prisoners in a court near my shop; and at half-past five Welsh came and took another piece; I ran out, and took Kelly at the corner of the court - this piece of cheese fell from his coat; I had seen him and Welsh in company together - I then took both the prisoners to the station-house.
   ROBERT WOOLEY. I am a Police-constable. I received the prisoners, and have the cheese; I asked Kelly how he got it - he said Welsh took it, and gave it him.
 WELCH -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped and Discharged.
 KELLY -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Transported for Seven Years.
 277.  BENJAMIN MORRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of November , 1 snuff-box, value 14s., the goods of George John William Ruthven.
   GEORGE JOHN WILLIAM RUTHVEN. My father set me up in business as a tobacconist- my shop is at No. 16, Wych-street. On the 8th of November the prisoner came and asked for half an ounce of returns - I served him; he gave me a shilling, and I gave him change - he then asked to look at a snuff-box in the window - I showed him one, and said the price was a guinea; he then asked to look at another, which was 16s. - he put on the lid, and ran away with it; there was another person with him, and we think he run away with another box - my uncle followed the prisoner, and just as he got to the New-church, in the Strand; the Policeman took him.
   THOMAS TIPPER. I am a Police-constable. On the 8th of November I heard a cry of Stop thief! in Newcastle-street - I followed the prisoner, who was running, and as I was bringing him back a lad gave me this snuff-box, which he said he saw the prisoner throw away.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. It is my first time of being here.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
 278.  ELIZABETH LONG was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of November , 2 shirts, value 4s.; 1 pair of drawers, value 1s.; 1 waistcoat, value 3s.; 1 hat, value 3s., and 1 handkerchief, value 6d., the goods of John Harris.
   JOHN HARRIS. I am a seaman On the 19th of November I went with a messmate to the White Swan, in Lower Shadwell; I had a bundle, containing this property - the prisoner came into the White Swan, and I treated her with a glass of spirits; I went out, and when I returned she and my messmate, and my bundle were all gone - I am sure she is the woman.
   GEORGE RIDDELL. I keep the public-house. The prosecutor came in with the prisoner and another sailor; the prosecutor had a bundle - he went out for a short time, and while he was gone I saw the bundle under the prisoner's arm - she and another female left the house with the shipmate, but whether she took it out I cannot say: the prosecutor came in and asked for his shipmate - I said they had all left.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had the bundle, but did not take it out.
     NOT GUILTY.

 279.  THOMAS HAGUE was indicted for perjury.
 MESSRS. ADOLPHUS and PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
 The following Documents were put in and read:-Two affidavits made by the defendant, sworn on the 25th of July, 1829, and the 4th of February, 1831, in the cause of Hague against Drury - in the latter of which the defendant had deposed that  George Vandepant Drury was indebted to him in the sum of 500l. for goods sold and delivered by the deponent, to the said George Vandepant Drury, and at his request - a writ for 500l., returnable on the 15th of April - and a warrant dated the 5th of February, 1831 - the copy of a nonsuit - and the declaration which stated that the plaintiff, Thomas Hague, had become acquainted with various matters and things connected with the defendant (George Vandepant Drury) which if generally known, would be very prejudicial to the defendant, and that upon his undertaking not to disclose the same, the defendant had undertaken to pay him a reward of 500l.; in another count it stated the amount to be due for work and labour - there were other counts stating it to be for money lent, advanced, laid out, &c.; the plaintiff was nonsuited, and to pay costs, 32l. 10s. - the bill of particulars of the plaintiff's demand, dated the 31st of May, 1831, stated it to be for 500l., pursuant to an agreement or promise made to or with the defendant in 1829 - another bill of particulars, dated the 11th of June, 1831, stated it to be "the balance of an account as stated and settled with the defendant."
 - DEVERELL. I was attorney for the defendant in this action; he is the prosecutor to-day - I attended a Judge of the Court of King's Bench, upon the delivery of the second particulars of demand - the agent to the plaintiff's attorney also attended; I said the general item as to the balance must be struck out, unless the account was furnished, to which the Judge agreed; they delivered no account at any time, but a third bill of particulars was delivered with that item struck out. On the 15th of June Mr. Justice James Parke made a rule to strike out that item.
