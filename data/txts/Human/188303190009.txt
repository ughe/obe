At a quarter past 7 on 25th February the last witness brought me four half-crowns in an old glove—I sealed them up and put them on the mantelshelf. FRANCIS HASTILL( Inspector E ). I gave a sealed packet to Garwood containing this glove and these pieces. THEODOCIA CURTIS. I am a female searcher at Bow Street Police-station—I searched the prisoner on 24th February—I found 1 s . and 4 d . in coppers—there was powder on the shilling, she had a great deal of powder in her pocket—the shilling is a new one—I also found two new pocket-handkerchiefs, each in a separate parcel—I gave them and the money to Garwood. WILLIAM WEBSTER. These four half-crowns found in the glove and the one passed to Mr. Lee are all bad—the one passed to Mr. Lee is from the same mould as one of the four—the shilling is good—the moulds are made of plaster-of-paris—there is some plaster on one of the half-crowns. CHARLES SIDNEY LEE( Re-examined ). Neither of these handkerchiefs was bought at my shop. FRANCIS GARWOOD( Re-examined ). This other handkerchief was found on Heard. CHARLES SIDNEY LEE( Re-examined ). This is the handkerchief I sold to Heard on the 24th.
   The prisoner in her defence stated that she was not in the shop and had nothing to do with the prisoner Heard . GUILTY. She then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction for a like offence in June , 1882.
 395. JANE HARRIS  (30) was again  indicted, with MARY ANN HEARD  (30) , for uttering a counterfeit half-crown to Charles Sidney Lee .
   The witnesses repeated the evidence which they had given in the former case . GUILTY. HEARD then PLEADED GUILTY * to a conviction of felony in June , 1876. HARRIS  — Eighteen Months' Hard Labour. HEARD  — Nine Months' Hard Labour.
