HARRY FORD. I am a driver to Messrs. Raphael Tuok and Company
-on October 26th I received forty-seven paroels to take to Sutton's
yard-I went to Foster's yard first-my packages were all right when I
got there, and as far as I know when I left-I then went to Sutton's
in Golden Lane, which is about two minutes' walk from Whitecross Street
I took my van into the yard with me-when I counted the parcels I
had only forty-six instead of forty-seven-I have since ascertained that
the parcel which had gone contained Christmas cards,
SAMUEL A LOOCK. I am a partner with Richard Allen, of Richard
Allen and Company, clothing manufacturers, of Basinghall Street-I sold
some petticoats to be delivered at Brixton on November 24th-these
(Produced) are a part of them- they were given, among other things, to
George Moore, our coachman; he had to deliver them at their destination.
Cross-examined by Annie. I did not say at the police court that I had
only sufficient stuff to make one petticoat.
GEORGE MOORE. I was coachman to the last witness--on November
24th I had certain goods given to me to take to Brixton-these were
part of them I put them in the boot of the 'bus-I went to the back of
the omnibus, and when I returned I missed a paroel.
By the Court. The petticoats were done up in a bundle, and I could
see the top one-this one looks like it.
RICHARD HOAR. I am traveller for Messrs. Aloook-I was with Moore
on November 24th, when some goods had to be taken to Brixton- I was
going with them-these petticoats (Produced) were among them-I put
them into the 'bus myself, and two or three minutes afterwards I was
told by the coachman that they were gone.
William Smoothy, in his defence or oath, said that he wons a dealer, and
sold knives, scissors, combs, and jewellery in the street; that he was taken
il about a fortnight before December 3rd with bronshitis and violent cold
and pains in his heart, that the doctor told him to keep in bed, which he
did, that the doctor came and saw him in bed and sent him medicine eight
or nine times, that on December 3rd he had just got up for his wife to make
the boil, that he knew nothing of the goods being in the house, or how
they came there, that he did not interfere with his wife's business, and never
asked her that she bought.
Annie Smoothy, in her defence on oath, said that she bought the ribbon
outside the house on December 3rd of a man who looked like the police officer,
and said he was a traveller, and asked if she would buy it, sohich she did
for 508.; that he had the trouser stretcher in a box, and said he would make
her a present of that, that the petticoats she had bought in the Cattle Market
for 108, about a month ago, and did not know they were stolen ; that she bought
the ties also in the Cattle Market on the previous Friday, with some Christmas
cards and some buttons on cards; that the man who sold them to her now
not the man who sold her the ribbon ; that she gave the police his description,
and that he was called "Old Solly."
