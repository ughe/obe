ELEANOR HOOD, 43, May Road, Plaistow. I lived at 111, St. Man's Road, Plaistow, on October 1 last. Hill was my lodger. He rented a back room at 2s. a week. Hayes came with Hill. They were joint tenants. They were not at home from 11 to 11.45 on pension might. I had been to sleep and woke up when they came home. I looked at my husband's watch, and it was 2 o'clock; that was half an hour fast. I heard footsteps but no voices. At 10 in the morning Stowe came down to wash. I asked him if he had any beer. He said, "Yes, send up a jug," and Miss Franklin took it up. He paid me up on pension day. The three prisoners went out together on the morning of October 2.
 Cross-examined by Stowe. I saw you in the morning between 9 and 10, when you came down to wash.
 Cross-examined by Hill. There are other people on that landing, but they were in before you came home.
 (Defence.) MINNIE STOWE(prisoner's wife). Stowe was in bed and asleep at one a.m. on October 1. (To the Judge.) He called it October 2.
 Cross-examined. It was a Tuesday. At the police court I did not even know what they were charged with. I learned afterwards. Mrs. Hood and Miss Franklin are strangers to me. HENRY STOWE(prisoner, on oath). On October 1 I left home about three o'clock. I arrived at the "Queen" public-house in Green Street about 5.30, when I met Hill and Hayes. I stopped in their company till about nine and had several drinks, which we generally do on pension day. I left them about nine or 9.30 and got home a little after 10, when my wife and mother-in-law were sitting there. They passed a remark to me and said I looked a little bit drunk. I said "Yes, I had several, I think I will go to bed," and I did. I went out the next morning and made my way to Hills house. I had arranged with him to have the rent off me, which he was owing at it the time of his arrest. That is all I say. I can safely say when this was supposed to have happened, between one and two on the morning of the 2nd, I was in bed with my wife.
 Cross-examined. I have heard what Mr. Thomas said about what occurred at the railway station. It is not true. I heard the coffee stall keeper swear he saw me outside the station. It is not true. ERNEST HAYES. (prisoner, not on oath). At 10.30 that night I went home and went to bed. Hill came home and found me in bed. THOMAS HILL(prisoner, not on oath). That is quite true. Stowe left me between nine and 10, and Hayes left me about 10.30. I stopped at the public-house till 11. I thought I would go down to Bromley, which I did, and left Bromley about 11.10. I got a drop of beer in a bottle and came back to Upton Park Station and went to the coffee stall, and asked for a hard boiled egg.
