I went to Reading after the prisoner, seeing his description in the"✗ and Cry"—I said, "I want you for a robbery at Mr. Gower's, at Green  wich, for a pair of coach-springs"—he said, "Oh, he can't hurt me for that, I bought the springs; in fact, he gave them to me to sell, and I sold them to a man on the road, in a horse and cart, but I don't know the man, nor where he lives."
   Prisoner's Defence,  Mr. Gower agreed to give me 27 s . a-week, and I only received 9 s . 6 d .; a man who lived with him heard us talk about my buying the springs, and that man told me he could not get his money; he said, “Mind, he is very deep, if you can't get your money buy the springs of him." I was up all Friday night, not being able to get money for a lodging; when I went on Monday morning, he said, "Halloo, young fellow, I thought I had lost you;" I said, "I thought you had no money for me;" he said, "Oh, I can always squeeze out some for you;" and afterwards he said he had none, and gave me 8 d . The Magistrate said he believed my statement was true, as he had been there before, trying to cheat his men, who had summoned him for their wages, and put him in the court for it. He told me he had a boy coming to work for him; and when the boy came, he said, "Oh, I am not going to work for him; they tell me I shall never get any money." He had no money to give me on the Monday morning, and he said if I had a friend to buy the springs, I should have them. I met a man on the road, and he said, "Those springs would look well under my cart;" I said, "You can have them," and I sold them. I was going home to get work where I served my time. You could buy such springs for 30 s . anywhere; 1 l . 15 s . is due to me. THOMAS GOWER re-examined.  All I can make out due to him 1 s . 5 d .—he told me himself that he only worked four days and a quarter—I em ployed several men out of charity last winter, on condition that if I could not pay them all their wages, they would give me time, and work till the spring—they then wanted me to raise their wages, which I refused, and they summoned me—some I have paid, and some not—I suppose that is what the prisoner alludes to—I agreed to give him 18 s . a-week when I could put him on work—I kept him a fortnight or three weeks at bay before I put him on—I gave him the springs to dispose of for me—he said he was sure the party would buy them, and I put the lowest price I could on them—he was not sent with them to hawk about—he said the person he was going to sell them to was a potato-merchant, in Pole-street, Wool wich, which is three miles and a half from ray house—I took him by the hand, under the impression, from the respectable shops he had worked in, that he was a good workman, and I said I would be a friend to him, and make it a constant place—I gave him 8 d . on the Friday, which was all the change I had in my pocket—he prejudiced the mind of the boy who came to work, because he wanted the place for a nephew of his own, and told me he had written to Bristol for him—I certainly never sold the prisoner the springs—he got them from me under the representation that there was a customer for them at Woolwich, and I authorized him to sell them to that customer—he asked me for money on the Monday morning, but I had no change—I had only a 5 l . draft in my pocket—there was no one present at the conversation between the prisoner and myself about the springs.
