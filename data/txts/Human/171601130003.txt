John Long, of the Parish of Stepney, was indicted for feloniously stealing 4 Grapling Irons, Value 34 s. 1 Iron Chain, 4 s. the Property of Edward Jones, on the 30th of November last.
 He was a second time indicted for feloniously stealing an Iron Anchor, Value 4 l. 1 Iron Swivel, 3 l. the Goods of Bartholomew Ward, on the 14th of December last. The Prisoner denied he stole them; but receiv'd the Goods from some Friends with Orders to sell 'em. The Prosecutors had their Goods again; which the Jury considering, brought him in Guilty of both Indictment under the Value of 40 s. [Branding. See summary.]
   Mary Moor, of the Parish of St. Anne in Westminster, was indicted for Stealing 3 Iron Choppers, Value 4 s. the Property of Charles Betty, on the 5th of December last. One of the Choppers was found upon the Prisoner, who own'd she Stole it; and did not deny it upon her Trial, The Jury found her Guilty to the Value of 10 d.
 She was a second time indicted for stealing an Iron Chopper, Value 1 s. 6 d. from John Pepperell; on the Day aforesaid; but that not being prov'd upon her, she was   acquitted. [Whipping. See summary.]

   Thomas Martin, was indicted for a Forgery in taking out a Warrant of Attorney for 120 l. in the Name of Thomas Broad . It appear'd the Prisoner was a notorious Offender in this Kind, and was suing Broad for a pretended Debt of 61 l. 15 s. to an Execution; upon which he went to an Attorney, and in Broad's Name took out the said Warrant. Two Letters were produc'd, written by the Prisoner: one in Broad's Name, and the other in his own; which exactly conform'd and prov'd the Forgery. The Prosecutor depos'd he ow'd the Prisoner nothing, who was a litigious Person; and the Jury found him   Guilty. [Fine. See summary.] [Pillory. See summary.] [Provide sureties for good behaviour.    Anne Smith of the Parish of St. Martin in the Fields, was indicted for assaulting and robbing, (with one Jane Evans ,) one Richard a Negro, on the Highway, and taking from him a Silver Collar, Value 15 s. the Goods of William Jordan, on the 6th of December last. Mr. Jordan swore, he sent his Boy of an Errand, who was met by the Prisoner: and complaining to her, that he had been long on his Errand, and fear'd to go home, she told him, his Collar would betray him, and therefore advis'd him to let her take it off; and carrying him to a Fruit-Cellar, the Prisoner and Jane Evans broke the Collar with their Teeth, and took it away. After this the Boy came home, and told his Master of it, who took up the Prisoner, and carrying her before a Justice she confess'd, that Jane Evans took off the Collar, and that she sold it. The Jury considering the Matter, found her Guilty of Felony only. [Branding. See summary.]

   Mary Chennery, of the Parish of St. Leonard in Shoreditch, was indicted for feloniously stealing 2 Silk Petticoats, Value 15 s. and other Things from Arthur Harrison, on the 9th of December last. It appear'd the Prisoner clean'd Shoes for the Prosecutor, whose Wife seeing her very poor, took her into the House, and gave her some Cloaths; but about a Week after, the Prisoner stole the Goods, and went quite away without Notice; but she was afterwards met with, and apprehended, with some of the stol'n Goods upon her which she confess'd before a Justice. The Jury found her Guilty of Felony to the Value of 39 s. [Branding. See summary.]
   Samuel Ellis, of the Parish of St. Martin in the Fields, was indicted for that he, together with one James Filewood and Thomas Day, not taken, did assault one John Crowder on the Highway, and rob him of a Hat, Value 10 s. on the 30th of December last. The Prosecutor depos'd, That upon Complaint that several loose Persons harbour'd about the Play-House, by which means several Persons of Quality, and others, lost their Watches, Snuff Boxes, Ec. he and other Servants us'd to scour the Avenues; and on the Night aforesaid, he heard some Persons say, D - n him, this is he; let's knock his Brains out; and then they fell upon him with the greatest Fury, broke his Thumb to Shatters, bruised his Legs and Body barbarously, and stole away his Hat. He was sure the Prisoner was among them, but could not tell who had his Hat. The Prisoner was also noted for a Pickpocket; but the Evidence not being positive against him as to the Robbery, the Jury thought fit to   acquit him.
   John Gerrard, of the Parish of St. Andrew Holbourn, was indicted for feloniously stealing 3 Pictures, value 3 l. the Property of Sir William Cary , out of the House of my Lady Copley, on the 15th of December last. It appear'd one Mr. Blake a Joyner, had brought home the Pictures just before; but my Lady Copley saying there wanted some Rings to fix them, he went home for some, and as soon as he was gone the Prisoner went in, and said he was Mr. Blake's Man, and wanted the Pictures; my Lady Copley thinking he was only to put them up, order'd her Servant to give them him, and then he went away and sold them; but one of them being hung out by the Person that bought it, it was known and the Prisoner produc'd, who confess'd the Fact before Justice Ellis. The Jury considering the Matter found him Guilty to the Value of 39 s. [Branding. See summary.]
   Robert Godfrey, of the Parish of St. Dunstan's Stepney, was indicted for feloniously stealing 1 Silver hilted Sword, value 30 s. the Property of Richard Hoo, on the 2d of January last. Mrs. Hoo depos'd, the Prisoner came to her House, and ask'd if it was Captain Hoo's; he said he was a Passenger, and came from the Captain at the New-England Coffee-House, who would be there presently; so the Prisoner came in and sat down; but took an Opportunity to take the Sword, which hung up in the Parlour; and then pretending he was a dry , went out, and promised to return immediately. Her Maid saw the Hilt of the Sword in his Bosom. After this, he went to another House with a Story of the same sort, and was apprehended in stealing a Spoon, which being in the Neighbourhood. Mrs. Hoo charg'd him with the Fact above-mention'd. He made no Defence; and the Jury found him   Guilty. [Branding. See summary.]
   Thomas Shorey, and Joan Pierce, of the Parish of St. Dunstan's Stepney, were indicted (he as Principal) for breaking the House of Mary Archer and Eliz Anderson , in the Night-time, and taking thence 2 Petticoats, 2 Cloth Coats, 7 Yards of Persian Silk, and other Goods from the said Persons; and she, as Accessory, in receiving the same, knowing them to be stole. It appear'd there was a slight Partition between one Mr. Briant's House and the Prosecutor's; that the Prisoner was Mr. Briant's Servant, and at proper Times took out a Board, and stole the Goods, as he afterwards confess'd he did at the Instigation of Pierce. The Place was once left open, which occasion'd the searching the Prisoner's Chest, where the two Coats, and some other Things were found. The Jury found the Man   Guilty of Felony; but   acquitted the Woman. [Branding. See summary.]
