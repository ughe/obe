1391. JOHN WHITE was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 23rd of May , 20 shillings, 10 sixpences, 36 penny-pieces, and 48 halfpence, the monies of John Clements Pink , well knowing the same to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.
 The prisoner had in this case also seen the money taken from the till, he was detained to be indicted as a principal.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1393.  JOHN HART was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , 2 pairs of shoes, value 5s., and 1 pair of boots, value 1s., the goods of Mary Ann Moore.
   MARY ANN MOORE. I am single, and live in Newman-street, Oxford-street. I employed the prisoner as a carpenter about my house, and as a porter in my shop - I dealt in brokery, but I have now sold off my stock; I saw at Mr. Shaves' shop, on the 10th of June, some shoes, which were part of my stock - I had missed shoes, and never gave the prisoner authority to pawn or sell them.
   JOHN SHAVE. I live in Union-street, Middlesex-hospital, and am a harness maker. The prisoner lodged in my house - he left in my debt; he agreed to pay me 5s. a week; he left, as security with me, nineteen duplicates and two boxes - one of the duplicates was for shoes, which were at Mr. Baxter's, and I took them out; I also bought of him a pair of child's boots on the 6th of May.
   GEORGE NORMAN. I am a shopman to Mr. Baxter, a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned two pairs of shoes on the 28th of April, and I gave him this duplicate.
   JAMES McCORMICK. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner on the 10th of June, at Mrs. Moore's. I have the shoes and boots.
   MARY ANN MOORE. These are my property.
 COURT. Q. Are you married? A.There was a ceremony, but in consequence of my first husband being alive, it was put aside - Moore, my first husband, was transported for fourteen years; it was proved he was alive, and I never lived with Mr. Case (my second husband) afterwards - I have not heard of my first husband for four years; I believe he is alive, but am not certain - the fourteen years have been up about a year, but he has not returned; I bought these shoes four months ago.
 Prisoner. Mr. Case came and took away several things, and she called me to turn him out.
   MARY ANN MOORE. He took a sideboard, but not the shoes - the shoes were up stairs.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had bought the shoes in the street.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1394.  MARY HAWKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June , 3 shillings, and 3 sixpences, the monies of Charles Hambleton.
   CHARLES HAMBLETON. I live in Exeter-street, Chelsea. The prisoner was in my service; I put three marked shillings and three marked sixpences into my breeches pocket, which I left hanging on the back of a chair at the foot of my bed, on the 6th of June, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning - I went up about two, and 1s. 6d. of the money was gone; I went up again in the evening, and it was then all gone - I had told the Policeconstable to be in the tap-room, and just as the prisoner was going home, about nine o'clock in the evening, I told her to stop in the parlour - the Police-constable then came in; I said I thought she had some money which she took from my breeches pocket up-stairs - she gave no answer; the officer said she must give up what she had or he must search her - she then gave up these two shillings and two sixpences, which are what I had marked; the other 1s. 6d. was gone; she said if she had had them she must have lost them.
 Cross-examined by MR. BALL. Q. Had you a good character with her? A. Yes; I did not give her the breeches to brush - she had not the care of any clothes; she did not say, "In brushing your clothes, the money fell out, and I forgot to give it you."
   RICHARD NEWLAND. I am a Police-constable. I was called in - the prisoner denied taking the money, and the prosecutor wished me to search her, which I did; I took from her these two shillings and two sixpences - she took her pocket off, and I found them in it; I asked what she had done with the rest - she said if she had that she must have lost it; the prosecutor stated he had marked the money - the marks are on it now; she acknowledged taking it afterwards.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 18.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Seven Days.
 1395.  LOUIS MANUEL was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of May , 1 watch, value 29s.; 1 chain, value 6d., and 2 seals, value 1s., the goods of John Baptist.
 The prosecutor and prisoner being Portuguese, the evidence was explained by an interpreter.
 JOHN BAPTIST. I lodge in  Cornwall-street. The prisoner lodged in the same room; I had a watch in my chest- one Friday, about a month ago, the prisoner slept in my room, and had been there that day; I saw the watch at Mr. Phillips' shop - it had a chain and two seals to it; the prisoner is a countryman of mine.
   WATKIN PHILLIPS. I deal in clothes. I bought this watch of the prisoner; he came one Friday with it - I laid the watch in my window, and the prosecutor saw it and claimed it - the prisoner said he was going away and the watch was of no use to him; he asked 13s. for it - I offered him 9s.; he went away, returned, and took it.
   WILLIAM CLAYTON. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY. Aged 25.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
