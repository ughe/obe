 2421.  WILLIAM COLE ABBOTT was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM WARD. I am a baker. The prisoner was my servant, and had 14s. a week, bread, and beer; it was his duty to account to me, or my daughter for the money he received.
   ANN BENGOUGH. I am a customer to the prosecutor. I paid 1s. to the prisoner on the 15th of October - he did not give me a receipt for it.
   JAMES QUINN. I paid the prisoner at the rate of 2s. a day for three years; I think I paid him last on Monday week, 2s. - I never had a receipt.
   EDWARD WILLIAM ADAMS. I paid the prisoner on the 4th of October 81/2d.; I never had a receipt for it.
   THOMAS TOMKINS(Police-serjeant C 12.) I took the prisoner, and found in his box twenty-five bills - he stated they had been given to him to give to customers, and he acknowledged having embezzled the money.
   WILLIAM WARD. The prisoner never paid me the 1s., not the 81/2d.; he has not accounted to me for this, nor a great deal more; I took him up on Thursday morning, and asked him if they had paid him, and he said they had not - the bread was put down, as not paid for; I did not know of it till he was taken up - he booked the bread, but never gave me the money.
   ELIZABETH WARD. I am the prosecutor's daughter. I booked the prisoner's bread on the 15th of October - he did not give me the 1s.; he booked it as not paid.
 Prisoner. It being the first time I was ever in such a place, I hope you will have mercy.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2422.  JANE BRANCH was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of September , 2 sheets, value 8s.; 1 tablecloth, value 2s.; 1 pillow-case, value 4d.; 1 basin, value 3d., and 1 spoon, value 4d., the goods of Jane Kingston.
   JANE KINGSTON. I live in Boston-place, Dorset-square; the prisoner lodged with me for a fortnight. I missed this property on the 29th of September - she had not left: I said, "What have you done with my sheets and tablecloth?" she gave me the duplicates, and said she would make them good; but she left on Friday.
   WILLIAM LANGFORD. I am a pawnbroker. I have a tablecloth, pawned by a person in the name of Ann Branch, but I did not take it in - this is the duplicate which was given for it.
   JOHN JAMES BARTLETT. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, pawned in the name of Jane Branch; I do not recollect the prisoner, but this is the duplicate given for it.
   THOMAS NEWTON. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, pawned in the name of Jane Branch; I do not know the person - I gave this duplicate for it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had some money to make up, and pawned the things.
 GUILTY. Aged 42. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Seven Days.
 2423.  ABRAHAM BARRETT and JAMES WILSON were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of October , 1 watch, value 6l., the goods of Robert Sime , from his person.
   ROBERT SIME. I am a musician, and live in St. George's-terrace, Commercial-road. On the 16th of October I and my neice were going home, about half-past nine o'clock at night, I was on St. George's-terrace; I observed some men standing together about the middle of the court, by a lamp - as we were passing them I felt a tug at my watch; the men then ran away - I felt for my watch, and it was gone; I cannot swear to the persons, as I had no suspicion of them.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Where had you been? A. I was out, doing a little business with my wife; I had been to a public-house but a minute before-I was not exactly sober, but I was fit to do any thing; I had drank very little - I might have a pint of beer, or a glass of gin - my wife was quite sober.
   CAROLINE PUTT. I am thirteen years old. I was with the prosecutor, who is my uncle, on St. George's-terrace; I saw Wilson and another man standing in the middle of the terrace; they asked my uncle if it was a throughfare, and then Wilson made a tug at my uncle's watch - there is a gas-light there, and I saw Wilson's person so as to know him again; they ran up the terrace, and across the Commercial-road, and down Essex-street - I followed, and never lost sight of Wilson till I saw him taken by an officer; I cannot swear to the other prisoner.
   EMILY TYSON. I was passing the street, and heard Putt cry Stop thief! I saw the two prisoners running over to Essex-street - Wilson stopped in a cart; Barrett ran on- Wilson said to him, "Don't run, you fool - we are all right now;" a Policeman took Wilson - Barrett ran on, and another officer took him: I had seen the two prisoners walking in company together, between eight and nine o'clock the same evening.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where had you seen them? A. In William-street; I had not known them before - they were running fast, but I kept up to them, and did not lose sight of them.
   ROBERT CREASEY(Police-constable H 130). On the night of the 16th of October I was on duty in Essex-street- I saw Wilson and another man, whom I cannot swear to, come running very fast; Wilson seated himself in a truck, and said to the other, "Don't run, you fool - it is all right" I took Wilson, and the other got on; Barrett was taken soon after.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you seen Tyson running? A. Yes, I saw her among the crowd.
   GEORGE DUMPER(Police-constable H 45). I was on duty about half-past nine o'clock that night - I saw three men running; I pursued, and overtook Barrett - I told him he must go with me; I took him towards the cry of Stop thief! and found Wilson in custody - some person recognized him as being one; he was walking at a smart pace, but I believe he had been running - when we got to the watch-house, an officer said, "I saw you and Wilson together in the Commercial-road between eight and nine o'clock this evening;" he said, "I am sure you did not - we were not together this evening, but were in the morning."
