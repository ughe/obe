   MR. PARRY . Q.  Have you ever sailed with Mr. Leith before? A.  No—he was a humane, well-conducted seaman, I only wish every one was like him—I am not going with him again; the Indian  has sailed. THOMAS BEATTIE. I was an able seaman on board the Indian , from Auck land—I remember Abrams coming on board—about three weeks after we got to sea, I observed that his hands and feet were sore—he was labouring under a certain disease, known in New Zealand as the yhacyhac  —I remember Mr. Leith ordering his hair to be cut, on account of the vermin—I have seen him whipped by Mr. Leith once or twice, and it was no more than he deserved—Mr. Leith's attention was called to his sores, and I never knew of his being whipped afterwards—he was very attentive to the boy—I have seen him order, and take him food and medicine, and give him clean linen, and stand by and see it changed—Robert Alexander called his attention to the boy's state four, five, or six weeks before his death—Mr. Leith was always liked by the crew—I had never sailed with him before.
   COURT . Q.  How did the boy seem for the first three weeks? A.  He seemed healthy when he came on board, and did his work till we had some rough weather, ten or fifteen days after we sailed, and after that he was of no use ROBERT ALEXANDER. I was an able seaman on board the Indian , from New Zealand—I remember Abrams being turned out of the forecastle on account of his dirtiness—I think that was better than three weeks after be came aboard—his hair was cut before that—I afterwards saw that he had on him a certain disease, which the natives of New Zealand call the yhacyhac  —I saw boils on his hands and legs—I have seen the mate whip him, I cannot say how many times—the men were always growling  about him to the mate he was very kind to the boy after I told him the state he was in.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How often used the boy to be whipped? A.  I do not know: only when there was occasion for it—he was not flogged every time a complaint was made about his being dirty—as soon as the mate knew he was badly he knocked it off  —I do not think he was flogged a dozen times altogether. JOHN JOHNSON. I was a seaman on board the Indian  —about three weeks after Abrahams had been on board, I saw he had some big dry sores on his hands and legs, and one on one finger—his feet swelled on account of his going without shoes—I have only sailed one voyage with Mr. Leith, be behaved kindly to me, and to the men generally.
   COURT . Q.  How often have you seen the boy flogged? A.  Never—he looked clean and in good health when be came on board—he had a bed then—we turned him out of the forecastle because he was dirty—he never went aloft—he had been to sea before. HENRY SENIOR.
