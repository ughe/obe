   Magarigle. Q.  You knew when you called on me that I was a general agent? A.  I knew it merely by the advertisements, and by what you had printed on the window—I did not expect to have to pay you a commission—I did not ask you to make inquiry about the character or position of Keighley—I placed implicit confidence in what you said—you represented him as an honest man, and that the business was a genuine affair-Cox said he had known you twenty years—you said you had known Keighley for years—you introduced me to Cox in your private office up stairs, and you left us together, perhaps, twenty minutes; not an hour—you said you knew exactly what we wanted, and did not allow me and Keighley to say anything—we had not agreed between ourselves—I had nothing to say in the matter—the agreement was drawn up by you entirely—it was what I required—it was read over to me—I paid the 100 l . with a country note—you said you could get it cashed at your bankers, and you gave me a receipt for it.
