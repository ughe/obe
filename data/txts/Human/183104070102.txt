 906.  MARY MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of March , 1 half-crown, 1 sixpence, 1 penny-piece, and 1 farthing, the monies of Julias Von Boeck ; 4 gallons of porter, value 5s., and 1 gallon of gin, value 9s., the goods of James Merry.
 EUSTACE VON BOECK. I am the wife of  Julius Von Boeck, who is a captain in the army, on half-pay. I deal with Merry, the publican, for beer and liquor; the prisoner was my servant - I occasionally gave her money to pay bills for liquor; she gave me the bills and receipts, but I have only kept the last. On Wednesday, the 12th of March, I gave her 3s. 1 1/2d. to pay this bill (looking at it); she brought me a receipt for it - (looking at two bills) these things were never delivered to me; I never ordered them, nor had them - I found the bills on the prisoner; she had lived about five months with me - I never sent her for these things.
 The bills being read, were for beer and spirits, and amounted to 8s. 4 1/2d., and 14s. 4 1/2d.
 JAMES MERRY. I keep a public-house. The last witness dealt with me; I know nothing of this bill, which has the receipt to it - it is not my hand-writing; the money was never paid me - these two bills are my writing; the prisoner had the beer and spirits charged in them on account of her mistress; I delivered the bills to the prisoner, and have never been paid; the receipt to the other bill is not written by me, or any of my servants.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that she had got spirits for herself, without so stating to Mr. Merry; and that at times when her mistress gave her money to pay the publican, she had laid it out for house-keeping. GUILTY, of stealing 3s. 1 1/2d. only. Aged 28.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Weeks.
 907.  LEWIS MYERS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , 1 ladle, value 10s.; 13 spoons, value 30s.; 1 pepper-box, value 15s., and 1 pair of silver tongs, value 5s., the goods of Henry Myers.
   HENRY MYERS. I am a stationer and rag-merchant, and live in White-street, Houndsditch. The prisoner is my brother; I went to Liverpool on the 27th of February, and left him living in the house - I am single; my mother and sisters live with me; he lived with them at that time; when I returned, I found he had absconded, and missed the articles named in the indictment - he had no authority whatever to pawn my property - he got his living by minding my warehouse in August last, but in February I hired a ship to send him abroad.
 Prisoner. Q. Have you never sent me to Barker's to pawn property? A. I never had occasion to pawn things, I have always had plenty of money - I know nothing about my mother sending you to pledge for her.
   THOMAS WHITLOCK. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 3rd of March, in Henry-place, Waterloo-road, and found four duplicates on him.
   JOHN HALL. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a soup ladle, 6 table-spoons, a pair of sugar-tongs, and a pepper-box, pawned by the prisoner on the 1st of March - he said it was for his brother (not the prosecutor); he said his brother had a bill to make up - I knew him well.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 18.
 The prosecutor here stated that he had been robbed before by the prisoner, and had equipped him for sea, at an expence of 100l.; that he left the vessel, and, upon promises of amendment, he had been prevailed on to take him into his employ, where he remained until he committed the robbery in question. Transported for Seven Years.
 908.  JAMES MARNER and ELIZA HARRIS, were indicted for stealing, on the 15th of March , 1 looking-glass, value 5s.; 1 bolster, value 3s.; 2 pillows, value 3d.; 1 blanket, value 2s., and 1 counterpane, value 3s., the goods of James Parker.
 JAMES PARKER. I live in  Riley-street, Cromer-street; the prisoners lodged about five weeks with me in one room, and owed me a week's rent. On the 15th of March I went into their room; Harris said her husband had gone out - I said, "I have come to see if my things are all right;" she said, "They are not;" I said, "Where is the looking-glass?" she said it was in pawn - I then missed the other things - she was taken to the station; the man passed the house soon after - I went out and said,"You are a pretty sort of a man, to rob me of my things, even the looking-glass;" he said, "Well, I knew the glass was gone."
 GEORGE NORRIS. I am an officer. I took the man in charge - he said he consented to the glass being pawned, but knew nothing of the other things.
   JOSEPH HALL. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Battle-bridge. I have a bolster and blanket pawned by the female prisoner.
 FRANCIS HENRY PARKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Somers'-town. The female prisoner pawned this looking-glass on the 14th of March.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Marner's Defence. I was aware of the glass, but did not intend to defraud him - I went out the morning I was taken to get some money to redeem them.
 Harris' Defence. I can only say the same.'
 MARNER - GUILTY. Aged 27.
 HARRIS - GUILTY. Aged 25.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Weeks.
 909.  ROBERT GOBBY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of March , 1 coat, value 22s., and 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of George Terry.
   GEORGE TERRY. I am barman to my aunt. On the 7th of March I went into the skittle-ground of the Britannia, Back-lane, St. George's in the East, and put my coat on a table in the ground; the handkerchief was in the pocket - I saw the prisoner there, and missed the coat in about three-quarters of an hour; he was then gone; he came in while I was looking for it - I went into the house, and inquired who had been out; I apprehended the prisoner on the 10th - the landlady found the duplicate of the coat in the sink of her beer-machine - I redeemed it.
