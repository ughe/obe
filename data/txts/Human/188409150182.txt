I saw the prisoner running in Warwick Street with a crowd of 40 or 50 behind him, no one was in front—he ran up close to me and said "Have you seen the man who stole a watch by here?"—I said "You vagabond, it was you"—he began to swear, and before I could get hold of him he got through the mob and got away—I took him on the Monday and said it was for stealing a watch last night—he said "Why did you not take me then?"—I said "I could not get you"—he said at the station "I did not steal his watch, but I know who did."
   Cross-examined.  I may have told the Magistrate that, or I may not—he said "This is the thanks a man gets for assisting the police"—( the words "Why did you not take me then?" did not appear in the deposi tions)—Joyce gave me a description of the prisoner's coat on the Sunday—the prisoner's mother has given me no information.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I did not pull Joyce by his coat tail—a cabman who had been robbed was brought in at the same time to see if he could identify any one. GUILTY.
   He then PLEADED GUILTY ** to a conviction at Newington in March, 1876, when he was sentenced to Ten Years' Penal Servitude.— Twelle Months' Hard Labour, to commence after the termination of his former sentence .
 967. GEORGE LEE  (42) , Robbery with violence on William Brooks , and stealing a watch, a seal, a watch-key, and part of a chain, his property.
     MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS  Prosecuted. WILLIAM BROOKS. I keep a shop at 23, Lower Park Road, Peckham—on the 21st of July I walking down New Kent Road with my wife—the prisoner came from behind in front of us, hit me on my mouth, pulled out my watch, and broke the chain—I cried out—he ran off—I went to the station and gave information—I saw the prisoner with others and picked him out—I also saw my watch, which was found on him.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I did not say to the sergeant "I don't know him, perhaps my wife may know him better." SARAH BROOKS. I was with my husband, and saw the prisoner come up and take his watch—I picked him out from four others at the station—he is the man.
   Cross-examined.  You had no hat on nor had the others. GEORGE MURRELL( Policeman M  394). On the 22nd of July, at 1.15, I took the prisoner on another charge—he took this watch from his pocket.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was at the Crown public-house, at the corner of Lambeth Road, when a party who is in custody brought me the I watch and asked me to sell it for him. When I was taken to the station I was put with four others, and the policeman knocked my hat off. The prosecutor said "I don't know that man, but my wife does;" and then I she said, "That is the man who robbed my husband." GUILTY.
   He then PLEADED GUILTY ** to at conviction at this Court in February , 1883.— Ten Years' Penal Servitude.
