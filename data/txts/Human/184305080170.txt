   Cross-examined by MR. CROUCH . Q.  Has she been a customer of yours? A.  Yes—with the exception of shoes, and such like, she had laid out the whole of her wages in buying things of me—I believe the room-door was not locked—she was not present when I searched the box—she was in my house then—I was sent for to go up to her room—the box was not locked—it had no lock to it that I am aware of—I did not tell her I was going to search her box—I did not go to search it—the lid was on it, and I just took it up—I went alone, and called the person whose house we were in—a person came to tell me I was wanted, and I went up stairs—I guessed what I was wanted for—I had simply told the young lady what I missed, and I said, "Is her box locked?"—she said, "No"—I said, "Then to-morrow morning look into her box"—she was to look, and then come to me—I called Mrs. Holdis, at whose house I was, to look in the box—she did not tell me I was doing wrong in not having the prisoner there—I never sold the prisoner anything myself—these are common-place goods, but this calico happens to have my private mark on it—the prisoner has bought things—I have only my own memory as to what is sold—I have two persons in my shop, but I generally look after my business, and know what stock I have—my business called me out frequently, and when I came home, I very frequently found out articles that have been sold without asking—I do not swear that these things were not sold to the prisoner, but I have brought the only person who has sold her things. CATHERINE CASSON. I serve in the prosecutor's shop—I was sleeping with the prisoner in the room at the next door—in consequence of some in structions from my master, I looked into the prisoner's box on Monday morn ing, the 8th of May—she was not there—I saw a piece of calico wrapped in her shawl—I did not open the shawl; I only saw the calico—I sent for Mr. Davy—he came, but he did not search the box; he only looked in—I am quite sure these things were in the box—I was in the room when it was opened.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You slept with her? A.  Yes—I recollect a shawl that I sold her—she paid me for it—I have had no quarrel with her—she has not refused to do work that I have wished her to do—she has nothing to do with my washing—I put it out—she has sometimes washed a few trifles—she had left the room about twenty minutes when I opened her box—I did not tell her I was going to do it—it had no lock—the calico was the shawl I had sold her—it was folded up, and the edge of the calico was outside—I have sold her things, but she never bought such a thing as this jean in the shop in her life of me—Mr. and Mrs. Davy, the apprentice, and me, are engaged in the shop—the prisoner spent most of her wages in buying things—all the people in that house have a ready access to that room, and her box was always open—my master's shop was in confusion—the things were all about—to the best of
