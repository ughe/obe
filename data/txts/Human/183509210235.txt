I take my stand between St. Paul's and Cornhill, at eight o'clock in the morining—my business is to observe the stage coaches that pass, and omnibuses likewise, to cheek the Somerset House returns—I stop till twelve o'clock at night—I have frequently observed Bray's omnibuses for the last three months—I have seen Bray's, Corbett's, and Silvester's coming one against the other—Bray's generally after the other—young Bray has driven his omnibus up, and by placing the pole against the door, prevented passeners getting in—that was a common thing—on one occasion, I observed Raines, one of the conductors, when young Bray derove up get, down when a lady was on the pavement, come and take a child out of her arms, and put it in thje omnibus while the lady was calling on the paverment—when persons have been calling they have been pulled about by different conductors, by Trecett and others, and Bray—they taken hold of people and said, "This is my  bus,  I am first"—when the people have been going to get into the "Em peror", that being generally first, the passagers would be pulled about by Bray's conductors: and the same conduct was pursued towards the "Albion" by each of Bray's omnibuses—both of the conductors were pugilists, Raines and Mann—sometimes Mann used to drive, and some times conduct—I know nothing of him myself.
   Cross-examined by MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Do you know that Rsines has been a pugilist? A.  It has been advartised—I do not know any other conductors pugilists—Murphy has been a pugilist, I have heard—I have heard of Savage—I do not know him—I know the driver of the "Emperor"—I have not been on the box of the "Emperor" when it has been going out of its way after Bray's—I have been on the box twice—I have not followed Bray's omnibus out of the road—I have gone all the way up to Paddington on the box of the "Emperor"—I have gone to Madia-hill upon that very omnibus, when it went out of its regular road to a public-house where they dine—I did not see Bray go there—I saw him before me part of the way—he was there—he dined there—young Bray did—It is about six pr seven weeks sinve I saw the lady with the child—I do not know the name of the public-house—the omnibuses was in the yard—the horses were in the stable—It was near Bray's stable—the "Emperor" did not wait till Bray's went—the "Emperor" started first—I did not see Bray's going just before us.
   COURT . Q.  What took you there? A.  I had been to Mr. Burn, a tailor, at Paddington—I went to Maida-hill for a ride.
   MR. JONES . Q.  How far was your tailor's from the house where you dined? A.
