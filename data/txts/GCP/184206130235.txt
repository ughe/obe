WILLIAM JOHN ARNOLD. I am a lighterman, in the employ of Mr.
Thomas Pillow. On the 23rd of May some sugar of his was put on board
a barge called the Grace, to be taken to the West India Docks-Jackson
Carmed me on the morning of the 24th, and I missed a bag of sugar from
The starboard-quarter of the barge-I went ashore, and ran up along the
vater-side to Battle-bridge plying-place, and when I got down to the stair-
head, I saw the prisoner shoving off in his boat-I went to Beale's wharf,
and watched the prisoner to Hayes' wharf-tier he made fast to the tier,
and began to bale the water out of his boat-I borrowed a ship's-boat,
went off to him, and all at once he shoved off his boat, and got up the side
ola schooner next to him-I got up after him, collared him, brought him
down, and found the bag of sugar in his boat, covered over with an old
coat– I told bim he must come with me-he begged and prayed of me to
let him go-1 said, “If you had got away with the bag of sugar, I should
have had to pay for it"-he said if he had known I should have had to
pay for it, he would not have taken it--it was the one we had last from
the barge-it is marked “S, and J. H." the tally was on it, and I have
one like it with me belonging to another bag--the bag itself was marked
"A"-they were all marked alike.
