SEDGWICK, Frederick W. (36, vocalist); obtaining by false pre
tences from Louis William Graff and others one motor car, within
tent to defraud.
Mr. Campion prosecuted; Mr. Jarvis defended.
JOR Pk Rye, 57, Ennerdale Road, Richmond, manager to Graff
Brothers and Göhr, South-Western Motor Car Company, 9, Royal
Parade, Kew Gardens. The main part of our business is selling
motor cars. About October, 1907, prisoner saw me and said his
wife was making him a birthday present of a car and he was now
ready to have it. He called and selected an Argyll. Louis and
George Graff were present when he called. He said his wife had
just purchased an estate at East Preston, Sussex, to the extent of
45 acres, and that a deposit of £750 had been paid; he was going
to build houses and bungalows and also make bricks on the estate.
He had a trial run on the car with Louis Graff. On the next day
ho expressed his delight with the running of the car, and talked
over the price, which was arranged at £257. He said he could only
pay £25 down, but his wife had some shares in the Sumatra Delhi
Rubber Estates Company, which she was about to sell, and which
would be realised in a fortnight, and he agreed to give bills at 14 days
for £200 and £32. Ho paid £25 and his wife signed the two bile
produced. Prisoner also told me that he was singing in a choir at
salary of £200 a year. He also said he was a full-fledged barrister.
The car was delivered and the bills fell due on. October 24, when
they were presented and dishonoured. A writ was issued on October
25, and, on the judgment subsequently obtained, the car WAS sineda
