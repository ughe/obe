AMELIA REDFORD. I manage Mr. Hawkins's fruiterer's, florist's, and

JOHN GADD. I am manager to Mr. Jackson, a florist at Hampton
Court-I deal with Brooks-on 9th June I bought two boxes of cherries,
for which I paid 28. 6d., this is the bill.
EDWARD BROOKS. I am manager to my son and superintend all the
business—these books are in the prisoner's writing-on 25th January I
had a conversation with Mr. Cranleigh about the lemons and then went
into the shop and asked the prisoner for his book, he gave it me-Mr.
Cranleigh said he paid the prisoner 38. 6d. for the lemons--I said, " Jack,
what do you mean by this there is only 28. 6d. entered”-he said, "It
is a mistake”-I said, “Did you pay the money in ?"-he said, "No"
-I said, “Was that a mistake?"-he said, "No"-I said, “Why?"
-he stood silent-I said, “You know this is a clear case of embezzle-
ment”-he said, "No, it is not, it is a mistake "-I said, “If you are
saucy I will call in a policeman and lock you up; get out of the van; if
ever you do this again I will lock you up-on 9th June I see entered
five boxes of cherries, 58., to Mr. Hawkins-I saw the cart loaded that
day; he took out five boxes of that particular kind of cherries-I find no
entry of the sale to Jackson of two boxes, 28. 6d.-on 11th June two
packages are entered as sold to Hawkins for 108., those entries are all in
the prisoner's writing.
Cross-examined. I did not stop 18. out of your wages on 1st January, I
stopped it when you embezzled 78. in March-you could sell things at a
lower price when coming home if trade had been bad, and you were never
grumbled at for so doing-I discharged you once for embezzlement in
March, and you came and asked to be taken on again, and I said no;
but you went and cried round my son and he took you on-since this case
has been on your conduct has been disgraceful, you have stood outside
the shop and made a bad noise with your mouth, even last night you
did it.
CATHERINE BROOKS. I am the daughter of the last witness-when the
prisoner returned from his daily round he gave me his day book-I re-
ceived this 58. of Hawkins on 9th June, I am quite sure it was not 68. 3d.
-on behalf of Gadd I received 48. 4}d. for potatoes on the same day,
but no money for cherries-on 11th June I received 108. for cherries-on
25th January I had 28. 6d., not 38. 6d.
Cross-examined. I have nothing to do with the prices--I have some-
times found something the matter with your goods--you always made it
up if there was anything wrong, but you have not made this up.
AMELIA REDFORD. I paid the prisoner 118. 7d. for potatoes, which he
has entered 108. 11d.
EDWARD BROOKS (Re-examined). I found this out on 11th June, and
the prisoner was discharged on 14th June- I went to all the customers
to find it all out.
