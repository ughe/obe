 What do you know about the other two boys? - I know nothing of them; I never saw them above once before.
 Prisoner. When I came to work he said, if I did not give him and a man in the house something he would have me taken up.
 Spencer. It is false; I never asked him for a farthing in my life.
 How came you to make a discovery of this, did you do it immediately? - I made a discovery of it on Sunday.
 You heard of it on the Friday? - I did not hear it all on Friday as we were working together; I did not hear of the money till Saturday.
 Did not you hear of the proclamation of a reward which came out on Friday? - No; I do not want a shilling.
 You do not desire any reward? - No.
 Prisoner. Mr. Spencer and  Joseph Kelly, who lives in his house, were always asking me if I could let them have money or they would turn evidence and have me taken up.
   RICHARD BROWN sworn.
 I went with Swift into the apartment and apprehended the prisoner on the Monday morning; under the bed we found a box which belonged to  Thomas Prior; there was a bag of halfpence in it; searching the room we found another box which Vanderbank said belonged to him; in that box we found two medals, a bag of halfpence, two rims of an etwee case, and an almanack. Prior said, They were chucked to him out of the fire; that the bag of halfpence hit him and cut his face. Vanderbank then owned they had them out of the fire.
   JOHN DUXBURY sworn.
 I am clerk to Mr. Langdale; this bag is the property of Mr. Langdale; it has my mark on it. This book (the Almanack) is Mr. Langdale's; I can swear to his handwriting which is in it; the figures on the back are his; here are some memorandums in it relating to the rise of rums; the other things I cannot swear to; Mr. Langdale had a medal like this, but I cannot swear to it; we tied the halfpence up in three guinea bags.
   WILLIAM CORNER sworn.
 I am clerk to Mr. Langdale. On Wednesday evening the 7th of June last, between six and seven o'clock, I saw Vanderbank in Mr. Langdale's warehouse, but I did not see him doing any thing; one of Sir  John Fielding's men came and took me to Clerkenwell to see if I knew the prisoner; they were taken out separately, and I knew Vanderbank.
 VANDERBANK's DEFENCE.
 Going along Shoe-lane I kicked a bag of halfpence; I carried it home; I put it in my box and went to bed.
 PRIOR's DEFENCE.
 My master cautioned me to keep out of the mob; as I came past Mr. Langdale's door the bag of halfpence were thrown in my face and cut it; there were a parcel in the house scrambling for the bag; a brewer's servant bid me take i t home.
 (There being no evidence to affect  James Prior he was not put upon his defence.)
 For the prisoners.
   PAXTON MARSHALL sworn.
 I know both the Priors; I have known them both I believe these three years.
 How old is the least of them? - I do not know how old he is.
 What sort of character have they born? - The time that I have known them very good characters.
 Are they apprentices to any trade? - Not that I know of. Their father is dead; their mother is alive.  Thomas Prior follows the plastering business; his father was a plasterer.
 Who does he work with? - I really do not know.
