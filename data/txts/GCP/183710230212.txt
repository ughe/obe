2418. ALEXANDER VICKERY was indicted for stealing, on the
25th of July, 1 gown, value 21. 10.; 4 shirts, value 15s. ; 4 table-cloths
value 13s. ; 1 pair of shoes, value 4s. ; 1 night-gown, value 1s. ; 2 petti-
coats, value 3s. ; 3 sheets, value 6s. ; 5 towels, value 28. ; 3 knife-cloths
value 1s. : 3 shifts, value 6s. ; 1 curtain, value 6d. ; 1 pair of drawers.
value 1s. ; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s. 6d. ; 1 bolster-case, value 6d.; 1
pillow-case, value 6d. ; 1 apron, value 1s.; 1 watch-guard, value 18.; 1
cap, value 6d.; and 2 pinafores, value 1s. ; the goods of George Parker.
Esther PARKER. I am the wife of George Parker, of Ship-street,
Deptford New Town, a coachmaker. I have known the prisoner some time
by coming to visit a friend of ours-on the 25th of July, about five minutes
past eleven o'clock, he came to my house and wanted me to go and borrow
half a crown for him at Mrs. Knowldon's of High-street-he said she would
have it all ready-I proposed my little girl should go, but he said he would
rather I went-I went, but did not get the half-crown-I was absent ten
or twelve minutes--when I returned my little girl was crying- I was in-
duced to go up stairs, and missed all this property-he was not there-he
had said he would wait for the half-crown--they were all safe when I went
out.
FREDERICK NORMAN. I live at Mr. Nash's, pawnbroker, London-street,
Greenwich. I have a pair of shoes which were pawned on the 25th of July
by the prisoner.
Prisoner. I did not pawn any shoes that day.
George PARKER. I am the husband of Esther Parker. These are my
shoes--I found them at the pawnbroker's in about an hour-I took the
prisoner on the 11th of this month at Brixton tread-mill, where he had
been committed—I told the constable to take him-he asked him, “Do
you know that gentleman ?"-he said, "I have seen him"--I have found
nothing else.
GUILTY. Aged 22.-Transported for Seven Years.
