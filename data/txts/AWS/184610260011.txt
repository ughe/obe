The prisoner, in a long address, denied having given the prosecutria the name
of any barrister, or held out to her any inducement to employ him in the business;
slated that he had never come to London with her, except at her on earnest solici-
tation, and had received no money from her but a few pounds to pay his expenses
that she and her relatives were in the habit of consulling fortune-teliers and
others, and upon their representation she would hurry him up to London that he
had always told her he tbas unable to render her any assistance in recovering
her property, and that it was a folly to prosecute her claim without having any
documents ; she was in the habit of concealing herself in his house to avoid her
creditors ; she had produced a letter to him, which she said was from E. H.
Powell, Esg., a barrister ; that the prosecutria's brother had been raising
money for years on the representation of his being entitled to this property
that she was in the habit of going to people who, by consulting cards, told her that
there was great property coming to her, and that they used to see in the magic-
glass how matters were going on; he concluded by contending that there toas
nothing but the unsupported representations of the prosecutriz of her having paid
him the money, and that he had always told her it would be an act of madness to
sell her annuity to attempt to recover the property; he denied having given her
any letters.
