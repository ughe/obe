JOHN VINCENT. I am in Messrs. Taylor's service. I assisted in look-
ing out fifteen pockets-I cannot tell whether nine of them were Pye's
Ginbey was very particular in wanting them of the year 1841–he was
by himself then, when he went up into the loft along with me I saw
the person who was with bim below, and believe it was Bean.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON.. Q. You will not swear it was
Bean? A. I cannot swear it rightly.
MRS. EVERETT. I keep & beer-shop in Marlborough-street, Black-
friars-road. About two months ago, as near as I can recollect, Bean left
a sack with me, saying it was not his, it belonged to another man-this
looks like the sack-I did not see its contents till the police came, and
asked me for it it was in the same state on my premises till the officer
took it.
John WOODHOUSE re-examined. I found this sack, and produce its
contents, which is what is called straining tackle.
WILLIAM Dunx. I am a hop factor in the Borough. The hops stolen
were in my custody I have examined the samples taken out of these ten
pockets, and have compared them with the hops now at the station- I have
no doubt they are my property-I believe the sample of hops produced
by Unwin to be a part of my hops--they exactly correspond with one part
of them
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How many partners have you ?
4. Only my brother-I speak to the hope from their similarity--they cor-
respond exactly-I think hops are very easy of identity--this samplemre
(opening one)- has two sorts of hops in it-it is what we call false packed,
consisting partly of fine hops, and partly of brown hops--they were per-
haps gathered from two gardens, not having sufficient in one to make a
Tall pocket-it is a very unusual thing to have brown hops with fine
do not know whether it is unusual to have the growth of two gardens in
the same pocket-my relations are all hop-growers.
MR. RYLAND. Q. Had you such a pocket among yours? 4. Yes,
we had but one, and lost that identical pocket--I have not the least doubt
about it.
Court. Q. Experienced dealers in hops can tell the difference of
growth by the mere feel, can they not, without looking at the sample ? A.
They can--they could tell one from the other if they were blindfold.
