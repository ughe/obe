1416. JOHN THOMPSON was indicted for a misdemeanor.
SUSANNAH BURNs. I am the wife of Patrick Burns, he belongs to the
Royal Artillery. On the 9th of March we were at the Tower---the prisoner.
who was a stranger, came to me and inquired for my husband, Patrick
Burns-he said he was my mother's brother's son-he said he had
come from Ireland, and he had brought me a box weighing four evt
containing tea and sugar, soap, chocolate, seven silk handkerchiefs, and
some silk dresses, a cage and two parrots—he said he had brought it from
abroad for me, and asked if I had any money, he wanted 28. 6d. he said.
to release this box from the Custom-house- I gave him half-a-crown-he
said that was not sufficient, and I gave him a shilling more-- he said,
" Cousin, don't think I want to do you any harm, don't think I would
take any thing from you, I would rather give you something"-he st
down a few minutes, and then went away, and I never saw him again till
my husband took him I never got my box--my husband met him on the
Sunday, the next day, and said, “Come along with me, you have robbed
my wife"-he said, " Take my coat and hat, and 11d. in halfpence, and
let me go"-he said, "No, I will give you in charge."
Thomas COOPER. I am an officer. I took the prisoner into custody
from Patrick Burns.
Prisoner's Defence. I did it from perfect hunger and distress--I had
not had a morsel of food for two days—I had been for two nights on the
bridges of London.
GUILTY.* Aged 31.-Transported for Seven Years.
1417. WILLIAM KEENE was indicted for a misdemeanor.
CHRISTOPHER GARNER. I am a labouring man residing in Kent. I
became entitled to some premises at Shoreham, and at Horsham, which
was left me by my grandfather, and considering the prisoner an attorney,
1 employed him to get possession for me-l employed him as a lawyer,
and had paid him before this from 151. to 201.--when I spoke to the pri
soner about it, he told me he was clerk to Mr. Collyer, in Chancery-lane,
and that he was authorised to act for bim, and to use Mr. Collyer's name
- he said he was carrying on the proceedings to aid me in getting posses-
sion of my property-he told me he wanted money to carry that on-
gave him 51. on the 9th of March, in consequence of what he said he was
to carry on the business for me I was to go to Maidstone, and see him
there on the 11th of March, but he was not there-l never got my 51
back.
Prisoner. Q. Have you got any account of the money you paid me!
1. No, I know I paid you from 151. to 201.--I did not tell you when you
wrote to me for money in the country to write for twice as much as you
wanted, to deceive my sister---you wrote to me once for 11., and when I
came you wanted two.
Prisoner. Q. Did I not accompany you to Kemsden, and to Shoreham,
and serve ejectments? A. You served something, I do not know what
--my sister saw all the letters that came, for I could not read them.
SAMUEL COLLYER. I am a solicitor, and live in Chancery-lane.
