CHARLES HARDING. I am a labouring man, and life at Woolwich I know the prisoner Smith by sight—on Saturday, the 7th of October I saw him near four o'clock in the afternoon, down Charlton Manor-way—I saw him in the turnpike road first—I went down the Manor-way, and he came down there, having turned out of the road—he came down close to Mr. Strother's marsh-gate, took a halter out by the side of a bush, close to the gate, and undid it—I said to him, "That is Mr. Strother's marsh and mare," and he went into the marsh, and put the halter on a black mare in Mr. Strother's marsh—I said to him, "I think that mare belongs to Mr. Strother"—(he must have seen me before he went into the marsh)—he said, "No, it don't"—he brought the mare out of the marsh, and went right up the Manor-way, and turned towards Greenwich, when he got on the turnpike-road—I did not see him unfasten the gate when he went into the marsh, nor take any lock away when he went—I did not see whether any lock was left there—he did not appear to have any difficulty in opening the gate. JAMES GIDDENS. I am a licensed horse-slaughterer, and live in Black friars-road. I know the prisoner Matthews by having a horse from him about ten years ago—I went to his place and killed it—I have seen him since then, and have had transactions in business with him, but that was my first acquaintance with him—on Saturday, the 7th of October, he brought a black mare to my house, near upon seven o'clock in the even ing—he came to my gate with it, and asked me to buy it—I asked him how much—he said 50 s .—I got a light and looked round the horse, and asked him where he brought it from—he said he had come from Squire Fuller's, at Beckenham, and had brought it from there—that he came from there—his expression was, " It  came from there"—he said he wanted it killed, and to have the tail cut off, which must go back—he went behind the mare, and said, "You must cut the tail off about here," pointing to the place—he said one of Mr. Fuller's men was coming to his house for the tail, next Sunday week—I looked round the mare—she appeared very fleshy, and had a rowel in the side as if it was under a doctor—it appeared too good to kill, as I thought—I told him I had my suspicion, and should stop the mare, and take her to the station-house, and he had better come too—he said he would—I took the mare to the station-house, and told the Inspector I had suspicion it was stolen—I left it there—I saw her after wards at Woolwich, in possession of Mr. Strother, and am confident it was the same mare.
