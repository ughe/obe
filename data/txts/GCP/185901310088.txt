had come up again, and the magistrate said I was to come to the grand
jury at the Central Criminal Court.
COURT. 0. How do persons get into your house ! A. There is no way
of getting in without my opening the door, unless they make a tremendous
noise-none of the lodgers have keys--the other lodgers were in doors
before I went to bed.
Amy WEAVER. I live at Mrs. Coglan's I remember the Monday the
prisoner was taken into custody-he slept at home on the night previous to
that-I am certain of that, and he came down at half-past nine.
Cross-examined. 8. Did you see him in bed $ A. No; I slept in the
next room.
CHARLES LLEWELLEN (re-examined). The prisoner gave his address at
26, John-street, Clerkenwell-- I went there, and also to St. John-street, but
could find nothing respecting him-after I found out where he did live, he
told me himself that he lodged there-that is the house kept by Mrs. Coglan.
Guilty.—Six Years' Penal Servitude.
