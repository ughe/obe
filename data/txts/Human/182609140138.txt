 1645.  JAMES LACEY, CHARLES NEWMAN, and ROBERT WRIGHT, were indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 1 hat, value 10s.; 1 pencil, value 1s. 6d.; 1 knife, value 2s., and 1 umbrella, value 6s., the goods of Henry Coles , from his person.
 HENRY COLES. I had a hat, a pencil-case, a knife, and an umbrella with me, at the corner of  Cowcross-street, on the 28th of July - I had a fall there, which stunned me, and scratched my face - I know nothing more; but the hat, pencil-case, and knife are here.
   CHARLES CLARKE. On Friday evening, the 28th of July, about half-past nine o'clock, I saw Lacey, Newman, and another with Coles, who was very drunk, and had had a fall; they were in Turnmill-street - I saw Lacey turn up the skirt of Coles' coat, and take from his pocket a handkerchief, which he delivered to Newman; they went round Clerkenwell-close, and when they got to Corporation-lane I met a watchman, and told him; Lacey was then taken, and locked up; as I returned I saw Newman with a different hat to the one he used to wear, which he was offering for sale.
 Cross-examined by MR. FISH. Q. What description of boy was the other? A. He said he was an apprentice - he was discharged by the Magistrate.
   SAMUEL SMITH. On the evening of the 28th of July, about half-past nine o'clock, I saw the three prisoners and Coles in Cowcross-street - he was very tipsy, and had a desperate fall; he dropped the umbrella out of his hand; it rolled into the kennel - I saw Wright pick it up, and walk by the side of Coles, and then go down a court by a public-house. I then saw Lacey, Newman, and another follow Coles, and at the corner of Clerkenwell-close I saw Lacey take the handkerchief, and by the New Prison Newman took Coles' hat off his head, and put his own hat on Coles' head.
 Cross-examined. Q. What are you? A. I am an apprentice to Mr. Hey, of Coppice-row. I knew Wright, by seeing him on Clerkenwell-green - he had the same dress he has now - I was not a yard from him - there was light by the public-house; he put the umbrella under his arm. I never stated that he had light-coloured trousers on.
 Prisoner WRIGHT. He stated at the office that the boy who ran away took the handkerchief and umbrella? Witness. No, I did not; he took up the umbrella, and put it under his arm; he then turned into a court, as if he was unlocking a door, I thought, and two children followed him. The prosecutor was quite senseless; Lacey took hold of him, and was dragging him along; Newman took his hat off.
   JONATHAN BROWN. I took Lacey, and one who is not here, to the watch-house. I saw the pencil-case and knife taken from Lacey.
   JOHN LEADMAN. I am a constable. I took up Newman on Friday, the 28th, about ten o'clock, on the information of Clarke and others. I found this hat on his head, which he said he got for 2s., and his own in exchange.
   JOHN POTTINGER, I took up Wright on Saturday - he said he knew the prisoners, but knew nothing of the robbery.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 LACEY's Defence. I was walking up Turnmill-street, and saw the prosecutor lay on the ground - I got him up, helped him along, and proposed taking him to the watch-house; the other young man took hold of him - I was then taken to the watch-house.
 NEWMAN's Defence. I met two or three people selling the hat - one man said, "Look at it?" I said, "What do you want for it?" he said 2s. 6d. and my old hat.
 LACEY -    GUILTY. Aged 22.
 NEWMAN -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined One Month.
 WRIGHT -    NOT GUILTY.
 1646.  MARGARET BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , 20 yards of cotton, value 14s., the goods of Thomas Davies.
   CAROLINE BRADSHAW. I was in Holborn on the 11th of September, near Mr. Davies' shop; I saw the prisoner go and take the cotton off the rail, and put it into her lap - she went a little way into the shop, then came out and went down Chancery-lane; I went and told the shopman, who pursued, and stopped her - I saw the cotton fall from her in Holborn.
   ROBERT MIERS. I am shopman to Mr. Davies. I pursued the prisoner and found this cotton in her apron - she said "Be off, you don't want me;" she let it fall out of her apron - it has our shop mark on it.
   JONATHAN NICHOLSON. I am the officer, and took the prisoner. She said at the office, she picked it up, and had a right to it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was very much in liquor.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. Transported for Seven Years.
 1647.  MARTHA BURGESS was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , 1 waistcoat, value 6s., the goods of James Payne and George Henry Bassington.
   JOHN YOUART. I am in the employ of James Payne and George Henry Bassington; they live in High-street, Whitechapel. On the 11th of August this waistcoat was in the shop; I did not see it taken, but in consequence of information I pursued, and found it in the prisoner's apron.
   WILLIAM HAWKINS. I was standing opposite the shop, and a man said "I believe that woman has taken something;" I looked round, and the prisoner was putting something into her apron; I had seen the waistcoat not a quarter of an hour before in the shop,
   WILLIAM DICKINSON. I am the officer. I took her in charge.
