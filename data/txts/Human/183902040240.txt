   Prisoner.  They were intoxicated. Witness.  We were not. HENRY GUY( police-constable R  159.) On Sunday night, the 13th of January, I was on duty in Broadway, Deptford, and saw Francis and the prisoner—he told me she had robbed him of 1 l . 7 s ., and desired me to take her into custody—I asked her if she had done so—she denied having taken any money at all, or having any—I took her to the station-house, and asked her to give me what she had in her mouth—she said she had nothing—I then took her by the throat, fearing she might swallow it, and she spat out a half-crown, and one shilling and one half-penny—the prosecutor was perfectly sober.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I met the prosecutor on this evening while I was in company with a young woman—he gave me a quartern of rum to drink at the Centurion—I said I would not stop there, as it was so public—we then went to the Lord Duncan, and had something there—he said he wanted to speak to me, and we went out—he said, "Cannot I go home with you?"—I said, "No, you cannot"—he said, "Cannot you come up here with me?"—I went with him up High-street—he was so tipsy he could hardly walk, and was very dirty—he then said be had lost his money—we went to the Lord Duncan, and Culmer asked me if I had his money—I said no, I had none but my own—they began to pull me about, and fearing I should lose my money, I put it into my mouth.
   J. T. FRANCIS re-examined.  The prisoner came to me in the Centurion, and I gave her something to drink there—I gave her some thing at the Lord Duncan also—I have known her from quite a child—her father and mother are dead—they were very respectable—her father was a warrant-officer—I never had any connexion with her in my life, but I understand she gets her living by walking the streets. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined One Month.

I produce some silk handkerchiefs which I received on Wednesday evening, the 23rd of January, from the serjeant-major of the rifle brigade—in con sequence of information, I went to Mr. Whistler's shop at Greenwich, and afterwards to the barracks, and took the prisoner—I told him the charge—he said he had received the handkerchiefs from a girl at Deptford. JOSEPH WILKINSON. I am a sergeant-major in the rifle brigade, stationed at Woolwich. The prisoner was a private in the corps—on the morning of the 23rd of January, I sent for him, and asked him if three handkerchiefs given to me by Ford, belonged to him—he said they did, and they were given to him by a girl at Deptford—he had one round his neck of the same pattern—I desired him to take that off, and reported it to the commanding officer, who desired me to give them to the constable to make inquiry about them—there was room for a dozen handkerchiefs under his waistcoat.
