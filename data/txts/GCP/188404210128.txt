Thomas SWEETING DAY. I am a chemist, of 28, Norwood Road, Herne
Hill-on 22nd March, about 1 o'clock, the prisoner came in and asked
for 38. worth of postage-stamps, and put down sixpence and a half-crown
under them, which I found was bad- I turned round to lock the door,
but he rushed out, leaving the money and the stamps; I ran after him;
he took off his coat and ran as hard as he could-he was stopped 300
yards away-he then threw himself on the pavement-he asked me to
look across the road, where I should see a gentleman who sent him for the
stamps-I looked, but saw no one-I took him to the station, and gave
the half-crown to the police.
Cross-examined by the Prisonor. You did not ask me to look at a gentle-
man in a cab.
FREDERICK Morr (Policeman P 189). Mr. Day gave the prisoner into
my custody- I found on him a florin, three shillings, two sixpences, and
three pence-the first witness gave me these two hall-crowns- the prisoner
said that a gentleman gave him the half-crown and six pennies to
purchase 38. worth of stamps-he said, “The coat don't belong to me, it
belongs to the gentleman who sent me for the stamps"-I tried it on
him, it fitted him exactly, and he is wearing it now.
WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. These three half-crowns are bad, and the
one from Mr. Day is from the same mould as one of the others.
Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing about that little girl. A gentleman
sent me for the stamps, and said he would give me 2d. for my trouble.
GUILTY of the second uttering.-Six Months' Hard Labour.
