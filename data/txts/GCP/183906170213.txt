stolen plate--I was told then that I ought to have been in the dock, but allow
me to explain : a regular dealer I had known for years by the name of Lau-
rence, well known in the trade, came to my shop one morning, and produced
some plate--he asked me for that plate 98.6d. peroz.--I offered him for that
plate 98. an ounce-he would not take it, and was going with it to the next
neighbour of mine, Mr. Warner, a silversmith in the Strand I sent for
him back again, and offered him 9s. 3d, for it-he would not take that
there being some articles among them which I wanted, (I should not have
given that price at any other time, but there being two or three articles
I wanted) i gave him 9s. 6d. an ounce for it--some few days after, to
the best of my recollection, my young man came home and said he had
seen a bill at Messrs. Bult's with a description of this plate, saying it was
stolen by a town-traveller of Mr. Lias ; that it had been absconded with
by a town-traveller of Mr. Lias, the same plate that I had purchased
-he discovered that it had been stolen-we sent immediately to Mr. Lau.
rence to give him information of it-he, in his turn, went to Mr. Lias,
and gave the information himself who he had bought it of, and sold it to
mewe gave the information from our house to Mr. Laurence, and he
went and told Mr. Lias--we had no business to inform Mr. Lias-we told
the man we bought it of-I gave evidence in the other Court when the
case was tried there I was told by the Judge that I ought to have been
placed in the dock, and tried-he told me I was a lucky man not to be
placed in the dock-that was in answer to a question I asked-I asked
who the plate was to be given to, to the best of my recollection I did not
ask if I was to have it, to the best of my recollection--he did not say I
was lucky in not being tried for receiving stolen goods-he said, "You
are a lucky man not to be placed in the dgek;" but I beg to tell you I
was then going to give the explanation to the Judge of our having
given information in the first instance of it; and he said, "There is no im.
putation on you, Mr. Solomon"-it was Mr. Sergeant Arabin, I think,
tried the case I do not know that I was complimented by the Counsel
-the case was tried at night, and, in the confusion, asking about the plate,
that observation did pass in the Court, and it was the general impression
in the Court that the Judge mistook me for Mr. Laurence it was night
before the case was finished—I do not mean to swear it was night-I know
there were lamps alight-since then Mr. Laurence has paid me back at the
rate of 98. 6d an ounce, in bills at two, six, and nine months, which bills I
have sent, with some others, to Ransom's, the bankers-he gave me the
bills with the rate of five per cent. interest.
