931. GEORGE NUNN and JACOB JONES, stealing 1 coat, value 158.;

to which
NUNN pleaded Guilty--Confined One Year.
MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES BLACKEY. When Nunn was taken, be made a statement to me,
and Jones was taken.
George Nunn (the prisoner). Jones was Mr. Blackey's ostler-he was the
second-person who came down to me after Dowling-he said he understood I
had given Dowling a waistcoat, and if I did not give him something to make
two or three shillings with, he would tell of me-in consequence of that I gave
him a pair of new trowsers in the stable; they were my master's, I stole
them from the warehouse-he said he wanted to make a few shillings, and he
must have them he gave me 3s. about three weeks afterwards.
Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you get Mrs. Turner to
pawn something for you. A. No; she used to come down to her husband-
I did not ask her to pawn an article for me, as I was obliged to shut up the
shop—I have been to Mr. Turner's on Mr. Blackey's business- I asked him
to make some alteration in my clothes, I never left any of my clothes with
him to be altered, and did not tell him I did not want them altered because my
father was dead- I swear Mrs. Turner was not engaged by me to pawn any
articles - the prisoner lived at Mr. Blackey's-he was ostler and shoemaker
- these trowsers were given to him about the middle of the week-he came
to me between six and seven o'clock in the evening while I was at tea at
Mr. Blackey's house-Ellen Ditch, the female servant, was there, but he did
not speak before her he came and had his tea, then we went out, and he
asked me-it is most likely she saw us talking--there was a window-the
trowsers I gave the prisoner were a check or a mixture of some sort, I could
tell them if I saw them.
Q. But if you can't tell whether they were a check or a mixture, how
can you pretend to say you should know them again? 4. They are a small
check mixture I went right into the warehouse; it is adjoining the shup
- there was no one there -- I can't tell whether Mr. Blackey was at home
or not, I did not ask Ditch to save me from the prisoner-I talked to him
in a small yard about ten minutes-I then went through the kitchen into
the warehouse, brought out the trowsers, and gave them to him-Ditch was
then up-stairs-I saw her go-I carried the trowsers through the kitchen
openly in my hand-she might have come down.
MR. BODKIN. Q. The prisoner was in the habit of having his tea and
his meals there ? A. Yes ; Ditch often saw us together.
