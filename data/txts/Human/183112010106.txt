 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you ever give him permission to sell Mr. Aspland's hymns? A. No, never.
 COURT. Q. Did you ever give him liberty to take paper and sell it without showing it to you? A. Never- this had not been considered as waste-paper; this had been in my possession ever since the prisoner has been with me; it is not much in demand, as there are two cheaper editons - this had lain by, and I was less likely to have my attention called to it.

 236.  JOHN VELLELEY was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of November , 21 lbs. of brass, value 5s., the goods of George Green and others, his masters.
 WILLIAM JUDGE. I am a Thames Police-officer. I met a woman in the Commercial-road, on the morning of the 17th of November, carrying something very heavy under her cloak; I stopped her, and found it was ingots of brass - I went to Mr. Green's yard, and saw the prisoner standing outside the gate; he asked me and my brother officer who was wanted: I said, "  John Velleley" - he then begged of me not to let the firm know; I said,"We must:" I heard him tell my brother officer, that he got the metal out of the yard, and he acknowledged at the office, that the woman was his wife.
   THOMAS GRAY. I was with Judge, and saw the prisoner near the prosecutor's yard; I asked where he got the metal which his wife was carrying - he said he got it from Mr. Wigram's yard, and gave it to his wife to get a trifle.
   NATHANIEL CATON. I am in the employ of George Green, Wigram, and Green - the prisoner was nearly four years in their employ, as a journeyman: I cannot swear to the brass, but I believe it to be theirs - I have fitted these ingots to the moulds, and they fit exactly.
 Prisoner. I am sorry for it, they have been good masters to me - I have a wife, and two children. GUILTY. Aged 32.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 237.  MARY WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of November , 1 half-crown, the money of William Jackman , from his person.
   WILLIAM JACKMAN. I am a sawyer. On the 27th of November, I was walking up Oxford-street with some friends, about one o'clock in the morning; I had had a glass or two of liquor, but I was not intoxicated - the prisoner spoke to us, said she was very cold, and asked us to give her something to drink; we went to the Three Tuns, and had three half-quarterns of gin, and two pints of porter - we went out, and the two young men who had been with me went away; I went up a mews with the prisoner - I felt her hand in my left-hand pocket, where I had a shilling and a half-crown; I felt my pocket, and the half-crown was gone - I said, "You have taken my half-crown;" she denied it; the Policeman came up, and took her - she said at the office that she had one half-crown, but it was her own.
 Prisoner. He asked the other men to pay for the gin and beer, and said he had no money. Witness. Yes, I did, but I had put this money by to pay away.
   JAMES BARTON. I am a Policeman. The prosecutor charged the prisoner with robbing him; he was quite sober; the prisoner said she had a half-crown belonging to herself, and she gave it up at the watch-house from her pocket, she had no more money about her.
 Prisoner's Defence. He accused me of robbing him, and held my two hands; I said, "What are you going to do?" he felt his left-hand pocket, and said, "You have robbed me of half a crown;" I said, I had not; I thought he was going to strike me, and called the Policeman myself - it is my first offence.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 238.  WILLIAM WATTS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of November , 2 pairs of shoes, value 10s., the goods of James Rowe.
 JAMES ROWE. I  keep a sale-shop, at Kensington. I took the prisoner in out of charity till he got a situation; we parted on the 5th of November - I missed my shoes, and found this pair on him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe his parents are in great distress? A. I went into their house that evening, they did not seem so; I know these shoes are mine.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Confined Six Weeks.
