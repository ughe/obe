1657. JOHN CLEMENTS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of
June, 2 pairs of shoes, value 8s., the goods of Joseph Waite, his master,
JOSEPH WAITE. I am a boot and shoe-maker, and live at Pimlico. The
prisoner was my errand-boy-on the 22nd of June the witness Boore came
and produced to me these two pairs of shoes--I knew them to be mine
gave the prisoner into custody- I went with him to the station-house-he
was asked what he had done with the money-he said he had thrown't
down the water-closet.
WILLIAM BOORE. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I took in a pair of
shoes of the prisoner, on Monday, the 19th of June-on Thursday be
brought another pair-after he was gone I suspected that they came from
Mr. Waite's- I went there and told him of it—he gave the lad into custody.
THOMAS WORPEL (police-constable B 136.) I took the prisoner--the
inspector asked what he had done with the money he got for these shoes
- he said he put it down a water-closet.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 13.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury. ---Whipped,
and Discharged.
1658. EDWARD GRANGER was indicted for stealing, on the 18th
of June, 8 razors, value 48.; 1 razor-case, value 18.; 1 razor-strap, value
6d. ; 1 pair of scissors, value 6d.; 1 comb, value 3d.; 1 pair of speeta-
cles, value 9d. ; 1 pair of gaiters, value 6d.: +of a lb. of tobacco, value
28.; and 1 bag, value 6d.; the goods of Joseph Guest.
JOSEPH GUEST. I am a journeyman hairdresser. On Sunday, the 18th
of June, the prisoner came, with another person, to Stanhope-street, where
I was doing business, to be shaved-I shaved the prisoner first, and then
the other-the prisoner went away first--they appeared to be friends
after the prisoner had left I missed all these articles I ran out into Clare
market, and found the person that came in with him sitting on a block-
I gave him into custody, and then the wife of that one went with us to
Stone-cutter-alley, where the prisoner lived I found all the property there
-it was all secure I never saw the prisoner before.
WILLIAM WINTER (police-constable F 33.) The prosecutor came to me
- I went with him, took the other, and, through the information of his
wife, I went to the prisoner's room, and found these articles the prisoner
was at home it was about four o'clock-he was not sober, but he knew
what he was doing.
Prisoner. I never was guilty of a dishonest action-it has been my
study to support myself and family in honesty.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 26.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor.
Confined Five Days.
