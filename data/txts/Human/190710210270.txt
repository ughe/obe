I have never in my life been to Pinner's Hall, and never had any con  nection other than I have described with Herbert Smith or with any other business excepting that I knew the Lombard Financial Corpora tion was run by Herbert Smith. Part of the coverings that Herbert Smith did were as to the Lombard Financial Corporation clients, and part of the notes that are traced out by Mr. Cash from Fraser's ac counts to Smith were on account of the very losses incurred for the Lombard Financial Corporation clients, which Mr. Cash will find were taken out on pay days. We did not know that Mr. Cash would be a witness until just before this trial started, and we did not know what he was going to say. The prosecution were put under an obliga tion to give us notice of new evidence by October 10. Mr. Cash has made a great fuss with regard to £70 drawn out on March 11 in notes and traced to Herbert Smith's account, but if he will turn no Ellis's account of March 11 be will find that that £70 went into Ellis's account and was paid out of Percy Ellis's account on different Stock Exchange transactions that were coverings for clients. The Lombard Financial Corporation is at 80, Lombard Street. That was started on or about July 9, 1902. The offices were taken by the real Herbert Smith from Dr. Bennett. I never gave any guarantee to Dr. Bennett; I never saw him in my life or wrote to him. I was not in any way responsible for Herbert Smith taking those premises. It has not been suggested that there is anything wrong with the Lombard Financial Corporation that I know of. I have known Parkinson for many years. I introduced business to him. I never gave him any orders for printing for Chalmers and Cox; he got them from Hawkes. I knew Hawkes was running Chalmers and Cox. With reference to the quasi guarantee given to Parkinson. What happened was this, Mr. Parkinson came to see me, saying that he was in serious trouble with his managing director, and the directors of the company he re presented. I knew some of them. He said there was a chance of his entirely losing his situation. I asked him what was the matter. He said his instructions were not to give anybody more than one circu lar credit—that is to say, the last circular had to be paid for before a new one was issued, or printed, by the Argus Company. He said that on my recommendation of Hawkes and Smith he had extended the credit and that they had not paid. I said I could not afford to lose my credit with the Argus (which was unlimited), and as I did not want to see him lose his situation I would do my best to stir no Hawkes and Smith to pay the accounts, and if he did not get paid in two months' time I would see that his firm lost nothing by it. The three accounts Parkinson referred to were Chalmers and Cox, Herbert Smith, and Fraser. I saw Hawkes at the first opportunity and pressed him to pay his account with the Argus, and he did so. Hawkes is Chalmers and Cox. I pressed Harris to pay the other one, and be could not afford to do it. The other two accounts were both practi cally Herbert Smith and Fraser. Harris not paying, I did.
