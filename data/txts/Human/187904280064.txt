( The RECORDER informed the prisoner that he was getting out evidence which told against himself. ) WILLIAM WARD. I am a meat salesman, of 442, Camden Road—my late clerk's son brought me the cheque for 5 l . on the British Association, T. H. E. S. Toler general manager, payable to E. Parker, Esq., and dated 1st October, 1878, to change for his mother, whose name is Heslop—I did not like the look of it, but he said "You know me very well"—I said "Yes"—I paid it to my bankers, and it was returned dishonoured—I went to the office of the Association over twenty times for three months, and saw Toler many times—he promised me the money, and pretended that he would get it from the party it was made payable to, but I never got it—anybody having the least eye to business might see that there was no business going on.
   Cross-examined by Toler.  I expected to find men of business there, and to find the master when he was asked for—I did not give you the 5 l . to collect for you—what you did was in Heslop's name—I refused to go and drink with you—I would not be seen with you—I never instructed you to take pro ceedings in the Lord Mayor's Court—you told me that you had seen Captain Parker eight or nine' times—here are two letters which you wrote to Heslop ( produced ).
