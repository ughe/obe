 793. ROBERT ASTON  (28) , Burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Thomas Cramer , with intent to steal. MR. AUSTIN METCALFE conducted the Prosecution; and MR. THORNE COLE the Defence. ALFRED POTTER( Policeman X  383). On the 22nd Sept. I was in Shel don Street, Bishop's Road—I saw the prisoner and two men go to the front entranco of the Prince of Wales public-house at the corner of Bishop's Road—I was about thirteen yards off—they raised the shutter and the prisoner got in; the shutter was then shut down by the two remaining men, who stood with their backs towards it—one came towards me and I took him into custody, but some companions rescued him—I went back to the Prince of Wales Hotel and found the shutter open about a foot—I raised it and wont in, and found the prisoner inside at the right-hand side in front of the bar—I asked him what he was doing there—he said "Nothing"—we struggled—I succeeded in getting him down when he was partly in the street—I searched him at the station and found three keys, a penny, and a halfpenny—on examining the shutter I found the stone so much worn that there is room to get one's hand in and raise it.
   Cross-examined by MR. COLE . The time was about 1.15—the house is closed at twelve—I found no skeleton keys or jemmy on the prisoner or in the house—the shutters are revolving shutters and easily moved—I do not know of anything having been removed from the premises. GEORGE TAYLOR. I am manager to Thomas Cramer, of the Prince of Wales Tavern in Eastbourne Terrace—I saw the house shut up on the 22nd, and the shutter put down—I was the last person up—I cannot say whether there was a catch to the shutter then—there is now—any one pulling up the shutter could get  into the bar. GUILTY.* He also PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of felony at Clerkenwell in January , 1867, in the name of   William Jennings — Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
