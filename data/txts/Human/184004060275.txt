On Monday, the 2nd of March, I came home, and found the room-door locked—it was fastened outside, and something put into the key-hole—the prisoner was on the stairs—he said, "MRS. Curry, have you any money for me, if you have not, I am b—if you go into your room again—I have fastened the door up"—I went into the room through my mother's room, which is the next to mine, on the ground-floor, and there is a door from it into my room—the prisoner went out into the street, and got in through the window, attacked me, and pulled me down, and tried to get me out of the room, between the two doors—he wanted to push me through the middle door to get me out of the room—he jammed my arm between the door—my child came and pulled hold of my petticoats, and said, "You will kill my mother"—he took the child by the throat, and dashed her against the grate—her head fell against the fire-place, and her back over the fender—she was very much injured—she was quite well before this, but was very ill the same night, like a burning coal, and complained of pain in her stomach all the evening—so she continued till the following Monday—I got her some me dicine in the course of the week, and on Monday she brought up blood—I took her to Mr. Buck the doctor—she vomited blood again on Wednes day, and I took her again to Mr. Buck, and on the Thursday I took her to the Dispensary in Waterloo-road—she had advice there three days, and she died on the Saturday.
