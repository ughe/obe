2373. WILLIAM WHITE and THOMAS BORHAM were indicted for
stealing, on the 19th of June, 21 bushels of a certain mixture, consisting of
oats, beans, and chaff, value 2s. 6d. ; the goods of Henry Gray, the master
of White.
Mr. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution.
JOsEPH BENTON (police-constable K 381.) On Wednesday morning, the
19th of July, in consequence of information, I went to the King's Arms in Bow-
street, with Samuel Benton, to watch Borham, who was the ostler. I hid myself
close by the yard-they could not see me-I was looking through the win-
dow of a house-I could see all over the King's Arms yard-about nine
o'clock I saw Mr. Gray's cart come to the premises-White was with itat-he
fed his horses with some mixed victuals, which he took from the top of the
cart-he then went into the house-Borham was in the yard, attending on
the horses-he fed them with some new hay, which he took from the top of
the cart-in about half an hour he fed them again, with some more new hay-
he then went in-he and White came out together, and bad some conversae
tion, which I could not hear-White got on the top of the cart, and handed a
full sack down to Borham-I gave Samuel Benton the signal, and he went to
the spot in the private stable, where Borham went to-I saw the sack in the
stable, and took it, and Samuel Benton took Borham-White was at the top
of the cart-I told him to come down-he got down, and said he was going
to feed his horses again-I told him I should take him on suspicion, as I
had seen him feed his horses three times before--the sack contained about
two bushels and a-half of mixed victuals.
Cross-examined by Mr. CRoUCH. Q. What distance was you from the
place A. About fifteen yards-the cart was in the yard about an hour and
a-half-the farm-yard is at Dagenham--ther was no other cart in the yard
-there were persons coming in and out of the house, into the yard during the
time.
