 There was no-counsel on the part of the prosecution; Mr. Silvester and Mr. Garrow were of counsel for Mr. Kaye; but there being no evidence at all to affect him, the Judge directed the Jury to acquit him; and said it was a most shameful prosecution, and he had never heard any thing like it in his life; the Jury instantly found a verdict NOT GUILTY.

WILLIAM FLINT was indicted for that he not having the fear of God before his eyes, but being moved and seduced by the instigation of the devil, on the 29th day of June last, in and upon Mary Dixon , spinster, violently and forcibly did make an assault, and her the said Mary, against her will, feloniously did ravish, and carnally know.
   MARY DIXON sworn.
 I was put  apprentice out of St. Martin's work-house, to a tambour worker; about a month or two ago I was sent to Tothill-fields Bridewell for doing bad work , and tearing a hole in some muslin; I was in Bridewell a month on the apprentice's side; there was never another girl but me; I had a place by myself; and the door was locked at the first; I was locked in half the day, and the other half I was about the yard by myself; there was no other girl besides me; and the gate keeper left the gate open three days for that Captain that was in Bridewell to come to me; the gate keeper's name is Flint; I was standing in the cell, and this Captain, as they call him, came in and pushed me down, and put his hands before my mouth.
 Court. That is not the present question.
 Mr. Silvester. How soon after did you see Flint? - Flint served me with bread and water, and nobody else; and one day, when he brought it, he called me into the hemp-shop, where the bad people pick hemp; they had done work; it was their dinner time; I cannot tell the day; I believe it was the middle of the week; and he pushed me down between the bulks, and put his hand before my mouth because I should not halloo; and when he had done what he wanted, he threatened to flog me if I told the Governor or any body.
 Court. But what did he do child? - He put his private parts into my body; I was afraid, and did not tell any body; I continued there a fortnight after; but he never let me out after, only to wash the cell; I saw nobody else; my master came about two days after; I told him, I was well, because I was afraid of being licked; Flint staid with him all the time; when I came out of Bridewell, my master put me to his journey woman, Mrs. Stunnell, in King-street, Golden-square; I did not tell her the first day, but she saw my shift, and took me to task.
