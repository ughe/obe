1253. SUSANNAH HOWE was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MR. ELLIS conducted the prosecution.
   CHARLES THOROGOOD. I am a constable, and live in Somerset-street, Whitechapel. On Saturday, the 1st of May, at half-past twelve o'clock, I was at the back of the Royal Exchange, and saw the prisoner and a man close together in company, come from under the Piazzas; when they parted, the man went into a biscuit-shop, and the prisoner went round the corner; I told my brother officer that something was not right, and told him to watch the prisoner; I turned my back, and saw the man in the shop- I saw the shopwoman in the shop, take up something off the counter, and go into the parlour with it: I took the man, but not the prisoner.
   GEORGE WILLIAM MORGAN. I am a constable, and was with Thorogood; I watched the prisoner, who was at the corner of Sweeting's-rents - she came back to the corner, as if to look towards the biscuit-shop; (the man was discharged by the Magistrate;) when she crossed the road I said, "I want you," and I saw her put something into her left hand - I said,
 "You have something in your hand;" she said,
 "No, I have not;" I took hold of her hand, and took her into a house - I found this bit of rag in her hand; I asked what it contained - she said,
 "Money - good money to be sure;" I asked whether it was shillings or sovereigns - she said she did not know, she had found it; it contained these five shillings.
 MR.  JOHN FIELD. These five shillings are all counterfeit; they are cast, and appear to be from the same mould.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a man run by the Royal Exchange - I then saw these, and took them up; I had not had them two minutes.
 JURY to CHARLES THOROGOOD. Q. Did the man who went into the shop offer any bad money? A. Yes, this bad shilling.
 COURT. Q. And they were in company together? A. Yes, and that induced us to watch them.
     GUILTY. - Confined One Year.
 1254.  MARGARET TAYLOR was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MR. ELLIS conducted the prosecution.
   MARY STEPTO. I keep a butcher's-shop in Farringdon-market. The prisoner purchased a bit of meat of me on Saturday, the 10th of April, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon; it came to 8d. or 9d. - she offered a very bad shilling; I stopped her, and would not let her have the shilling back - Hopkins looked at it, said it was very bad, and gave it to the officer - I am sure the prisoner is the woman: she was taken to be examined.
   WILLIAM HOPKINS. I am a butcher, and live next door to Stepto. I received a shilling from her on the 10th of April - she asked if it was a good one; it was a very bad one - I asked the prisoner if she had any more; she made some reply I could not make out - I sent for Johnson, the officer, and gave him the same shilling.
 ROBERT JOHNSON. I am a watchman. I received the shilling from Hopkins on the 10th of April - I marked it, and gave it to Lloyd about half an hour afterwards; I took the prisoner down to the clerk's office - she was searched by a female; nothing was found on her - in going from the office she said she did not care a d-n, for they had only found a shilling about her, and could not do any thing with her - Mrs. Lloyd asked her if she knew Mr. Powell, the solicitor of the Mint; she said, Yes, perfectly well, but he could not hurt her - she was discharged; she gave herself the name of  Margaret Taylor.
   JAMES LLOYD. I am a constable. This is the shilling I got from the witness - I am certain it is the same; I told the prisoner she had been up to the hall many times, and asked her if she knew Mr. Powell; she said Yes, but she did not care a d-n about Mr. Powell or me either.
   SUSANNAH STAPLES. I am a widow, and keep the Queen's Arms, St. Martin's-le-grand. On the 19th of April, about six o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came to the bar - she had a glass of gin, and brought this bad half-crown - I gave her 2s. 4d. in change, in two shillings and 4d. in copper; the gin came to 2d.; I put the half-crown into the till among some halfpence - I am sure I had not another half-crown there - Mr. Purser came, and asked what she had given me; I took out the half-crown and gave him, which he saw was bad - the officer had the half-crown; I am certain the prisoner is the person.
   WILLIAM PURSER. I have heard Staples' evidence; it is correct - I knew the prisoner's voice, and ran to see where she was; I saw her in the bar, taking up the change - I asked what she had given, and saw this half-crown.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I am a constable, and was sent for to Staples' - it was said the prisoner had offered this half-crown, which Staples marked and gave me; I found 4d. in halfpence on her, but no silver - she said she did not change the half-crown, but changed a sixpence; this was when Staples said she brought the half-crown - there was another woman with the prisoner when I took her, but there was nothing on her.
 MR. FIELD. This shilling and half-crown are bad.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined One Year.
 1255.  MARGARET TOMKINS was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MR. ELLIS conducted the prosecution.
   URIAH BRYANT. I am a tin-plate worker, and live in Beech-street, Barbican. The prisoner came to my shop on the 17th of April, to buy a sancepan; I showed her one, which came to 8d. - she gave me half a crown; I looked at it, and thought it was bad - I gave it to my man to go to my next door neighbour; he brought it back in a minute, and said it was bad; I gave it to my son, and sent for an officer, who took the prisoner - I am sure she is the person.
   DAVID OWEN. My master gave me the half-crown which I paid to Mr. Whitaker, and then gave it back to my master.
   ADAM URIAH BRYANT. I am the prosecutor's son - I received the half-crown, and sent for the officer; I marked it, and gave it to Mr. Ripton - the prisoner went by the name of Tobin.
   ANTHONY RIPTON. I took the prisoner, and received this half-crown - I was at her examination; she gave the name of Tobin there, I am sure.
