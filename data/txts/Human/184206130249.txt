DENNIS COLEMAN and WILLIAM HOLDER were indicted for stealing, on the   13th of May , 2 bushels of oats and chaff, value 4 s  , the goods of Thomas Brocklebank and another, the master of Coleman. JOHN EDMONDS HUBBARD. I am in the employ of Thomas Brockle bank, and another, timber-merchants, at Rotherhithe—Coleraan was in their service. THOMAS WEST( police-constable M  249.) On the 13th of May I was on duty at Millpond-bridge, between one and two o'clock in the day—I saw Mr. Brocklebank's timber-carriage drive up—Coleman, the driver, went and spoke to Holder the ostler, at the public-house—I then saw Holder go and take a bag three parts full of chaff and corn, from the tim ber-carriage—Coleman had just left him, and gone to the horses' heads—Holder took the bag about fifteen yards from the carriage, into a shed where he keeps his horses and corn—I waited till he came out, and then I asked him if that property belonged to him—he said yes—I said I did not believe it, and he must go with me to the station—I got him back to the carriage, and then he said Coleman was going to leave it till he called for it—he said he was going to put it into some cribs—I did not hear what Coleman said—there were four nose-bags of corn for the horses.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSQN . Q.  Is there any statement about the nose-bags in your deposition A.  I did not state that—the deposi tions were read, and we were all asked if we had anything to add—I did not lay one thing or another—there is a shed in a passage by the side of the public-house at Millpond-bridge, where Holder keeps his things as an ostler. GEORGE POTTER( police-constable M  39.) I was on duty on the 13th of May, near Millpond-bridge, and I saw the carriage stop at the Queen Charlotte—I saw Coleman go and speak to the ostler—the ostler then re moved some horse-cloths off the sack, took the sack in his arms and went to the rear of the public-house, towards a place where he keeps corn—I followed him down—I then went back and apprehended Coleman, the carter—I said I wanted him to go with me?—he said, "For God's sake don't take us down for that, it is only a little corn and chaff"—I saw some nose-bags—they were all three-parts full. JOHN EDMONDS HUBBARD re-examined.  From what I have heard from one of our carmen, the quantity of food Coleman had altogether, was about sufficient to provide his horses for the journey he had to go, pro vided they had sufficient before they set out, but they ought to have emp tied their nose-bags at Paddington—they were coming back.
   Coleman's Defence.  I went with the team—the man who was accus tomed to go told me he had been detained at Paddington an hour or an hour and a half, and if I had been the nose-bags would have been emptied, but I did not stay a quarter of an hour; when I got back to the Queen Char lotte I said to the ostler, "Empty this stuff into the crib, and feed the horses"—I thought I would keep the nose-bags full. NOT GUILTY.
