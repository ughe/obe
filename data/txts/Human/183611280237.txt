On the 5th of November I was coming from the Borough, about six o'clock at night, and called at Mr. Fenwick's shop—Lamb had got a bad shilling—Harrison came in and gave Lamb another shilling—in consequence of what Lamb said I went to the shop in Melina-place, and saw the prisoner there—I told him he had been passing bad shillings—he said he had not, or how could he do it, and began a unbutton his coat—he had nothing about him but one farthing—Harri son gave me two shillings—I gave them and the prisoner to a policeman. ROBERT CREASY( police-constable L  172.) On the 5th of November Dunn gave the prisoner and these two shillings into my charge. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin. These are both counterfeit, but not from the same die.
   Prisoner's Defence.  If they were bad I did not know it, as I took them in change in coming over Westminster-bridge, where I bought a comb—I did not look at them. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined One Year.
 223. JOHN SMITH was indicted for a misdemeanor. MARY CARELESS. My husband keeps a shop in Robert-street, North Brixton, and sells tripe. On the 12th of November the prisoner came there, and bought 1/2lb. of sausages—he gave me a shilling—I gave him 9 1/2 d .—I said, "I do not like this shilling, I do not think it is good"—he said, "I wish I had one more than I could carry"—he went out, and I looked at it again, and found it was bad—I put it into a tea-pot on a shelf, and kept it till I gave it to Mr. Slaughter—there was no other with it—on the 14th of November, about nine o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came again for a red herring, which I put on the counter—he tendered another shilling—I saw it was the same sort of coin as he had given me before—I said I could not give him change—he said he would get change—I then sent for a policeman—Slaughter came before the prisoner was out of sight—I gave him the shilling I first took, after I had marked it. JOHN MARTIN. I live in Bromley-row, Clapham, and sell shoemaker's materials. The prisoner came on the 14th of November about a quarter after ten o'clock for a pennyworth of nails, and gave me a bad shilling, I gave him 11 d .—while I was serving, he amused me with a tale of his having some onions to sell—I said I wanted none that prevented me from noticing the shilling so much as I might—I put it into my pocket—I had there two sixpences, and another shilling, a new one, which I had taken three quarters of an hour before—the shilling the prisoner gave me was the coin of George III—I had it in my pocket about two days—I soon after saw Slaughter—I did not give him the shilling till ordered by the Magistrate. GEORGE SLAUGHTER( police-constable P  152.) In consequence of what Mrs. Careless said, I went after the prisoner—I first saw him in the Clap ham-road—I saw him go to Mr. Martin's shop—I waited till the came out, and took him—Mr. Martin gave me this shilling—I searched the prisoner, and found two good sixpences and 10 d . in copper—I told the prisoner I had got something else against him, for passing a shilling to Mrs. Careless—he said he had not been that road that day that he came from Wandsworth—I took him to the station-house.
