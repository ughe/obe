and 17th of Dec.-I know this pipe-I fixed it inside against the wall, to
bring water from the upper part to the lower part of the building. I have

Prisoner. Q. Did you cut that end off yourself? A. No, I was not
there when it was cut off-I find this corresponds with the end it was cut
from-it was cut off with a saw-here are two saw marks which the man
told me he left in, when he took it off-I have matched this to the saw cut, in
the offset in the wall.
RICHARD LOWRIE. I am clerk of the works at the new Houses of Parlia-
ment. This lead produced is the property of Her Majesty-it is part of the
Government works at the new Houses of Parliament-the prisoner is a car-
penter-he was employed there about eighteen months by Mr. Grissell.
Prisoner. I saw this lead lying in the snow; a man saw me pick it up;
he was present on the first examination, but not on the second.
GEORGE RODWELL re-examined. Q. Do you know the place where this
lead was fixed ? A. Yes, I went and saw it-it was not so exposed that the
snow would get at it-it was in a covered place in a vault.
WILLIAM HILL re-examined. Q. How can you account for this pipe being
all over snow when the prisoner had it? A. If it were so it must have been
thrown over the fence into a passage that leads to the Committee-rooms, and
in falling over it would be buried in snow-there was no snow on it when he
took it-he must have brought it 150 yards from the place where he got it,
before it was thrown over the fence. .
Prisoner's Defence. If I stole it it is hardly reasonable I should be car-
rying it openly on my shoulder at that time of night; it was out in the snow
when I saw it; I was not on the premises after half-past five o'clock-I knew.
it did not belong to me; and when the policeman stopped me I did not know
what to say.

715. FREDERICK ATKINS was indicted for stealing 5 printed books,
value 18. 6d. ; the goods of Richard Francis Salter, his master.
RICHARD FRANCIS SALTER. I live in Park-place Villas, Maida-hill-the
prisoner was employed by me for about three months as an errand-boy-he
has been with me ever since I have been missing different things at various
times- I could not tell who to suspect I did not miss any books, but after I
missed other things, the policeman said it was necessary to go to the prisoner's
lodging, and there these books were found his mother is a poor wretched wo-
man--she said he had brought them home a week previous, and he said I had
lent him them to read-they are five old school-books of mine, of no particular
value-- he had 1s. 6d. a week to clean knives and boots--I said he might stop
when he pleased, and have his dinner, as his family was so poor he had no
dinner at home I have lost things of great value-a gold chain, and my wife
Jost a gold thimble, and I had marked some money which was found on him.
THOMAS WILKINS (police-constable D 117.) I went to the prisoner's
mother's house, and found these books-Mr. Salter told me the prisoner
lodged there, and I went.
