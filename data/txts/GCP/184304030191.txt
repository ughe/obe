LES MAGEE. I am in the band of the Royal Artillery. On the 29th of
March, about ten minutes past eight o'clock, I was on Woolwich Common, in
company with a female, on the public footpath, going from Woolwich towards the
Cadets-barracks--I saw two men pass me on the right-after they had passed
a considerable distance, they turned back, and opened out, and I felt a rope
round me very soon-I extracted myself from it, and pulled myself towards
the one that held it-1 had hold of it--then the other one, in company with
two men, came on me behind-I did not see them previously they pulled me
down, and ill-used me in a shameful manner, they kicked me, and said, "Gag
him, give it him”- they allowed me to get up, but, I think, through fear; for
when I got on my feet, I observed a gentleman at a short distance, and they
ran away--when I got up, I missed a black glazed uniform cap, and a pair of
gloves which were inside -- did not know any of the persons then I can
identify the prisoner-I am quite sure he was one--it was rather dark-I had
seen the prisoner several times before, but never knew who he was, or what
his name was-he must have been one of the two who passed me I think he
was the one that had the rope-I am quite sure he was one of the four.
THOMAS HUNTER. I live in Jackson's-lane, Woolwich Common. I was
not on the common on Wednesday last-about half-past eight o'clock, as I
was coming up from the barracks towards the White-gate, I met the prisoner,
with a cap on his head-it was like a soldier's cap- the top was torn off-it
was glazed round the peak-I had known the prisoner by sight before--I am
quite sure he is the person I saw with the cap. 9

Louis FERDINAND KELLY. I live at Woolwich I have been in the Artil
lery, and was discharged through a hurt. The prisoner lives next door to me
- last Thursday, between eleven and twelve o'clock, I saw him in the barrack
yard fronting his father's house, with a lightish-coloured cap on his head_I
looked at it-it appeared to me to be the cap now produced it was similar
to it-when he saw me he took the cap off, and put it under his arm OSB
WILLIAM GLADWIN (police-constable R 112.) I apprehended the pri-
soner on Thursday afternoon, between two and three o'clock, at his father's
house his brother and sister were also in the house he was concealed under
a bed-the windows were all barricadoed up, the shutters and door fastened
I got in by taking two or three tiles off the roof-I told him I apprehended
him on a charge of assaulting and stealing a cap from Mr. Magee, one of the
bandsmen-he said there were others concerned as well as himself-I took him
to the station-next day I searched a privy in front of his father's house, ten
or fifteen yards from it, in the yard, where the prisoner was at work in the
morning, and there found this cap.
JAMES MAGEE re-examined. I can swear this is my cap--my name is in
two places in the inside of it-the whole of it was covered with glaze, similar
to this front, but the top is torn off.
Prisoner's Defence. "I had nothing to do with the rope - I never had the
cap in my possession.
Guilty. Aged 17.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Months.com
