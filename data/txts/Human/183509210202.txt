Q.  Did Nonton say in Mr. Bardell's presence, that if the proprietors did not consent to your proposition, he would ruin Bolton, Pearee, Bird, and Trevett, or he would have his heart out and his limbs broken? A.  No: Bardell was not desired to ask his brothers if the proprietors would come to terms—I saw Mann this morning—I do not know whether he is a wit ness for me—he is one of the persons who complained that he was hunted—I never gave Mann directions to drive against Bolton—not any person—I do not know this man ( looking at a man named Archer )—I never saw him before, to my knowledge.
   Q.  Did you ever say to your conductor, in Archer's presence, "Go on wwith that omnibus ( pointing to one ), and stick to him; I will have the shirt off his back before I have done with him?" A.  Never in my life—I know Parish, the policeman, No. 45—I never had any conversation with him about this—I do not know that ever he was obliged to lead my omni bus out of the way—I know No. 7 has—I never spoke to him in the Poultry about my opposition, to my knowledge—I know a person named Alexander—I have no recollection of mentioning the name of Alexander to Parish.
   Q.  Do you recollect his saying that you could not expect the people would let you take the bread out of their months withour giving you some trouble? A.  No; I think I have heard such words, but I think it was from No. 7, not from Parish.
   Q.  Did you say, "I should have been very well off, if Alexander had not put me in the hold?" A. I said I should have been better off if Alexander had come out with me, as he promised—I possibly might say we should have made the Paddington masters look about them—I have been summoned two or three times by the police for causing an obstruction—I have heard of the names of Colson and Wilson as time-keepers—I have seen Colson, and spoken to him.
   Q.  Have you ever said this in his presence, That unless you could receive 100 l . from the proprietors, you would indict them all fro a conspi racy, and ruin them? A.  Never in my life to any soul—I remember two of Trevett's mem being taken into custody.
   Q.  Do you remember using this expression, in the presence of Colson, that you had been as good as your word; it was not the men you wished to hurt, as you could get nothing out of them, but their masters, and that the law must take its course, unless you were paid 100 l . to take your omnibus off?" A.  Never, to any man upon earth—I have spoken to Bates, the policeman, No. 7—he has summoned my driver two or three times fro obstructing the road and he summoned me twice, once was for taking time at the Bank—he said I should not take time—my man got off by paying 5 s .—I was summoned to Marlborough-street—one of Mr. Bol ton's omnibuses ran against mine—my son got into a gallop, and they were both had up  —It is the men that are summoned, but the master always at tends, and if they are fined he pays it—I know Bates has often told me of my men doing wrong—I said, "Then summons them, I have nothing to do with it; I have spoken to my men about it, and they said they did no such thing"—I cannot tell whether Mann is here—I have not subpœnaed him—I was not afraid that he would tell the jury that I ordered him to drive against Bolton—I never said so—nor about getting 100 l .—I know the policeman, No. 13—I do not know his name—I recollect his having two persons in custody one night at the Mansion-house.
