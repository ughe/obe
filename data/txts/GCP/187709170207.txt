were at Mrs. Charlton's house by seeing the drawers there-- I told my
husband that Charlton's had got the things without my consent or knowledge

things, and I did not lead him to believe that they were minding the tbings

were mine and I had a right to do what I liked with them-I lived at
Harringay Road, Hornsey, in May, 1876, going on for three years, there
was a bill of sale on the furniture, but I cannot say what date it was or
the sum-I value this furniture at 121.- I lived at 6, Edith Terrace,
Fulbam, in 1875-I only knew of one bill of sale—we moved and the bill of
sale went with us--Sarah Goodship was in our employment and was in the
house when the things were removed-she attended at the police-court every
day during the hearing ; she was called by my solicitor-she knew nothing
about it, then she was Lrought up by other parties--she was not put in
the box by my solicitor--I arranged with Mrs. Charlton to come and knook
us up early--the servant let her in- I locked up over night and saw all
safe--the prisoners got in at the back window; the bolt was not broken
-Mrs. Charlton did not say she got in through the window which the
servant had left unbolted-Mrs. Charlton came upstairs when I was leaving
the back room when I had seen the chest of drawers going over the wall-
I did not complain to her about it. (James Lovegrove was here called in.) I
know that man and I know Mr. Ward-those two men were engaged in
the removal—I, Mrs. Charlton, and Sarah Goodship were looking out for
the vans when they drove up to the door—I had not quite recovered, I was
not sober-I did not speak to the carmen more than necessary-I did not
know who Clayton was-1 did not tell Sarah Goodship that some of the
things were to go to ber mother-I was not in the room when some things
were taken to Mrs. Charlton's--I did not go to Mr. Goodship's house ;T

the Euston Road at 10 o'clock-I did not send out for brandy or spirits.
