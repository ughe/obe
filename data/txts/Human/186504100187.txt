   Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY . Q.  Have you never seen blankets sold? A.  No; I know they are sold, I have not been present at sales, but I know that all blankets that are sold are torn into four pieces—I don't know that Brooks served in the Crimea, except from his own statements—no man was allowed to have more than one blanket—I have examined those reins; the mark is simply impressed, they are stamped before they are made—this leather has been blackened since the hide has been out, and it is blackened on the wrong side. WILLIAM ENDON. I am a driver in the Royal artillery—on 21st February I was in the officers' kitchen, employed as a servant to an officer—I saw Harrison on that day between twelve and one, in the kitchen—I saw a roll of brownish leather about six feet long lying in the kitchen—Harrison asked a woman who was there, if she would take the leather out for him, I can't say who she was—she said, "No,"—he then asked John Hooton if he would take it out for him, I believe Hooton took it outside; I did not see him do so—I was in the kitchen again between nine and ten o'clock the same night and the leather was gone—nothing was said in my presence about where it came from—to tell you the truth Har rison said he had taken it from under the quartermaster's nose and he has not seen him—that same night I was at the Mitre Tavern, I saw Hooton come in; I asked him to drink with me—Harrison also came in, I can't swear whether it was the same night or not, nothing took place.
   Cross-examined by MR. H. PALMER . Q.  I believe you know Harrison, do you not? A.  That was the first time I ever saw him—the leather was lying on the floor, it was not covered over with anything that I saw, it was not wrapped up, you could see it was leather—I was out of the kitchen two or three times during that afternoon—I had charge of the kitchen, the woman was not one of the servants—she is not here to-day—officers do not give leather work to their soldiers to do privately for them, that I am aware of—there are men in the regiment who are in the trade—there are boot makers belonging to the service who work in the shop belonging to the Government, there are also harness-makers—a great many came into the kitchen that day, non-commissioned officers, no others were allowed in Harrison is a non-commissioned officer—my commanding officer returned that evening—three men were put in the guard-room, Hooton was one and I was one, it was for being half an hour absent from the barrok-room—during my absence from the kitchen a great many people might have gone in.
   JURY . Q.  Where is this officers' kitchen, in the arsenal? A.  No; in the West-square, going into the Artillery barracks—I was not put under arrest for having anything to do with the leather, nothing at all had been discovered about the leather then.
