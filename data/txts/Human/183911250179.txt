 262. JAMES SCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of Novem ber , 1 whip, value 2 s  ., the goods of Thomas Watkinson . THOMAS WATKINSON. I am a nephew to Thomas Watkinson; a baker, at Tooting Gravaney. I lost a light cart-whip on the 6th of November, about a quarter to seven o'clock in the evening, from outside the house of Mr. Holder, on Wimbledon-common, where I was delivering bread—I missed it when I returned to the cart, and also aquartern loaf.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are yon quite sure you saw the whip just before you went into the house? A.  Yes—I know it again by its having two quills sticking out at the top—I am quite sure it is mine—I could not have dropped it. CHARLES WREN. I am a baker, and live at Wimbledon. The prisoner was at the Bald-faced Stag public-house, Putney-bottom, on the 7th of November, and I bought this whip of him for 43/4 d .—I was to give him 1 s . for it, but I gave him a half quartern loaf in part payment—I gave infor mation to the police as soon as I got home, and delivered it up to the policeman.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time did yon buy it? A.  About a quarter before twelve o'clock—I knew the whip to be the prosecutor's, and bought it with the intention of giving it to him—I told the prisoner it was my friend's whip—he said it could not be, for he found it against West Barnes gate. NOT GUILTY.
