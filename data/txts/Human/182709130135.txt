1911. ANDREW RUGG, alias    RUBY, and MARGARET COX, alias    RUBY , alias   THOMPSON, were indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , 1 ring, value 2s., and 1 petticoat value 1s. 6d., the goods of Jemima Sarah Betts.
 JEMIMA SARAH BETTS. I am  single, and live with Mr. Rieg, in Middlesex-street, Aldgate- the prisoner Rugg lodged in that house, for two or three years - Cox came and lived with him as his wife, after he had been there a year; they both went by the name of Ruby there. On the 15th of August, 1826, I pulled off my ring while I washed my hands, and left it in the kitchen; after I had washed them, I returned and missed the ring off the table. I also lost a petticoat from the kitchen - I found the ring in pawn - Mrs. Ruby stood at the kitchen door, and saw me put the ring there - when I missed it I accused her of it; she said, if I accused her of it, she would knock me down; nobody else could have taken it.
   JOHN DELBRIDGE. I am shopman to Mr. Cordell, pawnbroker, of Aldgate - I have a petticoat, which was pawned on the 15th of August, 1826, and a ring pawned on the 15th of September, 1826 - I know both the prisoners well, by their coming to the shop, but it being so long ago, I cannot say they brought these - they were both pawned by a woman, in the name of Ruby.
   ANN MEAL. I lodge at this house - I knew this petticoat four years ago, when the prosecutrix's mother brought it.
   JOSEPH BIRCH. I am an officer. I took the male prisoner on Saturday, the 28th of July, I found on him forty-seven duplicates; the female prisoner was within a few yards of him - she was taken; no duplicates were found on her - Rugg gave me twenty-two duplicates at first, and said they were all he had - he then gave me twenty-five more, one of which was for this ring, and one for the petticoat; the prisoners are not married.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 RUGG'S Defence. They were pawned unknown to me.
 RUGG -    GUILTY. Aged 42. Transported for Seven Years.
 COX - GUILTY. Aged 40.
     Judgment Respited.

 1912.  JOHN DIXON and JOSEPH BARRY were indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 12 bushels of soot, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Badger.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
 THOMAS BADGER. I am a  chimney sweep- Dixon was in my service. On the 23d of July I directed an officer to watch my premises; Dixon was taken into custody, and in about an hour and a half Barry was taken; I never gave Dixon leave to take or give away any sack of soot from my premises.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You say you never gave Dixon leave to take away any soot? A. No, not by any means; he had been in the habit of conveying some from one of my premises to another for three months - but I had given him orders not to take any on the 23d of July. Barry was not in the habit of buying soot of me; I never knew of his having any soot till the 23d of July.
 COURT. Q. Dixon never had any authority to let Barry have any? A. No, and he never received any of mine; Dixon had been employed by me to take it for me from Queen's-court to Coleman-street.
   HENRY TUFF. I am a sweep, in the employ of Mr. Badger. I know the two prisoners - I have seen Barry at my master's cellar, and Dixon told me if I saw my master I was to say, "Master, do you want Jack?"
 Cross-examined. Q. How much did you get? A. We had part of one sack between us; Dixon was in my master's service - I knew Barry before Dixon came; he used to come to another man who was there. I have been there three years - I first thought of telling my master of this on Sunday, when I came home from school. I do not know when they took it rightly - they had been at it about six weeks; I had no share of it till the last; Dixon told me he would beat me if I told; master did not say he knew all about it, and he would beat me if I did not tell him; I had a share of it.
   JOHN GEORGE WILLIAMS. I am fellow-apprentice with the last witness. Barry never was in my master's employ - on Saturday, the 21st of July, I saw the prisoners in the cellar; Dixon filled a sack, and Barry took it away; then he came back with the sack empty; Dixon filled it again, and he took it away again - he came back in the afternoon about three o'clock, and then took another sack; he told me to cut the lumps down, and put the lightest of the soot in; Dixon gave me and Tuff 1s. between us - they promised us 1s. out of every three sacks.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you remember Barry coming there before Dixon was there? A. Yes, and Tuff knew it too; my master never lost any soot before Dixon came, to my knowledge. I did not tell my master of it till the Sunday, when we came from school, because they said they would beat us - we had only 1s.; Savage came and told our master, and then he asked us if we knew any thing of the robbery.
   WILLIAM ALLWINKLE. I am an officer. The prosecutor set me to watch on the 23d of July - I saw Dixon come out of the cellar about eleven o'clock, with a sack on his back; I asked where he was going - he said to Green-court; Barry was out in London-wall, about one hundred yards from Mr. Badger's - they walked on to Quaker-street.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were the other two little sweeps there? A. They were in the cellar - if they have said that Dixon and Barry were in the cellar, they have said what was not true.
   PETER SAVAGE. I deal in soot. On Saturday, the 21st of July, Barry sold me four sacks of soot. I went and informed Badger. I know nothing of Dixon.
 BARRY'S Defence. The soot I sold him I gathered myself. I have dealt with him for four years nearly.
 DIXON's Defence. My master told me to go to the cellar and turn the soot over, which I did. I then asked if I should go to Green-court to take some soot, and he said Yes: I had taken one sack on my back to go there, and the officer came up and took me.
   THOMAS BADGER. I had ordered him that day not to take any out.
     NOT GUILTY.
