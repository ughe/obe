PAMILA SAMUELS. I am a draper in White Alie Street—I buy job lots of silk sometimes—I have been in business twelve years—Mr. Harris shewed me some samples of silk in the early part of June—I do not remember the date—I looked at them—he said he had been asking 1 s . a yard for them—I said they were worth that—I would not give 2 s . 2 d . a yard for them—Mr. Harris made no concealment about it—I have been in my present, residence ten years—I have known Harris since he was a boy—he is a respectable tradesman—I have brought job lots of silk of Pawsons, of Lewis, and Cooks, and form my opinion from my general experience.
   Cross-examined by MR. M. WILLIAMS . Mine is a large business—this silk is not worth more than 2 s . a yard, but I am used to buying in job lots and damaged goods, so I should not like to say—I should not call this perfect, because of the way it is rolled up. LAWRENCE SAMUEL. I live at 38, Mansell Street, and am a job draper—my parents have been in business fifty years, and I have been in business six years—I was at Mr. Harris' premises on 5th June—he asked me what I thought some samples of silk were worth—I replied that it was difficult to give a precise judgment about that class of goods, because the qualities are so various, and I did not want to buy them, but I believed they were worth about lid a yard—I have been in the habit of attending large sales, and I. have seen goods of a similar quality fetch 10 d ., 11 d ., 1 s ., and 1 s . 1 d . per yard—I have known Harris all my life—I was in a situation with him many years—he has borne a high character amongst his neighbours for honesty and integrity.
   Cross-examined by MR. M. WILLIAMS . The silk produced is worth about 2 s . a yard—you may easily be deceived in silk—this other is perhaps worth about 1 s . 6 d . a yard, but I doubt if you were to offer it for sale that you would get more than 11 d . a yard for it—there are three qualities of silk here, but to an experience eye they might all be taken as one—any silk that has been unfolded I should call irregular, and it would not fetch so much. JOHN PEARTREE. I am a hat and cap manufacturer—the silk produced is used for lining bats; there are three qualities here, one is worth about 2 s . a yard, and the others 1 s . 4 d . or 1 s . 6 d .
   Cross-examined.  The red silk would be worth about 2 s . 8 d . a yard in its present state being out of order. JOSEPH SPIERS( re-examined ). I have carried on business in the silk trade 30 years—these silks are not ordinary widths such as draper's goods, which are 18 to 21 inches; these are 36 inches wide—the last witnesses have valued them as drapers widths.
