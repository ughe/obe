 Detective-sergeant ALBERT HAWKINS, N Division. On August 9 I saw the prisoners, Friend and Riley, at Dorking Police Court, and said to them, "I am going to arrest you both for stealing a cob and harness, which has been found in your possession." Riley said, "I never got in the trap. I do not know anything about it. I only went with Friend for a drive." Friend said, "I wish to make a statement. I borrowed the horse from a man named J. Lovell, on Monday night, in the Lea Bridge Road. Lovell lives at Bethnal Green, but I do not know where. Lovell had the cob which had the set of harnes on, which was on the animal when I was arrested at Dorking. He had a rug over the horse, and was carrying the saddle under his arm. I said, 'Hallos! what is the matter?' He said 'My trap has broken down, and I have left it up the road.' I not Riley shortly afterwards in the 'Ship Aground' public-house in Lee Bridge Road, and asked him if he knew anyone who had a trust Riley said his landlord would lend him one for a bit of silver. I saw Lovell standing outside with the cob, harness, and cart. He took it away. Next morning (Tuesday, August 6), he came round about 9 a.m. and asked if I knew anyone who would buy a cob. I said, 'Yes,' and took the cob down to Mr. Luck at Dorking, and sold it to him. Lovell left me, and I have not seen him since. On the way to Dorking, I met Riley and Gardner in the Old Ford Road, and took them for a drive. I made inquiries about the trap, and found it belonged to a man named Head, Riley's landlord."
 To Mr. Thomas. When I first saw Head he said he did not lend it, but when it was a question of coming to police court to charge Riley with stealing the cart he said he remembered that he had lent it to him.
 Superintendent GEORGE BOON, Surrey Constabulary. At 4.45 p.m. on August 6 I saw prisoner Friend in Pixham Lane, Dorking. He was driving a bay gelding in an old two-wheel cart. I stopped him and asked him if his name was Jack smith. He replied, "No, John Friend." I told him I was going to arrest him on another charge and conveyed him to Dorking Police Station. Ascertaining that two men had been in his company, I went out and found them, prisoner Riley being one and the other a man named Gardner. They were also taken to Dorking Police Station, where all three were charged with being concerned in the robbery. Friend replied, "I got the horse from a man named Jack Lovell, of Bethnal Green, and the other two men only came with me for a rids." Riley said, "That is right." The other man made no reply.
