Before Mr. Recorder.
1721. HENRY DAKEN was indicted for stealing, in the dwelling-
house of George Gurney, on the 10th of May, at St. Mary, Newington, 30
shillings, 16 sixpences, 6 groats, 60 pence, 120 halfpence, and 750 far-
things, his monies; and afterwards, about the hour of one in the night, fe-
loniously and burglariously breaking out of the said dwelling-house.
GEORGE GURNEY. I keep the Red Lion, at Walworth-road, in the parish
of St. Mary, Newington. I have a drawer in my bar with a patent lock to
it-I keep the silver in it, which is dropped into it in the course of the day
through a space, out of the other tills, every two hours, and at night I
empty it, and leave some silver on the sideboard for change-on Tuesday
morning, about a quarter after two o'clock, I observed a shilling with a
mark on it in the basket and left it there-I left my house that day and
did not return till the following evening, Wednesday, when I had informa-
tion about a quarter past seven-the basket would not be emptied in conse-
quence of my absence, not until the last thing at night-the prisoner was not
employed in my house at all.

THOMAS GARNER. I am barman at the Red Lion. I was at the bar
on Tuesday night about twelve o'clock, and saw Mrs. Gurney clear the
basket which stands in the drawer she left out 21. in silver for change for
the following day, and put it on the sideboard loose out of the basket about
ten minutes before twelve-we close the house up at twelve o'clock-I was
the last that left the bar—the money was safe then-I bolted the side shop
door-about five next morning I got up and found the side shop-door un-
bolted and half open-it opens into the street-I missed from the sideboard
the 21. in silver a wine decanter was removed from the shelf and put on
the counter-persons could come into the yard of our house until about
eleven o'clock at night-we have a coal cellar-I did not go into it over night,
but the door was closed--I cannot say whether it was locked it is in
the house, in the passage leading to the yard-except the coal cellar I had
examined the house, and if anybody was concealed in the house they must
have been in the coal cellar—the side shop-door must have been opened
from within—there were two casks placed so as not to let the door open
wide-I had seen the prisoner several times during the Tuesday evening
backwards and forwards in the passage leading to the yard.
