164. WALTER SALLIS, stealing, on 26th Nov., 1849, 2 mahogany
wards, value 21.; the goods of Joseph Drown Rigby, and another his masters.
2nd COUNT, of Money Wigram and others.
Mr. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
John William Lyon. I live at 4, Crescent-road, Millbank, and am
cashier in the employ of Joseph Drown Rigby, and Charles Rigby, sawyers,
of Holywell-street, Westminster. The prisoner was in their employ--we
receive wood for the purpose of baving it sawn-it was the prisoner's duty to
superintend the sawing and the mill altogether-he was to sell nothing but
slimmings, saw-dast, and blind stuff-if any mahogany had been sawn for
Messrs. Wigram, and they had paid for it, he would collect the money and
pay it to me-he has not paid me any money on account of the mahogany at
my time--he did pay me 151. 188. on account of Messrs. Wigrams, on 28th
Jan.- he did not tell me he had sold any slabs, or any boards.
ROBERT WILKINS Price. I am in the employ of Messrs. Rigby, as
carpenter. I remember some mahogany being sawn last November I saw
it upon the premises, but did not see it come it was sawa by steam into
boards, and some into timber-these are boards (pointing some out) and
imber is stouter-slabs are those with the outside on them-1 can speak to
this board (printing it out) as being one of those sawn in Nov.-I bought it
nyself, the others are boards of a similar character on a Monday in Nov.,
I saw some of the boards taken away in a cart-I cannot say how many, or
whose cart it was-Mr. Sallis superintended the loading-Fletcher was sent
to follow the cart.
Cross-examined by Mr. ROBINSON. Q. Does the mahogany come in in
the shape of trees with the bark off! A. I never saw them before they were
cut-the outside planks are often smaller than the others-it depends upon
whether the tree is straight-the outsides are sometimes faulty and irregular,
and sometimes there are boles running two or three inches into the tree-
I do not know Siburn-I purchased two planks.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. You considered he was entitled to sell them?
4. Yes.
MR. ROBINSON. Q. Did not you hear that the prisoner had said he was
allowed by Mr. Siburn to sell certain planks? A. No-1 asked him if they
were for sale, as I should like to have a piece of it, and he said I could have
piece-that was in the yard where I was cutting the boards.
JAMES FLETCHER. I live at 96, Regent-street, Westminster. In Nov.
last I was working at Messrs. Rigby's, and was desired to follow a cart from
the yard containing the mahogany slabs and board produced-1 followed it
till it came to 119, Lilington-street-I made this memorandum of the name
on the cart produced)-it is “Shadrach Izard."
Cross-examined. Q. Did you watch the things put in A. No.
WILLIAM GOODMAN. I am in the employ of Messrs. Rigby. I remember
two logs of mahogany being sent from Messrs. Wigram's four or five weeks,
perhaps, before Christmas-the prisoner was then foreman, and I heard him
give instructions as to their being sawn up-- I did not see them sawn, but I
w them afterwards-all of these produced are boards--the slabs are cut off
