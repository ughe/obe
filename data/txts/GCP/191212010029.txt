LOUISA SAPWELL. On August 3, 1905, I went through the ceremony
of marriage with prisoner. I had known him 18 months; he told me
his wife was dend. Last September his daughter oame to see me.
Cross-examined. I have had four children by him; there is only
one alive. He has been a good husband and a hard-working man.
LYDIA ELIZA DAY. I am the daughter of prisoner. Previous to
September 19 I had not seen him for about 10 years; it was outside
. public-house where I received money for my mother; I was then
living in Sayer Street with her.
Cross-examined. I do not remember where we were living before
that, but my mother from time to time changed her lodgings. Father
did not know where we were. Within the last 10 years an aunt of
mine, who was very much like my mother, died; I did not know that a
rumour got about that it was my mother who had died.
WALTER ALFRED DAY (prisoner, on oath) repeated the statements he
had made in the statement produced, and corroborated the evidence of
his daughter. He added, "My brother in-law told me that he had
heard that my wife was dead, and I never should have married again if
I had not believed that to be the case." (To the Court.) My brother
in-law's name is Samuel Roland. I believe he is dead; I have not
seen him for years; it is nearly 12 years ago that he told me he had
heard she was dead.
Verdict, Guilty.
Sentence: Two days' imprisonment.
WADHAM, William (31, carman), pleaded guilty of felonious
marrying Elizabeth Lee, his former wife being then alive.
According to prisoner's statement his wife had deserted him in 1905
after six years' married life. He contracted the bigamous marriage in
1911, not informing his second wife, who was a prostitute, of the
existence of his former wife. A number of convictions for larceny
were proved.
Sentence: Six weeks' hard labour.
