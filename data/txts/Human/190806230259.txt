If you had taken up yours as I have taken mine, you would not be in the trouble you are in." Calcutt's account of that con  versation is not correct. With regard to the conversation about the granolithic paving to the Workhouse entrance, at that time I was Chairman of the Workhouse Visiting Committee; we had been round on a survey in the afternoon and I met Calcutt in the grounds. I told him the Committee were going to recommend the Board to pave the path to the Master's house and in due course he would have par ticulars from the Surveyor. That is all. With regard to Barker's contract. I never heard Calcutt telling Barker that his son was to get certain contracts on giving certain of the Guardians £300. My actions on the Board have never been influenced by any motive resulting from anything Calcutt had given me. A search is being made in the books of the Guardians in respect to all my votes since 1898. Some of my votes have been in support of Calcutt and some against him. Every thing I have ordered in regard to the Scattered Homes would appear above my signature in the books. I was on the Committees from 1904, but during my Mayoralty I could not attend very much. I always triad to attend the Board meetings on the relief cases. Up till 1903 the jobbing work was done simply under Knight. If there was a difference between Knight and Calcutt I should have supported whichever I thought was right. If there was a dispute, Knight would take the accounts back and bring them up at the next Committee meeting. Then the Committee would recommend that he should try and arrange with Calcutt; then at the next meeting Knight would tell the Com mittee that he had arranged with Calcutt for so much, and we would pass the account. With regard to the Arbitration Committee, after Knight had put his case to the Committee and pointed out that his price was the correct one, I agreed with him to certify for £371. I do not think I was present when it came back to the Board. I think on the Committee Mr. Andrews and myself voted for Knight and the other members of the Committee voted for Calcutt. In re gard to the taking of No. 1, Bancroft Road, Calcutt offered his house and yard for £150 a year. I proposed an amendment that the house only should be taken at a rent of £60 per annum. After the voting Thacker said the Local Government Board had already refused it. I should think £65 was a proper rent. With regard to the tenders in which I actually voted for Calcutt, I knew Calcutt had always up to that time done his contract work well, and I supported him on those grounds. As a rule, if we got anybody in the Borough, or anybody that was near, we would support them in preference to people outside the Borough. When the Inquiry commenced, I volun teered, through my solicitors, to give all the information I could. I have taken up that position throughout, and all the documents I ever had are available.
