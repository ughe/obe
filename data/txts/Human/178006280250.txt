Cross Examination.
 What are you? - I have been out of place three months; I have lodged with Mr. Timmings ever since.
 Who lodges in this house besides? - Several young men; sometimes strangers every night.
 How came you to take such particular notice of that night? - Because I looked at the things particularly.
 How came you not to recollect these things when those gentlemen asked you about them? you said there were a sheet and china, and some trifling things you could not recollect? - Some cups and such things; I did not recollect the china; I was always with Timmings every night.
 Have you been there ever since you lodged with her? - Yes, I have never been out of the house since.
 Court. Are you sure they brought all the things which were brought? - Yes.
 Did they bring the silk gown there? - No, I must have seen it if they had. I was never out of the place.
 Did you ever see the watch brought there? - No, I did not. Some things were taken away in the morning; these were kept.
   MARY HARGRAVE sworn.
 I lodge in this house too; I was there between twelve and one o'clock, on the 6th of June.  Ann Charlotte, Gardiner, and Kelly knocked at the door; Mrs. Timmings let them in; they brought some things with them, and desired she would let them leave them there. She refused. They said they came honestly by them and where should they leave them but in their lodging. After some persuasion she let them bring them, and they left them below stairs, and went up to bed; she called to them afterwards to take them away. There was a cloak with fur on it, two sheets, one longer than the other a petticoat, and some other linen.
 Did you see any plates there? - Not till next day. She was going to bed when they came in. She was not out that evening.
     NOT GUILTY.

ELISABETH STEWART was indicted for stealing twenty nine yards and an half of thread lace for ruffles, value 10 s. a muslin handkerchief laced, value 20 s. a pair of treble lawn ruffles, value 10 s. twenty-four yards of lace, value 10 l. a child's cloak laced, value 10 s. seven yards of silk, value 30 s. a sattin gown and coat, value 10 s. a muslin apron, value 5 s. another apron, value 2 s. a sattin cloak, value 3 s. a man's camblet riding cloak, value 3 s. a linen gown, value 5 s. a black silk handkerchief, value 2 s. a necklace made with stones, set in silver, value 5 s. a cotton gown, value 5 s. a cotton work-bag, value 1 d. and four pounds ten shillings, in monies, numbered, the property of Margaret Holbird, widow, April, 9th.
   MARGARET HOLBIRD sworn.
 I live in  Mitre-court, Leadenhall-street. The prisoner was my servant. On Sunday morning, the 9th of April, I went into the country, and left a Mrs. Hussey and the prisoner to take care of the house. I came home on the Wednesday, and found the maid was gone; Mrs. Hussey told me she went on the Monday. I did not miss any thing then. The next day, Thursday, about five or six o'clock in the afternoon, I went to the closet and could not at first open it; at last I got it open, and immediately missed my box of money. I then missed my handkerchief and laced ruffles, and the next morning I missed my bag with all my lace in it. I then went to enquire after the prisoner; I found she had been at Mrs. Cole's, a dyer in Ratcliff-highway; they informed me she had fetched some things out of pawn which were at a pawnbroker's opposite. I went to the pawnbroker's to know if the prisoner had fetched any thing out of pledge, and enquired in what money she had paid for them? They said all in half crowns; my money was all in half crowns, except a five shilling and three penny piece of George I. I heard she was gone on board a ship; I got a warrant to go on board to find her. I sent a man to enquire, and was informed the ship was gone. I then concluded it was all over, and thought no more of it. On the 24th of May a young man came and told me that the person who had roobed me was in the Compter.
