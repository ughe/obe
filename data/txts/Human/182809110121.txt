WILLIAM PRICE. I am a watchman of Mary-le-bone. I got a box from the watch-house-keeper - the proseuctor claimed it; the prisoner was present, but I do not think he saw the watch-house-keeper produce it - at half-past two o'clock in the morning I found another box in Union-street; there was nothing found on the prisoner in my presence - the watch-house-keeper is not here.
   JAMFS DUNN. I am a shoe-maker, and live opposite the prosecutrix. I saw the prisoner standing by her shop with two snuff-boxes in his hand; I saw Mrs. Ashdown sake hold of him - he got from her, and I stopped him in Union-street, between Nos. 37 and 38; he threw down two snuff-boxes, and I took up one - Mrs. Ashdown saw it, and owned it; I gave it to the watch-house-keeper.
 Prisoner. Q. You said at the office, you did not know whether I dropped it or not? A. No; I said I saw him throw down two boxes, and I took one up.
 COURT to MRS. ASHDOWN. Q. Is the box now produced, one of those you lost? A. Yes; it is the one that was found by the watchman the next morning - this is not the one Mr. Dunn took up.
 Prisoner's Defence. They said they found two boxes, but they have brought but one; I came out of the country on the 4th of July, to get a situation - I met my consin, and stood talking with him; the prosecutrix came up and laid hold of me, and said she was informed I had been stealing some boxes - I said I had not, but I had seen some boys near her window, and they had run down Union-street.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Three Months.
 1757.  MARY KNIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , 1 petticoat, value 1s. 6d.; 1 gown. value 1s. 6d.; 1 handkerchief, value 3s.; 1 brush, value 1s., and 1 book, value 1s., the goods of John Garrett.
 JOHN GARRETT. I live in  Carburton-street, and am a carver and gilder. The prisoner had been my nurse; my wife was ill - she had been with me about four weeks, and had attended Mrs. Garrett in her confinement before; I had some suspicion of her - and on the evening of the 15th of August, about 8 o'clock, she went out; I sent a man to bring her back - she came back, and had a bundle; I said I suspected she had been robbing me - she said No; Mrs. Garrett had given her part of the things, and the other part she had lent her; I found in the bundle two wine-bottles, a gown, a prayer-book, a silk handkerchief, a petticoat, a muslin collar, and other things - we then searched her pockets, and found a bag of flour, some potatoes, some salts and wafers; she said she had taken them, or words to that effect, and begged to be forgiven.
   SAMUEL EVANS. I am a constable. I took the prisoner, and searched her; I found the articles the prosecutor has stated, and a great many more - the prisoner said Mrs. Garret had given her some, and lent her others; I found a furniture-brush under her arm, and some other things - the prosecutor claimed them all; she said she hoped she should be forgiven.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that her misthress had given her the salts to conceal them from her master.
   JOHN GARRETT. I believe she was not paid the last three weeks wages, but I do not know; my wife is now deceased.
 GUILTY. Aged 60. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined One Month.
 1758.  GEORGE WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , 1 hat, value 5s., the property of Isaac Hill.
   JOHN COLE. I am errand-boy to Mr. Hill, a hatter, of the Strand. At half-past seven o'clock, on the morning of the 8th of July, I was sweeping the door, a stranger came and spoke to me, and while I was talking with him I saw the prisoner coming out of my master's shop with a hat; I stopped him, and he asked me whether it was a 16s. or an 18s. hat, but before that he said, "Let me go! let me go!" I called my master, and a constable was sent for.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you mean to say this man had ever left the shop? A. No - he was coming out of it; he was coming towards me, and I took hold of him - my master was three or four minutes coming down; I took the hat in my hand - I had shut the door, and locked it; he did not attempt to get away - he could if he had liked.
 COURT. Q. How near the door was he when you stopped him? A. Near the step - I was by the curb-stone.
 ISAAC HILL. I keep this shop - I was called down and sent for an officer.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was there any concealment of the hat? A. No; he did not say he came to ask the price of it - there was no one else in the shop.
   JOHN GALL. I am a constable. I took the prisoner; I found nothing on him, and no money.
 COURT to JOHN COLE. Q. What questions were put to you by the other man? A. He asked me the way to Southampton-street - the name of that street is written up; he was on the curb-stone - the prisoner was coming out of the shop, about one yard from us.
 Cross-examined. Q. How near was the prisoner to you when you put your hand on him? A. I went to the step- he could certainly have overcome me.
 Prisoner's Defence. I entered the shop with no other intention than that of becoming a purchaser of a hat; I had 4s. 6d. which was taken from me by Hill or by the constable - I meant to have left that, and paid for the hat on Saturday night.
     NOT GUILTY.
