 1455.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of September , 2 shillings, the monies of William Griggs.
   ELIZABETH GRIGGS. I am the wife of William Griggs- we keep an oyster-shop in Whitechapel-road- I saw the prisoner come to the shop on the 2nd of September, and had four oysters - they came to 2d. - he gave my daughter 1s. - she had not got change, and put 6d. on the top of the place where the till was, and went out for change - a man and his wife were talking to me about the house - I was showing them the outside of the house - I turned round, and saw the prisoner's hand in the till - I ran in, turned quick round, and said, "You good-for-nothing man, what do you do with your hand in my till?" - he said he had not done so - my daughter came in with the change - I said,"What is in the till?" - she said, "2s." - she looked in the till, and they were gone, but there was one bad one in the till - she said, "You have taken two good ones out, and put one bad one in" - another daughter fetched an officer, who took him into custody - he gave the two shillings out of his pocket.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Did you say your daughter put any thing near the drawer on the till? A. No. There was a little chest of drawers, which we use as a till - my daughter had put out 6d. towards the change - the prisoner could not be putting his hand over to take the 6d. - I swear I saw his hand in the till - I did not like to give him in charge, as it was a disagreeable thing - I did not say I would have 2s. out of him - he snatched the bad shilling out of my daughter's hand, and gave the two shillings back - he had not left the shop before I sent for a policeman - he was gone out when the policeman came.
   ELIZABETH GRIGGS, Jun. The prisoner came for the oysters, and paid me 1s. - I put 6d. on the top of the till for him, and then went to get change - I had looked into the till to see if there was change, and there was not - there was 2s., and the 6d. laid on the top - they were both good shillings, I believe - when I returned, my mother told me what had occurred - I said, "You good-for-nothing man, you have taken two shillings out of the till, and put this bad one in" - he snatched the bad shilling out of my hand, and put 2s. into my hand.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you not insisted on his giving two shillings before he left the shop? A. I said, "Give me the 2s. you have take out of the till," and he gave it me directly - I might have taken a bad shilling without knowing it.
   EMILY GRIGGS. I fetched a policeman. The prisoner had given my sister the two shillings and gone away when the policeman got there.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you go after the policeman? A. I found him two or three doors off - nobody sent me.
   ISAAC JOHN ISAACS. I am a policeman. I took him into custody.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1456.  FRANCIS HAYES was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of August , 3 silver spoons, value 1l. 4s., the goods of Robert Owen Aland , his master; and GEORGE BLAKE was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
 There being no evidence against the prisoners, but a confession which was extorted, they were
     ACQUITTED.
 1457.  FRANCIS HAYES was again indicted for stealing, on the 16th August , 2 tea-spoons, the goods of Samuel Hart; and GEORGE BLAKE was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
 SAMUEL HART. I lodge in the house of Mr. Aland, the last prosecutor. I missed two tea-spoons - I was informed by Brillett where I could find them.
   JOHN BRILLETT. I found these tea-spoons pawned at Soames's, at Chelsea.
   EDWIN SOAMES. I am a pawnbroker. I have two tea-spoons - I do not know who pawned them - it was a youth- the duplicate has not been found.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1458.  SAMUEL CARTER was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of September , 1 sovereign, the monies of Edward Richard Phillips.
 EDWARD RICHARD PHILLIPS. I live in  Great Titchfield-street. On the 2nd of September, the prisoner was pointed out to me, and I gave him into custody of the police.
   ELIZABETH PHILLIPS. I am the wife of the prosecutor. On the 2nd of September, I saw the prisoner selling rabbits- I took him to my door, and bought two of him for 1s. 2d. - I had no small change - he heard me send my servant for change of a sovereign - she came back, and said she could not get change - she went out again, and came in, and said the prisoner had run away with the sovereign.
