With respect to Mr. Vernon and D M'Douall, if I had been allowed to have the documents in I could have shown you the very words I read in my speech—or I might have sent for Mrs. Vernon, who no doubt could prove that her husband has sent word to that effect; for she stated in the presence of several other people besides myself, that he had been compelled to pick oakum, and that Feargus O'Connor had been sent to about it. As for Mr. Keene's speech, I can see notthing that very material against me; the fact is, he had had intelligence which is confirmed by another gentleman, Mr. Cope, who belongs to the  Times , that Ireland was in a state of disturbance; and surely it was no great harm to repeat at a public meeting, that which appeared in the newspapers. As for the witness Bywater, perhaps there is nothing very objectionable in his evidence, he found the trunk containing these papers unlocked; many of them are not my writing, nor has there been any proof that they are the minutes of any particulars produced at any meeting. With respect to the sympathy we held with the people of Ireland, it is quite clear they were in a state of disturbance at the time I presided at this meeting: and are we on this side of the channel to be deprived of the rights of humanity, and be come amenable to the laws for sympathizing with those on the other side? for who among you, if he has not drained his veins of the milk of human kindness, would not sympathize with an oppressed people, standing on the threshold of civil war, and unable to say how soon the same horrors might surround your own dwellings? Does it not make your blood run cold, when you think that the maladministration of our internal affairs has driven the people to such a course, leaving them no other choice than open insurrection or unregretted starvation? But do not imagine that the coercive measures lately enacted were framed for the purpose of suppressing the spirit of free dom that is rising in the bosom of the honest mechanic will be laid aside when a few convictions have been obtained; on the contrary, when you have occasion to complain of ministerial oppression, and I do not think that many of you will say that the present Cabinet are doing that which is best calculated to promote contentment and prosperity in the country, that moment will you become subject to the same persecution, and those Acts of Parliament that have been framed to suppress us, will be unscrupulously used for your oppression; you may read a lesson of your future fate in the subterfuge which the present ministry resorted to on 10th April last, when the working classes of the metropolis attempted to exercise what has never been disputed to be a constitutional right,—the right of meeting and going in procession with the petition of their grievances to the Legislature. But, though they were determined to make no concessions, they were aware that there were sample reasons for complaining, and they felt no hesitation in raking up an obsolete act,—an act that had been consigned to oblivion for more than a century and a half, and one that had only been passed to prevent a debauched monarch from being interrupted in his royal revelries by the con gratulations of his loyal subjects;—I allude to the act of Charles II., which suppressed a constitutional right possessed by the people at that period: what are you to expect from such men as these, when you will no longer allow them to rob you with impunity? do you imagine that if Lord John Russell succeeds in suppressing the present discontent among the working classes, which is very doubtful, that he will next Session withdraw his bill for an additional income-tax, merely because it is not consonant with your
