 1963.  MARY GOUGH was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , 1 shawl, value 1s. 6d., the goods of John Cuss ; and that she had been before convicted of felony.
   ANN DONNELLEY. I live in William-street, Portland-town. My father-in-law, John Cuss, is a chimney-sweeper; I have known the prisoner by sight about twelve months, carrying fruit about - on the 12th of July she came to where I live; she was not much in liquor. but she wanted to lay down; I put up this shawl at the window, to make it dark; I afterwards went into the yard; I missed this shawl, and saw an older one put up in its place - I called to a young man named Cox, who took this shawl from the prisoner's bosom.
   RICHARD MOORE. (Police-constable S 47.) I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was very much in liquor, and throw myself on your mercy.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. - Confined One Month.
 The former conviction was not proved.
 1964.  MARY DRISCOLL was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 2 gowns, value 18s., and 1 shawl, value 2s., the goods of Eliza Patmore.
 ELIZA PATMORE. I am  single, and lodge in Queen-street, Seven-dials. On the 14th of July the prisoner left that house; she had lodged there for a fortnight -I missed two gowns, a cap, and a shawl; I found the prisoner in custody.
   ROBERT WILSON MEAGLE. I am shopman to a pawnbroker in Tottenham-court-road. I produce a gown and cap, pawned with me - I do not know by whom.
   WILLIAM WILSON. (Police-constable E 74.) I took the prisoner on the 16th of July; she told me she lodged in Church-street - I went there, and asked which was her room - the people of the house told me, and I found the duplicates there - the prisoner said she had made away with them, but she would restore them again.
 R. W. MEAGLE. These are the duplicates I gave, in the names of Margaret Flynn and Ann Richardson.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. What the Policeman says is quite false; I got a drop of liquor, and do not remember what I did, but I did not say what he states.
     GUILTY. Aged 21 - Transported for Seven Years.

 1965.  THOMAS PITHARD was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of August , 24 pairs of gloves, value 15s., and 30 skins, value 30s., the goods of Robert Pullman.
   ROBERT WILLIAM PULLMAN. I am the son of Robert Pullman, of West-street, a leather-dresser. On the 2nd of August I was in the first floor; the prisoner was in our service, and was my father's cousin -I came down, and heard some paper shuffling; I went to the spot, and saw Whitelock.
   ROBERT WHITELOCK. I am the prosecutor's porter. I saw the prisoner roll up some gloves and skins, and take them towards the door - I do not know what became of them; he was in the habit of taking gloves home for his wife to sew.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Do you not live next door to the prisoner? A. Yes; I sell gloves for the prosecutor.
   JOHN BAYS. I am an officer. I was in Earl-street, Seven-dials; and from information from Mrs. Reece, I went to her house, and found the property; I took her to the office.
 MRS. REECE. The officer found the bundle on my premises - Mrs. Pithard gave it me to be sewn; I am in the habit of sewing for different persons.
 COURT to ROBERT WHITELOCK. Q. Can you tell that it was the prosecutor's property which the prisoner took? A. No - I cannot recollect what I said before the Magistrate; I said I believed them to be what he took - I cannot say these are the same; I told the Magistrate that I saw the prisoner taking away the gloves produced - I do not know whether they are the same.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1966.  JOHN ROBERTS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 4 glazed window-sashes, value 2l., the goods of Mary Cock , and fixed to a certain building; against the Statute, &c.
   GEORGE HURST. I know Mrs. Cock's house - it is empty. On the 21st of July I saw the prisoner within four or five yards of that house, at half-past seven o'clock in the morning, with the sashes on his shoulder - I told my father, and he was taken the same morning.
   THOMAS SEAL(Police-constable G 145). I received information that the house had been broken into, and two men had been seen to leave it, and one of them carrying a number of sashes - I went one way, and saw the prisoner carrying these sashes; he went with them some distance, and then another man joined him; they then parted - I still followed the prisoner till he came near the station-house, and then I took him; he said a gentleman had given him 6d. to carry them to Bishopsgate-street.
   MARY MOORE. I had the key of the house - I had shown it to a person, and in a few days after there was an alarm, and these sashes were missing.
   MARY ANN WILSWORTH. The house is a leasehold-house, and belongs to my mother Mary Cock; she is a widow - I have seen these sashes fitted to the frame; the house had been locked, and there were marks of a forcible entrance.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that a man, whom he had met at Islington, had employed him to carry the sashes.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. - Transported for Seven Years.
