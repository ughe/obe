Henry Andis. I am in the employ of Williams & Co., 108, Minories -
the prisoner Howe has been known to me some little time; he has an oil
and colourman's business in Cambridge Heath Road- have been in the
babit of using the Three Colts public-house, a short distance from his house
--I had a conversation with his brother about some wax, and afterwards
with him-he handed me two pieces of wax, one dark and one light-he
said he should have abont 2owt. of it; this was given me as a sample-ho
said he wanted a quarter of a pound-I showed the sample to Palmer,
and also to Mr. Williams, my employer-1 afterwards saw Howe, and told
him they would buy half a cwt. at Mr. Williams's, and & cwt. at Mr.
Gadsden's.
Cross-examined by Mr. SLEIGH. I have been in Mr. Williams's employ
two and a half years- I was in respectable employment before then-I have
known Howe some years; he has borne an excellent character during that
time--I have not been paid 11. a day for my expenses; I had 1. the first
day, and a half-crown for a cab home--it was Howe's brother that showed
me the samples, not Howe himself-I said at first that it was Howe; but,
on considering, I found it was his brother-he gave me the samples in his
presence-he went and fetehed them from his premises; they are the same
premises as the prisoner's-he is a wholesale, as well as a retail, oil and
colourman--he keeps a man on the premises to mix wax-after I saw Mr.
Field about this, I told Howe that I had heard the wax was stolen, and
he had better not have anything to do with it --he said he should not
buy any more of it, and he was much obliged to me for telling him of it.
Re-ecamined. The shop is Howe's, I believe—the brother fetched the
samples from the shop, and brought them to me at the public-house and
asked if I could sell them-I said "Yes"-both the brothers were at the
public-house-I saw them afterwards several times, both in the shop toge-
ther-the prisoner sometimes took part in the conversation--this was
about Whitsuntide, I think ; about the middle of April.
