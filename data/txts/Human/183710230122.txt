MARY CHECKLEY. I am a widow, living next to Mrs. Maxton. On this evening the prisoner came into my shop for a quarter of a pound of tea—he produced a good sovereign—I had no change, and he went next door—I have no doubt it was the prisoner—my window and hers face each other—they are corner houses—I could sec him go in—he told me he lived at No. 17, Northampton-square—I saw him go out—he walked very quickly—there was a hue and cry, and he was brought back.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long was he in your shop? A.  Some time—he said he wanted very good tea, I said, "The six-shilling tea ii very good"—he said, "I want better than that." GEORGE HOPE WHITEWAY( police-constable G  189.) A young man in Perceval-street pointed the prisoner out, and I told him he must come back with me to the shop—he said I had better be careful what I was about; he was a respectable man; he did not get his living by passing bad money—I took him—he denied coming into the shop—the old lady said, "You villain, it was you that came in." GUILTY. * Aged 22.— Transported for Seven Years.
 2327. MARY ANN MULKAY was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of October , 6 printed books, value 10 s  ., the goods of Lucinda Vidler . LUCINDA VIDLER. I am a widow, and live in Bloomsbury. On the 12th of October I lost six books—one was on the counter, at the side of my shop, the others at the window inside the door, which was open. LUCINDA BRIMMER. I am the daughter of Mrs. Vidler—she keeps a circulating library. The prisoner was brought in about half-past one o'clock, and these books produced—they are my mother's. SAMUEL POULSON. I keep a cheesemonger's shop in Compton-street, Brunswick-square. On the 12th of October the prisoner brought these books to me, and offered them to sell as waste paper—I took her, seeing the name on the backs, to the bookseller's shop.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 15.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Six Days.
