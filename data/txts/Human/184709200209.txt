   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not ask me, at the police-court, what I had made away with, to pay the back rent, and did not I say, "Mr. King's cost?" A.  I know nothing about pledging anything to pay back rent—you said you had moved the things into Lant-street—that was a wrong address. ROBERT LEWIS KING. I am the husband of the last witness. When we were acquitted we went back to our house, and missed everything we had left there—I went to Lant-street, in search of the prisoner—he was not there—I found him in Billingagate, and gave him in charge—he said at the station he had pawned my clothes, and sold his mother's in Petticoat-lane—he did not say he had any authority to do it—he had none from me. ALEXANDER HAUGHEY( policeman, P  188.) I took the prisoner, and told him Mr. King charged him with taking all he had got from his cottage, and asked him where the things were—he told me he had sold them in Petticoat-lane—I asked if he had any idea who to—he said, "No"—I asked him about Mr. King's clothes—he said they were pawned at William's, in the Borough—at the police-court he said he had sold the things to a broker, (he would not tell where,)and part of them at the house where he lived—the Magistrate ordered me to go with him the following day to his lodgings, No. 5, Norfolk-street—we there found a lot of property—he took me to Long-lane, Bermond sey—we there found a great deal there—we have got the greatest part of it back—I produce some duplicates which I found at the prisoner's lodging. JAMES EDWARD MORGAN. I am a furniture-broker, at 168, Bermondsey-street. About the 5th of Aug. the prisoner came to me, and said a person named Reynolds had sent him, who had seen some furniture which he had to sell, but they were not good enough for him, and asked if I would buy it—I went to No. 5, Norfolk-street, and bought a bedstead, a bed, five chairs, and four boxes, which have since been owned by Mr. King. ROBERT LEWIS KINGS re-examined.  I have been to Mr. Morgan's and seen the articles—they are all my property.
