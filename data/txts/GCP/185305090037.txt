Seventh Session, 1852–53.
WILLIAM BEECHEY police sergeant, T 29). I am stationed at Arling-
ton. On the evening of 18th April, about 9 o'clock, the prosecutor came to
my house, with Luckett, and informed me of the robbery-he described the
dresses of the persons who had committed it-I went with him and Luckett
in search of the persons-we went to several beer shops, and at last found
them at the Crown public house in bed-the prosecutor identified them directly
- they were not awake when we entered the room-I awoke them up after
I had searched their clothes--I then asked the prosecutor if he was sure they
were the men that had robbed him-he said, "Yes"-I found on Walker's
clothes a pocket knife and a duplicate for a waistcoat, pawned at Birming-
ham-in Gibbs's clothes I found 28. 4d.
Cross-examined. Q. When they were told they were charged with robbing
this man, I believe they said they had seen him, but had not robbed him,
did they not? 4. Yes, they did - I did not find any razor, I saw one that
the prosecutor had before the Magistrate.
SEVERAL
WILLIAM LUCKETT (policeman, T 152). On the evening of 18th April I
went, in company with Beechey and the prosecutor, to the Crown public house
-I examined Gibbs's clothes, and found in his trowsers poeket these two
instep leathers, wbich Payne identified as bis.
GABRIEL PAYNE re-examined. This knife is mine, and these two instep
leathers were in my pocket at the time I was attacked.
Cross-examined. Q. How do you know the knife ? 4. There is a tooth
mark on the plain side of the handle-I had had it a long while, perhaps
twelve months or more-it is what I use for eating with - I bought the razor
of Gibbs, a good way on the road.

GIBBS-GUILTY. Aged 20: 3

575. JOHN COLE, forging and uttering an endorsement to a bill of

CHARLES VICKERS. I am a seaman. I know the prisoner; he was a
shipmate of mine-last Friday week we were drinking together at a public
honse in New Gravel-lane-I had a belt round my waist, inside my trowsers,
containing two checks, my discharge and register tickets, and several papers,
a gold chain, and 258. in money-the prisoner must have seen me with the
belt, as I took money from it to pay for liquor— I had too much to drink, and
feil asleep--the prisoner was there when I fell asleep, drinking with me
when I awoke he was gone, and my belt also.
Prisoner. I own I took the things from him ; I took them to save them ;
I have the belt now.
WILLIAM BONE. I am inspector of the police at Portsmouth. I had the
prisoner in charge on 4th May-I searched him, and found on him a check
and a duplicate- I asked him if the check was his property-he said, " It is
my pay paper"_I saw the name of Vickers on it-I asked him his name,
and he said Charles Vickers—I also found on him a seaman's register ticket
belonging to Charles Vickers, and a discharge paper of Charles Vickers's from
the Indian navy- produce them.
Prisoner. At the time he asked me about these papers, I never owned
them.
CHARLES VICKERS re-examined. These are the papers I lost from my belt
this check for 141. is mine-this is my register ticket which I got at Bombay,
and this is my discharge ticket from the Indian navy-they were in my belt
when it was taken.
