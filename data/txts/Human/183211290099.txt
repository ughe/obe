 213.  EDWARD WEBB was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of November , 3 shillings, 2 sixpences, and 24 halfpence, the monies of Maria Fermage.
 MARIA FERMAGE. I am  single, and live in Flight-street, Portland-town- the prisoner lives in the same street as I do; I keep a chandler's-shop- this money was in the till. On the 5th of November I went out, and left my sister in charge of the shop - she is very weak in her intellects - when I returned home she showed me the place the till was kept in; the till, money, and all were gone - I had left nine shillings and some halfpence and pence in it; the prisoner has parents, I believe.
   JAMES WATT. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 10th of November; I told him he was charged with robbing Miss Fermage's till - he denied it.
   JAMES OLIFF. I live in High-street, nearly opposite Miss Fermage's - I have seen the prisoner before. On the 5th of November I saw him with a till in Henry-street, between five and six o'clock in the evening; I did not speak to him - I did not see where he went to - I heard money rattle in the till.
   JAMES WHYBRID. I am a gardener, and live in Frederick-street, Portland Nursery-gardens. I found the till in my garden on the 9th of November - there was no money in it; I gave it to a Policeman named Dody, who gave it to the Police-serjeant; Miss Fermage claimed it.
   FANNY BAITS. Between five and seven o'clock on the 5th of November, I saw the prisoner run by me with the till; I am quite sure he is the lad - I have seen him playing at marbles before; his father and mother have turned him out of doors.
 MISS FERMAGE. I am sure this is my till - I have had it seven years.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not do it; I was not near the place.
 GUILTY. - Aged 13. -    Judgment Respited.
 214.  ANN WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of November , 1 silver spoon, value 2s.; 2 shirts, value 15s.; 1 book, value 2s.; 1 apron, value 4d., and 1 handkerchief, value 2d., the goods of Jane Wakefield.
 JANE WAKEFIELD. I am a  widow. I lost these articles - I missed the spoons on the 28th of August, and on the 16th of November two shawls - the prisoner lived in my house; I have known her thirteen years; she had access to every thing.
   JOSEPH CHIDWICK. I am a Policeman. I was called in on the 19th of November; the prosecutrix said she had lost a number of things; among others a silver spoon, two shawls, a book, and an apron - the prisoner denied the charge - I searched her, and found a number of tickets; she then told me where the shawls were pawned - I went there, and found one; the other was produced by a pawnbroker, who had the silver spoon; I found the book and the apron.
   WILLIAM MINDOCK. I am a servant to Mr. Clunes, of Liquor-pond-street, a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, pawned on the 10th of August, by the prisoner, for 4s., in the name of Ann Williams, Rosamond-street.
   RICHARD CHARLES. I am apprentice to Mr. Short, of St. John-street. I have a book, apron, and handkerchief, pawned at different times; the apron and book were pawned on the 6th of October, with two spoons, by the prisoner - I am quite sure she is the person; the book was pawned in the name of Rowland.
   GEORGE CLARKSON. I am apprentice to Mr. Drew, of Clark's-place, Islington. I have a shawl, pawned by the prisoner, on the 17th of August, and a tea-spoon on the 18th, in the name of Mary Clarke.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner handed in a petition for a lenient sentence.
     GUILTY. Aged 65. - Confined Six Months.
 215.  JAMES WARD was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of November , 1 mattress, value 18s., the goods of Daniel Biass.
   DANIEL CLEARY. I am in the employ of Daniel Biass, of Oxford-street, an upholsterer. On the 12th of November I saw this mattress safe, in the rack at the door, about ten minutes before the Policeman brought the prisoner into the shop with it.
   GEORGE ANTHONY. I am a writer and grainer. On Monday night, the 12th of November, I saw the prisoner in company with another young man, and after some time he walked by this shop, turned back, took the mattress off the rack, put it on his shoulder, and crossed the road; the Policeman stopped him - his companion made off: I should know him again.
   RICHARD PEARCE. I am a Policeman. I stopped the prisoner fifty or sixty yards from the shop - the other person ran away; I took the prisoner back, with the mattress.
 Prisoner. He had the other man in custody at the time. Witness. No, the other ran away.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 216.  SARAH WATTS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of November , 3 pairs of trousers, value 6s.; 1 shawl, value 6d., and 2 yards of linen-cloth, value 1s., the goods of Frances Kelly.
   FRANCES KELLY. I am a widow. On the 6th of November I lodged in Wilks-street, Spitalfields; the prisoner worked at slop-work for me - she came to my room for work; I gave her these things to take to Mr. Clark, in Union-row, Tower-hill, who they belonged to, and to bring me the money - it was slop-work; I have never seen them since; I had known her about two years.
   GEORGE MARCHANT. I am servant to Mr. Clark. These trousers were given to the prosecutrix to be made up; they have never been brought home - it was my duty to enter the goods that came in, and I am sure they never were returned.
   JAMES WAYLING. I went to Spitalfields workhouse, and took the prisoner - I asked her what had become of the trousers, the wrapper, and shawl; she said she met a woman, who gave her something to drink, and she was knocked down, and lost them.
     GUILTY. Aged 49. - Confined Two Months.
