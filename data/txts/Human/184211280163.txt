VICTOR DUMAR. Crawford was in my service—I have never received payment for these boots and shoes from Mr. Clark or Hamer—I went down to Epsom—I found no shop with a handsome glass case. JOSEPH ELSWORTH PETCH. About the 21st of Oct. I saw a person who said his name was William Clark—he said he was residing at Epsom, and he wanted some boots—he referred me to Mr. Hamer, of Bread-street-hill—I went there, and saw Morris—he said, "My name is Hamer"—Clark had said he was about to open a shop, and Morris said he was about to supply him with 60 l . worth of goods; he knew his family, and knew him to be a respectable man—I asked as to his honesty and Industry—he gave him a very excellent character—Clark obtained from me goods to the amount of 24 l . odd—he paid me 5 l  —the term I bad proposed was half cash—he took away my goods in a cart on the 28th of Oct.—I have never seen him since—if the reference had not been satisfactory I certainly should not have parted with my goods—the premises of Hamer were well stocked with crates and other merchandise. THOMAS FOOT PIPER. I am a stay-maker. On the 27th of Oct. William Clark came to me—he said be lived at Epsom, and wanted some goods—I required a reference—he gave me one to Thomas Hamer, Bread-street-hill—I sent my clerk there, and from his representation I ultimately parted with some goods—I have not seen Clark since. JOHN KNIGHT. I am clerk to Mr. Piper. I went by his direction to No. 20, Bread-street-hill—I inquired for Mr. Hamer—Morris came forward and said his name was Hamer—I told him I came to inquire the character of William Clark, of Epsom—he said, "I know Mr. Clark very well, walk into my counting-house"—he gave him an excellent character, said he knew his family, and he had no doubt we should be very safe in giving him credit—he said he thought we were perfectly safe in trusting him—at that time there were goods strewed about the warehouse in large quantities—I thought it had the appearance of a place of large business. RICHARD SWIFT. I am a boot-maker, and live in Greville-street, Hatton garden. On the 1st of November Clark called on me, and said his name was William Clark, of High-street, Epsom, and he wanted a quantity of boots and shbes, about 40 l . worth—he referred me to Mr. Hamer, of Bread-street-hill—I sent my clerk there, and after a report from him I delivered to Clark goods to the amount of 39 l . 14 s . with a bill of the goods, charging the ham per 12 s ., if not returned—Clark came with a horse and cart, and took them away—I went afterwards with an officer to Hamer's place—we went right through the house—we found several crates stuffed with straw, which were tied down, having the appearance of being full—some of the crates in the bottom warehouse had a few dishes and plates strewed at the top of them, but nothing under them but straw—we found several books, but no entry, except an old one of Jones Lloyd and Co., in 1821.
