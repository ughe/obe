JULIA CARTHY. I am servant to Elias Bendon; I had the care of his daughter Rachael. On the 14th of August I took her down Whitechapel in my arms - the prisoner, whom I knew before, came and asked me to give her the child, which I did; she took it up stairs in a house, and gave it a bit of victuals; it had a necklace on; she then brought it down, and took it to a cook-shop, where there were a number of people - she would not let me go in; she did not stay there above a quarter of an hour, and then brought the child out without the necklace; I asked her for it; she said she had not got it; I said "Will you give me the necklace, and I will say nothing to my master;" she said "I don't know, I have not got it."
 Prisoner's Defence. I took the child into a cook-shop and there were a great many girls there, some of whom took the child and kissed it, and took the necklace off its neck; I did not know it for above an hour when the witness came back, and then one of the girls gave me the duplicate - the witness went all down Petticoat-lane, and where the mob was; she never spoke about the beads till she came back with her master.
   JAMES LEA. I took up the prisoner, and she gave me the ticket.
   JOHN YOWART. I am a pawnbroker. I took in this necklace, about two o'clock, of a girl of the name of Maun, not the prisoner.
 ELIAS BENDON. This necklace is the one my child had on.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined One Month.
 1635.  JAMES RAY was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 1 cap, value 3s., and 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of John Lipson , from his person.
 JOHN LIPSON. On the 14th of July I was in  Gray's-inn-lane; the prisoner came up with two females; he took me up in his arms and put me down again - I then missed my cap and handkerchief, which was in it - the prisoner joined the two girls and walked up Gray's-inn-lane; I went and asked him for my property; he made me no answer; I was looking about the street for them, thinking he had not got them; and in half an hour the prisoner came back with the two females again - I asked again for them, but he made me no answer; I then told the watchman; he went after him and took him; he objected to the watchman searching him, and there was some struggling between them; and while that was going on, the cap fell from the prisoner's hat.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did he not appear very drunk? A. No; he returned back to the place where I was; when his hat fell off one of the girls took it up and put it on his head.
   WILLIAM BANTING. I was on duty on the 14th of July - the gentleman came to me and said, he had been robbed; he said there were two men, and one held him while the other took his cap from him - he described him as dressed like a sailor - we went down Gray's-inn-lane-road, where we saw them, and he said, "That is the one in the middle" - I took the prisoner, and he began fighting - his hat fell off, and the cap fell out of it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he not appear tipsy? A. I should not have supposed so; no doubt he had been drinking with the girls.(Cap produced and sworn to.)
     NOT GUILTY.
 1636.  FANNY SHEPHERD was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , 2 sheets, value 4s.; 2 blankets, value 4s.; 1 counterpane, value 3s.; and 1 bolster, value 4s., the goods of William Rowe , in a lodging room. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1637.  WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of June , 11 tea spoons, value 18s., and 2 table spoons, value 12s., the goods of William Bashall , his master.
 Mr. BARRY conducted the prosecution.
 WILLIAM BASHALL. I live in  Camden-cottage, Kentish-town. The prisoner left my service on the 13th of June; I had taken him on the 8th of June; he waited at table at dinner time, and then he went down to the kitchen, and went out at the coach-house door, which it was not necessary to open at all - I have never had the spoons since; he left without his wages.
   SARAH PLATT. I am servant to Mr. Bashall; the prisoner attended the dinner on the 13th, and after dinner the two table spoons were brought down; they laid on the kitchen table, the rest of the plate was in the kitchen cupboard - the prisoner was in the kitchen - I asked him to shake the cloth, he said, he could not till he had put the horse and chaise away; and that would take him the whole afternoon - I went down in about a quarter of an hour, and he was gone and the spoons also; he did not return.
 Prisoner's Defence. I left the place, as he was always grumbling about the horse.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Two Months and Whipped.
