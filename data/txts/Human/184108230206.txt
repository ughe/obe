JOHN STRACHAN FLEWEL was indicted for stealing, on the   15th July , 1 box, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 1 ear-ring, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., and 1 pair of spectacles, value 5 s  .; the goods of William Nordish ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. TERRY NORDISH. I am the wife of William Nordish—we live at Greenwich—in the beginning of July I left home, leaving my cash-box in charge of Mrs. Cotton—Ihere was a pair of spectacles and an earring in it—I have not found them. MARY ANN COTTON. I received the cash-box to take care of on the 15th of July—I he prisoner's mother lodged in the house where I was—the prisoner slept in the room where the box was that night, and in the morn ing he was gone, and the box also—I saw it safe at half-past nine o'clock the night before, and no one had been in the room but him from the time I saw it safe till he went out—he came back in three or four days—his mother accused him of it, and be said he took the box, and it was not of much value. WILLIAM WILTON( police-constable R  216.) When the prisoner was remanded I asked him what it was for—he said for stealing a cash-box, but they would not find it out, as he had put it in Davy Jones's locker  —he said he would not have taken it if he had known how little there was in it, that the woman had told a great story, instead of being 10 s . in it, there was only 5 s . CHARLES GRINHAM( police-sergeant S  21.) I produce a copy of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office—( read )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Transported for Seven Years.

JOSEPH KEATS was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of Au gust , 1 peck of a certain mixture of oats and beans, value 1 s  ., and 2 bushels of chaff, value 1 s  ., the goods of William Pershouse . JOHN WALKER. I am a policeman. On the 21st of August, at half-past eleven o'clock at night, I was in the Trafalgar-road, at Greenwich—I saw the prisoner go into a stable. belonging to Mr. Pershouse—he had nothing with him then—he was in about a quarter of an hour—he then came out with a sack on his back—he shut the door, and was going away—he got two or three yards off with the sack, when I took him by the shoulder and said I should take him to Mr. Pershouse—he said, "For God's sake don't take me, for the sake of my character; I will give you all the money I have got, which is 5 l ."—when I seized him, he dropped the sack.
