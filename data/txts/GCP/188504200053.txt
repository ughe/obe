EDWARD GLADWELL (Policeman R 281). On the morning of 21st February,
about 10 minutes to 12, I was on duty in Beresford Square, Woolwich-
I there saw De Groat crossing from the square from Plumstead towards
Cannon Row I stopped him and asked him where he had been at that
time in the morning lhe said he had been out for a walk and was just
going home he went towards Cannon Row-I communicated with
Constable Turner to have a watch kept in the neighbourhood-De Groat
is a lodging-house keeper and a brothel keeper-he has two houses in
Cannon Row, Nos. 6 and 7-he was somewhere about half a mile from
the prosecutor's when I met him about half past 4 the same morning I
saw Jackson and Wiggins come from the direction of Cannon Row across
the square-I spoke to Turner, and they were followed to the railway
station—they were taken in custody-there is a workmen's train leaving
4 6 o'clock-I never knew De Groat do any work besides keeping the
lodging-houses--I have seen him with his wife standing about, but
never saw him do anything himself.
Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHEGAN. I was in uniform when I met
De Groat in the morning —I do not think he saw me he passed about
twenty yards from me- there is something of a fish market held in
Beresford Square on Saturdays, there is a limited accommodation there,
and people want to get there early with their harrows to get a good
position-he said nothing to me about having been to a market.
CHARLES TURNER (Policeman RR 80). About a quarter-past 4 on the
morning of 21st February I met Jackson and Wiggins — Jackson asked
me if they had plenty of time for the workmen's train - I said "Yes"
Wiggins said he was going to London-I followed them to the railway
station and saw them taken in custody-I was in uniform.
