THOMAS WALLER. I am a wine-merchant, and live at No. 8, Cross-lane,
St. Mary-at-hill, in the City : the prisoner entered my service, as cellerman,
on the 1st of May, 1820. On the 2nd of Sept. last I gave him warning, and
told him that that day week I should dispense with his services I think he
said he was sorry for it-on the Saturday, when he came to receive his wages,
I told him that his week was up, there was his money, and I wished him
health-(the amount of his wages was a guinea) -he said he was sorry for it
he thought that I was peevish, and that I should have thought no more about it,
and then he began crying-I imagine he alluded to having been dismissed
from my service - 1 bad not found any fault with him, only that his faculties
gave way-when I gave him notice I said, “ Your faculties give way, you
don't know what you are about"--on the 2nd of Oct. I received this letter
signed by the prisoner, but written in another hand. Ehp
(Letter read)—"42, Watling-street, Commercial-road East, 2nd Oct., 1843."
