 1752.  JOHN JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September 1 jacket, value 5s., the goods of John Gage.
   JOHN GAGE. On the 7th of September I went on board the Emerald, in the West India-dock bason, to work as a labourer- I pulled off my jacket, waistcoat, and trousers, and put them on a chest at eight o'clock; at twelve o'clock the steward came, and asked me if I had any clothes - I ran on shore, and told the officer to stop the prisoner; he had my jacket on and the trousers, the waistcoat was in his hat; he had been at work on board that ship.
   JOHN BISHOP. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner come on shore from that ship; the prosecutor spoke to me - I stopped the prisoner; the prosecutor said that was his jacket and trousers - I took the prisoner in the office, and asked if he had any thing in his hat; he said No; I took off his hat, and found this waistcoat in it.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 60. Recommended to Mercy- Confined Fourteen Days.
 1753.  JAMES GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , 6 shillings, the monies of Mary Dickerson.
 MARY DICKERSON. I  keep a shop at Tottenham. On the 15th of September the prisoner came in, said he had the tooth-ache, and asked me to give him a bit of ginger - I gave it him; he went out immediately - a person came in, and said a boy had run out of my shop; I had had a bowl in my till, with 6s. or 8s. in it; I looked, and missed it all - I went in pursuit of the prisoner, who was taken the same evening; the officer brought him and the money to me.
   ANN SPENCER. I live near Tottenham church. I saw the prisoner run out of the shop - I had not known him before, but I am sure he is the same boy; he kicked my legs - I told the prosecutrix a ragged boy ran out of her shop: she looked, and missed the money.
   JOHN HUMPHREY. I was informed the prosecutrix had lost the money: I suspected the prisoner, whom I had seen lurking about - I saw him in Love-lane; he saw me, and ran - there were some boys before me; I called to them to stop him - a little boy ran after him; he ran round to very near the street; I came up, and he ran into some woods - I asked him for the money; he said he had given it to the little boy, who gave me 6s.
   EDWARD GRAY. I pursued the prisoner - he said,"Little boy, let me go," and gave me the 6s.
 GUILTY. Aged 9. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Whipped and Discharged.
 1754.  JOSEPH GORDON was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , 1 coat, value 7s., the goods of George Livesley.
 GEORGE LIVESLEY. I lost my coat on the 15th of September - the prisoner lodged in the same room with me, at No. 34.  Belton-street, Long-acre- I have seen my coat since; I never permitted him to pawn my clothes - I have known him from eight to nine months.
   WILLIAM HUMPHREYS. I am a pawnbroker, and live at No. 66, Long-acre. I have a coat, but I cannot say who pawned it - I gave this duplicate for it.
   EDMUND LARKIN. I am an officer. I took the prisoner - in going along he acknowledged to the prosecutor that he took the coat, and pawned it in Long-acre; in consequence of which we found it - this is the duplicate he described.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not know what I was saying - the next morning the officer said I had confessed; I was quite intoxicated.
 GUILTY. Aged 21. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 1755.  JOHN EADES was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , part of a gold chain, value 6s.; 1 seal, value 2s., and 1 key, value 1s., the goods of John Limbert , from his person.
 JOHN LIMBERT. I am a  grocer, and live in Tottenham-court-road. On the 9th of August I was in Oxford-street, opposite Harewood-place, leading to Hanover-square, about eleven o'clock at night; I was walking home, and was quite sober - the prisoner came to me, and snatched my chain and seals; I collared him, and took hold of his hand - I got the chain, which he had broken off, and the seals from his hand; there were two or three more about - he got away, and ran down Harewood-place; I pursued, and a Policeman stopped him.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Was there not a large quantity of people? A. No, not large - I did not lose sight of him; I never expressed a doubt about his person - I did not give him a good thrashing.
   JOSEPH FREDERICK CLEMENTS. I am an inspector of Police. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house, and this property was brought in by the prosecutor; I found two skeleton-keys on the prisoner.
 Cross-examined. Q. What did he say of them? A. He said one was his street door key, but did not give me his direction.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had not removed more than three days - my landlord's goods were seized, and I moved opposite; I went to get some keys at Mr. Cox's, and he lent me one to fit the door while he made one - there is no skeleton-key among them; this is the key I had made, and this one I borrowed.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Life.
