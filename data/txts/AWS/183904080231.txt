1445. HENRY PALMER and RICHARD HALL were indieted for
a misdemeanor.
Messrs. BODKIN and DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAs HAwEs WARREN. I keep a chandler's-shop, in Canterburye
place, O1d Kent-road. On the 16th of March, about four o'clock in the
afternoon, the prisonet Hall came into my shop-fe one penny-worth of
tobacco-he put down a bad shilling tried it, and it was bad-aI bit it,
and bent it, and gave it to him back went away--1 directed two of my
sons to follow him, and tell a policeman.
THOMAS WARREN. I am the prosecutor's son. My father told me to
go after Hall-my brother was with me-when Hall got as far as the
Crown and Anchor public-house, he crossed the road; and then threw
a
tobacco pipe at Palmer, and another man, who got away-they walked on
some distance, and then the other one went into a baker's shop-I went
into the did not see the prisoners taken.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you hear the other's
name ? A. Smith was the name he gave at the station-house.
ROBERT WARREN. I went out with my brother to watch Hall-he
went on towards the Crown and Anchor-l then saw Palmer, and the other
man-they approached together, and went on talking about 200 yards,
then the other left them, and went into a baker's shop, and the two
prisoners went on on the other side of the way-when the little one came
out of the baker' s shop, he went on eating a rollhe went on the same side
as the baker's-h did not join the prisoners again-my brother sent me on
to follow Hall and Palmer-u did so, and saw three policemen- told
them something, and they went in pursuit of the prisoners-whe the
policeman had been spoken to, I saw Palmer look round, and then run-
Hall ran after him-they ran in the same direction, and as they ran I
saw Hall hold up his hand, and throw something over Mr. Cormack's
garden-wall-there is a little bend in the wall there, and it was close at
the bend that he threw it.
