When the prisoners were going away, with their clothes tied up, they asked me to give them something to drink, for they were cold, and I told my boy to get a shilling's worth of rum, and give them. WILLIAM BEDWELL BRADFORD. I am captain of the Hartley.  The two prisoners were seamen on board my ship—they had been discharged, and paid their wages, on the 21st of September—they had no wages to receive at Laurie and Hamilton's, or elsewhere.
   The prisoner James put in a written defence, stating that he had told the prosecutor that he had received his wages, but had to receive some prize  money at Somerset House, which was true, and after that they had been trusted with the clothes.

 3038. MARY ANN WHITE was indicted for a misdemeanor. THOMAS PADDEN. I keep a carpet and general drapery shop in Rat cliffe-highway. I know the prisoner—Mr. William Blair has been a cus tomer of mine for eight years—he lives in Red Lion-street, Wapping—the prisoner came to my shop on the 10th of October, about three o'clock in the afternoon—she said, "Mr. Padden, I want seven yards of striped cotton shirting for my master, Mr. Blair"—she said, "My master told me you was to send a bill by me, and he will call in the evening and pay for it: he it gone into the City to get some money"—I let her have it, considering, from what she said, that she came from Mr. Blair—Mr. Blair had some shirts a day or two before—she returned in about half an hour, and said, "Mr. Padden, you have charged my master 1 d . a yard too much for the shirt ing; and have taken 1 d . a yard more than you charged him before; but he has sent me for seven yards more, and also for three yards of fine brown holland for my mistress's aprons;" and opening out the bill which I had at first given her, she said, "Master says you must put it all on this same bill; and he will call up and pay you this evening, or at least on Sa turday morning"—I cut off the shirting and the holland, and she took it a way. WILLIAM BLAIR. I live at Wapping. The prisoner had been in my service—I dealt with Mr. Padden some time before this occurred—I never sent the prisoner for seven yards of striped cotton, nor for three yards of Holland for my wife's aprons—I never sent her back to say Mr. Padden had charged 1 d . a yard too much for it. FREDERICK JOHN WOOD. I am a pawnbroker. I produce this striped cotton shirting, which I took in pledge of the prisoner—I am certain she is the person.
   MR. PADDEN re-examined.  These are the same two parcels of striped cotton which I delivered to the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  I should not have done it, but I got into a woman's company who advised me to do it.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 16.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Four Months.
