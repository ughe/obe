   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Do you believe this was done intentionally? A.  Yes, I should say it was—I should not like to swear that he was the man that actually did it—he fell down, and he clasped my leg in falling down—it does not look much like an accident—it appeared that it was a stab first, and as I pulled him up, it cut—I had been drinking—I began at the Magpie and Stump, at Brentford, about eleven o'clock, and finished at Mitchell's, at the Salutation—Shirley came in at the Magpie and Stump—he was not drunk then—he got drunk afterwards—I carried him on my back—I am a hard-working man at anything I can get to do; not at prize-fighting, I do not like that game—I had one bit of a turn up  five or six months ago—Spruce lives with me; I keep her. SARAH SPRUCE. I was at the Pack Horse about half-past nine o'clock in the evening on 18th July—I saw the prisoner and the prosecutor quarrelling concerning a fight—they went into the house, I went to the door—it was open—I saw them begin to fight—the prisoner had a knife in his hand—the prisoner said, "If so be you and I fight, you will remember it the longest day you have to live"—they then commenced fighting.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What sort of a knife was this? A.  Such as a man would carry in his pocket to eat his dinner—I cannot tell who hit the first blow—one blow followed the other as near as they could—the prosecutor is not quarrelsome if he is not upset—I live with him. JOHN WARDLE( policeman, T  219). I saw the prosecutor come out of the house, saying he was stabbed—I saw blood on his thigh. FREDERICK DODSWORTH. I am a surgeon, and live at Turnham-green. On 18th July, I was called to attend the prosecutor—I found an incision in his thigh, four inches long, and about an inch deep—I have heard him describe that it was a dig, and drawn along—he being intoxicated it might be done in that way.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Would the cut sober him? A.  Yes—this looked like a cut with the edge of a knife.
   Witnesses for the Defence. WILLIAM BLACKMAN. I keep the Old Pack Horse, at Turnham-green—I saw this row  —I cannot say how the leg got cut—I never saw a knife used—I saw Allen strike him down in front of the bar, and knock his head through two squares of glass—it was between eight and nine o'clock when Allen came to my house—he was drunk—the prisoner and Thompson were there—Allen asked for half-a-quartern of gin, which I put in somen water—he said to me, "Blackman, you gave me two months once—you are a strong fellow in the house, if you say a word against me to-night I will rip your b----y guts out"—he rushed past me, and knocked down the prisoner in front of the bar—he knocked down Thompson and then the prosecutor's wife with a child in her arms—he then said, "I am wounded"—he pulled up his trowsers—there was a wound but no blood.
