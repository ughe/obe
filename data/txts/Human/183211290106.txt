 242.  WILLIAM HILL was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of November , 13 padlocks, value 3s.; 2 bedwinches, value 1s.; 3 pairs of snuffers, value 1s.; 5 corkscrews, value 1s. 6d., and 2 snuffer-trays, value 4d., the goods of James Connolly.
 JAMES CONNOLLY. On Thursday, the 8th of November, I went into the  George and Crown public-house, Broad-street, with these goods, consisting of some locks and other articles - I put them down by my left-hand side, and turned round to show the goods I had to sell to some other persons, and this pocket was gone; I do not know who took it till the Saturday following.
   JOHN STORR(Police-constable F 78). I stopped the prisoner with this pack on the Thursday night - I asked what he had got; he said he did not know, but a cab-man gave it to him to carry; there were thirteen padlocks, some cork-screws, and other things - a key was found in his pocket, which fitted one of the locks.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I found them.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Transported for Seven Years.
 243.  WILLIAM JOHNSON and WILLIAM CROSS were indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November , 45 yards of flannel, value 2l., the goods of James Chapple.
   WILLIAM BRISTY. I live with Mr. James Chapple, of High-street, Pentonville. On the 24th of November I was behind the counter - Johnson came to the door, took up the flannel, and ran away with it: I ran out, and saw him carrying the flannel, with Cross behind him, not one door off - they were taken about two doors off the house; this is the flannel - I marked it on Friday night, and it was taken on Saturday night.
   JAMES FOX(Police-constable N 196). I was on duty near this shop on Saturday night, the 24th of November - I saw the prisoner go to the door together, and which took the flannel I could not tell, but Johnson had it when I took them into custody.
 JOHNSON - GUILTY. Aged 24.
 CROSS - GUILTY. Aged 21.
     Judgment Respited.
 244.  ANN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 canvas bag, value 2d., and 8 sovereigns, the property of William Incell , from his person.
   WILLIAM INCELL. I am a corn-chandler, and live on Stepney-green. On the 26th of November, at twenty minutes after nine o'clock in the evening, I met the prisoner in Whitechapel-road - she was a stranger; she asked where I was going - I said to Stepney; she said she lived there herself, and we walked on together - it was rather dark at the time; I had been taking a glass with a few friends - all on a sudden, when we got to Hanibal-street, she said she saw somebody she knew; I felt my purse, which contained eight sovereigns, drawn from my trouser's pocket - we were stopping at the time, as she said she lived there; I called the Policeman, who came and took her - I had not given her the purse, nor taken any liberty with her.
   GEORGE WALTER SHEW(Police-constable K 50). I was called, and saw the prosecutor holding the prisoner - she wanted to get away; it was about half-past eight o'clock - he accused her of stealing his money, and she denied it; my brother-officer searched her - I heard a sovereign drop, and picked it up; I found the purse after she had gone to the station, about two yards from where I first saw her - it was empty; seven sovereigns were found tied in her shawl.
   WILLIAM HOMER(Police-constable K 173). I took off the prisoner's shawl at the station, to search her, but the inspector said he would send for a woman - the shawl was then on the table - the inspector took it up, and found seven sovereigns, tied up at the end of it; this is the money.
 Prisoner's Defence. He accosted me first; the sovereigns are not his - he offered me 1s.; the purse fell down, and I took it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Transported for Fourteen Years.

   SAMUEL DIAS. I live in Brick-lane. These trousers were hanging on a pole, tied round with a rope - I did not see them taken, but my attention was drawn by my servant - the prisoner was brought back after I gave information.
   FRANCES FLOOD. I live with the prosecutor - I cannot swear to the prisoner, but I saw a hand go up, and the trousers pulled down - I gave information, and saw the prisoner brought back to the house afterwards.
   LEWIS HARRIS. The prosecutor on Wednesday, the 28th, called at my master's, and said he had lost a pair of trousers - the prisoner afterwards came and offered the trousers for sale, and I gave information.
