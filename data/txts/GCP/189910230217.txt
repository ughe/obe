of valuation by the directors, but a question of taking the securities at
their par value, and it ought to be stated; that was Mr. Lambert's
intention, I know-I know there was a very strong interview between
Mr. Lambert and Mr. Abbott on that-Mr. Wain resigned on December
14th, according to the minutes that would of itself have dissolved the
board, because there would be only one director then—I do not believe
there was after December 5th a properly constituted board meeting-
those letters that were written after the Scottish meeting were written a
without Mr. Lambert's knowledge-I do not remember that some cheques
were subunitted to Mr. Lambert about that time for his signature which
he declined to sign---the letter from me in which I offer founders' shares
for sale was a private matter of my own-I do not suggest that Mr.
Lambert knew anything about that; I was not selling the shares
privately; I was assisting the promoters, of which Mr. Lambert was not
one-we used to send letters out frequently, but they were written on
the instructions of the promoters— Ir. Lambert had nothing to do with
them; he had nothing to do with the foundere', and the founders' had
nothing to do with the bank ; because all the founders' were allotted to
Baker and his nominees--so far as I knew, Lambert never received any
money at the bank in any way except for directors' fees—anything be
had from the bank has been repaid, and in addition he had lent the bank
money-the bank were his debtors at the date of the liquidation ; he had
nothing whatever to do with my going to Scotland, or anything of that
kind-I have had a great deal to do with this company, from start to
finish–I will admit that I had a great deal more to do with it than ever:
Mr. Lambert has had so far as I know, there has never been any
proposition made to include me in a criminal charge with regard to this
matter.
