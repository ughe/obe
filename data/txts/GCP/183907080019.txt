WILLIAM HEWITT. I am a tea-dealer, and live in King William-street,
City. The prisoner was a shopman in my service for about seven weeks
in consequence of some suspicions I had, on Saturday the 22nd of June, I
sent the witness Meek with a half-sovereign, a crown, and two half-crowns,
all marked, to make a purchase at my shop- examined the till about five
minutes after the witness went, and found the two half-crowns and five
shilling piece, but no half-sovereign-I sent for a policeman, and called on
the prisoner to take the money he had out of his pocket-he produced the
marked half-sovereign, and I think a sovereign, and some silver, but I am
not quite certain the officer searched his box, and found 341. or 351.,
which he took possession of.
Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. What was he doing, when you
spoke to him in the shop ? A. Standing behind the counter--he was not
doing any thing particular-he was not writing-there were no customers
in the shop-he was not writing in any book-I am quite sure he was not
entering any thing in a book, not relating to this money-I am quite sure
he was not writing at all.
GEORGE MEEK. On Saturday morning, the 22nd of June, at twenty.

- he had previously supplied me with a half-sovereign, & five shilling
piece, and two half-crowns-he marked them in my presence I bought
41b. weight of 5s. tea, and paid for it all the money he gave me.
