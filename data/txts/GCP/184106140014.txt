MARGARET M'Carthy. I am a market woman, and live in For
court. I was going to Farringdon market about seven o'clock last Friday
morning with another woman, and stopped at Mrs. Smith's stall to have i
halfpennyworth of coffee-the woman with me said to her, "Have you
got Norah Sullivan in Newgate ?" she said, "Yes, and as far as an oath
will go I will transport her."
MARGARET SMITH re-examined. I never had such a conversation, nor
ever saw that woman before.
HONORA FOLEY. I live in Wild-court. The female prisoner is my
daughter--the male prisoner is her husband-I gave her up in marriage
to him--they were married in Lincoln's-inn-fields more than three yean
ago, by a Roman Catholic clergyman---they are both Catholics, they
have lived together as man and wife, but have very often fallen out, and
she was often living with me.
(John Sullivan, the prisoner's father, was called, but was too intoxicated
to be examined.)

1554. STEPHEN STOWER was indicted for feloniously receiving,
on the 21st of April, of a certain evil-dispored person, 104 yards of lace,
value 41. 128. : 6 collars and 2 shawls; the goods of Thomas Frost and an
other; well knowing them to have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.
CHARLES WESTBROOK. I am a linen-draper, and live at Milton, near
Gravesend. I purchased 104 yards of lace, six collars, a cape and a shawl,
of the prisoner, on the 21st of April-I had never seen him before-
asked what he wanted for them-he said two guineas for the shawl-be
appeared not to know the value-he said he bought them a bargain, they
cost him 41. 10s.- I offered him 41. for the lot, which he took--my shop-
man saw him before me--I sent the goods to London the same afternoon
believing them, from the man's manner, to be stolen-I asked his ad-
dress-he gave me 13, or 15, Little Carter-lane--as far as I can remember,
the paper they are in now is the one I bought them in.
