WILLIAM JONES. I am a labourer, and work in Aylesbury-walk, Dept-
ford. I have seen the prisoner twice the first time was at the Clock
House, on the 1st of April-he gave me some halfpence for singing-
saw him again on the 8th, between six and seven o'clock-I had the
been singing a song, and he and several others asked me to drink after
that, about ten o'clock, he was standing at the bar, and I was going through
to the tap-room to bid some young men good night-the prisoner was
there, and bid me good night, he told me to get a pot of beer-- I told him
it did not lie in my power to pay for it-he said he did not ask me to på
for it myself-he put his hand into his trowsers pocket, and gave me
shilling-he stood with his back against the bar, and when he gave me the
shilling I put it on the bar-Miss Passmore said it was bad, and asked me
who gave it me-I said, “This good man, here"- she said, "This is not
the first or second time he has offered bad money here to-night; I shall
send to my father"--the prisoner made no answer-I cannot say whether
he heard it-he was about half a yard off.
