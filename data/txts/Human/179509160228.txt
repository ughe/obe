 Q. What sort of a seal did you seal it with? - It was a seal that I borrowed of the landlord of the house.
 Q. What was on the seal? - I cannot recollect.
 Q. What was the landlord's name? -Holt, he keeps  the sign of the Bunch of Grapes, in Well-street, Oxford-street; I live just by, and I used that public house to have my beer.
 Q. Who was present when you sealed this letter, and put in the half guinea? -  Rebecca Hyatt, the servant of this house, She brought me a candle of seal it, she see it. Then I put my letter into my pocket, and went out till between the hour of four and five in the afternoon, then I came to the Bunch of Grapes again; I asked if Mr. Holt would be so kind as to give the postman that letter at six o'clock, when he usually came by; in the mean time the bell man came; I said, Mr. Holt, be so kind as to step out and give the man that letter; and I stood by the side of him and see him give the man the letter; the prisoner is the bellman; he asked him if he would take this letter? he said he would; he asked him if it would go safe? he said he would enter it in the book when he came to Lombard-street, and it would go safe; Holt asked him what it would come to; he paid him eleven-pence for the postage; and he doubled it once double in his hand, and he carried it away in his hand, he did not put it into the bag with the rest, and in the course of the week I had no answer to the letter that I sent, and I sent another letter, and had another letter describing that that first letter was not received; when I found the letter had not come to hand, I went to the inland office to examine the books, and they ordered me to come the next day at eleven o'clock; that was the Monday week after the letter was sent; the person that took it in was not there then; I then the next day, the person examined the books, and there was no such thing entered in the books; I went back to Well-street, and at five o'clock I met the postman, and I asked him what I was to do to get my money; that Tuesday, the same day as I was at LOmbard-street.
 Q. Where did you see him? - At the Bunch of Grapes door, where I put in the letter; I asked him whether I was to lose it or no? he said he knew nothing about it, he put it into the office; I asked him if that was all the recompence I was to have for it, I said if that was all I would go and get a warrant, and then I went and got a warrant.
 Q. Did you ever see this bell-man in your life before? - Never before, he took the half guinea, he was quite a stranger to me.
 Q. You never heard his name mentioned? - Never to the best of my knowledge, not I did not know his name when I got the warrant.
 Mr. Knowlys. The prisoner told you that it would be entered in a book? - He said he would enter it in a book when he came to Lombard-street.
 Q. I don't know whether you know the regulation of the office, that if a man does neglect to enter it in the book, and any thing is lost, he is obliged to pay for it? - I don't know, I don't want to prosecute the man, not hurt him, and she justice ordered us to make it up; I only want my money.
 Q. He did offer you the half guinea by the justice's recommendation? - At that time he did.
 Q. And who was the justice? - Mr. Scott.
