SURREY CASES .
   Before Mr. Recorder.
 562. HENRY WILMOT , burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Clare , and stealing 1 tea-caddy, and other goods, value 3 l  ., of John Clare .
   MR. BALLANTINE . conducted the Prosecution. LYDIA CLARE. I am the wife of John Clare, of No. 49, Commercial-road, Lambeth. On the morning of 27th April, about a quarter past 3 o'clock, I was awoke by a person named Jenkinson—I ran down stairs and found my parlour window open—I found that two dresses and a coat had been removed from behind the door to the centre table, and the tea-caddy and tea-pot from the sideboard, where they had been when I went to bed about a quarter past 10 o'clock the night before—I had fastened the window the night before, but had not put the shutters up—my husband and I occupy the back and front parlours—we were the last up in those room—we had lodgers in the house—we looked up the parlours, and took the keys with us stairs—when I came down in the morning I found the doors still locked as I had left them—the parlour doors open into the pas sage—nobody else in the house has a key of those doors.
   Prisoner.  When I was taken she said she could not speak to the fastening of the window from the Sunday previous. Witness.  I said I had not had it open since the Sunday; but I saw that it was quite secure when we went to bed on the night of the robbery—I did not fasten it, I saw that it was fast—I did not put up the shutters, because it darkens the room so. JANE JENKINSON. I am the wife of Thomas Jenkinson, of Lett's Wharf, Commercial-road, Lambeth. On the morning of 27th April, about half past 3 o'clock, I got out of bed and looked out of window, and saw a man and woman at the corner of Cottage-place—I saw the man open Mr. Clare's window, and get into the room—we live opposite them—I do not know what became of the woman, she did not go in—I went down stairs, and made an alarm to my husband, who is watchman in Mr. Lett's timber yard, and then went with him over to Mr. Clare's—I threw up the window, which the man had closed again after getting in, and he jumped out of the window, and knocked me down backwards—the prisoner is the man, I am certain of him—he did not strike me, he knocked me down by jumping out to make his escape—my husband ran after him, calling "Stop thief!" but he could not catch him.
   Prisoner. Q.  How can you swear to me? A.  I really cannot tell, but I can swear that you are the man by your appearance.
   COURT . Q.  What sort of light was there? A.  Very good—we are between gaslights—it was just getting daybreak. THOMAS JENKINSON. I am the husband of the last witness. My wife came down to me soon after 3 o'clock on the morning in question, and gave me information, in consequence of which I went with her over to Mr. Clare's—I saw the prisoner jump out of the window, I have not the least doubt he is the person—I followed him, but did not catch him—I called "Stop thief!"—he ran along Cornwall-road, across Stamford-street, and down Cornwall-road again, and then I lost sight of him—I did not see him stopped.
