PHILLIP GARROD. I am a sailor belonging to the Gleaner,  of York shire. On Sunday morning, the 31st of August, I arrived in town from Hull—I was in Tooley-street that night, and met Lynch—we got into conversation, and I went home with her to Brewer's, turning Vine-yard—I am sure I had my watch when I went into the house—we had very little conversation—she asked me for money—I told her I had none—the asked me to leave ray jacket—I said, "No, I will leave my watch in pawn," but I did not give my watch to heir—she afterwards left the room, and was absent about four minutes, and when she came up she asked me for my watch—I felt for it, and it was gone—I am sure I had it when I went to the house—an alarm was made, the landlady of the house came up, and a policeman was called in—they searched the room, but found no watch—they searched over the house and presently the prisoner Jones came into the room—I had not noticed her before—she was not new my person, but the watch was found in her bosom ( produced )—this is it—nobody but Lynch had been near me.
