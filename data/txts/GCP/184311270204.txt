HENRY JEFFREYS On the 10th of Nov. I was working for the prosecu.
tor, at No. 11 arch, Waterloo-bridge-she prisoners were at work there,
from six till half past six o'dock --I saw them leave together-Ellis had two
saddles, a breeching, and a pair of hames, on his left shoulder-Westwood
assisted him in uatrying them out- I saw then leave the areh with it
they did not come back I was there till very near side o'еlock.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you been in the prosecutor's employ!
1. Twelve months. I went to work about seven o'clock on Friday morning
I called at my master's house first for the key-be gave it to me at the door
w generally call for it if master is hot up, I do not take it, but wait till be
gives it me- I did not know where it was kept--the two prisoners were in
the stable on Friday evening, when I retined at five o'cloek-we all came
in about five-master lets out cabs--the two prisoners and two more men
were at work I remained there till they left, without going away no one
but us five were in the stable after five, except my master-bis son came in
with my master came about half-past five, and left-I did not see his
son go with him, but I did not see him after master left master returned
about balf-past six, or a quarter to seven-the prisoners were then gone-
no one but me was there I did not tell my master or his son that the pri-
soners had gone away with the barness we all three came away together
my young master locked up the stable-master had then gone on about ten
yards a-head-be could not have heard his sort locking the door this was
from half-past eight till nine-.when master left at half past six, it was to see
after the prisoners and the other men-I had to do their work I did not
know their names or addresses - I called them by nick names, Kingston Jack
and Fiddler I gave the others no names--I cannot tell at what time the
other men left the last time I saw them was at five, when we all came back
I have not seen them since the prisonets had no right to take the harness
out of the stable, without they were told by master or his son-I did not bear
them tell them to take it-master used to tell them to take it to his house, to
be put away it was new harness, and I thought they were going to take it
to my master's I told my master about it on the Sunday, when he spoke to
me about it at the stable before I worked for the prosecutor I worked for my
father, who is a plumber, and drove vans--the harness used to be fetched back
when it was wanted, and it was wanted on Sunday-I have heard nothing about
a reward - I do not know Brown of Griffin-about nine men were employed
about the stable on the Friday--the arch is fastened by a padlock- it was
quite secure that night, and also in the morning-I never saw my young
master, or any one, draw the staple when they went in, and push it in again,
Court. 2. What light had you in the stable ? 1. Four candles and á
large fire.
SUSANNAH WISE. I live in Commercial-road, Lantbeth, very near the
arches of Waterloo-bridge. About six o'clock on Friday evening. I saw Ellis
with a saddle on his shoulder-be stopped and looked in my face, and I saw
the curl on the side of his face he was carrying harness --it was slipping, and
Westwood went and helped it up-I was afraid of my window-I saw the
brass of the harness-I can swear more to Ellis, because he came to my shop
frequently, and the other not so often.
