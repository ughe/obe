673. ROBERT MURPHY (29), (a soldier), Robbery, with other persons
on Patrick Jully, and stealing from his person 7 of his money.
MR. BESLEY conducted the Prosecution.
PATRICK JULLY. I am a superannuated officer of the Civil Service, and
have a pension--I live at Southampton-on 18th April, about 5 o'clock, I
went into the Silver Cross, Charing-oross, to have a glass of ale I was tired,
and a friend or two had promised to meet me there I waited, and the pri-
soner and four other soldiers were standing close to me--they said, “We are
poor soldier chape, and liave not much morey to spare; you do not mind
giving us a treat," and I stood several treats I do not mind treating
soldiers but I drank very little I spent about a crown they kept very
close to me-- I took out some sovereigns, and from my right-hand pocket,
looking for some silver, and the prisoner turned round to the left side, threw
his left arm round my neck, with his thumb in my throat, his head against
me, and put his right hand in my trousers pocket, where my money
was-the other four closed up, so that I could not get away be took bis
hand out, and passed it round to the hands of the others - I took him by
the collar and said, "You ruffian, you have robbed me there was a row
we had a souffle, and be broke away and ran out immediately-Innafter
him, kept him in sight, and caught him under the arches of the Horse
Guards-he said, “Let me go, Sir, and I will give you all the money I have
about me " I said, 4 You have got the wrong man this time ; you will go
with me, but you will not go from me "—there was a struggle, and he broke
away again through the archway into the parade-ground I am sorry to say
that I have lost an arm, or I would have kept him firm-I thought it was
a duty not to allow a man to rob me with Her Majesty's uniform on.
