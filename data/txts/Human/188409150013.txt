   Wheatley's Defence.  I had not the slightest intention of using any I violence. I had the revolver with me, but it was useless. I simply in tended to intimidate, not to hurt any one. Garner says I did not intend to I strike him, or use any violence, though I was capable of doing so if I wished. When I was taken to the station I told the inspector that my revolver was useless. He took it down, and read it to me. He now says I did not tell him anything of the kind. I swear I did say to, and I told him where it was purchased. HENRY PARKER( Re-examined ). I did not write down anything that Wheatley said—I wrote down some marks about his body—I have not got it here; it is at the station—I wrote down nothing about the revolver—he did not say his revolver was useless, nothing of the sort, nor that he only had it to frighten—he did not say so to me or in my presence.
   By Wheatley.  I asked you where the burglary was committed—you said somewhere in the road—I am quite sure I did not ask you where you got the revolver—all I read out to you was from the charge-sheet.
