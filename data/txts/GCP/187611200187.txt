EBENEZER MOGFORD. I am employed as a letter carrier at Town Row,
Rotherfield-on the 4th August I saw the prisoner there-he overtook mo
as I was going down to Town Row, and asked me if I had a lotter for John
Reed, if I had he would take it I looked, and saw that I had a letter for
John Reed, and I gave it to him-it was a similar letter to this, stamped.
with red ink, which means officially paid-I got that letter from Charles
Payne, a letter carrier from Tunbridge Wells.
Oror examined. I have delivered letters in Rotherfield for nearly two
years--I have delivered to the prisoner several letters of his own, into his
own hand-the people in Rotherfield sometimes meet me in the street and
ask me for their letters—this was on the 4th August-the prisoner had
been to me two or three times before to wait for a letter—I don't know how
long after the 4th of August it was that I was spoken to about this; it
was nearly a month before I went before the Magistrate. U
Re-examined. I had never delivered any letters of Reed's to the prisoner
before the 4th of August-I knew that his name was Edward Smith-I had
known him about five months—his house was in my beat-I knew where he
"lived it was Sergeant Avery who first asked me about this, and I then
remembered that I had delivered such a letter to the prisoner for Reed.

Amos Avery. I am a sergeant in the Sussex Constabulary-in conse-
quence of information I received from my wife, on Sunday, 27th Angust, I
went to Town Row, Rotherfield, to the prisoner's house-I saw him
asked him what had been lost-he said “John has lost his box and bank.
book, and I have lost a pair of trousers, a necktie, and a ring"-I searched
round the premises to see if anybody had broken into the house-I found
no trace of anything of the kind, nor anything like it-I searched the hedges
and ditches round, and different places, supposing the box might have been
thrown away-he said John had lost his box and his bank-book, and that his
wife and John had gone to the post-office, and they could not find that any
maney had been drawn-they resided alone in that house on 31st August
1 obtained a warrant for the prisoner I went and read the warrant over to
him-he said "I know nothing at all about it" -I then looked him up-
before that, on the 29th August, finding out that he had paid some money
away at four or five places, I said to him “ You have paid away some money,
you have paid 11. to Isaac Diplock, of 5, Ashdown; 11. to Walter Noakes,
21. to your brother-in-law John; and you changed a sovereign at Bassett's
beershop and a sovereign at Buckstead" he said he had borrowed 21. of
Ned Bassett-I went to Bassett, and found he had not I did not tell the
prisoner what had passed between me and Bassett.
