he deals with a different period—I had to use the same materials that he had to a very great extent; for instance, to show the commissions earned, I took the details of the commissions earned up to March 31st, according to Mr. Abbott's statement, and then I went through the same books for the subsequent period, and then I went through the cashiers books to see if those amounts had been received or earned—the documents that I received on behalf of Beall when I took possession as Official Receiver were very, very numerous—there were some books, but very few entries in them—I looked through every one before I went to the Mansion House—I extracted from the papers about one hundredweight that appeared to have a bearing on the case, and the agreement with the Sberidans was one of those that was not put in; I cannot tell you why, except that there were a great many left out—there were 400 exhibits put in—I think I had the prospectus of the London Refuse Company; that is the document that was put in with the illustra tions—there were a large number of loose documents—the founders' shares were share warranto to bearer, and if 2,000 founders' sharps were payable to the promoters, so far as the company was concerned, they would only have to deal with the 2,000 founders' shares, they could not deal with any more—as they were share warrants to bearer, it was quite immaterial to the company how they were distributed—it is very unusual to advertise the dividends, and to let the holders of the warrants come forward and claim them, because in every other case that has come under my notice there has been a register showing the names of the original founders, and then it is invariably found, before the dividend is paid, how the founders' shares have been dealt with—my registration will not assist me to find out to whom the dividend is payable;
