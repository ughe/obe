DAWKINGS, Benjamin (42, printer), was indicted for and charged
on the coroner's inquisition with the manslaughter of George Daw-
kings; he was also indicted for that, having the custody of George
Dawkings. a boy under the age of 16 years, he did unlawfully and wil-
fully neglect him in a manner likely to cause unneces•ary suffering
and injury to his health.
Mr. Clarke Hall prosecuted.
ERNEST FORD, an offcer of the National Society for the Prevention
of Cruelty to Children. On July 23 1 called at 44, Durban Road, Tot-
ienham, and saw prisoner's wife and the boy George. The boy was on
a bed, partially dressed; be was groaning and crying with pain; he
looked very emaciated and ill and was in a very dirty and verminous
condition. I called in Dr. Rostand and on his advice the boy was
sent to the infirmary. • On July 25 1 saw prisoner and told him tho
boy was in a very serious condition. He said, I was not aware the
boy was so ill; I own the boy asked for a doctor, but I did not think
he was so ill or I should have called one in; I own I have neglect«i
him now."
Cross-examined by prisoner. I called twice on July 23 ; on ny
second visit I saw your daughter washing the boys legs.
CAROLINE BARKER. I and my husband live in the same house with
prisoner and his family. For a week previous to July 9 1 knew that
the boy George lay ill. On the 9th I heard something fall outside my
room, and on opening the door I saw the boy lying on the floor; he
said, 'Oh, Mrs. Barker, pick me up ' ' ; he seemed very ill. I carried
him to prisoner's room and laid him on 'he bed with prisoner ; prisoner
to the boy, Could not you lie quiet up there."
Dr. ANDREW ROSTAND. On July I was called by Ford and went
to 44, Durban Road, and saw the boy. He was in a most pitiful con-
dition, extremely emociated, and very filthy ; he had not been cleaned
for a long time; I did not notice any vermin. He complained of being
in great pain, and was so tender that he could only be moved
the greatest diffculty•, in fact, he could scarcely bear the weight
his clothes. I found his left hand was inflamed; there was a
collection of fluid, probably pus, in his right knee; the left knee was
also inflamed and verv tender: both ankles were inflamed; ther wu
a large wound below the right knee, which was very septic, with severd
sinuses or ulcers. His condition must have been perfectly apparent
to anyone who examined him • it clearly called for medical attention,
and this had certainly not been given. I sent the boy to the infirm'"
and did not see him again.
