WILLIAM WARD was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of January, 1 coat, value 10 s  .; and 2 handkerchiefs, value 1 s  .; the goods of Thomas Fulton , in a certain vessel, upon a certain navigable river called the   Thames . THOMAS BENJAMIN WALKER. I am a constable of the Thames police. I boarded a boat on the 22nd of January, with the prisoner in her—I asked him where he was going to take this coat that he had with him?—he said on shore—I asked where?—he said he did not know I took him on board the ship, and he confessed it belonged to the meter who was weighing the coals. THOMAS FULTON. I was on board the Rainbow , which was lying in Church-hole—this coat is mine, and these handkerchiefs—I left them on board the vessel on Saturday night, the 21st of January—the prisoner was one of the crew. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined One Month.

FREDERICK BLACKWELL and WILLIAM HOPGOOD were indicted for stealing, on the   15th of January , 231bs. weight of lead, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Charles Farebrother , Esq.; and fixed to a certain building; against the Statute, &C.; and that Blackwell had been before convicted of felony. PATRICK ROACH. I am a policeman. On the 16th of January, about a quarter after two o'clock in the afternoon, I met the prisoners, together in lngleton-street, Brixton—Blackwell was carrying this lead in this cloth—I asked what he had got?—he made no reply, but threw down the lead and ran away—I took the lead, and seized Hopgood—my brother officer pursued and took Blackwell. JAMES CURTIS( Police-constable P  156.) I was coming, down Brixton-road, and saw Blackwell making away—after half an hour's hard chase, I succeeded in apprehending him—on conveying him to the station, he said, "I know the other one will come it on me—I Will tell the truth—it was not me that took it at all"—I took the prisoners to Union Hall—on going, Hopgood asked what he should get done to—I said I did not know—he said, "It was not me that did it, I stood in the park, Blackwell got, on the summer-house, and threw down the lead to me"—I went and matched the lead to the place on the 17th, and found it corresponded exactly—the tiles had been taken off the summer-house of Mr. Alderman Farebrother, and this lead was taken from it—they had got off the fence on to the summer-Swuie—the lead tallied in all respects.
