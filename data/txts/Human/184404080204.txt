   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE ? Q.  Have you ever used the knife? A.  No, not for many years—it was fit to use—the prisoner was never in my service—I took her in out of charity some time in October—I had two men lodgers and a female servant—I put the money into the box about eleven o'clock on the Saturday night, and then went to bed—a person could get to my box without coming into my bed-room—the prisoner left on the Friday morning, between seven and eight, to go to Mr. Brett's, where she was servant—I am sure she did not go on the Wednesday—she was at Mr. Brett's four or five weeks before she left me—I had no words with her—Mr. Brett lives next door but one to me—I knew where to find her—I gave information to the police directly—I found the prisoner at Blackwater-college—females come into my house the same as they do to other houses—no girls come there, and go to a room with gentlemen—I cannot swear that they never have done so, and paid me—they have not done it to my knowledge. Between ten and eleven o'clock on Monday, the 26th of February, the prisoner came to my shop and asked if I could make her a dress and a cardinal-cloak by Friday—she brought me the materials, and I made the dress—this is it—I sold her a bonnet and a pair of stays—she paid me a sovereign, and 6 s . 6 d . in silver—she had scarcely any clothing—she said she was going to York to see her sister, that her mistress had given her leave—I asked why she had so few clothes on, it would give her cold—she said she took them off to wash—I asked where she lived—she said as kitchen-maid at the Mess at Hounslow-barracks, and the money was for wages her master had paid her. THOMAS BRETT, tailor, Barrack-road, Heston , The prisoner was in my service about seven weeks—she left on Saturday morning, the 2nd of March—she was living with me on the 26th of February—about eight in the evening of that day I saw her making up a flannel petticoat—she told me and my wife that Betsy Murray had given her these articles, 17 yards of silk to make a dress, some black velvet to make her a cloak, and other articles—on that day she was dressed very indifferently, and when she left she had the clothes now produced on—I do not keep the Barrack Mess—Betsey Mur ray is an unfortunate person, and has a great deal of money, and dresses in the first style—a few days before the prisoner left I saw this knife in her possession—she said, "I have found a little box with 6 1/2 d . in it, and an old knife, by the privy, and I have put it on a shelf ( pointing to a shelf )—I went to look for the box just now—it was gone, but the knife was left "—I went to look for the box, but she said, "No, it is gone; but the boys may have the knife"—I looked at it, and said, "It is a saw; the boys shan't have this "—on the 3rd of March I found a lock on the wall that goes round the copper in my kitchen, and a long bit of iron by the side of it; but I did not take notice of it—I flung it on the dunghill—this is the lock.
