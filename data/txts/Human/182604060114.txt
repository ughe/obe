Cross-examined by Mr. BARRY. Q. Do you know the prisoner? A. No.
   RICHARD EDWARDS. I was in Foster-street on the 26th of February, between six and seven o'clock in the evening - I saw Blacket at one corner of the street, and Bailey putting a key into the door of No. 3 - Lyall stood before him; I went up the street and saw Sawyer standing at the corner - I and another boy went and played in the Broadway for two or three minutes; we then went down the street again; I saw Sawyer go down and whistle - they then all went from the door - Sawyer went down the street and came to the corner again; Blacket and the others then went away - we went down again and saw the door open, which had been shut when we passed it before; we went to Walton, the officer, and told him - he came with us to the house - as we were coming down the street we saw the prisoners coming up together - Sawyer was behind them - they were coming from No. 3; we went down to the house - the officer went into the passage and said"Who is here;" the door was then opened, and Bailey said "It is me, Sir;" Walton caught hold of his collar, he wrestled in the road and got away, but left his hat behind; we found the drawers open in Miss Perkins' front parlour, and the things on the ground.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you know Sawyer? A. Yes; by working at Mr. Page's; I am an apprentice to Mr. Law, in the New Market, but since last October I have been living with my mother, as I did not like the business; I have been there eight or nine months - my master is willing to take me back - he has complained of me in a Court of Justice, and I was sent to the House of Correction; I once had part of the money for which a watch was sold, but I did not take it; I believe I have run away from my master about six times; I was once taken out of a lodging house, but I do not know that it was a house of ill-fame.
 COURT. Q. Upon your oath did you go to meet any girl or woman there? A. No, it was a lodging-house for men - I do not know who kept it - I was there only one night - I slept by myself - I went there about the watch concern because I was afraid to go home.
   THOMAS SHROPSHIRE. I was in Foster-street with Edwards - I saw Blacket at the corner of Half-moon-alley; it was then between half-past six and seven o'clock - we went on and saw Bailey standing, at the door of No. 3, making a noise with something, and Lyall was behind him on the curb - we went on and saw Sawyer standing at the top of the street - we went to the Broadway, and played at a step, and then we went down again, and saw them at the same place - we then went down on the same side as the house was, and saw Lyall and Bailey, two or three yards off the door - Blacket was at the same place as at first - we went round Peter-street, and came down again and saw Bailey and Lyall at the same place, and the door was open, which had been shut before; Sawyer then went down Peter-street; we went round again, and they all went different ways; we went to the officer - he went with us into Peter-street, and saw Blacket and Lyall just by a gas light in the same street, some distance off - we saw Sawyer come by, and we went to the house, and the officer knocked at the shutters, and said "Who is here?" Bailey came out, and said "I am;" the officer collared him- he struggled and got away - I went into Miss Perkins' room - I found the candle wrapped round with a black stocking, and these things about the floor - the lock of the drawer seemed to be broken.
 Cross-examined. Q. Bailey was the man who came out of the house? A. Yes; I did not know them before but I can identify their persons; I am a companion of Edwards' - we had been buying some sugar - candy, and were going home.
   JOSEPH WALTON. I am the officer. These boys fetched me to Foster-street; I saw Blacket and Lyall coming up the street about two yards from the corner, as I turned into the street, about ten or twelve yards from the house - I then went to No. 3, and saw Bailey - I struggled with him and he escaped; I found the drawers open, these things on the ground, and a pick-lock key in the street-door.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know the two witnesses? Q. Yes; I had Edwards in custody from his master for running away - but he liberated him again; I did not know they were the parties till after I found them out - I knew them well, and could not be deceived in them; the boys had told me a house had been broken open, but did not say who were doing it.
 The prisoners called -
   WILLIAM LAWS. I am a boot-maker, and live in East-street, Finsbury-market. Edwards was apprenticed to my father - after being a week on liking he ran away; we found he was connected with a notorious set of young thieves - my father put him into the House of Correction; he was with us, in the whole, fifteen or sixteen months, and ran away fifteen or sixteen times; he has been taken from a lodging-house in Chick-lane, and from the Royalty Theatre; he has been out three or four days and nights together, and seldom came home without being fetched.
     NOT GUILTY.

 880.  JOHN JEFFERS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of February , 6ozs. weight of tea, value 9d., the goods of the   United Company of Merchants, trading to the East Indies.
 Mr. BOLLAND conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN LINES. I am in the employ of the East India Company. I was employed to watch at their warehouse in Crutched-friars, where some men were mending chests - they went to breakfast at ten o'clock, and soon after the prisoner came into the cellar and went to a chest - I heard his hand go into the chest three distinct times; I then went for Mr. Frazer, the elder, and said I must search the prisoner; he said he had not got any thing - but after I took him down to the counting-house he offered me a sovereign to let go his hand; I found this tea in his breeches pocket - the chest had been plundered before - he had no business in the cellar.
   ALEXANDER FRAZER. I am an elder. I saw the prisoner - Lines searched him in my presence, and about six ounces of black tea was taken from his left-hand breeches pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took a book from George Williams - I went to tell him he had brought a wrong number, and I asked him to change it.    GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Six Months.
