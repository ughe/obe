FREDERICK GEORGE FRANCIS. I am a clerk in the London and South Western Railway Company, in the same office as the prisoner; I was under him. In consequence of suspicion, I examined the drawer under the funnel, on Sunday, the 2d of Sept., between seven and eight o'clock in the morning—I missed about thirty tickets—I made a communication to Mr. Weatherhead the same day, and I went to the drawer with him on the Monday evening—I found the tickets were there then, and they were not there the day before—I examined those tickets, and they appeared to have a stamp on them—the tickets in the drawers should not have any stamp—in a great pressure of business I should have recourse to the drawer, whether the prisoner were absent or present—the numbers run consecutively, and would be issued con secutively—the report of the tickets was made in the office by the clerk who booked the train.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Do you know that Mr. Blunden issued the tickets for the seven o'clock train? A.  No; I believe he booked the trains for the Saturday—I know the drawer in which the tickets are kept—I am not aware that the key was kept by any one—the drawer was always open when I have been there—I know the office well—I know the funnel—it has been fed by every body there; that is to say, by every clerk-quite as much by one man as another—it was very rarely that the prisoner himself issued any tickets—he did occasionally, to relieve another clerk, if someclerk called on him to relieve him, or if another clerk were absent-sometimes, in consequence of a pressure of business at a fair or races, another clerk would be sent for—I recollect one of the Dovor railway men getting there and serving our passengers.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Who was there on the 1st of Sept.? A.  Myself and Mr. Blunden, and the prisoner; I saw no other person—if I or any other clerk go to the drawer, we take the tickets in regular turn—there was no other person besides me and Mr. Blunden, and the prisoner, issuing tickets—I never took any tickets out of their regular number—it was the duty of the clerks who issued the tickets, to supply the funnel from the drawer. EDWARD MARTIN BLUNDEN. I am one of the clerks in that office. There were no other persons there but myself, Mr. Francis, and the prisoner—the persons from the Dovor train have free access to the office—they go down to a place underneath to wash their hands—they had nothing to do with issuing the tickets on the 1st of Sept.—nobody but me and the prisoner, and Mr. Francis, issued tickets on the 1st of Sept.—I supplied tickets from the drawer to the funnel during that day—I took them in their regular turn.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  I suppose the funnel is not kept locked? A.  There is a mode of locking it by a slide draw in front; it never was locked—I have one key of the funnel now, but I do not think there was one then—I had one key of the drawer, but it never was locked; that was the only key of the drawer.
