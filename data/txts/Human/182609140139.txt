 1648.  MARY ANN CARROLL and MARY CARROLL were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of August , 7 yards of lace, value 3s.; 1 gown, value 3s., and 6 yards of net, value 3s., the goods of James Dawson.
   CATHERINE DAWSON. I live in Church-street, St. Giles- Mary Carroll lived with me; Fleming lived in the same house. On the 22d of August, about four o'clock, I went out and left Mary Ann Carroll in my room, and this property safe; I went to the adjoining house, but did not go in - while I was there the two prisoners came out together - I saw Mary Ann with her apron up - I went into my house, and missed the articles.
 Prisoner MARY CARROLL. The witness asked me to go to lie down at her house, where my sister was; she gave me the gown to pawn, and the lace was in it; she knew I pawned it, and had some of the money. Witness. No, I did not.
   ELIZABETH FLEMING. I went out on this evening for some clothes, and left Dawson in the room - my gown was there, with her net and lace - in about five minutes I missed my gown, and the lace was gone.
   JOHN SYMONDS. I am a pawnbroker. I have a gown, pawned on the 22d of August, by Mary Ann Carroll, to the best of my knowledge, in the name of Mary Carroll.
   TIMOTHY RYAN. I am a watchman. On the 22d of August the prosecutrix took me to her house to take the two prisoners - I found no property or duplicates on them- this nett was sent to Marlborough-street, by a woman - the prosecutrix said if they would send it back she would forgive them.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Third Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1649.  LOUISA DOVER and SUSAN HAMMOND were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , 5 silk handkerchiefs, value 16s., the goods of John Graham.
   EDWARD BOYLE. I am shopman to Mr. Graham, linen-draper, of High Holborn. On the 22d of July the prisoners came together to the shop, and asked to look at some silk handkerchiefs which were in the window - I was in the shop, and they looked at them a long time before they attempted to purchase; a fellow-shopman was serving them; I saw Dover put one piece under her shawl; they continued to look at them a little longer - when I saw them going out I took hold of Dover, and said, "Are you not ashamed of yourself?" she then took it from under her shawl, and said, "If you will let me go, I will buy it of you;" Hammond might not have seen what was going on.
   SAMUEL FURZMAN. I am watch-house keeper - the prisoners were brought to me.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 DOVER's Defence. I called on this young woman to go and buy a handkerchief; I took one piece and went to the door to see what pattern they were, and this young man took me by the arm, and sent for the street-keeper.
 DOVER -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 HAMMOND -    NOT GUILTY.
 1650.  SARAH GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , 1 gown, value 5s., the goods of Elizabeth Harvey, spinster, now the wife of  George Garskin.
 ELIZABETH GARSKIN. I am now the wife of George Garskin. I lost a gown on the 6th of August; at that time my name was Harvey, and I was living with Mr. Richmond,  Ray-street, and lost this gown from there; I know nothing of the prisoner.
   MARY RICHMOND. I keep a cook-shop in Ray-street. I saw Garskin's gown hanging on the bedstead on the 6th of August - it was found at the pawnbrokers on the 7th; my shop door was open - there is a communication to the upper rooms, through the parlour.
   JOHN BOWYER. I am a constable; at a quarter before eight o'clock, on the 17th of August, I took the prisoner - she denied the charge.
   THOMAS CARPENTER. I am servant to Mr. Cross, of Turner-street, pawnbroker. I have a gown pawned by the prisoner, on Saturday, the 5th of August, in the name of Flower.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was on Clerkenwell-green, and met a woman who said she was in great distress, and wanted to sell a gown - she said she would let me have it for 4s.; I said she had better pawn it - she said she would rather sell it; I bought it of her, and then, to regain my money, pawned it - in a few days afterwards I went to pawn my shawl, and was stopped.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1651.  JANE GRAHAM was indicted for stealing, on 28th of August , 1 sheet, value 4s., and 2 night-gowns, value 4s., the goods of Benjamin Hatchard.
   MARY ANN SADLER. I am servant to Mr. Benjamin Hatchard; Mrs. Bulmar washes for them. On Monday, the 28th of August, the prisoner, who is the daughter of Mrs. Bulmar, came for the linen as from her mother, and I delivered the sheet and night gowns to her; on the Tuesday her mother came for the things as usual - I told her I had delivered them to her daughter.
   ELIZABETH BULMAR. I have washed for Mr. Hatchard some time - the prisoner is my daughter; I did not send her on the 28th of August for Mr. Hatchard's washing; she had been a few days from home; I never received the articles, nor sent her for them.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Chapel-street, Westminster. I received, from the prisoner and another young woman, two night-gowns and a sheet, for half-a-crown; the prisoner told me it was her mother's - she gave the name of the person who was with her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Three Months.
