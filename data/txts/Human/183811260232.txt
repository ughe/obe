   Q.  Have you been in the habit of accepting bills and procuring dis counts for each other? A.  I have for him, and he has for me, up to about the year 1831—that ended in 1831—I have been in the habit of giving him blank accommodation acceptances—I think I did so in 1831, before Walter's bank failed at Carmarthen—have not done so since, to the best of my knowledge—I have certainly not done so in the last year, in no part of it—I have not done so in 1836, to the best of my knowledge—I may have done so and forgotten it—I have not such a recollection as ena bles me to say I did not do it—I have been compelled to prosecute the prisoner in consequence of the loss of my sister, and other circumstances—Mr. Biddulph has nothing to do with the prosecution—I do not know of a person named Phillips having brought an action against the pri soner in the Borough Court of Carmarthen—I do not know that he had the prisoner in prison for debt—I gave 20 l . to get a habeas  to bring the pri soner to London—he has been in custody on civil process since August last year—he had been sued in the Borough Court of Carmarthen before I did that—I gave the 20 l . about two months ago, long after I knew of this forgery—all his friends and relations live in Carmarthenshire—I never authorised my attorney to indemnify Phillips against the coats in bringing him to London—I never told bun to bear Phillips harmless in the expense of bringing the prisoner to London, or in his costs—I preferred this hill is October, thirteen months after I found out the offence—an action had been brought against me about the bills in the mean time, and a verdict passed against me—I have preferred two indictments—one it for a 200 l . bill—that is the one on which the verdict was obtained against me, and on which I have obtained a new trial, on payment of costs, since this indictment—the prisoner was examined as a witness on the trial in August last—I heard him swear that I gave him authority to put my name to both bills—the trial was in Carmarthen.
