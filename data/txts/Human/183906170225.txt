   Q.  Had your father communicated to you that he had been buying the gold dust, knowing it to be stolen? A.  No; I had no suspicion at all that he had, till he sent me with the summons to Mr. Moses—I had not the slightest suspicion before that—I never spoke to Moses before, to my knowledge—I knew where he lived before my father sent me there—I did not know who Mrs. Abrahams was before I saw her—I believed he had a daughter.
   COURT . Q.  How did you know, then, it was Mrs. Abrahams at all? A.  I saw her at the office.
   MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Did any body tell you at the office that that was the person? A.  I heard a person say, "That is Mrs. Abrahams," when they were pointed out—that is the only way I know her.
   Q.  You had some gold to take out that morning; did you know where that gold came from? A.  I knew my father had been melting some—I thought he had been melting it—Mrs. Abrahams had been melting—I thought they had been melting—I was at Boulogne last year—I have not been there since—I did not remain in the Strand till the house was dis posed of—I have been away—I absconded at the time my father was taken—I went out of the way—I took a lodging in the Haymarket, at No. 32—my brother Selim, and Dean, were with me, nobody else—we re mained there secreted—Mr. Abrahams, the attorney, came to us there—he only came to take our statements—I do not know that that was when my father was to be made a witness—I did not say the statements were taken in Coventry-street—he brought us the depositions in Coventry-street—he came to us in the Haymarket, and took our statements to give to the at torney for the prosecution—mine, my brother's, and Dean's.
   Q.  Did you believe, when you gave your statement, that you should assist in getting your father out by it? A.  I imagined so—I do not know whether my statement went to the attorney for the prosecution first—Mr. Abrahams did not say it was to be sent there first—it was on his ad vice I went before the Magistrate.
