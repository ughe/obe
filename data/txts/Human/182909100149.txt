 1828.  MICHAEL DUFF was indicted for feloniously assaulting Thomas Turpin , on the 8th of August , at Christchurch, putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 sovereign, 2 half-crowns, and three halfpence, his property.
   THOMAS TURPIN. I am a painter and glazier; I live in Grey Eagle-street, and work for Mr. Hart. On the 8th of August, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, I was in Black Eagle-street; I had been drinking a little, but I was sober - I was leaning my hand upon a dray; I felt myself sick; I am often taken sick and faint in my business - the prisoner and another man came up; one of them struck me in the back, and I felt my waistcoat pockets lift up, and heard my money rattle; I put my hand into my pocket, and ran and called Watch! the prisoner and the other ran away - I lost a sovereign and two half-crowns from my right-hand pocket, and three halfpence from my other pocket; I left my hat behind me, and followed them about one hundred and forty yards; I stated at first it was two hundred yards, but I have measured it since - the prisoner ran down to Grey Eagle-street; he turned two corners, but I was close to him, and never lost sight of him; I took him, held him till the watchman came, and gave charge of him- I then went back, and got my hat; I am quite sure the prisoner is one of them: I am sure I had the money before that; for as we were coming out of a public-house I took the sovereign and two half-crowns, put them in my right-hand pocket, and the three halfpence in the other; that was about half an hour before - I was not knocked down; it was a blow between my shoulders; it was from a fist, I think.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. The two half-crowns and sovereign were in your right-hand pocket, and the three halfpence in the other? A. Yes; I was ten or eleven hundred yards from the public-house I had come out of; it was the Blue Coat Boy, in Dorset-street; there are warehouses where I was robbed; it was half-past twelve o'clock; I had just come out of the public-house, and was leaning my hand against a dray - I left work at eight o'clock, and went to have a pint of beer at the Seven Stars, in Brick-lane, with two of my shopmates and one of their brothers; we had two pots of beer there, and whether it was one or two quarterns of gin I cannot be certain - I changed a shilling there, and paid for one pot of beer; I put a few halfpence towards the gin - I spent 10 1/2d. altogether; my day's wages was 3s. 6d.; we then went to the Blue Coat Boy, had one pot of beer and one quartern of gin; we went there about half-past ten o'clock, and staid till twelve - we drank no more; I was quite sober; I saw the prisoner searched at the watch-house; I do not know that he stated the exact money he had in his pockets; no sovereign was found upon him; he did tell me to go home for a drunken fellow, and not to follow him, for he was not the man; I was calling Stop thief! because he was going away from me; I do not know any thing about him - I never was in a Court of Justice or before a Magistrate - I go to work at six o'clock in the morning.
 COURT. Q. You say there was a sovereign and two half-crowns in one pocket and three halfpence in the other, and the prisoner ran away, and said, "What do you follow me for? A. Yes.
   JOHN BUDMORE. I am a watchman. On the 8th of August I was on duty in Brick-lane, between twelve and one o'clock; I heard a cry of Watch! and ran as fast as possible; when I got to Black Eagle-street, where the drays stand, I saw a bat lay; I passed that, and went on; the prosecutor had hold of the prisoner, he said, "I give you charge of him, he has robbed me of a sovereign;" he did not then mention any half-crown; but in going along he said he had lost two half-crowns and three half-pence -I took the prisoner back; the prosecutor took up his hat, and we went to the watch-house - I could perceive the prosecutor had been drinking; but he walked, and told his story very sensibly.
 Cross-examined. Q. Would you say he told the truth if he said he was quite sober? A. He had been drinking, but he must have gone very rapidly to the place where he took the prisoner - he seemed capable of knowing what he was about; I think he had taken rather too much - he did not, at that time, say in which pocket he had his different monies; I saw the prisoner pull the money out of his own pocket - there were two half-crowns, and I think 7 1/2d.; but the watchman has nothing to do with that.
   DANIEL GRAY. I saw the prosecutor have in his possession one sovereign, two half-crowns, and some copper, because he changed 1s.
 Cross-examined. Q. What are you? A. A painter and glazier - I was with him at the Seven Stars, and we then went to the Blue Coat Boy: I left him in Brown's-lane, and hade him good night - that was close to his own house; it would not take him above five minutes to go home - he was not sick when I left him; he had not been drinking so as not to know what he was doing.
   MOSES SIMMONS. I was the watch-house-keeper. The prisoner was brought there, and searched in my presence - there were two half-crowns and 7 1/2d. on him; the prosecutor charged him with having robbed him of a sovereign, two half-crowns, and 1 1/2d. - he appeared to tell his story sensibly.
