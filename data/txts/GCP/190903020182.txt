Police-constable ROBERT FRISBY, 11 K. On February 17 I was on
duty in Barking Road sat nine p.m., and, on receiving a message,
went to 13, Peter Street. I there saw prosecutor outside the house
in the street. He was very excited, his hat was off, his clothes all
undone, his pockets hanging out, and his belt undone. He made
statement to me, and I went into the house myself. Prosecutor went
with me to the station. He was very excited, but did not appear to
be drunk. Within about a hundred yards of the station, he became
very dizzy, and I had to hold him to stop him from falling; he
caught hold of the railings and started to vomit. He could not say
anything when he got inside the station. I did not form any opinion
as to what was the cause of his condition. Prosecutor gave me no
description of the prisoners that night, and he did not come to me
the next day. A lad gave me the description of three men he saw
running away from the house. He was not called at the police court.
I saw & small boy at 13, Peter Street, iu de front room upstairs,
and he made a statement to me. At 11.10 p.m, next day I was on
duty in Hermit Road, and saw Atkins, and told him I was going to
take him into custody on suspicion of being concerned with robbing
& man at 13, Peter Street. He replied, "It is very strange. I was
in the house but did not have any of the money." I took him to the
station, searched him, and found two and ninepence on him and s
mouth-organ. He was charged and made no reply.
To Atkins. You did not say to me, "It is very strange, I was there
last night but I did not take any part in the robbery." What you
said was, “It is very strange; I was in the house but I did not have
any of the money." You made no reply when charged. You did not
say, "I know nothing about it."
