2226. THOMAS M'DONNELL was indicted for stealing on the 10th
of July, 1 coat, value 188., the goods of Isaac Joscelyne.
IsAAC JOSCELYNE. I am a tailor, and live in Shoreditch. On the morn-
ing of the 10th of July I put a summer coat at my door- missed it
in about an hour-it was brought back by my shopman, and this now
produced is it.
RoBERT SCALEs. I am a letter-sorter in the Post-office. I was near
the prosecutor's door that morning-I saw the prisoner and another lad
together-they went to Mr. Joscelyne's shop, and sat down a little while-
the prisoner then changed his apron with the other-the other then took
the coat off the horse, and the prisoner ran away with it.
Prisoner I was at work at Woolwich that day, seven miles from
here.
GUILTY. Aged 8.-Transported for Seven Years.
2227. MARTHA CORBETT was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of
September, 1840, 10 printed books, value 21. 9 blankets, value 21. 58.;
2 pairs of pillow-cases, value 68. 1 table-cloth, value 5s. 5 sheets,
value 2l. 10s. 1 carpet, value 3s. 2 handkerchiefs, value 4s. ; 1 sheet,
value 128. 2 towels, value 1s. 6d. 2 pillows, value 108. ; 2 quilts, value
108. ; 1 pair of boots, value 9s. ; 1 carpet-cover, value 10s. ; and 4 dishes,
value 18s. the goods of William Thurnell, her master.
MARY THURNELL. I am the wife of William Thurnell, of Regent
place, City-road. The prisoner was my servant-I searched her boxes,
and found nothing-] then searched her person, and found four duplicates
of a blanket, a bolster, a pillow, pawned at Mr. Somers's-they are
mine-my husband afterwards found about thirty-eight more duplicates in
the kitchen.
WILLIAM HUMPHREYS. I live at Mr. Somers's, a pawnbroker. I have
a bolster, pillow, and two blankets, pawned by the prisoner.
Prisoner. Mary Ann, their niece, asked me to pawn these things-
did not refuse it, but I gave all the money I received for them to Mary
Ann, and she gave it to her sister, who was discharged. On Satur-
day night, the 14th, my mistress accused me of taking these things,
and turned me out of doors at twelve o'clock at night-I told her her
niece gave me them, but she would not hear what I had to say, because
they were in my name. She owes me a quarter's wages, which is
21. 1s.
Mrs. THURNELL re-examined. I have a niece, named Mary Ann
Smith-she positively denies every thing of the kind-she was before the
Magistrate, but her evidence was not wanted-she is nineteen years old,
and has a good character-she has a sister, who is married.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Confined One Year.
(There was another indictment against the prisoner.)
