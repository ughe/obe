Prisoner. Q. When the 16l. was paid to Mrs. Fifield, did not you say you would give the balance, 5l. 13s. 9d. in wine, equal to that at 40s. a dozen from any winemerchant in London? A. No; I said the gentleman proposed to give two dozens at 40s.; I have no doubt it was worth that - I undertook that it should he; I have no doubt it was so from the respectability of my client - Mrs. Fifield endorsed the bill in my drawing-room; the bill is not in my possession; I do not know where it is.
 MRS. FIFIELD. The prisoner introduced me to Archibald - his brother is a baker, and had lived in Temple-street; I owed him 17s. 6d. and he called for it - the prisoner was with him, and this is the way I first knew him; he offered to sell me whiskey and different spirits; I owed a quarter's rent, and had a man in possession - the prisoner said if I had a good bill he could get it discounted, and introduced me to Archibald.
 Prisoner, Q. Did you endorse the bill in Archibald's presence? A. I endorsed it, but not in his presence - I did it at my brother's, in Red Lion-street, Holborn: Archibald was not there - my brother drew the bill by my authority, but I endorsed it; my brother is the acceptor - he was in my debt.
 COURT. Q. Did the prisoner apply to you about the wine? A. Never; I intended to dispose of it, and had a friend who would have taken it.
 The prisoner, in his Defence, entered in'o a long account of his first acquaintance with the prosecutrix, and stated that she had desired him to dispose of the wine, and promised to give him 3l. for his trouble in introducing her to Archibald. That after she had received the money they went to a public-house in the Strand, where he wrote the order in question which the prosecutrix did not sign, (not being able to write.)
   ANN FIFIELD. I swear I never gave him reason to suppose I intended to dispose of the wine, or to give him any thing for getting the bill discounted.
   HENRY SANDERS. Mrs. Fifield is my sister - I kept an eating-house in Red Lion-street. I signed her name as the drawer of the bill, as she was not competent at the moment; I also accepted it as drawn on me; I know she can write - I believe I endorsed the bill, but am not certain; she was to have two dozen of wine if I or White could find a customer - he was to sell it for her; this is what I heard from White afterwards; I never heard from her that she had any idea of either of us disposing of it; when the bill was drawn, I told her I would prevail on some friends to buy some - and I intimated to White that I should be glad if he could dispose of it.
   JOSEPH HURREN. When I took the wine I saw the prisoner; he asked why Mr. Archibald did not send another dozen of wine - I said I was only ordered to bring two; he said, "Tell him I will stand none of his nonsense - if he does not send another dozen he shall feel the consequence of it."
 MRS. FIFIELD. The prisoner never asked me to sign the order; nor did I know he had written it till after the wine was gone.    NOT GUILTY.

 Mr.  James Busby, No. 35, King-street, Goswell-street, London, feloniously did falsely make, forge, and counterfeit upon the said bill of exchange an acceptance thereof, with intent to defraud John Hipkiss; against the Statute, & c.
 SECOND COUNT, for uttering the same with a like intent knowing it to be forged.
 JOHN HIPKISS. I am a  nail-manufacturer, and live at Dudley, in Worcestershire. The prisoner was a pretended agent, to sell goods for people - I have known him several years, and have had dealings with him in business; a little more than two years ago, he applied to me to sell nails for me; he did so, and went on for about twelve months, and he got the account in such a confused state, that I came up to London myself, about November, 1827- he could not give any satisfactory account of what he had been doing, and I was not satisfied; I appointed another agent, Mr. William Harris, the prisoner after that had goods regularly from Harris; I was in the country, but on some complaint from Mr. Harris, I came to town in January last; the prisoner came to my inn several times, but nothing particular passed relative to this bill of 20l. - he promised to arrange his account; I do not recollect his saying any thing to me about this bill - there were several other bills.
