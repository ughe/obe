 605.  JOHN ATTERBURY was indicted for embezzlement.
   ROBERT COX. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Tothill-street, Westminster. The prisoner came into my employ on the 9th of December, as a porter, and to serve behind the counter occasionally - he was employed to receive monies for me; he had conducted himself with property.
   GEORGE DENHAM. I am in the prosecutor's service. On the 12th of February I saw the prisoner take a half-crown from a customer; he stooped down, and put it into his boot - I was in the shop to watch him; I had seen him do this twice in the course of the evening - I think the customer bought 2 lbs. of one shilling butter; he gave 6d. out of the till - his boot was examined; three half-half-crowns were found on him there, and one at the watch-house - he said he did not know he had any money there.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Where were you? A. At the head of the counter - I do not remember what customer it was; I can swear they bought butter, and I believe it was about 2 lbs.; he only said he did not know there was any thing in his boot - he did not say that if there was it must have got through a hole in his trousers - he said so after his examination - I did not observe whether his trousers came over his boots, but they were laced boots, and came up three or four inches.
   DANIEL REARDON. On the night stated Mr. Cox called me in: I went into the parlour behind the shop, and found the prisoner - I searched him, and found three half-crowns in a pocket on the side of his waistcoat - he said, "They are my own; I did not take them, Mr. Cox;" I took him to the office, and in the back room I said to him, "Have you any more about you?" he said No; I said, "Pull off your boots" - he pulled off one very gently, and said, "You see there is nothing there;" I took it, and found another half-crown in the toe of it - he then said they had fallen from the till.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Three Months.
 606.  JAMES WILLIAM HAMPTON was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 3 Venetian blinds, value 10s., the goods of William Smith.
   WILLIAM JAMES WILSON. I am a blind-maker, in the employ of Mr. William Smith. On the 15th of February I saw the prisoner going off his steps with these blinds; he went a few paces, and was delivering them to another person - I went out, and took him; the other ran off.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I am a blind-maker. These are my blinds.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Three Months.
 607.  MARY HALEY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of January , 1 bonnet, value 15s., and 1 cloak, value 15s., the goods of Ann Bradley.
   ANN BRADLEY. I am a servant, at No. 23, Grosvenor-cottages. On the 9th of January I was out of a situation, and lodged in the same room with the prisoner - about six o'clock that evening I lost my bonnet and cloak from the room; the bonnet was found on the prisoner's head; the cloak I have not seen - the prisoner went away without notice, and she was taken on the 22nd of January.
   DANIEL REEDON. I am a labourer. I slept in another bed in the same room - I saw the prisoner go out with the cloak and bonnet on; I laid on the bed, and she went out - I did not know whose they were them.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you know she had lent me them? A. No.
   CHARLES MAYHEW. I am an officer. I took the priprisoner on the 22nd of January, in St. Giles'; I had information that she had stolen these things about a week before - I took her to where she had lodged, and the occupier of the house said it was the prosecutor's bonnet; the prisoner said she was going to return it - I asked what she had done with the cloak, and she gave me no answer.
 ANN BRADLEY. This is my bonnet - it was a dark brown cloth cloak, for which I gave a guinea; I never permitted her to take and wear my things - she was a stranger; I paid her lodging - I do not know what her trade is.
 Prisoner. Yes you know very well I am an unfortunate girl, and have supported you and your child; have you not lent me that shawl you now have on? Witness. Yes, I did one night.
 Prisoner's Defence. She lent me that shawl about six o'clock in the evening, and said she was distressed for clothes - I brought her in some money; she said, "As it is cold take my cloak and bonnet;" I was taken up that night, and had fourteen days in the House of Correction -I was taken by this officer the day I came out.
   CHARLES MAYHEW. She told me the reason she had not returned the bonnet was, because she had been taken into custody.
     NOT GUILTY.
