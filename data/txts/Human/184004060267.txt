CHARLOTTE WHITE. I live in Marsh-street, Walthamstow. About four o'clock on the 6th of April, the prisoner drove up to my door with a pony-cart and pigs—he asked if I wanted to buy a pig—I said I did—I went to the cart to look, and the one he put out he asked 25 s . for—I gave him 23 s .—I had seen him round with Regan's cart before—he said he was selling them for Regan, who was ill at the public-house.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Has Regan seen the one you bought? A.  Yes. ROBERT BURROWS. I bought a pig of the prisoner about half-past three o'clock—he had three at that time he asked me 30 s . for it—I offered him 29 s .—I gave him 15 s . deposit—I asked where Regan was—he said he was ill at the public-house.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where is the pig now? A.  At my house—I told him he should have the remainder of my money to-day, and if Regan did not like it, he might come and take the pig back—he had part of file pots of beer at ray house—he was net drunk. JAMES M'CULLOCH. I am a policeman—in consequence of information I followed the prisoner to Tottenham, and took him at Barnes, which is about twenty miles from Walthanstow—I found the horse and cart about three-quarters of a mile from where I found the prisoner—them was another man in his company who came from Wathamstow—they were both together waiting in a field where a booth was erecting for a fair—they appeared cooking a dinner—the last pig was sold there—the prisoner told me he had sold it for 18 s .—I took him in charge and found a sovereign and a half in gold and four pence in silver on him—he said he had had a pleasant time of it and seemed to enjoy himself very well—he said he did not mind coming back as soon as he had his last dinner; that he had sold the last pig, and that was all the money he had.
   Cross-examined. Q.  He was going to return the horse and cart when he had dined? A.  Yes—there was a dinner cooking, and, I believe, for him—he seemed half drunk. JOHN HUNT. I am a policeman—I saw the prisoner in custody—he said, in going along, "If you had not come down we should have sold the horse and cart," meaning himself and the other man who was with him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not the other man in the cart? A.  Yes—he was not drunk—at the time he made use of this expression, we were on our road home to Walthamstow. JOHN REGAN re-examined.  The pig he sold to Charlotte White was one I valued at 28 s .—he would have to go past Burrows to go to the lady's—he was quite solid;  if he had been drunk I would not have sent him—he was only to show the pigs to the lady—I told him the price, as he asked me what they were if she should ask him—he was not to sell them, only to see if they were the right size for the lady—she would not have bought of him—he was not trusted to sell them for me and receive the money—I would not have trusted him with the money—he was to bring the pigs back to me in a quarter of an hour, whether she liked them or not—I only mentioned the price as he asked me.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Suppose he had said the lady meant to keep two of them; would you not have been satisfied? A.  I should have gone back to the lady to get the money—he was not to leave them—I did not send him to take the money—I nave never sent pigs by another person and received the money myself.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  of stealing the Pigs only. Aged 30.— Confined Six Months.
