1996. WILLIAM HART was indicted for stealing 5 bushels of oats and
chaff mixed, value 78.6d. ; the goods of Edward Maxwell, his master,
EDWARD MAXWELL. I am a farmer, and live at Enfield-the prisoner was
my father's carter. On the 25th of Sept. he left my premises with a load of
potatoes early in the morning-he had the care of my borses that day, and had
more than his allowance of corn and chaff-I saw it on the wagon in a bran-sack
- I did not allow him to take more than his allowance-I had told him not
to take more-he had no business with any corn but that in his nose-bags,
but he had about seven bushels and a half in a sack-it was my father,
William Maxwell's property-I live with him-I had not seen the prisoner
leave the yard.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doang. Q. It was the prisoner's business to start
very early in the morning? 4. Yes I heard that he had been with his
wife to a fair the night before, and had been carousing there he was not to
have mixed corn, in a sack on the wagon, besides what was in the corn-bag
-I did not measure the corn the police brought me I could tell the quantity
by the sacks--I saw the corn given him about five o'clock the evening be-
fore, by Ogleby.
HENRY Hase (police-constable N 342.) On the 25th of Sept., between
two and three o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner near the turnpike-
gate at Edmonton, with a wagon and horses -I noticed a sack in front of the
wagon-I stopped bim, and asked what it contained-he said, "Horses-bait".
-I said, "I believe you carry your bait in nose-bags ?”—be said he had
nose-bags full, but that was a little he had over--I asked if any one gave it
him-he said no, he took it himself - I went to the prosecutor's and
examined the sack-the prisoner was present-Maxwell said he had no
right to take it the prisoner said nothing to that there were six bushels
in the sack.
EDWARD MAXWELI re-examined. He had four horses, and ought to have
had the four nose-bags full, and none at all in a sack-the horses bave a
good feed before they go out.
Not Guilty.
