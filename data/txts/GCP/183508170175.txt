WILLIAM WHITE RICE was indicted for feloniously receiving the
come well knowing it to have been stolen, against the statute.
RDWARD RUSSELL GREAVES. I live in Goswell-street-road. On the
oth of August, I went to the Metropolitan baths at seven o'clock in the
morning I saw the prisoner Rice there-I paid him, and then I un-
dressed and bathed --when I went away, I missed my watch-I went
hack, and saw Peck-I told him I had lost my watch-he advised me to
Ink round the baths for it-which we did, but could not find it-we then
went into the next room, where Rice was-he came and looked, but could
not find it-Peck then asked my address, and the number of my watch,
which I gave him he said a number of valuable things were left there,
and they kept a book to enter them in-I said if they delivered me my
watch. I would give 21.--I went home, and returned in about an hour
I asked Rice if he had found it-he said he did not know, he would en-
quire-he opened a door, and called Peck-who said they had not found
I repeated the reward, and left a friend advised me to take a police-
man, which I did at five in the evening-I then saw Rice L I told him I
was the person who had lost the watch in the morning-he said he knew
I was the policeman then asked if Mr. Peck was in-he said he was not,
he was gone to the City-the policeman then said he should take him into
custody-he said he need not do that, for he had the watch, and produced
it from a drawer.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Did you not make this entry of
your name and address, and the number of your watch at Mr. Rice's
instance ! A. Yes; I believe I learned that it was customary to place
in a drawer in that room, such goods as were found-I believe he said
in the evening, " You need be under no alarm about you watch, it has
been produced to me, and here it is."
Cross-examined by Me. Doane. Q. Did he not say, “Here is your
watch, it has been brought by Peck ?" A. I believe he did there is
a notice put up, that goods left there shall be taken care of.

1878. ROBERT BRUCE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
August, 1 coat, value 31., the property of William Dodds.
WILLIAM Dodds. I live at West-ham. On the 5th of August I went
in my chaise to Highbury-park and I left my chaise, with my coat in it, in
care of a boy-I received information, and came out-I missed my coat-
I afterwards saw it-here it is.
Thomas BOARD. I live in Pocock's-fields. I saw the prisoner pass me
with this coat-I heard a cry of “Stop thief!"-he threw the coat down,
and I took it up.
WILLIAM PEACOCK. I heard the cry of “Stop thief!" and saw the
prisoner running with this coat on his arm, about three o'clock.
l'risoner. It was thrown on my shoulder by a boy-I heard a cry of
"Stop thief!" and threw it down.
(Thomas Bright, of Leonard-street, gave the prisoner a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 16.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Months.
