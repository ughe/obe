FREDERICK JOHN JOHNSON. I am a constable of Covent-garden market
On the 2nd of Oct. I saw Weaver in Bow-street, with a sieve on his head-1
could not see whether it was full or empty-he was walking in the direction
to Duke's-court-I saw Baxter, and made a communication to him-after he
bad watched he made a communication to me-I took Green into custody
and asked if he authorized any one to take any damsons from his master's
stand he said “No" I asked if he were sure of it-he said "Yes"
asked if he authorized Slaymaker to take any-he said "No"-I asked if he
authorized any one else-he said "No"-I took him to the station- I took
Weaver the same day, and asked where he took that sieve to, which I saw on his
head-he said, "To Green's house"- asked by whose authority-he said.
"By Green's."
GABRIEL HARDS. I am a fruit-salesman, and have a stand in Covent-
garden market-I live in Basing-place, Lambeth. Green was in my employ
about four years-he never sold, or had authority to sell for me, without my
daughter was present-I know Weaver-I had made no sale to him of any
damsons on the 2nd of Oct., and he had no authority from me to remove any
-when I left the market there were upwards of forty if not fifty baskets of
damsons-some damsons were pointed out to me by the officer as taken by
a man named Slaymaker-the others were not found.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. I believe Green was a tenant of
yours? A. Yes, he occupied a room of mine in this house in Duke's-court
-the other rooms are let out to a number of persons--I had no knowledge
of where Weaver lived till after the damsons were taken there-he has
greengrocer's shop at No. 14-I searched that house about an hour after.
(The prisoners received good characters.)
WEAVER-Guilty. Aged 53.-Confined Six Months.
