It was taken as "I vote for Calcutt" I have heard since, but it was never so intended. I did not move the resolution on July 18. I voted on February 14, 1907, for the granolithic pathway. Draper moved as an amendment that the work be done by the Guardians' own labour. I voted against that, and moved that Calcutt's offer be accepted for £38 10s. for painting the Isolation Hospital. I supported Calcutt because I thought it a very fair price. On the same day I voted for Calcutt's tender for repairs to 64, Tredegar Square of £68 15s. as against Newton and Brown. Up to 1903 Calcutt had done the work with no scale of prices. Knight had certified what he thought right. Then it was ordered that there should be a scale of prices tendered for. I never saw the schedule. I do not think tenders were invited for painting the Infirmary. It got the sanction of the Local Government Board. I never went into the Infirmary while that work was being done. At the finish it went into £2, 000 or £3, 000. Haseltine is not a particular friend of mine. I have known him for twenty-five or thirty years. With regard to Knight's statement that I told him Mr. Robb would not have much difficulty in proving that money had passed between "these people" (some of the Guardians) and Calcutt, I deny that I said anything of that sort. I can't suggest a reason for Knight inventing a story of that kind. It never came out of my mouth. I never heard any suggestion that the Guardians were benefiting by contracts until the Inquiry. I remember, on February 6, 1906, entertaining Ridpath and Calcutt at the "Ship and Turtle." There was no discussion about Guardians. I asked them if they would join me in a little lunch. We had an ordinary lunch and I paid for it. I have done that frequently to Ridpath and Calcutt and others. I can only sug  gest as a reason for Calcutt saying what he has about me that he asked me to stand bail for him and I refused. Up to that time he and I were on friendly terms. That was before he pleaded guilty. I could not have thought he was guilty. I refused to stand bail on account of my position as Mayor of Stepney. I supported Haseltine's contracts for supplying vegetables when I found him reasonsble. I don't think I ever voted against him. I did not know till recently that he was an undischarged bankrupt. I do not recollect Gilson having a contract for meat for the Scattered Homes. The bread contract was given to Maycroft. I have heard about Marble since the inquiry. I understood that Marble supplied Maycroft and Maycroft supplied it to the Workhouse. Marble was a member of the Board.
 Re-examined. The first contract placed with Barker, junior, was for something like £8, 000. I did not obtain the value of a pin's head through it going to him. If the contract for the Nurses' Home had gone through it would not have been any advantage to me.
