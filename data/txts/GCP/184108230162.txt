2214. WILLIAM HALL was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of Ja.
nuary, 1 sovereign, 1 half-crown, and 4 shillings; the monies of James
Beamont, his master.
John BEAMONT. I am a baker, and live in Sophia-street, Poplar. On
the 2nd of January the prisoner was in my service-I gave him a sove-
reign, a half-crown, and four shillings, to go to Ratcliff Mill for half a sack
of flour-I usually have my flour from there, I had sent him there once
before-he did not return, but was taken into custody six months after-
wards.
GEORGE Sweet. I know the prisoner-I have seen him several times
at Ratcliff Mill-at this distance of time I cannot charge my memory whe-
ther he came on the 2nd of January or not, I believe he did not, I have
no entry of his coming, and I enter all the goods I deliver-I delivered
several parcels of flour that day on account of Mr. Beamont, but I have no
recollection of seeing the prisoner.
WILLIAM SLADDEN. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner
on the 17th of August-I said, "Do you know what I want you for?"-
he said, “Oh yes, I have been very uneasy in my mind for the last
week; I have been expecting it"-as I brought him to the office, he said
he had been sliding, and lost the money, and was afraid to go back to his
master, and that he left the barrow at the mill.
Prisoner's Defence. I did not like to go back, because I had lost the
money.
MR. BEAmont re-examined. I had no character with him—he was with
me about twelve months-he once stopped away with a little money I gave
him to go to town with, but I forgave him that, and he worked it out-the
barrow was found at the mill.
NOT GUILTY.

21st of August, 9 yards of printed cotton, value 4s. 9d., the goods of
William James Stevenson.
JOHN GIBSON BROWN. I am apprentice to William James Stevenson,
a linen-draper, in Ratcliff-highway. On Saturday, the 21st of August, I
was serving a customer, and saw the prisoner come into the shop, go to
the counter, and look at a shawl which laid on it-she took it up- I saw
her pull up her dress, and put the shawl so as to screen herself-I
called Mr. Brown forwards, he came forward-she asked the price of a
shawl, which was 28.-she bid 18. 6d., and he went to the other end of
the shop, and she went out-I saw she walked with difficulty, and called
Brown again-she was fetched back, and as she came into the shop she
made a shuffle with her legs-Brown said, "Now I will search you, and
see what you have got"-he moved her away, and there was the dress
underneath-she had left the shawl on the counter before she went out-
I am certain the dress was not on the floor till she came back.
