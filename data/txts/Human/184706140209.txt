I am a labourer in the service of the contractor for the Southwark and Vauxball Water Company. On Saturday afternoon, the 15th of May, between three and four o'clock, I saw a van coming along Bowyer-lane with a nine inch branch and a five inch outlet, iron water pipes, in it—I told the driver there was not room to go by—I was shooting a load of rub bish—I saw the van coming, and took my horse's head, and I noticed these pipes in the van—Haines was sitting behind the van—as soon as he saw me he put his hand before his eyes—I had known him by our working at the same farm—there were four persons in the van—I did not take notice of the others—the van was coming form Camberwell New-road—I knew there had been a quantity of water pipes lying in that road—I did not go to where that pipe had been—I gave information to Dooley—the pipes I saw in the van were of the same description as those I saw lying in the Camberwell New-road—I am pretty well sure Cockling was the man driving the van.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How many person were there in the van? A.  Four—I am certain there were four—I did not speak to either of them—it never struck me to do so till the van went by so quick—I was not exactly acquainted with Haines, only by working with him—I did not know either of the others—I have had no quarrel with Haines—he has worked in the same employ as I have—I have put down the pipe, and he has paved the boxes afterwards—he is paviour—that is three of four months ago I suppose—he was getting his living by hard work at the time I knew him—I do not know how it was I did not speak to him on this occasion—I did not notice him till the van had gone by—there were four in the van, and Haines was in the hind part.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Are there any one of the others that you know to be there beside Haines? A.  I am pretty sure Cockling was there, but I am not right down sure—the way the van was going, would have led to Bankside. JOSEPH DONOVAN. I am in the employ of Mr. Plimbey, of Bankside. On that Saturday afternoon a van came, and brought a quantity of iron pipe—Mr. Chisholm came three or four hours afterwards, and claimed some of the pipe that was brought in that van—Haines brought the pipe—he gave me the name of Smith—I bought it of him at 3 s . a cwt.—I did not see any of the other prisoners there—he came to me by himself—he and one of our labour ers got the iron out of the van—Haines stated it was his own iron—I know a great many persons who come, but Haines was quite a stranger. ROBERT BRANFORD( police-sergeant M  12) I took Haines on Saturday night, the 15th of May—I told him it was for stealing a quantity of iron from the Camberwell New-road—he said he was in the van that day, but he had nothing to do with the iron—I took Cockling on the 17th of May—he said he had been employed by two men to carry some iron from the Camber well New-road to Mr. Plimbey's.
   Cockling's Defence.  On that Saturday morning, at half-past seven o'clock, I went to a house in Russell-street, Bermondsey, and moved some goods to Bankside; when I had half unloaded, a man came and said, was I engaged? I said, there was my master; he said he wanted some iron moved form Cam berwell New-road, and some men would meet me in Union-street; I went; two men jumped up, and took me to Camberwell New-road, they said that was the iron; I was employed to carry it; my master authorized me to carry it.
