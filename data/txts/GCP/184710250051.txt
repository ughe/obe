HENRY BACON PAUL. I am an accountant, and live at No. 26, Rodney.
street, Pentonville. I have known the prisoner for twenty-two years—I
have had opportunities of seeing him very generally during the last four years
-I bave latterly noticed a decided difference in his conduct and manner, an
excitement brought on by his troubles-I never noticed conduct equal to that
previously-it has displayed itself in various ways-latterly he could not
have cast up three lines of figures--before that be was a moderately good
sccountant, not first-rate-he was able to cast up his books and balance them
- he was not able to do that latterly-a lady, a friend of bis, committed
suicide this time twelvemonths-he has frequently alluded to that lately-it
appeared to me to prey upon his mind very much-he has made very frequent
allusions to it-I have frequently upon such occasions heard him give orders
to his men, sometimes quite the reverse of reasonable that has been latterly
-he would arrange a thing overnight, and next morning not enter upon it, or
entertain it, that was quite at variance with his former conduct-he has latterly
spoken to me of apprehensions of a conspiracy-his pecuniary losses since
1844 have been about 88001., and since May last about 11001; three actions
at law, one pending now, which will not realize a penny-he very recently
gave a person named Johns, of Newman-street, credit to a great extent for
goods-sufficient inquiries were not made as to the stability of that person-
and other friends urged him not to go on with it, till he had made inquiry,
but he would not-knowing his habits of business, I certainly think he
would not have given eredit upon a former occasion--he would have
been more cautious--some person who owed him money pleaded the Statute
of Limitations, upon which Mr. Ovenstone engaged himself in writing large
placards--I did not see where he stuck them-I heard him threaten what he
would do-be has been for many years living with two unmarried sisters
one of those sisters has recently married--that came suddenly upon him-he
felt that he was taken by surprise, and he said, in a desponding state, that all
the world was going to leave him and abandon him-he was very desponding-
bis sister had formerly taken care of the household, and he regretted her going
away from him-this is one of the sort of placards he wrote-(looking at one
