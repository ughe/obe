259. WILLIAM APPLETON, EDWARD CARRINGTON, and JAMES
NICHOLLS were indicted for feloniously assaulting Robert Roger Phillips, on
the 8th of Nov., putting him in fear, and taking from his person and against his
will, 1 watch, value 30s., and 1 chain, 5s., his property; and immediately be
fore, at the time of, and after the said robbery, feloniously beating, striking,
and using other personal violence towards him.
ROBERT ROGER PHILLIPS. I am a tailor, and live in Margaret-terrace,
Westminster-about one o'clock on the morning of the 8th of Nov. I was
returning from my sister-in-law's in William-place, William-street, Pitt-street,
Old Kent-road, with my wife, my sister-in-law, and cousin Archibald Penny-
cook-I saw a mob at the end of William-street fighting-we had to pass them,
and could not do so without touching them-Penny cook was walking a-head
of me, and was struck on the hat, I believe by Carrington, but I am not posi.
tive-I came up within a foot of him-Appleton asked me what I wanted-I
said I wanted to pass – I received a blow from him on the left side of the
chest and fell— I rose again, and he struck me, and at the same time caught
my watch-guard, which was hanging from my waistcoat-pocket, and ran
away with it, watch and all-as I was pursuing him, I believe Carrington gave
me a blow or a shove, and I went up against the wall-I did not fall down,
but ran again, and was surrounded by the mob—I called “Police !” and
" Stop thief I" as hard as I could—the policeman came-I told him I had
been robbed of my watch and guard- I could not see Appleton at that time,
but I saw Carrington, who was one of the party that shoved me as I was pur-
suing Appleton, and I gave him in charge.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHARNOCK. Q. I suppose it was rather dark !
4. There was a gaslight at the corner-I had come from St. Margaret's-ter-
race to my sister-in-law's between eight and nine o'clock- I had no supper
there--I had been drinking nothing to intoxicate me-I had had a drop of
ale at my sister's I cannot exactly say what I had drank-I bad no
spirits or beer the whole day-I was perfectly sober-I will not swear it was
Carrington that struck Pennycook--he shoved against me as I was running
from the corner of William's place to the Kent-road-Carrington was running
--there were about twenty people there-I should not say there were fifty.
Cross-examined by MR. CROUCH. Q. What sort of a night was this!
