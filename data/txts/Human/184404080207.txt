   Q.  You know the situation of the spot in the canal, could she by any means have fallen into it? A.  No—to get there a person must walk a dis tance round a fence—they must first enter into a private yard, and go the distance of fifty yards down the yard, then go down a pair of steps, and walk along a twenty feet plank, to get to the water in that spot where I saw the prisoner—she did not tell me how she got into the water—it was Very dark.
   COURT . Q.  Could she not have got in on the other side of the canal? A.  I should suppose from the situation she was in she could not—the canal is about sixty feet wide, and she was fourteen or sixteen feet from the wharf on the side I was—there is an upright wall there, and the water is about three feet and a half deep close to the side of the wall—the middle of the canal is the deepest, and that was about thirty feet from where she was—she could not have come there from the other side without coming through the deepest part, which is about five feet six inches—she must have been off her feet there, and she must have been off her feet where I saw her as it was about five feet deep.
 ----  HENRY COTTON ( policc-constable K  361.) On Sunday night, the 24th of March, I went to Mr. Gardner's house—I saw the prisoner there, and went to the station with her—she said the cause of her jumping into the water, was the ill usage.
   COURT . Q.  Did you ask her any thing? A.  No—she said she had some thing very heavy on her mind, and I cautioned her that what she said I should give in evidence in Court against her.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Then do not tell us any thing she said after you told her that; did she say any thing before you told her that? A.  Yes—she said she had jumped into the water through distress and ill-usage at the Union—Harrison saw her after that. GEORGE HARRISON( police-constable K  15.) I saw the prisoner at the station at Stratford—Cotton had brought her to the station—I did not hear him say any thing to her—I sat her by the fire.
   COURT . Q.  Did she appear sober? A.  I should say she was—she did not appear to have been drinking. SOPHIA MONTGOMERY. I was an inmate of the workhouse on the day the prisoner left, and am there now. During the time the prisoner was in the workhouse she had the care of this child George by night and by day—the child was in good health, all but a little breaking out on its face—it was well attended to—she did not in general treat the child well—it was dirty, and she used to beat it. SOPHIA DOROTHY ROSE. I used to work for the nurse in the Infirmary at Bethnal-green workhonse. I saw the prisoner in the morning of the day she left—she had the care of the child that is dead—the child was supplied with every thing there that it could want, as far as the house allowed—the child seemed in general good health—I never heard the prisoner complain of the usage of the child, or of anything—I know that when she left the workhouse she had 14 s . besides what she had from the guardians—I saw her go before the guardians—she was not turned out—she left of her own accord.
