WILLIAM LUCAS PEARCE. I am high-constable of Dartford. I saw the
four prisoners in the course of the day in question-when the coach broke
down, I was sent for-the prosecutor was hurt, and I suppose 150 people
were about--I observed the two female prisoners among the crowd,
very busy among the sailors- there were five or six sailors among the
passengers-Weyman and Hawkins have lived at Dartford together, as
man and wife, since November last-I occasionally see Haydon, and on
the day the coach was overturned they were all four together-Gell was
with one of the sailors, and as she came by, I Treard her say to Hawkins,
as it appeared to me, that one of the sailors had got plenty of sovereigns,
but it was tied so tight in, that she could not get at it, but she would
have it before morning about five o'clock next morning Mr. Dunn
called me up, and told me of his loss, and I went to Hawkins's and
Weyman's lodging--they occupied that room-it is a detached part of
the premises of the Granby public-house, let off in separate tenancies to labour-
ing people--the other two prisoners lodged on the opposite side of the yard.
Haydon. You did not see me that day, for I had been very ill, and
came from Gravesend ; and when I came home, I went to bed at
Mrs. Brown's-you never saw me before in your life. Witness. Yes, I
did--Mrs. Brown's husband keeps the Granby, which is opposite the
church.
