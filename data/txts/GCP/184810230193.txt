WILLIAM WOODWARD (policeman, B 157). On Sunday, 17th Sept., about
eight o'clock in the evening, I went to Lindfield's house, 24, Prior-place,
Bost-street-I was in plain clothes-I saw a young man there, named Wil-
liam Lindfield-I asked to see Mrs. Lindfield in private, and he took me
into a parlour adjoining the shop-I said I came from a young woman, Eliza
Wilson, living at Norwood-she asked how she was I said she was very
bad, she was in a feverish state--she asked whether she bad taken the medis
cine she had sent by her brother, and put her feet in warm water-I said I
did not exactly know-I asked her who brought the young woman to her
house, and she said a person living in York-street, who sold oysters-I
asked her if her name was Dryden-she said she did not know, she had not
seen her above twice-she said the person that brought her had given her
some pills and a bottle of savin water, which had caused a stoppage in the
bowels and the water-I told her I would call again in the morning, and let
her know how the young woman was I then went away-I called again the
next morning, about ten, I was shown into the parlour-she asked me how
the young woman was said, "She is very bad; you are not aware who I
am?"-she said, "No"-I said, “ I am a police-constable; consider your-
self in charge for using a certain instrument on Eliza Wilson, and procuring
abortion, and thereby endangering her life"- she then said, " The only
instrument I used on her was to draw off the water, and I can't recollect
whether it was three or four times," she produced this instrument (produced)
-I then asked her if she had received any money from the young woman
she said she had had 50s.; 88. 6d. she had given the woman that brought
her, 2s. she charged for medicine, and she had returned Eliza Wilson two
sovereigns--this letter was produced by her to the Magistrate, and it has
been in my possession ever since (produced).
James WILSON re-examined. This is the letter I carried from my sister
to Mrs. Lindfield-(read"Norwood, Sept. 14th, 1848.-Mrs. Lindfield,
I have sent my brother to inform you I am so bad, I cannot since Monday
night move any of my limbs. I have been unwell a little, but nothing else
whatever. Pray do not let them into the secret yet; they are very angry
because I will not have a doctor. I feel so bad, I think I shall not live,
pray send me word what I had better do. I am so very mueb swelled no-
thing goes through me. I have taken two doses of castor oil, bave the
goodness to send me word what had better be done. I have got such &
dreadful pain in my left side : my dear Mrs. Lindfield, do send me some ease.
E. Wilson.")
