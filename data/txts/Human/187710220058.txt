786. JOHN GRANT  (30) , Unlawfully conspiring with one John Draper , not in custody, to rob Robert Johnson of divers of his moneys.
   MR. BRINDLEY conducted the Prosecution; and MR. WARNER SLEIGH the Defence. ROBERT JOHNSON. I live at Lugar, in Ayrshire, and am a surgeon in practice there—on the 16th August I came up to London for a holiday, and on the 24th I went down to Croydon to see the races—when I went on the course Grant, turning to me, asked if I knew the names of the horses walking about—I told him I did not, and immediately after wards I turned to go away—he came after me and said "I think you are a Scotchman; I am a Scotchman too"—he said "I am here just now for holidays"—he asked me first if I was, and I said "Yes"—he told me his name was Grant—that he belonged to Paisley, his father was a shawl manufacturer there, and that he had a brother in the firm—soon after Draper came up, and Grant turned to me and said "Here is a friend of mine coming who does some betting for me," and he told me he was brother to the great jockey, Cannon—he introduced him to me as "Cannon," and they spoke aside to each other for a minute or two—he turned to go away, and when he had gone eight or ten yards, Grant came after mo and said to him, "You might see what you can do for my friend here"—he turned round and held up his hand and said "All right"—I went on—I turned round to Grant and said "I didn't come here to bet; I am not going to bet at all, I know nothing about it"—a short time afterwards Draper came back, put a ticket for a bet made in the hand of Grant, and handed one to me—I said I didn't authorise him to make any bets for me and refused to take the ticket at first—Draper said "Oh, I understood I was to make a bet for you"—I objected again, and Grant said "Oh, it is  all right"—Draper said, when I objected to pay the money, "Oh, but I have paid the money for you"—I think it was 2 l .—that having been represented to me, although I had not authorised him to do so, I gave him the money—Draper then went away—I thought I was in duty bound to pay the money—I took them to be gentlemen—Draper came back—I think it was after a race was run—he said there had been a bet on, and that I had won 6 l . 10 s .—I thought he was going to hand me the money, but he said "I have made another bet for  10 l ."—I objected again, and Grant said "It is all right"—I was asked for more money—I had only a 5 l . note in my pocket at the time, and I was induced to give it him—Draper said I was owing him something for what he had paid for me, I cannot tell how much—the next time he came running back after the next race, and said "Oh, I have lost you 10 l .," but before the race finished he had made another bet for me, whereby I had won 40 l .—he said he had risked 20 l ., but won 40 l .—I said "I am not going to have any more of this"—I told him I wished to get away—when they saw I was anxious to get away one of them said he would go and see if he could find the bookmaker, and let him come out and see if he could make arrangements for me to go away—he repre sented that I had won some money—Draper went over to where the crowd was, and came back and said "The bookmaker
