 1195. DANIEL RANCE and ANN RANCE were again  indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of January , 5 napkins, value 5 s  ., the goods of James Marshall . MARY NEESHAM. These five napkins are my master's—I am not certain whether they were taken from the kitchen drawer or from the scullery table-drawer—they were not missed till the officer found the dupli cates of them—the female prisoner was at my master's as much as her husband. JOHN TAYLOR. I took these two duplicates from the female prisoner, when she took them out of a box—they are for two napkins pawned on the 2nd of January, and three on the 17th of February. RICHARD WILLIAM FAIRLAM. I am a pawnbroker. I have three napkins, pawned on the 17th of February, in the name of Ann Rance—I have no knowledge of the person, but the duplicate the officer found is the one we gave. HENRY GIBBS. I am a pawnbroker. I produce two napkins, pawned by a person in the name of Ann Rance, on the 2nd of January—this is the duplicate we gave.
   A. RANCE  — GUILTY  . Aged 27.— Recommended to mercy. Confined Six Months.
   D. RANCE  — NOT GUILTY.

 1196. WILLIAM REYNOLDS was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of March , in the dwelling-house of William Simmonds , 1 20 l  . Bank of England note, the property of Daniel Evans ; to which he pleaded GUILTY.— Confined Nine Months.
 1197. JOSEPH SHEPHERD was indicted for feloniously forging a receipt for the payment of 1 s  . 6 d  ., with intent to defraud Samuel Sugden .—2nd COUNT  , for uttering the same; to which he pleaded GUILTY.— Transported for Seven Years.
 1198. JAMES LEACH was indicted for feloniously being at large, without lawful cause, before the expiration of the period for which he had been ordered to be transported; to which he pleaded GUILTY.— Transported for Life.
