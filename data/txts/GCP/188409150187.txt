and did work at the Oxford and Cambridge Laundry-I was there on the
day in question, when the prisoner came, at about 11.30 a.m., to the
bottom of the stairs and called the prosecutrix by the name of “Tits" -
she said she did not wish to have anything to do with him, and wished
he would go away and leave her alone-he said "I won't keep you a
minute if you will come and speak one word "-he came up into the
laundry--- he was begging her forgiveness and asking her pardon, and
with that I went downstairs and left them to themselves—when I was
downstairs the prosecutrix screamed, and I went up and she said he had
stabbed her with a knife, but I did not see anything of that or the knife
-I went downstairs and said to the employer “I wish you would put
Mr. Gann out, for I cannot work, and I shall go home”-he said he
would, and he called him down, and he was down there talking till about
1.15, and then he came upstairs again and asked her if he should get her
any dinner-she went to starch some collars, and he went up to her and
put his arm round her neck-he asked her to forgive him, and she said
she would not-she screamed, but I did not pay any attention to that-
she screamed a second time, and I went over and found she was bleeding
very much-the prisoner had this old knife in his hand; it had a piece
broken off—then he made a second attempt, and broke & second piece off
-I did not see him strike her with the knife the first time I said "Mr.
Gann, you have been and stabbed her," and he said "Yes, I have stabbed
her; I wish it was to her b--heart; if you take her part I will serve
you the same "-I went away to fetch a policeman, and left them to
themselves—I could not see how her shoulder was struck-he had the
knife with the blade downwards- I went out to fetch a policeman, and
when I came back he was standing out in the street asking for a lucifer,
and I said to a man "Don't talk to him, he has stabbed his wife," and
he said “No, my wife is in there cohabiting with the proprietor of the
Oxford and Cambridge Laundry "-I used this knife for my dinner--I
did not see how it broke--it must have broken in his hand, because it
was whole before he was very drunk-he was very good and kind when
ho was not drunk, and he need to bring her nice dinners- I did not see
any blood—the prosecutrix did not seem affected by it.
HENRY MORRIS SIMMONS. I am & surgeon, of 66, Camberwell Road-
I saw the prosecutrix at about 3 o'clock on the day in question I found
a very slight wound on the left shoulder and a cut on the right side of
the neck, about one inch long but of no depth, just severing the skin-it
was such a wound as might be produced by this knife—the wound was
recent-I thought the blow might have been sharply given, but just the
broken angle have caught her and divided the skin, and it being stated
to be an entire knife before, I looked at the fracture in the knife-I
thought it was an old cracked place, and the blow on the shoulder had
been produced with the back of the knife, which had doubled under the
impetus of the blow, and the knife had broken up.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrato. "She has been rather
cool to me lately and I have been drinking, and when I am drunk I do
not know what I do. I did not intend to hurt her, only to frighten her."
GUILTY of unlawfully wounding.-Four Months' Hard Labour.
