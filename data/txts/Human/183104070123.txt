 976.  CHARLES PROUTINGS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April , 7 books, value 4s. 6d., the goods of John Francis Tappin and William Cousans.
   WILLIAM COUSANS, I am in partnership with John Francis Tappin; we are booksellers, and live in High-street, Marylebone. On the 5th of April the constable came to our house, and brought some books, which I knew- we have a stall-board in front of our house, on which I had placed, among other books, Guy's grammar that morning, about eight o'clock - the officer brought that and the other books about two o'clock; they had not been sold.
 WILLIAM HUGHES. I am a Police-constable. I saw the prisoner in Queen-street, Edgware-read on the 5th of April - he had a parcel tied up in a handkerchief; I stopped him, and asked him what he had got - he said some books which he had bought of two boys in Paddington-street, and given 1s. 3d. for them - he was then about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I knew nothing of their being stolen - I bought them of two boys for 1s. 3d.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Whipped and Discharged.
 977.  MICHAEL MCLOUGHLIN was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of April , 1 half-crown, the money of Mary Bruce , from her person.
   MARY BRUCE. I live in the Almonry, Westminster. On the night of the 6th of April I met the prisoner coming up the passage into the Almonry - he asked me to go with him, but I would not; he then asked me to go and have some supper - we went to a cook-shop, in Tothill-street; I sat down by him, and while I was sitting I felt his hand in my pocket - he said he had dropped his tobacco-box; I said I would pick it up for him - he then said he had it in his pocket; I came out of the cook-shop with him, and when I came out I missed a half-crown which I had in my pocket about half an hour before; I charged him with it - he said he had not got it; I had it in my pocket, wrapped up in a piece of paper, when I went into the cook-shop.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you make any alarm in the shop? A. No; but I felt your hand in my pocket, and you took hold of my hand immediately - I told you I missed it when I got on the step; there was a hole in my pocket, but the paper could not get through it - the Policeman did not give me a half-crown in the watch-house to try it, and it went through.
   EDWARD BARTHOLOMEW. I am a labourer, and live in New-way, Westminster. On the night of Tuesday, the 5th, or rather on Wednesday morning, the 6th of April, I saw the prisoner being taken to the watch-house - I followed him, and saw him put his hand up as if he was going to put it into his waistcoat pocket; I then saw his hand drop, and saw this paper laying twisted up on the ground - I went up to the watch-house, and heard the charge - the prisoner was then set at liberty; I was going back, and the prisoner came to near the same place where I had seen the paper lay - he seemed to be looking about for something, or I should not have thought of looking; I then looked, and found this paper, with half a crown in it - this was in about seven or eight minutes after I first saw it.
 Prisoner. I was taken to the watch-house, and this man followed - what money I had about me, which was some copper, was returned to me; I stripped, dressed again, and as I was going away this man stooped down, and said, "I have picked up half a crown;" I was then taken back - the Policeman gave her a half-crown, and it fell through a hole in her pocket - it was a French pocket, in front of her apron; Bartholomew swore he saw me put my hand into my pocket.
   EDWARD BARTHOLOMEW. They did take down that you put your hand into your pocket, but I put up my finger, and said, "No, Sir;" I repeated what I had said, that I saw your hand as if you had put your hand into your pocket, but I saw nothing drop - I saw the paper on the ground; I never saw the prosecutrix till that evening; I heard of the charge as I sat in my own house - I went out, and followed him to the watch-house.
 Prisoner. I met this girl of the town; she wanted the to go home and sleep with her; I said I had a wife and three children - there was a woman in company with her in the shop, who said, "I know him to be married, so go about your business."
   JOHN FREDERICK BOHM. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and found 6 1/2d. in copper, which was given him again; he was then set at liberty, and I took him again.
 JURY. Q. Did you try whether a half-crown would go through a hole in her pocket? A. Yes, I tried it; it would go through with squeezing, but not in the paper.
   MARY BRUCE. The paper was like that, and I folded it in that way - there was a little mustard on it.
 Prisoner's Defence. She is a girl who would swear any thing; I know nothing of her, but giving her a basin of soup - when she found I would not go with her, she gave charge of me.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. - Transported for Seven Years.
 978.  JOHN DENNIS was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of March , 2 bottles, value 8s., and 1 glass knob, value 8s., the goods of Thomas Simmons.
   ALFRED SMITH. I am shopman to Mr. Thomas Simmons, a glass-man, who lives in Brownlow-street. On the 31st of March I was coming from the back part of the shop, and saw the prisoner in front of the counter - he took up a parcel; I asked him what he wanted, and he told me a gentleman had sent that parcel from Holborn - I said No, it was one I had put on the counter; his breast looked bulky, and I stepped to the door to look for an officer - I turned round, and saw the prisoner placing these bottles and this chandelier knob on the counter, but I did not see from what part of his person he produced them; the constable came in in about half an hour, and I gave the prisoner into custody - these are the bottles and the knob; they are Mr. Simmons' property - there were some odd drops, as patterns in the parcel which he took up; I had received it a short time before, from an out-door workman - these bottles had been on a shelf above the counter, and the knob on a shelf by the side of the counter.
