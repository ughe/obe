EDWARD DILLON (Policeman, K 19). In conseqnence of information, I
went to Bluegate-fields; I saw the proseontrix there ; she was wounded in
several places, and was bleeding very much--I saw a knife sticking in her
right shoulder; it was firmly fixed through her clothes and flesh into the
shoulder, right up to the hilt, as far as it could go; we could not get it
out-I placed her in a cart and took her to the hospital, where I saw the
surgeon take out the knife-I returned to the station, and told the prisoner
that the woman was stabbed in four or five places, and I believed she would
die--the prisoner said, “ Whatever is the matter with her; I have done it;!
the bas robbed me of my clothes, and all my money" - I had gone fo the
house a short time before the woman was stabbed, and searched it, and I
found a pair of trowsers between the bed and mattress, in the second floor
front-room, where the prisoner said he had slept with the woman-I bad
seen the prisoner walking about the street, about three-quarters of an hour
before-he was then in his drawers, shirt, and stockings; he complained of
being robbed at this house I went there and saw the prosecutrix, and told
her in the prisoner's presence that he accused her of robbing himsbe said,
I have not robbed him," and she rashed at him and struck him on the
hand with her fist-this was about three-quarters of an hour before she was
Mounded she swore a word I should not like to repeat, and called him

Awoke in the morning, the clothes were gone she was very drunk and

DAVID HYMAN DYTE I am house surgeon at the London Hospital-on!
Sunday morning, 27th July, about 7 o'clock, the prosecutrix was brought
thero-she had a punctured wound of the right shoulder, in which a knife
was firmly fixed-it was buried to the depth of four inches; the whole
Jength of the blade up to the handle--it required some force to get it out
--besides that she had a lacerated wound, two and a quarter inches long, in
the same arm; and two punctured wounds in the back, and a slight wound

was a very severe wound; the others were not so dangerous-she remained
there about three weeks she cannot move her arm very well yet, but she
is now pretty well-it must have required very great force to have inflicted
the wound on the shoulder.
