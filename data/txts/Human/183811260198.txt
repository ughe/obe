   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Are you sure that Hobbs said this? A.  Yes, quite sure—I got the key back in about ten days. JOHN LOWE. I am a grocer and live at Acton—that is about two miles and a half from Mr. Tubbs's cottage, at Harlesdon Green—on the 18th of August last, I saw the prisoner Norman—she told me her name was Nor man, and she had just taken Mr. Tubbs's cottage—she came to me for tea and sugar, and grocery—I supplied her with goods that day to the amount of 30 s ., and I continued to serve her on the following week—I know the prisoner Foster—he was in the habit of coming to my shop frequently for goods of the same description as Mrs. Norman had—I remember on the Saturday after Mrs. Norman had come to me, Foster came and ordered some goods—he had walked down, and he wished me to give him a ride back again in my cart, which I did—he admired the horse, and said his mistress had got a pair of horses, a very fine strapping pair, sixteen hands high, that they had ordered hay and corn of Mr. Billington, a neigh bour of mine, that the coachman and cook were coming on Monday, and I was to be sure to bring my bill in on Monday, and it would be paid on the Tuesday—the same afternoon the prisoner Grafton came for more goods, and it being a wet afternoon, she requested a ride back also, and I took her in the cart—I rather grumbled about going up so often with the things, it being a great distance, and wished them to give me the order in the morning and I would execute it—she told me her mistress came from Bath, and the cook was down there then—that she was coming up on Mon day, and then she would come with the orders, and would not give me so much trouble-Grafton said she had lived with her mistress six years—when Mrs. Norman came to the shop she wanted every article good, but did not say any thing about price—but Grafton said her mistress was not very particular about price so long as the things were good—the amount of the goods they had of me was 14 l . 6 s . 8 d .—it began on the 18th of August and ended on the 27th—they had of me tea, sugar, butter, soap, blacking, bacon, candles, and other things, and a great many things which I did not sell, I went and got for them.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDEKHAST . Q.  Did you not ask for her custom? A.  No, sir—she said she was the lady that had taken Mr. Tubbs's cottage, and I said I should be happy to have her custom—she told me she had got Mr. Tubbs's cottage, and I went to Mr. Tubbs's, but it was not from what he said that I trusted her—I had trusted her before I saw him, but not to the whole of this amount—she came to me on a Saturday, and I saw Mr. Tubbs one day in the following week.
