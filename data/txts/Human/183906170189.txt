   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  That was when Mrs. Abrahams was there? A.  Yes.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was it not proposed on the part of Mr. Solomons's solicitor, or himself, that he should refund such of the property as he had in his possession, and be admitted for the Crown? A.  Yes—this was stated openly and publicly before the Magistrate in the police-office, and his consent requested before it was done—the two Magistrates, on the re presentation of the counsel for the prosecution, gave their full consent to it—at that time Moss was a prisoner, and had not been examined as a wit ness—it was stated to the Magistrate that the best test that Mr. Solomon was about to tell the truth, was his delivering up the property, so that they could depend upon what he said—it was also stated by the counsel for the prosecution that it was utterly impossible they could make out any case without making some of the prisoners witnesses.
   Q.  And was it not under a full conviction on all your parts that Moss was not to be depended on, having given such contradictory statements, that Solomon was admitted as a witness? A.  Yes, but nothing whatever was done until every thing had been stated to the Magistrates, and their consent asked and given, in the presence of the prisoners—the value of the property given up by Solomon is about 2400 l .—I am quite satisfied we could not have gone to trial on the evidence of Moss, unless we had strong corroborative testimony—I think it ought to be strong.
   Q.  How long has Lewin Caspar been a clerk in your house? A.  I only became connected with the establishment in October, 1837—I found him. in the office then—very large quantities of money have passed through his hands in the course of business, frequently from 3000 l . to 4000 l . a month,—I had the highest opinion of his honesty till this transaction—large quan tities of property, as well as money, have passed through his hands, bullion and specie.
   Q.  You have not been able to find Allen? A.  We have not—I have not been on the look-out for him, nor directed any search for him—I have had no occasion to do so—I do not think it worth while—he absconded without giving me any notice, five or six weeks after the robbery—this transaction had been put in the train of legal investigation long before he absconded—Solomon had been taken up about three weeks before, to the best of my recollection—Allen absconded about a month before Solomon was first summoned to give an account of the property—it was, of course, talked of in the office that Solomon was taken up—on one occasion Lewin, Caspar had specie to the amount of from 130, 000 l . to 150, 000 l . in his care—that was on the 15th of December last—it was a consignment from Fal mouth—I was in London then, and he went with it to the Bank—Allen was our collecting clerk—he had access to the different offices—we trusted him, or we should not have kept him—I was apprised of this robbery on the 27th, while in Dublin—I came to town on Thursday, and Lewin Caspar was taken up on the following Tuesday—I have no doubt it was publicly talked of in the office before I came, as information was given the very day it happened—I found Lewin Caspar in his situation when I returned from Dublin, and he remained there till he was taken—he went to his own home
