Mrs. Wilson-she is married to Patrick Cavannah, but goes by the name of

the prisoner quarrelling between nine and ten o'clock-the prisoner came to
the door and asked Cavannah, his father-in-law, to let him in-he said,
" You shall not come in to-night, Robert"-he went and opened the door,
and then the prisoner went away-he came back again in about five minutes
and threw some little stones at the door-he said, "I want to come in"-
I am sure it was the prisoner's voice-master went across to the fire-place
and got the poker-he said, “You shall not come in to-night, Robert, but
you may burst the door open"-then I heard a pistol immediately fired off
the door was shut at the time the ball of the pistol went into master's
cheek-I saw master's cheek bleeding immediately after I heard the noise
of the pistol-master was stooping at the time, his head was close to where
the parlour-door opens, about the middle, above the lock.
Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Was it not immediately upon
your master's saying "you may burst the door open" that you heard the
report? A. Yes—the door had not been burst open-the prisoner could
not see my master.
FRANCIS BOSSEY. I am a surgeon. I was sent for to attend Cavannah
and found a wound upon his cheek about an inch and a half below the an-
gle of the mouth-I introduced a probe, which passed about an inch and
three-quarters by the side of the jaw-it came in contact with a pistol
bullet, which I extracted.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you see the prisoner? A. No-Cavannah had
been drunk but was in some degree sobered by the accident-he had evi-
dently been more drunk than he appeared-he was by no means sober
when I saw him.
MARK WELLS. I am a musician. I saw the prisoner the night this
bappened, standing opposite his mother's door (I bad heard the report of
a pistol about three minutes before)-I saw the prisoner take a pistol out
of his side pocket and fire it up the street-he said, “There is no ball in
that, but whether or not, I have discharged it in the air"-he was decidedly
drunk at the time.
THOMAS CLARK. I live in Powis-street, Woolwich, the prisoner came into
the shop on the 1st of April and wished to look at a pair of pistols-I showed
him a pair at 258.-he said "they will do, have the kindness to book them to
my mother, Mrs. Wilson"-I said I could not book them unless his mo-
ther was present and sanctioned it-he said, “ Well, I will take them;
come home with me, mother will pay for them"--I went with him about
a quarter to nine o'clock-his mother was asleep-he said, " I shall not
disturb her, I will pay you"-he had them of me, but had no powder or
bullets-he was sober then.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you see Cavannah at the house when you
went there ? A. I saw him just as I was coming away, but I did not see
the prisoner when I saw him.
CHARLES Gray. On the night of the 1st of April, about a quarter to ten
