WILLIAM GREGGS. I live in Long-alley, Finsbury, and am a hatter
some weeks before the 9th of Oct. I saw a van at the Swan, in the Dover-
ruad-Bashford was the driver, and there was a litule boy on it-I saw Doughton
riding on the box with Bashford-Bashford asked Doughton to g ve him a sheet
from the top of the van, under the tarpaulin, and Doughton let it down from
the top into a sack-Bashford took it up the yard by the side of the public
house-I saw him come back without it-on the 7th of Oct. I was at the
George-tap, Aldermanbury-I saw Bashford come in, and Doughton asked him
if he had got rid of the sheet-he said no, he should like to do so-Doughton
said he thought he could get him a customer-a sovereign was mentioned as
the price of it.
Cross-examined. Q. What are you? 4. I am not at present in any
situation - I had two days' work last week in George-street, Blackfriars-road
-Doughton formerly lived with Mr. Sweeting, in Cheapside-he drove his
cart-I was not in the habit of driving in his eart-1 have a brother who
lives up the George-yard, and it was himn who lost the harness--I never
heard that Doughton was accused of having stolen it-- 1 go to the George-
yard perhaps on an evening I had seen the harness-1 dare say I might
have seen it in company with Doughton- I frequently go in the stable-I only
know Bashford by seeing him once or twice--when I saw Doughton band this
tarpaulin to Bashford, I made no remark-I did not know but he might be
obliged to leave it there-hearing that Douhgton was seen with the policeman,
I mentioned the tarpaulin to my brother-we were merely talking about the
circumstance, and I happened to say that I saw it, and my brother brought
me up to the policeman.

- he was bringing a sack from one of the stalls in the stables -- he looked at

he said, “Yes, it is fifteen feet long" the sack was then put on Donghton's
