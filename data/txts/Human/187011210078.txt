 44. ELIZA JUDD  (26) , Unlawfully uttering a medal, intended to re semble a sixpence, with intent to defraud.
   MR. WALFORD conducted the Prosecution. VINCENT HOLFORD. I keep the "Pariah Arms," Stratford—on the night of 27th October, I served the prisoner with a glass of ale, which came to 1 d .—she gave me a sixpence, and I gave her the change—I kept the sixpence in my hand while I served another party, and then tendered it in change, and found it was bad—I gave her in custody with the coin, which I marked—the prisoner had been there six weeks before, when she tendered a bad sixpence for a glass of cooper  —I had no money in the till—I put the sixpence in, and afterwards tendered it in change, and found it was bad—about three weeks afterwards she came again for some kind of liquor, and gave me a bad sixpence—I gave it to a woman in change, without putting it in the till, and found it was bad—the prisoner had then gone. ROBERT LAKE( Policeman K  508). On 27th October Mr. Holford gave the prisoner into my custody—she said she did not know the coin was bad—she had taken 1 s . 6 d . of a man she did not know—I found a good shilling on her. WILLIAM WEBSTER These are three medals made to resemble, and intended to pass, for sixpences.
   Prisoner.  I beg for mercy. GUILTY*— Nine Months' Imprisonment.
