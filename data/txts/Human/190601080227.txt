Who is prosecuting?"—I said, "From the nature of the charge you might know; it is the Music Publishers' Association "—I took him to Bow Street police station, when he was charged at the same time with Tennant—he said in answer, "I am innocent of the charge "—there was nothing found upon him—on the following morning I went to his premises at Turnagain Lane, when I found this letter from "The Viaduct Press, 41a, Farringdon Street, E.C." dated December 11th, 1904, and signed, "Wallace" (  Ex.  173, read ): "Dear Mr. P.,—Will you please get 2, 000 at least of those music catalogues done as early as possible Saturday and Monday, and thereby greatly oblige Willetts and myself "—I found also this letter from "41a, Farring don Street, E.C.," signed Wallace and written to "Dear Mr. P." ( Ex.  175, read: Stating that Willetts gave him the enclosed, of which he wanted  250 printed, and asking him to set up " the poem" at once, and to get a number of the circular " set up in Farringdon Street last Friday about warning the agents " )—I arrested Bokenham on a warrant granted on November 30th, at 9.30 a.m. on December 4th in the Kilburn High Road—I said, "Are you Frederick Bokenham?" and he said, "Yes "—I said, "I am an inspector of police and I am going to arrest you upon a warrant for conspiring with Willetts and others to print and sell pirated music "—he said, "Have you been waiting long?" and I said, "No "—he said, "If you had come to my house I think you would have found me "—on the way to the police station in a cab he said, "I am not a member of the company "—I said, "Do you mean the People's Music Publishing Co.?" and he said, "Yes, but I went down to Paternoster Row on the day that Willetts was arrested, thinking that there might have been a warrant out for all of us. I think summonses would do as well as warrants in this matter "—at Bow Street the warrant was read to him, when he said in reply, "Very good "—on the same day I went to Cazenove Yard, when I found Clark, whom Bokenham had told me was his partner—some printing was going on—I seized 9, 300 copies and half copies of music, including a quantity of "Looping the Loop with Lacy," and ninety-three blocks, including blocks for printing "Eileen Allanah" and "Sleep and Forget," which two songs were being printed when I entered—these are the blocks for printing those songs ( Ex , 52a, 53a and  54a)—they have been compared with the music found in the van on November 30th—I found in his pocket this postal order from the Isle of Wight, dated October 31st, 1905, for 8s., made payable to "Fisher & Co.," ( Ex.  183) and these invoices, some addressed to "Tiny Tim" without any name at the top, with such items as "Six reams 'All Eternity' and 'The Gift' at 10s., £3" ( Ex.  184,—I found this letter headed, "Sunday" to "Dear Nob," and signed "Tiny Tim "—( Ex.  185, Read: Stating that " Po " did not keep his word by letting him have a delivery yesterday, and that he might let him (Tiny Tim) know how he (Nob) stood with " regard to the " jigs " of a number of songs, of which there was a list—
