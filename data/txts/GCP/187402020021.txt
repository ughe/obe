apprehended the prisoner on a warrant at his residence or lodging, 37,
Clarendon Buildings, Oxford Street; that was the address that had been
furnished to me I told him that I was an inspector of police, and beld &

"All right."
ALFRED MALVIN. I am a clerk to Cocks and Co., the army agents-I
have known the defendant many years I know his bandwriting-I have
no doubt this letter (the one produced) is in his handwriting (Read:
"May it please your Royal Highness-In now addressing your Royal
Highness, I desire to offer the amplest apology possible for the unprovoked
and unjustifiable assault I committed upon your Royal Highness on the 6th
of January. I know that your Royal Highness cannot suppose that I
intended any grievous bodily harm. My services in the field will, I hope,
plead in my behalf against anything unbecoming the conduct of an officer
and a gentleman. But for years I have been brooding over my wrongs
until they had produced in me uncontrollable morbid imaginings. In this
state of mind and feeling I came upon your Royal Highness, when I
thought the moment had arrived for bringing my case before the public.
Goaded on to the verge of madness, I committed the rash aot; not to
injure your Royal Highness, but to seek oocasion of ventilating my wrongs.
I do not seek by this letter to mitigate my offence, but simply to perform
an act which, as a gentleman, I feel myself bound to do, regardless of all
consequences. I regret deeply to have caused your Royal Highness the
slightest inconvenience, and from my heart I deeply deplore it, not
because of the punishment I so justly deserve, but for having given any
annoyance to your Royal Highness. CHARLES S. MAUNSELL"
