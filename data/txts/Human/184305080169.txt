   Cross-examined. Q.  Who brought them? A.  Payne, and a boy named Chapman—they were the only persons—my foreman, John Clark, sells as well as me—the prisoner said that Payne asked him to assist him with one pig on his shoulder, at Mrs. Lee's shop, and he had done so.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Was he asked in your presence whether he had brought a pig to your shop? A.  Not to my recollection—he had not brought any, and I never employed him, or authorised him to bring any. GILES SILVERSIDE. I am a salesman in Newgate-market. On the morn ing of the 6th of May, between six and seven o'clock, the prisoner brought a pig to me—he said, "I have brought a pig for you to sell"—he hung it up himself, and then walked down to the clerk—I did not hear in what name it was to be sold—I knew him before—the pig was sold by a person with me to Mr. Horsfall.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know a person named Scott? A.  I do—I do not know whether he keeps pigs. HENRY WASTON. I am clerk to Mr. Silverside. On the 6th of May Goodhall came to Mr. Silverside's shop, and said, "Mr. Watson, will you book in one pig?"—I said, "Who is it out of?" meaning by what carrier it had come—he said, "Book it in the name of Scott, where I sometimes work."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you know Scott? A.  No. THOMAS HORSFALL. I bought a pig at Mr. Silverside's, for 1 l . 13 s . 9 d ., it weighed 10 stone 1lb.—Mr. Lee and Mr. Hockley have seen that pig. CHARLES BROWN. I am butcher to Mrs. Lee. I got a pig from Mr. Horsfall—it is the same that had been lost on the Saturday morning.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you weigh it? A.  No—these pigs were con signed to us from the country to sell—we have a great number—I am not able to speak to all that are consigned to us—I was outside when these were sold. OLIVER DEATH( City police-sergeant, No.  201.) I received charge of the prisoner at the station—he did not say anything. HENRY DANIEL HOCKLEY re-examined. Q.  You told us that the pig that was lost weighed 10 stone 1lb.—was that the exact weight of the pig that was found? A.  Yes.
   MR. WYLDE called JOHN SCOTT. I live in Seward-street, Goswell-street, St. Luke's; the prisoner was a lodger of mine. About six or seven weeks previous to this charge, he had put a pig of his own in my sty—I killed it last Friday week, and he took it away on the Saturday morning, I expect—I called upon him that Saturday morning, at a quarter before six o'clock, and when I came back from milking, the pig was gone—I had seen it there when I went out, and never saw it afterwards—he said he was going to take it to the marked.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  It was a very good pig? A.  As good as any one need to be—I think it weighed about ten stone—I did not weigh it—the pig he left at Mr. Silverside's is not mine—I had not directed him to leave a pig there in my name—I did not allow him to do what he did.
