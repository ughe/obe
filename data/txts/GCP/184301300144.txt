Q. When you found this was the state of the business, what did you say?
A. I said, “How is this, that the business loses so much, when you told me
it would pay well ?"--this was about August, 1842-the reason of this pro.
missory note for 1261. 138. 11d., being in this account, is, I told Jenns I should
not carry on business any longer, in consequence of the loss, and he said he
was sure there was some errors in the account, and if the business was car.
ried on he would give me a bill that he would be liable for, as he was certain
the business paid and would pay-there is a second bill of exchange, which
was given for the difference, Mr. Rudge receiving so much less than Mr.
Jenns out of the concern--that bill is 1721. 28. 11}d.-Mr. Rudge was to
receive 41. a week also, and Rudge had drawn out, in point of fact, so much
less than this amount, and this was to go to the credit side of Rudge if the
business had answered, and they had gone into partnership-this other note
for 1261. 138. 11d. is the balf of the loss-the account was not settled, and it
was drawn as part of the loss, subject to going into the account with Mr.
Jenns-he said he was sure the business had paid, and he would give me that
bill—these accounts were made between Mr. Jenns and Mr. Rudge ; and sup-
posing it turned out that there had been no loss, then that bill was to bave
been destroyed, and Rudge and Jenns were to go into the original agreement.
Court. Q. Then this 1261. 138. 11d. was assumed as half of the loss!
A. Between them two it was-it never was by me- I took the bill.
MR. DOANE. Q. Did you go into the accounts? A. No, I could never
get him to go into them-he repeatedly promised me he would do so-I saw
him at the Haberdashers' Arms, I think, in the beginning of November, or
the latter end of October-he produced this paper, (looking at it,) and said be
had received 161. 38. 2d. of De Cane and Co. -he deducted 41. for himself as
his salary, and has charged me for solution, and incidental expenses, which
amount to 51. 38. 2d.-here is his own signature to it-this was on the 12th
of last November - I always paid the rent for the premises, and for the goods
that supplied the shop with stock--every thing was in my name I paid for
all the things employed in the manufactory.
HENRY JONES. I live in Houndsditch, and am a currier. I had work
done at the patent japan manufactory in Hoxton--I paid the prisoner on the
. 13th of May this bill of 77. 78.; on the 9th of July this bill of 161. 88., and
in Aug., this other bill of 11. 78.- they are signed by the prisoner.
GEORGE BICKERTON RUDGE. I am the prosecutor's son-in-law. I be-
came clerk to him in carrying on the japanning business in Hoxton Old
Town- I began about Aug. 1841-Jenns conducted the business. I paid
him a weekly allowance by Mr. Weedon's order--the first amount, I think,
was 31. a week, then 21.--the principal amount has been 21.-it was at one
time 31. in cash-I paid him generally on Saturday nights--I had a salary
of 30s. a week till within the last four months, and then I had 11. I did not
commence having a salary for three or four months--I paid Jenns and myself
at the same time-my father-in-law was keeping me and my family-these
accounts (looking at them) of 611. to me, and 1361. 158. 2d., and the
1721. 2s. 11 d. that Jenns had drawn more than me, are correct-this is a
correct account of what I received, and what I paid to Jenns in cash-
continued to pay him his weekly salary as long as he carried on the factory
up to Nov, or Dec. last-I made out the quarterly accounts, the bills, and
Mr. Jerns made out the invoices-I made out the bills in the name of
"Weedon, late Jenns"-Jenns had nothing to do with making out the quarterly
accounts to the customers--I made out the bills up to the end of September-
I never made any alteration in them at any time I did not make out this
