MR. HICKSON re-examined. Q.  What is the price of these taps? A.  Eight and sixpence a dozen, with two and a half per cent. off and carriage paid, which reduces them to about eight shillings and twopence a dozen, WILLIAM FISK. I am foreman to my uncle, Henry Fisk, a shop-fixture dealer in the New-cut. On 6th November the prisoner came to our shop, between ten and twelve o'clock in the morning—he brought me one tap, and asked me whether the taps would suit my uncle—he said he had about three dozen—he came again about one o'clock and brought thirty-four more—he had left the sample one—he said he would call on Saturday, about two o'clock, to ascertain whether my uncle would have them—I said, "They will not suit the governor; they are not long enough"—he came on Saturday, and I told him they would not suit—the officer came to our shop on Friday evening—when the prisoner called on Saturday I told him to take them away.
   COURT . Q.  You say he came on Wednesday with a sample, and he came again later and brought thirty-four? A.  Yes; I told him to leave them and come again on Saturday—he did not come to our place on the Satur day—he was opposite, and I told the officer that was the man, and he went and took him.
   Q.  Then when was it you told him the taps would not suit? A.  I think it was on the Thursday—he came again on the Thursday, the next day, and then he said, "I will call again on Saturday"—he asked me if he might leave them, as he was going on further—no time was fixed for his coming on Saturday—he said he would come about Saturday—he asked seven shillings and sixpence a dozen for them—he did not tell me where he got them—I did not ask him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you known him? A.  The last six years—he has been in the habit of dealing at our house almost every day—he has sold us things—it is customary for persons in that way to buy things at auctions and pawnbrokers—I am sure he said he would call on Thursday, and he did call—I knew him to be a very good, fair-dealing man—I never knew anything against his character; if he got any profit he would be satisfied—I have been in the Cut seven years, in my uncle's service—he has been known at our house fifteen years—I do not know where he lives—when I saw him on the Saturday he was walking along on the other side of the way.
   MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  Are you in the habit of buying new articles from him? A.  Sometimes—I never bought any taps of him—he was in the habit of selling cheap—the slightest profit would satisfy him—he would bring counting-house railings and other things. GEORGE AARON.
