REGINALD DRAKE. I am secretary of the Princess Louise Home
Kingston Hill-Jane Mary Cook was admitted to that school on May 29th,
1889-she left on October 6th. 1897--our school is really the National
Society for the Proteetion of Young Girls, but we took the name of the
Princess Louise in 1893-it is for the reception of girls who have nobody
to look after them-it is not free-this girl was paid for at the rate of
E10 a year by Mr. George Lillyman Fowler--the total amount contributed
to her maintenanee there by him was $83 10s. 8d.
Cross-examined. We had a letter about the girl, saying that her mother
was dead, and that her father neglected her--the matter of her pocket
money did not pass through my hands, exeept one, when I had a letter
from Mr. Fowler sending me E10 for maintenance and 58. for pocket
money-the girls generally write onee a quarter-the matron reads all
correspondence-I never saw the prisoner at the home.
Re-ezamined. We feed, elothe, and educate the girls for the E10 a year,
find them situations afterwards, and give them an outfit when they go
away.
By the COURT. There is no other expense for whoever pays the E10 a
year.
JAMES STEVENS. I am superintendent of the Islington Industrial
Sehool. Copenhagen Street, Caledonian Road-Charles William Cook
was admitted there on June 6th, 1889, and remained there until December
21st, 1894--the prisoner paid for him while there-he paid 215 128. per
annum-the total amount paid was $86 178.-that includes board and
schooling- do not know if that ineludes clothing-the boys have a uniform
which was provided by the school and included in the charge.
Cross-ezamined. I was not at the school at that time-I do not know
anything about the poeket money-the boys had holidays at the request
of the parents- do not know if this boy went for holidays.
Re-eramined. It was the rule in those days for boys when admitted to
pay 10s. for clothes and 7s. for boots-no further payment was made for
clothes- have an entry here that there was on June 6th a payment of
E3 18s., and a further payment of 17s.
ELIZABETH MATTHEWS. I am the wife of William Matthews, and live
at 8, Brunswick Place, City Road-my husband is the step-brother of
James Cook-after Charles Cook left school, in 1894, he came to live with
us for about three years-he went out to work-I arranged with the prisoner
that I should be paid 12s. a week-when the boy was in work he paid
part of the 12s. and the prisoner paid the rest; when the boy was out of
work the prisoner paid it all-he was very often out of work-he was
sometimes idle-he did odd job8-I do not know how much I received-
I gave a receipt to the prisoner for whatever I did receive.
