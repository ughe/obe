LAWRENCE JOHN MANDERS( Policeman K  46). On 1st August, Smith pointed out William Shadd to me and I took him, and said that he very likely would be charged with receiving a watch and chain, knowing them to be stolen—he said "I know different to that, last evening a man came up to me in Albert Road, who I do not know, and gave me the watch and chain and asked me to have them passed back to the prosecutor, and said that he wanted a little recompense, but I did not give him anything"—I took him to the station and charged him with receiving—I found the broken bow of another watch on him—I was present on the night of the robbery, Smith appeared sober, the prisoner Henry accused him of being drunk—I said he is not drunk; he smelt a little of drink; he appeared to know very well what he was about.
   William, Shadd 'produced a written defence, stating that on the night after the robbery a man gave him the watch, stating that he picked it up under one of the tables at the Three Crowns, and asked him to restore it; to release his brother; who was his only support, and that he picked up the watch bow some months ago.
   HENRY SHADD  — GUILTY — Fine Months' Imprisonment.
   W. T. SHADD  — NOT GUILTY.

   MR. REED conducted the Prosecution; and MR. BRINDLEY the Defence. EDWARD GAY. I live at 13, Perrin Street, Bromley—on the evening of August 7th, I had been drinking with the prisoner and his wife, and after wards went to Western Street, where a row  took place between my wife and the prisoner's wife—I and my wife went to fetch a baby, which had been left at Western Street—there was a young woman named Mrs. Clack, with a baby in her arms, and the prisoner shook hands with her—the prisoner's wife said "You had better take him home and sleep with him" and struck Mrs. Clack—my wife told her not to do that while she had her baby in her arms—the prisoner's wife was about to hit my wife, and I went to help her—my wife ran into the passage followed by the prisoner's wife—he stood in the doorway and said "If you come further into the passage I will bury this knife in your neck"—I went to my wife to get her away, and had not gone more than 2 feet when it was buried in my neck, and I fell out into the street insensible—there was a wound about 4 inches in length, I was in the hospital from Tuesday night till next Tuesday week.
