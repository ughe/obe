ALBERT SPIEGELBATER. -I am a watchmaker at 62, The Mount, White-
chapel Road, Mr. Kersten is a friend of mine-I remember being in
his house about the middle of August, when May, in my presence, asked
him for 71, and said he had engaged Mr. Besley, and had to pay him the
money-no receipt was given.
Cross-examined. I was there till you went away, sitting opposite you in
the bar parlour I came through the bar-I did not see you write the
receipt produced, and nothing more was said than what I have stated
you did not remain long after you had the money-I was first asked the
week before last to come forward as a witness— did not think anything
more of it, but I recollected well enough after I was told was surprised
to see in the papers that criminal proceedings were being taken against
you, but I did not think anything of it then.
HENRY WILLIAM LOCKYER (Re-examined). I did not receive 76 or any
money in respect of an action against Kersten in the Lord Mayor's Court,
and Mr. Besley was not instructed in the matter at all.
Cross-examined. You had cases with me about that time I should
have recollected if you had said, "I may want you for other cases in
the Mayor's Court," but I should not recollect if you said, "I shall want
you for another case shortly."

HENRY HAMBRO. I am cashier to Messrs. Cross, Sons, and Riley,
Waterloo Place on 3rd April I called on Mr. Pullagar and gave him
instructions to proceed with a fresh indictment in Lytton's case he said
he had not been very well and had got a gentleman atteuding to his busi
ness who would conduct the case in his absence, and he called you in and
introduced you-Mr. Parver was present, and it was arranged that I
should put myself in your hands, and you were to conduet the caseit
was an ordinary case; I cannot say what interest our client took in it-it
was necessary, the time being short, for us to go about every night for
witnesses, and we went about a good deal to gather materials for the case we
were going about and spending money-you made pencil notes which 1
believed to be items of expenditure in the case, but I did not examine
them, and during the whole time we were together you paid all the
expenses I had not to pay; it was not a matter I could expend money in
-you attended diligently to the case, and to the best of my recollution
when you left the case you had all the witnesses before the Grand Jury in
the second indictment in Davis's case, when Mr. Fullagar came up in a nge
at the Court the day the case was coming on-I cannot remember how
many adjournments had taken place I was present on the 23rd when you
handed Mr. Fullagar the papers-Mr. Fullagar appeared to say as little as
he could he was certainly out of temper at the Sessions House but not
then I heard you offer to make out an account, which you did in the
adjoining room- have tried but cannot remember complete sentences of
what was said I recollect him saying something to this effect : "Even
those figures would show that yon have not accounted for about 121.," and
I think you said "Very well;" and I remember you saying there would be
some remuneration due to you for what you had done for him.
