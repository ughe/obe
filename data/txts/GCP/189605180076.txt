SIR HENRY PEEK. I am one of the firm of Peek Brothers, of East-
cheap, and have had experience of she tea trade of London-it is not the
custom for person who buys goods, and does not pay for them, to deposit
them for cash at his bankers, and if after having obtained the goods he
Bells them, and then pledges the warrants, that would be doubly dishonest
-pledging warrants which are paid for, is perfectly legitimate and com-
mon-there never was an arrangement for paying once a month.
Cross-examined. This is true: "A merchant in the ordinary way may
deposit warrants with his banker as cover, even if he has given an
acceptance in respect of the warrants which is not then due”—but that
is an hypothetical case ; no such case arises in legitimate business--we
gave credit to his firm for £1,000, and he was not to exceed that-that
was on a four months' acceptance the goods were applied for one day,
and the bills given the second day, and they were taken to the bank on
the third day-that was not legitimate business-he had a credit of
£1,000, but not for fifteen years- his firm has very likely beeu paying
us £2,000 or £3,000 a year for upwards of fourteen years, but there
have been hitches- I think we have made complaints during that time,
but I cannot recollect without the books--I have left the City for more
than twelve months--I have only deposited warrants which have been
paid for, with my bankers- I cannot get them without paying for them
by cash, not by acceptances-I have not known of cases where warrants
have been delivered on arceptances, and then used by way of temporary
cover to the bankers- I have not done it myself, nor has my firm, to muy
knowledge--I deny it-I do not recullect the prisoner's father being
in our service.
