Workman asked me to lend him 57. till November and he would give me 10  l . for it, as he should have a fortune then—I said, "I have not got it," and offered to stand for a loan for him—the loan was for 10 l ., and we each had half—on a Monday or Tuesday night towards the end of August I went through Addington Squire, Camberwell, with Workman, and we met Patterson there, and went back to the Anchor and Hope and had something to drink, and they talked for a few minutes, and then Patterson went towards Peckham, and Workman and myself towards Camberwell—later in August I was going to the Strand with my pony and cart I have been a baker for 22 years, collecting sweepings and stale sacks from bakers, and kept a pony and cart—I was at 14, Cork Street, and offered to give Baker a ride to Pimlico—just before starting Workman came in and said, "I might at well go with you"—when we got to Westminster Bridge Road opposite a chapel near Oakley Street, he said, "Stop there, Bill; that's the place we are going to do,' pointing to a place with a coat of arms over it painted white—there was a wall about eight feet high, where they said they were going to get over to do it; and then on three or four occasions afterwards, after Workman had got into trouble, Baker had a look at the place when I gave him a ride—Baker often came in an afternoon or evening and said, "I must go round and see Patterson and his wife, and if the job comes off right I'll give Mrs. Workman 50 l . out of my corner, the others could give what they liked"—I knew Dowthwaite—Workman four or five times said, "Don't take any notice to Bob, as we don't want him to know, because we don't want to give him any of it; we only want to keep it between three, Patter son, me, and Baker"—that was after Fossitt had got into trouble—Fossitt made the keys, and when he got into trouble Baker was introduced into the case.
   Cross-examined by Patterson.  I never spoke to you in my life, but have seen you three or four times—last August I saw you once at the Station Tavern, and once at the Anchor—I had no appointment with you, and did not speak to you—Wells pointed you out as a relation of Workman's.
   Cross-examined by Arthur Workman.  You showed me two keys in the Crown—nobody else was with us—I know of no reason but for bravado——you have showed them to Teddy Paine, another convict—I did not expect to take any part in the burglary.
   By the COURT . I have known Workman since he came home from penal servitude—Fossitt was caught housebreaking last Whitsuntide twelve-months, and then Workman introduced Baker, because he knew he was a clever burglar, and but for my wife Baker would not have been here to-day.
