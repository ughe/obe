Mr. Abrahams, the attorney for Mr. Solomon, was allowed to be in the room
I cannot say how many persons came into the room to make statements
- there was Solomon and I think two of his sons-I do not recollect
whether there was any more-I do not recollect whether Mr. Dean was one.
Q. Were these statements signed by the parties? A. Yes--after they
had been read over to them, and every question strictly forbidden, and they
had been distinctly told that the Magistrates would only receive it as
voluntary confession, which might be used against them--when the papers
were read by the clerk to us, the persons making the statements were
asked, if such statements were true--the papers were each signed by the
parties making the statements--I cannot say into whose hands they im-
mediately passed, but I suppose into the hands of the counsel, as notes to
examine the witnesses afterwards in open court-I received the papers
from the hands of the counsel for the prosecution, before I went into the
room, and returned them to their hands--this private examination might
have lasted an hour and a half-I believe the connsel and attorneys for the
prosecution had private audiences with me several times, not every morn-
ing-we were asked for a private audience and gave it, and should have
given you the same if you had asked-Alice Abrahams was examined be-
fore me before this-I cannot say if she was examined on her oath, but she
was examined and made a statement as a witness--I have never been desi-
fous of any testimony, but when I found there was evidence to implicate
ber--there was none excepting her own statement the prisoners were to
be kept separate in Cold Bath Fields—there were various directions from
time to time-myself and my brother Magistrate were there when these
statements were read over-I am not sure whether Lea or Roe were there.
Cross-examined by Mr. Jones. Q. Were you there when Lewin Caspar
came to give information of this robbery ? A. Yes, I think it was about twelve
o'clock in the day-Lea, the officer, came and asked me whether 1 would
give a gentleman in waiting a private audience-I found that person to be
Lewin Caspar--when I saw him at the bar, I recognized him to be the person
I had seen in the private room--he said he had been to the Mansion-house,
and they had referred him to our office, for the purpose of detecting the
thieves—in consequence of his application, I directed the officers to give
the necessary assistance--in the interview young Caspar appeared agitated,
and expressed great anxiety about the matter, and be put some papers into
my hand, stating on these he had given an order for the delivery of
some chests of gold dust.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. You have told us some orders were given that the
prisoners should be kept separate ? A. Yes, and I believe they were kept
separate, so that they should have no communication with each other, ex-
cept wlien they came to the office-I should not have read these papers
in my private room, if I had not thought it detrimental to justice to read
them publicly.

for the prisoners, do you mean any body else but Solomon ? A. No.
