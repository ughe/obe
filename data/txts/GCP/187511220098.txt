Re-examined. The prisoner showed me the two eggs in his hand, and I
had previously sold two eggs to the prisoner.
CHARLES DAVIS. I am a printer, of 9, Carlisle Street, Soho-on 10th
November, about 9.80, I heard a cry of “Stop thief," and saw the prisoner
running from Greek Street along Church Street towards the Dials--I only
lost sight of him while he turned the corner--a policeman stopped him-
I first saw the prisoner in Greek Street.
Cross-examined. I was about 6 yards from you when I first saw you
running-you passed me, and I saw you knock a policeman down in Crown
Street.
RICHARD WOKELEY (Policeman ER 32). I was in Crown Street, and
heard a cry of “Stop thief"-I saw the prisoner running, and stopped him
-he threw something out of his right hand) which I picked up, and found
it to be the remains of two eggs—the prosecutor came up and charged bim
with passing a bad florin, which he handed to me--I found on the prisoner
58. 6d. in silver; there were two florins, & shilling, a sixpence, and 4fd. in
bronze—I did not show the remains of the eggs to the prosecutor.
Cross-examined. Policeman 291 went with us to the station ; he is not
here- I did not show the eggs to the prosecutor, because I had enough to
do to hold you.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Mint--these florins are
both bad.
Prisoner's Defence. I received the first florin at a public-house which I
thought was called the Hibernian Stores, in change for a half-crown--the
man ran after me and tore my clothes, and I was taken to the station-I
had nothing to throw away, and if I had I should have had no means of
doing 80-I am more likely to take a bad florin than a shopkeeper, because
he has the means of detecting it, and I have not the person who knockod
the policeman down ought to have been given in custody instead of me
I admit giving Mrs. Bohrback the florin, but I did not know it was bad.
