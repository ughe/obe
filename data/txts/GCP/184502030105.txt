THOMAS BEARD. I am a coach-maker; my premises are at Mount
Pleasant, St. Andrew's, Holborn the prisoner was in my employed
wheelwright. I missed a pair of new hind cabriolet wheels--they had had
the first coat of priming, before they were tired, and the stock-hoops had no
priming-I saw them safe on Monday night, and the tires were put on them,
and stopped--the prisoner worked there till one o'clock on the Wednesday
-I missed the wheels after he was gone--he did not return till the Saturday
morning--I then asked where the wheels were that had been tired-he said
he thought they were on the other side of the plank. I told him to go there
and see he went, and said, "I can't see them; I don't know anything about
them"-I then asked what new pair of wheels he was seen with last Wed
nesday morning about half-past eight o'clock--he said a pair he had made
overtime, and he bought the timber over Blackfriars-bridge I said, "Who
tired the wheels for you ?"-he said, "I can't think of the name now; I
have got his card at home; he lives the first turning to the left down
Drury-lane, Holborn"-I said, “You know where to go to the place for
he said, "Oh, yes"-I sent the officer with him, to see if he could find the
place, they came back, and could not find it-the prisoner had come to work
at my place on the Wednesday morning at seven o'clock, and it was not
his business to leave the place-he said he had sold the wheels belonging
to himself, on the Wednesday, to a cabman on the rank in Gray's Inn-
lane ; he did not know his name, but they put the wheels on a cab, and
drove off--he must have taken them from me on the Tuesday night--we
found these panels at the room where he lived- they are mine, and ought
to have been on my premises..
