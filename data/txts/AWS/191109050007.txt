Detective-sergeant FRANK GIDLER, H Division. At 11.30 a.m., on

(Defence.)
MARY BI.EY. Prisoner and I have been married 54 years. He
was advertised for in the papers for the next-of-kin. I went to the
parish clerk and told him about it, and he advised us to go and see
solicitor. I went to Robinson and Haynes, and asked Mr. Robinson
to take the case over. He said he would, and I gave him all the doou-
ments-w had. He said we were the real owners of some property in
Limehouse Causeway, because there was no owner there. He had the
case for over 11 months, and then said he did not have time to go on
with it. Three months afterwards he and the borough had the houses
done up. My husband and I cannot read or write. We only sent three
postcards, which were written by my grand-daughter at my dietation;
prisoner knew all about it.
Cross-examined. I posted them. The property belonged to Francis,
prisoner's unele. I do not think Mr. Robinson has swindled us out
of it altogether, but just a little bit.
Prisoner, called upon his defence, stated that he did not wish to
add anything to what his wife had said.
Verdict, 44 Guilty. We think his conduct was largely due to his
great ignorance."
Prisoner was released on his own recognisances in E20 to come up
for judgment if called upon.
