ROBERT BROWNLOW. I am a tobacconist, and live at Eagle-terrace, City.
road. On the 11th of May 1 observed my window had been broken, and
missed seven Mersham pipes and a roll of tobacco.
Cross-examined by MR. MELLOR. Q. What kind of a house is yours?
A. I pay 50l. a year--my window does not project--it is a great thorough-
fare.
Ann NANTIN. I live in the prosecutor's house. I was looking out of
window, and saw Brown put bis band in at the window, and take out a pipe
-Manley was with him.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you at the first floor window? 4. Yes-I
saw one of the boys in the road, which looked very suspicious-there were
very few persons passing-Brown had a cap on-when I first saw Manley be
was in the road-Brown came up under the window immediately-I saw part
of Brown's face looking down upon bim--he had an apron-they were under
the window about five minutes, I looking on the whole time-there are no
houses facing our window-the house is just out of the main road.
Manley. Q. Did you not say the person with me had a hat on, and a

PETER CARNEY (police-constable N 76.) I took Manley-I found on
him this needle--I put it to the marks on the window-it appeared to fit
them-by putting it under the putty it would shove the glass in-there were
three places where this needle had made a mark- Manley told me he worked
with this as a delaine-dresser - I took him about five minutes afterwards
- he had an opportunity of getting rid of the pipes.
GEORGE FIVEASH (police-constable N 159.) I took Brown-he said he
was not the boy ; but he ran away when he saw me, though I was in plain
clothes-he ran into a dark shop, and ran under the counter.
ROBERT BROWNLOW re-examined. I had seen my place safe, and the pro-
perty safe, at half-past two o'clock, and I found the window broken, and the
property gone, a little after three.
Manley. The lady said it was not me; she only saw me pass by the shop
in company with a boy who had a hat and a blue jacket; I work at delaine
dressing with this needle.
