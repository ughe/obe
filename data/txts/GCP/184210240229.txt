-she was not in the shop a moment she asked me for a certain quan-
tity, but I do not recollect the manner in which she asked for it-she was
jast there time enough to say so much arsenic, and I said, “We do not sell it"
-she went away immediately we have not very often had applications for
arsenic, not for small quantities--we sell an immense quantity in a larger
way, to manufacturers, but we very seldom sell it in small quantities--now
and then we do, to persons we know-we refuse selling it to persons we
do not know-I cannot tell how many customers come to our house in
the course of a day-perhaps near a hundred I cannot say how many
in an hour-sometimes more, sometimes less there is not five minutes
without a customer coming in.
JOSHUA ALLINGHAM. I am beadle of St. George's, Southwark. On
Sunday evening I received information of the death of the deceased-I
went to the house on Monday morning-I saw the prisoner-Mr. Dunster
was in the room-it is my duty to summon inquests-I asked how long
he had been ill-she said he had left his work on the Wednesday, com-
plaining of cold and pain in the bowels-I then asked when the death
took place-she said on the Saturday night, about ten o'clock-I asked
Why, as he had been ill from the Wednesday, she had had no medical man
to see him-she said that he had an objection to seeing a medical man-I
asked how long she had left the room previous to finding him dead-(I
asked that question to see if it was a case for an inquest to be held)-she
said she had been absent from the room somewhere about half-an-hour,
she could not be certain, that hearing a fall in the room she proceeded to the
room, and found him on the floor-I then asked her what she had done--
