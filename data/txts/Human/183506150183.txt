WILLIAM GLANNON and FRANCIS CHAPPELL were indicted for feloniously assaulting Benjamin George Gill , on the   9th of June , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, part of a watch-chain, value 10 s  .; 3 seals, value 30 s  .; and 1 watch key, value 6 d  .; his property.—2nd COUNT  , for assaulting with intent to rob him. BENJAMIN GEORGE GILL. On Tuesday, the 9th of June, about half-past ten o'clock, I was at the Crown and Anchor, in the Kent-road, in the long room—I remained there till nearly half-past one o'clock—I had been drinking—I had two pints of ale and some cyder—I drank with one or two others who I knew—I did not have above half a quartern of cyder—I might drink a glass or two of ale with others—when I got up to go away I was about half-and-half , not drunk or sober—I was aware of what I was doing, and what was done to me—on coming down stairs, I saw Glannon, whom I have known from a child—I went up to the bar, and told him I was very glad to see him out of trouble, and I partook of a glass of gin from his hand—the other prisoner drank—I do not know him—I knew him by sight before, but not to have much acquaintance with him—I had fre quently seen him before—he was in company with Glannon—after having the gin, I went outside the door, to go away—I left the house, and was followed by Chappell, who pretended to be very drank indeed—I had seen him in the house, and should not think he was drunk then—he ran up against me, and shoved me about—he shoved me up into one corner—Glannon was there at the time, and I was placed on a bench by him, on my back—my legs were put up in the air by some of them—I believe there were others besides the prisoners there—Glannon was lying on my chest, keeping me down on the seat, and I had something put into-my month—I thought it was a piece of old hat, or cloth—I was trying to call out "Murder," but could not from the pressure against my breast, and my mouth was stuffed full of something, what I cannot tell—I cannot say who did it—it prevented my hallooing and making a noise—at that time Chappell was at my fob, in which I had a silver watch, and three gold seals, a gold key, and gold chain—I made great resistance—I found my chain in Chappell's hands—it broke away in the scuffle—I cannot say whether it broke away, at that time or not—he had removed it from my person—I had a scuffle with him for the chain, and cannot say whether he dropped part of the chain, or I did, but it fell—I was lying on my back, and the chain was found on the bench, between my legs—it was broken, and part of it gone—ray trowsers were torn away to get at my watch—I said at the time that I was robbed of my watch, but it was found in my fob—Whitford, the policeman, was there—I saw both the prisoners in the custody of the police, and was quite positive they were the men, and said so—Glannon said at the station-house, that he hoped I should not press the case against them—I have been an old police-officer of Bow-street and Union Hall, for fourteen years.
