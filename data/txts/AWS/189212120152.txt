GEoRGe HAYWARD. I have lived at 68, Ordnance Row, with my
uncle for about two years-I am a stevedore's labourer and work in the
docks-I believe I have worked with a man of the name of Morris-
there are a lot named Morris-I heard of Morris's arrest for being in
possession of stolen stamps-Morris's wife came round on one occasion
as I was going out that was after her husband's arrest-I made
a statement about this to the police- did not see Mrs. Morris
when she came; I was going out as she came in-I had
worked with Morris as a stevedore-a gentleman came round to
see me-I went before the Magistrate I think the prisoner was the
man I saw there-some time before the arrest a man spoke to me about
going into the doeks with him one night; the prisoner looks like the
man--I said before the Magistrate, (0 I I know the prisoner, and have done
so for eighteen months, by the name of Little n"-the man is much
altered I should not like to swear he is the man now -I knew Little
Ben he had no whiskers-when I went before the Magistrate the first
time it was Little Ben I was speaking of-I have heard him called Ben
Morris-I work as stevedore's labourer, and to olean ships' bottoms-
hive on occasions worked with the prisoner-I remember hearing of the
prisoner's arrest; three weeks or a month before hearing of the arrest I
had a conversation with him-he spoke to me about going into the
dock with him-he asked me if I would take a walk with him ; he said
I should come to no harm-I was looking for a ship-I was to do
nothing that I know of-he said something about looking out-he said I
should come to no harm all I had to do was to look out ; something
of that sort-I did not consent to go with him I could not go-after
that, on a Monday morning, he said that the day before he had had
the longest three hours he had ever spent in his life-he said, 44 We
had a drink and went in as lightermen"- boasted that he had got
about E2,000 of foreign paper money, and he would take E50 for it if
he could get it-he did not talk about going abroad-I was trying to
get to sea-nothing was said about forging papers and going to sea-
the man who had this conversation with me was not going to forge the
papers for me; that is what I said myself- believe Morris also wanted
to go to sea, and wanted papers to do so-the first time I gave
evidence and was cross-examined I -did not nod to the prisoner
when I came into Court-it was not said in Court that I
did - the prisoner's wife called on me before I gave evidence
at all, for me to siga a paper for money - I did not see the
prisoner till Ist December-h had been in custody from 24th August
till Ist December before I gave evidence-when I first went into the

speak of-I believed the man I gave evidence about was the man I knew
as Little Morris-I never heard of the name of Bracket-I have
never seen him at my uncle's place, but I believe he used to visit
there
