   Chappell. Q . Did you see me put my finger to my hat? A.  Yes—I was net asked about it at Union Hall. GEORGE THOMAS. I am a shoe-maker, and live in Fryar-street. I was at the public-house with Smith—the prosecutor came from the public-house, up to where I was sitting, and pulled out his watch-chain—I told him to put it in again, and he put it into his fob—I could not see whether he put it all in—he pulled it out as far as it would come, so as leave the watch in his pocket—after this he sat down on the right hand, at a little distance from Smith—at that time there were from fifty to sixty persons sitting down in two rows of settles—there were others sitting at the same bench with us—I did not know them—there was one man sitting between the prosecutor and Smith—Chappell came and sat on my right-hand side, then moved round to my left-hand side, and appeared very dronk—he fell with his arm on the table, and his hat came off—I think it was sham, from his manner—he got up once, and appeared to be looking after something, and then he got up quite firm—he did not get up on his feet, but merely lifted himself up—he went towards the prosecutor—I did not see him do anything—the prosecutor moved himself, and Went towards the corner—I saw Chappell go towards him—what he did I do not know—four or five were round him when they were robbing him—I do not know Glannon at all—I got out of my seat, went a short distance, saw two policemen, and told them a man was being robbed—I had turned my head, and Smith said, "Don't you see they are robbing him?"—I saw them rummaging about—the policemen came and took the prisoners.
   Cross-examined. Q.  If any body had forced Gill away from where he was sitting, up into the comer, you must have seen it? A.  Yes; he went of his own accord—there were from fifty to sixty persons sitting in the different settles at the time—the policemen appeared to come out of a settle—I did not see Glannon at all—I advised Gill to tuck in his seals—when he pulled them out, he said, "See, they have been making an attempt"—he said they had done it in the public-house—I had been drinking but not much—I went to the station-house after the prisoners were taken. EDWARD WHITFORD( police-constable P  164.) I was near the public-house—Thomas called me—I saw the prosecutor lying on his back, on a bench—I took Chappell—the prisoners were on each side of the bench, quite close to the prosecutor—my brother officers took Glannon—the prosecutor said he had been robbed of his watch, and gave me these seals, and part of the chain—Glannon insisted on my searching the prosecutor—I refused to do so, and he instantly collared the prosecutor, and would not let him go till we got to the station-house—on searching him there, he had not lost his watch; he found it in his fob, and part of the chain to it, where it had been broken—I did not hear the prisoners speak to each other on the road—they were locked up—Chappell was not drunk by any means.
