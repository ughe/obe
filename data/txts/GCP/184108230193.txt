WILLIAM SAnkey. I am in the service of Mr. Chancellor, as an omnibus
driver from Kensington to Whitechapel. I became acquainted with the pri-
soner about eighteen months ago-he was in the habit of riding up and down,
and sitting by me on the box-I said to him, I should like to buy an omnibus
-I had a little money in the Bank-he came to me on the 28th of October-he
rode down to Kensington-he gave me half a dozen cards to distribute, and
said he had taken a public-house at last, called the White Bear, at Rateliff-
cross-- he had been looking for a business for some time- he came to me two
days after, and said he wanted to speak to me-he said, “You have got a little
money in the Bank; I have got 7001. ; I want you to let me have your 1001.
out of the Bank; I will deposit the lease of my house in your hands as secu-
rity for it-he said he wanted it to purchase the lease of his bouse, that
his brother and sister were in the house, and he had possession, and was
going on Saturday to get the lease; he would procure the lease on Satur-
day, and deposit it with me-I said, if he would deposit the lease in my
hands as a security I would let him have it--I met him, and went to my
broker, and sold out 1001. stock, and got 90l. in notes, six sovereigns, and
six shillings- I walked down towards Whitechapel, and saw the prisoner,
and gave him nine 101. notes, six sovereigns, and fifteen shillings, in the
presence of Mr. Collier-ho was outside the bar-I was induced to part
with my money because he said he would bring me the lease up in the
evening-I should not bave parted with my money if he had not told me
he bad 7001., and was in possession of the house - I saw him again on the
following Wednesday-Mr. Thompson was with me we went into a pub-
dic-house--we saw the prisoner there-I said, “You have got no public-
house, where is my money?" he said, “Do your best, and do your
Worst," and away he went-I could get no policeman to take himmy
friend went and spoke to him-he said, “He may do his best, and do his
Worst, he shant have a d-d shilling"- I looked for him afterwards every
day, and my answer was, he was gone into the country-I found him on
the 22nd of last month-I saw him on the Sunday morning after I gave
nim the money-he said, "Sankey, I have not taken that house ; come
Ver the way to-morrow morning, to my uncle Tupcott's house, and I will
give you the money"-I went, and saw his wife--she said he was gone into
the country-I never could see any more of him.
