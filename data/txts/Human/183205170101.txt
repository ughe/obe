   JOHN TISDALR. I am a constable. I was going up to Chalk Farm a little after seven o'clock in the evening of the 23rd of April; I saw a mob - the prisoner was sitting on a grass plot, and the prosecutor standing by, asking him for his money - the prisoner made use of very bad language, and swore he had not got a farthing about him; I took off his shoes, and found in his stockings two half-crowns, which I took through some boles; he made a great deal of resistance - when I found the money he said he was very sorry, and wanted the prosecutor to forgive him; he said he would give him 1l. - he said he knew he should be transported, and it would be a good job for him.
 Cross-examined. Q.Do you remember his saying he did not intend to steal it? A. I did not hear that; I recollect his telling the prosecutor they were all friends together - I did not notice any girls there.
 JOHN GAYTON JOLLY (Police-constable S 164). I was on duty near Chalk Farm gardens - I saw the prisoner in custody of Tisdale; I assisted to take him to the watch-house - I said to him, "Don't you think you are a pretty sort of a fellow, to sit with a friend and rob him?" he said,"I did rob him, and it would be the best thing for him and for me to be transported."
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   MARY ANN MITCHELL. I live in Frederick-place, Hampstead-road. On the 23rd of April I was at Chalk Farm, with the prosecutor and the prisoner; I had seen the prosecutor before - I went across the gardens to speak to a friend, and saw the prisoner and him quarrelling - I asked what it was about; the prosecutor said he had lost 7s. out of his pocket, and he could not accuse any one but the prisoner; the prisoner was very much intoxicated, and could not say much to him, but kept walking round and round - we had a good deal to drink; I saw the prosecutor at the Yorkshire Stingo on Sunday week, and on last Sunday; I said I was subpoenaed to come here - he said I had no call to come without I liked, but he did not say why; I had seen him a good many times at Mr. Donald's
 COURT. Q. Did you see the money found? A. No, I did not notice that - I considered it might be done out of a joke; I did not hear the prisoner deny having it.
   ELLEN SCOTT. I live in Portland-street. I have known the prosecutor and the prisoner some time - I was at Chalk Farm on the 23rd of April; I saw the prosecutor, the prisoner, and several others romping on the grass; the form was knocked over; the prosecutor said he had lost 7s., but he could not blame any body but the prisoner; I did not hear him say he found his hand in his pocket; I thought they were friends.
 COURT. Q.What are you? A. A servant out of place - I went to Chalk Farm that day, being Easter Monday. GUILTY. Aged 18.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1335.  WILLIAM WARE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of March , 1 saw, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Thomas Capps ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   THOMAS CAPPS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Old-street-road. On the 24th of March I missed a small brass-backed saw from a stall in front of my house - I had seen it safe half an hour before; it has not been found since - we had a young man to mind the property, but he turned his back for a moment.
   JOSEPH ROBINS. I live opposite the prosecutor. I was at my door a little after nine o'clock in the morning; I saw a person, who has been convicted, take the saw from the board - he gave it to the prisoner, who gave it to another person.
 WILLIAM ROBINS. I saw Porfleet, who has been convicted, take the saw and give it to the prisoner, and they all moved off together.
   ROBERT LOVELOCK. I am an officer. I took the prisoner in Wilderness-row - he denied all knowledge of it.
 CHARLES WALLER. I am a Police-constable. I have a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office - I was present, and know he is the person - (read.)
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1336.  GEORGE WILD was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of George Gallop , from his person.
   GEORGE GALLOP. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Berwick-street, Soho. I was walking in Old-street on the 13th of April, between one and two o'clock; I thought I felt something - I put my hand down, and my handkerchief was gone; I saw the prisoner rolling it up, and putting it into his bosom - I took hold of him, and gave him into custody; he said, "It is not yours."
   JAMES BUCKLE. I am an officer; I received the prisoner in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1337.  ANN WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of April , 4 lbs, weight of cheese, value 3s. 6d., the goods of Francis Bateman ; and that she had been before convicted of felony.
 MATTHEW BATEMAN. I am in the service of  Francis Bateman- he keeps a shop in Theobald's-road. On the evening of the 28th of April I saw the prisoner come to the shop; she did not buy any thing - I missed some cheese from the window; I followed her - I saw something under her shawl; she gave it into another woman's apron; the other woman turned up a mews, and threw the cheese down, saying she did not want it - the prisoner followed her; I kept close to her; I called my brother, and she was taken - this is the cheese; here are 41bs. of it.
   CHARLES NORMAN(Police-constable 48E). I took the prisoner from the witness, and have the cheese.
 THOMAS REED (Police-constable C 159). I have a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office - I know she is the person - (read.)
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. - Confined Seven Days.
