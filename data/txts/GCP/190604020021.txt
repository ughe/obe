NEW COURT; Monday, April 2.
(Before the Common Serjeant.)
DAY, John (49, labourer), pleaded guilty to uttering coun-
terfeit coin ; also to a conviction in 1892 for a similar offence.
A number of convictions proved. Sentence, 12 months' hard
Labour.
FULLER, Arthur James (25, cook), and SMITH, Cecil
Charles Victor Vidalli Fitzgerald (24, athlete). Uttering
counterfeit coin.
Mr. Partridge prosecuted; Mr. Davis defended.
EDWARD SWAIN." I keep a coffee-stall in Bomers Town, at, the
corner of Ossulston Street. On the early morning of February
23 Smith came to my stall and asked for two cigars and &
packet of Bandmaster cigarettes; these were supplied; cost,
5d. He tendered a coin, saying: "Half a sovereign," and I
gave him 9s. 7. change. I had received no other half-sove-
reign that night. I put the coin in the basin; in the morning,
on counting the takings, I found this was a Jubilee sixpence;
then I communicated with the police.
Cross-examined. I do not remember having had bad coins
passed at my stall before. The coin in question was very like
a half-sovereign and I was deceived. I have known Fuller as
& customer, not Smith. Neither has given me a false coin
before. Fuller is a coffee-stall keeper; Smith helps him.
• CHARLES GOODCHILD. Detective Sergeant, Y Division. On
February 25, at 9.30 a.m., I saw Smith at the Police Station.
I said: "I am a police officer, and am making inquiries respect-
fing & gilded sixpence which it is alleged you uttered to a
coffee-stall keeper at Ossulston Street on February 22 and ob-
"taining the sum of 9s. 7d. by false pretences." I showed him
the coin. He said: "No, master, I have never seen it before ;
I was never at the coffee-stall and know nothing whatever about
it.” At Somers-Town Station Smith was identified by Swain.
After Smith was put in the cell he sent for me. He said:
"I have told you a lie. I am an honest man and
wish to tell you the truth." I then cautioned him, and
he went on to make this statement, which I took down, and
which he signed. “... I have been assisting Fuller at
the Royal Café in Euston Road for the past two months. On
the 22nd inst. I was in the car; Fuller called me and showed
me a gilded coin, saying, "What do you think of this; is it &
genuine one or not 1 I said, I have my doubts,' and asked
him for a knife to try the edge. I cut the edge of the coin,
and said to him, I think you have been done this time, this
is not a half-sovereign.' Another man in the car said it was a
Jubilee tanner. Fuller said, 'I took it off a customer.' He
gave me a description of a man, and I went out to find him,
