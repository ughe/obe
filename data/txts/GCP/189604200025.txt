ROBERT HERIOT. I am one of the firm of C. J. Hambro and Son, of
70, Old Broad Street-we have a correspondent, Madame de Tejada, in
Cadiz, with whom we have had correspondence for some years—I am
familiar with her writing, and that of the olerk who writes her letters -
she has considerable means—we have funds of hers in our custodywe
wrote her this letter of March 2nd; I produce a press copy of it on folio
11 of letter-book 83896– I received this letter, dated March 10th, pur.
porting to be a reply to our letter of the 2nd, and to be signed by Madame
de Tejada-I believed it to be genuine I have since then examined it
more carefully, and I now say that, to the best of my belief, it is a forgery
-my firm wrote a letter to Madame de Tejada on March 16th; this is
the press copy. (This letter was objected to, as the original, which was not
produced, was not shown to have come to the defendant's knowledge.). I re
ceived this document G, dated March 21st, containing the document F,
by ordinary course of post-I did not examine them critically, but acted
on them in the belief that they were genuine (Gwas a letter from
Maria de Tejada, stating that she desired the £6,328 Russian 4 per cent
bonds to be realised, and the proceeds handed to Manuel Gomez de Tejada,
roho would call with her authority; and that she was starting that might
for Madrid, where she should stay at the Hotel de Roma. A was an authority to
sell the £6,328 Russian bonds, and deliver the proceeds to Manuel Gomes de
Tejada.) I have since critically and carefully examined G, and, to the
best of my belief, it is a forgery - the letter of March 21st was received
by post on the morning of March 26th these four letters, N N N
and E, bear, to the best of my beliet, genuine signatures of Madame de
Tejada; we have received them in the ordinary course of business from
her they are dated February 5th, January 4th, January, 17th, and
March 7th, 1896, respectively- they were acknowledged in due course
I came into the room where the prisoner was on March 26th, and I
directed Mr. Harry Hambro to put certain questions to him after he
had left, I examined and compared the signatures of letters, and directed
what telegrams should be sent to Cadiz and Madrid in consequence of
the replies, I communicated with the police on the morning of the 27th
on the 27th, after I had shown B to the prisoner, and heard what reply

station and charged him—the bonds were sold for £6,450 Ils. 2d, on
the morning of the 26th, in consequence of the letter that we thought to
be genuine.
