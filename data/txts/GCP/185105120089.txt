1171. MARY ANN FARMER was again indicted for receiving 2 brooches,
1 guard-chaio, 4 rings, and other articles, value 501. 153. ; the goods of John
William Tripe, knowing them to have been stolen.
JOHN WILLIAM TRIPE, M.D. I missed from my house two brooches, four
seals, a butter-knife, a pair of pistols, a powder-flask, and a variety of articles,
worth 701. or 801.--these articles (produced) are all mine-about 101. worth
are not recovered yet-this opal brooch is worth 201.
Edwin Johns. I took these articles from Dr. Tripe's at various times,
and took them to the prisoner to get what she could on them- she gave me
21. 38. for this opal brooch, and 15s. for these two seals; my share altogether
was about 31. 58.- I told her I took them from Dr. Tripe's.
John Hales. On 10th Feb., Farmer pawned this brooch at my shop-I
advanced her 11., subsequently another 11., and then 55,--the intrinsic value
is about 51.- I should be glad to sell it for that I gave it to the officer
seal was pledged with it for 138., which Farmer afterwards redeemed - I did
not purchase the ticket of the brooch, she offered it to me as security for the
last 5s. I lent her-she said she would call and take it away in a day or two
-I have had it three months.
Thomas KELLY (police-sergeant, & 2). I took the prisoner and told her
the charge--she said she never had the things in her possession-I believe
there are two or three buckles wbich she never did have.
Cross-examined by Mr. Robinsos. Q. You found some property at Dr.
Tripe's, which had been stolen by Johns, but had not been in Farmer's pos-
session ? A. Yes; they are not charged.
Prisoner. Half of the things I have never seen.
Guilty. Aged 36. - Transported for Seven Years.
(JOHNS received an excellent character, and was strongly recommended to
mercy by the Prosecutor. Aged 17.-Confined Six Months).
1172. JOHN FYNN and THOMAS MITCHELSON, stealing 1 band-
kerchief, value 2s.6d.; the goods of Dennis Woodin, from his person.
MR. CAARTEEN conducted the Prosecution,
Dennis Woodin. I am a clerk in a mercbant's house, and live at 11,
Downham-toad, Kingsland. On 6th May, about nine in the evening, I was
in Bishopsgate-street-Baun spoke to me, and I missed my handkerchief-I
had had it half-an-hour before I went up a court with him, about 100 yards
off, and saw two men coming down it as we went up-they passed us; it
was too dark to recognise them we went into a public-house, and then to the
station, where I saw this bandkerchief (produced)-it is mine, and is marked
--I had it that night.
JOSEPH Baun. I am clerk to a Berlin importer, and live at 58, Lombard-
street. On 6th May, I was in Bishopsgate-street, about twelve yards bebind
Mr. Woodin, and saw the prisoners coming towards me into the City-hey
passed me, turned back, and walked behind Mr. Woodin, across the road-1
went at the same rate as they did, on the other side of the road- Fynn lifted
up Mr. Woodin's pocket, put his hand in, took it out again, and put it under
his coat-Mitchelson was behind him, not half a yard from him, and turned
round to see if anybody observed him- they both went up Angel-alley-I
spoke to Mr. Woodin, and we followed them, and met them about half-way
-1 knocked at the door of a house in the court, to allow them to pass that
they might have no suspicion of our following them-I told Woodin to go
into a public-house, and I followed the prisoners into Bishopsgate-street, and
gave them into custody.
