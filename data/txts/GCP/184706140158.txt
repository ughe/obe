1489. ISAAC ELKIN was indicted for stealing 60 pieces of silk hand-
kerchiefs, value 621.; the goods of Henry Tucker and others :-2nd Count.
calling them 420 silk handkerchiefs.
MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM KAYESS. I am in the service of Henry Tucker and others, silk
mercers, in Trump-street. On the 26th of April the prisoner came to the
warehouse-he wanted sixty pieces of Korahs (that is East India silk hand-
kerchiefs) for a shipping order to Hamburgh; and he wished to have them
on approbation for the approval of a third party, if he chose forty or fifty
pieces out of them he would return them to us to ship, and the others were to
be returned to us-this is the invoice that was sent with the goods.
Court. Q. This invoice says, “ Bought on approbation;" that is, that
the party buying bad a right to reject? A. Yes.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Was it quite understood that these were to be re-
turned for shipment, supposing they were bought? A. Yes; I did not sell
them outright to the prisoner, but we keep no memorandum-book in our es-
tablishment-we keep no waste-book, therefore we send out an invoice of
this sort, and put on it "On approbation "-if the prisoner had wanted to
purchase for himself I should not have let him had them I had no authority
to let him have them-- I was not to be brought in communication with the
third party; but some of these were to be selected, then returned to me, and
I was to ship them-our terms for these goods were nett cash-it was under-
stood by me that the prisoner wished to have them for about a couple of days
-part of them would have been rejected, or the whole might have been
kept-in either case we should have shipped them I should not have parted
with them except on those terms-I directed them to be sent to Bucklers-
bury with the invoice- I went there about four days afterwards, and found
the prisoner's premises were closed-I was not able to find the prisoner.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. How long have you been in your
employer's service ? A. Fifteen years I have known the prisoner two or
three years, from having had a few dealings with him at our premises-the
first time was in 1844, to the amount of 1601.we had dealings with him up
to the time of this transaction this is the first transaction that has not been
paid, to my knowledge we never applied to him for custom to my know-
ledge-I cannot swear we have not-nett cash is cash from seven to ten days.
the bill for these goods (looking at one) is dated 13th October; it was paid by
a check, dated 11th November-I should not call that cash-our clerk who
made the entry in the book is not here-this might be a matter of accommo-
dation-- I cannot tell what our clerk would do--goods very rarely come back
to us to be shipped-I never had such a transaction myself before--there is
no drawback allowed on these goods-when the prisoner came to me, I de
murred to let him have the goods on approbation-I wished him to have
them in the usual way it is not the custom of our house to send goods out
on approbation-if he had bought them in the usual way I would have let
him had them-he might have had dealings with us ten days before this-
these are our invoices- I have all the books bere—they are in our clerk's
writing-we have nothing to do with the book or money transactions."
