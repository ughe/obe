 720.  WILLIAM RIDER was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of December , 2 bushels of mixed corn, called horse corn, value 4s., the goods of Stephen Trehern.
 STEPHEN TREHERN. I  keep the White Horse public-house, at Uxbridge. On the 31st of December my son caught this man in the act of stealing some mixed corn from my stable at the Red Lion public-house, at Southall, where I keep post horses; here is a sample of it, and I believe it to be mine - here is barley and white pease and Archangel oats, and some other corn mixed together.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Do other persons keep their horses there as well as you? A. Yes, there are a great many horses there; I do not know what they eat.
   WILLIAM TREHERN. I am the prosecutor's son. The prisoner goes about with a cart - I saw him at my father's stable door at Southall, on the 31st of December - he had this corn in a sack under some hay in his cart; I went up to him, and asked what business he had there - he said, he only came for a little rubbishing hay to make hands; we rent the stable of the person who keeps the Red Lion public-house: I made the prisoner turn out the hay, and when I saw the corn, I said, "What is here?" - he said,"I don't know;" I opened and found it was our mixtures, which we send up weekly - I compared it, and it matched exactly - it is an unusual mixture; I was on horseback: I do not know how long he had been in the cart - I missed some corn from my father's loft, where there were about forty bushels, which had been shot on the preceding day.
 Cross-examined. Q. But might not that have gone to feed your horses? A. It might - I cannot say how long his cart might have been there - there was some loose hay in it: I have not brought any of my father's mixture here, only a sample of what was found in the cart.
     NOT GUILTY.

 722.  MARGARET VAUGHAN was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of January , 1 shawl, value 5s., the goods of Mary White, Spinster.
 MARY WHITE. I lodge in  Mary-le-bone-court, up three pair of stairs. On the 24th of January the prisoner came to my door and knocked - I said, "Come in;" she said, she wanted to speak to me: she asked if I knew a person of the name of Olcrenshaw - I said Yes, and she said she wanted the loan of my yellow shawl; I said, she had shawls enough - she said she had been out the night before, and had lost her shawl; and she wanted to speak to me and could not come out without one: I lent it to her, and have not seen her since.
   MARY ANN OLCRENSHAW. I never sent the prisoner for a shawl; I knew her for some time - she had lived where I had used to call to see a friend.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met her the night before, and asked her to lend me a shawl - she said she had not one, but if I went to the prosecutrix I might borrow one of her: I had the misfortune to lose it.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined One Month.
 723.  JOHN FOX was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of February , 6 shillings and 3 sixpences, the monies of John Jones.
   ELIZABETH JONES. I am the wife of John Jones - we live in Maiden-lane. On the 4th of February I went into the shop to snuff the candle, and found the prisoner under the counter; he rose up, and I caught hold of his collar: I found the till drawn half way out, and the bowl in which the silver had been in the till, laying by his side on the ground; I had seen it safe when he came in at half-past seven - he asked for a pint of small beer - I said I would rather not serve him; he asked again, and then I said I would draw it for him if he would go out again directly - he put me down a sixpence, and then I saw the money was all safe in my till - there was six shillings and three sixpences; he then had a pennyworth of bread, and four pennyworth of meat, which came to the whole of his sixpence - he was at this time sitting on a form in the shop.
   JAMES GROOM. Mrs. Jones's girl came to my house, and asked me to step in - I went in and found the prisoner there; Mrs. Jones had him by the collar - she said, "He has taken out my till, and got out my money" - he said,"I have no money but four shillings of my own about me."
   BENJAMIN PARRETT. I took the prisoner - he produced 7s. 6d. and one halfpenny, which he said was his own money.
   JOHN HUTT. I am the watch-house keeper - I produce these six shillings and three sixpences, which I received from Parrett.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the shop - I asked for a pint of beer, which I got; I then asked if she had any cooked meat - she said, "No, but here are some saveloys;" I said, "Just warm one," which she did - I then had a penny loaf, and gave some persons who were in the shop part of the beer: I then knocked at the room door - Mrs. Jones came out and said, "What do you want here?" - I said I wanted another pint of beer, and that I had called for it twice, and that she had not heard me; she then asked the two persons in the shop if I had - they said they were asleep, and had not heard me: she then went to the counter, and said, "My till is out, and I have lost four or five shillings I know" - I said,
 "I have six shillings and three sixpences of my own money."
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
