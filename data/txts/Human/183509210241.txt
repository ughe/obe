I remember riding outside the omnibus called the "Emperor," on the Monday after Whit-Monday—It was driven by a man named Sylvester—I got up at Paddington—I observed an omnibus of Aaron Bray's, with its pole close to the door—that was at the end of Farringdon-street—a man of the name of Cuddy  Mann was driving—he drove against the hroses, and knocked them down, I walked on some time, and then they got up—I was then frightened at being outside, and went inside—the manner in which the omnibus of Aaron Bray had acted, frightened me—the pole was so close, that the passengers could not get in, and they would not go in either of the buses.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALL . Q.  Are you a proprietor? A.  No; I have got a sum of money lent to two omnibuses in the New-road, called the "Rose," but I am not a proprietor—I have no share in them—one formerly belonged to my father—one belongs to my brother, and the other to Meredith—my name is on it, but it is obliterated, and has never appeared—It was covered before it came out of the painter's loft—I have only an interest in the two "Roses," and one without the name at all, which belongs to Meredith—I do not know whether Meredith is connected with the defendants on the New-road—I am bail for Richard Carpenter, one of the defendants, not for any of the others—I am in the habit of riding from Paddington to my other shop, two or three times a-day—this was the second week that the omnibus started, called "Aaron Bray"—I cannot tell whether the "Albion" was there—I did not see it, but I have seen the omnibus of Braw go to fetch the "Albion" out of the stable—there were several of Mr. Bolton's there at the time I was riding.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Which omnibus did you see go to fetch the "Al bion?" A.  Two of Aaron's buses , one round at the end, the other square.
   Q.  How came you to lend money on these omnibuses? A.  There was a widow of the same name as myself in difficulty, her husband died—I lend a little money, till it came to 300 l .—I was compelled to take these omnibuses, and a man of the name of Lyons, whom Mr. Norton knows very well, contrived to do the widow out of one of them—I have always understood that Lyons and Nortons were related, and I was afraid to tackle  them.
