   Cross-examined.  I saw my father stab my mother, but not George—I said at the police-court that I changed my trousers, and that my brother George saw me do it—I said first that I changed them in the yard, and afterwards that I changed them at a neighbour's; I left them in a tub of, water there; I did not fetch them—my brother found them there, and gave them to the police—I did not before the police were brought in go into the yard and empty some dirty water, throw it into the yard, take some clean water, and put it into my mother's room—I did go into the yard; there is a water tap there; that is where we go for clean water when we want it—I know some people named Driver; I passed Martha Driver and went into the yard, and threw some dirty water away and took some clean water upstairs—I did not take any water upstairs—this knife belongs to my father and this other to my mother; I saw this one on that night ( A brown one )—my mother was sitting quietly on a chair when she was stabbed, but my father was not—she had all her clothes on except her bonnet—after the stab she fell back; the stab was like this ( A thrust ); the knife passed right through her clothes—I have been locked up for stabbing my father in the street; he was taken to the hospital, and very nearly died—I have never struck my mother; I liked her; she was good; she was quiet; we never quarrelled; she often got drunk—I have struck my father on other occa sions—I dislike him.
