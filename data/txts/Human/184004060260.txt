MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Do I understand you have no doubt the expres sion was used before she mentioned the teaspoonful? A.  Yes, I have no doubt of that—I am quite sure it was before she said she bad sent some laudanum in a bottle—I did not ask her whether the bottle had any label or direction—she seemed rather anxious to impress upon me that there was no label on it—I had recommended her to Mr. Kelly's family—I had a good opinion of her as a wet-nurse—I knew very little of her, except having been servant in another family—I believe she was seduced.
   COURT . Q.  You select wet-nurses from their physical qualities? A.  Unless I know any thing of their moral character—I believe the common people administer laudanum very rashly—there is a great consumption of opium among the lower classes in London—they deal very rashly with that drug—the same as ardent spirits, they give it to children, and to children of a certain age it is poison, if they give a certain quantity. WILLIAM JONES. I am a chemist and druggist, and live at No. 34, Tavistock-place, Russell-square—I know the prisoner—I have often seen her in my shop—she came to my shop one afternoon about the middle of March—she was alone—she purchased three articles, one was laudanum, the other acidulated drops, and the third I do not recollect—she had a small square bottle of laudanum—this bottle is very similar to it—it was the same size as this—she asked if I was in the habit of selling laudanum—I said, "I do sometimes spare it, but I always require to know for whom it is, and for what purpose"—she said her mistress, Mrs. Kelly, had de sired her to ask if I would spare a small quantity, a shilling bottle—I said, "I suppose I must not refuse Mrs. Kelly"—I knew Mrs. Kelly—I did not ask her for what purpose it was—she said her mistress was in the habit of taking it, being a bad sleeper—I put two labels on the bottle, "lauda num," "poison"—it was wrapped up in a piece of paper like this—( produc  ing a bottle)—I am not certain whether it was sealed or tied—the cork was secured like this—when I gave it her I begged her to give it into the hands of Mrs. Kelly, and desire her to keep it under her own care—it was pure laudanum—I heard of the death of the child between Tuesday and Wednesday, at the end of March—I did not see the prisoner at all on Tuesday, the last day of March, nor on Wednesday, the first of April—she never stated any thing to me about any accident happening by means of the boy—I never advised her what quantity would be proper to give to any person.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have not a bottle folded up as the one you gave the prisoner on the outside? A.  No—a person unaccustomed to the trade cannot fold up bottles—it is not a common habit with me to seal them—I very rarely seal them—I do sometimes, certainly—sealing would confine the ends of the paper.
   Q.  Do you think a person not in your business could fold up a bottle in that manner, and seal the ends in the way you send it out? A.  They could a bottle which had been previously wrapped up, they could wrap it up in the same paper, and seal it—it would not be so neatly folded—the labels were fastened on with gum.
