   JAMES BETHELL. I am the beadle. I was in the street at half-past nine o'clock, and heard the cry of Stop thief! I took the prisoner to the watch-house, and found nothing on him; the prosecutor charged him with this robbery, and he did not deny it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was walking with a friend, and got joking him - when we got to Probert's hotel, he was offended; I ran down Bedford-street, this gentleman was coming after me, and I thought it was him, as he had said he would put me into the watch-house.
 Mr. CHURCHILL. He said at Bow-street, that there was a row, and he ran, and I followed him, crying Stop thief! he said he heard the rattles sprung, but the watchmen do not come till ten o'clock.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1719.  JAMES FAYES was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , 1 pewter pot, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Samuel Cordeux.
 SAMUEL CORDEUX. I  keep the  Masons' Arms, public-house. Devon-shire-street, Mary-le-bone. This pot is mine; I never gave the prisoner leave to do any thing with it.
   WILLIAM MITCHAM. I was in Margaret-street, Cavendish-square, on the 14th of September, and saw the prisoner; I watched him down Castle-street, where I touched him on the arm, and saw this pot concealed under his coat, which was buttoned; I took it from him; he wished me to let him go, and said he was out of work, and he had been three weeks in London,
   ROBERT WILLIAMS. I am an officer, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner. I was in distress, and found it in the street. GUILTY. Aged 55.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1720.  ROBERT ROPER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of July , 1 fork, value 10s., the goods of John Jackson Cuff.
   JOHN CUFF. I am the son of John Jackson Cuff - he keeps the  Freemasons' Tavern. We had missed a fork about a week before the 13th of July. The prisoner had frequently been employed as an occasional waiter, and had access to it.(Property produced and sworn to).
   THOMAS WEBB. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner brought this fork to pawn, but we saw the name of Cuff partly erased on it, and detained him.
   SAMUEL LACK. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and found a number of duplicates at his lodgings.
 Prisoner. I leave myself to your mercy. GUILTY. Aged 37.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Months.
 1721.  RICHARD TRUELOVE and WILLIAM EDWARDS were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 1 boys' dress, value 7s., the goods of James Corss.
   JOHN HUBE. I am shopman to James Corss, a tailor, who has a shop in Chiswell-street. On the 12th of September I saw the two prisoners walk to and fro; I then saw one of them take the dress; they went up Whitecross-street; I followed, and they dropped it between them.
   JOHN FELLS. I am an officer, and took the prisoners.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 TRUELOVE'S Defence. I am innocent. I was going home to dinner.
 EDWARDS' Defence. I had been to Maiden-lane, and as I turned the corner the gentleman laid hold of me.
 TRUELOVE -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 EDWARDS -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped and Discharged.
 1722.  ELLEN SINGLETON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 6 decanters, value 3l. 17s., the goods of Barnaby Dunn , her master, in his dwelling-house.
 BARNABY DUNN. I am a  glass and china-man, and live in the Haymarket. The prisoner was my servant- she left me on the 1st or 2d of May, and after she was gone I missed these six decanters from a glass case in my shop, where they had been with many others; she had access to the shop in a morning, and when she went out; the cases are kept locked, but the key is always in the door - these are the decanters - two of them are odd ones, and I have the fellows; the others have the mark at the bottom, which is always taken off when they are sold; I am quite sure I had six decanters like these in my shop, shortly before she left.
   HANNAH LEWIS. I live in Princes-street - my husband is a boot-maker. On Saturday, the 6th of May, the prisoner, whom I had not seen before, came to my house with a woman whom I knew; the prisoner said she had let that old lady have a pair of decanters to get some fruit for her stall, and on the 4th of May she had let her have another pair to get some clothes for herself; she wanted me to lend her the money, and go with her to get them out before her mistress knew it; she said she lived with a lady in Regent-street, who performed at the Opera; I went to Mr. Cameron's, the pawnbroker, in the Strand, with the woman, but I did not get the decanters out; Mr. Cameron had stopped them - I did not own them as mine; I had known the old woman by seeing her stand every day with fruit at the corner of my kitchen window; I found there were six decanters at Cameron's - she said there were but four.
   HENRY POWELL. I am shopman to Mr. Cameron, a pawnbroker. On the 1st of May a woman pawned a pair of decanters for 10s. in the name of Mack; on the 5th of May she brought two pairs more; the same woman came on Saturday the 6th of May, with the prisoner.
