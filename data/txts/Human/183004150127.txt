I was on duty in Fludyer-street on the 21st of March; I came off duty at twelve o'clock and observed a man in King-street, opposite Fludyer-street, and suspecting something was wrong, I walked to the end of King-street, and saw Cole, a Police-constable, in Charles-street - I told him, then came back, and saw the same man opposite Fludyer-street; I went up Fludyer-street on the right side, and in turning back again on the left hand I heard a noise in the prosecutor's area and saw the two prisoners in the area against the window - they saw me and drew back; I sprung my rattle - Graves came up; I asked what he did there - he said some person had thrown his hat over into the area; I said I must take him into custody - he used very rough language; we had a struggle for a few minutes - he called to the other prisoner to came up, but he did not, and with the assistance of another man we got him over the rails; in the mean time Cole came up - I charged him with the man in the area, and with assistance I took Graves to the watch-house; on his person I found a centrebit and a broken knife; I returned and found Bennett in Cole's custody.
   JOHN COLE. I am a Police-constable. I was on duty in Charles-street between twelve and one o'clock; I heard a noise in Fludyer-street, and proceeded to the spot; I found Crane tussling with Graves - Crane said he had come out of the area, and that there was another man in the area; I got a light, went down into the area, and found Bennett in the coal-hole - I asked him what he did there; he said some one had thrown his hat over - I found a skeleton-key on his person; in the corner where he was concealed there were several other keys, a chisel, a small crow-bar, the handle of a centre-bit, and several other skeleton-keys, which I gave up to Prior.
 Cross-examined by Mr. HEATON. Q. He made no resistance? A. No, he seemed very much dejected.
   JOSEPH PRIOR. On Thursday night I went to Fludyer-street and found Bennett in custody; I went down the area - the kitchen window was lifted up, the pannel of the inside shutter bored throught with a centre-bit, and this piece and this small piece of the pannel were lying in the area; I fitted them into the pannel - I afterwards got the centre-bit and fitted it to some of the holes which had been bored; they agreed with it - after going inside the kitchen this blade of a knife was picked up, in my presence, by an officer, who is not here; I have compared it with the handle found upon Graves - they correspond exactly; the blade had been put through the hole, and I suppose snapped off, and fell inside - the impression on the shutter exactly corresponded with the crow-bar; the space between the inside of the window and the shutter I should suppose is not above two or three inches.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is the kitchen under the house? A. Yes; the coal-hole is under the street - I did not find the centre-bit.
 Prisoner Graves. Q. Where was the blade of the knife found? A. On the ledge under the window; I never said it was found on the table - I said I was near the table when it was picked up.
 MR. HYNDMAN. I was in bed, and was alarmed by the Police knocking at my door; I believe I went to bed last- I make a practice of going round my house before I go to bed; the shutters were fastened with a bar - the window sash, I believe was not fastened; there is a catch to it, but it is so stiff it is useless - I have no doubt the sash was down that night, but I did not examine that; it is the cook's business to fasten the window - she is not here; it might have been partly up for what I know.
 MR. HEATON. Q. At what hour did you go round the house? A. I think about half-past eleven o'clock - I had a light in my hand, and can say the shutters were whole; I distinctly remember there was no appearance of violence on the shutter.
     NOT GUILTY.

ELIZABETH BOYD was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Mary Townsend , on the 11th of April , and stealing 1 jacket, value 5s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 4s., and 1 pair of drawers, value 6d., the goods of James Bills ; 1 shifts, value 6d., and 1 petticoat, value 6d., the goods of Mary Townsend.
 JAMES BILLS. On Saturday night, the 10th of April, between twelve and one o'clock, I met  Mary Townsend in Tothill-street, and went home with her; I saw her fasten the door - I staid all night with her; I awoke about half-past five o'clock, and missed my things - she was in bed.
 MARY TOWNSEND. I live in  Union-court, Orchard-street. I took this man home, and locked the door - I only occupy one room; the landlady does not live in the house- I was awoke in the morning by Bills, and missed my shift and petticoat; I found the door open - Bills was making a noise about his things; Wilkins, the Policeman, came up to know if we had lost any thing - the staple of the lock was not forced; I do not know how it was opened.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not give me that bundle? A. No, on my oath I did not; you had not taken me home drunk on the Wednesday.
   JOSEPH WILKINS. I am a Policeman. About half-past five o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner cross Orchard-street with a bundle, and stopped her; she said they were her own things, and finding they were men's clothes I took her to the watch-house; I found the witnesses, who owned them - I lent the prosecutor a coat; he went to the watch-house, and claimed them in the prisoner's presence.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met that woman at the end of the court, as I was going home; she asked me to be so kind as to take these things to my lodging, saying a man had left them with her, as he had no money - I had taken her home drunk a few days before, and she burst her door open.
   MARY TOWNSEND re-examined. It is false; I did not give it her - there were no marks of violence on my door, but I am positive I locked it.
 JAMES BILLS. I did not examine the lock.
     NOT GUILTY.
