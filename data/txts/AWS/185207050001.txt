Before Mr. Recorder and the First Jury.
693. JAMES POPE (see page 107) stealing 11 cloeks, value 314.: the
property of Otto Alexander Berens, and another, his masters; and ADOLPH
HIRSCHFIELD receiving the same; and also as an aceessory after the fact:
to which
POPE pleaded GUITy.
Mr. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
SAMUEL WOoD. I am manager to Mr. Otto Alexander Berens, and
another, of St. Paul's Churehyard, it is a large establishment, and they
employ a great number of people-among other things they deal in clocks
-the prisoner, Pope, was in their service-my attention was called to his
conduct on 30th April-] went with him to a house where he lived, at
Dalston, and there found a good deal of property belonging to my em-
ployers-the next day Pope made a statement to me, in consequence of
which, on 3rd May, I went to the house of the prisoner Hirschfield, in
Bishopsgate-street Within-Pope remained outside-be gave me the number
of the shop-it is in the public street; it is a shop for the sale of elocks,
watehes, jewellery, and miscellaneous articles of that sort-| saw four
or five elocks in the window before I went in- found his wife in the
shop-she called him, and he came forward-] asked him to let me look at
one of the clocks in the window-he did so, and I recognised it as one of
Mr. Beren's-I asked him the price-he said either 558. or 658., I do not
exactly recollect which-) then asked for another, and so went through five
(looking at five clocks)- believe this marble one is the first one I looked at,
and these are the others-] only asked the price of one-they were all on the
counter at the time-four of them were in the window and one in the shop-
both the marble clocks were in the window-after examining the elocks, I
told Hirschfield they were stolen, and inquired where he bought them-he
said he bought them of some young man living at Kingsland, that his wife
purebased the first for 168., and the black one he gave either 15s. or 16s. for,
