The prisoner was my carman—it was his business to deliver my parcels—he went out with the cart—a paper of this kind was sent out with the parcels, and the different persons were to put their signatures to it, and the carriage and cartage is written against it—it was his duty to receive the money from the parties to whom he delivered the parcels—on the 28th of January, we sent three hampers, and one bottle, to John Heath—this is the paper sent to him—Mr. Heath's parcel was 1 s . 9 d .; and he brought home 1 s . 2 d .; a difference of 7 d .—the figure 9 has been made into 2—I got this paper from the book-keeper—he paid 1 s . 2 d . to the book-keeper, not to me.

ALEXANDER PAYNE was indicted for stealing for stealing, on the   27th of August , 1834, 1 pair of trowsers, value 1 l  .; 1 coat, value 2 l  . 1 waist coat, value 10 s  .; 4 handkerchiefs. value 1 l  .; 1 pair of stockings, value 1 s  .; 2 razors, value 5 s  .; and 2 shirts, value 18 s  .; the goods of James Shuttleworth , in the dwelling-house of Ellen Davis ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. JAMES SHUTTLEWORTH. I lodged in Market-street, St. George-the-Martyr, Southwark. The prisoner slept in the same bed with me for a mouth—I had seen these articles safe on the morning of the 27th of August, 1834, at a quarter before five o'clock—he was in bed at that time—I got up to go to work, and returned at three o'clock in the aftrernoon, and the property was all gone—there was a pair of trowers worth 1 l . a coat worth 2 l . a waistcoat worth 10 s ., a pair of stockings worth 1 s ., four handkerchiefs worth 1 l ., tow razors worth 5 s ., two shirts worth 18 s .—that is a verylow value—the prisoner never came back—I never saw him again till he was in custody—these things were all folded up, and in the cupboard—the door was not locked—I think no one could have gone there till the landlady did, at nine o'clock in the morning—he left an old coat, shirt, and waistcoat, there.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Had these things been long in wear? A.  No; about three months—Mr. West made my coat—it coat 3 l  —I could not have sold it for more than 1 l . 5 s .—I bought the shirts in Regent-street, and gave 9 s . a-piece for them—I could have got 8 s . a-piece if I had sold them—the prisoner was a tailor—he did not keep anything in that place—I had not known him before he came to lodge there—no other young men lodged in that room—I left him in bed at a quarter before five o'clock—he had no work to do, and I worked late and early—the landlady had called him that morning, and I got up. ELLEN DAVIS. I am landlady of the house, I remember the prisoner and the prosecutor lodging with me—I went into the room at nine o'clock in the morning—no one bad been in between five and nine o'clock—I saw the shirt and coat which the prisoner left in the room, and missed the other things—the prisoner left me that day without notice, and owed me 8 s . 10 d .—he had not paid his lodging.
