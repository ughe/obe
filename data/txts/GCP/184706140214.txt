about ten o'clock next morning, and went out again almost immediately
I did not see him follow my mother at that time, he went down stairs after

spoke to me-my mother returned up stairs shortly before twelve o'clock -

FREDERICK BROCK. I am a son of the prisoner and prosecutrix-I saw
my father when he first came in-be came up stairs-he had no knife then, it
was in his pocket, I suppose-about half an hour after he came in, he took it
out of his pocket, and ran down stairs after my mother, and ran her up again-
the knife was open-when he came up again be shut the knife, and put it into
his pocket-my mother remained down stairs till about a quarter to twelve
o'clock, she then came into the room again, and went round the table-he fol-
lowed her round, went behind her, and stabbed her-when he was in the act, my
brother struck his arm to ward off the blow-it was a back-handed blow-it
pointed towards the fleshy part of her arm-he appeared to be striking to
wards the left breast, before my brother struck his arm and diverted the blow
-at the station he said he wished he had finished her-lie was at the station
about half-past one-he had been home more than two hours-he had carried
the knife in his pocket for two years.
