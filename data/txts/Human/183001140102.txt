 MR. ALLEY. Q. Did you go on the Monday with the delivery-ticket? A. Yes, previously, and not seeing him there, and not receiving the money, I brought it back - I was told he was gone out.
 COURT. Q. What time would it take to convey the bristles from Botolph-wharf to Paucras-lane? A. About twenty minutes - that is not in the way to Crispin-street, but quite out of it.
   HENRY CRUMP. I am a clerk to the prosecutor. On the 4th of January, about half-past three o'clock, I was sent to Crispin-street, and staid there about a quarter of an hour - no cart came while I was there, nor did the prisoner come; I inquired for Mr. Pratt, but did not find him.
 THOMAS BROWN. I am warehouse-keeper at the wharf. I recollect the prisoner and Spaeth being there - I saw the bristles delivered into the cart; the prosecutor's clerk said they should not be taken away without being paid for - it was afterwards agreed that they should be taken away, and paid for at the prisoner's house, on delivery.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did not Spaeth agree to let them go, provided he would pay him next day? A. I heard no such conversation between them -I am rather deaf, but if there had been a conversation of that sort in my presence, I should have heard it.
 MR. ALLEY. Q.Upon what understanding did he agree that they should go? A.They were to be paid for immediately on delivery.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q.Was any time mentioned at which they would arrive? A. I think two o'clock was named - there was about time enough for them to get there by two.
 JOHN ROACH. I am carman to  Thomas Clark, a town-carman. On Monday, the 4th of January, I was engaged by Brown, of Botolph-wharf, to take these bristles - I saw the prisoner at the top of the gateway; when they were loaded I asked him where I was to go to; he said, "Never mind, I have left directions with the foreman, (meaning our foreman), to drive towards Spitalfields; I drove on towards Spitalfields, and happened to see Mr. Pratt just by Wormwood-street, in Bishopsgate-street, just before I got to Wormwood-street, and he told me to drive down Wormwood-street; I drove down a little way, and he said, "Drive further, carman" - I drove till I got to Broad-street; he accompanied me, and at the corner of Broad-street he told me to unload my goods into Harris' cart - (I had three packages which did not belong to the prosecutor); Wormwood-street is not in the way to Spitalfields, but the reverse - I told him I did not think it was right to unload into Harris' cart, for I could go as safe with them as that cart could; he told me to never mind but to unload them; I did so, and they were put into Harris' cart, which stood at the corner of Broad-street - the prisoner was present; Harris' cart turned up towards Cheapside, quite away from Spitalfields - I asked it he prisoner for the cartage; he said he had left directions for my foreman to call up on Saturday for the cartage - where the goods went to I do not know.
   JOSEPH HARRIS. I am a carman. On the 4th of January I took four casks of bristles out of a cart into my own, and took them to Pancras-lane, Queen-street, Cheapside - the prisoner directed me to go there, and came after me: he was there when I delivered them, and told me if I was asked where I delivered them, to say I delivered them into a waggon at Smithfield - they were housed in a warehouse on the left-hand side of Pancras-lane.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q.Had you before that carried goods for him? A. Never - I do not know who opened the warehouse, or who is the proprietor.
 COURT. Q.When was you applied to to go to Broad-street? A.Between two and three o'clock; I was at the regular cart stand in Bishopsgate-street - Mr. Pratt merely said, "Bring your cart this way as fast as you can;" Roach was there with his cart when I got up.
 JOHN ROACH re-examined. I was not there two minutes before the casks were taken out; Harris' cart was not in sight when I first stopped.
