HENRY MYER. I am manager to William Gardner and Co., sugar-
refiners, of Plough Lane, Battersea- I have seen a sack containing fifty-
six lbs, of saccharine, marked, “B. B. and Co."- we stamped that sack
for a customer as distinguished from our own mark, but it is one of our
bags, and the saecharine is exactly like ours-here are other sacks here
marked, "B. B. and Co.”--these others are bags, not marked, but exactly
like ours, and these markec, “Gardner, Hill and Co." are ours-we
never send out empty bags-we get some back and some we do not-
these two have been used-Hampton is our principal carrier-the
carriers' men have access to the place where the bags are stored-we
keep thousands of bags in stock, and have no means of checking the loss
of a few.
Cross-examined by Mr. Sands. We supply a great quantity of saccharine
-I believe there is one other manufacturer of it-I should not miss a bag
of it; there is nothing to identify it by-these are not old sacks, they
have never been used; this one has been used and has our mark on it.
HENRY PERRETT. I am a solicitor, of 75, Bank Buildings, Queen
Victoria Street-prior to 1890 I acted for the owners of two houses, 2
and 4, Hart Street, Battersea, and in April, 1891, Osborn Hinbest
came to know whether I had a client who would lend £450 on two
houses belonging to his wife-I told him I had, but I must have his
wife's retainer or authority to get the money-that was brought me,
signed by Mrs. Hinbest-I secured the money, and the loan was com-
pleted-I wrote this letter to Hinbest. This stated that as the prisoner
had not called according to promise, he would be charged 108. 6d. extra)._

she paid me the rest-I paid the insurance, which was made out in her
name also- I see her here; she is the female prisoner.

I inquired of her whether she was Mrs. Hinbest: she said, "Yes"-I
