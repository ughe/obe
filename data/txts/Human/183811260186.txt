   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How long had the prisoner been in your master's employment? A.  A fortnight—he had very likely been gaining his master's good will very fast—I know he had—I am not in the employment now—I was discharged—my master did not direct me to watch the prisoner—I told Johnson, on Saturday morning, of my intention of doing so—I had not heard that any money was lost—I had reason to watch him, for on the Wednesday or Thursday before, I saw him drop some money from his hand, and look very much confused—that was the reason I watched him—the 1 s . 9 d . is entered in the book, but very likely that was put down afterwards—he did enter it—he asked me to go with him to the desk, and look behind, to see if the shilling was there, but I thought he would put it there—I went directly after, by myself-Johnson was serving a customer in the shop at the time—I had had no words with the prisoner—I was by the side of him when he put the 9 d . on the desk—I went directly after and saw no shilling.
   Q.  If there had been one, you had the opportunity of removing it? A.  Yes, but I did not—I left a month after this, because I was ill—I was out one night-two gentlemen assaulted me, and bad to pay 5 l . each for it—I slept Saturday night in the station-house—I was with my sister and another young lady, in George-street, Strand, and they wanted to take the inside of the pavement—I was eight or nine months in the prosecutor's employ—I was discharged about a fortnight ago, on a Tuesday night—the assault was on the Saturday—I was ill on Saturday, and did not go back till Tuesday—I went to the theatre on Monday night, instead of returning to my master's—it was very foolish of me—I was out Saturday night, Sunday night, and Monday night, without giving him notice—I told my master I had been ill—I was discharged for staying away. THOMAS JOHNSON. I am in the employ of Mr. Moyce. On Saturday evening, the 27th of October, I counted the money on the desk, after Bryant—there was three half-crowns, a sixpence, a fourpence, and some coppers—these told me to count it—the prisoner was serving a customer while I did so—he took 1 s . 9 d . of the customer, and went and put some money on the desk—these went and counted it—I went afterwards, and found there was one shilling short of what the prisoner had taken—I did not remove any thing—I am quite sure there was no shilling there when I went—these might, if so disposed, have taken the shilling off before I went.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you not very much surprised at Bryant ask ing you to go and count the money? A.  Yes; he told me on Saturday morning that he suspected him—I had a slight suspicion myself, as be several times dropped money, and said it was very slippery—he said so in Bryant's presence—these was discharged for being out two or three nights—these asked the prisoner what he had taken from the customer, and he said, "1 s . 9 d . "—I did not look to see whether he entered it in the book—he ought to have done so, but it is sometimes not done when we are very busy—my master had not desired me to watch the prisoner—he never intimated any suspicion about him, or complained of money being deficient.
