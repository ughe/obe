Lewis Davis re-examined. These articles are mine—they are not made
for sale, but for the Navy.
Prisoner's Defence. I took them home to get a stain out of the one
found in my box—the others I took home to get altered, which it was my
duty to do--I had five suits of the clothes, and did pledge them, as I
wanted money– I put the shirts on a chair when I took them home the
one stained was left with my wife to get the stain out, and I placed it in
my box-the other two remained loose in the room for five days—I did
not secrete them-I was going to give them to a person to alter.
LEWIS DAVIS re-examined. These are navy shirts-it was the prisoner's
duty to receive them when made, and send a cheque round for payment-
--those which are not made right he was empowered to get altered-not to
take to his private lodging, but to give them to work-people who came to
the shop-- his wife worked for us.
GUILTY of stealing the jacket and breeches.---Aged 50.
Confined Three Months.

225. MARY LOVELL was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of Novem-
ber, 2 pairs of pattens, value 1s. lod., the goods of James Gilham.
WILLIAM GULHAM. I am the son of James Gilham, a shoemaker in
Nelson-street, Greenwich. On the 1st of November, while I was attend-
ing to a customer, I received information which induced me to go towards
the hospital-I overtook the prisoner, and asked what she had in her
basket-she made no reply-I saw through the top of the basket two pairs
of pattens--I told her she had stolen them from the door-she said she
had bought them of a man in the market-place-they were my pattens,
and had hung by the side of the doorway outside--I had seen them five
minutes before.
MARY POWELL. I am servant to Mr. Gilham. I was in the kitchen on
the 1st of November, and saw the prisoner take the pattens off the hooks
and put them into her basket-I told my master, he followed and took her.
