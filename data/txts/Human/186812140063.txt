I was with the last witness on this morning—I saw John Brown running—I ran after him up several streets, till he ran into the arms of a constable—in the gutter in John Street I found two watches and two gold chains—the prisoner had gone past there just before—they were identified by the prosecutor. WILLIAM FREESTONE( Police Sergeant K  58). I apprehended Thomas Brown—he seemed to be rather ill.
   Thomas Brown called MARGARET SWAINSON. About 11.30 on Saturday, the 28th November, I went to Thomas Brown's house—his wife and I were talking—he came in about 12.10, and said he was very ill, and asked his wife to put a mustard plaster on his side—I stopped there till 11 o'clock the next morning, the 29th, and he never went out.
   Cross-examined . Q.  What are you? A.  I am the prisoner's sister-in-law—I told the Magistrate he came home at 12.10—I don't sleep in the house every night, and can't tell what time he usually comes home. ELLEN GILBERTSON. I went to Thomas Brown's place last Saturday fortnight at 12.30—he was in bed, with a mustard plaster on his side.
   Cross-examined . Q.  You are his mother-in-law? Yes—I never saw John and Thomas together—they are not brothers, and I am sure he is not any relation.
   THOMAS BROWN  — NOT GUILTY.
