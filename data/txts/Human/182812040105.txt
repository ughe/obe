I was a few yards off - I came up; the prisoner, and a good many more stood in the street - I suppose five or ten stood together; the prisoner said "Watchman, it is of no value, it is only a duffer, not worth 5s. - I don't call it any thing to be given in charge for;" I asked if she insisted on the charge - she said Yes; she charged him with attempting to take her watch, and breaking the guard from her side; I took him to the watch-house, and he there hit her on the bridge of her nose, and knocked her down.
 Cross-examined. Q. About what o'clock was this? A. After twelve; I sprung my rattle when I got him in charge - there were two of them together; one wore a fustian coat; she said he was one, and so I sprung my rattle - I have not had the watch; she had hold of the seals, and he said to me, taking hold of the watch,"Watchman, it is only duffer:" I will not swear whether he had hold of it when I came up - I saw it in his hand, but did not see him take hold of it; I now know where Belasco lives - I have been there for Woodroffe three times: I did not know her before this: she has kept the watch herself - the Magistrate ordered it to be given to her.
 COURT. Q. What does duffer mean? A. I asked him what he meant; he said it had no works in it; I never knew Belasco's house before this happened - it is not in my parish.
 Prisoner's Defence. I hope you will put the watchman on his oath whether he thinks this is spite or not - it is nothing else; I have known the woman ever so long, but lately have not spoken to her; and seeing me talking to Ward, she and Belasco took and followed me, took hold of me, looked me in the face, and let me go; I said to Ward, "These young women are following us;" they came and stood round us; the watchman came by - she said, "I give charge of this young man:" he said, "What for? I won't take him unless you say the charge," and she said, "He has attempted to take my watch:" I have known her these nine months.
   FRANCIS TAYLOR re-examined. She said he had attempted to take the watch from her side; I did not give her time to tell me before I asked her what it was about- I cannot tell whether it is spite or not.
   LOUISA WARD. I lived at Belasco's four months; it is a house of ill-same - there was a dress lodger there besides the prosecutrix; I was in Belasco's service, not in hers; this happened on the Tuesday after the Friday on which I left; I met the prisoner in Brydges-street - he stopped and spoke to me; he walked on - I stopped to tie my patten; she and Mrs. Belasco came out of Mother H.' oyster shop - I heard her say to Mrs. Belasco, "Here he is, so help me God I will;" (I have often heard her mention the prisoner's name, and say she would send him out of the country if it laid in her power) the other young woman said, "No, don't Clara:" she said," I will;" she gave Mrs. Belasco her muff, opened her cloak, then took a brass watch which she wears, and gave it to Mrs. Belasco, who took off her gold watch, and gave it to her; and, before they could get to Bow-street, Mrs. Belasco stopped and took hold of this young man by the collar, and then let him go again; Mrs. Belasco and Clara then ran round him and began a quarrel with him for the space of five minutes; she called the watchman, and insisted on his taking charge, and when he was taking him to the watch-house Mr. Belasco went up to him, and said, "Let him go:" the watchman said he must do his duty - I did not go into the watch-house, for Mr. Belasco heard me say I would speak for him, and he wanted to give charge of me; I went away, but went before the Magistrate the next day, and stated what I have here.
 COURT. Q. Had there been any quarrel between Fisher and the prisoner? A. Not that I know of; they never associated together or cohabited; I cannot say why she wished to send him out of the country, for I never heard - she meant transportation no doubt; I saw her change the watch, and then she charged him with attempting to take it - she never had a gold watch in her possession before; she was only trusted with the brass one - I suppose she was ashamed to swear a robbery against him for the brass watch; he did not attempt to take it from her - he was never near her; they followed him - she gave him in charge at the end of Bow-street, she followed him all that way, and then called the watchman; I was behind her at the time.
 MR. CLARKSON to  FRANCIS TAYLOR. Q. Has any body given you any money since you have been in Court? A. No.
 Q. Not a farthing? A. Oh, yes: I did not know what you meant; the prosecutrix gave me 1s. to get my dinner, because she said the London cases were coming on- it was within a few minutes of five o'clock; I had had no dinner, and was going to get it, but was afraid I should be wanted; I swear it was only a shilling, and here it is - nobody said any thing to me about going to drink with the prisoner's friends.
   WILLIAM HENRY HEWETT. I live in Portland-town, Regent's-park. I am attending here on another case: while standing among the people yonder, my attention was attracted by some conversation between the watchman and prosecutrix: I heard her reprimand him for keeping company with the prisoner's friends over the way, or at least out of doors, and at the same time she gave him 3s.; I can be positive there were three sixpences, and I thought it was a crown, but am certain of 4s. 6d., at the same time saying, "If you want drink, here is money to get it, but till the trial comes on keep yourself sober?" there was something so particular about the admonition, I thought it right to mention it.
 COURT. Q. You are here on another trial? A. Yes: a sheep-stealing case; I am a carman and carrier - I will swear to the money put into his hand; I only know the woman as the prosecutrix.
 Q. How came you to know her? A. By the party standing in Court; she said, "What did you go among those out of doors for? there is a great many of them, and I don't want you to be along with them." making use of an observation; she put the money into his hands - she suited the action to the word; I am quite uninterested in the matter - I never saw any of them before.
   FRANCIS TAYLOR. On my oath I have received no more than 1s., and that she gave me for my dinner - I have not had a farthing from her for drink.
