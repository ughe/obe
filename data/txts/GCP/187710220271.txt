Cross-examined by Me. BESLEY. Fennell had been many years in the
service scrap iron and heavy iron are mixed, and there are cast borings
and iron turnings-I gave a special memorandum of each-the actual
weights would be put on the gate slate; that was Clarke's duty, but if he
was not there somebody else would do it-it is very rarely that he was
absent-I did not see any one make an entry on the slate on the 17th-
I cannot say when the slate entry was obliterated—I know nothing about
the weights on the slate on those three days I see some of the iron was
31. a ton, but the average price in Porter's contract was 21. including
turnings and borings—an allowance of 78. 6d. was made to Porter
Porter has been under contract with Penn's more than seven years-he
carries on the business of an iron merchant—there may be a few cwt.
given over to make up for exceedingly dirty lots--I never called on Porter
in reference to this matter, I never knew where he was I know that
he was in liquidation some years ago.
Cross-examined by Fennell. You must have given me & direct
memorandum, you would not give it to Neville-I always took the
memorandum the same as Mr. Neville--you would possibly hand me &
memorandum when the sale took place, and when the iron was taken
away.
Cross-examined by Mr. Sims. Clarke was about two years in the prose-
cutur's employ as a labourer, always under Fennell—he was afterwards
promoted to be gatekeeper-there are other gates, but he is the only
gatekeeper.
Re-examined. Iron taken by Porter would go out at Clarke's gate in
the ordinary course of business.
Cross-examined by MR. WILLIAMS. I said before the Magistrate the trans-
actions with Porter were on Porter's account-I always dealt with Porter
and Douglas as two distinct persons.
