1698. JOHN GLYNN and CHARLES CASTLEMAN were again
indicted for stealing 1 engraved copper-plate, value 501., the goods of
Patrick Johnson, the master of Glynn, in his dwelling-house.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
John Bell. I am one of the clerks in the employ of Mr. Patrick John-
son, No. 20, Basinghall-street, in the parish of St. Michael, Bassishaw-
bis dwelling-house is under the same roof-he does not dwell there him-
self, but his servants do-Glynn was one of his clerks, at a salary of II.
a week--this copper-plate belonged to a bankrupt of the name of Bray-
it was kept in the front office where I sit-it was in a box or a case-
Glynn was engaged in the back room on the same floor, in the front of
which this plate was, and he would have access to it-I missed the plate
think about June-I mentioned the loss of it to Glynn, and asked him 1
he had removed it-he suid no-I described it to him, that it was in a
case-he said he had never seen it, and he did not know such a thing was
in the house.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doang. Q. Did you tell him it was a copper
plate ? A. Yes we had no other copper-plate on the premises-1 told
him the drawing from which it was taken was in the upper room, and be
must have known it-I think I had this conversation with Glynn on the
26th of June- I think it was on that day that inquiries were made about
the plate, and then I missed it-I had been absent for several days.
