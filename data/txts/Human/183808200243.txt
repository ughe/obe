GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Six Months.
 2074. WILLIAM MILLER and CHARLES ROSS were indicted for stealing, on the   14th of July , 1 cask, value 3 s  .; and 22 gallons of oil value 3 l  . 5 s  .; the goods of Sir Charles Price and others, their masters.-2nd COUNT  , stating them to be the goods of John Scovell and others.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution . CHAPLIN WOOD. I am in the employ of Messrs. Scovell, of Seymour's Wharf, Tooley-street—I have the charge of the gate. On Saturday morn ing, the 14th of July, I was near the gate, and saw the prisoner Miller roll a cask of oil outside the gate—he and Ross put it into a truck-Ross was outside with the truck—they went up the lane—I followed them across Tooley-street, and informed the police—I had seen them working there the day before—they were in the employ of Sir Charles Price.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Had there been any oil spilled on the ground? A.  Not that I am aware of—I did not look.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Did they continue together? A.  Yes, to the corner of Magdalen-street, and put the truck down—they then came back—I do not know where they went to. HENRY SMITH. I live in Chicksand-street, and work at the wharf. On Saturday morning, the 14th of July, Miller spoke to me in Stoney-lane, and asked if I knew a person who would earn a shilling—I said, "What for?"—he said, "To draw a truck with a cask of oil on it"—I said I would have no objection to do it—he said he would give me a shilling and a glass of gin—I went with him, and found the truck in Tooley-street—he said I was to take it to his house—I went as far as King-street in the Borough—I left Miller in Tooley-street-when I got to King-street, a policeman stopped me-Ross then came up, and desired roe to take it back again, but the constable took me and the truck to the station-house. ANDREW BOURKE( police-constable M  154.) I stopped Smith in King-street, with the truck, on the 14th of July-Ross came and told him to take the oil back to Seymour's Wharf—that was before I stopped it—I then stepped forward, and said, "Where to?" and he said, "To Seymour's Wharf"—I said, "It has already come from there, you must go with me"—I had information before—Mr. Rumsey has seen the same cask of oil. JOHN WOODHOUSE( police-sergeant M  13.) I was in King-street, on the 14th of July, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning-Miller was coming towards me—I stopped him, and asked if he was not one of the persons engaged in moving the oil—he said yes, he was, and he was going to the station-house to order it to be brought back again, by order of Mr. Partington—he said it was taken away by mistake-as we were going along, he said it was of no consequence, it was mere pickings-up, what was allowed to coopers—I asked him what it was worth—he said about ten-pence a gallon, that there was twenty-two gallons, and it was going to a man at Lambeth to make cart-grease of.
