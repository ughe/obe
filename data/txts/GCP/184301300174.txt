760. WILLIAM WESTON was indicted for embezzling and stealing
28. 6d., the monies of Joshua Chipchase, his master.
Messrs. CLARKSON AND DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
Joshua CHIPCHASE. I am a shoemaker, and live in High-street, Shadwell.
The prisoner was in my employ about sixteen months-in consequence of
something in the week previous to the 9th of Jan. I agreed with Mr. Barrett to
mark a half-crown-about ten o'clock on the 9th of Jan. I went down into
my shop and asked the prisoner to account for what money he had taken--be
said 58. 6d. for a pair of boots, and 2s. 6d. for a pair of shoes, which bad
just gone out-I said, “Has there been nothing else sold ?"-be said
"No"-I said, “You sold a pair of slippers for half-a-crown at nine o'clock"-
he said, “Yes, but you had the money for them." I had been called down
at ten minutes past nine, and there was a customer in the shop, who bought a
pair of slippers for half-a-crown-I had got that half-crown-I said, "Yes,
but you sold a pair exactly at nine"-he denied it--if he had sold another
pair at nine, and had received half-a-crown for them, he was half-a-crown
short in the money he had accounted to me for- I told him that when I came
down at five minutes past nine I lifted up the lid of the desk, and in a
back place, where money never was kept, I saw the edge of a half-crown-
he said, " I suppose you put it there"-I said, “No, but I will tell you
how it came there"- I told him for a long time I had suspected he was
robbing me, and for the last few weeks I was quite sure of it-he denied it-
I said, "Have you a half-crown about you?"- he said, "Yes" -he then
threw down two half-crowns and two shillings on the counter-I told him
that the half-crown with which the slippers were purchased was marked, and
on taking up one it was marked-I sent for Mr. Barrett, and in the prisoner's
presence asked bim if he knew that half-crown-he said yes, it was the
one marked.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Had the prisoner lived with you
before ?-A. Yes, once ; he left to get married - I gave him a very bad
character, but the clergyman believed him rather than me there was 1001.
due to him on his marriage, if he could get a good character; and he applied to
me, but I gave him a bad character-I took him again, because I hoped he was
better-I agreed with him that he was to stay a year, if he conducted him-
self to my entire satisfaction, so that I could not discharge him within the
year--the errand-boy was present when I had this conversation about the
slippers--I never allowed the prisoner to give change out of his own
pocket-there were always persons in the house to do so, my wife or my
daughters-I never told him not to give change in so many words, but it was
not his habit-this is a book in which he ought to enter these things-he
has nothing to do with the daily expenses book.
Prisoner. The man Collins made a pair of bespoke shoes in the name
of Wells-he brought them to me while I had a customer in the shop,
and wanted to be paid-I said I had nothing less than a half-crown-I put
my hand in my pocket and gave him a shilling, and asked if that would do.
