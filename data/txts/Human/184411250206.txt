   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  If you had been in command, and on the paddle box of the vessel, should you have kept a good look out? A.  Yes—I think I should be able to see a boat if it was crossing the river, if it was about 150 feet before me—it would be my duty to keep at the same speed as before, if the boat was across my bows—would not run right into the boat of course—I could prevent that by easing and stopping the vessel—I should consider it my duty to do so.
   Q.  If you were in command should you think it your duty to go at the rate of ten miles an hour round a place without knowing what was be hind it? A.  Yes—I should not think it necessary to lessen the speed, although I could not see what was behind the place we were going round.
   JURY . Q.  Will you stake your knowledge of your business, and your own character, that you would not stop your vessel in going round such a place? A.  Very often in places we are obliged to go in that way, to get the vessel out of a little bother .
   COURT . Q.  Was there a little bother  on this occasion? A.  Well, I did not observe that—very often we have to shove  ahead at the rate of ten miles an hour to prevent an accident—I did not observe whether there was any such occasion here—I am not aware that it would have been proper on this particular occasion to have gone slower round the Point.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Were you on the look-out? A.  No, I was only a passenger; it was, of course, the business of the man on the paddle-box to look out and see whether there was a boat ahead—very likely he had an opportunity of seeing the boat before I did; of course he had, because he was higher than I was—he would have an opportunity of seeing it as soon as we cleared the end of the schooner—that might have been two seconds before I saw her—at the time he called out, "Ease her," I looked ahead and saw the danger—we were just past the schooner's stern when he called out, "Ease her"—he could not see her before that, that I am aware of—he saw her as soon as he could—she was a boat's length and a half from the vessel when I saw her—I saw her the moment he called out, and I believe he called out the moment he could see her—I do not know when he saw her—he saw her before I saw her, at least he called out to ease the vessel, I could not tell when he could see her; I was not ob serving, and did not see the boat till he called out to ease her—the thing that prevented him from seeing was the stern of the schooner—we were in the act of passing the stern of the schooner when he called out, "Ease her"—I do not know whether that was the first time he could see her—his attention might have been called to her—I did not see her till he called, "Ease her."
   COURT . Q.  The schooner hindered him from seeing the skiff for some time? A.  Yes—it ceased to hinder him at some time—I cannot say how soon it was after it ceased to hinder him from seeing the boat that he called out—we were just passing the end of the schooner as he called out, "Ease her."
