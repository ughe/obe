( Property produced and sworn to .) JOHN SHORT. I am uncle of one of the prisoners. He works on the river, and has charge of a barge belonging to Mr. Fuller, of Limehouse—in February last that barge wanted repairing, and it was to go to Lime-house-reach, to Mr. Tulley's yard, to be repaired—it was to pass the Gra vel Engine to get there—I had seen a stove on board that barge for three weeks before the barge went to be repaired—it was not concealed at all—the Gravel Engine is a place where people constantly deposit things—it was not necessary to deposit the stove on board the barge, while it is repaired—it was taken out and left on board the engine, but I do not know who by. I live with Alfred Simpson, a hat manufacturer, in Great Guildford-street, Borough. On Saturday evening, the 27th of February, about seven o'clock, I was making up the accounts of the men's work, and took it into the parlour to Mr. Simpson—during this time the prisoner was missing—he was a workman in the shop—I came back and paid the men—I called him three times, but he did not appear—I paid another man for him—he is a finisher off hats—all the men were paid off, ex cept him—four hats were missing out of the warehouse, between six and nine o'clock that night and the Monday morning—I paid the men between half-past seven and eight o'clock—the policeman H 44 gave us information, and produced a hat—the prisoner had access to the warehouse, as the door was open.
