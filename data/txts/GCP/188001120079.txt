and saw the defendant at his office--I said "I hear that one of your bills
for a small amount has been dishonoured, and I have come in consequence
to demand the mate's receipt sent to you a few days ago " he said. It is
impossible to give it you, as I have already sent it to my correspondent
abroad "-I said “That is unlikely, as there has been no mail for Australia

of the bill was a temporary affair, and he expected to put it to rights in a
day or two, when all things would go smoothly as before I said I could
not believe that, as a man doing his large business must be very hardly
pushed to let a bill of about 801. come back dishonoured, and I again
demanded the mate's receipt or the bills of lading, whichever he might have in
his possession then-he declined to have anything more to say on the subject,
and I then told him that I considered it a rank swindle, and should recom-
mend Messrs. Morgan to prosecute him-a few days after that notice of his
liquidation was sent to my office-these bills (produced), except the one
