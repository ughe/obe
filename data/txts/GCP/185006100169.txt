19 WILLIAM BARKER. I remember playing cribbage on Monday, 8th April,
a fortnight before the prisoner was taken-in consequence of our not having
money to buy anything we stopped at home and smoked our pipes, and drank
some beer-I played first, and then Green played three or four games, and
then I played again--the prisoner came between five and six o'clock, and left
between one and two in the morning
Cross-examined. Q. What are you? 4. A hawker-I hawk all manner
of things, fruit and fish-I live with Green, in Waterloo-street- I hawked
yesterday-Green did not hawk with me, but he was out selling cocoa-nuts-
the day before yesterday he was round with his barrow in his usual business
- he works every day, I know all about him-he was not out all the week
because he could not buy anything, but he was out on Saturday with goose-
berries and rhubarb-I should say he took 118. or 128.; he earns something
every day when he is out-I hardly remember where I was on 6th April,
I think I was out-the first time I was asked to come here was on Saturday
Jast-Mrs. Smith asked me to come and speak what I knew of the day he
was playing cards with me she asked me what day that was, and I told her
-Green did not go out at all during the time we were playing-I am sure of
that, as far as I can recolect-- I believe I played first-I think Green was
rather the luckiest I think we had between two and three pots of beer--I
think it was three-Mr. Smith that I lodge with fetched the beer-I think,
on 4th April, I was out round the streets with my barrow selling fish-I have
never been in trouble in my life.
Prisoner. Q. I think if you charge your memory you will find that Green
went out ? 1. I do not remember it-I will swear positively that you did
not leave the house till twelve o'clock at night.
JAMES GRANT re-examined. The public-house is about a mile and a hall
from where I apprehended the prisoner-I know 5, George-street--that is
about a mile and a half from Moore's.
