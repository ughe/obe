JOHN HENRY HOARE, gate porter at the Infirmary. In April, 1906,
I was night porter, the day porter being Thomas Clark. One day in
April, 1906, as I came on duty at 8 p.m., Thomas Clark spoke to
me. My duty was to enter in a book the names of persons entering
or leaving the work house. On that night & cab came in with two
persons. As the cabman drove in one of the occupants said either
"Medical Superintendent " or "School."
Mr. Marshall Hall objected that as there was no evidence of this particular book
having been destroyed secondary evidence of entries could not be given.
The Judge ruled that there being evidence of a series of books of which this ww
one being destroyed, the evidence was admissible,
I entered in the book "Two visitors," and the exact time it was
between 11 and 11.30 p.m. The cab remained about 10 minutes and
then left, and I made the entry of its leaving. Payne attended
the weighbridge. Bond's carts frequently passed in. I should not
alter "2" into "3" if a third one came, but would make a separate
entry. There is a long drive and you would see at once how may
were coming in.
