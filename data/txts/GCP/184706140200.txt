1530. ARTHUR LEARY and THOMAS WYATT were indicted for
unlawfully receiving from Edward Ransby the sum of 2s. 6d., under pretence
of helping him to recover part of the sum of 1971. 108., the amount of an
order stolen from him by John Jenner, (see page 417,) they not having caused
Jenner to be brought to trial for the said felony.
MR. PARNELL conducted the Prosecution.
EDWARD Ransby. I live at Bawsey, in Suffolk. On Monday, the 26th
of April, I was in town-I went to the Yorkshire Stingo, Blackfriars-road.
that afternoon-I had a check for 1971. 10s., and 188. or 198.-I parted with
that check to John Jenner, the landlord of the house, and made an arrange-
ment for him to meet me at the bank the following morning, to restore it to
me-he did not meet me, and I went to his house--I ultimately gave him in
charge on the 27th of April-we went before a Magistrate, and I charged him
with robbing me of my check--the Magistrate heard my statement, and bad
Jenner remanded-having left the police office, I went with the constable to
search different houses after the person who had cashed the cheek-while we
were going, I saw the two prisoners, (I had seen Leary at the Yorkshire
Stingo the day before, but he was not in the room at the time I gave the
cheok to Jenner)-Leary asked me how I was getting on; I said I hoped to
transport the landlord and one or two others, if I could get them-he said,
"I suppose you won't care about that, if you could get your money returned"
--I said I should not like to let him off so-he said I should not get my
money if I went on with it-he said, “I suppose you would rather have your
money"-I said, “Yes; I should like to get my money back”-he said, "I
you will go down in the country in the morning, you shall have your money
returned, every shilling"- in order to get my money, I agreed to go down in
the country in the morning, and I said I should detain one of them while the
other fetched the money-Leary stopped with me, and Wyatt went and fetched
the money-he was away half an hour, or three-quarters of an hour-when
he came back he told me he had got a party waiting outside with the money;
if I would write him out a receipt for the money, he would go and fetch it-
I wrote out a receipt for the full amount of the check, and gave it him—this
is the receipt I wrote for 1971. 10s.-he went out and brought me in a bag
which contained the money as I thought-I found in it 1911, all in gold-the
prisoners told me the sooner I went away the better, and advised me to go as
soon as possible for fear I should lose my money again- I wanted to go as
soon as I could, to carry my money home-Leary said if I was not in town
the next day, Jenner would be liberated, and I did not know any better
suppose, that was why I was to go in the country, that he might be liberated
-I did not make any remark- I went into the country on the following
morning-I did not go before the Magistrate-it was in consequence of my
receiving this money that I did not go before the Magistrate on the 1st of
May, Wyatt came down to my house at Bawsey; he said he had come down
on behalf of Jenner and his wife and family, and I should be doing them a
great favour if I did not attend at the Court to go on with the prosecution-
he said that Jenner's attorney had told him that if the letter the summons
was in, was not rightly directed, I might seal it up and send it back, and
they would not know it, and I was not obliged to open it if it were not righus
directed-I said I intended to go on with the prosecution-I intended to
come up the following morning, as I had received the summons- I have not
