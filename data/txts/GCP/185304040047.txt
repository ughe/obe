489. JAMES BRITTON, feloniously uttering, in December, 1847, a
forged order for the payment of 71., and another forged order for the pay-
ment of 4l. 10. ; with intent to defraud.
MR. PARRY conducted the Prosecution.
ROBERT Carr. I am landlord of the Royal Oak, Wapping-lane-about
December, 1847, or the beginning of 1848, the prisoner was living six or
seven doors off me I knew him for a very short time-be used to come in
and out of my place when he was at home at one time be came to me, said
he had got a ship, and asked me if I could cash his notes- I said I had no
objection-he produced these two notes-this is his writing on the back of
this one-1 have had them in my possession ever since [These being read were
seamens' advanced notes, one for 71., payable three days after the sailing of the
ship, Caroline Augusta, from Gravesend, to Thomas Burns; and the other for
41. 108., payable to James Britton]-I saw the prisoner write this at my
counter-1 advanced him 111. 10s.- I said, “This appears a very ugly
advance;" and he said, "It is all right, Thomas Burns is going to be cbiet
mate"-Burns was not there the 71. was payable to him-it is a common
thing for sailors to get advances on these notes-about five minutes after-
wards I was going out to Linehouse, and was passing the prisoner's door
he and his wife were in a cab, and I never saw any more of them till now-
cannot say whether there was any man with them the notes have never been
paid, although I presented them duly at the American Consul's-I named the
matter to the police, in consequence of which the prisoner was taken-be
said he was very sorry, he did not wish to cheat me, his uncle and he were
coming down to pay me.
