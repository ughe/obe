2682. MARY CLARKE was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of Au gust , 1 cap, value 6 s  ., the goods of William Spice . SOPHIA SPICE. I am the wife of William Spice, and live at Chelmsford, in Essex. The prisoner and her daughter came to me at the assizes—the prisoner took my lodging for her daughter and some young man—she used to come backwards and forwards—when she left she told me she should be back in a few days, and take the lodgings again—she came on the 3rd of August, but I was not at home—she dined there, and on Sunday morning she went out, and did not return—on the Monday I missed my cap and some other things—I went and found her on board the bulks at Woolwich with the cap on her head—I asked her if she knew me—she said, "No"—I asked her if she recollected being at Chelms ford—she said, "No"—I said, "You took my lodgings at your son's trial"—I said I had not followed her for her bill which she had not paid, but for my cap, which she then had on her head—she said it was a bought one—I said, "I know it is, for I paid for it"—I knew it by the particular make and trimming—I gave her in charge of the Wool wich constable—a key was taken from her which I have examined with my drawers—it locks and unlocks them—when I saw her at the station-house in town she had not got the cap on—it was lost.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Was any one with you besides the policeman? A.  Yes, another man—we came from Woolwich to Lon don by the steam-boat—the prisoner was on board too—she had my cap on all the time—my friend was on board too—I did not take the cap off, because I had no business—I missed fifteen yards of black silk, a watch, a gold seal, a key, and a silk shawl, two pairs of stockings, a table-cloth, and a brooch—I have seen none of them since—the prisoner, slept in my house on the night of the robbery, but not the daughter—I have not found the daughter—the prisoner had a bonnet on over the cap.
   COURT . Q.  How was it she was not taken before the Magistrates at Woolwich? A.  She was, and he told the policeman to take her the sta tion-house—he said, "Go as fast as you can, and see that she makes away with nothing, especially that cap"—I do not know, that any one else came to my house—it is an eating-house. SARAH EMERSON. I am servant to the prosecutor. I remember the prisoner being there on the 3rd of August, and leaving on the 4th—the drawers that the key fits are in the sitting-room up stairs—I saw them locked on the Friday before my mistress left—on Sunday morning the pri soner left, and said she would come back in half-an-hour—my mistress re turned on Monday—she found the drawers still locked, but the property gone—the prisoner had the use of that room—no one else had been in it between the Sunday and Monday.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is this an eating-house? A.  Yes, people go in the rooms, but these were private rooms—the bed-room was not locked—there are four rooms up stairs—I don't know what is become of the daughter—she went away—I saw the prisoner leave—she had her bonnet on—there was nothing remarkable about her. THOMAS WILLIAM REDFORD( police-constable L  52.) The prisoner was brought to the Tower stairs—she was very wet—her hair was hanging about—I said, "Where is the cap?"—the officer said, "On her head," but she had no cap on—I asked what she had done with the cap—she said, "Find it"—I asked her what she had to say to the charge—she said, "Nothing"—
