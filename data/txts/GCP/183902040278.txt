WILLIAM PEARCE. The prisoner solicited an order from me for some
clogs. I gave him an order for a dozen pairs--he brought them on the
25th, and I paid him 178. on account of his master,
JAMES PEPPER (police-constable L 51.) I took the prisoner on the 5th of
February, in Mint-street, Borough. I told him I wanted him for abscond.
ing with his master's money. he said, "I know I took the money, but it
was for want of bread-my master can't make a felony of it, it is a breach
of trust."
Prisoner's Defence. I took home these clogs-previous to going, I
asked for sixpence to get me something to eat, which was refused--I then
said, “Lend me a penny," which was refused.
Mr. Kent re-examined. This is partly true, but he had been paid the
commission on these clogs previous to the order going in, because he pleaded
distress on the morning he speaks of, he wanted some more, and I would
not let him have it.
GUILTY. Aged 18.- Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Weeks.
876. JOSEPH STOWER was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of
February, 2 scarfs, value 1l. 12s., the goods of William Kingston and
another, his masters.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
JOSEPH HARVEY. I am in partr.ersluip with Mr. William Kingston, w
are linen-drapers, and live in Westminster Bridge-road. The prisoner was
our porter-he came on the 1st of December, and, as I understood, he was
in very bad circumstances we received information which led us to su
pect him, and on the evening of Monday, the 4th of February, I placed
myself at a skylight from which I could have a view of that part of the
shop where he and the other porter slept together went about eleven
o'clock at night, and when I had been there some tew minutes, I saw the
other porter leave his bed-side, and go down stairs- I then saw the pri-
soner go to a distant part of the warehouse to the shawl department-he
returned with something under his arm--I saw the black fringe on his
white shirt, and it appeared to me to be shawls which he had-he stooped
down, folded up the shawls, and seemed to have great difficulty in getting
them in somewhere, I fancy it was into a pocket-I went to the station-
house, and requested the assistance of an officer, and at seven o'clock the
next morning the officer came to our shop, and took him into custody-
Mayor, our clerk, went down, and fetched up the prisoner's coat-I asked
the prisoner if it was his-he said “Yes," and if he had had it that mora-
ing-he said "Yes"-the policeman took these two shawls out of the
poeket of that coat.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is it possible you could have
made any mistake about the person who did this? A. No, they are scarfs,
or long shawls.

5th of February, when the prisoner was taken, I saw the officer find these
scarfs in his coat-pocket.
