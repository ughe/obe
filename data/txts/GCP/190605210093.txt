THOMAS WATSON, chief clerk to Mr. Freighter, estate agent
to Colonel Gascoigne, Aberford, Leeds. I acted as secretary
to the late Colonel Gascoigne. He died June 12, 1905, and
was ill only four or fre days before he died. He very fre-
quently gave small sums -'v cheque in answer to begging letters.
He was not particular about his accounts. I put the estate
accounts before him every month; he did not trouble about
them much as long as the book was balanced. He was a colliery
owner, had estates near Leeds, was a very wealthy man, and
dealt with large sums. He went to Italy about October, 1904,
and returned about May 3, 1905. The turnover in his pass-
book, for 1903 was £69,000 at Drummond's, beginning the year
1905 with a balance of £4,000.
Cross-examined. Colonel Gascoigne was in the habit of
answering begging letters and sending cheques during the past
14 years while I have known him. He was about 92 years old.
ELIZABETH TASKER, stationer, 9, Hammersmith Road. Letters
were received at my shop for Mrs. Collins, and were called for
by Maud Willing. The prisoner Peach on one occasion came
for letters for Mrs. Collins.
Cross-examined by Mr. Thorne. Peach called in May, 1905.
I do not remember whether the man who called was wearing
a beard. I remember his face. I first identified him at Bow
Street in the dock.
Re-examined. I was not called at Bow Street. I recognised
Peach without being invited to identify him.

for 10s. was sent to and handed by me to her.
