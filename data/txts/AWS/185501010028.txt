215. SOPHIA HYSON feloniously killing and slaying Elizabeth Hyson.
Mr. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution.
MARY PAGE. I am the wife of Henry Page, of No. 1, Garden-walk,
Willow-walk, Shorediteh. I knew the deceased, Elizabeth Hyson ; the pri-
soner was her daughter-seventy-tw was the age upon the deceased's coffin
-they had both been living with me for six years and a half; they used to
quarrel very frequently at times-on Tuesday, 14th Nov., about 2 o'clock in
the afternoon, I was alone in my front parlour; the prisoner and her mother
were at home in their room, the first floor back-I could not hear anything
which took place in the room unless I was listening-I did not hear any-
thing, but the prisoner came down into my room very much excited, and
said, I think I have given the old devil her death said, 46 What
have you been doing "she said that she had stuek a pair of sharp pointed
scissors into her mother's hand-I said, 4 Oh, dear how came you to do
"-she said, 4 She had been teasing me, because I was so long over my
bonnet ; she would interfere with me and my bonnet". said, 44 What is
that on your hand I"-she said, 46 It is some of the old woman's blood," and
took up her apron and wiped her hand-I said, Pray do, Sophia, put on
your bonnet and go out ;' but before that she took up my scissors, I being
at needlework, and said, "I have seratched her across the hand in that
position"- did not think it a very serious matter, and did not go up, nor
did I on the Wednesday; but on Wednesday the prisoner came and told me
how bad her mother's hand was-I asked her what she had done to it ; she
said she had put some dripping to it-I said, Pray do not put that to it
any more, put a poultice to it ;" and she did so-n Wednesday night she
told me that her mother's hand was very much worse, and was full of pain
-I told her to apply a warmer poultice, and to send for a doctor in the
morning, and she did so-n the Thursday morning, before the doctor came,
I went in to see the old lady, and I never saw such a wound in my life it
was on the back of her hand, across these three bones-the deceased said,
in the prisoner's presence, 46 The girl has done this ;' the prisoner said
nothing to that-a doctor came, and attended the deceased from this time
-she died on the 19th-the prisoner remained with her mother till about
the Thursday week, and was then sent away by order of the doctor, and a
nurse came from the workhouse.
Prisoner. I think it was on the 7th that I hurt my mother's hand, it
was the day after Guy Fawkes day ; my mother lived six weeks after I
hurt her hand. Witne8s. It was on the 14th, Tuesday; I am sure I saw
your mother's hand on the Thursday, before the doctor came.
Prisoner. On the Tuesday I did it, and I went and said to my mother,
Poultice your hand; and she said, 4 Go away."
FRANCES ROYAL. I am a nurse in Shoreditch workhouse. I was sent for
in Nov., to nurse the deceased I cannot say the day-the doctor saw her
every day-on the day she died, I had gone away to the house for my food
a short time before, and left Ann Deeson with her when I came back I
found that she was dead-I believe that was on 19th Dec.
