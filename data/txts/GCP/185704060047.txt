489. JAMES CLARK and WILLIAM SMITH were indieted for &
like offence.
Mr. Ellis conducted the Prosecution.
HENRIETTA BRANDT. I am a German, and am the daughter of Mr.
Brandt, a tobacconist, of Fleet Street. On 12th March, about 7 o'clock in
the evening, a person resembling Clark came for half an ounce of tobacco,
and gave me a good half crown; I gave him 28. 4 d. change, and he went
away directly-Smith then came into the shop and asked for half an ounce
of tobacco; he gave me a half crown, I gave him change, and he left-
policeman came in and asked me for the money; it was still in my hand; I
gave it to him, and he said that it was bad-Smith was not present then,
but two policemen brought them both back-the man who came first was
as tall as Clark, and was dressed like him.
WILLIAM LARRANDON. I am an interpreter of languages, and live in
Cloth Fair; I have been in the police fifteen years, and resigned some time
since. On 12th March, at 7 o'clock in the evening, I was in Fleet-street,
and saw the prisoners standing together within four or five doors of the
station, and ten or twelve yards from Brandt's shop-I fancied I knew
Clark, which catused me to stop—they looked right and left, as if watehing
an opportunity, and a third man crossed the road and gave Smith a porte
monnaie, which he put into his pocket without looking at the contents, and
the man went away; in a minute or two Smith opened the porte monnais,
and showed the contents to Clark-T was three or four yards off-immo-
diately after that Smith went into the shop; I passed, and saw him being
served with some tobacco; I went in, and saw Miss Brandt with a half
crown in her hand; I said, " Allow me to look at it;" I bit it, and said,
