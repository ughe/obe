JOHN LASH. I am shopman to Mr. Brier, 44, King William Street, City. On Friday, 9th Oct., the prisoner came into Mr. Brier's shop, and brought a written order for some haberdashery and hosiery—I was scarcely able to read it, and I asked her by word of mouth for her address—she gave, "Mrs. Humby, Cavendish Grove, Cavendish Square, Wandsworth Road, the corner shop"—I told her that the goods would be sent on—they came to 5 l . 7 s . 3 d .
   Cross-examined. Q.  How was the order written? A.  In pencil—I had not seen her before I served her—she was in the shop above an hour—she knew nothing about the goods she was purchasing; she left it to me. JAMES JOSEPH COLTMAN. I am a warehouseman in the same employ as the last witness, and have the management of the parcels department—I sent a parcel by a porter, named William Brown, to Mrs. Humby—it was sent three times to that address; on the 9th, 10th, and 12th—on the third time, the 12th, he brought back a 5 l . note in payment of the goods—I wrote, "Mrs. Humby, Cavendish Terrace, Wandsworth Road, 12, 10, 7." upon it—this ( produced ) is it. WILLIAM BROWN. I am a porter in Mr. Brier's employment I took a parcel of goods on the morning of the 12th of this month—I took it once before, on the 10th, but I did not deliver it because I found no one there; the shop was closed—I took it on the 12th to Cavendish Grove, at the corner of Cavendish Terrace, Wandsworth Road—I looked in at the doorway; there were two shutters down—I saw the prisoner in there—I knocked at the door, and she came and opened it—I told her I had brought the parcel from Mr. Brier, of London Bridge, for Mrs. Humby—she said, "Come in, it is all right"—I took the bill for the goods out of my pocket, opened it, and gave it to her, and told her the amount of it, 5 l . 7 s . 3½ d .—she opened a till underneath the counter, and took out two notes, one a 5 l . and the other a 10 l .—she gave me the 5 l . note—I asked her whether she had not got any silver to pay the remainder—she said she had not any more money besides those two notes; the gentleman had not left her any money, but she expected him in a minute or two—I waited there half an hour or more, but found no gentleman ever came—I told her I must go; she said I must take some of the goods back to make the money balance—I looked in the parcel, and saw a dozen hose at 6 s . 9 d ., and I took them back—the balance over was 7 s . 3½ d .—I selected something as near as I could to it—there was 6½ d . more to come to me—I then left the shop, and brought the 6 s . 9 d . parcel along with me and the note, which I gave to Coltman when I got back.
   Cross-examined. Q.  She made no objection to your waiting? A.  No; she said the gentleman would be in a few minutes. JAMES HENRY TURNER. I keep the Globe public house, Regent Street. Lambeth. On the 8th of this month the prisoner came to my house, and asked me to oblige her with change for a 5 l . note—she placed one on the counter I looked at it, and I said it was a funny looking note, and I did not like to change notes for strangers—she said, "You know me very well; we have dealt here for years; it is for my father; you may rely upon its being good for he took it at Somerset House"—I never knew what her father was; I have Known him by sight for years; I asked her name, and she said, "Finch, No. 5, Gray's Place"—I asked her to write her name and address on it; she said she could not write, would I write it for her; and I wrote, "Finch, No. 5, Gray's Place"—that is the note—I gave her four sovereigns and 1 l . in silver, change.
