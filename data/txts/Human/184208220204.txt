JOSEPH MALLETT. I am a congreve-match manufacturer—the prisoner was my porter. About ten o'clock in the morning of the 13th of August , I sent him out—after he was gone I had something told me, and I went to  the place in my factory where I kept my money—I missed one of two five-shilling packets of halfpence, which were safe about five minutes before—I followed him, and said, "If you will give me back the five-shill lings-worth of halfpence, nobody shall know anything about it"—he said he had got nothing—I told him to go back to the factory, to justify him self—I saw him go to the factory—he came back again, and said he had cleared himself with my partner, and would go on  with my goods—I said, "Have you found the five shillings?"—he said, "It is not found"—I followed him with my eye to a little house, before you arrive at the factory—I saw him put his hand into his pocket—then he came back to me—I said I would not let him go with my goods—he left my goods, and went away—he came back on Monday morning—I have since seen the packet of copper.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Why did not you put a stop to this, by searching him at the time? A. I  did not know I was allowed—he has never been searched—the officer took him on the Tuesday—he came for his wages on the Monday—I did not pay him—he came again on Tuesday for his wages—I did not pay him—I owed him 2 s . 9 d .—he had 7 s . a week—no victuals or clothes—he used to work from six o'clock in the morning to seven at night. NOT GUILTY.
 2491. JAMES M'CULLOCK was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of August , 16 yards of oil-cloth, value 2 l  . 8 s  ., the goods of William Element and another. FRANCIS WILLIAM HALL. I am in the employ of William Element and Mr. Knight, carpet manufacturers, in Holborn. On the evening of the 24th of August, I saw the prisoner and another man at the shop-door, standing talking, one on one side, and one on the other—the other said, "As you like," and then they lifted the roll of cloth on the prisoner's shoulder—he walked up Holborn—the other one went away—the oil cloth had been inside the door—I followed the prisoner about twenty yards, and asked him to stop with the cloth, as it was not his—he said a gentleman' asked him to take it to Oxford-street, and said he would give him 1 s .—I called my master, who took him into the shop, and gave him to a policeman—this is the cloth—it is Mr. Element's and his partner's. FRANCIS MORRIS( police-constable E  78.) I took the prisoner—Mr. Element said, "Take this man for stealing the oil-cloth"—I said, "You must go with me to the station"—he said, "Very well"—in going, he said, "This is the way an innocent man gets taken"—that was all he said to me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did he not say a man employed him to carry it? A.  He said that to Mr. Element.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Three Months.
