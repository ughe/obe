EDWARD Roe. I am a surgeon, and live in Brown-buildings, Islington.
I accompanied Mr. Lord and a policeman to Mr. Templeman's cottage,
about twenty minutes after eleven o'clock - the policeman got through the
window, opened the door, and let us in—I saw the deceased with only a
shirt on, lying with his feet towards the fire-place, and his head towards
the door of the room-the body was in the bed-room, towards the door,
which is between the two rooms--he was lying on his back, rather inclining
to the left side, facing the window-his eyes were firmly bandaged with a
stocking-if he had been alive, he could not have seen there was a good
deal of blood on the stocking, which I think must have been saturated with
the blood on the floor, after it was tied on his eyes I have no doubt his
head had fallen on the floor, and the stocking got into the blood-his hands
were securely tied by a cord in this posture (describing it)-& small
cord secured the other cord fastened between the wrists, through a noose
in the cord, to make it more secure-I did not notice the wrists then-I
did after the cord was removed, and they were galled, and the hands and
nails blue-that would unquestionably show that it had been put on during
life-I have no doubt he struggled—the blueness would be occasioned
by the pressure of the cord I observed several bruises on his face, a
severe blow over the left temple, and a cut on the nose the lower jaw
was fractured on both sides--I observed four clean cuts on the back of the
head, and one or two bruises on the breast bone, in conjunction with the
collar-bone-his knees were much abraised, rubbed up, as he had, no
doubt, been struggling on the floor--there was some blood on the floor
immediately under the deceased's head-] should say perhaps ten ounges
-the wounds would most likely continue to bleed some time there was
no contused wound on the left temple to cause the blood I have no
doubt a good deal of the blood came from the jaw, and also the clean cuts
on the back of the head—the blow on the temple was most probably given
by a blunt round instrument, similar to a policeman's staff-it might be
done by a piece of wood-the same instrument might have produced the
fracture of the jaw, and one blow might produce both fractures- I think
the wound on the nose was from a kick, or something of that description,
not done by a sharp instrument-the wounds on the back of the head
were inflicted by a very sharp instrument,
Court. Q. Were they inflicted as with a knife, as if a person bad
cut, or a hatchet, as if they had struck ? A. Neither--I should say like
a chisel, as if a chisel had been struck on the back of the head.
