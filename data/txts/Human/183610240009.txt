THOMAS ARNOLD. I am a policeman. I was on duty the same night, along with Argent—I was crossing the road with him a little before one o'clock—I saw two men run by me, in a direction from the prosecution—they were going the same way as he was going—the next I saw were the two prisoner—they walked by me, and the prosecutor close behind them—he told he should give the two prisoner into custody for robbing him of 18 s  —I took them—I heard Elms say he was quite innocent of it, he know nothing about it—the other said the same—I told the prosecutor to he quite positive of them before he gave them in charge, as it was a serious charge—he said, he was quite positive—I heard Bell say it would go hard with him, for he was quite in custody before attempting Mr. Pearce's, the superintendent's pocket.
