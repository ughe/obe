1712. WILLIAM HARISS was indicted for feloniously breaking
and entering the dwelling-house of Jemima Jane Nicholls, on the 25th of
April, at Camberwell, and stealing therein 6 rings, value 15a. 5 brooches,
value 158. 3 seals, value 108. I pair of ear-rings, value 58. 2 pencil-
cases, value 128. 4 spoons, value 148. 1 knife, value 28.; 1 eye-glass,
value 48.; 1 neck chain, value 28. 1 pair of bracelets, value 38. 1 cross,
value 18.; 1 snap, value 28. 1 scent-bottle, value 1a. 1 pair of studs,
value 1s. 1 necklace, value 1s.; 2 sovereigns, 1 shilling, 1 sixpence, 2
groats, and 1 silver coin, called a 44 threepence," the property of the said
Jemima Jane Nicholls.
MR. LILLY conducted the Prosecution.
JEMIMA JANE NICHOLLS. I live in Denmark-road, in the parish of
Camberwell On the 25th of April, between eleven and twelve o'clock at
noon, I left my house with my son who lives with me-I left the
front door double locked, and the gate, the parlour door, and the
door at the end of the passage, locked, but the outer door, which goes into
the garden, was open, and ajarthere is a garden and a field in the rear of
the house, and a road that runs parallel with our road and the cross-road
leading to it] received information, and returned to my house about five
'eloek, I found the gate still locked, the street-door on the single lock, the
back-parlour door open, and also the door leading to the kitchen, which I
had left locked-on going up stairs, I found the drawer of the looking-glass,
which I had locked, forced open--a desk in the parlour was broken open,
and the things seattered about--I missed some coins from the desk, and
from the looking-glass drawer a pair of bracelets, six rings, four brooches,
and other articles, the whole worth between 51. and 60. lost a table and
tea-spoon.
