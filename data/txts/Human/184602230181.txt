MICHAEL CONEY. I live in James-place, Gravel-lane, and am a hair and size manufacturer—Mr. Bartlett came to me in July, about being security for Olpin for five guineas—I said, "If it were my own father, I would not"—I did not mind 50 s ., but no more—he sent me notice afterwards, and I went to Olpin, and said what did he bring my name in question for—he said there was some difference about Mr. Bartlett, and there was some bill—I went away and heard no more—Mr. Bartlett afterwards came and said, "About that bill of 5 l . 5 s ."—I said, "I know nothing about it"—he went back to Olpin, and he told him it was my son—Mr. Bartlett came back and said, "It is your son"—I asked my son if he did it—he said he did not—it passed on from that time till the prisoner was taken to Union-hall, and they sent for me—I went, and said to the prisoner, "Did I do it for you"—"No," says he, "your son did it"—my son was not at home—I did not make this cross on this bill—I did not authorise any person to do it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Can you read this bill? A.  No—I do not know the contents of it—when I said I would not mind being answerable for 50 s . I was not told I should have to give a written paper—I am not in the habit of mak ing marks—I was security for Olpin to a loan society with another man for 5 l . or 6 l .—I laid hold of the pen then, and the clerk wrote my name—I did not make a cross—I have known the prisoner seven or eight years—I have seen, him as I have any other person in the street—I do not become security to lost societies for any other person I see in the street—I have only been secu rity for him once—I believe that is twelve months ago—I will swear that on the 18th of July I was not at Olpin's house—I cannot tell what day of the week the 18th of July was—I will swear before God and man, that on no day about the middle of July I was at Olpin's house—I can do that honestly—I never was in any trouble—I never was in a court-house in my life, I am quite certain—I have never been in this gaol—I have never been indicted in this court or any other—I never heard of an indictment for perjury against me—I mean to swear I have never been indicted for perjury—I never was under a lock in my life yet, except my own door—I never dealt in cart-grease—I never was indicted for swearing that I did not receive a load of cart-grease, whereas I did receive it—I do not know Mr. Margeson of the new leather-market, Bermondsey—I will swear he never indicted me for perjury, nor any body else, thank God—after this bill became due I did not go to Olpin's and say, "You had better say my son accepted it to get a little more time"—I am certain of that, and I never saw the bill till I saw it with Mr. Palmer. PATRICK CONEY. I am the son of Michael Coney—I never put this mark on this bill.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Can you read and write? A.  I can read very well—I can write as much as my own name.
   COURT . Q.  Do you remember your father being charged with perjury? A.  No, I never remember his being tried for it—I never heard of it.
   MR. PARRY . Q.  Just remember—do not you recollect his being indicted for perjury, and being committed to gaol for it? A.  No, I do not—I do not know Mr. Margeson of the new leather-market, Bermondsey—I do not remember my father ever being in trouble, or in gaol since I have known him—I have never been in trouble myself.
