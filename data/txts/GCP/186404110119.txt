473. HENRY BROWN (23), Unlawfully having in his possession 3
counterfeit shillings, with intent to utter them.
MESSRS, POLAND and UNDERDOWN conducted the Prosecution.
Ann Thomas: I am the wife of Hamphrey Thomas, No. 47, Weymouth
street, Walworth-we keep a chandler's shop-on 27th February the prisoner
came into my shop-he asked for half an ounce of tea, and he gave bad
shilling-after he was gone I found it was bad—I followed him and gave
him in charge--I gave the shilling to the policeman--this is the shilling-I
put a mark on it.

JAMES PAY (Police-sergeant, P 44). On the 27th February Mrs. Thomas
pointed out the prisoner to me--I took him into custody, and she gave me
this bad shilling-when I took him he put his hand into his left-hand coat-
poeket, and seemed to be fumbling with some papers--I said, "What have
you there?”-he bummed and hawed something I said, “Let me see what
you have there," and I took out of his haud a piece of paper-I unfolded
it, and found it contained two counterfeit shillings wrapped in paper, with
paper between the shillings-I said, "O here are two others wrapped up
separately he said, “ It is usual for me to wrap my money up separately."
-I then took him back to Mrs. Thomas --I searched him, and saw he was
very fidgetty to put his hand into his pocket-I did not search that pocket,
for a moment, but finding he was so anxious I said, “Well, let me see what
you have there," and I took this other counterfeit shilling from that pocket,
that was the waistoont pocket-I produce that shilling, as well--when I
found that, I said, “O, here is another one"-he did not say anything to
that-I found on him a small parcel of tea, about two ounces of butter, a
cap, another besides the one he was wearing; it was not a new one, two
sixpences, and sevenpence-balfpenny in copper, that was good money
