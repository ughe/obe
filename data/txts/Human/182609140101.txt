   ELLEN HADLIN. I pawned this Bible and Prayer-book for 8s. on Tuesday evening, the 5th of September, about a quarter before eight o'clock - the prisoner gave me them to pawn - I gave him the money - I had known him eight months ago - I am married, but my husband does not live with me - when I met the prisoner he asked me if I would do a kindness for him - he took the books out of his hat - I said "I don't like to take them;" he said "There is no danger at all;" I said "Very well, what shall I ask;" he said 10s. - they offered 8s. - I went back and asked him- he said "That will do;" I got it, and gave it him - he stood at the lamp opposite till I came out.
   JAMES SMITH MORTON. I was in the parlour behind the shop, and noticed the prisoner at the shop-window.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the bottom of Strand-lane, and returning, I saw two lads in a corner, and when they were gone I went and kicked the dirt, and picked up a bundle, which I put into my hat - when I got to the top of the lane I took the rag off, which was an old towel - I did not know what the books were, but I showed them to a young man, who told me - I then met this young woman who pawned them.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Three Month.
 1517.  MARY McDERMOT was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , 1 hearth-rug, value 5s., the goods of Michael Macklin.
 MICHAEL MACKLIN. I  keep a coal shed in Stretton-ground, Westminster- the prisoner had a furnished room in my house; I took her in last winter, when she was destitute, and turned out of her other lodging - she had been with me four or five months - this rug was taken from a closet, the lock of which was pushed back - I had not seen the rug for two months before.
   GEORGE PAUL. I am a broker, and live in York-street, Westminster. On the 4th of August, about twelve o'clock, the prisoner came into the shop with this rug; I asked if it was her's - she said Yes; she wanted 2s. for it; I said "Where do you live?" she said "No. 8, Tothill-street," and gave her 2s. - I then said "I will go as far as Tothill-street with you;" we went about ten yards, and then she said "What are you going for?" I then took her to the office.
 Prisoner's Defence. I leave myself to your mercy.
     GUILTY. Aged 56. Confined Two Years.
 1518.  GEORGE MYLAM was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 3 leather soles, value 1s. 6d., and 3 pairs of leather heels, value 1s., the goods of Thomas French.
   CHARLES BARRY. I was in Thomas French's shop on the 21st of July, and saw the prisoner there, who had selected some leather out of a drawer on the counter - I saw him put his hand twice into his left-hand coat-pocket, very quickly, and accused him of having leather here - he said he wondered how I dare to accuse him of such a thing - French then laid hold of his collar, and took five or six pieces out of his pocket - he asked forgiveness.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESWELL. Q. Was he sober? A. He appeared so - I will not swear he was.
 THOMAS FRENCH. I am a  leather seller. The prisoner came to my shop; the witness drew my attention to him, and he was stated fairly what took place; I found these pieces of leather in his left-hand coat-pocket.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known him? A. Three years and a half; I always thought him honest - it might be distress that drove him to it. GUILTY. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1519.  GEORGE NORRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of July , 1 bonnet, value 20s., and 2 yards of ribbon, value 2s., the goods of Sarah Chamberlain.
   ANN ORRITT. I am servant to Mr. Briant, of Euston-square. On the 15th of July, at three o'clock in the afternoon, I came into the kitchen and saw the prisoner, who had come down the area steps, and was going out of the kitchen, with the bonnet in his right hand, which belonged to my fellow servant - he dropped it when he saw me, and ran away; I called Stop thief! he got as far as the end of the square, when he was taken.
 SARAH CHAMBERLAIN. I am  cook in this house. My fellow servant had brought down my bonnet to put the ribbons on for me.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Confined Three Months.
 1520.  ELIZABETH NASH was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 1 half-sovereign, 1 half-crown, 4 shillings, and 4s. in copper monies, the monies of John Moye.
 JOHN MOYE. I am an  oilman, and live in Drury-lane- the prisoner lodged in my house for nine months. On the 25th of August I put half a sovereign, 4s. 6d., and a piece of paper, into my piano-forte, by the side of the door, in my first floor; I went out, and returned about ten o'clock in the evening; I cannot tell whether the room door was locked; when I returned the money was gone - I made inquiry, and the servant gave me information - I got an officer, and he went to the prisoner's door; I said "Mrs. Nash, I want to speak with your boy (who is about nine years of age);" the officer went into the room - she wanted to push him out but he would go in, and asked the little boy if he had any pictures, and while he was searching the boy, the prisoner took up a caddy, and ran down stairs with it - the officer ran after her, and stopped her - she screamed "Murder, for God's sake! one shilling is mine;" this money was found in the caddy.
   JOHN HUGHES. I went on the night in question and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in bed and asleep when they came in - I was looking for the key of the caddy - they took it and said they could open it - what money was in it I do not know.
 JOHN MOYE. The boy owned that he took the money out of the piano-forte, and carried it up to his mother.
     NOT GUILTY.
