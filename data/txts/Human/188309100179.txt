ALFRED COLE. I am a numerical printer in the service of McCorquo dale's—I received the tickets to number, and handed them over to Mary White to do—I did not see them after they were numbered—I know the numbers—several tickets were shown me at the police-court, and amongst them were tickets that had not been numbered by me; they were not ours at all, duplicates—Langrish showed me some type and some print ing ink in a paper—to the best of my belief I should say that type would produce the numbers on the forged tickets—the lines on these betting cards ( produced ) have been erased and fresh ones stamped in on both sides—the first of these cards ( produced ) is all right; it has got the numbers on; the second one has had the lines erased; I can read the remainder of the numbers—on the third card fresh lines have been put on—the red and black ink are both printing ink—I stamped these numbers with the type and ink.
   Cross-examined by MR. GEOGHEGAN . I have seen the bottle found on Taylor; this is it ( produced )—I have had twelve or fourteen years' expe rience, and know no chemical that will erase printing ink completely—I have not tried this stuff—the type on Hoodless's tickets is not of a very common fount—it is not very uncommon for tickets; the figures are cut nicer—I have seen similar type before.
   Re-examined.  I never saw any type cut on both sides before. MARY ELLEN WHITE. I am employed at McCorquodale's to number—I numbered the tickets for the August Bank Holiday meeting—some of the tickets shown me bear our numbers and some do not—I have looked through them. FREDERICK CATHCART. I live at Ferndale Road, Clapham, and am manager to the Croydon Racecourse Committee—Mr. Frederick Verrall is clerk of the course—we issue tickets for entrance to the large enclosure, at 2 s .—before the Bank Holiday meeting we had 2,000 tickets printed for that 2 s . enclosure, numbered 1 to 2,000 consecutively, by McCorquo dale's—the practice is, a person buys a ticket at the box and gives it up at the entrance as he goes in—anybody coming out would get a pass, which Would be one of the same tickets as sold at the box—neither of the prisoners are connected in any way with the committee—after the day's racing on Bank Holiday the tickets taken on that day were brought to me by the checktakers—I handed them to Langrish, who found among them 48 forged tickets—I am a wood engraver; I can tell the difference between the genuine numbers and the forged—the red ink is the bookmaker's—the type found on the prisoner would decidedly print 23 the same as on this forged ticket—anybody going into the enclosure with a forged ticket might come out and get a good ticket.
