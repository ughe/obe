SAMUEL WHITLEY. I am the son of Thomas Whitley, a shoemaker On Tuesday I was at the King's Arms public-house at Eating I saw the prisoner there, and in the skittle-ground, and saw him change a sovereign for a pint of half-and-half—I saw him with two sovereigns and a half-sovereign and some silver—he put his money back into a little cloth bag, rather dirty—it was something of a canvas cloth, and had a little string it was a common bag. JOHN BALL. re-examined.  My money was in a sort of canvas bag, dirty and it drew up with a bit of string. RICHARD FRUIN. I am a policeman. On Tuesday last I apprehended the prisoner at Mrs. Hope's—he said, sooner than be locked up he would pay the money back—I found 4 s . 6 d . in silver, and 2 1/2 d . in copper on him, but no bag. NOT GUILTY
