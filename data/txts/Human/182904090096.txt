 860.  JOHN ROBINSON and HENRY WOOD were indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 1 hat, value 4s. 6d., and 1 apron, value 6d., the goods of Robert Roy.
   ROBERT ROY. I live in Hatton-wall, and am a letter-founder. On the 24th of February, my hat, and the apron in it, were on the parlour-table at the Coopers' Arms public-house, West-street; the prisoners sat in the room - I was not above three minutes in the house when I missed them; the prisoners went out, and returned again - my hat and apron were safe then; I missed the hat directly, and accused Wood with having taken it, as they were nearest to it - after that Robinson went out, and was taken by the watchman, and in half an hour I saw my hat and apron at Giltspur-street - both of the prisoners were in custody - they were strangers to me.
 Prisoner WOOD. Q. Did I leave the house before you gave me leave to go? A. He left before they were missed, then returned, and I missed them.
   JOHN CUNNIFF. I am waiter at the public-house. On the 24th of February I saw the prosecutor go into the parlour; the prisoners were in the room; he suspected the prisoners of having taken his property - there were three of them together; the three came into the room first, and had a pint of beer; two went out with their hats on, and one came in again without his hat - I saw the prisoners in custody, with the hat and apron.
 Prisoner WOOD. Q. Did I go out at the same time as the other prisoner? A. The two prisoners went out together after the hat and apron were missed; I lifted the hat up to wipe the table, and put it down in the same place.
   JOHN HUTCHINS. I am a watchman, of West-street, West Smithfield - this public-house is on my beat. I met the landlord, and in consequence of what he siad to me, I took Wood - Rogers took Robinson in a coffee-shop, and brought him to me; I took him into the public-house, and Wood was there - the prosecutor gave charge of both of them, and said they had stolen his hat; I found the hat and apron in a coffee-shop in West-street, which Robinson was brought out of - I found it concealed under the table, covered over with the apron.
   GEORGE ROGERS. I am a watchman. I took Robinson out of the coffee-shop, where the hat and apron were found; asked him if he knew any thing of them; he said he did not; I took him coming out of the door - the hat and apron were found there half an hour afterwards, by Hutchins; I did not see Wood in the coffee-shop.
   JOHN HUTCHINS. Wood was taken in the Coopers' Arms.
   WILLIAM HOOKER. I am a constable of the night. The prisoners were brought into the watch-house about eleven o'clock; the hat and apron were afterwards brought in, and claimed by Roy.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 ROBINSON's Defence. I was at my lodgings. A young man in light hair came in, and asked for a night's lodging: he sat down, and talked with me about the house, and asked what time they shut up; he said he would go and have a pint of beer, and asked me to go with him; I asked this prisoner to go with us - we went to the Coopers' Arms; I went out, and left all the company there; I went to Smithfield, and then to my lodgings, and just as I got inside the door I was taken - if I had put the property there, it is not likely I should have gone there to sleep - the mistress of the Coopers' Arms said a young man, with light hair, had come in without a hat, and gone out with one on.
   GEORGE ROGERS. I found he did lodge at this coffee-shop.
 WOOD's Defence. I was the only person in the room at the time the hat was missing, and he acquainted me of it - I said I knew nothing of it; he then said it must be the two who were with me; I went out to find them, leaving my hat there, and when I returned, Robinson was in custody.
     NOT GUILTY.
