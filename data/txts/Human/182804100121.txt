I was with Gibson and saw the prisoner stooping down in London-house-yard; I asked him where he got the pork; he said "If you go to the Cap and Salutation tap. they will give you particulars;" Gibson went there, returned, and said the publican said he had been there, but had left about ten o'clock; the prisoner then said if I would go to one Smith, who worked with Mr. Fisher in the market, he would give me another answer; I went there, and they were shut up; I then asked the prisoner where he lived; he said "Go to old Price;" I left them under his care; I went there and he said he had not - I went back and told the prisoner.
 JURY. Q. Did you enquire the next mornign of Fisher? A. Yes; I went and found Smith, his foreman, he told me he never sold him any such thing.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you tell Smith you were an officer? A. Not till afterwards, but they pretty well knew it; I said "I am an officer, and if you sold them to Jones you had better come up to the Hall."
 Prisoner's Defence. I have been a working-man about Newgate-market; I throw myself on your mercy.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 29. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. - Confined 2 Months.

 1067.  JOSEPH HUGHES was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of February , 1 truck, value 4l. the goods of George Durrant and others.
   MORRIS ELLIS. I am in the employ of George Durraut, Brown and Co. - they are silk men, and live in Winchester-street. On the 14th of February, about five o'clock, a truck was lost from their door, where it usually stood; at half past six o'clock I went to put it away for the night, and it was gone; the next morning the officer came to the counting-house - I went with him to see the truck, and knew it was my master's.
   JOHN GLEESON. On the 14th of February, in the evening, I saw the prisoner in company with another man in Rosemary-lane - they offered me a truck for sale - I did not buy it; they went up the Minories with it; the officer came and asked me if they had offered me the truck - I said, Yes; I believe it to be the same that I saw again the next morning; I am sure the prisoner took an active part in offering it for sale.
   JAMES ROBERTS. On the 14th of February I saw the prisoner and two other men an Tower-hill, at a quarter past seven o'clock in the evening - they stood talking together for 5 or 6 minutes, when the prisoner, and one of the other men, went to a dark corner in Tower-hill, and took a truck out; they went up to the last witness, and when they separated from him I went to him, and asked what they were going to do with the truck - he said they had offered it him for sale for 15s., and that the wheels were worth two guineas; I pursued them, and took the other one, who was tried and convicted here last Session; the prisoner got away at that time.
 JURY. Q. Did you see the prisoner take the truck? A. I have no doubt he is the man; I went to the witness and said, "You know that young man who offered the truck for sale - he said, "Yes; his name is Joseph Hughes."
 Prisoner's Defence. That man stopped me, and asked if I wanted a job - I told him, Yes, and he said if I wheeled the truck down the Minories he would give me 6d; I took it down, and he asked if I knew any body who wanted such a thing - I took it to the witness, who offered to buy it, and told me to take it down the first turning; when the officer came I ran away.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 1061.  MARY CLIFFORD alias    BURKE was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of March , 1 gold ring, value 6s., 1 half-crown, and 2s., the property of Henry Harris.
   MARY HARRIS. I am the wife of Henry Harris. We live in Augusta-street, Hampstead-road. On the 22d of March, I met the prisoner coming over Blackfriars' - bridge; she was quite a stranger - she nearly stopped me, by seeming to pick up something under my feet; which appeared to be two seals, a ring, and a chain. She stopped me, and said, I should have half; we walked on till we came to the second street over the bridge - she asked me if I knew where there was a silversmiths, I said, No; we walked on till we met a young man - she asked him if he knew where there was a silversmiths; he said, he worked at one - we walked on, and I asked how far she was going to take me - she then asked him the value of them; he said 30l., but we should not get more than 28l. if we went to sell them; she then told me, if I would go with her into the country, she would give me the half part of it - I said I could not; she then told me to give her my ring, and what money I had, and wait at the end of Fleet-market, and she would come to me in an hour and a half, and bring me 12l. I gave her my ring and 4s. 6d., and waited for her till three or four o'clock, but she did not come - I met her again on the following Monday, near the bridge; she crossed the road when she saw me - I crossed after her, and asked her for my ring and my money; she said she did not know what I meant - but I insisted upon having them, or I would raise the street; she had another young man with her then - she said if I would not make a noise, I should have them; we walked on till we met the young man whom I had seen the first time. I asked her again for my property, and she said if I would hold my tongue, I should have them in a few minutes; and the two young men went for it, but they did not return - I waited twenty minutes or half an hour; then called assistance, and gave her into custody.
