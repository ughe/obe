MR. HORRY. Q. Did you not say to Margaret Hurley and Mrs.
Goodwin, that you were sorry for what you had done! 4. I said I should

BENJAMIN ROBERTS (policeman, G 1). I took Hurley on the night of
12th May, about half-past 2 o'clock in the morning-he was charged by the
last witness with an assault upon her-there was a cut over her eye, which
was bleeding, and there were marks of violence upon her throat-I took bim
to the police-court in the morning-he was there charged with the assault, and
afterwards with this-I was present when Mr. Collins recognised the voice
and cough; they were Hurley's.
Cross-examined by Mr. Horry. Q. Was not Wheatman's charge aban-
doned ? A. No; the Magistrate remanded him-1 told Mr. Collins to attend
at the Court that was in consequence of a communication from Wheatman
-I know big Mick very well, but have not looked for him; I knew it was
of no use.
GEORGE NBALB (policeman, G 204). On 20th May I went with Wheatman
to a beer-shop in Spitalfields--she pointed out Catton, and I took him-she
bad seen hin go in first, and I went in and asked for George Catton-his
master said, “That is him."
HURLEY--Guilty.** Aged 21.-Transported for Ten Years.
CATTON-GUILTY.t Aged 20.-Confined Nine Months.

1875. JOHN CASTLEDINE, feloniously forging and uttering a bill of
Exchange for 15l.; with intent to defraud Frederick Mighell, and another-
Two other Counts, with intent to defraud James Croucher.
MR. BIR is conducted the Prosecution.
FREDERICK MIGHELL. I live at Barking, in Essex, and am a farmer. On
10th Jan. I sold a mare to the prisoner for 5l.; he left me a 151. bill, payable
at one month-be said he would bring the cash in the morning, or the fol-
lowing day, and take the bill away-1 asked him who he had the bill of; he
said of an old retired gentleman at Loughton, whose name was Croucher, for
two cows-be did not come next day, or the day after, and I went and made
inquiries-he has never paid me for the mare.
