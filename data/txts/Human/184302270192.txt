I conduct the business of a publican, for my father—I have known the prisoner about two years—I bought these six num bers of Froissart's Chronicle  of him—I took them in monthly—I first took them seven or eight months ago—the prisoner came there, and I was shown the works—I paid him a shilling a number for them—sometimes I paid him in money, and sometimes he had spirits—I cannot tell how much money I have given him—we used to have a settlement, sometimes in two or three months—these twenty-three numbers of Knight's London , produced from the pawnbroker's I bought of the prisoner, and gave them to him to get bound—I never went to any shop with him—I always bought at home—I knew the prisoner lived at a tailor's shop, in New Inn-passage—I went before the Magistrate, and heard the charge made against him, and found that the twenty-three numbers I gave him to bind he had pawned at Lamb's—after that I called on Mr. Gilbert—I did not tell him that the prisoner should be well defended—I took my father's attorney with me to say that we would not give up any more books to Mr. Gilbert, but produce them before the Magis trate.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Does your father keep the Crown Inn, in the Borough? A.  Yes—I live at the Crooked Billet, in Wych-street, and manage the business for my father—I bought these books in a regular way, and gave a regular price for them—I took in Knight's London  from the first—we did not send beer to the prisoner, but sometimes he has fetched it. NOT GUILTY.
 1013. JAMES SIMS was again  indicted for stealing, on the   1st of Sept ., 1 pamphlet, value 1 s  .; and 2 books, 1 s  .; the goods of Samuel Gilbert , his master. SAMUEL GILBERT. On the 9th of Feb., I gave the prisoner into custody—I went from information to Mr. Winton's—I there found a copy of Frois  sart's Chronicle, which I can speak to as having been once in my shop, from an off-set of the tissue paper which has injured the colouring of it, and here is a break in the first quarter of one of the banners—my attention was called to it, as on one occasion I offered it to a person for sale—he saw this and would not have it—I put it aside as unsaleable on the shelf—I never sold it to my knowledge—I can distinctly swear to this as having been in my possession at that time—I found at Mr. Mountcashell's, in Bedford-court, Covent-garden, two books, one " Ball-room Guide ," and "Fancy Needle-work"  —they were not published by me, but I had such in my stock—I cannot say I missed them as my stock is extensive—the prisoner had no right to sell any copies of mine, and never bought any to my knowledge.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You cannot swear you did not sell the copy of Froissart? A.  No—I did not sell it to the prisoner, but I can not swear that nobody in my establishment did. THOMAS GILBERT. I am the prosecutor's brother—I did not sell this book to the prisoner—I have seen him buy Chamber's Journal  now and then—they are 1 1/2 d . a number. GEORGE MOUNTCASHELL. I am a bookseller, and live in Bedford-court, Covent-garden. I bought these two books of the prisoner. HENRY LUKE WINTON. I bought this copy of Froissart  of the prisoner. NOT GUILTY.
