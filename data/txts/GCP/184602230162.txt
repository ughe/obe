the officer ? A. 178. 60.--I took out a half-crown to change, and put the

about 3d. or 4d.-I paid 1s. 2d. away-a knife was found on Cooper I be

-the gates had not been left unlocked during the dinner-hour for weeks pre-
vious to this transaction, and the men are never there unless I am present

JAMES BAKER ( policeman.) On Saturday, the 14th, I went with Single
ton to the Mint, and found both the prisoners there-Winton first-Cooper, 1
believe, ran out of the house-Singleton brought him to me--I took both to
the station--in going along Winton said to Cooper, " They cannot swear to
the money"-I searched Cooper at the station and found on him a knife.
two half-crowns, sixpence, a penny, and one halfpenny; and on Winton, one
half-crown, two shillings, and fourpence-I asked why they left the workhouse
---they said they had heard of another job-I asked where--they said at the
docks--I asked which docks--they could not tell me-I went to the work.
house, examined the outer shed, and saw some sharp instrument had hooked
the bolt of the lock back, and the marks corresponded with the knifo-
examined the counting-house door, and found a piece of wood had been cat
away by screwing the knife round, and there is a little gap in the knife-
tried both the locks to see if I could put them back with the knife after they
were locked, and I did.
MARY Dagg. I am a widow, and live in the Borough-I sell seconda
hand clothes--on Saturday, the 14th of Feb., the prisoners came to my shop
together-Cooper bought this coat of me, and Winton bought a jacket.
WILLIAM CHANDLER. I live in Ball-alley, Mint-I was at work at the
stone-yard—I left at one o'clock to go to dinner-I returned to work at two
o'clock, and as I went I met the prisoners coming away from the workhouse
--they told me they had got a job and were not coming to work this after
noon-I asked where the work was they did not tell me, and said I might
take some of their wood to make up my lot.
Winton's Defence. We left work at one o'clock, and in going along, we
went into the Ship and Blue-coat-boy, and just as we got to the Elepban
and Castle met a gentleman with two boxes; we asked if we should carry
them : he said, " What will you take them to the Dover Railway for
said 18.; he said, " Make haste;" when we got there he gave us 18d.; W
thought it not worth while to return to work, being so late ; we are quite
innocent of the crime.

Cooper's Defence. I left the yard with Winton, intending to return; we
called at the Ship and had a pint of beer; we came out and said we did not
think we should return that afternoon; we went to the Elephant and Castle
and met a gentleman with two boxes; we carried them to the Dover railway,
and he gave us 18d.; I then said to Winton there was a man over the water
that owed me 23. and I should go and get it ; we were sitting down to tas

up; whether Singleton and the officer called once or twice I do not know!
heard him the last time; I came down with him, and was accused of stealing
