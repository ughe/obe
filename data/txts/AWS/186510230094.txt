ROBERT COPELAND. I live at 180, Tooley-street, Borough- remember a
person resembling the prisoner coming there in July last-two females
brought some luggage to my place on the eveuing of 27th July-they called
again on the morning of the 28th-in consequenee of what they Said to me
I refused to give up the property-I saw one of those women at the Police-
court; she was pointed out as the prisoner's wife.
ALPRED MANSPIELD (Policeman, P 312). In consequence of information
I went to Mr. Copeland's on 28th July last, and received from him two bags
and a hat bor-one bag contained two pair of shoes, one pair of side-spring
boots, two shirts, two collars, and one pocket handkerchief-the other con-
tained one large knife, one opera-glass, two bottles of scent, and a box ol
gold ear rings the hat box contained two pair of kid gloves, two collars,
one pair of stockings, three silk ties, one tie-ring, one Turkish cap, one
Seoteh cap, two newspapers, and a hook in French.
MARY ANDREWS. I live at 180, East-street, Walworth-I have known
the prisoner between two and three years-I know that he went to Paris
somewhere in July last, I can't tell the date exactly, it was a few days
before he was taken into eustody-I remember his return from Paris-he
said he had been to Paris-he did not say anything about any gold watehes
-I remember pledging a brooch for him, he did not tell me anything about
that brooch-I saw Mrs. Manley after the prisoner was taken into.custody,
on the subject of this robbery-Idon know whether the prisoner had any
money when he came from Paris-I was with him at the time he was taken
into eustody-] went with him to the Leipsic-road--I was not constantly
with him at the other apartment, I had been visiting there-I did not iden-
tify the brooeh at the pawnbroker's-the prisoner did not tell me that it
was the proseoutrix's brooch-I will swear that-he did give me a chain
to pledge--I did not receive a letter from him while he was in Paris-some
time back he asked me to buy the ticket of a gold chain, that was last
winter-he never told me anything about any notes, not a word, I am cer
tain of that.
COURT. Q. Were you one of those that went with the bags to Mr. Cope

ARTHUR CHARLES DILLON. I am an officer at Beal's wharf, Tooley-street, it
is a bonded tea warehouse-I know the prisoner, he was in ehabito working
there-he was there in July last-he left on Friday, 21st July-I did not
see him again after that until he was in eustody-h did not say that he was
going away-he never gave any notice to any one-I am not the person
that would have the notice, but I have made inquiries-I paid his wages to
his wife when she came for them on the following Monday, the 24th-he
used to have half-a-crown a day, aud three weeks before he left his wages
were raised 1s. Gd. a week, which would make 16s. 6d. a week-when I paid
wages on the Monday, 7d. was deducted for beer money- paid 14a. 5d.,
that was up to the Friday night.
