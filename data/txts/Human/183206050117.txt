Prisoner's Defence. I was suddenly thrown into prison, without the means of letting my friends know, or I should have been able to contradict a great part of the evidence against me; if the prosecutor had been here, I could have asked him a few questions, which would have been of great consequence - I was not in the house after seven o'clock that evening.
     GUILTY. Aged 34.
 1717.  JAMES YOUNG was again indicted for stealing, on the 27th of March , 2 pillows, value 7s., and 1 blanket, value 7s., the goods of Charles Burrows.
   ELIZABETH BURROWS. I am the wife of Charles Burrows: we live in Bunhill-row. The prisoner took a furnished lodging at our house, on the 7th of March; he staid three weeks - he only paid one week's rent; on the 27th of March, at two o'clock, he borrowed a pen and ink, and soon afterwards I found the key of his door; I kept it till the next morning, when I called in an officer to open his room door; I found on the mantel-piece a small piece of writing, and the duplicate of two pillows and a blanket, which had been let to him with the room.
   JOHN BURBRIDGE. I am shopman to Mr. Sams, a pawnbroker. I have two pillows, which were pawned on the 16th, and on the 26th of March, in the names of David Franklin, and James Franklin; I have no knowledge of who pawned them.
   REGINALD CHARLES OTTER. I am shopman to Mr. Latter, a pawnbroker. I have a blanket, which was pawned on the 16th of March, by a man, for half a crown; I cannot say who - it was in the name of James Franklin.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1718.  JOSEPH SHEPHERD was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of July , 8 shillings, and 2d., the monies of Thomas Pimlott.
   MARY PIMLOTT. I am the wife of Thomas Pimlott. On the 3rd of July, a sailor came to lodge at my house; the prisoner came with him - the prisoner gave me half a sovereign to fetch half a gallon of beer; I put the change on the mantel-piece, and the prisoner took it - it was the sailor's money, and he is not here.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1719.  WILLIAM SYKES was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of a person unknown, from his person.
   WILLIAM OLIVER(Police-constable M 51). On the 26th of June I was on duty in Hyde-park. I was holding Colonel Rowan's horse, and saw the prisoner standing behind a crowd - I saw him take the handkerchief from a gentleman's pocket; he was making his escape - I secured him; he said, "I have done nothing, and have got nothing"- I said "I will see what you have got;" I felt down his trousers, and took out this handkerchief.
 Prisoner. A woman came up, and said, she saw me take it off the grass, which I did. Witness. A woman came by and said, "Say you picked it off the grass," and I said,"You ought to be ashamed of yourself to tell the boy so;" I was obliged to take the prisoner back to the horse to secure him, and I lost sight of the gentleman.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1720.  BENJAMIN STANTON was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of June , 1 coat, value 17s., the goods of George Farrant and another.
   PETER HALDANE. I am shopman to George and William Farrant; they are pawnbroker s. On the 23rd of June a coat was stolen from their door - I had seen it at half-past ten o'clock, and missed it about eleven; I saw it brought in by the officer, between seven and eight, the same evening.
   WILLIAM LAW(Police-constable H 88). On the day stated, I saw the prisoner go into a house in Wentworth-street; he hung this coat across a chair-back; I went in, and asked him who it belonged to - he said to Mr. Sheen, who was there asleep; I awoke him, and asked if it was his - he said, "No, I know nothing about it."
   ANN SHEEN. I am the daughter of William Sheen, of Wentworth-street. I was cleaning the door between twelve and one o'clock; the prisoner brought in this coat; the officer came in and took him away? - I never saw him before, I swear that; the door was open and he came in- my father is a wheelright; he was asleep on a table, as he had been out all night on a job.
 Prisoner. They do know me, and her brother has been tried here. Witness. He may say so, but I never saw him in my life before.
 Prisoner's Defence. My mother is dead, and my father has three of us to keep; these people buy things, and I went there to sell it.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1721.  ROBERT THOMPSON and SAMUEL RAWLINGS were indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of June , 1 ham, value 11s., the goods of Theophilus Walter.
   THEOPHILUS WALTER. I live in Hoxton, and am a cheesemonger. On the 22nd of June I had a ham hanging on the door of my shop; I missed it about eight o'clock in the morning - this is it.
   FRANCIS FEATHERSTONE(Police-constable N 88). On the 22nd of June I was on duty in Kingsland-road, about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's; I received information from Mr. Dyer, that two boys had gone down a court with a bag - I went down, and saw the two prisoners standing, and a bag on the ground, close to them; I examined it, and found in it this ham.
   MARY ANN DYER. I am the wife of John Dyer. I was standing at my door that morning, and saw two lads cross the road with a bag - they went down a gateway; I told the officer, who went and took them with the bag.
   THOMAS HARRIS. I am a sweep. I was in Fleming-street, and saw Thompson with a ham in his arms - he crossed the road, and Rawlings met him - they took a bag from a hearth-stone barrow and put it into it.
 Thompson's Defence. I was coming down the street, and this lad asked me to carry it to Shoreditch church, and he would give me 3d. or 4d. - when we got to Kingsland road, he said, "Turn down this turning;" he then said, "Here are two Policemen coming;" I said, "You must have stolen the ham;" he then said, "Throw it away."
 Rawlings' Defence. I took the ham - this lad is quite innocent.
 RAWLINGS -    GUILTY- Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 THOMPSON -    NOT GUILTY.
