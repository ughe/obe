RACHAEL DAVIES. I am a widow, and live in Clarence-terrace, St.
Andrew's-row. I am the landlady of the house, and the witnesses lodge
there, and two others-one of them has two rooms up stairs, and one down
stairs—they are married-Mr. and Mrs. Sherwin lodge in the two rooms
up stairs, and a man and his wife in the back-room-I cannot speak
the name the two witnesses have the front workshop, and a room up
stairs, I have no servant-when the two witnesses go out, they lock their
door and bring the key to me- put it into my room, which we call the
kitchen, and keep it till they come back at night-nobody could get at it
wben I was at home-when I was out they might on the Thursday I
was at home all day-I was at home about five o'clock-I had not been
out at all—I work in the kitchen when I am at home-I go in and out to
the yard—I do not take the key with me, I never loek the kitchen door
-the key was safe all day- Ferderer went out between eight and nine in
the morning, and Hofmire directly after-I was in the kitchen, as near as
I can say, from nine to five-pretty nearly all that time I was in the
kitchen when Hofmire came in for the key--as far as I know, nobody had
had it since he went out in the morning-nobody could get into the work-
shop without my hearing them it is a small house the key was left in
my care-I do not know who let Hofmire in at five-I did not the
lady up stairs did, as they say—the two female lodgers were at home at
five--I heard the knock at the door, and one of them let him in- I cannot
say who it was.
