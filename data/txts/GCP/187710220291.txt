HARRY TITTERTON. I am second clerk at Southwark police-court-on
10th September I was present at the hearing of this summons pro-
duced it is against William Timmings for unlawfully causing the steam
barge Swift to be navigated without having a licensed man on board
--the defendant appeared and called the present defendant as a witness
on his behalf-he was sworn, and I produce my notes of his evidence.
(These being read, stated that the defendant store that he was on board the bargo
Swift during her passage up the Thames on 22nd August, 1877 ; that he was
not navigating the said barge, but was on board the barge Willoy, which was
being towed by the Swift, and that Benjamin Elliott, the complainant, never came
near him or spoke to him on that day.) The question about Blackfriars Bridge
was repeated several times by the Magistrate in my hearing, but the
prisoner adhered to his statement that Elliott never came near. So
Cross-examined. Before the prisoner was examined the Magistrate
made some observation about his apparent state -I noticed that his
manner in the witness-box was peculiar, as if he had been drinking-I
cannot say that the Magistrate was somewhat exacting in his manner of
putting questions—his strangeness did not diminish under the Magistrate's
questions, but I cannot say that it increased under them.
