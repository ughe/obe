JAMES WILD (police-constable R 141.) I received information about
these coppers from Mr. Whiffen on Friday, the 17th-I had some conver-
sation with Fraser-I went to Mrs. Jackson's on Friday, the 17th-I asked
her to show me the copper she had previously shewn to Fraser-she stated
she had not the copper by her, she had sold it the day before to Myers, a rag
merchant in Spitalfields—I asked who she purchased it of-she said of two
boys-I asked her to show me the book-she said she knew the boys'
names, it was Parsons'-I asked where they lived-she said in Dowling-
street, Deptford-nothing was said about any other copper than that shewn
to Fraser-I went to the prisoners' mother in Dowling-street, and there
we took Henry-I took James at the tide mills in Church-street-we
kept them apart at the station-I asked James where he got that copper
which he sold to Mrs. Jackson-be said," I bought it of a man at Lewisham-
bridge for a half-crown"-in going along, I said, "Where did you get the
other one from !"-he said, "I bought that at a house at Lewisham-
bridge," or "Lewisham watch-house," I will not swear which I asked
him what he gave for that-he said half-a-crown-after that I went to
Mrs. Jackson again, and told her about buying the second copper, and
asked what she gave for the first which she bought of these two boys she
hesitated--I said, “Consider what situation you stand in ; what you state
I must state again"-after I told her that the boys had said they had sold
ber two, she stated she had bought one previously of the two boys Parsons,
and had sold the two to Mr. Myers, of Rose-lane, Spitalfields—I went to
Myers, and got these two.
