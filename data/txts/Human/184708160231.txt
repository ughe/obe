   Prisoner.  I was not three yards from the house; you asked me what was the matter; I said a man of the name of Shelly and his woman had been blowing  me up for what I was not guilty of: you said, "Come along with me, you shall not he insulted;" did not you? Witness.  Yes—it was right round at the back part of the orchard—you were without your bonnet—you did not come towards the house till you were taken—you were not drunk. SARAH EVERLY. I am the wife of John Everly, and live at the station-house, in the Greenwich-road—between one and two o'clock in the morning in question, I was desired to search the prisoner—she was very reluctant to be searched, and said she had nothing whatever about her—I found a purse in her bosom—she said it was her own, that she kept it to keep duplicates in, and said there were two in it then, but it was quite empty—it was shown to the prosecutor.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did not I say, if I had got a purse it was the one I kept my tickets in; and when you found it, I said, "That is my own?" A.  No, you said the duplicate were there.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The man gave me the money to mind till the morning, and I took it, as I was drunk and he was drunk; he said, "We wen't go to bed till we have had a drop more rum, bring half a pint;" he said, "Get it in a half-pint bottle for 1 s .;" I went to the first public-house, and could not get anything to drink; that was when I stood and saw the policeman; I was coming towards the house again; I did not attempt to run away; I no friends. WILLIAM FREDERICK SEELEY. I was formerly in the police—I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction—( read  — Convicted April , 1845, of larceny from the person, and confined one year )—I was present at the trial—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY.  Aged 22.— Judgment Respited.
