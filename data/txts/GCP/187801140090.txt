Cro88-examined by MR. SAFFORD. On 10th November I was acting-
inspector at Kennington Lane—the deceased came there with Robert

ment-at that time Robert Carnaby was the only man there-I asked
Mrs. Smy if she could tell me how her husband came by his injuries
she said that she could not, it was dark, and she was so frightened that

nesses I think were examined before me, and among them thuse who
have been examined to-day-none of them could say that they saw a
blow struck, and therefore Robert was discharged.
By Mr. REED. I heard no more of the case for some time, and after-
wards Robert disappeared.
ROBERT HIGGINS LLOYD. I am medical superintendent of Lambeth
Infirmary-on 13th November the deceased was brought in with a
wound on the back of his head about an inch and a half long, which
might have been inflicted ten days or a fortnight before by a blow or s
fall-it gave rise to diffuse inflammation of the membranes of the brain
and to pyæmia (blood-poisoning)—it must have been a violent blow-I
made a post-mortem examination-all the organs except the lungs, which
were, studded with pyama, were healthy, and the disease in the lungs was
caused by the blow.
Cross-examined by Mr. SAFFORD. I said at the police-court that the
wound might have been caused by this jug produced), but I explained
that I could not say how it struck the deceased-it is possible that the
blow might be caused by a blow on the fender.
