 682.  JAMES WILLIAM PEAT was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of February , 1 hat, value 5s., and 1 pair of gloves, value 3d., the goods of Richard Perry.
 RICHARD PERRY. I am a  boot-maker. On the 6th of February, I was in my shop, in Bath-street; a person gave me information, and I missed my hat and gloves: I went out, and overtook a boy - I found my hat and gloves in a house.
   MARY ANN CRAWLEY. The prisoner came into Mrs. Chapman's shop, and asked if he might leave a hat and gloves, and two blacking-bottles there; he left them on the counter - the bat was put under the counter.
   JOSEPH HASSAY. The prisoner came into my shop, about four o'clock in the afternoon, with two bottles of blacking, and in about half an hour I saw him in Perry's custody; I live about three minutes walk from Chapman's.
   CHRISTOPHER SHOILS. I saw a boy go into Perry's shop with the blacking to sell - I live opposite, and saw him run out with a hat in his hand; I went to the door, and saw him come out of Chapman's shop without anything - I went and told Perry.
   SARAH CHAPMAN. A boy came to my shop, bought a penny-worth of sweetmeats, and asked if he might leave the hat, gloves, and blacking - I cannot swear to the prisoner.
   ALEXANDER MOON. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I left nothing in that shop.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 14. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Seven Days.
 683.  GEORGE KNIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of January , 6 bottles of wine, value 9s., the goods of Andrew Leonard Jacks.
   WILLIAM JONES. I saw the prisoner bottling some wine, belonging to the doctor on board the Tender, at the Tower-stairs- he put six bottles into his own locker, and locked it- he is ward-room steward; I told nobody of it for a day or two - the first Lieutenant and Master were on board.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is the doctor here? A. No; he told the prisoner to bottle off the wine; he did not say it was his wine - it was kept in the gun-room- that is not the doctor's room; the prisoner was caterer of the mess.    NOT GUILTY.
 684.  JOHN PARSONS was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of November , 1 potatoe-fork, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Wallis.
 THOMAS WALLIS. I live at Tottenham. I lent this fork to Deller about a week before it was lost - I know it to be mine.
   DANIEL DELLER. I live at the Ship public-house, Wallis lent me this fork; I laid it on the rack, in the stable, on the 17th or 18th of November; I saw it safe on the evening of the 18th, and on the morning of the 19th it was gone; the prisoner used to sleep in the hay-loft - I know the ostler, and he allowed me to leave it there; I saw the prisoner there the night it was stolen.
   JOHN CASTLE. I live at Tottenham. On Monday, the 19th of November, about half-past five o'clock, I saw the prisoner as I came out of my house to work - he had this fork under his arm, and asked me to buy it; I said No, but I bought it at last, and kept it till the officer came with a warrant to the farm I work at, and took it.
   JOSEPH FORSTER. I am an officer, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not sleep there; on the morning I sold him the fork I found it under a hedge, in the field, and did not know it had been in the loft; a witness knows I was not there, but I could not afford to pay him to come.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Transported for Seven Years.
 685.  FRANCIS SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 watch, value 4l.; 1 ribbon, value 1d., and 1 key, value 1d., the goods of Elizabeth Marrs.
 ELIZABETH MARRS. On the 26th of November, about four o'clock in the afternoon, I lost a watch, which hung in my parlour, in  Lisson-grove; three boys came into the shop; I got up to shut the door, after they were gone, and missed it - they came first in the morning; the prisoner was with them then, but I cannot swear he came in the afternoon.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You have convicted three other boys? A. Only two were convicted - three were tried.
   GEORGE TEASDALE BUCKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Brentford. This watch was pawned in the name of Daniel Cooper; I believe, by the prisoner.
   THOMAS HENRY THOMPSON. I apprehended the prisoner on the 16th of Janurary - he had been out of the way ever since I took the other boys; he told me he could prove where he was when it wa stolen, that he was at Clerkenwell, with his mother or aunt.
     NOT GUILTY.
