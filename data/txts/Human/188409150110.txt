   MR. PEACOCK submitted that there was no proof of any intent to defraud, or that any person could be defrauded, as the Foreign Office had received their fee. There might be an attempt to deceive, but not to commit a particular fraud or a specific wrong. (See Stephen's Digest, p. 288.)  MR. POLAND  stated that the indictment charged an intent to prejudice, injure, defraud, or deceise, and referred to Reg v. Toshack, 2nd Dennison: the forgery consisted in obtaining something which was to be put of as a genuine document for as improper purpose. The RECORDER  , having consulted MR. JUSTICE HAWKINS  , ruled that there was evidence of an intention to defraud. GUILTY.— Two Months' Imprisonment.
