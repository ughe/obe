 1939. ELIZABETH WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 3 sovereigns, the monies of Elizabeth Percival . ELIZABETH PERCIVAL. I am single, and live in Little Chapel-street, Soho. On the 3rd of June I received my income, 18 l . odd—I had a little to drink, and was taken with a giddiness in my head, and lost my senses—when I awoke, I was in bed with the prisoner—I never was in that place before—I was in a strange place—we had some breakfast—the prisoner said the would go and see me home—I went down stairs, and pulled out a sove reign—I gave it to her, and asked her if she would go and get something to drink—she went out, and did not return—I had eight sovereign there, and missed two more, besides the one I gave her—she had an opportunity of taking them—there was no one else there when I went in.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I brought back the silver, and gave it her. Witness.  I never received it. ROBERT TAILOR( police-constable F  37.) I know the prosecutrix—she is represented to me by her landlord as not in her right senses—I have con versed with her, and think she is rather weak—the landlord called me to the door, and I told him if he thought proper to give the prisoner into custody I would take her—he declined it—I found the prosecutrix half drunk—she said she had eight sovereigns in her box, and then there were only fire—I then went to look for the prisoner, and found her counting a handful of money—she had 3 l . 4 s . 6 d .—there were two sovereigns, and the rest in silver. CHARLES JAMES LANCEFEILD. I live in Little Chapel-street. The pro secutrix, who lodges with me, came home with the prisoner at half-past ten o'clock that morning—I went down to expostulate with her—I heard some sovereigns on the table and heard them quarrelling about putting them into the box—I went for a policeman, and during that time the prisoner went out for change by the prosecutrix's order—she returned and went down—I then listened on the stairs—I never heard her give her the change—she then came up—I said to her, "Do you know that woman?"—she said, "Yes, for some years"—I said, "Give me your address"—she said, "Mrs. Wood, Oxford-street"—the policeman then came, and we let the prisoner go—we then went down, and saw the prosecutrix—I asked if she had lost any money—she said no, she had got eight sovereigns in a box—we looked, there was only five,—the policeman ran after the prisoner, brought her back, and she was quite drunk and very abusive—there was no silver found in the prosecutrix's room, although the place was well searched directly.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The money found on me was my own; I have kept a stall in Oxford-street many years. GUILTY. Aged 55.— Confined Two Months .
