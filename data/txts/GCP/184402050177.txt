the dripping and wax ends come from Lord Liverpool's house to our house
for sale--I do not know what his lordship does-other noblemen and gentle-
men send their dripping and wax ends-I do not know whether it is the
practice of our house to allow the house-steward or the principal servant 1d.
a pound on every article brought-there is a perquisite allowed- I do not
know whether that 1d. a pound would be put down as a separate item-the
prisoner's department was in the counting-house-he did not usually attend
the shop, only when other people were absent, but he might come into the
shop-I do not know who has got the prisoner's place, or whether it is settled
-my wages were raised about five weeks ago, or a little more--it was since
this affair.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Did you look at all the tubs in which dripping was
usually put? A. Yes, and I examined them all on my return these tickets
are given by the person in charge of the shop at the time, to those who bring
the goods instead of cash, as perhaps we might not have change at the time
--there was no deficiency of cash on the 9th of Sept.- if any of these tickets
had been given after twelve o'clock that day, the money would appear among
the cash-one ticket is given to the person, and one is put into the till, and
in the evening, when we have sufficient change, the money is rolled up in the
paper for the persons when they come for it-I am a yearly servant, and am
paid quarterly--my wages have been raised from the 1st of Jan.- they were
161. a-year, and are now 181.

Joun TARRANT. I am shopman to Messrs. Tucker and Co. I went to
Gravesend on the 9th of Sept., about twelve o'clock-on the evening of the
8th I balanced the cash-book-I returned on the 25th, and on that evening
Murrell made a communication to me, and on the 26th I communicated with
one of the partners--the money in the till on the evening of the 8th corre-
sponded with the cash-book, when I balanced it.
Cross-examined. Q. Do not some of the nobility and gentry keep an
account at your house for dripping and wax ends? A. Yes I do not know
that the servants are allowed id. a pound on dripping or wax ends brought
to our house--if we buy the wax ends of servants, of course we give them the
money--there is something allowed to the servant for himself-it is entered
in the book as for goods bought-I have never seen the prisoner mako
entries in the evening, of goods that he had purchased in the morning - I will
swear that he has never done 80-I go to dinner at one o'clock, and to tea at
five-I sometimes go to the Bank, and sometimes to collect money-I am
perhaps absent two hours--the prisoner has found fault with me for what he
said was negligence-he has reprimanded me for neglect in making entries
in the book - I am Mrs. Tucker's nephew-I remember some time ago
omitting to enter 721bs. of candles sold to Messrs. Ferguson and Co., of Ox-
ford-street, and some sold to Watkins'- I do not remember omitting to enter
goods sold to Hughes, in May Fair.
