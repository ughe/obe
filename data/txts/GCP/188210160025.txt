Cross-examined. The bill of sale and the declaration were on our
printed forms--we require a declaration as a rule—we do not charge for
it, the charge is for the money-I do not remember whether the solicitor
was sent for to take the declaration-Wood said he had some furniture
in the Nassau Road, Brixton—a clerk went to his house, and from there
to where the horses were–I can swear the furniture was not the first
thing he offered because he said he had very little furniture-he had
231. or 257. in gold and 271. by cheque, about half in each, because he
wanted sufficient to pay out an execution-be did not call and tell me
that Mr. Lutwyche had seized the engines-he told me Mr. Lutwyche
had seized his private furniture; that was some time afterwards, but I
knew it-he also said Lutwyche had no right to seize his furniture-he
asked me what he should do I said I would investigate Lutwyche's
security, and see whether he had a right to seize he came to see me
in order that I should not prosecute him-I heard of the seizure about
a week afterwards, and I sent to the Registry Office to search for any
other bill of sale-I got certain information, in consequence of which I
obtained a copy of the bill of sale given in favour of Lutwyche, and I
communicated with him--that was before the prisoner came to me-I
had counsel's opinion upon it-I gave it over to my solicitor, and put
the matter in his hands before the defendant called upon me I told
Wood I had done so--the attorney was inquiring into the validity of
this security-he said he was very sorry the first time I saw him after I
knew of the existence of the bill of sale.
Re-examined. I do not know why I did not take proceedings before
I saw Wood read the document.
