114. JOHN DAVIS (13), Stealing 1 shirt, the property of James
Rankins.
Isaac REEVES. I am in the employment of James Rankins, of 22, Holborn.
hill, a hosier-last Thursday afternoon I was bebind the counter- I turned
round and saw the prisoner in the shop, with another boy-I turned to ring
the bell, and saw him move from the counter and snatch at a flannel shirt,
which he put under the tail of his coat-I jumped over the counter and
seized him, kept him till my master came, and gave him in charge-he
threw the shirt to the further end of the shop-they let the other boy go,
because they did not know whether he belonged to this one.
Prisoner. The shirt was lying on the ground when I went in. Witness.
No, it was hanging up-it is my master's property.
GUILTY.**+-Confined Three Months, and afterwards Five Years in a
Reformatory.

115. WALTER KING (30), "Robbery with two persons unknown on
Elizabeth Condon, and stealing from her person 1 pocket, three pieces of
paper, and 31. 58. in money, her property.
MR. DALEY conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH CONDON. I am the wife of Thomas Condon, of 8, William-
street, East-lane, Walworth-on-18th November, about half-past 8 in the
morning, I was in Devonshire-street, St. George's—I saw the prisoner in a
publio-house drinking with two men-I called for two pots of beer and
three-pennyworth of brandy-I gave a sovereign, and received 198. 1d.
change-I paid four shillings to the cabman, and put the fifteen shillings into
a nankeen pocket, which I put down my bosom-the pocket also contained
three lockets and 21. 108. in gold-the three men saw me do that, and the
prisoner said, “I know where your sister lives; I will take you to her"-
I said, “I do not care what I give to see her, because I have not seen her
since my mother went to Australia"-I had spoken about my sister before
that-when I went out, the three men followed me nearly to the bottom of
the street, and the prisoner held me by the throat, and nearly strangled me
-I hardly know whether he was in front of me or behind me--I halloed
out, “Murder !” “Father!"--the other one tore my bosom open-bere is
my gown all torn, and I am all sore now-they tore my breast and chest,
and took out the pocket and the money, and ran away-I spoke to a police-
man, and went with bim down Devonshire-street-I saw the prisoner at the
station at 2 o'clock in the morning.
Prisoner. Q. Who did you come in with ? A. With two shoemakers-I
had never seen them before they had the two pots of half-and-half, and I had
the brandy—I did not treat you—I left the shoemakers there with the beer,
when you three followed me out the man who robbed me did not ask me
to drink a glass of porter, nor did I tell him to drink it, and then put down
a shilling, and say, “Fill another”-I paid the cabman outside, four shillings
out of the nineteen- a dark man did not say, “ You have treated me, I will
treat you with the best there is in the house"-I had not some chops and
steaks in my arms- I did not give you seven mutton chops---you were
remanded for a week, I did not appear, and you were remanded again-I
did not then write a letter to say that I would have nothing more to do
with the case : my husband must have done it.
