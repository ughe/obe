THIRD COURT.-Thursday, September 20th, 1877.
Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
682. WILLIAM CHAPMAN (18), Stealing a watch, chain, and other
articles of Edward Thomas Hawkins, his master, and HENRY GEE (20),
receiving part of the same ; to which CHAPMAN PLEADED GUILTY.
MR. HARRIS conducted the Prosecution ; and Mr. W. SLEIGH defended Gee.
EDWARD THOMAS HAWKINS. I live at 138, Upper Park Road, Hampstend
-Chapman was in my service nine months up to the time of his being
taken in custody--Gee had been my groom and left about eighteen months
ago-he lodged near our place at the time of the robbery-I left home
about 5th or 6th August, and on the 27th, in consequence of some informa-
tion returned home-before going away I had locked my billiard-room,
and put the key in my bureau and locked it-I found that in the same
oondition as when I left with the exception of the things taken from the
bureau mentioned in the indictment-I found my Lilliard-room had been
opened and four pieces of carpet, a piece of Chinese matting, a leather bag,
a pair of mittens, & silk hat, a pair of trousers, and other articles had been
taken from it-I also found that a silver wateh, a gold pin, a locket, a pair
of sleeve links, & signet ring, two blankets, and various other artioles of
clothing to the value of 20.. had been taken from my place-I went with a
detective to Gee's place --on leading us to his room I observed my carpets
on the floor, and I said “These are my carpets," and then I said "Now I
want two blankets belonging to me "he immediately turned up his bed
and gave them to me--they were between the mattrass and the iron part
of the bedstead they were nearly new--the policeman asked him if there
was anything else, and Gee said “These are all the things I have belong-
ing to Mr. Hawkins"-on further searching the room we found the four
pieces of carpet, the piece of Chinese matting, a leather bag, a pair of
mittens, & silk hut, & shaving brush, &c.--he said he had bought the things
to help Chapman, on the road to Hamburg—the trousers were found on the
prisoner-they were a pair belonging to my groom that he left, and which had
never before been in Chapman's possession; they had been abstracted from
the billiard-room--I had not given any of the things away-I bad last year
4 gold ring, which Gee admitted at the police-court, at Hampstead, he had
lost.
