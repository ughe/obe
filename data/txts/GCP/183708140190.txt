COURT. Q. If you knew nothing of him before, to lead you to su
pose he was a subject of apoplexy, and saw him fighting, and afterward
drop down dead, should not you naturally attribute it to the effect of
fighting? A. Not unless I saw certain appearances-he was a heavy man
and a complete subject for apoplexy-I think it might be produced by
excitement on his own part without blows-it might easily be caused bř
excitement, if the extravasation of blood had taken place in his falline.
ten minutes afterwards would be much about the time the effusion
would take place after the blow was struck-it would not take twenty
minutes for the effect to be developed-it depends on how fast the extra-
vasation took place-sometimes apoplexy comes on slow-excitement
might have caused it equally the same as blows-I found the blood on the
posterior part of the brain, in such a situation as a blow might be given
by a person behind.
Cross-examined. Q. It must be an exceeding hard blow with a fist in
such a place to produce that effect? A. Why the man was an exceeding
likely subject for apoplexy--Vessels so full of blood might easily be rup-
tured by concussion alone-I think it impossible to distinguish whether
his death was caused by excitement, or irritation, or blows--I should be
sorry to decide that point.
Joseph Pugh. I am a carpenter, and live in Church-terrace, Waterloo-
road. I saw the prisoner and deceased in the act of closing at the end of
the fight-I afterwards saw the deceased looking for his hat-while he was
doing so the prisoner struck him unawares at the back of his head-he had
left off fighting then, and was inquiring for his hat-the blow caused him
to go into the road-he being a much stronger man than the prisoner was
able to put the prisoner under him-he staggered from the blow but did
not fall-he caught the prisoner by the collar afterwards--I am sure they
had done fighting when the blow was given the deceased expressed that
he did not wish to fight, but to keep the prisoner there to give him into
custody.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean to state that there was no fighting
after the blow was struck on the back of the head? A. There was no
fighting after-there were only two rounds-there was no other man who
threw the deceased.
Q. Was there no man that you pointed out as having pushed the de
ceased into the kennel when he fell? A. There was a man that was not
the prisoner--that man was not fighting with the deceased--the deceased
just rolled over--the deceased remained five or ten minutes after it was
over before he fell-it was not so much as twenty minutes the policeman
desired him to go away-the policeman asked the landlord to give him into
custody for making a disturbance and insisting on staying there--the
deceased refused to go as he was waiting for his hat-he said he was
waiting for a wonder, and it would be a wonder if he got his hat again
this conversation was about ten minutes after he had received the blow
from the prisoner-he stood against the left-hand door post of the beer
shop.
