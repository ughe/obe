 1038.  ANN COPELAND was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , 1 watch, value 30s.; 2 seals, value 10s.; 1 ring, value 2s.; 1 watch-key, value 4s.; 1 cross, value 4s., and 1 watch-ribbon, value 1d., the goods of John Miller , from his person.
 JOHN MILLER. I am a  seaman. I met the prisoner on the 8th of April, a little after two o'clock in the morning, in Ratcliff-highway. I had been out with a friend, but was quite sober; the prisoner was sitting down in the street; she accosted me, and asked me to go with her- we went to No. 4, Angel-gardens; we went into a room; I asked her if she had any key, that I might lock the door - she produced one which would lock it outside, but not inside; we went to bed with the door latched; I was in bed about three hours - when I awoke she was gone: I went to my clothes, and missed my watch and 10s. in silver; my watch had been in my fob, and the silver in the left pocket of my trousers, which were in the chair at the foot of the bed - I am sure they were safe when I went to bed; I went down to the door, and saw a watchman - I asked him what place it was, and told him what I had lost. I saw the prisoner in custody in the afternoon; my ribbon and ring were found on her person.
   HENRY WADE. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was brought into our shop by the prisoner and another woman, to be pawned, and I stopped it; the prisoner said the person it belonged to was in a public-house; she went for him, and was taken.
   BENJAMIN BLABY. I am an officer, and took the prisoner on the 8th of April; I found this ribbon and ring on her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. He told me he had no money but some copper, and I saw none.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1039.  MARY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , 2 steel plates, value 13s., the goods of John Hurdlestone , from his person.
 JOHN HURDLESTONE. I am a  manufacturer of gold and silver lace. On the 8th of April I had been to take some lace home, and was returning to my house about half-past ten o'clock at night; I was in Whitechapel, the prisoner came up to me, and asked me to go with her; before I could speak to her, I found her hand in my right hand breeches pocket; I pulled it out - she ran away, and I immediately missed two steel plates from the pocket which her hand had been into - I pursued her, she ran into a young man's arms; I went to take her and the man knocked me down. I got up, and still pursued, but could hardly keep sight of her - I ran nearly a mile, till I could run no further; and then I called to a watchman to stop her, which he did. I had not lost sight of her; she went behind a man and woman, and was taken - one of the plates was found in the road, along which she had run.
   HENRY PHILLIPS. I am a watchman. I heard the prosecutor cry Stop thief! and saw the prisoner running in Old Castle-street, towards Wentworth-street; I pursued, and stopped her - the prosecutor came up, and said she was the girl.
   MOSELY GLOVER. I am a watchman. I picked up this steel plate, at the top of Old Castle-street; the other has not been found.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going down Castle-alley, and this man was standing there; he took hold of me, and said, "Now, I have got you;" I said, "I will give myself up to the watchman;" he came up, and said "What is the matter?" I said, "This man says I have robbed him, and I will give myself up to you;" the prosecutor said, "You were with another girl, who took my plates out of my pocket."
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1040.  WILLIAM JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 2 files, value 9d., the goods of Joseph Elliott ; and 32 squares of glass, value 2l. 8s., the goods of John Elliott ; and fixed to a certain building of his; against the Statute.
   CHARLES GOODMAN. I am a watchman, of Islington. I was on duty, on the 7th of April, between three and four o'clock in the morning; I heard the spring of a rattle, and the cry of Stop thief! I saw the prisoner running in Cloudesley-square, and took him; I asked him what was amiss - he said, "There is nothing amiss; let me go." I took him to the watch-house; and these two files were found on him.
   CHARLES LEWIS. I am a watchman. I was on duty on the night in question, in Chapman-street, and saw the prisoner in the road, with this glass in a handkerchief, under his arm; I asked him where he was going - he said he came from Highgate. I told him he must come with me to the watch-house; he threw down the glass, and broke every pane of it - he then bolted. I pursued him, and sprung my rattle.
   JAMES THOMPSON. I am a watchman. I was near Lewis, and saw him stop the prisoner - I was going to him, and saw him throw down the glass, which I took up; this is it - I saw the prisoner searched, and these files found.
 JOSEPH ELLIOTT. My brother's name, is John Elliott; he is building a house for a gentleman at  Islington- and I was at work there; I left these two files in the house, on the Saturday evening - I particularly fastened the house up; the house had been entered, by forcing in the sashes, which were only tacked in - I suppose this glass was in the frames; but it is all broken now - the glass was gone from the frames.
