WILLIAM ELSWOOD. I am a groom and gardener, at Chapwick, near
Blandford, Dorset-on 28th November I saw an advertisement in the
Western Gazette headed “Money I Money! Money !" and applied to Mr.
Edward Upton, of 101, Caledonian Road, for a loan of 401. I received
this letter of 1st December asking for 78. 6d. for inquiry expenses; also
this memorandum, saying that the chief office was at 33, Hoxton Street;
also on 3rd December this letter headed “Cash Ledger 1019, B Book 189,"|
and applying for 31. 48. for interest-I sent my promissory note and 37. 48.
in a registered letter to 33, Hoxton Street-this is a copy of it--this is the
receipt for the registered letter-I also received this letter from that address,
asking for 11. 128. 6d. travelling expenses of witness to document, and
saying “Say if you prefer gold in place of notes -I wrote on 7th
December stating that I should send no more money-I received a reply on
the 9th, stating that he (Mr. Upton) had just returned from the country, and
that I must send the 17. 128. 63. before I could have the loan-I replied on
the 10th, saying that he could have the 11. 128. 6d. if he called for it, and
not otherwise, and I would get a clergyman to witness the document. I
believed the prisoner's statement when I sent the 31. 48.--I never got any
loan.
Cross-examined. I engaged a solicitor, who gave the letters to the
police-the signature does not appear to me to be the same writing as the
body of the letter.
