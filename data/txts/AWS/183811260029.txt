WILLIAM JoNEs. In July last I lived with Mr. Walmsley, a pawnbroker
in the Borough. A man named Chappell brought some kerseymere to the
shop do not know what day it was-I would not take it in pawn, but I
purchased part of it, and had it made into trowsers, which I now produce
know Ellen Fletcher-I do not know whether she was in the shop at
the time, but I saw her before and afterwards-] cannot positively say she
was in company with the person who brought it.
JOHN West. I keep the St. George coffee-house, Blackman-street I
know Samuel Fletcher, sen., and John Fletcher-I have seen them at my
house] have also seen Chappell at my house in company with Tibbs and
Fletcher, sen.- think I have seen them twice, but I am certain I have seen
them once together-it wasat the latter end of June or the beginning of July.
THOMAs WATeRMAN. I keep a public-house in Kent-street, Borough.
Ellen Fletcher came to me at my house with some kerseymere in Auguat,
and asked me to buy some of her, saying her father-in-law had sent it out
of Yorkshire for her to dispose f-she asked me 78. 6d. a yardI sent for
my tailor, and then offered her 78. a yard, which she took-I bought four
yards and a quarter of hershe was alone, but while bargaining for it, she
sent for Midgley to cut it off-during that time I sent for my tailorI
have the trowsers here it was made into-I have worn them.
