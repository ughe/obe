WILLIAM HAWKES. I am a journeyman butcher, and live at No. 17, Raw.
stone Place, Clerkenwell Green. I am fourteen years old—I was in Plackroso's
service when he was taken into custody, and had been for some time-
have been to Hicks's for meat, at his direction, and taken it to Newbiggin's
cart-the first time I went was in March - 1 fetched two flanks.of beef,
and put them into the cart—when I went to Mr. Hicks's for them, I saw
Ramby and Wilson I spoke to both of them, and said that I had come for
some meat for Mr. Pluckrose--I cannot remember which of them gave it
me, but they were both in the shop at the time, and nobody else I did not
see it weighed--some time after that I went again, and got a Scotch foro
quarter of heef-Ramby and Wilson were both in the shop, close together,
and they said, “ There is your Scotch fore qnarter," and chucked it on my
shoulder-I put it into Newbiggin's cart by Pluckrose's direction, and
about a fortnight or three weeks afterwards I fetched a flank of beef from
the shop-they were both there at the time, and one of them said, " That
is your flank of beef”– I picked it up, and put it on my shoulderon May
Ist I was standing outside my master's shop, when Newbiggin came-he
told me to take something into the oart, which was tied up in a cloth —
I put the cloth and contents into Newbiggin's carton occasions when my
master has sent me for meat from other salesmen, he has had it weighed
before I went from it--I have seen him weigh meat, and I have weighed
meat for him at Allen's scale, opposite, but I never saw the meat weighed:
that came from Hicks's--I have seen Ramby, and Wilson, and Pluckrose,
all together, and sometimes two of them together.

-I was not told that if anybody was in the shop but these two men, I was
