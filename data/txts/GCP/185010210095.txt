WILLIAM Upton. Mr. Jenks gave me ten marked shillings I went to
his shop, and purchased goods to the amount of 118. 4d.--I paid the prisoner
those ten shillings and three sixpences of my own, and he gave me 2d.
change-- he put the money into the drawer.
John PHILLIPS ( policeman, T 23). I went to Mr. Jenks's between eleven
and twelve o'clock-I had seen Mr. Jenks leave at ten, and had seen four
persons go to the shop-T went to the till with Mrs. Jenks, and found
158. 3d. there, ten shillings of which were marked—when Mr. Jenks came
back, he told the prisoner he suspected he bad been robbing him, made bim
turn out his pockets, and there was 58. 111d., and six marked shillings
besides-Mr. Jenks told me to take him into custody-the prisoner said he
hoped he would forgive him.
Cross-examined. Q. You are quite accurate about that? 4. Yes-he
made a rambling story about taking the money out of the till, and mixing it
with his own on the counter-I did not exactly remember that at the moment
-I do not ordinarily suppress half a conversation when I am talking about
what a prisoner has said - I have not forgotten a great deal more the
prosecutor did not at that time say he did not know what to do, he had been
going back lately; and I did not hear the prisoner then say he could explain
the reason-he said he was sorry-I do not know that he said he had never
robbed him of one penny-I have known the prisoner some time, and have
drank with him, but not to sit dowo together-I have had no quarrel with
him-I know Upton, but we are not at all acquainted.
Mr. Jenis re-examined. I have two tills--the other is only for copper-I
do not know what was in that one.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Guilty. Aged 19.-Strongly recommended to mercy by the Jury, and also by
the Prosecutor, who engaged to re-employ him.-Confined Fourteen Days.
