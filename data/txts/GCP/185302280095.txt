Court. Q. Did you see your master ever strike him at all? 4. No; my
Daster has been very kind to me, and to all the men-I did not see bim strike

WILLIAM CORTON, Jun. I am brother to the prisoner. On Friday, 4th
Feb., I recollect being down in the workshop with the prisoner and the lad
Salter-the lad was rather saucy to bim, and he gave bim a clout on the head
-it knocked his cap off, and it fell on a piece of iron that a man was working,
and the boy said he would smash his nose for it-the boy cried, and my father
came down in the cellar--the boy did not make any complaint to my father
that I heard-there was no complaint made, but my father ordered my brother
out because the lad was crying.
Q. When your father came down stairs, did he not ask the boy wbat he was
crying for? 1. Not in my hearing - I have not stated that before that I am
aware of-I am not aware that the boy said the prisoner had been knocking
him about--I did not hear it-my father ordered the prisoner out-he did not
go out directly-he was carried up by the policeman and my father went
with them to the station - I did not see the prisoner strike my father any blow
-the blow was struck before the policeman came down.
COURT. Q. Before the policeman came, had your brotber gone up in the
shop and come down again? A. Yes; he went up the first time of his own
record, and the second time he was carried up by my father and the
policeman,
Mr. Lock. Q. Well, your father made a charge against him at the station ?
4. I believe he did-1 did not go in-I returned with my brother from the
station-house-I believe my father went to the Mansion House-on our way
from the station-house the prisoner said to me that if his father did not leave
off, or something to that effect, it would be the worse for him-we got home
