Cross-examined by ME. FILLAN. I was well acquainted with the firm
when Mr. Scott was the principal-Bott was his nephew, I always under
stood from Mr. Soott, and Brown was his clerk-when Mr. Scott died I
understand he left the business to them, the largest portion to Bott, and
Brown was to be a partner with a smaller proportion-Brown generally
transacted our business--they sat together at the same table Bott
usually conducted the conversation with my mother.
Ro-examined. We received our dividends twice a year regularly, but
now we have lost everything--2,0001., which they paid interest on, was
left in their hands to invest.

letter in reply. (From Bott and Brown, dated July 22, enclosing oontract note for

you shall have the receipt"-in August I received this letter. (From
Bott and Brown, enolosing stock receipt for 4,9551. 68. 5d., and stating that,

til the next Tuesday.) In consequence of not receiving the voucher for the

this letter of 6th August in reply. (This stated that Messrs. Price, of whoma

morning, we want to see you particularly, say about 12 o'clock "-I called

and getting no reply, I got uneasy, and one of the clerks spoke to me
I never saw the prisoners afterwards till they were in custody, and have

wards received a circular about liquidation proceedings.
Cross-examined by Mr. BESLEY. I got the circulars of 8th and 27th
August, 1879, and instructed Messrs. Pike and Son, of Old Burlington
Street-I was aware of the liquidation meeting where it was proposed to
pay 58. in the pound, and after three or four months my solicitor handed
me 81. 158.--I know that the payment was ordered by the Judge to
be an instalment according to his means-on 28th November, 1880, I
got this circular produced)-I knew the late Mr. Scott 40 years and
more; he was a highly respectable gentleman, or I should not have
listened to his nephew-I had had transactions with Bott and Brown
before July, 1879, and had no reason to complain.
