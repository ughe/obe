MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution
John WILMOT LINDRIDGE. I am a lime-burner, my works are at Wold-
sham, near Rochester; I had a wbarf at 78, Great Cambridge-street,
Hackney-road. The prisoner came into my service on 25th March-it was
his duty to receive, and pay money at the wharf on my account-In Oct.
last be handed me this acceptance for 35l. odd (produced)-it was accepted
the same as it is now, except the Dotary's paper; it purports to be the
acceptance of a Mr. Stiles- I had a customer of that name at Dalston
he told me that was an acceptance of Mr. Stiles for money that he owed
me for goods-1 put my name to it as drawer, endorsed it, and paid it
into my bankers' (read—“ dated 18th Oct., 1850, for 352. 15.6d.; drawn
by John Wilmot Lindridge, on Stephen Stiles, payable two months after
date")-before it became due the prisoner was taken into custody-I did
not know that there was anything improper or irregular in it until it
became due, it was then returned by my bankers.
Cross-examined by SERJEANT JONES. Q. You reside at Rochester ?
A. Yes; I left the whole management of my business at Hackney to the
prisoner to take orders, execute them, and receive and pay money-I did
not know the state of my account with Mr. Stiles-when I first saw the
bill, which was on 18th Oct., it was not lying open on the desk in my
counting-house, he handed it to me; it was an acceptance for what Mr.
Stiles owed me the prisoner had 12s. a week---We settled our accounts
every week-I do not believe there was a balance in his favour above
once-I had authorised bim to draw this bill before I saw it-he told me
he could get a bill for the money that was owing to me- it is very likely
he knew more of my business than I did; I did not know at the time
I received the bill how much Mr. Stiles Owed me I did not authorise
him to keep a running account, so as to debit me in his account with his
travelling and other expenses-1 told him I would pay for his little
incidental expenses merely--I did not order him to be out on my
business from six in the morning till six at night, it was from six till four
in the afternoon.
