   Cross-examined. Q.  How long has King been with you? A.  Nearly two years—he has a wife, and I believe one daughter.
   Shillum. Q.  Did I hesitate to accompany you? A.  Not at all—I left you outside the station-house, and found you there—I have entrusted you with notes, money, and checks, and always found you correct before—I did not say if you told the truth I would not cause you to be locked up—you were locked up before that—when you were at Union-hall, King sent for me, and wanted me to be merciful—I said there might be some mercy shewn them if they would tell their confederates, and what had been done previous to this.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  You found King and Shillum at Union-hall? A.  I did not see Shillum—King said, "Master, I hope you won't punish me for this little offence; I hope you will forgive me"—I said, "I can't, it must be left to my employers."
   Shillum's Defence . It was from an inducement held out to me by the prosecutor, that I stated what I did; several policemen stated that if I spoke the truth my master would not hurt roe; it was in consequence of that, and King saying that I gave it him, that I stated what I did, but I am inno ccent of the transaction as a child.

 192. MARTHA TAYLOR and GEORGE WILSON were indicted for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. DOANE  and SCRIVEN conducted the Prosecution . THOMAS PICKETT. I am a chandler, and live at Upper Tooting. On the 16th of Nov., about four or five o'clock, Taylor came to my shop and purchased a small quantity of cheese, which came to fourpence half-penny—she gave me in payment a half-a-crown—I gave her the change—she did not speak, nor did I, but she took the two shillings, and left the three halfpence on the counter, and was going away—I called her back—she came and took the three halfpence, and I saw no more of her—I afterwards saw Mr. Barnett—I gave him the same half-crown, as I got from Taylor.
