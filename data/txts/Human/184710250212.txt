   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  You seem to have behaved very honestly in this matter? A.  I do not know—I know the difference between being honest and being a thief—I did not know I was helping a thief—I did not get the new pair of boots—that was not the reason I informed—I have been in the habit of living in the George-yard some months, and there were three sets of harness stolen from there—I did not get share of the harness, I never saw it—I never saw what it fetched—I informed against the prisoners about the tarpaulin—I knew in whose service Bashford was—I thought the tarpaulin I saw come out of the cart was his master's—he held the bag, and I dropped it in—I thought he was going to put it on the van again—it is a customary thing to put them in sacks—I have never done it before—after it got in the yard I began to suspect, and I asked who it belonged to, and he said to the Company—I had no reason to disbelieve it—I did not think it ne cessary to give information then—there was nothing said about my  having a pair of boots of shoes—I supposed the parties who were to get the tarpaulin were to have them—I did not expect to get anything—I went to the railway and assisted to unload—I did not tell any one that one of the tarpaulins was gone—I cannot tell why I did not, I have not through about it—it was about a fortnight or three weeks after that I met with Packman—I had seen him coming into the about the case of the harness—I had nothing to do with that, only it was in the yard where I had been in the habit of working—it was not in my stable—Packman did not accuse me of stealing it—I heard that he mentioned it—it is not a bargain, that if I gave evidence against the prisoner, they will not proceed against me for the harness—the harness was in the same yard as the tarpaulin—I have been in the habit of being in the yard for several months.
