   MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Do you mean to persist in the statement that, in your opinion, the horse you bought was only worth 9 l . or 10 l .? A.  I should not like to give any more for it; but I am not a horse keeper—I do not believe he was worth any more for any poor man to buy—I was examined twice before the Magistrate—it was on the second occasion that I told the Magistrate the prisoner said that I need not pay for him, but could work it out for the firm.
   COURT . Q.  You say that you took the brown horse back to the premises yourself? A.  I returned him by my young man, who is not here—he took the brown horse back, as well as the grey, about 3 o'clock to the stable—I cannot tell whether that is a busy time, but I should say not—not many of the young men are going about then, they are out with their horses.
   COURT . to J. K. MEAD . Q.  Is 3 o'clock a time when you ars all about? A.  We are all there at that time—it is quite possible that I may have seen the milkman coming in from the stable, and not known the horse in harness—we have a constant succession of carts and vans coming in at all times of the day—our men might know our horses if they saw them in the shafts of Mitchell's cart, but it is not likely—the stable man would, but he would not be there at that time of day—besides Messrs. Green and Peek's horses, others come in twenty times a day. CLARA COLEMAN. I am a widow, and a greengrocer, of 23, Bedfordbury—on 23d March I bought a brown horse of the prisoner for 9 l . 10 s .—he said he was a cripple and unfit for the work he was doing.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you receive any information with respect to its value before you purchased it? A.  No—I know nothing about horses, I have lately lost my husband, and my business cannot be carried on without a horse—the prisoner did not tell me that the horse was Peek, Frefen & Co's. but I heard that he was employed there.
   MR. LILLEY . Q.  Did not he say that the horse was not fit for the factory work? A.  Yes—we were close to the Dookhead factory at that time. JOHN TIMOTHY HUGHES( Policeman, M  249). I took the prisoner on 3d April, and told him he was charged with stealing two horses belonging to his employers, Peek and Frean; he said "Mr. Peek said I might shoot the horse;" I said, "But there are two horses;" he said nothing to that—he did not say to me which Mr. Peek it was, but at the police court next day he said Mr. James Peek.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When he said that Mr. Peek told him to shoot the horse, was it not "to shoot or sell the horse"? A  It is quite possible—I will not swear he did not—he did not ask me to send for Mr. Mead, the general manager—he did say that he was so satisfied that he could clear up everything he had done that he wished Mr. Mead to be sent for.
   MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Were you present next day when Mr. Mead was at the police-court? A.  Yes; the prisoner had an opportunity of speaking to him—I saw another person, Mr. Welsh, the night before, to whom the prisoner said that Mr. Peek gave him permission to sell. CLEMENT LOWER. I am a veterinary surgeon—I examined a brown and a grey horse, which I afterwards saw at the police-court—they were worth about 40 l . or 45 l .—they were not fit to be shot—they were both lame, but in pretty good condition, performing their ordinary work—they were fit for heavy work.
