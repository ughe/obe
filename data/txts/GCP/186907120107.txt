692. JOHN CURTIS (27), Stealing two forks and nine knives, of Francis,
Earl of Bandon, his master.
MR. HUNT conducted the Prosecrtion.
EDWARD BUCKNELL (Policeman / 142). About 8.30 on Saturday even-
ing, the 8th July, I went to Mr. Folkard's, e pawnbroker in the Blackfriars
Road, where I saw the prisoner—the assistant showed me this part of a
spoon or fork (produced) it is silver-he said in the presence of the prisoner,
. This man has brought this to sell," and he gave it to me to look at -
asked the prisoner how he came by it-he said, "It was given to me by a
man at the top of the road, he gave me the name of Hoppy - I took him to
the station and charged him.
Henry Norley. I am manager to Mr. Folkard, pawnbroker, in the
Blackfriars Road-on Saturday evening, 3rd July, the prisoner came to the
shop and offered me half a silver fork for sale I sent for a constable on
the 26th June he brought some knife handles to me there is a orest on
the spoon and on the knife handles as well--they were very much defnoed
when they were brought, I had to file them to see if they were silver I
did not see the crest till they were cleaned-when be came first be handed
them to me and asked me to buy them, and said he had got some more at
home-he said they belonged to his grandmother, and had been some time
in the family, and the blades were rather old, so he had brought them
to sell -- he said he lived at Kennington Road- the whole of the knives
would be worth 21.-I gave him into custody.
Prisoner. Q. Did you see me in the shop! A. Yes I did not take
them from you--they were handed to me-you asked if it was silver I
did not know your name - I did not see any crest at first.
WILLIAM GREEN. I am butler to the Earl of Bandon, 111, Eaton Square
I know the prisoner-I engaged him as odd man on the 22nd June-he
left on the 3rd July-I identify these knife handles as being the Earl of
Bandon's property, and under my care- the crest is on them-- it was the
prisoner's duty to clean those knives-from information I received I looked
over the list of plate-I missed two forks and about nine knives--they were
in the pantry, and the prisoner had w008 ta theni.
Prisoner.Q. When you employed me did you find me take anything
unknown to you! 4. I have not missed anything except these-you were
called away at different times to run messages for me-the greengrooer and
butterman pass through the place where the things are cleaned.
Prisoner'i Defence. I was coming home on the Saturday night and saw
these things in a piece of rag; I picked them up, I did not know they were
silver, I never noticed the crest on them ; I am innocent of stealing the
things; I picked them up.
GUILTY.
He was further charged wtth having been before convicted, in June, 1861, in
the name of William Moore, to which he PLEADED GUILTY**_Ten Years
Penal Servitude.
