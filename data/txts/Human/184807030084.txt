Gentlemen, recollect this is not a meeting of a sect or of a party; this is a meeting of the English Chartist body, and of the Irish Confederation; Englishmen and Irishmen have all one evil to complain of; it is one tyranny, one despotism, that is sucking the vitals of the people of the two countries: let not Englishmen forget the [ blank ]; as an Eng lishman, I solemnly declare that I have no interest in any measure, in any treaty, any public negotiation which the aristocratical Government of this country will make; I have no interest in it. Englishmen, I call on you, as you love your freedom, look at that brave people; they put a man on the throne, having confidence"—I believe he was referring to France—I see that something is omitted by my book—I could not hear—" the fellow violated the confidence that was reposed in him, and that people acted as a noble example, that all people similarly situated ought to follow—to drive a tyran nical Sovereign, wherever or whatever he is, from the throne.
