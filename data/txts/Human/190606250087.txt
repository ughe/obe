Major Whyte was dying from exhaustion as the result of blood-poisoning. The object of the death certificate is to notify to the Registrar the cause of death. It is not usual for a medical man to become a policeman; but it is usual for a medical man, if the case is not treated properly, to refuse his certificate and notify the Coroner if there is any possibility of the certificate being given by anyone besides himself. If the certificate is not given by the last medical man in attendance that would excite the suspicion of the Registrar. In this case there was a public duty, and any man with a sense of public duty would have done as I did. When I was called in and saw Major Whyte I saw he was dying, and that there was nothing to be done except having the parts as clean as possible. I said in the deposition, "I agree with Sir Victor Horsley that Major Whyte was a doomed man for eight, perhaps 10 or 12, weeks before his death." But I agreed with Sir Victor's evidence, and he said, "According to his power of resistance."
