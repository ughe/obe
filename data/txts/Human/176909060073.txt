Then he said he would bet two guineas out of that quantity of money to one guinea, that we did not. Upon that the prisoner said he had just been paying away a great deal of money, and he had not hardly any about him. The farmer said to me, How much have you? Then the young gentleman went out of the room again, and left them to talk to me. After they had talked to me a great deal, the farmer asked me how much money I could make, if I went home. I said, I must dress my master, and could not come out again soon. He asked me if I could not get into the house, without my master seeing of me. I said, No. (As he said if I could raise fourteen or fifteen guineas, he would make up the rest, and it would be a great service to me if we won to keep me in the country, as this gentleman has so much money. The young gentleman went in and out seven or eight times.) They made me drink another glass of brandy, and with a great deal of persuasion I went home, and brought fifteen guineas and a great deal of silver, five crown pieces among it. When I came in, the young gentleman goes out of the room again. Then they would go to the King's Arms tavern, in Cornhill. When I came out, they were at the corner of Change-Alley. I followed them in. There was a bottle of port, and four or five glasses on the table. They asked for a pen and ink to be brought into the room. They took a glass of wine each, and made me drink one. Said the farmer, I'll convince you that you are wrong (to the young gentleman) to guess three times out of four what a halfpenny shall be, heads or tails, to hide it myself under my own hat. Upon that the young gentleman and the farmer played for a shilling. The farmer put the halfpenny under himself. The young gentleman went to the farther end of the room and guessed. The farmer won seven or eight times. Upon that they played for half a guinea a time; he won again. Then the young gentleman quitted the room. The prisoner and farmer fell to persuading me to play. I told them I never gamed so high as a six-pence in my life, at cards or any thing else. They kept trying to persuade me. The prisoner said, If you don't get his money, somebody else will. The whores will, for he will spend it. Perhaps all this money will not last him above a month. Then he will go to sea again; and you may as well have it as the whores. I still stood in it I would not play. The farmer and the young gentleman played again. The farmer won again. Then they made me drink again. The young gentleman went out of the room again. Then with over and above persuasion, the farmer saying he had won so often, I might be convinced I should win, and the farmer would go partners with me. Then I consented to play, but would go no more than half a guinea. They wanted me to go ten or twelve guineas at once, the farmer was to go half; he stood at my right hand; we won the first half guinea, but the farmer would not let me have mine that I won. We went half a guinea each against the young gentleman's guinea. He said he would pay me before I went. Said the prisoner, You have no room to doubt the gentleman. You may as well go farther. Said the prisoner, How much can you go farther? Sir, You may as well go as much more. You see this man has won so much. You may as well go for ten or twenty guineas at once, as well as such a trifle. Then the young gentleman was out of the room. Upon that I shewed what I had got. With a great deal of persuasion I went for ten guineas at once. I took out ten guineas, and counted them in my hand, and the farmer took them out of my hand. I made a resistance. Said the prisoner, You can't suppose you will be cheated of your money; you are safe enough; you are in the room. As he was my partner, I could not suspect but he was an honest man. He threw my ten guineas into a hat upon the table, and the farmer threw ten guineas into the hat out of his pocket, but he would not let me look at them, and the young gentleman threw his great green purse into the hat, saying, If you win, you will be sure of your money; for there is five hundred guineas. Said the prisoner to me, You may be sure, for there is plenty to pay you. I took a halfpenny, and put it under my hat. When I put the hat down, the farmer always made that a rule to lift the hat up and see what the halfpenny was, and call to the young gentleman. He would not let me call once. His method was, What will you have?
