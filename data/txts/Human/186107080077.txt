HENRY HATES. I live with my father at 14, Tonbridge-street—on Monday 17th, about a quarter to 8 in the evening, I was at the corner of Argyle-place—I saw the prisoner Batchelor in the street; he had some money in his hand and a purse, and he gave some money to a lame man.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The lame man was not Adams? A.  No. GEORGE BALL( re-examined ). I saw the prosecutrix on the night of the 17th—I live at the station, and was there when she was brought in—she was in a filthy state; she smelt as if she had been made water over, and she was one mass of mess from top to bottom, just as if she had been drugged—she was in a regular silly state; there was not the slightest sense about her then, nor was there next morning; indeed, she did not recover her senses for two days afterwards—there is no doubt she had been dreadfully drugged—she is rather short—she has not exactly got her right senses about her.
   The prisoner Adams put in a written defence, stating that he met the prosecutrix at a public-house and remained in her company for some time, but left her at the Wellington, and knew nothing of the robbery.
   ADAMS  — GUILTY.
   BATCHELOR  — NOT GUILTY.
