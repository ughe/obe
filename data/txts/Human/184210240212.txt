   COURT Q.  Did you go there on a Sunday, before you ceased to board at Skeet's? A.  Yes—I used to call at the door in general—Mrs. Skeet was not on shore till the evening—I used to come ashore with her and walk up to the house with her. ELIZABETH SKEET re-examined.  Elgar took his meals at my house—the property was in my bed-room—he was not there, to my knowledge, after the Sunday before the prisoner left, but I was with my husband on the Sunday on board a ship—I never noticed any intimacy between the prisoner and Elgar—Hodges is here—no other seaman but Elgar was in the house—I have traced no more of the property—I do not know what has become of the basket I lent her—she had her own basket, my basket, and a bundle, when she went out on Tuesday—she said she should return to tea, and asked me to keep the tea-kettle by the fire until seven o'clock, when she should come—Hodges was there on Monday and Tuesday—the prisoner always paid for the lodging.
   MR. DOANE Q.  Had not she given you reason to believe that she was going to return to her mother in the country? A.  Yes—as she said Hodges was going into business again—she said she was going on the Wednesday, but she went on Tuesday—she said she expected a post-office order from her mother—she said if the order was there, waiting for her, she should return that night, about seven o'clock, and if not, I was to wait till next morning—Hodges said he was to meet her at Westminster-bridge at ten, if she did not return at seven—I heard nothing about her having to go to Mr. Smith's, in Whitecross-street, or that her fare was to be paid at the office at Hereford—she said she was going to stop there four or five days—Elgar owes me 2 s . 7 d . for trifling things—he told me that he had been to my house on a Sunday, but I did not see him—he bad his victuals with us on Sundays on board a ship—he sometimes went on shore, and I have heard that he has called at our house—the prisoner said so—I asked how long he stopped—she said "Oh, he did not stay above a quarter of an hour, he was rather in liquor."
   COURT . Q.  Where did you lose your two sheets from? A.  From the linen-chest in the room I occupied—all the things I lost were from my room—Hodges was a policeman of the R division, and acted as such until the last day, (Tuesday)—he was taken up on this charge, and let out on bail, I believe—he gave up his police situation on that Tuesday, and gave up his clothes the very day she left—Hodges was at home and in bed at times, when the prisoner carried out bundles, as he was on night duty—my mother's things were in the same box—I had told the prisoner I did not go to that box, as I could not bear to look at the things, having recently lost my mother—Elgar did not know where I kept them—he was never up stairs to my knowledge.
