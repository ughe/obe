THOMAS MILLS. I keep the Glass House, Brewer Street, Golden
Square; I have known Errington about two years-about the end of last
year or the beginning of this he introduced me to Dr. Mark and Captain
Traher as directors of the Fish Dinner Company, and the three asked
me to introduce them to my bankers, the London and South-Western Bank,
Regent Street branch-he brought a man named Fuller to me-I cashed
this cheque for 21. on the Grosvenor Bank at Mr. Knowles's request.
(Payable to B. Fuller, and signed "Frederick Pope.")—it was returned dis-
honoured—I summoned Fuller at the County Õourt about six weeks ago,
and he paid the amount-I think Dr. Mark and Captain Traher chose
the wines for the Fish Dinner Company-Errington introduced them-
they required a particular brand of champagne, I do not remember the
name of it-I had not got it and I ordered it at 9, Water Lane, and
supplied it to the amount of 177. 28.-I have never been paid- I got this
bill for 18l. 158. (Dravon by Errington and accepted by Traher.)
I never got anything for it-I received this prospectus (produced)
from the customer who supplied the wine-I received a 301. bill
from Errington-he said that Captain Traher and Dr. Mark
were very hard up, and I gate Errington ll. on it-I also
received this 121. bill from Captain Traher; I gave him 127. for it, and
have never been paid-I received this cheque for 51. from Errington on
the Birkbeck Bank, and gave him 51. for it, less 78. for two bottles of
whisky which I sent for-I never paid it in, as a note came asking me
to hold it over for a couple of days—I did not see Errington till two
months afterwards, when I told him I had been very badly treated, and
I wanted my money-he said he would see that all the things were
paid-I received this cheque for 21. 108. from Errington or one of his
clerks. (Dated January 5th, 1884; drawn to self, G. Welis.)-I parted with
297. 58. altogether, in cheques, bills, and goods.
Cross-examined by Pope. I had no transactions with you direct-Knowles
was my partner-I have never had any representations from you with
regard to the other transactions,
Cross-examined by MR. DILL. The 301. bill for Traher and Mark was &
purely private transaction between ourselves-Errington had been doing
a little business with me, such as buying wines-I don't know whether
he went to Brighton on my account-I know Traher and Mark went,
and I paid for it.
