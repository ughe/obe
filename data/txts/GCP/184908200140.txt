1651. THOMAS LANGSTON was again indicted for stealing Utlbs.
weight of nails, value 1s.; and 1 piece of paper, 4d.; the goods of Arthur
Williams, his masters. MR. CLARKSON conductedt he Prosecution.
THOMAS FITZPATRICK KNIBB. I am foreman to Mr. Arthur Williams.
On 16th July, the prisoner was in his service-be applied to me that day to
go into the cellar for a hammer-handle-I gave him permission to go-ho
was gone too long, and I went down and saw him putting a paper of nails
into his pocket-he had not taken any hammer-handle-he said, "I have
been helping myself to a few shoe-nails, Mr. Knibb"-he went up-stairs-I
asked him if he had got his bainmer-handle-he said, no; he did not want
it-he then went into the back shop-I went after him, and said, "Where are
those nails you have got ?"-he said, “You do not want to see them"-I
said, "I do, and I must"-he said, "I have not got them"- said, "What
have you done with them?"-he said he had put them awayI said,
"Where?”-be said he had sent them home-I said, "Go and fetch them"-
he went and fetched them, and when he came back he was dreadfully agitated,
and cried, and begged I would not say anything about it to Mr. Williams on
account of his wife and family-I said, "I must"-he caught my arm, and
begged I would not-he went to work, and I went into the shop and put the
nails away-I did not tell Mr. Williams till about a week after the other
circumstance.
Cross-examined by Mr. REPTON. Q. Did you make any remark to the
prisoner when you saw him putting the nails in his pocket? A. No; I came
up from the cellar-he had not the hammer-handle in his possession when be
came up; I swear that-I swear the handle was not fitted in my presence-I
did not drink gin with him after this transaction-I did not ask him to stand
something—there was no one in the shop but me and bim-I have been three
months in Mr. William's employ- this is the paper of nails; it has my
writing on it-I tied the nails up afterwards.
