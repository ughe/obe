I am a carman and a contractor—my brother and myself were contractors for that sewer that was building in Victoria-road, Kensington—I know Marson—he had nothing to do with building the sewer—we took the ground of Mr. Vallotton. THEODGRE JAMES VALLOTTON. I live at 2, Hyde-park—my father is the ground-landlord of the ground on which this sewer was built in Victoria-road—I do not know Day—he has nothing to do with that land.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Does not Mr. Barry claim that land? A.  I do not know—he claims all Kensington—the land on which the palace is built he claims. CHARLES BURGESS GOFF( police-sergeant, L  8.) I took the prisoner in White Hart-court, Drury-lane—I passed him first, and from the description I had I believed him to be the man—I turned and said, "Good morning, Mr. Day"—he said, "Good morning"—I said I wanted him for being concerned with Marson in obtaining pipes from Mr. Doulton—he said, "You must have been planted on me"—he wanted to go to his solicitor—I took him to the station, and found on him this letter—( produced. )

 253. JOHN BRIAN , stealing 7lbs. weight of lead, value 15 d  . the goods of Thomas Henry Maudsley and others, his masters; and WILLIAM TAYLOR , feloniously receiving the same; to which
   BRIAN  pleaded GUILTY . JAMES SHERIFF. I am foreman to Thomas Henry Maudsley and others, engineers—Brian was in their employ. In consequence of suspicion I watched him on Thursday, at dinner time, to Taylor's, a marine-store dealer's shop in the Upper Marsh, Lambeth—I saw him take something from his person, which was put into a scale, weighed, and then thrown under the counter by Mrs. Taylor—next day, at twelve o'clock, I watched him there again, and between twelve and half-past five, I got a constable to go with me—he went into the shop, and took possession of something—I immediately followed, and when I got in, Taylor said to the constable, "Do you call that lead?"—he said, "I do"—Taylor said, "It is zinc"—he was asked if he had bought any other articles of the man before—he said, no; he did not know that he had ever seen him in his shop before—Brian had taken the lead from his person—the policeman asked him where he got it—he made no reply—I believe it to be Mr. Thomas Henry Maudsley's and Joseph Field's. JAMES CUDDY( policeman. ) On Thursday evening, 28th Nov., I received information from Sheriff, watched Brian into Taylor's shop, and saw him take a piece of lead from under his jacket—Taylor's wife was inside the counter, and put the lead into the scale—he was passing in and out, and might not have seen it—he only said afterwards, "You don't call that lead; it is zinc." NOT GUILTY .
