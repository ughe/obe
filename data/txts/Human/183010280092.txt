 2057.  MARY BRYANT and CATHERINE WHITE were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of October , 3 sovereigns, 1 half-sovereign, and 5 shillings, the monies of William Sawyer , from his person; and that the said Mary Bryant had before been convicted of felony.
   WILLIAM SAWYER. I am a groom and gardener, and at the time in question lived at Mr. Boardman's, Clarendon-place. On the 1st of October, between eleven and twelve o'clock at night, I was at the White Lion, at Paddington, with a friend; the prisoners came to us - they asked us to let them drink, and we gave them part of our ale; we went out, and they followed us, and with a deal of persuasion I went home with them, to No. 27, Nightingale-lane, at a green-grocer's; we were all four in the room - I had three sovereigns and a half, and 5s. 6d. in my purse, in my right-hand breeches pocket - I staid there about half an hour; I was with Bryant - I then missed my money; I am certain it was safe when I entered the room, for I took the gold out in the room, wrapped it in paper, and put it into my fob; as I was in the bed I heard money jingling, and that was their putting halfpence into my purse, instead of my money - I got up, and accused them of robbing me; my breeches were at the head of the bed, the gold in the fob, and the silver in the purse - Bryant was at the foot of the bed; I accused her of stealing it - White was in the middle of the room; I jumped out of bed, and Bryant ran down stairs - she was not completely dressed; she left her gown in the room - White stood talking to three men on the stairs; Bryant returned - I turned up the bed and every thing, but could not find the money; I had not been to sleep - as I followed Bryant down White blew the candle out; I insisted on having a light - White sent Bryant for the steel, and a light was got; I gave them both in charge - one of the men said, "Who *** have you got there?" Bryant said, "Don't make such a noise;" we had all four been in bed - no money was found.
   THOMAS KEY. I am a groom at Hamilton-mews. I was with the prosecutor - we had three pints of ale; the prisoners came out with us - we went home with the prisoners; I heard the money chink - I had seen the prosecutor put the sovereigns into his fob - the money has not been found.
 Bryant. While the prosecutor was gone for the Police, this man told me to run away. Witness. No, she said she should run away - I said, "It will be better for you - they are sure to find you;" she went out of the room.
   EDWARD BALL. I am a Policeman. I was sent for, and found the door fast - they would not open it; we forced it, searched the prisoners, and found only 4s. 6d.; the prosecutor was very doubtful about the prisoners.
     NOT GUILTY.

 2059.  EDWIN NORRIE was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 15 shillings, the monies of John Clements , his master.
 JOHN CLEMENTS. I am a  broker, and live in Little Pulteney-street- the prisoner was my errand-boy. On the 6th of September, about eight o'clock in the morning, I came down with 3l. worth of silver in my hand - I put it into a small box in the parlour, which was not locked, and in the evening I missed 15s.: the prisoner should only have been in that room at his meals, but he could have access to it - I said nothing to him on that day, but on the Thursday I charged him with it; he said he had taken it, and spent it at the theatre, with some boys - I found an opera-glass on him, which he said he had bought with part of the money.
   THOMAS JOSEPH WEST. I took the prisoner - he told me he took 15s., to go to the theatre with another boy.
 Prisoner's Defence. My friends are respectable, and my master will bear witness to my general good character - it is my first offence, and I beg for mercy.
 MR. CLEMENTS. I believe his friends are respectable - for himself I can say nothing.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
