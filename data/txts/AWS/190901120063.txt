MARTIN HOLLAND, 52, Red Lion Street, horn polisher and mounter.
I have been engaged in this business for 20 years. The pair of
mounted horns produced to me are Ox horns and worth about 16s. in
their present state. I should say that they come from South America.
Some of the horns come over on the animals and some are imported
by shiploads for sale. In a raw state I should think they are worth
about a shilling a pair. The most I should give for either of the
three sets is 15s. 6d. in their present condition. If retailing them T
should want a bit more, 25s. or 30s. a pair. If they were real buffalo
horns they would be worth at least three times the money,
Cross-examined by Mr. Green. I am a journeyman. Looking at the
largest pair I should say they were the horns of Texas steer; the
seeond pair may be South American or from the Cape; the third
pair, Cape or Ameriean. Looking at the horn itself it is difficult to
say what part of the world it comes from unless I saw the marks on
the parcel. Without marks no one could tell even by examination.
I have never bought any in the raw state, but they can be bought at
the glue boilers in Bermondsey, wholesale. I should be surprised to
hear that the wholesale price of the large horns is 5s. a pair In the
raw state they have to be seraped and filed, pumioed, papered, polished
and afterwards mounted. To finish a pair would take me three days.
With a lathe I could do the finishing in two days 176. 6d.
would be a fair rate of pay for the three days work. In
offering 15s. I should not be losing much, as that would be under the
setual cost of production, but a good deal would depend upon the olass
of work. The material resembling skin put between the horns when
mounting is called Iceland frieze, or caracul. Imitation caraoul such
as has been used here would cost 6d. or 9d. a yard. It would take the
yard to mount the three pairs, I have never bought any of the
material. I should think 3s. or 4s. per yard would be an exeessive
price for it. I should describe the horns as of medium eolour. Some
people prefer dark horns to light.
To Mr. O'Connor. There is a great differenee between buflalo horn
and cow horn.
