   Q.  Did Vincent tell you nothing about the butter more than to go and take it away by virtue of that note? A.  No, nobody heard him give me the order that I know of—there was no other carman by, I know—I went into the warehouse out of Vincent's sight, and there got the butter, which I brought out, and put  in the cart—he was about ten or twelve yards from me in the street when the butter was being delivered—Mr. Barber's people put it into the cart, I did not—I did not point out Vincent to them as having any thing to do with it—they did not ask any thing about it—I gave them the note, and they gave me the butter—they saw nothing of Vincent that I know of—when I drove off he walked on the off side of the cart on to Tower-hill—he then got on the pavement and walked on—I did not see him speak to any body there—I had often drank with him—It was about a mile from the warehouse that we had been when he said, "Make it a pint"—there were plenty of people there, but nobody that I knew—I did not ask where the butter was going to—Vincent did not tell me—I stated that it was a four-horse van.
   Q.  How long after the transaction did you hear of it again? A.  About an hour after—my master said Mr. Barber wanted to see me—I went to him and told him I took it down Whitechapel-road, and put it into a van with four horses—I did not then tell him who employed me—I did after wards—I did not then know why he was inquiring about it, or that it was stolen not till he said it was a forged order—he said so then—I did not then tell him Vincent had employed me—they took me up, and put me in the Compter—I was taken before the Lord Mayor two or three days after—I was in custody during that time, but nobody came to me that I could mention about Vincent to—I did not tell the officer who took me to the Mansion-house, of Vincent, nor the Lord Mayor, because the Lord Mayor asked me no questions—I was remanded—I was at the Compter from Friday till Monday, and then went to Lambeth-street—I then told about Vincent.
   Q.  Had you heard in the meantime that any body else had been taken up? A.  Yes—I was committed as a witness—I was never in prison before on any charge of felony, only for a row  —I was in the service of Mr. Thomas Bailey, as carman—I left'on account of a row  with the fore man, and he accused me of stealing some silk—Mr. Bailey did not dis charge me, it was the foreman—I was not discharged from any other ser vice in the same manner—Mr. Hill pays me weekly, unless I have money in the course of the week—I have frequently kept money back in the course of the week, which is taken out of my wages on Saturday.
   Q.  Were your wages under stoppage at any time? A.
