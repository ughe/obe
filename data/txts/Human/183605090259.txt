   COURT . Q.  You found three hams on his legs, and he said he meant only to steal one? A.  He did, and he showed me the cask from whence he took them—the hams were wanted to go away, and four were returned which are not here. GEORGE TAYLOR. I was called—I had just done swinging the vessels—the witness pointed out the man lying on his belly, with three hams on his legs—Hutchings called him out, and asked what he was going to do—he said he intended to take one ham, if he had not come so quick upon him—Hutchings asked him where he got the hams from—he said they came out of the hogshead—Hutchings said, "Show me the hogshead they came out of"—he got in between the hogsheads, and showed him—he was quite sober, but tried to appear drunk.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How could a man, lying on his belly, put three hams on his legs, when he had not room to move himself? A.  He could put them over his shoulder, on his legs—his head might be a yard and a half from the hogshead which was lying on its bilge—the hams were all shot out—some of them were lying close to his hand—he could not get out say other way than by putting them on his legs—he had not room to turn himself—he was obliged to back himself out astern. WILLIAM LEGG. I am warehouseman to Mr. Humphery. I saw these hogshead safe on Saturday night, between nine and ten o'clock.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Whose property were these while they were an Mr. Humphery's wharf? A.  They belonged to him—I call him Alderson John Humphery.
   Prisoner . I was drunk between twelve and one o'clock, and do not know how I came there.
