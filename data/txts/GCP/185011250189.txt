Thomas GARFORTH ( policeman, L 151). From information on Friday,
8th Nov., I went to Mr. Simpson's shop in Lower-marsh, Lambeth—I
found there three dozen of these brass taps-I afterwards went to the shop
of Henry Fisk, a general outfitter for tobacconist's shops in the New-cut,
where I found two dozen and eleven of these taps- I found one tap at tlie
bouse of Mr. Bliss, a gas-fitter-on Saturday, 9th Nov., I saw the pri-
soner go into a public-house in the New-cut-I went in after him, and
said, "You must consider yourself in my custody for having had a quan-
tity of brass taps in your possession"-he said, “I have been to Doulton
and Watts this morning"-- he did not say for what purpose I had not
mentioned the name of Doulton and Watts to him-I asked him how he
came by them--he said he bought them at a sale in Leicester-square-
asked him if he knew the man be bought them of-he said he was a dark
Jew-looking man, with a thin nose - I asked him if he had a catalogue of
the sale-he said, "No," the sale had commenced about an hour before
he got therehe afterwards said he bought them at Jones's sale-room on
Monday the 4th, in the evening—there is an auctioneer of that name-
there are two auction-rooms, but Mr. Jones's is not in Leicester-square,
it is two doors down a passage-the prisoner described the place where he
bought them-be said, "Jones's is at the further end of Leicester-square,"
which would be the further end from the police-court-I went to
another auction-room, Mr. Thomas's-1 got a catalogue from Mr. Thomas.
Court. Q. There is another auction-room in Leicester-square? A. Yes,
Mr. Thomas's, which is three doors from Green-street; that is in the
square-I was informed by the foreman that the prisoner had been to
Messrs. Doulton's.
HENRY ADAMS Simpson. I am a marine-store dealer, of the New-
cut, Lambeth. On Monday, 4th Nov., I saw the prisoner in my house
about twelve o'clock in the day I have known him fifteen years, De
attends sale-rooms-I never knew anything wrong of him-he buys lots,
and sells them in the trade to ironmongers, marine-store dealers, and furni-
ture dealers-he called on me that Monday about twelve o'clock, and said
he was going to Jones's sale-room for twelve dozen of taps to be sold, what
would I give him for them-I know Jones's sale-rooms; they are called in
Leicester-square; they are within two doors of it-I do not know whether
they have any window which looks into Leicester-square- said I could
not tell what to give for them unless I saw them on the following Thurs-
day he brought me a sample tap-I told him I would take three dozen at
78. a dozen-be came back in about two hours, and said he had sold three
dozen to Fisk, and six dozen to another place, and I should have the
three dozen, and I had them he came back about five o'clock, and
bronght the three dozen with him."
Cross-examined. Q. You have known him fifteen years! . Yes, he
has been dealing at my shop-I always thought him a respectable young
man-it is a common thing for parties to buy things at sales, and sell
them at a profit; that is the way he has got his living-he was well
known in the trade-all the brokers in the Cut know him-I never bought
taps of him before.
