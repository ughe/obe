   Cross-examined. Q.  Did your father go by the door? A.  Yes; he did not come in—he went straight on the other side of the way, and these two men were on first—my father had nothing in his hand then; no quart pot and no poker—I threw a bit  of china when I saw him on my father—they both threw my father down, and Walters held him down with his knees.
   Cook. Q.  Was not I by myself? Q.  No; Walters was with you; my brother and my father were not on top of you. RICHARD DENNIS. I am the prosecutor's son—on 20th March, he and my mother were drinking in the public-house—I went into the tap-room and asked Walters, who was there, to drink, which he did—I went to my father and mother, and went back—the prisoners came in, and I asked Walters whether he would come into the parlour, and sit with me—soon afterwards Cook insulted my sisters, and when I went back he said, "Are they your sisters?"—I said, "Yes, what is the matter?"—he said that he had lost his watch—Miss Day ordered him out of the parlour, and they went out—my father went out, and I found him lying down, and picked him up—when we got to Rutland-place, I saw my father knocked down again by Walters; he was cut, and bled very much—I took him home; Cook did nothing to my father; he was three or four yards away from him; the prisoners ran away.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not you see Walters trying to get Coot out of the public-house several times? A.  Yes, he took him round the waist; I did not see that Walters was bleeding, but there was a bason of blood and water when I went into their house to give them in charge—they both live in one house.
   Cook. Q.  Did I ask you to drink? A.  You asked Walters; you were excited with drink. EDWARD MARSH( Policeman, R  311). I took the prisoners; I found this knife ( produced ), on Cook—it has stains of blood on it; Richard Dennis said that Cook had stabbed his father; Cook said, "Serve the old b——right"—Walters said, "You do not mean to put this on me, do you."
   Cook.  The stain on my knife was from my handkerchief, which I had had on my head; I carry them together. DUNCAN LEWIS. I am a medical practitioner; I was called in, and found Dennis partially bent double, as if from exhaustion, and literally deluged with external blood—the temporal artery was cut through; it would have cost him his life if it had not been cut through, as that caused it to contract—I considered him in danger; he was pulseless; I found several scratches on his head, and an incised wound on the summit; also, a deep incised wound measuring two inches from the margin of the ear to the angle of the jaw, so clear and deep that if I had not required to cut it, to take up the temporal artery, I could not have made a clearer incision—there appeared to be a degree of malignancy in the cut, for it had actually cut through a portion of the cartroied gland; such a knife as this would produce the wound.
