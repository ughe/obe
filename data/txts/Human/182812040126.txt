 248.  DANIEL GREEN WHALEY was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of November , - of a yard of kerseymere cloth, value 5s., the goods of Isaac Izard , his master.
 ISAAC IZARD. I am a  tailor, and live in Gloucester-street, Queen-square. The prisoner was my apprentice, and had been with me about fifteen months; I sent for a constable, on the 3d of November, and asked the prisoner in his presence, to deliver up the key of his box - he, with some reluctance gave it up, when I had threatened to break it open; the constable and I drew it from under the work board, and in it were found this piece of cloth, which I particularly identify as mine.
   JOHN MARDON. This is the cloth; I took it out of the box - as we were going to the watch-house, I said it was a great pity to see such a young man taken for robbing his master; he said he was sorry for what was passed, but he could not recall it, and he was ruined.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Three Months.
 249.  JOHN SMITH and SARAH SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of December , 1 shawl, value 8s., and 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Margaret Mahoney.
   MARGARET MAHONEY. I met the prisoner, Sarah Smith in the City-road, between seven and eight o'clock, on the 4th of December; I knew her before, and went to her room - she asked me to stop to supper; I sat on the bed and she told me to lie my head down if I were sleepy - I I put off my shawl and bonnet and fell asleep; I awoke about three o'clock in the morning, and missed my shawl - I took up my bonnet and asked her for my shawl; she said I did not bring any there; I did not miss my handkerchief from my neck till I got to the watch-house - I did not have any supper; the prisoners were both in the room when I awoke - I was sober when I first met with them but we had some gin; I paid 4 1/2d., and then the man said he would have some more - he had some, and then a pint of porter; that was in a public-house - I am quite sure I had my handkerchief and shawl when I went from the gin-shop to their room.
 Prisoner  JOHN SMITH. When I came home, she was waiting at the door, and asked me for the key; I gave it to her, and went for a pot of porter, and when I came back, she was in a state of intoxication on the bed - I was not away above ten minutes.
 Prisoner  SARAH SMITH. Q. Did you not say to me,"Send your children home, and let your * * * go to * *" and say there were to be some races at the Eagle? A. No; I never said any such thing.
   WILLIAM HALL. I have the shawl and handkerchief which I found in a dust-hole in the cellar of the house where the prisoners live, they were covered with a saucepan; there are other lodgers in the house; this was on Saturday morning last - I had taken the prisoners on Friday morning; the watchman gave them to me - the prisoners said the prosecutrix did not bring the articles there, and that she was brought there quite drunk by two girls.
   WILLIAM FINCH. I am a watchman. I took the prisoners at three o'clock that Friday morning: the prosecutrix cried out Watch! from the one pair of stairs window - she said she had been robbed of her shawl and handkerchief; I went and took the prisoners, but could find nothing.
     NOT GUILTY.
 250.  JOHN ROLF and DANIEL MULLIS were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of December , 40 yards of canvas cloth, value 15s., the goods of William Bradley.
 WILLIAM BRADLEY. I am a  green-grocer, and live in Old-street. Mullis has been employed by me occasionally; I saw him last Thursday afternoon near my premises with Rolf, and another person who has been acquitted - they were there three of four hours; I missed the canvas cloth from my back-premises, and suspected Mullis; I went to the officer, and took him to a house in Kingsland-road, kept by Myers - Mullis was with us, and Lewis Myers said in his presence that he bought it of two boys, but Mullis was not one; I had told Mullis if he would tell me where the property was, I would not punish him, and then he told us that he and the others had taken it, and it was at Myers'.
   JOHN CLARKE. I am a headborough of St. Luke's. I was present when Mullis was taken; as we were going to the watch-house Mr. Bradley questioned him; he said that he and two more had committed the robbery, and had sold the cloth to Mr. Myers, in Kingsland-road, and he stood outside while the other two went in.
   JOHN KNIGHT. I took up Rolf, who said he knew nothing about it.
   LEWIS MYERS. I bought the cloth on Thursday evening of Rolf, and another young man; I cannot say whether it was Mullis: I gave 2s. 8d. for it.
 ROLF'S Defence. I was coming down the road, and we met two men; one of them had the cloth on his shoulder: he gave us 6d. to take it in there.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoners received a good character, and were recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor.
 ROLF -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 MULLIS -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined One Month.
