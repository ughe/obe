 846.  ANN MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of February , a bonnet, value 15 s.; a veil, value 15 s.; a pair of stockings value 3 d.; a pillow case, value 3 d., and a napkin, value 1 s., the goods of Robert Searl.
   RHODA SEARL. I am the wife of Robert Searl, and live in Wentworth-street, Spitalfields. About the 10th of February, I missed these things from a box in my room. On the night of the 17th of March, the prisoner came to my house and brought me an affidavit from a pawnbroker, and said she had found my bonnet at Mr. Fleming's, a pawnbroker, in Whitechapel, but refused telling me who had pawned it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CRESSWELL. Q. Do you keep a lodging house? A. Yes, the prisoner lodged there when I lost my property - I mentioned it to her the same night when I missed it - I had offered a reward of 5 s. for the discovery of the things; she said she knew who had pawned them, but would not tell me who it was.
   JOHN VAUGHAN. I live at Mr. Fleming's, pawnbroker, Whitechapel - about two or three hundred yards from the prosecutrix's. I do not know who pawned these things.
 Prisoner's Defence. The person who told me of their being at the pawnbroker's, said her name was Louisa Martin, and said if I would go with her she would make an affidavit.
     NOT GUILTY.
 847.  ELIZABETH ROBINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of March , a dressing-case, value 2 s.; a yard of lace, value 1 d., and a sheet, value 1 s.; the goods of James Powell.
   ELIZABETH CLEMENTS. I live in Chapel-street, Curtain-road. I was at work for Mrs. Powell, who keeps a stay warehouse in Norton Falgate; I saw the prisoner come down the stairs with the dressing-case in her hand, and a sheet in her lap; I followed and brought her back - she said she took them from up stairs and wished to be let go.
   JOSHUA ARMSTRONG. I am an officer. I asked the prisoner where she lived - she said she did not know, but she came from Windsor.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had come from Windsor, and had walked that day to town; I went to look for a young woman who lived there, and seeing the things distress tempted me to take them.
     GUILTY. Aged 52. Confined One Month.
 848.  HENRY SCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of April , thirteen yards of printed cotton, value 9 s., the goods of Timothy Smith.
   WILLIAM GREEN. I am shopman to Mr. Timothy Smith, of Grace's-alley. On the 8th of April I saw the prisoner at the door with the print by his side; he was lying on the ground; I heard the cry of Stop thief! I believe he had fallen in the scuffle, he had not got half a yard from the door.
   JOSHUA WILLIAMS. I live in Grace's-alley. I saw the prisoner take the print from within-side the door, where it had been hanging, so near that persons passing by might take it: I collared him, he fell down and pretended to be intoxicated.
   JAMES STARLING. I am a constable. I took him into custody; he appeared to be intoxicated.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had got a little intoxicated - I might have struggled and fell down, but I do not know what I did.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Four Months.
 849.  JOHN STOCK was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of March , a watch, value 15 s., the goods of Charles Bolt.
 CHARLES BOLT. I am a  baker. On Sunday evening, the 27th of March, I hung my watch in my bed-room, in Cow-cross-street; I did not miss it till the Wednesday following: the prisoner is a lodger in the next room. I got an officer and had him taken.
   JAMES ROBERT CASSELL. I am a pawnbroker. The watch was pawned with me on the 30th of March, by the prisoner: he said it was his own, and it had cost him 50 s.
   WILLIAM PAYNE. I took him into custody, and found on him a duplicate, but not of the watch; it led me to Mr. Cassell's, where I found the watch.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was very much intoxicated; when I came home I saw the door wide open, and the watch lying on my bed - how it came there I do not know.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. Confined Two Months.
 850.  JOHN TURLEY and SAMUEL MERRITT were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of February , four saddles, value 8 l.; four bridles, value 4 l., and four girths, value 7 s., the goods of Henry M'Donald.
   ROBERT LOCK. I am headborough of St. Lukes. About seven o'clock, in the morning of the 18th of February, I was in Whitecross-street, and saw the prisoner Turley going up Playhouse-yard with a bundle of bridles under his arm, and eight girths rolled up in his hat - he came out of Chequer-alley, not in a direction from Mr. M'Donald's. He said a man met him at the Star, public-house, in Old-street, and gave him a shilling to take them to Mr. Dixon's - I took him into custody.
 HENRY M'DONALD. I am a  livery-stable keeper: I missed these articles about eight o'clock, in the morning of the 18th of February, when the men came to work - Merritt lived with me for some time - I know nothing of Turley.
   JOHN BEE. I went to Mr. M'Donald's stables - I found the locks and bolts all right, but I saw some foot-marks on the side of the wall, and one on the parapet.
   ROBERT LOCK re-examined. Q. When you were bringing Turley back, did you see Merritt? A. Yes, I met him with four saddles on his shoulder - I showed them to Mr. M'Donald, who owned them as his: Merritt made some resistance, but Turley did not - they both said they had been employed by another man to carry them.
