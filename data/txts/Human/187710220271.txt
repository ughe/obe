   Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY . Fennell had been many years in the service—scrap iron and heavy iron are mixed, and there are cast borings and iron turnings—I gave a special memorandum of each—the actual weights would be put on the gate slate; that was Clarke's duty, but if he was not there somebody else would do it—it is very rarely that he was absent—I did not see any one make an entry on the slate on the 17th—I cannot say when the elate entry was obliterated—I know nothing about the weights on the slate on those three days—I see some of the iron was 3 l . a ton, but the average price in Porter's contract was 2 l . including turnings and borings—an allowance of 7 s . 6 d . was made to Porter—Porter has been under contract with Penn's more than seven years—he carries on the business of an iron merchant—there may be a few cwt. given over to make up for exceedingly dirty lots—I never called on Porter in reference to this matter, I never knew where he was—I know that he was in liquidation some years ago.
   Cross-examined by Fennell.  You must have given me a direct memorandum, you would not give it to Neville—I always took the memorandum the same as Mr. Neville—you would possibly hand me a memorandum when the sale took place, and when the iron was taken away.
   Cross-examined by MR. SIMS . Clarke was about two years in the prose cutor's employ as a labourer, always under Fennell—he was afterwards promoted to be gatekeeper—there are other gates, but he is the only gatekeeper.
   Re-examined.  Iron taken by Porter would go out at Clarke's gate in the ordinary course of business.
