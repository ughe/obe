WELLINGTON WEBSTER re-examined. I have had the brush since, it
was at Union-ball-there was a mark referred to there, but it is not quite
visible--no mark has been put since I have had it—when I first had it
Mrs. Bromfield pointed out a mark—there was an impression, but I can-
not say it was a B.
Mrs. BROMFIELD. I think the mark was here it was a capital Bin pencil
-on my solemn oath, I did not put the brush into the prisoner's pocket, nor
know of its being put there-I was always anxious he should get forward.
and had no occasion to find fault with bim-Mr. Bromfield raised his wages,
and was talking of making him warehouseman if he could write better.
Hannau HOLIDAY re-examined. I did not put the brush into his poc-
ket, nor did any body, to my knowledge—I did not meddle with it at all
it was quite accidental that I noticed it in striking against his coat-I
had no idea there was a brush there—I whispered about it to mistress, and
she sent him out of the warehouse.
Jury. Q. Did the coat hang outside the door or inside ? / Outside
the door opening hid the coat it was shut when my arm touched it-it
leads from the warehouse into the shop-the coat hung on the front
shop door-I was in the front shop, and going into the warehouse, when I
brushed against it.
MR. BROMPIELD. The front of the premises I call the warehouse-be-
yond the warehouse there is a workshop-there is a door from the ware-
house into the workshop-it opens into the warehouse--a coat behind that
door would be hid when the door opened-in opening the door it must
have hung before her--the door is very small and narrow, and the coat would
hang down low-I never noticed where it hung-he latterly wore a coat
with large pockets, which he put on when he went away-a person passing
from one room to the other must touch the coat to pull the door open-
there is no lock nor latch to it-I believe there is a little bit of wood to take
hold of.
Prisoner. My coat did not hang on the door at all, but on a post at the
side, about half-a-yard from the door when it is open. Witness. There is
a rack behind the door, with poles to put brooms on, and large shelves
separated by posts.
