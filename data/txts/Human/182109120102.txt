 1249.  THOMAS MORLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , one shawl, value 12 s.; four yards of linen, value 4 s.; one remnant of cambric, value 3 s; three yards of muslin, value 2 s.; one yard of lawn, value 1 s.; one yard of cotton, value 1 s., and one pair of gloves, value 1 s., the goods of James Shoolbread and John Ferguson.
   JAMES SHOOLBREAD. I am in partnership with John Ferguson, we are linen drapers and silk mercers, and live in Tottenham-court-road. I only know the property.
   WILLIAM RIVERS NASH. I was servant to the prosecutors. On the 11th of September, the prisoner was porter to the house. I had suspicions and missed a shilling. I took him up stairs and told him, I suspected him. I left him in the room, and on returning, I found a duplicate partly torn on the ground, and part in his hand, but before I attempted to pick it up, he had torn it all. I took it off the ground, and by it I found the silk shawl at Wood's. I found the other property at the Grafton Arms, public-house, Grafton-street; he told me he had left a bundle there - I went and found it, and gave it to the head-borough.
   WILLIAM REASCROFT. I am apprentice to Mr. Wood, a pawnbroker, who lives in High-street, Bloomsbury. The duplicate produced belongs to our shop, and is for a shawl pawned on the 10th of September, for 5 s., in the name of John Smith. I took it in, but do not recollect the person.
   CHARLES COUSINS. I took the prisoner in charge on the 11th - the headborough gave me the bundle.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. They promised if I would confess and tell every thing, they would not hurt me, that they suspected somebody else more than me.
 W. R. NASH. I suspected both the porters, and told him if he acknowledged where all the property was, I would do all I could with his master.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined Six Months and Whipped.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.

 1251.  JEREMIAH SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , one watch, value 10 s.; one coat, value 10 s.; one hat, value 5 s., and one pair of shoes, value 2 s., the goods of Neil Moran , from his person.
   NEIL MORAN. I am an artificial-flower maker, and live in Greek-street, Soho. On Sunday night, the 9th of September, between twelve and one o'clock, I was locked out of my lodging, and wandered about the streets until five o'clock in the morning, I was quite sober. In George-street, St. Giles's, a man shoved me off the footway. I asked why he did it - he then knocked me down, and as I was getting up he knocked me down again; the prisoner then came up, and pretended to take my part, and another tall man with him - the man who knocked me down went away. I took the prisoner and the other man to a public-house, and treated them with half a gallon of beer for their civility; they told me they knew the man who had abused me so, and if I would go and lay down at their lodging and take some rest, they would get me some breakfast in the morning; they took me to No. 9, Church-street, into the parlour, there was a bed in the room and a woman, who I understood to be the wife of one of them; they told her to get breakfast ready by the time I got up; the prisoner helped me off with my coat as it was tight - I was quite sober. I took off my shoes and hat - I had my watch in my fob. It was about seven o'clock when I laid down, and about nine o'clock, I found the prisoner fumbling in my waistcoat pocket. I asked what was the matter, he said, "Nothing, I am only putting the clothes on you;" nobody but him was in the room. I got up and they were all off, and my coat, shoes, and hat also, and my watch was gone from my fob. I found the slider and ring of it on the bed - it was fastened to the ribbon - I had my watch with me when I left my lodging. I have found nothing since. I went out and alarmed the neighbourhood; the prisoner was taken between four and five o'clock that day. We took him to the public-house, and the landlord said he was one of the two who were with me, and he did not deny it - I am certain of him.
   JAMES JORDAN. I took the prisoner in custody, he denied the charge. The prosecutor might have been drinking.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw this man and another stripped, and fighting, in George-street, the prosecutor was in liquor, and the other was striking him. I pulled him off - they fought for a quarter of an hour, then the prosecutor gave me some beer; he said he should like to lay down, and the man who drank with us said he could lay down at his place; four of us, and two girls of the town went with him, the man pulled off his coat and shoes. I went home in ten minutes, as I did not lodge there.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
 First Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
