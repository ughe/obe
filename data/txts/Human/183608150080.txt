 1859. THOMAS ROBERTS was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of July , 3lbs. weight of soap, value 1 s  . 3 d  ., the goods of James Soames and another. JAMES JOHN FOSTER. I am foreman to William and James Soames, of Spitalfields, soap manufacturers. The prisoner was in their employ—he, with two other men, were at work with me on the 16th of July—I received information, and the prisoner asked me if he might go to breakfast—I said "Yes, but I must search you first"—I found on him this bar of soap.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS Q.  Is your master here? A.  No. The prisoner had been in their employ about four years—I have been fifteen years there—when I said I must search him, he said, "I have nothing about me;" but when I found it, he never uttered a word—it is usual to leave a piece of soap for the men to wash with, but not bars of soap—it was concealed in his frock.
 (The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he had found the soap: he received a good character, and the prosecutor engaged to con  tinue him in his employ.) GUILTY. Aged 30.— Recommended to mercy . Confined Six Days.
 1860. HENRY HAYESMAN and JOHN REEVES were indicted for stealing, on the   7th of July , 1 shawl, value 9 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of William Butland . WILLIAM BUTLAND. I keep a pawnbroker's-shop in Whitechapel-road, at the corner of Black Lion-yard. On Thursday evening, the 7th of July, about six o'clock, a boy gave me information—I examined my shawls and missed a reddish one—it has not been found.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  When did you miss it? A.  About seven o'clock in the evening—I had seen it about one o'clock in the day. JOHN MOSES. I sell cherries.
