 507.  THOMAS CAMPLIN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of February , 5 shillings, 4 sixpences, 24 halfpence, and 4 farthings, the monies of Henry Ranson , from his person.
   HENRY RANSON. I sell fish in Shoreditch - on the 13th of February I had the misfortune to sprain my ancle in going across the road in Shoreditch, just before twelve o'clock at night; my wife was with me, and the prisoner was coming by - she said, "For God's sake assist my husband out of the road, as I think he has broken his ancle" - he gave his assistance, and when we got to Shoreditch church another took hold of my left arm - they led me on to Middlesex chapel; the other one then left me and ran away - I then sent my wife on to open the door, as I was in such pain - when I got between three and four doors of my house, the prisoner went away the same road as the other - I then put my hand into my pocket and missed my money, about 9s. in all; about 2s. in copper - there was one farthing I can swear to - I called "Stop thief" - I saw the prisoner stopped at the corner - I have seen the farthing since.
   DAVID JONES. I was near the end of Thomas's-street - I heard the cry of "Stop thief," and saw the prisoner running towards Shoreditch church as fast as he could - I pursued him - he gained upon me - a policeman crossed towards him - he turned back, and I took him - I gave him to the parties who cried "Stop thief" - the prosecutor's wife said that he had robbed them of all the money they were about to pay their rent with - there was some silver found on him; 5s. in silver, and 1s. 71/4d. in copper - he objected to being searched, and denied having any money about him.
   JEREMIAH O'SULLIVAN(police-constable, N 220.) I took the prisoner - what has been stated is correct - I found the money stated on him - this is the farthing which the prosecutor can swear to - I took the coppers and 3s. 6d. in silver out of his different pockets.
 Prisoner. The magistrate asked him if he could swear to any of the money, and he said "No."
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Transported for Seven Years.
 508.  JACOB FRIEDLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 shirt, value 3s; 1 waistcoat, value 1s. 6d.; 1 pair of stockings, value 1s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s. 6d.; 1 tobacco box, value 1s.; and 1 penknife, value 6d., the goods of William Gray.
   WILLIAM GRAY. I lost these things from Mr. Griffiths', No. 13, Noble-street, St. Luke's- the prisoner lodged with me - I lost them on the 26th of November, and he left the house directly - on Thursday last my master brought him home, and he asked me what one thing and another was worth - I said they were worth 17s.
   THOMAS GRIFFITHS. I employed these two men - they both lodged in one room - on the 26th of November the prisoner came down with a bundle - we had given him warning to leave - he washed himself and went away with the bundle without his breakfast - he left some cards of steel pens behind him - when I took him he asked the prosecutor what the articles were worth.
   FRANCIS GRANEY(police-constable G 87). I took the prisoner - he said he would give 8s. to settle it, or if not, he did not care whether he got three months or seven years of it.
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor wanted to settle with me; he asked me 1l., then 15s., and then 12s., and I offered him 8s.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Three Months.
 509.  DUNCAN GRANT was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of February , 1 silver spoon, value 5s., the goods of Saint George Gregg, to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 15. - Confined for Five Days.
 510.  ELIZABETH KEYMER was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 2 half-crowns, 1 shilling, and 2 sixpences, the monies of Thomas Keen , from his person.
   THOMAS KEEN. I deal in yeast- I fell in with the prisoner on 12th of February, about seven o'clock in the evening - I had seen her once or twice before - I was a little fresh - she asked me to give her something to drink - she took me to a private house - I sent for two pots of beer- there were four or five of us - we then went to a gin shop and had half a pint of gin - another lady then came up, and she asked me to give them some more gin, which I did- she then said if we would go over the way, she would stand a pot, and we did - the prisoner then asked me to give them a little more; I said I could not; the prisoner then took my jacket off my arm, and took two half-crowns, two sixpences, and a shilling out of my pocket - she ran out, and I pursued, but lost her - I saw her at the station-house the next day.
   JOHN CLARK. I saw the prosecutor come into the public-house with a pipe in his mouth - the prisoner took something from his pocket, but I do not think it was without his own will.
   THOMAS COOPER. I am an officer - I took the prisoner the next day - she said he gave her the money to stop with her.
     NOT GUILTY.
 510.  ABRAHAM SIMMONS was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM BRADBURY. I am a hay salesman- the prisoner was in my service nearly twelve months - it was his duty to receive money, and to account to me for it - when he sold any hay, he should bring the cart into the market, and give me the money - this hay was my own - he came into the market on the 1st of February, about a quarter past two, with his team, and then he disappeared - I waited some time, he did not come; I went to some places, and could not find him - on the Wednesday afterwards I saw him in custody - he had given me no notice of leaving me.
