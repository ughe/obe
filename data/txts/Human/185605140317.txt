CHARLES ABBOTT. ( policeman, K  322). I took Howes into custody on 29th April, in Park-street, Borough. I said I took her for being concerned with Mrs. Horne, in removing her things away from her husband—she said she knew she had done wrong, and her husband would have persuaded her not to have gone—she said, "I will go and tell Mr. Borne where his wife is"—I said "Very well, come with me"—we took a cab, and went to No. 77, Brook-street, Lambeth, to Mrs. Hughes, who, Mrs. Howes said was her sister-in-law—when we got there, Mrs. Howes said to Mrs. Hughes, "Do you know where Mrs. Horne is gone to?"—Mrs. Hughes said, "Perhaps she is over the way, where she has taken the room"—we went over the way, to No. 46, and when we got there Howes asked where Betsy was gone—the old lady said, she did not know, she had not seen her that morning—Howes then said that a man went away in a van, and Mrs. Horne had gone with him—I then took Howes to the station.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where was it that Mrs. Howes said this? A.  In Brook-street, when we came away from No. 46. HARRIET SMITH. I am the wife of John Smith. On the morning of 21st April, I passed Mr. Horne's house, and Mrs. Horne called me in—I went in, and staid several minutes—I went away, and came back again, and went in myself—Mrs. Horne was there, and Howes, and the next-door neighbour—I saw one box and two bags on the floor—Mrs. Horne asked me to help her, which I did—I carried one bag, and Mrs. Horne, and Mrs. Howes, and I, all proceeded to Blackwall, carrying those things—I left them there and went home. JOHN SULLIVAN. I am a detective police-officer at Liverpool. In conse quence of a telegraphic message, I found the prisoner Fennell in a public-house, in Robert-street, Liverpool, kept by Mrs. Pearce—Mrs. Horne was with Fennell—I asked Mrs. Horne what her name was—she said, "Mrs. Jones"—I then asked Fennell what his name was—he said, "Mr. Jones"—I said, "No, your name is Fennell; you absconded with this woman, taking with you a quantity of property, and between 300 l . and 400 l ."—he then said, "My name in Fennell;" and he said he merely met that woman at the railway station at London—I took them to the police office, and two bags, a carpet bag, a box of provisions, and other articles—I searched Fen nell, and found this belt round his body—I asked him what was in it—he said "Gold"—I asked him what amount?—he said, "I can't tell within 7 l . or 8 l .; I think about 120 l ."—I opened it, and found in it 127 l . 10 s . in gold; and on his person a half-sovereign, and five shillings, a sixpence, and 5 1/2 d . in coppers—I asked him where he got the gold—he said he had been working in the Crimea, and brought the money from there—I asked him if he knew who paid him then—he said he could not tell me—I also found on his person this shipping ticket for John Jones and Elizabeth Jones, to Australia, and 25 l . paid for their passage—the date of sailing is 5th May—Mrs. Horne said she knew nothing about the money—I found a silver watch and a chain in one of the boxes which Fennell claimed.
