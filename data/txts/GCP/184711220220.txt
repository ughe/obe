231. GEORGE CLARKE, stealing 1 sack, value 28.; and 1} bushel de
oats, beans, and chaff, 28.; the goods of William Shott, his master : and
MARY ANN PHILLIPS, feloniously receiving the same ; to which
CLARKE pleaded Guilty. Aged 45.- Recommended to mercy by the
Jury.-Confined Three Months.
WILLIAM SHort. I am a corn-merchant, at Thomas-street, Horsleydown.
Clarke was my horse-keeper-be had a mixture of oats, chaff, and beans to
give to my horse-I could not miss any of it-this sack produced is mine,
and the mixture also I believe-it corresponds with mine in all respects.
THOMAS WEST (policeman, M 249.) On Friday, 12th Nov., about seva
o'clock in the evening, I saw a cart standing in Cross-street, Horsleydown-
Clarke was standing at the back of it-I saw a sack, containing something in
it-I went into Fair-street, and watched Clarke-he went to Mr. Phillips
shop, remained there two or three minutes, came out, went to the cart, and
took the sack into Phipps' shop-it had about a bushel and a half of some
thing in it-I saw him go through the shop into the back parlour-Phipps
was sitting at the table in the parlour-Clarke remained two or three minutes
- he put the sack down, and came out-I remained outside a few minutes,
then went in, and asked Mrs. Phillips if she had any corn brought within the
last hour into her shop--she said she had not-I asked her if a man with a
white smock-frock, who walked lame, bad brought anything there she said
he had not, but she knew that man I went into the back parlour, and saw
this sack there-I asked her who brought it-she said she did not know, it
was brought when she was putting the children to bed-I asked her what
time she put the children to bed she said an hour and a half ago; that
when she came down she saw it there, and did not know who put it there-
Clarke had taken it in about three minutes before I have shown this sack
to Mr. Shott-it bears his name—it contains about a bushel and a half of
beans, oats, and chaff.
