KELLY, Mayor.
John CHRISTOPHER Liomin. I had some cows pastured on Wimbledon-
common on Thursday night, between the 8th and 9th of June.I lost
heifer in calf, which I afterwards found at the other side of Uxbridge—it
has calved since the prisoner lived with me some years ago for a little
while.
John CASSWELL. I saw the heifer safe on Thursday the 8th-I have
since seen it at Uxbridge in the prosecutor's possession.
GEORGE BAINES. I am a beast-salesman and dealer at Smithfield.
On Friday, the 9th of June, I saw the prisoner offering a heifer for sale
--it was tied up regularly-I asked the prisoner the price-he asked me
51. 10s.--I bid him 41.- he said it was his father's-I asked him what he
was-he said a labouring man in Wimbledon-park--that they bred it
themselves, and it cost them half-a-crown a week-I bought it for 4l. 10s.,
and then I said, " Before I pay you, you must bring some person forward
to convince me it is all right-he called himself William Young-he said
he expected his father in town, but was not certain-about two hour
after, he brought a person from some part of the town, I believe it was
from Fenchurch-street, to say it was all right-1 directed him to my
money-taker to pay him, and to take his address, and who came to vouch
for him-on the Monday following an application was made to me.
WILLIAM CHAPPING. I am an ostler, and live in Northumberland-street.
I knew the prisoner when he lived with Mrs. Punter-he came to me on
the 9th of June, and said that he had brought a heifer up and sold it for
41. 108.; that it had been eating its head off-he asked me to go up to
Smithfield to say I knew him as living at Mrs. Punter's-my wife had
washed and mended things for him, which came to 58., and he said when
he got the money he would pay me-he told me his name was William
Young- I went and said it was all right as far as I learned-he paid me
the 58., and gave me 1s. for my trouble.
CHARLES CHURCHILL. I am a horse-patrol. I took the prisoner.
Prisoner's Defence. A person of the name of Joseph Crouch told me
to take it and sell it, and bring him the money, which I did he refused
to pay me till some one came forward—I did not know who to get, and
I went to Chapping, who came and spoke for me.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor and
Jury, believing he had been duped by others. - Transported for Liſe.
1676. JOHN JOYCE was indicted for feloniously breaking and enter-
ing the dwelling-house of Wilberforce Pike, on the 31st of May, with intent
to steal, and stealing therein, 7 printed books, value 30s.; and 2 pairs of
boots, value 98.; his goods.-2d Count, stating the goods to belong to
various persons.
WILBERFORCE PIKE. I am a school-master, and live at Edmonton. On
the 31st of May, when I went to bed, I went round to see that every thing
was secure, about 11 o'clock--the windows were shut down-I was the last
person up-in the morning I found the house had been broken into!
could not see how they had got in--the windows had been merely pulled
down-they were not fastened-they are about four feet from the ground
-I missed the books stated, belonging to my scholars- I lost two pairs of
boots.
