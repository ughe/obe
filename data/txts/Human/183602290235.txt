I was coming in with the coals, I saw George Cranston in the passage, at our parlour door, on the outside—I knew him very well—I could see him very well—it was not dark—he had a kettle in his hand, and asked me what sort of a fire I had—I told him it was almost out—I unlocked the door, and he rushed in without my will—he followed me in, and put his kettle on the fire—he did not stay many minutes—he went out of the room, and went up-stairs and brought down two pieces of bread—he then took away his kettle, and went up stairs again—his mother occupies the back-room up stairs—in our apartment there was a box belonging to my father, containing two coats, and two pairs of trowsers—it was locked, but I saw my mother put the things in—I remained in the room till a quarter past six o'clock—my father and mother had not come home then—I went into the yard, and locked the door—as I was locking it, I saw Charles Cranston standing in the passage—I took the key with me—I was not quite ten minutes out—when I came in again, before I unlocked the door, I head a rap at the window—I went to the street door—I then unlocked the par  lour door, and saw George Cranston on the outside, putting the shutters too—I missed my father's box—the window-sash was up—it had been shut when I left the room—I saw my mother fasten it—I had not un fastened it—one pane of glass was broken—I had left two shillings on the mantel-piece, in a wine-glass—they were gone—I had known the prisoners before.
