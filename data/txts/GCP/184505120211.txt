1220. THOMAS LILLYWALL was indicted for stealing 1 handker-
chief, value 1s., the goods of Joseph Hunt, from his person; and that he
had been before convicted of felony.
Joseph Hunt. I am a waiter, and live in Union-street, Southwark,
On the 1st of May, a few minutes before twelve o'clock, I was in the Bel-
videre-road at the entrance of the Suspension-bridge-I felt a tug at my
pocket-I turned, and saw my handkerchief in the prisoner's hand-I
seized him by the collar, and told him to drop it-he dropped the handker-
chief, and Evans took it up-it is mine-this is it-I gave him to the
officer.
Thomas Evans. I live in Robert-street, Bedford-row. I was standing
close to the prosecutor-I saw the prisoner drop the handkerchief from his
hand behind him-I took it up-the prosecutor called out, “That is mine"
--the handkerchief is here now.
O agrond
Jeremiah LYNCH (police-constable M 188.) I produce a certificate of
the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Onslow's office—(read
--Convicted 4th of July, 1843, and confined three months)—the prisoner
is the man.
GUILTY. Aged 27.--Transported for Ten Years.
1221. ELIZABETH WATSON was indicted for stealing 1 purse, value
3s.; 3 sovereigns, and 5 shillings; the monies of John Taylor, from his
person,
o to
JOHN TAYLOR. I live in Salisbury-crescent, Walworth. On the 30th
of April, about ten or eleven o'clock at night, I was in the Dover-road, on
my way home-the prisoner addressed me, and asked me to give her a
glass of ale-in order to get rid of her I consented, as I had been annoyed
on similar occasions--she wished me to go to a public-house which I did
not like the appearance of-I said I would give it her at another house-
we crossed the road for that purpose, and she was leading me a way I did
not like, and wished to get away- I turned and saw two men who were fol-
lowing us, the prisoner being close to me, and speaking vociferously to me-
one of the men then passed very close, and thrust the prisoner against me
--the men then went off, one one way, and the other the other-imme-
diately after I said, " I must go," and the prisoner let go of my arm-I
put my hand to feel for my purse and watch, and I missed my purse which
contained three sovereigns and some silver, which I had seen safe about
two hours before.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. Was there not something tender
going on? A. No-her cheek was close to mine-I kept her about ten mi-
nutes, and then gave her into custody-I believe there was only 2 d. found
on the prisoner-I was sober-I am not married I was walking with the
prisoner for ten minutes or a quarter of an hour-she had hold of me.
WILLIAM Davis (police-constable M 125). I took the prisoner--there
was nothing found on her but 2}d.
Nor Guilty.
