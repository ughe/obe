 634. JEREMIAH COLLINS was indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Peter Stacey , about two in the night of the   20th of Jan ., and stealing therein 1 tea-caddy, value 1 s  .; 1 box, 6 d  .; 1/2lb. weight of tea, 2 s  . 1 d  .; 1lb. weight of sugar, 6 d  .; 1lb. weight of bacon, 8 d  .; 4 sovereigns, 4 half-crowns, 25 shillings, and 10 sixpences, his property. ELIZABETH STACEY. I live at No. 40, Church-street, Rotherhithe. I am the wife of Peter Stacey—we keep a beer shop—on the 20th of Jan., on going down stairs at night I noticed a door closed which I usually left open—I put my hand against it, and felt something against it, some resistance, something outside, and on my shaking it the handle of a broom fell—on open ing the door there is a window, as you come to the bottom of the stairs, which shows light into the bar—I saw the gas alight, and saw a man standing with his back against the window, with somethings lying on the table, he appeared to see me because he ran right to the door and escaped—I think the door must have been on the jar, closed, but not latched,—I did not hear him lift the latch—he had no shoes on—he had on a round jacket, and a cap, and cord trowsers—the gas was burning in the room—I missed four sovereigns and 2 l . worth of silver—I had left the money in a box, wrapped up in a piece of paper in a closet on the stairs overnight—some articles had been removed—a tea-caddy was taken off the mantle-piece with 1/2lb. of tea in it—I had seen the prisoner at our house the night before, but do not remember seeing him for several weeks before—I will not positively swear he is the man I saw in the parlour—but I have every reason to believe he is.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Other persons were fetched to your premises, were they not? A.  When he ran to the door I called "Police"—a man crossed and asked what was the matter—I was frightened and did not tell him, but ran in doors and told my son and husband—I never heard of any body else being taken—the prisoner did not give himself up, he was taken at the Eagle, just by my house—he came to my place, and said, "Does master want me?" I said, "I don't know, you had better call again," he went to the door and I had given information about him—I did not ask why he thought master wanted him—I thought he would run away if I questioned him—I could not detain him—I went immediately to the station—I was disturbed about a quarter past two o'clock in the night.
