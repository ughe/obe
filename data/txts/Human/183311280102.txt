   JOHN ARSCOTT. I was Lord Bristol's house-steward, and was at Ickworth, in October - there is a box for the letters which has a lock and key - I kept the key - there was only one key to my knowledge - if a letter was put into it and required to be taken out again, I was applied to to open the box - on the 9th and 10th of October, I believe, I took the letters out of the box to put into the bag for the post; it was my duty to do so; I put them into a bag to be sent to the post-office, at Bury St. Edmunds - that bag had a lock and key; there were two keys to it - I kept one, and the post-master at Bury St. Edmunds, kept the other - I locked the bag on those days and did so always - I gave the bag to a person kept on purpose to take it to the post-office.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long have you left Lord Bristol's service? A. About a month - why I left the service is a matter between his lordship and myself - I will swear it does not arise from the present affair - I knew nothing of the present affair until last week, and I dare say I could produce testimonials from his lordship; I am now in his lordship's service at another place - I have no other reason to remember this particularly, except that it was a duty conferred on me, and I know I performed it - I took the letters out of the box that day; I cannot say how many there were; it is quite useless to try to remember. I believe Lord Bristol is a Member of the House of Peers; his lordship's letters generally come directed to himself - the number of letters vary from five or six to fifteen or sixteen a day; it depended entirely on what company was in the house - I have no recollection how many there were on the 9th or 10th of October - I have sometimes examined the addresses, if a party has wished to know if a frank had been put into the box for them - I cannot swear whether I examined the letters on the evenings of the 9th or 10th of October; I sent the bag to the post-office by Ralph who assists in the stables and fetches and carries the letters; he generally takes them; he always brings me the box - I cannot say whether he took them or not; I have sent them by another person once; I only remember doing so once.
 MR. SHEPHERD. Q. Did you dispatch the letters from the box up to the end of October? A. I did.
   LORD JERMYN. I recollect franking a letter for Margaret Newberry, about the 10th of October - it ought to have been directed to Grafton-court, for I went to the place myself to engage the wet nurse, and knew she lived there - I recollect the person's name I directed it to was Dennis Newberry, and I should have thought that I directed it to Grafton-court, North-street, Manchester-square, but I may have made a mistake - the impression on my mind is not sufficient to say whether I took any particular notice of the letters - I cannot say whether I found it in my dressing-room or not - I generally found them on the table of my dressing-room - when I came to dress in the evening my servant generally asked me if there was any frank to be given away - after I franked letters, I gave them to my servant to put into the box; and the impression on my mind is that I left them in the room; while he was out of the room - I recollect sending him out of the room for the Christian name - it would be Pope's duty to put the letters into the box.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is your lordship a member of the House of Peers? A. Of the House of Commons - I sometimes write my full number of letters - I cannot say I recollect on my own knowledge what day of the month it was I directed this letter, but I inquired of my nurses, and they told me it was the day after I returned from an excursion in the country - I referred to a memorandum, and found it was on the 10th - I recollect it by comparing dates - I recollect going away the week after the Bury fair, which I believe was Friday, the 4th; and I think I went on the following Monday, and stayed two nights - I have every reason to believe it was on the 10th of October that I franked the letter - I cannot say I have a distinct recollection of the address I wrote on the letter - I cannot say whether it was Grafton-court, or Grafton-street - I cannot say whether I directed it correctly or not - I know how I ought to have directed it - I cannot say whether North-street was on it or not - I knew at the time that was the place where the person lives - I should say that North-street was probably on the letter - I thought the house was No. 9, but I cannot say, but I believe the number was on the slip of paper which was with the letter - I have not a very distinct recollection of the number; I conceive I copied it from the slip of paper - my impression is that I referred to the slip of paper, for I remember "Mr. Newberry" being on the slip, and thinking it was not the right way to write to a person of his station, I sent to know the Christian name - I had Margaret Newberry's address on a slip of paper, and I once went there to Grafton-court; it is a small place; I should not have called it a street, if I have done so, unless I had been in a hurry.
