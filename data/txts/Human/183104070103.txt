 910.  FORTUNATO MARIE PEREIRA was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 2 pairs of shoes, value 20s.; 1 pair of ear-rings, value 10s.; 1 handkerchief, value 2s., and 1 opera-glass, value 5s., the goods of Samuel Astell.
 MARY ASTELL. I am the wife of  Samuel Astell, of Wilstead-street, Somers'-town, builder. We have known the prisoner a year and a half - he lodged with us twice; the last time for four months; he owed us nearly thirty pounds - he went out in February last to go to his attorney, as he said; I missed these things while he was with us.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe he is a Portuguese, who was obliged to fly from Don Miguel? A. Yes; he paid us honestly as long as he had money; and being distressed, I believe my husband at times gave him leave to pledge a few things, and he has redeemed them - he might have intended to redeem these.
     NOT GUILTY.
 911.  PHILIP BURGIN was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of March , 1 carboy, value 7s., and 84lbs. of turpentine, value 2l. 9s., the goods of George Austin.
 GEORGE AUSTIN. I live in  Perceval-street, Clerkenwell. On the evening of the 10th of March this carboy of turpentine was taken from my door - I found it at the station-house next evening.
 JAMES CHAMBERS. I am a Policeman. On the Friday Davy came to the station-house, and said he had found this property over night - I went to his house, and brought it from there; he is since deceased - his body was found on Saturday, previous to the Grand Jury sitting, and an inquest have returned a verdict of wilful murder against some persons unknown.
 CHARLES SCOTCHMER. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 11th of March; he said he knew what it was about, for he had heard about it, and that the night before he was coming from the doctor's, and met a man, who asked if he knew where Davy lived - that he took him there, and told him he had brought the man who owned the turpentine, but that he had had nothing more to do with it.
 Prisoner's Defence. What the witness has said last is entirely false.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Second Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 912.  WILLIAM FINCH was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , three handkerchiefs, value 7s., the goods of George Howard.
   GEORGE HOWARD. I am a hosier, and live in Oxford-street. On the 1st of March the prisoner was brought into the shop, accused of stealing these handkerchiefs, which I had seen safe five minutes before - they were partly within the door-way.
 JAMES KENNEDY. I am a music-copier, and live in Dover-street, Oxford-street. I was in Oxford-street, on the opposite side to Mr. Howard's-shop, and saw the prisoner, with another about four years older than himself, lurking about this shop; his companion crossed over the road - I looked towards the shop door; the other then went over, and took from the door-post the handkerchiefs - they were in one piece; the prisoner stood close at his side, held up his apron, which the other put them into, and both ran away - I ran over, and laid hold of the prisoner; the other quickened his pace, and got away - I took the prisoner within four doors of the shop, without losing sight of him; I took the handkerchiefs out of his apron, and took him into the shop.
 WILLIAM SIBLEY. I am a Policeman. I received the prisoner in charge, with the handkerchiefs.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming along Oxford-street, and saw this boy with the handkerchiefs in his hand; he gave them to me - I asked where he got them; he made no answer, and I was taken.
     GUILTY. Aged 12. - Transported for Seven Years.
 913.  JAMES REEVES and JAMES JAY were indicted for stealing, on the 9th of March , 105lbs. of lead, value 8s., the goods of Nathaniel Gould , and fixed to a certain building.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to belong to  William Cox and Thomas Cox.
 CHARLES MATTHEWS. I am an assistant overseer at the  Houseless Poor Asylum, in Playhouse-yard, Whitecross-street, St. Luke's- William and Thomas Cox are owners of the premises. On the 9th of March, about eleven o'clock in the morning, Attfield gave me information - the prisoners were then in custody; I went on the roof, and all the lead was stripped from the trap-door - the space is about five feet six inches, by three feet four inches; Attfield brought it on the 11th, and we compared it to the place - it fitted by two nails, which had been driven through into a board, which I have here - I have not a doubt of its belonging to that place; the prisoners were in the asylum for nine or ten weeks, as helpers and stokers - I saw them there on the 9th of March.
   WILLIAM ATTFIELD. I am a constable of Worship-street. I stopped the prisoners about half-past nine o'clock that morning, about one hundred and fifty yards from the asylum - they were both carrying a load, which appeared very heavy, wrapped in a jacket and a pair of trousers; they turned into Cowhell-alley - one went into a Chimney-sweeper's there, and I pushed the other in, and with great difficulty took them to the office - they had got this lead; I went to the asylum, and on the 11th I saw the lead compared to the roof; it fitted the vacancy exactly - I observed this board; the holes in the lead corresponded with it - I have not a doubt the lead was taken from there; they said they brought the lead from home, but refused to say where.
