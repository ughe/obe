   Q.  How many times has your conduct been questioned in courts of justice, for gross usury, extortion, and fraud? A.  Now I mean to say, upon my oath, that sucj things may have been said and counsel will take liberty to say such things, and most untruly sometimes, but nothing of that kind ever occurred—I never was before a Magistrate on any charge, not even a watch-house charge—I have never been questioned in any case in courts of justice.
   Q.  Do you discount bills? A.  No I—do not make loans to people, and then supply them with snuff-boxes—only in a way of business, like other tradesmen—I never was a money-lender in my life—my name is Hudson, of Oxford-street, a tobacconist—I take the liberty of saying that I am notorious for not being a money-lender. JOHN COLE. I am a chemist and druggist, and live at No. 101, High Holborn. I know Bray's omnibuses—I have observed other omnibuses near them when they beem going along the road—I suppose a thousand times—the others were driving continually before and behind them, and trying to prevent passengers, alarming ladies, and driving so close behind it as to make it dangerous for females to get in—I have never seen Bray's do so to the other omnibuses. RICHARD PRIESTLY. I live in High Holborn, and am a bookseller. I have seen Mr. Bray's omnibuses, and other omnibuses with them, one behind and one before, quite close—I have seen that frequently—I do not know the names of the other omnibus—one was the "Emperor.
