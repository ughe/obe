John WALLER. I am a cabinet-maker, and live in Wood-street, Wal-
thamstow. In Nov., or it might be the latter end of October, the two
prisoners came to me by the recommendation of Mr. Pridgeon--they said
they wanted a shop fitted up, as they had taken a shop in the clothes line-
both of them spoke together-I did not know one from the other I fitted
their shop up-it was not opened for general sale---one time one shutter was
down and one time another--they said they were going to bring down some
goods--when I first went there was a lot of shoe-makers' cuttings and tips
in the shop, but nothing of any consequence I waited a week or ten days,
and then I went down again the prisoners were both there I did not know
the name of Hammell till I saw it in the paper---the shop was never opened
for business-I live within 150 yards of it--they were there eight or nine
weeks--when I went down to the shop there was a bale or two of goods in
the shop and they said they should open on the Thursday following and
have a great many goods. gw la sor t id
