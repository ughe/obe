EDWIN FRANKS was indicted for unlawfully and maliciously cutting and wounding Charles Wheeler in and upon his nose and right eye, with intent to disfigure and disable him.—2nd COUNT  , with intent to do him some grievous bodily harm. CHARLES WHEELER. I am a coal-porter. I live in what is called the Square, in Old-town, Clapham—North-street is further up than that—I am in the employ of Mary Ann Harvey—last Tuesday week, between six and seven o'clock, I was in her coal-cellar, and was struck with a stone—it was thrown down in the cellar at me, and I was struck on the arm and on the shoulder—I did not see the prisoner throw the stone—when I went out with my goods afterwards, I saw the prisoner in Walker's toy-shop—I pulled him out, and shook him—I told him it was for throwing a stone at me in the cellar—he took up a stone, and said, "I will break your b—head with this before I leave you this night"—he then ran, and I ran after him—he shot under a bar, then turned round, and threw this stone at me, and knocked me down senseless—he hit me in the eye—a young man picked me up, and led me to Mr. Parrot, a surgeon—there were other boys throwing stones at the time, but they were behind me—there was no other boy before me but the prisoner.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  It was dark, was it not? A.  Yes—I did not see the stone come—I could not see people—it was a wooden bar—I did not strike myself against it—I did not get to it—I was struck with a stone while I was in the coal-cellar, but I was not in a position to see who threw it—the boys do throw stones at me sometimes about there—the prisoner was in the toy-shop—I do not know what he was doing—I went up to him and shook him, and then he threatened to throw a stone at me—I cannot say how many boys were there—I cannot say whether Stephen Love land was there—the boys behind me were not throwing stones at that time—I did not turn round to look at them, but I should have felt them—I can not tell how many boys were shouting after me—the prisoner was from ten to twelve yards from me—there was no other boy in front of me that I saw—I Will swear I did not turn my head round—I do not know whether the other boys scampered away when I fell down, as I was senseless on the ground. GEORGE BARR. I am a butcher. On the evening of the 15th of Oct. I was opposite my master's shop, Mr. Terry's, at Clapham—I saw the prosecutor standing by my side, and I saw the prisoner with a stone in his right hand—I was not close enough to see whether it was a large stone or not—I heard him threaten the prosecutor—he said, "I will do for you to-night, you b—b—; I will split your b—head open "—Wheeler then ran after him, and I ran a short distance—I stopped, and I beard Wheeler say, "He has cut my eye out"—Wheeler groaned, and I ran and picked him up from the ground—I took him to the doctor's, and left him there.
