2165. WILLIAM DARVILL, unlawfully assaulting Julia Servin, with

2166. SOLOMON COWAN, feloniously receiving 9724 cards, value 41.,
the goods of John Dickinson and another, knowing them to have been stolen
Messrs. CLARKSON and BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE WARPLE (City-policeman, 25). On 8th Aug. I went to the
prisoner's premises- I told him who I was, and that I took him on suspicion of
receiving a quantity of cards stolen from Messrs. Dickinson, in the Old Bailey,
and said he had better give me what cards he had to save me the trouble of
ransacking his premises--he pointed to a shelf, and said these were all he had
-I took them off the shelf, and I produce them-I asked if he could
account for who he bought them of, and if be had any receipts to show-he
said he had no receipts, and be either bought them of a person of the name
of Sharp, or some name I could not exactly understand some of these cards
were in the window, and some at the door- I took him to the station, and
there I asked him who it was he said he bought them of-I believe he said it
was of no consequence.
Joan NICHOLAS. I am assistant to Wilson and Sons, of Cheapside. I know
the prisoner, and have at various times purchased cards of him- the last I
bought were on 3rd Aug.- I produce part of them--I purchased eleven gross
of large cards, and ten dozen of small ones I knew them to be Dickinson's
by their general appearance--they are well known in the trade-I paid 48s.
a gross for the large, and 28. 6d. a dozen for the small-I paid him on that day
261. 68. altogether we print on the cards.
Cross-examined by MR. Bopkin. Q. Had you any other transaction with
bim? 4. Yes, I have known him fifteen years I have bought occasionally
of him-be keeps an open shop in Goswell-street, I believe I never went there
---I have frequently seen him at sales years ago, and buying paper-I never
knew of Dickinson's cards being sold at sales stationers sell off their stock
when they have been bankrapts, but paper is frequently sold at grocer's and
other sales.
Thomas Patrick. I have been in the employ of Messrs. Dickinson about
eighteen years. These cards produced by Mr. Nicholas are Messrs. Dickin-
son's make-the nett price of these large cards to the trade would be about
618. 8d. the gross, and the small ones about 888. 3d.- they are never sold
from us at a lower price--we have lost a considerable number of cards of this
description, perhaps 10001. worth in the last twelve months--I never heard but
of one sale in which our cards were sold, and that was one half-gross—there
are persons in our employ who would have access to these cards, and have
opportunities of taking them--some persons have been charged with it, and
have been found guilty.
Cross-examined. Q. Is there a mark of Messrs. Dickinson's on the cover ?
4. Yes, just as we send them out-we sell them to the trade for them to
sell again--we sell them in London--we do not employ any agents.
