   MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE . Q.  You have dwelt a good deal upon inflam mation; assuming the injuries to have been inflicted on the Wednesday, and the death on the Saturday, in your judgment must there have been ap pearance of inflammation? A.  I think it is highly probable.
   COURT . Q.  What are the symptoms of inflammation? A.  A deposition of a pecular fibrinous matter on the surface of the pleura or the peritoneun, with an increased turgescence of the vessels of the parts—there may also be the effusion ofserous fluid as stated. BARNARD HOLT. I am surgeon at the Westminster Hospital—I have heard the evidence in this case, and the opinions that have been expressed by Mr. Partridge and Mr. Duke—I concur in those opinions—if he injuries had been inflicted on the Wednesday, I do not think it would be possible that there should have been no symptoms of them observable till the Saturday.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE . Q.  That applies to the whole of the injuries, I suppose? A.  That applies to the whole of the injuries. NOT GUILTY.
