 1517.  HENRY VENDWORTH DUNOIR was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , 1 pair of boots, value 2s., the goods of Charles Bohag.
   SUSANNAH BOHAG. I am the wife of Charles Bohag, a shoemaker- we live in Tothill-street. On the 29th of June, about ten minutes to nine o'clock at night, I saw the prisoner put his foot into the shop, reach up, break the string, and take the shoes off a nail; I followed, took him about five yards from the house, and brought him back -I did not know him before.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not offer to set me at liberty if I paid 2s. for them? A. Yes, I thought it would save me the trouble of coming here.
 DANIEL LYONS. I am a Policeman, and received the prisoner in charge.(Property produced and sworn to)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he was intoxicated.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 Before Mr. Recorder.
 1518.  ELIZABETH ROSE was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of June , 4 table-cloths, value 9s.; 1 candlestick, value 6d.; 1 pair of shoes, value 3s.; 1 shift, value 2s.; 1 holster, value 6d.; 1 sheet, value 2s., and 2 spoons, value 4s., the goods of James Partridge, her master.
   ANN ELIZABETH PARTRIDGE. I am the wife of James Partridge; we keep a tavern in  Paul-street, Finsbury. The prisoner was three or four months in our service, during which time we lost the articles stated in the indictment - she was apprehended on the 3rd of June, at my house, on suspicion, and all the articles produced at Worship-street.
   JOHN BATEMAN. I am a journeyman to Mr. Johnson, of Providence-row, Finsbury, a pawnbroker. On the 4th of April I took a table-cloth in pledge for 3s., in the name of Mary Jones, from the prisoner - I am certain of her person; on the 20th of April, a table-cloth was pawned in the same name, but not in my presence; on the 3rd of May I took in another table-cloth from her, in the name of Eliza Jones, from 6d. - on the 5th of May she pawned a candlestick for 6d., and another table-cloth for 6d.; I have a holster-case, pawned by her on the 24th of May for 4d., a holster, on the 1st of June, for 2s. 6d., a child's shift, on the 2nd of June, for 6d., a pair of woman's shoes, on the 2nd of June, for 1s. 6d., and on the 3rd of June she offered a glass goblet to pawn, with Partridge's name on it - I asked how she came by it; she said it belonged to hereself - I sent for Mr. Partridge; she then made her escape from our shop.
   WILLIAM PAWLEY. I am a shopman to Mr. Attenborough, of Crown-street, I have a tea-spoon, pawned in the name of Ann Jones - I do not know who by; also another spoon and shirt, which I did not take in.
   ELIZABETH PAYNE. I came into Mr. Partridge's service immediately after the prisoner, and found some duplicates in a glove, in a cupboard up stairs - I delivered them to mistress.
   THOMAS WHITNEY. I am a constable. I apprehended the prisoner at Partridge's house, the Paul's Head; she had gone home hereself - Partridge gave me this goblet.
   JOHN BATEMAN. This is the goblet she offered me.
   JAMES BROWN. I am an officer. On the 16th of June the prosecutor brought me a glove, containing twenty-one duplicates, twelve of which relate to the articles produced; some are for wearing apparel, which are not claimed.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 MR. PARTRIDGE. I have the duplicates - they are all in the name of Jones.
 JOHN BATEMAN. They are the duplicates of these pledges.
     GUILTY. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1519.  MARY ROBINSON and MARY CORBYN were indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 sovereign, 2 half-crowns, and 1 shilling, the monies of Patrick Dunn , from his person.
 PATRICK DUNN. I work at the Trinity-house, as  ballast-man. On Sunday, morning, the 26th of June, about nine o'clock, I went into the tap-room at the White Hart, Ratcliff-highway, to look for some ballast-heavers; I had been in bed all night - I was alone; my money was in my trousers pocket; the prisoners were strangers - I laid my head on the table, and fell asleep; while I was asleep the money was taken out of my pocket - I did not see the prisoners there - I am sure it was safe when I fell asleep.
 Corbyn. Q. Did you count your money? A. I counted it about two minutes before I laid my head down - there were a good many men and women in the house; I might have been seen to count it.
   ELLEN SULLIVAN. I went into the White Hart with a friend about nine o'clock, to have a pint of beer; Dunn sat in a box opposite me, reading a book - I saw the prisoners sitting in the next box to me; when Dunn fell asleep they went over to where he was sitting - I was not in the house above five minutes; nobody but Dunn and the prisoners were in that box - I saw Robinson sit down by his side, and put her band into his pocket, while Curbyn stood before her to conceal her: they did not stop five minutes - he was asleep when they left the house; I had not seen him count his money - I told a man who was in the box with me, that I would go over and tell him; he advised me to have nothing to do with it, and I did not tell - when Dunn missed his money, he came over and asked if I had seen any body pick his pocket; I pointed Robinson out, who stood at the tap-room door - he went over, and told her if she would give him part of it back, he would say nothing about it; she said, "Go and look for your money," and then he gave her in charge.
 Robinson. Q. Did you not see me sitting in the same box as your friend? A. No: when Robinson was taken I went home - Corbyn was taken in about a quarter of an hour; I am a char-woman.
