 2561. HENRY LEADBURY was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of July , 1 pair of trowsers, value 4 s  . 6 d  .; 1 waistcoat, 1 s  .; and 1 handker chief, 6 d  .; the goods of Joseph Maybank . JOSEPH MAYBANK. I do not know the prisoner—I never sent him for my trowsers, waistcoat, and handkerchief. SUSANNAH MAYBANK. I am the prosecutor's wife—we live in Creek-street, Deptford. On the 21st of July, the prisoner came and told me my husband had met with a misfortune, and had sent him for some clothes—I believed his story, and gave him a waistcoat, a pair of trowsers, and a hand kerchief I am sure he is the man.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  You never saw the man before? A.  No—it was on Friday he came to me, and on the Tuesday afterwards I saw him in the churchyard, and gave him into custody—he said some vitriol had been dropped on my husband's clothes—I was rather alarmed—he said my husband was not hurt—I have said that I thought the prisoner was the man.
   COURT . Q.  Are you sure he is the man? A.  Yes—I think he is—he came to me on Friday the 21st of July, at twenty minutes or a quarter before twelve o'clock. THOMAS BRYDGES. I live at No. 9, Creek-street, Deptford, four doors from Mrs. Maybank. On the 21st of July, a man who I think was the pri soner, came to my place, from a quarter past eleven to half-past—he asked if there was a man named Chuck, who drove a cart—I said there was an old man—he said he did not want the old man—he wanted the young one.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you hear that Mrs. Maybank had been imposed on by somebody? A.  Not till I came home at night. WILLIAM AYRES. On Friday, the 21st of July, I was at my master's yard in Cooper's-lane—two or three minutes' walk from Creek-street—about twelve o'clock the prisoner came and asked if a man worked there named Chuck—I said, "No"—he said he did not want the old man, he wanted the young man—he asked me if he was at home—I told him no—he asked his name and where he lived—I said his name was Maybank, and he lived the third house down Creek-street—I am sure the prisoner is the man.
   Cross-examined. Q.  That was the first time you saw him? A.  Yet—I did not see him again until he was in custody—I heard, about two hours after I saw the man, that a trick had been played.
   MR. HORRY called MARIA ROBERTS. I live at No. 47, James-street, Camberwell New-road. My husband is a salesman—the prisoner and his wife lodge in my house—on Friday, the 21st of July, he was at home till twelve o'clock—I had known him for about a month previous—he was taken into custody on the Tuesday follow ing.
   COURT . Q.  How do you come to recollect Friday the 21st? A.  I have been in the habit of giving his wife washing, and I did so that day—some of the things wanted mending, and I wanted her to sit in my room and do them—she said she would rather not, for her old man was up stairs, and she wished him to go out, as she did not like to have washing about in the room when he was at home—I know this was on Friday as that is my washing day—she has always washed on Friday ever since she has been there—Mary
