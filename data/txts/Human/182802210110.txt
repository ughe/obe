 665.  CATHERINE CONNOR was indicted for stealing on the 9th of January , 1 waistcoat, value 2s.; 1 pair of boots, value 6s., and 1 piece of calico, value 1/2d., the goods of Thomas Pattison.
   MARY PATTISON. I am the wife of John Pattison - he lives in Dogget-court, Moorfields; I am an upholsteress. On the 31st of December, the prisoner came to assist me, as I was ill - she continued there, and in a few days I missed this waistcoat; I asked her about it, and she said she had seen one, and brought me an old one - two or three days afterwards I missed some other things, and these boots; I taxed her with it, and she brought me the duplicate of the boots - I never allowed her to pawn any thing.
 THOMAS PATTISON. I went with the duplicate, and got these boots and this waistcoat, which are mine.
   EDWARD PERKINS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Whitecross-street. I took these boots and waistcoat of the prisoner on the 9th of January.
 Prisoner's Defence. She was very often sending me to pawn things.
     GUILTY. Aged 53. Confined Fourteen Days.
 666.  RICHARD DENMAN and WILLIAM JILKS were indicted for stealing, on the 26th of January , 2 loaves of bread, value 1s. 6d., the goods of James Scott.
   JAMES MCINTOSH. I am a street-keeper. On the 26th of January, I saw a basket of bread on a wheelbarrow, in Devonshire-street, Mary-le-bone- I saw the two prisoners together, and Denman took two loaves out, which he put into an apron - Jilks was looking at him; I followed them to Harley-street, and there took them; Denman said he took them out of a baker's basket.
 JAMES SCOTT. I am a  baker. I left my basket of bread in Devonshire-street - I went to deliver some loaves; the officer came, and asked me if I had lost any loaves - I looked, and missed two.
 DENMAN's Defence. I had been out of work, and did it for want.
 DENMAN -    GUILTY. Aged 27.
 JILKS -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Three Weeks.
 667.  DAVID DAVY, JOHN HUMPHREYS, and WILLIAM BRANDON were indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 1 veil, value 2s., the goods of George Tolfree.
   HARRIET TOLFREE. I am the daughter of George Tolfree - he keeps a general clothes-shop in Upper Whitecross-street. On the 17th of February, about a quarter-past seven o'clock, the prisoners came to our shop - Brandon bought some bread and butter, and as soon as they were gone I saw the wire work of the window was open, which had been shut when they came in, and I missed a white lace veil from the window - I went to the door, and saw the prisoners on the other side of the way; Mr. Steel and I went over to them - I asked them for the veil, they denied having it, but Davy dropped it - we took him, he had stood close to the wire in the shop.
   WILLIAM STEEL. I live at the house. I was going home, and saw the prisoner in the shop - Tolfree called me from the back room - I went and took Davy, who dropped the veil.
 THOMAS BROAD. I am a watchman. I was called, and took Davy to the watch-house, with the veil, and next morning I took the others.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 BRANDON's Defence. I went to the shop, but I did not take the veil; while I was paying for what I had, the other two boys went out and I followed them - I did not know they had any thing.
 DAVY -    GUILTY. Aged 12.
 HUMPHREYS -    GUILTY. Aged 13.
 BRANDON -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped and Discharged.
 668.  THOMAS ELLIOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 15th February , 1 keg, value 1s., and 10 quarts of a certain compound spirit, called cloves, value 20s., the goods of Ann Hawkins Swaine and others.
   WILLIAM KENDRICK. I am a publican, and live in Sutton-street. On the 15th of February I was lodging at the Horse and Groom public-house, St. John's-street, Clerkenwell- I was looking out of the window between two and three o'clock, and saw a cart loaded near the door - the prisoner came and stood by the side, and another person who was with him got on the wheel, took a keg out of the cart, and gave it to the prisoner, who walked down Jerusalem-passage with it - he had an apron on, and looked like a porter; I pursued and brought him back with the keg - I gave him into custody.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Then the other seemed to employ the prisoner? A. They came together up to the cart - the other man took the keg from the cart.
 COURT. Q. How was the other man dressed? A. I think he had a blue coat, a black silk handkerchief, and a white hat.
   WILLIAM BALLS. I am carman to Swaine, Allport and Co. - Ann Hawkins Swaine is one of the partners - they are distillers, and live at Holborn-bridge. I was to take this keg of cloves to the Borough - I had some goods to deliver in St. John-street, and left my cart at the public-house door for two or three minutes; I am sure I was not absent more; and during that time the keg was gone - I did not see the prisoner till he was brought back with it.
   JOHN ROBINSON. I am an officer. I was coming down St. John's-square, and one or two persons were bringing the prisoner down - I took charge of him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was looking for work, and was met by the man, who asked if I wanted a job - I said, Yes; he said he had a keg to take to Spa-fields.
   WILLIAM KENDRICK. The other man was not with him when I took him.
