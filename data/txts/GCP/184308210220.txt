Hugh M'GREGOR. I am police-sergeant at Plaistow-station. On the 29th
of June, in consequence of information, I searched Barker's house - I found
nothing-I afterwards went into the privy-we had the place opened, and
emptied, and in the dirt found eleven bottles, all broken but three-I bare
the bottoms and tops-in the necks of three of these bottles I found corks,
which I have here there was some wire on the top of two of the bottles-I
searched Brockwell's house, which is next door but one to Barker's-(Barker's
is 25, Brockwell's, 27,)-I searched the privy in the same manner as Barker's
and found the paunches of two lambs, three bottles, and the neck of a fourth
there were no corks in those-they were all broken-I took Barker and
Brockwell into custody on the morning of the 6th of July, in a house in
Keate's-court, Tbrawl-street, Whitechapel-I had been searching for them be
fore, but could not see them till then-they were in bed together-I searched
the bed, and found a stock, three centre-bits, and a small chisel, between the
bed and the sacking-Barker and Brockwell used to work at Mr. Tucker's
I had been there and inquired, and could not find them there-in taking them
to the station, handcuffed together, Barker said, “You would not have found
us if you had not been told"- he also said, he supposed we had taken
them for a robbery on account of their running away, and he said, Mr. May,
my mate, seemed very merry at a poor fellow being transported—May was
with me-these are the three bottles I found-I also produce a stone ja
which I got from Mardell-I also produce some pads which I saw May take
out of a pair of stockings in Keate's-court-they are used by housebreakers to
walk lightly on the floor.
