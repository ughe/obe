 1159.  CATHARINE ARMSTRONG was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , one gown, value 7 s., the goods of James Hulme.
   THOMAS PERRY. I am apprentice to Mr. James Hulme, a pawnbroker, who lives in Museum-street, Bloomsbury. On the 26th of July the prisoner came to the shop to pledge two articles, and as she went out she took this gown down, put it under her shawl and went out quickly. I sent the boy after her and followed myself; I saw him stop her and take it from under her shawl.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1160.  ANN BRADSHAW was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , one set of coral beads, value 5 s., the goods of William Ramsay , from the person of Isaac Ramsay.
   JANE LESLEY. I live in Pitfield-street, Old-street-road. I was nursing Isaac Ramsay, I was in Shoreditch with him; three women passed me - I turned round, and saw the prisoner take the beads off his neck - I asked her for them, but she ran away. I called Stop thief! and she was stopped.
 (Property produced, and sworn to.)
   THOMAS HAYCOCK. I am a constable. I stopped the prisoner, and saw her throw the beads down.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Six Months.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1161.  JAMES JOHNSON and WILLIAM JAMES were indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , one necklace, value 14 s., the goods of Charles Ray Woods , from the person of Charles James Woods.
   HANNAH WOODS. I am the wife of Charles Ray Woods. On the 28th of July I was out with my son Charles James - Sarah Wood was carrying him - we were going through Sadler's Wells fields about half-past seven o'clock in the evening, the prisoners followed us. Johnson came up, and took the child's necklace off, I secured him. He gave his hand a sharp jerk, and I have not seen the necklace since. James ran to the place where he threw it - a gentleman held him while I fetched an officer; another officer secured James  Another boy was with them.
   THOMAS BURRIDGE. I am a constable. I saw a crowd, went up, and took James; he said he was with Johnson.
 JOHNSON -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 JAMES -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Three Months, and Whipped.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1162.  HENRY WRIGHT and JOHN MATTHEWS were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , one handkerchief, value 4 s., the goods of a certain man unknown, from his person.
   WILLIAM BIDGOOD. I am a carpenter, and live in Castle-street, Oxford-market. On the 16th of July, about nine o'clock at night, I was in the narrow part of Spring-gardens, going into the Park, and saw the prisoners together, feeling several gentlemens' pockets. They followed a gentleman along Cockspur-street, and at the corner of Pall-mall Wright took up the skirt of his coat, and took a handkerchief out. I went up, told the gentleman, and seized them both. Matthews threw the handkerchief down, and wrested himself from me; I picked it up, called Stop thief! Gillon stopped him in my sight. The gentleman claimed the handkerchief. The gang came round, I told him to follow me to the watch-house, but he did not. I do not know his name.
   JOSEPH GILLON. My father is the watch-house-keeper. I heard Bidgood cry Stop thief! saw Matthews running in the road, and secured him.
   JOHN RAYMENT. I am a smith. I saw Matthews running and seized him - on going up Church-passage he dropped a silk handkerchief.
 WRIGHT -    GUILTY. Aged 15.
 MATTHEWS -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Life.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before J. Vaillant, Esq.
 1163.  EMMA SMITH and MARY BYRNE were indicted for stealing, from the person of Henry Fielding Corfe , two 10 l., one 5 l., and three 1 l. Bank notes, his property.
   HENRY FIELDING CORFE. I keep a school at New Church, Isle of Wight. On the 6th of July I lodged at the Nag's Head, James-street, Covent-garden. About half-past eleven o'clock at night I was going to my lodgings, the prisoners accosted me in Brydges-street, and solicited something to drink. I wished them to pursue a better course, Smith said she should be very happy to do so, but was afraid her friends would not take her back. I walked with them, conversing on that subject, and then went to their lodgings in a court near the Theatre, I might have remained there three or four hours. I sat on the bed, and being particularly tired, I fell asleep - Byrne had left me with Smith. I had taken my coat off, the notes were in the pocket. I awoke about four o'clock, and missed them, Smith was then gone. I got Gooding, and we found the prisoners together in Wych-street - I am certain they are the women - we secured them, and took them to a house. As Gooding was going up stairs with Smith, something fell from her; we called for a light, and found, a half handkerchief lying on the ground, containing my notes; they have my writing on them. We charged her with dropping them; she cried, but said nothing.
