On the night of the 17th of March I was in Charles-street about half-past nine o'clock, near the house of Mr. Carr - I saw a person near his house, it was a young man - I was two or three yards from him, and I stopped and looked at him; he called me several had words, and said, if I did not go on he would kick my little guts out - that was John Ballard; I went on, and stood in the middle of the street, and saw a young man come out of Mr.Carr's area; John Ballard was at that time standing against the gateway; they crossed the road together - I have no doubt about John Ballard being the person - I had known him by sight before, and observed him particularly.
 COURT. Q. Did you observe the dress of the other man? A. Yes - he had a dark blue coat and trousers.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How old are you? A. Eleven years: I have never been in a church or a chapel in my life; I have never been taught my prayers; I know what the meaning of an oath is - if I tell a lie I shall go to hell; no one told me to say that - I have heard it from several people just before I went to the office - it was not told me by any of the officers - it was told me by a gentleman, who was a stranger to me -I did not ask him what business he had to tell me that- I have never seen him since, and had no conversation with him but that; he came into our house about seven o'clock in the morning, while I was clearing up the place - it was the day I was coming up to Hatton-garden - he asked me, whether I knew the nature of an oath? -I told him no, I did not: he said, if you tell a lie, you will go to hell; he stopped in the tap-room - he did not say, if the magistrate asked me, I was to give him that answer - the gentleman had never spoken to me in my life before - I should not know him again; no one ever told me the nature of an oath but that gentleman; I have a father living, he never told me so.
 MR. SMITH. Q. Your uncle keeps a public-house, and has a good deal to do? A. yes; we work on Sundays as as well as other days.
 COURT. Q. Was that the first day you ever heard hell mentioned? A. No; I suppose bell to be a wicked place - that person told me if I told lies I should go to that wicked place - I believe that now; I have heard about religion before that - I do not know the Commandments -I do not know my duty to God - I have lived in that public-house three years; I have known Hawkins, as living in the service of Mr. Carr - if the other young man had been Hawkins I should have known him; but I am sure it was not; when they crossed the road they went towards Brooke-street.
   SAMUEL SANDELL. I am a hair-dresser. and live at No.1, Dorrington-street, Brook's-market - I occupy the shop and parlour; there is a yard just large enough for the water-hutt, and there is a tenement there, consisting of two rooms, which is occupied by George Ballard and his wife; they have three sons, who live with them - a passage leads into their apartment; I have a door leading from my back parlour into the passage. On Tuesday night, the 17th of March, a little after ten o'clock, it might be a quarter past, I heard something fall down in the passage very heavy - it gave an alarm; I took the candle, and went out - I saw two persons; John Ballard was one - I have no doubt of him; he was sitting on a chest, and the other was standing on the step of the door with his back towards me; I said, "What is all this noise about?" Ballard told me to call his mother, and said he wanted no light - the noise I heard shook the partition: I asked Ballard who his mother was, and he said Ballard - I opened the back door, called Mrs. Ballard, and then I went into my room; the next morning I opened my door to get some water, and Mrs. Ballard said to me, "Come up stairs, and I will shew you the box that was let down last night" - she said it was let down by her son; I went up stairs, and saw a wooden box with some mouldings in it for cielings, such as plasterers use - I did not go near enough to see whether the mouldings were broken, or not; I cannot say whether it was the box which I had seen the night before - I did not examine it at all.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You would not know what sort of a noise it would make? A. No; it is not a private passage - there are a good many persons come into it: I knew John Ballard before, but I did not know him that night till he spoke, and ordered me to call his mother; I believe they have three sons and one daughter, but  John Ballard has a more manly voice than the others - the eldest of the others is about fourteen; I do not swear to him by his voice - I am not very found of dogs, but I have had a good deal to do with them.
 Cross-examined by MR. BONKIN. Q. There was another person? A. Yes, on the stop of the door - his back was towards me; my candle was lighted, and I went out directly after I heard the noise - I had not heard any person call a man at the door to help him.
 COURT. Q. You do not know that it was Ballard, except by his calling his mother? A. No - I saw his face then; I did not know him till he spoke; I have seen him before going up and down - I did not see his face; he held his head down - I took no notice that night of the face or person of the other man; his mother came down directly.
   ANDREW DOWDEN. I am a cabinet-maker. On the night of Tuesday, the 17th of March, I was with the last witness, about ten o'clock; I heard a great noise in the passage - it seemed as if all the partition was coming down; what it was I cannot tell - I went into the passage along with Sandell; I saw two young men - one was sitting on the box, and the other standing on the step of the door; I was not near the box- I was behind Mr. Sandell: I was not near enough to see what sort of a box it was - the one on the box said to Mr.Sandell, with an oath, "Go in, for I want no light here;" he asked him to call his mother, and Sandell asked him what his mother's name was - he said Mrs. Ballard; Sandell called her and when she came down she asked him for a light; he did not give her one, as the young man said he wanted no light there; I did not notice who they were.
   JAMES OSHORNE.
