Cross-examined by Mr. F. E. Smith. I do not know the time the
cheque for £100, dated April 19, was presented. The Middlesex
Banking Company, through whom it was paid, is not a clearing house
bank; it would come through the Metropolitan Bank. It was drawn
on the 15th and paid on the 17th. Probably it would have got to the
Middlesex Bank on the 16th and be presented by them to the London
Joint Stock Bank on the 17th. The proceeds would get back to the
Middlesex Bank on the same day, the 17th. Assuming the cheque
got to us about 12 o'clock, it might have got to the Middlesex Bank
directly after they received it if it came by post. They would pay
it into the Middlesex Bank at 10 or 11 o'clock. With regard to all
the Middlesex Bank cheques which are held by the Metropolitan,
directly they are cleared in the hands of the Metropolitan the Metro
politan would communicate to the Middlesex that their balance is so
and so. At the close of business on the 17th they would know every
thing was paid.

JOSEPH PERCIVAL Hudson, clerk in the Bank Note Department of
the Bank of England. I produce sex £50 bank notes, 97427 to 97432.
No. 97431 bears the stamp of the Finsbury Pavement Post Office.
On the back of Nos. 97428-9-30 there is the endorsement "W. Tyler,
54, Old Broad Street, E.C." These notes were cashed at the Bank
of England into gold, 97,428 on April 24 and 97,429-30 on April 26.
The handwriting of the three endorsements is, in my judgment, the
same. No. 97432, endorsed "W. Tyler," was cashed into gold on
April 25. The handwriting is different. When a note is cashed
into gold at the Bank of England it is usual to ask the person to
endorse it, or one of a series cashed at the same time.
