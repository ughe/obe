He acknowledged having put the galloon and shoes into the box, on the way to the station-house, before Ayletbury had him in custody—he acknowledged it at the station-house also. JOHN TOBY. I made these boots, and make for a great number of people—I have sold the same sort and size as these to Mr. Wallis; but I cannot swear to having sold him these in particular—I went into Wallis's shop at the time the large box was brought down—the prosecutor was rather con fused, and I unlocked the small box—the prisoner having given me the key; he owned to putting the shoes in, but not the boots—on the road to the station-house, he admitted putting the boots into his trowsers—Willis asked him how long ago he had stolen them—he said he could not tell—I asked him whether it was one week or three—he said, he supposed it was two or three.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you not sold dozens of that sort to other people? A.  Yes; but not in the neighbourhood—the prosecutor and I are friendly—he has paid me a good deal of money.
   Prisoner's Defence , I have a witness to  prove he put them into my box. MARY ANN GOODING. I am married. I lodge in Wallis's house, in the next room to where the prisoner slept—I was in the house the day his boxes were brought down stairs—I saw Mr. Wallis in the boy's room that day—he did not remain there more than a minute or two—the door was open—it is not two yards from my room—I saw him settling something is the box—his hands were in the box, and he seemed settling something—it was only a momentary glance—the lid of the box was open—I did not see the boxes down stairs—it seemed a small dirty white box—I am positive it was open—I saw Wallis with his hands in it, at the side of the bedstead—the room is large enough to hold a bedstead, and no more—the box was on the side of the bedstead—in a few minutes afterwards, I saw the boy and Mr. Wallis running up stairs—the boy said, somebody had been at his box—I think Wallis was in the room when he said that, because I heard his voice—I went out on the stairs, and said to the boy, "Why, Mr. Wallis has;" thinking that the boy might think it was an impertinence of ours, our room being so near—I said this in Wallis's presence, for fear the pri soner should think that any of my family had taken the liberty of going into his room—there had been no quarrel between Mr. Wallis and me, till that day—I worked for him, and my husband worked for him—we have been very good friends till that day—and that day Wallis came up and asked me what business I had to tell the boy he had been to his box, and he denied it; but I stuck to it ever since that he was at the box.
   COURT . Q.  As you say there was only room for the bed in the room was the box against the bed? A.  No; on the bed—I am not mistaken about Wallis being at the box—I saw the box open—I could not see the contents of it—I was standing on the landing, opposite the door—I cannot say I saw the lid; but I saw the box open—I did not see the inside of the lid—I saw part of the lid in a position to satisfy me it was open—I am not mistaken—I saw the box open, and his hand in it.
