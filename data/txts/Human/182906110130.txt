 1296.  WILLIAM WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 1 waistcoat, value 3s.: 1 shawl, value 5s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 2s.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s.; and 1 purse, value 1s., the goods of John Prigmore.
 JOHN PRIGMORE. I am a  tailor. The prisoner was my apprentice; he had been twelve months with me; he absconded during the absence of myself and wife, on the 1st of May, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning - I saw him, at half-past six next morning, playing at pitch and toss, in Elizabeth-street, Whitechapel - I had missed these articles, and a good many others; when I took him, he said he had sold the waistcoat and shawl -I found on him a purse, containing 4s. 5d. and two knives - he said nothing.
   JOHN NORRIS. I am an officer. The prosecutor gave the prisoner to me; his friends live near me, and his master thought he would come home; he found him playing with some sweeps - this purse and two new knives were found on him.
 JOHN PRIGMORE. This is my purse.
 Prisoner. Forgive me this time; I will never do the like again.    GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1297.  JAMES WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of May , 1 live tame fowl, price 7s., the property of Robert Tyler.
 ROBERT TYLER. I am a  labourer, and live at Stanley Cottage, King's-road. I put my fowl in possession of Mr. Williams two months ago - it was a game cock; I heard it was stolen on the night of the 23d of May, and saw it at Queen-square; I know it by its feathers, and there are two or three feathers cut off at the front of the wing.
 JOHN WILLIAMS. I had the fowl to take care of six weeks or two months before it was stolen - it was safe on Saturday, the 23d of May, and I missed it the next morning; the prisoner is a  butcher- I do not know exactly where he lives; this is the fowl.
 GEORGE UPWARD. I am an officer. I took this fowl from the prisoner on the 24th of May, between four and five o'clock in the morning; he said it was his own - I was not satisfied, and took him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to Chelsea on the Saturday before, and saw a man with this fowl; I gave him 4s. for it - I was going to my employer's, Mr. Chorley, of Putney, when I was taken.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined Two Months.
 1298.  ALFRED WILKS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of May , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of a certain man whose name is unknown, from his person.
   HENRY YATES. I am a porter. I was in Nassau-street on the 25th of May, about one o'clock; I saw the prisoner and another person go up to a gentleman - the prisoner took this handkerchief from his pocket; I had my knot on my shoulder; I ran and knocked him down - he got up and ran again, I pursued and took him; I had fifty or sixty people round me - this is the handkerchief.
 Prisoner. Q. Were not you a Police-officer? A. Yes, for ten years; I lost my situation through not sending for a surveyor to inspect some goods at the London Docks.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was walking down the street, and a young man dropped this handkerchief at my feet and ran away.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Two Months.
 1299.  HENRY SELWOOD. I went home with this woman to her house in  Westminster; I had two 5l. notes, seven sovereigns, one guinea, and one half-guinea, besides some silver, a watch, seals and key; I was not drunk, but very tired and sleepy - she asked if I was going to give her something to drink; I gave her two half-crowns - she fetched some gin, and I had a little glass of it; when she came in with the gin she locked the door, I said "Don't lock the door as I must be off;" I fell asleep in the chair, and when I awoke she was gone, the door was open, my watch and my money were gone; I am sure she is the person - she stopped me in the road, and enticed me with her.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you swear you were not tipsy? A. I was not; I had been out two nights looking after my master's barges, and met her in Tothill-street - she was the only woman who caught hold of me; I had been talking with another woman, but not stopping with any other; the other woman asked me if I was going to give her any thing to drink - she walked about the street with me, but I took up with this woman; I suppose I should have gone to sleep by myself if this woman had not caught hold of me - it was Duck-lane we went to; I cannot say what number; the officer told me the name - I did not tell the Magistrate I was intoxicated - I said I had been working two days, and up two nights, and I had had a glass or two; the last publichouse I was in was the Crown; it is in Westminster - we had a quartern of gin and a pint of beer between another man and myself - I left there before ten o'clock; that was all I drank besides the glass of gin the prisoner gave me - I had been on board the Fame, where I am employed; I left about nine o'clock - I had been in a public-house in the morning; we had a pot of beer there between three or four of us; I drank no gin or spirits
