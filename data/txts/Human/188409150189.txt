   Cross-examined.  When I said "How came the blood on your shirt," you said "Mrs. Gorman hit me with something." ELLEN LLOYD( Examined by the Prisoner ). I spoke to you first, I asked who scratched your face and you said "Hush"—you pulled off your coat and showed me the blood in front of your shirt. LOUISA PATEY. I am single, and live at No. 1, Royal Tent Court—I remember Rosina Squires going but with the prisoner about half-past 7 on the morning of this occurrence—the prisoner came, back at 2 o'clock—the prosecutrix came back about 4 o'clock, and went upstairs—after that I heard quarrelling about money—she said, "Give me my money,' and he said, "I will crack your neck"—soon after that somebody told me something, and I went up into their room and found the woman with her throat cut and bleeding—before that It saw the prisoner go down the court with a bundle in his hand—he came home again about 4 o'clock next morning, and I went for a constable and gave him in custody. JAMES FARRER( Policeman M  186). On the evening of 2nd july, about a quarter past 6, I was called to the prisoner's house by James Saxton—I went upto the top room front, and sent the prosecutrix there lying near a pool of blood—I at once sent for Dr. Bedford to attend to her, and she was removed in an ambulance to the hospital—I saw Elizabeth Gorman in the room—she was examined before the Magistrate—she was in bed asleep when I went into the room; I awoke her; "she appeared to be drunk—I saw this razor in the room, and took possession of it; there was wet blood on it. ROBERT STEELE( Policeman M  132). On the morning of 3rd July, about 4 o'clock, the witness Patey came and spoke to me—I went to the corner of the court, and saw the prisoner standing there—I said, "I want you"—he, said,' "Don't be in a hurry, old man, I will go with you"—I said, "I want you for cutting a woman's throat last night"—he made no reply—I took him to the station, and after the charge was read over to him he said, "I shan't deny what I have done"—he was wearing two shirts; they were both covered with blood, the right wristband more particularly—I was before the Magistrate, and heard Elizabeth Gorman examined as a witness—the prisoner had the opportunity of cross examining her—I went to St. Saviour's Infirmary, walworth, last Tuesday, and there saw Gorman ill in bed—she seemed to be very ill indeed—Mr. Grose, the medical superintendent, attended her.
