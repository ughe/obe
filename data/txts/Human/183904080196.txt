 1403. ELIZABETH COLCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of March , 2 sovereigns, the monies of James Rayner . MARY RAYNER. I am the wife of James Rayner, of Nelson-street, Bethnal-green. On the 15th of March I asked the prisoner's parents to let her come and stay with my children while I went to the doctor's, which she did—I had in my drawer a purse containing nine sovereigns—I left the key in the drawer, and when I came back, I sent the prisoner home—on that day week I missed 2 sovereigns—I went to the prisoner at her mother's, and asked her if she had taken them—I said it might be better for her if she owned to it—no one else could have taken it. WILLIAM PRICE. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner—I asked her if she knew the difference between a sovereign and a shilling—she said she did, and then she told me, voluntarily, that she took the two sovereigns home to her mother. NOT GUILTY.
 1404. GEORGE BEDFORD was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of April , 1 coat, value 4 l  ., the goods of John Stewart . WILLIAM UPTON. I am in the employ of Mr. John Stewart, of Cheap-side. On the 12th of April I had a coat thrown across the block in the shop—I saw the prisoner, about eight o'clock in the evening, walk into the shop, take it off the block, and walk out immediately—he ran and I ran, and called "Stop thief"—I never lost sight of him—he was stopped in Honey-lane-market—he dropped the coat—it was taken up and given to me—this is it—I am sure the prisoner is the person who took it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was returning home that day, and seeing the coat, I thought I could make a trifle of it, to make up my things, which I had lost—I was half groggy  at the time. GUILTY.— Confined One Month.
   OLD COURT .— Wednesday, April  17 th,  1839.
   Fourth Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arobin.
