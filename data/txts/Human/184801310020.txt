   MESSRS. BODKIN and ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. JANE CUTMORE. I am the wife of William Cutmore, a baker, of Grafton-street, Soho. On Sunday morning, 17th Jan., the prisoner came for a half quartern loaf—she gave me a half-crown—I gave her 2 s . 2 1/2 d .—I put the half-crown with the pound's worth of silver that I had taken the change from—there was no other half-crown there—it remained there till the evening—I then looked at it, it was bad—I took it from the pounds worth of silver the next morning, expecting she would come again—I gave it at last to the policeman—On Monday, the prisoner came for half a quartern of flour—she gave me half-a-crown—I took it up, it was bad—I asked her if she was aware she was passing bad money—she said, no she was not, she had it given her—T told her she had passed one on the Sunday—she denied it first, and then acknowledged it, and hoped I would forgive her—I sent for Peisley, and gave him the two half-crowns. SOLOMON PEISLEY( policeman, C  92.) I was sent for, and took the pri soner—I produce the two half-crowns. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. These are both counterfeit, and cast in the same mould.
   Prisoner's Defence.  A gentleman gave me the half-crowns; I did not know they were bad. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury. Confined Four Months .
