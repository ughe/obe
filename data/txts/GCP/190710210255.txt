Mr. Muir stated that this Exhibit was found at No. 2, Tokenhouse
Buildings.
Witness (continuing) Exhibit 217 is a letter purporting to be
written by Perryman on notepaper headed, “2, Tokenhouse Build
ings, April 23, 1907," to Messrs. Pope and Westlake, offering to sell
founders' shares in the Fullers Earth Union at £125 net, for reply
by return. Exhibits 219, 220, 223, and 224 are letters relating to
the same matter, Exhibit 224 being a cheque with an endorsement
on the back in Perryman's own handwriting in his own name. I do
not recognise Exhibit 225 as being in Perryman's handwriting. It
is signed "p.p. O. W. Perryman." "G. H." it looks like. The letter
of May 2, with no year appearing on it, but which was this year,
1907, is a letter from Perryman to our bank requesting our bank to
hand the bearer of the letter a cheque book and Perryman's pass book.
This request was complied with, the bearer of the letter giving
receipt for them. This letter, to the best of my belief, is in the same
handwriting as Exhibit No. 225, md it is signed in the name of "c.
George." I did not see the person who got that cheque book. 0.
George" is in the same writing as "G. H." on the other letter. (To
Mr. Justice Lawrence.) The body of this letter is in Perryman's
handwriting. (The documents were handed to the Jury) (To Mr.
Muir.) I only know Harris by sight. I have seen him in our bank-
the London City and Midland Bank, Queen Victoria Street, where
Perryman has his account. I have seen Harris in the bank while
I have been at the counter, and I have received credits from him
to the credit of Perryman's account. I have not seen him often, as I
am not always at the counter ; he may have gone to any of the other
counters. The credits I received from Harris for Perryman's se-
count were latterly, I should think, in gold, the amounts ranging up
to £100; the greater part of it was new gold invariably.
Mr. Marshan Hall stated that he understood the witness had been
recalled on the question of handwriting on certain documents, and
he had had no notioe of any further evidence.
Cross-examined by Mr. Marshall Hall. I have never seen Perry-
man write. I have been at the bank 15 years, and I have seen Perry
man's signature a lot. While I have been at the bank my only con-
tact with Perryman's writing was the honouring of cheques drawn
by himself, and perhaps an occasional letter such as the order for
the cheque book, which, of course, I never saw actually written, but
which I believe to be in Perryman's writing. With reference to the
papers I have picked out, they might correctly be described as
“picking needles out of a bundle of hay." In my opinion, they are
