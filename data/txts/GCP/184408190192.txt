JAMES KENT. I am a farrier-I was in the prosecutor's employ. One
morning, I cannot remember the day,) my master came to the shop with a
man named Norris—he called me from the fire at the other side of the shop
where I was working, and pulled this pair of shoes out of his pocket, and
said, "Do you know anything of these, Jem!"-I said, “They look very
much like your make"-he said, “You can swear to them?"- I said I could
not-he then pulled out a crooked horse-shoe-I said, “That looks like
Homewood's make"-he said, "You can swear to this?" I said, "No"
then went to my fire, and he said, "Put a private mark on them," which I
did-we went out into the back-yard, and called Homewood out of the house
-the prosecutor presented this pair of poney-shoes, and said, “Do you know
anything of these shoes, Homewood ?"-he said, "No, I do not”- the prose-
cutor turned round from him, and said, “It is my property, you have robbed
me of them"-Homewood denied it, and said he had not-Norris did not say
anything--the prosecutor told me to fetch a policeman-I did not go, and
then he sung out to a man who was working in the shop, named Bill, and
said, “Go and fetch a policeman," but some one called bim back--the pro-
secutor was then going away, and Homewood said to Norris, "Just call him
back, and we will tell him the whole truth"-he called him back-the prose-
cutor turned round, and Homewood said, "Now, master, don't be spiteful;
come here, I will tell you all about it; I lent these few pairs of shoes to this
man, till such time as he got his place up, and then he will pay you again"-
I did not hear Homewood say anything to Norris more than I have said, that
I am confident of.
John Bovis (police-constable R 139.) On the 11th of July I went with
the prosecutor to Norris's shop- the prosecutor took down four pairs of aboes
which were hanging on a nail, and said they were his property -Norris did
not say anything the prosecutor said he should give Norris in charge 1
said to Norris, "Do you know what you are charged with?” he said, "I
should like to know"-I said, “You are charged with receiving these shoes,
knowing them to be stolen"-he said, "Very well."
HENRY DUNFORD. I am an auctioneer at Greenwich. On the 11th of
July I saw the prosecutor and Norris--the prosecutor said to him, "You
have got some of my property in your shop, I saw them"-Norris said he had
not, he had nothing there but his own property-the prosecutor said he had,
and then Norris said, "There is such a thing as a pair of shoes in the shop,
bought them of Homewood."
Cross-examined. Q. I believe you were not before the Magistrate ? A.
No-Norris was my tenant for two years.
