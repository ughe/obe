I do not know what shop it is to be- it is not open yet-there is no conve-

had not looked into the box from the time I put them there till I went to

ever knew of any being put there-Roe came to search the house when
Mr. Solomon was taken into custody--that was about a week or ten days
afterwards--I knew what he came to search for.
Q. How came you, being an honest young man and a Christian not to
conduct them up stairs, and say, "Here is what you are searching for?" A.
Because I did not think proper-I certainly do not think it would have
been honest to do so—it was given into my custody, and I did not think I
bad a right to give it to the officers—I saw the gold dust come to the house,
and I had suspicions--I saw it melted and alloyed so as to disguise it-I
then took it away, and concealed it in a box in the loft--there were stairs
leading to that loft-I saw the officers come to search for that gold, but
told them nothing about it-afterwards, when I was requested, I told Les
where it was, and conducted him to it, I gave it up at Mr. Solomon's re-
quest-Roe was not there at that time—the box was precisely in the same
situation as where I had left it-the door of the loft was not locked or se-
cured in any way.
COURT. Q. You say you made a mistake, and desired to be corrected,
on what day was that mistake made? A. The first time I was examined,
on the 7th of May, in the private room-I went there from the Haymarket,
where I was concealed-Mr. Abrahams came to me there a few days pre-
vious to my going before the Magistrate, and it was then I made the
statement, which he took down-Mr. Abrahams came to me a week ago to
leave the deposition.
Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. I do not understand you rightly
about Mr. Abrahams; you gave him a statement ? A. Yes, and told him
what I could prove--that statement was taken down in writing by him
it was that statement that was read over to me in the private room at
Lambeth-street office-Mr. Hardwick asked me whether that statement
was true or not-I stated that it was true I was then taken into the
public office, and sworn to it, and was examined to it at the public office in
the presence of all-to the best of my belief, Mr. Coombe, the Magistrate,
was there also.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Now, in the private room did the magistrate or
any body else put any question whatever to you, or was any thing more
done than your statement handed in by Mr. Abrahams, read over to you, and
you asked if it was true? A. Nothing whatever that was the statement
which came from my lips--nobody suggested a single word to me on the
subject-I did not, at the time I made it, know any fact that Mr. Solomon
had said or was to say- I did not know at that time whether Mr. Solo-
mon was to be examined as a witness or not, I have never seen Mr.
Solomon since that time to have any conversation with him the plan
produced does not faithfully represent the situation of the furnaces.
