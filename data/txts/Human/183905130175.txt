   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You had passed the day at Mitcham, had you? A.  No—I cannot say what time I got down there—I was sober when I got there—I left town by the coach about two, or three, or four o'clock—I cannot say what time—I do not know how far Mitcham is from London—I never went there before—I went to meet my friend there—it was a gentleman—I never mention any friend's name—I met him at a friend's house there—it does not signify to yon whose house—he took me to a place—I do not know the place—I went to have something to drink there—it was a public-house—I call that a friend's house—I do not know the sign—I was to meet him not far from the cricket-ground, and did so—I knew the cricket-ground, at I was there once before, and saw them play at cricket—I did not say I had never been to Mitcham before—I have been there once, but never but once—that was about a week before—I was not with a friend then—I met my friend this time at the first inn, opposite the cricket-ground, on the right-hand side—I had seen him in town two days before, and promised to meet him at Mitcham—he mentioned the first pub lic-house opposite the cricket-ground—it was there I got a little drink—I did not dine there—I dined at home—I was very much in liquor, very sick, and very ill—I am a gay woman, that is the truth—I cannot recol lect any body but Clarke coming in the coach with me—he awoke me, asked if I was cold, and covered me over—whether he came in more than once, or whether any body else came in, I do not know—he might come more than once, and not awake me—I am not aware that I said when I was taken up at Tooting, that I had no money, and could not get home, and wanted a ride—I will not swear I did dot say so—I made no agreement with him—I only saw two persons on the coach when they took me up, one tall and one shorter—the shorter one is not here, I suppose, but I do not know—Clarke is the only one I know—I saw Jackson when I got off the coach-box—he was not the person on the box when I got in—I never saw him till I got out.
   COURT . Q.  Then, was the person sitting with Clarke, a different per son to Jackson? A.  I did not notice his face—I do not know whether he is the second man or not—my foot was not cut or scratched.
