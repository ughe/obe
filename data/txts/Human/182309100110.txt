Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. You understand all-fours, don't you - A. No; very little; but they said play how I would, I was sure to win.
 Q. You laid your money into the hat, to be won or lost as the game should go - A. Yes; I lost the game.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1276.  MARY EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , two half-crowns, the monies of John Steins , from his person.
   JOHN STEINS. I am a journeyman tailor. On the 4th of August, between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, I was walking in the Hampstead-road; the prisoner came up and asked me for some halfpence - she had two little girls with her. She followed me a long while, and said they had nothing to eat - and I said I had no halfpence. I put my hand into my pocket, pulled out my money - I had some silver and copper together; the prisoner snatched two half-crowns out of my hand and ran off. I followed and overtook her in five minutes, without losing sight of her; she had not ran far; I took one half-crown from her hand, but could not find the other. I did not see her searched, but the other was found upon her - I was sober - I knew nothing of her before.
   CHARLES COUSINS. I am an officer. I searched the prisoner, and found the half-crown in her hand. She said he gave it her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never said any such thing. GUILTY. Aged 15.
 Strongly Recommended to Mercy. - Judgment Respited.

 1278.  JOHN COX was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of September , a glass domb, value 6 s. the goods of James Smeehart.
   HORATIO TAYLOR. I am shopman to Mr. James Smeehurst, lamp-manufacturer, New Bond-street. The prisoner was in his service.
   THOMAS CLEMENTS. I am an officer. On the 2d of September, a little past nine o'clock at night, I was in Bond-street, and saw the prisoner carrying a basket. I stopped him, and asked what he had got. After some hesitation, he said a glass domb; that he was going to do a job for himself with it; on our way to the office, he said he would tell me the truth; that he worked for Mr. Smeethart, in Bond-street, and took the domb to force a few plants in his garden.
   HORATIO TAYLOR. I cannot swear to the domb - we have so many we could not miss it.
 Cross-examined by MR. ALLEY. Q. How many men have you - A. Five; he was eleven years with us, and was never allowed to take things off the premises.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1279.  ANN PENNY was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , a child's pincloth, value 15 d., the goods of Elizabeth Holroyd.
   ELIZABETH HOLROYD. I live in Bluegate-fields. I lost a child's pinafore. On the 29th of August, I put it in the yard to dry, between two and three o'clock in the day. I missed it about six, and have never found it since.
   ELIZA SIMPSON. I was sitting at work opposite the prosecutrix's house, and saw the prisoner take the pinafore off the line, and come out with it - she rolled it up - I heard of the robbery afterwards, and told what I knew.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went into this house at five o'clock, and stopped till eight, when a person came and told me what I was charged with. I immediately went to the prosecutrix; she fell upon me and tore my bonnet off my head - I told her to send for an officer - she sent to my husband, and said, if he would give her 5 l., she would make it up, and he gave her 1 l. The officer saw her strike me.
   ELIZABETH HOLROYD. I never lifted my hand against her, or spoke to her husband about anything of the kind.
   STEPHEN CADMAN. I am an officer - I did not see her strike her, or ever hear that she had.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1280.  THOMAS WICKS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , a watch, value 30 s., the goods of Thomas Rigby , from his person.
   THOMAS RIGBY. I live in the New-road, St. George's. On the 17th of August, about half-past nine o'clock, I was in Whitechapel-road; I had a child in my arms, and my housekeeper by my side. I passed a doctor's shop, at the corner of North-street- the prisoner gave me a shove and turned me right round - there was a gas-light at the corner, and at the doctor's shop also - he came from behind, and shoved me, turned me right round, and snatched my watch from my fob, and ran up North-street - I pursued, calling out Stop thief! he was not taken then - I saw him in custody four days after, and cannot swear to him, as I only saw his back.
 Cross-examined by MR. ALLEY. Q. Did you recover your watch - A. Yes; it was sent to me a week or ten days after.
   SARAH RIGBY. I was in company with Mrs. Rigby, walking on his left side - he was carrying the child. I noticed a man following us, and looked round several times, thinking he knew us - he was alone. At the corner of North-street, he came behind us and gave Mr. Rigby a shove on the left shoulder, I did not see him snatch the watch - he ran down North-street; an alarm was given. Mr. Rigby ran after him - I took the child from him, and another person came behind and threw him down - I saw the prisoner in custody on the 22d - there was a gas light from the shop, and one from the corner - I believe him to be the man, and am sure that he is.
 Cross-examined. Q. You never knew him before - A. No; I never fixed on any other - I was coming out of a friend's house in North-street, and Mr. Rigby said,
 "This looks like the man who tripped me up." I said,
 "It is not him, for he was a great deal taller." I described him to the officers, and when I saw the prisoner, I was certain of him.
   GEORGE ELLIS. I am an officer. Mr. and Mrs. Rigby gave me a description of the men on the Monday after the robbery, and in consequence of the woman's description, I apprehended the prisoner on the Wednesday following. I knew him before. The woman never hesitated about him.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
