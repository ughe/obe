in my old age, and I will tell you exactly how matters stand. The pro-

ago at Hoxton. He had another wife; she is dead. Her children are
in Paris, and are brought up by the Jewish Institution. Jaffe had the
money furnished him by a man named Posnianoski. The latter was
manager to Meyer at Berlin, who committed suicide 19 years ago. At that
time I became acquainted with Jaffe; I was introduced to him accidentally
in a warehouse in Commercial Street, E. He asked me how long I had
been in England. I told him since 1851. He said "Then you are an
old Englishman; then you ought to know England well by this time.'
He took me into a public-house called Welb's, and treated me with drink
and cigars. Then he said. If you could find a trustworthy person who
could keep a secret, but he must be an engraver.' I said I happen to
know some one, and I believe he is a genuine man, but I am afraid to
ask him such things of that description; it would be an insult. Then
please let me know the nature of the case, and I will then endeavour to
feel the pulse whether the party will consent to do anything of that kind.'
