Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  You were quite peaceable, en devouring to keep peace? A.  I do not know—I had not been in there hardly any time at all—I believe I was quite peaceable—I had nothing to do with the party—I was quite peaceable at the time I fetched the other man out of the parties' hands—I will swear I was quite peaceable till some words occurred—I did not strike any body in the face—I am work ing on the rail-road—I never prosecuted any one else for a similar charge—I do not know the Northumberland Arms in Charles-street—I have been once to Mathew's house—I never made any charge against anybody out of that house—I was never a witness before at any criminal Court—I did not strike anybody that night to my knowledge—I was not sober—I will swear, to the best of my knowledge, that I did not strike anybody that night—I am quite satisfied I did not—I believe not—not to my knowledge.
   COURT . Q.  Did you strike Roberts? A.  I did not, nor James.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Are you sure of that? A.  Yes; nor anybody else to my knowledge—that is all the answer I can give—I did not strike either of the prisoners—I cannot be positive about other persons—I did not strike a man in the face to my knowledge—I do not know a man named Cord went to my knowledge ( looking at him )—I have seen that man—he was there that night—I did not strike him to my knowledge—there was a lot coming out of there—there was a scuffle at the door—I was in it, going out at the door—I was not taking any part in it.
   Q.  Will you account for the reason you will not give me an equally positive answer about striking the man in the face? A.  There was such a quantity of people at the door, one shoving one way, and another another. I cannot be positive how I might put up my hand—I cannot swear whether I gave a man knock across the face with my first, but I did not to my knowledge—I did not try to toss with Cordwent, not to my knowledge—I had been drinking a little—I was not very sober—I did not challenge to loss for a sovereign.
   Q.  Did you feel in your pocket before you went out that you had no so vereign in it? A.  I did not—I did not say, as I could not give the sovereign I would toss for 10 s .—I know a man who used to belong to the place—I know that man ( looking at George )—I have known him for the last three years—I think he was there that night—I do not know Norris—I did not challenge anybody to toss for half-a-crown with me, nor call the man a b—thief when he refused—Mr. Phillips, the broker, went in with me—George did not offer me drink, nor did the prisoner Roberts—I did not call him a b—counterfeit—I did not get up from the box, and strike Roberts a violent blow in the face—I am positive about that—the landlord advised me to go out of the house—he did not tell me not to disturb the peace and good order of his house—he said the evening was getting late, and advised me to go home, because he was afraid of some disturbance—it was about twelve o'clock.
