 1522.  ELIZABETH OSBORN was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 1 looking glass, value 6s., the goods of Ann Bampton, spinster.
   JAMES HOOTON. I know Ann Bampton - she lives in West-street. On the 12th of August, between three and four o'clock, I was in her house, and saw the prisoner with several others before the door, kicking up a kind of stir - and there was a bonnet taken off the line; while Mrs. Bampton ran after that, the prisoner came and took the looking glass off the door - Bampton asked her what she was going to do with it; I cannot tell what she said; but I ran up and laid hold of the glass; she pulled it so hard I let go - her companions then came up, and she ran down a turning, and I saw no more of it.
 ANN BAMPTON. I know the prisoner by seeing her walk in the street, but am not acquainted with her. About three o'clock in the afternoon, five or six young women came to my shop, where I  sell a little furniture and wearing apparel; they took a bonnet and put it on; the prisoner then came and took the glass - I asked what she was going to do? she said, "To put a little paint on her face;" she made her way up a gateway; I have not seen it since; she knew that I knew her - I believe they did it in a lark.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1523.  CHARLES PHILLIPS was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of September , 1 card box, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Robert Gooding.
   ANN GOODING. I am sister-in-law to Thomas Robert Gooding; he is a fancy cabinet maker. On the 2d of September, between eight and nine o'clock at night, I heard my sister scream out Stop thief! I ran up stairs and saw her outside the shop door - I picked up the box outside the door - I did not see the prisoner.
   MARTHA GOODING. I was in the parlour behind the shop and heard a noise; this box had stood on the glasscase all day; I went out, and saw the back of a man - I followed, calling "Stop thief;" I missed the box which I had seen five minutes before.
   JOHN WILKIE. I am an upholsterer. I was passing Mr. Gooding's shop, in Mortimer-street, and saw the prisoner rush out of the shop, and put something on the grating - I tried to stop him, but he ran by - the watchman took him without my losing sight of him, I returned and found Mrs. Gooding fainting.
   THOMAS BOX. I am a watchman. I heard the cry of Stop thief! between eight and nine o'clock - I ran and stopped the prisoner; Wilkie came up, and he was taken back.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Twelve Months.
 1524.  JOHN REARON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of July , 1 pair of clogs, value 4s., the goods of James Ewen.
 JAMES EWEN. I am a  brush-maker, and live at the corner of North Audley-street, Grosvenor-square. On the 11th of July, between one and two o'clock in the afternoon, a lad came into the shop and said, a man had taken a pair of clogs; I ran out; a person beckoned to me, that the man had gone into the next door, where they are building a church - I ran in and took him with the clogs in his hand - I asked what he was going to do? he said, "To try them on;" he was drunk; and I think, scarcely knew what he was doing; he did not conceal them.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1525.  THOMAS RAWLINGS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 32lbs. weight of lead, value 4s., belonging to George Haddon.
 GEORGE HADDON. I have five houses in the  Willow-walk. I have not examined to see whether this lead corresponds with that on the roof - the houses have been empty since December, and are out of repair.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1526.  THOMAS SORRELL was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 hat, value 3s., and a bonnet, value 2s., the goods of William Free.
   EMILY FREE. I am the wife of William Free - we live in Montague-street, Brick-lane. On the evening of the 26th of June, I had a hat and a bonnet, tied to a chair, in my little parlour, near the window, which was open - a person might have got them by opening the blind.
   CHARLOTTE KAYE. I live in Montague-street, and was looking into this shop window, because there was a room to let; I saw a young man put the blind open, and take the hat and bonnet out; he gave it to another young man - they ran down Brown's-lane; I am not certain that it was the prisoner.
 JOHN FREE. I was sitting on the counter, and heard an alarm, ran out, and the prisoner tapped me on the shoulder, and said, "It is of no use your going that way, they are gone this way."
   ROBERT DAVIS. I am an officer. On Monday, the 26th of June, between nine and ten o'clock at night, I saw a crowd of persons round the prisoner - I took him to a public-house - Charlotte Kaye came and said, (without hesitation,) he was the man.
 CHARLOTTE KAYE. I thought so then, but I do not think so now - no one has been talking to me about it.
     NOT GUILTY.
