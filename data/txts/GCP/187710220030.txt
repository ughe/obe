CATHERINE CRASK. I am married, and live at 26, Eagle Street-on 3rd
October I was working for Mrs. Turner, and heard the prisoner come
downstairs I know him and know his voice-he went to the door
several times and said “Open the b- door"-he pushed it and went
into the room, and we heard him walk across the floor-he stayed in the
room a quarter of an hour, and then I heard him go upstairs, and my
attention was then called to smoke coming out of the room, and a little
girl came and called out, and I took some pails of water and threw over
the fire-the bed was all piled up in a heap by the fireplace and blazing
--there was a little fire in the grate but very small, not enough to have
caused the bed to catch fire-it was a dead fire, merely a few cinders-
the floor was burnt where the bed was, and also part of the fireplace,
the wainscoting.
JOHN HEALEY. I am a bootmaker, of 24, Red Lion Street-the
prisoner is my landlord-on 3rd October he came into my shop a little
before 12 o'clock and stopped there some time until he saw Mr. and Mrs.
Lintott go out, and then within a minute he went towards the kitchen,
and was absent about a quarter of an hour, and came back and sat down
in my shop again, but said nothing till some people passing saw smoke
coming from the front kitchen, and I said, “It looks as if the house was on
