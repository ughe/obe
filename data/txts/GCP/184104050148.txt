1175. MARGARET LEWIS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of
March, 1 pinafore, value Is. ; and 1 boot, value 1s.; the goods of George
Bush, from the person of George Bush, the younger.
John RAYMENT. I am a chimney-sweeper, and live in White's-alley,
Chancery-lane. About six o'clock in the evening of the 22nd of March,
I was in Chancery-lane, and saw the prisoner in company with another
woman--the prisoner was carrying a little boy, and as be passed me he
cried very much, and said, "I want to go home to my mother"--I passed
on about thirty yards, turned back, and stopped her in Cursitor-street-I
said, “Do you know that child ?" she said, "Yes"-I said, " Is it yours?"
she said, “No, but I know its mother"- she dropped the child, and
dropped a boot at the same time-the child said, "Give me my pins
fore"-I asked her if she had got the pinafore-she said no, its mother
had got it-I asked her several times she said the mother had got it-
the child turned round and said, " No, you have got it in your pocket"-
I gave her in charge, and the pinafore was found on her.
Ann Bush. I am the wife of George Bush. I do not know the pri-
soner-I did not give her my child's pinafore or boot-she had no busi-
ness with the child-this is his pinafore and boot.ru
Prisoner's Defence. The child was at the corner of White's-alley, cry
ing very much-I asked what was the matter-he said he had lost his
way–he had the pinafore in his hand-I took him in my arms, took the
pinafore in my hand, and asked where he lived—the man came up and
said I wanted to steal the child-the boot fell off itself—I gave the pina-
fore and child out of my hand—I had no pocket on at all.
John RAYMENT re-examined. After saying several times she had not
got it, the policeman came up, and she pulled it out of her pocket.
FREDERICK DUNSTON. I am a policeman. When I took her, she said,
“Have I not a right to strip my own child if I choose ?"
GUILTY. Aged 27.-Confined Nine Months.
1176. EDWARD BURNS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
April, 1 chair, value 11. 10s., the goods of William Henry Hammond;
and that he had been before convicted of felony.
JOHN WARREN. I live in Chancery-lane, and am foreman to William
Henry Hammond, upholsterer, of Bell-yard. On Monday, the 5th of
April, about eleven o'clock, I missed a chair-I went out, and pursued
the prisoner to St. Giles's church-I gave him in charge to a policeman
with the chair, which is my master's-he was walking with it the whole
way, and I following him till I got a policeman.
John Hooper Wells. I live at Mr. Hammond's, I saw the prisoner
on the other side of the way, in Bell-yard, with the chair on his shoulder
-I knew it by the ticket on it-I went and called the foreman.
Prisoner. How do you know me? Witness. I saw your face, and swear
to you.
