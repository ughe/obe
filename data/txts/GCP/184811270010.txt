15. CHARLES LEN, stealing 8 yards of velvet, value 6s.; the goods of
William Couchman.
JOSEPH MARK. I live at 56, Cornwall-road. On 18th Nov., I saw the
prisoner, and a younger one at the corner of Farringdon-market--the young
one drew a piece of velvet from Mr. Couchman's door-the prisoner opened
his coat and received it-I collared him, he struck me in the eye, and dropped
the velvet in the scuffle-he said be picked it up, and was going to take it
into the shop-the other got away.
WILLIAM Couchman. This velvet is mine--I was in the shop, but did
not miss it till it was brought by Brown--the string was cut.
Thomas Brown (City-policeman, 215). I found Mark struggling with
the prisoner-I took him, and the velvet which was on the ground, about
two yards from the door-there was a pile of goods there.
Guilty. Aged 21.-Confined Six Months.

19. WILLIAM FRENCH was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS. Ellis and BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
FREDRRICK RAYNER. I keep an eating-house, in Broad-street, Ratcliffe.
On 7th Oct., the prisoner came between seven and eight o'clock for a quarter
of a pound of meat-it came to 3d.-he offered me a bad crown-I did not
find it to be bad, and put it into my pocket where I had no other, and gave
the prisoner change-be went away-in ten minutes, or a quarter of an hour
my wife came into the parlour, and brought me a crown-piece, and asked me
to give change- I thought it was bad, took it to the prisoner in my shop,
and told him it was bad- I gave it him, and told him he was the man who
tendered the first-he said he was not, he never was in the shop before-I
gave him into custody, and gave the first crown to the policeman.
