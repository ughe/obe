 1301.  THOMAS BLAIR was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of April , 1 time-piece, value 10s.; 1 ewer, value 6d.; 1 basin, value 6d.; 1 case of drawing-instruments, value 6s., and 1 neck-chain, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Henry Richards , his master.
   THOMAS HENRY RICHARDS. I am an auctioneer- I live in Worship-street, but I have an auction-room in Brown's-lane; the prisoner has been employed there for me upwards of two years; I lost a time-piece from the mantel-piece there, and the ewer and the basin from Worship-street, where we had a sale; I lost a case of instruments and a neck-chain, the case from a drawer, where I believe he had put it, and the chain from the parlour, where I had put it when I took it off my wife's watch.
 ELIZABETH STEPHENS. I am single. I have been living with the prisoner nearly seven years; we lodge at Great Saffron-hill; he brought home the time-piece when we lived at Finsbury-court - it was there four or five months; he told me to pawn it, and I did; he brought home a case of instruments when we lived at Saffron-hill, and the neck-chain, which he gave me for my little girl to play with.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. When did you first see the time-piece? A. Eight or nine months age - he had made use of his master's money, and he told me to pawn the time-piece - I pawned it in my own name; it had stood on a sideboard, and was not concealed; I was not quarrelling with the prisoner when the officer came - he knocked me down.
 JOHN BATEMAN. I am a pawnbroker. I have the time-piece, pawned by Stephens, on the 24th of March.
   THOMAS HENRY RICHARDS. This is my time-piece, and so are the other articles.
 Cross-examined. Q.What character had the prisoner? A. I considered him trust worthy - I never trusted this time-piece to him; I had no transaction with him in a way of business - the time-piece was for the use of our office, and when it was missed he charged another person with stealing it; I was once in the Court of Exchequer for selling some goods for a man, who afterwards took the benefit of the Act, and I had to pay the fine; I have borrowed 5l. of the prisoner's brother, but I never had any money transaction with the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. The time-piece was not pawned by my order.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Confined Six Months.

 1303.  WILLIAM DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of May , 1 bag, value 4d.; 1 sovereign, 3 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the property of Joseph Foster.
   JOSEPH FOSTER. I keep a ham and beef shop in Lower Seymour-street. On the afternoon of the 16th of May the prisoner and another lad came to the shop, and asked for two 1d. saveloys; I said I had no 1d. ones, they should have two for 2 1/2d. - the prisoner paid 1 1/2d. for one, and left the shop; I went and spoke to my wife on the stairs - I then came into the shop, and saw the prisoner behind the counter, at the end of it; I asked what he wanted- he said, "You said we should have two for 2 1/2d.," and he threw down 1d. and took a saveloy; I went to put the penny into the till, and missed the money stated - I ran after the prisoner, and brought him back to my shop; he threw something away, went down on his knees, and begged pardon; he afterwards showed me my money in a dish- there was the bag and 1l. 3s. 6d. in it; I am sure it had been in my till; I had counted it ten minutes before they came in.
   JAMES WELLS(Police-constable S 30). I was called to take the prisoner - I saw a dish in the shop; I took from it this bag, with 1l. 3s. 6d. in it: I took the prisoner into the parlour, and found a saveloy on him - he went on his knees, begged the prosecutor to forgive him, and said he would do so no more.
   JOSEPH FOSTER re-examined. Q. Are you sure you saw him throw something towards the dish? A. He made a motion that way; I did not see it go, but he pointed it out in the dish, among the gravy.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went for a saveloy - a boy ran from behind the counter, and I asked him for it; he went to some beef, and began to pick it.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1304.  WILLIAM CHILMADE was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of April , 1 pair of trousers, value 8s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 3s.; 1 shirt, value 4s., and 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Joseph Bishop , his master.
   JOSEPH BISHOP. I am a clothes-salesman, and live in Goswell-street- the prisoner was my errand-boy for nearly twelve months. On the 12th of April a pair of trousers were discovered under the stairs; I left them there, and in the evening I sent him out - the trousers were then gone, and the officer took him with them.
   JOHN MARTIN(Police-constable G 202). I apprehended the prisoner on the 12th of April; I took him into his master's house, and found this pair of trousers under his own; I went to his lodging, and found a pair of shoes, and on his person I found a duplicate of a coat, waistcoat, shirt, and handkerchief.
   JAMES JONES. I am a pawnbroker. I have a coat, waistcoat, and shirt, pawned for 1l. 2s., by Ann Chilmade, who is not here.
   JOHN FOY. I apprehended Ann Chilmade.
