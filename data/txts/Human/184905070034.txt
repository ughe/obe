 1112. CHARLES HILDITCH was indicted for embezzlement. ANN NORTH. I paid the prisoner on 2d April 6 s . 6 d . for Mr. Hayes, his master. CHARLES JAMES HATES. The prisoner was in my service—it was his duty to receive money if I sent him for it, and to pay it me in the evening—he has not paid me this 6 s . 6 d .
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  May he have lost it? A.  When I asked him for it he said he had not received it; he has been with me twelve months, and was the best boy I ever had—I do not wish to hurt him; I think he has had quite sufficient punishment. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Six Days .
 1113. WILLIAM RADFORD , stealing 2 bunches of artificial flowers, I value 9 s  . 3 d  .; the goods of John Foster and others, his masters.
   MR. SLEIGH conducted the Prosecution. DAVID FORSTTH  I am clerk to John Foster and others, artificial flower—I makers, of Wigmore-street. The prisoner had been porter there some time—I it was part of his duty to dust the boxes—on 25th April, about eight o'clock I in the morning, I saw him meddle with the boxes on the counter—he went I out a short time afterwards—I found in his coat, which was lying on the I counter, these two bunches of artificial flowers; I sent for Mr. Duncan.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE Q.  What became of the coat? A.  I left it there; he has it on now. CHARLES DUNCAN I am one of the partners of John Foster and Company. Porter called me—the prisoner returned shortly afterwards—I said to him, "Is it not too bad, after you have been away three days from your work, that the first thing you do, you should rob us ?"  —he said, it was—I saw the flowers; I identified them. GUILTY Aged 30.— Confined Six Months,
 1114. FREDERICK MEAD , stealing 1 bridle, and 1 pair of reins, value 12 s  .; the goods of George Penson . JOHN SLATFORD I am groom to  George Penson. I lost a brdle and 1 pair of reins; these are the reins, and I lost such a bridle.
   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIOH Q.  Has Mr. Penson many horses? A.  Ten; he has had these reins perhaps ten or eleven weeks; I know them by cleaning them. JOHN SEYMOUR I am a harness-maker. I made this bridle for Mr. George Penson. WILLIAM HOLMES I am a watchman. On 14th April, at a quarter-past ten o'clock at night, I found the prisoner under the platform, from which they load the goods, at the Castle and Falcon Yard, in Aldersgate-street—I sent for a policeman, when the prisoner saw him coming, he pulled the bridle out of his pocket, and threw it down; the reins were found round his body at the station.
