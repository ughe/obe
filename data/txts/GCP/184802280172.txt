SIMON CALDCLEUCH. I am a licentiate of the Apothecaries' Company
On 9th Feb., about ten o'clock, I was called to the deceased at Lamberk
Workhouse-I found her perfectly insensible-she gradually got worse, and
died-I afterwards made an examination-I discovered nothing externally.
but on opening the membranes of the brain, I found a vast extravasation
blood on the left side of the brain, down the base of the brain-after clearine
away the blood, I found a hard substance at the termination of the carotid
artery-I cut it, and found it to be part of a sac-I passed my finger into 1
larger opening, and found it to be a dilated artery or an aneurism-I should
think it had been of some standing- I attribute the death to the bursting of
that sac-any concussion might cause it to burst, or any sudden exertion or
jumping forward--a blow such as has been described might produce it.
Court. Q. Suppose, for the purpose of interfering between the women,
she suddenly jumped forward-might that cause the bursting of the
aneurism? 4. Probably it might-there might have been a blow without
my discovering any external mark of it, but a slight blow might catre
