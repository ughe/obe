 1615. CHARLES WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of May , 1 shilling, 1 sixpence, 1 halfpenny, and 1 farthing, the monies of Elizabeth Crow , from her person. ELIZABETH CROW. I am single, and live in Long-walk, Bermondsey-square. On Tuesday evening, between five and six o'clock, I was at Greenwich fair with my sister—I felt a hand in my pocket—I turned, and the officer had got the prisoner—I had a shilling, a sixpence, a halfpenny, and a farthing—my money was gone, and his hand was taken as it came out of my pocket—the money fell on the ground. GEORGE POWELL. ( police-constable E  10.) I saw the prisoner with another—I saw the prisoner put this hand into the prosecutrix's pocket—I seized it, and drew out one shilling, one sixpence, one half-penny, and one farthing—he dropped it—I took him to the station-house—he said he should not have done it but for the other boy.
   Prisoner  She said she would not appear against me, if my father gave her 5 l ELIZABETH CROW. I saw his father on Sunday—I said I would not say more than I could help, but nothing, was said about 5 l GUILTY.* Aged 13— Transported for Seven Years.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1616. JOHN CONLAY. was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of May , 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  ., the goods of John Hagell , from his person. GEORGE VICLERS( police-constable L  54.) I was in Greenwich park on the 23rd of May, and saw the prisoner in company with another person—I saw the prisoner try several pockets, and then take this handkerchief from the prosecutor's—I took him with it. JOHN HAGELL. I was in the park, and lost this handkerchief from my pocket—I did not feel it taken—the offer spoke to me, and I saw the prisoner near me.
   Prisoner  I saw the handkerchief at the gentleman's feet and the officer said I took it.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 17— Confined Three months.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1617. JOHN LONG was indicted for stealing, on the   26th of May , 1 coat, value 2 l  ., the goods of James Fagan . JAMES FAGAN. I am a painter, and live at Royal-hill, Greenwich. On the 21st of May, I was in the Eight Bells there—I had been in the house but two or three minutes—I went in between two and three o'clock in the morning—a man challenged me to fight—he broke the glass I had with some bran day and water in it—there was no place to sit down—I took off my coat and lost it, but I do not know who took it—I put it on the counter of the bar—we did not fight—the man ran away, and I ran after him—it was not the prisoner but another man that wanted to fight me—I missed the coat before he ran away, and I suspected he was connected with the parties who were there—there were seven or eight persons standing together when I went in—they appeared to be conversing about different subjects.
