Saturday, December 3rd, 1904.
HERBERT GORGE THOMAS (eramination continued). Lawson's agreement
of, I think, July 18th, was that he was to have a commission on all business
he introduced to the company--100 per cent. were the original terms, 1
believe-on a deal of the magnitude of the Blackpool Tramway Company,
£100,000, he would alter the terms, as we did eventually-on £100,000
he would have to get £100,000, according to the agreement-if Lawson
brought in such a deal as would produce £100,000 profit he would be en-
titled to half the profit that the company niade--that would be 100 per
cent.--that is the best way you can work such an agreement out; it is
always open to a little alteration as time goes on-the directors would de
termine the alteration, with Lawson's consent, I presume-possibly he
would take the whole of the profit, I do not see why he should not in his
own company--the Coustruction Company was his property pure and
simple, and that of the Syndicate, they were running it to
gether—there was no public money-Paine and Hooley were going
to turn it into a public company-Hooley was interested with
Mrs. Hooley--Lawson put the company into Hooley's hands with
the eventual idea of promoting. it at such time as we did
good business--the company was fronted as a publie company when
shares were issued to the public-Lawson, I believe, put a large block
of shares into Hooley's hands, and Paine joined Hooley with the idea that
the three of us should be interested in the eventual promotion of the
company as a company when it was in a fit state the agreement of
November 26th, 1900, is what I have in my mind-probably I knew all
about it at the time, but it is impossible to say four years afterwards
you have the minutes, you can answer that question better than
the £350,000 introduced applied to various schemes-1 do not think
the company ever got that sum for the schemes-Lawson's letter of
November 15th shows that the company was in such a condition that
Lawson took whatever he thought advisable-I think the books show
about £20,000 advanced to Lawson at or before that time I do not
remember the deed of release, but the figures would most certainly be
put on the table at the time--the deed of release would not be signed
without we knew about the terms and went into the matter-you have the
minute there and it purports, I presume, to be correct-I am not saying
it is not correct--the “services rendered" refer to the fact that Mr.
Lawson was finding and buying Tramway Orders and things of that sort-
we could always reckon that he was entitled to a fair remuneration for
the work he did, independently of his engineer's fees, and his commission
for introducing business on the terms of his agreement-he never pushed
the point of his 100 per cent.--he dealt fairly and honestly with us -1
