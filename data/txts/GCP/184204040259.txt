Praser. Q. Were we not at the public-house, by London-bridge, eat-
ing bread and cheese when you came in. A. I do not know anything
about you- I did not give the cabman 1s., on the road, to pay for a pint
of rum-I did not say that two was company, and three none, and say
I would give you a trifle if you got out of the cab--I bought a set of ra-
zors at the foot of London-bridge, and gave 28. for them the man did
not show me a piece of cloth for velvet, nor did I say I would not wear
such a paltry thing, that I was a velvet-weaver, and could always cabbage
enough.
COURT. Q. How could the woman know all this if she was not with
you? 4. They might be standing about the Railway Tavern, so they
tell me- I did not tell them anything in the cab about my buying razors
- I bought the razors of a man who was standing there.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Did any man offer you any cloth for sale? A. No;
it is all a fiction-I certainly bought the razors, and paid for them-I did
not tell the prisoners I was a velvet-weaver, nor mention it at the tavern
I said it at the office no woman was going to get into the cab with me at
the tavern-I rode off by myself-the cabman had asked me to let a woman
ride with me, but I expected he had forgotten it, and I was very glad of it
-I did not say, “Let the woman come in"-I never saw the women
then.

escape--I caught Thornton, and called out to my brother constable to bring
