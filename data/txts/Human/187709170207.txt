she did not know who he was—I did not then know that it was the land lord's man—we moved on the Thursday, and we arrived in the Euston Road a little after 6 o'clock; the last piece of furniture was put in, I dare say, about 10 o'clock—it was put into two large rooms and remained there; I looked it—I did not miss anything then—I went with the detective to Mrs. Charlton's house the same day; I saw two pictures on the wall—I said at the police-court "I went with the detective to Charlton's house and saw the goods that I bad missed in the house, the pictures displayed on the wall, and the drawers were broken open"—the drawers were unlocked; I had the keys in my pocket—my husband spoke to me about the furniture—I heard him give his evidence at the police-court—I only knew the things were at Mrs. Charlton's house by seeing the drawers there—I told my husband that Charlton's had got the things without my consent or knowledge—I may not have used the word "stolen," but I said "They have the things," and I did not lead him to believe that they were minding the things—he blamed me for leaving Hornsey Park Road—I did not say the things were mine and I had a right to do what I liked with them—I lived at Harringay Road, Hornsey, in May, 1876, going on for three years—there was a bill of sale on the furniture, but I cannot say what date it was or the sum—I value this furniture at 12 l .—I lived at 6, Edith Terrace, Fulham, in 1875—I only knew of one bill of sale—we moved and the bill of sale went with us—Sarah Goodship was in our employment and was in the house when the things were removed—she attended at the police-court every day during the hearing; she was called by my solicitor—she knew nothing about it; then she was brought up by other parties—she was not put in the box by my solicitor—I arranged with Mrs. Charlton to come and knock us up early—the servant let her in—I locked up over night and saw all safe—the prisoners got in at the back window; the bolt was not broken——Mrs. Charlton did not say she got in through the window which the servant had left unbolted—Mrs. Charlton came upstairs when I was leaving the back room when I had seen the chest of drawers going over the wall—I did not complain to her about it. ( James Lovegrove was fare called in. ) I know that man and I know Mr. Ward—those two men were engaged in the removal—I, Mrs. Charlton, and Sarah Goodship were looking out for the vans when they drove up to the door—I had not quite recovered, I was not sober—I did not speak to the carmen more than necessary—I did not know who Clayton was—I did not tell Sarah Goodship that some of the things were to go to her mother—I was not in the room when some things were taken to Mrs. Charlton's—I did not go to Mr. Goodship's house; I know him, he lives in the country—Mrs. Charlton was not in our house at the Euston Road at 10 o'clock—I did not send out for brandy or spirits.
