I went and saw Ryland-he said he had seen his friend, and he had pro-
mised to be there, and he was agreeable to put down 201., to make the
match-we waited about two hours, and his friend did not come--be ex-
pressed his sorrow, and told me he would see him again, and come to my
place on the following Sunday or Monday morning, and let me know what
he said he did not come on either of those days, but he came on the Tuesday
evening, the 21st, and said his friend had given him 5l. to make the match
with me, and if I did not think that sufficient, I was to meet him at the
Spotted Dog, in the Strand, at nine o'clock, and there the money would be
made up to any sum I pleased-I wished to know on what particulars the
match was to be made, and he pretended to find a paper, (but could not.)
stating the terms which his friend had written down--the prisoner is a
Chelsea carrier, I believe.
Q. How came you to tell the Jury, just now, that you seldom play, and
then only for a shilling or a pot of beer, when you went to find a stranger
to get him to play? A. I did so, from what the prisoner said when I
play, it depends on chance whether I win or lose I won the man's 101, and
kept it-the money had been deposited—when I played at whist with the
prisoner, the money had not been deposited - I never said it was.
Q. Then you never directed your attorney to apply for the restoration
of a sum of money deposited in his hands ? A. I went to my solicitos,
and he asked in what manner I should wish it to proceed-I said, a
quietly as possibleI did not wish to make any oration about it I wanted
my money—I did not direct my attorney to apply to him for a sum of
money deposited in his hands--I heard his clerk had written--it was on
the 21st I lost this money, and I went to the attorney the next day.
Q. How did Mr. Humphreys know where to find him? A. He did
not know- he wished me to make inquiries--I understood his cart came
to the Spotted Dog in the Strand- I went there and got the address, and
then I went to Mr. Humphreys directly.
Q. Then, on the 22nd of October, you knew that he lived at No. 1,
Cheyne-row, Chelsea, why did you not go to the cart and take him di-
rectly? A. He did not drive the cart, for I watched for several days after
Mr. Humphreys showed me his answer—this is it—(read)—“Sir, In answer
to your application to me, of the 23rd, 1 beg to inform you, as a solicitor,
I had no money, as stated by you, deposited in my hands by Mr. C. Ireland.
I have been advised to answer your letter, and inform you of the same, as
whatever inconvenience I may be put to in consequence, all parties coul-
cerned shall have due notice previous to their illegal proceeding, as threat-
ened by you ;--further, if it is the intention of you to proceed, after this no-
tice, (if by bailable process,) my solicitor will give an undertaking to your
officer, if you will inform me his name, or will receive any process for me,
which will prevent your sending so far.
J. RYLAND."
Q. When did you apprehend bim? A. I do not know I believe it
was on the 30th of October-I cannot say when I spoke to the poliee about
it-I spoke to two or three-I took him on the 30th, a little before he got
to the Spotted Dog.
Q. Now did not you before the Magistrate state, in the presenee of Mr.
Wontner, that you gave no information to any officer, till after you had
taken the man into custody ? A. I do not recollect that I did I do not
believe I did I should expect that if I had been asked the question,
should have said I did.
