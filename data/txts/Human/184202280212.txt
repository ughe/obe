 (  Witnesses for the Defence. ) WILLIAM JOHN LLOYD. I am the prisoner's brother—he lives with his mother, at No. 6, William-place, Prospect-row, Walworth-road—he has been latety engaged in transacting business for Mr. Staples, an up holsterer at Chatham, making purchases for him, and sending them down by van—I remember Thursday the 17th of February, the day before my brother was apprehended—I got to my mother's house that night about half-past nine or a quarter to ten—my mother was dangerously ill at that time—my brother was then gone in search of a medical man, and returned in five minutes after I arrived—Mr. Prior, the doctor came in less than five minutes after my brother returned—my brother let him in—I was in the passage—my wife was with me—I live at No. 2, North-street, East-street, Walworth—I had occasion to go home, and told my wife so—I looked at my watch then, and it was half-past ten—I had left my house on the latch, and no person in it—I went out of the door about a quarter to eleven—at that time I believe my watch to have been ten minutes too fast, but I did not know that till Saturday—when I went out of the house it wanted less than a quarter by my watch—I then walked up and down the place for about ten minutes with my brother—he and I came out of the house together, and we had some conversation while I was waiting for my wife—I think it must have been eleven o'clock when I parted with my brother—within five minutes, either way—I parted with him at the corner of William's-place, which is about three-quarters of a mile from the King's Arms—my brother wore very light drab trowsers that day—I never knew any charge made against him in my life—when he left me he turned to the right—there is a beer-shop at the corner, and he turned, I believe, to go there—that is away from the King's Arms—I be lieve he turned into the house—he said he had some instructions from Chatham—we walked about 100 feet from my mother's house. ANN HARBERD. On Thursday, the 17th of February, I was acting as nurse to the prisoner's mother, who had been very poorly for a few days—I remember that Thursday—the prisoner was at home all day, till he went for the doctor in the evening, which was, as near as I can guess, between nine and ten o'clock—he returned in about a quarter-of-an-hour, and had been unable to find the one he went for—he then went for another, and was gone about ten minutes—he then returned, and did not leave the house any more till he went away with his brother—his mother was seri ously ill—we were in very great fear of her till the doctor came—the pri soner was very much hurt to see her so—he appeared distressed, and even cried.
   COURT . Q.  What time did the doctor come in? A.  About ten o'clock—it was Mr. Prior—he staid about twenty minutes—when the prisoner went with his brother, he had to go for some orders a few yards off, which his master sent for him at eleven o'clock that night—he came in again before twelve, and slept at home that night.
