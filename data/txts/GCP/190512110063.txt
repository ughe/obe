Re-examined. To have taken the 10s. would have been a serious matter
to us and we never thought of doing so—when I said in cross-examination
I thought better of it, I meant I thought more of myself than to take it-
I had not been inclined to take it-I am doubtful whether the prisoner
asked us to have a drink or not, but I am not doubtful about not saying,
* Shall we risk it?”-I did not say it I did not arrest the prisoner,
because I did not think he was likely to abscond.
GEORGE Rose (353 Y.) On October 20th I was with the last witness
on special duty and went to Wells Terrace-I there saw Arnold take
betting slips, but nothing with them—he took two slips during the period
from 1.40 p.m. to 1.45 p.m.- I was not then with Fullick; I was keeping
observation separately- I rejoined Fullick afterwards—we then went up
to Arnold-we were both in plain clothes-about 3.15 p.m. the prisoner
came and spoke to us-he said, “I hear you have been after Johnny;
will you take half a sovereign to square it? It is all I have got ; you can
have it in the saloon bar of the Blackstock or in the urinal”-we refused
the money-he then said, " If that is not enough I will meet you where
you like and I will give you £l or £2 if you will only let the matter drop "-
I asked him if he knew the consequences of attempting to bribe police
officers-he said, "I am quite aware of that "-we then walked away-

he said " Johnny," I knew whom he meant-I have heard that Arnold

making our observations, we each make a note and then we go on to
