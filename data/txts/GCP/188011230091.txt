JOHN LAIRD. I live at 53, Gloucester Street, Bloomsbury on 7th
November, at 11.15 p.m., I came from a refreshment-house in Fox Oourt,
Holborn-some women were quarrelling I stopped to look on the pri
soner came in front of me, struck me on my head, and knocked me down
-my head went against the wall, and I was insensible when I came to
I missed 38. 101d. and & penknife-this is the knife (produlood).
Cross-examined. I went on a visit to my uncle about 3.80, and left at
10.30—we had some beer at 4 o'clock; that was all we drank when I
heard this noise I was not in a public house, but in a sweet-shop-Fox
Court is not well lit; there was a crowd I did not see the prisoner till
he knocked me down, and yet I can swear to him.
Ro-examined. The court was lighted enough for me to see who knocked
me down-I was sober-I am lame.
JANE DYNEs. I am a widow, and live at 9, Green Street, Paddington
-on 7th November, at 11.15, I was waiting in a house in For Court and
heard a great noise-after it was over I saw a lot of men and women,
who had been quarrelling-I saw the prisoner knock Laird down, and his
head came in contact with & window one of the men fell on him, and
the prisoner knelt down and put one hand over his mouth and the other
in his trousers pocket-I heard money rattle and ran for & policeman,
who took the prisoner, and took the money out of his left hand.eu
Cross-examined. The court is dark the prisoner came from the crowd
-I did not see him till he knocked Laird down-I could not say whether
the prisoner knooked him down with his fist or simply cannoned against
him it was another man who got on him he was on the left, the prisoner
on the right; they were together there was a light in the window, and
he was knocked under the window-I was not far off_I did not go out
of the court to find a policeman.
