 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did Rotton live in your room the whole three months that he was there? A. Yes; I did not sleep in the prison; Mr. Peel and another person lived in the same room with my husband: they were not in the room when this took place - I had often seen Ivey and Rotton together; I never saw Bailey till I saw him here; I have not seen Moody since the 15th of April - Ivey is a carpenter; Rotton's father is a solicitor, I believe - his father never came to see him to my knowledge.
 MR. EARLE. Q. You frequently saw Ivey and Rotton together? A. Yes, several times the week before; Ivey used to come every two or three days, and bring him victuals and what he wanted, also money.
 COURT. Q. You say Rotton desired Ivey to go to the solicitor, and see him righted - was the solicitor's name mentioned? A. Yes, it was; he did not know the name: he told him to go to the solicitor - I never heard the name nor where he lived; I never inquired.
   WILLIAM IVEY. I am the defendant's father. I have known Rotton's father thirty years - I was a tenant under him at Frome, Somerset; he was a solicitor - I am a carpenter: my son has known Rotton from his childhood - I always considered Rotton an upright young man, without a flaw in his character.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. What was he brought up to? A. I never knew what trade he was - I believe he rented a farm; I have not seen him for seven years, as I have been living in London.
 COURT. Q. Did you visit Rotton in prison? A. I called on him there - I live in St. Andrew's-row, Horsemonger-lane; I have called since the 15th of April - he never desired me to inquire about any solicitor; I know he employed Mr. Boydell, a solicitor, on the case; he was in prison upon - he has been his solicitor ever since he has been in London; I know nobody else as his solicitor.
 MR.  WILLIAM BERRESFORD. I am clerk to the Magistrates at Guildhall. Bailey was brought there on this charge on the 25th of April, before Mr. Alderman Garratt - I find by my minute-book that William Ivey was the first witness against him; he was remanded till the following Monday, at his own request, and on the 2nd of May he was brought before Mr. Alderman Venables; Ivey appeared on that day, but was not sworn; I read the evidence to the Alderman, and he ordered Ivey into custody.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. He was ordered into custody? A.He was ordered to find bail, and did so, after the first examination.
 Bailey's Defence (written.) It must appear very strange that Moody is not prosecuted; but the reason is very plain, for the defendants and Moody are endeavouring to throw the blame on me, who was no party in the transaction. The only evidence which concerns me, is that of the turnkley, who swears that I asked him a great many questions respecting Rotton, and how he was to be discharged - but the turnkey's evidence is very much in favour of me; for if I were a party concerned, why should I ask the turnkey questions, and thus create suspicions? Why could I not ask Rotton or Moody? If I had asked them, they could have given me the same information as the turnkey; and therefore I could only have asked the turnkey to satisfy myself that there was no fraud in the transaction, and from a determination not to be concerned in a transaction contrary to law, The defendants counsel throws all the blame on me, to exculpate his clients, but yet he disclaims all knowledge of me in this transaction, and states not that I was to get the writs sealed, but that a clerk of a solicitor was to go to the Prothonotary's. I became acquainted with Moody by visiting two builders named Ward and Richardson, confined in the prison; for whom Moody was accustomed to carry messages, and he asked me to do this for him; and in order that it should be quite correct, I asked the turnkey the questions which he has stated. You will observe that the clerk at Guildhall states that I was remanded at my own request, that I might produce the persons implicated in the transaction.

   THOMAS BICKERSTAFF. I am a carman. The prosecutrix lives in Dunk-street, Mile-end; she placed me to watch the prisoner on the 25th of June; at half-past six o'clock that morning I saw him come in at the back window, and go to the till in the bar - I heard the money chink - I went to him, and he at first said he had no money, but he gave me 7s. 6d. in copper, when I said it would be the best thing from him; he tumbled himself out of window again - he had gone very cautiously to the bar, and made very little noise till he dropped some of the money; I sent for the constable, and had him taken.
 COURT. Q. Did he pass through the tap-room to go to the bar? A. No, through a back parlour; the copper was in the night-cap when he gave it to me.
