THOMAS PATRICK. I am in the employ of Mr. John Dickenson and Mr. Longman. These cards are their make.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How do you know them; A.  From their particular make, the material, and the mode of doing them up—I think nearly all the trade would know they were Messrs. Dickenson's make—I do not know of any offshoots from Messrs. Dickenson's business. JAMES CHARLES DIX. I am a printer, in St. Ann's-lane, Aldersgate-street. I know the prisoner—I bought one gross of cards of him on 30th. June, for 30 s .—these are them—I knew whose make they were—everybody in the trade knows that—the prisoner did not tell me how he got them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who sent you to the prisoner? A.  No one—he came to my place, and I purchased them of him—I gave information as soon as I saw the first account in the paper—I then went forward with what I had left of the cards—I had used some in my business—I am an engraver and printer—the prisoner gave me this invoice—he put his address on it, No. 40, Alie-street—he did not tell me he had five per cent, for selling them—I took them to be his own goods.  1985. RICHARD KEYS , stealing 27 cigars, value 4 s  .; the goods of the St. Katherine's Dock Company : 2nd COUNT  , for receiving the same.
   MESSRS. CLARKSON and PLATT conducted the Prosecution. HENRY POPE. I am a constable of the St. Katherine's Dock. On 19th July, I saw the prisoner come in at my gate, which is No. 7—he went towards the warehouse, I followed him, and found him and Kippax within the warehouse door—I said to Kippax, "What are you about?"—he made some evasive answer, and went away—Iwent to the prisoner, and said, "What do you want here?"—he said he came to see a person—I said, "What have you in your hat?"—I took his hat off, and found in it these twenty-seven cigars in a handkerchief—I said, "I expect Kippax gave you these"—he said, "Yes."
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is Kippax? A.  An apprentice in the cigar department—I saw him every day—he came to the gate I was at, but he never came out—I expect he is in prison—I rather expect there is a good deal of smuggling going on—we do all we can to prevent it. JOHN PASSMORE MUMFORD. I am superintendent of police, at St. Katherine's Docks. The prisoner was brought to me by Hope—after I had taken his name and address, I asked him if he wished to say anything rela tive to the cigars—he said, "Kippax gave them to me"—I said, "Do you mean the young one, or the old one"—(there are two Kippaxs' there)—he said, "The young one"—I said, "He who works up in the cigar-floor"—he said, "Yes"—Kippax was then brought by his father—he was asked some questions, and he made a statement. JOSEPH WARD KIPFAX( a prisoner. ) I was working on the cigar-floor, in St. Katherine's Docks on 29th July—I had known the prisoner nine months—he came to me that afternoon—I gave him twenty-seven cigars—he knew that I had no right to give them away—he knew they were the property of the Docks, and under their charge—the officer came up about five minutes after—I was taken to the superintendent, and made a statement of what had passed between me and the prisoner.
