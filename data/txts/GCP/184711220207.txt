919. JOHN CHITTENDEN, forging and uttering an order for the
delivery of 7 pockets of hops, with intent to defraud Francis Nash and others.
-Two otber Counts, calling it a request; and four others, stating his in-
tent to be to defraud Robert Collins.
MR. HUDDLESTON Bonducted the Prosecution.
Robart COLLINS. I am a hop-factor, 17, Maze-pond, Southwark-I
know the prisoner-he came to me in the beginning of Oet.-I showed him
a sample of hops that went by the name of "Andrew's hops" from the grower,
the bulk of which that was a sample was lying at Messrs. Nash, Hicks, and
Co., Pickle-herring wharf-I saw bim again on 8th Oct.--he said he could
sell those hops if I would take 70s, per cwt. for them-I had mentioned 75s.
us the price-I told him I would take 70s., provided they were sold to a
respectable person, and delivered and sold in my name, and for me to receive
the money-be said he had sold them for 72s.--the 2s. was his profit-he!
aid he had sold them he had not bought them-he said he could sell them
at 721.- I said, "Whatever you sell them at beyond 70s., shall of course be
your profit"-he then said he had sold them for 72s., to Messrs. Haynes and
Son, maltsters, of Paddington-be did not say anything about the invoice till
the Tuesday or Wednesday- referred to the Directory," to see who
Messrs. Haynes and Son were-I found their name there, and said to the
prisoner they had been living there seven or eight years-he said he had
kaown them twenty years, and that they were respectable-I said, “Will
you give an undertaking to pay for them, if Messrs. Haynes do not!"She
said he would, and he did give it--the policeman has it--the hops were
worth about 221.- he asked for an order for their delivery, and I told him I
would send the order to Mr. Haynes, and inclose him the invoice-on that
he said that Mr. Haynes had not any bops by him, and that he wanted them
immediately, that he was going to Paddington, and would take the order
with him I gave him this order (produced) - this writing, which is on the
back of it, was not there then-I believe it to be the prisoner's writing.
