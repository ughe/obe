MR. WILLIAMS submitted that there was no case for the Jury: first, because
the prisoner was not a British subject. (He had, on arraignment, stated himself
to be an American, and elected to be tried by an English Jury)—and on the
authority of “Rex v. De Pardo," Ist Taunton, also reported in Russell and
Ryan, p. 134, and "Q. « De Matos," T, Carrington and Payne, 458.
there was no jurisdiction in this Court to try an alien for an offence com
mitted in a foreign country. As to the Counts alleging the offence to be
committed on the high seas, the evidence showed the vessel to be in the port of
Bordeaux.
