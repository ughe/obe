"It appears to me that the Attorney-General's right to reply is in the nature of a prerogative right; it is a right on the part of the Crown, exercised by the officer of the Crown, the Attorney-General, and I do not see that that prerogative right is taken away at all by this Act of Par  liament; whenever the Attorney-General not only appears in person, but makes a statement that he appears really on behalf of the Crown, he has a right to reply, and that right is not affected by the question of whether or no the Defendant's Counsel calls witnesses; he has the right if he chooses to exercise it.    MR. HARRIS  and MR. D. MICHELE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM DRISCOLL. I live at 2, Baker's Arras Alley, Whitechapel—on 10th June, about 2 p.m., I was going in to my dinner and saw Mrs. Lordan coming down the court from her dinner—I live at No. 2 and she lived at No. 3—her throat was cut, and she turned round to me and told me to take hold of her husband, as he did it—he was coming out of their own door, following her—I took hold of him and held him till the policeman took him. PATRICK NORMAN.
