   COURT . Q.  Did you know that in the weekly statements all the over due bills appeared which had accumulated from the time the 38,000 l ., had been written off? A.  I presumed so—I presumed that that statement con tained the whole of those bills, whether bankrupt bills or not—I don't know that they also included forged bills; it is very possible—I think they included all the past-due bills of every description, forged or not, at their full value. ROBERT PROCTOR. I am a cheesemonger, and was a shareholder in the Unity Bank—I am appointed by the shareholders as liquidator, and was appointed one of the audit committee to go into the question of how the bank got into its present condition, and to investigate the balance-sheet—the defendants attended before us, at the latter end of February or the I beginning of March—surprise was expressed to Terry that statements were put in the balance-sheet which appeared so false and wrong—he said that he had followed the custom of other joint-stock banks in London; I asked him if he had any instructions from the directors as to the manner in which he should prepare the balance-sheet; he said his instructions were only general, and were conveyed to him through Alderman Mechi, that he was to prepare a true and correct balance-sheet—Burch was asked several ques tions on the subject, and said that he had followed the directions of Mr. Terry in putting down the items, and that he did not consider it his duty to estimate their value—I asked him whether if he had been the manager he would have felt justified in placing such items in the balance sheet, and he said that he should not—we were investigating for the share holders generally; we were considered antagonistic to the directors, but I do not know upon what grounds—after this investigation the report was adjourned to a meeting of the co-liquidators—the balance-sheet of June 30, was generally circulated among the shareholders, and I received a copy.
