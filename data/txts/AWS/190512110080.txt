115: CHARLES ROBERT ELDERTON (43), Feloniously receiving
the eareases of two dead pigs and three loins of pork, the property of
Charles Robert Rilling, which had been feloniously stolen.
Mr LEYCESTER Prosecuted; Mr. W. B. OAMPBELL Defended.
CHARLES ROBERT RILLING. I am a butcher, carrying on business at
365, New North Road-on October 27th I was in the Meat Market buying
meat for my business-I bought from Brewer & Co. seven pigs, and from
Darrington & Co. two pigs and two pair of hands, some spare ribs and
three pigs plucksthe pigs from Darrington were Cornish pig8-I can
tell a Cornish pig from a Devonshire pig-on Cornish pigs the shoes are
left on the toes; on other pigs they are generally taken of-from Taylor
& Sons I bought three loins of Dutch pork and some back fat-when the
fat is cut off the loins we generally call it hog meat, and this that I bought
was hog meat-it weighed 6 stone and the pigs weighed 19 stoneI
engaged Frederick Greast to colleet the things and put them on my van,
which was in Charterhouse Street--Greast is a porter whom I had em-
ployed there for years in the morning8| went away and left him to colleet
the things about 9 o clock-I returned about 9.30, when Muokenfus, who is
a butcher in the market, spoke to me-I looked at my van and 8aw some
beef and mutton there which I had bought, but of the other things which
I have mentioned nothing was there except some pigs' plueks and the
back fat-the meat which was lost was worth about f9 5s. d.-the pige
bought from Brewer were still in the shop and had not been collected
Greast was not there-I went all over the place, but could not find him or
his truek, and I went to the police about 10.15 or 10.30 a.m.this was
a Friday-I saw Greast again in the afternoon and had a little conversa-
tion with him, but I have not seen him since-that evening I went with
some police olfieers to 48, Red Lion Street, where the prisoner carries on
business as a buteher-I did not know him-we got there about 9.30-
his shop was then open-I walked up a little distance and came back
again in about ten minutes-the shop was still open and a little later
Alfred Greast, the brother of Frederick Greast, and I went to the shop
and found it was shut-that was about 9.45-Alfred Greast knoeked,
