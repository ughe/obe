HARRY ERNEST WATTs, managing clerk to E. A. Whitehouse, soli
citor, Queen's House, Queen Street. I was introduced to the prisoner
by a friend at Clapham Junction on August 14, 1905. He told me he
had an invention in connection with submarines, and we discussed the
matter. He produced plans and sketches, wbich I went through
with him at his apartments. He told me he required some money for
his personal expenditure. He said, "You know inventors are always
hard up." I told him I would introduce him to my principal, Mr.
Whitehouse, which I did on August 16. Prisoner did not inform me
he was an uncertificated bankrupt, nor did I know it from any other
source. Mr. Whitehouse, myself, and the prisoner had an interview
at the office, and Mr. Whitehouse inspected the plans. Prisoner made
certain representations with regard to other gentlemen financing him
with reference to this patent, up to about £100. We discussed the
matter, and Whitehouse arranged to lend prisoner £25, subject to his
representations being correct, and instructed me to see Mr. MoCully,
vho prisoner said was financing him up to £100. Whitehouse then
lefe for his holiday, leaving the matter in my hands, and, after seeing
McCully, I handed prisoner an open cheque for £25, which he en-
dorsed. I then sent a junior clerk out to cash the cheque, and handed
the proceeds to prisoner. Document produced is some particulare
which he handed to me, and which he signed in my prasence. I saw
prisoner several times at the office before Mr. Whitehouse returned.
In October, 1906, I first found he was an undischarged bankruptu
