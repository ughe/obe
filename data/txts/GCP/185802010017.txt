CHARLES LAWRENCE. I am a greengrocer. On 22nd Feb. the policeman

RICHARD CLARK (Policeman, N 296). On 22nd Jan., between 6 and 7
o'clock, I met the prisoner in Upper Clapton-Mrs. Eaton made a communi-
cation to me; I went with her to Lower Clapton, where I saw the last
witness--when I took the prisoner, I saw him draw his hand from his right
hand trousers' pocket and drop a shilling; the last witness picked it up and
gave it to me this is it-I took the prisoner to the station, and Mrs. Eaton
gave me this other shilling there the prisoner said the woman was mistaken,
he had not been in the shop-he afterwards stated that the two pieces would
lick him-I found on him a good sixpence and 8 d. in copper.
Prisoner. When I was taken he said, “I know you belong to bad com-
pany; I will get somebody to convict you," and he whistled three times.
Witness. No, I did not-Mrs. Eaton was certain this man was the person-
she did not point out any body else as being the person.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These are both bad.
Prisoner's defence. I went out to get a job; I was going towards Clapton-
gate; I kicked my foot against something; I thought it was a stone; I
kicked it three times; I looked, and it was a piece of brown paper; I looked
in it, and found this money; I was going to the water side, and I gave one
of the shillings in change; I did not know whether it was good or not.
GUILTY.-Confined Nine Months.
231. JAMES WARD, alias Powell (26), was indicted for feloniously
uttering counterfeit coin; having been before convicted.
Messrs. BODKIN and LAWRENCE conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD TANNER. I am a detective policeman, A 29—I produce a cer-
tified copy of a conviction-(Read: "Ai this Court, in Feb., 1856, James
Ward was convicted for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin, and sentenced to
be confined nine months”)— I saw the prisoner tried; he is the person.
JOHN SANDERSON. I live in Amwell Street, Somers Town. On 28th
Dec. I saw the prisoner come from Middlesex Street towards Alderman Street
with Mary Ward-it was about a quarter before 2 o'clock-they were walk-
ing and talking together-I went into a house; I came out in about five
minutes, and I saw the prisoner running away from the policeman towards
Clarendon Square.
