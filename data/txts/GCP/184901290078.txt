Schmidt was a lodger of mine. On the night of 27th Dec., Boyall came to
my house, about half-past eleven o'clock, and asked me to let him wait for

549. ROBERT JOHNSTON, stealing l purse, value 6d.; 11 sove-
reigns, and I half-sovereign; the property of Montford Mortimer, in his
dwelling-house : and WILLIAM SMITH feloniously receiving the same.
MR. SLEIGH conducted the Prosecutior.
MONTFORD MORTIMER. I am a compositor, of Goswell-terrace-Johnston
lived in my house with his father. On Sunday, 31st Dec., I gave my wife
thirteen sovereigns and one half-sovereign, to put away-I afterwards
missed it.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Did not some question once
arise, between you and your wife, respecting her not applying money in the
way you directed her ? A. Yes.
ALEXANDRINA MORTIMER. I am the wife of Montford Mortimer. On
Sunday, 31st Dec., he gave me 131. 108. in gold to put away-I put it in
a purse, in a drawer, and locked the drawer-I went again the same day,
found the drawer unlocked, and the purse gone-Johnston was not in that
room; he left my house on the Tuesday morning-this is the purse (produced.)
John Davison. I am a coffee-house keeper in Acton-place, Westminster-
road. On the night of 2nd Jan., the prisoners came and asked for a bed-I
gave them one-I saw the officer find the purse in Johnston's pocket, with
the money in it.
WILLIAM COCKRILL (policeman, L 108). I found this purse on Johnston,
with six sovereigns, a balf-crown, and a sixpence-when I took Smith, he
gave me Il. 8s. 60. out of his waistcoat-pocket, stating that was all he had
when he got to the station he said he had 41. more-1 found three sovereigns
and one shilling in his shirt-tail.
JOHNSTON-Guilty. Aged 12.-Confined Three Months.
SMITH. Nor GUILTY.
