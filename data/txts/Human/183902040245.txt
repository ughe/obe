 836. JOHN FREDERICK was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the shop of Henry Simmons , on the   13th of January , and stealing therein eight horse-shoes, value 4 s  ., his goods. JAMES STARTUP. I am a blacksmith, in the employ of Henry Simmons, who has a little house near the cage, in the Greenwich-road. On Monday morning, about seven o'clock, when I went to work, I missed some horse-shoes off some pegs in the workshop, under a window, which I had seen safe on Saturday afternoon, when I left work—the wire-work which had been nailed over the window was torn away sufficiently to get a hand in, and the frame-work broken in—the glass had been broken before, and the wire was put to prevent a hand being put in. WILLIAM PULLEY. I am a policeman. On Monday, the 14th of Janu ary, I was in the yard of the Horse and Groom, at Blackheath, when the prisoner offered some horse-shoes for sale—I asked him where he got them—he said he found them in Back-lane, Blackheath—I asked if any one was with him when he found them—he said his younger brother Was, and they were wrapped up in an old horse-cloth—I told him to go and fetch his brother, and the cloth they were in—I waited for him half an hour—he did not return, and I went in search of him—I found him on Blackheath-hill afterwards, and took him into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. CARDEN . Q.  Were you in the yard when the prisoner came in? A.  No, I was called in and found him there—I heard the smith ask what he wanted for them, and he said 1 s .—this was full half a mile from the prosecutor's—they are all new shoes. JOHN CLINKER. I am a farrier, at Blackheath-hill. The prisoner came to ray shop, and offered these eight shoes for sale—I called Pulley's atten tion to it, who was standing outside the shop, and asked the prisoner what he wanted for them—he said 1 s .—I asked where he got them—he said he found them on Back-hill, in a sack—I said, if he brought the sack I might tell who they belonged to—he was away a good while, and the policeman went and took him—he said he did not steal them—they are worth 3 s . ( Property produced and sworn to ) NOT GUILTY.

 837. THOMAS LOW and THOMAS COOK were indicted for steal ing, on the   7th of February , 1 coat, value 10 s  .; 1 waistcoat, value 1 s  .; and 2 shirts, value 2 s  .; the goods of William Quinton . WILLIAM QUINTON. I am a carman, and live at Lea. I lost a coat, waistcoat, and two shirts, on the morning of the 2nd of February—the shirts hung to dry on a hedge near the house. WILLIAM DYKE. I am a constable. On the 7th of February, at ten o'clock in the morning, I met the prisoners in Lewisham, about half a mile from Lee—Low had a bundle—I asked what he had got there—he said a coat, which he had given to him—I asked what was in his bosom—he said a shirt and waistcoat—I found two shirts and a waistcoat on him, and four farthings on Cook—be said he knew nothing about it—I had seen them both in company, about an hour before.
