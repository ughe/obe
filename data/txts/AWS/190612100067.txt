OLD COURT; Wednesday, December 12.
(Before Mr. Justice Lawrance.)
EVERED, Thomas (33, blacksmith) indicted and charged on
coroner's inquisition with the manslaughter of Thomas Howe, pleaded
guilty.
Mr. Puroell prosecuted.
On the afternoon of November 30 prisoner was with Howe and some
other men in a publio-house at Stratford, Howe being the worse for
drink and quarrelsome. On his striking one man in the face, pri-
soner said, W What is that fori" Howe replied, I will serve you the
same," and proeeeded to strike prisoner about the body and face.
Prisoner made no response, but endeavoured to ward off the blows.
He left the public-house and fetched a hammer. He returned to the
public-house, outside which was Hove. Ultimately there was a rush
on the part of Howe, and prisoner threw the hammer, the handle
of which struck Howe on the throat, breaking a bone in the trachea,
lacerating the museles, and causing a saffusion of blood, which in 8
few minutes resulted in suffocation. Prisoner was a quiet and peace:
able man, and bore an excellent character,
Mr. Justice Lawrance said that although prisoner had properly
pleaded guilty, because he had no right to use the hammer, there
was no doubt that he had been ill-treated by Howe who was much
more powerful man than himself. Prisoner would be bound over in
his own recognisances in E10 to come up for judgment when called
upon.
