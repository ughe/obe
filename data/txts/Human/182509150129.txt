 Cross-examined. Q. I suppose you searched Mason's room, to see if any other man was concealed? A. After we took Crook, I did, but not at first; I had seen a man resembling Samuel Crook about the house by the sewer, but will not swear to him - I have had the care of the silk ever since, and have brought it here.
 COURT. Q. Do you know Mr. Waterlow's house? A. Yes, it is in the parish of Christ-church, Middlesex, commonly called Spitalfields - Christ-church, Middlesex. is the correct name - I know of no other Christ-church, in Middlesex.
 MR. THOMAS WATERLOW, JUN. I am in partnership with George Sawyer. We carry on business at No. 27,  Church-street, in the parish of Christ-church, as silk-weavers- it is my dwelling-house, I live and sleep there, but not my partner. On this Monday night I slept out of town, and I returned about half-past nine o'clock, next morning - I had left fifteen shillings in my writing desk - that was gone - I missed seven hundred and sixty-eight yards of silk altogether, worth 150 l., and found the whole of it at the watch-house; it belongs to me and my partner - in consequence of what was told me, I called Samuel Crook down, and gave him in charge - I saw his hat on the table in the room under where he sleeps, near the window - the keys are kept in that table drawer - he had no business with the keys - he takes his supper in that room, they sup about twelve o'clock - the whole of the silk is mine, every piece is worth more than 40 s. - there are fourteen pieces, seven in each parcel - I know some by my private mark, some by the border and figure - they are worth from 8 l. to 12 l. a piece.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is your father alive? A. Yes, he has no concern in the business - Mr. Sawyer lives some where at Islington - I pay rent and taxes; we have only been in business nine months - I shall have to make a charge for the warehouse.
 Q. His hat might be in that room every night? A. He supped there - the table is not the place for his hat - the servant brought the keys to me. I have no doubt but they were found in the table drawer - I never saw his hat there before; I returned on Tuesday morning; I had left town about four o'clock on Monday afternoon.
   GEORGE HUBBARD. About twenty minutes past twelve o'clock, I found a hat in the street - Mason claimed it at the watch-house.
 MR. WATERLOW. This iron instrument is mine, it was used in the silk trade four years ago, but never at this house - the slides under the counter, where we kept the silk was broken open - there are bolts to the ware-house door, back and front, but no locks - they were all inside fastening - there is a lock to the street door, but not to the back door, that bolt is inside - the wash-house opens into the passage.

JOHN CLARK and HANNAH GALE were indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Elizabeth Green , widow , about one o'clock in the night on the 1st of August , at St. Dunstan, Stepney , with intent to steal, and stealing therein, two pieces of crape, value 2 l.; four yards of calico, value 2 s.; a handkerchief, value 5 s.; two pairs of breeches, value 6 d.; two frocks, value 1 s.; two petticoats, value 1 s.; four yards of flannel, value 4 s.; a table-cloth, value 2 s.; eight napkins, value 3 s.; a tippet, value 2 d.; a purse, value 2 d.; four caps, value 1 s.; a pocket, value 4 d., and a half crown, the property of Maria short , widow.
 ELIZABETH GREEN. I am a widow, and rent a house in  Silver-street, Stepney. Mrs. Short lodges in the back room on my first floor. On the 1st of August, about half-past one o'clock in the morning, I was alarmed - it was moon-light, or it would have been dark. I got up, and found a ladder which is kept at the farther end of the yard placed under Mrs. Short's window - it was at the other end of the yard the night before.
 MARIA SHORT. I am a widow, and lodge at Mrs. Green's, on the first floor. On the 1st of August, about twelve o'clock, I went to bed. I shut the window down, and put a gimblet into it, but whether it caught the outer sash or not I cannot say, but am certain it was quite shut down. I went to sleep, and was alarmed about half-past one - it was moon-light - I could see my black petticoat on the floor - I was laying on my right side, with my baby sucking at my left breast, and on opening my eyes I saw a man reaching inside the window. I saw him take some of my clothes out of my box - the window was open, and he was leaning within it - both his arms were in the room. I was so alarmed I could not call out - I endeavoured to kick my eldest boy, who laid at the foot of the bed, but could not awake him - he took the clothes out of my box, and threw them out of the window - after that I heard the window above stairs open - the man was then within my window - I then saw him go gradually down - I got out of bed, and gave an alarm - Mrs. Barns gave the first alarm. I went into the yard, and found the ladder under my window. I had seen him going down the ladder, and Mrs. Barns said, "There is a man in the yard." I noticed that he had a dark coat on, but cannot swear to his person. I first saw him in the yard, and afterwards saw him get over the wall into the field. I missed the articles stated in the indictment (enumerating them). I gave information of the robbery next morning, and went into the taproom of the Red Lion public-house, and saw a man sitting down with a yellow silk handkerchief round his neck.
