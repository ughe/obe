MR. PAYNE . Q.  But when you advanced him these particular sums of money, did not you trust to the particular statements he made at the time? A.  I did, unquestionably—I gave him the money, and the clothes to go to the Court of Chancery, on the particular statements he made at the time—I drew 1,300 l . from my business between May, 1839, and Au gust, 1840—the prisoner and his family lived with me, not I with them—I provided for them, and supported, clothed, and housed them during the whole time this supposed suit was going on. JOHN TAYLOR. I am a solicitor, in partnership with Mr. Shave, in Fenchurch-street. I remember some time in 1839, the prisoner coming to me and bringing a person with him who he represented to be Wall—he stated that Mr. Wall was entitled to large property, that he had been ad vertised for that—there were proceedings in Chancery, and he brought some papers, but what they were I do not remember—by my desire he showed the advertisement to me—it referred to Messrs. Teasdale and Symet, solicitors, in Fenchurch-street—in consequence of that reference I went to Messrs., Teasdale and Symes's, leaving the prisoner and the man he called Wall in my office—I came back and told them that the suit had been disposed of by the Court of Chancery, and there was nothing for Wall, he had applied too late, or something of that sort—the prisoner did not em ploy us as his attorneys for Wall in the Chancery suit—I never saw the prisoner afterwards till he was in custody—I did not go with him to the Court of Chancery to pay for the costs, or any thing else—there was no pretence for saying there was any suit going on about Wall's property in our office,
   Cross-examined. Q.  He did show you some papers which he brought with this person? A.  He did—I had an opportunity of looking at them if I pleased—I do not remember what their contents were—I can only recollect the circumstances from going to Teasdale and Symes's—I have no entry of the time, and cannot tell exactly when it was—I remember there was a newspaper with an advertisement—I considered that Wall was too late to obtain this property, from my communication with Teasdale and Symes's—I should suppose he originally possessed some claim from what little I did see, but I cannot say.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  But you did not carry on the suit? A.  No—I made no bargain with him that our names were not to be mentioned.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  I suppose as a respectable solicitor, you would hardly take up a suit of that kind? A.  No—there are a good many that would be very glad to do it. WILLIAM PEARSON.
