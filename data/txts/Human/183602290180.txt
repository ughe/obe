WILLIAM JAMES and JOHN ROBERTS were indicated for a robbery on William Foulkes , on the   16th of February , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 sovereign, 2 half-crowns, 7 shillings, and 4 sixpences, his monies. WILLIAM FOULKES. I am a carman, and live in Rowlett's-buildings, York-street, Westminster. On the night of the 16th of February I was at the Adam and Ere Public-house, in York-street, and saw the two prisoners there—I went from there to the Blue Anchor—when I left there I went in a direction towards my own home—I passed the two prisoners as I came out—there were three together, if not more—it was about twelve or a quarter past twelve o'clock—it might be later—I had got up my own court, about forty or fifty yards from the house—just as I turned up, I heard same people behind, and heard a person say, "There goes the old blank "—I was turning round to go up the court—I had not gone above a dozen yards up the court, before one or two came up behind me, and caught me fast by the back part of my collar and kicked my heels up, and I felt on my back—I saw both the prisoners, and a third person with them—after I fell I received several blows and kicks from the parties—I caught James's hand in my pocket, and caught it by the wrist—there was one sovereign 2 half sovereigns, and some shillings and sixpences—I had get change at the Bell about nine O'clock—I had to the amount of 14 s . alto gether in silver—he got his hand out of the grasp of my hand, and I caught him fast by the leg, and I felt in the meantime the watch pulled out of my pocket—I felt it go—it was fastened by my brace button—I felt it is, I think, James's hand—I cannot be certain it was his—I did not see it—not when I was down—I felt it in one of the prisoner's hands—I endeavoured to recover it—I said as they had had my money, I prayed them not to take my watch—I cannot swear I saw it in the man's hand, but I felt in—I endeavoured to raise myself, and the moment I raised my head, I was struck in the forehead by a kick and a blow, as well from the first—then was an alarm of "Murder", and the police came up—I turned over on my right side when I found them at my watch, and by that means I got the watch out of the prisoner's hand—it was fastened to my brace button—I recovered it in consequence of that—the sovereign and silver were gone—some of the money dropped on the ground when he got his hand out—I am certain some of my money was in his hand at the time I caught hold of it—some of the money had been removed from the bottom of my pocket by the hand before I seized him—that was James's hand.
