MARY ANDERSON. I live in Woolwich. On Tuesday, the 2nd of August.
the two prisoners were in the Royal Standard skittle-ground, at Woolwiek
playing at skittles-they went into a public-house-Istaid there till the house
was shut up-1 went in at four o'clock, and left about half-past ten-they were
there all that time-about seven the next morning I went to the same public
house, and found the prisoners there-Whittle asked me whether I would
show them where the pawn-shop was-he said he wanted to go and pam
his watch-I went with them as far as the pawnbroker's door-Whittle
went in, and White stopped outside- returned to the public-house again,
after that, with them, and staid there till eleven o'clock, when a piquet came
to take them away.
CHARLES Hirons (police-constable R 228.) I was in duty in Wool-
wich about ten minutes to five o'clock in the morning of the 3rd of Au-
gust-I saw the prisoners in Nightingale-vale, coming from Plumstead.
common-White asked me which was the way to Woolwich-they had
turned up a wrong turning, and came back-I am quite sure they are the
two men.
PATRICK COLEMAN. I am a private in the 73rd Regiment of Foot. On
Wednesday evening, the 3rd of August, I was sentry at the Royal Artillery
Barracks at Woolwich--I saw Harris search Whittle-I got a handkerchief
from White, and asked him if it was his-he said no- I asked him if it
belonged to his comrade-he said no-- I kept it- this is it-(producing
it)-the duplicate of a watch was found on Whittle-White was in con-
finement, under military punishment-the prisoners were privates in the
73rd Regiment, which has been at Woolwich seven or eight months-1
never saw the prisoners till that time, to my knowledge,
GEORGE HARRIS (police-constable R 158.) In consequence of some
information, on the afternoon of Wednesday, the 3rd of August, I went to
the barracks, and took Whittle into custody- I told him he was charged
with robbing a man of his watch, and assaulting him on the Queen's high-
way--he said he was quite innocent of the charge-I searched him, and
found, in his left-hand trowsers' pocket, the duplicate of a watch, pawned
for 20s.--I said, "Here is a duplicate, what duplicate is this?"- he said, "It
is my own watch, I have had it for a long time"-I found two handkerchiefs
on him-(one was very bloody indeed, which I found in his breast,) and
48. 6d. in silver-I then went in search of White-I found him in a bed
half an hour after-I searched him, and found on him a comb and a hand-
kerchief.
MICHAEL BROWN (police-inspector R.) The prisoners were brought to
