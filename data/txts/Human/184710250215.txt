   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  What are you? A.  A general dealer—I do not know that Bedford is the wife of a person in Newgate market—I was once in prison for an assault, five or six years ago, and had six months—that was the only time—I had not three months for robbing the poor in the City kitchen—I was employed in carrying the coals for coals for the poor—I was not punished for taking them to my own house instead of to the poor people—I have seen Fury before—I do not know that they are employed at the London News  —I only know Fury by being a fighting-man—I was not perfectly sober on this night—we did not lurch  against Keating—Bedford had not got hold of one of the prisoners by the neckcloth, trying to strangle him, when the others interfered to get her off—I did not strike at all; I never lifted my hand—I do not know that Mrs. Bedford was fighting with Mrs. Hawkins a night or two before this—the front of Fury's shirt was torn, I did that myself, in trying to pull him off Mrs. Bedford. ELIZABETH BEDFORD. I pass by the name of Hockham, as the prosecu tor's wife. About one o'clock in the morning of the 27th of Oct. I was with him in Stamford-street—just opposite Bennett-streets he turned round—I looked round, and saw Fury with this handkerchief in his hand—the other two prisoners were there—Hockham said to Fury, "That is my handkerchief; you are not going to rob me of that"—Fury up  with his hand, and struck him in the face—he fell—Fury fell also, and as soon as he got up he struck me a vio lent blow in the face, knocked me down, and knelt on me; and while I was down, M'Duff caught me by the throat, and pinched me—I said, "Oh they are throttling me!" and Keating came up and kicked me in the ribs—I be came insensible, and do not recollect anything more for a long time—I after wards went to the station in a cab  —I was too ill to walk—I afterwards went to a surgeon—this must have occupied several minutes—I have no doubt of either of the prisoners.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How came you to call yourself the prosecutor's wife? A.  I did not—it was he gave my name—I was too ill—I did not say my name was Elizabeth Bedford—I was not asked—I did not take hold of the front of Fury's shirt, and tear it—I do not recollect touching him—I never fought with Mrs. Hawkins—I have fought with a woman—I am not now under recognizances to keep the peace—I was once for three months, but it is twelve months ago. JAMES BIGLIN( policeman L  133.) I heard a cry of "Police" in Stamford-street—I went to the place as quick as possible, and found all the three pri soners and about half a dozen more—Fury was lying on his side on the ground, and Mrs. Hockham was lying on her side, insensible, by the side of him—Hockham was holding Fury, and M'Duff and Keating were standing by the side of Mrs. Hockam—M'Duff and Fury were given into custody—Keating followed us to the station, to see if they were locked up, and he tried to get bail—I tool him a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes after wards.
