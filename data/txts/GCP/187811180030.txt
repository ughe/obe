HENRY BUTLER. I live at Reigate-in June, 1876, I was staying at my

prisoner answered the advertisement he called at our place of business, 13,
Fenchurch Buildings--I was present at a portion of the interview-he gave
his address as 230, City Road.
HENRY BUTLER, SEN. I live at Elmore, Chipstead-in June, 1876, I
advertised for an indoor servant-the prisoner answered the advertisement
by calling at 13, Fenchureh Buildings- I asked him several questions as to
his age, &c.—he said he had been living with Mrs. Kingswell, 5, North Gate,
Wakefield, Yorkshire, four years, and with Sir Walter Stewart, Belgrave
Square, two years I wrote to Mrs. Kingswell—Albert Buckley was the name
the prisoner gave-I received this reply. (This stated that A. Buckley lived
in her service four years, wus strictly sober, honest, most obliging, and enjoyed
very good health ; good waiter ut table, thoroughly understood his duties, and
was very clean in person and habits)—upon that I engaged him-I am not sure
whether he left of his own accord, but I had no cause of complaint except
that: he had his meals with the other servants.

NEW COURT.-Wednesday and Thursday, November 20 and 21, 1878.
Before Mr. Recorder.
34. JOHN HOLT (56) was indicted for wilful and corrupt perjury.
MR. POLAND and MR. D. STRAIGHT Prosecuted; MR. ASPINALL, Q.C., Defended.
LAUNCELOT CHARLES LIPSCOMBE. I am an officer of the Probate, Divorce,
and Admiralty Division of the High Court of Justice-I produce the original
petition of Rebecca Holt, filed on 7th February, 1878, against John Holt,
praying for a judicial separation on the grounds of cruelty and adultery, the
adultery being alleged to be with Elizabeth Ohlsen. (The Petition was read.)
I also produce the original answer, filed 20th May, 1878, and the reply, dated
25th May, 1878. (The documents were read, also the Answer as to the
Alimony.) The petition came on before Sir R. J. Phillimore on 11th July
last-the petition was dismissed, and there was no order as to costs.
Cross-examined. The solicitors for the petition were Mackinson and
Carpenter, of the Temple, agents for Stevens and Danger, of Liverpool.
