On December 20 I was house surgeon at Guy's Hospital when prosecutor was brought in suffering from a fracture at the ankle joint; both bones of the leg were broken, and there was also a graze on the near side of his foot and a twist of the foot characteristic of the fracture, which might have been caused by his being kicked or else by his twisting his foot as he fell. We kept him in for about 11 days. He will have to be in plaster another five weeks, and then in all probability there will be a permanent disable ment of the ankle joint. He will be able to get about all right, but he will not be able to resume his present employment. CHARLES RIDDING(prisoner, not on oath).
