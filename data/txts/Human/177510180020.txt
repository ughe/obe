Q. Why, both of you knew he was to be hanged if he was found guilty.
 Atkins. I don't know.
 Q. But you told him he should be prosecuted then?
 Atkins. Yes.
 Counsel for the Prisoner.
 You went and got a warrant to apprehend Burdock?
 Atkins. Yes.
 Q. When did you get that warrant?
 Atkins. I think the fourth of September.
 Q. What constable did you give the warrant to, to execute?
 Atkins. Smallwood and Sturton.
 Q. Did you go along with them to execute the warrant?
 Atkins. No; but my husband did.
 Q. What instructions did you give them about the warrant?
 Atkins. I did not know that I had any occasion to give them any.
 Q. But did you desire them to go to the house and call for a pint of beer?
 Atkins. Not to my knowledge.
 Q. Did you desire the constables, or either of them, to take Burdock aside and tell him the business, and if he saw him inclinable to make it up, to come to your husband and settle it, because the constables are in court?
 Atkins. The last time it was, those words were mentioned, the other time no such thing was mentioned. One came and said he could not find him; that he was absconded, and likely he would send some friend to make it up. I said, I did not desire to make it up; but we would go as far as the law would go.
 Q. Did not you tell one of the constables that we will get as much as we can and then let the affair drop?
 Atkins. No.
 Q. There was not a word then about getting what you could?
 Atkins. No. I have had several people mention such a thing to me to be sure.

 THE CONVIVIAL MAGAZINE, AND POLITE INTELLIGENCER; Or a real REPRESENTATION of the CHARACTERS and SENTIMENTS of the TIMES.
 CONTAINING
 The Portraits of the most celebrated Toasts; Men of Rank conspicuous in the World; Statesmen, Orators, Preachers, Petits Maitres, Actors; and Buffoons.
 TO THE PUBLIC.
 THE Authors of this production will not attempt to delude the Public by a parade of words and specious promises, which they are incapable of performing, and shall therefore only mention such particulars as are within their reach, and which they will assiduously exert themselves to display to the almost.
 Besides their seizing every Object that can contribute towards the Entertainment and Instruction of their readers, they will be enabled to lay before them Topics that never yet appeared in a Magazine, by the assistance of some Gentlemen of consequence in polite life, who have promised to transmit them all the amusing conversation that shall pass in the most respectable parties. This will not be confined entirely to the Metropolis, but Bath, and all the other polite Watering-places will furnish them with Materials for cultivating this Correspondence. In this pursuit they will extend their Information from Scarborpugh to Brighthelmstone, and whether the subjects be Gallantry, Intrigue, Politicks, or the Drama, they will equally find a place in this Miscellany, if judged either Amusing or Instructive. This deportment of their Magazine, which is entirely upon a new plan, and which will never exceed the bounds of the strictest truth, will justly entitle it to the Appellation of the CONVIVIAL MAGAZINE.
 In fine, every possible Endeavour will be exerted to render it one of the most entertaining and instructive production extant; in the execution of which the proprietors are resolved that no Pains or expence shall be spared. To this end, they have engaged some of the most capital Artists to furnish them with copper plates which shall not be inferior to those of any Monthly publication whatever.
