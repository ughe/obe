CHARLES HAOGAN (Police Inspector). In August last I was instructed to
make inquiries about the South African General Stores and Trading Company
at 137A Strand—I kept observation upon that house from 20th to 24th
August-I found that Wraxall occupied the first floor there a warrant was
obtained, and I got access to the premises on the 24th-when the postman
brought letters, I looked at them from the Tuesday to the Saturday, on
the Saturday after Wraxall was arrested by an officer placed there to wait
for him, I searched the office and found 12 envelopes and letters
addressed to the Company, to Crowley, and to Sir James Graham, a large
number of envelopes, which had been opened, and which contained letters,
and 36 other letterson 28th August Crowley was handed over to me
at Glasgow by the Scotch police-before I went there I went to
Wraxall's private house, 25, Lambeth Road, and there found this post-
card addressed to him from Crowley: "Dear Sir Harry, If any letters
should come for me, please forward same to me at Queen's Hotel, Man-
chester, but not after to-morrow night. Yours, G. R. Crowley."
when I took Crowley he gave me a great quantity of papers
and at his lodging I found 630 letters answering the advertisement, 60 of
them showing that guineas had been sent, and twenty-three showing that
the guinea had been refused on the way from Scotland he said that he
had met a person who told him he was Sir James Graham who had
originated this scheme, that he had only seen him three times, that on the
third occasion he had part of the money that had been received, atid he had
told him to keep the remainder for himself and for the expenses of the office;
that on the second occasion Sir James Graham took some of the money, about
121., that up to then had been received, and told him to keep the remaindet
for himself, and office experises, that he had not seen him afterwards, and
he then came to the conclusion that the thing was a swindle, but seeing
how easily it worked he had continued it on his own account from the letters
I found, it appeared that a brother of his is mate on board a ship called
the Edith Carmichael, trading to Nova Scotia, there are two or three letters
from his brother to him all these documents found at his lodging are in the
keme handwriting--I found on him a letter of introduction to a gentleman
at New York, showing that he was going there - Wraxall said he had only
had one of the post-office orders, which he cashed at Somerset House.
Cross-examined by MR. PURCELL. He said that order had been given to
him in payment of the rent-I found among Crowley's papers a receipt for
the rent, corresponding with it.
Cross-examined by Mr. GRAIN. I also found amongst Crowley's papers
some letters from a Mr. Harris, a merchant of Bristol, who has an office in
Cannon Street; he was in his employ for 18 months up to March, 1877—
there was a guarantee from the Provident Clerks' Society for 4001, guatan-
teeing his respectability for 10 years back I don't recollect the date of it;
it was for Mr. Harris.
