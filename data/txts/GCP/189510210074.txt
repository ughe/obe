JOHN WHITLOCK. I am a medical practitioner of 17, Behring's
Road-I was called to the body with Mr. Robertson - I was there an
hour before I live close by-I was there when he came up, I waited for
him-I did not touch the body till he came up, I heard that a police-
surgeon had been sent for-I examined the body and afterwards assisted
at the post-mortem--I am acquainted with the evidence he has given and
agree with the conclusions he has drawn.
WILLIAM NASH (Detective Inspector X). I received information on
the early morning of September 8th, and went to where the woman lay
-I was there when the doctors came-I caused inquiry to be made as to
who the woman was I received the knife and the clothes the prisoner
was wearing and sent all of them to Dr. Luff for examination—the stone
was given to me by Sergeant Osborn.
Cross-examined. I have made inquiries in this case, and presented a
report to the Treasury-the report stated that the prisoner's brother
Frederick committed suicide at Bishop Auckland on December 16th,
1892, by cutting his throat-his grandmother, Mary Wingrove, died in
Hanwell Asylum in May, 1883; she had been an inmate there three
years-Constable Conway reported to me that the prisoner had attempted
suicide-he left the army in January, 1895.
