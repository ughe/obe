WILLIAM ASHFORD. I am shopman to Mr. Hawes, a pawnbroker, in
High-street, Shadwell. On Thursday, the 25th of March, a jacket was
pawned by the prosecutor's wife for 78.- on the following Saturday it was
taken out by the prisoner between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning
-she produced the duplicate, paid 78. 14d., and took it out I have both
the duplicates here-- people often come to redeem things which they have
not pledged themselves-- when the prisoner brought the duplicate, she
said, " Make haste, I am in a hurry;" and as soon as it came down, I
gave it to her, she paid me the money, and took it away-nothing further
passed---she did not mention her name she was dressed in black--I have
not the least doubt of the prisoner being the person-the jacket is worth
208. in the trade-- it would cost the man about 21. I should think it was
a beautiful jacket.

of her head when she took the jacket out the policeman has three teeth
in his possession, which he found in her pocket.
John CROXFORD (police-constable K 307.) I apprehended the prisoner
on Saturday evening, the 27th of March-when charged with this, she
said she knew nothing of it-I found 228. 6d. in money on her I took
her to the pawnbroker's, about ten o'clock, to be identified-I did not per-
ceive any person with us, I was standing behind her in the shop, and did
not see her countenance-I put her into the box, for the pawnbroker to
look at her-he did not express much difficulty in identifying her he did
a little-he said, he could tell better by daylight-he did not undertake to
say it was her at that time I did not find any thing on her afterwards
-a female searched her, but I was not present-I have a purse, contain-
ing some teeth, which the sergeant delivered to mor I did not see them
found-the sergeant is not here.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you go to the prisoner's lodgings? 4. Yes
