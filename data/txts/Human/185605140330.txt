ALFRED STRIDE. I am Mr. Munday's cousin, and assist him in his busi ness; he is a butcher, at Woolwich. On Saturday night, 5th April, the pri soner was in Mr. Munday's service—he and I were in the shop, and Mr. Mun day was outside—the scales are about two feet six inches from the till, and the prisoner was between them—there had not been any customers for a few minutes, but about 11 o'clock, Mr. Munday told the prisoner to weigh a piece of fat—I went to get it from a quarter of beef which hung up, and heard a jingle of silver in the till; I turned round, gave the prisoner the fat, he took it from me with his right hand, and I saw him drawing his left hand from the till, with silver in it; his fore finger was not clenched, as he had a bandage on the tip of it—I believe his hand came out of the till—I did not see him literally take it out—on Saturday evenings we take the till and put it near the scale quite open—I whistled, made a signal to Mr. Munday, and pointed to the prisoner's hand—whistling is a general custom of ours, but it had not been agreed upon—Mr. Munday came in, and as he came in the prisoner took out his handkerchief, wiped his nose, or pretended to do so, and as he returned his handkerchief to his pocket, I heard the jingle of silver—he had put the fat on the scale—Mr. Munday said, "George, what have you put in your pocket?"—he said, "Nothing"—he drew his handkerchief out, and there was 14 s . and 1 d . or 2 d .; the prisoner took a slight glance at the money, and then Mr. Munday asked him how much he had, and he said, "About 14 s . 6 d ."—and then, I believe, he put it into his side pocket, from which he had taken it—the pocket was loose; it was a round frock—there was no occasion to go to the till; there was a customer in the shop, but no money was exchanged.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Do you know how much was in the till? A.  No; there might be 20 l ., and there might be 100 l .—I do not interfere with Mr. Munday's business sufficient to know—the prisoner has been there two or three months—I have been there all the time he has been there—there has been no quarrel between us—he has used a great deal of abusive language against me, but never in my presence—I thought it was a boyish action and let it pass; it is about six weeks ago—perhaps he wanted to shut up the shop of an evening, and I wanted to go elsewhere; it was not my duty to do so—I am not paid, I only go to oblige Mr. Munday—I work at the Arsenal, but not as a butcher; I shall not answer you in what way, it is not requisite.
   COURT . Q.  Tell it? A.  Well, Sir, if you have a wish to know, I will tell you; sometimes I assist an engineer there.
   MR. RIBTON . Q.  What else? A.  I think that is sufficient—I shall decline your question—I will not answer—there was not much meat on the counter—the prisoner was to put the piece of fat into the scale to make weight—I did not tell him to move the piece of fat—he was weighing a piece of fat for a customer in the shop—I am confident there was a customer, because Mr. Munday passed the meat in to be weighed—I do not recollect whether I gave the customer any change; I put the money into the till—Mr. Munday went to look for an officer, and the prisoner went across to a public house—while the prisoner and Mr. Munday were talking, the customer paid me—I made no entry of the weight of the beef in any book—I sleep in Union-street, Woolwich—I have done work and been paid for it up to Saturday at dinner time—I was working for Government last Saturday—I do not consider that I am bound to answer when I last received money from anybody for work—I received money from Govern ment last Thursday or Friday—the prisoner's hand was about two inches
