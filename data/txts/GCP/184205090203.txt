RICHARD HOLMES re-examined. This is my handkerchief.com
Prisoner's Defence. I went to the Ordinance Arms with a person
who works at the docks-he went to work, leaving me there--the
prosecutor asked me to drink-I said I had no money to join his company
che afterwards asked me to go with him-we went to the Salutation,
but did not go in; we went from there to the Prince of Wales, and
drank together; he went away with one woman, and I with another ;
he being tipsy, and likely to quarrel with a stone-mason, I did not wish
to remain with him; I left him for two hours, went to the Duke on Horse-
back, and found him there drinking and very tipsy; he called for five
or six pots of beer; as I was leaving the tap-room, he followed me, say-
ing, “If you don't like to stop there, come into the parlour;" he called for
another pot; he sat by my side, and laid his head on the table; he fell
down with his hat on; and was struggling as if in a fit, or in romance ;
I heard a rattling in his throat, and untied his handkerchief and collar;
in a short time he turned over on his side, and I observed under him his
tobacco-box, two half-crowds, and a sixpence, which I took up, and his
bandkerchief laid there; I put that into my hat, I got him up, and put
his handkerchief on, and told him I had got his handkerchief and money:
he said I had robbed him, words arose, and he collared me; I was angry
at the charge ; he said he would call a policeman, opened the parlour
door, and said to the landlord, “I am robbed;" I said, "No, he is not';
I have got two half-crowns and a sixpence;" I brought the bar-maid before
the Magistrate to prove whether I did not offer him the money at the bar;
I hope her evidence is here; I expected her here she swore I did offer
him the money; I did not at the moment recollect that I had his band-
kerchief; he could not know what be was doing: if I was disposed to
have robbed him, I could have walked out at any time. Al s
