 851.  WILLIAM WARE was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of March , a pair of boots, value 25 s., the goods of James Simms , privately in his shop.
 JAMES SIMMS. I am a  boot and shoemaker, and live in Upper East Smithfield. About twenty minutes before eight o'clock, on the 29th of March, two women came into my shop, and gave me some information: I looked in my window, and missed a pair of boots which I had been dusting about five minutes before - I had gone towards the parlour-door and turned back, and then I heard the intelligence - I saw them next morning in the possession of the officer.
   JAMES FOGG. I belong to the Thames Police. On the night of the 29th of March I saw the prisoner in Rosemary-lane with this pair of boots - he had another man with him. I asked where he got them, he said he had made them that afternoon at home - I asked where that was - he said it was of no use telling me, for if I went I should not be satisfied.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I have lately returned from St. Domingo. I had been to the West India Docks, and as I returned, I met a young man with a pair of boots in his hand; he asked if I would go with him and sell them; he said he and his father had made them - I took the boots to carry, and as we were going the officer met me; I said I had made them myself - from what the young man had told me; I should not have said so, but I thought he was a gentleman who wanted to buy them.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined One Month.
 852.  CHARLES WHITE was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of April , a till, value 2 s.; seven shillings and a hundred and twenty penny pieces, the property of John Jones.
 JOHN JONES. I live at the  Red Lion public house Silver-street Golden-square. On the 4th of April, the prisoner whom I have known for some years, came into my house with a Mr. West; he stayed there about an hour and a half - I saw him go out running, but did not see anything in his hand; I followed, and saw him running - he turned round a street to Carnaby market - he there fell down - I assisted him up - and when I returned, I found the till down in the passage of the bar - it was safe about five minutes before.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was it not lying on the counter? A. No, it was in its place, the prisoner had been drinking and appeared a little intoxicated.
   MARIA JONES. On the night of the 4th of April, the till was safe about half-past eight o'clock; the prisoner had been there some time - in two or three minutes afterwards I heard a running in the passage - I saw the till being conveyed over the counter by the prisoner, and then it either fell down by accident or purposely; he did not move off with it, but when it fell, he ran off immediately - he was very tipsy.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was very much intoxicated, and heard the cry of Stop thief! I ran and was taken.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Recorder.
 853.  WILLIAM RUDLAND was indicted for embezzlement.
   FRANCIS ASHDOWN. I am a dyer. I live at No. 33, Brook-street, Holborn. The prisoner has been in my employ since September twelve months; he was intrusted to take out small parcels - to receive bills and give discharges - it was his custom to bring my money every day as he received it - it was sometimes entered by him, and sometimes by me, but always on the day it was received; on the 11th of January, I sent him to receive money and he called on a customer named Mrs. Barker; Maria Thacker was her servant; I have no entry of any money received from her on the 11th of January, or since that time; I am quite certain he never paid it to me, or to any other person belonging to me; I told him on Thursday, the 17th of February, that he must leave on the following Saturday; in consequence of which he did not come to me the next day; I gave him a sovereign a week, and his board, and he had had it on the Saturday before - he never called afterwards to receive what was due to him for the remaining days; he was apprehended on Friday, the 25th of February, I knew him before I took him, and had formerly lived in the same situation with him; I called on Mrs. Barker the Saturday after he left me, and found her money had been paid.
   MARIA THACKER. I am in the employ of Mrs. Barker, No. 6, Kirby-street. The prisoner lived at Mr. Ashdown's; he called at our house on the 11th of January, and brought a bill of 1 l., 7 s., which I took to my mistress in the parlour; she gave me the money which I paid him; I do not know whether I gave him two sovereigns or not, but I am quite sure I gave him one; I took him the pen and ink, and he signed the bill while I stood by; Mr. Ashdown called afterwards, I told him I had paid his servant.
   SAMUEL LAUNDER. I am a constable. I apprehended the prisoner on the 25th of February - I told him the nature of the charge as we were going along, and he said it was but little, could it not be made up? I said he had better ask the Magistrate.
     GUILTY. Aged 39. Transported for Seven Years.
