RICHARD KEEN (prisoner, not on oath). A few days after
I had spoken to my mistress about Mrs. Munro stopping me
going with Louie, I met Louie and she passed right by me.
She would not look at me, and she made me very wild. When
I got home I sat at the table and thought of these different
things--the way she had spoken to me when she threw me
over, and all the things that had happened, and I wrote that
etter. I can explain anything that I wrote. She told me her
mistress used to keep her in late so that she could not meet
me. As her mistress had told her I would make a nice sweet.
heart for her if I did not go too far I thought that must be lies.
Then she said her sister said she would have no more to do
with her if she did not break off acquaintance with me. I
thought to myself, “Her sister has never seen me yet." I told
Louie that when she said her sister said I was not fit for her.
