1771. ROBERT SIMONS, was again indicted, with SUSAN HARRIS,
for stealing four pairs of gloves, 1 pair of cuffs, and 1 handkerchief; value
17s.; the goods of Edward Lawson and another, the masters of Simons—
2nd. COUNT, charging Harris with receiving: to which SIMONS pleadd
GUILTY.
CHARLES DAVISON. I am barman to Mr. Gurney, of the Clarendon,
Oxford-street. I have known the prisoners eight weeks—Simons used to leave
parcels, and said he would call for them himself, but if the female called
was to have them ; but she never did—sometimes he took them away
she was with him, and sometimes he used to take them away alone—he went
by the name of Mr. Harris. and she Mrs. Harris—the female called n
Saturday, 28th Sept., about eight o'clock, and asked if Mr. Harris had
there—she left again, and I followed her to Mr. Lawson's, 211, Oxford.
street—she waited by the door a few minutes, and Simons came out of the
shop to her—they spoke to each other, and he went in again—a few minute
after he came out and spoke to her again, and a third time also—she
then three or four doors from the shop, and he appeared to put his hand to
his coat-pocket and to put his hand towards her, I cannot say exactly, as I
was on the opposite side of the way—he then went into the shop again, and •be
went to Mr. Gurneys—I got back first, and was in the house before her—when
she came she had a small parcel—I then served her with something—my
master sent to Mr. Lawson, and he came himself—there were then two
parcels at our house, which Simons had left, one on the Thursday and one n
the Friday, and which he was to call for that night—Mr. Lawson examind
those—about the fourth time Simons came, he told me he was going to com.
mence housekeeping—the female prisoner never brought any parcels but
one that night.
EDWARD LawsoN. I am a draper, in partnership with John Palmer—
—Simons was in our service six months—on Saturday night, 28th
about nine o'clock, I received information, called a policeman, and went with
him to Mr. Gurney's, where I found three parcels of our goods—this is a
I made of them at the station (reads—c• 23 muslin collars, 18 pairs of gloves,
9 cuffs, 30 postage stamps, 2 lengths of blonde, 6 cambric handkerchiefs, 7
lengths of ribbon, 1 length of lace, 23 handkerchiefs, paper of pins, 4
bunches of flowers, I cap front, 12 shop tickets, 1 knife and fork, and half-
a-yard of lace insertion)—these are them are our property—I
know them by the private marks on them—I gave the female prisoner into
custody, and went to 19, Cooper-street, Finsbury, which address she gave u
her lodging, and there found other things—I examined Simons's boxes, and
found 2 lengths of cloth and some silk—he lived at our house, and sleß
there, except on Saturday and Sunday nights—he told me that on
nights he slept at his uncle's.
JAMES MACKINTOSH (policeman, D 68). I took Simons into custody at
Mr. Lawson's, and Harris at Mr. Gurney's—sbe said she lodged at 19,
Cooper-street.
MARY ANN BRIDGE. I am the wife of Thomas Bridge, of the Marylebone
station-house, and search females brought there—I searched the female pri•
soner, and found 4 pairs of silk gloves, and a silk handkerchief in her hand'
which I saw her take from her pocket, and these cuffs she had on (produced).
Harris's Defence. I was not aware they were stolen.
HARRIS, GUILTY, on 2nd Count. Aged 25.—Confned Two Months.
