FANNY GOLDSMITH, and MARY ANN GOLDSMITH, were indicted for stealing 251bs. weight of coals, value 4 d  .; 13lbs. of candles, 6 s  .; 81bs, of soap, 3 s  .; 1 yard of carpet, 6 d  .; 8 candlesticks, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 butter-stamp, 6 d  .; 1 cup, 3 d  .; 3 jugs, 1 s  .; 2 towels, 8 d  .; 1 printed book, 3 d  . 1 roasting-jack, 6 d  .; 1 brush, 6 d  .; 21bs. of coffee, 2 s  .; 21bs. of tea, 10 s  .; 2lbs. of butter, 2 s  .; 14 eggs, 1 s  .; 21bs. of sugar, 1 s  .; 1 tumbler, 10 d  .; 1 pot of jam, 1 s  .; 3 basins, 3 d  .; and 1 quart of groats, 6 d  .; the goods of Angelina Pope . WILLIAM FULLER( police-constable P  190.) On Thursday night, the 12th of Feb., I was watching the house of Mrs. Pope, and about eleven o'clock I saw Fanny Goldsmith come from her house, towards Camberwell—I stopped her when she got about 100 yards, and asked what she had got under her cloak—she said, "Some coals and wood"—I said, "Then you must go back with me to the house"—she produced two bags containing coals—she said she had them unknown to any one, and if I would let her go, she would take them back—I took her back, and Mrs. Pope gave her in charge—as I was taking her to the station she dropped a bag—I said, "You have dropped something"—she said, "I do not know anything about it"—it was a bag containing candles and soap—there was another policeman there, but there was no one else who could have dropped it—I took her to the station, and returned to Mrs. Pope's—the prisoner Mary Ann was there, and Mrs. Pope gave her in charge—I was taking her to the station—she said she knew no thing about the things that were taken—she went a little further, and then said, "I suppose we shall both be transported"—I then went with Mr. Campbell to Fauny Goldsmith's house—I found there several articles of stolen property, and some duplicates—these are them.
   Fanny Goldsmith. Q.  When the policeman followed you from the Rye he said, "Stop, I have something here," and it appeared to be the bag? A.
