   Q.  Tell us very particularly what she had on when she left on Sunday? A.  A blue bonnet, which I should know again, a shawl with a dark ground and coloured flowers on it, a dark printed dress, and black boots—the bon net now produced is hers, and what she went out in that day—she also had such a dress as this on—I believe it to be the same—I have seen her wear it many times—I cannot say these are the boots—they are like what she wore—this is her rent-book—it has my own handwriting in it—I do not know this fur tippet.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I suppose you have seen all the articles of wear ing apparel before? A.  Yes, they were produced at the Police Court-the woman took my lodging—I cannot positively say that when she took it she said, "My name is Jane Jones"—I think I can swear I have heard her call herself Jane Jones—that was on her board, and she was always called by that name—they called her Mrs. Jones—I wrote "Jane Jones" on her rent-book—the board now has "J. Good" on it, but before it was " Jose  Jones," not "J"—it is nearly three years since Jones's name was tuned into Good—I noticed that—I cannot positively swear it was Jane on  it before, but it was "Jones"—I was first called on to give evidence on the 7th of April—the police spoke to me.
   COURT . Q.  Did you see her go away on the Sunday? A.  Yes, she had no bundle with her.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Have you any doubt of these being the things she wore as she went out on the Sunday? A.  Not the least. SARAH YOUNG. I am a widow, and live at No. 89, East-street, Man chester-square. I knew Mrs. Good—she was called Mrs. Good, and lodged at No. 18, South-street, at Mrs. Brown's—she came to me on Sun day morning, the 3rd of April, and asked if I would take the little boy for one night, whilst she went down to him—she particularly mentioned that it was for one night only—I knew the boy who lived with her—he was not with her then—he was at school—he came to me about half-fast twelve o'clock, went to church, came back in the evening, and slept at my house—he went away about half-past eight in the morning, and did not come back again till next day—he did not sleep at my house at all after Sunday night—I never saw Mrs. Good after the Sunday.
