ELIZABETH PAYNE. I live at No. 18, Providence-place, Lisson-street; I
am single; I know Mr. Guyenette very well by sight. On Saturday, 21st
May, between 1 and 2 o'clock, I saw him at Mr. Weet's, in Clarges-street-
I bad been to Mr. Nerton's, and on returning bome went in there-Nerton
was the former landlord- I heard Mr. Guyenette ask for a pint of brandy
-be asked him whether he would take pale or brown.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Give the words as near as
you can ? A. I have told you as near as I can I was twelve months in
the house when it was kept by Nerton-I knew the prisoner by his coming
backwards and forwards- I was servant of all work-I cannot tell in what
terms he asked for the brandy-there might be six or seven people there,
sitting and standing about the bar-I might have been there half an hour or
three quarters- I was in the parlour-if any one went out I could see them
-I could not say who came in when the prisoner asked for the brandy I
was against the parlour door-I remained for a quarter of an hour-
remember the prisoner asking for brandy, because he came in and shook
hands with me I did not see him go away, or see him served with the
brandy.
MR. PARRY. Q. You say he came into the parlour and shook hands with
you; was that before he asked for the brandy? A. Before.
