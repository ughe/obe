The statements of the prisoners before the magistrate were read as follows:
Edward Stack says, “It is the first time I have ever been in a court or
prison in my life. I left the mother in-doors in charge of the child, when I
went to have a glass of ale with a party. I did not think I was in duty
bound to take care of the child at all, being so young." Honora Stack
says, “When the child first was born, he never cared about drink-
ing from the breast. I put it in his mouth, and he would give one sack,
and leave it out of his mouth again; the nurse or the doctor never ordered
me any castor oil for the child. I gave it castor oil ; & tea spoon full at
a time, as far as it wanted. Likewise I used to give it a little drop of
my own gruel at night, and then I got a little biscuit in boiling water,
milk, and sugar, beat them up very fine, and give it to him, and then the
nurse told me it was a seven-month child, and she never came to see the
child. She said she would send the doctor. The doctor came on the Friday,
found me in the bed, and he said, "That little child will not live; it is over
seven-month child ; between seven and eight months ;' then the nurse never
ca me till the Sunday to look after the child, and I told the doctor I had to
wash it myself as the nurse did not come. The doctor said, It is too soon
for you to put your hands in water.' I told the doctor I did not open the
child at all, but washed its legs down, to keep its legs clean till the nurse
came. I tried as well as I could, and every day I went out I washed the
child and cleaned him, and did not open him at all. I went out for my
husband for fear of his taking a drop to drink too much. The child ate
some victuals on the Sunday morning, and then the Sunday evening he did
not take anything, only drink. On the Monday he closed his lips entirely
against drinking or taking anything from me."
JAMES KEEN. I am & surgeon's assistant in Norton Folgate-on Friday,
the 11th August, the day after this child was born, I went to No. 6, Little
Pearl-street-I merely made an official visit to see if the midwife had
attended to her duty-I saw the child; it did not require any medical
attendance that I was aware of-I did not tell the female prisouer that the
child would not live-I said nothing to her about it, or about it being too soon
for her to put her hands in water-I have nothing to do with those things;
I always leave them to the midwife.
Cross-examined. Q. How long did your visit last ? A. About three or
four minutes - it was merely as a matter of form, to call to see that the mid-
wife had attended to her duty--on the average we visit about one hundred
women every day who have been confined
