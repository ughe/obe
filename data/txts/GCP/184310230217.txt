MR. CLARKSON. Q. What time did you dine on Wednesday ? A. We
generally dined about two o'clock-we took tea about six-I think he did not
stay at home the rest of the evening-he merely took his meals with me--he
told me at supper that he had been to chapel-he went out after tea, and re-
turned in the evening. I cannot say at what time, perhaps about nine-he told
me the text, and the particulars. or

Court. 0. Did you ever visit at his house? A. Yes I have called,
and have seen one female servant there--the younger prisoner is his only
grown up son--there are three children altogether the others are quite
children, not old enough to be witnesses I know the days on which I saw
Mr. Bridgman to be the 5th, 6th, and 7th, because I went down on the 23rd
of Aug., and had been there a fortnight all but a day--my eldest son was to
have come down to spend the last week-I intended remaining four weeks,
but the weather being exceedingly fine I wrote to him at the end of the
second week to come down a week earlier, and he came down on the 5th, the
day Mr. Bridgman did-he is not here I have a letter here which he wrote on
the Monday, saying that he should come down the following day if fine-
here is the post-mark of 4th Sept. on it on the front is the Walworth
post-mark, and on the other part is the Ramsgate post-mark of the 5th
Sept.- he says on the outside, "I intend coming down to-morrow"-he
dined and drank tea with Mr. Bridgman as well as myself.
EDWARD MASON THOMPSON. I am the son of Mr. Thompson, who has
been examined-I was with him at Ramsgate in Sept. -I know Mr. Bridg-
man, and saw him at Ramsgate on Thursday the 7th of Sept., between
one and two o'clock, on the pier with my father.
Court to GEORGE GARFORD. Q. You state that this occurred on Wed-
Desday the 6th, what fixes the day in your mind ? 4. I can scarcely say
what can bring it to my remembrance now-I know I was with Mr.
Tawney on the following Monday.
JOHN COLSON. I am an officer of Guy's Hospital. The younger defen-
dant has been a student of surgery there for some time, and had the charac-
ter of an assiduous, zealous student.
J. W. BRIDGMAN-Guilty. Aged 17.-Confined Twelve Months.
