 1389. EDWARD GEORGE was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and ESPINASSE conducted the Prosecution . JOSEPH WOOD. I am thirteen years old. I live in Rhode-street Greenwich, with Allen, a police-constable—on the 7th of April I saw the prisoner between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, in Rhode-street—he asked me to go into Mr. Moore's shop, to get him a quarter of an ounce of tobacco, and he gave me a sixpence to pay for it—I got the tobaca, and gave Mrs. Moore the sixpence—she gave me change—I did not see where she put the sixpence—I went to the prisoner and gave him the change, and he ran away—he had promised me a halfpenny for my trouble before I went in, but he did not give it me.
   Prisoner. Q.  Was there any body with me? A.  No—I gave you 5 1/4 d . change. AMELIA MOORE. My husband's name is George Moore. He is a beer-seller in Rhode-street, Greenwich—I saw Wood on the 7th of April, at near nine o'clock in the evening—I knew him before—he came for a quarter of an ounce of tobacco—it came to 3/4 d .—he gave me a 6 d .—I gave him change—I took the sixpence up, and found it was bad—I gave it to my husband to examine, and he went out with it to see for the little boy—I got the sixpence from my husband, and put it on a shelf by itself—I afterwards marked it, and gave it to the policeman. GEORGE MOORE. I am the husband of Amelia Moore. I received a sixpence from her on the 7th of April, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening—I looked at it, and said it was a bad one—I went to the uncle of Joseph Wood, and I gave the sixpence to one Fisher—he gave it me back, and I gave it my wife—she put it on a shelf—nobody but me my wife has access to that shelf.
   Prisoner. Q.  You said you took it to the boy's mother? A.  No, he has no mother—it was not out of my hand. MARY ANN BEDWELL. I am the daughter of James Bed well; he lives in Thames-street, Greenwich. I saw the prisoner on the 10th of April—he asked me where, they sold tobacco—I told him, in Mr. Barnes's shop—he said that would not do—he then gave me a sixpence, and told me to go and get a pennyworth of tobacco—I went to Mr. Home's shop, and asked for a pennyworth of tobacco—I got it, and gave the sixpence which the prisoner gave me—I got the change, and I went back to the prisoner—I gave him the change and the tobacco—he gave me another sixpence to get a penny candle—he did not tell me where to go—I went to Mr. Waller's, and got a candle, and gave the sixpence for it, which he gave me—I got the change and the candle—I gave the change to the prisoner—he gave me the candle for my trouble.
