I live with my aunt, Mrs. Fox—I retired to bed about twelve o'clock—I was the first that came down the next morning—the shop was then broken open, and the property gone—I had not heard any one in the night. SUSANNAH FISH. I recollect the prisoner coming to me on the Sunday morning, between nine and half-past nine o'clock; he tendered me some farthings—I thought they were bad, he said they were good—he gave me eight farthings, and asked me to give him 2 d . for them, which I did—I marked one of the farthings—this is it. MARY JOHNSON re-examined.  I missed some farthings from the till, I cannot say how many—I know two of these farthings produced by Mrs. Fish—I offered them in the shop on the Friday, and Saturday they were refused as being bad. GEORGE FORTUNE( police-sergeant K  42.) I traced the footsteps to the back of Mrs. Fox's premises—I had suspicion of the prisoner, and compared his shoes with the footsteps—they corresponded exactly, nail to nail. JAMES WHITE( police-sergeant K  13.) I compared the prisoner's shoes with the marks with George Fortune—the property was found concealed in a dunghill; it was in a putrid state, and could not be produced—the pro secutrix saw it. GUILTY  of Stealing only. Aged 31.— Transported for Seven Years.

PATRICK MURPHY was indicted for stealing, on the   20th of January , 1 ring, value 3 s  .; I star, value 3 l  .; 1 sovereign, 1 half-sovereign, 1 crown, 1 half-crown, 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence, the property of Charlotte McLean : and JOHN MILES  , alias    Sweeney , for feloniously receiving 1 star, part of the said goods, well knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.—2nd COUNT  , for receiving 3/4 oz. weight of silver. CHARLOTTE MCLEAN. I am widow of General Sir Joseph McLean; I at present live in Wood-street, Woolwich. On the 24th of December I resided at Woolwich Common—my house was destroyed by fire that night, and the furniture that was saved was moved to a stable in the Royal Artillery barracks at Woolwich—about a fortnight after the fire I saw a star of the Hanoverian order of Knighthood at the Commandant's house—it was in a chest of my drawers—I had possession of the keys of the drawers—some of the furniture was afterwards removed from the stable to Wood-street—the military assisted in moving it—after it had been so removed I examined the drawer in which the star had previously been, and it was missing—there were marks of the lock being broken open, picked, as if the drawer had been forced—I also missed a gold ring which was cracked, and a purse with some gold and silver in it—I afterwards saw some pieces of silver at the Commandant's house at Woolwich, which decidedly belonged to the Hanoverian Order, but there was not the whole of it—it was mutilated.
