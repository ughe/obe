EDWARD BARKER( Policeman ). In consequence of information, on Sun day,28th March, I concealed myself in the prosecutor's house, when he went out; the prisoner came in and went up stairs, and when he came down, I came out from my hiding place and took him; he had fastened himself in with the padlock—I found the half crown and florin on him—he had 9 s . 1 d . altogether.

 494. WILLIAM CARDING  (27) , Burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling house of Richard Cooke , with intent to steal. GEORGE ROOTS( Police sergeant, D  7). At half past 12 o'clock, on the morning of 1st March, I was on duty in Orchard Street—I heard a noise in the Samaritan Hospital—I went back, got over a wall, and examined the premises—I found the place had been broken open—I came back and got assistance, and it the house I found the prisoner, apparently drunk and asleep—Igot him no—I am positive he was sober—I asked him what he was doing there—he said, "Nothing"—I asked him how be got there—he said he did not know—I found marks on the donation box corresponding with this knife which I found on the prisoner. LOUIS RICHARD COOK. I am house surgeon there. I was called up about half past 12 o'clock—I went down, and saw the prisoner—my impression is, that he was not quite sober; he could not have been very drunk, or he could not have got in—I did not notice any smell of spirits. JANE MOULTON. I am nurse in the Samaritan Hospital. I saw marks on the passage door on the next morning—it had been forced open—it was all right the night before—the panes of glass were all right on the Sunday night—Iknow the prisoner, his wife was in our service—I had seen the prisoner there.
   Prisoner.  My wife told you the night previous how I was situated. Wit  ness. She said you were very drunk. GUILTY.*— Confined Twelve Months .
