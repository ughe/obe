JOHN HOOTON. I am a gunner in the Royal Artillery, Harrison is a
bombardier- I was in the officers' kitchen one Tuesday-Harrison asked me
to carry a bundle of leather out for him-I said I would, and I did carry it
for him, it was on the floor in the kitchen, wrapped up in matting—it was
about five feet long I should think I could not tell what the colour of it
was, the matting entirely covered it, it felt like leather-Harrison went in
front of me and I carried it about a mile from the barracks--I dou't know
the name of the place, we went to a beer-shop kept by Brooks-Harrisou
said, “ Put it down there" and I put it into a little parlour on the right-
hand side, Brooks was in another room at that time he did not see us come in,
-it was about half past six or it might have been seven when we got there
-I went to the bar and had & glass of ale, Harrison did not go with me :
while I was drinking at the bar i saw him and Brooks go into the parlour
where the leather was put, I could see the door, it was shut-I should say
they were there about ten minutes the leather was on the floor, the room was
lighted by gas--they afterwards came outside, and I left the house soon
after-there was a window in the parlour facing the street, the blind was
drawn down on the Thursday following I pointed out the house to a police-
constable-I knew it well, I don't know the name of it
Cross-examined by Mr. H. PALMER. Q. What time did you go to the
officers' kitchen that day! A. I was there all day in and out-I am an
officer's servant, it was my right place to be- I did not notice the leather till
about half-past six in the evening, it was then in the centre of the kitchen
on the floor- I have been in the service twelve years, I have deserted once,
only once, there was a second charge for desertion preferred against me, and
a third-1 suffered punishment, there is nothing against me now-I was put
under arrest on this night-I was not charged by some men in the regiment
with having stolen this leather, never--the provost-sergeant spoke to me
about this he asked me if I knew anything at all about some leather that
was taken out of the barracks-I had not spoken to anyone about it till
then, and I should not if I had not been asked about it I did not know
what it was—I was not called out of the ranks by my colonel on parade and
told that I was worse than Harrison-I saw no one at the beer-shop except
Brooks, I was there about ten minutes, it was dusk then.
John NEWTON. I am a sergeant in the Royal Artillery-on Thursday even-
ing, 23 February, I went to Brooks's beer shop with some of the other soldiers
-I remained downstairs with Crouch and Brooks—some leather bad been
found then I said to Brooks, "I suppose it has cost you as much as if you
had bought it lawfully”-he said, "D- d near As much "-Crouch said,
"You seem to take it pretty coolly"-Brooks said, " It is no use doing any.
thing else ; I have got into it, and I must get out of it the best way I can."
Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY. Q. Where were you at the time A. In
a little parlour, what he called & shop-he had got harness hanging round
it; I suppose it was a parlour at one time, and he had turned it into a shop
-I made no memorandum of the conversation-I put questions on my own
account-I belong to the military police.
