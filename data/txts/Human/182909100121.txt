 1776.  HENRY TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of July , 7 books, value 2l., the goods of Robert Wilks.
   ROBERT WILKS. I am a bookseller in Chancery-lane. On the 10th of July the prisoner came between five and six o'clock in the evening, and looked at seven volumes of Law Reports, which were in my window; he said he had a brother in the law, who he was pretty certain would be glad to buy them, if I would send them to an office he had in Leadenhall-street - I thought he was on no good, and wanted to get rid of him; he said if they were sent that evening, and I would call the next morning, he would settle for them - I then went out, and did not return till eight o'clock; my shop was then shut up.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not tell the boy to dust them? A. No; you remarked that they were dirty, and I said the boys could easily clean them - they might be put on one side for the purpose of being dusted - these are the books, and here is another book which I know nothing of.
   THOMAS WOOD. I am shopman to Mr. Wilks. The prisoner came after Mr. Wilks was gone out, and said he was waiting for me to take the books down to his chambers in Leadenhall-street - I was not present at his interview with Mr. Wilks; I then went with him, and left the books at No. 25, in the East India chambers - he opened the chamber with a key, and opened a desk which he said he meant to put the books into; I did not return to my employer till the next morning, when he desired me to go anddemand the books: back - I went, and waited till the prisoner came; I said I came for the books to save me from any further noise - he said it was a fair bargain betwen Mr. Wilks and him, and his brother could not come to town that day; I went across the way, and saw the prisoner come out and go to Mr. Higham's - when he came out, I went there, and asked if he had come to sell or to buy books, and the books were on the counter; I told them to detain him if he came again.
   SARAH HIGHAM. My father is a bookseller, and lives at No. 54, Chiswell-street. On the 11th of July the prisoner came there, about nine o'clock in the morning, with a porter, and brought these books, which are Dowling's Reports; he wanted 4l. for them - my father said he would not give that, as one of them was spoilt with ink; he then said he would take 50s. - my father said he would not have them till they were looked over, and he was desired to call at two o'clock; he said he could not call till four - he requested to have a few shillings, and we let him have 3s.; the witness then came in, and gave us information - the prisoner came at twelve o'clock, and we told him they were so much damaged he had better take them away, and return the few shillings; he said he had more to sell, and he would bring them - Mr. Wood came in, and he was detained.
   JOHN LEWIS. I am a porter. I was employed by the prisoner to carry the books.
   ANDREW WOOD WEST. I am a headborough. I took the prisoner when Mr. Higham sent for me - he asked where I was going to take him; I said to take him up - he wanted me to go to Mr. Wilks, and then to Hackney; I said I was not authorized to go any where - when we got to the corner of Bunhill-row he ran off, and I called Stop thief! a man ran against him, and I came up to him.
   ROBERT WILKS. These are the books. I never intended he should have them, and had not sold them to him - they are labelled nine guineas.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to Mr. Wilks' shop to look at the volumes; he ordered them to be dusted, and said they should come that evening - I went in an hour after, and saw the shopman; I said Mr. Wilks had agreed to send them to my office - the shopman took them down; a person came, who looked at them, and said they were in so bad a condition they were not worth 5s. - under that conviction, and thinking Mr. Wilks would take less, I asked the low price I did for them; Mr. Wilks now says he did not intend I should have them - but he said I should, and he knew they were for immediate sale; Mr. Higham said they were worth nothing but for waste-paper, and wished me to take the covers off them.
   LINDLEY JOHN HIGHAM. I did not say so - I had a suspicion, from the manner in which he took the 3s., that they were not honestly come by, and left word with my family to tell him to return the 3s., and take them away.
   THOMAS WOOD. The name "Mr. Turner" is on the chamber door.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 1777.  EDMOND TOMS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 1 watch, value 10s., the goods of James John Tyler.
   JAMES JOHN TYLER. I am a working goldsmith. On the 24th of July I lost my watch from my house, No. 6, Great Chesterfield-street. Marylebone- I did not miss it till the next morning, when the woman it belonged to came and asked if it was ready; I then missed it from my work-board, where I had left it - I know the prisoner by his coming two years ago, and offering me some spoons, which I did not buy.
   MARY ANN TYLER. Between six and seven o'clock that evening the prisoner came to sell a seal - he remained about a quarter of an hour; the shop-board was open to him at the time - I left the board while he was there, but we did not miss the watch till next morning.
   DANIEL CARTER. I am in the service of Mr. Daniels, a baker, in South-street, Manchester-square. On the 1st of August the prisoner came up to me in George-street, and asked if I wanted to buy a watch - I said Yes, I was going to buy one soon; he took this watch from his pocket, put it into my hand, and said he wanted 7s. for it - I saw there was one of the hands broken, and it wanted a glass; I gave him 4s. 6d. then, and 2s. 6d. in the evening - I took it to Mr. Berquez to have it repaired.
   FRANCIS BERQUEZ. Carter brought this watch to me on the 1st of August; it is worth 5s. or 6s.
