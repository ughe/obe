Before Mr. Justice Maule.
602. GEORGE JOHNSON, alias Adam Mumford, was indicted for
stealing, on the 29th of November, 1 mare, price 10l., the property of
Samuel Beckwith
SAMUEL BECKWITH. I am a smith, and live at Hoddesden. On the
28th of November I had a mare in Hoddesden-marsh- saw her safe at
eleven o'clock that morning- missed her on Wednesday, the 1st of De.
cember, about ten in the morning-] went on the Friday following to Mr.
Large's stables, at Lambeth-I saw my mare there, and got ber back-
she is worth 101.-the prisoner was servant to a horse-dealer in our town
about eighteen months ago-he went by the name of Adam Mumford.
Cross-eramined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. What was the name of the horse-
dealer he was servant to! A. Thomas King-I am sure the mare is mine
-I had not used it often for my own use-I had seen it twenty times in
the last three or four months-it had three white legs and a white face
had been in the habit of shoeing it for two years before it was mine.
THOMAS THEAKE. I live at the Fisher and Eel publie-house, opposite
the mead at Hoddesdon. On Monday, the 29th of November, I saw the
prisoner at the house-I had not seen him before, but I am sure it was
him-he was there between five and six o'clock in the evening-] cannot
say what time he came-he asked my master who that mare belonged to
with the white face and three white feet, which was over the bridge in the
mead-my master said he did not know, except it was Beckwith's--th
prisoner said King had sent him down after it, to get it up for him, while
he was gone to Harlow fair-when he left our house he went over the
bridge.
Cross-examined. Q. What makes you sure the prisoner was the man
A. His face-] might be with him half an hour-I was sitting down
while he had the conversation with my master, who was having his tea-
the prisoner was having a pint of beer-J have to look after the lock.
EDMUND LARGE. I am a green-grocer, and live in Lambeth-walk
On the 30th of November a mare was brought to me to be changed-I
changed it for another mare-I should know the man who brought it, if I
were to see him-I should say it was not the prisoner, decidedly-I after-
wards found my mare in a knacker's-yard-I then supposed the other
mare was stolen-I went to Smithfield and gave notice to the horse-
dealers, and through that Mr. Beckwith heard of it, and claimed it.
WILLIAM GORDON. I am a coal-dealer; and live in Bird-street, Lam-
beth. I was present when a man gave Mr. Large another mare for his-
the prisoner, to all appearance, is much larger than that man-he was
quite a slim boy chap.
(RICHARD LETCH, being called on his recognizance, did not appear.)
JAMEs Eves (police-sergeant H 14.) On the 3rd of December I was
in Whitechapel-road, between eleven and twelve o'clock at night--the
prisoner came out of the Royal Oak public-bouse, and I took him-]
asked what his name was-he asked what I wanted to know for-I said I
had a reason for asking him-he said he should not tell me-I asked
where he lived-he said in London- asked what part of London-he
said, Any where"- then told him I believed his name to be Adam
Mumford, and he was wanted for stealing a mare from Hoddesdon-marsh
-he said he knew nothing about it, his name was George Johnson-
