MARIANA LIMPERANI. I was walking with my mother that evening—I perfectly recollect three persons being about us—I identify the prisoner as being one of them—I have not any doubt about it—we met them first —they then turned back and walked immediately behind us—I turned then and looked at them a second time, and after my mamma had disco vered the robbery I saw the prisoner at the corner for a moment, but he turned away—I did not see him again till he was in custody—I have not any doubt that be is the man.
   Cross-examined. Q.  This was about five o'clock? A.  Yes—it was a light afternoon—I had noticed the three men—I have always been equally positive that the prisoner was one of the men I saw—this is my handwriting to this deposition.
   Q.  Did you not say, "To the best of my belief the prisoner was one of those persons?" A.  Yes; I only swore to the best of my belief, and so I do now.
   COURT . Q.  You have said to-day, "I am sure he is the man;" do you mean to the best of your belief? A.  Yes, of course. THOMAS BALES( police-constable P  191.) On the evening of the 12th of November I saw the prisoner at the corner of the Albany-road—I knew him—I afterwards received information from the prosecutrix, and went in the direction she pointed out—I met the prisoner—I asked if he knew any thing about a purse—he said, "No, what purse?"—I took him to the station—he pulled out of his left-hand pocket ten shillings and two six-pences, and he said that was all he had got—the two shillings now pro duced are part of what he produced.
   JURY . Q.  How long elapsed between the prosecutrix speaking to you and your finding the prisoner? A.  About five minutes—he had got about a quarter of a mile from the place—I looked over the road, but did not see the purse. GUILTY.† Aged 25.— Transported for Ten Years.
