1050. RICHARD LANE was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of May , three live tame fowls, price 6 s., the property of Joseph Cable.
   JOHN DAVIS. I am a watchman of Clerkenwell. On the 19th of May, about half-past four o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner with something in a bag under his arm, in Arlington-street - I spoke to him - he threw it down and ran off. I turned my rattle, and my brother watchman brought him back in about five minutes. A dead fowl was in the bag; he had another fowl in his hat, and had dropped one.
   JOHN TAYLOR. I am a watchman of Clerkenwell. I was with Davis, and pursued the prisoner, and overtook him about one hundred yards from Davis - he dropped a dead fowl from under his coat in coming back. I have shown the fowls to the prosecutor, who claims them as his.
   JOSEPH CABLE. I live at No. 5, William-street, Spafields, about two or three hundred yards from where the prisoner was taken. I kept five fowls in my area - they were all safe at half-past eleven o'clock when I went to bed, but three of them were gone in the morning. The watchman shewed them to me at Hatton-garden, and to the best of my belief they were mine - I swore to them They were all cut on the left wing.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going along the fields, and found a bag with the fowls near it - I took them up, and was going along when that gentleman stopped me.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Two Months.
 1051.  THOMAS KING was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of May , a silver fork, value 14 s., the goods of John Underwood.
   THOMAS SPARROW. I live with Mr. John Underwood, in Gloucester-place. I was acquainted with the prisoner; he was out of place, and called on me on Saturday, the 29th of May, about three o'clock - I asked him down stairs, and took him into the pantry; he remained there about a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes, and then went away. The plate was in the pantry, and about three hours after he was gone I missed a large fish fork; I had seen it in the morning. I made enquiry, and reported it to my master. I saw it on the Wednesday following.
   ROBERT MARCHANT. I keep a silversmith's shop, in Mortimer-street, Cavendish-square. The prisoner came to me on the Saturday, and asked if I bought old silver - he laid down on the counter a fork, broken in two pieces; I said,
 "I can't buy this, it has got a crest upon it, and is almost a new one;" he said,
 "Give it to me again" - I said,
 "No, I can't, I must send for an officer, and secure you." There were two gentlemen in the shop, and I begged of them to detain him till I got an officer.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM SELLERS. I am an officer, and took him in charge.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. Confined One Year.
 1052.  CHARLES BRITTON and HENRY SCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of May , twenty-eight yards of printed cotton, value 17 s., the goods of James Hodgson,
   GEORGE ELLIS. I am an officer. On the 31st of May, I was in Mile-end-road, about nine o'clock in the evening, and met the prisoners coming across the road - I am certain they were in company. Britton had a bundle under his arm - I asked what he had; he said some printed cotton; that he picked it up at the back of the London Hospital. I took him into custody, and Scott attempted to escape - I told my brother officer to stop him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. As soon as you saw Britton, and asked what he had got, he said cotton - A. Yes; he never attempted to conceal it.
   HENRY POULSON. I took Scott into custody.
   JAMES HODGSON. I live at Shadwell, and am a linen-draper. The officer showed me this cotton on Tuesday last, the 1st of June, and I knew it immediately; we had not sold but half a yard of it. I had seen it on Monday.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1053.  ALICE BOWSER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of May , three sheets, value 10 s., the goods of William Cruikshanks.
   JOHN OLIVER. I am a plasterer, and live in Samuel-street, at Mr. William Cruikshanks'; on the 13th of May, while I was in the right-hand room, on the first floor, I heard a noise, and I went out, but saw no one; but I saw wet foot-steps upon the staircase. I opened the window, and saw the prisoner going out of the house. I went down and informed Mrs. Cruikshanks; she pursued her, and took her - I saw her taken; she had some sheets in her apron. I heard her say,
 "Take the sheets, and let me go."
 ESTHER CRUIKSHANKS. I am the wife of  William Cruikshanks; I know nothing of the prisoner; Oliver gave me information, and I went out and saw the prisoner about one hundred yards from my house. I found three sheets in her apron - she said,
 "Take the sheets, and let me go;" they were my property, she had no business in my house; they had been in the left-hand room on the first floor.
   FRANCIS JACKSON. I have the property.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. As I was passing by, I saw a person drop a bundle; I picked it up, and walked on with it.
     GUILTY- Aged 33. Transported for Seven Years.
