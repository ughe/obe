BESLEY, Mayor.
John BOURNE. I am foreman at the Gas Works, Blackfriars--the two
prisoners worked there-Redford was a fireman-he left on 23rd January
and did not return-I don't know why he left-I called at his house to
know why he had left, and he was not at home-he had worked there eight
or ten years-he did not give any notice that he was going to leave-he was
at work on the 23rd, but did not come after that.
Redford's Defence. I found the Post Office order on the 24th, between
12.30 and 1 o'clock, at the corner of St. John Street, with the envelope
open, signed "Francis Goodlake" on the order, and I went to Lowe's house,
and said, "Take this to the Money Order Office," and he went with me.
That is all I have to say.
Lowe's Defence. This man came and asked me to take the order. I can't
read or write, and I took it in. I did not know what it was, or what name
was on the order.
GUILTY of uttering.-REDFORD-Eighteen Months' Imprisonment-
LOWE received a good charatcer-Strongly recommended to mercy-Three
Months' Imprisonment.
249. JOHN EDWARD CLARK (37), Unlawfully obtaining lo. 6d,
and 168., of Thomas Morley Wood, by false pretences.
MR. DUNNE conducted the Prosecution.
Thomas MORLEY WOOD. I am a stationer, of 24, Milk Street- about
1st January, the prisoner called at my warehouse and requested me to give
him & commission in connexion with others that he had in the trade, upon
orders for account books and printing, to which, after making inquires, I do-
oeded upon certain terms; he was to have 5 per cent. upon one particular class
of goods, and 10 per cent. upon another-he asked how I paid my commission
travellers-I said once a month-be said he was rather in low water, and
if I could make it once a week he should like it much better-I said it
made no difference to me, I would break my rule for once-on 8th January,
be brought me five or six orders, amongst them one from Mr. Pearce, of
Camden Town, for & ledger at lls. 6d.; the prisoner's commission upon
that would be about 18d., he took his commission for the whole week, 106,
the 18d. would be included in that-on 15th January he brought me eight
orders, one was from Mr. Parsons, of Brentford, amounting to 198. 6d., for a
ledger and two dozen pass books with his name printed on them-ho was
paid 158. commission on the whole of the orders that week, including that-
the goods were sent in pursuance of these orders and were returned to me
as never having been ordered; they were all spurious orders-the other
orders were from Robert Gough, of Windsor, 11. 18.; Mr. Gurney, of
Uxbridge, 11. 68. ; Charles Robinson, of Uxbridge 11. 128. 6d.; W. H.
Matthews, 11. 58.; Mr. Richardson, of Turnham Green, 6s. 6d.; and Mr.
Coles, of Brentford, 11. 68.-I paid him his commission upon all those
orders, supposing them to be genuine-he said he had received the orders
from all those persons, and he entered them in this order book-some time
afterwards, in consequence of what I heard, I gave him in charge-he said,
"It serves me right all I get, you have been a good friend to me, and if you
come to my brother-in-law in Holborn, he will pay you the amount, what-
ever it is" - I had lent him money to go on his journeyshe asked me the
amount I should lose by him--I said I had not had time to go into it, but
I should think it would be very little short of 207.
