 1473.  JOSEPH BAXTER was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of September , a pair of shoes, value 2 s., the goods of George Wood.
   GEORGE WOOD. I am a shoemaker, and live in Castle-street, St. George's. On Saturday morning, about nine o'clock, I saw the prisoner go into my shop, and take a pair of shoes. I was on the opposite side of the way, talking to a neighbour; he had put them under his jacket, and was helping himself to more - I went in and took him with another pair in his hand, which he dropped.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 52. Confined One Month.
 1474.  GEORGE WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of September , two shirts, value 6 s.; two pair of stockings, value 2 s.; a handkerchief, value 6 d., and an apron, value 6 d., the goods of Ann Nichols , widow, from the person of Edward Copeland.
   ANN NICHOLS. I live in the Hampstead-road, and am a laundress. I was going with a child on the 18th of September to take some linen home; the child had the bundle, and was close to me. I was knocking at the door of Mr. Cooke, in Little Russel-street, opposite the church, when the child had the bundle snatched from him which contained Mr. Cooke's things - he ran and cried Stop thief! I did not get sight of the person he was following; but he was stopped, and I saw him at the watch-house: the bundle was picked up and put into my hand before I get to the watch-house; it was the bundle the little boy was carrying for me.
   EDWARD COPELAND. I am thirteen years of age. I was with my grandmother taking some clothes home, last Saturday week - they were tied up in a bundle, which I had in my hand; we were near Bloomsbury-chuch when a person came and snatched them from me. I saw who it was, and ran after him crying Stop thief! I am sure I did not lose sight of him, till he was taken - the prisoner is the man; he threw the bundle behind him while running; it was picked up and given to my grandmother.
   JAMES OXLEY. I am a gentleman's servant. I was at the corner of Bury-street, about eight o'clock that evening, and saw the prisoner with a bundle under his arm come out of Little Russel-street; he ran, and as he turned into another street he threw it down. I pursued, but the watchman stopped him before I came up - I never lost sight of him.
 EDWARD DECASTER. I am constable of the night. The prisoner was delivered into my custody; the prosecutrix described the things before the bundle was opened. I found it all correct.
   GEORGE FORDYCE. I am a watchman. I heard the rattle sprung about eight o'clock, and a cry of Stop thief! I saw the prisoner running first. I overtook him; this little boy came up and said
 "That is the man who took my bundle."
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been into Oxford-street, and was rather late. I was running when this man stopped me.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Life.
 1491.  JAMES BOWLER was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 48 lbs. of lead, value 8 s., belonging to Thomas Steele , Esq. and fixed to his dwelling-house; and CORNELIUS QUINLAND was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen.
 FREDERICK  WILLIAM TABER. I am a builder, and live in Old Cavendish-street. I know the house inhabited by Mr. Steele - I had repaired it for Mr. Johnson, who sold it to Colonel Steele  about two years ago. The prisoner Bowler was at work for me, and had been so about three weeks as a bricklayer - I gave him no orders to do any work at Mr. Steele's. On the morning of the 20th of July I heard Mr. Steele's house had been robbed of some lead; Bowler was at that time at work for me at No. 14. Mr. Steele's is No. 17; there is a communication from one to the other over the roof - I said nothing to Bowler, but went up to the top of No. 14, and saw the lead had been stolen from No. 17; it had been cut, and partly torn from a dormer flap - it appeared to have been done with a trowel. I got an officer, and charged Bowler and Welch, his labourer, with having stolen it. Bowler called me aside - I told him it would be better for him to tell what he knew. In consequence of what he said we found the lead at the house of Quinland in the afternoon of that day, or the day following; Bowler was with us, and we told Quinland that we came for some lead which had been stolen; he keeps an old iron shop in Oxford-buildings, Oxford-street; we found a number of pieces of lead under the counter, and among others three pieces of about 48 lbs.; we asked Quinland where he got it; he said he bought it, and mentioned what he gave for it; we then took the lead to Mr. Steele's house, and it fitted the dormer exactly. I have not the least doubt but it had been stolen from there - the edges were quite fresh, and it must have been lately taken away.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I am an officer - I apprehended Bowler between twelve and one o'clock; he denied the charge most positively - I afterwards went to Quinland - Bowler went before us, and went into the house; I said we had a search-warrant for some lead that had been sold to him by Bowler, pointing out Bowler; he said he had bought some lead, and pointed to where it was; there was a large quantity of lead; we selected the pieces which fitted the dormer - it fitted exactly, and appeared to me to be taken from it, the edges were quite fresh cut.
