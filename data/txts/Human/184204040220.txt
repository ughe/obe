On Thursday afternoon, the 31st of March, the officer brought the prisoner there with the child—she gave her name Mary Ann Dunn—it was my duty to enter her name and the child's name in the admission book—I asked her about the child—self said it was her own—I asked her what name I should put down—was it christened?"—she said it was no name—I said I must enter it as Dunn only—she said "No, don't enter it as Dunn, it is not my child"—she seemed very much excited at the time, and afterwards she seemed very penitent, and said she would tell me the truth—that she had been very much excited by being questioned by so many, but the fact was she was in a public-house drinking with some people, they got tipsy together, and that the mother gave her the child to hold while she went out—she did not return in half an hour, and she went out for about half an hour looking for her—she returned again, and the landlord said nobody had been in quiring about the child, and she said, "Unfortunately, not knowing what to do, I took it down to my parents at Linfield, I was taken befor a Magistrate, who said I had better return to London, and I was returning when I was taken into custody. WILLIAM HENRY COLLINS. I keep the Sugar-loaf public-house, King-street, Drury-lane. I remember the prrisoner, the mother of the child, and two men, coming into my house on the 29th of March—the prosecutrix was carrying the child—they were there two hours and a half—then a soldier came in—they might be a little fresh, all of them, but not intoxicated after they were in some time a fiddler came in and played, but I stopped him—I remember the prosecutrix coming and calling out for her child—the prisoner was gone then and the child—the prisoner never came to make any inquiry afterwards.
   Prisoner's Defence.  She was quite tipsy. I was not in liquor at first. I was in company of a young man at the time—the mother was showing the child, and put it over the table. I said, "What age is it?" she said three weeks. I looked at the young man with me and said, "Three weeks old, and the mother tipsy;" she said, "Will you take it?" I took it in my arms; she said, "Come with me to the Sugar Loaf public-house." I went there with her; she and I were out together; she had the child in her arms afterwards, and when we came back the husband was gone out; she said to me, "Take hold of the child." I took it, and she never came back to me. I sat there half an hour, and said I should not stop there any longer, and I went home.
