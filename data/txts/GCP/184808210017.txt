Emma Hillier. On Saturday, 5th Aug., I was in my father's shop-he
is a grocer, at Willesden--it was the fair-day-the prisoner had a booth there
-Thad seen him in the course of the day-about eight or nine o'clock in the
evening he came and bought some bacon, tea, and sugar, and paid 3s. 100.
for them-be then asked if I had any sixpences and halfpence, as he should want
them before the night was out-I gave him six sixpences and 38. worth of
halfpence-he put down four shillings, and said I had given him but 4s.
worth-I said I gave him 6s. worth-he then took out 2s. more, and said I
ww quite right-he gave me six shillings-after he was gone I saw the
money was very black-I put it into one corner of the till, apart from other
money-in about two hours I called my mother's attention to it, and gave it
10 her-I had remained attending to the shop the whole of that time-no one
else had put anything into the till—I took all the money that evening-wben
I opened the till the shillings were in the same corner where I had placed
them-these are the shillings he gave me (produced).
CHARLOTTE HILLIER. I was in the shop when the prisoner came in-my
daughter afterwards brought me the six shillings-I gave them to the police-
man on Monday.
MARY HILLIER. I was in my mother's shop when the prisoner came in
for the change-I saw my sister give it him-I heard the dispute about the
amount-I am sure he is the man.
