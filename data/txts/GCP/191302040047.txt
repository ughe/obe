SYDNEY BURNETT (prisoner, on oath). I am employed in the Wool-
wich Arsenal. I have played whist some 20 years. It is absolutely
a game of skill. I used to organise whist drives on behalf of an asso-
ciation. They presented me with a gold watch in recognition of my
services. In October, 1911, I commenced whist drives at Free-
mason's Hall. Ninety per cent. of the players are Arsenal men and
their wives. Most of them are expert players. The drives originally
were ordinary progressive. Since the case of Morris v. Godfrey I
introduced partner drives. I consider them legal. I consider it &
game where skill predominates over chance. It increases the skill
of the game not to have the trump card shown. In addition to the
ordinary prizes there is an aggregate prize. Those that do not win
a prize hand in their cards at the end of the evening and they choose
their partners for a whole month. At the end of the month the scores
are totalled up and the lady and gentleman with the highest score
take prizes. The games are conducted in silence and proper time
is given for playing each hand. The games are over at 11 o'clock
at the latest. The profit for the 14 months of the whist drives and
refreshment bar was £58.
