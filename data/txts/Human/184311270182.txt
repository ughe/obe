 266. RICHARD BRUMHEAD was indicted for feloniously killing and Slaying Richard James Maddon .
   MR. CHARNOCK conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS MADDON, hatter, King's-court, Union-street, Borough.  On the 28th of Oct., about one o'clock in the day, I sent my son, Richard James, into Princes'-street—he was about nine years old, and was in perfect health—I next saw him about five or six minutes after, in a doctor's shop, in sensible, covered over with blood—I went with him to Guy's Hospital—two policemen and two other men assisted him—he died at half-past six that evening—I came away about five minutes before he died—I did not see him again—I saw his body on the Monday. JAMES BURBERRY, shoemaker, Union-street.  On the 28th of Oct., at one o'clock in the day, I was in Union-street, and saw the prisoner and a boy named Bates, and the deceased, playing on the opposite side of the road—they had a dog with them—the prisoner was setting the dog on Maddon—he ap peared alarmed, and ran backwards away from it—as he was running back wards, Bates followed him and told the dog to bite him—he still continued to set the dog on, and as the boy was running backwards, he ran with his back up against the shaft of a cart, which knocked him down, and the wheel im mediately went over his head—he was picked up by the man who was driving and a gentleman who I do not know—I heard the prisoner say nothing but "Bite him, bite him;" on that the dog jumped up on his person—when the boy went under the wheel the dog was in the road close by his feet—he did not take hold of the boy at all—he was taken to a surgeon's in Union-street, and from thence to the hospital.
   Cross-examined by MR. WILKINS . Q.  Did not the boy appear to be playing in the first instance? A.  Yes—the dog was a brown and white spaniel, half-bred—I cannot say that the prisoner was laughing when he said "Bite him"—he appeared in the act of playing—I did not hear him cry out, "Mind the cart." BENJAMIN BATES. I live in Union-street. On the 28th of Oct. I was in Union-st.—not on the same side with Maddon—the prisoner called me over—there was a dog there—Maddon was coming up towards us, and Brumhead called him butter-fingers  —his thumb was tied up—then Brumhead set the dog on him—he said, "Bite him, bite him"—the boy appeared to be fright ened—the dog went towards him, and he went walking backwards—the dog followed him up, but did not bite him—the boy ran, and went about a yard and half before he came to the wheel, when the shaft knocked him down, and the wheel went over him—I did not see him afterwards.
