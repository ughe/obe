I searched her, and found in the left side of her mouth (which was bleeding) 9s. 6d. in silver, and there was sixpenny worth of copper in her hand; this half-dollar, and two quarter-dollars, the prosecutor gave me, and told me he took them out of her left hand.
 Prisoner. When he got up into the room he asked me for a light, and said he thought he had lost some money - he pulled out a 5l. note, and said, "Some of those girls in the public-house have robbed me;" I said, "Recollect you have not lost it here;" and then he sent me for something to drink, and gave me 6s.  1730.  CHARLES LINSCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June , 1 silver spoon, value 14s., the goods of Mary Clay, widow.
   MARY CLAY. I am a widow, and live in Wapping, with my son, who is a baker; the prisoner used to come to the shop very often; he came into the bake-house, on the 6th of June; some of the girls gave him something to eat; in two days afterwards the spoon was missing.
   MARY ISRAEL. I keep a silversmith's shop. The prisoner came into my shop, two or three months ago - I cannot tell whether it was June or July - I was before the Magistrate, but do not know when - I have a very short memory.
 Q. Did you state to the Magistrate, at the examination, on the 24th of August: - "The prisoner came to my shop, and threw the spoon on my counter - I asked where he got it; he said his mother sent him with it?" A. Yes; it was broken. I do not know his mother - I weighed it; it was 13/4 oz. - I paid him 8s. 3d.; here is the spoon - I live in at No. 4, Upper East Smithfield, near the Mint - I deal in clothes as well as silver - I did not ask where his mother lived. I sell my silver to the refiners.
 Q. Is this spoon worn out? A. It is; because it is broken; it is not worn out.
 Prisoner. Q. What day did you buy it of me? A. I cannot tell.
   RALPH CLAY. I am the prosecutrix's son; she informed me she had lost a spoon. I was accidentally passing Israel's shop, about a week after, and saw a spoon which appeared to me to be the same. I got an officer, who went and brought up Israel; the prisoner had been in my employ, and was discharged on the 6th. If this spoon is ours, it is much disfigured since I saw it last. I told the Magistrate the spoon produced there was mine - I cannot swear to it now. I knew the spoon I saw in the window was my mother's; if this is the same, it has been much worn, and knocked about since. I believe the one I saw to be ours, but the parts of this do not fit the bowl.
   MARK ISRAEL. The spoon has been in my possession since - I have not altered it.
 - JEFFREY. I was before the Magistrate - Israel produced a spoon there.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1731.  JAMES M'DOUGAL was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 1 bundle of small-hand paper, value 10s., the goods of Joseph Bargett.
   THOMAS WEBB. I am a watchman of Newman-street. On the 24th of August, about half-past nine o'clock, at night I saw the prisoner take a parcel from under his arm from Bardgett's shop-door; he reached in, and took it - I sprang my rattle, and called Stop thief! he threw it down in Well-street, and I took him.
   JOHN BARGETT. I am a tailor. This paper laid on my shop floor; the door was shut.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Month.
 1732.  HENRY PERRY and JOHN NOBLE were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , 1 table-cloth, value 4d.; 2 petticoats, value 4s.; 1 cap, value 4d.; 1 pair of stays, value 4s.; 1 bed-gown, value 1s.; 1 shift, value 1s. 6d.; 2 shirts, value 4s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 6d., and 1 apron, value 6d., the goods of Mary Summerfield, widow.
   MARY SUMMERFIELD. I am a widow, and live in Ann's-place, Hackney-road, and take in washing. I sent my son James for a bundle of dirty linen, from Mrs. Donald's, near the Rose and Crown public-house, Cambridge-heath; he was brought home without it - I found the prisoner in custody.
   JAMES SUMMERFIELD. I am near eight years old. About half-past nine o'clock in the morning, I went to Mrs. Donald's, who gave me a bundle for my mother - Perry came to me, just over the bridge, and said, if I would take a note for him he would give me 6d. - Noble was on the other side of the road - I said, very well; he took me down a street opposite, about four yards - Perry showed me the house I was to go to; he told me to put my bundle down, and he would mind it, white I went with the note, which he gave me - I put down my bundle, and saw Perry run away with it; he gave it to Noble, who was across the road, and they ran away together - I called out, and some people came - I saw Perry caught - I am quite sure of them both.
   WILLIAM GLASSICK. I am a butcher. I was in Pollard's-row, about nine o'clock in the morning, and saw this boy crying; the two prisoners were running before him - Noble had the bundle under his arm - I followed, and saw Noble throw it down - I took it up immediately - Perry was running first.
