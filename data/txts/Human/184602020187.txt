I am gardener to Mr. John George Shaw Lefevre, of Wimbledon-common. I have the care of his fowls—on the morning of the 14th of Jan. I was gone to Croydon—when I came home in the evening I missed a light-coloured fowl—it was one of last year's fowls—I lost three fowls between the 11th and the 14th—they are principally the Dorking breed—I think we have got the best breed of Dorking fowls upon the common—the others are more of a mixed breed—the light-coloured one I lost was a greyish one. RICHARD GOLDING( police-Constable V  31.) I received information, and went on Thursday, the 15th of Jan., to Frost's house—I found both the pri soners there, and took them—I found in the room down stairs an uncooked shoulder of mutton, and three pieces of the chine of a sheep; and on moving a loose board in the floor I found a fowl's wing and feathers, another shoulder of mutton, not cut in a butcher-like manner, thirty-six snares, and two iron gins—on searching Hogg's pocket at the Police-court I found a quantity of small white feathers and some oats—I found in Frost's house two steel traps. RICHARD STREATFIELD( police-constable V  114.) I went with Golding to the prisoner's house—when the wing of the fowl was pulled up from under the floor, Frost took it up and put it into the fire—he said it was of no good now—it was the wing of a dark fowl—I took it out again.
   Frost.  I own I just put it between the bars, but I said not a word.
   FROST  — GUILTY. Aged 25.
   HOGG  — GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined One Year .

JOSEPH HASTINGS , MATTHEW GIBSON, and RICHARD BOXALL, were indicted for stealing 1 sack, value 6 d  .; 2 tame geese, 5 s  .; 8 live fowls, 13 s  .; 1 saw, 1 s  . 3 d  .; and 2 walking-sticks, 3 s  .; the goods of George Willis . JOHN HAYTER. I am in the service of Mr. George Willis, at Hearn-hill. On the night of the 14th of Jan. his premises were broken into, he lost the geese, fowls, and other things stated—this saw I can identify—I put it in the tool-shed about five o'clock in the evening, and missed it the next morn ing between nine and ten o'clock—this is the saw, it is my master's, it is broken now—it was not broken when I feft it—the fowls and geese were kept close by the tool shed.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  How do you swear to this saw? A.  One or two of the teeth are broken out, and two or three years ago I broke the handle in this place—I had used it the day, it was left—the point of the tooth is broken off. CHARLES ANSTRIDGE. I am cow-man to Mr. George Willis. At a quarter before five o'clock on the night of the 14th of Jan. I looked up my master's premises—I locked up the fowl-houses—there were eight fowls—the geese were not locked up—they were close by, round the pound—this sack was in the cow-house, which is close to the fowl-house—I came there at half-past six o'clock the next morning, and they were all gone—I examined the geese and fowls and sack which were found—they were my master's—these walking sticks I know—they were standing against the cow-house door.
