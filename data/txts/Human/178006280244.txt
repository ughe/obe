The magistrate then dismissed him, and desired he would be ready to be called for whenever he was wanted. He told them he would. He then went away about his usual business, and I heard no more about it till the Wednesday following, which I think was the 21st, then the woman went down to Mr. Sherwood's and laid an information, and Mr. Sherwood's people came and took him. He was at large all that time up and down with the prisoners; he is a servant to Mr. Newport.
   JOHN SADLER sworn.
 I attend the publick-office in East Smithfield.
 Do you know Downs? - Yes.
 Do you remember the night Lebarty's house was burnt? - Yes, Wednesday the 7th of June. I was in company with the prisoner and Wilkinson that night at the Ship, till ten o'clock. Mr. M'Kenzie keeps it; I left the prisoner there.
 Cross Examination.
 You attend the publick-office? - Yes, as an headborough of the parish of Aldgate. I went to the Ship that evening between the hours of nine and ten; he staid with me from that time till near twelve.
 That was the first time you saw him that evening? - No, I saw him before in East Smithfield, pretty near the Maypole at the King's Arms. I made an agreement to meet him at the Ship? - About nine, or a little after, and staid with him till near twelve.
 Who were with you? -  John Wilkinson, Sarah Aldin, Sarah Hart, and Mr. M'Kenzie.
 Who did the prisoner go away with? - I cannot tell, I left him there; Wilkinson went with me.
 Court. How far is the Ship in East Smithfield from Lebarty's house? - Three or four hundred yards. As I was going home I met one of Lebarty's lodgers; he called to us and said the mob were pulling Libarty's house down. I said if we can be of any service I will get our men to try and disperse the mob; he said it was of no use; they were then pulling it down.
   JOHN WILKINSON sworn.
 I was at the Ship the night Lebarty's house was pulled down; I was there between nine and ten in company with Sadler, Downs, Mr. M'Kenzie, Armstrong and his wife,  Sarah Aldin and Sarah Hart. I left the house about a quarter before twelve o'clock; going home we met one of Lebarty's lodgers; he said they were pulling Mr. Lebarty's house down.
 Cross Examination.
 What are you? - A runner.
 Did the prisoner go with you? - No, we found him there.
 What liquor did you drink there? - Nothing but porter. We drank I suppose four or five pots; I paid for one. We left him there.
   MARY M'KENZIE sworn.
 I keep the Ship, a publick-house.
 Do you know Sadler, Wilkinson, and Downs? - Yes, they were at my house on Wednesday night the 7th of June. Downs came in about nine and staid till about half after twelve o'clock; just about half after twelve he desired to have another pint of beer, and paid me for it; I saw him to the door and saw him turn round the corner to go home; he went home with Mr. Hart.
 Cross Examination.
 When did Sadler and Wilkinson come into your house? - Between nine and ten o'clock.
 What time was Lebarty's house pulled down? - I cannot tell. About half after twelve they said there was a great fire on Tower Hill; that the mob were pulling down a house in St. Catherine's-lane.
 People came in after nine o'clock? - Yes, some.
 A great many? - No; our house is not so overflowed with business.
 You could not see Lebarty's house from your house? - No; only the blaze of the fire on Tower Hill.
