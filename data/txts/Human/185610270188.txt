JAMES WRIGHT. ( policeman, W  294). I was with the last witness and took the female prisoner to the station house—she told me it was a very bad job, and the effects of beer that caused her to do so—she said she had taken the things out of a lark , and it was her intention to take them back to Mrs. Skinner the next morning—she did not tell me she was with Mrs. Skinner from 1 to 4 o'clock. SARAH ALCROFT. I lodge at Mrs. Skinner's, and occupy the back room. I returned home at about 9 or half past 9 o'clock on the evening in question—the clock was half an hour fast—it was half past 9 by the clock, so it was 9 o'clock—when I went in, the door was open—I went straight through into my back room to get a candle—I saw Mrs. Pritchard, and she asked me how I was—she was in the mangling room, standing at the right hand side of the room—she had got a small bundle underneath her cloak—she asked me how the baby was—I told her as well as I could expect—I took a light and went into my own room—I heard her in the mangling room—after that I saw Mrs. Pritchard pass round the mangle on to the left hand side, and take a bundle out of the clothes basket—it was either in the door way or on the table, I cannot swear which—I heard a voice say, "Hold up your right hand"—it seemed to me like a man's voice—there was a very heavy footstep, but I could not say whether it was a man or another woman, because the window would not allow me to see—they did not remain there long after that—I gave information to Mrs. Skinner the next morning.
   Q.  Did you know Mrs. Skinner was not there? A.  I do not know any thing about Mrs. Skinner's affairs—I had been out all day in the country, and had just returned home—I did not know whether she had had any company—I told her what I saw when she told me of the robbery. STEPHEN SYMONS. ( policemcm, V  306). On the night of the robbery I was in the neighbourhood of the prosecutrix's house—about fifty yards off I saw Mr. Pritchard—he was walking from Mrs. Skinner's house—he came up towards me, and said, "Policeman, I think there is something up  there, and if you will go with me, I will show you"—I went to the back of some houses—he said, "If you go round to Europa-place, No. 5, I will show you some one; some one has been attempting to steal some brass taps and some lead pipe from some water butts"—he went one way and I the other, and I met him at his door—he took me into the back place and showed me a place as if somebody had given the pipe a blow—he said his wife had seen somebody there the night previous—I was away about a quarter of an hour—I discovered afterwards that the room at Mrs. Skinner's had been robbed.
   John Pritchard.  It is perfectly true what he has stated about the cistern and the water pipe; this was at a quarter past 8 o'clock. Witness.  The clock had struck nine; I was on early duty.
 (  The prisoner, Ann Pritchard, in her defence, stated that she had been drinking with the prosecutrix, and merely took the things in a lark in order to frighten her. )
   John Pritchard's Defence.  I am as innocent as God in heaven; I did not know of the affair till ten hours after.
   JOHN PRITCHARD .— NOT GUILTY.
