CHARLES WRYGHTE pleaded Guilty. Aged 20.-Confined Pour Monthe.
Charles Clarke. I keep a public-house, in Ironmonger-row. On Satur
day evening, 3rd March, a eonstable came to me with a bottle of brandy, one of
sherry, and one of port-I knew the port to be mine by the seal—I drew some
brandy from the cask, and found it to be of the same temperature as that in
the bottle—the prisoner Charles was in my service; I gave him into custody
-be had access to the wine-cellar-I know the prisoner Horatio, and have
repeatedly wished him to leave my house - I saw them both in the tap-room
together that afternoon, at four o'clock.
RICHARD Moss policeman, G 195). On Saturday evening, 3rd March, I
stopped the prisoner, Horatio, in St. John-street, and asked what he had got,
and where he was going—he refused to tell me I said I was a constable, and
again asked what he had got—he then said he had got some wine, which his
brother had given him--at the station he said that Mr. Clarke, of 12, South-
ampton-square, had given it him- I went there ; no Mr. Clarke lived there
I then went to the prosecutor's, and took Charles-he said he had had some,
but not so much as Mr. Clarke said.
Horatio Wryghte's Defence. My brother asked me to take the three
bottles of wine : I did not know they were stolen: I made the fale statement
because I did not want my brother to get into any trouble.
