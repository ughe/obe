   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  If a ship had come on Sunday, you would not expect it on Monday morning? A.  No—there was no ship expected at all on Monday—every body in the office knew she had arrived, and the boxes with her. THOMAS KINGSTON. I am foreman to Hartley and Co. at the Iron-gate wharf—I remember a person coming to the wharf in a cab,  on the 25th of March, about twelve o'clock—I spoke to the person ( looking at Moss )—that is the man—I spoke to him while he was in the cat—he asked if the agent was in town—I cannot exactly say whether he said the agent or Mr. Hartley—I said not, but the wharfinger was in—he then mentioned Mr. Bristow's name, and directed me to call him out—I gent to Mr. Bristow, and he came out and went to the cab  —I noticed that he and Moss spoke together—Moss then came out of the cab,  and went into the counting-house with Mr. Bristow—he remained there not more than two minutes, and I brought out one box, and another man brought out another—Mr. Bristow desired me to do so—Moss was present—I put the boxes into the cab  —Moss got in, gave me 1 s ., and drove off in the cab  —I had seen Lewin Caspar at the wharf that morning, about half an hour before Moss came.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How often have you been to the police-office on this business? A.  Twice, during the examination—the prisoners were all in the dock at the time—Moss was in the dock with the rest—I knew him—none of the officers showed him to me, to my recol lection—I cannot recollect that be was pointed out to me before I recog nised him—I cannot swear positively, one way or the other—I took very particular notice of Moss in the cab  —I saw him in the cab  and spoke to him; there was nothing remarkable in his appearance that I recollect—I observed his whiskers, they were black and middling-sized—I should imagine they were the same size then as they are now—I reckon them black, not sandy—I should not call them large whiskers—if he was describ ed as having large black whiskers, I should say that description would apply to him—I should say he has now large black whiskers—I should say, if asked to describe him, that he had large black whiskers—it is quite remark able, any body could see it—I am an Irishman—I should know him by that again, at once, it would be the chief feature I should fix on—I do not recollect what kind of neckcloth he wore—it was not the whiskers in par ticular that attracted my attention, but I did remark them—I should not agree with any man who said he had sandy whiskers—I should say they were as large now as then—they are large now—I knew Allen the clerk—he was in Mr. Hartley's service, attending the office daily—he would have the same opportunities as the other clerks of learning about ships coming in, and so on—I cannot tell the day Allen ran away—it was about a fort night or three weeks after this—there was a great stir made by Mr. Hartley about this, and Allen went away after that—he did not give notice that he was going.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Where was Moss when you first saw him after seeing him in the cab ? A.  At Lambeth-street—he was with five or six people in the room adjoining the office—I was taken there to see if there was a person there I knew, and I recognised him—I knew him myself.
