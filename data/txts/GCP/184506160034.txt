Q. You have said there is no commerce carried on at the Gallinas but the
slave-trade; from your knowledge of the business carried on there, are you
able to say whether slave-fittings could be procured at the Gallinas? 4.
Such as water-casks, I have seen on shore at Gallinas, fit for vessels, and
rice for feeding the slaves—they are the principal things—there was a fast
number of shackles and bolts at Gallinas at the time the barracoons were
destroyed-we took them away, but to my own knowledge, on the day of
the vessel's arriving at Gallinas, I am not aware that there were any shackles
and bolts-before the barracoons were destroyed I should consider a vessel
might very easily be fitted out there with the necessary implements for the
slave-trade, not that I have ever seen one fitted out; I am only giving my
own opinion from what I have seen at Gallinas, and what I have known of
the place--when the barracoons were destroyed I found implements that are
used for the slave-trade, on board and on shore both-the slaves are ironed
on shore and on board both.
Q. How long would it take to fit out a vessel at Gallinas, and put on
board a cargo of slaves ? A. If they had prepared the necessary things be-
fore hand, it would occupy but a few hours to put the slaves on board, I
mean, after the cargo was discharged, and if the vessel was ready to receive
her slave equipments, and they were prepared on shore—if the parties
there expected a vessel to arrive, they could procure the necessary things
