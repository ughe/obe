Cross-examined. I carry on business as Thomas Okey, trading as the
National Reliance Advance Co.--there is a plate on the door with "The
National Reliance Advance Co." on it that Uo. is personified in myself
there is no sham in it, I am the Co. legitimately-the first money I lent
was 151. in cash-I gave that to the man, who handed it to the woman
-I charged 61. for interest and expenses--I asked her if she could pay
61. for the advance, and she said "Yes, payable at 158. a week in 21 weeks"
I charged 58. 3d. inquiry fee and 3d. for the application form-I did
that in each case I went to Carlton Street and made inquiries as to who
Mr. Lewis Was I found him to be a respectable man-I did not call at his
shop in Hampstead I have been paid 151. or 161. out of the 211.,
not more there was & renewal of that loan-on both the trans-
actions I liave been paid something like 251.there is a balance
of 5l. on that I have been paid more than the principal without
interest, in both cases-the weekly payments were continued up to the
time Mrs. Lewis left her home; that would be about five months Mr.
Kisch the solicitor represented me before the Magistrate -I told him as
much as I knew I did not bring any proceeding to try and get my 51.-
I did that on principle-I did not say anything about Horne or Mrs. Slous
before the Magistrate-I also charged money for inquiry fees in those
cases--they were paid by the female prisoner-I saw the prisoner on four
occasions-he is an illiterate man; he writes very badly--it was Mrs.
Lewis who came and spoke about borrowing the money--it was she who
I saw at her house it was not till the papers were to be signed that he
was brought there.
Re-examined. I did not take these proceedings for the purpose of re-
covering my money, it was on principle, as I have got between 500 and
600 customers, and it was done to deter others-I did not seek out Mrs.
Lewis or the prisoner.
