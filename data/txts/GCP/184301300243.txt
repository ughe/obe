Court. Q. What are the provisions of the deed? Do they follow the
blank deed, and only alter as to the sums? 4. It purports to be a convey-
auce by Miss Constable to the prisoner and Miss Blackwell, in consideration
of 40001. and 88., composed of a mortgage debt of 23501, 11091. 78., the debt
due to Stanley's executors, 161l. 18., the amount paid to Sutcliffe and Birch,
and 380l. for the amount of our debt and interest, altogether making 40001.
and 88.--there is no provision of any annuity or any surplus for her-she ex-
pressed her readiness to give the prisoner up any surplus there was, and there
is a covenant by the prisoner and his sister to indemnify her for mortgage
debts, taking them to themselves-there is mention of the estate in Wales-it
is a very small estate indeed-Miss Constable and the prisoner both said it
was a very small cottage--there is also some property in Huntingdon that is
not valuable, worth 3001. or 4001., I believe, not more.
MR. CHAMBERS, Q. She attended, on the fourth occasion, for the purpose
of executing this deed ? A. Yes, and I believe she then executed it, though
I am not the attesting witness.
COURT. Q. Before she executed the deed, did you explain it to her? A.
I explained it to her fully-I did not read it in full, but I explained it to her,
and she understood it--the consideration was supposed to be exactly 40001. of
this deed; and when I came to add up these executed deeds, it came to
more; therefore it became necessary to have the stamp altered, and she went
into the calculation with me-I checked it with her, an l the prisoner-I made
her quite onderstand, that the prisoner was not to be her trustee ---that she was
to have no expectation-tbat she was to discharge her father's supposed debte
and have nothing she never gave me to understand that there were any debts
of her father's beyond this mortgage debt, and those debts mentioned here
-all the property was to go in liquidation of those debts-she was to be free
of the debts. Wid
Q. And also free of any inheritance,-to be abroad in the world without a
shilling? A. Yes; but she always stated the property to me, and the prisoner
too, as worth very little more than the mortgage debt-I did not take any
pains to sift into the reality of any debt to the Stanleys- I did not know whe-
ther he was a man capable of lending 18.--I heard nothing of him--I have
got a minute the first time I saw Miss Constable and the prisoner, that he
(Stanley) was a friend of Mr. Constable, and acted for him-I only knew
what they told me, that Mr. Stanley was a friend of Mr. Constable, and that
he was an elderly gentleman-I understood that I knew nothing except what
Miss Constable and the prisoner told me.
Q. Did you not think, for the protection of a lady, ignorant of law affairs,
that it was your duty to satisfy yourself that this consideration existed ?
4. I did, in the fullest way possible--I explained every thing to her, and I
think Miss Constable knew very well what she was about, from the four or five
interviews I had with her, and if she misled me I cannot help it-I acted for
her afterwards in several things.
