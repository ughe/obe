Mr. Gill submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury upon the conspiracy
to commit an indictablo offence, namely to cbent at cards. It was no offence to
win or lose money at cards, nor any oflence to induce persons by false represente
tions to play at cards, there being no evidence of cheating.
Sir Charles Mathews said there was strong evidence for the consideration of the
Jury whether there was not between the three prisoners an unlawful agreement to
induce people to play at cards with the certainty of winning. It was not
charge of conspiracy to commit a criminal offence but that the prisoners entered into
# combination to secure for themselves the money or cheques of other person
whom they might induce to play.
The Recorder said he must leave the case to the Jury on the general charge of
conspiracy, but he entert ined considerable doubt as to whether there was anything
more than grave suspicion that the score was not properly kept
Sir C. Mathews submitted that the bankers' evidence and the equal division of the
£60: the uniformity of loss having regari to the time occupied in card playing; and
the prese ace in all cases of Brown as the scorer, were evidence for the Jury,
The Recorder agreed that that was certainly evidence which the Jury most deal
with.
Verdict, Guilty in all cases on the conspiracy counts; Not Guilty
on counts charging false pretences.
