I charged Kilronen with knowing who had stolen it, and charged him with stealing my tobacco-box - the prisoner came to me the same night, and asked if I had said he stole the watch, and I shook hands with him, and gave him some beer - he denied taking the watch - Kilronen gave me my tobacco-box. I did not tell the prisoner I did not suspect him.
   JOHN BURK. I live at St. Giles. On the 7th of August, at night, in going to the Hare and Hounds for a pint of beer for my father, the prisoner spilt part of it in the house - I kept up to him, and asked why he did that - he did not answer me; I heard one Herring say to him, "Is it all right?" he said "It is;" I saw Shehan have a watch in his hand, the key and chain slipped between his fingers, as he put it into his breeches pocket - it was a silver watch, a brass chain, gold seals, and gold ring - he ran off as fast as he could.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you been told how many seals the watch had? A. I used to see it every day - my father told me it was silver, and a brass chain; it was just after eleven o'clock - it is a dark place, but there is a gaslight - I was never in trouble; Donohoe sells fruit, and my father sells fish.
 COURT. Q. When did you mention this? A. Next morning, when I heard the prosecutor had lost his watch.
   JAMES DONOHOE. I shook hands with the prisoner next day; I had heard what Burk had seen then.
   MICHAEL KELLY. I am a watchman. On the 8th of August, at nine o'clock, I was going my rounds, and met the prosecutor, who said he believed the prisoner had took his watch, for he had been seen with it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was that the following day? A. Yes - he said he had been talking to the prisoner, and he had offered to go before a Magistrate. I went and found the prisoner in bed.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was waiting at the door to see my wife, to get 6d., to go into the club-room; I went in, borrowed 6d., and the prosecutor called me to him; they were pushing one another about; Sullivan left the room with me about eleven o'clock - the prosecutor was then accusing Kilronen of taking his tobacco-box and watch; I went home, and next night heard I was accused; I went and drank with him.
   WILLIAM SULLIVAN. I was chairman at this free-and-easy club - I left the chair about a quarter to eleven o'clock, and went into the parlour; Shehan and two others went with me; he was in the room till I left the chair, I am certain; while we were drinking in the parlour the prosecutor came into the parlour, walked out, returned, and said he had lost his watch; the prisoner had not been out of my company: we went out together, and saw him at the bar, accusing Barney Kilronen of robbing him of his watch.
 COURT. Q. Was no search proposed? A. None; Shehan sat near him. Kilronen was taken to the watch-house, and to the office next day.
   THOMAS KENNEDY. I know this public-house - it is a dark dull gateway; there is no gas-light there; Burk could not see what he has described - it is the darkest place in the parish; it is called Rats' Castle - I am not one of the club.

 1799.  MICHAEL COLLINS, WILLIAM MURPHY, and DENNIS MURPHY, were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , 164lbs. weight of lead, value 16s., the goods of our Lord   the King.
 SECOND COUNT, calling it the goods of  Benjamin Charles Stevenson.
 THIRD COUNT, stating it to be the goods of the  Right Honorable William Sturges Bourne, William Dakers Adams, Esq., and Henry Dawkins, Esq.
 MR. BOLLAND conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM OXLEY. I am a constable of St. Martin in the Fields. On the morning of the 27th of July, in consequence of information, I went to Scotland-yard, at five o'clock, with Lemon - I saw the three prisoners digging there; no other person was near them - I waited about three quarters of an hour, and saw them take a large piece of pipe out of the earth - open the trap-door of a dung hole, and put it in there; they then took two or three shorter pieces, and put them there - they worked on till eight o'clock. I then saw Collins and William Murphy take it up, chop it into pieces, and put it into a barrow, which Collins wheeled down Parliament-street; William Murphy ran and joined him in Parliament-street (Dennis Murphy remained at work). I followed them to Schofield's, in Dean's-yard - I saw them take the lead out and carry it into the shop; I immediately went in, seized William Murphy, and told Schofield it was stolen goods; here is the lead, it is pieces of large pipes.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. William Murphy and Collins took it away? A. Yes; but Dennis came to the very spot where they were taken, in about ten minutes - eight o'clock is the time they leave work; they were working at the office of the Board of Works, Scotland-Yard.
   WILLIAM LEMON. I am an officer. Oxley's account is correct; I saw them take the lead and put it into the dunghole - I followed William Murphy and Collins to Schofield's; Dennis came there afterwards and was taken.
   WILLIAM SCHOFIELD. I am a plumber and live in Dean's-yard.
