WILLIAM EDMONDS. I keep a beer-shop in Skinner-street, St. Mary, Lambeth, and keep the house. On the 19th of July I locked a purse con taining this money in a drawer up stairs at four o'clock in the afternoon—I took the keys down, and hung them on a nail in the other room—I went out, leaving James Kenny in the room with the prisoner—I was away three hours—I went to my drawer in consequence of information, and missed the purse containing the money stated in the indictment, and the other articles—I had left the prisoner in the tap-room when I went out—this was Friday, and on Sunday Hopkins gave me information. MATTHEW HOPKINS. I am a labourer, and know the prisoner well. On Sunday, the 21st of July, I saw him, and said, "You have got new clothes"—he said, "Yes, I bought them down the New-cut; we have been to Edmonds's, and had some money there;" he said they had done a job there, and took four silk handkerchiefs, six silver spoons, a pin or brooch, and about 9 l . in money; that he got it up stairs in a big chest in the back room. MARY ANN TAYLOR. I am the wife of Joseph Taylor, and live opposite the prosecutor's. I was at my door on Friday, the 19th, about half-past five o'clock, and saw the prisoner run out of Edmonds's door, and run up the street—somebody ran after him—I swear he is the man. WILLIAM BURTON( policeman. ) I took the prisoner in charge on the 23rd, and told him the charge—he said he knew nothing of it—I said, "You are differently dressed to what you were on Sunday"—he said, "I have no clothes but these"—I was in the habit of seeing him every day almost, and he was dressed differently.—I had seen him on Sunday in Wellington-street with a new suit of clothes on.
   Prisoner.  I was never in the house in my life. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Transported for Ten Years .

 2174. ROGER CREADON was indicted for feloniously cutting and wounding Robert Butter worth on his head, with intent to do him some griev ous bodily harm.
   MR. SIMON conducted the Prosecution. ROBERT BUTTERWORTH. I am a carman. On the 15th of July I went to the Three Compasses, in Little Guildford-street, St. Saviour's, for some beer for supper—I had a jug with me—Mr. Cole and Mr. Parsons were there—I drank some beer with them—while standing there the prisoner came in and called for a pint of beer—Mr. Peacock, the landlord, drew it—he put his hand into his pocket and pulled out some halfpence; it was not sufficient; he then pulled out an old coin—the landlord said he did not take such money as that—he drank the beer, and wanted another pint—the landlord refused—he struck the pot on the counter twice—the landlord came round from the bar, turned him out, and closed the door on him—about five minutes after wards he came back and rapped at the door—I, being close to it, opened it—I was going home, and before I could get away from him he cut me on the head with a pair of tongs, and I fell senseless on the ground—when I came to myself I was at the station-house—I was hurt on my head, my left knee, and both my legs—the skin of my head was broken—it bled a great deal—a sur geon's apprentice attended me for about ten minutes—I did not go to him again—I had never seen or spoken to the prisoner before.
