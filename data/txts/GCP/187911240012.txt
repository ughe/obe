CHARLES WATKINS. I am & carman in the employment of Mr. Patten-
on 5th December I was ordered to go to 10, Worship Street --I had been
there before, but not that day-I knew Watson; we used to call him Mr.
Lordan-I also knew Summers and Mannion by sight, but not by name
I went on the 5th with a horse and eart, and loaded it with seven or eight
bales of leather of about 1fcwt. or 2cwt. each lad called Darkey and
another young chap helped me to load-I then took it to West Brompton,
a corner shop-the young chap went inside: he came out, and told me to
turn round to the railway station to a new shop on the left-hand side, and I
went there and unloaded I saw a tallish man there, neither of the defendants
-I then went back to 10, Worship Street, where I put in two boxes from
the second floor and ten or eleven bundles of skins I took them to Mrs.
Ellis's, 10, Claro Terrace.
RICHARD WILLIAMS. I am employed by Mr. West, who keeps & reposi-
tory at Fulham-in consequence of instructions from him on 6th of De-
cember I went to 10, Claro Terrace, West Brompton, to look over some
goods there and to fetch them-I saw a shopman there tamed Foley-I
took away 12 bales of butts and 63 hides, and three bundles of skins con-
taining 15, 17, and 18 skins each—I have my book here in which I entered
them at the time—those goods remained at our premises a little less than
a fortnight, and they then went away by our carte- I have seen Summers
in the shop at 10, Claro Terrace.
Cross-examined by MR. TORR. It was a boot shop, Mrs. Ellis's.
JOHN EYLES. I am employed by Mr. West about the 14th or 15th
December last I took some leather goods, butts, bales, hides, and skins,
from my master's repository to the North-Western Station, Camden Town-
I saw them weighed on the weigh-bridge, and they weighed a ton-I
unloaded them and helped put them in a truck labelled “Northampton"-
I took with me a ticket from Mr. West, which I left in the office, and
brought back a receipt, which I took to 10, Claro Terrace next morning,
and gave it to a young man in the shop.

WILLIAM EYLES. About the 14th or 15th December I was a packer at
Mr. West's & man named Foley came there and sorted out some parcels
of leather, some of which went to the North-Western Station, and some I
took to Watson Austin in Hogarth Terrace, close by our place, about
17 cwt-I only saw Foley there; he told me to deposit them; I did so,
and went away.

believe a boy brought them--I had not ordered them-I asked Summers
