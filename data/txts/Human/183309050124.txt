   HUMPHREY JONES, I am living on my property, at No. 3, Little Marlborough-street - I was in the prisoner's omnibus when this unfortunate occurrence took place; I was as near the horses as could be, looking through the front window; I could see the horses - the one next to me was not galloping; I could not see the off side one- I saw this poor child, I was sitting with my face towards the church; we were a little beyond the church, and looking through the window - there had been a little stoppage just before, and it was impossible to gallop without galloping over a carriage - I saw a child run from my back, she ran almost as quick as a bird could fly, she appeared to wish to get over the road before the carriage passed; I saw her come all of a sudden, and run so fast, I could not tell whether it was male or female - I was afraid there would be an accident; the driver was by no means to blame, he must be something more than man if he could have prevented it - I never saw him before.
   SABINA WALTERS. I was in the service of Mr. Brown, of Ludgate-hill - I was in the omnibus that run over the child; the horses were not galloping they had stopped a short time before, I think it was to take up a passenger; we had got about eight or nine yards from the place when the accident happened - I saw the child leave the footpath, there was no crossing at that place; the child ran into the danger- the driver pulled up as soon as he possibly could; I don't think he could have avoided the accident; he was sober.
 COURT. Q. Were you inside the omnibus? A. Yes; if I could see the child leave the path, the coachman could - the other omnibus was between us and the footpath; it was not close to us, we were passing the other omnibus; we had come up to it - I saw the child through the windows; the other omnibus was not between us and the path - I was sitting on the right-hand side, in the middle of the omnibus, on the opposite side to where the child was; and the other omnibus was at my left, and the pathway to my left - there were no better means for me to see the child more than the coachman; he was more forward than me, and higher; he was turning his head when the child was leaving the pathway.
   JAMES WYBRUN. I was the driver of Shillibeer's omnibus - I saw the child under the wheel of Bardell's omnibus, after I had stopped and turned my head round- I don't know at what rate Bardell's omnibus was going; I was going about six miles an hour - I was a-head of him; I was not racing against his omnibus; I did not know he was so close behind me; I was not racing against anything - I think Bardell's had the best horses, but the prisoner had never attempted to go by me, all the road from Paddington.
   FRANCIS SKINNER. I was conductor of Shillibeer's omnibus; I had come with it from Paddington - Bardell's at that time I think had the best horses - on our way from Paddington to the Bank Bardell's omnibus had not made the least attempt to pass us, except when we stopped to take up a passenger - I had the opportunity of observing the accident; the prisoner pulled up as soon as possible.
   WILLIAM HEDGES. I am a sadler, and live in Edgware-road - I was in Shillibeer's omnibus, on the day in question - I was taken up in the Edgware-road - I rode to St. Giles' church, they came very pleasantly all the way; I should think about five or six miles an hour, at a regular rate - there was no racing - I remember hearing of the accident perfectly well; we were standing still fifty-five yards on this side Moumouth-street, when I saw the child in the road, and I said, "That child will be killed," and it was run over at that very moment; it was not a proper crossing place at all - no blame at all was to be attributed to the prisoner.
 - CLARK. I was in St. Giles's, near Shillibeer's Omnibus; I am a baker and live in Junction-terrace, Edgeware-road; I witnessed the accident, there was no blame to be attributed to the driver of Bardell's omnibus, he pulled up immediately.
 - HAND. I am a milkman, and live at Hampstead. I was sitting in a window, in St. Giles's, and saw the omnibuses going at the rate of seven or eight miles an hour - I observed the accident; they were not racing - no blame at all was to be attributed to the driver - he pulled up as quick as he could.

 1403.  JOSEPH STONE was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 5th of August , from an evil disposed person, 1 tea-chest, value 1s.; and 80 lbs. of tea, value 17l., the goods of John Nicholson , knowing the same to have been stolen.
 MR. AYCKBONE conducted the Prosecution.
   JOHN NICHOLSON. I am a tea-agent, and live in Fenchurch-street. I purchased the chest of tea stated in the indictment, at the June tea sale; it stands in my list in the third class, and is described by the Company's tea tasters, as middling Congon - I had it at 2s. 2d. and the duty is the same, which made it 4s. 4d. per lb.; it was left in the warehouse as is usual till it is wanted, and I had never made out any order at any time for its being delivered - about the 12th or 13th August, I heard it was gone I then missed it from my warrant.
