   MR. DUNBAR . Q.  You do not know who sent for you to Guildhall? A.  No; two officers came—I told the Magistrate he was not the man—the prisoner had no whiskers when he was before the Magistrate—his face was the same as it is now—Andrews was the officer before the Magistrate—I told the Magistrate I could not swear to either of the men.
   Q.  Did not you tell the Alderman you would not swear the prisoner was not the man, for you could not be certain of any of them but Smith? A.  No more I am; I know the stout man. JAMES ANDREWS re-examined . The prisoner had whiskers before the Alderman, and has for the last two months—they were not very large.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Have you been round to instruct counsel since the trial began? A.  Yes; when I heard the waiter go on as he did, which is highly improper—this is not a case got up by the police—I do not know that the inspector has retained counsel—Fagan spoke to me something about it—I do not know whether he instructed counsel—he is sitting behind the counsel—I do not know that the brief is his getting up.
   MR. DUNBAR . Q.  Was the witness asked before the Magistrate whether he could speak with certainty to the prisoner's person? A.  He was; he said it was impossible to swear to either of them, as there was so many persons coming in and out of the house—he said he could not swear to any person at all. JAMES BATES re-examined . I have seen the prisoner about the town a good deal—he is in the habit of wearing whiskers. THOMAS SMITH re-examined . The prisoner had whiskers when I met him, and before the Magistrate.
