133. JAMES MOORE (33), Feloniously setting fire to two stacks of
grain, the property of Henry Barnfield.
MR. MAHAPPY Prosecuted.
FRANCIS RICHARDS (81 T.) I am stationed at Bedfont--about 1.10
8.m. on Sunday, December 27th, I was on duty at the police station
there--the prisoner came in and made a statement, which I took a note
of-he said, "I have come to give myself up for setting fire to those
stacks "-I cautioned him, and he said, "About 12 o'clock I went into
a field on the left of the road just beyond the village, and laid down under
& stack for a sleep, as I had walked from London and had no bed to go
to; I struck & match and lighted my pipe, and then threw the match
down, when it at once set the stack on fire; I did not try to put it out;
I did not know what to do; I went into the road and watched the fire
until the other staok caught, which took fire through the wind blowing
the flames on to it; I did not call or give alarm until two chapa came out
of the house there, when I told them the stacks were on fire; I then
walked back towards London "-later on I charged him on his own con
fession with setting fire to the stacks-he made no reply.
"By the Court. The station is about a mile from the scene of the fire.
William RANDALL. I live at 69, Cambridge Grove Road, Kingston-
on December 27th, about 12.30 a.m., I was in the Staines Road, Bedfont
- saw some corn stacks on fire-I saw the prisoner in the road going
towards Bedfont and away from the fire-I stopped him and asked hinn
if he knew anything about the fire-he replied, “No " on the same day
I identified him at the station.
By the Court. The stacks were blazing when I saw the prisoner-
I did not go up to them-I do not know who put them out-I was going
home-the prisoner was about fifty yards from the stacks.
