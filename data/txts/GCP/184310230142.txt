2985. MARY BLABER was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of Sent.
2 water bottles, value 58.; the goods of James Blenkinsopp, her master.
JAMES BLENKINSOPP. I live in Christopher-street, Finsbury, and am
oil merchant. On the 10th of Aug., the prisoner came into my service-
missed two water bottles, a wine decanter, and a great many other things
these are my water bottles-(looking at them)---they have my mark on them.
ELIZABETH IMPET. I bought these bottles of the prisoner, one a forte
night after the other.
JAMES BRANNAN (police-sergeant G 20.) I took the prisoner-she said
she took the bottles, but she knew nothing about the spoon.
Prisoner. Mrs. Blenkinsopp gave the bottles to me-my master knew
where to find me. Witness. Mrs. Blenkinsopp was examined at Worship
street on her oath, and she swore to the contrary before the prisoner.
1 MR, BLENKINSOPP re-examined. When she had been with me about
month she came home drunk-I went into the kitchen, and missed a great
number of things, and on the 16th of Sept. I discharged her.
Guilty. Aged 43.-Confined Six Months.
2986. MARGARET THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th
of Oct., 2 shirts, value 68,; and 1 gown, 58.; the goods of Cuthbert
Elsdon.
JANE ELSDON. I am the wife of Cuthbert Elsdon, we live at Shadwell.
On the 5th of Oct. I was absent about ten minutes, and missed from my
back kitchen two shirts and a gown-these are them.
JAMES Moody. I am a pawnbroker. I took in these things of the pri-
soner and another woman--the prisoner bad half-a-crown for the two shirts,
and the other woman had 28. for the gown.
MARY ANN GULLY. On the 5th of Oct. I saw the prisoner go into Mr.
Elsdon's house-she had nothing with her, but when she came out she had
something in her apron-in five minutes Mrs. Elsdon missed her things.
JAMES ROOKER (police-constable K 243.). I took the prisoner from the
description of Mrs. Gully and the pawnbroker - I took another woman first
- I found the prisoner at the Thames Police Court, where she was charged
with being drunk and disorderly.
Prisoner. Q. Did not you take me to a broker's shop, and did not the
woman say I was not the person? 4. It was a second-hand shop they
said it was another woman who offered them for sale-I took you to the
pawnbroker, and he identified you-Mrs. Gully identified you the moment
she saw you.
