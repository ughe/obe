317. MARGARET THOMPSON, stealing one half sovereign; the
moneys of William Seaton.
EMMA SBATON. My husband's name is William ; we live at Stratford,
On 20th Jan., I went to Mr. Scott's shop, and made some purchases-I had
a half sovereign, which I laid on the counter while I went and held a piece
of bacon to be cut-I put the half sovereign on the counter towards Jane
Scott, who was serving me--the prisoner was in the sbop very near to me,
and no one else but Miss Scott and myself—when the bacon bad been cut I
turned round to pay for it, and the half sovereign was gone I spoke to
Miss Scott about it; I thought she must have taken it up, and put it in the
till-she said she had not taken it; she had no gold at all the prisoner had
bought a piece of pork, and was trying to get out of the shop as soon as she
could, but she first turned her pocket out, and said all she bad was 61d.
that was before she was accused of taking the half sovereign-she said she
must go home to prepare her husband's dinner, and left.
Prisoner. Had not I 9d.? Witness. 65d. was all I saw ; it was in a

Jane Scort. I am daughter of Mr. John Scott, of Stratford, and attend

recollect seeing the hall sovereign on the counter-Mrs. Seaton afterwards
spoke to me about it-I am quite sure L bad not taken it up-I looked in
the till, and I had no gold at all; and I looked all over the floor, outside
the counter and inside.
WILLIAM JAMES LAWES. I am a pawnbroker, at Stratford-On 20th Jan.,
