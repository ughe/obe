Yes—I did not see it in the barge for eight or none hours after—I do not know whether other persons had been in the barge—there was a policeman in charge of it—I was relieved at nine o'clock, and another sentinel placed there—I had stood and looked at what was going on—I saw everything distinctly—I saw them all three in their turn shifting the canvas and rope—I saw that from where I was standing—it is my duty to watch—I saw a bread bag removed, and what appeared to be metal. STEPHEN ELLIS( policeman R  162.) I am employed at Woolwich Dock-yard—on Friday evening, the 30th of July, about half-past seven o'clock, Kendall gave me information, and my attention was directed to a boat along side of a barge—there were three persons in it; Court was one, I knew him—I cannot speak positively to the others—I saw them take something from the boat and put it on board the barge—I suspected it was not right—the three persons then left the barge, got into the boat, and rowed it to the Bell Water-gate pier—I ran round, and saw Court in the boat, which belonged to him; it had his name on it—the other two prisoners left the boat, and got on the pier, and Court was handing them some things which appeared to be sailors' clothing—I asked them what they had got, they said property they had brought from the Spiteful  steamer—I asked them their names—they gave the same names that they did afterwards to the Magistrate—I am quite sure the prisoners are the persons.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you take them into custody then? A.  No, they were taken the next day—i had never seen Adams before, to my knowledge—he gave the name of Henry Adams, and when he came before the Magis trate, he gave the name of Richard Adams—I am sure he is the same man. WILLIAM JAMES BUDD( police-inspector. ) I was on duty at Woolwich Dockyard, on the 30th of July—some information was given me, and I called on Sergeant Glascock and Turner to go on board a barge—I went on board about eight o'clock, and found a great many ashes, some rope, canvas, copper sheathing, and a piece of copper bolt, in a corner of the barge; and a great many clews and lashing in the force part of the barge—they could not well cover them, there were so many—these produced are the articles—there was this pot and this pipe, and this sheet-copper—I then left the barge in possession of the constable—this is the canvas and rope.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is this the greater portion of the canvas? A.  I think about half of it—there was something alongside the barge, which is used for putting ashes into, previous to bringing them to the barge—the barge was at anchor about one hundred yards from the shore—there was nothing between that and the wharf.
