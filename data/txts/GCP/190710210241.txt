FREDERICK REED, Elmeroft, Bedfont, no occupation, In April this.
year I received circulars 16 and 17 from W. Tyler and Co., pointing!
out the advantages of buying at once shares in Rio Tinto. I deter-
mined to buy 100 shares and sent a cheque for £100 to Tyler and
Co., filling up the yellow application form as directed. That cheque
has been paid by my bankers. In reply I got letter No. 18, and a
volume giving the history of the Rio Tinto Mine. I also got the
contract for the sale of the 100 shares. (Exhibit 19.) I was an
invalid and did not read the terms of business on the back. I posted
the letter, enclosing the cheque, before the last post for London,
which goes out between seven and eight. I had crossed the cheque
I got a letter from Tyler and Co., dated April 18 (Exhibit 21), and
determined to buy 400 more Rio Tinto shares. On April 20 I for
warded two cheques to W. Tyler and Co., one for. £50, which I
crossed, and another for £350, which was uncrossed. Both have
been paid by my bankers. I got back Exhibit 23, dated April 22,
and on the 24th or 25th I got Exhibit 24, which is dated April 24,
which latter refers to the fact that my letter enclosing the two
cheques did not reach them till the Monday morning. I received
a contract for the Rio Tinto shares, dated April 23, selling to me 400
Rio Tintos at 92, cum. dividend for the end April account. On the
back there are some conditions printed, but I did not read them.
It is very unusual for any contract note sent by a broker to bear
any conditions at all. I watched the quotations in the financial papers
and then wrote to Tyler and Co. I said I considered Rio Tintos
were rising up to the time that the dividend was payable. I noticed
that from the tape quotations. I received Exhibit 26, stating the
cover had run off on the 24th. I received no other letters from Tyler
and Co. I came up to London on April 29 with the intention of going
to Tyler and Co.'s, but went somewhere else, and, in consequence of
what I heard there, instead of going to Tyler's I went to my soli-
citor's and afterwards to the Guildhall Police Court. At the time I
received their circular I believed Tyler and Co to be dealers in Stock
Exchange securities, but not that they were members of the Stock
Exchange. In parting with my money I understood I was giving £1
cover for every £100 of stock, and that would cover the number of
shares I purchased from Tyler, and that the control of those shares
would give me the right to the dividend that was approaching
quickly. I was going to Tyler and Co. with reference to the pay
ment of those dividends.
Cross-examined by Mr. F. E. Smith. I did not consider I was
running any risk. I thought the real question was the payment of
the £3 dividend on the 36th. I thought I was certain to get the
dividend and make more than I had invested. After the payment of
the dividend I thought the shares would rise to their original value.
The dividend did not come from Tyler and Co., but from the com
pany. It was no speculation because the dividend had already been
declared. I did not contemplate the shares falling more than the
