Barker's contract of December 22, 1904. I often used to go to Spooner's
bouse to get a drink and collect money for a philanthropical society of
which I was chairman. There is no truth in the suggestion that I was to
receive money from young Barker for voting for him on that contract, nor
did I know of any Guardians doing so. I have never seen Calcutt in
Spooner's house. I remember taking exception to something in
one of Barker's contracts. My attention was called by an
inmate to a very large hole half as big as this Court. He
mid,Mr. Trott, go and look down there; they are digging
big hole there and taking lots of sand away." I went and looked
at it and askud the foreman of the job about it, and he mid he knew
nothing of it. I told my colleagues on the Roard that Barker was taking
the sand without the knowledge of the Guardians. I think Knight was
remiss in his duty. He said it was being done for the lavatories, but they
were right away from it. It was in 1907, or might be January, 1906. It
was dacided that young Barker should pay £100 for the sand he had
taken. I did not think it enough. It is false that Mrs. Caloutt went
upstairs and got £10 out of the safe when I called at the house, or any
other sum. I went round for a Christmas account (£5 2s.6d.) about 1908.
The latter end of 1904 Calcutt gave me an overcont. I never wear such
thing. I was going up the road and he came along and tapped me on
the shoulder and said, “Where are you off to?" I said, "I am taking a
stroll round." He said, “Come and have a drink. I am going to Loftus's;
I am going to try some clothes on in Loftus's." I went with him, and he
mid, "Why don't you get measured for a suit of clothes ?" I said, "I
don't want any; I have two or three suits at home." He said, "The cold
weather is coming on; why don't you get measured for an overcoat?" I
mid, "I don't want it." He said, “Here, shove the tape over him," and I
mid, "You are a bloomingnuisance." It was done ina moment, and that is how
that transaction was done. I gave the coat to the arman to wear. I never
thought it was given for anything I had done on the Board. I keep no
books, I remember going to Southend with Allen and meeting Mrs.
Calcutt on the front. Mrs. Calcutt begged me to get on the telephone.
She said " Jim will be wild if you don't," and he came down. I believe
they were staying in apartments. We went for a drive and lunched at
bis house. I also met him on another occasion, accidentally, going to the
tmin coming home. Those are the only two occasions I was with Calcutt
at Southend. It is not true that I was ever entertained by Calcutt to
champagne in the "Ship Hotel" at Southend. It is an absolute lie. I was
regular attendant at the meetings of the Board. Whenever I voted for
Calcutt I did so conscientiously. When Caloutt was elected, the Guardians
now charged supported him; but I think somebody had a spite against
him. I heard someone say, "Why always Calcutt, why not give someone
else a chance ?" At Christmas, 1905, I had a present from Calcutt of s
bundle of cigars and a bottle of whisky. No turkey. I used to have
two or three come otherwise.
