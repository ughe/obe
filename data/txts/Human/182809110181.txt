   ELIZABETH SOUTHATE. I saw the prisoner go to his lodging on the Sunday evening, a little before ten o'clock- he had a shirt and a red shawl on the top of it: the next morning, at half-past eight I was at breakfast. and my window was up - he came out of the house with a bundle under his left arm.
   JAMES WALKER. I am a Bow-street patrol. On Tuesday, the 9th of September, I went with Wilkins to take the prisoner, in Chapel-street - he denied all knowledge of the clothes; I found nothing on him but a key and 1s. 6d.
   JOSEPH SUTTON. My brother-in-law keeps the Crown and Sceptre public-house; I met the prisoner in the street, on the 9th of September - I said there was a charge against him - he said he was entirely innocent, and would go anywhere - he went to the Crown and Sceptre; I went for an officer, and when I returned, he was taken.
   JOHN WILKINS re-examined. Q. Do you know what clothes the prisoner had? A. No more than he stood up in and a shirt; there was no servant in the house - no one but him slept with me.
 MRS. HANNAH WRIGHT re-examined. Q. Was there anybody there but the prisoner on Monday? A. No, there are other persons who live up stairs, but they never come into my place - they could have got in; the parlour door is sometimes open, but the street-door has always been shut.
 Prisoner's Defence. On the Monday morning, I got up at half-past eight o'clock, and when I came out of my room I brought away my dirty things; I had put my clean ones on - I had a black waistcoat, one pair of Angola stockings and one pair of clean white ones, and two handkerchiefs; they were tied up in a loose red shawl; my landlady had pressed me for the rent; I did not return; when this man came up to me, I said I would go anywhere, and I went to the Crown and Sceptre, and staid there an hour and a half; I then came out, and met the constable.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1949.  ANN ELY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , 1 gown, value 7s.; 1 shirt, value 6s.; 1 waistcoat, value 2s.; 1 petticoat, value 3s.; 1 shift, value 1s.; 1 cap, value 5s.; 1 towel, value 1d., and 1 pair of stockings, value 6d., the goods of Thomas Vickers.
   JANE VICKERS. I am the wife of Thomas Vickers, and live at Poplar, On the 9th of August, I gave the prisoner the articles stated in the indictment, to wash; she was to return them at six o'clock in the afternoon, but she did not- she lived in Green Arbour-court: I have never seen any of them since - in the afternoon I went to her place, and she was not there; I went again next morning, but she was not there - I met her about three weeks afterwards in Cloth fair; I called her by name - she pretended she did not hear me; I followed her till I came in front of her, and then asked her how she was, and where she lived; she said at Hoxton - I said "You might as well come and see me;" she said she did not like on account of the things - I said "Never mind the things at all; you may as well come and see me" - she said she did not like to come on account of the people in the next room; I said they had nothing to do with me, for I had had words with them - after a good deal of persuasion, I got her to come; when my husband came home, I gave charge of her - she said at first she had pawned the flannel petticoat, and then she denied it - when the officer came, she said she had had a little drink and lost the things.
   ISAAC NEWTON. I am a street-keeper. I was sent for and found the prisoner there; I asked her if she had pawned the things; she said No, they were stolen from her, either in going home or bringing them home - I forget which: I searched her and found nothing - I again asked her if she had pawned them, and said it would be better to give up the duplicates; she said No, they were stolen.
 COURT to JANE VICKERS. Q. Did she say in the officers presence that she had pawned the petticoat? A. Yes.
 Prisoner. I did not say so.
     GUILTY. Aged 46. Confined One Year.
 1950.  CHARLES FERGUSON was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , 2 pairs of boots, value 1l. 16s., the goods of John Huttley.
 JOHN HUTTLEY. I am a  boot and shoemaker, and live in Middle-row, Broad-street, St. Gile's. I did not miss these boots till the officer brought them - I looked into my place, and when I came to make a calculation, I found there were more than these gone.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You cannot say that you have seen the prisoner? A. No - I have between four and five hundred pairs; I am confident these had not been sold - my shopman, who sometimes sells in my absence, might have sold them; I can only answer for myself - when we sell boots and shoes, we enter in the book the price they sell for; every boot in the house is numbered, but we do not enter the numbers, only the price: we cannot tell the numbers of the boots we sell.
   JAMES LEDGER. I am an officer. I produce two pairs of boots which I found on the prisoner, in King-street, Seven-dials, about two hundred yards from the prosecutor's place, at a quarter past nine o'clock, that night - he was carrying them in a bag, and I asked him what was in it: he said "Two pairs of boot;" I asked him what kind of boots - he said "One pair of top, and one pair of Wellington" - I asked where he got them from - he said they were his own making, but declined to give me any more answers.(Property produced and sworn to.)
