CHARLES THOMAS UPCOTT. I live in Wood-street. On the 5th of July, about ten o'clock, I was near the New Post-office in Cheapside—I was told something, and missed my handkerchief—I followed the prisoner and took him—this now produced is my handkerchief, and the one I lost. JOHN DENNIS. I live in Friday-street. I was near the Post-office, and saw the prisoner behind the prosecutor, with his coat in his hand—I saw him take his hand from under his coat—I told the prosecutor, and he fol lowed him. JAMES BRADFORD( City police-constable, No.  216.) I found the hand kerchief in the door-way of Mr. Millard, in Newgate-street.
   MR. UPCOTT re-examined.  The prisoner crossed from Cheapside to Paternoster-row, and then through Panyer-alley to Newgate-street—there is no doubt he dropped the handkerchief before the officer came up to him. GUILTY.** Aged 20.— Transported for Ten Years.
 1891. GEORGE ABBEY was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of June , 1 pair of shoes, value 3 s  ., the goods of John Barnjum ; and that he bad been before convicted of felony; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 12.— Transported for Seven Years—Convict Ship.
 1892. FRANCIS CARNEY was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of July , 1 brush, value 1 s  ., the goods of Henry Goodwin Marner , his master. HENRY GOODWIN MARNER. I live in Charles-street, Hatton-garden. The prisoner was in my service—in consequence of suspicion I got an officer, and had the prisoner and his bundle searched—this brush was found in it—it is an old brush, which has been in our family sixty or seventy Years. HENRY REDMAN( police-constable G  224.) I searched the bundle—I asked the prisoner what he had got in it—he said, What was that to me?—I took him back to his master, and found the brush in his bundle.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I know nothing about it—I did not put it in—I have come away every Sunday morning, and was never stopped in the street before. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Six Months.
 1893. GEORGE GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of July , 1 printed book, value 2 s  . 6 d  . the goods of Frederick Ensoll . FREDERICK ENSOLL. I am a bookseller, living in Paddington-street, Marylebone. I had this book safe in the morning, on the 1st of July—I had not sold it—it has my private mark on it—it has not been bound above six weeks. ABRAHAM SIMS. This book was offered to me for sale by the prisoner—I stopped him with it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The book is mine; I bought it six months ago. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Three Months.
 1894. JOHN JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of June , 1 sixpence, 1 penny, and 8 halfpence, the monies of George Bishop . MARY ANN BISHOP. I am the wife of George Bishop, a publican, in Westminster. On the 29th of June the prisoner came for some porter—he gave me a bad shilling—after giving him change I saw it was bad—I said it was bad—he said it was not, and ran out.
