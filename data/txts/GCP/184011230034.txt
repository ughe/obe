46. WILLIAM GROVE PRIESTLY was indicted for a misdemeanor.
Messrs. Ellis and BODKIn conducted the Prosecution.
JOSEPH WORMALL (police-sergeant H 16.) On the 28th of October I
went with two other officers to the Red Cow public-house, Shoreditch-I
concealed myself at the back of the premises in a skittle-ground-in about
half an hour I saw the prisoner come to a certain place, and put his hand
down to a corner of the wooden fence-I heard the sound of money rattle-
he went away, and returned to the public-house-after he was gone I went
to the place where he put his hand, and found a bag with 14 half-crowns
and 22 shillings, all counterfeit-I took out three half-crowns and three
shillings, and gave them to Burcham to mark-I then put the bag in
the place I took it from-I then sent one officer to the front of the house,
and the other to the back-I staid in the skittle-ground, and in about
an hour the prisoner came into the skittle-ground again, ang went to the
same place where the bag was- I heard the sound of money, and as he
was returning I followed, and stopped him-he wanted to know what was
the matter-I said, “Nothing particular," but I wished to be satisfied
before he left the ground-I sprung my rattle, and Burrham came-I
searched the prisoner, and found 28. 6d. in good money, a bad sixpence,
one shilling, and a halfpenny in copperI went back to where I had left
the bag- I could not find it-I then turned on my light, and found the
earth was removed, and the bag was covered with mould-up other person
had been to that corner between the time I was there and the prisoner
going there-I showed the bag to the prisoner-he said he did not know
any thing about it-there was an attenípt to rescue him, but we got him
away-there was no deficiency in the bag-it had been removed about two
feet, and covered with mould I cannot swear that when he first arrived
there was no bag there-it was sufficiently light for me to see the man that
came first-there was a light in the passage I was at the end of the
ground, and I could see his person quite distinctly.
THOMAS BURCHAM (police-constable H 33.) I have three shillings
which I received from Wormall.
TIMOTHY TOOMEY (police-constable H 89.) I produce three half-
crowns and a sixpence-I was with the sergeant-the prisoner is the per-
son that came in the fence where the bag was is where the boards are put
to keep the skittles--we could see him plainly come in on the left-hand side
- he put his hands down, and we turned on our lights the first time to see
the bag, we then kept our lights under our coats.
MR. JOHN FIELD. Here are 22 counterfeit shillings in the bag, all cast
in the same mould, and 14 half-crowns all cast in the same mould, and all
quite fresh-here is one counterfeit sixpence.
GUILTY.. Aged 59.-Confined Two Years.
