663. ANN MCARTHY was indicted for a like offence.
SARAH YOUNG. I am the wife of James Young, who keeps the Two
Brewers, Little St. Andrew-street, Seven Dials. On the 27th of Jan., about
ten o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came for half-a-pint of beer she
gave me a sixpence-I put it into the till-there was no other money there,
except balfpence-I gave her 5 d., and when my bar-man came for change
for 18., about ten minutes after, I took out the sixpence, and found it was
bad-I put it at the further end of the till, in paper-she came again next
morning for half-a-pint of beer-my bar-man seryed her she gave him a six-
pence, and he brought it to me for change-I kept it in my hand while he said
he would go to get change, but he got a policeman-I gave both the six-
pences to him.
JACOB Cox. I am barman to Mrs. Young. On the 26th of January a six-
pence was given to me by the prisoner-I gave it to my mistress.
WILLIAM HOWARD (police-constable F 94.) I got these two sixpences
from Mrs. Young.
Mr. John Field. These sixpences are both counterfeit, and both cast in
one mould.

664. MARY COOK and JANE PENN were indicted for a like offence.
WILLIAM Druce. I am the son of Thomas Druce, butcher, Weymouth-
street, Marylebone. On the 27th of January the two prisoners came to the shop,
and one of them asked if we had any pieces of meat-they both pulled the
pieces over-I sold them three quarters of a pound for threepence-Cook gave
me a sixpence-Penn was close by and saw the sixpence-I put it in the bowl
-it was quite a new-looking sixpence—there were two very old sixpences in
the bowl-both the prisoners went away together-after they were gone I
took the new-looking sixpence out of the bowl, before any other had been put
in-I looked at it, and let it be in the bowl till a man came in and told me
something I looked at the sixpence and marked it-I afterwards gave it to
Allen.
