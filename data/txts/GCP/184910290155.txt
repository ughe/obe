HENRY JAMES MELLER, Esq. I am a barrister, of the Middle Temple,
and occupy chambers at 4, Brick-court. On 25th Sept. I attended the
Middlesex Session professionally, and defended two men named Fisher and
Garrod-there was a third, of the name of George, who I did not defend-I
was not instructed to do so I had received some communication from my
clerk, who is here, but I did not defend him.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you know that the prisoner is clerk to Mr.
Townshend, in Clifford's-inn? A. I believe he is-I believe he lives in the
Inn-up to this time I have always considered him a decent respectable man-
I saw him in Court about an hour after the men had been tried--they were all
convicted-it was a very bad case, especially as it regarded George-it is a
matter of opinion whether George was in good luck, to get off with twelve
months, being known before. be
JOHN BROOKS. I am clerk to Mr. Meller. On Tuesday morning, 25th
Sept., the prisoner spoke to me-he did not give me a brief, or any instruc-
tions to defend a man of the name of George.
Cross-examined. Q. He said he had something to do with a young man,
but he did not know his name? A. He said it was something with regard
to a young man with two others for stealing a looking-glass, but he did not
know the name.
Robert Hall. I am a clerk in the Register-office of Attorneys. I have
examined the roll of attorneys at the office of the Incorporated Law Society
in Chancery-lane I do not find the prisoner's name there as an attorney-
Mr. James, the solicitor for the prosecution, called, and I examined the list
in consequence.
Cross-examined. Q. The Incorporated Law Society have nothing to do
with this prosecution ? A. No.
MR. PRENDERGAST called
WORLEY. I live at Rockingham-row, New Kent-road. I have
known the prisoner about five or six years he has borne the character of an
honest person-I have consulted him as Mr. Towshend's clerk.
(Three other witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.)
GUILTY.-Strongly recommended to mercy by the Jury.-Confined Three
Weeks.
