GEORGE GRESHAM BELCHER. I am a cotton-broker, residing and carry.
ing ou business at Manchester-when I came to town they put me up at

will show the exact amount-in consequence of a communication made to
me in April by Dawson, I applied to the Court of Chancery, and was
appointed Receiver-on 2nd May I went to the premises in order, anong
other things, to take an inventory-the female prisoner was present-I
told her that I wished to take an inventory of certain articles of mine,

linen as head housekeeper-I never made any suggestion to her to leave
her father's house, or anything that could be construed into such a state
of things-she had said tu me that she was very unhappy at home, so
ill-used by her father, and so uncomfortable, that she intended to leave
home and go into service-I never interfered with her at all-I never
Buid. Will you leave your father's house, as I am going to ruin him.

brother has turned round upon you”-I never made any such statement,
nothing to that effect at all-I never suggested to her to pretend to get &
