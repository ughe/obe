   Cross-examined. Q.  If you merely wanted to look for dirty linen, why shut the door? A.  I had no particular reason—I did not mind who saw it—Mrs. Gooding's room is four or five steps higher than the prisoner's—she told me that day that she saw me at the box, and I told her it was false. NOT GUILTY.
 1574. WILLIAM CAWLEY was indicted for stealing, on the   30th of May , 3 hat leathers, value 6 d  .; and five half leathers, value 1 s  .; the goods of Benjamin Rider and another, his masters. BENJAMIN RIDER. I am in partnership with my father—we live in South wark. The prisoner was about five weeks in our employ—on the 29th of May I missed some hat leathers—I went to the prisoner's house in consequence of suspicion, and saw a female who I believe was his wife, on the 30th of May, and purchased of her three hat leathers, out of about eight dozen, which she produced—the prisoner had never bought any of us—I then informed my father, and went with a policeman and searched his house—I found three whole hat leathers in his parlour; and fifteen halves up stairs—I knew them to be ours by our stamping—we sell them in that states; but he never bought any—I have the machine that they were cut by, which enables me to know my own work—I only know it to be the prisoner's house in consequence of what his brother said—I never saw him there myself. ALEXANDER KERR. I am a policeman. I searched the house which Mr. Rider took me to—I do not know it to be the prisoner's house—it was No. 11, Pitts-place—I never saw him there, or had any conversation with him about the property found there. NOT GUILTY.
 1575. JAMES EDMONDS was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and STURGEON conducted the Prosecution . JOHN SIMPSON. I am a chandler, and live in St. Alban's-street, Lam beth. The prisoner came to my shop about half-past nine o'clock at night, on the 6th of June, and asked for half an ounce of tobacco—I weighed the tobacco—he gave me a counterfeit shilling—I told him it was a bad one—he said he had taken it by selling things in the street—I told him I much doubted it, and gave him in charge—I marked the shilling in the presence of the officer, and gave it into his hands. JOSEPH METHERILL( policeman L  142.) On the night of the 6th of June I was on duty near Mr. Simpson's shop—he called me and gave the prisoner in charge—he said he had given him a bad shilling—I searched him, and found another bad shilling on him and some duplicates. JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of counterfeit coin to the Mint. These are both counterfeit, and impressions from the same mould.
