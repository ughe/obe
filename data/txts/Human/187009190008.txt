 711. WILLIAM JOHNSON  (39) , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin. MESSRS. COLERIDGE and WEIGHTMAN conducted the Prosecution. JOHN MOYSER. I am barman at the Clarence public-house, Charing Cross—on Sunday, 21st August, between 8 and 9 o'clock, the prisoner came in and called for a pint of four half,  which came to 2 d .—I knew him before, and suspected him—he gave me a shilling—I tried it and found it was bad—I had seen him before, on 7th June, when he gave me a gilt sixpence, representing a half-sovereign—my master gave him in custody with the gilt sixpence.
   Prisoner. Q.  When you put the change on the counter, did I touch it? A.  No, I did not give you time to take it—I told the Magistrate it was a counterfeit sixpence, but I found it was a good one, gilt—you did not say that you wanted change for a sixpence. WALTEB NOAD( Policeman G  61). Two months ago I was in the A division—the prisoner was given in my custody, on 7th June, for trying to pass counterfeit coin—Mr. Moyser gave me the half-sovereign, and I gave it to my inspector, and took the prisoner to the station—no more coin in found on him, and the charge was not pressed. DAVID BALDRY( Police Inspector A ). I was on duty when the prisoner was brought to the station, charged with uttering—he gave his name John Porter, and was discharged. JOHN COCKSON( Policeman ). Moyser gave the prisoner into my custody for passing this bad shilling ( proofed ) on 21st August. WILLIAM WEBSTER. This is a good sixpence, gilt—this shilling is bad.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I gave the sixpence for a sixpence, and wished for my change. The barman said "Do you know what you have given?"I said "A sixpence, and I wish for change for a sixpence. "I looked at him with surprise when he put down change for a half-sovereign. I never touched the change. NOT GUILTY.
