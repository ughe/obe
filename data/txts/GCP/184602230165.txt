JONATHAN Cook (police-constable P 254.) On Sunday evening, the 25th
o Jan., I was fetched to the prisoner's house-I tried to get in at the front
door I could not it was locked-I was obliged to get in at the window I
found the prisoner's wife lying bleeding, with her throat cut, on her back, in
the parlour, near the parlour fire-place-the prisoner was holding her hands,
apparently to keep her still—he had his shirt on, and a very great deal of
blood upon it-she had her night-gown on and stockings, but no night-cap-
the prisoner cried out to me what was be to do-Mr. Sharland, the milkman,
was there and the brother-I said, “Send for the doctor immediately," and
ve sent for one at the time the man was gone for the doctor the prisoner
asked his wife who did it-she gave no answer, but looked very hard-I asked
her who did it, and she looked very hard but gave no answer-she looked at
both of us--we were both before her-I found this razor at the right side of
the woman, and the left side of the man, about as far from her as she could reach
with her right arm, and about where he was kneeling with his left knee--it
was not quite close-there was blood on the handle, as it is now, and partly
cloved-Mr. Otway came to the door-he tried to come in-I asked the pri-
soner for the key-he said he did not know where it was he was sure-Mr.
Otway got in at the window afterwards-after she was removed to the bed I
asked the prisoner where the key was, and he went to his coat pocket and
took it out-when I asked him before, he said he did not know where it was,
for he was out having a glass or two on the Saturday night-after that the
prisoner asked her who did it, and I asked her who did it, and she laid still
and looked very hard-she then rose herself up in the bed and shook hands
with him, and kissed him, and pointed and showed him where some money
was, which he found under the bed—I saw him take it from there some
pen, ink, and paper were given to her a few minutes after the prisoner was
there at that time-she then wrote these words on this paper-(producing
1)-I took the prisoner into custody-she held up her fist at me, as though
sho dared me to touch him, and then I put my hand out and shook hands
with him.
Cross-examined. Q. And when you did that she seemed pleased, did she
not? 4. I did not see any difference-she laid still and looked hard-when
I took hold of her husband she still laid still-she first of all shook her fist
at me, but after I shook hands she laid still-- when she shook her fist at me
I was not about to take him-I was talking to him, and she shook her hand
at me and then put her hand out as though she would shake hands with
him--she did before that, and kissed him-not then-Mr. Otway was in the
room at that time, and the witness Vasey-tbe kissing and shaking of hands
attracted a great deal of attention from all of us-we were all looking at it-
I am quite sure it took place, and not very likely I should forget it-the
prisoner appeared in a very distressed state to meno doubt he was-he kept
saying, "Who did it, who did it ? how was it done!"-he said so three times.
MR. ROBINSON. Q. I suppose you were paying particular attention to
the state of the woman? A. I paid attention to the woman and the man too
I cannot undertake to say what was seen by other persons at the time of
her shaking her fist at me I had not made any attempt to take the prisoner
into custody-I only told him before her, that he must not leave the room-
I was in my uniform.
