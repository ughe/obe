   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you keep close to him? A.  Yes—I had him in custody and followed him wherever he went—there was another constable with us.
   JURY. Q.  Did Williamson stoop down in the cellar, or merely kick the sawdust? A.  He stooped, but did not pick up anything. JOHN THOMAS VOUSDEN. I am potman at the Pied Horse, Chiswell-street—on the evening of the 9th, about eight o'clock, Rudland and William son came—I went to the Broad Arrow, saw Mandell, and asked if there had been such a thing as a 10 l . note lost—he said, "No,"—I asked if he should have found it if there had been—he said, Yes he knew every corner of the house—I asked if there had been any parties inquiring about one—he said there had been no inquiries made—the Pied Horse is about five minutes' walk from the Broad Arrow. CHARLES RUDLAND re-examined.  I had searched the front of the bar, on Wednesday about three o'clock, to the best of my recollection, there were no chairs, if there were it was only one or two—I searched myself and am posi tive there was no note there—the gas was alight.
   Mandell's Defence.  On sweeping up the place I found the note, gave it Mr. Williamson directly, and said, "You can take better care of it than I can"—he said he would keep it and advertise it, and whatever reward was offered I should have—I did not have it in my hand three minutes.
