WILLIAM AYRES and RICHARD KENNEY were indicted for stealing, on the 25th of October , 4 coats, value 7l; 4 waistcoats, value 12s; 1 pair of breeches, value 5s.; 1 pair of boots, value 5s.; 1 hat, value 15s.; 1 pair of drawers, value 6d.; 1 pair of shoes, value 5s., and 1 piece of baize, value 1s., the goods of William Davison , in his dwelling-house.
 WILLIAM DAVISON. I am a  coachman, and live at No. 3, Little Grove-street, Lisson-grove, St. Mary-le-bone- it is stables: I sleep there occasionally, and have a bed there constantly - I keep part of my clothes there. On the 25th of October I lost some property out of the stables - I had seen the stables about nine o'clock in the morning, before the things were stolen; I cannot say whether they were locked - I was at the stables again in the evening, about seven, but did not go up stairs: the room I sleep in is not generally locked - I had seen the clothes a day or two before: I found the prisoners in custody on Monday morning, the 27th of October, and some clothes were produced which I thought to be mine - I had bought them old; I saw some coats, but cannot say whether there were four -I believe they were mine; I have a doubt of it.
 Q. Had some coats been taken from your room between the morning of the 25th of October and the time you saw them at the office? A. I should not like to swear that - I could not swear it: it was dark when I got up; I cannot swear I left any clothes there.
 Q. Were they there the night before, when you went to bed? A. There were very few things there when I went up at night; I lost things from the stable, because the things I had seen there and worn before were not there.
 Q. Well, I ask you whether the things you had there were gone in the morning? A. Yes - I did not understand you; I lost a box-coat, and saw a box-coat at the office - it did not look so fresh and so well as mine, when I saw it last; I thought it was mine, and swore it was mine to the best of my knowledge - I might have had it four months; it was of the same colour: it was very large, and would fit almost two of my size - it was very much tumbled about- I thought it was mine; I should not like to swear to it: I saw some breeches at the office, and supposed them to be mine - I had had them about the place for a week; I should be sorry to swear to them: I thought the boots mine, but cannot swear to them - there was a hat, which I believe to be mine, but there is no particular mark on it; there was a pair of new shoes - I had never worn them; they had no mark on them - I never fitted them on; I lost a pair, but should not like to swear to them - a piece of baize was produced; I suppose it to be mine, but should not like to swear to it.
 Cross-examined by MR. SMITH. Q. Are you sure your name is not Davis? A. Yes; I have passed by the name of Davis when I was in a gentleman's service, but it is Davison.
   WILLIAM WESTBY. I am a watchman. On the 25th of October, about eleven o'clock at night, I was on my beat in Princes-street, Mary-le bone, about one hundred yards from these stables, and saw three men going along Princes-street; one of them was some distance in front of the other two - one of them was Ayres: I suspected they had something, as Ayres and another had bundles on their backs - when they had got about one hundred yards I went after them; they were walking very fast - I came up to them; they had just put their bundles down: I asked Ayres what he had got there - he said, "It is nothing to you, I dare say;" I said, "If it don't belong to me, most likely it belongs to some person - I should like to look at it;" he said there it was, I might look at it if I liked: I stooped to examine it, when he and the other man ran away - I sprang my rattle, called Stop theif! and Ayres was stopped; I saw him in five minutes, and was certain of him - the bundle he had contained a box-coat, three body coats, four waistcoats, a pair of breeches, a pair of drawers, a white neck-cloth, a pair of boots and shoes, a hat, and a piece of green baize; he was taken to Mary-le-bone office, and the things were produced: I saw Davison there on the Monday - Ayres told me at the watch-house that he was employed by two men, named David Evans and Richard Kenney, to carry these things, and they were to give him 6d. for his trouble; Kenney was taken the same night, for assaulting a watchman - I could not identify him as the man who dropped the other bundle, for he hung down his head; what he dropped was a box-coat; it was taken to the office, and seen by Davison.
   JOSEPH STOWLEY. I am a baker.
