Ro-examined. She had told me that she was insured, she spoke about
it several times she had not told me what was insured, whether it was
the goods or the house, but she told me she was insured—I was the
manager, the business was not up to much-we turned over about 41
& week-I cannot say what the rent of the house was I have been
there abont six months, I think 41, a week was about the average that
we turned over all the time I was there-it had been going rather
barlly for a fortnight beforé the fire the profit of what we turned
over in the business was about a third or a little over a third-she bought
things in London and sold them again down in Surbiton--there was
about 407. or 501. worth of stock in the shop when the fire took place, and
the furniture in the house was worth about 1001. as near as possible-I
had my wages up to the week before-on that day I was the only person
with her on the premises-I had been to church in the evening to choir
practice, and was away two hours—I told the prisoner that I was going,
and she let me out-I returned home about 10 o'clock or a little after-I
did not see Blandy bring the fire lighters back, nor did I see where they
were put-I did not see anything of them from the time he brought them
until the fire-I did not go to the cupboard, there was nothing kept there
of mine-there was a box and a safe there standing on the floor, and by
getting on those you could, I think, reach the hole in the ceiling-on
the Monday before the fire the prisoner ordered me to cut the broken
ladder up, and when I had cut it up I carried it into the sitting-room
at the back of the shop, which was used as a sitting-room; we had all
our meals there it was put down in the fender-there was no fire
there at the time it was about two or three minutes altogether from
the time the prisoner called me and said that there was a fire until I was
out of the house it was not more-I had never seen the box which
contained plate before I did not know of its existence-the prisoner
had a plate-basket to keep the silver spoons and forks in which were
used.
By the Court. I have been with her six months-before that I was
at Wain's, in Newington Butts, an upholsterer's—I was an upholsterer
there I was there two years and a half-I shall be 20 years old next
birthday-before that I was at Morrison and Austin's, in Worship Street,
over two years, and then I went to the prisoner-she, came to Wain's
and wanted a manager, and I was recommended, and she had me.
