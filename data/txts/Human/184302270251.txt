   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Walker had been in your service about four months? A.  Yes, this last time—he took out eggs and other things to sell to anybody—not to any one particular customer—if he had sold these to any person of the name of Grayson they would have been pro perly sold—I do not know that he has been in business for himself—he has never charged himself with goods to my knowledge—when he came home on the 24th of Jan. he produced his book to me—the name of Grayson was not in it then—it was on the 27th that he entered the goods sold on the 24th—here is no entry in this book on the 24th—I have been in business where I now am for four or five months—before that I was in business in Broadway, Westminster—about twelve months ago I passed through the Insolvent Court—I was then out of business for some time.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Were you at all aware, or did Walker give you reason to believe, that he was carrying on any other business but yours? A.  No, I never sanctioned his carrying on any business in such articles as I deal in—when he returned he was to account to me for the eggs he took out, by giving me the money or the names of responsible persons—I gave him a discretion to sell to strangers, and give them credit, if he thought proper—Mr. Grayson lives in John-street—the basket that was found at Gilberts had recently been in my possession—it had only been imported into this country about six weeks. HENRY LUPTON( police-sergeant L  7.) At the latter end of Jan. and the beginning of Feb. I watched a street near the premises in which Gilbert lives. On the 1st of Feb. I noticed a cart there, and the two prisoners were with it—I have noticed articles taken out of that cart in White Horse-street, where the cart stood, and they were carried by Gilbert to No. 11, Windmill-street—Walker gave her the articles out of the cart—on Saturday, the 4th of Feb., I went with the prosecutor to No. 18, Windmill-street—there was a hamper found there containing 300 eggs, all but three. SAMUEL HARRIS( police-constable L  177.) Windmill-street, New-cut, is in my beat. I have seen Walker draw up with a cart in White Horse-street, and have seen Gilbert there at the time—this was done frequently during different weeks in the month of Jan.—I have seen Gilbert talking with Walker at the cart, and I have seen parcels taken out of the cart by Gilbert—the cart was then twenty or thirty yards from No. 18, Windmill-street—it is a mile and a half from the prosecutor's—I afterwards received Gilbert in charge—she was told by another officer what it was for—she said she certainly had fetched things from Walker's cart, and was very sorry for it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Can you tell me when in January you had seen any thing? A.  I cannot give any date—I did not tell Gilbert that these goods had been stolen from Mr. Miles—she said to a woman who was in her company that she was sorry for Walker; she certainly had fetched things from the cart. GEORGE GRAYSON. I live in St. John-street, Clerkenwell, and am a cheesemonger. I deal with Mr. Miles—I had no dealings with him in January last—the last I had was a week before Christmas I think—I did not buy 300 eggs of Walker or of Mr. Miles on the 24th of Jan.—I think the last things I had of Mr. Miles were eggs—I know Walker by his calling on me—I have dealt with Mr. Miles through him—I suppose he was aware I was a customer of Mr. Miles.
