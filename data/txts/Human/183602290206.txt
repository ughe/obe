MARTHA ENDLICH. I am the wife of John Endlich—we live in Ca therine's-street, St. George's. The prisoner came to lodge with me on the 6th of February, I think, and on the 25th my husband called me up to the room she lived in—these articles were all gone—I asked her where they were—the furniture of the bed was the first thing I missed—she said they were all safe—my husband then called a policeman, and she gave the duplicates to him. WILLIAM WILSON. I am shopman to Mr. Watts, of the Commercial-road, a pawn-broker. I have a sheet, a blanket, a pillow-case, and several other things, pawned by the prisoner at various times. MARMADUKE JACKSON HOWLETT. I am shopman to Mr. Talford, of Ratcliffe-highway. I have some bed-furniture, pledged by the prisoner, and some other articles, which I do not know who pawned—these are the duplicates.
   Prisoner . I had no work, and was in great distress. GUILTY. Aged 46.— Confined One Month.
 836. JOHN ANDERSON was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of February , 2 wooden poles, value 1 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of George Desgranges . GEORGE DESGRANGES, I live in Johnson-street, St. George's. I am a wood-cutter—I had some scaffold-poles at my yard, on the 15th of Fe bruary—I missed two of them the next day—I have never seen them since—I was very ill, and forced to go to my daughter's WILLIAM PENNINGTON I am a dustman, and live at Little Union-street, St. George's. My yard is at the back of Mr. Desgranges—on Tuesday night, between seven and eight o'clock, I was coming out of my yard—I saw the prisoner on the top of the paling of Mr. Desgranges' yard, handing one pole after another to a man who was taking them—he handed two poles over—I am sure the prisoner is the man that was handing them over—I asked what he was doing, taking those things away—he gave me no answer—I then went for my master, and then the man ran away—the prisoner was over then—my master sent me for the policeman, while he and another man were minding the place.
   Prisoner's Defence . I lodge next door to the yard—I was going home, and being dirty, I got over the yard fence, got to the back yard, and cleaned myself—I heard a disturbance at the door—the officer came with a lantern where I was—he said, "Is there any one here? "—I said, "Yes, I am here"—I went into the street with him, and Pennington said I had handed these poles over the gate, which I had not. WILLIAM PENNINGTON re-examined . I am sure he is the man—I was not gone away five minutes, and I left my master at the gate when I went—I have not had any quarrel with the prisoner—it was between seven and eight o'clock—I saw his face, as there is a lamp a few doors off. THOMAS WILLIAM SHIPLEY( police-constable K  174.) On Tuesday last, Pennington came to me—I went to Mr. Desgranges' premises, and searched round—I heard the young man say, "Here is the man"—I came and asked the prisoner how he came there—he said, he was taken short, and got over that way to the back premises—I understand he does live next door. GUILTY. Aged 52.— Confined One Month.
