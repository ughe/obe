CHARLES MEURANT. I am a commercial clerk, now living at 5, Port-
land Street--in a Lille newspaper, where I was then living, I read an
advertisement, in consequence of which I wrote to Mr. Jules Barnard
and received this letter of 26th July. (This gave a list of places which
seemed suitable; asked him to let them know sohich would suit and to enolon
10 francs for inquiry fees.) I wrote enclosing 10 francs, and in answer
received this letter of 3rd August from J. Barnard. (Stating that his
statement had been sent on to Charles Linden, importer, 61, Hatton Garden,
requesting him to write relative to securing a couriership, and hoping
that when the contract was signed he would forward five per cent. commission
on first year's salary.) I then wrote to Mr. Charles Linden, 61, Hatton
Garden, and got this letter in answer. (Stating that with reference to
the place as courier, for which they had charged Mr. Barnard to find a steady
person, their terms were 700 francs a year, payable monthly, without reckoning
travelling expenses, which would be 88. a day while in Paris and second-clas
travelling fare; that he would have to deposit 3,000 franos as a guarantee;
that he could enter the service in September, and that they were willing to accept
him if he accepted the conditions; while, if they considered an interview
necessary, they would inform him.). After that I came to London and went
to 21, Noel Street on 13th August, where I saw Barnard and Launet-
the latter came forward when I asked for Barnard--I asked for the
house of Linden and produced the correspondence, as they did not know
me-I said to Launet "I find it rather strange that a commercial clerk
is required to deposit & sum of money like that" he said the reason was
because I should have to collect money to a large amount in Paris-I
accepted the explanation-Launet said "Come at 5 o'clock”-I came
and found the police in possession.
