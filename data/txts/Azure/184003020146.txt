RJDDINGTON. I am a bootmaker, and live at No. 15, Fins.
bury-place North, very nearly opposite Mr. Caspar. I made the prisoner

ALLEN. In March last I was in the employ of Mr. Riddington.
J remember taking a pair of boots home to the prisoner on Saturday, the
23rd of March—I went to him on Monday morning—it was within a fev
minutes of nine o'clock—I saw bis two sisters, and afterwards saw the
prisoner come down stairs—he had on a pair of dark trowsers, and was ig
his shirt sleeves, and a pair of morning slippers—I had a little conver-
sation with him about the boots, for to the best of my recollection tbe
boots did not fit him—to the best of my recollection I took them away,
and returned to my master's shop—I suppose I remained in the houe
about five or six minutes—I was shown into the parlour at the back
the shop.
COURT. Q. What makes you recollect the 25th of March in particular?
A. It was the morning the robbery was committed—I cannot exactly say
how soon after I heard of it—I do not know that I heard Of it the same
day, but very soon after.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Were you here as a witness at
the trial of Ellis Caspar? A. No, I was called on by the Caspar family
—l did not refuse to come, butl was not subpcenaed, so I did not come—
I know of no other reason why I did not come.
MR. CLARKSON to MR. RIDDINGTON. Q. Were you here on the last
trial ? A. No, I was not subpænaed—l was not asked to come—I had
not given my evidence to any body—I had not seen Mr. Yates, the attor'
ney—l would have come if I had been called on to come.
MR. JONES to ALLEN. Q. Would you have come if you had been askeA

Q. Do you know of your master having made an entry in his book of
