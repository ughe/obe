995. DAVID REEDY (18), Feloniously cutting and wounding Janies
Purcell, with intent to do him some grievous bodily harm.
MR. DOYLE conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAS FOGARTY. I am a labourer. I was at work at Lambeth on
10th June, with Purcell ; after we left work we went to a beer shop, and
the prisoner came in—we played at skittles, and had some beer; then five
of us went out together; the prisoner was one-some quarrel took place
between him and Purcell, and Purcell shoved him down; the prisoner said
he should suffer for that before night-he came up, and Purcell did the
same again-we were talking, and the prisoner beld up his hand—I did not
see any knife-I came up, and said, * What is the matter, Purcell 9"-he
said, "I am done for !" and he put his two hands to the knife in his face,
and pulled it out-he was falling down - I had not seen them both on the
ground-Purcell did not fall, because I caught him I was walking behind
them-I saw the prisoner draw his hand, but I did not see there was any
knife - I was not so close that I could see the hand go to his face.
MICHAEL SHEA. I was with the last witness-I saw the prisoner and
Purcell quarrelling-Purcell knocked him down—the prisoner got up, and
Puroell pressed him against some railings, and tried to press his weight
against him, and he said to Parcell, “I am done; I want no more to do
with you"-Purcell knocked him down, and he got up, and pulled his cap
off, and chucked it down, and Parcell said, "When my two sons grow up
they shall fight you for me" - Purcell caught him again, and knocked his
head against the fence-I saw the prisoner had a knife, but I did not see
him stick him with it.
Court. Q. Was not this what is called rough play! A. I do not know
-- he caught him by the collar of his waistcoat, and swung him round, and
he caught him & second time and whirled him round, and chucked him down
in the road on his back--they were drunk at the time.
