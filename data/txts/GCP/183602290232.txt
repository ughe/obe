in consequence of what he said, I took the prisoner into custody at the
Crown public-house, in Dulwich I had another policeman with me
told him he must go along with me to the station-house-he told me he

• Ann Dowse. I am the wife of Thomas Dowse; the prisoner work
ed with my husband on the road. I remember Wednesday, the 24th
- about five o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came and asked
me if my husband was at home-I said no, he was gone to London-
he said he wanted to borrow his gun-I lent him the gun-this is the same
gun I lent-he went away-that was about five o'clock-I saw him
again between nine and ten o'clock that evening I heard the to
port of a gun just as I opened the door, and he said he had brought the
gun-there was a knock at the door-the report was before the knock-
I asked him if there was any danger-he said no, he had just let it of
-- I gave the gun to the policeman on Friday morning.
Cross-examined. Q. When was your husband discharged from work-
ing on the roads? A. On Wednesday morning he knew it-he came
back about ten o'clock.

ELIZABETH BEAMSLEY. I am a shop-keeper, and live at Dulwich; !
knew the prisoner perfectly well for a great many years. On Wednesday
evening, the 24th of last month, he came to me for an ounce of gun-
powder and a quarter of a pound of shot-1 served him-he paid me
24d.--the shot was No. 6-1 counted it out into the scale, put it back
into the bag, and gave it him with the bag.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you known him long? A. Yes--my house
is a quarter of a mile from his.
