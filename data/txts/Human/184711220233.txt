I have known the prisoner six months—he is of a very good temper when he is sober—he sometimes gets drunk—on Sunday night the Simmond's called out "Police!" and "Knife!"—he is in the habit of doing so—I do not think he is right in his head—on the Monday the prisoner came home—he could not get in to have his supper and go to bed—when Simmonds and his wife had a few words, the prisoner said, "You have been taking your pleasure all day, I will sit down and take mine, I will sit down till eleven o'clock and read the newspaper"—the mistress caught him, and gave him a shove  —there were blows—the landlord got the handle of the broom and began cutting away at this man—he sung  out "Police!"—I said, "I will go seek for one"—I went and could not see one—I staid out all night.  252. RICHARD DAY , for obtaining, by false pretences, 50 earthenware pipes, value 5 l  . 12 s  . 6 d  . the goods of Henry Doulton and another.
   MR. O'BRIEN conducted the Prosecution. HENRY DOULTON. I am partner with my father, we are potters, at High-street, Lambeth. In consequence of a communication, in August, I went to Victoria-road—the prisoner and Marson, who was convicted last Sessions, came there soon after—Marson said he required 600 pipes, the value of which was about 70 l . for a sewer he was about to build—(there was a sewer being built)—he introduced me to the prisoner, as the ground-landlord of the pro perty through which the sewer was to pass—he had said in the counting-house that the ground-landlord would be there; and when I got there, he said, "This is Mr. Day"—Marson walked a little way, and took me to another part, and then we returned to Day—we went into the Builders' Arms, and there Day gave me a written guarantee—it was handed to Jury on the trial of Marson, and I never saw it again.
   MR. COOPER. Q.  Was it produced by yourself, or the attorney? A.  by me—I have not made inquiries here for it—I am quite certain as to the words of it.
   MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  He said the prisoner was the ground-landlord? A.  Yes—the prisoner represented himself as the ground-landlord—Marson said, "Mr. Day, some gentlemen have been here to inquire the rent of your land, and I named a sum"—Day said that was quite right—Day was repeatedly referred to as the owner of the land—Day told me Marson was a very respectable man, he had known him a long while—he asked Marson how many pipes he would want—he said about 600—Day said, "Then, Mr. Doulton, you will send some over in the morning"—I sent one load next morning, it was the 24th or 25th Aug., by Stimpson, to the amount of 5 l . 12 s . 6 d .—in conse quence of what Stimpson said, I did not send any more—I wrote to Day—he had given me his address, "Richard Day, Esq., Eltham"—he replied by a letter, which I have here, in answer to mine—( read  —"Eltham, 6th Sept. Gentlemen—I did not know the pipes were delivered; but I shall see Mr. Marson on the subject, when he will write you, and make a settlement when the contract is completed")—I have not received any money for the pipes I sent—I am not prepared to state that I should not have parted with the pipes, through Day had not guaranteed it.
