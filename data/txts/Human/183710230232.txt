Redshaw. Q.  Did you not meet me on Thursday, about half-past nine o'clock, along with Denton? A.  I met him in Blackfriars-road, with a man they call Denton, on Thursday evening—that was acci dentally, not by appointment—Denton was with the prisoner at that time. Redshaw.  Denton was with you—I met you walking together, did I not? A.  Denton was walking with Redshaw, not with me—I did not ask them both to go and have something to drink—I had no conversa tion about 3 l . 10 s ., nor any sum of money owing from me to Denton—I did not tell Redshaw to come down on Saturday, about half-past three o'clock.
   Redshaw.  It is no use asking him any thing further—he denies every thing. DIANA HAYES. I am a widow, and live at No. 43, Little Surrey-street. The prisoners lodged with me between three and four months—on Satur day, the 14th of October, Redshaw left my house about three o'clock, or a little after, to the best of my recollection—I do not recollect whether Page went out with him—I do not think he did—I did not see Redshaw again till he was in custody—I do not know what time Page left—I do not re collect seeing him since between two and three o'clock—I think I saw him between two and three o'clock—we generally dined together between twelve and one o'clock—I think he dined with me that day—I do not know the exact time he left my house, but I believe it was between two and three o'clock—I will not be certain whether the prisoners left together—I do not recollect seeing them both go out together—I saw Redshaw go out of the parlour—Page was not in the parlour at that time—I cannot say how long he had left it—it might be half an hour—they both lived toge ther, and were on friendly terms—they did not go out much after they came to my house—they sometimes went out together—neither of the pri soners have directed me to make any application to the prosecutor since they have been in custody—I have seen the prosecutor, and spoken to him—I did not see him after I had seen the prisoners—I have not seen them all the week since last Monday, at the Hall, and I had not spoken to the prosecutor then—whatever I said to the prosecutor was out of my own head.
   Redshaw. Q.  Do you know Denton? A.  Yes—I saw him last on the Saturday you were taken.
   Redshaw.  No, it was the Saturday week. Witness.  It was on Satur day morning—I cannot say whether it was the day you were taken—there was some dealing between you, but I was in and out of the shop, and do not know particularly—I saw a rough coat, which you said you had sold him, and a dark pair of trowsers—Denton took them away, I believe—I had seen them before—they were yours.
