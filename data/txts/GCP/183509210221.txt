Q. What is the expense of running an omnibus from Paddington if you
ont no customers ? A. About 146. a-week; it depends on the number
of journeys it runs, and whether a man is fortunate in his cattle; that is
for one bus, five journeys a-day- I do not know how many proprietors
there are-I never reckoned them up-I know Thomas Evans and George
Bingham
2. As far as I understand, your being at Regent-street was a mere ac-
cident? 1. I was there for the purpose of watching-I think it was after
dinner-I thought I would take a walk down the road, to see in what
manner Bray worked his bus against my father.
Q. You say that you believe the number of your father's omnibus was
177: just recollect, was not the No. 177 upon the “ Albion" that was put
against Bray on the 15th ? 4. I will not swear to that being the number
on that day-there is more than one bus of my father's on the road-when
a new license is taken, there is not always a new number-Thomas Corbett
is a boot-clicker-he is foreman to my father in his shop-I remember the
time when it was thought that Bray was off the road, when his horses went
for sale I do not know that the " Albion" had the horses arrayed in blue
ribbons--I supped that night with the people at the White Lion--my fa-
ther was present--I did not see Bolton there, nor Cowderoy-he might have
been there without my seeing him—there were a great number of persons
the room was full--they were the coachmen and the conductors of any om-
nibuses, I believe--I do not know who paid-I never saw my father pay any
thing I do not believe he did pay any thing I do not think he could
pay without my knowing it-I sat close to him- I did not pay any thing.
Q. Tell me soniebody that was there beside your father was Gough
there! 4. I think he was-1 did not see Sullivan there-I do not
know Smith---I did not see either of the Bardells, nor Carpenter, nor
either of the Birds-I do not know that Johnson was there, nor Hill, nor
Pearoe-he might be there--Thomas Evans was there, to the best of my
knowledge--the room was full of a number of people.
Q. Am I to understand that you do not recollect any person but
Gough, your father, and Evans ? A. I will not swear to any other person
being there— I was there, I should think, an hour and a half-I had a glass
of gin and water after supper—there was nobody in the chair that I know
of, or at the head of the room; it was all confusion, sometimes one sat at
my right hand, and sometimes another--my father sat next to me on my
left hand, part of the time I cannot tell who sat at my other hand any
part of the time I do not know who paid I understood that Evans and
Cowderoy's omnibuses had had a quarrel, and had been racing together
for a few days, and had settled their difference, and they had a supper
over it-I did not see blue ribbons on the horses in the "Albion.
Q. Did you not individually give a sum of money towards that supper
in the morning? A. No- I did not see my father give any thinghe
goes down in the City in the morning, and from time to time he goes with
the bus-I cannot tell whether Cowderoy does--I have seen him on buses,
ariving--my father's first time in the morning, is about twenty minutes after
nine o'clock, and he has three times-his next journey is about twelve
Oclock or a few minutes after, and his next time about a quarter before three
o'clock-the "Albion," which runs against Bray, came to my father's yard
every night.
