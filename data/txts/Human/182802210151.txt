   JOSEPH EARLY. I am the son of Richard Early, who tenanted the house of Mrs. Campbell; I and a friend let it to the prisoner, by my father's consent; a paper was drawn up; the prisoner took possession of it; I was all over the house the day before; I gave him possession; I am a plumber, and repaired the flap at the top; I was all over the roof; it was in a good state; the lead would have lasted for many years - the cistern was in a good state; it was a heavy substantial one - the lead pipe was in a good state; part of it was quite new - there was no reason to remove any part of the lead. The prisoner took possesion on the 5th of September, when he signed the agreement - I have not been there since.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where is your father? A. At home; he is a carpenter and builder; he is ill; I do not know what is the matter with him; he was out yesterday; he knows nothing about Gray; he is under Dr. M'Donald's hands; I am not a carpenter - I swear all the lead was in perfect order, when the prisoner took possession; it had been repaired about two months before by myself and brother, who is twenty-six years old; he is in the same line of business; he is not here. The prisoner was to take the house as a tenant at 45l. a year, then there was a written agreement made, and I believe he was to take the rest of my father's lease - I never examined the house after he took it; I have been to the door; he took it for four years and a quarter - my father lives with me in County-terrace, New Kent-road.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Your father was not present when the house was let? No; nor my brother.
   GEORGE WALKINGTON. I am a labourer; I live in Essex-street, Whitechapel. The prisoner applied to me two or three weeks before Michaelmas to take the lead off the top of this house, in Devonshire-street; he came up while I was at work, and told me to cut the lead off the flat and cut it to pieces; it was the lead which goes over the roof to protect it from the wet; there was no plumber there - the prisoner went away and left me at work; the lead was put into a cart, in Devonshire-street, by the prisoner's order, about twelve o'clock in the day - the cart drove away; I was afterwards employed by the prisoner to take away the lead of the sink, which was fixed; that lead was very bad indeed - it was cut up and put down in the yard; it could not be used again; I said nothing to him about it, but did what he bid me - I took a leaden cistern away from facing the kitchen window; I chopped it up and put it into the kitchen; I said nothing to him about it; the sink and cistern were carried out in the cart; I helped to load it; the prisoner paid me for my trouble - I was there again nearly three months after, but took nothing more away; there was a leaden gutter went under the floor; I took that up by his order, and it was taken to a plumber's place, and I came away, leaving the prisoner there; nothing was said about selling it in my hearing.
 Q. Was anything done to repair the roof from which the lead was taken? A. We pitched some part and, were going to pitch the rest, but the rain came on, and it came through the ceilings; a tarpauling was put on one night, and some boards were put on - they partly kept the rain out; Mr. Gray slept in the house; only the first floor bed-room was furnished, besides the kitchen - there was no servant there; I was there nearly every day - I do not know why the tarpauling was moved - it was only there one night; I left the premises before Christmas - they were in the same state as I put them in in September.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe you white-washed and painted about the house? A. Yes; some part of the lead was very bad; I cannot say whether it was fit to remain there; the cistern was in very good order - the other things were bad: Gray remained in the house all the time - he did what he could to keep the wet out: he did not direct me to conceal anything; it was all done in open day.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Was any repair done while you were there? A. A trunk was afterwards put from the top of the house, to carry the water away; the flat would not last many years. I am not a plumber; I was bred to no trade - I am no judge of the state of the lead: there was no pipe put to carry the water to the trunk. The house is two stories high - I painted and washed the kitchen, nothing else; I only painted the dresser - the trunk was put up about a month after I moved the lead - it was a wooden pipe, down from the bottom of the house to the top.
   ABRAHAM GREEN. I am a shoemaker. I came to London on the 10th of September, and met the prisoner at the Poulterers' Arms, Leadenhall-market; I got into conversation with him; he said he knew my brother; I said I wanted a house, or part of one, as I was coming to town, and I and my brother were to live together; he said he had a small house, and should have part of it to let; I went there on the 15th of September - he took me into the kitchen and the first floor; I saw Walkington chopping up lead, and bending it into a small compass; the prisoner took me to the second floor, to show me the apartment, and then on the top of the house, and while I was there he ordered Walkington to rip the lead from the top; I left Walkington there, and came down with Gray; a genteel man was in the shop, whom Gray said was going to have the parlour on the first floor - we all went and had some thing to drink; a person then came for the prisoner, and we all went over to the house; there was a cart in the street; the man said, "I have come for the lead;" he asked me and the young man to help put it into the cart, which then went away; the prisoner then said he had some business to look after, and told us to go to the public-house, and he would come to us in half an hour; I did not go, but went away. On the 17th my brother came to town - we both went on the 18th, and took two rooms on the second floor; I slept there that night, and next night the rain came through so much, we were obliged to go up to the third floor, to put things to catch it; we told Gray of it next day, and he said it should be repaired.
