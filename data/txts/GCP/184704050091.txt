John BEMERSIDE HAIG. I was house-surgeon at St. Bartholomew's Hose
pital on the 23rd of March, between one and two o'clock in the morning, when
Longman was brought there-he was in a veryexhausted state from loss of blood
- there was a wound on the left side of his head and face, about four inches
long-the centre was deep, the ends were superficial—it cut through one of
the branches of the temporal artery-he was bleeding very profusely-there
was a wound on the forehead, just above the nose, about two inches in length,
extending to the bone, a superficial wound on the left eye-brow, and three
or four smaller wounds of a slight character about the head and face he was
in the greatest danger, from loss of blood from the wound which divided the
temporal artery, and from that aver the nose-such an instrument as this razor
Would inflict the wounds-I should conclude that the wound on the face was
about the length of the razor-blade- it is quite bealed, but you can see where
I was, very plainly-the razor has a very long shaft or bolder-it could be
held so as to inflict this wound without a man's cutting his own hand, or
without its giving way.
