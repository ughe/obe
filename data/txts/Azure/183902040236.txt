827. JOHN SAWYER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th
January, I pair of boots, -value 38. 6d., the goods of John Esterford, hit
master.
JOHN ESTERFORD. I am a shoemaker, and live at Royal Hill, Green.
wich. The prisoner was in my employ in the beginning of January—I
missed boots and shoes; and one day I missed a pair while he vas waiting
for his work, and found them in his basket, as he was going out of the shop
with them—I found another pair of my boots at the pawnbroker's.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Was any thing said about yout
overlooking it if he confessed ? A. Yes, I said I would overlook about
the shoes—the boots were taken first—I have given him work for a year
and a half—he calls at the shop for it, and goes away with it—I am quite
certain I did not give him these boots to make—I never gave him more
than two pairs to make at once—these boots were all ready for sale—they
did not want any thing done to them—I did not give them to him as a
pattern to make others by—I may have done such a thing, but I never
gave him these—I saw them hanging on the door-post before he came inø
the shop, and when he was gone I missed them—he promised to give me
the duplicates of these boots—he gave me one, but I could not find the
Other.
JOHN LEVICK. I am shopman to Mr. Nash, a pawnbroker, at Green-
wich. On the 15th of January the prisoner pawned these boots for IL—
he looked like a shoemaker, and they often pledge work.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 22.—Confined Two Months
(There Were two other indictments against him.)
828. ELIZABETH JONES, alias Taylor, was indicted for stealing,
on the 13th of January, 2 candlesticks, value 28. ; 1 towel, value 2d. ;
1 tablecloth, value 2d. ; 1 fork, value Id.; the goods of Joseph Wilson :
and I handkerchief, value Is. 6d., the goods of George Henry Wilson.
GEORGE HENRY WILSON. I am a labourer, and live at Woolwich with
my parents. On Saturday night, the 12th of January, the prisoner asked
me to give her a night's lodging—I took her home, and she slept with me
down stairs—my father and mother slept up stairs—when I awoke the
next morning the prisoner was gone, and I missed my handkerchief—I had
not given it to her.
DANIEL RIORDAN (police-constable R 207.) met the prisoner in Green-
Wich market-place, on Sunday morning, the 13th of January, about nine
o'clock—I observed a bundle under her arm, and asked if what she had
belonged to herself—she said yes, it had belonged to her some time—I
found she had two candlesticks, a tablecloth, a towel, and frock.
JANE WILSON. I am the wife of Joseph Wilson, a pensioner. These
articles are his, except the handkerchief, which belongs to my son—the
things were safe on Saturday night, at eleven o'clock.
