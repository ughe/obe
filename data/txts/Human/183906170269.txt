 1974. MARY HARRIS and MARY DAVIS were indicted for steal ing, on the   17th of May , 24 yards of printed cotton, value 18 s  ., the goods of John Goss Fleay. JANE SMITH. I an the wife of Saul Smith, a labourer, of Hanover-street, Lewisham. I was passing the shop of Mr. Fleay, in the Broadway, Deptford, on the afternoon of the 17th of May—the prisoners were both tending together, and Davis said to Harris, "You go in there"—Harris then went inside the lobby, and stood there with a basket on her arm—she looked first to the left and then to the right—in the mean time Davis took this print from the left-hand side of the lobby, inside, put it into her apron, and went away—Harris stopped a minute or two afterwards, and then walked away in the same direction—I did not see that she joined her—she could see what she was about, as she stood in the midway, and Davis was just inside—Harris did not offer to buy any thing, or do any thing—she walked on with the basket of eggs—I gave information, and pointed out Harris.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How many persons were about the shop? A.  There might be four or five—I did not see any gentleman—Harris had a basket-full of eggs—I do not know whether she was sell ing them—she did not attempt to touch any thing—the whole thing took place in a minute—Harris went twenty or thirty yards—I saw the police man take her. GEORGE SOLE( police-constable R  174) I was on duty in the Broad way, Deptford, on the 17th of May—I received information—I ran up the Kent-road, and overtook Davis about a mile from the shop—I found this print in her apron. JOHN TUBB. I am assistant to Mr. John Goss Fleay—I followed Harris, and gave her into custody—she said she knew nothing of it—she did not say whether she had been in the shop—I took her back—I then went in pursuit of Davis, and found her with this print.
