 561.  STEPHEN WEST was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 31st of August , two bushels of malt, value 10s. the goods of Samuel Whitbread, Esq. and
   JOSEPH PARKER for feloniously receiving the same goods the same day, knowing them to have been stolen.
 The case opened by Mr. Knapp.
 - LYON sworn.
 I am a lighterman. On the 21st of August I see the prisoner West, he is  porter to Mr. Day, and Mr. Whitbread has part of his warehouse, to put malt in, as a store house; I observed the prisoner West take from the warehouse of Mr. Day, a sack marked R. S. I. in a heart, and which was marked Henley, it appeared to contain about two bushels of malt, or rather more, when he came out I observed him go towards the house of Parker, it is about a hundred yards from this place.
 Q. What is Parker? - He collects sacks for the West country barges, he keeps an iron shop, and buys things of that description, iron and rags.
 Q. How did West take this sack? - He took it on his back.
 Q. Did it appear to be weighty, or how? Not very weighty, I see him take it, and go in great haste to Parker's house.
   Q. Did you see him go into the house? - I did.
 Q. What next happened? - I had occasion to go to Parker's house, for thirty quarters of sack of this very mark that I saw on the prisoner's back. Parker goes to different brewers about the town, collect the sacks for the west country barges; I saw that come out of Parker's house.
 Q. Did he bring any thing out with him? - He did not.
