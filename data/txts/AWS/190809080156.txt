George Freneh and Co., carmen and fodder importers| 22 bales South
African fodder, E5 17s. 6d. Paid G. French 13, 7, 1908." I am not
sure if Freneh was present when I made the bargain, but I know I
paid him in cash. I afterwards bought two tons from Phillips and
Campbell through Wood. I had agreed to buy the one ton at 2410s.
I paid that amount at my office, and afterwards had the receipt made
out at E5 17s. 6d.
Cross-examined. When I started feeding the horses on this fodder
it had no effect on them at all, only made them weaker. I do not
consider it proper fodder for working horses. It is not worth nearly
5 17s. 6d. a ton. French never had any conversation with me about
the fodder. The receipt was made out for E5 17s. 6d. because I told
Mr. Everitt, a corn merchant, that I bought it at E4 10s. I believe
he went to Campbell's to try and buy some, thinking he could buy it
cheaper. The account was made out at E5 17s. 6d., so that if my
merchant came again I could show him the bill and tell him that Was
the price of it. Wood told me to do that.
Re-examined. After I had tried the fodder I bought some from
Campbell, Phillips, and Co., at E5 10s. I had only then used the
two sample bales.
