MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did the mother go to the hospital? 4. Yes; and

SARAH ANN DAY. I am the female prisoner's sister, and live with my
mother in Cambridgeshire. The child was with her some time it was very
ill, it scarcely enjoyed a week's health during the eighteen months it was there
-it never was strong--the mother's conduct to it was very kind indeed, and
the father's also- I have visited them in town since the child came up, it
still continued unhealthy.
Cross-examined by Mr. Cleri, Q. Were you living with your grand-
mother? A. Most of the time; I was in service six or seven months-1
believe I saw the child the day before it left its grandmother ; it was vety
unhealthy then-my mother had a doctor's bill of 168. to pay when it left-
I was not at Mrs. Salter's while they lived there - I have not been in Somers-
street above once.
MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you see the child while you were in service!
A. Yes; it came to me several times, and I always found it unhealthy.
LYDIA Ashton. I am the sister of the female prisoner, and live at 2, Red
Lion-passage, Holborn, a quarter of a mile from Somers-street, I frequently saw
the child-my sister was always very kind to it, and her husband also---it was
always very delicate and weak-the mother's conduct at the hospital was
very kind and attentive, I have given them money several times to support
them, and produce duplicates of their pawned property.
Cross-examined. Q. How often have you been to the house? 4. Two
or three times a week, before and since Christmas--I saw no bruises on the
child at the hospital.
