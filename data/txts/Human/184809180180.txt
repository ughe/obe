On 31st May, about half-past eleven o'clock at night, I saw Mrs. Morris in the front room in Webber-street, lying on the ground—her arm was broken at the elbows and fore-arm—there were wounds on her legs near the knees—I thought one leg was broken, but it was not—I put the arm right for the time, and had her taken to the hospital—I have seen her there several times—she has left two or three weeks, but is still an out-patient.
   Prisoner's Defence.  2212. JOHN BACON and ELLEN BENBOW , stealing 30 yards of cloth, value 3 l  .; the goods of Robert Simmonds ; and MARY DOUCH and ELIZABETH OWEN , feloniously receiving part of the same. ROBERT SIMMONDS. I am a woollen manufacturer, of Friday-street—Bacon was in my service—he left on 16th Sept.—I did not see him again till he was in charge—this woollen cloth is mine, and was in my warehouse—I did not miss it till he was in charge—it is not made for any other house.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  How long did he live with you? A.  One month the last time, but he had been with me twice before—the first time was in the autumn of 1847—I do not manufacture my own goods, they are made for me at Huddersfield—I bought these of Tolson and Dalton, of Huddersfield—there is no private mark on them—I know them by the quality and pattern—I saw it again at the station before I knew it was lost—I cannot say how much I had sold, without looking at my books—I had sold some seven people—when I identified it, I could not tell but what I might have sold it. ELIZABETH METCALF. I am the wife of George Metcalf, of Reynold's-row, Southwark.
