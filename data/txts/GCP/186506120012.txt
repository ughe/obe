SIMBON STONE. I am a miner, and live at Mr. Hiscock's-on 6th May.
I saw Mr. Hiscock, Mr. Rawlinson, and the prisoners in the lane-Mr.

wrapped in paper-I pointed out the place to Rolfe-I gave the shilling to
the inspector.

Anne HASLETINE. I am the wife of Samuel Hasletine, of the Mason's
Arms, Buckingham-place-on 26th April, the prisoners came in-Peck
asked for a glass of ale, which came to 11d.-he gave me a half-sovereign-
I gave him 98. 6d in silver, and was going to give him the coppers, when
Burgess said, “Why need you change a half-sovereign when I have got
coppers "-I thought there was something wrong, looked at the change on
the counter, and found a bad half-crown amongst it-I said, “You have
taken the good half-crown out, and put a bad one in "-Peck said, “It is
all good and right," and took the change away-Young was in my house at
the time.
Cross-examined. 8. Did you see a third man with the prisoners ! A.
No; I got the change from my bar-parlour-I had several pounds' worth
there-they had plenty of opportunities of changing the half-crown while
my head was turned to take the change for the glass of ale-I saw Peck in
custody at the station about half an hour afterwards.
MR COLERIDGE. Q. Are you perfectly sure that you gave two good balf-
crowns in change? A. Perfectly ; I had taken the silver in my hand, and
examined it-the prisoners' half-crown was more black than mine.
