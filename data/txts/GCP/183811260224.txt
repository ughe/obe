240. SAMUEL POLLARD was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd d
November, 1 bag, value 1d.; 1 half-crown, 6 shillings, and 1 sixpence;
the goods and monies of John Tick.
John Tick. I am a fishmonger, living at Greenwich. On the 3rd of
November I saw the prisoner near my stall, in Beer-lane, Greenwich-1
sent him on errands-he behaved with the greatest propriety from Friday
till Saturday-on Sunday, when I came home to dinner my wife asked
me if I had taken a bag out of the drawer containing 93.-I said no-
I then sent for the prisoner—he came, and I accused him of it—he said be
did not touch it-he said, “Why should I rob you, when you behaved >
well to me? "- then put it into the policeman's hands, and he asked him
why he should rob this poor man, after he behaved so well to him-he
asked him several questions, and then the prisoner got up, went to the
balustrade of the square, and pulled out a purse containing 58., which was
part of my money.
WILLIAM SUTTLE. I was present when the prisoner went for this
money-he gave it into my hand.
GUILTY. Aged 16.-Confined Four Days.
(The prosecutor promised to take him into his employ again.)
241. JAMES RUSSELL was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of
November, 1 pair of trowsers, value 58., the goods of William Oliver.
WILLIAM OLIVER. I am servant to James Cornish, of Plumstead. On
Saturday night I purchased a pair of trowsers in the Kent-road-I put
them into a sack, on the wagon which the prisoner was driving for mehe
went on to Blackheath, and said, “Bill, your trowsers are lost, some one
has taken them"-I said, “You must know something about it"-he
denied it--I said there had been nobody in the wagon but our two selves
-the policeman came up, and the prisoner began to cry--the officer pulled
the trowsers out from under his waistcoat—the prisoner said, “You wont
hurt me."
URIAH EDEN (police-constable R 81.) I pulled the trowsers from
under the prisoner's waistcoat, and took him in charge.
GUILTY. Aged 23.- Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor.
Confined One Month.
