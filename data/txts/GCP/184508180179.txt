THOMAS Stevens. I am the last witness's husband. On Thursday,
the 31st of July, my wife gave me a half-crown- I gave it to the police-
man, who took the prisoner into custody.
JOHN MAY ( policeman.) On the 31st of July I was in Stevens's booth
about ten o'clock, and saw the prisoner there-- I saw him put a half-crown
on the counter-Mrs. Stevens picked it up, and put it on the counter again
- her husband took it up, and gave it to me- it was never out of my
sight-1 produce it- I took the prisoner into custody, searched him, and
found 18d. in a tobacco-box, and 6d., but no bad money.
HENRY JACKSON ( policeman.) I produce two half-crowns; one I re-
ceived from Hall, and one from May.
MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint. These two
half-crowns are both counterfeit, and both cast in the same mould.

1768. JOHN BURKE was indicted for unlawfully uttering and having
in his possession counterfeit coin.
MARK SAUNDERSON. I am barman to Mr. Griffiths, of the Rising
Sun, York-road, Lambeth. On the 15th of July, about half-past one
o'clock, the prisoner came, asked for half a pint of beer, and offered a bad
sixpence in payment -I said, “This is a bad one; do you know it?"-he
said he did not-I asked if he had any more money-he said no-I asked
where he got it-he said from a man named Simmons, a plasterer-I asked
him where Simmons lived he said he did not know-I then told him I
should know where Mr. Simmons lived before I let him go I let him go
out, followed bim a quarter of a mile, and gave him in charge to Hughes
- I held the sixpence in my hand till then, then bit it, and gave it to him.
JAMES Hughes (policeman.) Saunderson gave the prisoner into my
"custody by the Cornwall-road-I searched him, and found five other bad
sixpences in the corner of the collar of his coat, wrapped up in this piece of
paper-he had a good 4d. piece, and a penny on bim-this other sixpence
I received from Saunderson after he marked it
MR. JOHN FIELD. These sixpences are all counterfeit, and cast in the
same mould.
Prisoner's Defence. I did not know they were bad; I picked them up;
if I had known they were bad I could have thrown them away; I work
hard for my living, and have a wife and family.
Guilty. Aged 44.-Confined Six Months.

1769. HENRY SHERLOCK the younger, was indicted for stealing
1 pair of trowsers, value 78. ; 1 cap, 18.; and 1 waistcoat, 2s. ; the goods
of Henry Sherlock; to which he pleaded
GUILTY.* Aged 17.--Transported for Seven Years.
1770. JOHN PARKER was indicted for stealing 14lbs. weight of lead,
value 28., the goods of James Easton and another, his masters; to which
he pleaded
Guilty. Aged 36.-Confined Six Months.
1771. JAMES DENNIS was indicted for stealing 1 flannel-jacket,
value 28., the goods of George Frost; and that he had been before con-
victed of felony; to which he pleaded
GUILTY. Aged 32.-Confined Six Months.
