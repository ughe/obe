GEORGE OSBORNE. I am a labourer, and attend at a skittle-ground On the evening of the 14th of July the prisoner was there—he offered as sell me a shawl like this—I bought it, and gave it to my wife to wear—I gave 7 s . 6 d . for this shawl, with some lawn, and two handkerchiefs—I took it to Mr. Brooks when I heard of this.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you known this boy very long? A.  I should think about twelve months—I knew a little boy of the name of Goodwin—at the time of buying this shawl he came over to me, and asked me to buy the things, and said he picked them up in Blackman street—I bought it all for 7 s . 6 d .—I did not know the value of them—I never said, at any time, that I gave 1 s . for the shawl—nor 1 s . 3 d .—I paid the money to the prisoner—I did not go and inquire the value of it—I thought it worth 7 s . 6 d .—I never bought a shawl before from Goodwin—I did not say so before the Magistrate—I did not buy the lawn at my own house, but at the skittle-ground. JAMES REYNOLDS( police-constable M  66.) I took the prisoner, I found 2 s . on him, and the keys of the stable.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you ask him what he knew about some silk stockings? A.  No—he asked me what I wanted with him—I said I had information that he had stolen some silk stockings. NOT GUILTY.
