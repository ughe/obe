Cross-examined. Q. You know as a fact that he was given in custody, and
brought off to prison at 9 o'clock at night A. Yes--no communication

last November—a communication was made to him in April, that there was
& defalcation of 58.— I don't think he was required to pay that I will swear
he was not asked for 58.-- he paid the sum of 58 last Tuesday week-he sent

left it at the bar of the public house, and when I came from taking him into
onstody, I found it at the bar-I have been secretary since last November
--he was secretary just previous to me--we are speaking of July, 1863—I
had a loan of 51. from the Society at the end of September—that is not con-
trary to Rule 14-it was not too soon for me to have it - I was not refused
that 51.- the prisoner did not oppose my having it, that I am aware of-I
was auditor at the time I andited his books--we could not find this defal.
antion out then-we did not find this 17. out till last Saturday week-we
met every Tuesday, and the members paid their money to the secretary-the
prisoner always took the books to his own house-I never had possession of
them until after this difficulty occurred--the prisoner kept the books, and
would not return them; we used to drink a little--not more than a pint of
beer each, and pipes—the officers were never drinking or smoking—there was
some squabbling going on about this time—the prisoner did not drink during
business hours; somebody might pass the officers a glass—the prisoner never
complained of the confusion or squabbling I know that one night during
that quarter his friends attempted to turu me out of the Society, and they
closed the books, and would not have any more business; they wanted it
passed by a resolution—the prisoner was turned out for his inattention to
his duties by a resolution at a general meeting-he might have received
from 351. to 401. a quarter while he was secretary—there were about 140
members at 3d. per quarter each—we have somewhere about ninety loans
outstanding--we have about 4007. capital—the Society has been in exist-
ence five years the secretary would have to enter every member's payment
opposite a number, and cast it up at the end of the quarter-I don't know
that by law you can put three charges of embezzlement in one indictment; 1
swear that I have no other indictment but this, we made no charge about
the others, because they were condoned by his friends-I gave the prisoner
into custody—the present trustees authorized me to do 80-I asked the
advice of the Chief Clerk at the Mansion House, and he said, You don't
want a summons; give the man into custody the moment you see him, for
embezzlement"-I heard something about his being defended by a subscrip-
tion amongst his neighbours- I have seen one of the members of the Society
here to give him a character, Mr. Samuel Pearce, one of the prisoner's
friends; he is a defaulter to the Society, like Mr. White; they have been the
pulling down of the Society--there are two or three of the defaulters coming
up; there is no doubt about that.
