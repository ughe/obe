GUSTAVUS GOLDBERG (by an interpreter). I am a merchant, at 8. Duka
street, Aldgate. On 18th Aug., between ten and eleven in the morning-
met the prisoner, in Bishopsgate-street-he led me to Bury-street-he said
"There are two policemen waiting for you, and there is a reward of 1002
offered for you, you may escape if you give me 101." - I said, "I have
nothing to give you, you have no claim on me"-he led me from there.
down a dark passage, and said, “You have nothing to do with me! I will
show you"-be laid hold of me and took my watch, and 21., which was
wrapped up in paper-he seized me by the collar and dragged me alone
towards Bury-street-I made a noise and cried, and the Rev. Mr. Asher
came and said we should both go to Mr. Levine's close by, to settle the
matter amicably-the prisoner said to Mr. Levine, "I have the watch, but I
won't let him escape"- Mr. Levine went to get an officer, and the prisoner
went away also.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'BRIES. Q. How long have you been in Eng-
land? A. Three weeks-I came from Germany to seek employment-
was dealing in embroidered goods, which were imported from the house where
I was employed-I was brought up as a merchant-I have known the
prisoner as long as I have been in England-- he lived in the same house with
me-he borrowed a pair of shoes of me when I met him that day I spoke
to him and asked him for my shoes- I have never been before a Magistrate-
I did not leave Germany because there was a charge of forgery or embezzle-
ment against me-I never committed a guilty act-I was never charged with
any-when Mr. Asher came up the prisoner had hold of me I could hardly
speak-I said, “This man wished to get 101. from me."
