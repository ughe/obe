JOHN PUNCHARD. On the 29th of August I was sent for to go to Davis's, in Brick-lane—I went and saw Clark, Bonnell, and Davis there—I went there to look at a lot of books—Bonnell said they had got a lot of goods there to sell—I cannot say whether Davis heard that—the goods were in a box at the front of his counter—they were day-books, ledgers, memorandum-books, ciphering-books and copy-books—they were taken over to the public-house, by a couple of men—I think Vincent was one—I bought them—Clark was along with them—I do not know whether he helped to carry them—they were taken to my warehouse on Bread-street-hill, by Vincent, and Mrs. Punchard paid for them—I have found this note of it, 7 l . 2 s . 6 d ., beside the expenses—it was given me by Clark, who walked down with me—( see document No.  4.) WILLIAM FITCH. I saw these goods at Punchard's—they were the goods I sold to Bonnell. WILLIAM ROSSER. I was at the New York Coffee-house, in Sweetings alley, and met Bonnell there promiscuously, calling for half a pint of beer—I had got a lot of goods going to Whitechapel—I think he went out before me—he asked me, as he knew I was in the stationery line, if I knew any body who. would buy some new account-books—I went and said there is no harm in looking at them—Bonnell sent a person I never saw before, of the name of Clark with me—I introduced Mr. Punchard to the gentleman Bonnell sent with the goods—we went to Davis's—Clark and Punchard went with me—I walked behind them—Bonnell was there nearly at the same time—I think the books laid by the side of the counter, in a box—I have said I saw them in Davis's cellar, but it was a mistake—I said Mr. Punchard went down stairs—I was not inside. ROBERT REEVES( police-sergeant G  19.) On Monday, the 18th of September, I was attending these sessions, and Mr. Battle, an ale-brewer, came to me, and said in Davis's hearing, "Do you know a man named Vincent?"—I said, "No"—he said, "I want to give him in charge for felony, for obtaining some ale, and afterwards selling it 1 l . a barrel less than he gave for it"—Davis then came up and said, "Do you know that party"—I said, "I cannot say I do know him"—he said, "If you should see him, take him into custody, he is one of the witnesses against me; he is one of the parties who has been riding about with your beautiful in spector, Mr. M'Craw"—I mentioned this before the Magistrate—Vincent was taken before the Magistrate—Battle did not come forward, and Vincent was discharged.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Did you go to Mr. Battle? A.  No—no one did—I knew where Battle lived—it was on his not ap pearing that Vincent was discharged—this was on the 18th of September last—I never saw Vincent before, to my knowledge.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Were you at the office the following day? A.  I cannot say—I do not remember Vincent coming again of his own accord the next day. GODFREY GODDARD. I am the attorney for the prosecution. I have supplied Vincent with money, to go about to find the defendants—he has had 5 s ., 7 s ., and 10 s .—I was present when he was taken before the Magis trate—he was discharged the same evening—Battle did not attend—M'Craw desired Vincent to attend the next day—he did, and Battle at tended, and Vincent was discharged.
