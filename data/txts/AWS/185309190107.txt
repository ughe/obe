MARY ANN PRESTON. I am single, and live at No. 5, York-buildings,
East-lane, Walworth. I remember being in the Walworth-road, near the
King's Head public house, on a Sunday night in August-I do not know the
date-I saw the prosecutor standing against a shop door-I passed him-I
came back to the corner and saw him again, and the female prisoner with him
-1 saw her face-shortly after, I went down York-street as far as the corner
of James-street-wher I got to the corner I heard a cry of 44 Murder
turned round the corner on bearing that, and I saw the prosecutor and the
female lying on the ground-1 saw the male prisoner; he had his knees on
the prosecutor's breast, and his hand on his mouth-the female struggled to
get away-I saw she had a watch, and she gave it to the male prisoner-he
tan away-I told a man who was there to call a policeman-) have not always
told the same story that I have now, beeanse I saw the state the woman was
in, and I took compassion on her felt for her as if she were my sister, but
she has offended me since-I told the Magistrate the first time that I did not
see the male prisoner-the female was in her confinement; I took compassion
on her, but she has offended me since.
