 154. ELLEN BROADBENT and MARIA MURRAY , stealing 16 yards of silk and other articles, value 8 l  . 9 s  .; the goods of Mary Ann McArthur , the mistress of Broadbent: to which
   BROADBENT  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 26.— She received a good
   character.— Judgment Respited.
   MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM GLADWIN( policeman, R  122). I went to Murray's house—Broadbent was lodging with her—I searched the house, and found all these articles in Murray's room ( produced )—Murray told me it was her room, and she was present when I searched it—I found part of this property in the drawers, the hearth-rug on the floor, and part of the property in a band-box, which Murray said was hers—another bandbox was produced from Mrs. Brown's—she came in and said, "I want one of you"—at the time I found the goods I saw no other band-box there—the officer Newell brought in a bandbox, and Murray said, "It is that bandbox that I took into the next house; I found it under the bed; I did not think there was any harm in taking it there, as the constable did not search it"—another constable had searched the premises before me—directly Newell came in with the box, Murray said, "That is the box; I did not know what was in it"—I said to Murray, "Have you got any property in your house that does not belong to you?"—she said, "No, I have not," and then I said, "I must search your house"—I showed her the things I found, and asked her if they were her things, and she said they were—she pulled them out of the drawers herself, and told me they were hers—I found this spoon in a cupboard in Murray's room—I asked her, and she said it was hers—that was after the corded box was brought in.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  Broadbent had been taken into custody before this? A.  Yes; there were other articles in the drawers—I believe Murray only occupies one room; her husband lives with her there—I heard that Broadbent had gone from her mistress' there to be confined—there were two bandboxes and things in drawers MARY ANN MCARTHUR. I am the wife of John McArthur. Broadbent was in my service—I missed a number of articles while she was with me—these things found in the box that Murray said was hers, are mine—this spoon is mine, it has the initials of my maiden name on it, "Mary Ann Edwards"—it was in this state when I lost it—the letters are nearly rubbed out.
