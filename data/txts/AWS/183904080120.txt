SARAH GEoRGE. My husband keeps the Sun public-house in Lone
acre. On Tuesday morning, the 26th of February, from seven nine
o'clock, Field came to my husband's house, for change for a 610 note--
refused him the first time-he came again, and asked me to give bin
change-] declined it again-and he came a third time-he said he was a
market-gardener, he wanted to pay some money away, and should be Very
much obliged if I could accommodate him-he said any kind of chang
would do, either silver or gold, as he had 9l. to pay away-I was induced
ultimately to give him change, 7. in silver and 3l. in gold- asked bis
name and address he wrote on the note, but I was not able to read
it; and I called to him as he was going out of the door-he said he would
be back in a few minutes, but be did not return-I gave the note to my
husband-be took it to Newgate-market, and paid it away to a salesna
there-this is the note-(looking at it)-it is No. 32,739.
Cross-examined. Q. Who told you it was Field that came to your hour
that day A. Nobody-the first time I saw him at the office I was
little doubtful- thought it was rather a taller person than him ; but that
doubt was cleared up the second time-I went home from the office, after
the first examination, in a coach, with the young person who was in my
bar and saw me give the prisoner the change-I did not speak to either of
the other parties on this subject particularly- spoke to the policeman who
fetched me there, but not on this subject-he paid the expense of the
coach, and I spoke about that-there was no allusion made to this sob-
ject-I had not seen Field before that morning to my knowledge.
Mr. DOANE. Q. At first you had some doubt? A. Yes; I heard him
speak after that, and I was sure he was the person-I have not the least
doubt now that he is the person.
LETITIA PAGE. I live servant with Mrs. George. I remember Field
coming to her for change for a E10 note, on the 26th of February.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you there on each of the three times
came A. I was there the second and third times-on the first mor-
ing after leaving Lambeth-street, my mistress asked me was I certain
that was the man-I said I was almost sure it was- I did not talk to
any policeman about it-I only saw the prisoner once at our house, but I
remember his image and his face-I was cleaning the taps at the bar that
morning-when I was going home from the office, I said I was almost sure
that was the man-the policeman told us his dress, and all that, and cross-
questioned us-my mistress spoke to me, and asked if I recollected such
a person coming for change for a E10 note-I said I thought I did-the
officer said he had rather thick lips-I recollected he had-when saw him
I said, from the description, I was almost certain it was the man; and
looked at him a little bit, and then said he was the man-he had a large
coat on- did not exactly examine the colour of it-it was folded over-
I think he had a black hat on, I am not quite sure-I can swear to his
face.
