Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Had you other persons about the house when the prisoner called on you? A.  There was only one person—it is seldom that persons come there—I only showed my pigeons to gentlemen, and persons who I thought would not come and steal them.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  On what occasion was it the prisoner came to you? A.  To kill some rats, which were troublesome to my pigeons—he stood on the bank and looked into the pigeon-loft. ROBERT PURYER. I live in Hare-street, Bethnal-green, and deal in pigeons—I have known the prisoner since Christmas last. On the 25th of Jan. he brought me four pigeons, which I gave him 3 s . 10 d . for—one of them is here—they were fancy pigeons, but of no value—we could get plenty of them at Is. a piece—he asked me to buy some more, and I bought three more for halt-a-crown.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You bought them readily? A.  Yes—I knew he was a fancier of pigeons—he has bought of me, and has sold me some. JAMES M'CULLOCH( police-sergeant N  36.) On the 26th of Jan. I accom panied the prosecutor to Bow—I examined the prisoner's house, and found the pigeons—I afterwards examined the shoes he had on his feet—I had ex amined some foot-marks at Mr. Clarke's premises, and in my opinion they corresponded with the prisoner's shoes. JOHN ANDREWS. I am a baker, and live at Low Layton—there is a public-house called the Blackbird, about a mile and a half from Mr. Clarke's—I saw the prisoner at that public-house, about five o'clock on the Monday evening before the robbery, and saw him again about eight the same even ing, at the same house—I knew him, and have not the least doubt of his person.
