   Blagg. Q.  Is this hay used for the horses of the regiment? A.  It is used by Colonel Jones for his horses.
   COURT . Q.  Do you know whether it has been distributed by the proper officer to Colonel Jones? A.  I do not—it is only used by Colonel Jones.
   MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Whose horses are Colonel Jones'? A.  They are his own property, but this hay was in the King's stables. WILLIAM BOYLIN. I am Serjeant-Major of the Royal Riding-horse Ar tillery. WILLIAM BOYLIN re-examined. Q.  Had you any conversation with the prisoner Blagg on the subject to which that key or list referred? A.  No—I saw him again about eight o'clock the next morning—he led me to a yard in the possession, as I understood, of Burley, and there was some hay—I saw Burley there—he did not deny it—he took me to stable—I had no reason to doubt that it was Burley's yard—he came out after I entered the yard—I asked him where the hay was which he had purchased from Blagg—he said he had purchased no hay—I said the prisoner had confessed bring ing some hay there, and also had led me to the place—he said he had not purchased any, but he found three trusses there on Sunday morning—I asked where it was—he pointed to a shed and said it was there—we found there two trusses of hay—I asked him where the third truss was—he said he knew nothing about it—I then asked him if he had a stable—he said, "Yes" he went and opened the door with a latch—he went in, and there was a horse feeding at the rack—I took a portion of the hay out of the rack, and said, "I suppose this is part of the third truss?"—Bombardier Wilson went further into the stable, and found part of a truss—he called to me and said, "Here is part of the truss"—this was all in Burley's presence—he then went into a loft above the stable, and began counting some other trusses—I got upon a kind of hurdle, so that I could see into the loft, and he counted, in the stable and loft, eight trusses, including the broken truss—I then left a sentry in charge of it, while I went and communicated it to the commanding officer—I did not go again—Burely came to the barracks and asked me whether he could see Colonel Jones—I said he was gone to the office with the reports, but I was going there also, and would let him know—I said he was very foolish for trafficking with a soldier in any such way, or something to that effect—he then said he had been troubling him
