As to the two men not in custody I thought the action of the first
ra suspicious because he went off in such a hurried way. When
Garron got from the 'bus opposite Sweeting's it was stationary; he
turned back from Newgate Street to get into it. I thought his
setion extraordinary as he could have caught a 'bus going east at
the Mansion House. I should have arrested him otherwise.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'Connor. The first time I met Shuard
would be about 25 minutes to two in Queen Victoria Street. I had
then left Gurron standing about a foot away from Mappin's door,
Bhuard and I, as near as I can remember, went through Bucklers-
bory; we were frequently meeting and parting. I did not see
Knight until a quarter to four. Of the two men not in custody I
saw the first one about half-past one o'clock and the second about
two o'clock. I may have communicated my suspicions to Shuard;
but a lot occurred during three hours. I think it was Shuard who
first noticed the second man. I was in and out of Poultry, Cheap-
side, Grocers' Hall Court, and Old Jewry perhaps 30 times. To the
best of my belief Inspector Lyon first directed my attention to
Knight; I had already formed an opinion when he spoke to me. I
did not notice other cyclists waiting opposite some baulks of wood
in Queen Victoria Street (one of the positions occupied by Knight).
I suggest that when Knight was stooping behind his bicycle at Bird-
in-Hand Court he was watching me and other persons passing. I
saggest he was there for the purpose of watching the men coming
out of Mappin's under pretence of mending his bicycle. There were
not many people passing on that side of the pavement. I suggest
that from the position in which he was he could see them leave the
door of Mappin's premises and tell who they were. It is impossible
to see the door, but you can see anybody emerge from the door.
Immediately a man put his foot outside the door he could be seen;
I have proved it. Where Knight was standing was just in & line
with the handrail round the hoarding. When the iron gate opposite
Mappin and Webb's door is shut it is flush with the front of the
other buildings. I did not see prisoner Knight do anything with
spanner. His gear, including the spanner, was all wrapped up
in cloth in a little tin box which I produce here. The cloth is
marked with a "K." When I lost the second man I asked Shuard
it he could say where he had gone; he said he could not. King
Street is 51 yards from Bird-in-Hand Court. No one was left at
the corner of Bird-in-Hand Court to arrest Knight and there was
nothing to prevent him riding off down Bucklersbury.
