931. BENJAMIN GILBERT was indicted for stealing 1 iron wrench,
value 9d. ; 1 pair of shears, 9d. ; 1 hand vice, 9d.; and 1 pair of compasses,
9d.; the goods of Robert Sandall, his master.
ROBERT SANDALL. I lost these tools, I believe these produced are them.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. The prisoner worked in your shop!
A. Yes, for six months-I had a good opinion of him--I have known bim
six or seven years.
John GREEN (policeman.) I stopped the prisoner with these thing
in Liverpool-road last Saturday evening-I asked him what he had got
in the bag, he said some iron chains which he brought from Holloway, and
they belonged to his father-I said he must go to the station--he said, "I
wish I could meet my master, it would be all right"-on the way to the station
he said, "Pray let me go, I know I have done wrong"-after the charge was
taken, he said, "I may as well now speak the truth, the tools in the bag,
except the hammer, are my master's."
Guilty. Aged 25.---Recommended to mercy.-Confined One Month.
932. MARTHA BRUSER was indicted for stealing 1 handkercbiel,
value 2s.6d.; and I pencil-case, 18.; the goods of Augusta Amherst
Anstin.
AUGUSTA AMHERST Austin. I live with my father in Thayer-street, Man-
chester-square-I missed a handkerchief and a pencil-case in Jan.- this is my
pencilcase, and this handkerchief I believe is mine.
WILLIAM BrewsTER, pawnbroker. This handkerchief and pencil.case were
pawned for 38. by the prisoner, as I have every reason to believe.
WILLIAM CHING (police-constable D 97.) I took the prisoner-I told her
it was for stealing a bandkerchief and pencil-case---she said she did not steal
them, that Charlotte stole them, and gave them to her to pledge-Charlotte
denied it in the presence of the prisoner.
Avgusra AMHERST AUSTIN re-examined. I spoke to Charlotte about
it, and she denied it-the prisoner and her are both servants to my father.
Guilty. Aged 29.-Confined Six Months.
933. DANIEL SIMS and EDWARD BATTY were indicted for steal-
ing 1 pair of shoes, value 8s. 6d., the goods of William Murray.
WILLIAM MURRAY. I saw my shoes safe about seven o'clock at night on
the 9th of Feb., and missed them about twelve o'clock on the 10th-these
are them.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Did you see one of the prisoners in
your shop? Q. Yes, I saw Batty there the evening before.
WILLIAM PENTECOST, shopman to Mr. Burford, pawnbroker. These shoes
were pawned on the 10th of Feb. by Jims.
WILLIAM HOAD (police-constable T 148.) On the 10th of Feb. I took
Sims-he said he knew nothing about this--when I had both the prisoners in
custody, Sims said, "It would be a hard thing for a poor fellow to suffer for
doing another man a kindness"-Batty said, "Yes, it would, and I can prove
the shoes are not Murray's."
