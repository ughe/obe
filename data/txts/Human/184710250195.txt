MESSRS. BALLANTINE and PARNELL conducted the Prosecution. HENRY DOULTON. I carry on business in partnership with John Doulton, in High-street, Lambeth. On the 24th of Aug., about three o'clock, in con sequence of something I heard, I went to the Victoria-road, Kensington, and saw the prisoner there—I told him I understood he had called at our count ing-house, and required a quantity of pipes for a sewer he was about to build in that neighbourhood—he said he did, he should want 600 of 9-inch pipes—I said, "I understand that the ground landlord of the property will be here, and will give me a guarantee for the payment"—he said he was there, and introduced me to a gentleman who he called Richard Day, Esq., of Eltham, who he said banked at Herries, Farquhar, and Co.—I and the prisoner walked on together, and left Mr. Day there—we went to Kensington new workhouse—it was not quite finished then—there was a sewer being made in the neigh bourhood—the men were at work at it—he said, "You see that they are getting on with it, and if you don't send the first thing in the morning, they will be standing still"—he was almost within hearing of the men, I think they could have heard him, and he pointed to them—after that I went back to Mr. Day, and went to a public-house with him, and he gave me a guaran tee for the payment, which I produce—it was given in the prisoner's presence—the next morning he sent a man, saying the works were standing still, and in consequence of what had passed the previous day, I sent fifty stone pipes, worth 5 l . 12 s . 6 d .—a day or two after that, in consequence of something that my man told me, I went to look after the prisoner—he was not at home—he had left an address in my counting-house—I found the pipes were at Marl borough-road, Chelsea, at the premises of Messrs. Humphrey and Thirak, on some premises that were building about a mile and a half from the sewer—I have never been paid—I made repeated applications, and wrote to Mr. Day, and he said when the order was completed, he would see that Mr. Marson paid it.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  You applied to Mr. Day more than once? A.  I did—the prisoner gave his address Henry-street, Marlborough-road, Chelsea—I called there after the goods were sent, not before—I did not see him—I found that he lived there—it was a miserable house—a man that lived in such a house would not take such a contract—the guarantee was given in a public-house—the prisoner introduced me to Mr. Day as being the ground landlord—he had left word at my counting-house that the ground landlord would give me a guarantee—I should have parted with the pipes without the guarantee, under the circumstances, because when he said, in the presence of these men, he was building the sewer, I thought it sufficient guarantee to his respectability, that the guardians of the union should let out the work to him—I took the guarantee as an additional security—I did not require it—it was offered me, and I thought it right to accept it—I did not positively require it—I told him that I understood Mr. Day was willing to give it, and I was willing to receive it, and he gave it me—I did not mention it before I spoke of the price of the pipes—I had bargained about the pipes before he wrote the guarantee, but not I think before he mentioned that Mr. Day was willing to give it—I should, under the circumstances, have given the 70 l . worth of pipes without receiving the guarantee, from the gentleman representing himself as the ground landlord—I really cannot say when I
