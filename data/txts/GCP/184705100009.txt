WILLIAM THOMAS RENNIE. I am in the employ of Mr. Adolphus Leopold
Fill and his two sons, ironmongers, of Broad-street, Bloomsbury-I have known
the prisoner above three years-he has been a customer at my master's shop
--on Tuesday evening, the 6th of April, I saw him in our yard about half-
past seven o'clock-Mr. Barrett came and told me to go and watch him,
which I did he looked out three bars of iron and one bundle of rods-he
went into the shop, and the bars and rods remained in the yard- the shop
is the place where he would have to pay for them he had a man with
him, and he was employed in tying up the bars while the prisoner went into
the shop-I saw the prisoner come back, and when he came out, he took
down a rod of iron and laid it on the scale with the other three bars of iron-
there was no one at the scale then but the prisoner's man-Bugler had
weighed the other iron, and it was tied up by the prisoner's man--Bugler had
then gone to serve another customer-. when the prisoner took down the rod
of iron and placed it on the scale, he helped his man with the iron on bis
shoulder, and the prisoner immediately followed him with a bundle of rods-I
came down and asked Bugler what the prisoner had had, and I told what I
had seen.99 1900
