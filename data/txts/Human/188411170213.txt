 72. WILLIAM JOSEPH LANGFORD  (39) and AUGUSTA WIL  SON (35) , Stealing a watch, the property of Alice Lanson , to which
   WILSON PLEADED GUILTY.
   MR. WILKINSON Prosecuted. ALICE LAUSON. I reside at 80, Ormside Street, Old Kent Road—the female prisoner is my daughter—the prisoner Langford had been living with her as her husband—on the 11th of this month, about 6 o'clock in the evening, my daughter was at my house—I had a gold watch then; it was hanging up on a hook over the mantelpiece—I missed it directly after she had gone—I had seen it safe at a quarter to 4 o'clock on that afternoon; this is it ( produced )—I value it at 10 l . FREDERICK RICHARD PRESTCOTT. I am assistant at Rogers and Co.'s, pawnbrokers, 185, Walworth Road—I produce a watch pledged on the first occasion on 11th November, by the prisoner Langford, in the name of William Wilson, for 2 l .—he came again on 3rd November with the female prisoner; he said he had come to get a declaration on his watch, as he had lost his ticket—he said it was his own watch, a family relic—in consequence of that I made out a declaration, which he took away, and brought back and received a new ticket.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  You went outside and said you would ask your wife whether you should take the 2 l . which I offered or not, and you came back and said "I had better have the money." GEORGE PURBROOK( Police Sergeant R ). I arrested the prisoners—Wilson said in Langford's presence "I stole the watch, Langford was waiting at the top of the street; he pawned it the same evening in the Walworth Road"—Langford said "That is quite right." ALFRED MARSDEN( Policeman R  41). I took Langford in charge—he said "She stole the watch and gave it to me, and I pawned it for 2 l ."—he gave me the ticket, which I produce.
