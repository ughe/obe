   GEORGE FENNELL sworn.
 I am clerk in the Pay-office.
 Do you remember any man coming to your pay-office, to demand the wages of the  Stirling Castle? - No, Sir; all that I remember, is, one day, while I officiated for Mr. Slade, there was a man that came for some business of a ship; it was on or about the 4th of April; but what ship, or what man it was, I cannot say; and on referring to the book, there was an observation that the will was supposed to be forged; upon which, I told the man, whoever he was, he must go to Mr. Knight, or Mr. Dyson.
 Court. Was there any more than one person that applied to you at that time? - There was a woman.
 Was there any other man? - I do not undertake to say that there was.
 In what character did that man apply to you? - As the representative of the seaman.
 Are you clear in that fact? - I did not attend so very particularly to that fact.
 Can you recollect, whether the person at that time applied to you as being a representative of the executor to the will, or as coming from him in any other character? - He applied as executor; because there was no power of attorney, only the probate of the will.
 Mr. Garrow. You have but a very imperfect recollection of this? - Yes.
 For instance, could you tell, at this distance of time, whether it was a probate, or letters of administration to the seaman? - I cannot.
 Can you tell whether there was an objection to the administration, or the probate, which it was you do not recollect? - No.
 Might it not be, then, quite consistent with all your recollection of the subject, that that person might come as his attorney to enquire whether the objection was removed? - They certainly came for payment.
 It was on or about the 4th of April? - Yes.
 How many days might you officiate for Mr. Slade? - Four days in that week.
 Might there not be other men in the four days? - There might; but I do not recollect that there were; I am not so perfect in my recollection, as to say that they were not.
 Mr. Baldwin. Did any other man apply to you, besides this man, from the Navyboard; not on that day, but about that day? - I cannot say.

 459.  ELIZABETH WELLS was indicted for endeavouring to obtain two gallons of red Port wine, the property of Murphy Bates and others, by false pretences.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
 460.  FRANCIS POOL SHEPHERD was indicted for endeavouring to obtain a great coat, the property of Henry Flower Freeman , by false pretences.
 The prosecutor declaring he did not believe the prisoner, and did not intend to let him have the coat, on the representation he made, the indictment could not be supported.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
