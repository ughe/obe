I knew the deceased—I went there on the Thursday, the day she died—I was present when she died, and assisted in laying her out—I had seen her that same morning, but not lately before that—I found her sitting in the chair—she said she was very ill—I went away at that time—I was sent for a half-past eight o'clock and found her in her daughter's arms vomiting blood—she died in my arms directly afterwards. WILLIAM OLDS. I am fourteen years old, and lived with the prisoner and the deceased, my father and mother. On the Saturday night before she died I was at home—my father and mother and I were in the house above nine o'clock, nobody else—my mother had had some money left her—I believe it was kept in a drawer of a cheat of drawers that was there—when my father came home he said, "I want some money"—he was not sober my mother said, "No," she would not give it him; and he took hold of the drawers by the bottom and turned them over—I believe the money was in those drawers—I never saw any there—my father went backwards to get the hammer to break the drawers open—my mother took the hammer out of his hand, and threw it over the pigstye—he then began quarrelling and hitting her—they were both quarrelling—I did not see my mother hit him, nor my father hit her—I went out to fetch a policeman—my mother was then in the front room—she was standing up—I did not see her down upon the ground at all—I went for the policeman because my mother sent me—I did not see my father do anything to her—she was sitting down in a chair when I went for the policeman—my father was standing by the fire-side—he was not quiet, he was making a noise and row  —I did not see him doing any-thing else—I should have seen it if it had been so—my mother hallooed one two or three times, "Run for a policeman," and I did so—I did not see anybody hold my mother round the body.
   COURT. Q.  Had your mother had her supper at that time? A.  Yes—there was some beer there for mother and father's supper—we had not had our supper—the cloth was on the table—we were going to have some bear and oysters—the oysters were on the table—when my father came down he took the beer and shoved it off the table—my mother did nothing upon that—she was not pleased at it—she said, "That did not hurt me"—he then knocked out the candle—there was only one candle there—then he began his row  —I heard him rustling about, and my mother hallooed out two or three times, "Run and fetch the policeman"—she said nothing else.
   Q.  Recollect yourself; although the prisoner is your father, you must speak the truth; what makes you say he began to make a row  when the candle was out; what did you hear? A.  Mother said, "Run, he will kill me, he will kill me!"—I did not hear the noise of any blows struck—the table was not turned over—as soon as the row  commenced I ran out—it was quite dark—there was no fire—I do not know what the rustling was about—there was no one else there to rustle about, except my father and mother—when I came back again I had a policeman with me—there was no light then—the policeman turned on his lantern—I went to bed two or three hours after that—I had no supper afterwards—there was a light got before I went to bed—my mother lighted a candle—I did not see any mark on her face that sight—I am quite sure about that—I first saw any mark on her face on the Sunday morning—I was not disturbed in the night after this.
