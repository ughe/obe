678. ANN HARPER, stealing 1 coat, value 14. the goods of John Thomas
Smith.
JOHN THOMAs SMITH. I am a soldier in the Royal Artillery. About a
fortnight ago I was in the Crooked Billet publichouse, Tower-hill-I went
into the tap room between 2 and 3 o'clock-) took my coat off, and laid it on
a form-I was talking to some soldiers of the Guards-I got up to go away,
and missed my coat- have never seen it since-I saw the prisoner in the
tap room-she had not been in company with me--I had not seen her before
tit was a blue cape coat, like this one.
EDWARD JEMMETT. I was in the Crooked Billet, on 11th May-] went in
about 7 o'clock in the evening, and stayed till about 9-I saW Smith talking
to some foot guards-tl prisoner was on the opposite side of the form- am
sure it was herw-I saw Smith with a blue coat like this onethe put it across
a form-] saw the prisoner put it under her arm, and take it onteI am quite
sure she is the person went with the soldiers to look after her, but could
not see her.
NATHAN NICHOLAS (policeman, H 36). On the evening of 12th May I
took the prisoner at the Crooked Billet--as we were going to the station she said
she was going to take it to a leaving shop in Castle-street, to get some money
on itshe said she had taken it to a pawnbroker's in the Minories, and they
were shut up, and, as she was going down Rosemary-lane, a woman stole it
from her arm, and she had not seen it since.
Prisoner's Defenee, I went to get half a pint of beer, and a young woman
said she was to take this young man's coat, and get some money she asked
me to go with her when we got out she asked me if I would pawn it; I said
I did not know where it was; she asked me again, and I took it, and as I was
going along a woman ran against me, and took the coat I went after her, but
could not find her I went and inquired I went the next day to the Crooked
Billet, and was taken.
NOT GUITY.
