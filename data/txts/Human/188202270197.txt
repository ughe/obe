 378. JAMES MARSHALL  (43), Unlawfully attempting to utter a counterfeit shilling.
   MESSRS. POLAND and HORACE AYORY Prosecuted. ELIZABETH WILD. I keep a refreshment shop at 32, Redcross Street—on 24th January, a little after 10 p. m., I served a man with a pennyworth of eels—he tendered what I took to be a shilling—I gave him 11 d . change and put it on a shelf at the back—the prisoner came in then, and the men spoke to each other—the prisoner tendered another coin for a penny worth of eels—I took it to my husband, who ran and stopped the prisoner—I then said "I have one very much like it," and put the other coin on the counter—the prisoner picked the first coin up and chucked  down a penny—the other man had run away directly I had given him change—the prisoner tried to get away—the coin I gave my husband was like this, but much whiter.
   Cross-examined by the prisoner.  I became aware the coins were bad before you left the shop—I am sure you spoke to the other man—you were close to him. CHARLES WILD. On 24th January the prisoner came to our shop and put down what appeared to be a shilling—my wife served him with a pennyworth of eels—my wife took up the coin, looked at it, and showed it to me—I saw it was bad—the prisoner stood there eating his eels, the other man ran out—I shut the shop door and said to the prisoner "You will not go out"—he struggled with me, we were up and down five or six times—I said "It is no use your trying, you don't go outside of my door till a policeman comes"—he said he would pay his penny, it was not his shilling—when a constable came I gave him into custody—I gave the coin to the constable—the prisoner took up the coin which my wife put on the counter—I think he put it in his mouth, it was not left in the shop.
