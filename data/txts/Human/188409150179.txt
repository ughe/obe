MR. POYNTER Prosecuted. HENRY GEORGE( Detective V ). I had the prisoner in custody on another charge of burglary on 11th August—he was searched, and duplicates were given to me to make inquiries about—I served the prisoner in the House of Detention with notice of this charge—he said "The watch and chain you are speaking of belong to my father and mother, who had it for years; it is an heirloom, of our family."
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  Two duplicates were found on you. DALEY FUSSELL( Policeman V  220). I had the prisoner in custody on another charge—I searched him and found these spoons and these two duplicates in his left-hand trousers pocket. EDWARD GARDNER. I am assistant to Mr. Amhurst, a pawnbroker, of York Street, Westminster—this duplicate of 26th July, 1884, is from our shop—it is for a silver watch and Albert, pawned for 9 s . to the best of my belief by the prisoner. JOHN LONG. I am Mr. Underwood's son-in-law, and reside with him at 42, Kearsley Street, Battersea—on the night of 25th July I closed and bolted up the house—all the fastenings were secure—the following morning I found everything ransacked—an entrance had been effected by the latch of the back window being pushed back and the window being opened—of my own property I missed some links and an old cruet stand of solid silver, a silver shield with a cornet and a cricket bat, some rings, and a pearl necklace—the value of the property missing was about 30 l .—the silver chain is my mother's; it was in the house that night—I produce a photograph of it.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  The chain is peculiar, having one long link and the others different, the circumference being alike. WILHELMINA WORNAST. I was in Mr. Underwood's service—this is my watch ( produced )—it has a private mark inside where it was put up for sale—it was safe in the house on the night of 26th May—the chain belongs to my mistress.
   Prisoner's Defence.  That watch has been in our family 20 years, my father gave it to my brother three months before he died. I have no relations and no witnesses to prove it. What the last witness has said is utterly false. GUILTY  .— Judgment Respited.
