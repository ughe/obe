The questions left to the Jury were : Did all or any of the defendante
conspire together and or with Bond to contravene the Corrupt Praction
(Public Bodies) Act, 1889, in regard to the coal contract. Did all or any
of the defendants, whether guardians or officials, conspire together and
with Bond, at any time from July 1, 1903, to the present, to cheat and
defraud the West Ham Guardians ? Did the respective defendants named
receive corruptly from Bond in regard to the coal contract, contrary to the
Act of 1889--Crump, £10; Tarrant, £10; Anderson, £10; Skinner, £10;
Watts, £10; F. W. Hill, £10; Lewis G, Hill, £25; Riches, 35;
Baird, £10; Hodgkin, £25 ?
Verdict: Tarrant, not guilty; all the other defendants guilty on al
counts; Baird and Riches recommended to meroy on the ground that
they occupied subservient positions.
Sentences: Crump, 2 years' hard labour; Lewis G. Hill, 2 years' hard
labour ; Hodgkin, 18 months' hard labour; Anderson, 15 months' hard
labour; Skinner, 15 months' hard labour ; Riebes, 9 months' bard labour;
Watts, 9 months' hard labour; F. W. Hill, 6 months' hard labour; Baird,
6 months' hard labour. Each prisoner was adjudged incapable of being
elected or appointed to any public office for seven years from the date
his conviction.
Mr. Muir desired to call attention to the services which Chin
Inspector Kane and the officers acting under him had rendered to the
cause of justice. The public were deeply indebted to them.
Nr. Mansfield, on the part of the defendants, also acknowledged the
kindness they had received from the hands of the officers.
Mr. Justice Jelf said he entirely agreed in those observations, and if any
expression of opinion on his part would be of any use to Chief Inspetor
Kane, either with regard to his future career or to an immediate recogal-
tion of his services, he would be glad to give it. His experience was that
the better the officer was and the keeper in the discharge of his duties the
more he was inclined to be kind and fair to accused persons.
