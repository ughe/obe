   Q.  Were you not going home, and did not Glannon say you should not go home, until he had proved you had not lost your watch? A.  Nothing of the kind passed. JAMES SMITH. I am a ladies' shoe-maker, and live in Trafalgar-place, Lock's-fields. On Wednesday morning, I was at the door of the Crown and Anchor, about half-past one o'clock, sitting on a bench outside—Gill came and sat down on the bench opposite to me—he told me there had been an attempt to rob him of his watch—I saw his chain and seals hang ing out in the usual way—he did not pull his watch out—I gave him some advice, and he shortly afterwards shifted himself from sitting opposite to me, and came and sat down by my side, and he either put his chain and seals inside his flap or pocket, so as to conceal them—Chappell came up and pretended to be very much intoxicated—his hat fell off several times, and each time his hat fell off, he got nearer to the end of the table where the prosecutor was, and then he fell into the prosecutor's lap—he went from one seat to the other, across the table, to fall into his lap—the prosecutor put him out, and then he fell into his lap again—Glannon was sitting on the seat, and when he came, the two prisoners began to hustle the pro secutor—he was hustled on to a bench, on his back, and his feet upwards—he was surrounded by half a dozen persons—I jumped over the table to get out of the way—his legs were up in the air—I did not see any thing that they did to him—a constable came (No. 156, I believe) and took them—the prosecutor got off the bench, and said, "I have been robbed of my watch"—Glannon, I think, said, "Search him before he goes away from the place, as he says he has lost his watch"—the prosecutor instantly gave part of the chain and seals to the policeman, out of his left hand.
