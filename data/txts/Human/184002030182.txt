On the afternoon of the 23rd of Jan  uary the prisoner came into the shop, and asked for a Valentine—I served him with one—he put a 5 s . piece on the counter—my mistress came in and took it up—the prisoner received 4 s . in change—when he was gone my mistress showed me the crown-piece—I went out to seek for the prisoner, but could not see him—my mistress kept the 5 s . piece.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  It was rather early to apply for an article of that kind? A.  Yes—I had never seen him before—he had a flannel jacket on and an apron round his waist—I noticed his dress altogether—I noticed his face because he had his eyes more on the ground than on any thing else—he looked at another valentine with a rose, but that would not do because it was 1 s . 6 d .
   MR. SHEA . Q.  Did he look at several? A.  Not a great many—I do not suppose he was there more than ten minutes—I have not the least doubt that he is the person. MARY FERGUSON. I am the wife of Charles Ferguson, who keeps that shop. I came into the shop while the prisoner was there—it was quite light—it was a little after three o'clock—I received the crown-piece, and gave the change—he left the shop directly, and immediately he left I dis covered that the 5 s . piece was bad—I put it into my apron pocket, and kept it separate from all other money—I gave it to Emmerson the policeman on that day week—it was marked in my presence—I am certain it was the one I received from the prisoner—I had no other.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you seen him before? A.  No—he looked down on the floor—he did not look us in the face—he had bought the valentine before I came in—he had a hat on, and was dressed in a flannel jacket—I saw him again on the following week—I had given a description of him as having a downcast look, and I described his dress. ELIZABETH MILLARD re-examined . I told the policeman he was dressed in a flannel jacket, and had an apron round his waist—I did not say any thing about his having a downcast look. THOMAS EMMERSON( police-sergeant V  16.) Between three and four o'clock in the afternoon, on the 23rd of January, I was in Lark Hall-lane, and saw the prisoner, and a female with him, going towards London, away from the prosecutor's shop—on the 30th of January I was near the Vaux hall gate, and saw the prisoner and two more persons in custody, one of whom was the female I had seen the prisoner with on the 23rd—I went with the prisoner to Mr. Ferguson's shop—I saw Mrs. Ferguson—she gave me this crown-piece—I went with the prisoner to the station-house at Clapham, and found on him 1 s . 6 d ., and a new pair of braces were found in his hat, which are worth about a shilling.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had Mrs. Ferguson previously given you a de scription of the man? A.  She sent the description by another sergeant to the station-house.
   COURT . Q.  What description had been left? A.  A young man, with a flannel jacket, and an apron, and a pencil sticking out of his pocket—the prisoner was so dressed when I saw him, and also when he was taken, and he had a pencil in his pocket, and part of it stuck out.
