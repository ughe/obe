EDWARD Cock, surgeon, St. Thomas-street, Southwark. On the night of
the 24th of Oct. I was sent for to Guy's-hospital, where I am assistant-sur-
geon, and saw a child who was brought there—the woman who brought it
said it was her child-I examined, and found its head very much swollen and
mis-shapen-a great swelling on the right side, produced by extravasation of
blood- I had no doubt the brain was injured, and that there was a fracture of
the skull—it was alive then--I did all I could for it, and left it in care of the
dresser and sister of the ward—I hardly expected it would live more than two
hours--I saw it dead next morning-1 attributed its death to the injury on
the head-I made a post mortem examination, which entirely confirmed my
opinion.
