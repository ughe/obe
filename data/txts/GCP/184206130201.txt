1938. JAMES KAIN and JOAN FARRELL were indicted for steal-
ing on the 6th of June, 9 ivory balls, value 11. 78. 6d., the goods of
Robert Edward Docking.
ROBERT Edward Docking. I am the landlord of the Coach and
Horses, in Bunbill-row. On the 6th of June the prisoners were drinking
in the tap-room-they afterwards went up stairs to play at bagatelle--I
have lost nine ivory balls, worth 11. 78. 60.--they have not been found.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHARNOCK. Q. Is this a public room? 4.
Yes-the prisoners went into the room about four o'clock, and went away
about half-past five-I was sitting in the bar all the evening, and not a
soul went up stairs after them, and none but them-I missed the balls at
eight o'clock.
RE
John Reis. I and three fellow-workmen were in the room- I remem-
ber the prisoners playing with these balls at bagatelle-Farrell was the
last person who was playing-Kain came about half-an-hour after the
other was there-I left them at half-past five o'clock-Farrell was amusing
himself with the balls, and Kain looking on-no persons were left in the
Toom but the two prisoners--my fellow-workmen went with me I saw
eight white balls there, and one red one.
JAMES GROOM. I am pot-boy at this house. Farrell was the last per-
son I saw playing in this room, and Kain was looking on-four of De la
Rue's men were in the room-they went, and the two prisoners only were
left-I saw them go away after the other men were gone I cannot swear
whether any other persons came after they left.
JAMES BRANNAN. I am a police-sergeant. I took the prisoners—they
denied it.
