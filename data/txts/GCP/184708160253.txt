WILLIAM HALL. I live at Bayswater--on the 3rd of July I let some
premises at No. 33, John-street, Surrey, to the prisoner there were two
Fanges of buildings, one a workshop, the other a stable--the prisoner repre-
sented himself as a master cowkeeper, and took the place to make a dairy-
he said he should bring in fifty cows-there was some lead in the gutters on
the roof, and some round the skylights, some lead cisterns, and a lead force-
pump-there was from three to four tons of lead--the prisoner was to have
the place till the 10th of Aug. on sufferance before he commenced rent, and
then to have it for three years, this was all pnt in writing-I went to the
premises about ten days afterwards, and found the lead bad been stripped
away from the gutters and the cistern, and the lead pipe taken away off the
pump-I did not find any cows there—the prisoner never brought a shilling's
worth of property into the place there were no signs of preparation for
dairy-the utmost that was brought in was about four penny worth of lime
I went to inquire where he had lived before, and I went to the premises a
second time-I then found that even the paving-stones were being pulled up-
I should say there were from two to three tons of lead gone it was worth
from 301. to 401.--I should say the stones were worth 41. or 51., but I un
derstand he sold them for 338.--there was a man employed to put zinc on
the gutters.

Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Who was the tenant before the pri-
soner? A. Mr. Brown, an engineer-I do not know that he is here--he
gave me a regular notice to quit-I agreed to take 8l. a year from the pri-
soner for three years from Michaelmas next—there were hardly any repairs
to be made-I saw a labouring man with a brush in his hand there were no
wells to be emptied the lead had been on the roof twenty or thirty years
it was not in a very bad state and full of holes—I had no complaint of it-
the engineer had used the place as a workshop-it had been an engineer's,
and was to be a cow-keeper's—it wanted no alteration-I had been over the
place by myself two or three days before the prisoner entered, and saw the
gutters and roof in a very satisfactory state ; the prisoner and his wife went
over the place with me—they did not go on to the roof.
LEONARD MATTHEW. I was employed about the 10th of July by the
prisoner on the premises in John-street, whitewashing --he afterwards em-
ployed me and Gardner, a carpenter, to get the lead off the guttering and roof
-I lent a hand to do so. I took the lead with a track to Wright, & marine-
store dealer, at 73, Redcross-street--we took four trucks full of lead, and
some in a sack-I only went once by myself with the truck-I received
118. 8d., which I gave to Mr. Hatton on the premises—the other times the
lead went I was outside I went twice with Hatton, and twice with Gardner
- I took altogether about 9 cwt.
