Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. From where did it drop? A. From his trousers inside - I desired him to take it out. There are three Mr. Bramah's; he had been working there about eight days.
   SAMUEL PEAKE. I am an officer, and took the prisoner; what the last witness has stated is true.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did you compare the lead? A. No; it was sheet lead - he had knocked it up; Mr. Bramah makes no mark on his lead.
 COURT. Q. It was not very convenient to have the lead dangling in his trousers for hours? A. No; he could not have done it.
   DANIEL DAWKINS. I produce the lead - it weighs 52lbs.; I did not compare it - I received it from Howson.
 Prisoner's Defence. The property is my own, and I took it there concealed, because I thought Mr. Bramah would claim it - I had no place to leave it at; and I brought it out, concealed in the same manner as I took it in.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. Confined Six Months and Whipped.
 712.  JAMES MORAN was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of January , 1 watch, value 1l. 16s., and 1 seal, value 4s., the goods of William Macoy.
 WILLIAM MACOY. I live in Bloomsbury-square, and am  servant to Mr. McIntosh. On the 16th of January, at night, about eleven o'clock, I was rather the worse for liquor, and had been at Mr. Maxwell's public-house, near Leather-lane - I do not know whether the prisoner was there - the watch was in my fob; I missed it while I was at Maxwell's - the prisoner might have been there without my knowing it; I looked at my watch at ten o'clock - whether I put into my fob, I cannot say; I might have dropped it; I went into the yard - I mentioned it in the house when I missed it; the next day I saw it at Hatton-garden - the prisoner was in custody; I never knew him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You might have left the watch in the yard? A. Yes; it is more likely that I did than not. The prisoner said at the office that he had found it in the yard.
   THOMAS MAXWELL. I am a licensed victualler - my house is in Holborn, above bars. The prisoner and the prosecutor were at my house; the prisoner is a working lad- he was at my house that night, with his father and other friends; I heard that the prosecutor had lost his watch, and I closed the doors immediately: the prisoner was gone - I had pushed him out of doors myself, that he might go home.
 Cross-examined. Q. The boy heard no outcry about the watch? A. No. I have known him about seven years, and always found him honest.
   ANDREW LLOYD I am an officer of Hatton-garden. I received information, and apprehended him; I asked where the watch was, and he said his mother had it; I was searching him, and he pulled it out of his right-hand pocket.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you hear that he had shown the watch to anybody? A. No; I received my information from the landlord's brother.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the yard, and picked the watch up.
     NOT GUILTY.
 713.  JOSEPH LITTLEWOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 1 piece of tarpauling, containing 3 yards, value 10s., the goods of Joshua Beddall and Philip Beddall.
   GEORGE STEPHENS. I am a labourer. Messrs. Beddalls live in High Holborn, and are carpenters. On the 13th of February, about seven o'clock in the morning, I was going to work at Mr. Turner's, about three doors from the building where the tarpauling was; that is in Middleton-street, Clerkenwell. I saw the prisoner coming out of the building, with it on his shoulder, and I stopped him; it was about three or four yards of old tarpauling - he said he was going home with it: he did not say where he lived, or that he worked for Beddall's.
   WILLIAM TAYLOR. I work for Messrs. Beddalls - their names are Joseph and Philip; I worked for them in Middleton-street; I left the tarpauling nailed up the night before, when I left work at half-past six o'clock. The prisoner is a stranger.
   JOSEPH COOMBES. I am a constable. The prisoner was delivered over to me - he said he had found the tarpauling in the building.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going by the building, and went in for a necessary purpose; I saw the tarpauling laying, and I took it up; the place was all open.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Whipped and Discharged.
 714.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of February , 1 pair of shoes, value 4s., the goods of John Blake.
   JOHN WILLIAM BLAKE. I am the son of George Blake, of Constitution-row, Gray's Inn-road. These shoes hung on some nails in the shop; the prisoner came in last Wednesday, about half-past seven o'clock - my father had known him about three years ago; he asked if my father was at home - I told him he was not: he said I could do as well - he wanted three or four pairs of shoes for a person at Finchley; he gave me the measure: I looked them out, and while I was making out the bill, he sat down in the shop, where the shoes were, within his reach - I made out the bill; he said he was going to the haberdasher's, and would return in about a quarter of an hour; he left - I looked, and missed a pair of women's shoes: I went after him - he was not above twenty or thirty yards off; I asked him where he was going to - he said to the haberdasher's; I said "I think you have got a pair of shoes;" he shook his pockets, and said, "No, I have not, upon my honour"- I felt, and found a pair in his pocket; I took him back, and sent for a constable.
   BENJAMIN BARNETT. I have a pair of shoes; the prisoner took them out of his left-hand pocket, and gave them to me - he went down on his knees, and wished me to let him go.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 68. Confined One Month.
