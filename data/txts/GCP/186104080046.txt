338. JAMES MILLER (37), Stealing a coat, value 20s, the property of
Joseph Lynes.
HENRY CATLIN. I am shopman to Joseph Lynes, of 80, Biskopsgate
street-on the night of 12th Maroh, I saw a coat safe about twenty minutes
to 9 o'clock, and missed it at a quarter to 9-it was my master's property.
GEORGE DAVIDSON. I am a printer, of 51, Bookham-street-on the night
of 13th March, I was coming out of the next house to Mr. Lynes', and saw the
prisoner take the coat-I caught hold of him, and said, "You vagabond,
you have stolen a coat "- he tried to get away, but I kept hold of his collar,
and when he had got about a dozen yards, he passed it to somebody else,
and gave me a blow in the face, and they both got away I caught prisoner
again, and two or three times altogether, but he got away eventually.
DAVID ROBERT JONES. I was in Union-street-heard a cry of “Stop,
thief !” and saw the prisoner running, followed by a crowd of persons—I SAY
a person attempt to stop him, but the prisoner knocked him down-I cangbt
hold of him by the collar—be struck me three or four violent blows on the
side of the head, and threw me against the shutters somebody said, "Hold
him fast; he has stolen something"-a policeman came, whom he also struck,
and threw himself down-he kicked me violently on the thigh-he was
taken to the station.
CHARLES BARRETT (City-policeman, 644). I went to Jones's assistance,
and took the prisoner - he was very violent-I believe his drunkenness was
put on, as he did not show it till he got to the station- he was able to run
Prisoner. I was in liquor, and know nothing about it: I am guilty of the
assault, but not guilty of the theft.
