JOSEPH FRANOR WOOLMER. I am a clerk in the Ludgate Hill branch
of the City Bank-Rideout opened an account there in December, 1884-
it continued in that name until May, 1885, when it was transferred into
the name of G. E. Halley, and it continued in that name until some time
in July ; it was then closed- this is the pass-book issued to Halley on
the transfer into his name-at that time there was a balance of 8887. 2s.
to the credit of the account-considerable sums were received after that
date-this ( produced) is a certified copy of Halley's account from 8th
May—the last entry is 31st July on debit-the balance then was 471.
48. 3d., which went into the Bankruptcy Court-between 8th May and
30th June, when the account was balanced, there was a sum of 5,0121.
198. 5d., including the 8881., to the credit of the account; that is, over
4,0007. received between 8th May and 30th June, apart from the balance
-on 28th July there is a cheque drawn to self for 3001.
Cross-examined. The account shows on 8th May the payment to
Collingridge, the printers of the paper, of 6151. 38., and on the next day
981. salaries, and on 16th May 1501. salaries, on 5th June 1001. salaries,
on 13th June 1201. salaries, and on 20th June 1001. salaries, with
other large payments at intervals.
LUCINDA WICKTON MONICHOLL. I am the widow of William McNicholl,
a labourer, living at 40, Hospital Street, Glasgow-I was living there
with him the whole of last year-we had lived there 16 years no such
person as D. L. Wesson lived in that house last year--a man came one
Sabbath Day a fortnight after my husband came up to give evidence in
this case, and asked if there were any letters for him my husband
spoke to him-I did not hear the conversation-I asked him if his name
was Mr. Wesson; he said “Yes" when I asked him, but he could not
tell his name at first-my husband told him to wait till he put on his
clothes-he did not come again.
