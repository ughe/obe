571. ELIZABETH HAMILTON (39), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit
coin.
Messrs. LLOYD and CRAUFORD Prosecuted.
MAY Roscoe. I am assistant to Mr. Hooper, a linendraper, of 480,
Oxford Street-on Friday, 23rd December, I served the prisoner with a
small wool shawl for 6d.-she gave me a half-crown-I examined it, saw
it was bad, and gave it to Mr. Hooper-it was rather light.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. Other customers were in the shop-no
one tendered a half-crowd.
Josiah HOOPER. Roscoe brought me a half-crown-I fetched & constable
and told him the charge in the prisoner's presence she said "I did not
know it was bad”-he took her in custody with the half-crown.
JOHN BARRINGER (Policeman D 174). I was called and took the
prisoner-she said a gentleman had given her two half-crowns
I said " What was the other coin ?"-she opened her purse and
produced this good florin-she was taken to the station, remanded, and
discharged on 5th January-she gave her name Mary Ann Wright -I
received this half-crown from Mr. Hooper.
Louis MORETTI. I keep a confectioner's shop at 227, Southampton Row
-on 8th May, about 9 o'clock, the prisoner asked for twopennyworth of
cake-she handed me a shiling-I told her it was bad, and she had
better be careful-she walked out of the shop-she said she had no more
money-I gave her the bad shilling back and took back the cake-I next
saw her passing with a constable on Wednesday, the 9th-I said to the
constable “ You have got the right woman; that is the woman who tried
to pass a shilling on me”- she said she had never seen me before-sho
had a piece of sticking plaster on her nose when I saw her before, but
none then—the shilling felt very queer and light.
JOHN HENRY WRIGHT. I keep the Fish public-house, Fisher Street,
Holborn-on 10th May, about 10.15 p.m., the prisoner paid for a half
pint of stout and mild with good money-she afterwards tendered a bad
florin for a half-quartern of gin in a bottle-I said “Do you know
what you have got here?” - she said “A two-shilling piece”-I said
" It is a bad one, it is no good ”-she said she was not aware of it-I
said " Have you got any more of them ?”- she said "No, I have not got
any money at all"-I put the coin down in front of her and fetcheda
policeman and gave him the coin.
Cross-examined. You had some plaster on your under lip, not on your
nose.
JOSEPH DURHAM (Policeman E 511). I took the prisoner at Mr. Wright's
shop-he gave me the coin-she said "I had it given to me”-I re-
cognised her from a description I had of her, and from having seen her
in the Albion public-house, 7, Vernon Place, Southampton Row, on
Monday night; that is about 80 yards from Moretti's house--she then
had some plaster on the left side of her nose-passing Moretti's shop
Moretti said “ You have got the right one ; she was in my shop on
Monday night”-she was searched, and ifd. in bronze and a large key
