DAVID REEVE. I live at Brixton. I have worked for Mr. Cooper-I.
made this rule, and sent it to the building-I know it-(looking at it) --it is!
just as it was when I made it-I had some words about it with Mr. Cooper
I said it would never be fit for a floating-rule--I said it was a very shaky
piece, not proper for the purpose--I am sure this is the rule-it is not exactly
in the same state, it is a little narrowed at each end-it was straight all
along when I gave it to the boy to take down-I made it about twelve months
ago last April.

Joux MILLER. I am a bricklayer. I worked for Mr. Cooper last May
I worked for a short time at the building where the prisoners were at work
I remember, about May, Byfield came there, and Mr. Cooper accused him of
stealing the rule--he asked him why he put his name on the rule-Byfield
said it was his-Mr. Cooper said it was not, it was a rule belonging to himself
-I do not recollect anything else.
PETER CHARLES. I am a bricklayer. I was at work at Mr. Cooper's
buildings in May. I remember Mr. Cooper asking Byfield what business ho
had to put his name on his rule-Byfield said it was his rule; and he said as
far as this, that it was his intention to take the rule away-Mr. Cooper said,
"It is mine, and you may depend, if you take that rule away, I will lock you
up"-Byfield said he would insist on taking it.
GEORGE BRYDGES (police-constable P 128.) I was in Coalharbour-lane on
the 7th of August-I saw Haines with this rule, and I took him into custody
by Mr. Cooper's direction-I afterwards saw Byfield, and took him-I took
both the prisoners to the station-I was at the police office afterwards--the
Magistrate said Mr. Cooper might take his rule.
