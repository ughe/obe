Mr. Phillips to Mr. Brown. Q. How long have you known Ellis Cas-
par? A. Since 1818-he is a watchmaker-I purchased a watch of him, for
which I paid him 511.- I recommended one person to him-in his dealing
with me. I found him fair and honourable-I believe him to be an honest
tradesman.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Have you any knowledge at all of his character
for the last ten or twelve years? A. Not for so short a time.
GEORGE RICHARD JOHNSON. I am an assayer of metals, and carry on busi-
ness in Maiden-lane, Wood-street. On the 2nd of April, some assays for
Henry Solomon were made at our house-there were three assays tried for
gold and silver both, and one of silver only-on the 3rd of April, there were
two assays of gold made for the same party-in consequence of some mes-
sage I afterwards received, I sent the two last assays to Mr. Bult, in
Cheapside, by the desire of John Dean, Solomon's man---the writing on
these two assay papers is mine-I believe they are what were sent to Mr.
Bult.
John Down. I am one of the clerks in the banking-house of Williams,
Deacon, and Co. On the 2nd of April, I received this £300 note on ac-
count of that house--I have the memorandum-I received the note from
Adamthwaite and Friend, our notaries.
EDWARD CLARK. I am clerk to Mr. Bult, of Cheapside. On the 2nd
of April I made a payment to some person of Solomon's establishment-it
was 1241. 1s., in one Bank-note of 1001., No. 1888, dated 5th of February,
1839; four notes of 5l., dated 12th of January, 1839, and the rest in cash
-on the 4th of April, I paid 1561. 178., in two 201. notes, three 5l. notes,
and the remainder must have been in cash, as I have no entry of thaton
the 8th of April, I made a further payment of 1111. 78. 7d. in cash ; on the
12th, I paid 1001. 4s. 10d., in one note of 501. ; two, of 201., and one, of
101.- I think that was paid to the elder Mr. Solomon-I cannot recollect
who I paid the others to. .
