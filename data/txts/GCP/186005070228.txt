ture-I cannot swear to the person who presented the cheque-Streaker is

HARRIS TALLEMAN. I am a general warehouseman, living in Houndsditch
-I keep an account at the Unity Bank-this order for a cheque book is not
in my writing-I never signed this cheque, but it is very much like my
signatnre-I know the prisoner Barrett; he was in my employment for about
five or six weeks, about five or six months ago—he had frequent opportunities
of seeing my writing-he has transacted business for me at the Unity Bank;
taken cheques there, and received money.
RALPH PAYNE. I am in the employ of Mr. Goad, jeweller, in the
Borough-on 16th April I saw Barrett about 8 o'clock in the evening-he
looked at two rings which would come to about 31. 158., and asked me if I
could give him change for a 101. note—I told him I would see if he would
put his name at the back-he put a name at the back of the note- I said I
was afraid I should not be able to give him change for it-he said he was
going as far as the Elephant and Castle, and would call again—this is the
note--I read the name—it was "J. Williams, 18, City-road "-I know this
note by the name-he went out of the shop, leaving the note I kept it till
he returned, and then told him that I was afraid I should not be able to
change it for him-I asked him where he got it from-he said that he had
& friend who came into 3,0001.- I told him I was afraid I should not be able
to change it for him, and he said he would go and ask his friend if he could
lend him the money-hereturned, and said that he was not able to find him-I
told him if he left the note I should be able to change it for him in the
morning-he said that he should be going past the Bank of England himself
in the morning, and could get it cashed-he left me 108. as deposit on the
rings, and went away, taking the note with him-Mr. Lyons afterwards came
and brought the note—he took it away again with him I went and gave
information to the police- I went to Mr. Lyons' shop that evening and saw
Barrett there there was a policeman there, who asked him if he bad any more
notes, and he denied having any more-I said that I had seen him with
some more notes in a bag when he took the 101. note ont-I saw a woman in
Mr. Lyons' shop with him he had not given me a 201. note that evening to
change, nor had I given him a 101. note that evening.
