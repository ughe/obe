PAMILA SAMURLS. I ara a draper in White Alie Street-I buy job lots of
silk sometimes- have been in business twelve years-Mr. Harris shewed
me some samples of silk in the early part of June---I do not remember the
date--I looked at them - he said he had been asking ls, a yard for them
I said they were worth that I would not give 28, 2d. a yard for them--Mr.
Harris made no concealment about it, I have been in my present residence
ten years I have known Harris since he was a boy-he is a respectable
trdesman--I have brought job lots of silk of Pawsons, of Lewis, and Cooks,
and form my opinion from my general experience,
Cross-examined by Mr. M. WILLIAMS. Mine is a large business--this silk
is not worth more than 28. & yard, but am used to buying in job lots and
damaged goods, so I should not like to say-I should not call this perfect,
because of the way it is rolled up.
LAWRENCE SAMUEL. I live at 38, Mansell Street, and am a job draper
--my parents have been in business fifty years, and I have been in business
six years- I was at Mr. Harris' premises on 5th June—he asked me what I
thought some samples of silk were worth-I replied that it was difficult to
give a precise judgment abont that class of goods, because the qualities are
so various, and I did not want to buy them, but I believed they were worth
about 11d. a yard-I have been in the habit of attending large sales, and I
have been goods of a similar qnality fetch 10d., 11d., 18., and 18. 1d. per
yard-1 have known Harris all my life, I was in a situation with him many
years-- he has borne a high character amongst his neighbours for honesty
and integrity.
Cross-examined by Mr. M. WILLIAMS. The silk produced is worth abont
2. a yard-you may easily be deceived in silk--this other is perhaps worth
about 16 6d. a yard, but I doubt if you were to offer it for sale that you
would get more than 11d. a yard for it-there are three qualities of silk
here, but to an experience eye they might all be taken as one--any silk
that has been unfolded I should call irregular, and it would not fetch so
much.
Joan PEARTREE. I am a hat and cap manufacturer—the silk produced
is used for lining bats; there are three qualities here, one is worth about 2s.
a yard, and the others 18. 4d. or 18. 6d.
Cross-examined. The red silk would be worth about 28. 8d. a yard in its
present state being out of order.
JOSEPH SPIERS (re-examined). I have carried on business in the silk trade
30 years—these silks are not ordinary widths such as draper's goods, which
are 18 to 21 inches; these are 36 inches wide--the last witnesses have
valued them as drapers widths.
