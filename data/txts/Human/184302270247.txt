1097. JOHN WILLIAMSON was indicted for feloniously receiving, of a certain evil-disposed person, on the   23rd of Dec ., 1 bottle, value 2 d  .; and 1/2 oz, of oil lavender, 2 s  .; the goods of John Fuller ; well knowing them to have been stolen: against the Statute, &c.
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. JOHN FULLER. I am a chemist and druggist, and live in Crosby-row, Walworth—Weatherhead is in my service—he came the latter end of Oct., or the beginning of Nov.—I received information, and spoke to him on the 13th of Feb.—on the day after he made a communication to me, and in the after noon I went with the officer, to a place called Camberwell-gate, where we saw the prisoner with a stall, containing cocoa-nuts—I gave him in charge—he asked me what for—I told him my name was Fuller, and he knew my boy better than he knew me—I left him with the officer, and went to his house with the sergeant—I found there this bottle of oil of lavender—I can swear to this bottle as mine, and I had oil of lavender of this description—there was about a shilling's worth of oil of lavender in it—on going to the station I said to the prisoner, "Where have you been in the, habit of getting your essences?"—he said, in the Borough, near a street which I understood was Trinity-street—(he would want them to put into his sweetmeats; they use a great variety of essential oils in sweetmeats)—I asked if it was at Mayne's—he said he didnot know, but he thought it was—I said, "When did you get it there?"—he said in the autumn of the year, mentioning, I think, Oct.—I said, "Where have you got them lately?"—he said Mr. Fuller's boy had brought him some—I said, "That is what I wish to know"—he then said, "I have paid for them."
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Then you said, "That is what I want?" A.  Yes, and I was satisfied when I had obtained what I believed to be the truth—Weatherhead is now in Newgate—I fetched him out to go before the Grand Jury—I have not been to him in Newgate—I wished to see over Newgate, but they would not let me—when I went to the prisoner, he ap peared to have given up business for the time, on account of the snow or sleet—I have 4000 or 5000 bottles, but not one like this one—the lip of this has been cut off apparently by a diamond—I suppose it is worth 1 1/2 d .—before I gave Weatherhead into custody I said to him, "I am determined to have justice; if it puts you to inconvenience it is your own fault, but I will give you every recommendation in my power"—I went to get him before the Grand Jury as I thought it would forward justice, as he could explain the matter—this essence that I found at the prisoner's was in a jar in an up-stairs room—it appeared to be a place where he makes his sweetmeats—his wife was in the house, but not in that room. WILLIAM WEATHERHEAD. I am fourteen years old. I was in the employ of Mr. Fuller—I have known the prisoner for the last two years—I had known him before I went to Mr. Fuller—he sells sweetmeats about, and keeps a machine called a dolly—I saw him when I had been about a week at Mr. Fuller's, but he did not say anything to me then—I saw him again a little time afterwards—he then asked me if I saw such things lying about as essence of lemons or cloves, and oil of lavender—I said, "Yes"—he said if I got him such things as he wanted to use in his trade, he would put 2 s . or 3 s . in my way—I did not say anything to that—I know this bottle—I saw it in my master's cupboard—I put into it some oil of lavender, which I found in a small bottle on the shelf—I took it to the prisoner—I do not know on what day—I did not say anything to him, but I gave it him—he did not give me anything then, but on Christmas-day he gave me a sixpence, and on boxing-day
