Cross-examined by Sir EDWARD CLARKE. I had nothing to do with it
after the money was paid-a question was raised as to whether a Limited
Company should be substituted for Mr. Sarl, (A letter from Mr. Sarl to the
Commissioners was here put in authorising them to substitute for his name
that of Mr. George Newman, of Budge Row, Cannon Street)—the Com-
missioners asked for Mr. Newman's references, and they were given, and
on that Mr. Newman's name was put in as the substitute, and after that
another name was put in the negotiations lasted nearly a year between
his applying for the bond and it being settled, and ultimately a sale was
proposed as he was a very old man-I originally asked £30,000 for the
sale of the interest --while Mr. Sarl was in possession, a valuation was
obtained from Messrs. Virgo, Buckland, and Garrod, but not on Mr. Sarl's
behalf; but whether he negotiated with other people I cannot say—it
came to Mr. Newman and Mr. Wright through Mr. Steele's agency,
& commission agent; he had no interest in the matter; we paid him
on commission on our side, and I believe Steele told me that he was paid
£1,000 commission on the other side, by the purchasers.
By the Court. We pay him £1,000 to get as much as he can, and the
other side pay him £1,000 to get as little as he can--we paid him the
£1,000 out of the £16,000, it was quite immaterial to us whether we paid
the whole £16,000 into our bankers or drew a cheque for it.
By Mr. Mathews. That £16,000 received by Sarl was a clear profit
subject to survey; they let it at the ground-rent, and Sarl sold it at
£16,000 profit, subject to commission, and the clear profit was what
was left.

Cross-examined by Mr. WILLIS. Mr. Sarl considered it a very valuable
site-I got a valuation from Mr. Garrard; he valued the rent at 50 per
cent, more than was paid for it; Mr. Sarl's hope and calculation was to
make something like a quarter of a million if he carried it out himself;
there was about a million of money involved --he afterwards decided to
sell his interest ; he was a very old man and very sanguine, and I advised
him rather to sell it than try to work it.
Re-examined. He died at the commencement of this trial; he had been
ill for a long time.
