Mr. Martin called me to arrest the prisoner for stealing a bicyele-he 8aid

him sign his deposition-(Read) "I am the defendant. I live at 3,
Dallion Street, Kennington. Having been out of work for six weeks, and

low down I was. This young man asked me if I would like to earn a
shilling or two; I said yes. He said he wanted to borrow a bicyele and to
raise money of some friends at Harlesden, as he was in want himself. He
asked me if I knew where to borrow one. I knew of Mr. Martin, where I
used to hire bieyeles, and went to him and got a bicyele and took it to his
lodgings in Frith Street. He said he was going to Harlesden, and told me
to meet him at Chapel Street between six and seven, and he would give
me the bicyele and the money to pay for it and something for myself.
I went there, and stopped till seven o'clock and did not see him. I went
back to Lyle Street and met him. He said he must have missed me on
the road and had got the bicyele at home. I said I should like to take it
back at once. He said he wanted to keep it till Sunday afternoon to
enable him to see some more friends. I consented to that. I did not
see him on the Sunday. On the Monday I went and saw his landlord.
He told me the man had gone, bieyele and all. I failed to find him since.
I hired the bicyele for two hours. I had known the young man a year.
I knew him as Arthur. He said he was a commercial traveller. i did
not live with him."
Prisoner's defence The statement I gave at the police court is a
