Accordingly he came with Steers to the Amsterdam coffee-house, I took them into a room and asked Buckey, if this was the man that had the India bond? they both answered no, that the man was at the Fleece alehouse in Threadneedle-street. They had before offered to sell them for 5 l. under value, I offered them 90 l. a piece for them. There were then 2 mentioned. Steers said he would go and ask Stevens whether he would take that money; he went, and returned, and said, Stevens humm'd and haw'd about it, but believed he would take it. I told them, I could not then stay, but if they would go to the Fleece, I would come to them. I did accordingly, then there were the two prisoners and Buckey; I made an excuse and went to the officer in Change-alley, Mr. Sivers, he was not at home; I left word, that two of the bonds that had been advertised had been offered to me. I went to the Fleece. Steers was gone, there were Stevens and Buckey; then they went to the Four Swans in Bishopsgate-street, there they were all three together. I told them, I had brought the money and desired them to give me change out of a 200 l. note; Stevens then produced the bonds to me from out of his pocket-book; I compared them, and found they agreed with two of the three lost. I made an excuse out, and Buckey followed me. I said, had they not the other? he said, he believed they had not, but he would ask them. We went in again. I wanted to go out, but knowing they would not trust me alone. I desired to speak to Buckey. I talked to him about the danger of purchasing them; he said, I had better let them alone. I had not been there three minutes, but the officer and the constable went down the yard; I gave the constable charge of the prisoners. Stevens took out the bonds and laid them down on a bench; I took them up and delivered them to Mr. Sivers. Stevens, before Sir Charles Asgill, said, he had them from a stranger; at last he said, he had them from Steers; and Steers said, he had them from Stevens.
   John Buckey. I have known Steers seven or eight years; I have known Stevens but a trifle of time. Mr. Steers asked me, if I knew any body that would buy an India bond. (The rest as the former witness.)
 [Stevens in his defence said, he had the bonds from Mr. Steers; and Steers in his defence said, he could prove where he bought them.]
 For the Prisoners.
   Thomas Russell. I live in little Hermitage-street. I am a slop-seller. Mr. Steers and I went together to Gravesend on Sunday Morning, the 26th of Sept. We were there together 5 or 6 days; then he said, he had business in London, to pay his workmen.
   Joseph Watson. I live in Shadwell-market. I was in company with Steers about the middle of Nov. at his own house. He brought two India bonds; I saw him pay for them, but cannot tell how much; I heard him bargaining about 200 l. this was about 3 or 4 o'clock in the afternoon; I cannot be certain to the day; I looked at the bonds; but I am no judge of them.
   Nathan Lee. I live at the King's-head in Leaden-hall-street. I remember, he told me, there was a man had some India bonds to sell, and he was going to buy them. I saw him pay the money; it was in the kitchen at the King's-head; Mr. Watson was there; he bought them of one Solomon, a Jew; they told me since, he is absconded.
   James England. I live in Shoreditch. I have known Steers about 5 years; he is a man of substance; he employs 40 or 50 men in the summer time. I look upon him to be a very honest man; I would trust him with a thousand pounds worth of goods, was he at liberty now.
 The prisoners called several persons, who testified their characters to be very good.
