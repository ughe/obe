GEORGE FERGUSON. I saw the prisoner that Wednesday night, in a house, in Bunhill-row; he said he had a truck for sale, and asked where he could sell it - I declined to do anything with it; and next morning I saw him going down Holborn with a truck; he spoke to me - I returned in an hour and a half, and he then took the truck and went away.
 Prisoner. Q. What time were you in the Borough with me? A. I went to the Borough with him I think four weeks ago last Wednesday; he said it was on business for his master.
   JOHN LEADBITTER. I live near the prosecutor's shop. I saw the prisoner there on the 20th of August; he looked at the shop, went in and came out; went in again, and took the board of the truck into the shop - he then went and took the truck out of the yard.
 Prisoner. Q. Have you not seen me before? A. Yes - I have seen you in Gray's Inn-lane; I never saw you in the house, nor the shop - I have seen him in the yard, and in the Bull public-house: I have to mind the shop while the men go to dinner - it was at dinner time I saw him.
 Prisoner's Defence. In the deposition it was stated that I had hired the truck and was to pay 6s., and I did not return it; I had worked for him and hired the truck.
 COURT to the PROSECUTOR. Q. Have you seen the truck. A. No, and when I asked the prisoner for it, he d-d me, and said I might get it as I could.
 COURT to  THOMAS McGRATH. Q. Did the prisoner say anything about the truck? A. No - Mr. Spurgen said if he would bring it home he would forgive him the hire of it.
     GUILTY. Aged 48. Confined Three Months.
 1832.  MARY MOULD was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 6 lbs. weight of bacon, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Porter.
   CHARLOTTE PORTER. I am the wife of Thomas Porter, he is a chandler, and lives in Baldwin's-gardens . On the 14th of July I lost a hock of bacon, it was found in the bed-room in the workhouse.
   MARY ANN LEWIS. I live with my father in the prosecutor's house - I was standing at the door when the prisoner went out with a piece of bacon under her arm; I did not know but that she had bought it.
   CHARLES CARTER. I am porter of the gate at St. Andrew's work-house - the prisoner was nurse of one of the wards; she went out on the 14th of July; I let her in, and in a few minutes the prosecutor came and asked to speak to her; I sent for her and the master - they asked about taking the bacon, she strongly denied it; the master sent me to search the ward, and I found it in the bed-room under the bed - the prosecutor swore to it.
   WILLIAM KIRBY. I am a constable. I received the bacon and the prisoner at the work-house; I kept it as long as I could, and then gave it up.
   CHARLOTTE PORTER. It was my property.
 Prisoner's Defence. The gentlewoman is very wrong; she swore it had never been cut - I had half a peck of beans in Leather-lane, and a man offered me the bacon for half a crown.
 COURT to  WILLIAM KIRBY. Q. Did she say so at the office? A. No; she said she must be tipsy.
     GUILTY. Aged 57. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1833.  PETER MACGENNIS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , 1 pair of trousers, value 9s., the goods of Thomas Jones.
 THOMAS JONES. I am a  tailor, and live in St. John-street-road. On the 26th of July, about ten o'clock at night, the prisoner came in to purchase a waistcoat - I was measuring another person at the time, to alter a pair of trousers for him, they then lay on the counter; I sold the prisoner the waistcoat at a low price - and when he was gone, my wife told me he had something in his hat; I jumped over the counter, took hold of him, and asked what was in his hat; he said an old waistcoat; I took his hat off, and under an old waistcoat was my trousers; he dropped on his knees, and offered me five shillings to let him go.
   SARAH JONES. I am the wife of Thomas Jones. I saw the prisoner take the trousers from the counter and drop them at his feet; he then put them into his hat and something over them.
   WILLIAM CHAPMAN. I was coming past the house on the 26th of July, and saw the prosecutor with the prisoner by the collar - I took him to the watch-house, and found ten shillings on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went and bought the waistcoat; I know nothing about the trousers: I took my coat and waistcoat off; and laid them on the ground to try the waistcoat on.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Three Months.
 1834.  HENRY KEMP was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , 1 ham, value 8s., the goods of Isaiah Cox.
   WILLIAM GREVILLE. I am shopman to Mr. Isaiah Cox. On the 4th of August I was standing in his shop, which is a cheesemonger's; and saw a ham move, I was at that time at our third window - some persons told me there was a ham taken from the door; I went out, and saw the prisoner in the middle of the street with a bundle - I went up to him and he dropped the ham; I took it up and pursued - a person attempted to stop him, he fell down, and I collared him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM TRANTER. I am an officer. I took the prisoner and produce the ham; this is the handkerchief which was round it.
 Prisoner's Defence. It is the first time.
 COURT to  WILLIAM GEEVILLE. Q. Was the ham in the handkerchief? A. Not when he dropped it; I am sure he is the person I saw with the ham, but I do not think he stole it - he seemed as if he was urged by some person to make his exit; I saw him drop it in three or four minutes after; I saw it move in the shop.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Month.
