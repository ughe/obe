I am a carrier—at four o'clock on 24th March, I put two baskets of linen, from the Reform Club, into my cart, to take to my mistress, who is the wife of John Walton—I went into the Junior Club with another basket of linen, and, while there, somebody took away cart, horse and all—that was at a quarter-past four o'clock. MARY FRENCH. I am housekeeper to the Reform Club—these articles are their property—I counted the articles myself, and put them into the basket—I saw Callaghan take them away.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I saw them publicly exposed in Petticoat-lane, and bought them to turn a penny. NOT GUILTY.
 997. EDWARD MOORE and CAROLINE BICKERS , stealing 2 capes, 1 shawl, and 1 victorine, value 3 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Helen Mary Stocker : 1 shirt, 1 waistcoat, and other articles, value 16 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Thomas Bliss Stocker: to which BICKERS  pleaded GUILTY.
 Aged 20.—  Recommended to mercy.— Confined Six Months . THOMAS BLISS STOCKER. I live at Kentish-town; Bickers was my ap prentice. On 24th Jan. I went to Church, leaving her at home—I came back about eight o'clock in the evening, and she was not there—I missed the articles stated—I had had the scarf safe within a week, and had spoken to Bickers about it—I said, "How is it that there is no one but you in the house, and this is the second scarf that is missing"—she said she knew nothing about it, where could she take it to—on 29th March I went with a policeman to 9, Tottenham-place, and found her lodging—there; and in a box, which I knew to be hers, I found these things ( produced )—she acknowledged it—Moore was not there. HELEN MARY STOCKER. I am the daughter of the last witness—this dress is my mother's, this cardinal and victorine are mine, and this shirt my father's—these blinds were safe in a drawer in my bed-room a day or two before Bickers left—the dress she has on is stolen. WILLIAM RANSLEY( policeman, S  61). I went with Mr. Stocker to 9, Tottenham-place, and saw Bickers—I found these things, some in her boxes, and some in Moore's—I went again about a quarter-past four o'clock, and found Moore in the back kitchen—he said, "I suppose you are the man who has taken my wife away"—I said I had taken a female away, and must trouble him to go with me to the station—he said, "I will go ; did you take anything out of that box?"—I said, "No"—he said, "Did you take anything out of that one?"—I said, "Yes"—he said, "That is my box"—going to the station I said I thought it was very foolish for him to have a young girl like her there—he said she was his wife, and then that she was not—he said he assisted in bringing the boxes away, but he did not know that any of the contents were stolen.
