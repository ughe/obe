1808. WILLIAM SHIPGOOD, THOMAS FITCH, and WILLIAM
LEE, stealing 2 pecks of beans, chaff, and barley, value 8d. ; the goods of
Thomas Wagstaff and another, the masters of Fitch.
THOMAS WAGSTAFF. I am a farmer, in partnership with my brother, at
West Ham. Fitch was in our employ-I sent him to London, in charge of
a wagon, on 5th July-I think my horses bave lately been worse than they
ought to be-I bave since seen a mixture of split beans and green barley, not
ripe--it was a mixture which was given to Fitch to feed the horses-he wa
to feed them where he loaded the dung-he had a nosebag for each borse
the nosebags all hold about a bushel.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. Where is the White Swan!
About three miles from our place-Fitch was to go from West Ham to
Spitalfields, wbich is about four miles and a half--I know the other two
prisoners,
WILLIAM SMITH (policeman, K 220). On 5th July, about ten o'clock in
the morning, I saw Fitch driving Mr. Wagstaff's wagon-another man was
driving another wagon-they came to the White Swan-the other two pri-
soners then came, and had a pot of beer with Fitch and the other carma-
Fitch then went to the door of the house, and Shipgood went and emptied the
contents of a posebag into an apron-Lee took the apron to the front of the
door and shot it into a vessel, where there was other horses' food-Le is
ostler there--I was in plain clothes- I think they did not see me-I was by
the side of a shop door, not more than fifteen or twenty yards from them -
put back about the quantity of the mixture that Lee put into the vessel.
WILLIAM DAVIDSON DAY (policeman, K 74). I was with Smith
the three prisoners drinking a pot of beer-I then saw Shipgood take a nose-
bag from the wagon, and empty it into Lee's lap, and Lee went and emptied
it into the vessel-Fitch stood in the doorway.
Cross-eramined. Q. Was he watching it intently? 4. Yes I was in
plain clothes--they could not see me if they had looked-I should say the
nose bag was half full when Shipgood emptied it into Lee's lap.
SHIPGOOD-Guilty. Aged 46.-Confined Three Months.
FITCH and LEE-GUILTY.-Confined Six Months.
1809. JOHN HAYNES, stealing 2 iron screw-clips, value 28.; the goods
of Alfred Rodwell; and JOHN WILLIAMS, feloniously receiving the same;
to which HAYNES pleaded Guilty. Aged 15.- Confined One Week.
JOHN HAYNES (the prisoner). I went in Mr. Rodwell's yard on Twicken
ham-common-I saw the clips, and picked them up-I heard Williams ery
rags and bones, and I sold them to him for twopence- afterwards saw
him and the sergeant, who asked Williams what he had got in his cart-the
clips were afterwards produced-these are them.
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Were these in a bag with rags? 4.
Yes-Williams put them in the scale with the bag of rags-he did not take
them out he would not see them unless he opened the bag said to him,
"I have got two or three bones, and some rags"-I did not say anything
about the iron.
