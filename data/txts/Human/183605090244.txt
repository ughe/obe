   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Did you mention to Mr. Harding's partner about these expressions? A.  Yes; those used on Saturday morning.
   COURT . Q.  Was you with your husband when he talked to the prisoner in the morning, do you remember the people coming and saying Mrs. Haggerstone was sick? A.  Yes; and after that that she was drowned—when the prisoner heard that he cried, and said he could not leave his children and baby, and my husband said, "Oh, I suppose you have done it, that is the reason you will not go." THOMAS WALLACK. On the night of the 16th of April, I was acting as watchman at Merton. I was in the White Hart about twenty minutes before twelve o'clock—I saw Mrs. Haggerstone about sixty yards from the White Hart, as near as I can tell, between that and Saunders's beer-shop, going towards Saunders's, which is in what is called the Rush—William Claridge was with her—I know his person—he was dressed in a short white round fast—they were stopping in the road, talking together—I could not hear what they said—they were eight or nine yards from me—they were alone—they continued in my sight two or three minutes—Mrs. Hedges then came out of the White Hart, and called Mrs. Haggerstone several times—Mrs. Haggerstone did not make any answer, but still stood there with Claridge, for two or three minutes, till Mrs. Hedges and her husband came up, and then they all four walked down the road together—Mr. and Mrs. Hedges, Mrs. Haggerstone, and Claridge. ELIZABETH HEDGES re-examined. Q.  Who went with you when you found Mrs. Haggerstone, after calling her, after coming out of the White Hart? A.  We went down as far as the Rush together, but I never saw Claridge, I will be on my oath.
