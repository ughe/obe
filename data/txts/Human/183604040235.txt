Barking are in different directions—you turn to the right to go to East Ham and to the left to go to Barking. GEORGE HOLLOWAY. I am a constable of East Ham. Aldridge brought me a warrant against Bradshaw, Morris, and Monk, on Saturday night, the 19th of March—I apprehended Monk and Bradshaw the same night—I told them it was for a highway robbery on a man named Aldridge—they said nothing, except that did not know any thing about it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know where the prisoner's father lived? A.  Yes, I believe the prisoner lodged with him—I have known him over since he was a child—I have seen him frequently, for three weeks or a month before, up and down the place—I do not know that he ever went away.
   Prisoner's Defence . When I left him at the Duke's Head, I went home, and never saw him till I saw him at the Cock. SOPHIA MONK. I am the ironers's mother—the day before he was taken up, I was at the Cock public-house, about four o'clock in the after noon, as near as I can recollect—my husband came after I got there—he is very, ill indeed at present—Joseph Duckel was there—the prosecutor, Bradshaw and Morris, and my son, were there—the prosecutor did not charge my son or Bradshaw with robbing him—he said Morris had robbed him. and my son and Bradshaw were present—he offered to settle it for 1 l .—it was not given him—he then offered to settle it for 10 s ., and them for 5 s ., but it was not given him—I said I would make it up with him if they were guilty, but they all said they were not—neither me nor my husband said if my son was guilty we would pay him whatever he had lost—my son and all of them said he was not guilty, and would not let my husband or me pay him any money—I had not offered to pay him money—my husband said if they were guilty he would pay it, and my son told the prosecutor, he would not allow his father to pay a farthing, as he was not guilty—Duckel was present when he said that—my son lodged with as—he was at work as usual for three weeks before the meeting at the Cock, and sleeping at home at night, so that the constable might have found him.
   COURT . Q.  Did the prosecutor say, if you did not give him any thing he would have him taken up? A.  Yes—my son went home to our house that night—he was taken up next night—he had staid at home all day. JOSEPH DUCKEL. I was at the Cock, with Mr. and Mrs. Monk, the Prisoner . and the rest, on the night of the 18th of March—I remember the prosecutor's talking of his being robbed—he asked a sovereign at first to make it up—it was not given him—the party said they were not guilty—he then asked 10 s ., and was refused, and then 5 s .—Monk's father said, before he would have him taken up he would pay whatever he liked to charge—the prisoner said if he had his pocket full of gold he would not give him a farthing, for he was innocent of the charge—we all drank—the prosecutor paid for it—there were seven of us—I saw him pay 4 s . 6 d ., and he paid something before.
   COURT Q.  What are you? A.  A market-gardener—I am no relation of the prisoner's—i and Monk were walking along the street that night, and met the prosecutor—he asked him if his name was Monk—he said, "Yes," and then we went with him to the Cock, and I went to find out his friend Morris, to get them all there together—the prosecutor asked me if that was the young man I was looking after—we sat there drinking till, I suppose, nine o'clock. NOT GUILTY.
