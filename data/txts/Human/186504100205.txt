COURT . Q.  Had she been provoked in any way before this? A.  No; Mr. Hutton only told her not to use bad language, and if she did he would put her out of the room—she said he could not, he went towards her to put her out and she struck the blow. FRANK HUGHES. My father and mother are dead—previous to this I had been living in the streets—I was in Herbert's-buildings on this day, inside the gate—I saw this woman strike the old man with a boot last, which she had in her hand by a piece of string—I am now living in Si George's-house my uncle took me there.
   Prisoner. Q.  Were you not on the other side of the way? A.  No—I was waiting for Mrs. Hutton's son Johnny to come home—I was playing buttons.
   COURT . Q.  This occurred in the room did it not? A.  Yes, where she struck him; I could see into the room, the door was open. MARY HUTTON. I am the wife of the deceased—I was out when this occurred—when I came back I found him bleeding very much from the temple, he sat all the evening with his hand up to his head and continued so every day for a week after—he sot delirious and loft his senses—I fetched Mr. Wakeham's assistant nearly three weeks afterwards—Mr. Wakeham is dead, a young man saw him—my husband was thirty-eight years of age and in quite good health, he had been at hard work all day.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went and asked Mr. Hutton for the last and he said "There's one, I don't know where the other is, the children bare taken it out and lost it"—I said, "It is very kind not to return things that are lent, "and I sat myself down on the bench, and said, "I shan't go without it, "before I could say anything else he caught me round the waist and chucked  me out on the stones—I gave myself a twist out of his arms and gave him a push. GUILTY.— Confined Six Months . The prisoner was also charged on the Coroner's Inquisition with the manslaughter of the said William Hutton and acquitted.
