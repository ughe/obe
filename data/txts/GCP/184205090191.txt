1657. RICHARD HITCHCOCK was indicted for a misdemeanor.
C MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. Die W
Henry Hinton. I am out of business at present, and live at Stock, in
Essex. In the early part of 1839 I carried on business in Queen-street,
Cheapside, in partnership with Mr. Taylor, and resided in the country-
I became acquainted with the prisoner in May, 1839-I rendered him some
service at that time-in August, 1839, the prisoner and his wife called on me
at the warehouse in Queen-street-his wife said in his presence that her
husband had a valuable property case for a person named Wall, at Whet.
stone, and from the great kindness I had rendered him in his distress, it was
his determination I should receive a portion of the property when recovered
- his wife said that, and he rejoined by stating that I had been a great
friend to him, that this property would amount to a very large sum, and it
was his intention that I should have a share of it, but at the same time they
stated their circumstances would not allow them to carry on the suit unless
furnished with money for the purpose, and also for their support during the
time the Chancery suit was in hand-bis wife said this, and he himself stated
so afterwards-shortly after, in October, 1839, he brought me a copy of a
will- this is it-(looking at it)—be said he brought it for my satisfaction,
to show me that there was such a Chancery suit in existence, and to satisfy
me as far as was in his power—he said he could show me that there was
such a property to which this Wall was entitled, and he would introduce me
to Wall, and also to other members of the family-he did introduce me to
Joshua Wall, I think in September, 1839, at the Anchor in Newport-
street--that was previous to his bringing me the copy of the will on the
24th of January, 1840, I gave him a cheque for 151., which I producer
previous to my giving him that cheque he said that he was desirous of
relieving Wall's family until the business was settled-I asked him what
he proposed to allow Wall and his family until the business was settled —
he said he thought 158. a week would be sufficient-I told him to make
the most he could of the money that I advanced him, but I should have
no objection to his advancing from 158. to 208. a week to Wall until the
business was settled on the 22nd of February he came to me again at
Queen-street, and stated that he was going to the Court of Chancery, and
it was bis intention to make a present to some of the clerks—that he found
the clerks in the Court more ready to attend to the business he had in hand
if he made them presents-no one was present but our two selves when he
stated that he got from me a cheque for 51. on that occasion he said he
was going with Mr.Taylor to the Court of Chancery--that he (Mr. Taylor) was
the acting man in the business at the Court of Chancery-this cheque is dated
the 22nd of February-on the 2nd of March, he came to me again, and
said he was going to the Court of Chancery with Mr. Taylor, that they
should have to discharge some heavy fees at the Court, and on that occa-
sion he obtained this cheque for 201.--he was in my warehouse two or
three times a week on the average, all the year round, and was constantly
pestering me for money-I drew 80 much from the firm that it almost
embarrassed us, and caused a dissolution of partnership between me and
my partner-about the beginning of December, 1840, as near as I can re-
member, the prisoner went to town-before he went, he expressed his
