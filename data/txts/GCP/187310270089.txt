Cross-examined. I was not called before the Magistrate-I was ill at the
time--I had a severe cold-I was not desired to go I was doing my bar-
man's work-I heard Parker say “ By God, Charley, he has got a knife!"
I told my master I heard that, but he did not take me as a witness--there
was a lamp about ten yards off—I saw Grant distinctly; he took no part in
the scuffle with the prisoner and myself-he was on the opposite side of the
road-I did not see Grant struck on the head by anybody I did not see
any stones or sticks thrown.
Re-examined. I am positive the blow struck at Parker was struck by
the prisoner immediately after he rose up.
ELLIS JONES NORRIS. I am a surgeon, residing in the Victoria Dock
Road on 13th October, about midnight, my assistant was called to Mr.
Morsley's, but I saw Parker about 12 o'clock on the 14th, and have attended
him since he was very faint from loss of blood, and he had a wound on
the left side, over the region of the heart; it was superficially small, but an
inch and a half deep--we dressed it, and it got on uncommonly well, and
he is now, I may say, recovered the wound appeared to be the result of a
stab, such as might be done by a knife like this--it slanted inwards, down-
wards, and upwards the point of the instrument impinged on the rib-
bone, and saved his life.
Cross-examined. I would not say that it could not have been caused by
falling against the knife.
WALTER WILLIAM MORSLEY. I am the son of the landlord of this
tavern—when I got down by the gate I saw my father talking to the men,
and he said to me “Pick up Toby," that is the dog-I put him on the
top of the fence and he fell over, and ran away-I got over, and Morrison
came towards me and struck me under the chin, and knocked me down-
I went up because I saw the prisoner and Parker in a fighting attitude-
when I got up I saw the prisoner on the top of the barman, who was on
his back-Parker went and pulled the prisoner up, and I heard Parker
halloa out he was stabbed--the prisoner ran away, with Grant-they ran
about twelve yards, and then parted - I saw Grant caught I went out
afterwards to see whether we could find the knife, and found a cap-I
afterwards went on board the Wild Deer, and saw the prisoner in his
berth--that was about 12.30, an hour afterwards---I saw one spot of blood
on the pillow where the prisoner's head was-the constable told him two
or three times to get up, and he pretended to be asleep he told him
he wanted him for stabbing the waiter of the Tidal Basin Tavern, just
outside the Dooks-he said "I will own I was there, but I was looking
for the Dock gates "--the chief mate persuaded him to get up and go quietly,
and he got up and came to the station.
Cross-examined. I ascertained he was third officer of his ship-I saw
that Grant's forehead was cut when he was taken into custody-it had done
bleeding then, it was dry blood—I did not see any stones or sticks thrown
- I don't know how Grant got the cut on his head.
ALBERT YOUL (Policeman K 158). I found this knife about 6.30 the
next morning, where the scuffle took place—the place was pointed out to
me-it was about 6 yards from a lamp-I gave it to Sergeant Leman.
Cross-examined. The knife was shut.
