Third Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
842. HENRY SMITH was indicted for feloniously forging, on the 26th
of February, a request for the delivery of 1 packet of tenpenny nails, with
intent to defraud Charles Houghton, against the Statute.--2nd Count, for
uttering a like forged order, well knowing it to be forged, with a like in-
tent.
CHARLES WOODWARD. I am an apprentice to Charles Houghton, an
ironmonger, in Farringdon-street. On the 26th of February the prisoner
Mlled at the shop I had known him before in the employ of Mr. Brees-
(he had left our account from Mr. Brees soon after Christmas) - Mr. Brees is
a customer of ours—the prisoner brought an order, signed "Brees," on the
26th of February, and said he wanted the nails contained in the order-I
read the order, and gave him seven thousand tenpenny nails, believing the
order to be genuine, and that it came from Mr. Brees-he went out and
got a porter while we were getting the nails ready-he said he was going
for a porter- I have been in the trade about three years the prisoner con-
ducted himself as all men do that come for orders—there was no appear-
ance of strangeness about him—this is the order he brought. bara
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. When did he deliver your account
from Mr. Brees? A. The latter end of January—I did not see him for
more than five minutes then-he had called during that week for orders as
well — he was with me ten minutes or a quarter of an hour, when he
brought the order-I was looking out the nails it took me at least ten mi-
nutes--he said nothing to me before he gave the order to me, further than
that he wanted the nails--I had only seen him four times.
CHARLES HOUGHTON. I keep the shop. I was present when the pri-
soner brought this order-Mr. Brees is a customer of mine the prisoner
did not exhibit any extraordinary conduct-nothing different from other
people.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you in the shop at the time? 4. Yes; I
was engaged ordering goods from a gentleman-I had some conversation
with the prisoner for about ten minutes.
Joux BREES. I am an iron plate-worker, and live in West Smithfield.
I have known the prisoner two or three years—he was in my service for a
Week only-he left me the latter end of January-he was not in my service
at this time—this order was not written by me--I never authorized it to
be written-I know nothing about it.
Cross-examined. Q. He was only a week with you 2 A. No; he
came to me about the 20th of January-I sent him with the prosecutor's
account-he was well during the week he was with me I cannot give the
exact date that he left me I do not think it was in February-I know he
was ill the week after, as Mr. Hutchinson, of Farringdon-street, attended
him-I believe he sent for him himself-I had no employ for him; he only
came to me till he could hear of a situation is connexions are very
respectable.
