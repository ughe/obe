GEORGE WILLIAM Moody. I am assistant to Mr. Corke. On the 7th of
July I saw a parcel sent away-I thought there was something wrong, and I
directly called Mr. Corke's attention to it-I asked if he thought he had got
all honest people about him--I saw the prisoner give the parcel to Cheeseman
-I had seen her come into the shop, and take this parcel of satinett from the
shop into the work-room-I went to the Parcel Delivery Company, and
found a parcel-I opened it, and called Mr. Cork's attention to it.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you remember having a conversation with Mr.
Corke about an oath that he took at Weymouth? 4. I have heard him
speak of an oath he had taken at Weymouth, that he would prosecute
to the utmost everybody that was robbing him-he said something of the
sort, that that was his reason why he prosecuted the prisoner-I never had
any quarrel with the prisoner-she never, to my knowledge, charged me with
beating the apprentices when I was drunk-she might have charged me with
being drunk- I have beaten Sharpe she might have spoken about that-I
cannot remember it.
GEORGE JOHNSON (police-constable P 142.) Mr. Corke called me in on
the 7th of July, to take charge of the prisoner.
MR. CLARKSON called
Miss BAKER. I am in Mr. Corke's employ - I have had articles from the
shop against Mr. Corke's rule-I have omitted to enter them for a day-never
more than that-I receive a salary-I have not perquisites on everything that
is sold I have on remnants of ribbon-I have never omitted to enter any-

prisoner take things, and had them entered one day afterwards--we who are
in the shop have the privilege of taking anything we want, if it is entered-I
have taken them-I have seen the prisoner take things, and she has told me
to enter them-I saw her do that on the morning she was taken into custody
-I bave seen her sister-I heard she was a milliner-I have heard the pri-

1785. LOUISA HALL was again indicted for stealing 8 yards of crape,
value 53. ; 2 yards of silk, 98.; 2 pairs of boots, 88. ; 8 pairs of shoes, 103,
20 yards of quilling, 1s. ; 6 yards of lace, 18. ; 2 pairs of gloves, 1s; and
30 yards of ribbon, 1s.; the goods of Matthew Corke, ber master, 4 by
GEORGE JOHNSON (police-constable P 142.) I was called into the pro-
secutor's house - I went up stairs with him and the prisoner these articles
were found in the prisoner's box, in her bed-room-while Mr. Corke was
looking over another box, I saw the prisoner take this parcel and put it into
ber pocket-she took these boots and shoes and placed them under the foot
of the bed-after Mr. Corke bad done looking at other boxes, I called his
attention to the prisoner's pocket, and then to the foot of the bed.
MATTHEW Corks. The prisoner was in my service these articles are my
property-I can swear these boots, and shoes, and crape, and silk, and-blond
quillings have not been entered.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. How do you know these boots
and shoes? A. They bear my private mark--they are new-there are shop-
tickets on these other things I never missed any of these things-I never
put any question to the prisoner till I had been told by Moody that he had
seen the parcel go out-the prisoner was a person of great trust.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Guilty. Aged 21.---Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury.
