1212. EDWIN SMITH was again indicted for stealing 9 bagatelle-balle,
value 30s.; the goods of Robert Graham.
ROBERT GRAHAM. I keep the Rose and Crown beer-house, in William.
place, Walworth-road. On 2nd Feb. I had nine bagatelle-balls, and I lost
them between a quarter past nine and ten o'clock, as near as possible--I saw
the prisoner that night at my house-he came up-stairs into the room, and
requested to play bagatelle-I did not know him before-I am quite sure he
is the person--he came with a person named White, who has been convicted
-I knew White before-they played up-stairs, in the club-room--there was
no other person there--I saw them leave both together, and I had my suspi-
cions– I sent my wife up-stairs the next moment-she called out, and I went
out after the men-I ran them down a street, and they escaped into the
gardens--they ran, and got away-Wbite was afterwards taken-I am quite
sure the prisoner was one of the two.
Prisoner. Q. You say two persons came into your house? A. Yes, there
were more persons down-stairs, but only two in the room up-stairs, you and
your companion- I gave information to the police, and gave a description of
the two parties- I went to Horsemonger-lane to prosecute White-I know
Rawlings-my testimony and my wife's did not convict him and White-
that was for another offence--Rawlings was not at my house on 2nd Feb.--
I do not know your wife-she might have called at my house-since you
have been taken on this case, no one has called to ask me to give them infor-
mation respecting White and Rawlings-your father called on me one night
-I did not swear that White and Rawlings were in my house--you were
dressed in a check coat.
SOPHIA GRAHAM. I am the wife of Robert Graham. On 2nd Feb., about
a quarter or half-past nine o'clock at night, two persons came into our house
-I am quite sure the prisoner is one of them--they asked if any one was
up-stairs—there was no one but my husband--they went up, and asked my
husband for a pint of beer and two pipes--my husband left them in the room,
playing at the bagatelle board, which he gave them-I suppose they had
asked for it-I had seen it before, and it was quite safe-I afterwards saw the
two men come down-stairs--my husband was not in the room some part of
the time-when the men left he was in the bar-when they came down the
other man paid for what they had had the prisoner waited for him, and they
went out together-after they had left, my husband told me to run up-stairs-
I went up, and the balls were gone-I am quite sure the prisoner is the man.
Prisoner. Q. Were you a witness against White ? A. Yes ; Rawlings
was tried with him-Rawlings was sentenced to four months, and White to
twelve months-I and my husband were not the only witnesses-we did not
swear that those two men were the only men in our room that night-we did
not swear to Rawlings-White was the person that came in with you-Raw.
lings was not convicted on our case, but on Mr. Gardener's case-White and
Rawlings were convicted for Mr. Gardener's case.
JAMES GRANT (police-sergeant, P 11). I took the prisoner on 22nd of
April- I told him he was charged with being concerned with another in'steal.
ing bagatelle balls from the Rose and Crown beer-shop, on 2nd Feb.-- he said
he did not know where the house was, and never was in it.
