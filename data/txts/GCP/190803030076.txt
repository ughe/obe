Cross-examined. Prisoner has never shaved in my presence. I
am a journeyman fishmonger and have been for six years. My son
has been deaf since he was seven. He went to school, but did not
get on well. I think he was 14 when he left. I believe he was in the
fifth standard. He was always a good son and was only too willing
to do work if he could get it. He was very fond of his wife, only the
mother-in-law prevented him from being so. I have not been friendly
with his mother-in-law for about 12 months. I am sure it was about
10 days before Christmas inat my son came back to our place-two
Saturdays before Christmas. He stayed at our place until two or
three days after Christmas. On January 20 he was not in regula
work, only doing irregular jobs. Four shillings would be a day's work
for him. He did carter's work. His hours were from half-past five
in the morning until about six or eight at night. On the Monday be
went out at half-past five to search for labour, and came home about
six. After his tes he had to go out again to see to the horses. I
cannot tell you if he came back after that. He may have been in
two or three times before this occurrence. When he rushed in at the
door he seemed funny. I got hold of him and tied his neck up, and
said, "Oh, Wal.!” Then the constable came in, and he was laid on
the floor and began talking about the man Kebble, saying that bis
wife was in drink. I knew my son was making arrangements to go
and live with his wife, and he was looking forward to it, but she de
clined to live with him.
