   Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN Q.  Are you a member of the College of Surgeons? A.  No—I am a surgeon of the Navy—I attended the prisoner in both her confinements—the last was between two and three month ago—her two children are living—she is in my debt for my attendance on both occasions—she has not paid me the expenses of her first or her last confinement—she always paid me the fee, but not the bill for medicine, on either occasion—they have been sent in—before she was confined she said, "I will call some day and pay your bill"—I never asked her for it—I had sent in the bill, and she excused herself on one occasion for not paying the last—I took the contents of the stomach home, and subjected them to the usual tests—I reduced the arsenic into the metallic form, by the black flux—I have no doubt at all the stomach contained arsenic—I also tried it by Marks' apparatus—I was assisted in making the analysis by my assistant Wevell—he is not here—he was before the Coroner, and ex amined—he serves customers who come to our shop—the crust of pudding which was given me, was not what was rejected by the deceased's stomach on Wednesday—I had no means or opportunity of examining that—one of the earliest symptoms following taking of arsenic is violent sickness, and it is frequently cast up, if it is a largish quantity, with scarcely any injury—I think arsenic was taken on the Wednesday, and rejected from the stomach—I should say it might be incorporated in the fruit of the pudding, and not in the crust; or it might be taken independently, and food being taken at the same time, would be thrown up, by the action of the poison on the stomach—castor oil is a very powerful counter-irritant—it is no antidote—it would certainly be likely to lessen the acrimony of the poison, and carry it off the stomach, by its purgative qualities—it is impossible to say at what time the arsenic might have been taken—I should say twelve hours is the extent—I do not remember stating, shortly after this matter occurred, that it was im possible to tell that it might not have been taken, some days—I am sure I did not say, "There is nothing in its not having acted immediately, in a hot climate; in the East Indies a man might hare arsenic in his stomach six days"—I said nothing of the sort—I never mentioned the East Indies—the prisoner did not, in the conversation I had with her on the Satur day, tell me that she got the rhubarb from my assistant—at the time of the Coroner's inquest she said that he had served her—I do not know what day that was—I was not in Court at the time.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  How much did they owe you for medicine? A.  Not more than 2 l . or 3 l .—I should not have objected to go if I had been sent for—if the quantity of poison I found in the stomach had been admi nistered on the Wednesday, it would have occasioned death earlier than the Saturday.
