ELIZABETH REEVE. I keep a chandler's shop in Twisters-alley, Bun-
hill-row. The prisoner came for a penny candle, which I gave her, and
she gave me a sixpence which was very much battered-I thought it wa
worn-she said she was sure it was a good one-it remained in a drawer,
where I kept it till about nine o'clock-I then took it out to get some
beer, and offered it to the publican's son-his mother said it was nothing
but pewter-I saw the prisoner again on the following Sunday, the 26th-
she asked for a halfpenny candle, and gave me another sixpence-
husband was there- I said, “Look at that sixpence, she is the same girl
that gave me the other sixpence"-she said she had not been in the shop
before-1 sent for a policeman--one sixpence my husband had, and the
other I gave the policeman.
Prisoner. I did not know the first was bad.

with the candle-she gave a sixpence-my wife called me, and asked
was good-I said it was bad- I kept it in my hand till I had the policeman
called, and gave it to him.
