   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  You were here yesterday? A.  Yes; in Loder's case—I got some iron from him—I gave its worth for it—he was convicted—I had to give up more than six hundred weight—it was bought honestly—I know I bought this of the prisoner, as it was the day before I was locked up—there is no mark by which I know it. WILLIAM ACTON. I am superintendent of the London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway Company's police. Charles Pascoe Grenfell is the chairman—the prisoner was a plate-layer in their employ, at the West Station, Croydon—I took him, and said it was a charge of stealing the Company's property; that some iron pipe had been found at Steven's house, which he said he had bought of him the evening before—he said he was very sorry; he did not think he doing any harm; he picked it up on the line—it is worth 1 s . 6 d .—it was worth 2 s . 6 d . or 3 s . before it was broken up—the Company do not sell their pipe, or break it up—the prisoner had no right to break it up, or to sell things found on the line.
   Cross-examined. Q  Did not he say he found it in the lane ? A.  No; on the line—I found about 18cwt. of stolen iron at Stevens's. CHARLES BONNER. I am deputy superintendent of the locomotive depart ment. This piece of iron pipe belongs to the Company—the believe it was recently broken when I first saw it—we do not break up iron or sell it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is done with pipe accidentally broken? A.  It is put on one side, and I suppose would be sold, but we never had a sale before yesterday. JOSEPH BUCKMASTER( policeman, R  39.) On 22nd Feb.
