   Cross-examined by MR. COOPER . Q.  You have known the prisoner almost all your life? A.  No—I have seen him three times in the last eighteen months—I knew him at the Bank of Glasgow—I did not see him often there; I knew him to speak to, and since he has been in town he has called on me three times—he did not tell me that morning that he had an appoint ment with the member of Parliament for Cavan—he came to call on me, and said that he hoped to have a situation very shortly, I think he said in China, through the member for Cavan—he did not say that he hoped to get a charac ter from me—I was up a flight of about thirty stairs—after he was committed by the Magistrate, I returned to the Magistrate's room, but I did not say that I did not think he intended to steal it—I might have said something to miti gate the punishment—I had been detained in London three weeks longer than I should otherwise have been, and said that it was a pity that he was com mitted, there being nothing found against him—with regard to the pledge tickets found on him, I made a request to Sir Henry Muggeridge that his deci sion as to his committal to be tried here might be mitigated—I have no idea how long he was at the Bank of Glasgow—he bears a very high character.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did he ask you to lend him the pencil case, or any money? A.  He had previously borrowed some few shillings of me, which he has not paid—I did not authorise him to take this pencil case—he was running in St. Paul's Churchyard, and when I went up to him, he said, "I did not intend to steal your pencil case." FRANCIS STANWAY. ( pity policeman , 311). On Saturday afternoon, 26th April, I saw the prisoner running in St. Paul's Churchyard—I stopped him, and found this gold pencil case in his hand—I searched him at the station, and found on him twenty-one duplicates, twelve of which relate to coats, a 3 d . piece, and a hand vice—I ascertained that he lived at a coffee house in Back Church-lane, Whitechapel.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not he say that he did not intend to steal the pencil case? A.  Yes, and that he was hurrying off to see a gentleman in; New Cannon-street—I do not know whether the member for Cavan lives in New Cannon street—I could see the end of the pencil case in his hand.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  How was he running? A.  Very fast, there was a policeman and several persons following him.
   COURT . to JAMES KAY MACKAY . Q.  When you turned to write, the pencil case was in his hand? A.  Yes.
   MR. COOPER . Q.  Have you a timepiece in your room? A.  No, nor down stairs—I did not cry "Stop thief!"
