Q. Had Solomon before that, said any thing to you against Mrs. Abra-
hams, or Mr. Moses, her father? A. No- I saw nothing of his sons till
the day they were brought up and examined, when the gold was given up
--Mrs. Abrahams was afterwards made a prisoner, and Solomon admitted
as a witness for the crown-a man named Rees was also taken into custody
in consequence of something she said-he is brother-in-law to Solomon-
Moss, his wife, and sister were also taken into custody.
Q. How soon did Moss begin to give information, and try to be made
a witness? A. I should say about the 10th of April-there were some
suspicions about it for two or three, or three or four days I was not in
any private room, when Moss was examined before Mr. Hardwick, the
counsel and attorneys--he was taken out of the office into a room in front
of the house, but what was going on there I do not know-I cannot say
who was there I was in the office.
Q. Well, who disappeared from the office ? A. Mr. Hardwick left the
office-I do not recollect whether the counsel and attorneys left-Mrs.
Abrahams was not there-that occurred only once-I should say they were
absent from the office about an hour or an hour and a half-Mrs. Abra-
hams was then committed as a prisoner-tbe servant, Jane Bradley, has
been in my care ever since I placed her at the Flying Horse in Lambeth-
street-it is a very respectable house--she has been acting as servant there
-she has been there ever since I have seen her there many times I
have not conversed with her—it is about ten weeks ago since she was ex.
amined at the police-office-she has taken means to refresh her memory as
to what she said that was not by my advice-she went to Mr. Hum.
phreys to have her statement read-she requested to go-I did not put it
into her head at all-she said it was so long ago she would like to have it
read over.
Cross-examined by MR. JONES. Q. Was Lewin Caspar the first person
from whom you received information of the robbery? A. Yes, he came to
the office about three o'clock as near as I can recollect-I went with him
immediately to the wharf-I am sure I went to the wharf first-it was
not to the office in John-street-I do not think we called at the office in
our way to the wharf-I am quite sure we did not-I recollect we went
straight from our office to the wharf, and John-street would be out of our way
a good deal-we went along Swan-street, through the Minories and Tower-
billwe did not go down John-street, George-street, and across America-
square-I have not the slightest recollection of calling at the office-I
would not swear we did not-I know Mr. Blundell-I saw him at the of
fice that morning-Lewin Caspar gave me a description of the man who had
taken the boxes away, at the office in John-street, and he also gave some
description when he came to the police-office-be appeared to be very much
agitated, and put himself in a variety of attitudes--I could hardly make
him out at all-he was knocking his head about-he appeared very much
hurt about it-I do not know whether it was put on or not-he appeared
very different that evening and the next day-he expressed great anxiety
that I should go with him immediately, and render every possible assistance
-I did not see Mr. Blundell at the wharf before I went to the office
should say I went to the wharf before I saw Mr. Blundell-I will not
swear I did not see him before I went to the wharf.
