Detective THOMAS Evans. On May 15, 1907, I arrested prisoner
at 45, Warwick Street, at 1.45 p.m. I told him I was a police
officer, and should take him into custody for embezzling money.
He was charged with embezzling 19. 5fd. and 11s. 6fd. on May 29,
and with embezzling 9s. 6ļd. on May 22. When charged he made

RHODA SMITH. From September, 1906, to March, 1907, I was
cook to Mrs. Cresswell, 31, Eccleston Square. Prisoner used to call
for orders from Broadbent, greengrocer, and sometimes deliver the
goods, which were entered in a book left at the house and made up
weekly, the money being paid to the prisoner usually on Tuesday.
He would take the book away and bring it back receipted. On
March 13 I paid the prisoner 11s. 64d. I left the service on that
day, so did not see the book again.
Cross-examined. I never paid Mrs. Broaldbant at the shop; I
always paid prisoner; he always asked me to pay him. The book
was receipted sometimes by his initials and sometimes by an india-
rubber stamy.
Apa LAURIN. I entered the service of Mrs. Cresswell, 31, Eccles-
ton Square, on March 13, on the last witness leaving. Prisoner
called for orders from Broadbent, with whom we dealt for green-
grocery. Book (produced) is Mr. Cresswell's book. On May 8 I
paid prisoner 9s. 64d. I gave the prisoner a sovereign and he
brought back the change. There is no receipt or stamp in the book
for that amount. I have not seen the book again until now. I once
paid Mrs. Broadbent in the shop when she stamped the book with &
blue stamp. On May 14 I saw Mrs. Broadbent in the shop and had
3 conversation with her. She then called the prisoner from the back
of the shop and told him that I had called about some goods that
had not been delivered. She asked me for the book. I said, "You
have the book.” She told prisoner that. Prisoner said, “Yes, I
know." She said, "I sent for it yesterday," and that prisoner bad
said Mrs. Cresswell had a jumble sale on. That was not true. I
said that the bill had been paid, and she asked prisoner for the book.
He brought the ledger. She said, "I do not want that; I want Mrs.
Cresswell's book," and prisoner then brought this book. She said,
"It has been paid.". Prisoner said, “Yes, I know." She said,
"I have not had the money."
Cross-examined. I have only once paid Mrs. Broadbent in the
shop--I sometimes paid prisoner at the shop when he has initialled
it. (To the Jury.) I do not know what he did with the money. I
have never seen him take it upstairs.
Re-examined. The 9s. 64d. was paid at Mrs. Cresswell's house on
May 8. On no occasion when I paid prisoner was the book stamped
with an indiarubber stamp.
