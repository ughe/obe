JOHNSON, Mayor.
Charles Francis. I am clerk to the London Conveyance Company-
Murphey was in their employ-I knew Butler merely by his coming to the
Company's yard for dung-on the 19th of May I gave Murphey three
sacks of oats, beans, and chaff- this is one of the sacks--it is not the Con
pany's sack, but was in their care-Murphey had no right to part with
any of the provender.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Whose cart had Butler! 4.
His master's, Mr. Way, and his horses too.
Robert BLORE. I have several partners, we form the London Con
veyance Company-they send omnibuses about town.
Cross-examined. Q. Is there any deed of partnership? A. I do na
know-they are directors, and their names are in the license- they shan
the profits.
BUTLER-GUILTY. Aged 30.-Confined Three Months.
1595. JAMES FLATTERY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th d
April, 120 glass bottles, value 24s., the goods of John Rigg; and 1 be-
ket, value 78., the goods of William Bousfield.
WILLIAM BOUSFIELD. I am agent for the sale of Guinness's stout-1
served Mr. Rigg in Brompton-row-George Osmont is in my service-i
is his duty to deliver goods, and call for the empty bottles--on the 30th d
April I sent him to Mr. Rigg's for some bottles-when he came back there
were ten dozen missing-this basket (looking at it) is my property, but 1
cannot identify the bottles.
GEORGE OSMONT. I was sent to call for these bottles at Brompton
row-the prisoner who was a stranger, introduced himself to me in going
along the Strand-I bad a truck and he offered to assist me, and did so,
when I got to Brompton-row I untied the basket-he took it in- I told the
servant I would call for the bottles when I returned when I called
heard some one had been for the bottles—this is the basket I left for them
--it is my master's-I did not authorise bim to go and get the bottles.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is this all that passed between
you and the prisoner? A. Yes, I was in two public-houses drinking with
him-I treated him-I paid 4d. at one place, and 2d. at another--he asked
if I could give him a drop of beer, and I did it to recompense him for assiste
ing me, I was not able to draw the truck myself, I did not desire him to
do any thing with these empty bottles--I said if I had time I would meet
him at another public-house opposite Brompton-row-a friend of the
prisoner's joined us, but he soon left us.
HARRIET PIKE. I was servant at Brompton-row-Osmont called and
said he should call for some empty bottles-the prisoner was with him-
he heard what Osmont said he left a basket and went away with the pri-
soner--the prisoner called before Osmont did, and said he had called for
the bottles-he put them into the basket and went away with them
thought he had a right to take them, seeing him with Osmont.
WILLIAM WOLFE BONNEY. I am a wine merchant, and buy empty
bottles. On the 30th of April, the prisoner brought me nine dozen and
nine-he brought them in this basket.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you known him at all? A. I had seen him
before and bought bottles of him half-a-dozen times I did not know
where he lived.
