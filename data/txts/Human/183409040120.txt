 1449.  MARY BLEWIT was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of September , 1 shawl, value 10s., the goods of James Aldous.
   JOSEPH NOTLEY. I am in the employ of James Aldous, who is a pawnbroker in Berwick-street. On the 22nd of September the prisoner came to the shop - I knew her before, and watched her - I saw her go up to a shawl, and unpin it - she went to a partition in the shop, returned, and took another pin out - she returned a third time, and took the shawl down and retired again - and the fourth time, she went and took a pin out of another shawl - two gentlemen came in to purchase a gun, and then she went out - I pursued, and overtook her six yards off, and found the shawl on her.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you sure you saw all this? A. Yes; she has pawned at our house to the amount of 3l. or 4l., I should think.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I found the shawl on the ground when I went in - I have been in the habit of going there for nine years - he has got a great deal of my property in his house.
     GUILTY. Aged 53. - Confined Three Months.
 1450.  HENRY HART MORDECAI was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 30 shillings, the monies of Thomas McGillivray.
 THOMAS McGILLIVRAY. I am a  surgeon in the East India Company's service- the prisoner was in my employ for some time, to go out with a variety of curiosities which I brought from India from time to time, to dispose of them - I paid him for his trouble - I gave him a gun to dispose of - he returned and said he only got 4l. 10s. for it, and he had got 6l. for it - he was to sell it for me, and bring back whatever he got for it - I told him that I had discovered he had sold it in St. James's-street, instead of Piccadilly as he had told me, and for 6l. instead of 4l. 10s. - he said he sold it first for 4l. 10s. in Piccadilly, and went and purchased it again, and sold it for 6l. - the money was never in my possession.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1451.  DANIEL HARDING was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , 2 pictures, value 40s., the goods of James Black.
   WILLIAM REYNOLDS. I am a constable. I was going down Rodney-street, and saw the prisoner and another, about four o'clock in the afternoon, near the prosecutor's house - on my getting near them they moved - I went up Henry-street and watched them - they returned, and sat near the same spot again, and in about ten minutes the prisoner went up the prosecutor's gateway, he was in the fore-court a few minutes, and then returned to the other - there was some music playing in the street - they went to it - I saw the one not in custody, get in at the window - the prisoner stood close to the window, and lifted it up while the other got in, and delivered these two pictures out to the prisoner, who ran towards the road - the other man ran in a contrary direction - I ran and secured the prisoner with the property - I asked where he got it - he said he found it.
 JAMES BLACK. These are my pictures, and were in my room.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming along the road - a lad asked me to carry the pictures to Charles-street.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Six Months.
 1452.  MARGARET BENSON and ELLEN BENSON were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , at St. Mary-le-bone , 1 topaz cross, value 30s.; 1 turquoise cross, value 1l.; 10 rings, value 10s.; 1 pair of brooches, value 5s.; 3 brooches, value 20s.; 1 buckle, value 10s.; 2 lockets, value 5s.; 3 pair of ear-rings, value 16s.; 3 gold drops, value 4s.; 1 watch key, value 3s.; 1 miniature, value 2s.; 7 pieces of lace, value 30s.; 1 ivory fan, value 6s.; 4 cups, value 4s.; 2 plates, value 2s.; 8 yards of flannel, value 4s.; 4 pieces of linen, value 8s.; 2 pieces of muslin, value 7s.; 4 plumes of feathers, value 10s.; and 12 remnants of linen, value 3s.; the goods of William Archer , in the dwelling-house of John Wyatt.
   CAROLINE ARCHER. I am the wife of William Archer. I lodge in the house of John Wyatt, in Edgware-road, in the parish of St. Mary-le-bone. The prisoners used to clean my room, but they had no regular occupation - I admitted them to my room from kindness - I am not in any business - they were out of situations - while they were there, I missed all this property from the back room second floor - some was in boxes, some in drawers - on the 29th of June, during their absence, I discovered some boxes opened, and on their return, I named several articles that were taken, but I had not examined the boxes from the time I came there, which was the 29th of April - (Ellen helped me to move) - they were in my room during my absence - the property was found in their apartment - Ellen went to a situation, I understand, on the Tuesday preceding - they had left my place without notice - when I came home on Sunday, the 29th of June, Margaret was gone - I had been out to dinner, and left her there, and came back.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Did not you desire Ellen to seek for another place? A. She was seeking for a situation all the time she was with me - I have a sister named Mrs. Hance, whom I lived with before I came to where I am now - she did not come backwards and forwards to my lodging, to my knowledge, nor any of her family - I left my sister because she left her house - I never charged my sister with any thing in particular - I may have missed thing during the time I was there - I do not think I charged her with robbing me - I swear to the best of my knowledge I did not - I lost things - I did not charge her with breaking open my boxes - Hance was not at my apartments, to my knowledge - the apartments were in the care of the prisoners.
