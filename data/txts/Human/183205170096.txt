 1311.  JAMES HARRISON was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 2 pillow-cases, value 3s., the goods of John Welford.
 FRANCIS SHIRLEY. I am a pawnbroker. I have two pillow-cases, which were pawned, on the 14th of April, by the prisoner.
   JOHN WALKER. I am an upholsterer, and live in Newman-street- the prisoner was in my employ as a French polisher. I had some property of Mr. Welford's to take care of; it was in drawers in my bed-room, which the prisoner had access to.
   JOHN WELFORD. These pillow-cases are mine; I had left them in Mr. Walker's care,
 Prisoner's Defence. There were a great many more people going in and out the place; I know nothing of this property - I bought the duplicate.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 19.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1312.  GEORGE HOWARD was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 1 pair of boots, value 7s., and 1 pair of shoes, value 5s., the goods of James Pope.
 JAMES POPE. I am a  farmer, and live at Finchley; the prisoner had worked for me in the hay time. These boots and shoes were taken from a room adjoining the house where they clean them, and where the man sleeps.
   SAMUEL AADY. I am a labourer. The prisoner came to my house on the 12th of February, about twelve or one o'clock, and asked me to lend him a knife and a brush to clean these shoes - I lent them to him; he cleaned them, and put the boots on - he sat a little while, and then offered the shoes to me for sale; I had not money enough, but I gave him half a crown and an old shirt - he then went away with the boots on; when I heard that such things were lost I made inquiry, and gave them up - these are them.
 WILLIAM MORRIS. I am a constable. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I worked for Mr. Pope a long while - he never knew such a thing of me.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1313.  JOSEPH KING was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of April , 1 pair of trousers, value 12s., the goods of James Leach.
   CHARLES BURNS. I live with Mr. Fuller. Mr. James Leach's shop is opposite to us; on the 9th of April, I saw the prisoner standing by the lamp-post, and my mistress told me to watch him - he went towards the square; another young man snatched a pair of trousers from Mr. Leach's door, gave them to the prisoner, and they both went away; I gave the alarm - the prisoner was taken.
   HENRY ROBERTS. I am in the employ of Mr. James Leach, a pawnbroker. Burns gave me information - I pursued the prisoner, and think I saw him throw away these trousers; I took them up - they are my master's.
   JOHN GEORGE BRAUND. I saw a mob, and went up; a gentleman had hold of the prisoner, and said he had stolen a pair of trousers.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1314.  JOHN LAWRENCE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 1 yard of floor-cloth, value 4s., the goods of Samuel Ames ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
 MARY BRANBURY. I live near Mr.  Samuel Ames' shop, at Islington. On the 1st of April I saw the prisoner shake a piece of floor-cloth; he shook it a second time, then put it down by his side, covered it with some cloth, and took it away from the stone coping.
 RICHARD REYNOLDS. I work for Mr. Ames. Branbury's husband informed me of this; I pursued the prisoner about one hundred yards; he had this floor-cloth, and was walking off - he gave it to me, and I took him.
   WILLIAM HOLT(Police-constable N 17). I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JAMES CHAMBERS. I am a Police-constable. I produce a certificate of the conviction of the prisoner, which I got at Mr. Clark's office; I was a witness, and know he is the man.
 Prisoner. I was in distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1315.  CATHERINE LATCH was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of April , 1 watch, value 30s., the goods of William Knight.
   WILLIAM KNIGHT. I lost my watch at Mr. Harley's, a publican; I think it was about the middle of January -I went to his house about that time, pulled off my coat, hung it across a chair, and sat there half an hour; I took my coat, came out, and put it on; I missed my watch from the coat pocket the next morning; I had put it there to prevent its being scratched with some money which I had in my pocket - I do not know that I saw the prisoner, but she lived servant there.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. You had your watch in your waistcoat pocket before? A. I had it there then, and took it out and put it into my coat pocket, but I had another watch in my fob - it was not a club night; I went to see Mr. Harley - it might have fallen out.
