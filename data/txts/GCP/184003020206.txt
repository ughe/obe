Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
1003. ELIZABETH BURN was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of
February, I basket, value 6d.; and 9lbs. weight of beef, value 4s. 6d.; the
goods of George Cole, from the person of Elizabeth Susannah Cole : and
RICHARD BURN and JANE BURN, for feloniously receiving the same,
well knowing them to have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.
GEORGE COLE. I live in Tower-street, Waterloo-road. My daughter,
Elizabeth Susannah, is eight years and six months old-on Sunday, the
23rd of February, between eleven and twelve o'clock in the forenoon, I
went with her to Mr. Budge's, a butcher's shop, in the New-cut, Lambeth
- I bought a piece of beef, which weighed five pounds all but two ounces,
and a piece of salt beef, which weighed four pounds four ounces--the two
pieces of meat were put into a basket which I had with me-I gave the
basket to the little girl to take the meat home, and I gave her 4d. to buy
some turnip-tops and potatoes— afterwards went to a potato-shop, and
found her crying-the basket and meat were gone-she stated how she
had lost it-in consequence of what she said I went with the policeman to
No. 89, Carlisle-lane-1 found the three prisoners there-it was then about
a quarter past twelve o'clock-I found my fresh beef before the fire roast-
ing—I said, “This is my beef"—the man said, " It is not yours, I bought
it in the Cut last night" - I said, "Where is the salt beef?”-I was then
looking into the cupboard, and the policeman said to Jane Burn, "What
are you standing there for ?"-he pulled her aside, and found the salt beef
in a tub, with some turnip-tops, which I have no doubt were mine-I
found my basket on the top shelf in the cupboard, close to the ceiling
this is it-I can swear to it-it has a leather strap, which I put on it, be-
cause the handle was broken off-the meat was taken to Union Hall-
took it home afterwards, and ate it.
ELIZABETH SUSANNAH COLE. I am eight years old. I went with my
father to the New Cut, where he bought a piece of fresh beef and a piece of
salt beef-he paid for them--he gave the meat to me in a basket to carry it
home, and 4d. to buy some turnip-tops and potatoes- I bought the turnip-
tops, and put them into the basket-as I went along I met the prisoner
Elizabeth Burn-she asked whether the basket was not heavy-I said
yes--she asked if she should take one handle-I said no, I could carry it;
and then she took one handle out of my hand-she asked where I was
going--I said, to the potato-shop-she pulled, and I pulled, and she got the
basket from me- I went to the potato-shop, and she said she would wait
for me at the corner of Robert-street, and when I came out she was gone
with the basket and the meat-I saw her up a turning, and called to her
- I do not know whether she heard me or not, but she did not stop-
ran up one street and another till I lost her—this is the basket - I am
quite sure Elizabeth Burn is the girl that took it.
