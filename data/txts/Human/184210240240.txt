Cross-examined by MR. HORRY Q.  When you saw them go along the lane you could not see the turkeys? A.  They were going the same way that I had seen the turkeys go along the lane—the men went two hundred yards down the lane before they were out of my sight—they then parted, two turned, and the prisoner went further down the lane—I saw the prisoner join the other two again as they were going gently along the path.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Was there time before he joined them again to go where the turkeys were? A.  Yes. MARY GOULD. I am the wife of William Gould. I had some turkeys safe, I counted them on the evening of Wednesday, the 28th of September—I missed one about one o'clock on Thursday—I went into Norwood-lane, and found a quantity of feathers lying there—they had the same ap pearance as the feathers of my bird—I afterwards saw the head, neck, and legs—I am quite sure they belonged to my bird.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were not your turkeys right on Thursday after noon? A.  No, I had only twenty-eight then—I never said they were safe then—the head I saw I believe was the head of my turkey—I believe these to be the feathers of my turkey. THOMAS ROBERTS( police-constable S  131.) In consequence of informa tion I went to the prisoner's house, at Roxworth, near Harrow, several miles from where the turkey was stolen, on the 29th of September, and found the head, legs, and neck of a turkey—I went into a closet adjoining the bed-room, and found a quantity of feathers in a basket, and some en trails, and a quantity of congealed blood, in a dust-hole—the feathers were partly burnt—these are some of the feathers from Norwood-lane, and they correspond with the others.
