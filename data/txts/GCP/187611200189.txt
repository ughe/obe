JOSEPH HAMMOND. I am & labourer at Hunston, I lodged with Mr.
Upsdell at a beer-house-on Thursday, 26th October, I was working in Mr.
Burchell's garden on the right of Pearson's—you cannot get from Upsdell's
to Pearson's without going round the road or breaking through the hedge
Mr. Burchells garden where, I was working, is about 15 yards from Pearson's
garden--I herrd talking between Pearson and Upsdell in Pearson's garden ;
I could not hear what they said for several minutes, it was very loud talking
-I heard Upsdell say "You have done for me now " -- those were all the
words I heard--the voice seemed to come from the upper end of Pearson's
garden, the end nearer Upsdell's garden than Burchell's-T went on with
my work, and about ten minutes after I saw Mrs. Upedellin consequence
of what she said to me I went round to Upsdell's garden and found him at
the upper end of his own gardens towards the straw yard he said "Help
me get my calf in, see what Pearson has done for me he had his bead
tied up with a handkerchief, and he was covered with blood blood was
running from him then I saw him walk indoors-soon after that I went for
Dr. Bucklo- I did not see anything of Pearson.
Cross-examined. They used to have words sometimes and make it up
again in about five minutes I was about 15 yards from Pearson's garden
when I heard the words, and Pearson's garden is about 35 yards long. I was
about 45 yards or so from where I heard the voices, and it did not strike
me as anything the matter.
By the Court. I thought it was a joke I did not know what to think
when I saw him bleeding, it frightened me.
ELLEN UPSDELL I am the widow of the deceased on 26th October, 1
saw him and Pearson go away_about half-way down my garden, Pearson
turned round and said "Your calf is in my garden," my husband followed,
and passed him about half-way down the garden, I don't know what passed
between them afterwards, my husband came back in about fifteen or twenty
minutes bloeding very much from the head and making a dreadful noise,
groaning very much he knew what he was about I set him down in the
chair and washed his head he said “Go for Hammond and get the calf out
of the garden;" I did so, and Hammond got the calf out of the garden when
.. came back my husband was in the garden with a towel on the top of his
head, I asked him to come in, I got him in and laid him on the sofa, and
ne bled very much indeed and screamed, and said Pearson had hit him
could soarcely hold him, he seemed racked with pain I asked Hammond
to go in for Mr. Buckle immediately, and Mr. Buokle came within about half
an hour afterwards- when my husband went out that afternoon he had a cap

when I got him on the sofa he took it off his head and put it on the table,

When he came back it was torn like this he died on the Wednesday follow-
