HENRY SMITH. On the 26th of Sept., I went to my mother's house—I live there—she showed me a paper parcel, containing lump and moist sugar, and tea in a separate paper—I took it with Mrs. Pemberton to Mr. Handley, in the same state as my mother delivered it to me.
   JURY . Q.  How came you to take it to Mr. Handley? A.  In conse quence of tricks  I have heard the prisoner used to make I looked at the sugar, and saw there was a difference in it—the tricks  were against me. JAMES HANDLEY. I received the parcel from Smith—the larger por tion of it was moist sugar—there was oxalic acid in it, I am positive from what I observed, from the taste, the appearance, and its effervescing—it had no smell—it is very difficult to say how much there was, without the whole was analysed—I should say there was an ounce, or two—a less quantity than that would kill a person—it depends on the peculiar con stitution of the person—I believe as little as two drachms has been known to kill—that is a quarter of an ounce. SARAH WIGGINS. I have heard the prisoner say, after mentioning to me, that she would poison the man if she could, that she would poison the wretch of an old woman if she could, for it was through her ill treat ment that she was ill treated in the way she was—this was about six weeks ago.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you ever said this to anybody before? A.  I mentioned it to Mr. Smith—I was examined before the Magistrate—I did not say anything about the mother then.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Whom did you mention it to? A.  To Mr. Smith, and Mrs. Smith heard me. MARY MCNALLY. I heard the prisoner say she would poison Smith if she could—I understood that to mean my brother. JAMES HANDLEY re-examined.  I do not profess to analyse—I sent the parcel to Mr. Simpson by Smith, who brought it back to me four or five days afterwards, saying the gentleman declined analysing it, as he could not pay him for it. HENRY SMITH re-examined.  I took the stuff from Mr. Handley to Mr. Simpson, and brought it from him to the police-station the same day—I did not leave it with Mr. Simpson at all—I gave him a portion of it to test—I brought the rest away at the same time, and took it to the police-station—it never passed from my sight—I never received a present of that kind, or anything from the prisoner. THOMAS HICKMAN( policeman ). I had this from Smith—I took it to the station, and left it there in custody of the inspector, who locked it up—he is not here—it is usual to keep all property there—it was in the place where property is usually kept—I took it from there afterwards to the police-court—there is a little room at the station for the express purpose—I did not see it put in there, but left in the charge-room—I did not see it taken from there—I received it from the inspector to take to the office—he received it from me, and gave it me back—after the prisoner was re manded I took it back to the station-house, and lost sight of it in the same way—I received it again to take at the second examination—I then took it back to the station the third time, and brought it from there to take before the Grand Jury—I then took it back to the station, and brought it here myself—I had left it in charge of the inspector in the charge-room—we are bound to take everything to the inspector—and when we want them we apply to him, and he gives them to us.
