Benjamin Bullock and Moses Samuel (vide Seventh Sessions, 1825, page 540). The prisoners were convicted of a lur-
ceny--the indictmeot charged it to have been committed in the parish of St. Botolph, Aldgate--but the parish was
proved to be St. Botolph, without Aldgate. Their Lordships' vere unanimously or opinion, that, as in case of larceny, the
proof of the parish was uunecessary, and as the prisoners had not proved that there was no such parish, the couriction was
legal.
John Lowe and Samuel Roberts, (vide Eighth Sessions, 1825, page 662). The prisoners were charged with breaking and
. entering the dwelling-house of John Young, in the day time, (the said John Young, and others being therein), and steal-
ing yoods, value 198.it was proved in evidence, that John Young was in the dwelling-house, but no other person, their
Lordships' were of opinion, that Young being in the dwelling-house, was sufficient to constitute the offence, it bring ouly
required by the statute that a persou be therein, and as such the prisoners were rightly convicted.
Thomes Winter Preston and Henry Reading, (vide Eighth Sessions, page 688) were called to the bar, and informed
by Mr. Justice Park, that he had respited the judgment in their case, upon precisely the same point as that decided in the
case of Love and Roberts, the decision of which was equally binding in theirs. Lowe and Roberts, Preston and Reading,
have accordingly received sentence of deutli. Bullock and Samuel, to be imprisoned for the terin of one yeur.
