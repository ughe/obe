He said, they were in the neighbourhood; he himself had not the possession of them; that he had only possession of them a few days past. He had pawned them. He had five pounds lent upon them by a man of the name of Byerley.
 Q. Did he say at what time he had lodged them with Byerley - A. I do not recollect. I think he said he had got them that morning. He was ordered by the commissioner to fetch them. Mr. Mayhew (Mr. Price's partner) accompanied him. He came back with the books. The books are here.
 Q. Now, sir, in consequence of any suspicion you entertained, had you, before that, gone to the house of Mr. Bond, in Fleet-lane - A. I do not think I saw him until the third meeting. I am not certain.
 Q. I wish you would recollect - A. On the first private meeting, the 26th of February, Mr. Hemmings and I went to Bond's, in Fleet-lane. I am not satisfied as to the day. It was before the third meeting.
 Q. Did you go for the purpose of making enquiries respecting Bond and his debt - A. It happened that me and my brother assignee went together to Bond's, in Fleet-lane, to make enquiry respecting the debt, and to enquire how it arose.
 Q. Upon that occasion did you see a woman in the room whom you now know by the name of the Baroness Minkwitz - A. I did. At that time I did not know who she was. Nothing at that time past with her.
 Q. How soon after you had seen her at Bond's did she come to you and make a discovery - A. About two days after, she came to me and made some communication. I was alone.
 Q. Now, take into your hands the books of the bankrupt, and tell me whether you find certain names I am going to ask you about debitted for goods. Do you find Count Holmar debitted for any goods - A. Yes, I do. In folio 141, debitted three hundred and eleven pounds twelve shillings.
 Q. Do you find the Baroness Minkwitz debitted for any goods - A. I find her debitted for one hundred and forty-one pounds sixteen shillings.
 Q. On what articles is she debitted - A. Pearls, two gold watches. The whole debt is one hundred and forty one pounds sixteen shillings, and they give her a credit of eighty-two pounds, leaving a balance of fifty-nine pounds.
 Q. Now, sir, do you find Signora Moranda - A find her debitted for sixty pounds, and credited at - , leaving a balance of forty-nine pounds.  John Shehey, I find, to him, two hundred and twenty-six pounds; his account balanced by bills. William Halliday, three hundred and seventy-seven pounds debitted; his account balanced by bills. Edward James, two hundred and forty pounds seventeen shillings and eight pence. Edward James, no credit whatever.
 Q. Had you known by Baroness Minkwitz, before you were possessed of these books, that there were these debts - A. I was in possession of the books two or three days before, but I had sent them to my brother assignee.
 Q. Had she made any communication to you respecting them names - A. Yes, each of the names, excepting Halliday and Shehey she mentioned.
 Q. Now, sir, after you examined the books, did you make any enquiry of the bankrupt,  John Folkard, who these several persons were - A. I did. I made the enquiry at my house repeatedly when he came.
 Q. Who did he tell you Countess Holmar was - A. He represented her being a woman he had long been acquainted with; a very good woman; that he had been induced to give her credit. I asked him when he first became acquainted with her. He said, she was introduced to him by the Baroness Minkwitz, who was also a creditor.
 Q. Did you ask him what the Count was - A. Yes; he gave me his address, Sloane-street; that she and her husband had removed from there; he did not know what had become of them; he believed they were gone out of the country. He was gone to take possession of an estate, or some property abroad. It was a very safe debt.
 Q. Did you enquire who the Baroness Minkwitz was - A. He said, he had known her for a length of time, but did not know what had become of her. Her debt appears to have been the day before the commission. On the 10th of January appears her debt.
 Q. Did you enquire who she was - A. She was an Opera dancer, or singer; she belonged to the Opera-house; he did not know where she now lived, but when he credited her she lived at No. 14, in the Haymarket. I applied at No. 14, in the Haymarket; they knew no such person. I asked him who  John Shehey was. He represented him having unfortunately, since he so credited him, gone into the Fleet.
   Q. William Halliday : did he tell you who he was - A. I had some knowledge of him myself; I questioned him on that account. He said, the first conversation he had with Halliday, he thought he was doing well; unfortunately he had gone into the Fleet a few days after he had credited him. Edward James; he said he knew nothing at all of him; he was recommended to him by the Baroness Menkwitz, and the only place he ever saw him was at the Black Lion, Wapping, independant of his own house.
 Q. Do you remember one day, when you were walking with  John Folkard, he being accosted by any person - A. Yes; that was the Baroness Minkwitz. They conversed together for a few minutes. I was with him. They conversed apart from me. She quitted him. I asked him who she was. He told me that she was a Miss Smith, an old friend and customer of his. I knew at that time who she was. At that time she had communicated to me, and I had communicated to my attorney.
 Q. In answer to a question that my learned friend put to you, that the bankrupt was examined by the commissioners privately - A. Yes, he was.
 Q. to Mr. Price. You have told us that you became solicitor to the commission after the second meeting - A. I did.
