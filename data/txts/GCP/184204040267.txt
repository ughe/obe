JOSEPH TUCKFIELD. I am foreman of the Deptford sawyers. I was
going from the City over London-bridge, on the 6th of April, between
seven and eight o'clock in the evening-there were a good many people
assembled there, looking at some exhibition in the street - I was making
my way through the crowd, and four or five men came round me the
prisoner, who was one of them, put his hand round me, and at the
same moment I felt my watch go from my pocket-I instantly seized the
prisoner by the collar, and told him he had stolen my watch--he said,
"Let me go, I have not ;" and three or four men said, "Let him go."
and tried to extricate him from me I held him as well as I could he
struggled very hard to get from me, I succeeded in getting him into a
beer-shop-Price came to the beer-shop, and took the prisoner to the
station-I have not seen my watch and chain again- I cannot say exactly
the value of it-it cost me 5l. two or three years ago.c o
Cross-examined by MR. PAILLIPS. Q. About how many persons were
there altogether? A. About fifty--it was rather duskish--there were
plenty of lights I had never seen the prisoner before in my life he
struggled and broke from my grasp, but I never lost sight of him for a
moment-as he got from me I caught him again-le was searched in the
beer-shop, and nothing of mine found on bim.
