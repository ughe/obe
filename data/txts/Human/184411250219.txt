Before Mr. Common Sergeant .
 184. ALFRED PRATT was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of Oct., 1 bag, value 1 d  .; and 5 sovereigns; the property of Samuel Groves , from his person. SAMUEL GROVES On the 22nd of Oct., I had five sovereigns in my bag, in my  right-hand pocket, at twelve o'clock in the day—it was a life. annuity I had received—I was then in a lane near the Two Brewers, at Clap. ham—I got to that public-house just before twelve—I staid there some time, and then went to a beer-shop—I came out about nine in the even ing; and when I got half-way home, in Manor-lane, the prisoner came rustling up against me, and put his hand up my thigh—he put his hand in my pocket, which had no button, and thrust me down—he got my bag—I saw him with it—he put the money into his pocket—he shook the bag in my face, and wanted me to take it—I took it, and found there were only two sovereigns out of five—I said I would be one with him, 1 would have him taken up—I gave the bag up to the policeman, and the two sovereigns—I can swear I had five sovereigns in that bag when he came up to me—I had not taken them out, or changed one of them—I had twelve or fourteen in my pocket beside.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  What may you be? A.  A bricklayer's labourer—I know the prisoner—we had been drinking toge ther at the Two Brewers—I had received the five sovereigns on the 14th—I never saw them after twelve o'clock on the 22nd—the first public-house I went into was the Two Brewers, and the prisoner met me there, as I was going in—we staid there an hour, or it might be longer—we had two or three pots, perhaps—we then went to a beer-shop, about three o'clock, and remained till nine—we were there six hours—I paid for seven pots of ale, but several more came in—one had a glass, and another had a glass—we did not drink it ourselves—we smoked our pipes—I left first, and the prisoner came running after me—I could walk as well as 1 can now—the prisoner did not lead me—I did not want leading—I did not rest my arm on his—I never asked him to see me home, and say, after some time, "Let me try if I can't go alone," or put my hands into my pocket, and then reel down—he thrust me down—we only had one glass of gin apiece at the Two Brewers—I said, before the Magistrate, that the prisoner shook the bag in my face, and said, "Here is your money, Sam"—when I had fallen down, the prisoner endeavoured to help me up—after this occurred, the prisoner walked away from me—after I said he had robbed me, we walked about twenty yards together—Mrs. Edwards, the witness, knows me per fectly well—I went home—I did not call a policeman—I could not find one—this was in Manor-lane, not 100 yards from the beer-shop, about 400 yards from Nelson-row, where I live—I slept that night where I lodge, which is at a beer-shop. SARAH EDWARDS. I am the wife of William Edwards, a coal-dealer, in Little Manor-lane—at half-past nine o'clock in the evening, on the 22nd of October, I heard a person fall under my window—I looked out and saw Groves on the ground, and the prisoner's hand in his pocket—Groves said, "You have robbed me of my money"—the prisoner said he had not—I think the prisoner staid with him five or ten minutes, and then he dragged him across the road—I did not see any more of him.
