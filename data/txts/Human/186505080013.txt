ESTHER WISEMAN. My husband is a tobacconist—I am a sister of the last witness—on 24th of April the prisoner came for half an ounce of to bacco, and gave me half a crown—I saw it was bad directly—he asked me to give it back to him, but I said, "I shall keep this"—he turned to go—I followed him, he took me by the arm at the door, and said, "Will you give me the half-brown?"—I said, "No"—I followed him to Mr. Girton's shop, where he said, "I am only looking for my partner"—I said to Mr. Girton, "This villain has passed three bad half-crowns in my shop in a fort night" (I had taken three)—he ran away—Mr. Girton ran after him, and I saw him in custody ten minutes afterwards—I gave the half-crown to the constable—I found a bad half-crown in the till on 17th April, which I gave to my sister.
   Prisoner.  I plead guilty to that. PHILIP BRADLEY( Policeman, H  59). I saw the prisoner running, followed by Mr. Girton—I stopped him and took him to the station, but found no money on him—I received this half-crown from Sarah Edis, and this other from Mrs. Wiseman.
