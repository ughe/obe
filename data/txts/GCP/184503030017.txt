JAMES BATCHELOR. I live in Coal-yard, Drary-lane. I am a coach-
lace and carpet manufacturer-on Saturday, the 28th of Dec., between
three and four o'clock in the afternoon, I went to Mr. Dismore's- I was
at the bar, and saw Elliott and another man there-Elliot had a parcel
under his arm-he asked Mrs. Dismore if she would purchase a silk dress
he said he had got a piece of silk, seventeen yards, he could sell to her
very cheap, he would take 18. 6d. a yard for it-he said he had taken it
for a bad debt-she paid him the money and bought it-I saw Elliott
next a fortnight after at Bow-street-I did not know him before, but have
no doubt of him.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. I believe there were several
people in the bar ? A. There were two or three at the bar-I am in
business for myself—I have a shop, and 1000). worth of property in it-
I went there for change to pay my men, and was not there above five or
six minutes- I was never in custody, or charged with anything I was
once at Bow-street for being a little the worse for liquor-I was never
charged with taking pots.
MR. ALLEN. Q. You were never transported or hung? A. Never.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Vincent was not the man at the public-house
with Elliott? A. No.
JOSEPH SHACKELL re-examined. This pattern of silk found in Elliott's
pocket-book corresponds with this large piece with the bole in one
corner where it has been cut out.
THOMAS Cox re-examined. This silk and linen are my property, and
has my private mark on it-my house is in the parish of St. Paul, Covent-
garden--the value of the whole property is from 1801 to 2001.-1 live
in the house, and am the proprietor of the whole.
ELLIOTT-GUILTY. Aged 34. Transported for Fifteen Years.
VINCENT-Not Guilty.
