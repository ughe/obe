MR. CLARKSON. Q. What would a check on "self" denote? A. That
it was drawn for my own private expenses-the prisoner took the check

Thomas Briggs. I am a clerk, at Messrs. Lubbock's. There is a partner
named Sir John William Lubbock, Bart.-there are others-Mr. Keyser
keeps an account there-on 30th July I paid a check for 201. to the prisoner,
over the counterI gave him two 51. notes, 78407 and 78408, both dated
1st July, and 101. in gold--this is one of the notes (looking at it) it is the
custom to return paid checks to the customer-it was the prisoner's custom
to receive his master's pass-book each week, with the paid checks. 2009
Cross-examined. Q. Do yon hand cancelled checks back? A. Yes;
when the pass-book is asked for the checks are put into the pocket, but not
by me; they pass through three or four hands before they are returned--they
are entered in the pass-book by the juniors-no one but the prisoner ever
received Mr. Keyser's pass-book, to my knowledge- I do not always band it
back; there are four cashiers, I am one-when check-books are asked for, 1
generally give them without any order in writing-I take no signature from
the parties for them-if any one beside the prisoner asked me for a check-
book for Mr. Keyser, I should not give it to them, not even if I knew him
to be the clerk of a person on the Stock Exchange, unless he brought an
order-the entry is,“ 201. paid clerk"-I am certain I paid it to the pri-
soner, but bave no recollection of it independent of the entry in the book.
COURT. 'Q. When checks are paid over the counter, what is done with
them? A. They are placed on a file, and the pass-book is made up from
them; they are then placed behind cards, in a box, with the name of each
customer, and three or four days afterwards are taken out-when the book is
wanted, the ledger-keeper takes the checks from behind the cards, and
compares them with the ledger; they are then placed in the book sideways,
not in the pocket, but for the junior elerk to write them off on the debit side,
place the checks in the pocket, and give them to the ledger-keeper to
compare the book with the ledger-after the ledger-keeper las compared the
book, he places it in a drawer for the cashier to give up when it is
inquired for.
