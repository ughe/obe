Mr. Jones. Q. Had you any other reason for not liking to have Jem
in the house except his poverty? A. That is all I have never been in

Q. You have been asked whether you believed the prisoner was serious
when he said he was going to commit a robbery: did you believe it at
the time? 4. So many jokes have passed between me and him, I did not
know how to take it I believe it now, from what has happened,
Court. Q. What time was it he came to you ? 4. About twenty

MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Where were you standing? 4. I was coming
home, and he was standing by the window as I was coming, just between
the window and the door--the first observation he made to me was, "Jem,
you are just the chap I want to see"--I said, "What do you want to see
me for 1" he said, "I want for you to lend me two screws and a darkey"
- I should say he meant skeleton keys and a dark lantera I said he
might as well ask me for a £500 note as to ask me for such things as
them-nothing else was said on that subject-I said I had no money for
beer or else I would treat him, and he said he had none my brother-in-
law was along with me he bad not been with me all the time he
came up along with me when I came to the door, but I don't think he
beard any thing of what he said, but I cannot say whether he did or not
I said to my brother-in-law," Bill, you stand a pot of beer-he said, "I
have not got any money without going home to fetch it, but go over to
the public-house, call for a pot of beer, and I will come and pay for it"
we went to the Blue-coat Boy public house in Dorset-street, and had a pot
