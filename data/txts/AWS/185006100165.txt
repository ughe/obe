Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
1211. EDWIN SMITH, stealing 9 bagatelle-balls, value 21.; the goods
of Benjamin Moore having been before convicted.
MR. HUDDLESTON conducted the Prosecution.
BENAMIN MOoBe. I keep the Rose and Thistle, in Londonderry-road,
Walworth-] keep a bagatelle-table in my house. On 8th April several men
came to my house, I cannot tell how many-| remember two men coming,
about a quarter-past eight o'clock ; they went into the parlour, where the
bagatelle-table is, and staid about ten minutes-when they left I went
in, and the lights were turned out-as soon as I lighted them I found the
nine bagatelle-balls were gone-they were worth about 21.-1 am not able to
identify either of the men-one had left a basket with Mrs. Moore in the bar,
and he took it from her when they came out-I saw the man leave it-I
cannot swear to his features, I did not see bis face.
Prisoner. Q. I think you stated that you a had a subscription skittle-
ground A. I have-there were three or four persons playing in that
ground that evening-mine is a spirit-house, a general house of business-
this was on a Monday-my attention was called to that day by my having
paid my brewer-] went out after the loss to give information at the station-
house-] saw the policeman Grant next day- usually attend to the persons
who play at skittles- attended to the bar that evening- was not sitting in
my parlour at the back of my bar, when the two men came in ; I was
standing in the bar, in a corner that projects into the passagemy parlour-
door is down the passage-I could not have seen that parlour if I had been in
my back-parlour-after the first examination, the policeman Grant called on
me; the next day, I believe; he has frequently been to me-I have not
treated him, he always paid for what he had--I did not undertake to describe
you at the police-station, no furthar than your hat; it was a peculiar hat-I
was present when my servant gave a description of the person to the police
heard her describe you-I was in the bar, and saw two men-I will not
swear to your having been in the house.
Mr. HUDDLESTON. Q. What hat had he on A. One of those Paris
hats, with no beaver on it, or that shuts up-I will not swear that it
did shut up.
JANE ELIZABETH PEARSON. I am servant to Mr. Moore. On the night
of 8th April I saw the prisoner at my master's house; I saw him when he
was going out; be came from the parlour where the bagatelle-table is, along
to the bar-he asked Mrs. Moore for a basket he had left, and she gave it
him-I am sure the prisoner is the man-there was a person came out of the
parlour at the time the prisoner did; I do not know that he was with him-
it was twenty-five minutes past eight 'cloek.
