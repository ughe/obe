and the answer was "Willie"—I told the prisoner the baby looked bad, and said "Are you going to take it to the doctor's?"—she said "I have taken it to the doctor, and it is getting all right"—I said "It does look a deplorable little crea ture"—I have said to her that it was a shame to go away and leave the child crying like that, and she went upstairs—when Mrs. Barales came she sent for some water to wash the bottle, and of course I took it up and saw the child—it was lying on the bed with another child belonging to Mrs. Langridge—the gentleman gave the child the bottle, and it took it as if it was going down a sink almost—I used to hear the child all the week it was there, night and day—it used to make my heart ache to hear it cry, being the mother of eight children—I heard crying and moaning too—when I went up into the room I saw that it was in a neglected state—it was afterwards taken to the workhouse, and the lady and her husband came back again about 5 or 6 o'clock—I did not think the child would live till it got to the workhouse—the gentleman offered me a sovereign to take it there and then—the prisoner has no husband; she goes out nursing people, and did so during the time the child was there—she would never let any body know her business—she has sometimes had three or four children there—she got her living, as far as I know, by going out nursing and taking in children.
