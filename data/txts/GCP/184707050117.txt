I did not see the prisoner at his duty on the day in question-as he was

Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Are you speaking of the morning
or the mid-day duty ? A. The mid-day duty, the letter-carriers are then in
a different office should say there are three times more persons engaged in
the morning than at mid-day-the mid-day duty occurs every day-the In-
dian mail does not arrive every day-when the Indian mail is connected with
the mid-day duty more exertion is required; but I should say there is not
more bustle-letters are taken about from one to the other in the same
way in the morning-there may be bustle on both occasions ; but I
think not more at mid-day than in the morning- if one person had
nothing particular to do he would be employed in some way or other--I have
heard since that an inspector sometimes tells a letter-carrier to put over a
charge to the teller--there are seats or desks at which the letter-carriers some-
times leave their letters while tbey go to some other part of the place-they
are sometimes left for a long time--if letters not properly belonging to that
Seat are put on it, that could only be corrected by the letter-carrier sorting
his letters--the seat is only a small desk for the letter-carrier to arrange his
letters on - he cannot get his letters for his walk until they have been pre-
viously charged by the teller-when he gets them he frequently lays them
down on his seat or desk, and goes away-if any other letters were put there
in his absence he could only ascertain that by sorting the letters, or by
finding, when he went to deliver them, that he had more.
