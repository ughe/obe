COURT. 2. Where were you ? 4. Standing at my door, to the aft of
the fore-cabin-I heard the rushing of water after that up to 12 o'clock-I did
not see any water in the ship, for I never looked the boats were ready for
leaving the ship when I heard the rush of water orders had been given for
leaving the ship-when I turned out that morning I got my orders to lend
a hand to pass the provisions out, to put in the boats, and we were passing
provisions till after 12 o'clock-I had not packed up any thing at that time,
nor had the other sailors, that I know.
MR. GIPPARD. 0. Did the cook bring you a message to take to the
captain A. Yes that the captain of the Portuguese brig wished to know
at what time we left off pumping the ship-I gave that message to our
captain, who told me to say, "That morning," which I did this was about
9 o'clock on Sunday morning, the 25th-I thought it was not true, but I
took the message--I never heard or saw any pumping that morning--this
was after the captain and carpenter had gone into the lower hold, but
before I heard the rush of water, which was about 10 o'clock that was
the only time the Portuguese captain oame on board - I did not notice how
long he stayed-this conversation took place a few minutes after he came on
board.

Cross-examined by ME. SERJRANT BALLANTINE. Q. You say that the
captain and carpenter went together to the lower-hold & 4. Yes, the
ourpenter went ahead with the parcel, and the captain followed him-I saw
the carpenter take up the angurs, and I have no doubt the captain saw
him also I did not see the oook at that time he could have been in the
fore-cabin without my seeing him-I have no right to disbelieve that he was
there, nor yet believe it I did not mention a single word to any human
being about these sugurs, till I came to England ; not even to Hanlon,
when he called my attention to the rush of water. I have remained
in England ever since I returned, and am kept by the Salvage Associa-
tion- I do not know that some of our sails were carried away, but I
saw the sail maker mending some sails—there had not been rough weather
-the sailmaker had to work every day, except a few days, when he was
sick ; that was at starting-I did not see him in the fore-cabin at the time
of the transaction about the augurs.
MR. GIFFARD. Q. Why did not you mention about the augurs! 4. It
was not my business; such things pass my notice ; and if I am not asked,
I say nothing about it. I have had 11. 186. A week from Lloyd's Salvage
Association, that is, 188. for my board, and 11. to keep myself-I do not
earn so much at sen--my wages are 41. a month, but it costs more to live
ashore-I am fed on board ship.
