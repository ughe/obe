 1290.  ANN BURNS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of June , two crowns, one half-crown, five shillings, and five sixpences, the monies of Margaret Chappell , widow , from her person.
   MARGARET CHAPPELL. I live in George-yard, Whitechapel. On the 25th of June, about half-past four o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came and asked me to walk with her; we went to a wine vaults, and had a quartern of rum - she wanted me to buy the duplicate of a blanket, but I refused - she said, if she had 1 s. 6 d. she would take it out of pawn. I went home for my money, and put a pound in my bosom, which I had to go to market with. It was two crowns, a half-crown, and silver. I lent her 18 d. and then called for another quartern of rum; I found my head heavy, and fell asleep. A young man awoke me, I found my money was gone; I caught her over the way at another wine vaults, and said,
 "Where is my money, that you took out of my bosom;" she said, if I would go to her sister, she would make it all right. I caught hold of her hand, and a half-crown and 1 s. 6 d. fell from it on the ground.
 Prisoner. Q. Were you not tried here - A. Never; I was at Hick's-hall for an assault.
   JAMES CATHERWOOD. I went into the wine vaults to get some beer, the prosecutrix was asleep. I saw the prisoner with her arm round the prosecutrix's neck, and her hand at her bosom; she pushed the corner of her handkerchief out; I suspected she was robbing her; she untied the corner of the handkerchief. I told the landlady of it, and she awoke the prosecutrix. The prisoner was crossing the road at the time the prosecutor went after her - I did not say anything to her. I am a horse dealer, and live in George-yard, Whitechapel.
   JOHN SEABROOK. I was attending at the opposite wine vaults. The prisoner came in, and asked me to take care of 15 s. for her; I have known her a long time - she had scarcely put it down before the prosecutrix followed her, and seized her by the collar; she directly said,
 "If you will go to my sister's, I will make it all right with you;" they went out together. She has put money in my care before.
   FRANCIS FREEMAN. On the 25th of June, the prosecutrix brought the prisoner to the office. She was discharged at the second examination, as we did not know the money was left with Seabrook. I found two sixpences on her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I received 30 s. on the 24th, I was coming home, and met the prosecutrix, and went to drink with her; she asked me to lend her some money, as she had lost a good deal by mackarel. I said, I had none to lend her - we drank two or three hours. I went to leave my money with this young man; she came over and said, I had robbed her, and took 2 s. 6 d. out of my hand.
   JOHN SEABROOK. The prisoner said nothing at my house about the prosecutrix having no money.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1291.  GEORGE WALDUCK was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of June , five jugs, value 5 s.; six dishes, value 3 s.; two chamber pots, value 18 d.; twelve cups, value 18 d.; an ewer, value 18 d., and a basin, value 10 d., the goods of Sarah Goodman.
   SARAH GOODMAN. I live in Walbrook-place, Hoxton. I employed men to hawk earthenware. The prisoner came to live with me on the 21st of May. On the 3d of June I sent him out with a basket of things - he never returned; he had given me no notice. There were altogether twenty-six shillings worth,
   WILLIAM CONSTANTINE. I apprehended the prisoner at Pickford's wharf, in the City-road. On the 14th of July, the prosecutrix fetched me to take him; I told him the charge; he said he would go and work it out.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1292.  JOSEPH HOPE was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , a seal, value 30 s.; a watch key, value 15 s., and a ring, value 1 s., the goods of Richard Price , from his person.
   RICHARD PRICE. I keep the  Magpie and Stump, public-house, in the New-road. St. George's in the East. On the 6th of September, between five and six o'clock in the afternoon, I was in my bar - the prisoner came and asked for a glass of gin - he went into the tap-room; I took it to him; he said,
 "This won't do for me, I want my gin in a measure;" I put it into a measure, and gave it to him, and asked him for the money - he gave me 1 1/2 d.; I said I wanted 2 d.; he said he should give me no more; I insisted on having 2 d., and said I should turn him out if he did not pay me. He was going to sit down; I took and carried him to the outside door; he immediately made a short turn, and snatched at my watch; the ring broke, and he set off with the seal, and I followed and secured him about twenty yards off. He scuffled a good deal - the seal was found just by; he had thrown it into the road while I pursued him.
   JOHN CHURCHILL. I am servant to Mr. Price. I picked up the seal three minutes after he was secured; it was eight or ten yards from the door.
 (Seal produced and sworn to.)
