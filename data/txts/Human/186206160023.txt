 635. WILLIAM SHARP  (33), was indicted for bigamy.
   MR. RIBTON conducted the Prosecution. HENRY MALHAM. I live at 18, Queen-street, Brompton, and am clerk at the parish church of Old Trinity—I produce the original register of mar riages on 4th March, 1851—here is an entry of a marriage between William Smith Sharp and Sophia Leonora Harrison; it is witnessed by me—the prisoner was the person then married. MARY ELIZABETH HARRISON. The prisoner was married to my sister Sophia Leonora; she was alive at 8 o'clock this morning—they lived together in 1851 and 1852—I know the prisoner.
   Prisoner. Q.  You said on a former occasion that I deserted your sister? A.  You did—you did not come to me from Southampton and tell me the reason—I fetched her up from Southampton when I heard that she was there—you did not go with me in a cab till I sent for you—you did not tell me you had sufficient money to pay both your fares up—you went with me be cause you were afraid—you walked with me to Waterloo-bridge station—you met me in the evening when you brought my sister up—you lived with her afterwards, and she left you in consequence of your beating and striking her. ELIZA ANN TAPLIN. I was married to the prisoner in May, 1859, at St. George's, Hanover-square, and lived with him till last October, when I was obliged to leave him.
   Prisoner. Q.  Why? A.  Through your ill treatment; you threatened to run me through with a knife—I did not leave you to cohabit with another man, a carpenter, at that time—I was with him till last March—after I left him, I asked you for some money, and you said you would see me b—first—I did not intend to take money from you—you treated me very badly in Scotland; you kicked me in the chest, and I had to go to London to see a physician—I knew before I left, that your first wife was living—I did not bring these proceedings forward before, because I could not find her—I did not know that you were married at the time I married you; you told me you had lived with a woman, but she was dead—you came to me in the name of William Cooper, and said that you were a single man. HENRY KIRK( Policeman, D  358). I took the prisoner and told him the charge—he said, "Very well"—I produce the two certificates.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did I make any remark? A.  You said that you had not seen your first wife for five years.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I wish to call attention to the time which elapsed between the time my wife deserted me and my second marriage—a report had reached me that my wife was dead. GUILTY — Confined Twelve Months .
