27. ELIZA CLARKE, unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
Messrs. Ellis and Bookin conducted the Prosecution.
William Moore. I keep an eating house, in Church-lane, Whitechapel.
On 28th Oct. the prisoner came for three quarters of a pound of roast mutton
-I am sure she is the person-I served her-it came to 9fd. she paid
with a 58. piece-my wife took charge of it-I gave her the change, and
she went away-on 1st Nov, she came again for two quarters of a pound of
pudding-1 served her-she paid with a 58. piece-I noticed it was bad, but
notwithstanding that I gave her change, and let her go out of the shop-I
followed her-she went up Colchester-street, turned back, and went towards
Whitechapel-she crossed over to Osborne-street, and then a man came up
to ber, and spoke to her-he then went away-she went on further, and I
went up to her, laid hold of her, and said she had passed a bad 58.
piece-I asked her to go back to the shop-in going along she put 43. 6d.
into my hand- I said, "Still you must come back; I think I have another
58. piece of yours”-a mob came round-she addressed them, and said it was
very hard to see a woman dragged along the street in that way I saw a
policeman and gave her into custody-I gave the policeman the last crown,
and I applied to my wife for the other crown, and gave him that.
Prisoner. Q. What did you do with the crown? 4. I kept it in my
hand- I gave you 48. out of a bag, and I went to the till and took a new
sixpence out, I did not place the crown in the till-I served somebody with
some pudding, but I kept the crown in my hand; I followed you, and stopped
you in Osborne-street-I said you had passed a bad 58. piece I did not ask you
to give me the difference-I did not say, "Give me the difference, it is all I
require "-you tried to go up Wentworth-street, when I accused you of
giving me another bad 58. piece-you did not make any observation to that
I can take my oath that you are the person who came to my shop before I
gave the first 58. piece to my wife-she put it away in a drawer-I afterwards
took it to my butcher's and found it was bad.
Mr. Bopkin. Q. When she came the second time did you remember her
as the person who passed the first 58. piece ? 4. Yes; when I followed her
I knew that the second 58. piece was bad- I believe I did say to her, "It
was either you or your sister passed the first one " after that I took par-
ticular notice of the prisoner-I am now prepared to state that she is the
person—I went to the butcher's myself with the first 58. piece, and I brought
it back-it was not out of my sight.
ELIZABETH Moore. I am the wife of the last witness. On 28th Oct. I
saw the prisoner come into the shop-she had some roast mutton-she paid
for it with a 5s. piece-Mr. Moore took it, and gave it to me-I put it apart
from other coins, and afterwards gave it to him to go to the butcher's-on 1st
Nov. my husband asked me for it, and I gave him the same 58. piece I had
no other in the house.
