   JUBY . Q.  Did you keep the prisoner forty-eight hours without food? A.  No; we did not; we were obliged to put him in that ward because he wag a very bad character; we did not know what to do with him.
   COURT . to MR. BULLEN . Q.  Was the prisoner unable to work? A.  Not at that time—he fractured his collar bone about three months ago, but he was able to work at that time, at the work he was put to, which was picking oakum—he was released from hard labour by my direction.  1068. JOSEPH SMALL . feloniously uttering two 5 l  . notes, with intent to defraud.
   MESSRS GIFFARD . and BAILEY . conducted the Prosecution. EMILY PALMER. I live with my uncle, a dairyman, at Camberwell, the prisoner is a tenant of my uncle's. On Wednesday, 1st Oct., lie came and asked me if I would take 12 s . for two weeks' rent, and give him the change for a 5 l . note—I took the note, and I noticed two names on it—this is the note—here is the name of Thomas Taylor on it—I asked the prisoner to write his name, and he did so—I took 12 s ., and gave him 4 l . 8 s .—I passed the note away in the course of business, and it was returned as forged—on Saturday, 4th Oct., the prisoner came again about half-past 9 o'clock—he had a note in his hand, and he asked me if I would give him change for it—I took it, and looked at it—I said, "It appears to be a good one"—he said, "I hope it is, I had it from my father in the country—I returned it to him, and said that I had not enough change—he asked me if I would send over to the Waterloo Arms to get it changed for him, as he was a stranger there, and the people did not like changing a note for him—I said, "I should think any of the houses you are in the habit of using would change it for you"—he then asked me if I would give him 4 l ., and give his wife the remainder on Monday morning—I told him I could not do it—I did not send him for change on that or any other day.
   Cross-examined by MR. METTCALFE . Q.  You hare collected the prisoner's nut for some time for your uncle? A.  Yes; the prisoner has been living there about two years—not a quarter of a mile from us—I knew him well, and he knew me—I did not see him very often—I received the rent from his wife generally—it is a private house where I live. JANE LEWTY, jun. My father keeps the Waterloo Arms, at Gamberwell On Saturday afternoon, 4th Oct., the prisoner came—I was standing at the bar—he said, "Can you give me change for a 5 l . note, for Miss Palmer; I wish to pay her some money, and she has only change sufficient to pay the men"—I took the note to my mother, in the bar parlour—she gave it me back, and told me to give change for it—I gave the prisoner four sovereigns and 1 l . worth of silver—I gave him a pen and ink, and told him to write his name on the note, and he did so—he asked if he should write his address—I said, "Yes, if you please"—he did so—this is the note—in the course of the next week it was returned as forged.
   Cross-examined. Q.  In consequence of this address you went to him and found him? A.  Yes; my brother found the note was forged, and the pri soner came to our house on the Sunday. JANE LEWTY.
