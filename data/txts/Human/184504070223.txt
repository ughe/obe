MR. ROBINSON . Q.  It was a tin box of the prisoner's in which this money was kept? A.  Yes, the gold was in another box inside the tin box—I indicted him for stealing the eleven sovereigns—I have been told the Grand Jury have thrown out that bill—I had frequently missed money—I do not apprehend it was taken in sovereigns. MARY ANN HUDSON. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw the eleven sove reigns and 13 s . 6 d . in the prisoner's box on the Friday afternoon, and that evening I marked two shillings and two sixpences—I gave the two shillings to Mr. Fairing ton, and the two sixpences to Mrs. Pritchard, to lay out at our shop—on this sixpence produced here is a scratch at the nose, and a piece cut out at the side, in a line with it—I know the mark, and the date of the sixpence was taken as well—I can speak to this as my own mark—I never marked any other sixpences in my life. WILLIAM FARRINOTON. I am a baker, and live in Windsor-place, Old Kent-road. My wife showed me two marked shillings—I sent the servant girl with them to the prosecutor's shop for some articles—I took a me morandum of the date and reign of the two shillings—I received two marked sixpences from Mrs. Pritchard's sister—I put down the date and reign of them—they were marked on the top of the nose and on the edge—I sent the boy out with both the sixpences—this sixpence is one of them—it is of the year 1840—it is one that Mrs. Pritchard's sister gave me, and one I sent by the boy.
   Joseph Biggs.  I am employed occasionally by Mr. Farrington to go on errands. On that Saturday morning, about twenty minutes to eight o'clock, he gave me two sixpences, to go to Mr. Hudson's shop, to fetch 2oz. of tea and 1 lb. of sugar—they came to 1 s .—I paid the two sixpences for them that I had from Mr. Farrington—I did not notice them.
   BENJAMIN  —( police-constable M  107.) I searched the prisoner's box with Mr. Hudson—I found in it eleven sovereigns, fifteen shillings, four sixpences, and a lot of coppers—I heard Mr. Hudson say to the prisoner, "There is no doubt the sovereigns belong to me; if you will give me the sovereigns, you may take the silver, and go about your business"—the prisoner said part of the money belonged to his brother—this is the box—it has been a lucifer-box, I believe—this is the marked sixpence I had of Mr. Hudson.

I know he had money in his possession about last June—I saw it—there were six sove reigns and some silver—he was of a remarkably saving turn—I know of his having a suit of clothes nearly two years—he got them at his first place—they were left by his master for mourning. CATHARINE EVANS. I live in Waterloo-street, Walworth. I have known the prisoner three years—I saw some money in his box, where he placed his clothes, three or four months before he went to Mr. Hudson's—he had six sovereigns, besides some silver, which I did not see counted he had a black coat, black trowsera, and a blue satin waistcoat.
