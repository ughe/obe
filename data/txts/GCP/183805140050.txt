1212. MARTHA EDGECOMB was indicted for feloniously uttering
counterfeit shilling to Mary Gray, well knowing it to be counterfeit, she
having been previously convicted as an utterer of base coin.
Messrs. Ellis and BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I produce a copy of the record of the con-
viction of Martha Edgecomb at the Central Criminal Court, in the Feb-
ruary session, 1836, for uttering counterfeit silver-I have examined it
with the original record in Mr. Clark's office, and it is a true copy-(read.)
FRANCIS Cook. I am a policeman. I was a witness at the trial of
the prisoner in February, 1836, in this or the other court, for uttering
a
counterfeit shilling-she was sentenced to twelve months' imprisonment-
I know she is the person I had apprehended her.
MARY GRAY. I am the wife of Charles Gray, a tobacconist, in Foley-
place; we sell beer also. On the 27th of April the prisoner came and asked
for half-a-pint of beer-I supplied her with it, and she gave me a shilling
-I gave it to my husband, seeing it was bad, and he detained her.
Prisoner. She put it into the till and was rattling about for change
Witness. I did not put it into the till at all-I weighed it in a scale at
the counter, and gave it to my husband I weighed it against another shil-
ling, which I took out of the till, but I am certain I did not mix them-i
never went out of my hand till I put it into the scale, it was not out of
my sight-I weighed it because I was not sure that was bad-it was before
I gave it to my husband--the good one was the heaviest-I put that into
the till again.

Prisoner. After giving it to her husband he called several people out
of the tap-room, and gave it into their hands to look at. Witness. He
did not.
CHARLES Gray. I am the husband of the last witness. On the 27th
of April, I saw the prisoner in my shop—my wife said to me in her pre-
sence, “Here is a bad shilling"-she gave it to me, and said she got it from
the prisoner-I told her I should give her in charge- I kept the shilling in
my hand till the policeman came, and gave it to him-he came up to the
door just at the moment-I did not hand it to any one till I gave it to
him-I am quite sure of that.

ELIZA CLARK. I am the daughter of William Clark, a tobacconist, in
Charlotte-street, Portland-place, I serve in the shop. On Saturday night,
the 7th of April, the prisoner came for half an ounce of tobacco-I served
her-it came to three-halfpence farthing-she gave me half-a-crown-I
thought it was bad, and did not give her change-I kept it in my hand till
my mother came and gave it to her-I am sure I gave my mother the same
half-crown as the prisoner gave me.
MARIA CLARK. I am the mother of the last witness. She gave me &
half-crown-I looked at it and told the prisoner it was a bad one-she said
she did not know it-shew as sorry for it, and if I would give it to her again,
her husband was at the bottom of the street, and she would get another
I did not allow her to have it, but sent for a policeman and gave her ir
charge-I gave him the half-crown.
