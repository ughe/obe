I looked out of the window, and saw a man, who asked, if I had authorised any one to take the lead from my house. I went and found it at the watch-house, returned and missed it. I know nothing of the prisoner.
   FRANCES BURDETT. I am wife of last witness. I went up and missed a sheet off the bed of the top room.
   ROBERT WILKINSON. I am apprentice to Mr. Burdett. Isaacs came up when I was in bed at my masters - he sent a girl up to call me. I got up and came down; he asked if I could give him a lodging; I said "No;" he said, if I did not, he should be put in the watch-house or something. I agreed to it, and in the morning he got up an hour before me, and awoke me about half-past five o'clock; he asked if I would run away from my uncle; I said, No, I did not want to go. I went down stairs on purpose to get him down; he asked if he might put on my shoes. I said Yes; as they were an old pair, and about four doors from the house he tried the shoes on, and said he could not wear them. He went up again, and brought the sheet down, he said, "Now go away with me." I did not like to go back, and we came away together, and when we got out of the street, he said, he had some lead, and asked me to carry it as it was all falling down in his breeches, and I did.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not ask me to fetch you a wh - e - A. No; I did not persuade him to run away with me.
   FRANCIS MANNOCK. I was officer of the night. About half-past five o'clock in the morning, I left the watch-house to go home, and saw the prisoner, who appeared a suspicious character, with something like a sheet stuck out of his coat, which was buttoned. I asked what he had there? he said it was nothing to me. I said he should not pass till I was satisfied; he then said it was a sheet which he was taking from his mother to his aunt. He could not tell where his mother or aunt lived - I took him and Wilkinson to the watch-house. I found a sheet round the prisoner's body, in his hat a shirt, and in his pocket a dirty cloth - he said the sheet was his aunt's. On Wilkinson, I found 8 lbs. of lead in one pocket, and 1 lb. in the other; he said the prisoner stole it from his master, and after he left gave it him to carry, as he had made up his mind to leave his master.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. This lad's uncle came to him at the watch-house and said, "What Bob, you have robbed me six or seven times before, and I shall go to the utmost of the law, and what have you led this young man into it for."
   ROBERT BURDETT. I never said any such thing.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.

 1226.  JOSEPH HUDSON, was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 2 s. 1 1/2 d., the property of Anthony Starkey.
   ANTHONY STARKEY. I am a fishmonger, and live at Hackney. On the 12th or 13th of September, I marked my money and put it on my desk in the parlour. The prisoner has lived five or six years with me, I suspected him, as he dressed a good deal - I marked the shillings and halfpence in the evening, and next morning 1 s. 7 d. was gone. I sent for an officer, who searched him - he gave me a key of his box from his pocket, which I tried to my desk, it fitted it perfectly well, he then gave me some silver from his waistcoat pocket, 2 s. of which I knew, and in his coat pocket three halfpence were found, which I had marked - he had no occasion to go to the desk.
   JOHN GARVA. I am beadle of Hackney. I was fetched and took the prisoner in custody - found two shillings and three halfpence in his pocket, and a key which opened the desk - he said it was the key of his box, and that he had no money about him.
 MR. STARKEY. The money is mine.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Judgment Respited.
 Second Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1227.  STEPHEN HUNTLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of August , three tea-spoons, value 10 s., and one table-spoon, value 8 s., the goods of Richard Battley.
   RICHARD BATTLEY. I am a commercial agent, and live at Clapton. On the 3d of August, about half-past three o'clock in the afternoon, I was sitting in my back parlour, and saw the prisoner in my back garden, he went into the kitchen and took three tea and one table spoon from the drawer; when he came up, I asked his business; he asked if I wanted any shavings for my stove - I said, "Yes," and he immediately ran away - I followed and caught him, he gave me a tea-spoon, and said, that was all he had taken, and hoped I would forgive him. On searching further, I found the other spoons in his basket among the shavings.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Three Months and Whipped.

   JANE TEWART. I am a widow, and live at Ratcliff-highway.
