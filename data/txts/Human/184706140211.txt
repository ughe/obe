THOMAS GORE. I saw the van coming from Camberwell New-road to Bowyer-lane—that would be the way to come from Sydenham—I am quite sure Haines is one who was in the van—I saw a considerable quantity of iron in the van—I did not take particular notice what iron it was—there was a quantity of water-pipe in it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did you not swear before the Magistrate that you were quite certain Haines and Cockling were two of the men in the cart? A.  No—this is my mark to this deposition—( the deposition being read, stated , "I saw the two prisoners. Haines and Cockling, in the van—Cockling was driving—Haines was in the van—Haines knew me—he put up his arm to prevent my seeing him—I am quite certain Haines and Cockling were the two men.")—I said there were four men—Haines was one—I am pretty well, sure Cockling was the man who was driving—I saw the van about four o'clock.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You gave information of this? A.  Yes, to Dooley, whom I was driving rubbish for. ALEXANDER CHISHOLM. I received information of this from Mr. Plowman.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who is Mr. Plowman? A.  He is foreman to Mr. Hayward—he told me of this, and that some of our men had been pursuing the thieves. JOSEPH THRELFALL CARTER. This pipe is mine—I gave no authority to any person to have it removed.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is the value of it? A.  7 l . or 8 l .—there are several lengths of pipe of a very remarkable sort—I suppose hardly any of such a sort in the trade—if a person gave 2 l . 17 s . for all mine, and 8cwt. in addition, it is not anything like the value of the pipe—old iron sells very low. JOSEPH DONOVAN re-examined. Q.  How was this iron when it was brought to you? A.  All broken up, of no use but for melting again—I should not have bought it for old iron if it had not been broken.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time did it get to your place? A.  As near as I can recollect, about five o'clock. GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined Six Months .

 1543. WILLIAM GEORGE LIPSCOMB was indicted for embezzle ment. JOHN PEEL. I live in the New-cut, Lambeth, and am a printer. The prisoner was in my service as lately as the 20th of April—a person named Snelling was indebted to me for some work done—it was the prisoner's duty to receive money on my account—the course of business was for the money to be entered in the cash-book by the person who received it, the day of the month, and what sort of money was received, and to put the money through a crevice into the desk—I asked the prisoner if Mr. Smith had paid him 9 s .—he said I should find it in the book—I have examined the book, and I do not find it entered—the prisoner has not accounted to me for a sum paid by Mr. Snelling.
