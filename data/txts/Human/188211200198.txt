   Cross-examined.  It is the fact that I discovered that I had a counterfeit coin before the police or anybody came in and spoke to me—it was then a few minutes to 10 o'clock as near as possible, and I sent my pot-man out for a policeman, and told him to follow—he did not come back for some time, half-past 10 or a quarter to 11 o'clock—I did not see Gilroy again till 11 o'clock, when he was brought to our house for me to recognise as he passed the station—Stingimore the police constable brought him to the front door, not into the house—I was asked to go out, and he said "Is this the man?"—Stingimore is a Dorking police-man—my potman is not here. ANN NEWMAN. I keep the tap at the Bed Lion Hotel, Dorking—on the night of 8th Sept. two men came in late, from a quarter to 20 minutes past 10 o'clock—I identify the prisoner and Seabrook as the men—Gilroy asked for a pint of ale—I served them, and Gilroy gave me a two-shilling piece to pay for it—I gave him 1 s . 9 d . change, in good money—they were there about six minutes, and left together—Superintendent Lambert came in five or six minutes afterwards, and I showed him the two-shilling piece which Gilroy had given me—I then marked it and laid it on a shelf close by, and next morning I gave it to Superintendent Lambert—I examined the till when Lambert came in, and that was the only two-shilling piece in the till at that time.
   Cross-examined.  When the two-shilling piece was taken out of the till there were only a few shillings and a few sixpences there, which had been taken that evening—I was at work in the bar all the evening and that was the money taken from the customers. GEORGE LAMBERT. I am superintendent of the Dorking Police—on the night of September 8th, about 10 o'clock, I received information with regard to some counterfeit coin—I went in search of the prisoners, and found Seabrook in the High Street—I took him to the Bull's Head, and afterwards to the police-station—I searched him at the Bull's Head and found on him this counterfeit florin ( produced ) and 2 l . 18 s . in good money—there were two sovereigns, one half-crown, five shillings, twelve six-pences, three threepenny pieces, and 8 s . 9 d . in copper—I afterwards saw Gilroy at the police-station about 11 o'clock.
   Cross-examined.  I was present when Gilroy was searched—Stingimore searched him, and he has got the money with the exception of the gold—he can give you exact evidence as to the money found—I only found one counterfeit coin on Seabrook—the last train leaves Dorking for London on the South-Eastern line about half-past 9, and on the London, Brighton, and South Coast about 5 minutes to 10 at night—I know the potman at the Bull's Head—I know that the landlady and the mother sent the potman out on this evening, but do not know at what time—I did not see him come out—I saw the potman in the High Street between 10 o'clock and the time that Gilroy was taken in custody—I did not see him in a public-house treating Gilroy to something to drink—I do not know that the potman was in Gilroy's company at all—I do not know that he gave him something to drink, and that he went from there home to the bed which he had engaged.
