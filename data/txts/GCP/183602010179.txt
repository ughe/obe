564. WILLIAM ROTHERHAM was indicted for that he, having been
declared a bankrupt, feloniously did conceal part of his personal estate, to
the value of 101., and upwards.
Messrs. WIGHTMAN, CLARKSON, GURNEY, and BAILEY, conducted the
Prosecution.
WILLIAM Foy. I am a warehouseman. I have known the prisoner
from seven to nine years-he has carried on the business of a linen-draper,
in Shoreditch.
Cross-examined by Mr. BODKIN. Q. Did you know him intimately 1
4. Nothing beyond his transactions in business-he kept, I believe, as many
as thirty or forty persons in his establishment.
GEORGE WARD. I am clerk to Ellis and Everington, and have been so about
nine years. Mr. Everington joined the firm in August, 1884—I have
known the prisoner four or five years-he was in the habit of dealing with
our firm--some of these bills (produced) are drawn by Wynne Ellis
alone, and the others by Ellis and Everington-they are accepted by Wil-
liam Rotherham, the prisoner -I think I can speak to his hand-writing-I
believe these to be his hand-writing--I have seen him accept bills.
Cross-examined by Mr. Bopkin. Q. Do you mean you saw him ac-
cept some of these bills ? A. I cannot say positively that he accepted all
these, but some of these are my writing-it has been usual for me to draw
them at his house, and he has accepted them I have often seen him write
-I believe they are his hand-writing.
(The bills produced by the witness were as follows one for 681. 155.,
one for 2031. 198., one for 2477. 188., and one 501. 98. 6d.)
MR. BODKIN. Q. To what extent has the prisoner dealt at your bouse
in the last few years ? A. I do not know-I cannot swear that it has not
been to the amount of 4,000l.
