NEW COURT.—Wednesday, Feb. 25th, 1852.
PRESENT—Mr. Ald. FAREBROTHER; Mr. RECORDER; and Mr.
CUBITT.
Before Mr. Reeorder and the Fifth Jury.
314. THOMAS MILLER, stealing 19 scarf shawls, value 351. ; the good,
Charles Grant Wilson, his master.
MR. COOPER conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES GRANT WILSON. I am a woollen merchant, of 18, BasinghalL
street. The prisoner came into my employ as town traveller, on 6th Sep.—he
first came on a commission, and afterwards on a salary of 1001. a year—he
in the habit of taking out patterns—one morning I bad occasion to want some
small patterns—the prisoner had put his packet of patterns on the end of the
desk, and had gone up stairs—I undid them, and found a piece of cloth
amongst them—through a friend, Mr. Brown, I charged the prisoner with
robbery, and he gave me up some duplicates—these are them—I have traced
some goods to the pawnbrokers'—tbe prisoner had no right to pawn any
my goods.
ALFRED ROBERT WOOD. I conduct the business of a pawnbroker fur my
mother, Mrs. Wood, in High-street, Bloomsbury. This duplicate (looking
one) is my writing—I produce this shawl—it was pawned on 1st Oct., J do not
know by whom—it is in the name of Cameron—it is very common for persons
to give a name different to their own.
ALEXANDER ADAMS. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a shawl pawned
with me on 20th Sept., in the name of John Miller—I also produce another
shawl pawned with us.
JOHN OAKLEY. I am assistant to Mr. Young, a pawnbroker of Princes.
street, Leicester-square. This duplicate (looking at it) is the writing ot
a young man who has left our employ—I know this is one of our duplicates—
this is the shawl that was pledged.
MR. WILSON re-examined. These shawls are all my property—this one is mine
—I bad not sold or disposed of it—no one bad these goods in London but myself,
and I have not sold them in London—my mark was on this one, in this place—
it has been torn off—I know the goods by the quality, and the border—
they were manufactured in Roxburghshire—the manufacturer had a large
stock, and sent them to me—I had upwards of 1001.-worth of them—I hap.
pened to be there at the time. and they were very unsaleable from their colour—
he asked me to take them, which I did—since I got these goods from the pawn.
broker, I have looked over my stock —I have missed between thirty and forty—
I have got eight back, by the duplicates given me by the prisoner—he did not
make any statement when he gave them to me—I did not say anything to
him, nor did anyone in my presence—my friend saw the prisoner on the
Saturday afternoon, and the duplicates were given me on the Monday after.
noon—the prisoner said he expected to be able to replace them, and be would
pay the money—he put these duplicates into my hand—my friend had made
him a promise—he had no authority from me to hold out any expectation Of
mercy, no further than a general authority to act for me on this occasion—I
was so overwhelmed myself, I did not feel equal to it.
Prisoner. Q. Who did you get these duplicates from? A. From you;
I made no condition when I got them—after you made a general statement,
and handed me back all my account, I expressed my deep regret that you had
