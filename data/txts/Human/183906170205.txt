   COURT . Q.  Did you drink with Davis? A.  I dare say I might—I cannot say whether I did or not.
   MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Do you mean to say yon doubt whether you did or not? A.  Yes, I do—I am sure I do not know whether I did or not—I did not sit down in his company, for I was very busy in the office, backwards and forwards, at the time—I was not on duty at the office that day—I left business about seven o'clock—Moss was in custody that day—I partly had the care of him, but there were some policemen there to take charge of him also.
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  As soon as this robbery was discovered, you were applied to, and consulted about it—is that so? A.  Yes—I was applied to on the 25th of March—for a few days all my transactions were with the two Caspars—it was on the 16th of April, Roe and I went to search Solomon's house—Dean was there at the time—he did not then show me any gold in a trunk up-stairs, nor give me any in formation about it—I think it was two or three days after that Dean and the two young Solomons disappeared—I "searched Davis's house in Co ventry-court, on the 2nd of April, and found Alice Abrahams there—it is a house of ill fame—a house of accommodation—the door is kept open—Mrs. Davis was sitting with Alice Abrahams in the parlour—I asked her no questions, and she told me nothing—I think it was about a fortnight after, that she made some communication—I think it was somewhere about the 13th or 14th, she made some communication, in consequence of which she was examined at the office on the subject—her communication to me partly referred to Solomon—it was on her communication that I pursued the scent with relation to Solomon—I saw her I think about twice or three times between that time and my going after Solomon  —I saw her on the Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, and then I went to Solomon with Roe and made the search."
   Q.  After that did you see her examined before the Magistrates at Lam beth-street? A.
