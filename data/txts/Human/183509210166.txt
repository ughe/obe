 2083. ELIZABETH SPEARS was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the   5th of September , 1 cloak, value 5 s  .; 3 coats, value 1 l  .; 2 pair of trowsers, value 5 s  .; 1 dressing-case, value 5 s  .; 1 umbrella, value 2 s  .; and 8 printed books, value 10 s  .; the goods of John Jasper Boreham , well knowing them to have been feloniously stolen against the statute &c. LYDIA BOREHAM. I am the wife of John Jasper Boreham, and live at Rateliff. On the 4th of September, I delivered a box to Lewis Buckeridge, the Epping carrier, which contained the property stated—these are the articles. LOUIS BUCKERIDGE. I am a carrier. I received the box from this lady to take to Epping—on the 5th of September, a person, named John Lay, asked to ride in my cart, which he did—when he was gone. I missed this box from behind the cart—I do not know what had been in it—this is the box. JOHN EMMERSON. I received information, and went to the prisoner's lodging at Woodford—I heard that Lay lodged there—I found the pri soner—I asked if she knew any thing about the loss of the box, or the contents of it—she said she did not—I found the box in a shed, a little distance from her house—I went to her again in the evening, and asked her if she knew nothing about what was in the box, as I had traced it to her house, and likewise from her house—she said, she did not know about it—I then went to the door, and heard her draw something from under her bed, and she threw down stairs abag, containing these articles.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How do you know she did not begin to search directly? A.  I do not know that she did not—I traced the box to her house by a man's footsteps—I have looked after Lay, but I cannot find him. HANNAH ATKINSON. I live in the same house with the prisoner After the officer was gone, the prisoner took this umbrella from under the bed, and gave it to me—she said sha had forgotten to give it up—Lay had lodgeed in the hay-loft, but he has run away.
   Cross-examined Q.  Had she not a good character? A.  Yes; up to this time—she is a widow.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I am not guilty.
   COURT to JOHN EMERSON . Q.  Do you know Dr. Robinson's writing? A.  Yes; to the best of my belief, the signature to this deposition is his writing. ( Read .)—"The prisoner says, I found the umbrella and bag under John Lay's bed—I took them, and put them under my own—when John Emerson came the second time, I was gone into the coach-house—I chucked  the bag down stairs. " NOT GUILTY

   Third Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
