NEW COURT, Tuesday, March 1st, 1870.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
269. CAROLINE BOYD (39), Unlawfully endeavouring to conceal the
birth of a child. MR. HUNT conducted the Prosecution. Nor Guilty.
370. PATRICK READING (32), was indicted for wilful and corrupt
perjury.
MR. BROMBY conducted the Prosecution; and Mr. STRAIGHT the Defence.
WILLIAM HILL (Policeman C 340). On 29th January I was at Marl-
borough Street Police Court, and heard the charge against a man named
James Reading- I have not got a copy of the summons.
Cross-examined. l. There was no summons, was there ? A. No, it was
complaint by George Pennington, for assaulting whom I took James
Reading in custody on Saturday, 22nd January—the charge was entered on
the charge-sheet, and he was looked up till Monday morning.
MR. BROMBY. Q. Was he remanded for a week 1 A. Yes, and that
came off on the 31st-I was then in Court, and heard Patrick Reading, the
prisoner, examined.
MR. STRAIGHT. Q. Did the man sign the charge-sheet? 4. Yes; it is
then taken to the Police Court, and the clerk takes down in his note-book
what the oharge is, and then the investigation goes on.
MR. STRAIGHT contended that the Magistrate's Clerk ought to be present with
his notebook, which would be the best evidence of what the charge was. Mr.
BROMBY proposed to examine the other witnesses while the Magistrate's Clerk
was sent for. The COURT considered that the materiality of what was sworn
depended upon the shape of the charge; that some documents ought to be pro-
duced thowing that the Magistrale was engaged in a judicial enquiry; and
that the prisoner had a right to claim that the trial should proceed at once, and
therefore was entitled to his acquittal.
NOT GUILTY.
