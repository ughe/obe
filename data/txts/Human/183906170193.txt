name on it, but when I went next morning it was taken off—I know master's sister, Mrs. Levy—my master did not go out on Tuesday till late in the afternoon—he was walking about the place—Mrs. Levy came that Tues day morning, and in the afternoon a truck came—when my master went out in the afternoon, he did not return any more to New-street—a truck of things went away that day to Mansel-street, and the rest of the things next day—only master moved on the Tuesday—my mistress returned about one o'clock on Tuesday afternoon—she went out again directly she had had her dinner, and returned about five o'clock—on Wednesday morning a van came and took the rest of the things away—I remained there all Tuesday night, help ing them to pack up—my master has a daughter named Rosetta—she kept her clothes in a deal box—she told me something about that box, when I came there on the Tuesday morning, and in consequence of what she said I looked at the box, and her clothes were in a drawer and not in the box—that is the box they used to be kept in—( looking at one produced )—that box was underneath the bed in her father's room on Tuesday morning—I looked at it—it was tied up with a cord—from what the girl said to me I tried to lift it, but could not, it was very heavy—that box was taken away in the truck by the carman—I know Mr. Reed—he is the landlord of the house in New-street—they told me about some things going to be pledged, and Mrs. Moss gave me five sovereigns, which I gave to Mr. Reed, the landlord, by her direction—I do not know what rent my master paid a year—I think the five sovereigns was for the quarter's rent—I went to the lodgings in Mansel-street on the Wednesday—I saw Moss there on Wed nesday evening—he was at home all day on Wednesday in Mansel-street, and he went out in the evening—I was at Mansel-street all day—I know Isaac Davis—Mrs. Moss and Mrs. Levy assisted me in packing up the goods on the Tuesday night, and on the Wednesday morning Davis assisted—I went to the lodging in Mansel-street about seven o'clock on Thursday morning, and saw Moss there—I saw old Caspar there that morning—I opened the door to him about a quarter to eight o'clock—he asked for my master, and master went and spoke to him just in the passage—after speak ing together old Caspar ran down stairs, and master went out in a very few minutes after—he did not come home to breakfast that morning—I was kept in-doors, and not permitted to leave the house from that Thursday morning until the Saturday evening—I slept there—that was not usual with me—on the Sunday before the robbery was committed, old Mr. Cas par came to the house in New-street about dinner-time, and staid about half an hour—I had frequently seen him at my master's house before that—on the Saturday after
