He did not think my bank was good enough, and advised me to go to Lloyd's Bank, which I did. Harris was supposed to be secretary. Harris did not introduce me to Lloyd's Bank, I went there myself. The manager did not appear to know him, the account was not opened there, and I was introduced by Ernest to the London Trading Bank, where I opened the account as Chalmers and Cox, giving my name as "James Wilkes Cox," 2, Tokenhouse Buildings. Perryman only had the control of money in connection with stocks that I had opened with him. If I said the contrary I ought to have been more careful. Harris had the cheque-book, not Perryman, and Harris filled the cheques up. Towell read my statement to me, and I signed it. I may have been not careful enough in making corrections in it. I generally had £2 a week from Harris, sometimes £3. I do not think it is true to say that Perryman had the whole of the money—there was none to have; it all went in expenses. Practically, I was a weekly servant. I paid the money or cheques into the banking account, but sometimes I handed banknotes or postal orders to Perry  man—I nearly always owed him money. On one or two occasions he may have drafted a letter or portion of a letter. I have no recol lection of letters being typed at 43, Moorgate Street. I knew the business of Chalmers and Cox was not being conducted properly, and I did not like it; I thought there was a great deal of sharp practice, and that Perryman had put me into a very dangerous position—actions being brought against ma through acting on his advice. I will not swear that I did not say to Towell that Perryman said, "You will never make a bucket shop keeper," but I do not think he used the word "bucket shop." The pencil letters produced were written by me while ill in bed. I do not think the contract notes were made out by Perryman—I consulted Harris about them. It was understood the business of Chalmers and Cox must be carried on on proper lines, and that, if people won, they were to be paid. Watson is dead—he had the Chalmers and Cox papers.
 Re-examined. Ottaway asked me to go to the City Solicitor. No threat of any kind was made to prosecute me. Ottaway found me through Dove. The evidence I have given this morning is, as far as I know, correct. I left a lot of waste-paper at 2, Tokenhouse Buildings. WILLIAM STAPLEFORD, 57, Holborn Viaduct, 15th Middlesex Rifle Volunteers. I met Tyler in South Africa, and, on the recommenda tion of the commander, he was made acting secretary of the battalion, in which I was an officer and member of the finance committee, handled the whole of the funds, and, at the disbandment, received a hearty vole of thanks for his excellent conduct. He then became secretary to my firm, William Stapleford and Co., Cape Town, had £5,000 a month through his hands for 18 months, gave the highest satisfaction, and was then put in charge of the Johannesburg branch until the winding-up of the business, which he conducted perfectly satisfactory. I formed a very high opinion of him. He moved in the best military circles in the colony.
