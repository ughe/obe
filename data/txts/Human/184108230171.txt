GEORGE HANBURY( police-constable D  102.) I stopped the prisoner in the Uxbridge-road, on the 26th of August, with a basket on his shoulder—I asked what he had got—he said, "Chips, and a bottle"—I searched, and found these nails—I asked how he came by them—he said he brought them from where he had been at work, at Mr. Jay's, the builder, and was going to nail a box up—I searched him at the station, and found on him as many more nails as I could hold in my hands—I said next morning that he was very foolish in taking them—he said he picked them up in one room and another.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 32.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Two Months.
 2235. WILLIAM COLLINS was indicted for stealing, on the   8th of June , 1 saw, value 2 s  ., the goods of Joseph Fletcher .
   MR. CURWOOD conducted the Prosecution. JOSEPH JEX. I am apprentice to Mr. Joseph Fletcher, a ship-builder, it Limehouse—his yard is at Poplar—the prisoner is one of my fellow-ap prentices. I lost a saw of my master's about three months ago—I inquired, but could not hear of it—Mr. Dicker showed it me again—this is it— ( looking at it )—I know it by two marks on the handle of it, and a mark on the back. JAMES RICHARD WATTS. I am servant to Mr. Dicker, the pawnbroker, in Commercial-road. I know the prisoner—I had seen him before—on the 8th of June I took in this saw of him—I am sure he is the person.
   Prisoner.  I am innocent; I have pledged tools frequently for one Clark; he told me had a chest of tools, and seeing the distress he and his mother were in, I pawned them, not knowing I was doing wrong.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 17— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Eight Days.
 2236. WILLIAM COLLINS was again  indicted with JOHN CLARK for a misdemeanor.
   MR. CURWOOD conducted the Prosecution. STEPHEN DANIEL SKELLY. I live in Ebenezer-place, Commercial-road—I am an ironmonger—my father is foreman to Mr. Fletcher—I am in the habit, on my father's messages, of supplying tools to Mr. Fletcher's apprentices. On the 17th of August Clark came to me, and said he had, come for a plane by my father's order—I let him have a plane, fully be lieving he was authorised to come by my father. DANIEL SKELLY. I am foreman in Mr. Fletcher's yard. When tools are wanted I am in the habit of sending the apprentices to my son for them—I did not give Clark any order on the 17th to get any plane from my son. THOMAS DICKER. I am a pawnbroker. On the 18th of August Collins came in with this new jack plane to pawn—I asked if it was his own—he said no, it belonged to an apprentice of Mr. Curling's, who was outside—I told him to bring him in—he went out—Clark came in with him, and said it was his. STEPHEN DANIEL SKELLY re-examined.  This is exactly the sort of plane we sold to Clark.
   CLARK— GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Three Months.
   COLLINS— NOT GUILTY.
