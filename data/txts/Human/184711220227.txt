WILLIAM RICHARD FRANKLIN. I am pot-boy to Mr. Morgan, at the Rose and Crown. Shorlin lodged there for some time—on the evening of 2nd Nov. he was treating the people with liquor—I saw Stepney sit on his right hand side—Moggeridge was on the other side of the same box—Shorlin had a hawker's license—I saw Stepney look at it in his presence—I afterwards heard him call out that he was robbed—Stepney, Moggeridge, and others, were then in the box with him—they got up and went out of the room—Stepney and Moggeridge were not away more than five minutes; they then came back—I have seen Shorlin with gold about him at different times; two, or three, or four pounds.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALF. Q.  That was some time before? A.  Yes—he sometimes drinks a good deal—he was very drunk that night—Rosewell was drunk when he went away—I do not know whether he was at the time of the robbery. ELIZA KING. I am the wife of William King, of Garrett-lane, Wands-worth. Stepney and Moggeridge lodged with me—on Thursday morning, 4th Nov., the prisoner, Battock, came to their lodgings—Stepney was taken on the Thursday night, and of Friday Moggeridge came and asked me if I would swear to the scissors, as that was the only thing that would betray them—I have examined these scissors.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  They wanted you to do a little bit of perjury? A.  Yes—I had lent them a pair of scissors—they wanted me to swear I had lent them a pair, and that was true—I cannot tell whether these scissors are mine—they look very much like mine—I believe them to be mine—they were tight when I lent them, they are loose now—they are not uncommon scissors—I have not had mine back.
   MR. PAYNE. Q.  Did you not say first, at the police-office, that you thought they were yours, and then say you thought not, because yours were not broken in the rivet? A.  I said I did not think they were—I did not re member that they were broken in the rivet. AMELIA WILLIAMS. I live in Garrett-lane, Wandsworth. I know Stepney and Moggeridge by sight—on the morning of 6th Nov., Moggeridge came to my house and had some breakfast with me—she brought a basket, and left it at my house—the officer took it—it was in the same state as when she brought it. JAMES WEBB( policeman, V  134.) On 4th Nov. I went with Povey to the house in Garrett-lane, where Stepney and Moggeridge lodge—they were together in bed—I took Stepney into custody—I saw a basket at the side of the bed—I asked Stepney whether the whole of the contents of that basket was her property—she said, "Yes"—I found in it some ribbon, several trifling things, and this pair of scissors produced—I asked if they were hers—she said they were, and said whose did I think they were—I saw Shorlin in Stepney's presence as soon as I got out of the lodging-house—I asked him to describe his scissors—he described them, in Stepney's presence, as being very old and loose in the rivet—he had not seen them then—they corresponded with that description—I took Stepney to the station, and afterwards searched the room.
