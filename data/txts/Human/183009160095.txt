   CHARLES WRIGHT. I live at Enfield-highway. I had a house in Chapel-street, Spitalfields- the prisoner applied to take it, and referred to a person at Islington; I saw him again in a few days, and told him he should not have it - I came to town again four or five days before Midsummer last year; I found he had been to the neighbour, got the key, and taken possession - I went to him; he said my rent should be sure as the day came due - I let him abide: he left my premises on the 1st of March last - I went soon after, and found the articles stated gone; there was a partition that divided the two cellars, the grating, the bad from the gutters, two window-shutters, the bricks and tiles from the wash-house: I have never seen them since.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. How long did the prisoner occupy the house? A. From the latter end of May till the 1st of March; all I got for rent was four guineas - the value of these things was from six to eight pounds; he asked me if I should have any objection to his making improvements - I said I could have no objection to that; he said he was going to make a place to put scaffolding boards in - I never saw him making alterations; I did not say he might make what alterations he pleased - he said he was going to make a shed in the yard; he let the house out in lodgings.
   JOHN COLLINS. I am a basket-maker. I lodged in that house - I saw the prisoner pull up the grating over the area, pull the partition down, and take the bricks away; I did not see him take down the shutters, but he made a show-board for his son with the partition, to put out in the street - it was taken from the premises, but I do not know who took it; the bricks were taken away by the prisoner, in a basket, and lifted on a labourer's shoulder - I do not know when the shutters went, but I think they were gone before; the prisoner lived there till March, and the things might have gone a month or two before - in my judgment the removal of these things could not have been any improvement.
 Cross-examined. Q. It might be a month or two before he left? A. Yes - the bricks were removed between seven and eight o'clock in the morning; the partition was pulled down in the middle of the day.
   WILLIAM WARE. I am a bricklayer. I repaired the house before the prisoner went in; I saw it after he was gone - the grating was then up, the partition down, and the chimney in the wash-house down, the tiles of the wash-house partly off, and the gutter gone.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1669.  JOHN PRIME was again indicted for stealing, on the 24th of June , 6 boards, value 3s.; 1 dresser, value 3s.; 3 shelves, value 1s.; 2 doors, value 1s.; 100 bricks, value 3s., and 1 piece of stone, value 1s., the goods of Mary Stevens , and fixed to a building of hers; against the Statute, &c.
   MARY STEVENS. I am a widow. I have a house in St. Luke's, which I let to the prisoner on the 25th of March last, and he left it on the 24th of June; he had not paid the rent or taxes - they sent me word that he had moved; the house was full of lodgers when I let it to him - my nephew went to the house, and found he was gone; we sent to him - he said he had only moved a few of his things because his wife was gone into the country, and he was going to white-wash the room - that some of his property was there still, and he would pay me the rent on the following Monday; he came on Monday with a man, and said if I would show him the lenity to wait till Saturday, he would pay me, on the word of a man, and he was going to set that man to work - he went away, and I saw no more of him.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I take the house of you? A. Yes, you came to me personally - I saw no repairs that you did.
   SAMUEL YOUNG. I lodge in the house. I remember the prisoner coming and going away - I know he was digging up the kitchen during the time; I never saw him take any thing away - the articles stated are gone.
   RICHARD STEVENS. I am apprentice to Young. I saw the prisoner take the bricks out of the kitchen window, and put them into a barrow; they were taken to a house opposite - I saw the dresser and shelves packed up ready to take away, but did not see them taken; I suppose this was about a month before Midsummer.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not I cut the wood up to lay over the cesspool? A. There was one or two boards laid over the cesspool; they were all sound boards - I saw you take the bricks out, and the stone was taken from the hearth.
   JOHN YOUNG. I saw the prisoner dig up the hearth about a month before he went away, and one of Mr. Smith's men took it to a house opposite, and the bricks likewise - I saw the flooring ready to be taken away.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me white-wash the staircase and colour the front? A. Yes; I did not see you whitewash the kitchen.
   RICHARD SMITH. The prisoner came to a house I was repairing, and asked if I would buy some old bricks of him - I went and looked at them, and agreed to give him 5s. for them; there was a stone with them - I saw no boards or shelves.
   THOMAS VANN. I took the prisoner, and told him it was for stripping the kitchen; he denied it, and said he had it to repair.
 Prisoner's Defence. When I took the house the King's tax-gatherer came to me for 8l. 6s. 8d., which Mrs. Stevens said had been paid; I laid out 3l. or 4l. for repairs - I took nothing away; when I took up the cesspool I took up the floor and covered it - I took the house of young Mr. Stevens, and said, "It is in a very bad state, and the water stands a foot in it - I must have the privy emptied;" he said, "You must be half the expence;" I said, "Very well. I will dig a cesspool," and I took out two loads of rubbish; I began to white-wash and colour the premises; I had not been at work above a day when a gentleman said he should put a broker in for King's taxes; I went to Mrs. Stevens, and she said it was paid, but could not find the receipt - they said they would send in the course of the day; I was going to repair the tiles, but was annoyed by these things, and I left the premises.
   RICHARD SMITH re-examined. Q. When did he sell you the bricks? A. Some time in May.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. - Transported for Seven Years.
