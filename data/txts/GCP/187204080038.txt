Cross-examined. I found a seal-skin purse and a small memorandum-

CHARLES UNDERWOOD (City Detective). I was on Ludgate Hill with
Wines on 29th February, and saw the two prisoners against several ladies
-what Wines has stated is correct-I took Heary into custody, and told
him he would be charged with the female prisoner with picking pockets-I
pointed to her-Wines had her in custody-he said he knew nothing of
her; he had never seen her before in his life; that he only arrived in
London that day-at the station I told him I should have to search him,
and he could give me what he had in his possession-he handed me a purse
containing 151. 10s. in gold, 21. 98. 3d. in silver, 5d. in bronze, a bronze
medal, 58 postage stampe, a knife, and a number of other articles.
Cross-examined. I call this a bronze medal-I have heard that it is &
foreign coin, but I do not know that it is.
ROBERT REODES. I am a coffee-house keeper, at 128, Holborn-the two
prisoners slept at my house together on 27th February.
Cross-examined. I supposed they were man and wife-they addressed
each other so, and appeared to me as such.
WILLIAM MITCHAM. I am a coffee-house keeper, at 22, Charles Street,
Hatton Garden—the prisoners slept at my house together on 28th February
-I concluded they were man and wife,
SABAR GOODWRIGHT. I am the wife of Robert Goodwright, and reside at
Oakhill, Surbiton-on 29th February I was in St. Paul's Churchyard—I
saw the female prisoner quite close to me--I had had my purse in my
pocket shortly before—it contained 98. 6d. in silver, a bronze coin, 12
postage stamps, a small memorandum-book, and two keys, one of them a
watch-key-this is the coin that I had-I can swear to it-it was in the
purse with the other things-putting my hand in my pocket, I found my
purse was gone, and the pocket was cut-I did not see the purse any more
--the only things that I missed and have since identified are this coin and
memorandum-book, which contains entries of deaths.
Cross-examined. I saw the case in the Times, and I wrote at once to the
detective, and when I went up the woman was shown to me-I particularly
noticed her bonnet as she walked close by me, I don't recollect seeing the
man-I had not missed my purse when I saw the woman-I missed it about
five minutes afterwards.
JOSEPH THOMPSON. I am a detective sergeant at Hull—the prisoners
were lodging together in Hull in October, 1870, as man and wife.
Cross-examined. The male prisoner denied that she was his wife ; and
she denied that he was her husband.
GUILTY.
They also PLEADED GUILTY to having been before convicted, McGomery in
October, 1870, and Heary in January, 1871. There were three other indict-
ments against the prisoners. -Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
