SAMUEL WILLIAM PERRY. I ama silversmith mounter. I was in Mellish's
service—I afterwards came into the prosecutor's employ—Mr. Mellish en-
gaged me on 5th Aug., 1850—he settled the amount of my wages, 20s. a
week—I am still in the service ; my wages were raised to 228. a week in
July—from 5th Aug., 1850, to July last year, I did not receive more than
20s. pr week—some time before I was examined before the Magistrate I saw
the wages book on the desk—I looked into my own amount of wages, and
in consequence of what I saw I made a communication to Mr. Thomson
—on the week ending 31st Aug., 1850, I did not receive 248., only 208.,
and also for the week ending on 7th Sept. (MR. CLARKSON stated that by
the wage. book, which gas put in, tie wages of the witness appeared as 24'. a

268. was entered to Perry, Jua.," at that date, which was after Melli8h had

Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. Q. DO you know whether Douglas had
been in the habit of advancing money on loan to the men ? A. I did hear
that he advanced a loan to a man—I do not know it of my own knowledge
—he has not advanced any to me—I once lost time to the amount of a few
shillings, and I asked him not to take it all that week, if he could oblige me,
and he did so till the next week; he never lent me any money.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. If Mellish had looked as you did at that book,

book—I never saw the book to read it but that once—I have seen it on
Mellisb's desk—the wages book used to be referred to before the payments
were made to the servants—it was a part of the duty of Mellish and
Douglas too.

until 23rd—I did not receive my 20s. week in the interval, I belonged to a
club, by which I sustained myself—I received nothing at all except 58. Bd.
