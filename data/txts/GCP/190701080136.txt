Cross-examined. I have had considerable experience in the milk
business. The four days I tried the business were quite sufficient
satisfy me that everything Fowler said was quite false. I went :
the police on October 2, as soon as I found that he did not come back,
I was very angry with him. The first false pretence was that he wa
carrying on a large and profitable business as dairyman. If he was
doing ten barn gallons a day at 4d, a quart it would be a living and I
should not complain of his having said it was a large business. He
was not the first to tell me there was another little shop near where
they were selling milk. I learnt it before he disappeared-before
parted with the final balance. I say that in order to defraud me be
said there was no other milk shop on the estate. He told me he was
doing £15 & week in the shop alone. I think the wife was there &
the time. I complain that he told me he had been in the shop
for eight months. It did not matter much as long as the trade was
there how long he had been there. He told me that he had bought
the round for £200, and I believed him Fourpence a quart gives a
profit of 100 per cent in the summer time. Off the £7 or £8 profit I
should reckon to deduct rent, rates, taxes, and the wages of one man
leaving a net profit of about £4 10s. On the day I went the round
with Endon we called on 100 or 120 people. A great number were
chance customers, and some of the numbers were empty houses. I
have had no conversation with Blay about this case.
Re-examined. The takings for the shop on the five days I had it
open were £4 1s. 44. Fowler guaranteed £15 a week on an average.
