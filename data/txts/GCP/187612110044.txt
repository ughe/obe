Re-examined by MR. COLE. They all live about that neighbourhood -1
never saw Cook in their company-he is not a companion of theirs; I swer
positively that I had nothing to do with the assault in 1871; it was my
brother Henry--the only imprisonment was when I was a boy, and had four
months-in the assault in which I was bound over; I had two or three
words with a man and his wife - I went to fetch my wife away and they
gave me a smack in the face, and we had a fight and the wife summoned
me, and I was bound over to keep the peace towards them--they were not
bound over to keep the peace towards me; I did not summons them--when
Lewis came to arrest Cook, four or five other lodgers were present; Davey
and Lovett, and Rose Cook, and I think Cole was there--this was on the
ground floor-the constable was only there five or ten minutes, and those
people were there during that time--no conversation went on while the
constable was there.
By A JUROR. I reside about half a mile from the cattle market-I never
saw Cook driving cattle, but that is his trade-I do not think he is known
to the police, or whether they would have an opportunity of observing him
driving cattle--when my wife repairs trowsers for the lodgers we do not
generally lock them up in our room all night, but we did not care about
doing them just then, because we were going out marketing-if Cook was
taken ill in the night be would have had to go downstairs in his shirt--we
do not generally keep trowsers all night, this was an exceptional case-I
get up on Sunday mornings, about 7 or 8 o'clock; I do not care about
turning out so soon on Sunday, but the baby kept me up on this night-
there is do knocker on my front door, and there is no key--there are two
bolts—they have no latch keys, they have to knock before they come in, and
they cannot go in and out-the door is open all day long up to the time I
bolt it.
By THE COURT. We always lock the parlour door when we go out, and
we kept it looked at night, because of the baby being ill--we always lock
it when we go to bed, because it is where we sleep.
ARTHUR CLARK. I sell fish and poultry with a cart, and lodge in Wilkin
son's house-I heard of this assault on the next day Sunday, between 1 and
2 o'clock at the Queen's Arms public-house-I came home on the night in
question at 12.20 and went to the room where Cook was, wbere I sleep
Cook was then in bed--this may be the door, my bed is here, Cook's is the
next, and bere is the next, there are three on one side and three on the
other-Cook has a pair of cord trowsers; I never saw more than one pair-
I was talking to Cook till very nearly 1 o'clock in the morning, because I
had sold my pony on that day-I then went to sleep and awoke again when
the landlord came in next morning with a pair of trowsers on his arm--that
was a little after 5 o'clock, about half-past.
