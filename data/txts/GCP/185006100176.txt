Thomas GARFORTH policeman, L 151). On 27th May, about six o'clock
in the morning, I was watching Mr. Bennett's premises with Romain
about seven I saw a cart under the granary-window-I saw a sack of oats
being let down-when the cart was loaded I saw it leave the wharf, and I fol.
lowed it with Romain from the time it left the wharf till it got to Cheyne-
mews I did not once lose sight of it-Ward was driving it-when it got to
Cheyne-mews, it drove down and stopped opposite Rayment's stable--I saw
Ward get on the eart, and hand a sack of oats on to Rayment's shoulder-1
have no doubt there was a word or two passed between them, but not many
words-Rayment took the sack into the stable, and Romain followed bim in
- I took charge of Ward and the cart-1 said to Ward, “You must consider
yourself in my custody, for stealing a sack of oats that is gone in the stable"
- he said, “Steal them, eh! I was ordered to deliver them here by my
employer"--the sack of oats was brought down shortly by Rayment, and
Romain followed him down-I eventually took care of the cart, and Ward
and Rayment were taken by Romain-I and Romain went with the cart
afterwards to the barracks, and Romain took a sample of the oats - after-
wards went to the Belvedere Wharf, and saw Skelton--the foreman asked him,
in my presence, how many oats he had put on Ward's cart that morning-he
said, “Twelve quarters, I think," and after hesitating a little he said, "After
I had given him twelve quarters, as I thought, he said he had not enough by
one sack, and I took a sack and filled it and gave it him"-I then asked him
whether they used sacks with different names on them-he said, "Yes, we
do use odd sacks sometimes"-I said, “Did you have a sack with the name
of Anderson on it?"-be said, “We had a sack with that name"- then
took him into custody- I kept sight of the cart all the time I did not see
Rayment in St. Giles's, or anywhere else, put a sack of oats on the cart.
Cross-examined by MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. Have you refreshed your
memory at all by your depositions, since they were taken? A. No; I wrote
down a part of the deposition, a few words that Skelton made use of in the
loft- I wrote it down, because I had so many things to think of- often do
make a memorandum-Romain is similarly employed- do not know
whether he made a copy of anything I have not seen the depositions, nor
heard them read over-nor copies of them from the time they were made-
yes, I beg pardon, they were read over in my presence at Mr. Bennett's; I
had forgotten that for the moment I have not got the bit of paper here that
I wrote: I left it at home I keep a memorandum of all cases that I have
I look at it before I come out.
Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q. How came you at Mr. Bennett's!

the eab with them, and Romain came and asked if I would take anything
