1696. JANE GILL and JOHN JONES were indicted for stealing 1 cash
box, value 2s. ; 3 pair of sugar-tongs, 24s. ; 1 child's coral, 168. ; 3 necklaces.
101.; 14 rings, 61. 108. ; 1 butter-knife, 123.; 2 veils, 11s. ; 1 pair of spec
tacles and case, 18.; 10 sovereigns, 10 half-sovereigns, 2 half-crowns, and 2
shillings; the property of Elizabeth Barkley, in her dwelling-house; and
THOMAS WEBB was indicted as an accessary to the said robbery.
MR. BALDWIN conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH BARKLEY. I am a widow, and keep a house of accommodation
in Rupert-street, Whitechapel- the prisoners Gill and Webb lodged in my house
for about a fortnight-they left a fortnight before the robbery-I desired
them to leave-on the 17th Gill came to my house-she said she had met a
friend from the Indies, and she was coming that evening to stay-she came
about ten o'clock in the evening with the prisoner Jones-we were in the kitchen
- they fetched some gin and half-and-balf-they went out again and came back
in about an hour--it was before twelve o'clock-(when Gill came she said she
was coming with a friend from India, and told me not to tell Thomas Webb,
as she did not wish him to know when they returned I fetched some brandy
-Gill and Jones made some brandy and water-one made it and the other
sweetened it-I drank half a glass of it, and did not like it-the rest was
chucked away-my servant Woodward drank some of it-Gill and Jones
went out of the room to go to bed, and took a candle and candlestick with
them--their bed-room was on the second floor-I had told Gill the room
she was to go to-she knew all the rooms in the house--it was not the
room she had occupied when she lived there, but she particularly wanted
me to save her the best room-I found afterwards that no room had
been occupied-after I drank the brandy and water I fell asleep-at that time
the outer door of the house was open-it is not usual to go to sleep and leave
the door open--my servant was in the room-I awoke about six o'clock in
the morning, before the servant-I shook her and awoke her-I then went to
my bed-room, which is on the same floor as the kitchen, to go and lay down
- I found the room disordered, and a great hole in the bed like the mark of a
cash-box-I examined my drawers, missed my cash-box, and 15l. 10s. in
gold, and two lace veils-I had seen the things safe the same evening, the
17th--the cash-box had been locked in a drawer, which had been opened as
if unlocked the veils laid on the box-the box contained a number of trin-
kets and jewellery- I found the candlestick which Gill had taken up to go to
bed, in my bed-room-I am sure it was the same-the candle was put out-
there was a small piece of candle left in it-this is the candlestick-the candle
was four or five inches long when they had it-there was a very small piece
left in the morning-it might have burned about an hour I went up to the se-
cond floor room, and found nobody had been in it whatever-nothing was disor-
dered-I am confident no one had slept there--some one slept in the first floor
they are trades folk, a gentleman and lady who always come there-they were
there till one o'clock in the day--no other bed in the house had been slept in
-I know this pair of spectacles by a mark adjoining the hinge, and I know
the case I have had them a long time they were left in my care-I did not
use them I saw them safe on the 16th, the Thursday before I was robbed,
on a small press bedstead in my kitchen-Jones had not been in my house
before to my knowledge-I know this cash-box and ear-rings to be mine-the
ear-rings and the rest of the things were in the box the day before-I am
certain Jones is the man who was there that evening.
