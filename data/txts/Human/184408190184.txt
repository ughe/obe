   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How wide is the footpath? A.  Very wide indeed—the cart overtook us—Jones was on my left side at first—we had changed sides about five or six minutes before this happened—she stopped to pull up the heel of her shoe, or pick up a pin—that did not take her an instant—I suppose we might have got five or six steps after that when this happened, not more—she had then come to my right side—the wheel did not catch her clothes, it struck her on the shoulder—I saw it distinctly—the left wheel struck her violently on the shoulder, and struck her on the ground, and her left temple coming in contact with the stone produced the white mark I saw—we had no particular reason for walking in the kennel instead of the footpath—it was between three and four o'clock in the afternoon—no one else was walking the same way—the child was alongside of me—it was not above three years old.
   MR. HORRY . Q.  Were you on the footpath before you were in the road? A.  No, we were in the kennel all the way. THOMAS GIBLETT. I am a carrier, and live at Frome, in Somersetshire. On the 9th of July, between three and four o'clock, I was in the Stepney-road, near Stepney-green, with Allen—I was going towards King John's Palace, and saw the prisoner in a cart coming from it, driving at a very fast rate, I should say ten or twelve miles an hour—we passed two women—I cannot say whether Wilson was one of them—the prisoner passed me, and I had passed the women—after he had passed me I saw him in the middle of the road, as if thrown from the cart—I then turned, and saw the deceased lying with her head on the curb, about five or six yards from the prisoner—the cart was in the middle of the road—I went up to the deceased, took her up, and placed her head between my knees—blood was flowing from her nose—she did not move, but died almost directly—she was put into the cart, and I got into it—the prisoner was drunk—he drove part of the way to the hospital—he went at a good smart trot—I begged him not to drive so fast, for if there was life left in the woman, he would destroy it—another man got up, and I got out.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you see the cart far? A.  No—I could not say whether the prisoner had been driving all the journey—I had been walking on the footpath—it is a wide one—there are gravel-stones on the footpath—perhaps the road is smoother—after I got out of the cart, I ran after it as fast as I could to the hospital—I was determined not to leave the woman—the road was rather dusty, but not so as to obscure the view of persons—there was a calf in the cart. WILLIAM SELF. I am a surgeon, and live in Lucas-place, Commercial-road. I was on the spot, and saw the wheel come in contact with Mrs. Jones—I saw her soon after she was thrown down—I found her insensible, beginning to change colour, blood issuing from her nose and ear—I imagined that she had fractured her skull, and that turned out to be the case—I was at the post mortem  examination.
