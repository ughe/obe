MARY MITCHELL. I live at Bexley-heath. I was in the Coach and
Horses public-house, at Woolwich, on the morning of the 19th of Februi
ary, between eight and nine o'clock-I was in the tap-room, and saw the
prisoner with a comrade-the prisoner asked me if I would stand a potell
beer, and I did-he asked me if I would stand another-I told him 1
could not afford it, as I had paid for one pot-he then asked me, wil
would not stand a pot, if I would be a pint-I said I would-I had this
bag (looking at one) with me, and a small one contained in the large on
I took the smaller one out of the large one, intending to pay the twopence,
and he caught it out of my hand-he made no more to do, but got out the
back way as quick as possible, and he ran away with it-I followed him
--he ran as hard as he could- I made an alarm, and cried after him-
opened this bag, took the small one out, and flung the large one down a
a poor woman's door-he kept the small one--it contained 198. 2d.--there
were three half-crowns, and the rest in sixpences and shillings- I gave in
formation to Chittenden, the constable- I picked up the large bag before !
told Chittenden.
William Thomas CHITTENDEN. I am a constable of Woolwich. The
prosecutrix complained to me that she had lost her money-I went is
search of the prisoner, and found him in the George the Fourth public
house, about one o'clock at noon- I told him I wanted him he came out
from behind the table where he was sitting-I told him I took him on
charge of robbing a female of some money-he said he knew nothing about
it-he was all right-1 took him into a little room, and searched him-|
found two half-crowns, one shilling, and 9d., in copper.
JOHN ALSFORD. I am beadle of the night-watch at Woolwich. Mary
Mitchell was in my custody on the morning of the 19th of February--se
was sent there as a destitute character by one of the church wardens
the night of the 18th, about eleven o'clock-she had been sitting down
near where the churchwarden lived, and he called one of the watchmer,
and had her detained till the morning, when she was discharged-she had
18s. 6d. in silver, and 31d. in copper--there were three half-crowns, and
the rest in shillings and sixpences.
MARY MITCHELL re-examined. I got the money that was taken from
me by hawking brooms.
SAMUEL WOOD. I am a butcher, but am out of work at present. I wa
in the Coach and Horses in Woolwich when Mitchell was there-she paid
for a pot for me and another man--she treated the prisoner and another
man with a pot-I saw her pull out her bag--the prisoner took it out
her hand, and ran out of the door-at the time I thought it was a joke, bat
he did not return-she went out after him directly-the prisoner was
altogether sober-this was at an early hour in the morning.
Prisoner. The female is telling a great falsity-she says she had 196.
and the officer says she had 18s. 6.
