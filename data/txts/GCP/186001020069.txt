CAROLINE BRAIN. My name was formerly Lovell I am sister to the pri-
soner's first wife-they were married from my house in 1847, I cannot say
exactly the time he lived with her after their marriage, about eighteen
months at his aunt Saunders--they went to Prospect-row, I think, after
that; they went to Bloomfield-row then; then he locked her out of doors,
and she sheltered in my house he took her from my house to Devonshire
I have heard from her lately-I have been corresponding in letters always; for
almost two years she did not write to me-I wrote to the Post-office, Devonshire
--I lost sight of her not quite two years—I did not have any conversation
with the prisoner in 1857-two years ago he had me up to the Magistrate
at Woolwich, because I chastised him about burying the other wife and
walking about with another person; I thought it not right, and my poor
sister alive-I said, "It is very fine of you to pass my house with another
person, and you have just buried a second wife, and my poor sister in Devon
shire not able to get a living"-be said he would very well make me shut
up my noise, and he took out a summons-I never spoke to him but that
once, and he said he would have me up for it, and so he did I never lost
sight of my sister--I have got her here with me I never said anything to
him about supporting my sister-he never offered to do so.
