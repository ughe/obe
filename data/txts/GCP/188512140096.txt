Cross-examined by MR. GILL I had known Webster some years, as a
hard-working, respectable man-I remember the firm of Richardson and
Webster being started on the death of Richardson's father--the business
was in a great measure thrown upon Webster, Richardson was not much
assistanes-I cannot say anything about his drinking, I do not know-
I have made myself conversant with the nature of the business carried
on by Richardson and Webster—the indebtedness to me in 1882 was in
my opinion the result of misfortune-l received bills from them in
reference to hops bought from me the particulars of the transactions
with Kitehiner I know nothing of-I took the bills from Kitchiner in pay.
ment of goods; they were accepted by Richardson and Webster-I did
business with them for four years, to the amount of 4,0001. or 5,0001.
a year, up to 1882—since then I have been on friendly terms with
Webster-I sold them a few goods in 1884-they warehoused hops with
me-I have from time to time discussed with Webster the state of his
affairs-he said that in time he believed he would be able to pay me-
he came to me before the meeting of creditors, to speak to me about the
matter between 1882 and 1884 I sold them large parcels of hops-
I knew that they were doing business with brewers in good position-
the season of 1884 was a very lamentable one; there was a general
depression of trade and a large fall in the value of hopg—when the new
hops come in the old ones get put aside, and would be very much
depreciated and more difficult to sell at the end of 1884 there was a
great fall in the price of hops when Webster came to me he told me he
had been served with a writ for 4001.- I do not think that I had hops
warehoused for them then, I am not sure, there might have been some, at
any rate a very small quantity-Herring and Co. may have been men-
tioned as an asset--at the second meeting something was said about stock
at Birmingham-I do not remember what value was put on it-Mr.
Sendall was there I think I saw a list of the stock at Birmingham-
I do not remember an offer to transfer everything to the creditors—I think
they offered to transfer all the assets to us, whatever they might be-
there was some dispute about Steinberg's debt-the English creditors
would have been willing to take what was offered properly secured, but
the foreign creditors declined---no security was offered-I believe they
offered to pay so much down, and the rest over an extended time-there
