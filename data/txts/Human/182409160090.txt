1411. LYDIA DOWLING, was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , twenty yards of printed cotton, value 20 s., the goods of William May Lander and Thomas Dingley , privately in their shop.
   WILLIAM MAY LANDER. I am in partnership with Thomas Dingley. On the 14th of September, about half-past nine o'clock at night, I saw the prisoner in our shop, she looked at some things, and bought half a yard of plaid, for which she paid 8 d., she then left the shop, I looked at the window and saw something under her arm: I went out and told her she had something she ought not to have; she said she had got it, and if I would say nothing about it she would give it up to me, but she did not; I took her to Queen-square, and the print was taken from her there; she is, I believe, a respectable woman.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined One Month.
 1478.  JOHN COOK was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , a bonnet, value 16 s. 6 d, the goods of Mary Balaam, widow.
   MARY BALAAM. I live at Bull Cross. My little girl was sent on an errand with her bonnet on on the 16th of August, about four o'clock, and she came home without it.
   PHILLIS BALAAM. I am thirteen years of age. On Saturday, the 16th of August, I was playing in a barn with some children, and pulled off my bonnet, and hung it on a nail in the barn: I went on Sunday night to look for it, and it was gone; I was going on an errand on Monday morning, and met a woman with it.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   JAMES CLARKS. I live at Mr. Cole's, Bull Cross. This little girl lives servant there; my master sent me after this man, when she said she had lost her bonnet; I pursued him three miles, and found him with a bonnet and a gown; he said he had bought the bonnet, and he would not deliver it up till he had found the man he bought it of, who lived at Mr. Bottomly's. This was about nine o'clock on Monday morning.
   JOHN MEAD. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner. The bonnet was given by me to Clarke before the Magistrates.
 Prisoner's Defence. A man came up to me as I was going to Waltham Abbey; he goes by the name of Baker or  John Russel- my wife said he had a bonnet to sell - if I would buy it she would be obliged to me.
   JOHN MEAD. I made enquiry for the person he said he had bought it of, but could not find any such person - the prisoner has no child of an age to wear this bonnet.
     GUILTY. Aged 25.
 1479.  JOHN COOK was again indicted for stealing, on the 13th of August , a gown value 1 s.; six aprons, value 4 s.; and six towels, value 2 s., the goods of Henry Hill.
   CATHERINE HILL. My husband lives at Bull-cross. I hung out a gown to dry on Thursday, the 12th of August; we missed it next morning with some other things.
 (Gown produced and sworn to.)
   JAMES CLARKE. I took the prisoner with the gown on his back; he wanted to leave his bundle behind him at the lodge at Mr. Bottomly's; but as we were coming back I met some gentlemen, who asked me if he had a bundle. I went back and got the bundle, which the prisoner afterwards opened - the gown was in it - he said he had swapped away a jacket for it.
   JOHN MEAD. The prisoner said he bought the gown of the same man he bought the bonnet of.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 Before Mr. Recorder.
 1480.  WILLIAM ROLFE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , at Littleton, a cow, price 3 l., the property of Mary Rutter, spinster.
   JAMES WARREN. I am in the employ of Mary Rutter; she is a single woman, and lives at Littleton, near Chertsey. She had a large red cow, with wheel horns; she was full of milk. I had been suckling her between six and seven o'clock in the evening of the 5th of August; she then went into the field, and I missed her at five the next morning. There is a large hedge round the field. The gate was closed. I saw the cow again on the Saturday evening at Mr. Lansdown's.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was this a good milch cow - A. Yes; she was worth 5 l. or 6 l. to us, but would not fetch more than 3 l. 10 s. in the market. The boy who has the care of the field generally pins the gate; he is not here. I did not put her into the field, nor go in again that night. I have known the prisoner some time, he is a man who sells cows and calves; we have had dealings in that line together. I have known the cow and had the care of her for fourteen years.
   THOMAS CURTIS. I am hostler at the Coach and Horses, public-house, on the Hounslow-road. I saw the prisoner at our door with a red cow, with wheel horns on the 5th of August, about twelve o'clock at night. Our house is five or six miles from Littleton. He asked me if there were any Chertsey people there; I said there was Blake and Turner there; they are butchers of Chertsey. He said,
 "I believe I shall not stop, I shall go gently on - good night."
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he say, tell Blake and Turner I am gone - No, he did not tell me to tell them, or not to tell them. I went into the house about twenty minutes after, and told them what he said. He did not say what road he was going, but he went up towards London. He had got the cow in a string; he was not more than two minutes with me. I saw that it was a red cow, with wheel horns - there are many cows of that description. I have known him twelve years. He always had an honest character.
