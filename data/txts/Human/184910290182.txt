   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  When you lost your purse you looked for it on the ground? A.  Yes; I presumed I had dropped it—I could not find it—the policeman came up while the bother was—the purse was found at the station-house sticking out at the prisoner's collar, he had got his coat buttoned up, and the purse hung out between the button holes, any one might see it—the prisoner was in liquor. FREDERICK BAKER. I am an inspector of police—the prisoner was brought to the station, which is a quarter of a mile from the spot where this occurred—I observed this purse sticking from the breast of his coat—I think it could not have got there during the struggle, because the part the money was in, was under the coat—his coat was similar to what he has on now, but it was not buttoned up quite so high—the prisoner said he knew nothing at all how it came there. ROBERT BARNETT( re-examined ). When he struck me I put out my hand to defend myself as well as I could—I did not see him make any attempt to rob me. JOSHUA GOLDING( policeman, M  87). The cries of the children brought me to the place, when I went the prisoner and prosecutor were both on the ground, not one over the other—the prosecutor charged the prisoner with assaulting him—I took the prisoner, and immediately the prosecutor said he had lost his purse—he looked on the ground for it with a candle—I saw the inspector find the purse—I had not seen it before—I had searched the prisoner's pockets, but I had not a light. WILLIAM BEAZLEY. I am a cabman, the prosecutor hired me from the stand that morning to take him home, saying his wife was very bad—I took him home to the door, and he was paying me the money, he was asking me for a sixpence—the prisoner came along and shoved against him—one word brought up another—one asked the other if he could fight, and away they went at it—they were both down, and rolled over two or three times, and when the prosecutor was at the bottom, the other man hit him—the prosecutor said that man bad hit him—the policeman catched  him off the prosecutor—the prosecutor came to me, and said, "I have dropped my purse, I wish you would look for it;" which I did with a light, I could not find it—they got to the station, and came and fetched me.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Have you ever been in trouble? A.  I have had summonses connected with the ordinary business of cab -driving—I have not been in any other trouble—I have never been charged with stealing—I drive a good deal of valuable property—I do not know whether you call it stealing—I carried the job , I will not deny—I was to be bailed out, but I could not get the bail—it was no robbery on my side, the job the things belonged to was a robbery—I was coming by and was called, and carried the job—I had been to Greenwich, and took up this job at the Broadway, Deptford—I took the people to the place I was ordered, and put them down—I do not know whether they escaped.
   COURT . Q.  Were you not charged with having stolen goods in your possession, and facilitating the escape of the persons? A.  That was the charge, I believe—I was not to know whether it was stolen. NOT GUILTY.
