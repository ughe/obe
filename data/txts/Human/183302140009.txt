I had the key with me then, and they told me to be sure and try it, and give it to them at the Magpie and Stump, Fetter-lane - I had not before that had an opportunity of trying the key; I went home and left Smith with them - when I got home my father was in the drawing-room; I went into the room - it was agreed that I should leave the door ajar, and return to them at the Magpie and Stump, and give them the key if it fitted, and that was to be a signal that I had left the door open; there is a fan-light over the door, and they were to put a piece of paper on the fan-light, as a signal for Pearce that they were ready to come out - Pearce was to be outside, Coney and Cook were to be in the warehouse, and the tall man, at the kitchen stairs; Smith was one of the party on the Sunday evening, and I asked him if he should be at the house with them; he said No, he would not, and he would never sell his liberty for such a thing as that - I tried the key while my father was up stairs in the drawing-room; it fitted - I was to meet them at the Magpie and Stump between eight and nine o'clock, and went there; I left the door ajar, as I had agreed - I was to deliver the key to one of them at the tap-room in Fetter-lane, as a signal that the door was ajar, but I found the tall man at the door of the Magpie and Stump; I gave it to him, and he said "We can't find George," meaning Coney - Coney came running up directly, and whispered to him; I then walked down Bartlett's-buildings, and saw Coney and another man (I cannot say whether it was Cook or not) dodge round the bricks to go in at the door; Coney was nearest the lamp and I saw his face - they went in before me; they were to hide themselves behind the counter in the warehouse till I was in bed - I went home directly and found the door open, and shut it; while I was at Coney's on the Sunday, I asked what the whipcord was for, and they said it would be useful, and I saw Pearce sharpening his knife - I asked the use of every thing; he was sharpening his knife on a new whetstone - I believe this is the knife; the life-preserver was on the table - I saw Coney at one end of the room, tying something, but I could not tell what, and just before I left I saw the life-preserver laying on the table, mended in the state it is now; Smith had it in his hand, and struck me with it several times in play, and said it would hit any body a good crack - I went to bed about nine o'clock, and after I was in bed I heard a ring at the bell, and a knock, and heard a man's voice; I do not know the watchman's voice - I had been in bed I should think three quarters of an hour, or an hour - I had not gone to sleep; shortly after that I heard footsteps running up the stairs, and the person that ran up tried one of the doors - he made a noise in running up - it appeared a man's footstep; I returned the Forget Me Not to Coney a few days before the robbery; I gave it into his own hands - I did not see the dog next morning till about twelve o'clock; I remember Mr. Wheeler's making inquiry on the Monday about this, and again on the Tuesday, and I made a statement to him, and afterwards saw Forrester and Cope - I disclosed to them what I knew, and where Coney lived; I afterwards accompanied them to where he lived, and to the public-house in Clare-market, where he was apprehended, and then went with them to No. 47, Red Lion-street.
   DANIEL FORRESTER. I took Smith on the Tuesday, before Coney was taken.
   JOSHUA JOHN JAMES re-examined. I went to a house in Great Mitchell-street with Smith; I waited outside while he went in; it was before the robbery - I have been there with him several times, and he told me the property was to be taken there - it is at the corner of Brick-lane, and a grocer's shop; the last time I went there with Smith, he said, "Mr. Wheeler little thinks that some of his prime stuff is coming here."
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is it for fear you have not done mischief enough to Smith that you add that, without being asked? A. No; Smith is fifteen years old - I should not have done this, if I had not been frightened at being muzzled or scragged - I was afraid of their threatening; I was not so much afraid at the beginning as at last - I never was with them in any robbery, not with Coney; I am sorry to say I have committed a robbery.
 Q. Will you tell us how long it is since you began? A. I do not know - it is about three years ago.
 Q. I should not be surprised if you do not say it was Smith brought you into all the robberies? A. He did into this; I have done wrong things three years ago, and have been robbing at different times - I once took 1s. from my father; I stole it - I have taken some books from him, and have taken halfpence several times; I do not remember taking any thing particular of my mother's; I have stolen things from her three or four times, but do not remember what they were.
 Q. Was it because you repented of this that you told? A. It was, Sir, I assure you; I was afraid something would happen to me if I did not tell - it was through fear and repentance both; I was afraid of being taken up - I have been in the infirmary at the Compter ever since this.
 Q. You have said a great many things here which you did not state at the Mansion-house? A. I was not questioned so much then; I was sworn to tell the whole truth; what I said was taken down in writing - I do not remember whether I said there, that Smith kept the key to prevent my father seeing it; I do not think I did - nobody has refreshed my memory in the prison; I have seen Mr. Wheeler there, and my father and mother - I did not state at the Mansion-house about leaving their tools there to show it had been done by the profession - that is the expression they used; I forgot to state it - I did not say that Cook said he would muzzle me, because I was not asked, nor that the tall man was to stop at the kitchen door, but Mr. Wheeler has all that in writing - I did not mention their asking me to poison the dog, nor about the barker, nor about Coney mending the socks, that I recollect; I was not so long at the Mansion-house as I have been here - I was about a quarter of an hour being examined.
