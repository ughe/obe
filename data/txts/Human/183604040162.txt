1007. JOHN DAVIS and GEORGE RAVENHILL were indicted for stealing, on the   7th of March , at   St. Leonard, Shoreditch , 4 coats, value 7 l  .;1 pairs of trousers, value 2 l  .; 2 waistcoats, value 1 l  . 5 s  .; 5 handkerchief, value 6 s  .; 1 brooch, value 7 s  .; 2 pairs of stockings, value 3 s  .; 1 scarf, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 pair of braces, value 6 d  .; 1 spoon, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 stiffener, value 4 d  .; the goods of Robert Gunn , in his dwelling-house. ROBERT GUNN. I live in Maidstone-street, Hackney-fields, in the parish of Shoreditch. I rent the house—the prisoner Davis's mother is my wife—On the 7th of March I left home about ten minutes to seven o'clock in the evening, leaving my wife at home—I had some boxes safely locked, in my bed-room—I returned at about a quarter-past seven o'clock in the morning—I am on night work at the gas-works—I missed all the articles stated in the indictment, worth about 7 l . together—two locks of the boxes were broken—Davis lodged in the house—the other prisoner did not—the things were all safe in the boxes before I left the house, and the boxes locked—Davis was not in the house when I left—I have not seen him since that night, till 13th—a twopenny-post letter came on the night of the 8th, with three duplicates in it—my wife gave it to me next morn ing, and I delivered it and the duplicates to Sergeant Glibbery. ANN GUNN. I am the prosecutor's wife. Davis is my son by a for mer husband—on Monday, the 7th of March, he came home about nine o'clock, George Ravenhill came with him—he said, George was come for me to go to his mistress, because she was very had; and George said I was to go to his mistress directly—he lived where Davis worked—her name is Mrs. Standfast—Ravenhill said, I had better go directly, because him mistress was very bad—I said I did not like to go very well, but did not like to disoblige Mrs. Standfast—he said, she would not want me above a couple of hours—I left Davis at home taking tea—Ravenhill went with me as far as Birdcage-walk, in Hackney-road—Mrs. Standfast lives in Norton Falgate—he left me in Birdcage-walk, saying, he was going to fetch Mr. Standfast's brother's wife—I went to Mrs. Standfast, which is nearly a mile from Birdcage-walk, and found her very well—I returned home directly—my son was not there—I did not find any body—the house was empty—I found my husband's boxes broken, and his clothes taken—I missed a tea-spoon which my son had used at tea—he did not return that night—I told my husband of this in the morning—on Tuesday 1 received a letter by the post, with three duplicates in it—this is it—my son came home on Saturday night—I said, "Is it you, John?"—he said, "Yes"—that was all he said—I asked him how he came to do this, and he did not answer me—my husband told the policeman to take him when be saw him—and he was taken. HUGH HENRY CAMPION. I am shopman to Mr. Fry, a pawnbroker, in High-street, Shadwell. I produce two coats and a black silk handkerchief, pawned on the 8th of March, in the name of John Jones—I think the prisoner Ravenhill was present at the time—I do not know him, but I have some recollection of seeing him, at the time the things were pawned, in the shop, and he offered me a white silk scarf to pledge—I did not take it in, because he wanted more on it than I considered it was worth—here is the counterpart of the duplicate for the coats and handkerchief—this one is what I gave the person who pawned the articles—I do not think Davis is the man.
