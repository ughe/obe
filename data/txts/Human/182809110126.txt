 Cross-examined. Q. Have you known the prisoner any time? - A. Yes, for years; he knew I was an officer; I did not know that he referred people to his father's house in consequence of the obscure situation he lived in; I searched his place and his wife, but found nothing.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am innocent - on my oath I never saw the money; and I do not believe the prosecutor ever had it.
   MARY GRIFFITHS. I was twelve years of age last August; I remember a piece of work being made about some money which Mrs. Towell said she had lost - she accused me of taking it; she offered me a sovereign to tell her the colour of the pocket-book; I know nothing about the pocket-book; she said if I did not mention some colour for the pocket-book, my father and mother should be transported; she said she would send for the street-keeper to take me into custody - she did not at first mention about any notes at all; she said at first she had lost a pocketbook with some silver and sovereigns in it - she did not make any mention about notes to me; she kept me about an hour and a half; she afterwards gave me 6d., and told me to go home - she told me not to take any notice, not to tell my mother and father - but I did tell my mother; as I was coming down the steps she told me not to tell my father and mother anything about it; she said she had laid this money under her pillow - I do not know the prisoner.
   RACHAEL GRIFFITHS. I am the mother of Mary Griffiths. I remember Mrs. Towell accusing her of stealing this money; she told me in the conversation I had with her, that she put this money into the drawer on Saturday morning, and did not miss it till Sunday afternoon, when she went to the drawer to get out her brooch, and then missed the pocket-book; upon my oath, as an honest woman, she told me that; I did not hear her accuse the child, but I went to know what she meant by it, and to know about the pocket-book that was lost; I asked her how she came to accuse my child of it - she said she thought she had put it under her pillow as she usually did - but she admitted she had accused the child.
 COURT. Q. Are you or your husband acquainted with the prisoner? A. His father kept a grocer's shop close by, but we have not spoken to him for some years.
 MR. PHILLIPS to  HENRY TOWELL. Q. Did you yourself ever tell any body, or say in the presence of any body, that your wife had missed this on the Sunday when she went to look for a brooch? A. I did not; I never said it before Forbes or any body.
   JOHN FORBES. I am street-keeper. I know Towell - I remember meeting him on the subject of this money; I asked him when the money was missed, and he said "We had no occasion to go to that drawer, but we were going out in the afternoon to meet some friends; I wanted a little more money; I did not consider I had enough in my pocket, and she was going to give me some more."
 COURT. Q. When was this conversation? A. Between six and seven o'clock on the Sunday evening - what he said was, they had no occasion to go to that drawer, but they were going out in the afternoon, and he considered he had not money enough in his pocket, and he asked his wife to procure him some; it was the Sunday afternoon he spoke of - this was after I found the drawer had been broken.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Who did he say were going out in the afternoon? A. His wife and him; he said they were going out that same afternoon, and then they missed it; he said they had no occasion to go to that drawer, but he considered he had not money enough, and he was going out that afternoon to meet some friends; he did not say anything to me about a brooch.
 COURT. Q. Was Mary Griffiths there? A. Yes; I went in with Mr. Towell, and the child was crying; I went directly to the parlour, and the bolt had been shot; Mrs. Towell said she was very happy to think she had not given the girl into custody: when I first met Mr. Towell, he told me he had been seriously robbed; I went with him to Griffiths's house, and from there we returned to Towell's, and the girl was crying - Mrs. Towell said she suspected the girl, that she had been robbed by a former girl, and she owned it to the officer afterwards; he said he considered he had not money enough in his pocket, and he sent his wife to get him some - that she went to put the key into it and it would not unlock; he did not say what money his wife had given him; I have known the prisoner twelve or thirteen years; I have not been particularly intimate with him - he has not been living in that neighbourhood lately.
 Mr. PHILLIPS to  MARY GRIFFITHS. Q. What time on the Sunday morning did you go to Mrs. Towell's? A. A little after seven o'clock, and stayed till about three - Mrs. Towell was there, but she did not in all that time make any charge against me; it was half-past five or six o'clock before I was sent for.
   WILLIAM CHAPMAN. I am the prisoner's father; I saw Mrs. Towell at the Police-office, I heard her say, "There is your father, let him pay me the money, and I will not hurt a hair of your head;" my son has lived in rather an obscure situation, and his letters and parcels have been directed to me.
