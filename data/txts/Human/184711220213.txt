Cross-examined. Q.  It was in the yard that you saw him left off the Roman candles? A.  Yes—it is a small yard—I certainly did not ask him to let them off—I saw them—he said he always worked them damp—he had them rolled up in a wet cloth—I did not see them on the hob, or anything of the kind—I have seen him take them out of the cloth, and fold them, and I have seen him tying them up—I never saw him put them on the hob—I cannot say how long the prisoner's brother had been working there—I saw the prisoner put a quantity of gunpowder on the hen-house on one occasion to dry—I never saw anything put there to dry but the powder—that was on the Thursday, the day before the explosion—I saw him filling the squibs by the hen-house—what I saw in his room was about a week before the accident—I saw Vincent let off a squib in the yard, on the Tuesday before—the boys worked in the same room with the prisoner—I saw them at work at fireworks—I have only known him by his coming to the house—he had lodged there I should say about two months—I believe he was very much hurt, and in jured himself—he has been in the hospital till this time. GEORGE BOWN. I lodged in the house, and was present when the ex plosion took place—I have known the prisoner five or six weeks, during which time, he, his wife, and the two boys, have been employed in making fireworks—I do not know what they did with the cases when they had made them—they were little round cases—I saw them rolling them one morning, and at another time I saw them filling them, with what I supposed to be gunpowder.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know the age of the prisoner's brother? A.  About 16. CAROLINE BOWN. I am the wife of the last witness—I was at home when this explosion took place—I was just going to have my tea when the door was burst open—on the day before, the prisoner called me to the window of his room, which was open, to look at some candles that he was making—I am not certain whether they were Roman candles—I understand that is the name they go by—I went—he told me to look at them, and he threw a little bit of something black, about the size of the end of my finger, into the fire, to show me how it went off—it made a buzzing noise, and went up the chimney—I said to him, that it was a very dangerous business, and I did not much like it, and many accidents happened from it—he said he never had met with any himself—I said, "It is that that gives you courage, "and he said, "Yes"—that was all that passed—I know fireworks when I see them—I have seen squibs, crackers, and Catherine wheels about at times, since the prisoner came to live there—I saw one of the Roman candles in the yard, by the dust-hole—I did not see who did it—the prisoner was there at the time, and I have seen him in his room making squibs and crackers.
