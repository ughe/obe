CHARLES IRVING. I am a Member of the Royal College of Surgeons
and a Licentiate of the College of Physicians-I practise at Tunbridge-
on Sunday, 30th September, I was called to see the deceased, George
Breeze, and I went at 5 o'clock in the evening-he was in his bedroom
on the bed, but not quite undressed—he was suffering from the effects of
a wound on the inner side of the knee, a little above the knee, and
from the commencement of delirium tremens-I saw the wound, it
was inflamed all up the thigh to the groin-he was partially delirious
then he would rest for a moment, but every second or third question he
would answer irrationally-I ascribed that to delirium tremens, and
prepared for an attack of delirium tremens, as those are the beginning of
the symptoms, and I also treated his leg-I had never seen him before
in my life I noticed nothing else at that time but the delirium and the
inflammation of his leg—I next saw him on the following morning,
Monday; his condition was then worse in every respect--I had pre-
scribed for him on the Sunday-I treated him with narcotics to try and
induce sleep and endeavoued to cut off his alcohol, but it was not cut off
-it is common for persons to be afraid of carrying out the doctor's orders
-by degrees I stopped it altogethermy orders were slightly disobeyed,
I found that he had whisky or port wine, but not in any large quantity
-on the Monday the symptoms of delirium tremens were more marked-
I saw him next on the Tuesday morning, when his condition was worse
in every respect—his leg was decidedly much worse, there was a great
deal of swelling and inflammation—I was with him more than an hour
on the night he died, which was early on the Wednesday morning, and I
had also seen him on the Tuesday evening earlier-I was not with him
when he actually died, but I was within an hour of his death-he was
simply comatose and moribund when I last saw him-I was called in to
give a certificate of his death, and my first certificate was delirium tremens
simply; I afterwards added to that that the exciting cause of the delirium
tremens was a wound on the leg-I express my opinion now that the
delirium tremens was caused by the wound.
By the COURT. I think the delirium tremens would not have come on at
all if it had not been for the wound-the wound provoked the delirium
tremens, and but for that he would not have died—I am able to say that
the alcohol he took afterwards did not aggravate the symptoms he took
very little. Q. If you were able to say that but for what he took himself
after the wound he would not have had delirium tremens that would make
a great difference -A. I believe it was very slight, and it would have
