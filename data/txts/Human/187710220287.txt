   Pateman's Statement before the Magistrate. "  On Saturday night I went to Turner's house with three more, and I tied one donkey to his scraper—when I went out I got on the donkey and went up the road, when my saddle slipped off, and I jumped, off, ran forward, and turned back the other two donkeys and buckled my saddle up, and went back towards Turner's house, and turned to the left, went to Swanley Bottom, and went home."
   Reynolds's Statement.  After I left Turner's house, me and my brother got up on a donkey a piece, and galloped along past Beenham's house—we stopped there to see if Pateman was coming, and we then went home, tied the donkeys up, had our supper, and went to bed—I didn't see Pate man till Sunday morning between eight and nine."
   The following witnesses were called for the Defence. GEORGE REYNOLDS. I am the brother of the prisoner Thomas—I am also a donkey proprietor and driver, and live near Bromley—it is the custom of myself and brother to go to Swanley Junction with our carts—I did so last summer—on the 14th July I was with Walter Pateman, and we went to the White Hart at Orpington at about 9 a.m., and remained there till 5 p.m.—Orpington is the next station below Chisle hurst, and about three and a half miles from Lullington Castle—when we left at five, we went to Birchwood; we had four donkeys with us—we then went to Swanley, where a society called the Swanley Club meets—it met that night, and we went to the meeting—my brother was at the Club; we met him not much past six—I suppose we left the club at 8.10—the two prisoners, myself, and a man named Bilsby, and we went to the Woodman (Mr. Turner's)—we did not stop there long; we had one pot of beer—we divided our donkey money and went home, straight along that road to the Lullington Castle ( showing on the plan )—we did not go in—we left Pateman behind us, between Mr. Beenham's farm and Mr. Mill's, I remaining with my brother—Bilsby remained with us till we turned into our road, which leads the Heathfield farm—we went to the house-carts, to my mother's first—we told Bilsby to take the donkeys to Swanley—he turned to go; we never turned to see—I suppose it was not quite nine o'clock—it was 8.45 when we came out of Mr. Turner's house, and we had a very short distance to go—we are just at the top of the railway—if trains pass by we could hear them—a train came in soon after we got home, I suppose what they call the 5 to 9 train—when we got to my mother's house there was William Pateman at home, the prisoner's brother, who is married to one of my sisters—my brother stepped indoors—we remained the night—I came out to go and tie my pony up—my brother was up in the van dressed—presently I came back again, and my brother-was still there, and then we stopped all night—my mother was left in the van when I went to tie up the pony—when we first got to the house-cart, Mary Reynolds, my wife, was there, William Pateman, his wife, my sister—I was present when Terry arrested, my brother—some little time after Terry called upon me—Sergeant Fisher was there, and I was arrested, and was handcuffed to my brother and Pateman—I was discharged on Tuesday.
