 2030. EDWARD KELLY , stealing 1 box, value 2 s  ., and 841bs. weight of cocoa, the goods of the London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway Company . ( See page  858).
   MESSRS. CLARKSON and HUDDLESTON conducted the Prosecution. JOSHUA NUNN I am warehouseman to Francis Chambers and Henry Lambert, Tower Royal, Watling-street, cocoa-manufacturers. On 26th Jan. I sent a tea-chest with 3/4ewt. of cocoa to the Nag's Head to William Stone and Son, Newhaven, by the goods train, via Lewes—I was here at the trial of Garratt—I then saw a portion of the box which I had sent the cocoa in. JAMES WOOD. I am warehouseman at the Nag's Head, Borough. On the evening of 26th Jan., a box was brought from Chambers and Lambert, directed to Stone and Son, Newhaven—I signed the deli very-book—next day it was sent to the New Cross station, to be sent by the railway. GEORGE GROVES. I am in the service of the London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway. I was at work at the station between five and six o'clock—on 27th Jan. I saw Garratt there, who was afterwards tried in this Court—I saw a chest which had the appearance of a tea-chest amongst the goods on the railway—it was directed to Stone and Son, Newhaven—I saw Garratt handle the chest, and I saw him go off the platform—I did not see him take anything with him—I missed the chest, and gave information. JAMES VOLLER. I am a porter at the New Cross station. About five o'clock, on 27th Jan., I remember a small cart coming with twenty-five tubs of butter—the prisoner drove it—he gave me a delivery-note for a larger num ber of casks than he had brought, and he said his mate would be there shortly with the remainder—after the prisoner's cart was unloaded Garratt brought the remainder of the tubs of butter in a large cart—I signed the note and gave it to Garratt—the prisoner left the shed first. JAMES CORRALL. I am one of the foremen of the Goods Department of the London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway. On the evening of 27th Jan., I received information from Green, and went in pursuit of two carts—Garratt drove the larger cart—I cannot say who drove the other—I went to the Lord Wellington, which is about a mile and a half from New Cross—I saw the two carts standing at the door—I jumped into the smaller cart, and found the chest covered with two tarpaulins and a mat—I found a part of the direction on it—the rest appeared to have been torn off—that was produced when Garratt was tried—there were no carmen with the carts when I got there; Chapman the ostler brought out Garratt—I waited a quarter of an hour to see it the driver of the smaller cart would come, but he was gone away.
