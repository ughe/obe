I should consider so - it must have taken me more than half an hour to go there from Shoreditch church - I was a good deal agitated, but I am not deceived in the person; I did not say before the Magistrate that the man had a light jacket on - I always said it was a dark coat; another person was taken from my description, but that was not the person.
   MARY CRAWLEY. I am sister to Mrs. Clarck. I was with her in Brick-lane - I am positive the prisoner is the person who took the parcel; my sister appealed to the people about to assist, but nobody did; I saw the prisoner's face - he had a dark coat on, a shabby black hat, and corderoy trousers.
 Cross-examined. Q.I suppose you were a good deal frightened? A. Yes, but I noticed the person of the prisoner; I did not see him again for eleven days - I am sure my sister did not say he had a light jacket on, or a light coat.
   MARY ANN FOSTER. I am the wife of John Foster- we live in London-street, London-road; the prisoner's father and mother occupy my front parlour - they have lodged with me four months; the prisoner lived with them. I recollect, on the 10th of April, I went into their room for rent - I saw the prisoner and his mother there; she was ill in bed, and was mending his trousers, which I believe were corderoy or fustian, and he had a short jacket on, either fustain or corderoy - it was a dark colour; my house is a mile and a half from Wentworth-street; as near as I can judge it was twelve o'clock, for directly I left I went to get my little boy from school; I left the prisoner in the room, waiting for his trousers.
   SAMUEL PRENDERGRASS. I am an officer of Lambeth-street. On the 10th of April I was in Old Montague-street, opposite to Wentworth-street - I went to a public-house, and saw the prosecutrix there, complaining of her loss; she was much agitated - I asked her particularly the description of the person, in consequence of which I apprehended a man in five or six minutes afterwards; she said he was not the man - she then described the prisoner; I looked at my watch when I had been two or three minutes in the house - it was half-past twelve o'clock, but I afterwards found my watch was a quarter of an hour too slow.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you make an entry of the charge in the book? A. Yes - she said he had a fustian or a light jacket on, that he was about five feet four inches high, and of a pale complexion.
   JAMES JOHN CONNOLEY(Police-constable H 135). I received information on the 13th of April, and on the 14th I saw the prisoner coming out of Wentworth-street with two others - I followed them; the prisoner was going to turn into a passage on the right-hand side, towards Whitechapel, but he saw me, left his companious, and crossed the road; I went to him, and said, "I think I want you, I am not certain - will you go with me to Bethnal-green-road?" he asked what I wanted with him - I said that was not a usual thing to state there, because if he was not the person it would be generally circulated; he then went with me to No. 51, Bethnal-green-road, where Mary Crawley lives - she came down stairs, and before she got into the shop, she said, "That is the man that robbed my sister;" he said,"You must be mistaken;" I took him to the station, booked the charge, and he was sent to the office - he was remanded till the following Saturday, when Mrs. Clack attended - there were sixteen or twenty persons together, but she picked him out from the others; she said she was quite certain of him by his face.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was not nearer to Whitechapel on that day than London-bridge; I then went to Euston-square, to see for my uncle; I did not leave my father till past one o'clock.
   MARY ANN COLLINS. I live in the same house with the prisoner. On the 10th of April I met the prisoner and his father in the London-road, about half-past twelve o'clock; I was going to the public-house for my dinner beer when my father came home - there is a clock at the public-house.
 COURT. Q. Are you quite sure you noticed the clock? A. Yes, I went on purpose to see the time and for the beer; they were going towards the Elephant and Castle.
   JOSEPH BULL. I am the prisoner's father. On the 10th of April I left my house with my son, at twenty minutes past twelve o'clock, and we came to a public-house in the Borough.
 COURT. Q. Had you seen Mrs. Foster before you left? A. Yes I had; we came from the London-road towards the Elephant and Castle - we met  Mary Ann Collins; we came over to St. George's church, and went into a wine-vaults there - I had a pint of ale for my dinner; I had brought my own victuals from home, as my wife was very ill, and that is a house I always call at; I was coming to my brother's, in Golden-lane - I did not go near Wentworth-street; I left my son at Cannon-street, and told him I did not want him to go any further.
     NOT GUILTY.

   WILLIAM BURLEY. I am postillion to Lord Beverley, who lives in Portman-square. I missed two sovereigns out of a portmanteau in April, and then I missed some silver - the coachman then marked a sovereign and ten shillings, and I put them in the portmanteau from which I had lost the other money; I went to it at half-past eight o'clock the next morning, which was the 8th of May, and I missed a marked shilling and a marked sovereign from there; I asked the prisoner to lend me a shilling, which he did - I asked him to lend me another, which he did, and the second one was the marked shilling I had missed from the portmanteau; it must have been got by forcing open the portmanteau at one end of the lid.
 JAMES FRUIN. I am servant to Lord Beverley's eldest son. On the 8th of May the prisoner asked me for change for a sovereign, which I gave him - the sovereign I received from him I gave to the officer.
   JOSEPH WELLS. I am coachman to Lord Beverley. I marked a sovereign and ten shillings; this is the sovereign, and this is one of theshillings.
