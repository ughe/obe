I met him in Upper Street, Islington, about three years ago, and he was sent to penal servitude about a year or 18 months after 1880—he got five years, but from what I hear the Earl of Enskilling did something to get three years of his sentence remitted. ALEXANDER CLYOE( Police Sergeant A  35). In June, 1885, I was warrant officer for the Marylebone Police-court—I recollect the prisoner coming to me there for a warrant for the apprehension of her husband, William Patten, and he was bound over in his own recognizances in 50 l . for six months to keep the peace to his wife. JOHN SUMMERS( Police Sergeant N ) About 6.30 on 22nd November I saw the prisoner in Chapel Street, Clerkenwell, and told her I should arrest her for marrying Henry Smith, her husband, William Patten, then being alive—she said "I thought he was dead, I have not seen him for 10 years, and Smith knew all about it." GUILTY.— One Day's Imprisonment.
