MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. JANE STEVENSON. I am in the service of  Mr. M'Niel, of Charlton; the prisoner came into his service on the 17th of March; he had been a week or ten days before working in the garden. On the 17th the plate was given to him to lay on the table, and after that I said I would wash it—he said, "Never mind, I will"—he washed it and took it up stairs—on wednes day, the 20th of March, my master left home, leaving my young master, the the prisoner and me in the house—I went to bed about a quarter-past nine o'clock, and about two next morning I was awoke by a call of "Murder and thieves"—I heard the prisoner say, "For God's sake, come and help me"—I got a light, alarmed my young master, and went down—I found the pri soner in the kitchen—he had his trowsers and his stockings on, but no shoes, coat, or waistcoat—I was so frightened, I do not well remember what he said—I saw no thieves—the prisoner was taken into custody the same morning—I missed these two spoons—they are my master's. CHRISTIAN WILLIAM M'NIEL. I am the prosecutor's son. I was disturbed that morning—I went down, and found the prisoner is the kitchen—he slept in a room opposite the stable, which is one staircase above the kitchen—he said that three men had entered his bed-room window, that he had followed them down into the kitchen, and, as he entered, he was knocked down; that he rose up, and was knocked down again, and, on his calling for help, they ran away—I saw the poker, which he attracted my notice to, lying on the floor, nearly bent double—he said one of the men struck at him with it, but had missed him—I did not observe any marks of violence on him, as if he had been knocked down—I went down the moment I heard the cry—be tween my hearing it, and going down, he had not time to go up stairs and put on his stockings and trowsers. THOMAS PINKCOMBE. I am the prosecutor's gardener. The prisoner was taken on Thursday morning, the 21st of March, and from that time I assisted my master in the stable till Fleming came on the 25th—from the time the prisoner was taken, till Fleming came, my master kept the key of the stable door, and no one could have access to it—I never went to the stable but when ray master was there—there had been a ladder in the green-house on the Wednesday night, and on the Thursday morning I found it under the pri-soner's window. JOHN FLEMING. I was engaged by the prosecutor, as groom—on the 25th of March, I went into the stable, and under some fitter I found a silver desert-spoon, which I gave to Stevenson; and about the centre of the stall I found a silver table-spoon, which I took to my master. WILLIAM M'NIEL. I received a spoon from Fleming—I had left home, but was informed of this robbery, and I returned on the Thursday—I gave the prisoner into custody—I let nobody go into the stable after that till Fleming came—no one could have access to the stable.
   Prisoner.  There were plenty of people employed about his premises be sides myself. Witness.  No one could have got the key but from my pocket, or from the key-basket in the parlour. CHRISTIAN WILLIAM M'NIEL re-examined.  I did not take the key from the basket—I did not put the spoons in the stable—the prisoner told the policeman four men attacked him, but he told me three. JANE STEVENSON re-examined.  I did not take the key from the basket, nor put the spoons in the stable.—no one could have taken the key without my knowing it. JAMES PARRY( police-sergeant R  8.)
