THOMAS SMITH. I am a horse-hair manufacturer, and live in Minton-
street, Long-lane, Bermondsey-I first became acquainted with the prisoner
about the 2nd or 3rd of June, 1842- he went by the name of Felgate in the
first instance-I always addressed him as Felgate, and he answered me !
have no recollection of bis ever stating that it was Felgate, but I was in the
habit of seeing him three or four times a week, and always addressed him as
Mr. Felgate-he always answered to that name at that period-he did not
say his name was anything else-between the 17th of June and the 8th of
Aug. I sold him goods to the amount of 201.--I afterwards called on him
with a stamp, and was about to draw a bill as usual, in the name of Felgate
and Wood, when he stated that Mr. Felgate had left the concern, and
that the firm was now Charles Wood and Co.--I said I was surprised, as I
had always taken him for Mr. Felgate--he said, "Oh no, that is your mis-
take, to think my name is Felgate; my name is Wood he said the bill
was to be drawn on Charles Wood and Co.-I drew the bill on Charles Wood
and Co., and the prisoner accepted it as soon as drawn-I saw him accept it
—it has not been paid-(the bill was dated Aug. 8th, 1842-accepted, O.
Wood and Co.)
