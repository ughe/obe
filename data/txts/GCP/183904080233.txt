Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
1448. THOMAS HOY was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of April,
4 pairs of boots, value 1l. 2s., the goods of William Freeman,
WILLIAM FREEMAN. I am a shoemaker, and live in Greenwich-road.
I was sitting in my back parlour on the 10th of April, and in consequence
of something my wife told me I ran out of doors- 1 saw the prisoner rather
running-I pursued him, and took hold of his collar-I said, what have
you been taking ? and at that moment I saw a boot fall from his person
Í took it up, and with the boot and the prisoner I proceeded towards the
station-house-Mr. Judson my neighbour came up-I wished him to go
and see if there were not some more boots-he said, "If you will go I will
hold the boy "-I went back, and close to where the prisoner had been
standing when I took him, I found seven more boots-they were all mine.
JOHN JUDSON. I am a licensed victualler. I was standing at my door
deaning a bird-cage I saw the prisoner running along-I run after him
with the prosecutor-I saw one boot drop from the prisoner-I took him
to the station-house.
Prisoner's Defence. I went up the gateway, and the gentleman came
and said, "Have you stolen any thing?" I said, “No”-he took me
up the gateway and found the boot.
GUILTY.* Aged 17.- Transported for Seven Years.

Before Mr. Recorder.
1449. MARY RHODA JONES was indicted for a misdemeanor.
MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
ELEANOR MARY ELIZABETH LOCKER. I am the wife of Edward William
Locker; be is one of the Commissioners of Greenwich Hospital. On the
31st of December the prisoner called at my house she was quite a stran-
ger-she stated that her husband was transported, and she was very
anxious to join him that a ship was lying at Gravesend, and her
children were on board, and she was very anxious to raise money to go
there she showed me this card, which she said had been given her by
the owners of the ship, and it was to sail very shortly seeing the card I
believed her story, and gave her a crown and some trifling articles she
did not give me the card, but laid it down and forgot it-I found it after
she left.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Have you kept the card ever since ?
4. No, I sent it to Mr. Gordon if this is not the card she produced, it
is so like it that I cannot tell the difference-I read it while she was
there—and after she left, and kept it till I sent it to Mr. Gordon-- I gave
the prisoner a crown-piece as an act of charity and humanity, supposing
what she said was correct.
