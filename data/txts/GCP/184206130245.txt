Costeramined by MR. JONES. Q. How long have you known the
riconer? A. Some two or three years I knew him in Mr. Wilson's
Service-I was also in Mr. Wilson's service-he has always borne the
character of a respectable man, and been a first-rate man in his business,
he was in Mr. Wilson's service when I went, and I left him there--he
had the management of their cap business-he at that time carried on busi-
ness for himself in the London-road-he worked on Muller and Simpson's
premises -I never recollect his bringing his own materials to make up-I
remember some braid being fetched from his premises, which was made up
for Muller and Simpson, and we had some old hat-boxes of the prisoner
-I have sold the prisoner an article we call "threshers," what the country
farmer boys wear- I do not recollect any thing else--there was a cap orn
dered of me-I ordered it of Simpson and Muller, and the person did not
call for it-I could not afford to lose it, and the prisoner took it of me-I
was examined at the police-office-I swear I never sold the prisoner any
thing else—he never supplied the prosecutors with any buttons I know
that he employed some of the same workwomen as the prosecutors did
-I swear positively I have never seen him giving them work to do for
himself on the prosecutors' premises—there is a boy named William Trimm
in the prosecutors' employ-he might look out hats for the prisoner, not
caps and velvet-I think the prosecutors bought the cheni velvet at Man-
chester-I do not recollect whether they deal with any one in the Old
Change-I'do not know the value of the velvet-it is my duty to enter
articles which I sell of the prosecutors-Messrs. Muller and Simpson
sometimes make entries of the articles they sell—the prosecutors are not
in the habit of selling materials to their work people we have never done
80-I told the prosecutors of the cap the prisoner ordered, on the day of his
commitment-I told Mrs. Harris, the forewoman, I wanted a cap of a
certain size, and she got it made for me-I paid the prosecutors for it-the
prisoner made some allusion to a cap, and I said the only cap I sold him
was the one I had entered—I entered it at the time I bought it, before
had it away from the premises, after I had ordered it-I left Mr. Wilson's
employ about eighteen months ago I had been in his service three years
I collected orders for him, and occasionally received cash-I left him
suddenly-I was out of employ six months before I got to the prosecu-
tors' --Mr. Gibbs, Mr. Wilson's partner, gave me a character to the prose-
cutors--I swear I referred to them for a character-I had a dispute with
Mr. Wilson before I left, about money matters-he was my partner at that
time--I swear that Mr. Gibbs was not partner with me, but he was with
Mr. Wilson-- I was in business in Castle-street, Southwark, as a hat-manu-
facturer, and Mr. Wilson was my partner there--" Edwin Hunt" was over
the door-Mr. Wilson's name did not appear-his partnership with Mr.
Gibbs was not made public, and mine with Mr. Wilson was not made pub-
lic-I was in partnership with Wilson-our term was seven years—the
business did not answer-Mr.Wilson got the money-there were deeds and
an agreement-I left Mr. Wilson because he charged me with misappro-
priating his money-I am not aware of the amount--it might be 2001.-I
left him the next day-I had been two years in his employ, receiving no
salary-I was his partner and his servant-I have not taken any proceed.
ings against Mr. Wilson to enforce my partnership agreement-I at one
