MARIA GOODFELLOW. I am the wife of George Goodfellow, of Winchester-
court, Monk well-street. I know the prisoner-I remember seeing her in
Oct. last-she told me she was going to have a nurse child-on the 27th of
Nov. I saw her at my house, between two and three o'clock in the afternoon
- she said she had been to fetch the baby which she was going to bave to
nurse, and she brought it with her at the same time-I afterwards saw the
child at the police-office-I could not swear that it was the same child, but it
appeared to me, though very much altered, to be the same-- I thought it was
the same I went with the prisoner to Mrs. Cook's.

REBECCA Cook I am the wife of Thomas Cook, of Richardson-street,
Bermondsey. The prisoner came to lodge at my house on the 23rd of Oct.
- she was in mourning-I asked if she was a widow-she said no, she was
a married woman-she went out that evening to see her little boy, as she
said-she told me about a Captain Jones, who was then away, but would
return the latter end of Dec. or the beginning of Jan.-she said he was her
husband, but afterwards she said he was not her husband-she told me she
had been put to bed on the 12th of April with twins-she was lodging with
me in Nov.--I remember her going out about the end of that month-1 can-
not say exactly how she was dressed, but she was in mourning-I have seen
a bonnet produced by the officer-that was left in my room, and it is a sim-
lar bonnet to the one she had on when she went out in Nov.-she was going
to throw it on the dust-hole on the 30th of Dec., when she left my room,
and I begged it of her on the 27th of Nov. she went out about ten in the
morning, and returned between seven and eight in the evening with an infant
-she said she had had a few words with a person, and had brought her
baby home, and it was a little boy-1 thought it was her own--it was
called John-I did not find it out to be a girl till the Friday before she lett
on the Saturday-the captain came on the Friday night, and then the pri-
soner said the child was to be turned into a girl, and to be called Alice,

that to be done; how was the old gentleman to know whether it was a girl
