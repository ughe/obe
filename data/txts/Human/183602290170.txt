 791. WILLIAM LAWRENCE was indicted for embezzlement. SAMPSON MORDANT. I am a general machinist, in partnership with Gabriel Riddle. The prisoner was in my employ—he was engaged in August last as town-traveller—it was his duty to solicit the trade gene rally, in the glass line, for orders—he had h is regular set of patterns—he was to go out from day to day for that purpose—his appointed hours were from nine to seven o'clock—it was his business to be at home at seven o'clock in the evening, to enter the orders of the day in a book ex pressely kept for those orders—we have a regular collector of cash—it was no part of the prisoner's business to receive money—he had orders not to take it—he has paid money to me, or Mr. Page—the excuse he gave was, that some person had said, "I wish you to take this money." NOT GUILTY
 792. HENRY GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the   27th of Fe bruary , 1 silver spoon, value 20 s  ., the goods of James Johnston ,—2nd COUNT  stating it to be the goods of John Cooper and others. JAMES JOHNSTON. I live in Raquet-court, and am a paper-hanger. The prisoner is a stranger—I lost a silver spoon—the last time I saw it it was on the dining-room table—Mr. John Cooper is trustee of my pro perty. SARAH TUCKER. I am cook to Mr. Johnston. I know this silver spoon is his—the prisoner is my cousin—I missed the spoon on Sunday-morning—he had been to visit me on the Saturday.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Had he been in the habit of visiting you often? A.  Yes; I went out of the kitchen for three quarters of an hour while he was there—there was a great quantity of plate he might have taken—he was taken up last Saturday night—from the time he went away till he was taken I had no opportunity of seeing him—I did not see him after he was taken up—there was none left about except what was in the plate-chest—there were eight large table-spoons, eight tea spoons eight desert-spoons, and the silver sugar-tongs, all of which he had an opportunity of taking—I left no one in the kitchen with him. CHARLES SIMMONDS. I am a goldsmith, and live at No. 3, South ampton-row, Russell-square. On Saturday evening last, the prisoners came and asked if I purchased old silver—I said, "Yes"—he asked what I gave an ounce for it—I said it depended on what he had—he then said he only had this spoon—I said it would not suit me, "How was it broken",—he said it had been trodden on—I said it had been broken on purpose—he then said, could he not have it repaired—I said, "Yes, on Monday morning"—he said could he not have it sooner—I said, "Yes, in two hours" he said could it not be done in less—I said, "Yes, in an hour"—he gave his name "Jones," and said he would call again for it in an hour—I then sent for the officer.
