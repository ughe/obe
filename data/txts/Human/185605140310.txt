   MR. GIFFARD . Q.  Did you find some plate in a slide under the larger chest? A.  Yes; the old spoons—the goblet appeared to hate been left by mistake—Charnell told me that he had left his own watch and chain on his bed, and that they were taken at the same time; that he took them off when he went out, and left them on the bed—he sleeps in the pantry. THOMAS THURSTELL. ( continued ). There were several marks on the pantry door, out not sufficient, in my opinion, to make an entry—the box of the lock had not been broken, and when I locked the door, I could not push it open—the box of the lock would make considerable noise in breaking it—few days after the robbery, I am not sure of the date, I called at No. 7, Princes-road, Pimlico, and saw Peshell, Martin, and Sale—I do not think I was in uniform, but they knew I was an officer—constable Fells, who had been there before introduced me as his inspector—Martin said that a friend of Peshell's, a person named Fisher, but whose real name turns out to be Holt, called on him on the Friday, and stated that there was to be a ball at William's-rooms, Brewer-street, Golden-square, that he, Martin, went to 30, Baton-place, and asked Charnell if he would go, and returned in the evening, saying to Peshell that Charnell would go, and had written to invite a friend to accompany him—that friend was not Sale, it was Tillett, a servant at Westbourne-terrace, Hyde-park—he said that later in the evening Sale called on them, and they asked him to accompany them to the ball, and they all went together to 30, Baton-place, at half past 10 o'clock, that Martin went into the house, and Sale and Peshell remained outside, but Gharnell came to the area gate and called then in; that they were very reluctant to go in, thinking it improper, but were overruled by Charnell who pressed them—they stated that before they got to the house they found that Tillett was there; that they waited a considerable time, that Charnell left the pantry frequently to see if Mr. Fisher had retired to bed; that they became very impatient, add at length Charnell said that it was all right, that Mr. Fisher had gone to bed, and they then left the house together by the area door and steps—Sale and Peshell added, that on leaving the house, they fancied that they heard a rustling of a dress, apparently on the stairs leading to the upper part or the house, and told Charnell that they thought it was not all right, and wished him not to leave home, but he replied, that there was nothing to fear, and he had frequently gone out without Mr. Fisher knowing anything about it; that they got into a cab at the comer of Eaton-place and Belgrave-street, and were driven to Brewer-street, Sale stating that he left them there, he having previously stated to the others that he could not go to the ball, as he was not properly dressed, and that his ball days were over, and he had no desire to go, but was driven there, it being part of his way home; that when they found the room closed they went to the other Willis's-rooms at St. James's, thinking that the ball might be there, found those rooms closed also, and then called at several public houses and coffee shops about the Haymarket—(Charnell mentioned Evans's, but I do not know whether the others did or not, that is in Covent-garden)—that Peshell left them in the Haymarket, they had some little dispute about taking some more refresh ment, and Martin, Charnell, and Tillett walked together to we end of St. Jaraes's-street, where Tillett left, and Martin and Charnell came on their way home to the corner of Arabella-row, Pimlico) Martin going home, and
