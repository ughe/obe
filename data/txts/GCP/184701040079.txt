437. WILLIAM QUINTON was indicted for unlawfully uttering coun-
terfeit coin.
MR. CLARKE conducted the Prosecution.
JOSEPH SHIRLEY. I am a publican, and live in Gravel-lane, Southwark.
On the 30th of Dec., about twenty minutes before twelve o'clock at night, I
was in my own house-the prisoner came for 11d. worth of gin-be paid me
with a shilling-I put it amongst the farthings, as I had great suspicion of it,
just as he got to the door, but I could not leave to follow him the farthings
are kept in a round tin-there was no other shilling there-I looked at the shil-
lings before the prisoner well got to the door, and bent it double--the prisoner
came again in about ten minutes for 1d. worth of gin-he gave me another
shilling - I gave him 11d. change--I looked at that shilling, and bent it double
in the same way-I then sent for an officer, and gave him into custody.
JAMES MORGAN Davis (police-constable M 132.) I took the prisoner, and
have the two shillings- I found Ild. on the prisoner, the change of the second
Mr. John Field. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint. These
shillings are both counterfeit, and I believe both cast in the same mould.
Prisoner. I only went for one pennyworth of gin.
Guilty. Aged 20.-Confined Six Months.
438. WILLIAM PILCHER was indicted for stealing 2 flannel shirts, value
6s.; I map, 9s.; and 1 glove-stretcher, 5s.; the goods of William Doubleday.
WILLIAM DOUBLEDAY. I am master of the barque Mary, lying in the
Commercial-dock, Rotherhithe. I missed from a chest on board, a flannel
shirt, a map, and some gloves-these now produced are them--they are mine.
George GILLMAN. I was at work on board the barque Mary. On the
24th of Dec. I saw the prisoner go from the fore part to the aft part, and as
I was going on deck to get my pipe alight I saw him at the chest, and I
afterwards saw him with the flannel, which appeared to be shirts--they were
placed under the stern sheets of the barque, and the mate found them there.
