 1017. JAMES PARSONS was indicted for stealing 27 deals, value 3 l  . 10 s  .; and 57 wooden planks, 9 l  .; the goods of the Company of pro prietors of the Grand Surrey Canal .—2nd COUNT  , stating them to be the goods of Charles Walton and others.
   MR. HORRY conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM PETERS. I am in the employ of the Grand Surrey Canal Company. On the 6th of March my attention was called to the deals—there ought to have been forty-six—I found by the book there were forty three deficient—I did not make the entry in the book, but I saw the deals after they were entered, and I had sold several parcels from that pile—there was a pile of 147, and fifty-five and forty-fix had been delivered—the rest, which had been sold to Mr. Scarf, remained—I saw them safe on the 4th of March, and missed forty-three of them on the 6th—I then went to look at sixteen which I had sold to Mr. Hinton, and out of tkem I missed four teen—I missed in all fifty-seven planks and forty-three deals, eighty-four pieces in all—I went to Archer's Wharf, and saw a punt lying there, which I had seen the day before four or five ships dowa, and the eighty-three pieces of wood were on the brick-wall—they were the pieces I had seen safe on the premises of the Canal Company on the Tuesday before—I saw the prisoner shortly after, on Mr. Archer's wharf in custody—I said I was sorry to see him there—he said, "I only hired the punt for Johnson"—I knew the prisoner—I cannot tell whether he was employed on the water.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLAMTINE . Q.  What did you go to the book for? A.  I knew the deals were missing, and I went to see if anybody else had delivered any of them—I could not have ascertained that feet without the book, because I am not the foreman—the delivery-book is not here—Griffiths and three or four others have a right to deliver goods—they take orders, and go and mark off the goods—I have never been what I call suspended—I would rather not mention what it was. WILLIAM LANGPORD. I am a lighterman. On the 3rd of March the prisoner and Harwood came to me—Harwood said the prisoner wanted a punt—I said mine was stolen, but I could get them one for 3 s .—the pri soner said, "The old man will not mind 3 s ."—I showed them Patey's punt, and they said it was too big for them, they wanted one of twelve or fourteen feet for scaffolding boards—they both Baid the little one would do—I took the punt, and put her into the canal, and made her fast—the prisoner said, "Take her below the ships, and I will leave you a pint of ale at the Bull"—he said Harwood would take charge of her—that wat on the Wednes day, and on Thursday I saw Harwood, who came that morning from Blackheath with the old gentleman—he said he had taken a punt from the Commercial Docks with a short 100 of deals in it, and he had turned them into the punt that I took up, I should find the punt at Smith's Lock. WILLIAM PETBRS re-examined. Q.  You say Griffiths and several other persons might deliver deals? A.  Yes, but they could not deliver these, because they were stolen—they are not here, and the book is not here. NOT GUILTY.
