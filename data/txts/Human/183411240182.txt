what passed I cannot say—I received Clarke into my custody, and as I brought him towards the station-house, he looked over his shoulder, and said, "Your brother constable has got the man who has done the deed"—I afterwards asked Clarke if he had any knowledge of passing me in Russell-street—he said he knew what he was taken for, for that they had been drinking—he said he was aware what we took him for, and stated that Bush proposed that he should knock the man down, and Clarke should rob him, and Clarke said he would have nothing to do with it at all; and then they went along towards the barracks, in company with Delves—that Clarke got a-head some hundred yards, then Bush went back, and afterwards came running to him, and said, "I have done  him, I have got his silver, but he has no watch," and offered to share the money; but he (Clarke) said, "No, I will have nothing to do with the money at all; you will get your self transported, and me likewise"—that, as they came along, Bush offered him half-a-crown to get something to drink, but he said, "No, I will have none of the money; I wish I had not come out with you"—when I took Clarke into custody, I heard some coppers fall, I do not know from whom—I searched him at the station-house, but found nothing on him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  At what hour did you see them again in Grove-Iane? A.  I suppose a quarter or ten minutes after ten o'clock, or it might be half an hour—when I came up my brother policeman had stopped them—I had about a hundred yards to come—I could see him stop them—Clarke was already in custody of the other policeman, when he told me he was aware what I took him for—I was not near enough to hear what passed between them and my brother policeman—I had not seen Wilson from the time I had seen them at Rotherhithe till I saw him take them—Clarke told this story openly the moment he was accused.
 (The prosecutor's deposition was here read, in which he stated that he had been robbed of a purse containing about eighteen or nineteen sove  reigns, some loose silver and copper, and a silver watch, for which he had given 4 l . 10 s .; and that his father had given him the money to purchase it the day before.) GEORGE WILSON re-examined.  I stopped them in Grove-lane—I had received information of the robbery perhaps half an hour before, or not so long—my beat joins Rotherhithe—York-street is not five minutes' walk from Grove-lane—my brother constable, Clarke, gave me the information—I was present when Bush was searched—I did not see Clarke searched—my attention was directed to Bush.
