WILLIAM GREEN. I live at 2, Falcon-place, Coppice-row, and have
known Cliff some time I knew he worked at a silversmith's, but did not
know where--about three weeks ago, I went with him to Mrs. Ryan's he
took a fork which looked like silver, and gave it to her I went there with
him again next day--he took her a silver spoon-she left the house, came
back, and gave Cliff 98.-he gave her 13., and me 45.-sho gave him 48. or
53. on the other occasion, and he gave her 6d. or 18., and I received 15.-
I heard him ask her to sell them, and she went out-the spoon and fork
were both straight-I took the 4s. because be offered it to me; I had no
elaim to it.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he say he found them? A. No; I was taken
up at first, and then made a witness.
ROBERT PACKMAN (City-policeman, 138). In consequence of inform-
ation, I went with Turvey to Green's residence-Cliff, who I had taken over
night, went with us, and showed me where Green lived-I went with Green
and Cliff to 81, Little Bowling-street, Clerkenwell, where Cliff pointed out
Ryan, and said, “That is the woman we sold the spoon and fork to" I told
her we were police-constables, and wanted the property, as it was stolen---she
said she had bought no such things of the boys, and never saw them before
in her life-I took her to the station as I was telling the inspector the
charge, she said, "the boys brought her the spoon and fork, and said they
had found it, and she believed they had done so, and she sold the spoon for
58., and the fork for 9s."
(James Kenny, grocer and cheesemonger, gave Ryan a good character ; but
Herry Webb, City-policeman, 258, stated that her house was a place of resort
for young thieves.)
RYAN-GUILTY. Aged 46.-Transported for Ten Years.
