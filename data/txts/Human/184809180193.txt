   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Your arm is bound up? A.  That is the effect of the wound on the head—it is not an old complaint—my arm is drawn up and inflamed—I went to the public-house about half-past ten o'clock—the prisoner and others were there—I had a pint of beer, and the prisoner drank with me—this happened after the house was closed—I did not say anything about his having taken a job from me—there was no dispute about a hay-making job—Shillito did not say to me, "Go on, Bob "—he did not say, "Well done;" nothing of the sort passed—I did not strike or push the prisoner—he had not the scraper in his hand—he crossed the road and picked it up. JOHN SHILLITO. I am a tailor. I was at the Crown and Anchor on 5th Sept., between ten and eleven o'clock—I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner in the tap-room—they were laughing, joking, and drinking together—there was no foul language while I was there, till they came out—Jones came out first, and Sutton after him—Jones called Sutton a parish-bred b----r—Sutton. went up to him, and asked him if he called himself a man to call him that—Jones then went and took the scraper, and knocked him down with it—Jones ran away, and I ran and overtook him—Sutton was not at all offensive to Jones while I was there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long were you in the public-house? A.  I went about a quarter before eleven o'clock—the prisoner was not at all tipsy—Sutton was not angry; he took all in good part—they were both in the house when I went in—they did nothing but chaff  one another inside—there was no violence till they got outside. GEORGE TETCHNER. I was inside the public-house—I heard the prose cutor and the prisoner laughing and joking about their situations—the prisoner called Sutton a hay-maker—I went out first—I saw them come out—Jones called Sutton a parish-bred b—r, and Sutton went and asked him if he called himself a man—Jones went to the side of the road, got the scraper, struck him on the head, and knocked him down—Jones threw down the scraper and ran away—some one picked it up and gave it to me—I gave it to the officer. JOHN DENDY( policeman, V  93). I took the prisoner, and produce the scraper, which was given me by Tetchner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  The prisoner said it was an accident, that he did not do it wilfully? A.  Yes. JOHN PEARCE. I am a surgeon, of Larkhall-lane. On the night of 5th Sept. Sutton was brought to me, suffering from a severe contused wound, close on the skull, as deep as it could go—I saw him the next day—there was a great deal of constitutional disturbance, which might have arisen from an old wound on the hand—it might have been the wound, or it might have been from drink—it might have happened without the wound—I saw him on we Thursday following—he was very ill, and suffering a great deal from the head—I was obliged to bleed him—this wound was not dangerous, because there was no fracture—his arm is bound up from a constitutional disturbance he may be subject to fits from the effects of this wound.
