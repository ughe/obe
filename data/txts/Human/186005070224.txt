   MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Is your work very heavy, and requiring horses of peculiarly good condition to do it? A.  Not very heavy—they have to go long distances—Mr. Edward Peek seldom or ever comes down there—Mr. Frean is the partner—he would be consulted in the business, but nothing would be done without my being made fully cognisant of it—if there had been any to be bought or sold it would have come under my super intendence.
   COURT . Q.  How many horses did you ever buy? A.  There were two bought just as I came—the prisoner was never authorized by me or by any of the firm, as far as I know, to buy or sell any horses. JAMES MITCHELL. I am a dairyman, of 6, George Yard, Berraondsey—I work for the prosecutors—early in March the prisoner called at my plaee and asked me if I knew any one who had got a horse to let on hire—I told him I did not, and that I wanted to buy one myself, a cheap horse—he said that he had two cripples of the firm for sale, and I could take either on trial—I first had a bay horse—I drove him to Waterloo station and back with milk to the premises, and then returned him, as he did not suit me—I went round a day or two after, and he sent me a grey horse, which he asked 10 l . for—I tried it, and gave him 9 l . 10 s . for it; 3 l . 10 s . first, and then 6 l .—I paid the 6 l . on 22d March; this ( produced ) is the receipt for it—on 2d April he came to me, and told me he was disappointed in a horse coming in, and asked me if I could lend him the horse to work for the firm at 7 s . per day, and next morning he went to my stables for the horse—I sent to the firm for it next day, but did not get it back—the brown horse did not suit me, and the prisoner sold it in my presence to Mr. Coleman for 9 l . 10 s . and allowed me 1 l . for my trouble and for the introduction—after I had paid for the hone, and got possession of it, the prisoner came to my stable one morning, and said, "Do not say anything about having paid for the horse until quarter day, and then I shall have replaced the other horse."
