CATHERINE O'CONNBLL. I am the widow of the deceased—on November 17th I was lodged in the married quarters of the Marylebone Workhouse—I did not see anything of what took place in the recreation ground—I heard some moaning—my husband had only been out about half-an-hour—it was directly after I heard the moaning that I saw him—he came into my room and fell on the floor—I sent for a doctor—I saw what a state he was in—when he fell on the floor he could not get up—he was dying then—he never got up himself—I saw his face—he had a black eye and was bleeding from the nose and mouth, he had a cut across his nose and also on his cheek—he was insensible—he was put to bed and he never got up—he died on Monday night—he never got better; he was dying all the time—I did not know of any disagreement between the two men—there never had been, as far as I know—my husband was sometimes short-tempered—I heard the story of his having charged a man with opening a window over his head—that sort of thing would have been enough to make him angry—it was done for spite—the window was over where we were sitting—my husband was 80 years old on the 15th of this month. WILLIAM RAYNER.
