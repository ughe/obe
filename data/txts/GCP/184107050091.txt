WILLIAM PORTERFIELD. I am sergeant-major of the sixth battalion of
Artillery, at Woolwich. I was directed to put handcuffs on the prisoner
on the 14th of June, a short time after eight o'clock in the evening-he
was at that time in a cell—I went there, and said, " I must put the hand-
cuffs on you"-he folded up the sleeves of his jacket, and prepared his
hands so that I might do it with convenience after I had finished putting
them on, he said, I have been a good soldier up to the present day, but
now I am a murderer" - I had not said any thing to him, or asked him any
questions; I only said I must put the handcuffs on him-he said he bad
been vexed, and irritated, and maddened I said, "What vexed and mad-
dened you? was it the bombardier that did so ?" --he said, “ No, by no
means, the bombardier did not vex me"-and he also said the bombardier
was the best friend be had in the world-he said he had been fighting in
the Canteen, and be turned round, as he laid on the bed in the cell, and
pointed to a little wound in the cheek, and said, " See what he has done to
me”by that I understood him to mean the man whom he bad fought
with in the Canteen. hosted by adines Sudogule
Q. Did he ask you any thing about the state of the deceased ? . 4. Yes
---as far as I recolleet, his words were, " Is he dead yet ?"-I told him no,
that he was not yet dead he said he hoped to God he would not die-he
then asked me to stop with him in the cell during the night I told him,
28 I was sergeant-major, on duty for the week, it was impossible, but if
he wished any body to stop with him, I would make his request known to
the barrack officer, and no doubt he would gratify his request-he said,
"Never mind, I will do without”-he then asked me to stand before him,
that he might see me-- he said several other things, which I cannot recol-
lect he was apparently very vexed at what took place; and it appeared
to me he had been drinking, perhaps, during the former part of the day,
and was recovering from a drunken state-a man in the cell asked if he
had been searched--the sergeant proceeded to put his hands in his pockets,
and be said, "I have got nothing to injure you or any other one"
said I would come again and see him, which I did at six o'clock next
morning-I asked how he was, if he had slept any during the night---he
said, " No, very little;" and after a very considerable pause, he asked if he
was dead yet -- I considered he alluded to the bombardier.I said, " Yes,
he is dead; he died at three o'clock this morning"-he turned round
on his bed, and sobbed violently he made no direct reply
