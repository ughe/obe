87. GEORGE BAILEY (36), was indicted for stealing five sovereigns, the
property of Benjamin Witham Wall.
Mr. Wood conducted the Prosecution.
BENJAMIN WILLIAM WALL. I am a butcher, carrying on business at
Stockwell Green-I have taken the shop of Mr. Hilsley, and still use his
name- I have known the prisoner as a carrier of calves and lambs-on the
30th September I bought a call at the Cattle Market of Mr. Marrell, the
salesman-I instructed the prisoner to bring the calf home in his van-I
said, “Here, Bailey, take this money over to the bank, and bring home the
calf _I gave him five sovereigns—the things are paid for at Lacey's Bank
--he brought the calf home in the evening-be has not paid for it-I
received a letter from Mr. Marrell, and in consequence of that I looked after
the prisoner for two or three weeks I could not find him at the Cattle
Market, or his usual haunts-I went and sent continually-I gave informa-
tion to the police, and next saw him in custody-he said "I confess I had
the money, and I suppose if you have it returned that is all you require"
-I said, “You are too late now; if you had come and seen me, I could
have put up with any conditions rather than had any trouble " - I had to
pay Mr. Marrell for the calf-I bought it in the name of Hilsley, and sent
the money in that name-he told the officer he had given the money to
William Tagg.
JOHN CHATER. I am a detective of the N division-I took the prisoner
into custody, at No. 1, St. George's Market, London Road, Southwark, where
he lives, the 30th October I had been watching his house for three or four
days I saw him go into his house about 11.20—I told him he was charged
with stealing 51., the property of Mr. Hilsley, butcher, of Stockwell Green
-- he said, “Oh, it is right enough, I had the 51., and instead of paying it
into the bank, as I was directed to do, I gave it to a boy, who was working
for me, of the name of Tagg, and he stepped it with the money"-he said
he had looked for Tagg, but could not find him-I made inquiries at the
Cattle Market for him, but could not find him-I found that a lad of the
name of William Tagg was sentenced to six months' imprisonment, on the
2nd September-he used to live in the same neighbourhood as the prisoner.
WILLIAM ENGHAM. I am clerk, at Lacey's bank-we did not receive 51..
on account of Mr. Hilsley, on the 30th September, nor subsequently.
GEORGE PIKE. I am a detective of the Q division--I know the prisoner
as a cattle-carrier, and have known him twenty years-he had a boy of the
name of Tagg, that occasionally worked for him-on the 30th September he
was in gaol.
Prisoner. I have a wife and five children; my wife is near her confine-
ment; I was willing to pay this money.
GUILTY.
Strongly recommended to mercy. He also PLEADED GUILTY to having been
previously convicted of frony, on the 15th January, 1864.--Six Months' Im-
prisonment.
ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, 13th DECEMBER, 1869,
