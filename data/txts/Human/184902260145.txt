JOHN STACE. I work for Mr. Jenner, of Southwark-bridge-road—the back of his yard comes to the prisoner's house—I picked up these two handles in our yard and gave them to the policeman.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Your house is not situated alone? A.  No. GEORGE WILD( policeman, M  94). I found the part of the barrow in the loft—as I was searching, the prisoner's wife put out the light, and I missed her—I got on the ladder, which went through the roof—something passed me, which I caught hold of, and when a light was brought I found it was the prisoner's wife's boot—I know Jenners' yard—anything could find its way there from the roof of the prisoner's house.

It might have been half-past ten o'clock—I believe Hunt went round and got the two handles about eight, but I am positive it was after I got hold of the woman's boot—we took away everything supposed to have been stolen—there was a truck-load. RACHAEL HYAMS. Last Thursday seven weeks my boy went out with my husband's barrow—this is the brand I branded it with.
   MR. PARRY called ELISABETH SWEETMAN. I am married, and reside in Upper Marsh, Lambeth.    WYATT  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 23.— Confined Three Months . JAMES WARD. I am a beer retailer at Vauxhall. On 17th Feb., Wyatt came and asked for a pint and a half of ale—I asked whether she was going to take the pots home as she had no jug—she said yes, she was going to Smith's, of Vauxhall-wharf—she took it away in two quart pewter pots—my son told me something—I went to George-street, Vauxhall, three or four hundred yards off, I saw my son with one of the pots—I asked Wyatt, who was in the house, for the other—she said it was not empty, and then that she had sent it back—I got a constable, went to the back room first floor, and took both prisoners.
