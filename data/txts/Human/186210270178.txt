Turner to purchase any, he would give me 3 s ., or 4 s ., a cwt for disposing of it—I went to look at the rope for Messrs. Turner—I asked him, when I met him in Billiter-square, whose rope it was—he said, "The rope is mine;" and that he had advanced a from of money on it—I afterwards went with my brother to see the rope, but neither of the parties in question were there—we could not see them—I never saw it again.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Before the occasion on which you saw Gray at Tur ner's, and afterwards went into the street and met the man, had you ever seen the person whom you supposed to be Evans? A.  No—there was no particular conversation till we got over the bridge—when the conversation about the distance took place, we had passed through Tooley-street—I do not know the name of any other street there—I do not know Bermondsey-street, nor Dockhead—I think I noticed that the Roman Catholic chapel was near the place where the rope was—the conversation about the distance was some minutes before we arrived at where we saw the rope—the public-house where the man stood at the door was not very for from it; I cannot my to a few hundred yards, or to a few minutes—it was a short distance; that is all I can say—the man who was spoken to at the public-house door accompanied us, bringing the key with him—I think he did not take any part in the conversation when the door was opened—I think I have seen him since—he was convicted—his name was Bassett, I believe—there were very few persons passing when I saw the prisoner in Billiter-square—I should think it would be about 1 o'clock in the afternoon—I have had experience in rope, and in the price at which rope was retailed at that period—some of the rope that I saw at Kempster's could, no doubt, have been sold for 38 s ., at that time—the quality was mixed.
