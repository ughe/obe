 117. HYPPOLITE LONGUAY  (62) , Feloniously receiving seven pairs of boots, the property of James Cotton , knowing them to have been stolen.
   MR. LEIGH . conducted the Prosecution;  and MR. HARRIS . the Defence. WILLIAM WIGG. I am assistant to Mr. Bravington, pawnbroker, 51, Princes Street, Leicester Square—I produce two pairs of women's boots, pledged by the prisoner for 8 s ., on 25th August, 1869, in the name of Hyppolite. JAMES COTTON. I am a boot and shoe maker, at 24, Cleveland Street—the boots produced are my property—they were stolen from my stock, about 22nd August—these two pairs are worth about 24 s .—I have seen the prisoner at my place repeatedly; he came to see Berthier and Christians, my two lodgers—they were convicted here last Session, for a jewellery robbery.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who is your attorney in this case? A.  The gentle man behind you—I instructed him yesterday—I had never seen him before—a friend of mine introduced him.
   COURT . Q.  Are those boots your own manufacture? A.  Yes—there were two odd boots stolen, and I have the fellows to them here. GEORGE CLARKE. ( Police Inspector ). I produce two pawn tickets, referring to the boots produced—I found them at the prisoner's apartments, at 11, Gerrard Street, on 28th October. RICHARD JORDAN. I am assistant to John Aldous, pawnbroker, of Ber ick Street—I produce five pairs of boots, pledged on 25th August—they were pledged by two men—I can't say who they were. JAMES COTTON. ( re-examined ). These five pairs of boots are mine. GEORGE KING. ( Policeman D  76). I took the prisoner into custody—he said a man asked him to pawn the things, and he did not know they were stolen.
   The Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : "Berthier gave me the things to pledge, and I did not know they were stolen." GUILTY.— Eighteen Months' Imprisonment,
