   COURT . Q.  There was evidence that the ground had been disturbed for other purposes? A.  Yes. GEORGE GARFORD. I am a clerk in Messrs. Richards and Co.'s linen factory, in Bread-street, Cheapside, and am the son of Mrs. Courtnall; at the time in question I resided with her and my step-father, next door to Mr. Bridgman's, in West-street, Walworth. On Wednesday night, the 6th of Sept., I went to bed about eleven o'clock—I slept on the second floor, at the back of the house, which overlooks the chapel-yard in question—I put out my candle and drew up my blind, and on doing so, I fancied I saw some thing moving in the burial-ground—it immediately attracted my attention—I looked again, and in about five minutes' time I observed three men coming from the direction of the wall that separates the burial-ground from our garden, and go in the direction of Mr. Tawney's tomb—that was in the direction they would have come from Mr. Bridgman's house, without going through the chapel—I did not at that time know whose tomb it was—one of the persons appeared to have nothing on but a shirt, and a cloak over it—one of the others had a cloak, and the other, as far as I could see, was dressed in plain black clothes—I did not see the tomb opened—I observed that it was open—the stone that had been on it was removed to the side—I saw one of the men, I believe the undressed one with the cloak on, descend into the tomb, and saw something handed from the tomb—I also saw it carried away by two of them, one carrying it at each end—they appeared to carry it with great difficulty—I saw some ropes—I could not distinguish whether or not it was lifted up by those ropes—there was perhaps not sufficient light for me to dis cern whether what was taken out was a coffin, but I made the remark at the time that it was a coffin—it made that impression on me at the time—the third man then got out of the tomb—the other two carried the coffin in the direction of the wall from where I first saw them come—that is in a direct line with Mr. Bridgman's passage—after they got to a certain extent under the wall I could not see them, from the position of my window—it would lead them towards the house—(before I saw the coffin taken from the tomb, I had given an alarm to my father and mother)—the men afterwards returned from the wall where they appeared to have deposited what they took away, and all three returned to the tomb again—I then saw a lantern—it appeared to be brought from Mr. Bridgman's stable, which is in the burial-ground—one of them descended into the tomb with the lantern, the other two remained about the tomb—after the space of about ten minutes, I observed the party come out of the tomb again—I saw nothing more of the light after it was taken into the tomb—I saw a man bring a lever, or long wooden pole, from the direction of the stable, and replace the stone.
   Q.  Had you, in the course of this time, an opportunity of observing any one of the three persons, so as to enable you to speak to him? A.  Only from his general appearance and action—I could not see his face, on account of the distance—I have known young Bridgman for a long time—I believe him to have been one of the three, the one that was undressed and had the cloak on—I could scarcely see whether he had shoes and stockings on—the wind blew about his cloak, when he had the lantern, which gave me an op portunity of seeing his person and appearance, and that assisted me in coming to the belief that it was young Bridgman—I had not seen him wear a cloak at any time that I remember.
