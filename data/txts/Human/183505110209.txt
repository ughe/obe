PETER JONES, Esq. I am a major in the Royal Marines, quartered at Woolwich. The prisoner was my servant for about three months—he ceased to be so on the 23rd of April—I missed some trowsers, a waistcoat, an eye-glass, and other things—I got a search-warrant, and found them at Mrs. Hoafe's, a pawnbroker—I had only missed one livery waistcoat.
   Prisoner . When my master found the coats, I told him there were more things, and I gave up the duplicates. Witness.  Yes, he did; but, I believe, that was after I had discovered the articles I mentioned. ROBERT THORNDELL. I am in the employ of Mrs. Hoafe, a pawnbroker. I produce a waistcoat, three pair of trowsers, an eye-glass, and two shirts—the eye-glass, the two shirts, and 1 pair of trowsers, were pawned by the prisoner.
   Prisoner. Q.  When did I pawn them? A.  The trowsers and eye-glass on the 4th of April, and the shirts on the 9th of April—there was a ring pawned with the eye-glass.
 (  Property produced and sworn to. )
   Prisoner's Defence.  I intended to take them out again, at I expected some money from my father, but before I got it, the Major put me in the guard-house. GUILTY  . Aged 18.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor. Confined One Month.
 1333. JAMES HESDALL was indicted for stealing, on the   21st of April , 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of a man whose name it un known, from his person. JOHN MURPHY( police-constable G  215.) I was af Greenwich fair, on the 21st of April—I saw the prisoner there in the early part of the day, trying several gentlemen's pockets; and in the afternoon I saw him follow ing a gentleman—he drew a handkerchief from hit pocket, and put it into his own—I took him, and he drew this handkerchief from his own pocket, and threw it on the ground—the gentleman went off, and I could not tell his name—there was a companion with the prisoner, who escaped.
   Prisoner's Defence.  What he says is false. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Six Months.
 1334. JOHN THOMAS was indicted for stealing, on tke   20th of April , 16 yards of floor-cloth, value 3 l  . 4 s  ., the goods of James Cobbett . JAMES COBBETT. I am a floor-cloth manufacturer, and live at Deptford bridge, Greenwich. This floor-cloth is my property—I lost it on the 20th of April—it is worth about 3 l . 4 s ., at the wholesale price. CHARLES CHAPMAN. I am a miller, and live at Brixton. I was passing the prosecutor's shop on the 20th of April, between seven and eight o'clock, and I saw a boy with his hand on the floor-cloth—he said to the prisoner, Why don't you come on?"—I went across to my cousin, and I saw the other boy try to put it on his shoulder—it slipped down, but he took it up again—I then lost sight of him—I crossed, and my brother had got the prisoner with it on his shoulder. WILLIAM CHAPMAN. I was passing with my brother—I saw a boy take up this floor-cloth, and drop it again—he then took it up and put it on the prisoner's shoulder—I ran and stopped him with it. HENRY PRICE( police-constable R  56.) The prisoner was given into my custody—I asked if he knew any thing about the floor-cloth—he said he took it from the shop, another boy gave it him. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Six Months.
