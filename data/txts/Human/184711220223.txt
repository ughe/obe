 242. WILLIAM CLAYTON , stealing 1 pair of boots, value 2 s  . 1 cap, 6 d  . 1 pair of trowsers, 2 s  . 6 d  . 1 frock, 1 handkerchief, 2 stockings, value 2 s  . and 3 sovereigns, the property of Edward How , in a vessel on the Thames. EDWARD HOW. I am master of the Arab  —it is lying at Mill-hole, on the Thames—I left the articles stated and my money safe in my bed-case in my state-room, on 12th Nov.—I missed them on Sunday morning the 14th, bet ween five and six o'clock—the prisoner was my apprentice—he deserted the ship, and could not be found—I went after him, and found him at Islington—he was brought out of the theatre to me by his uncle—I asked him how he could do what he had done—he said he knew nothing about the money—I gave him in charge to the officer—he was dressed differently to what he was before—he had my boots on his feet—my cap was found in a bag which was in his aunt's possession—he was a good boy. WILLIAM SIMMONDS,( Thames police-constable , 62.) I went to the pri soner's lodging with the caption—I found the prisoner at the City of London theatre. EDWARD HOW re-examined. Q.  How did you know it was the prisoner's lodging? A.  From the Marine Society's office—I afterwards spoke to the prisoner about the things found in the bundle—he said nothing about them, but on the Sunday morning he told me he took three sovereigns out of my box. CAROLINE ELTHAM. I am the prisoner's aunt. He brought the bundle to my house at eight o'clock on the Sunday morning—I got an order, and took him to the theater—he told me the captain gave him the boots—there was a crack at the side—I got them mended. GUILTY of stealing the boots.  Aged 17.— Judgment Respited.
