The prisoner in the mean time had brought the draft to me, two or three evenings after I had been to Mr. Boys—it was all done within a few days—the prisoner called on me twice at Pimlico—he brought me the draft, and said he had brought it for my perusal—I said, "Mr. Blackwell, I cannot understand it, it is a great roll of paper, I shall not understand one-half of it, and if you comply with your promises to me, that is all I require," and he took it away with him again—it was soon after I arrived in England that he brought the draft to me, and soon after his first visit to me at Pimlico—no one was present when he brought the draft to me—he begged that no one might be present.
   Q.  When did he beg that? A.  On the evening that he first proposed it—I proposed that I should name his offer to some of ray friends—he said, "No, don't do that, you will be vacillating about it, you won't find any one else that will make you a better offer, and don't mention it to any one"—I did not open or look at the draft at all—I did not make inquiries respecting the furniture when he brought the draft—I paid a great many visits to South ampton-street after that—it was before he brought the draft that I went there and saw Mr. Newhall there—that was between his first and second visit to me at Pimlico—I did not ask him about the furniture when be brought the draft, because he came so late in the evening and hurried away again—I do not know how long he was there—he scarcely sat down—when I saw the roll of paper I said, "This is all leather  and prunella  to me, I shall not understand it; and if you make good your promises to me that is all I shall require"—I had told Mr. Boys the same.
   Q.  When you went respecting the deed, you said be need not explain it, it was all leather and prunella to you? A.  Yes.
   Q.  Then you signed it, making that observation, did you? A.  It was not ready to sign at that time—Mr. Boys appointed me to go there one day, and there was a mistake in the stamp, and then he appointed for me to come on the next evening—I went next evening—the prisoner came and fetched me from Pimlico in a cab , and I then signed the deed—that was on the 14th of Dec.—it was then that the observation was made about Phoebe Blackwell's name being in it.
   Q.  Was any portion of it read then? A.  I said that I thought it was of no use to read it, I did not care about it; if Mr. Blackwell fulfilled his en gagements, as I had no doubt he would, I did not want to hear any thing about it—Mr. Boys said he never allowed any lady or gentleman to sign any thing in his office without reading it, and in that case he read a part of it—I believe that was what Mr. Boys said—he read the part attaching to Phoebe Blackwell and her brother—I said to Mr. Boys, "Stop, sir, if you please, I don't want such a name as Phoebe Blackwell, I know nothing about her," and he then made the remark about her being so mixed up with her uncle's affairs.
   COURT . Q.  Was any copy of this deed delivered to you? A.  No.
