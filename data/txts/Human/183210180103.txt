 2457.  SAMUEL KERRY was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of October , 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s., the goods of George Taylor.
   JANE TAYLOR. I am the wife of George Taylor; I am a laundress; I lost these two handkerchiefs from a line in Lucas-street, Salisbury-street, where they hung to dry.
   WILLIAM WILLSHER. The prisoner was given into my charge by the prosecutrix's son; I took him, and found this one handkerchief on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2458.  MARIA KNIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of October , 1 set of bed-furniture, value 5s.; 2 blankets, value 5s.; 2 pieces of carpet, value 5s.; 1 bolster, value 3s.; 2 pillows, value 2s.; 2 sheets, value 3s.; 1 looking-glass, value 1s.; 1 candlestick, value 1s., and 5 knives and forks, value 2s., the goods of Ann Moorwood.
   JOHN PRINCE. I am a private gentleman. I was opposite Mr. Cassell's in Camden-town on the 13th of October, about half-past seven o'clock in the evening; I saw the prisoner carrying a basket, I followed her, and gave information to a Policeman, knowing her before - we then found the owner.
   ROBERT BUGDEN(Police-constable S 28). I followed the prisoner into Pratt-street; I took her with this basket; she told me where she lived, and we went there.
   ANN MOORWOOD. I am a widow. This basket contains two wine glasses, and two rummers of mine; I also lost two pieces of carpet, two blankets, a bolster, two sheets, and bed furniture - the pawnbroker has them; the prisoner lived in my house nearly three weeks; the whole of this property was let to her - she gave me no warning; I did not miss the property till she was taken.
   JOHN WILLIAM CASSELL. I am a pawnbroker; I have a sheet, two blankets, a pillow, and curtains, which were pawned by the prisoner.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am a pawnbroker. I was in my shop when the prisoner pawned this bolster, pillow, two pieces of carpet, looking-glass and decanter.
 MRS. MOORWOOD. This is my property; she came to me as a married woman, and I let it her with the room; her husband who lived there with her, is a coach harness maker - he came home at night, and went out in the morning; have lost more articles.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that it was her intention to have replaced the property.
     GUILTY. Aged 42. - Transported for Seven Years.
 2459.  BENJAMIN LAMB was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of September , 44 lbs. of flour, value 10s. 6d., the goods of Robert Christopher Payne , his master.
   ROBERT CHRISTOPHER PAYNE. I am a baker, at Hammersmith; the prisoner was in my service about three months - I had no other person. On the 14th of September I missed about three pecks of flour out of a sack in my bake house - I have found it since; no one else could have taken it.
   JOHN REYNOLDS(Police-constable T 1.) On the morning of the 14th of September I was on duty, about a quarter-past six o'clock; Kelly came, and said he had found a bag, containing a large quantity of flour, in a ground near Mr. Payne's premises - I called Mr. Payne out, and asked if he had lost any flour; he went back, and found he had - I went into his premises, and found a place where a bag had been placed with flour, against a water-butt - I went down the yard, and saw a mark where a bag of flour had been drawn over a wall, nine feet high, and the same on the other side - when I was bringing the prisoner here, he asked me if Counsel would be of any service to him; he said, "I did not place the flour where it was found" - I said, "No, but there is no doubt you drew it over the wall;" he said, "I don't say but I did."
   JAMES KELLY(Police-constable T 113.) I found this flour, and gave information to Reynolds.
 Prisoner. I am innocent - I had to take flour out of the sack to make my white bread.
 R. C. PAYNE. The flour found in the bag corresponds in quality and quantity with what I lost.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Six Months.
 2460.  WILLIAM McCARTHY and JAMES RYAN were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of October , 1 handkerchief, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Frederick Goddard , from his person.
   AUGUSTUS FREDERICK GODDARD. I belong to the Stock-exchange. On the 10th of October, about half-past four o'clock, I was in St. John's-lane, Clerkenwell, walking steadily - I felt something at my pocket, and missed my handkerchief; I turned, and saw McCarthy with it - I took hold of him with one hand, and took the handkerchief with the other; the other prisoner, and two other boys, were close to me; Ryan ran off, but he was taken - the other two escaped.
   WILLIAM HARRISON. I am an officer. I took the prisoners, and have the handkerchief.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Ryan received a good character.
 McCARTHY -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
     Confined Six Months.
 RYAN -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped and Discharged.

 PHILIP HENRY SHELTON COLSON. I am a  surgeon, and live in Goswell-road. The prisoner was in my service, as nurse, for four or five weeks; my wife came home on the Sunday night previous to the Saturday the prisoner was taken - she took off a brooch and laid it on the glass; that was missgin - we inquired, and could not find any thing of it; my wife then ordered her to look up the plate, and the two spoons were missing - I gave her in charge.
