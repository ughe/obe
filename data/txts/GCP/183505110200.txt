SARAH SIS. I am the prisoner Sims's mother. I heard of this rob-
bery about an hour before I was sent for-I heard my son was in trouble-
that was on the Wednesday evening on which they were taken-Parker
sent for me.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Before you saw your son, did you see the prosecutor?
1. No; nor Firmin-Firmin's sister came to my house, and said if I would
go to her mother's, where the prosecutor was, I should hear something there
I went there, and saw Parker, and he said if I could get my son to
say any thing, he would not hurt a hair of his head, and would go out of the
way-I told my son what he had said, when I went to him in the cage.
MR. DOWLING. Q. Had you any conversation with your son in the
cage? A. Yes ; I afterwards went into the yard of his lodging with the land-
lady-there was some dirt in the yard-the landlady moved a little stone-
I did not see her take any thing up, but she gave me a watch it had some-
thing black round it-I pointed out where it was to be found-she searched
and produced the watch--my son had directed me where to find it-I after-
wards gave it to Gutteridge, wrapped in the same black thing, which was
quite damp when I received it-the watch could not be seen without mov.
ing the stone.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Did the female prisoner live in the same place?
1. Yes; I was told so-Firmin's sister said she came from Parker and
her mother, to tell me to come, and that her son should not be hurt--that
he pitied my case very much, and promised me faithfully he should not be
hurt.
CHARLES FIRMIN re-examined. I gave no advice as to taking Parker's
watch-I swear I did not suggest to them to take it.
(John Nuttman, carpenter and builder; Francis Moss; and John

racter.)
SIMS-GUILTY of Larceny only.--Recommended to mercy by the Pro-
secutor and Jury.-Confined Three Months.
HILL NOT GUILTY.-SULLIVAN-NOT GUILTY.
