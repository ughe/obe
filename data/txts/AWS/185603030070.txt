Before Mr Justice Wightman and the Fourth Jury.
343. WILLIAM BOUSFIELD was indicted for the wilful murder of
Sarah Bousfield he was also charged on the Coroner's Inquisition with the
like offence MEssrs. BODKIN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution.
MARY ANN BENNEIT. I lodged in the same house as the prisoner, in
Portland-street, Soho. The prisoner and his wife lived on the ground floor
of that house-they kept a shop for the sale of cigars and tobaceoi-they
occupied the shop and back parlour-the shop opens into the back parlour
there were other lodgers in the house-Mrs. Bousfield attended to the
shop-her Christian name was Sarah-there were three childrenthe
prisoner was at home during the day--on Saturday night, 2nd Feb., at half
past 12 o'cloek, I went down and rapped at their parlour door wanted
three bundles of wood-t sold wood-I asked for the woed-Mrs Bous
field answered me-she said they were in bed, and could not oblige me with
the wood-the prisoner was at that time in the room, and in bed-I heard
his voice-Mrs Bousfield spoke in a very cheerful voice-I had seen then
together several times on that day-they appeared to be on very good terms
-I never saw Mrs. Bousfield alive after that Saturday-I had lived in the
house with them about two years-they often had words, I mean now and
then, occasionally, because he did not provide for bis children-Mrs Bous
field said that to him several times- never heard him make any complaint
to her, or of her.
Cross-excamined by Mr. BALLANTINE Q. I suppose you knew nothing of
his circumstances A. No, nothing whatever-he was at the theatres at
night, in some employ there-he got very little indeed by that-I knew
nothing about the way in which he lived, or the state they were in at home
-the eldest child was six years old, the other four, and the other ten months
-I have occasionally seen him nursing the two younger children-I have
not seen him kissing them.
