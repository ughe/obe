671. WILLIAM FORD (27), Was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRS. CRAWFORD and HODGSON conducted the Prosecution.
John RICHARD CRISWICK. I am a ginger-beer maker, of Little Earl-street
Seven-dials-I remember the prisoner coming to me early in June, and
purchasing a penny bottle of ginger-beer- I gave him change, and he left
and I then found the shilling to be bad-I bent it, and put it away by itself
and afterwards gave it to constable, F 80—on the following Sunday evening
the prisoner came and asked for another bottle of ginger-beer-he tendered
a 58 piece I found it was light, and was about to break it in the detector.
and he said, “Don't do that, bere is a penny"-I gave him the crown and
he gave me a penny, and left the shop.
THEODORE MELLARTS, I am an egg-merchant, of 11, Little Earl-street,
Seven-dials--on Thursday, 4th July, the prisoner came to my shop and
asked for three eggs, which came to twopence-my wife served him; her name
is Mary Ann Mellarts-I saw the coin that the prisoner gave her ; it was s
28. piece-she gave him change and he went away as soon as he was
gone, my wife gave it to me- I had not let it go out of my sight the whole
time-I saw it was the same that the prisoner gave her—it was bad- I put
it on one side, marked it, and afterwards gave it to constable F 80.
DANIEL AHEARN. I am a greengrocer, and live at 37, New Compton-street
- on Saturday, 27th July, the prisoner came into my shop and asked for
two pounds of potatoes, which came to 14d.-I served him, and he gave me
a bad 28. piece-I did not like the look of it, and gave it to my wife to take
next door to try it, and while she was away he offered ine a good sixpence,
and I took it in my hand, but I would not let him go-my wife called
policeman, and I gave him in charge.
ANNA AURARN. I am the wife of the last witness-on 27th July, I
received a florin from my husband and gave it to the constable--I never let
it out of my hand till then.
SAMUEL HAWKER (Policeman, F 80). I was on duty in New Compton-
street, on the night of 27th July, I was called to the last witness' shop, and
the prisoner was given into my custody there-he said, "I know nothing of
the 28. piece"-.when the wife returned with the 28. piece, he said, "You
can't lock me up for only having one piece "-I took him to the station-I
received some money produced) from the last witness, and this shilling
(produced) from Mr. Criswick-I searched the prisoner, and found on hiun
three good sixpences and a halfpenny-I asked his address; he said he bad
none I have produced two florins
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These coins are all counterfeit, and the two floring
