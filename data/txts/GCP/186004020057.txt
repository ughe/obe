357. GEORGE BROWN (25), Stealing, on 23 January, 1859, 25
spoons, a fish slice, 15 forks, and a soup-ladle; the property of Charles
Frederick Phillips, in his dwelling-house. 3 5

MR. M. J. O'CONNELL conducted the Prosecution.

MARIAN CRAGG. I am a single-woman and am now servant to Dr. Willis
of Barnes-last January twelvemonth I was servant to Mr. Charles
Frederick Phillips, of 48, Inverness-terrace, Bayswater--I was out one
morning in that month, and came home about 12 o'clock-I came back to
the house down the area and saw a man in the paintry-I see that man now
--the prisoner is the person--the pantry is through the soullery, through
the kitchen, and at the end of a passage I saw him in the pantry- he was
taking the plate out of the basket-I went into the kitchen--he could see
me tben- he came to me into the kitchen and said he was very sorry for
what he had done and he hoped I would forgive him he had his hands full
of plate ; silver-forks and spoons-he said he would put it all down if I
would let him go-- he put down what he had in his hands I said I would
call assistance-he said I should not; he would leave the plate after he had
put it down he attempted to go, and I took hold of him-he then took
some more plate out of his pocket, some forks and spoons and a soup-ladle
-the plate was all put on the kitchen dresser-I caught hold of him by
bis coat when he attempted to go, and he pushed me back after he had
put down the second quantity of plate, he again tried to get away, and
stoceeded, after I had called for help-- he got away through the scullery,
and up the area-steps—I went after him and called out butterman
caught him, of the name of Bird-he was brought back to the kitchen my
mistress was there then the plate was then counted and it was found to be
right-my mistress then let the prisoner go. I don't exactly remember how
many forks and spoons there were there were upwards of forty on the
dresser--they were made up of what he had in his hands when I saw him
first, and what he afterwards took out of his pocket-I am quite sure the
prisoner is the man.

Cross-examined by MR. TAYLER. Q. I suppose you were somewhat
frightened on this occasion ? A. I was rather surprised at seeing a strange
man-I have not said I was frightened--the police came to fetch me some
time after and took me to the station-house; and the prisoner was brought
out with five or six men--that was rather more than a month ago I had not
seen him in the interval-he was then in custody--the policeman told me
they thought they had got the man who had stolen my master's plate, and I
was to pich him out-I recognised him when he was brought out and I
I picked him out-I should have known him anywhere if my attention
had been called to him-he wore a brown coat at the time and a felt cap,
not a hat and darkish trousers-his features altogether I think are rather
peculiar-I had described him to the police at the time of the robbery- the
police came, after the occurrence, to my master's house, and I described him then.
