he died from the effects of the violence to which he had been subjected—from the character of the wound I should think he must have been stooping at the time the blow was struck, or he might possibly have been on the ground; I can't say—he was a taller man than the pri soner—I should say the person who struck the blow was in front of him—the bruises I have spoken of might have been caused at the same time, by the back of the same weapon, I mean the graze on the wrist and the jamb on the finger.
   Cross-examined.  I made a microscopic examination of what was on the bill-hook, not of the bill-hook itself—I could find no recent marks of its having been knocked about—it is very blunt—I examined it on the Satur day, the 28th, following the Thursday—I stated before the Magistrate "There were two hairs adhering to it which were human hairs, but they had evidently been there some time "—that had reference to the pole—there were either rabbit or hare hairs, on the bill-hook, they corresponded with either—I was before the Magistrate and also before the coroner—the deceased never complained of being struck with a sharp instrument—I have been qualified as a medical man two years—there was a scalp wound.
