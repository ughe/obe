     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 38.
 Before Mr. Baron Hullock.
 1271.  EDWIN MARTIN VAN BUTCHELL was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying William Archer.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 EMMERSON ARCHER. I am a silk manufacturer, and the brother of  William Archer; he had no complaint for months before the 10th of May, that I know of; we were always together, and I should have known if he had - his last severe illness was last Christmas twelve months; he at times said his inside was a little out of order: for a week before the 10th of May he stated himself to be better in strength and spirits than he had been for months - I lived next door to him: he came to me on the 10th of May, about five minutes before he went out, and asked the way to Golden-square - I told him; I went out for about three hours, and when I returned he was at home, lying on the sofa, so unwell; he could not rise without assistance - this was between one and two o'clock: I persuaded him to get up and come down to dinner, in order to rally him, but he hardly took any thing; he appeared in great pain, and great agitation - towards five o'clock he got so extremely ill that I persuaded him to go to bed, and helped him up stairs - he got gradually worse; I got Dr. Lloyd about eight o'clock that evening; he died on the Saturday morning following - from the time he took to his bed he was certain he should not live, and arranged his affairs; he said he should never recover - he said nothing more than that, as to the certainty of his death.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. Had Mr. Lloyd been attending your brother for any length of time? A. Not for two months; he then paid Mr. Lloyd two or three visits; he had a depression of spirits in consequence of the death of his wife - there was nothing the matter with him at this time; his inside had been out of order - that was set right by a little medicine: I believe Mr. Lloyd wrote for him - his wife died of a complication of diseases- I have heard she had some stoppage in her inside, what it was I cannot say, and there was a tumor also. I attended the Coroner's Inquest; Mr. Van Butchell was not there - the verdict was returned without his being there: no proposal was made to delay it till he could he sent for; an attorney came from Mr. Van Butchell, and requested to see my brother's body, on Friday, when it was too late - he came about three hours before the funeral: the friends were all assembled, and the body fastened down; Mr. Van Butchell was taken into custody on the day of the Inquest(Wednesday); we refused to grant a view of the body, as it was fastened down, and not in a fit state to be opened - I saw Mr. Lloyd after the Inquest; I know of no application being made for a surgeon to see the body till the Friday I mention; if any body had come to see it on behalf of Mr. Van Butchell in proper time, he should have seen it; I did not say if I had the Coroner's authority they might see it - a paper was brought with a request from the Coroner, the day after the funeral.
 COURT. Q. Do you know whether your brother had walked from Golden-square? A. He walked to the first coach-stand, and rode the rest of the way.
 MR.  EUSIBUS ARTHUR LLOYD. I live in Frederick's -place, Old Jewry, and am a surgeon. I called on the deceased about the 8th or 10th of May, and found him in a state of great depression of mind and body, apparently suffering great uneasiness; he complained of great uneasiness in the abdomen, and greater pain in the lower part, towards the left side - he said, "I am aware I have received such an injury in my bowels as I shall not recover;" my opinion from an examination of him, and from his report of an operation he had undergone, was that his bowels were seriously injured: I stated that I considered his life in a very uncertain state, but that at that moment it was impossible for me to say what injury he had sustained: independent of the injury he had received, there were not those symptoms to lead me to suppose he must necessarily die; I had several conversations with him - I endeavoured to encourage him, but his expression always was, he believed he should not recover. On the evening of his decease I examined his body, and found the whole of the abdomen very much extended, and on cutting it I found it arose from gas; the contents of the abdomen altogether exhibited appearances of inflammation: the general appearance of the covering of the viscera was that of recent inflammation - the parts were gathered together by a sort of lymph and serum, mixed with flakes of lymph in the cavity of the abdomen: the appearances of inflammation were greater in the lower part of the abdomen, and on endeavouring to examine the upper part of the rectum, I found such inflammation had gone on to that part, that another portion of the intestine was adhering to it - I was anxious to examine that part particularly, and as some injury might be occasioned by removing the parts at once from the body, I examined them in their natural situation; for that purpose it was necessary to separate a portion of the intestines, which was adhering to it - I did so, and at once perceived the rectum had been perforated, so that its cavity, and that of the abdomen were injured - I proceeded to remove the injured part from the body, and the portion of the intestines that adherred to it; I was then enabled to examine them more particularly, and was then confirmed in my opinion, that it was occasioned by violence, and not by disease; I have subsequently examined them again and again, and am the more fully confirmed in my opinion; any uneven instrument being introduced would have produced this injury, whether sharp or blunt - I have here a straight clyster-pipe, six inches long, by which a similar accident was occasioned.
