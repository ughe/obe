I am a carman, and live in Thomas-street, Lambeth. I have known the prisoner about twenty-five years—he came to me in Feb., and said that my brother was dead, in South America—I have a brother, who has been settled at Buenos Ayres about twenty years—I have not heard any thing of him for the last ten or twelve years, and I thought there might be some truth in it—he said I had been advertised for twice—he said he had left between 3000 l . and 4000 l .—he said he would make an application to the Court of Chancery for the money—a few days after, on the 13th of Feb., I saw him again—he brought me this piece of parchment, and said he had ap plied to the Court of Chancery, and paid 12 s . 6 d . for it—I believed what he said, and gave him five half-crowns—he called about a week or ten days afterwards, brought a petition, and wanted two guineas, but I did not give it.
   Prisoer. Q.  Where do you reside? A.  I have two houses, one is at No. 10, Gibson-street, and the other No. 1, Thomas-street, but they commu nicate with each other—I have lived there nearly twenty years—I did not make in agreement with you before any business was done—I did afterwards—I was to pay you 2 1/2 per cent.—this is it, but it is not in the same state—the 3 l . has been put in since—( This paper was signed by the witness, author  ising the prisoner to make application to the Court of Chancery for unclaimed property, and agreeing to pay any fees not exceeding 3 l., to be returned if the witness was not the right party )—you told me the fees would be 2 l . or 3 l .—this agreement was made after I paid you the 12 s . 6 d .
