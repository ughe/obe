GEORGE SMITH. I am a policeman. On Saturday evening last I was called in to Nash—there were words between him and the prisoner—they were quieter, and before I had got a few yards from the house, I heard a dreadful smash, and a cry of, "Murder and police"—I went back—Nash passed me as I went to the house—the blood was running from his head—I have his clothes here—I took the prisoner into custody. HENRY PETTNER FREEMAN. I am a surgeon and dresser at St. Tho mas's hospital. Nash has been under my care since Tuesday—I examined his head on Tuesday—it had been dressed before I saw it—he had an in cised wound, similar to one made by glass, about an inch, or rather more, long—it was only one wound—it extended nearly to the bone—it was not dangerous, but might have become so—he complained of pain in his head, but had no fever—I saw the fragments of the bottle—it appears to be a common champagne bottle—I consider that a dangerous sort of instrument.
   Prisoner's Defence . He refused to pay my wages; I told him that was wrong; he got up, seized hold of me, and told me to protect myself, I got hold of the bottle, and hit him twice over the head. GUILTY  .— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor.— Confined Six Months.

EDWARD THOMAS was indicted for breaking and entering the counting-house of Thomas Evans and another, on the   13th of August , at   St. Saviour, Southwark , and stealing therein 1 pair of spectacles, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 hat, value 2 s  .; 1 coat, value 6 s  .; the goods of Thomas Evans; and 3 pairs of compasses, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 2400 pence, and 4800 halfpence, the property of Thomas Evans and another. THOMAS EVANS. I am in partnership with John Lascelles, of Great Guildford-street, St. Saviour, Southwark. On the 13th of August, about half-past six in the morning, I went to the premises, and found the counting-house door open, and the desks forced—I missed out of my desk a pair of spec tacles, three pairs of compasses, and some loose copper of my own; also some coats, a waistcoat, and hat—the key of the iron-chest, which had been locked up in my desk, was taken out—20 l . in copper belonging to the firm was gone from the chest—I had locked the desk about half-past one the day before—the counting-house is in a yard, at the back of the house of Mr. Burgess, our clerk—it is enclosed by a wall, and there is a door leading to Mr. Lascelles premises from it. JOHN LASCELLES, JUN. I live with my father, and am employed in the counting-house—about half-past seven or a quarter to eight o'clock, I shut up the counting-house, locked the door, and took the key—it was all safe at nine o'clock.
