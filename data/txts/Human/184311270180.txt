JANE CHRISTIE SWINYARD. I am the wife of Edward Swinyard, a baker, of New-row, Deptford. On Friday, the 24th of Nov., about half-past eleven o'clock, I caught a glimpse of some one from my yard—I went into the shop, and there found Myers on the counter, with his hand in the till—I said, "What do you do at my till?"—he jumped up off the counter, and said, "I was not at your till"—I had put four penny pieces in it just before, and they were gone, and I saw the money in his hand.
   Myers.  It was the money I went in with. I did not put my hand into the till. TIMOTHY DARLEY. I followed the prisoners from Rotherhithe, which is upwards of a mile from the prosecutor's—I saw them looking in at the pro secutor's shop—Myers then went in—I afterwards saw Griffiths leave the shop-window, and in the meantime Mr. Swinyard came in and took Myers, who was in the act of taking the money out of the till—I took Griffiths back to the shop, and gave him in charge. JAMES MAHONY( policeman. ) I took Myers, and found on him a knife, a fourpenny-bit, and four penny pieces in his hand—he slipped them into his pocket—they dropped down through his trowsers, and I picked them up. DANIEL M'PHERSON(policeman.) I produce a certificate of Myers' for mer conviction—( read )—he is the person.
   MYERS  — GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined One Year .
   GRIFFITHS  — GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Six Months .

I heard Ward say to Mr. Holmes, that Mr. Stanley would be obliged to him to let him have 30 l . till the following Tuesday, which he was to take him when he went for his orders—I delivered him a parcel of silver, which Mr. Holmes gave me. SAMUEL HOLMES. I delivered the parcel, containing twenty crowns, 120 half crowns, 150 shillings, eighty sixpences, and thirty groats, to Temple—Ward was in my service, and was to deliver it to Mr. Stanley. ABSALOM JOHN STANLEY. Ward did not bring the 30 l . of silver to me—I have not received any part of it. MARIA ANN BAX. I keep the Star at Canterbury—between six and seven o'clock in the evening of the 25th of Oct., the prisoners and two men arrived at my house with a horse and cart—the prisoners asked for beds, and slept there—in the morning they got up, and Rafferty asked if I could give him gold for silver—he produced 15 l . in silver, and I gave him fifteen sovereigns for it. JAMES EPPS( police-constable C  5.) I apprehended tne prisoners at Can terbury, and charged them with absconding with 30 l . in silver from Deptford—when we got to the station, Rafferty said that they were the boys, and they might as well speak the truth—Ward said, "I am the boy that took the money"—I found on Rafferty eight sovereigns, five half crowns, a silver scent-box, a gold ring, and a knife.
