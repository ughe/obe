GEORGE STEWART. I live at 21, Orwell Place, Edinburgh I was
confidential clerk to Mr. John Barry Russell, at 15, Hope Street,
Edinburgh, a wine merchant-in September, 1887, Mr. Russell
advertised for a London agent, and eventually engaged Fearns on
commission-Fearns procured the orders produced from Wells and Co.,
amounting to 51. .148., 111. 88.; and from Goodwin and Co., 41. 8.,
4 guineas, 2 guineas, and 4 guineas; and from Lewis and Co., 51. 148.,
and other orders, amounting altogether to 1491.-of that amount only
10 or 12 guineas were paid-we supplied the goods in the ordinary
way of trade.
Cross-examined by Mr. Moyses. Mr. Russell engaged Fearns on 27th
December, 1887–Mr. Russell was sole proprietor; he is in Chicago
Fearns had no business to collect moneys for our firm-a good deal of
correspondence passed between Mr. Russell and Fearns--Mr. Russell
managed; he wrote letters or dictated them to me-we had one clerk-
the affair was pretty large at times ; it has gone wrong, I believe-all
we asked Fearns was if he was ia the neighbourhood of parties ordering
stuff, to look them up, and send the remittances to us—the prosecutor
brought the letters; I have a few here to refresh my memory-I do
not remember a letter from Fearns, stating that he would do his best
to collect accounts, and get money in; I cannot swear there was not
his commission was 10 per cent on accounts being paid-Mr. Russell
failed in April; he commenced business in 1858—if Fearns wanted
samples he wrote, and we sent them free of cost-we inquired as to the
stability of the customers he introduced -I said at the Police-court
there was an account from Mr. Steel still unpaid of .31. 168.-I cannot
see why Mr. Steel's cheque should be returned through the dead-letter
office in July-We supplied one order to a Mr. Meiter-he paid us.
Cross-examined by MR. BURNIE. We give 30 days' credit.
