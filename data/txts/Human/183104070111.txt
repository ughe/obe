 937.  GEORGE BLAKE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , 1 chaise-rug, value 2s., the goods of John Cotton ; and 1 pair of boots, value 2s. 6d., the goods of William Everett.
   THOMAS JOSEPH WEST. I am a Police-constable. On the Tuesday before the 5th of March, I was in Hopkin-street, St. James; I saw the prisoner carry something under his arm, into an old iron-shop - I followed him, and saw he had this pair of boots and this chaise rug; he asked if they bought such things - the girl saw me behind him, and said they did not; I asked what he had got - he said an old rug which his master had given him to cut for horse-cloths - I went to inquire at a place he sent me to, and it was denied - I said to him the next morning, "It was useless for you to give me so much trouble last night;" he said, "I will not give you any more trouble; I took the rug from Howard's livery-stable, in Jermyn-street;" the boots, he said, were his own, and he was in distress - his wife and children appeared to be so.
   WILLIAM EVERETT. I am coachman to Lord Romney, of York-street, St. James'. These boots are mine - I kept them in Howard's stable, in Jermyn-street; the prisoner had been at work for a few days there - I missed them the day after they were lost.
 EDWARD ROBERTS. I am groom to John Cotton,  Esq., of No.12, Carlton-chambers, Regent-street. This rug belongs to him; I did not miss it till the officer brought it me - I had seen the prisoner working two or three days at Howard's livery stables.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. - Confined Two Days.
 938.  JOHN BOSTON and JAMES CLARK were indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 1 handkerchief, value 2s. 6d., the goods of a person unknown, from his person.
   THOMAS JOSEPH WEST. I am a Police-constable. On the night of the 24th of February I was in company with Hobbs, in St. James-street, about nine o'clock - the two prisoners were pointed out to me by a person, named Dorrell - I saw a gentleman and lady there - Boston went behind the gentleman, and drew a handkerchief out of his left-hand pocket, took it in his left-hand, and put it behind him to the other prisoner - I put my arms round them, and called to Hobbs, "They have got it;" Hobbs came and took one - I took the other, and the handkerchief laid on the ground at their feet - it was an illumination night, and owing to the gangs that were about, it was impossible to get the gentleman without losing the prisoners.
   THOMAS HOBBS. I am a Police-constable. I was with West - I saw the prisoners going up against gentlemen's pockets; we watched them some time - West called to me; I went up, put their feet on one side, and took up the handkerchief between the two prisoners.
   GEORGE DORHELL. I saw the prisoners together on the night of the 24th of February - they attempted several pockets, and I mentioned it to the officers; I saw Boston take hold of a gentleman's pocket, put his hand into his pocket, and take out this handkerchief.
 THOMAS HOBBS. This is the handkerchief. I asked Boston what was on it - he said he did not believe there was any thing on it, but there is J. C. on it.
 Clark's Defence. There was a mob of two thousand people - the handkerchief was picked up behind two women; I was two yards from this young man - I had not been in the crowd two minutes before I was taken.
 Boston's Defence. The officer caught hold of me first, I asked what he wanted, and he let me go - the J. C. are the initials of my second father's name.
   JAMES COSGROVE. I am step-father to Boston. I have known him since he was three months old - he never knew his own name till lately, as he was always reared with me and served his time to me - he always went by my name till he was taken, and then he gave his right name.
 JURY. Q. Had you any silk handkerchiefs? A. Yes, of different colours, but it is seldom I wear one - my son has taken them when he thought proper; I do not know whether I lost one or not.
 BOSTON -    GUILTY. Aged 18.
 CLARK -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Life.
 939.  WILLIAM CARR was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of April , 1 chaise-harness, value 40s., the goods of Samuel Ewins.
   JAMES BOYCE COX. I am a Police-officer. On the night of the 4th of April I was in St. John-street - I saw the prisoner and another come out of the Pewter Platter-yard; the prisoner was carrying a set of harness on his shoulder; he crossed into Wilderness-row before I stopped him - I asked what he had there; he said it was his own, that he was going to take it home, and I might go with him and see where he took it to - I said it was of no consequence where he was going to take it to, but I wished to know where he got it; he then went down Pewter Platter-yard (there was another man with him); he threw down the harness under a cart, and said that was where he found it when he went down to a place of convenience - I took him to the station; but before I took him away, I saw a coach-house door open; when I had taken him away, I returned and got a light; I then found the coach-house door was broken open, and this piece of a lock was inside.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. He took you to the yard where he said he found the harness? A. Yes, but I had seen him come out there - this was about half-past twelve o'clock.
   JESSE EAST. I am in the service of Mr. Samuel Ewins, a bookbinder, who lives in Duke-street, Smithfield - he has a coach-house in Pewter Platter-yard. On the afternoon of the 4th of April I fastened the door of the coach-house, leaving this harness inside it - I found the door broken open, and the padlock broken; this is his harness.
