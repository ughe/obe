 762. SAMUEL SALE was indicted for stealing 10oz. weight of leather, 11 d  ., the goods of Frederick Lennie . FREDERICK LENNIE, shoemaker, High-street, Bow.  The prisoner worked for me at home—I marked the materials I delivered him with an iron stamp—on the 2nd Feb. he brought me some small boots—I examined them and felt the soles of one, and found it was very soft, which it should not be, knowing the stuff I had given him—I said, "This it not filling enough"—he said it was wet—I saw a stamp that was not mine—I said, "I suspect you have been forging the mark, if I find you have I will prosecute you"—there is one pair of soles and three pair of heels which are not mine—I had given him out the materials for the shoes with my own mark—I let him have fresh work, and he went away—I looked at these again and found they were an imitation of my mark—in the evening I went with a policeman and gave him into custody—I found all the leather that was not made up there—it was worth about 10 s .
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When did you give him the leather which you charge him with stealing? A.  Between the 21st and the 22nd Jan. and the 2nd Feb.—I gave it out myself—I cannot say how much I gave out between those times—this pair of shoes was made up by the pri soner with my leather—this coloured leather is different leather—he had leather which in process of making would come to this colour.
   COURT , Q.  How many have the stamp which is not yours? A.  Four pair of top pieces and one pair of soles. JOHN PAROSE( police-constable K  384.) I took the prisoner—he said he had not robbed his master, but at the station he said, "I admit having changed one sole, having met with an accident and burnt one of Mr. Lennie's." NOT GUILTY.
 763. JONATHAN BARTHOLOMEW was indicted for stealing 31 live tame fowls, price 3 l  .; 10 s  ., the property of John Forster . WILLIAM MASTERS( police-constable N  242.) About a quarter before three on the morning of 1st Feb. I saw the prisoner and another person in Compton-street, St. Paul's-place, I slington—there was another person about fifty yards from him—the prisoner had a bag—I asked what he had got in it—he said, "Fowls"—they had just taken them from the Cambridge wagon—I said he most go with me to the station—he said he would—he set the bag down, the other took it up and carried it some distance—they both followed me—they set them down, the third man came up, and he carried them a little way—they then put the bag down—the others ran away and left the prisoner—I took him—I found twelve dead fowls and two live ones in die bag—the dead ones were quite warm—the bag was covered with blood at the bottom—I found on the prisoner a knife, and a piece of rope and some feathers in his hat, and a handkerchief—he said he had been to see a shipmate, and in returning met these men who asked if he would earn 1 s ., they took him to the bottom of a lane and told him to wait there, and they brought these things to him to carry.
