 949.  MICHAEL WALDON was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of March , 1 plane, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Martin.
   THOMAS MARTIN. I live in Lower-street, Islington, and am a carpenter. On the 12th of March I lost a plane from my bench, in an unfinished house in Chadwell-street- I left it safe at dinner-time, and did not miss it till about four o'clock; the prisoner was employed on the same premises - this is the plane.
   HENRY FOWLER. I the service of Mr. Cordwell, a pawnbroker, in Exmouth-street, on the 12th of March the prisoner brought this plane to out shop - he pawned it for 6d, in the name of John Davies, for his father; he came twice to the shop with it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not on the first occasion doubt whether this was the person?
 A. I did not recognize him in the first instance, but that was on a different charge; his brother was brought up, and while he was gone for his brother another person asked if I knew him, and I said No - there is a brotherly likeness between them; I believe he is older, but not so tall- I did not express a doubt whether the prisoner was the person who pawned the plane; I did not entertain any doubt about that.
 THOMAS LOW. I am a constable. I took the prisoner into custody.
 Prisoner's Defence. The pawnbroker said when I went with my brother, that I was the lad who pawned it. and I was not - he said I went there between three and five O'clock.
   THOMAS MARTIN re-examined. Q. Did you know the prisoner's brother? A. Yes - he never worked there but one day, which was after the 12th of March, when his brother was ill - I think the pawnbroker's shop is half a mile from where I worked.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 19.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. - Confined 1 Month.
 950.  GEORGE THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 1 tea-kettle, value 1s,6d. the goods of Richard Jones.
   WRIGHT WOODHOUSE. I live with Mr. Richard Jones - he keeps a public-house at the corner of Plumtree-street and Broad-street, St. Giles'. I used the tea-kettle on the 28th of March, about seven o'clock in the morning; the prisoner came in about that time - I missed the kettle between half-past seven and eight o'clock; I had put it down by the side of the pump, in the corner, over the counter - I went on with my work, and when I came to look for the kettle, I missed it.
   WILLIAM HARRIS. I am a Police-constable. On the 28th of March a latl came running to me from a shop to take a man with a kettle; I went to take him for stealing other things-he had this kettle, which he said was given to him by woman to sell for 1s.
 JURY. Q. Had he the kettle in his hand? A. No - it was on the counter, where he offered it for sale; it was stated in his presence that he had offered it.(Properly produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defenee. A woman met me at the corner of George-street, and asked me to sell this kettle for her - I went to try to sell it, and the woman sent for the officer.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Six Months.
 951.  JOHN SCARFE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of March , 1 pair of shoes, value 6s., the goods of Clara Collyer.
   WILLIAM SIBLEY. I am a Police-constable. On the 24th of March I saw the prisoner and another man in St. Martin's-court - I watched them for three-quarters of an hour; they were dressed very shabby, and were looking into respectable shops; I told the prisoner to go away - he went one way and I went the other; there were two persons going the same way as he was - I saw him go to the prosecutrix's shop, take these shoes, and put them under his arm - I took him with them.
   CLARA COLLYER. I am single. I keep a boot and shoe-shop in St. Martin's-court. The officer brought the prisoner and the shoes to my shop about five o'clock - they are mine, and are worth 6s; I had seen them safe about half an hour before.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.
 952.  GEORGE STONE was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 2 loaves of bread, value 1s. 4d. the goods of John Elwin.
 ARN ELWIN. I am the wife of John Elwin - he lives  in Wellington-street, Bethnal-green, and is a baker, On the 25th of February I was in a little room which joins the shop - there is a glass window between them; I saw the prisoner, who had just taken two loaves from the window, and was walking out with them - I opened the door, and called to him; he put them on the threshold, and ran away - I am quite sure he is the person; Mr Gardiner brought him back - put the same loaves on the counter, and the policeman took them.
 EDMUND GARDINER. I live in Wellington-street, Bethnal-green. On the 25th of February I was in Selby-street - the prosecutor's shop is at the corner; I saw the prisoner running fast from their door - the prosecutrix came to the shop, and called to me to stop him, which I did.
 MICHAEL JOURDAN. I am a Police-constable. I was sent for, and took the prisoner - I received two loaves from the prosecutrix and her husband.
 Jury to ANN ELWIN. Q. Was the prisoner in the habit of coming to the shop? A. No - I thought I had seen him pass the door.
 GUILTY. Aged 17.
 Recommended to mercy by the Jury.
