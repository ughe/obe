GEORGE Angus. I work for Mrs. Nash. On Tuesday, the 9th of
April, about twelve o'clock, I was in the field minding the sheep, and saw
the two prisoners in the road, looking over the hedge into the field
where the sheep were-I counted the sheep with Cockerell, in the even
ing, and there were thirty-four-I missed one next day.
NELSON SMITH. I am a policeman. I was sent for on this occasion-
I went into the field and saw footsteps of two persons, which lead to the
end of Sweetbriar-walk, about three quarters of a mile from the field-I
obtained a search-warrant, and went to Julph's house-he lives with his
mother, and Price lodges there occasionally-I found the two prisoners in
bed together, at least Julph was in bed, and Price at the foot of the bed,
partly dressed-I searched the premises, and found in a box which was
locked, two shoulders, two legs, and part of a loin of mutton-I have
compared that with the skin, and am certain it belonged to it-I compared
the prisoners' shoes with the footmarks in the field, and they quite matched
-they are both very peculiar shoes—there was blood on the pair belong-

1410. MARY ROWLEY Was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of
April, 1 quartern of flour, value 9d., the goods of John Bell. RSS
MARY ANN BELL. I am the wife of John Bell, a corn-chandler. On
the 12th of April, about half-past six o'clock, the prisoner came into the
shop for a pint of split-peas and some oats-while I was gone round for
them, I missed a quartern of flour-I told her she bad got my property
in her apron-she said she had nothing of mine I took hold of her wrist,
and took the flour out of her lap- she resisted, and burst the bag open-
this is it.
Cross-examined by Mr. Lord. Q. Did she offer to pay for the flour?
A. No-she said nothing whatever about it-it was on the counter-she
had her apron up before her, and I suspected her.
