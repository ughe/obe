1854. MARY GOUGH was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July,
1) pint of wine, value, 3a. ; 2 bottles, value 6d, ; 1 pint of brandy, value
Ss. 6d. ; 1 bag, value 18. ; 1 inkstand, value 6d. : +lb. weight of tea, value
1a. and 1 peneil-ease, value 6d. the goods of Richard John Moscrop, her
master.
ELIZABETH MOsCROP. I am the wife of Richard John Moserop, and live
in Covent-garden--the prisoner was my servant. On the 27th of July I
missed two bottles, and this wine and brandy, and other things-in con-
sequence of suspicion, I sent for an officer, and these things were found in
her box in my presence.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. What was the first thing you
found A. I think it was the bottle of brandy-these are the
things-I know this bottle by the seal on'the cork-it has not been
drawn-the prisoner was a very confidential servant-she attended to the
people who came, as a waitress-she might get a trifle from there, but
ours is not a house where much money is given-ours is the Market-
house, Covent-garden--] cannot swear to this brandy-probabl some
persons might have given her these things-the inkstand cost about 18. or
la. d.-there is no mark on it-I know it was on the shelf, and it was gone
-the house was crowded about the election--] cannot exactly say how
lately before I had seen the inkstand--I cannot swear to the tea, but I can
swear to making this bag the night before it was taken-the tea was
found in the bag in her box-the bag is scarcely worth any thing.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 2.-Recommended to merey by the Jury and Prosecutrin.
