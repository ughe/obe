GEORGE BARTON (Re-stamined by MR. GRAIN). I said at the Police
Court, when cross-examined by Wymark, “I have changed a cheque for
you before, and it was all right”-I had done so-I received a letter

The Prisoners' Statements before the Magistrato.-Laird: "If the Criminal
Investigation heard anything about Laird and Co. two years ago, as Mr.
Wontner says, they should have followed it up. I am not aware that the
prosecution have proved anything beyond Thompson's 108. 6d., or Green
and Co.'s 91. 108., and a matter of 38. 6d. The most of the hours I have
spent in prison has been taken up with making inquiries. I wish to
reserve my defence." Barres : “I am innocent of the charge. I only
know Laird as a friend." Hodges: "I am in no way implicated. I
have been a merchant all my life. I have the one misfortune
of taking such a servant in my employ. I will go through and clear
myself."
