MARY ANN DRABBLE. I live with Mr. Jonathan James Tarrant, who is my stepfather, in Chiswell-street, St. Luke's. The policeman brought the prisoner into the shop—I had observed him five minutes before, looking in at the window, standing just in front of the window which was found broken—the policeman brought him in, and asked if I was aware we had been robbed—I opened the case inside the window, and found 1lb. of cheroots gone—I had seen them safe ten minutes previous—there had been 4lbs. there—I weighed them, and 1lb. was gone—the officer said, "Come out side, and see what is lying on the pavement," and there were three cheroots—that was after I had been to the station—I found a large piece of glass out of the window (it was cracked before, but perfectly safe) a person could put his arm in, and reach the cheroots—they were right in front of the pane—the window forms part of the glass case they were in—when I returned from the station, I found three cheroots on the pave ment at the next shop, which was closed—they were precisely the same size and quality as I lost.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is not the parish called St. Luke, Old-street? A.  I think I have heard it called so—I did not know the quantity of cheroots lost till I weighed them—it was half an hour after that I found three on the pavement—I never saw the prisoner before. NOT GUILTY.
