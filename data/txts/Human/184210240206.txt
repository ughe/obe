   MR. DOANE . Q.  Was it just before you left the Bull and Mouth that you saw these people, and the others you have named, frequenting the house? A.  It was not long before—they used to come there three or four times a day sometimes—I saw them till I did leave—Mr. Sherman is the owner of the Bull and Mouth—Mr. Saunderson is his son-in-law—I am in the same service now. WILLIAM SWANSON. I am porter at the Swan-with-two-Necks. I have seen Shackleford there—I first saw him there towards the end of May last—he asked if I had any parcels for Mr. Beaumont, of Maidstone—I said I had one, it was a box—I gave him the parcel, directed to Mr. Beaumont, of Maidstone—he left it in my care again, and went into the coffee-room—he came again the same day, and took it away in a cab  —a great many more parcels came directed to Mr. Beaumont, of Maidstone—I saw Shackleford daily for a fortnight or three weeks after—the parcels were sometimes boxes, and sometimes brown paper parcels, sometimes small trusses—they were always taken away by Shackleford, with the exception of three, which were carried away by a porter—Shackleford was there when the porter came for the first, and of course he had the others by Shackleford's directions—one or two letters came directed to Mr. Beaumont, which were left at the bar—they were given to Shackle ford—I saw him open one or two—be generally took parcels away in the cab  —I addressed him as Mr. Beaumont, and he always answered to that name—there is a gateway going into Lad-lane—I always fetched the cabs  for Shackleford, with the exception of one or two—the cab  was driven up the gateway into the yard to the door of the hotel, the goods were put in, and Shackleford drove off—people passing could not see what was going on up the gateway, unless they stopped to look up the yard—I saw him there for a fortnight or three weeks, sometimes once a day, sometimes twice a day—he always came to fetch parcels for Beaumont, of Maidstone. Cross-examined by MR. PRICE Q.  You saw no others there taking any part in this? A.  No; there were others—I do not know that Shackle ford's name is not Beaumont—when a man has luggage to take away, the cabs  generally stop in the street—if we fetch a cab  for a gentleman, we leave it outside—all this was done under Shackleford's direction.
   MR. DOANE Q.  There were others with him? A.  I saw one or two in company with him, but that was generally in the coffee-room—I never spoke to them—I have seen him in company with the same persons more than once—the parcels were not sent to Maidstone, because Shackleford called and desired all parcels directed to Beaumont, of Maidstone, to be taken care of.
