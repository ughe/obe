you first come to No. 15 and then to 16-the two rooms had a communicat-
ing door-Miss Toovey had been my seeretary for eight or nine years-
at the street door there is a hall porter's box, where the keys are kept
-8 porter is there on duty throughout the day, and letters are also put
there for tenanta-in my room there was a safe with large doors, and
inside it two drawers, each with a separate lockif the doors of the
safe were unlocked and opened you would see a big space above and
two drawers below-in the upper space I was in the habit of keeping
my books of accounts, cheque books and pass books: also some old
used cheque books-there were two keys to the door, which opened the
safe; I kept one and Miss Toovey-the other-the keys for the drawers in
the safe were kept exolusively by myself-there were no duplicates of
those keys-the safe had been there for two or three years before Septem-
ber, 1904-I kept the key of my door-my key would not open her door
and her key would not open mine, they looked alike, but one would not
open the other-s far as I know, these were the only two keys of the corti-
dor doors-I have no recollection of leaving my door key with Miss Toovey,
but she would have access to it, because I sometimes deposited it with the
hall porter below when I went out, and if she had wanted it she could

it to her-up to September of last year I had three banking accounts in
