   JOHN MICHAEL JOLLY(Police-constable T 164). I took the prisoner, and found on him two duplicates; one of an umbrella, pawned for 2s., and the other a pair of salts.
   THOMAS PEWTNER. I am a pawnbroker, I have the salts, which were pawned by the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)

 195.  FELIX PERRY was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of November , 1 cap, value 8s., the goods of Joseph Spencer , from the person of John Spencer.
   JOSEPH SPENCER. The prisoner was brought to me by a neighbour, and I was informed he had stolen a cap from my son John's head - this is the cap; I know it is my son's- he had been sent to school with it, and as he came back this happened; he is six years old.
   ROBERT MANN. I was coming along Myrtle-street, and heard the child scream; the prisoner ran towards me: I caught him - he said, "Pray, let me go," and the cap dropped from him - he said, "Allow me to go back to the child, and I will give it him."
   JOHN BRAND(Police-constable N 199). I have the cap, and took the prisoner.
 GUILTY of stealing only. Aged 14. Judgment Respited.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 196.  JOHN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Harvey Hill , from his person.
   HARVEY HILL. On the 26th of November I was walking down the Commercial-road; when I got through the Cannon-street-road turnpike, I observed the prisoner behind me; I missed my handkerchief, and saw him crossing the road - a young man stopped him for me, and the officer took him; he asked him if he had my handkerchief - he said he had not, but he was searched at the watch-house, and it was found in his possession; this is it.
   GEORGE CHAMBERS(Police-constable K 99). My attention was called to the prisoner; he was asked if he had the prosecutor's handkerchief - he denied it, and said he had not; he was willing to be searched, and to go to the station - I found this handkerchief in the flap of his trousers.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 18. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.

 WILLIAM MARTIN. I  keep the  Blue Posts, in King-street, Golden-square. The prisoner was in my employ for eight or nine months; I gave her money at different times to purchase articles, but not any specific amount.
     NOT GUILTY.
 198.  ANTHONY ARNOLD was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 1 wooden plank, value 17s., the goods of William Evans ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
 WILLIAM EVANS. I live in Shepherdess-walk, Hoxton. On the 28th of August I missed an ash plank from Mr. Sturts' wharf, on the bank of the  Regent's-canal- I had seen it safe on the Saturday, and missed it on Tuesday morning; I found it the same day at Upstill's, a wheeler, in the Canal-road - I know it by my mark on it; it is worth 17s. - the prisoner had no right to sell any timber for me; I do not know that I ever saw him.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not take money of the man for the plank? A. No, he did not look it out; he asked the price of it, and said he would come on the Monday and see if he could deal with me for it.
   CHARLES UPSTILL. I am a wheelwright, and live in the Canal-road. On the 24th of August I went to Mr. Evans' wharf, and asked the price of the plank - he asked 17s.; on the 27th I went there again - I saw the prisoner and another man; the prisoner asked what I wanted - I said I had looked at the plank, and asked if the governor was in the way; he said No, but he could do as well - I said it was all right, and I would send for a truck - he said there was no occasion for it; he would bring it home for half a gallon of beer - he brought it, and I saw my wife pay him for it.
 Prisoner. Q. You asked if you had not given too much for the plank? A. No, you asked what I wanted, and I told you what the price was - I said it was 1s. a foot; I did not ask if there was a truck in the yard - I proposed to go for one, and then you said you and your mate would take it home; I suppose it was five or six o'clock- I did not see you at the Bricklayer's Arms; it was a very wet day - you received the money.
   JAMES GLIBBERY(Police-constable N 248). On the 28th of August I went with the prosecutor to Upstill's; I found the plank there.
   THOMAS HOBBS KING(Police-constable N 248). I apprehended the prisoner last month at Pentonville - I told him it was for stealing an ash plank of Mr. Evans; he said he had seen Mr. Evans, and settled with him - I took him to the station, and then went to Mr. Evans', who said he had not seen him.
   THOMAS COPE. I know the prisoner. On the 8th of February, 1829, I apprehended him for stealing from Messrs. Kings, carpenters, at Islington, two deal planks - he was found guilty, and find 1s.; I have the certificate of his former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office.
 Prisoner. It must be my partner who sold it; I never received the money - when he got it outside the yard, I thought I would help him take it home.
