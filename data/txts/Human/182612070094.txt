 252.  JOB CLAY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of April , 1 truck, value 3l. 10s., the goods of William Hebborn.
   GEORGE HALES. I am a labourer at Botolph wharf; the prisoner is a labourer; Mr. Hebborn's truck stood down the gateway - I was standing there on the 1st of April; the prisoner came and said he came for McCree's truck; Hebborn is a porter, and had lived with McCree; I asked if he was sure McCree had sent him for it; he said he did, as he had a quantity of baggage to bring down; he took it, and went towards the Custom-house with it - I saw no more of him.
 WILLIAM HEBBORN. I am a porter. This is my truck. I never sent the prisoner for it, or authorised him to get possession of it. I have not seen it since. I took him on the 17th of November. He said he had taken it away, but never said what had become of it. I could not find him before.
 Prisoner's Defence. A man came and asked if I wanted a job; he took me up the wharf, and I assisted him with the truck. - Another man joined us, and they took me to Bow, and at last both went away; - they returned, and began swearing, and saying it was not worth the trouble; they could get but 12s. for it.
     GUILTY. Aged 53. Confined Three Months.
 253.  JOHN GLOVER was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of December , 1 shoe, value 5s. the goods of George Goodeve.
   EUPHEMIA GOODEVE. I am the wife of George Goodeve, a shoemaker; we live in Fenchurch-street. The prisoner came in on the 5th of December and offered some saw-dust for sale; I said we wanted none; he still stood there; I told him to go along, and at last he went: - he had been standing with his back to the window in the shop - he returned in a few minutes - stood in the same place, and told me the price of the saw-dust; I told him to go along; he was turning round, and I saw this shoe in his hand, under a sack, which he had on his shoulder: I called a gentleman in, and he was secured; he dropped it, and the gentleman brought it in. - The fellow one was taken the first time he came, I suppose, as both were there before he came.
   JOHN JUDD. - I am an officer, and have the shoe.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Six Months.
 254.  RAYMOND DRAKE was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM HUMPHREYS. I am a green-grocer, and live in St. Dunstan's-hill. The prisoner was my servant, and intrusted to receive money for me; he had 13s. a week, and his breakfast; he should account to me directly he received money; Reynolds bought coals of me - the prisoner never accounted to me for money received from him on the 30th of October.
   RICHARD REYNOLDS. I am a broker. On the 30th of October, between three and four o'clock, I paid the prisoner, 3s. 2d. for two bushels of coals - I am sure there was one shilling among the money I gave him; he gave me this receipt. (read.)
 WILLIAM HUMPHREYS. I did not ask him for this money; he was discharged on the 4th of November, and on the 6th I received information; he called on me - I said I had learned that he had received some money, and not accounted for it, and said I should be obliged if he would tell me if he had received any more; he said he had received this of Mr. Reynolds.
     NOT GUILTY.
 255.  RAYMOND DRAKE was again indicted for the like offence.
   WILLIAM HUMPHREYS. The prisoner never paid me any money received from Elizabeth Read on the 30th of October; he took out goods, and if he was paid for them, he should bring me the money directly.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BARRY. Q. You have a good business I suppose? A. Yes, and keep books; I discovered this account before the prisoner called; he acknowledged receiving it; he had to pay me several small sums in the course of the day.
 ELIZABETH READ. I deal with Humphreys. The prisoner brought coals and potatoes to my house; I paid the prisoner four separate shillings on the 30th of October, for his master; he gave me no receipt.
 Cross-examined. Q. How do you recollect the day? A. I remember it very well.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Six Months.
 256.  JAMES DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., the goods of William Wyllie , from his person.
 WILLIAM WYLLIE. On the 9th of November I was on  Snow-hill- I put my hand to my pocket, and my handkerchief was gone; I turned round, and saw the prisoner drop it; I seized him, took him into a shop, and gave charge of him.
   GEORGE CORBY. I am an officer. I took him in charge, and found a handkerchief on him, which he said was his mother's.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Several witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined One Month and Whipped.
