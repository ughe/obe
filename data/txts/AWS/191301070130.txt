DEMETROS APOsTOLL (prisoner, on oath), through an interpreter, said
that since he had come to London he had intended shooting himsell
but he did not intend shooting anyone else. He had been on Chelses
Bridge and would have jumped into the river, but that there were people
about. He would have shot himself first and then tried to jump into
the river. When he was firing his revolver he was always shooting
towards himself. He left himself in the hands of the Court, but
anyhow he meant to commit suieide.
SINEY REGINALD DYER, Medical Officer at Brixton Prison. Prisoner
is a very exeitalble foreigner, but I do not consider him to be insane
He has threatened suieide and we have had to have him very closely
watched. I think he was mad drunk and really did intend to shoot
himself.
The jury were unable to agree upon a verdict.

WILIAM EDWARD GREEN, grocer, Hammersmith. Prisoner has been
a eustomer and on July 17 of last year he was indebted to me to the
extent of 12s. He same into my shop and gave me a cheque for E3
It was signed by Murray Thompson. I gave him E2 8s. change
Before I paid that in on July 19 I saw prisoner's wife and 8s. Was
owing. A cheque for e3 similarly signed was handed to me and I gave
E2 12s. change. The cheques were not honoured. On August 3
prisoner called on me and I drew his attention to what had happened.
He gave me a cheque for e25, drawn by Ernest Cox. He told me be
had seen the drawer that morning and that the money was in the bank
to meet it. He had goods value 15s. 3yd, and I gave him E3 4s. 8id.,
so that brought the total to E10. I gave him a receipt for the E25
cheque. I paid that. cheque into the bank, but it was not met. On the
following Sunday he sent a note requesting a further E2, but I did not
send the money. I saw him on August 5. I had not then paid in the
cheque. He asked me for E2 and I let him have it on his giving me
his word of honour that the cheque would be met. He never paid me
any money.
