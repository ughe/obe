 1201. THOMAS BRYANT , feloniously receiving 1 spirit-pump, value 14 s  .; the goods of John Hammond , well knowing it to have been stolen.
   MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution. JOHN HAMMOND. I keep the Star and Garter, Woolwich. A man named Brown was in the habit of using my house—I had a copper pump in my stable, worth 14 s . or 15 s .—I had not seen it for some time when Gladwin came, and in consequence of what he said I found it was not there—Brown was apprehended by my direction, and I went on 17th May with him and Gladwin to the prisoner's shop—Gladwin asked Bryant if he had brought any copper or pump—he said he had brought some copper—Gladwin asked how much—he said, "2lbs. or 31bs."—he produced this, and said, "That is it" ( produced )—that has nothing to do with my pump—this metal is much thinner—my pump weighed 6lbs. or 7lbs., and had a sort of cylinder, or basin, at the top.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL . Q.  What is Brown? A.  He is a porter—the prisoner's book was produced—there was an entry corresponding with this copper, but the date did not correspond—he pointed out an entry referring to copper on the 13th—the weight to that was I think 3lbs.—there was no entry of copper on the 16th. GEORGE BROWN( a prisoner. ) On 16th May I took a copper pump of Mr. Hammond's, about three yards long, and broke it into three pieces—each piece remained a portion of a pump—I took it to Mrs. Ford—she refused to buy it, and I took it to the prisoner's, who is a marine-store dealer—I asked whether he would buy it—he said, "Yes"—he weighed it, and said it came to 15 d .—I do not know what it weighed—he did not ask me any questions at all—I was half tipsy—he went out of his shed to his house, got the money, paid me, and I left—we had to go into the street to get to his house, leaving the copper in the shed.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.  A labouring man—I was employed that day to wash two carriages—I am not in constant employment—I live in High-street, Woolwich—I was applied to to be a witness since I have been here—I was not told it would be better for me—I hope I shall get off—this is the first time I have been charged with anything—I was not acquainted with Bryant—I have never been in Maidstone Gaol—the copper produced is not part of what I sold the prisoner. MART FORD. I keep a marine-store shop, in Union-street, Woolwich. On 16th May, Brown brought a big round piece of copper, like a spout—he was tipsy, and I refused to have anything to do with it—I saw him go to Bryant's shop, and come out again with him—he then had no parcel—they went to Bryant's dwelling-house, and then Brown went away—he had nothing with him then.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you been a marine-store dealer? A.  Between five and six years—I live next door but one to Bryant—I am on good terms with him—I have lived in the same house twenty-seven years—I never kept a brothel—I kept a lodging-house—the parties lodged there a night, and away again  —I give 5 d . for old copper.
