 1584.  JOSEPH RICHARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 1 piece of gold, value 1l., the goods of our Lord   the King.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND and LAW conducted the prosecution.
   JAMES CAMPER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Whitechapel-road. On the 6th of September, the prisoner came to my shop, about six o'clock in the evening; he showed this piece of gold, and asked if it was gold; I had my suspicion, and asked, why he supposed it was gold, as it looked more like copper than any thing else - he said, by the weight of it, and he had picked it up opposite Whitechapel-church - I asked if any one saw him pick it up; he said, they might or might not; it was close by a stall, where some women were selling fruit - I asked if he worked at the Mint; he said no, nor any of his relations or friends; that he worked at the West-India Docks, and his name was Thompson; he lived at No. 33, Chicksand-street; I weighed it; I supposed it to be a blank sovereign, I told him to follow me, and show me where he lived; I went there - he said, he wanted to go up stairs, but I prevented it - a young woman then came forward, and I asked if he was her brother? she said, Yes; I asked if he worked at the Mint? she said, No; nor any of her friends or relations - the prisoner heard this, and they endeavoured to communicate with each other, but I prevented it - I then brought the prisoner along Chicksand-street, and when I got near the top, his sister came running after us, and wished to speak to him again; I would not allow it - and she then said, she would tell me the whole truth, his name was Richards, and he did work at the Mint- I took him to Lambeth-street officer, and seven shillings were found on him - I gave information at the Mint; Mr. Bingley, the assay master, cut a piece off this blank, which I have here - it was wet and muddy; it had rained, and the roads were muddy.
   ROBERT FINCH. I am one of the moneyers of the Mint. I was on duty on Wednesday, the 6th of September; I was attending the whole process from room to room; he prisoner had been employed there eighteen months, and was gernerally employed in the cutting-out room- he was in the cutting-room; he had the opportunity of taking away blanks; there were thousands of them there - he had access to them, and must know what they were; he left a little after five o'clock - when gold comes in it is received in bars, each bar is worked separately, and weighed up with a ticket each night; and if in the several weighings which that bar goes through, it is found to be correct - it is then taken to the cutting-out room, and is cut into blanks like this, with the exception of this piece, which is cut off it - there had been some gold there that day, the property of the Bank of England; Henry William Atkinson is a provost of the Mint, and there are others; the Mint is liable to the owner of the gold sent to be coined, to make up the weight - on the day in question, there was, I think, three ounces more gold missing than is usual, from the quantity worked.
   HENRY BINGLEY. I am assistant to the King's assay-master, at the Mint. This blank was brought to me to be assayed; I cut this small piece of it off in the presence of Mr. Atkinson and Mr. Camper - this is such a blank as would make a sovereign if it were stamped; it is the right quality and quantity of gold, and worth one pound.
   JASPER ATKINSON. I am one of the moneyers of the Mint. I attended at Lambeth-street, on the 7th of September; I had a blank delivered to me, which Mr. Camper brought to the Mint, and I weighed it there; it was a standard piece, 5dwts. 3qrs., and a fraction, the exact weight a blank would be, and worth one pound; it was weighed before it was cut.
   HONILY . I am an apprentice to the moneyers of the Mint. It was my duty to weigh the gold. On the 5th of September, I weighed all the gold bars delivered out that day; the tickets were all attached to be bars - I put the account of the pieces cut out of these bars, and delivered it to Mr. Finch, who was the moneyer on duty that day.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Confined Six Months.

 MR. ANDREWS conducted the prosecution.
