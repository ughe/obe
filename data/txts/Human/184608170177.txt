WILLIAM JOHNSON. I had the misfortune of being convicted two years ago next October for a misdemeanor respecting a check—I suffered three months for that—I have known the prisoner by the names of Pool, Ford, and Pitch-ford—on the 2nd of July he engaged my son to ferment some molasses—I have been at his house, No. 22, Wellington-street, many times—I have seen sacks there several times—the first I saw was about four months ago—they were in the back parlour, and were marked, "Truman and Hanbury, "and about six weeks ago I saw some sacks in the loft at the back of the house in Wellington-street—they were marked "Truman and Hanbury"—the prisoner offered to sell me them for 1 s . 6 d . a piece—I would not buy them—he afterwards offered me 6 d . a piece to take the names out of some sacks that were in the yard—I have seen him at a house in Playhouse-yard—I never saw him at work there—I have left him to work at the still there—I took his horse and cart home one day while he worked there—when I saw the sacks, about four months ago, I said I thought it very imprudent to have such sacks lying on the floor, as he was liable to be detected by the police or the excise—he said he bought them for 1 s . a piece—I said there was a society for prosecuting persons having sacks, and he would be liable to be called up to say who he bought them of.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you ever the misfortune of being in trouble before you were convicted of the misdemeanor? A.  Yes, by the same party—I was innocent of that, and acquitted—I was innocent of the second charge in a measure, but I pleaded guilty—it was about getting some pigs and giving a false check—MR. Hill's partner died suddenly, and he owed me a good deal of money. WILLIAM JOHNSON, Jun. I am the witness's son; I am a blacking-maker, and live at No. 7, Wellington-street, Kingsland-road. I know the prisoner by the names of Pool and Ford—I have worked for him to ferment molasses for the still in his work in Playhouse-yard—I continued to work for him till Saturday, the 4th of July—on the Monday afterwards I went with the prisoner with a horse and cart to Playhouse-yard, but I did not go into the premises—while I was at work at the prisoner's I saw two sacks with the name of "Tru man and Hanbury"on them, brought from the cart into the premises, with yeast in them—I was with my father at the prisoner's residence, No. 22, Wellington-street—I saw the prisoner there, with some sacks in his hand with the name of "Truman and Hanbury"on them—he said to my father, "I will give you 6 d . a sack if you can take the names out of these"—that wai about six weeks or two months since.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was it not three months before this charge was made? A.  No, I believe not—it might be—I was not present when my father gave him a warning about the sacks—I did not hear the prisoner say he bought them—I cannot swear whether there was any one else present—I know my father and two more men were charged with felony by the prisoner—I wonder 1 was not charged myself, to keep us out of the way—it was respecting some spirits of wine—the two men'were charged before this indictment, and my father after—I do not know that two policemen were looking for my father before.
