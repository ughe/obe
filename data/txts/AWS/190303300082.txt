Evidence for the Defence
CATHERINE CHAMP. live at 35, Horseferry Road, Westminster,
and am the wife of Henry Champ-he is now dying in hospital-the
prisoner is my son-I knew his wife in January, 1899-I met a woman
named Hannaford, who told me that my son's wife was dead-I asked
who had told her, and she said the woman's son, Antony also
met a man named Ted Sefton, and he also told me that Antony had
told him that his mother was dead-James Madden told me the same-
I wrote to the prisoner and told him his wife was dead--he came back
to London in Mareh, and I went with him to Ayre Street, Saffron Hill,
to try and find out if his wife was really dead--we could not find any
evidence of it, they only told us that they did not know anything about
it-I then went with the prisoner to St. Martin's Street, Leicester Square,
where Sefton and Madden lived but they had moved on account of the
buildings coming down, and we could not find them-I did not see them
after my son came home.
Cross-ezamined. Before my son came home I did not take any steps
to find out if his wife was dead.
GUILTy. Three months' in the Second Division.
