   COURT . Q.  How often did the prisoner strike him after he struck his eye? A.  Four times—then Mrs. Bonner came into the room, got between them, stopped them, and got a black eye herself—I cannot say who gave her that—the deceased did not strike Bonner in the eye before Bonner struck him with the quart pot—I do not recollect saying before the Coroner that the deceased struck Bonner before he hit him with the quart pot.
   Q.  You named to the Coroner about being in the back part of the room? A.  Yes, they asked me if I saw the deceased strike Bonner in the eye, and I said I did not see the blow given, but he put his arm out—I am not quite sure who struck first.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  There were a good many people in the room, I sup pose? A.  Yes, quite full—the deceased sent for the doctor—it was my master's doctor who came—I have seen him in the house before—after the deceased's head was dressed, I saw him in my master's house that night about nine or ten o'clock—I think about three quarters of an hour after his head was dressed—Mr. Page and Mrs. Bonner were there among others—three half-crowns were offered the deceased by Sherlock, the prisoner's son-in-law—he refused it at first, but finally took it—Mrs. Bonner was present at the time—she consented to his having the money—I do not know whether the de ceased got any more money from any of the family afterwards—Harwood went for the doctor.
   COURT . Q.  The prisoner gave him brandy and water when he came back from the hospital? A.  Mrs. Bonner did before he went to the hos pital—he asked for brandy and water, saying he was faint—Mrs. Bonner offered him any thing he could wish for.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was Page spending the evening at the house that night? A.
