   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON . Q.  Were you on duty? A.  Yes, in plain clothes—I did not know Berry—I had been following them nearly three hours—when M'Carthy went down Hampton-street I was opposite the corner of the street—he went ten or twelve paces up the street—I was sometimes fifty yards from them, but when there was anything material to be seen, I got as close to them as I could. JAMES BROOK( police-constable L  118). I was with Goff on the 29th of Sept.—I know Mrs. Bicknell's shop—I saw Berry go into that shop, and M'Carthy was on the opposite side of the way—I was afterwards in the neighbourhood of Mrs. Cross's shop—I secured M'Carthy nearly opposite to it—I found these two counterfeit shillings in his hand, they are bitten and cut—he made a resistance. LOUISA GODFREY. I am the wife of George Godfrey, a tobacconist, in High-street, Camberwell. On the 29th of Sept, Berry came to the shop for a pennyworth of tobacco—he put down a counterfeit shilling—I took it up, bit it, and said it was not good, and he went away without the tobacco—he said he took the shilling on the Green—this is it—I bit it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Which is the bite? A.  Here on the edge of it—I did not make this bend on it. MARY BICKNELL. I keep a tobacconist's shop in Regent-place, Wal worth-road. Berry came there on the 29th of Sept for a pennyworth of tobacco—he offered me a shilling—I took it in my hand and cut two pieces out of it—this is it—I did not take it—he said I ought not to have done that, as he had just taken it down the road—he left the shop with it.
