U. up to the Judge; he looked at the signature, and re quested me again to hand it to the prisoner; and again the Judge asked "Is that your signature?" and he said "No"—the Judge asked him whether he could write, he said "Yes"—the Judge requested me to give him a pen and ink; I did so, and said "Now write your name;" that paper has been mislaid, and I cannot find it; but he wrote "William" or "Wiltam;" how-ever, the Christian name was spelt wrong—I handed it up to the Judge, who said, "Can you spell your name?"—he said, "Yes;" and spelt "William" correctly—the Judge then cautioned him, and asked him again whether it was his signature—he said, "No"—on that the prosecutor and his brother were examined again, and they swore positively that they saw him sign it and an order was made that he should be committed.
   Cross-examined by MR. BEST . Q.  Is it part of your duty to examine the cases instead of the Judge? A.  Not instead of the Judge; but I do put the question!, by his permission, to facilitate business—I have not got the original copy; the prisoner has that—he appeared as sober as he does now, when he was examined; but in the after part of the day he was excited when I insisted on his putting certain papers in his pocket—the Judge ad dressed him before his committal to prison—he kept perpetually denying his signature—he said "I owe the money," and commenced pulling a quan tity of papers out of his pocket—I said "Do not excite yourself; calmly look at that piece of paper, and tell the Judge whether the signature, 'W. Hogan,' is your writing;" and he said "No; it is not;" he stuck to the "No" all through—a man that was not sober might think he could deceive by signing his name wrong; I think so now.
   MR. ROBINSON . Q.  What was the interval before the first committal? A.  When the Judge ordered him to be committed, it was half-past 1, and I think, before he was sent to prison, it was about 3 in the afternoon. JOHN JONES. I am Usher at the Country-court—I administered the oath to the prisoner—I saw him during the whole time he was in Court; I saw nothing like intoxication about him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you never cases of drunken people at your Court? A.  Yes; and we put them on one side till they are sober—drunken persons are not always excited. ROBERT PARKE.
