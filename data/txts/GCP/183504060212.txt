WILLIAM MITCHELL. I am a policeman. Last Saturday week the pro-
secutor fetched me to his house-Bradbury was called from the parlour and
Tyler from the bakehouse--the prosecutor said, “ George, I am going to
give you into custody about the salt" -Tyler said, “Very well, you may
do as you please; it would not have happened had you behaved to me
you ought to have done"--the prosecutor said, “In what way have I be-
haved to you ?"-Tyler said, “I shall not state that here"--the prosecutor
said, "You ought to know better"-he said, “Why, the other boy used to
do it-I told Bradbury to get the salt, and I certainly had some of
the beer and tobacco which was bought with the money"--on the way to
the station-house, I said, “Tyler, you are the father of children, you
ought to have known better than to persuade a boy to do this kind of act"
--he said, “I should not have done it if Mr. Chester had behaved to me
as he ought to have done."
Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean to swear he said he had part of the
money? A. Part of the beer and tobacco which the money produced.
JOSEPH CANT (police-constable L 174.) I was on duty on Monday,
the 4th, in St. George's circus, and was called in with my brother officer
Mr. Chester told me he suspected he had been robbed I heard Tyler say
he knew the prosecutor had been robbed before, by a servant who
previously lived with him-Chester said, “Why should you allow such a
thing? I always pay you well"-he replied, that Mr. Chester had used him
ill lately, for some reasons, but he should not tell him what, then.
Tyler's Defence. I did drink out of the beer that the boy fetched, and
I knew he did it-after he did it, he told me he had done it-I gave him
money to buy the tobacco myself.
(William Slacker, baker, Charlton-crescent, Islington; William Whit-
tock, Hornsey-row, Islington; and Joseph Andrews, painter and glazier,
Waterloo-road, gave the prisoner a good character.)
