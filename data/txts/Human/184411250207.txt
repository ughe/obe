I am a leather-dresser. I was a passenger on board the Waterman  steamer on this day. I was on the cabin stairs at the time the accident happened—I did not see the blow struck, but I heard and felt it—before I felt the blow the engines were positively stopped—there were several passengers on deck—it was rather a wet morning, and many were down in the cabin. THOMAS LILLY. I am a waterman. I was a passenger on board the Waterman  steamer on the 25th of Oct.—I saw the schooner aground by the Lower Tunnel-pier—I was standing on the starboard side of the pad dle-box, just before it—I saw the skiff, I should suppose, about half a minute before we rounded the schooner's stern—as soon as I saw the skiff, I heard the orders, "Ease her," "Stop her," and "Turn her astern," given by the master—those orders were given in succession; I suppose, in half a second, as soon as he could repeat them—the skiff was in the eddy when I saw her—the eddy had a great effect on her—nobody has the power of pulling in a boat when it gets into an eddy—she was quite un manageable—she was turned round towards the steamer, not exactly round—in my judgment, the eddy, and nothing else, caused the collision—I can give no other account—had no schooner been there, and nothing to cause the eddy, the boat would have gone into the stairs before the steamer found her.
   COURT . Q.  Were you very much astonished when you saw the eddy? A.  No, not at all, because I was certain that the schooner lying aground would cause an eddy—I knew there must be an eddy before I saw it. RALPH SCOTT. I am a waterman's apprentice. On the morning of the 25th of Oct. I was on the Upper Tunnel-pier, and saw the accident—I did not see anything done by the steamer in order to avoid it—the accident would not have occurred if the schooner had not been where she was—she created an eddy—in my judgment the skiff could not have been seen by the steamer before she rounded the schooner.
   COURT . Q.  The captain of the steamer would see the schooner there, would he not? A.  Yes—the eddy drew the boat's head down, and threw it out into the tide again—if the captain had seen the boat a hundred yards before he came to her, and had stopped the engine, or turned the helm starboard, that would have prevented the accident. SAMUEL BRISTOW. I am mate on board the Waterman steamer, No. 6. I was on board on the 25th of Oct.—I saw the skiff when I was abreast of the schooner's stern—the first thing I did was to hail the skiff.
   COURT . Q.  Whereabouts were you? A.  I was on the fore starboard deck—the prisoner was on the paddle-box—it was his business to look out—he was the person who was directing the motions of the vessel.
