529. CHARLES JOHN WILLARD (61), Feloniously marrying
Elizabeth Sharpe, his wife being alive.
MR. HODGSON Prosecuted, and Mr. GEOGHEGAN Defended.
EDWARD BURLON (Detective Sergeant, K). On July 8th, at 10.30, I
saw the prisoner in the Bow Road-he said, “ Are you a police officer 1"
-I said, "Yes" -he said, "I want to give myself up for bigamy"-I
asked him the circumstances-he said, "I married my first wife at a
church in Essex Road in 1882. I left her in 1884. I married my second
wife at a registry office in Liverpool Road, Islington, in 1892. I thought
at the time my first wife was dead, as I had not seen her since 1884; but
now I know she is not dead, when we get to the station I will give you
all the information"-at the station he said that his wife lived at 107,
Old Street, St. Luke's, and his second wife at 14, Stockmar Road, and
he believed the witnesses to the first marriage were both dead-his wife
came to the station, and brought the certificates-he said they were quite
correct.
Cross-examined. This is not a police prosecution-the solicitor, 1
believe, represents the Married Women's Protection and Legal Aid
Society-when she brought the certificates she said that she never would
have taken proceedings if he had paid her maintenance-her age is fifty-
seven, and he is sixty-one.
WILLIAM MUMFORD. I am general assistant relieving officer of the Hol-
born Union-in 1886 the prisoner's wife was chargeable to the Union, and
an inmate of the workhouse—I searched, and found him-he was taken
before a Magistrate, and acknowledged her as his wife; he was charged
with neglecting her, and it was adjourned for fourteen days—he took her
out on March 24th-she has been back to the Union since.
ELIZABETH SHARPE. I went through the form of marriage with the
prisoner on December 26th, 1892, at a registry office in Islington--I had
known him over three years; he described himself as having a wife, but
said that he had not seen her for many years-he said that about a month
before we married.
Cross-examined. I am not a party to these oriminal proceedings; he
has been a very good and affectionate husband to me-- he said that he
had not heard of her for some time, and did not believe she was alive-I
never had a cross word with him I do not subscribe to this society,
