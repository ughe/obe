Sixth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq .
 2488. MARY ANN MILLS was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of August , 1 painting and frame, value 3 l  ., the goods of Sigismund Gans . SIGISMUND GANS. I am a tobacconist, and live in Co vent-garden—the prisoner was my servant. On the 11th of August I missed a picture after eight o'clock at night—this now produced is it. RICHARD WHITEHEAD( police-constable F  53.) I took the prisoner last Saturday—I told Her it was for stealing a painting from her last master, Mr. Gans, in Bow-street—she said, "I don't know any thing about it"—I found the painting, pawned at Mr. Sean's. RICHARD SAVER. I am in the service of James Sears, a pawnbroker, in Drury-lane. Between eight and nine o'clock at night, on the 11th of Au gust, I took this picture in pledge of the prisoner—I am sure it was her.
   Prisoner's Defence . I never saw the picture; I went to see Mrs. Gans, to get a character, the day before he lost the picture. GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Three Months.
 2489. MARY OAKES and CATHERINE DANIELS were indicted for stealing, on the   26th of July , 1 gown, value 2 s  .; the goods of William Farrell . JULIA FARRELL. I am the wife of William Farrell. On the 26th of July I was sitting at my stall, in High-street, Shad well—the two prisoners came and spoke to me—Daniels asked me for three pence—I told her I had not three pence to lend her—she said she would soon go home and raise sixpence—she said to Oakes, "Come on, Mrs., Oakes, I will soon raise a tanner " which means sixpence—they both went away together—they were not with me above four or five minutes—I went home, and missed my gown, which was hanging in "my room a quarter of an hour before—this now produced is it—Daniels lives in the same house, and Oakes next door.
   Daniels. Q.  Was I ever inside your door more than once in my life? A.  I cannot say—the house is let out in tenements. MARTHA ANN BROWN. I live in the same room as Farrell—Daniels lived in the house. On the day this gown was lost I saw the prisoners come into the passage together—they went out together—Daniels then had something in her apron—shortly after Mrs. Farrell came home, and made a complaint—when the prisoners came into the house, they told Mrs. Farrell's boy to mind her child—the boy and child went into the yard—I did not see either of them go to Mrs. Farrell's apartments. THOMAS WALKER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Waterloo-terrace, Commercial-road. This gown was pawned on the 26th of July, by Oakes, in the name of Smith—I am sure of her—I did not know her before—it was applied for on the following morning—Daniels was not with her. JAMES MALIN( police-constable K  99.) I took the prisoners—I asked what they knew about the gown—they said, "We know nothing about it, we have been helping a friend to. move"—Daniels had a fender in her hand, and was very obstinate. NOT GUILTY.
