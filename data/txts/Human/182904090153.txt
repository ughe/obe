 Cross-exmined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was it before the money was counted that the sister said she did not think her brother carried two faces under one hat? A. Yes; I was standing at the yard door - lvy then came down with a light, as he had some suspicion of somebody overhearing them; Hanks went into the passage to avoid being seen, and I went into the water-closet; and said in a good loud voice, "Who is there?" I said, "Me;" he said who was me - and I did not answer him; Hanks was somewhere about the passage - I do not know whether he was near enough to hear Ivy call; Ivy looked about, and opened the yard door, but could not see Hanks - he looked about the yard and the dust-hole, and then went up stairs; I believe Hanks did not come back into the yard.
 COURT. Q. You heard nothing but conversation? A. No; I did not hear the money counted out, nor any thing about that, because I was further off than Hanks was - I was by the yard door,holding it open in case any body should come with a light.
 Q. What do you mean by saying you did not hear the money counted out? A. Because Hanks heard it - he said so to me.
 Q. You told the Magistrate they counted money - how came you to state that, if you did not hear it? A. Because old Ballard said it should not see sun nor moon; and so I thought that he did have money.
 Q. Was that your reason for supposing he did have money, and for saying they counted it? A. Yes, I have got a slight memory - I did not recollect it.
   JOSEPH MARRIAGE. I live with James Brown, a chimney-sweeper, in Feathers-court, Fox-court, between Brooke-street and Gray's Inn-lane. I found an iron-chest on some old ruins, about six o'clock on Wednesday morning, the 18th of March - it was where a house had been pulled down; I dragged it into my master's yard - when my master came he asked where I got it - I told him, and he gave information.
 COURT. Q. Was it left in your master's yard? A. Yes, and Mr. Carr came to look at it about an hour and a half or two hours afterwards - it is here.
   JOHN LEWIS. I am a bell-hanger and locksmith, and live at No.13, Tothill - street, Laystall-street. I remember being at the Blackamoor's liead public-house nearly all day on the 17th of March - I remember seeing Hawkins and John Ballard there in the afternoon; I knew them perfectly-these are the persons; when I went in, Ballard was playing at bagatelle - there was young man there whom I knew, named Williams; Ballard lost two pots of ale - he paid the landlord for three pints, and said he would pay the other next day; the landlord said he ought not to play without he had money - he then went away; Hawkins then played for half a pound of beaf steaks, and lost - he went out to fetch them, but did not return; I went home to tea about five o'clock, and re turned about seven - Hawkins came in and sat down by the side of Hawkins - they spoke to each other, but I did not hear what they said; Ballard rose up, pulled a chisel out of his breast, and showed it to Hawkins - Hawkins turned round side ways, and said, "It is just the thing;" my attention was drawn to it; I thought there was something going on wrong, and I observed it particularly, so that if I saw it again I should know it - I have seen it since in the possession of the beadle; I did not take hold of it till I was at Hatton-garden - I was sure it was the same that I had seen: they said about an hour longer at the public-house, and then went away together - this was about a quarter past eight, I believe, but the clock did not go right; they returned about a quarter past ten, and called out a young man named Riley, who had been asleep there for four or five hours they touched him, and he followed them out; they paid something - I remained there till a quarter before twelve; I am quite sure this is the chisel- it is made out of an old file.
 Cross-examined by MR.PHILLIPS. Q. This chisel they were showing openly? A. No.; I had an opportunity of seeing it for about a couple of seconds - I knew it instantly when I saw it the next day; there were some of Ballard's relations in the public-house, playing at bagatelle, and one young man that beat him out of the steaks- I have been here before for my own property; I was tried for a game cock, as they called,it, but I was honourably acquitted - I was not up above a minute and a half, and you were my counsel; there were one or two witnesses examined, but it did not last above a minute and a half - I never heard of any body saying they could swear a spot off a tea-kettle; I never said that unless I got a pound, I would swear a spout off a tea-kettle - I never said, in the presence of Badland and another person, that I knew nothing about this robbery; they offered me 10l., but I did not see why I should not be in it as well as other people; they tried to buy me to keep out of the way - I was never accused of any thing beside the game cock, unless it was fighting now and then; there never was a young woman who lost any clothes, and brought a charge against me - it was in October Session I was tried about the game cuck; I know Badland perfectly well - he is the landlord of that public-house, and I know  Richard Badland- it is William Badland who keeps the public-house; I do not know John Griffths by name-I never told any body I made this chisel myself, but I told where a piece of a silk roller came from that was to it, and Crowder, Mr. Ballard's brother, had some to sell.
