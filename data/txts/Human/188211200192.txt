When I called on Everett after he opened I learnt he had advanced 100 l . to Johnson—I spoke to Johnson about the transfer of the licence to Everett—I said he must have the licence transferred to some one, and "as Everett has told me he advanced 100 l . I have made inquiries; he is a respectable man, and the proper man to have it trans-ferred to "—Johnson said "I do not think he is fit for it, I shall go there myself; I shall have it in my own name"—he made an application to me for it—I said if he brought Everett up I would transfer it to Everett, but it was not to go out of our possession till it was transferred to Everett.
   CHARLESYINEY ( Detective Sergeant ). On 30th September I received a warrant for the prisoner's apprehension, and apprehended him on that day in Leadenhall Street—I read it to him and charged him with obtain-ing from Everett 100 l ., and 40 l . from Evans—he said "I did not think they could do that"—I said "They have done it"—he said "Everett has more than enough in the house to pay him, and Evans has had 151. out of the house and another 5 l . "—he said afterwards "Everybody would have had their money, but Worthington's people owe me 2,000 l .; they would all have been paid "—I searched him at the station and found 251. in gold, 5a. in silver, and a watch and chain.
   A. J. MORRIS ( Re-examined ). No. 13, Winchester Terrace, belonged to Mr.  Bailey, and the plot at the other corner belonged to him too—Mr. Johnson has no interest in that—there is a footpath across the plot.
   Cross-examined.  I am Mr. Bailey's agent—'Mr. Bailey told me he had assigned the plot to Worthington's—I can swear he had told Worthing-ton the plot cannot be dealt with, I heard it repeatedly—I can't remember when, some time ago—since then I cannot tell what representations he has made.
   The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY,— Ten Months' Hard labour.
