CHARLES WINDSCHUROL, Leadenhall Buildings. I received a letter
on March 4 last headed, " Freeman's Vinegar Co., London and Bor-
deaux, manufacturers and importers. Offices and showrooms Queen's
Road, London," and asking us to quote for acetic acid. We replied.
quoting for same, and then received a visit from Reuter, who gave me
card. He wanted to buy half a ton of acetic acid. I agreed to
supply on references or cash. He said he would not be prepared to
PAV cash, as his principals were not in town, and they only met once
fortnight, and would only pay every other Saturday, when there is
meeting, and the cheques would have to be signed by both; but he
gave me two references, which I applied for, and they were good.
The goods were supplied, £17 13s, 6d. We were not paid. I gave
turteen days' credit on Reuter's representations. Mr. Laidlaw called
for payment, and we wrote twice-March 25 and April 8.
Cross-examined. It was the first time I have heard that these
people were and are prepared to pay my bill.
To Reuter. You did not give me to understand that you were one
of the principals.

ALBERT MILLS, 68, Wyndham Road, Camberwell. On May 3, pri
soner Reuter called on me, saying he represented Freeman's
Vinegar Company, giving me the card produced. He wanted some
vinegar bottles. I told him I had none by me, but agreed to get
kme on terms he mentioned, cash on delivery, and send them to 373,
Queen's Road. William Collins, the carman, took them, with instruc-
tions that it was to be cash on delivery. He returned with a sealed
envelope, which contained a note, "Your carman arrived too late
for Mr. Freeman, so am returning crates and will bring you cheque
£9 8s. to-morrow." The next day I sent my man, and went myself to,
Queen's Road, where I saw Hewan Freeman, who said he was the
yard foreman. He afterwards admitted he was Mr. Freeman, but
said he could do nothing in the matter till he saw Mr. Reuter. I
sked if the latter could not be found, and that he should tell me
where he lived. He said, No, he should not think of doing that;
it would be unbusinesslike, or something like that. He said Reuter
would be there on Monday morning. I said I would make an ap-
pointment, and he said, "All right, call on Monday morning at 10
o clock." I went on Monday morning and saw Mr. Freeman again,
waiting about an hour and half, but no Mr. Reuter came. I asked
Freeman if he intended paying me. He said he would do nothing at all
till he saw Reuter. I have not seen Reuter since, and, being unable
