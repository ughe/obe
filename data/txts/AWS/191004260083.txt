PERCY BERNARD LEO, police-constable, L.B. & S.C. Railway. In
Reynolds's newspaper of October 10 last year I saw this advertise-
ment E1 weekly. Addressing envelopes (work at home). Particu-
lars, addressed envelope, Supply Company, Mortimer Street, London.'
I answered it and on October 16 I received this letter (Exhibit 20),
similar to Exhibit 11, and attached to it was a form of agreement (Ex-
hibit 21) similar to that attached to Exhibit 11. I filled it and sent it
on October 18 to the Stylo Supply Company with a P.O. for 3s. 6d. and
a penny stamp. I did not want the pen; I only sent the 3s. 6d. to
obtain the work. I thought they were going to supply the envelopes.
I received the pen pn October 26. I had been on October 22 to the
company's offices, but could not get any reply. There was a plate
on the door saying that they were only to be seen between 8.30 and
9.30 a.m. I went there at 3 p.m. I communicated with the police
on the same day. I sold the pen for a shilling.
Cross-examined. I had no communication with the police before
I answered the advertisement. This memorandum as to the dates was
made at the time. I never tried to write with the pen. I got this
printed notice similar to Exhibit 16, saying the money would be re-
fanded if I did not entertain the employment with the first letter. I
had nowhere to send the pen back to as the office was empty. If I
thought they would have been there in the morning I should have taken
it myself. I did not think it worth while to withdraw my complaint
after I received the pen. I got the list of names and addresses with
the pen.
