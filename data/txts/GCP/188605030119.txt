578. THOMAS GREGORY (23), Maliciously breaking and destroying
two panes of glass, the property of William Whate.
MR. Wilis Proscouted.
NEAL Ross (Polioman N 858). On the night of 9th April, about
nine, I was in Paradise Street, Rotherhithe, and heard a loud smash of
glass, in consequence of which I went to the Barley Mow publio-house,
where I saw the prisoner and a crowd of people--Mr. Watts, the manager,
gave him into oustody-I asked the prisoner who broke the window-ho
said "I did, sir, with two stones"-I asked why he did it-he said. "I
meant to "-on the way to the station he said to Mr. Watts “I told you
I would pay you some time."
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I did not see you smash the glass.
WALTER Warrs. I am manager to Mr. Whate, of the Barley Mow,
Paradise Street-at nine o'clook on this night I saw the window breaking,
I went out immediately and saw the prisoner standing there-he said "I
have broken the window, don't hold me "-I asked him why he did it
he said "I wish to go away for a long while "-the constable came up
and I gave him into custody-as he was going along he said "I told you
I would pay you some day-I have seen the prisoner about the streets,
that is a)-two separate windows were broken of the value of 91.
The prisoner in his defence stated that no one had seen him break the
windows and he denied that he did it.
Guilty.—Pour Months' Hard Labour.

570. MARY PORTER (22), Unlawfully, wilfully, and maliciously
breaking three panes of glass of the value of 431.
MR. ERNEST BEARD Prossouted.
WILLIAM SAUNDERS. I am a shoeblack of 66, Church Street, Deptford
-on April 8th, about seven o'clook, I was in the Broadway and say the
prisoner chuck something against one of Mr. Bartlett's windows and
break it; there were two windows broken before that she went away
directly-she was drunk--I followed her up the Broadway and saw her
in charge of the police -I am sure she is the woman that did it.
FREDERICK QUINNEY (Policeman 402 R). I received information of
this damage and took the prisoner-she was very drunk-I told her the
charge, and she mumbled out something which I could not understand
this is the largest of the stones I found near the window-the glass
broken was a quarter of an inch thick, I should say, and the windows
were three yards long and one and a half wide, and four yards by four
and a half yards—the value of the glass broken was 401.-I should say
it was worth more than 51. GUILTY.—70 Months' Hard Labour.
