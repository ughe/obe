THOMAS BUEY (police-constable S 252.) I was called about half-past
six o'clock, by Mrs. Chapman, who told me something- went up and
found her husband in the loft, and this lead on the roof, doubled up-it
had been taken from part of the gutter, about eighteen inches of lead was
carried away--there was a good deal more partly taken off, to be carried
away-this quantity was quite offI took it down-I found the prisoner
in the neigbbourhood, and took him about half-past nine the same evening
-I asked what he did on Mr. Chaplin's stables-he said he did not
know that he had any business there-I cautioned him not to say any-
thing, and he said no more-the lead was fresh torn off.
Prisoner's Defence. I went there after Dover on the 26th, to get mea-
sared for a foot-iron; he was not in the shop I proceeded to the stable-
yard and saw the door open; I could not see him in the stable, and seeing
the loft-door and sky-light open, I went up to see if he was there; as I
came down I saw Mrs. Chapman she asked what I wanted I said I
wanted Mr. Dover; she said I had no business there, and that evening I
WAS taken I was taken before a Magistrate, and they said they could
do nothing with me; I was taken again on Monday.
JOHN CHAPMAN re-eramined. We have lost lead before-the prisoner
could have no business on the roof-Dover is a blacksmith, living at a
cottage just by there-he does not live at the stable.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 8.-Confined Two Months.
