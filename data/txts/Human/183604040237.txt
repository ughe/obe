MR. RAWLINS Conducted the prosecution . JACOB BOOTH. I am a silversmith and pawnbroker at Woolwich. On the 18th of March, the prisoner called on me for the purpose of bying a keeper ring—I went to get the key of the drawer, and on my return missed her from the shop, and a shawl—I looked out, and saw her in the street—I ran after her, and she ran—I caught her in the next street, and she had the shawl in her possession then—I had not sold it.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Did you know her before? A.  Yes—I know her very well—her husband is employed in the Dock-yard—I never heard any thing against him or her before—she said she had lost a shawl, and that this shawl was her property—she fancied this was one she had lost—I did not know where to find her at the time—I believe the 17th of March is St. Patrick's-day—the prisoner is an Irish woman—this was the afternoon after St. Patrick's-day.
   COURT . Q.  She was sober enough to run away? A.  Yes the shawl is the property of myself and Robert Jacob Booth. GUILTY. Aged 34.— Confined Six Weeks.
 1098. THOMAS SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the   31st of March , 1 handkerchief, value 3 s  ., the goods of Thomas Leonard . WILLIAM SPENCER. I am shopman to Mr. Thomas Leonard, a pawn broker at Greenwich. On the 31st of March we had a handkerchief about a foot and a half inside the door—I heard a noise about one o'clock—I turned and saw the prisoner running with this handkerchief in his hand—I ran after him, and brought him back to the shop—I saw the handker chief inside his coat—he put it between his legs—I took it from him—he wanted to leg pardon and be forgiven—he said he did not thieve  it, but picked it up—if it had fallen down, it would have fallen in the shop.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you see me take it? Witness , No. I heard it, and you were close to the door when I saw you with it in your hand—your foot was on the step.
   Prisoner's Defence . It was lying blowing about—it was a windy day—I did not know who it belonged to. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Three Months.
 1099. JAMES JOHNSTON and HENRY SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the   5th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 6 d  ., the goods of David M'Donald , from his person—2nd COUNT  , stealing it to belong to a man unknown. JAMES ANDREWS. I am a policeman. I was at Greenwich fair on Tues day the 15th of April—I saw the prisoner in company together for three-quarters of an hour—I saw Johnson attempt four different person pockets—Smith was standing near him, apparently to hide him—Johnson took handkerchief from prosecutor's pocket—I touched the prosecutor on the shoulder, and immediately collared the two prisoners, and Johnston then threw the handkerchief down—I picked it up, and the gentleman came to the station-house—they were close together when they attempted the pockets. DAVID M'DONALD. I am a tailor. I was at the fair—a person touched me on the shoulder, and said I had lost my handkerchief—I immediately for, and missed it—he picked it off the ground, and produced it—the two prisoners were next to me at the time.
