   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Repeat what passed between you and the prisoner when you were in the shop with M'Knight? A.  M'Lean said he must have these goods—the prisoner said, "You mean the goods I bought of this young man?"—I said, "Yes, what have you done with them?"—he said, "I have sold them, I am not obliged to keep them"—I said, "To whom did you sell them?"—he said, "To a stranger, if you will allow me to go perhaps I can find him"—we said, "Perhaps you can give his address?"—he said "No, it is somewhere in the City."
   COURT . Q.  Then the word "goods" was mentioned throughout in the conversation? A.  Lengths they were called—I called them twelve lengths—the prisoner said "remnants."
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you know whether pieces of velvet are cut up for stocks? A.  Very few—I have never seen them.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Were the words, "twelve lengths," used by any body in the prisoner's presence? A.  By me—when he said they were remnants, I said they were twelve lengths for waistcoats—he said they would not make boys' waistcoats. FRANCIS M'LEAN. I accompanied Evans and M'Knight to the prisoner's shop at Newington—Evans and M'Knight went in first—they afterwards called me in—I asked the prisoner where the twelve cuts were—Evans and him were talking—he said he had sold them to a stranger for 2 s . profit—he also said he was a poor young man, and that was his shop—I searched the shop, but found nothing—I said he must go with me—he said he thought if I would let him go he could find out the person he had sold them to—he began crying two or three times, and said, "Will you go down with me to the other shop?"—I accompanied him there—it was about a hundred yards—he was crying almost all the way—when I got there I found it was Mr. Davis's shop, who it appeared was his employer—I asked Davis if he knew any thing about the sale of them—he said he did not know any thing about them—some conversation passed, but it was just at the Sabbath, and they begged me to let him go back to shut up the shop, which I did—I made no search at Davis's—I took the prisoner to the station, and there he said he had not bought more than six cuts—he acknowledged to the twelve at the shop—he said he gave 9 s . for them. JAMES DICKONS SALTMER. The value of the twelve lengths of goods pointed out by M'Knight would be 5 l ., if cut into twelve waistcoat lengths—I should certainly say such articles are not used for velvet neckcloths—I have seen velvet neckcloths, but never any of that sort.
