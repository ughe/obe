was brought back. The money was tied up in a piece of wash-leather, and I
am sure it was in my left-hand trowsers pocket when I got into the cab. I
must have fallen into the stupor the moment I got into the cab. The last
house I had been in, was halfway between Deptford and London Bridge, and
I bad walked from there to where I took the cab. I was quite sensible when
I got into the cab. I recollect calling the cab, and, to the best of my know-
ledge, about five 'clock. It was not dark. I have no recollection of going
to a public-house, or getting out of the cab. The first thing I recollect is
when I got out after Murphy. I do not remember going down a court, and the
cabman coming after me. I do not remember being knocked down by Mur-
phy after the constable took him. As far as I can recolleet, Murphy was
standing on the road near the cab, and got in as a friend of the cabman's. I
