RICHARD VASKY re-examined.  My mother gave her evidence at the police-court, and it was taken down in a book—her name was Susannah Vasey. LLEWELLYN GUNN. I am clerk to the Magistrates at Lambeth. I recol lect Susannah Vasey giving her evidence on the 20th of Jan., in the prisoner's pretence—I took down her statement in this book—she was sworn—( read )—"Susan Vasey, sworn, says, 'I am a widow, and live at No. 3, Halford-terrace, Penton-place, Newington. The prisoner is married to my daughter, Johanna; I have lived with them nearly a month; he has been married three years; he has very much ill-used her; the first thing yesterday morning I heard the prisoner call "Murder!" that was a little after eight o'clock yester day morning; I ran down stairs, and met the prisoner at the foot of the stairs, without his shirt;. his wife was lying down on the floor, with her throat cut, and bleeding; I said to my daughter, "Who did it?" I and she pointed to the prisoner, and said, "You did it;" I slapped him on the shoulder as he passed, and said, "Oh, Fred , put your shirt on! look at your hands!" my daughter fainted away; he made no observation, but ran into the back parlour; he was sober; he came home about half-past twelve the night before; they slept together in the back parlour; it was in the front parlour she was on the floor; she had only her night-clothes on; I picked up a razor that lay on the left side of my daughter, on the floor; I called, "Police!" and gave the razor to, the policeman; the prisoner ran into the back parlour, and fastened himself in; I think the prisoner heard his wife say he had done it, and he made no observation, but ran out of the room; I called to my son; the key of the front door was lost, and I was obliged to open the window to key people in; I did not hear the prisoner say a single word; they had been quarrelling last Thursday night, and I heard her say, "I will not take a false oath for any. one," and I heard him say he would do  for her; her face was dreadfully bruised some time ago; he has been in the habit of ill-using my daughter.
   Cross-examined.  I and my son lodged there, at 3 s . a week; I am quite sure that the remark, "I'll do for you," I was not made by my son; I have not heard my daughter say anything about who did it since yesterday morning; I remained ten minutes with my son in the front parlour; when I first entered the room my daughter was on the floor; I did not observe her attempt to pull the wound open; her hands were lying down by her sides; her eyes were rolling when I first saw her on the ground, and she appeared quite powerless; she did not make use of any words but "He did it;" she raised up her handy, and said, in a low voice, "He did it;" I do not know if my son has received any notice from the prisoner to quit; I know that the prisoner took out a summons against my son.'"
