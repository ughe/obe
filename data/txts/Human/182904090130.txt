   PATRICK KENNEY. I am a labouring man. On the 30th of March I lodged at No. 8, Lion and Lamb-court. About two o'clock in the morning the prosecutrix came into the house, and said I was the person who knocked her about outside; I had been in bed since between nine and ten o'clock, and did not get up till this affair; I am married, and my wife is in Ireland - she said, "That is the man."
 COURT. Q. Was she pointing to you or to any body else? A. She did, and the watchman said, "He is not Burke;" I do not know the watchman; (looking at the witnesses) I cannot swear to any of them - I cannot say whether they were there that night; I think three watchmen came in with her - I have known O'Donnell two years and Daley half a year; they are labourers, and bore good characters.
   ELIZABETH COOMBS. I do not recollect seeing this man; I swore to nobody but O'Donnell.
   JAMES McDONALD. I went into the room with Coombs, and saw Kenney; Coombs did not speak of him at all; he was in his shirt - she said nothing to him.
   WILLIAM FINCH. I went with Coombs; Kenney jumped out of bed in his shirt when we went into the room; Coombs made no charge against him in my hearing.
   ROBERT WOODS. I do not recollect seeing Kenney there.
   PATRICK SHERIDAN. I live at this house, and am the landlord. I was in bed with my wife and asleep; I was disturbed about half-past two o'clock, and the very moment Coombs came in she said, "This is one of the men - I will take my oath this is one of them:" I had been in bed since nine o'clock.
 COURT. Q. Did she address herself to you, that you were one? A. Yes; the moment the watchman turned his lantern over me, she said, "That is one of them;" there was no light in my house before the watchman came.
   JAMES McDONALD. Sheridan and his wife were in bed- Coombs did not charge him as being the man, or one of them; she did not speak to him down stairs, nor of him - the watchmen were looking about the house, but these people were in bed.
   WILLIAM FINCH. Coombs did not charge Sheridan as being one of the men in my presence; I saw him in bed - two of us were up stairs, and one down.
   ROBERT WOODS. I saw Sheridan that night: Coombs said nothing about him in my presence - the watchman were looking about in different places, some in one place, and some in another; Sheridan slept in the parlour, and Kenney up stairs.
   PATRICK SHERIDAN. All the watchmen had lights. I have known the prisoners four years; they are honest hardworking men.
   JAMES GHRIMES. I am a labourer, and lodge in this house. I was asleep up stairs on this night; the prosecutrix said nothing to me, but she gave charge of Sheridan and kenney, who slept in my bed - I have known the prisoners three years; they are honest men.
   ELIZABETH COOMBS. I never got my 1s. 6d.; it could not have fallen out of the handkerchief it was tied in.
 Six witnesses deposed of both the prisoners' good characters.

 965.  WILLIAM BAKER was indicted for that he, on the 21st of February , at St Andrew, Holborn, feloniously did falsely make, forge, and counterfeit a certain order for payment of money, which is as follows:
 St. Ann's Union.
 MR. THOMAS, - Sir, You will please to pay the bearer, for  Richard Power, three pounds, for three weeks due to him, a country member, and you will much oblige, yours, &c.
 2, Brown's-building, Stanhope-street, J. BESWICK, Mr. Thomas, Gray's Inn-lane. Feb. 21, 1829. with intention to defraud John Thomas, the said  John Thomas, on whom the said false, forged, and counterfeited order for payment of money was made, then and there, being entrusted with, and having in his hands and possession, as the landlord of a certain public-house known by the sign of the Crown and Barley Mow. in Gray's Inn-lane, a certain large sum of money, to wit, 30l., belonging to William Ireland and others, members of a friendly society, called the St. Ann's Union, meeting at the said public-house, for the purpose of enabling the said John Thomas to pay, and that he might pay all orders for payment of money made by John James Beswick, the Secretary of the said Society, upon the said John Thomas, for claims made, and monies due to any of the members of the said society, according to the rules and regulations thereof; against the Statute, &c.
 2d COUNT, that he, on the same day, at the same parish, feloniously did utter and publish as true, a certain false, forged, and counterfeited order for payment of money, well knowing the same to be false, forged, and counterfeited, which is as follows - (setting it out as before.) with intent to defraud the said  John Thomas; against the Statute.
 3d COUNT like the second, only stating the intent to be to defraud  John James Beswick.
 4th COUNT the same, only stating the intent to be to defraud  William Ireland and others.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN THOMAS. I am landlord of the Crown and Barley Mow, Gray's Inn-lane. The St. Ann's Union Benefit Society is held at my house, Mr. Beswick is the secretary; he leaves money in my hand to pay any orders he may send for money; on Saturday, the 21st of February, I remember seeing the prisoner in front of my bar in the evening, between six and seven o'clock (my wife, who has seen him before, is not here) - he produced a cheque, and said he had brought that from Mr. Beswick for 3l. for a country member who had broken his arm - I looked at the cheque, and saw that it was not Beswick's hand writing; I told him it was not Mr. Beswick's hand-writing - he said he knew that, Mr. Beswick was out of town, at a funeral, being an undertaker, and that Mrs. Beswick directed her son to write it; I accordingly gave him the 3l., and put the cheque on the file of the society.
