903. CHARLES GARRETT, for embezzlement,
Thomas FIELDER. I am a baker, at Lower-street, Islington, and Brewer-
street, Somers-town. The prisoner was in my service, to carry out bread
and receive money, which he should give to me or my shopwoman as soon as
he got home-he keeps a hook for his private customers--Mr. Larby owed me
21. I received no money from the prisoner on his account-on 2nd Feb, he
went out, and did not come back for nine or ten days-he then came to my
shop at Islington, and said he was very sorry for what he had done, and, if I
would agree to it, he would pay me when he got another situation-I said it
would not do, and called a policeman.
SARAH ELIZABETH CHATER. I am Mr. Fielder's shopwoman. The prisoner
never paid or accounted to me for Mr. Larby's money-he went out with
bread on 2nd Feb., at one o'clock, and I did not see him again till he was
before the Magistrate.
MARY LARBY, I am the wife of James Larby, of Draper’s-place, St. Pan-
cras. On 2nd Feb., about eleven o'clock, the prisoner came, and I paid him
21. for his master-I did not take a receipt, I gave him my book-he was to
bring some bread back.

904. HENRY ORME, stealing 2lbs. weight of gutta-percha, value 58.,
the goods of Edward Baldwin.
WILLIAM SMITH. I am in the employ of Edward Baldwin. On 15th
Feb., about nine o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came into the shop, and
asked to look at some gutta-percha soles I showed him several pairs-he
said they were all too stont, and wished to see a lady's pair-I left him, and
went into the back shop-in about a minute 1 came back--he had altered bis
position-I asked what he had put into his pocket-he said he had got
nothing, and had done nothing-I said I was pretty certain he had, and put
my band to the left side of his pocket, felt something, and called a porter to
fetch the police-he took a pair of soles from his pocket, gave them me, and
repeated that he had done nothing--the soles on the counter appeared as if
they had been moved.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Had you told him the price? 4.
No--they were all marked--some were marked 6d. he may have held his
hand up when I came in-he did not then give me the soles--I rubbed him
down several times, and sent for the policeman first.
GEORGE PATTT (City policeman, 641.) I was sent for, and took the pri-
soner- I found 2 d. and a half-farthing on him--be said his father had given
bim a sixpence to buy a pair of ladies' soles, but he had lost it.
Cross-examined. Q. Did not he say he had them in his band, but they
had not been in his pocket? A. Something like that.
