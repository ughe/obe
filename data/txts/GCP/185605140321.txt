Seventh Session, 1855–56.
JOHN ROBERT REYNOLDS (policeman, R 289). On the evening of 14th April
I saw the four prisoners in High-street, Sydenham-I apprehended Ponman
and John Reynolds-Ingram took the other two-I searched them at the
station; on Ponman I found a half sovereign, a piece of paper, 78. 6d. in
silver, and 5d. in copper the half sovereign fell as I went to lay it on the
desk; whether it fell from the paper I could not tell-I also found a
tobacco box and a knife on Ponman; and on John Reynolds a tobacco box
-when they were charged with the robbery, Ponman said he knew nothing
of it the others did not say anything in my hearing and
Cross-excamined. l. Did not each of them say they knew nothing of the
- robbery A. Not in my presence or hearing--I recollect no one speaking
but Ponman at the station---the paper may have been separate from the
half-orown, for aught I know there was a piece of brown paper in his
Spocket, something similar to what sugar is wrapped in there was nothing
else in the same pocket but a piece of string—the tobaoco box was in a
different pocket, but I have kept the paper in it since the purse was found
in a gentleman's garden-I did not see it found the person who found it
pointed out the spot to me, but could not state the day on which he found
it-it was about half a mile from where I took the prisoners into custody
Ponman was with his barrow when I met him-he had it nearly filled with
iron ware, shovels, and hoops, and things—the other boys were with him
I did not see that they had anything. LOV ENOG
Witnesses for the Defence DAR
JOSEPH JOHN HUSSEY. I am a blacksmith, of No. 3, Charlotte street,
Turvillo-street, Bethnal-green-I have known Ponman two or three years
he has been working for me for the last five months. I always considered
him honest and industrious--I never knew anything against him.
Oross-examined by MR. LILLEY. Q. How came he to have his head
cropped so? 4. He has been ill since his imprisonment, and has had his
head shaved-I livelinear him he is married I know all the other prisoners
perfectly well, and have done so for two or three years Ponman worked for
me up to the time of his being taken into custody. I sent him out on that
day to dispose of goods he had with him about 3. cwt. of goods, worth
between 57. and 61. -I got the goods back from the policeman at the station
-about 16 worth had been sold he has been in the habit of travelling for
me with goods in that way, daily, for five months, to every part within
twenty miles of London, and he used to account to me daily I have known
the Reynolds' from their infancy, and never knew any harm of them they
are employed by their father, who is a hawker of hardware Gore was
employed by Ponman to help push his barrow he lives in the same
street.
