 929  LYDIA SHEPHERD was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of March , 1 sovereign and 1 sixpence, the monies of Thomas John Freeman , from his person.
   THOMAS JOHN FREEMAN. I keep a coal-shed in New Gravel-lane. On the 16th of March I was at the Ship and Unicorn public-house in New Gravel-lane- I found the prisoner there; it was between one and two o'clock - I was sober; there were but a couple of pots of beer between four or five of us - the prisoner lived within two doors of me; I have sold her coals, and when I went in she said, "What, are you come you old b-r?" I said,"You had better use better words and pay me for my coals;" for she owed me 8d. - she said, "I will have your life before I will pay you;" as I was sitting against the window they put some flour all over my clothes, and threw some in my eyes, then threw me backwards, throttled, and robbed me of a sovereign and a sixpence - the prisoner was kneeling on my breast, and was choking me; I felt her hand in my pocket - I could not call out, because she strangled me; there were a good many persons there in the same line - she keeps a bad house; there were two or three men there - they went and told my wife, who came, caught hold of her, and broke her hold: I did not go to tell any officer - next morning my eyes were so bad that I could not get up; before I got better she went and got a warrant for me, and when I told my tale they committed her.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How many people were in the room when she choked and robbed you? A. Five, six or seven - there was not a single man besides myself; two men went and told my wife, but they did not see any part of the row - after they went out of the house I did not knock the prisoner down, I am sure; I could not go that night to an officer or a Magistrate - I could not see out of my eyes; I did keep a house, but I had left it that day - I have had two lodgers, but I never took in lodgers by the hour, nor by the day; I have by the week, never by the night - I have been six or seven months in the potatoe business; I cannot tell whether I called for a glass of gin in the house - I never drank one; if I had asked for ten glasses of gin I could have had them - I have understood the prisoner's husband has died since this happened.
   SARAH GINGLE. I went to the house, saw the prosecutor on the floor, and the prisoner on his breast - she had her right hand in his right-hand waistcoat pocket, and said"You old b-r, before I have done, I will have your life;" I came out directly - I saw there was white-wash or colour on his back, and all about him; I afterwards saw his wife take him home.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is not the prosecutor your father? A. Yes; the report was given by the fiddler and plasterer- I ran over; I did not notice my father's eyes - I did not see him for two or three days afterwards.
     NOT GUILTY.
 930.  CHARLES LEDGERWOOD and ABEL SAMELLS were indicted for stealing, on the 19th of February , 1 bushel of coals, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Job Cook.
   JOB COOK. I am in the coal trade, and live at Hammersmith. On the 19th of February, a barge had come in with twenty-one chaldron of coals, about five o'clock in the afternoon; I sent a person to watch it, as it was to remain at the wharf all night.
   WILLIAM HEDGES. I was sent to watch the barge: I was playing with a white dog between seven and eight o'clock, and saw the two prisoners come on the barge; I told them they had better get off, or it would be worse for them: they would not - but they threw down the coals to two others, and they ran off with them - I ran after them; Ledgerwood dropped his piece of coal - the others got away with theirs; I knew the two prisoners before.
 Prisoner SAMELLS. He said he never saw me before - but a bricklayer's boy told him it was me. Witness. I know him very well, and knew him at the time.
   JAMES SMITH. I am a constable. I took up the prisoners, and accused them of stealing the coals - and as I was bringing them to town, they acknowledged taking a few; they were very nearly rescued from me.
 LEDGERWOOD's Defence. He took my brother - I went to the cage to see him, and this boy swore to me - the officer then came and said, "I want you for the coals;" I walked with him - he had no trouble with me.
 SAMELL's Defence. We have had seven weeks in Clerkenwell prison - and, if we were guilty, I think it is quite punishment enough - I know no more of them than a new-born baby.
 LEDGERWOOD -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 SAMELLS -    GUILTY. Aged 15. Whipped and Discharged.
 931.  WILLIAM SAUNDERS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of April , 2 pairs of shoes, value 3s., the goods of Robert George Brodie.
   ROBERT GEORGE BRODIE. I am a ladies' shoemaker. and live in Cranbourn-passage. About a quarter after three o'clock, on the 6th of April, I was going over the way to a person who conducted my business during my illness, and the prisoner took these shoes from the corner bar, but I did not see him; they have not been found.
   JOSEPH BURKE. I live on the first floor of a house opposite the prosecutor's. About a quarter past three o'clock on the 6th of April, I was looking through my window, and saw the prisoner looking in the prosecutor's window - he went to the door, and unhooked the two pairs of shoes - before I could open the window he was gone; I went down, and saw the prosecutor; we went to Castle-street, and saw the prisoner loitering about, as if he was coming back again; I do not know what became of the shoes; I am sure that he is the lad - he was taken in three or four minutes.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Month.
