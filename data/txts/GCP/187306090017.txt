WALTER GREGORY (the Prisoner). I was in the employ of McRae and Co.
for seven or eight years, in the warehouse, at 24s, a week--I have pleaded
guilty to stealing a very large quantity of skins from my masters.I know
the prisoner Dunn-I became acquainted with her by her coming to the
warehouse and asking if I had any pieces I said "No "alghe then asked
if I had any broken skins I said "Ño "she afterwards said "If you can
let me have some skins you can have the money"--I said "I cannot
she said "It will be all right "she kept coming to the warehouse, and
asked me several times I at last stole some kips of leather and let her have
them-she agreed to pay me half the price that Messrs. McRae sold them
at-on the 7th, 15th and 23rd March I took ten or twelve kips of chamois
leather to the receiving office, and gave it to Mr. Charles Bartlett some
skins are worth 18. 8d., some 16, 6d. I sent the prisoner about twenty
parcels of leather in 1872, value abont 1007., for which she was to pay me
half the value, but I have not received half of what she promised to pay
me yet-I stole goods from my masters to the amount of 6007., and supplied
them to the prisoner, but I have not received the price of 100%, worth-I
went on supplying her till I was taken in custody on the very day I was
taken, another large parcel was sent-I told her I could not go ou any
longer, as my conscience pricked me.
Cross-examined. I was not foreman, but I have been-I did not tell her
that I was the foreman when she came--I swear I never offered to make
her up a little paroel and send it down ; she persuaded me to send her a
parcel by the carrier—I made out one invoice myself, but only one, that
was the first and the last--that was not for the first lot of things she only
asked me for an invoice of one lot--I have had 1001. from her, but I
expected a great deal more than that, and there is mure than 1504. owing
now-- I told her I wanted the money to pay my master, as my conscience
pricked me.

agent for the Parcels Delivery Company--I know Gregory, he has been in
the habit of bringing purcels there--he brought about forty in 1872, all
directed to Mrs. Dunn, and weighing about 1 ewt. each one of them was
cracked at the bottom, and the children were playing with it, and pulled a
piece of leather through on March 7th, 15th, and 23rd, Gregory left
parcels directed to Mrs. Dunn, and, by the charge for them in the book
(produced), they would weigh about 1 cwt. each in the ordinary course of
business they would be collected at the head office, and from thence they
would go to Mrs. Dunn.
Cross-examined. That is the usual way of sending leather-I have
received other parcels in that way.
