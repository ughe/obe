proved that the child went to sleep again, and awoke at six, screaming, and

whatever to indicate the presence of arsenic ; it could not have been in the
system at that time-I think I understood that the child did not vomit in the
night ; now vomiting is one of the most prominent symptoms of arsenic, and
where the bowels are at all obstructed it must exist in a most intense degree
-it appears that the mixture sent by Mr. Woolmer's assistant, on Thursday
morning, was given about eight, and up to the time she left by the train &
period of an hour and a half, there are no symptoms referable to arsenio-
in crossing to Ryde there was a discharge of blood from the bowels; that is
exactly what I should expect where intersusception has lasted fifteen or
sixteen hours ; for when the bowels become constricted the congestion of the

arsenic had then been in the system, and gone on to produce gangrene and
ulceration, the destruction of the lining membrane would have caused blood
