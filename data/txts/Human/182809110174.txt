 1924.  JOSEPH WATERS was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of August , 1 watch, value 3l.; 1 seal, value 1d., and 1 key, value 1d., the goods of John Roach , from his person.
 JOHN ROACH. I was at the Admiral Hagel public-house, between nine and ten o'clock at night, on the 2d of August - I lost my watch from my pocket; I did not feel it go, nor see who took it; I saw the prisoner in the house - I had been drinking, but was not in liquor; I had my head on the table, and went to sleep; I awoke, and missed my watch - I have not found it.
   WILLIAM SULLIVAN. I was at the public-house with some friends. and saw the prosecutor fall asleep - I saw the prisoner take the watch from his pocket, and put it into his own; when the prosecutor came in, the prisoner stood up and shook hands with him, and they drank together; the prisoner and another man went out together, and then the prosecutor said he had been robbed, and went out; I am certain the prisoner took the watch.
 Prisoner. Q. Where did I sit? A. At his right-hand side; I sat at the corner of the table - you sat next him, and his friend on the other side of him; you came in before them.
 Prisoner to  JOHN ROACH. Q. Did we not go out together for the beer? A. No, when I went in, you stood up and shook hands with me, and asked me to sit down and drink.
   EDWARD HEDGES. The prosecutor came to me on Monday morning - I went with him, and took the prisoner, who was in bed with a little girl, but found nothing on him; I charged him with the theft - he said nothing.
 Prisoner's Defence. Sullivan was tried here last Session; when I went out, the prosecutor had not missed his watch - he came out himself to the corner of the street- his friend asked if he had lost anything; he said Yes, his watch - his friend accused me of stealing it; I said,"Search me," but he would not; they suspected Sullivan, and went back to the house, and on Monday morning they sent for me.
   WILLIAM SULLIVAN. I was tried last Session, and acquitted. I did not see the prosecutor and his friend come back to the house; I saw the prisoner in the street, but did not give charge of him; I had heard of the loss - there was some wrangling in the street; I went up, and said he ought to return the watch - he said he would knock me down.
 JOHN ROACH. I did not suspect Sullivan - I missed my watch just as I came out at the door; my friend and I came out just after the prisoner, and my friend accused him of it; he said he could knock his head off.
 WILLIAM SULLIVAN. I did not hear what passed at the door - I kept my seat in the house.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Fourteen Years.

 1926.  WILLIAM CAREY and ANN CAREY were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , 1 watch, value 3l.; 2 seals, value 5s.; 1 snuff-box, value 15s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 13s.; and 13 shillings, the property of James Hughes.
 JAMES HUGHES. I am a  tradesman out of business. On the 4th of August this watch and money were in my trousers, and the snuff-box in my coat pocket; I met Ann Carey in Camden-town, at one o'clock on Sunday morning. the 3d of August, and went with her to a room in a house at Battle-bridge- I went to bed with her, having put my clothes on something that felt like a chair, but I went to bed in the dark; I did not awake till the morning, and then missed my property, and accused her of being an accessory - I had heard a great noise in the night, which she attributed to rats; I said, "They must be very large ones indeed" - when I accused her of the robbery, she said she knew nothing about it, nor who had taken them, but she admitted they were gone; I saw the watch, seals, and snuff-box at Hatton-garden on the 9th, but I have not got the trousers and money.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you tipsy? A. I was sober enough to know what I was doing - I was as conscious of it as I am now; she took me to Providence-row - I have heard she was married.
 Q. Do not you know that the rat was her husband who came and took your trousers to punish you? A. My opinion is, that he was in the room under the bed at the time - Ann Carey went to the pawnbroker's to get me a pair of breeches to put on; the pawnbroker lent them to me, as I had no money.
   FRANCIS CURTIS. I am a labourer. On Monday morning, the 4th of August, about nine o'clock I was at breakfast, the male prisoner came and asked if I would take a walk; I said I was going to work - he urged me to take a walk, and I did; and while I was changing my trousers, he came up stairs, pulled out a watch, and asked the value of it - I said I was no judge, but would go and inquire; I went to Mr. Pickett's, who said it was worth three guineas and a half - he then asked me to go and pawn it, which I did for 2l., and gave him the money; he then pulled out the snuff-box, and asked me to pawn that, but I would not, and he pawned it himself.
   WILLIAM PACKER. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. On the 4th of August Curtis pawned the watch.
   JOHN HARRIS. I am assistant to a pawnbroker, and have a silver snuff-box pawned on the 4th of August, by William Carey.
   JOHN LIMBRICK. I am an officer. I have a sovereign, a half-sovereign, three half-crowns, a shilling, a sixpence, and some halfpence which I found on the female prisoner on the 4th of August; the prosecutor gave me information, and I took both the prisoners at the Bell public-house - the pawnbroker described the money as what he had given.
