ROBERT TELFORD. I am a bair-dresser, and live in London-road,
Southwark. In August last Brown took two rooms on my second floor at
108. a week-a wife and two children were with him--they all lodged there
they were there seven weeks at the time Brown was taken up-he was
away for two or three days at the beginning of the week-they had no ser-
vant- I do not think any one came to visit them Brown generally went
out in the morning at nine or ten o'clock and returned in the evening-he
appeared a regular man.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Smith was not in the habit of coming
there? A. No.
JOHN HEARD. I am a porter at the East India Chambers. Brown had
been several times about an office, and on the 31st of August he concluded
an agreement for it-he took two rooms, letter C-he was to pay thirty
guineas a year-the desk and things in the room were mine, and he agreed
to buy them for 51.-be took possession on the 31st-I did not see him
after that-be did not pay me the 5l. -- there was no rent due-an old man
named Twisdale was acting as clerk to bim-he came there repeatedly-
there were plenty of things came from tradesmen and the parties took them
away again, because I represented what I thought he was- I do not know
a man of the name of Hadwin.
John REYNOLDS. I am shopman to Mr. Richards, a hatter in the
Strand. On the 21st of September Brown drove up to the door in a
double-bodied chaise-Smith was sitting inside-Brown came in, and
said, "Is Mr. Richards in ?"-I said "No"-he said, "I want a hat," and
he tried on one-he then said, "My servant wants a hat " Smith came
in and tried one on, and got one to fit him-Brown then said he wanted
one with a gold band, not so good a one as that I said we had very good
ones at 138. 6d.at last he got one that fitted him-Brown said to Smith,
"Is that one that fits you ?"-he said " Yes"-I showed Brown the pat-
tern-book for the band, and he chose one at 12s. 60.-- he said, "I will
take these two, and you can send that before five o'clock "-he had thrown
down his card before that, as residing at the East India Chambers-he said, I
must send it before five o'clock, as he left town at five, for his house at
Blackheath-Brown took his hat away on his head-I put Smith's in a box.
thinking he was the servant-I had some doubt, while I was putting it into
the box, whether I should let them go, but Brown took up a leather-case
and asked me the price of it-I said 108.- I thought I would let them
have the two hats at a venture-I sent the other hat and the gold band at
five o'clock, and they were not there.
Cross-examined by Mr. JONES. Q. I suppose the hat Brown tried on
was such as you expected a gentleman like him to wear ? A. Yes--
Smith was not in livery-Brown said, "My servant wants a hat," and he
beckoned Smith in the hat that Smith had was not so good as Mr.
Brown's, but it was better than the one that was to have the gold band-
he did not call Smith his servant in his presence.
