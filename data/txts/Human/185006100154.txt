DAVID SIMPER( policeman, R  310). I received charge of the prisoner at the station—he said he took the money, and on going to the lock-up place, he said he took the money from the box—I asked what he had done with it—he said he spent it at Greenwich.
   Prisoner's Defence.  A friend came to see me, who enticed me to go out to drink; I got too much, and absented myself for six days; I then gave myself up to the sergeant of the guard; the sergeant-major came to me, and asked if I had spent all the money; I said, "What money?" he said, "You will be made to know," and the next day I was ordered before the Colonel, who asked me if I would be tried by a Court-martial for selling my regi mental boots, and the next day I was called and asked if I knew anything about Corporal Laing's money; I told him I did take it, but I solemaly declare I know nothing about it; I did not know what I was doing or saying, for I was not in my reason, being afraid of being flogged; I thought it better to throw myself on the civil Court, and should I be so fortunate as to be acquitted, I intend, by God's assistance, to. be a good soldier, and not swerve from the duties of my calling. GUILTY. Aged 23.— Confined Six Months .
 (  The prosecutor stated that the prisoner had been tried by three Courts-martial. )
 1197. ROBERT MATTISON was indicted for embezzling 16 shilling; the moneys of Charles Edward Matson , his master. CHARLES FRANCIS GALE. I am a solicitor. On 29th May, I was return ing from the Derby—I saw the prisoner, who was acting as guard to one of the coaches—I asked him for two places for myself and a friend—he spoke to some persons, and we were allowed to get on the box, but it was agreed that if a passenger returned, one should get on the roof—it was not agreed then what I should pay the prisoner, but on the road home it was, and I paid him 16 s ., that was 8 s . a piece for myself and friend before I arrived in town—Church was the coachman. EDWARD WILLIAM CHURCH. I am in the service of Charles Edward Matson—on the Derby-day I was at Epsom—I drove the coach to which the prisoner was acting as guard—I did not know of any extra passengers—I did not see Mr. Gale taken on the coach—I did not receive any money from him—I received a sovereign from the prisoner previous to going in the morn ing—I did not receive any money for passengers taken at Epsom—if he took any money be did not pay it me.
