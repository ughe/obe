ABRAHAM HAM. I am beadle of St. Stephen's, Coleman-street, and live
in Little Bell-alley. On 10th March, about a quarter past 7 o'clock in the
evening, I was crossing Moorgate-street, and saw a large crowd of persons-I
crossed to see the cause, and saw Ginn attempting to take the two prisoners
--they were struggling with him, and Peters was kicking him-I took
Quaid by the collar, and I said if he had done no harm he could not object
to go to the station-he took Ginn by the collar-I took hold of Quaid, and
kept him at arm's length from Peters—I saw Ginn with his staff, and saw
him strike Peters a slight blow on the side of the head-Peters was kicking
him very violently at the time, and Ginn called to me," He has got my
truncheon," and I saw it in Peters's hand-Peters said, "It is mine"-
I seized Peters by the right wrist, and prevented him from using it, while
I had Quaid by the collar-Ginn was nearly exhausted—I saw Peters taken
to the station-I went with him—there were four to take him-I got a
severe kick on my hand-when at the station I examined Peters's shoes-
they were tipped at the heels, and had hobnails at the front, enough to kill
a man with a kick.
