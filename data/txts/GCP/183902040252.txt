JURY. Q. Do you consider him in full possession of his intellect?
A. I know nothing to the contrary-I never observed any deficiency at
all-he was very handy in what he undertook to do the whole employ.
ment he had from me since the 17th of November, was five days and
three quarters- I believe he had a little work elsewhere, but he wanted
to work for me, I had some which I said I could not have done till after
Christmas-I really think, at the time, that he was in great need of the
means of providing for himself and children-he had three quarters of a
day's work on the 30th of November.
MARGARET PASSFIELD. I am the prisoner's daughter, and am thir-
teen years old. I think my brother was between six and seven years-I
do not know the day of the month he died-he had been ill about three
weeks or a month before he died-he complained of his head being bad-
he had the measles, I believe, and he used to cough-I never heard him
hoop-he had had a cough a good while before he died-he died in
Counter-alley-I remember Mr. Boulter coming there the day before he
died-we had nothing but a few shavings there, no bed or bed-clothes-
we bad something to eat that day and the day before my father gave us
all he could-he was not in employment at the time my brother died-
when my father had food he gave it to us as well as took it himself-I
never knew him take food himself and not give us any-he did not go and
ask for medical assistance when my brother was ill-I had had some bread
to eat the day Mr. Boulter came, and my brother too-my father had not
long been gone out when Mr. Boulter came-he was gone to see and get us
some victuals-he slept in the same room with us, on the shavings-he al-
ways gave us part of what he had he was pretty kind to us we had been
living there about a month, I believe-my brother had a very bad cough-
he coughed at night-my father would sometimes spend his money some-
where else he used to get us in some victuals first, and then he used to go
out, leaving me and my brother in the house by ourselves-he sometimes
came home drunk, not very often, and not when we were without food-we
have not been a day, I think, without something or other--we sometimes
got bread and dripping, and sometimes bread and potatoes sometimes we
had bread given to us-sometimes people gave us victuals, and sometimes
they would not-my brother had a coat given to him, and a pair of trowsers
my father gave him, and a shirt my father bought him-I know Mrs. Bant-
lin-my father slept at her place once on the floor-my brother was well
then-it was before he was taken bad, not long before-we had only
some old pieces of baize and carpets over us--my brother had no doctor-
he was very bad for about three weeks or a month, and about a day or two
before he died he could not stand up on his legs-he died on Saturday-
