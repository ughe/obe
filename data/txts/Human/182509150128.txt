   MARY ANN FOX. I am single, and live at No. 22, White's-row, on the second floor; Mason lives on the first floor - it is the third door up White's-row. On the Monday evening, previous to the robbery, between six and seven o'clock, I saw the prisoner Samuel Crook there - he asked me if Mr. Mason was there. I said, I did not think that he was - he went up stairs; he had a fustian jacket and trousers similar - they were light. (looking at a jacket) I saw him in that jacket.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he not dressed in the blue coat he has on now? A. No, he had a fustian jacket. I get my living by wharping canes for the weavers.
   SARAH CARKETT. I am the wife of Hector Carkett, we live at No. 22, White's-row, on the second floor. On the Friday morning before the robbery, a little after seven o'clock, I opened the street door, and saw the prisoners William and Samuel Crook - they came in and followed me up stairs; one of them knocked at Mason's door, and called Ned - he was let in, and when the door was unfastened I went up to my own apartment, and soon after heard a Christian name called, but what I do not know. I looked into the street out of the window, and saw Samuel Crook come from the step of the street door, and stand under the window. William Crook soon after came from the door, and joined him; and soon after Mason came up fastened his neck handkerchief round his neck, and all three went away together - I watched them altogether as far as I could see. On Monday night, the officers came to the house and took Mason, and part of the property. Barrs, the officer, told me to mind the door of Mason's room. I went down to the street door, then returned with a candle in my hand, and was going to close Mason's door, and saw William Crook sitting in the room. I said "Oh! there you are, are you." I then double locked the door on him, and told Barrs.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you know the Crooks before? A. I had repeatedly seen them, but not to know their names. Mason is a  braca maker, not a weaver.
 HECTOR CABKETT. About eight o'clock on the Friday morning, previous to this robbery, I saw the prisoner, Samuel Crook, in fustian jacket, with a roll of black silk; he went up stairs, and came down in about two minutes without it.
   THOMAS ALMOND. I am inspector of the watch, and live in Fashion-street, Spitalfields. On the Monday, the night of the robbery, about a quarter past twelve o'clock, I and Barrs were in White's-row; I observed two men coming down White's-row; they passed the door, talking, then returned, and went into the house - it is where Carkett lodges. I went into the house, knocked at the door, and was answered by the female prisoner; I said, there were two men come into that house; she said there was not. I said I was confident of it - that I had seen one of them come out again, and there must be one there then; I asked her for a light - she assisted me in searching the lower part of the house; I found nobody there. Barrs was stationed there - he called me, and said there was a man coming out of the first floor window. I ran to the door, and saw a man getting in at the window - I believe it to be the prisoner Mason - we ran up to the first floor room; the window of which room I had seen the person at, and Mason stood there with the female prisoner - I laid hold of Mason. Barrs found some silk on the bed, and the moment it was found the female prisoner ran down, and escaped. I followed her - she was taken, and brought back. Next morning I went to Mr. Waterlow's premises, and examined the three outer doors - none of them appeared to have any marks of outward violence, nor inside, not on the doors - two of the slides under the counter, and a writing-desk, all had marks of violence on them - there were only two slides with locks, and they had marks of violence upon them; the two locks secured the whole four slides. I questioned Mr. Waterlow, and Samuel Crook was produced to me - I thought by his appearance he was the man I had seen go out of the house, in White's-row, and who had gone in with the other - he had neither coat nor hat on - I wished him to put them on; he went up, and did so. My opinion was then stronger that he was the person I had seen; I do not swear positively to him. I went up stairs to his room, and found a piece of iron behind his bed - I applied it to the marks upon the desk and slides - they corresponded particularly on the slides. I also found a pair of dirty shoes in his room - I asked him who they belonged to; he said they were not his, and afterwards that some person had given them to him; I found the jacket produced in the room.
 Cross-examined. Q. What do you call that iron? A. I do not know what to call it - it is not a crow-bar; it is common for thieves to enter premises by false keys. I saw nobody let the men into the house in White's-row - the door was open - it was a quarter or twenty minutes past twelve o'clock at night. I was about twenty yards from them; I had never seen Samuel Crook before: there is a gas-light over the door. I am not positive of him; I am not certain whether the one who came out was the one who went in.
 MR. PHILLIPS to JOHN CHAPPELL. Q. Did you see him clean his shoes when he came in that night? A. No; I saw the shoes there in the morning, when the bed was turned up.
