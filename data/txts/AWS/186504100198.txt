EDWARD WINGFTELD. I am a time-keeper in the service of the South-
eastern Company-I knew John Cumming, who was injured in the accident
-he died in the Royal Marine hospital at Woolwich from the injuries he
received.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate entered the company's
service two years last August as porter, which situation I held up to a week
previous to the accident, when I was placed in the signal-box to do duty for
the signal-man who was ill. I don't believe I had done more than twenty
duties in the signal-box, that is to say, when there was any signalling to do,
before I took charge of it, so that my previous experience was but little.
However, when I took the duty I hoped I should have been able to have
performed it satisfactorily. My duties were to attend in the signal-box from
7 in the morning until 6 in the evening, with the exception of the hour
allowed for dinner. After coming out of the box at 6, T had to wait the
arrival and to attend the 6-17 down train. Whilst in the box I had
to receive and give telegraphic signals to and from Lewisham-station,
and to and from Angerstein-junetion. I had beside to enter signals in
five different places for each train in the signal-book in addition. I had
for each train or engine to attend to the semaphore and distinct signal, and
upon each train passing to shew my flag or hand-signal that all was right. I
will now state briefly the circumstances connected with the lamentable acci-
