John COBLEY. I am a police-constable. The prisoner gave me up the

Prisoner's Defence. I was very much distressed through my husband's
leaving me, and going to live with a common prostitute-I was obliged to
do what I did-my intention was not to leave the house when I came
home, he called me into his own room, and said he knew there were
things gone--I said I had not left the lodgings; but when I did, I should
leave every thing as I found it-when I was at work, my husband used
to annoy me-I could not get my bread honestly, so I put these things in
my mother's maiden name, in which I got work.

Tuomas GODBY. I am a clothes-salesman, and live at No. 36 and 87,
Brook-street. I have one partner-the prisoner was my shopman for eight
years, and occasionally employed in collecting and going out for orders--
was his duty to go out and receive monies, and account for them the
same day-he entered it in a book.
Moses CHARLES BIDMEAD. I deal with Mr. Godby-in September
last I paid the prisoner 61. 93., on account of his master-this is the bui
and receipt-(read.)
Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. He gave you this receipt, which
at any time would be proof that he had received the money ?-. Yes;
I think it was about twelve o'clock.
Thomas GODBY, re-examined. He did not account for the receipt of this
61. 98.- I did not speak to him about it, as I did not know he went after
it-he went into the City to get orders, and he absented himself on the
22nd of September--we paid him weekly, on Saturdays—he went away
on Tuesday.

A. Never; there was never a-penny mistake all the time he was there

thing could not happen to the prisoner-the book was always to be had,
