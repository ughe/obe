 Cross-examined. Q. I believe he gave you his right address? A. Yes, he did, and from inquiries I have made I have heard the best of character of him.
   WILLIAM COLLARD. The prisoner had come to my house on the 18th of March, but he had not spoken to me about the lodging; when I got home on the night in question I missed a pair of trousers belonging to Mr. Pilkington - I went to the station, and they were taken off the prisoner.
 GEORGE PILKINGTON. I lodge in the house. These articles are all mine - the linen was in the drawers, and the trousers were in the room.
 Cross-examined. Q. When had you left? A. In the morning, to go to chambers - I had seen the trousers the day before.

 1051.  WILLIAM JARVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , 2 coats, value 17s., and 1 waistcoat, value 3s., the goods of Haggady Baker.
   HAGGADY BAKER. I live at No. 5, Union-row, Great Turnstile. This is my property.
   RICHARD SIMMONS. I am a carter and carrier - these coats were in my van, in my possession. On the 24th of February I was carrying them for Mr. Baker, and going up Queen-street- my man told me I was being robbed of some clothes; he said, "There is the man;" I saw the prisoner, followed him up Holborn, up Kingsgate-street and Eagle-street, and saw him drop the clothes - I took them up, and saw him taken.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Is the man who told you of it here? A. No; they had been in the bottom of the van; I saw them safe in Holborn about five minutes before.
   JAMES JENNINGS. I was going through Eagle-street, and met the prisoner with two coats; I made way for him to pass - I then heard a cry of Stop thief! I pursued him; he dropped the coats not far from me - I followed him till he was taken.
   JAMES BENSON. I am an officer. I was standing in my shop, and heard the alarm; I ran out, saw the prisoner running, followed him to Red Lion-square, and took him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was walking up the street, and saw them; I picked them up; I was running, and heard Stop thief! called - I dropped them.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. - Confined Twelve Months.
 1052.  THOMAS LAWRENCE and ROBERT TAYLOR were indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of March , 1 stove, value 10s., the goods of John Povey ; and that Lawrence had been before convicted of felony.
   GEORGE MACKENTILL. I live in Oxford-market. I was opposite Mr. Poney's shop, who is an ironmonger, on the 2nd of March, and saw the prisoners there; they each of them took hold of an end of this stove, and carried it off - I followed, and took them with it; I held them till the prosecutor and the officer came.
   GEORGE HOBBY. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoners into custody - this stove stood by their side.
   JOHN POVEY. This stove is mine.
   WILLIAM JAMIESON. I am a constable of St. Pancras. I have a certificate of the former conviction of Lawrence, by the name of John Lawrence; I was a witness, and know he is the man - (read.).
 LAWRENCE -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 TAYLOR -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Months.
 1053.  EDWARD OXBY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 1 hat, value 18s., the goods of Samuel Gravenor.
   SAMUEL WELLS, I know Mr. Gravenor's shop, in Bethnal-green-road. I saw the prisoner and another boy pass his door on the 28th of March, and the prisoner took a hat from the door; I called Stop thief! and pursued: some gentleman tried to stop him - he threw down the hat, and a gentleman brought it to me.
   WILLIAM STRAY. I attend to the business of Mr. Samuel Gravenor. About eight o'clock in the evening I was informed there was a thief - I ran out, and saw the prisoner running with the hat - he threw it down; we ran on to Cabbage-court, where I lost sight of him for an instant, but a Policeman took him - I met him with him - I know he is the man.
