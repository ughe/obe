ABRAHAM BERLYN. In Nov. 1840, I was living in Back Church-lane, Whitechapel, and carried on the business of a boot and shoemaker. After the fire at Hoare's-wharf, I purchased some Hessian linen canvas, and other things, at the sales—there were five successive sales—I saw a man named Nathan at all the sales, and he saw me—on the 4th of Nov., Nathan came to me at Goulston-street, where I have a warehouse—he said to me, "Well, Mr. Berlyn, have you sold all your linen yet?"—I said, "No"—he said, "I think I can sell a great quantity for you; what will you allow me?"—I said, "I will allow you 5 l .; per cent. on all that you sell for me"—he said 5 l .; per cent, would not pay him, and it was ultimately agreed that I should allow him a farthing a yard, which made it come to 6 1/4 l .; per cent—he asked me to give him samples of the various sorts I had, and the quantity of each, which I did—he came again the same day and said he had seen the person he wanted the linen for, that he was captain of his own vessel, and a man of large property, that the captain wished to see me himself to bargain for this linen, and if I would go with him at half-past four that afternoon, after 'Change time, to No. 15, Baker-street, Lloyd's-square, Captain Cornish would try to deal with me—we arranged to go that afternoon at half-past four, after 'Change time—about twelve o'clock Nathan came again and said he had seen the captain since he saw me, and the captain wanted a quantity of flock—I said, "Flock is a thing I do not sell, and I am no judge of it"—he said, "I could not haw come to a better person than you; I know you have a few pounds at com mand; I know a person who has a large quantity on hand, and has a bill to pay, and he would be glad to sell it at a little loss to turn it into cash"—I said I
