WILLIAM INWARD (City Detective). I arrested the prisoner on Monday,
16th May, in consequence of his not surrendering at the Mansion House
I had occasionally watched his house from Saturday-I saw him at
8.30 p.m. in Ball's Pond Road—I told him I was a detective, and that there
was a warrant for his apprehension for fraud-I had seen the prisoner leave
his residence, 43, Holly Street, Dalston, previous to this, he said " This is

him I could not, and that he would have to go to the City with me then-
he said "I will come; I knew it must come to this sooner or later" -I took
him to the station-I don't think he made any reply to the charge being
read to him.
ALFRED OUGHTON. I am a clerk in the employ of Messrs. Wilkins, of
19, King's Arms Yard - they were solicitors for the trustee in bankruptcy
of the late occupier of the Horns, Alexander Anderson-Mr. Barlow
eventually bought the business—the agreement was signed on 16th January,
and a deposit of 2001. was paid on that day.
Cross-examined by MR. WILLIAMS. It was paid to Mr. Wilkins- I was
present-Mr. Ashdown was the trustee.
MR. WILLIAMS submitted that there was no such direction, in writing soith
regard to the two sums in question as was contemplated by 24 & 25 Viet.
chap. 96, sec. 75, and that the 1007. had been deposited with Bale some time ante
cedent for the purpose of his handing it over to the prisoner, and Bale parted
with it to the prisoner by his cheque (See Reg. v. Tatlock), and that the 88.
did not come within the statute, Mr. Powis having endorsed a cheque for that
amount and requested the prisoner to find the other 621., therefore the paying
of the deposit of 1501. was conditional on the prisoner finding the 621., which
it was not incumbent on him to do. (See Reg. v. Christian, Law Reports,
VOL. II, 1872-5.) MR. STRAIGHT contended that the letter enclosing the 88.
was one from which specific direction could be construed as to the application
of that particular sum of money, and that throughout the whole of the case of
Reg. v. Christian there was no specific direction by the lady for uchum
Christian was acting, and that the natural inference to be drawn in this cas
was that there was direction in writing as to both the sums in question
The COURT considered that there was not a sufficient direction in writing with
respect to the 1001., and that it would be safer to rely on the case raised by
the 881. only.
