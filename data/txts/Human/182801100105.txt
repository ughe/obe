 478.  WILLIAM WATSON was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of January , 7 sovereigns, and 6 half-sovereigns, the monies of William Banks , in his dwelling-house.
 WILLIAM BANKS. I live in  Blakeley's-rents, Bartholomew-close, and am a publican; the prisoner was formerly my pot-boy, for about four months, and had quitted me three or four months; he had been at my house two or three times since, and was there on the Friday before, the 6th of January. On the Saturday, at half-past twelve o'clock at night, my money was all right in a little galleypot - I locked it up in a cupboard in the dining-room, and had the key in my pocket - there were seven sovereigns and, I think, six half-sovereigns. On Sunday night, at half-past ten o'clock, I was going to put some more to it - I found the cupboard unlocked, the gally-pot removed, and the money gone - I have never found any of it since; I inquired who had been up stairs, and could not find that any body had, except the prisoner, who had gone up at two o'clock, to shift his shirt, but he had not asked my leave, and had no right there. I had him taken about half-past eleven o'clock that night - he said he knew nothing of it, and had no gold or any money about him - I am sure of that; he was searched in my presence, and 1l. 2s. 6d. in silver, and some halfpence were found on him.
   REBECCA DARTSSE. I was working at the prosecutor's on the 6th of January - I saw the prisoner there about two o'clock; he asked me for a shirt - I aired it, and gave it him; he went up stairs to put it on, and brought down his dirty one.
   GEORGE SMITH. I am tapster to the prosecutor. On Sunday, the 6th of January, I saw the prisoner with his shirt on his hand, going towards the stairs; he came down again in ten minutes or a quarter of an hour - there had been several persons there on the Saturday night, and there were five or six persons in the house on Sunday.
   CHARLES BANNISTER. I am a servant out of place. On the 6th of January I was at the Tavistock Arms public-house, Tavistock-place, Brunswick-square, between six and seven o'clock in the evening; the prisoner came in and called for a quartern of gin - I knew him before, and had part of it; he paid for it with a half-sovereign; he had it in a little bag - he put his fingers into the bag, and said he was not without money, and I saw, I thought, ten or twelve sovereigns in it.
   HENRY BUTT. I am pot-boy at the Hand and Chair public-house, Colonade. The prisoner came there about seven o'clock, and had some refreshment; I saw him open a little bag with six or seven sovereings in it.
   RICHARD DAVIS. I was constable of the night; the prisoner was brought to the watch-house about half-past eleven o'clock on the 6th of January; I found on him 23s. in silver, and 1s. 2d. in copper. He said he lodged in Coram-street.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, denying the charge.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First London Jury - before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 479.  WALTER WEBB was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of December , 250lbs, weight of cork, value 5l. 10s., the goods of William Peter McAndrews.
 WILLIAM PETER McANDREWS. I am a  fruit-merchant, in partnership with my brother, and live in London-street. I employed the prisoner to discharge, unload, and warehouse a quantity of cork, which I had purchased; the cork in question was in a cellar in George-lane; the whole delivery was complete on Saturday, the 8th of December, and we discharged the prisoner that evening; he had superintended the delivery, and had men under him; his accounts were all settled that evening; and on the Monday I gave him in charge, on suspicion of stealing cork from George-lane; he had nothing to do with cork after the 8th.
 Cross-examined. Q. When did you speak to him about this? A. On the Tuesday; he came to my house with oranges - I gave him in charge.
   ALFRED WALKER. I live with my parents, in George-lane, and work at a jewellers, in Great Queen-street; the prosecutor's cellar is under our house. On Monday, the 10th of December, about a quarter to nine o'clock, the prisoner came and asked for a light; my brother was at the door with me; he brought a truck, went into the cellar, and took away three bundles of cork on the truck; he had been there before, about cork; the cellar was not locked; my father has the care of it; the door is inside the house.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Does not your father keep the key? A. There is no lock; it is bolted; we have two lodgers - I had never seen him there before myself; he had a fustain coat on, I believe. When I was at the Mansion-house, I was desired to look among the prisoners for the man, and it was five minutes before I fixed on him - I passed him several times; my brother pointed him out, and then I knew him.
 COURT. Q. Can you now say, you are sure he is the man? A. Yes - I always said so.
   WILLIAM WALKER. I am nine years old, and live with my father in George-lane. On Monday, the 10th of December, a man came with a truck, and took away three bundles of cork; the prisoner is that man, he asked me for a light, and I gave it to him.
 Cross-examined. Q. How often had you seen him before? A. On the Friday and Saturday; he had a fustian jacket on; my brother was with me.
   DAVID WRIGHT. I am a ship-agent, and live in Thames-street. I had a truck in my care on the 10th of December; it was missing that morning, from about nine till near twelve o'clock - I live about a quarter of a mile from George-lane. I know nothing of the prisoner.
   JOHN HALL. I am a fruit-cooper. On Monday morning, the 10th of December, I saw the prisoner with a truck, and three bundles of cork in it, going up Pudding-lane, close to George-lane, it was about nine o'clock in the morning.
