 COURT to BROWN. Q. Is this the lead? A. The lead I saw at the watch-house, was that taken from the house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you any mark upon the lead? A. It was cut off flushing for entablatures - I have no mark on it, but I have a perfect knowledge of it; it was kept in a place locked up - I had seen it several times, and I missed it from there - this is seven inches wide, but it has no mark on it, and is like the lead I saw there.
   JAMES LITTLE, JUN. re-examined.  1141.  JAMES SCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of James Simmons Atkinson , from his person.
 JAMES SIMMONS ATKINSON. I am  clerk at a carpet-manufactory, in Regent-street. On the 30th of June, I was in St. Paul's-church-yard at a quarter before ten o'clock in the evening, I felt for my handkerchief and missed it; I turned, and saw the prisoner in custody of two officers with my handkerchief at his feet - this is it.
   JOSEPH LLOYD. I am a patrol of Farringdon-within. A few minutes before ten o'clock, I saw the prisoner going down St. Paul's-church-yard following the prosecutor; I saw him take this handkerchief from his pocket - I caught hold of his right hand with the handkerchief in it, he threw it down, and the gentleman turned and said, it was his - I took the prisoner to the Compter.
 Prisoner. It is false; I was walking through St. Paul's-church-yard, some person threw the handkerchief at my face, it was falling to the ground, I picked it up, and this man took me.
     GUILTY- Aged 20. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1142.  LOUISA CURTIS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of June , 1 shawl, value 18s., the goods of Asher Isaacs.
   JOSHUA ISRAEL. I am in the service of Mr. Asher Isaacs, he lives at the corner of Cutler-street, and keeps a clothes shop. On the 17th of June, it rained, and the prisoner stood there to shelter herself - I had taken in the clothes, and desired her to move on, as the door was small; I then went to the next window, and heard my young mistress call out, "Father, she has taken the shawl;" I jumped down off the steps, and took her with this shawl under her arm - I fell down, and she fell at the same time, she had got round to the other door.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Had not this woman cheapened the shawl? A. No, nor any thing; I had no words with her, I only told her to move on, because the door was so small - I did not strike her; there is a mark on the shawl.
   DINAH ISAACS. The prisoner came to shelter herself, I saw her take the shawl.
 Cross-examined. Q. You did not strike her? A. No- I have never said any thing about making this matter up, if I received money.
 Prisoner. I have witnesses who saw me bid for the shawl.
   HENRY LACEY. I was out that day, and stood up in consequence of the rain opposite to Mr. Isaacs - I saw the prisoner, who appeared rather the worse for liquor; she ran to Mr. Isaacs, and this witness objected to her coming - I saw her handling a shawl, and could see she was in the act of bargaining for it - when the rain abated, I heard an altercation between her and the man, and I went over; I saw him strike her repeatedly, and (whether over the threshold, or what I can't say, but) she stumbled, and caught the shawl with one of her hands.
 COURT. Q. Do you mean she never had the shawl in her possession? A. No, she never had - she did not walk away till she was pushed - she had got three hundred yards from the door before she was taken - the shawl was never carried from the prosecutor's house.
   JOSHUA ISRAEL re-examined. Q. I understood you that she had got two doors from your house? A. She had got to our second door; it is a corner house, one door is in Cutler-street, and the other in White-street; from one door to the other is about ten yards - I was on the step at that door when she was running, she would have run further if I had not caught her, we both fell.
