   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL . Q.  Had you anything in the world to do with this man yourself, either by employing him, or receiving the accounts from him, or receiving money from him, or giving him direction? A.  I employed him, as one of the partners—he was in our employ some years as a labouring man—I did not pay him his wages—I took this stock-paper from him before it was completed—I was not in the habit of receiving his papers, or receiving money or accounts from him; that was all done through out cashier—the prisoner was employed at Great Scotland-yard, and he was employed at Nine Elms before we put him there—he was employed the last two or three years at Scotland-yard—he had to come to Nine Elms to give in his accounts—his wages were 20 s . a week—he had been with us probably six or seven years—we have filled up his place since he left, and we are giving higher wages.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  In what respect is this paper not complete? A.  I think he has not charged his expenses—he has mentioned here the amount of goods sold—he was transcribing it from the slate and the day-book when I entered the warehouse. GEORGE KEEF. I am a builder, and live in King-street, Soho. I deal with Messrs. Francis—I obtained goods from the prisoner at Scotland-yard—on 4th June I paid him 1 l . 10 s .; he gave me this receipt.
   Cross-examined. Q.  This is, I see, a bill made out in the name of Ansell; did you know about Francis' being in the business? A.  I knew they were in the business—I got the things from the prisoner—I never saw anything of Francis in the matter—I had dealt with the prisoner for some time before this—this was a payment in part of the account—there was 2 s . 5 1/2 d . left to pay to make it all right—I have paid another account since—I suppose this was added to the other.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Is not the name "Francis" over the door, in Scotland-yard? A.  Yes; I dealt at the yard previous to the prisoner being there—I paid the money with the intention of paying it into the firm, on account of the goods I had had from the yard. WILLIAM LAYCOCK. THOMAS FITZGERALD. I am in Mr. Laycock's service—in July I got 1 l . from him—I gave it to some one in the office, I really cannot tell who, as it is some time ago—I received from the person this receipt and the change—I cannot tell how much change I got—I saw the mail write this receipt—I had been to that place once before.
