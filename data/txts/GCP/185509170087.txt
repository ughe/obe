THOMAS BELL I am shopman to Mr. Thurston, a linen draper, of
Hackney-road. On 27th July Conton came there to purchase a parasol
it came to 38. 11fd. she gave me a 5l note, and asked me if I could
change her a 101. note- I looked at it, and told her it was only a 52. note
she said that she had another, and I gave her the change-I asked her
what name, and she said, "Mrs. Mitobell, 36, Cumberland-street" -I wrote
that on the back of it this is it, and here is my writing on it- she asked
if I could change her another-I gave it to my master, and he gave her
change, in my presence-I know it was a 51. note I saw no other woman
--Mr. Thurston banks with Rogers, Olding, and Co.

IRVING WATSON. I am a detective police sergeant, of Manchester. On
24th Aug, I saw the two female prisoners there together, and from infor
mation I had received, I took them to the station house, and told them they
were charged with stealing 551. from a person in London--Pollitt said, "I
know nothing about it "-Conton said, “If she says I stole it, she tells &
lie"-they were locked up, and I afterwards heard Pollitt say to Conton,
"I wish I had never known you"-I brought them to London, and took
them to Bow-lane station they refused to give any address Conton went
by the name of Conneton.
