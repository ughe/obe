GEORGE TEAKLE. I was with M'Carthy when I took the prisoner—he told him what he took him for—he made no answer—on the way to the station-house, the prosecutor said he had struck him with a hammer—the prisoner heard it—he made no answer—I returned back to the prisoner's house, and found this hammer in the cupboard by the side of the fire—the prisoner did not see it—he said nothing till he was before the magistrate.
   Prisoner's Defence . This prosecutor came home about twelve o'clock—he came to my door, and knocked as if he was going to burst the door in, and kicked it, calling me and my wife all the gravy-eyed b—he could think of—for nearly half an hour that lasted; and at last I opened the door, and asked him what he meant by calling me and my wife such names—he came into my house when I opened the door, and attempted to strike me two or three times, but I escaped from him; and in the house he laid hold of me by the collar, and attempted to strike me on the back, just as I was by the fire-place—I stooped down by the fire-place, and got a little bit of a poker—I told him, if he did not let go of me, and get out of my house, that I should strike him—he would not leave go of me, but dragged me nearly to the door, and told me to come out—I said I would not and if he did not leave go of me I would hit him with the bit of poker I had in my hand—that is all. SARAH RUTTER. I am a neighbour of the prisoner's, and live nearly opposite him. I heard the prosecutor come home, about twenty minutes before one o'clock in the morning—apparently by his voice he was tipsy—his next door neighbour was with him—he made a noise and awoke us—the next door neighbour's stairs join mine and I heard him go up-stairs—it was not a minute after that I heard the prosecutor cross the road to the prisoner's door—he commended knocking violently—he said, "Come out, you b—I have just come home in good trim for you"—I heard the prisoner's door open and shut quickly again—I heard the prosecutor say, "You cowardly b—, you have cut my head with a hammer"—the pro secutor's wife by that time came down, and said to the prisoner, "Come out, you coward, and stand before me"—she said she would have his life—the prosecutor and his wife then cried "Police," and an Irish policeman, (I know by his tongue,) came up—he asked the policeman to take charge of the prisoner, and he said he could not, as he did not see the blow—the prosecutor said, "What! can't you take charge of him? "then I am to be murdered because you did not see it done?"—he said, "Then I'm b—if you do your duty"—by that time the policeman and the prosecutor went away—the wife desired him to go to a doctor and get the wound dressed—I think it was about twenty minutes or a quarter to two o'clock when I heard them come back again—I heard nothing then till I saw the prisoner brought thought his next door neighbour's house—that is all know—I believe the prisoner struck the prosecutor, but in his own defence—he never went out of his own door by what I saw—I saw the light shine through my binds, and the door shut again directly.
