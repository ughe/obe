2. JOHN WINSHIPP) was convicted of affaulting C. Lowther, Eſq;
in his Coach, between St. Pancras Church, and the Halfway Houſe, on
the 25th of May laſt, and taking from him a ſilver Watch, and a Purſe
with 10 Guineas.
He ſaid he was 22 Years of Age, Born in the Pariſh of St. Paul Cosent
Garden; was put Apprentice to a Carpenter, but was of too roving a
Mind to follow that Buſineſs, but liv'd as he could, and ſometimes drove
Coaches in the Streets for a Maintenance.
He alſo ſaid, that tho' he was ſo very young in an ill Courſe of Life,
he could blame no Perſon for ſeducing or leading him aſide, tho' the
Gangs of Higkwaymen he aſſociated with, always courted and careſs’d
him for his uncommon Agility and Activity of Body, which generally
made him the principal Man among them.
He ſaid that he was indeed very deſirous of Life and would willingly
go to any part of the World even for the whole remainder of his Days.
to avoid the shameful and ignominious Death of a Brute-Beaſt: But ſaid,
that this was not ſo greatly his Deſire for his own Sake, as on the account
of an aged Mother he had, whoſe Grief would end her Days (he being
her only Child) if he caine to ſuch a diſmal End.
He deny'd that in all bis Robberies, which he own'd had been very
numerous for one of his Age, he had never committed Murther : For
which Reaſon he hoped, tho' he had been guilty of all other Sins, God
would graciouſly pardon hiin.
When he was ask'd if he rob'd a Perſon in a Chaiſe during Divine Ser-
rvice, who was watering his Horſe before the Church Door? He ſaid, he
had no Buſineſs to confeſs any thing to any Man; but he ſuppos'd that
Action was the fame wherever it was done, and at what time foever.
He would by no means own he was guilty of the Robbery he was
convicted of; ſaying he would not diſcover any Robbers, becauſe no one
ſhould be made Evidence againſt the reſt, for that he ſaid, was what
took away his Life. But it was no wonder that he ſhould deny his
Crime, becauſe he declared when firſt Condemn'd, that he would confeſs
nothing, nor had any one any Buſineſs to ask hiin any Queſtions. der
Being ask'd why he refus'd to go over to Africa, as he had engaged ;
he affirind that 'twas not he, but one Downing, reprieved at the fame
time, who receiv'd 50 l. of the Duke of Chandois, on condition of going
over to the African Settlements. But he ſaid he offer'd his Grace to go, if
he would pleaſe to procure him any ſmall Commiſſion; he added, he had
no occaſion to go otherways, having an Uncle in Kingston in Jamaica,

Pirate Veſſels with his Privateer Ships, raiſed his Fortune to 2000 l. and
