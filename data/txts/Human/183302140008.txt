it did not matter which side - I was to get the flat side of the counting-house key and press it level with the soap; I was to wet the key before I pressed it to the soap, and I was to hold the key up afterwards, to see that no soap was sticking in the wards - I received these directions from both Coney and Smith in the passage; I took the box of soap, and had it in my pocket - I left them, and came home; my father was searching my pockets on the following Monday for a key, and saw the box of soap in my pocket - I had at that time got the impression on the soap; the lid was on the box - my father saw it, and tried to take it away from me, but I was quick and put it into another pocket, and I ran off to Coney's house in, Red Lion-street, and saw Coney, who was cutting wood; I told him the box had been seen - he said he hoped nothing was suspected, and while I was there he lent me a book called the Forget Me Not; this is the book, to the best of my knowledge - it had a cover exactly like this; Coney said they only wanted 20l. - my father had no opportunity of examining the impression of the key: this was at nine o'clock in the morning - I was to call again at eleven o'clock (this was the Monday before the Sunday of the robbery); I came home then, and lent the Forget Me Not to my mother - I went to Coney's again about eleven o'clock, and found Coney and two or three more men there; Smith was there - I now know a person named Cook, and another named Pearce, and there was a tall man who I do not know the name of; they were the three persons who were there; I should know them all three again - when I returned at eleven o'clock, Coney was filing a key in the vice; (looking at a vice) it was about this size, but not so rusty - the box of soap was on a window ledge by him, with the impression on it, and Pearce was making a little cart of the top of the soap box; he put this little axletree to it, and I saw him put these two wheels in, and saw him cut two other pieces to fasten the wheels to - he made the cart, tied a string to it, and told me to give it to my little brother, so that my father might see it, and be deceived, that he should not suspect any thing was in the box which he had seen; Coney and Pearce together told me that; I waited until Coney had finished the key, and after he had finished it, he put it in the fire, and then gave it to me to try if it would fit - putting it in the fire took away the brightness; I took it home, and had no opportunity of trying it until the Sunday night of the robbery - I delivered it to Smith, as I could not try it; he had it in his possession to prevent my father seeing it - he had it while I went in to dinner on Tuesday, and returned it to me again, but I had no opportunity of trying it; on the Friday before the robbery, I went to Coney's house - Coney then said, that he and another would call at Mr. Wheeler's with a job to mend, to see the situation of the premises; I was to meet him at the White Horse, Gilbert-street, Clare-market, on the Saturday afternoon - I went, and Smith with me; there was Pearce, Cook, and the tall man, and several others there, and Coney came about half an hour afterwards; I was talking with them, and when Coney came he asked me to bring the dog to Red Lion-street the next afternoon; I said several times to them on that Saturday, that they had better leave it alone, because I was afraid my father would be suspected - Coney said they would leave all their tools there, to wipe off the suspicion from my father, and to show that it was done by the profession; on the Saturday, Cook said, if I ever split, he would muzzle me (both the prisoners were present) - Pearce said if I did not go on with it, he would scrag me, and the tall man said he would take me before a Magistrate if I did not proceed with it; I am certain both the prisoners were present then - Coney said the tall man was to watch at the kitchen door, to see that the servant did not awake; Coney wanted me to poison the dog - I said I would not, because it was a favourite dog, and one we had hed by us for many years; when I would not-poison it, he asked me to bring it to the house on Sunday; I went home on Saturday, and was to come between five and six o'clock on Sunday with the dog - I went with it between five and six o'clock, and found Coney at home, and in a little time Cook, Pearce, and the tall man came and Smith came in - they saw the dog, and one of them remarked, "Have you got the barker?" and Coney said, "We have him all right;" the dog was running about there until the men came in - Coney then told me to come with him; I went with him through a yard into a shed, and he tied the dog there with a string to a post in a shed in the yard - we then all drank tea together; after tea I was sent for 3d. of sperm oil in a tea cup - I went for it to Cow-cross; I believe Mrs. Count was the woman I went to, but I cannot be certain - this is the sort of tea cup Coney gave me to fetch the oil in; I brought it back to him, and he then put some into a dark lantern, some into a phial, and some remained in the tea cup (looking at a dark lantern) - this is the lantern; I cannot speak to the phial - Coney said "I have got some laudanum, could not you contrive to put some into the servant's and your father's beer;" he said this in the presence of all the rest - I refused, and he said, I was a great fool, and Mrs. Coney said I had better not; after tea, Coney brought out all the housebreaking implements - Pearce, Cook, and all were present at the time (looking at the articles produced); there was a piece of whipcord like this on the table - I think this is the piece and the bag of keys; they were shook out of the bag by Pearce, shown to me, and put in again; I looked them all over in my hand, and then they were put into the bag again - it was such a bag as that; this centrebit I certainly saw, and the stock-handle - the bits were in this corderoy case, as they are now; a little before that, Coney was mending a pair of worsted socks - I asked him what they were for - he said they were to put on over his shoes, to prevent his making any noise - this is one of the socks, and the other was like this - they were odd ones; I saw them at the examination - they are the same as I had seen at Coney's, to the best of my knowledge; there were four or five oranges on the table, one of which was given to me, and I eat it; I asked what they were going to take the oranges with them for - they said they had got bad coughs - after tea on Sunday, Coney said he had been to Mr. Wheeler's, and seen a young man, and he asked me what was in the drawers in the chest opposite, and I told him all the silver;
