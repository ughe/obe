SAMUEL PLANK. I am an officer. On the 12th of August the prosecutor applied to me. I went to Knights-bridge barracks for Bellamy, and the moment the prosecutor saw him, he said he was the man. I asked him what he had done with the watch and money? He said he knew nothing of either. I asked him where Collis was? He said he knew nothing of her. There was a general inquiry all over the barracks for the watch, but I could gain no information; the other soldier belonged to the same regiment. I took Bellamy away, and about a week after the prosecutor stopped Collis herself, one night. I asked her about the watch, but she denied all knowledge of it or the prosecutor. After that she told me the watch was in pledge, that Plant had the seals, and Brown had the duplicate. I applied to him for it and he gave it to me - the seals have not been found.
 (Watch produced and sworn to.)
 COLLIS'S Defence. He went home with me, and said he had no money; Bellamy came up. He was very much in liquor, and said he had nothing but bad notes, and asked Bellamy to buy them. He gave me the watch, and said he had had a good fight to get it, for he had robbed a man at Bayswater, and it was half in and half out of his fob.
 BELLAMY -    GUILTY. Aged 30.
 COLLIS -    GUILTY. Aged 27. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1361.  JAMES CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , three sheets, value 16 s.; one apron, value 6 d,; three frocks, value 10 s., and one pair of stockings, value 6 d., the property of William Cleghorn.
   WILLIAM CLEGHORN. I keep a potatoe warehouse, and had taken the prisoner into my service from the workhouse. I missed this property at different times, and sent for Van, who found the apron concealed in his breeches - he said he had pledged the sheets. I found them at the pawnbroker's.
   JAMES WALLER. I am shopman to Mrs. Fothergill, who is a pawnbroker. On the 9th of July a sheet was pledged with me in the name of Clark.
 THOMAS MILLER. I am a pawnbroker. In July a sheet was pledged with me.
   THOMAS VAN. I am a constable. On the 4th of August I apprehended the prisoner, and found an apron in his breeches. He told me where he had pledged the things.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I am sorry for it.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Six Months.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1362.  SARAH FORSE was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , one hook, value 1 d.; 2 lbs. of pork, value 2 s., and 1 lb. of sausage, value 1 s., the property of Robert Dolphin.
   DAVID DOLPHIN. I am the son of Robert Dolphin, who is a pork-butcher, and lives in Marchmont-street. On the 14th of July, in the afternoon, the prisoner came and asked for two ounces of beef and two ounces of pork. She asked to look at a German sausage; it was put on the window-board; she then told me to fetch the meat. I turned round, she ran out, I followed her, and she gave up the meat and sausage. I took her to the watch-house.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in distress - my husband has deserted me, and I have three children to support.
     GUILTY. Fined One Shilling, and Discharged.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1363.  ELIZABETH FAIRBROTHER and HENRY COOK were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , three pieces of silver coin, called sixpences, the monies of William Newell Ellison , from his person.
   WILLIAM NEWELL ELLISON. I keep the Royal Oak, public-house, Seven Dials; I have known Fairbrother for a year. On Sunday morning, the 18th of July, about ten minutes after twelve o'clock, there was a violent knocking at the door - the house was closed. I knew Fairbrother's voice, and told her it was shut up for the night. About ten minutes after I heard my shutters being forced, and on going out, I found her forcing them; the moment I went out, she seized me, and in less than a minute afterward Cook came and seized me - he is the patrol; while he held me the woman got her hand into my pocket. I told him to take her into custody. He said,
 "No, I will take you to the watch-house." I said,
 "She is robbing me, and you are holding me the while." He said,
 "D - n you, you have nothing to lose," and immediately struck me across the lip with his stick. She dropped the money, and stooped down to pick it up - Cook had seized me by the throat, and almost strangled me. A person, named Emmet, came up, and said,
 "Why are you treating the man so ill?" Cook laid hold of him, and said, I shall take you likewise to the watch-house, which I believe he did. I went in, and shut the door at the request of the other watchman.
 COURT. Q. Did you give charge of them to that watchman - A. He was on the other side of the way; I mentioned it to him, and told him that I had been robbed.
 Q. Did you give them in charge - A. No, I was beaten so much, that I hardly knew what I was about. There was a great mob about the place.
 Q. Why not give them in charge - A. The watchman did not pay attention to me - they did not give me in charge. On Monday, Emmet called upon me to go before the magistrate, which I did, and told him that I had been robbed. The man swore that I knocked him down.
 Q. Did he indict you for it - A. Yes.
 Q. Then you prefered this indictment - A. This indictment was prefered before.
 Q. When - A. On the first day.
