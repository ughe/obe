NEW COURT.-Tuesday, June 22, 1847.
PRESENT—Mr. Alderman Moon; and EDWARD BULLOCK, Esq.
Sixth Jury, before Edward Bullock, Esq.
1511. JOHN CHARLISH was indicted for feloniously cutting and
wounding Edward Jarvis on the head, with intent to do him some grievous
bodily harm.
MR. BALDWIN conducted the Prosecution.
EDWARD Jarvis. I am a smith and farrier, in the employ of Mr. Jones,
in Great Leonard-street, Shoreditch. The prisoner worked there for about
fourteen months-he left on the Saturday evening before Easter Sunday-he
came to the shop on Monday, the 31st of May, about half-past six o'clock in
the evening-I was working there--he had left some tools there-he came to
fetch them away-I think he had had some beer-he was there a quarter of
an hour before I spoke to him-I was at work at the forge making a shoe
for a gentleman-I was half an hour about it, and he kept on blackguarding
me-I told him to be quiet and hold his tongue-I had done that shoe and
then I went to do another little job-he came up to me and, “Jarvis, you
are a d-d thief, and a rogue"-I said, “If you call me that again, I will
give you in charge"-I pushed him from me, and told him to go away, and
come when Mr. Jones was at home at that time he had got his tools in his
apron, and he said, “You are Mr. Jones's foreman, look at what tools I have
got"-I looked, and saw a hammer which did not belong to the prisoner-it
had been there two years before he came-he had put a handle in this ham-
mer, and was going to take it away-I told him it was not his-he said, "If
it is Mr. Jones's hammer, it is my handle, and I will saw it oft"-some time
after I went to put my hand to him to take the hammer, and I said, "You
go away" he said, “I will go when I like" -I went to put my hand to
it, and he took up the hammer and bit me twice on the head with it-I bled
a great deal, and was taken to the surgeon- I was in bed from Monday till
Thursday, and did not leave my room till Saturday-I had not struck the
prisoner before I received those blows.
Cross-eramined by MR. PARNELL. Q. The prisoner was a little in drink when
de came? A. I do not know whether he was- I told him he was-I thought
De was, by his appearance I was thinking he was, but he told me he knew his
own business-he did not speak to me for a quarter of an hour-he stood in
the shop and spoke to the other men--the first thing he said to me was, that he
was going to take his tools--- I told him afterwards that I should fetch a police-
