747. ROBERT JAMES HALL (42), Burglariously breaking and entering!
the dwelling-house of George Williams, and stealing therein 38., his money.
ELIZABETH WILLIAMS. I am the wife of George Williams, who keeps the
Horns Tavern, Rotherhithe-on 16th August, about 4 a.m., the police
knocked at our door, I went down and found the front parlour window, about
4 or 5 inches open, which I closed the night before about 12 o'clock, 38.
had been taken from a little shelf behind the bar, not in the same room
where the window was open--the prisoner is a waterman plying at the Horns
Stairs-he is a custumer of mine, and had been at the house that night till
closing time.
Cross-examined by MR. FULTON. He has been a customer about two or
three years-be is a licensed waterman.
JAMES MAGUIRE (Policeman R 392). On the night of 15th August, I was
on my beat at the Horns Stairs, and saw the prisoner, at several intervals
from 11 till 4 o'clock in the morning-at 4 o'clock I did not find him at the
stairs- I went to examine the Horns tavern and found the window about
3 or 4 inches up; with the light of my bull's eye I saw the prisoner stand-
ng on the parlour floor agrinst a table with his arms folded towards the
wall-I recognised him as the man I had seen on the stairs-a lamplighter
came past and I borrowed his stick and told him to stand at the window
while I rapped at the door-when Mr. Williams came down I went into the
house-I saw the prisoner come round to the back door wbile I was at the
front door-be went over the fence down on to the water, I took a boat
directly and went over the water to Limebouse, but could not see anything
of the prisoner there, the other boat was about halfway across before I
started - I did not see what became of the prisoner; I rowed back again-
before I started in pursuit I saw the waterman Butcher there, I had not
seen the prisoner before 11 o'clock that night, I did not know him or his
name.
