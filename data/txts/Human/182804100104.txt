 1009.  JOHN HALL and THOMAS GRAY were indicted for stealing on the 22d of March , 1 silver watch, value 25s.; 1 metal key, value 2d.; and 1 metal seal, value 4d., the goods of Joseph Mayes , from his person.
 JOHN MAYES. I am a  watchman, of Piccadilly. On the 2d of March I was at the ruins of the Brunswick Theatre, with my wife and son, between twelve and one o'clock in the day - the crowd pressed very heavily on me, and I found my right arm confined to my side; it was done intentionally; I missed my watch from my fob as soon as my arm was at liberty - Gray was as close to my right side as he could be; I did not notice Hall till afterwards; they were both taken into custody while I was there: I am certain my watch was safe when I entered the crowd.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was there not a moving crowd there? A. A gig came by which made them move.
   GEORGE WADDINGTON. I was at the Brunswick Theatre; there was a great rush; I heard the prosecutor call out, "Don't hurt my wife;" I saw Gray had got hold of the prosecutor's coat on his right hand side, and his other hand was under the coat: Hall pushed against him close to the prosecutor, and in a moment Newton called out"He has got it" - I looked and saw the watch fall; it dropped as if from Gray, but I did not see it in any one's hand; there was a crowd at the time, in consequence of some vehicle going by.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. There was a rush backwards and forwards? A. Yes; we were all pushed against the prosecutor.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Is it remarkable for a man to lay hold of another, to save himself from falling? A. He had hold of his coat, and the prosecutor called out, "Don't hurt my wife."
   JAMES NEWTON. I was with Waddington, and saw Hall with his hands in a direction towards the prosecutor; he gave Gray something, which he put into his right-hand trousers pocket - Hall was in front of him, and Gray close at his right-hand side; I collared Gray directly; he put his hand into his pocket, and took something out, and the pocket with it; and in struggling I saw this watch in the kennel, between his legs; I did not see it fall - it was something small that Hall gave Gray.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you hear Waddington examined? A. Yes; we were all pushed together; Hall was pressed violently against the prosecutor- he could not prevent it; there were above a hundred persons there: Hall was hardly master of his hands.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. And yet you saw Gray put something into his pocket, and take it out again? A. Yes - there were several people pushing against us; I did not hear the watch fall; I first saw it on the ground; Gray had not been pushed about at that time- I kept him pretty steady.
   WILLIAM McNAB. I went to the Theatre, and in two or three minutes there was a strong rush; Newton sang out, "George, he has got it;" I looked round, and saw the watch falling close to Gray, just by his feet; I turned round, and saw Hall on my right. I did not see the prosecutor till next day.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. What are you? A. A journeyman baker; I saw the watch falling. I assisted to secure Gray.
   PHILIP WAINWRIGHT. I am an engraver. I was present, and saw a rush; I met Waddington, and saw Hall going through the crowd; I saw Gray putting something into his pocket, and heard Newton say, "George, he has got it;" I had seen Hall do nothing but press close to the prosecutor - his hands were towards him; I looked down, and saw the watch near the gutter. Waddington took Gray; I assisted to take Hall - I saw Newton pick the watch up.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Would it not be natural for Hall to put out his hands when there was a rush? A. Yes.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. You did not see Gray's hand on the prosecutor? A. No.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 GRAY'S Defence. I am innocent.
 GRAY -    GUILTY. Aged 23. Transported for Seven Years.
 HALL -    NOT GUILTY.
 1010.  STEPHEN CABLE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of March , 2 seals, value 20s.; 1 watch-key, value 6s., and 1 ring, value 4s., the goods of Samuel Tyso.
 SAMUEL TYSO. I am a  groom, and live with Mr. Lyons, in Holborn. On the evening of the 5th of March I was at the Green Man, public-house, in Bond-street; the prisoner, who is an acquaintance of mine, came in; we went home together to my lodging in Oxford-street- we slept together, and the next morning the prisoner got up, and went out; I missed my seals and key about half an hour after he was gone; I found him on the evening of the 7th of March, and asked him about my seals - he said he had never touched them: we went together to the Green Man, and there I gave charge of him; I saw the seals found in his watch pocket, under his own watch; they were not on his watch-chain.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Were you not both servants out of place? A. Yes; I got into a squabble with some girls, and went to the watch-house; the prisoner said he had advanced 2s. for me. After the seals were found he said he took them merely out of a frolic; I have known him five or six months - he has been an honest good lad.
   JOHN LACY. I am a beadle. I took the prisoner, and said to him, "If you know anything of these seals, give them up;" he said he knew nothing about them; I searched him, and found them in his fob.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined Two Months.
