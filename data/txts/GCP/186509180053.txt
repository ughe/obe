JOHN ALLENSON. I am a surgeon of No. 1, Norton Folgate-about half-
past six, on the evening of 28th August, I was called to No. 6, Little Pearl-
street, Spitalfields—I went to the front room, first floor-the door was
fastened, how I do not know-I pushed it open and wept in, and found a
child lying on the bed it was apparently about eighteen daysold—its face was
covered over with a coverlid-it was apparently in a dying state-there was
no one else in the room--the only sign of life I saw abont it was wheu I
took hold of its band, there was a slight contration of the fingers-it bad
a night-dress on-I remained in the room from five to ten minutes when
I left it was still alive-I did not leave until I saw the parents, some one
went for them- I saw the male prisoner come in ; he was the worse for liquor
-I asked him if any medical han had been in attendance on the child-
he said, “No, not since the day after its birth "— I told him I could not give
him a certificate of the cause of death, the child was in a dying state, I
could do nothing for it, I heard some one muttering behind me, and he
said, "I beg your pardon, sir, take no notice of her, she is the worse for
drink" - I supposed it to be his wife, but I did not turn round to look-I
then left-on the following afternoon I received an order from the coroner,
and on the Wednesday morning, the 30th, I went to the same room about
half-past seven o'clock-I there found the same child lying dead ; the two
prisoners were also in the room and three other childreu- desired them to
leave while I made the examination--they did so-the male prisoner re-
