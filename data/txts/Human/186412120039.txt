blood on each side of the workshop, particularly at one end—the marks were like drops of blood as if they had spurted.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I suppose you know that young men in working establishments are in the habit of larking , and giving one another a slap in the face occasionally? A.  The deceased was not—he was very quiet—the further end of the workshop, where I saw traces of blood, is not near the washstand, but at this end where "coke" is marked on the plan—the wash hand room is in the lower floor at the further end, to the right of the door as you enter, near the coke bin, where the blood was in the corner—the first thing that attracted my attention was not as of some persons disputing—I did not hear Miss French make use of that terra—I heard a loud groan—I got up immediately on hearing the noise, perhaps in a few seconds, not more—there were no traces of blood in the upper floor—it appeared to be traced from the corner where the coke bin is, to the door near which the deceased was standing—there appeared to be several pieces of mortar knocked off, perhaps seven or eight—there is one step into the coke bin—the gas-light was about in the centre between the coke bin and the door—it would afford light to the place where I saw the most remote appearance of blood—there is a kind of bench by the window—it is used for laying tools and things on, and working on—there was a pair of iron tongs on it, I think, nothing else—the melting is done in the basement—there would be several instruments there.
