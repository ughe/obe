1741. MARY ANN FLEMING, ELIZABETH SIMMONDS, and
SARAH LEVY, were indicted for assaulting James Greig, putting him in
fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 sovereign, and 3
half-crowns, his monies.
JAMES GREig. I live in Bloomsbury-street, and am a seaman. On the
night of the 19th of Aug., I met Fleming about twelve o'clock- I went
to a house with her in Princes-court, near Covent-garden-I gave her 5.
-by-and-by she denied having received 58., and said I only gave her
half-a-crown-she wanted some more money, and I would not give her
any-I had had nothing to do with her-I was quite sober-I wanted to
get out-she prevented me, and called, "Mary"-the prisoner Levy came
up and asked what was the matter- I told her how it was, and she in-
sisted upon my giving a sovereign to Fleming, and wanted some money
for herself-I would not give anything to either of them—Simmonds then
came up, and they threatened to scratch my face, and tear my clothes--
they swore they would do it, and tear all the skin off my face-Simmonds
then took up a pair of snuffers, and said she would dig them into my
brains-that I might get out, I gave Fleming a sovereign, but before that
Fleming and Simmonds had struck me with their fists, and Levy had
scratched my face-I gave Fleming the sovereign after they struck me-I
then tried to get out-Simmonds prevented me, and said I must give
her some money too-I said, "What for ?"- she said I had torn her frock
--I had never touched it-I had bad no struggle--the door was fastened,
