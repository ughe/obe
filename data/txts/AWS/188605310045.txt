JOHN BELL. I am a clerk in the Foreign Branch of the General Post-
office-on 8th April last I was clerk in charge of the Continental mails
from Cannon Street to Dover-I was at Cannon Street to see the mails
put into the train; it was my duty to see that the French and German
mnails were Separated from each other--the Cannon Street portion of the
train was first made up; it consisted of the engine, guard's break, two
sorting carriages, and a tender, a luggage van, and a first-olass carriage
-I cannot say how many compartments there were in that-the sorting
carriages and the tender were in communication with each other, and all
separate from the luggage van-the Ostend mails were put in the luggage
van-the Berlin bag would go via Ostend- went in charge of the mails
to Dover-I move about the sorting carriages from one to the other-
there were nine sorters there and a porter and myself-when the mails
were put in the van I saw the van door elosed and bolted; it was fastened
by an iron pin let through a staplethis is a correet drawing of the van

seen inside; there was no glass, it is all wood-no one could have left

Robert Hambley to the man who takes charge of them from the sorting
carriage to the Ostend boat-he is assisted by sailors from the boat, but
he is responsible for them--I saw that done- had finished then.
