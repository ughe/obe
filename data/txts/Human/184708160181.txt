THOMAS HOWARD. I live in Crown-street, Westminster. Mr. Munn gave me some money—I went to the shop and paid the prisoner four half-crowns—this is one of the half-crowns I paid him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time was this? A.  A quarter-past five o'clock—I bought 3lbs. of tea—I paid the prisoner four half-crowns and three shil lings—they were all marked—I saw no one in the shop but the prisoner, who served me with the goods. HENRY SMITH( policeman A  28.) I went to the house, No 147, Edg ware-road—I saw the prisoner, and told him I was a police-officer, and was going to take him into custody for stealing a half-crown from the till—he said, "Search me and welcome, I have not got it"—I searched him, but did not find it—I went up stairs, and asked which was his box—he told me—I did not find the half-crown there—I went down and asked the woman there, in the prisoner's presence, if he had been up stairs—he said he had not—the woman said he had—he then said he had been to wash his hands—I went up again, and then found this half-crown in his box—he said, "It is my first time, forgive me."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he not say he had never been changed with any thing before? A.  No—when I went up the first time and searched his box I found nothing—that was about half-past five o'clock—the prisoner went up with me, I searched the box in his presence—I took him down again to the bottom of the stairs—I then went up again and found this half-crown amongst some rubbish in a paper bag—the box was unlocked—there were two other beds in that room, and there were other boxes—I took all the things out of the prisoner's box the first time, but I did not examine the little bag so closely as I did the second time—there was another young man, who came out from his tea into the shop. WILLIAM CLEW( police-inspector. ) This is Mr. Long's handwriting to this deposition—( read )—the prisoner says, "It is the first time I ever attempted such a thing; I have a wife dependent on me."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you present when the prisoner was examined? A.  No, I know nothing about the case. GUILTY . Aged 30.— Confined One Year .
 1916. JOHN POLISHER and JOHN PHILLIPS were indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  .; the goods of John Bond Haydon , form his person; Phillips having been before convicted of felony.
   MR. WILDE conducted the Prosecution. JOSEPH DALTON( City policeman  366.) On the 6th of Aug. I was in Cheapside in plain clothes—I saw the prisoners, and, knowing them, I followed them to Holborn-hill—they stopped near Chancery-lane, where there was a crowd—I saw the prosecutor—the prisoners went near him, and I saw Polisher put his hand into his pocket, and take out this handkerchief—the prisoners then walked away—I told the prosecutor his pocket was picked—I took the prisoners to the station, and took this handkerchief out of Polisher's pocket.
