Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Had you known Francis before this? A. Yes; he had been groom to Mr. Mills for five or six months - I have not heard him say that he had been in the habit of making loans of hay to the cabman's servant - Gisson said he came to borrow two trusses of hay - I know that persons who drive cabriolets come in at all hours - Gisson left his cabriolet on the stand, and came there.
   THOMAS BOYCE(police-constable S 159). I was called in - I recollect Gisson saying he had come to borrow two trusses of hay, and Francis said, "I have no knowledge of him."
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you state before the magistrate that Francis said he had no knowledge of Gisson? A. Yes.
 JOHN MILLS. I live at No. 14,  Euston-square; the stable belongs to me. I was shown the two trusses of hay, and on comparing them with what I had I believe it to be the same - I gave no authority to Francis, who is my groom, to part with any.
 Cross-examined. Q. But I dare say it may have happened that you have lent and borrowed hay? A. I would lend it to any gentleman in the mews, but not to a cabman- I had desired Francis not to have any thing to do with these two men - he had given me satisfaction.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1101.  WILLIAM GEE was indicted for embezzlement.
   JOB ASHTON. I live in Bridge-place, City-road-basin- I have known the prisoner five or six years - I met him on the morning of the 29th of November last - he said he was very much distressed, and asked if I could employ him - I did, and sent him the next morning with two bushels of malt, and 8lbs. of hops - he never returned nor paid the money.
   JAMES CHILD. On the 30th of November, the prisoner brought me two bushels of malt, and 8lbs. of hops - he brought the bill of 26s., and my wife paid him. GUILTY. Aged 36. - Recommended to mercy. Confined for Three Months.
 1102.  MARY BARNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of May , 4 shirts, value 6s.; and 2 pair of trowsers, value 8s., the goods of Mary Webb.
 MARY WEBB. I am  single. I do slop work- the prisoner lodged with me about four months - I received some trowsers and shirts to make for Mr. Allows - I made the trowsers, and the prisoner made the shirts - I am responsible for them all - on the 27th of May, she said she was going to take the shirts home to Mr. Allows, and I said she might.
 Prisoner. You know we used to pawn the work every Saturday. Witness. Never in my life.
   OLMENA ALLOWS. I employed the prosecutrix to make these articles - the prisoner did not bring them home on the 27th of May - I got this one shirt at a pawnbroker's in the Commercial-road - I paid 9d. for it.
 MARY WEBB. This is one of the shirts.
   JAMES HENRY ANDREWS(police-constable K 104). I took the prisoner - she was very much in liquor - I was about to search her - she said it was no use, for she had torn up the duplicates - this one shirt was found by going to different pawnbrokers.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Confined for Three Months.
 1103.  GEORGE PERRY was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 2s. 6d., the goods of William Kirby , from his person.
 WILLIAM KIRBY. I am an  embosser. On the 25th of June I was in the City-road, about eleven or twelve o'clock in the forenoon - I was looking into a shop window, and, on turning, I saw two young men by my side - I suspected them, and felt for my handkerchief - it was not in my pocket- I looked in my hat, and it was not there - I went on, and they were before me - when they came to James-street, one of them, who was the prisoner, ran off - I pursued, and cried, "Stop thief!" - I picked up my handkerchief in the track he ran - I pursued him to Old-street, and he was taken - there was no one but the prisoner and I in the street, when I picket up my handkerchief.
   THOMAS BILBOROUGH(police-constable G 140). I took the prisoner - he said he got the handkerchief from another boy, but did not take it himself.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going along, and a boy came by in great speed - I stepped into the road - a gentleman pursued me - I said, "There goes the lad" - he would not go after him, but took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1104.  ELIZABETH PINKERTON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of June , 1 watch, value 3l.; 1 watch-chain, value 1s.; 1 seal, value 6d.; and 1 key, value 6d., the goods of John Burt , from the person of William Punchard.
 JOHN BURT. I am a  seaman. I met William Punchard on the 10th of June, at the Ferry-house, in the Isle-of-dogs - I gave him my watch to take care of - I was going over to Greenwich - it was an old-fashioned silver watch, and the maker's name was Brown - it had a steel chain, a seal, and a key.
 WILLIAM PUNCHARD. I received the watch, and as I was returning home that night, about half-past ten, I met the prisoner and another woman - they asked me to go with them - I went to a house in  Limehouse-hole- I went to an up-stairs room with both the women - I laid on the bed, and went to sleep - I had given them four shillings between the two - I awoke about two o'clock, and missed the watch which had been in my fob - I had pulled my jacket, off, but my trowsers were on - I opened the window, and sung-out for the police - the prisoner was gone - the other woman was there - she was taken, but discharged.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Your friend asked you to take care of his watch? A. Yes; I had been drinking a little when I went with the women; but I was not drunk - I had been in one public-house, and stayed about half-an-hour with a young man who is now just gone in to Sydney - after I left the public-house, I had to call at a doctor's - I had the watch safe then - that was about one hundred yards before I met the women - the prisoner laid on the bed; the other woman on the floor - I had been in the house about half an hour when I fell asleep.
