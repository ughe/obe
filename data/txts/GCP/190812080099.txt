ELIZABETH RICHARDS, widow, nurse and housekeeper to Mrs. Kath-
leun Carlton, Delaware Mansions. I have been with Mrs. Carlton
from October 30, 1908. I saw prisoner every day during the week
ending November 7. I thought he was very strange in his manners;
he did not seem to rest in either of the rooms and worried me very
much about the food, asking me to cook it when it was not time to
cook it. He always took his meals at the flat. On Friday, November
6, his sister was out. He came into the kitchen in the afternoon and
said to me, "Will you get little Miss Kathleen ready for Ireland
dress her.” I said, "No, sir, I could not do that as the mistress is out
and I must not dress her before she comes home." Then he said,
* Would you get the dinner ready at oncel" I said, “It is not time
yet-I have got to fetch it first. He asked me to give the child a
little bit of cake, so I said, "She does not want it now." He insisted
upon having the little child sitting upon a chair. She was very good
but he said she was very naughty. On the Friday night when I took
his supper in he stood with a revolver pointing it at the pictures, so
I said, "What are you doing?" He said, "Don't talk, don't talk, I
am worried." He did not seem to realise that his supper was on the
table. When his sister came home she was in a dead faint. I said
to prisoner, "Do help me to get her out of the faint." He went on
whistling. He never seemed to understand that she was in a faint. I
said, “Do not whistle, the mistress's head is very bad.” When he was
pointing the pistol I said, "Don't be silly, your sister will soon get
better." He kept going in the bedroom, and worrying his sister whilo
she was so ill he did not seem to realise she was ill; he kept going
in and out of the different rooms as if he did not know what he was
doing. He sent me out for 4d, worth of bitter and insisted on my
baving a glass. On Saturday morning, November 7, he came and
brought the milk into the kitchen, whistling. I said, “Don't make &
noise, I have been up half the night with your sister. She has been
so ill." He said, "Get my tea and breakfast at once." I said, "You!
must allow the kettle to boil; you must give me time." I got his
breakfast all right but be sto very little, he kept walking about, just
ate a little of the egg and that was all.
