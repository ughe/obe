JOHN TOTTINGHAM (City-policeman, 173). I apprehended the prisoners
I asked Wilson where he bad got the paroel from he said, "From the
City"-I said, “Where in the City he said, "Old-change "-on going
down Redcross-street, he said, “You must take the parcel "-I searched
the prisoner Wilson, and found a comb, a knife, a purse, and 7 d., not re-
Jating to the charge-Edward Harter, who was with me, assisted to take
Henderson to the station.
The prisoners' statements before the Magistrate being read, they both said :-
"I wish it to be settled here."
Wilson's Defence. I was in the City on 2d May, and as I was going
towards Old-change, looking after a situation, a gentleman asked me to go
to Leaf's and fetch a parcel left for Mr. Jones. I said I did not mind. I
saw him go down Old-change and go into Leaf's, and he came out with
the parcel, and gave me directions to take it. I was going to ask the
constable the way, and he charged me with stealing it. I was going along
Barbican, and the policeman took me I gave him a straightforward ac-
count. When at the station-house they opened and found it was collars,
and they were going to send two men round the City. I said, "I will save
you the trouble ; they came from Leaf's, in Old-change." Going along, I
handed the policeman a bit of paper. That paper was at the station-house,
and when I was at Guildhall I wanted that piece of paper produced, but it
could not be brought up.
JOHN TOTTINGHAM (re-etamined.). I did not take any paper.
Henderson's Defenot. The policeman came and tapped me on the shoulder,
and asked this man what he had got there ; then he said I must come to the
station. I am a stranger to this gentleman. I swear he never saw me
steal these things, and never saw them in my MONOBion.
