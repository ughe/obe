   Q.  Did not he turn you out a considerable time before the prisoners left, and did not you want to fight with them? A.  No such thing—I know Mr. Banham, the undertaker—he came in just before I left—I did not produce any silver before him in the public-house—oh! I dare say I did in the public-house—Benham is a respectable man—he has been there many years, I belive—I persist in saying I did not attempt to beat either of these men in the street—it is no such thing—had not the police have come up, I certainly must have been killed—this man kept kicking me on the head as I held him by the leg. GEORGE FOWLER( police-constable  B 85.) On the morning of the 16th of February my attention was called to a passeage in York-street by a cry of "Murder"—I found the prosecutor there lying on his right side on the ground—he had hold of the prisoner James by the leg—he said he had lost a sovereign and some silver—he said the prisoner James and two others had knocked him down in the passage, and robbed him of a sove reign and some silver—the prosecutor was lying on his right side, with his face covered with blood—he was bleeding a great deal—he had a cut is the eye, and three or four grazes about the forehead, as if he had been kicked apparently—it was the mark a shoe would make, or by knocking his head against the wall—the skin was grazed, and it was bleeding—I told James in the direction of the station-house, and as I was going along the prosecutor pointed out Roberts, who was coming round the corner of Great Chapel-street to meet me—about a hundred and twenty or a hundred and thirty yards from the spot—that was in a direction from Broadway into York-street—he was almost at the corner—not five yards from it—it was in the Broadway, coming to meet me—he was coming on the road as I was coming along—coming up to me, I saw his face towards me—the prosecutor pointed him out, and said, "That is one of the others"—I said to my brother constable, "Go and catch hold of him"—he said, "You know him, you had better go yourself"—as soon as he saw me advance towards him he ran away—I ran after him, and caught him just at the corner of St. Orman's-hill—it might be a hundred or a hundred and fifty yards—when I got nearly to him he stopped and walked, and I told him into custody—he asked me what I wanted; what had he done?—I told him he must go to the station-house, and there he would be satisfied—when I came up and saw the prosecutor on the ground, his watch was of his fob, hanging by a button or guard like a ribbon—I saw a black rib bon attached to it—it was four or five inches out of his fob—I found 2 s .31/4 d . on Roberts, and 11/2 d . on James—I did not take the third man.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you search them accurately? A.  I did—the prosecutor said he had lost a sovereign and some silver—it might be 16 s ., 17 s ., or 18 s ., but he did not know how much—I found no sovereign either of the men.
