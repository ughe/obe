   COURT . Q.  Do you mean that it was both before and after the deed in blank was executed, advances were made to Mr. Constable? A.  No, to Mr. Constable, and to Miss Constable after his death—that the claim of Stanley's executors consisted of advances made both in Mr. Constable's life-time and after his death—I was so told at the time—the last item in this account is Oct. 30, 1838—that was supposed to be after Mr. Constable's death in 1840.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  At the second interview, had this deed of the 14th of Dec. 1841, been prepared? A.  No—at the first interview Miss Constable expressed a wish to convey the surplus of this property to Mr. Blackwell alone, and it stood over for me to see Miss Blackwell, which I did—they were both interested under the will of Mr. Stanley, as executors and devisees—I saw Miss Blackwell in the mean time; and, at the second interview, re ference was made to her being a party to the deed—I have got the memoran dum of what took place at the second interview, with Miss Constable and the prisoner—we further discussed the matter, and I took instructions to prepare the deed—according to Mr. Kemp's, my conveyancer's opinion, in the best way—allusion was made to the sum which ought to have been inserted in the deed in blank, because the interests of some of the parties whose names were in the deed in blank, without sums, had to be attended to in the new deed—1109 l . was the sum mentioned to be inserted in the deed as the sum due to Stanley's executors—( referring to the draft )—that was to be taken as the sum due to his executors, and was to include the sum which ought to have been in the blank deed.
   COURT . Q.  How could that possibly be, when Stanley did not die till six months after Mr. Constable? A.  As I was instructed, there was a sum due to Stanley—I was instructed that there was a debt, part paid in Mr. Consta ble's life-time, to him, and part to Miss Constable, after his death—it was only part of the sum that ought to have been in the blank deed.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Then, having received these instructions with regard to the preparation of this deed, did Miss Constable and the prisoner attend at the time it was executed? A.  They did—that was not on the third occasion—I saw them once before—I saw them on the 13th the third time—the first interview was on the 10th of Dec., the second on the 11th, the third on the 13th, and on the 14th I saw them again—on the 13th I explained again to them the nature of the intended deed.
