NICHOLAS BUTLER( police-constanle P  314.) On Sunday night, or Mon day morning, I was on duty in Camberwell-road at a quarter after one o'clock, and about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's house, I met the prisoner and two others coming in a direction from the prosecutor's house towards London—I observed that the prisoner had no shoes on, and no cap or hat—I took particular notice of him—I carry a light with me and used it—which means beat a constable or somebody—I folowed them about ten yards—they were walking three abreast, very fast—the prisoner and the one nearest to me were somking pipes—I have no doudt about the prosoner—I described him to my fellow-constable in the morning.
   Cross-examined. Q.  They walked pretty freely? A.  They walked very fast, right by me—the prisoner spoke loud—Ihave heard the word malleting  used before in the West of England, and in London as well, and different places, before I was in the police—I was them a bricklayer—it is used among bricklayers as well as others, and means the same thing—I had no grounds for stopping them then. GEORGE ROUS( police-constable P  219.) On the mirning of the 5th of may, about ten road, half-way between Camberwell-gate and the George—I was turinng to go into the square, about mid-way form Southampton-street to where my brother constable was, which was neared the gate, and saw three man pass me in a direction for Londom—I saw one of them without shoes—I did not see his face—I observed his feet when he had passed—I looked after him sufficient to see he was without shoes—I observed his height and genertal appearance, and have no doudt the prisoner is the man—I did not observe his head—he was outside the other two, and about half a step before them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was anybody with you? A.  No—I did not observe that he was talking—I never rememner having seen him before—I did not observe whether he had a hat or cap on—I could hear him slouching  as if without shoes—that induced me to turn my attention to him—he made a different noise to what he would if he had shoes—I could hear the noise—Butler was nearer to London than I was.
