ROBERT BIRD. I live at No. 7, Lower North-street, Chelsea. On Mon-
day night, the 30th of Nov., I was set to mind a pawnbroker's shop, in
Exeter-street, Chelsea- I was outside the prisoner came and asked me to
let him in-it was past the hour that pledges are taken in-he told me to go
in to my master and say, “Here is a man with a saddle"-I carried the mes
sage, and took to the prisoner the answer to go away, as it was too late that
night, and he left-I saw he had a saddle and two cusbions-I saw the corner
of them--they were covered with blue cloth.
Cross-examined. Q. What makes you recollect it was the 30th ? A. I
do not know-it was Monday night I think it was three days before I went
before the Magistrate-it was between seven and eight o'clock-we generally
shut up at nine o'clock, but the private door was shut that night- I had
never seen the prisoner before I know he is the same man, because I saw
him come that night-a great many people come to that door to pawn.
MAURICE MULCAHY (police-sergeant B 2.) I took the prisoner on the
1st of Dec.- I told him what he was charged with—he denied it-he said
what he stated to Organ was out of a lark.
Cross-examined. Q. You have known his father? A. Yes, he has lived
next door to me for two years—he is a respectable man, and works at the
Pantechnicon-I know nothing against the prisoner.
COURT. Q. How far is the pawnbroker's from the stable ? A. 200 or
300 yards.
