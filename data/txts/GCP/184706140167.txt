WILLIAM BROCKING. I am in the service of William Watt, a grocer, of
Park-terrace, Liverpool-road. On the 10th of May the prisoner came to the
shop, he said he was in the service of William Hunt, and asked me to show
him some shoe-brushes I showed him a box containing several-he took
three-he asked me the price-I said, "1s. 4d, each"-he said, “ Put them
down to my governor, you know who I mean"-I said, “You mean Hunt,
across the way ?"-he said, "Yes"--that is Mr. Hunt, a stable-keeper-he
took the brushes away-on the afternoon of the same day he came again, and
brought the brushes back, saying he would exchange them for three others,
as Mr. Hunt supposed the first expense would be the least, and he would
have a better quality-he then asked for a body-brush for the bodies and
insides of carriages -- he asked me the price-I told him they were marked
outside he had four brushes, three in exchange, and one extra-he said be
would exchange them for Hunt, and that his master would come in the course
of the week and settle for them-he took them away with him-he gave me
his name, as Harry Smith-when he came & second time, he said, " It is cus-
tomary for us men to have an invoice, merely to show our employer-I gave
it him-on Friday, the 14th, he came again for a bottle of blacking—he took
up a bottle of blacking, saying, " Add this to the brashes," and took it
away.
