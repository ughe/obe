 PADDEN'S Defence. It is true that I was in the shop, bargaining about the desk, and requested, in case they would take the money, it might be sent home - this was between two and three o'clock; I went down the road, and in twenty minutes I returned - was it likely I should fix myself opposite his shop; Mr. Leather had hardly moved from his shop when the cry of Stop thief! was raised; I was fifty yards off, and could have run directly; I lifted my hat in consequence of being exceeding warm; I rubbed my hands, but it was not a signal to Manley - I know nothing of him.
 MANLEY -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 PADDEN -    NOT GUILTY.
 1535.  RICHARD HART was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of August , 1 plane, value 4s., the goods of Robert Fuller.
 ROBERT FULLER. I left my plane at an unfinished house on the 20th of August - I missed it on the 22d - the prisoner worked for my master; I asked if he had seen it- he said No; I found it at Gideon's, the pawnbroker's, on that day; I told the prisoner I had found it, and asked him to go with me - he said he would - he ran away and I pursued him - an officer was sent for.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not I offer you a book till I got the plane out, and tell you what induced me to take that liberty with it? A. He offered me a book after he tried to give me the slip.
   JAMES GIDEON. I am a pawnbroker. I have a plane pawned by the prisoner on the 21st of August.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM BETTY. I am the officer. I took the prisoner, and found twelve duplicates on him, and one related to this plane - most of the others are owned.
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 45.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1536.  JOHN THOMAS ROBINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of June , 2 promissory notes, value 1l. each, and 2 Bank notes, value 1l. each, the property of James Boston.
 SECOND COUNT stating them to be the property of  Thomas Charles Robinson.
 MESSRS. GURNEY and SHEPHERD conducted the prosecution.
 JAMES BOSTON. I lived at Lane-end, Newcastle, Staffordshire; I was  member of a society called the Loyal Rodneys, which was held at the Cambridge Arms public-house, Upper Brook-street, Grosvenor-square - Mr. Thomas Charles Robinson was the secretary of that society. I directed a letter to be sent to him by my son - I do not know that it was sent on the day it is dated, 25th of December, 1824; but it was sent about that time - I sent in it a 1l. note of Mr. Kinner's, the bank at Newcastle, payable in London - I do not know whether I received an answer to this letter; but I think I must, or I should have been uneasy about it - I afterwards received a letter from Mr. Robinson, and my son wrote a letter in consequence of it, in which I inclosed two local notes of 1l. each, one of them was Mr. Kinner's - I believe.
 These letters were here read: - the first dated 25th of December, 1824, stating that a 1l. note was enclosed, and the second dated 24th of May, 1825, enclosed two 1l. notes. I did not receive any answer to this letter for some time; and I then wrote to the landlord of the house.
 COURT. Q. Who put this letter into the post? A. I did; it was to pay my arrears of 1l. 14s. at the club.
 THOMAS CHARLES ROBINSON. I am  secretary of the benefit society called the Loyal Rodneys; they hold their meetings at the Cambridge Arms, Upper Brook-street; Mr. James Boston was a country member of that society; I received his remittances by post for many years, directed for me at the Cambridge Arms; neither of these two letters ever came to my hand - not having received the first 1l., I wrote a letter in May for his arrears; he was reckoned a defaulter and excluded from the society.
   JOHN HICKLINGTON. I am a stamper of letters at the Post-office; these letters arrived according to their dates, and I stamped them on the days on which they arrived.
   JAMES ST. LEDGER. I am a letter carrier; Upper Brook-street was in my delivery. In May, last year; I took this letter to the Cambridge Arms and inquired for Mr. Robinson; but I found no Mr. Robinson there; the landlord referred me to a house where I should hear of him - I went and found the prisoner at No. 37, Grosvenor-square, where I was directed by a gentleman's butler - I offered him the letter - he opened it in my presence, and it contained two 1l. notes - he read the letter, and said he did not know any thing of the parties, and it was not intended for him - he returned it to me with the notes - he applied to me again the next day, and told me it was intended for him - I told him he must bring me a letter from the same place, with the same hand-writing to satisfy me, as it contained property; he said he had one, and he showed me the first letter, which appeared to come from the same person, and to be the same hand-writing - this was on the third day after I made the application to him; I then gave him the letter and the notes - he paid me 10d.; 1s. 8d. had been paid in the country, it being a treble letter; I afterwards, in July, in consequence of some inquiry being made, went with Mr. Woods, the deputy-inspector, to the prisoner at No. 17, Northumberland-street, Mary-le-bone, when Mr. Woods asked if he had not the letters; he produced them out of a box, I believe, and gave them to Mr. Woods.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BRODRICK. Q. When was this? A. In July. I had heard the prisoner was a servant out place; I found his name happened to be Robinson.
   WILLIAM WOODS. I am inspector at the Post-office; I went with St. Ledger when the prisoner was apprehended; he was taken to Mr. Peacock's house, who was then ill; he asked him if he had ever written to any person; he said he had not.
