THOMAS PARKINSON, works manager of the Argus Printing Com pany, recalled, stated that the total amount of work done for Mr. Perryman was £4,500, covering a period from 1895 to 1898, and was in respect of a newspaper called the "Mining Herald." Since then there had still been an account, but smaller. For Fraser the amount of work done was £376 19s. 6d.; for Chalmers and Cox, £345 17s. 4d.; and for Herbert Smith, £931 4s. 7d.; and including Perryman's later account, the total work done was upwards of £6,000. HERBERT KENYON BRIGHT, clerk at the head office of the London and County Bank, Lombard Street, stated that the head office had from time to time received payments into the account for the credit of the Farnborough branch account of C.W. Perryman from 1903 to 1907. The payments were principally in notes. HENRY LISTER BASS, recalled, expressed an opinion as to certain manuscript additions and alterations upon circulars and other docu ments being in the handwriting of Perryman. WALTER GEORGE BLEACH, recalled, gave evidence as to cheques drawn by Perryman in his favour. Cash and Stone, chartered accountants, 90, Cannon Street. I have had a large experience of accountancy, and particularly in connection with transactions on the Stock Exchange. I have examined the exhibits in the case of Mrs. Butcher, and find she purchased on February 16, 1906, £5,000 Canadian Pacific Stock (250 shares) at 177 1/2 cum bonus. I produce a copy of the "Stock Exchange Weekly Intelligence," which shows that the stock was quoted ex dividend on February 22, the dividend being $3 per share less tax. The letter from Ablett of March I says that the quotation has fallen away entirely owing to the general fall in securities dealt in in New York. I have taken the tape prices in the "Financial News" as issued by the Exchange Telegraph Company. The opening price on March 7 was 173 3/4, to 174 1/4, and on February 28, 175 1/2. The fall between February 16 and February 28 is in part accounted for by the fact that the shares had become ex dividend. A person who had bought cum dividend would be entitled to be credited with the dividend, which would ultimately be collected and handed over to the purchaser. Under these circumstances the three points cover on the transaction would not have run off. I take the date February 28 because the letter from the defendants contain ing the first intimation that the shares bad fallen is dated March 1. I start from their own date. Mrs. Butcher did not send any more money. The letter signed by Ablett of March 5 asserts that "The price did fall to 173"; also that if the price fell "naturally you would be obliged to have them closed to obviate the necessity of sending more money, in the first place; and, secondly, because you were guaranteed from any further liability."
