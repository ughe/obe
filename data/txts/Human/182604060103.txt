 837.  ELIZABETH BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 1 watch, value 30s., the goods of James Forbes , from his person. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 838.  EDWARD CLARK SPENCER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March , 1 pair of sheets, value 2s.; 1 shirt, value 4s.; 1 pair of stockings, value 6d.; 2 towels, value 6d.; 1 night cap, value 2d., and 1 apron, value 6d., the goods of George Kent , from the person of William Kent.
   ELIZABETH KENT. I live by Stepney church. On the 13th of March, about twelve o'clock, I sent my grandson, William Kent, with a bundle, containing these articles, to take about two hundred yards, to his mother's, to be washed - he returned and told me they were gone.
 WILLIAM KENT. I am eight years and a half old. My grandmother sent me with a bundle to my mother's for the things to be washed; the prisoner met me, and asked me to go down  Globe-lane to get two bundles of silk; and said, he would hold my bundle - I gave it to him - and when I came back he was gone.
   WILLIAM MOGRIDGE. I am an officer. I was walking down Mile-end-road, and saw the prisoner running across the field with a bundle - I pursued, and took him near Stepney church - he said he had met his own mother in Whitechapel-road, and she gave him the bundle.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence, (written). I was persuaded by  Charles Gigg, a neighbour, to go to a bull-hunt - as we were in the road, he said "There is a boy with a bundle, we will frighten him" - he told me to send him for two skeins of silk and hold his bundle the while - and when I had it, Gigg ran away and told me to follow - I became alarmed, and asked him to take the bundle - but he said two men were following us.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped and Discharged.
 839.  ELEANOR SHEA was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of March , 1 bag, value 1d.; 1 snuff-box, value 6d.; 19 sovereigns, and 2 half-sovereigns, the property of William Hanington , from his person.
 WILLIAM HANINGTON. I  keep a public-house in George-street, Grovernor-square. I was at the Red Lion, public-house, Duke-street, Manchester-square, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening of the 12th of March - I was a good deal the worse for liquor, and happened to go in there, when I saw the prisoner, whom I had known for upwards of twenty years - she once kept a house near me; she was drinking with another person, named Ryan, who had a little girl with her - I spoke to the prisoner, and gave her something to drink; I went home with them to their room in Gee's-court, Oxford-street- I should think it was between ten and eleven o'clock, but I am not certain; we had some more to drink there; I believe the little girl went out for liquor, but I am not certain; I went to sleep on the floor, and about half-past four in the morning, I awoke; I went out into the passage to go home - I missed my bag, with nineteen sovereigns, and two half-sovereigns, and requested them to give it me; I did not miss my snuff-box till afterwards; I went to their house; I changed something out of it after I met them at the public-house - it was in my left-hand breeches pocket - when I asked them for it they both laughed at me; the watchman happened to be going by and I called him - he found the snuff-box in the room, and the officer found the bag on the Monday following.
   CATHERINE RYAN. I am the daughter of Johanna Ryan- she lives in Gee's-court - the prisoner was an acquaintance of hers. I am in the workhouse - I was at the public-house, and saw the prosecutor come in, and have some gin and water; he did not speak to the prisoner, but she took hold of his glass and drank out of it - my mother and I went home, leaving them there; when we had been at home about an hour, they came there together - the prosecutor sent me out for half a pint of rum, which they drank; they then sent for half a gallon of beer; I went out for another half pint of rum, and afterwards I saw the prisoner pull up her clothes, and dance before the prosecutor; my mother said she ought to be ashamed of herself, and then she sat upon his knee for a quarter of an hour; we had not much candle - the prosecutor then got up to go away, but he fell down on the floor, and soon afterwards the candle went out - I then went to bed - when I awoke in the morning the prisoner and my mother were laughing at the prosecutor; I then went and sat on the stairs - the prosecutor called the watchman - he could not open the door, and the watchman broke in - he got another watchman, and they searched, but could not find the money - they went to the watch-house.
 COURT. Q. Do you recollect any thing about a watch? A. Yes; the prisoner went up to the prosecutor, and pulout his watch - she said "By G-d he has got a good watch," and then put it in again - she then went to his other side, and took out a bag of sovereigns - he awoke, and said "What are you at?" he then pushed it into his pocket again, and she went and sat down on the bed - I then went to sleep, and did not see any more - the candle went out.
   WILLIAM VARNDILE. I am a watchman. I was calling the hour of half-past three, and heard a great noise - I came on towards the passage, and heard a rustling - I said "Who is there?" and this gentleman said "It is I;" I said "Open the door;" he said "I can't;" I then told him where the bolt was, and we got it open; I went in and took the prisoner - I found a half-sovereign, four shillings, four sixpences, and four-pence halfpenny, on her; the half-sovereign she took from some part of her clothes, on her right breast - she had before said she had no money about her.
