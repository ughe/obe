2455. GEORGE CARR was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August, 1
pint of ale, value 5d., the goods of Thomas Pickford and others, his masters
MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE LIGHTFOOT. About nine o'clock at night, on Saturday, the 12th
of August, I was employed as wagoner, at Mr. Pickford's wharf, in the City.
road-I was near the bank where the goods are landed- I saw the prisoner
and another near the bank-) afterwards spoke to Hawkins, the watchman-
the prisoner was standing at the end of the bank, and when I came back with
my ropes he was gone-he appeared to be watching Hawkins—the ale was
deposited under a shed, within three or four yards of the bank-as I was
going past I heard the pail handle rattle-I informed Hawkins--I had then
missed the prisoner and the other man from where they had been standing-
the shed is rather dark-Hawkins went away from me directly.
Cross-examined by MR. WILDE. Q. How long have you been in the
prosecutor's employ? A. Nearly twenty-nine years the prisoner has been
with them three or four years.
CALEB HAWKINS. I am a watchman in the employ of Messrs. Pickfords.
At half-past nine o'clock at night, on the 12th of August, I was on duty on
the bank, and saw the prisoner-I had received particular orders to watch
some casks of ale there, in consequence of some pilferings- I received infor
mation from Lightfoot-I afterwards went towards the shed where the ale bad
been deposited-I heard the pail, and the liquor running from the cask-I
went in directly, and there found the prisoner, in a bending attitude--the pail
was against the head of the cask, and the liquor running into it-I laid hold
of him-I found the pail contained ale- I took it to the lodge-keeper-the
prisoner said, "For God's sake, have mercy on me; consider my wife and
three children."
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you been in the employ? 1. Twenty-
two years—this was Burton ale-I am sixty-nine years old-I use spectacles,
but not to look at a man in the face-I knew the prisoner well.
MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you look at the cask afterwards ? A. Yes
-I got a lantern, and found a hole made with a gimlet, and a peg in it-
that is the hole where the liquor came from-I said to the prisoner, "Cart,
it is no use, I must have the pail."
WILLIAM WHITE (police-constable G 105.) The prisoner was given into
custody for stealing the ale-he said he hoped I should have mercy on him,
for his wife and family; he was sorry for what he had done.
JAMES WARD. I am a clerk in the employ of Messrs. Pickfords. These
goods are in the custody of Thomas Pickford and others.
GUILTY. Aged 35.-Confined One Month.
