ELIZA CLARK. I am the wife of George Clark, and have lived in Old Nicholl-street four years. On the 2nd of June, about half-past eleven o'clock at night, I was going up Old Nicholl-street, and saw Griffin come out of Mason place and go to Shepherd's-court, with a bundle on her arm—I went to the corner of Maidstone-court and saw Cole, and another man with him, about twenty yards off Griffin-Cole had something white across his arm—I supposed it was sheets—it was white like cloth—they went to the left—the water-closet is right opposite as you go up the court—I stopped at the bottom of the court—I went with Sarah Cowles to give information at the station—a policeman came, and saw the things taken out of the water-closet. THOMAS HUGHES. I live in Vine-court, Spitalfields. On the 2nd of June, about twelve o'clock, I went to No. 1, Maidstone-place, I found the door bro ken open, a padlock lying inside, and the bed stripped—I afterwards went to Shepherd's-court, and found part of the property in the water-closet—I saw Griffin crouching behind the door of No. 4, and Cole standing at the corner of the court, a few yards from her—I went in search of another man, who was suspected, came back, found Griffin in custody, went to her room, and found the remainder of the property on the bed—this is it. LOUISA COWLES re-examined.  These are my mother's property—they were let with the room. JOHN HOPE( policeman. ) On the 2nd of June, between eleven and twelve o'clock, I was passing up Old Nicholl-street, and saw Mr. Cowes, Griffin, and a person named Mills, who told me something—the prisoner Cole was not present—I went into the room, and found the bed stripped—I saw an apron at the head of the bed—it was handed to me by Mills—I went in search of the prisoner Cole, and found him at the corner of Church-street, by a public-house—I told him I wanted him, and he followed us to the station.
   Cole's Defence.  I was not there, and know nothing of the robbery. NOT GUILTY.
