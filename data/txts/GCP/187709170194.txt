THIRD COURT.-Wednesday, September 26th, 1877.
Before Mr. Common Serejant.
728. JAMES GLAVE (42), Unlawfully failing to discover to his trustees
part of his property, his affairs being then under liquidation.
MR.F. H. Lewis and MR. TICKELL conducted the Prosecution, and MR. SERJRANT
BALLANTINY and Mr. STRAIGHT the Defence.
THOMAS BLAOKLOCK. I am a clerk in the Bankruptoy Court at Leeds-
produce the proceedings in the petition for liquidation filed by John Edward
Bennett and James Glave, of 45, Cheapside, London, and of Leeds, woollen
manufacturers, and trading as Bennett and Glave-the petition produced
was signed on the 16th of April, 1875, and was filed in the London
Bankruptcy Court on the 20th April—the first meeting of creditors was on
the 7th May, 1875-I produce the accounts filed by the prisoner-the
liabilities are put at 25,6101. 88. 9d. : unsecured creditors, 25,0841. 46. 9d.;
fully secured, 21,0001. 198, 5d. ; securities held by them, 22,0621. ; creditor
fully secured, 1761. 168. 10d. ; value of their securities, 1001. 128. ; liabilities
on bills, 2,01611. 198. 5d., of which only 4501. was expected to run against
the estate, giving as total liabilities, 25,6101. 8. 9d. the total assets are
18,8961. 28. 100., as shown by the statement and including the amount
supposed to be over and above the securities--the appointment of Mr.
Henry Bull, as receiver, is dated the 13th April, 1875; and the appoint-
ment of Jaines William Coles, as trustee, is dated 2nd June, 1875—I also
produce the examination of the bankrupt taken at Leeds, on 25th April,
1877; also what I call an order to prosecute, by the Judges of the Court-
the words are “To be at liberty to prosecute - there is also an order of
commitment.
ANDREW CALDECOTT. I am one of the firm of Caldecott, Sons, and Co,
of Cheapside-we were customers of Bennett and Glave-I paid them 302, 161.
on 8th April, 1878; on the 10th, 20th, and 15th, 821. 128. and 101. in
cheques which have passed through my bank and have been paid.
Cross-examined by MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE. The prisoner was the partner
in London, the other partner was at Leeds- I have dealt with them some
two or three years- I do not know the prisonor personally,
JOHN BATLAND PONSFORD. I am a woollen warehouseman, in partner
ship with Mr. Southall, in Cannon Street-we were customers of Bennett
and Glave-I paid them in April, 1875, 802. 188., and on 12th April 591. 86;
both cheques passed through my bankers.
Cross-examined. I had known the firm twelve or fifteen years--they car
ried on a very large business--the prisoner was always an upright, honour-
able man; he has about nine children.
John BALDERO. I am a clerk to Messrs. Spencer and Co., of Lisson Grove
--the cheque dated 9th April, 1875, for 1231. 48. passed through our bank,
it is payable to Bennett and Glave.
Cross-examined. We have carried on business with the firm for eight or
nine years, we considered the prisoner perfectly upright and trustworthy.
