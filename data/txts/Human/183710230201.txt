JOHN GRAY. I am a constable. I took the prisoner, and have the property. GEORGE BIGGS, I am a farmer. On Tuesday evening, about seven o'clock, I had business at Mr. Gray's, and Miss Gray stated about the wheat being stolen—I went up the road and met the prisoner with some wheat on his back in a sack—I stopped him and took it from him—he said it was what his wife had been gleaning—I gave him into custody—the wheat which he had was not owned by any one. HENRY GRAY re-examined.  I have compared the wheat left with my daughter with what was in my barn—the prisoner said he took it, he could not tell why, he thought it must be the devil, who had very nearly got the upper hand of him the day before, but he beat him then—this was only the second day he had worked for me. GUILTY  . Aged 33.— Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. — Confined One Month.

 2407. ROBERT PURKISSON was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of October , 7 pecks of oats, value 6 s  ., the goods of Thomas French , his master. ROBERT FRENCH. I drive a fish van for my brother, Thomas French. The prisoner was in his employ—my brother keeps horses at Chadwell—purchase all the corn for his horses there—I have compared some oats which Weston has with others which I keep of my brother's—I will not swear to them—they are white oats and very good—they are just like what was in the sack taken from the prisoner—we did not grow them—I did not authorise him to take any oats to London for my brother.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  How long have you known him? A.  Sixteen or seventeen years. The oats were taken from the stable at Chadwell in Essex. GEORGE WESTON. I am one of the horse patrol stationed at Rom ford.
