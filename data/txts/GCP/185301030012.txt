EDWARD STEVENS. 1 formerly resided at No. 13, Short-street, New-cut. In
Sept. I was about to proceed to Australia-for that purpose I went to Messrs.
Thompson Brothers' offices, and on 13th Sept. I paid 151. for passage money, and
got this certificate (produced)-before I received this, I paid a 10l. and a 51. note
to Mr. Thompson, on 13th Sept., and on 21st Sept. I paid the remainder, 154.
- there is a receipt on the back for the whole amount-(this being read, was
similar to the one read; on the back was written, "London, Sept. 18th, 1852.
Messrs. James Baines and Co., 16, Cook-street, Liverpool. The within
mentioned passengers, having paid the balance of their passage money, can
embark when the ship is ready. J. Thompson and Brothers ")- I got that on
the 21st-I paid altogether 281. 6s. 60.--I agreed to pay 301. for my passage,
and have my passage retained for me and my wife to Liverpool, which was
168. 9d. each-- I was induced to part with my money by the idea of going to
Australia-I parted with my money to Mr. Thompson, because I believed
him to be an agent for Mr. Baines, of Liverpool : at least, I did not know
who he was agent for, but he promised to procure me a passage in the ship
called the South Sea- I did not know who was the owner of the vessel, or
who were the brokers, till I got the contract ticket-I believed that Thompson
Brothers had power to give me this order to embark--I have never re-
ceived any of my money back-I did not go to Liverpool - I called at the
office each morning, in the expectation of seeing Mr. Thompson, but I never
saw him there-I saw Mr. Shotter on several occasions- I called about six
mornings in suecession-- he put me off from day to day, saying the ship
would sail on 1st Oct. at the latest-I went again somewhere about 2nd Oct.,
and the office was shut up-I think it was on 2nd Oct., but I cannot swear
to that I saw Tuckey and another person there I had some communication
from them, and went to the Mansion-house-I was examined as a witness.
EDMUND NICHOLLS. I am a labourer, residing at Bagshot, in Surrey. I
made up my my mind to emigrate in Sept. last went to the office of the
defendant, in Riches-court-l there paid 151. for my passage, and got this
contract note-I paid 1l. more for bedding, and got this receipt for it-the
certificate and receipt were read)- was induced to pay the 161. from seeing
an advertisement in the paper-that led me to the office- paid the money
to go to Australia - I believed when I paid the money that this ticket would
give me a passage on board the South Sea-after paying the money, and
getting this ticket, I went back to Bagshot-I came up to London several
times, and went to Riches-court-I was told that the South Sea had not sailed
- I was desired to go home, and to wait till I had a letter from them-I did
so, but no letter came- I came up again, and was told to wait ; I should be
sure to hear from them I came up the last time in consequence of receiving
a letter from the Lord Mayor-the matter was then being inquired into before
the Lord Mayor-I have never got any of my money back.
