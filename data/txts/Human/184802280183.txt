WILLIAM DALBY. I am the nephew of Mr. Thomas Dalby. On 23rd Dec. I left his warehouse a little before six o'clock—I last saw the two bags of wool safe about four—I went next morning about nine o'clock, and the bags were gone—I saw an impression on some manure there, as if the bags had been on it—they would not have occasion to be put on it in the ordinary course of business. ELIEA M'DONALD. I left the premises safe on Thursday night, 23rd Dec., next morning I found them all locked, as I had left them. WILLIAM DALBY re-examined. Q.  Can you state how a person could get in? A.  Over the gates outside, and by opening a trap-door—supporting that to be done, the manure was in such a position that the wool would be thrown upon it, and there were some locks of wool on the manure—a person might have got in at the scutch-door without breaking at all. WILLIAM SWAN. I live with my father, a butcher, in Grange-road. Between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, on 23rd Dec., I was passing from Grange-road into Grange-walk—I saw Carney's horse and cart standing under Mr. Dalby's scutch-loft door—I knew Carney before—he was in the cart—directly the cart was backed up, the door was opened, and one bag was thrown down—it nearly went on Carney, and before he moved that one the other was sent down—I saw some persons in the loft, but I could not see who they were—I should say there must have been more than one, as the bags were thrown out so quick—Carney set the bags to rights and went away—I went and told my father. THOMAS CARNEY. I am the brother of the man who was tried here last Sessions. On the Wednesday before Christmas, Verrall came to my mother's house—I did not know him, only by seeing him about the street—I did not know his name before—he used often to come to my mother's house for the horse and cart—I knew he was in Mr. Pettit's employ—mu brother was out at the time, delivering coke, and Verrall said he would call up again when he was ready—he said he wanted my brother to move some hurdles and things—he said his master sent him up for the cart—I told him I would send my brother down—Verrall went aways, and I did not see him again. JOHN BARRETT. I am a coal-whipper—I am brother-in-law of Carney, who was tried here last Sessions. On the night of the 23rd Dec. I was at Carney's house, when Verrall came—I heard him say his master had sent him up for Carney to do a job for him—Carney said he would go, as he was ready—he went about seven o'clock, with his horse and cart—that place is about a quarter of a mile from where the wool was taken—next morning I was riding with Carney, and he got out and went into a yard in Russell-street—I saw the prisoner come down from the yard—he took Carney into a public-house, and paid him some money, and stood  a pot of beer while he was paying him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you even seen the prisoner before? A.  No—this was on the Thursday before Christmas, about twelve or one o'clock in the day, at the Rose public-house—I was in the cart, and they called me to have a glass of beer—a young woman served us—I think I should know her if I saw her.
