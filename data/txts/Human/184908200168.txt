The Rev.  ARTHUR STOCK . I live in the Clapham-road, and am curate of St. Michael's Church, Stockwell. In May, 1848, in consequence of hearing that the Church had been broken open, I went there—I missed my scarf from a drawer in the vestry, in which I had locked it myself on the Wednesday evening previous—I found it unlocked on the Sunday morning—there were marks of its having been forced—I examined the Church, and found the east window broken—the lock of a cupboard under the pulpit had been forced, and a pannel had been cut out of the door which leads into the vestry—the entrance had been effected by a pane of glass being cut out of the east window, large enough to admit a hand to undo the fastenings—I also missed Mr. Kemble's surplice and two keys—I have since seen my scarf—this is it ( produced )—I know it from its general appearance—I cannot swear to it, but I am sure it is mine, from its length and breadth, and from the crease in folding—I have not seen the surplice since. MARIA GOBBETT. In May, 1848, I was living with the prisoner at Dock-head—I recollect one morning his bringing home this scarf—he desired me to go and pledge it—I did so—two or three weeks after he asked me to sell the ticket, and I did so to a person named Moss—I went by the name of Mrs. Cannon.
   Prisoner. Q.  Used you not to go and pawn things for the landlady of the house, in my name? A.  Decidedly not. CHARLES COOPER. I am assistant to Mr. Walter, a pawnbroker, of Ber-mondsey-street. On 6th May a scarf was pledged with me by last witness, in the name of Cannon—it has gone out of my possession—it is impossible for me to say whether the one produced is it. JOHN FAYERBROTHER. I produced the scarf—it was pledged with me on 8th Aug., 1848, for 6 s ., by a person who said her name was Ann Moss.

ROBERT CROSS. I am a stationer, and have something to do with Ebene-zer Chapel, Bermondsey. In Jan., 1846, I received information, went to the chapel, and found the door partly open, and I missed a clock, two tables, and a table-cloth, which I had seen safe the evening before—the clock was over the mantel-piece in the school, to which there is a communication from the chapel—I have seen the glass; it belongs to the trustees of the chapel, of whom I am one.
