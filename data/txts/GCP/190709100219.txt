Detect.ve-sergeant WILLIAN PHIPPS, Walthamstow. On July 12 I
received information concerning this case. In company with Keen
and Brown, I went to the Bromley Co-operative Society's stables on
August 4, where Brown identified the bay mare. We then went to
Dorking, where I met the prisoner Luck in the Watham, a small open-
ing in the Dorking High Road. I told him I was a police officer and
was making inquiries about two horses stolen from Chingford on
July 10, one of which he had sold to the Co-operative Society for £35.
I said I believed he had the other one in his possession. He said
"Yes" and invited me to his home in Pixham Lane. In the stable
we saw the bay gelding. After I had oadtioned him he said, "A man
named Smith brought the horses to me at Dorking Station. I bought
them in exchange for £9 and two ponies. Smith, I think, lives at
Hardinge Street, Commercial Road. I had seen him about three or
four months previous to this. I had no receipt for the horses. I
thought they were straight." I said, "How do you account for the
horses being consigned to you a few hours after they were stolen!"
He made no reply. I then told him I should arrest him for being!
concerned in stealing and receiving these horses. He replied, "Smith
sent me a wire saying they were coming. They got here about mid.
day. I did not know they were crooked (stolen). I conveyed him
to Dorking Police Station, where he was detained all night. Next
morning while waiting for a train at Dorking he said, "I saw Smith
last Sunday. I have been to his house. I will tell you about it.
Smith lives at 15, Drewton Street, Hardinge Street." As a matter of
fact, Smith does not live there, but his sister or sister-in-law does.
