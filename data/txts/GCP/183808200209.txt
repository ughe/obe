S Ross. I live in Castle-street, Long Acre-the prisoner is a
dress-maker. On Monday, the 23rd of July, at half-past nine o'clock at
Icht. I was with my sister in Drury-lane, and saw the prisoner with her
der on the opposite side of the way, behind me--she kept behind me
all through Drury-lane- I went into a shop to get some cheese, and when
I came out again she was gone--I saw her again a few minutes after in
Belton-street, Long Acre, as I was going with my sister to fetch some
beer-she seemed to be watching at the public-house door where I went
in-she was then alone-when I came out I went on home, and she fol-
lowed close behind me I then felt my back as if I was all in a flame of
fire-I turned my head round, and she went from me to her sister, who
was on the opposite side of the way, and said to her, “Come along, Hen-
rietta, don't be frightened," and they walked away-when I felt the fire I
put my hand behind me, and felt the stuff; and my hand was all wet-it
was liquid—it burnt my hand the instant I touched it—there was
nobody about at all when she did it-I went home and took off my frock
directly I got home-it all dropped into holes—the prisoner and her
mother bad made that dress-she seemed as if she had something under
her shawl as she went along the street.
Cross-examined by Mr. Doane. Q. How long before this had the
frock been made ? A. About four or five weeks-it was the prisoner's
being behind me that induced me to put my hand behind me I then
found the stuff which burnt my hand-it was after that I felt my back
bunt--my frock was burnt all across the waist, and all the back was
burnt out.
Cecilia Ross. I am the mother of the last witness. She came in on
this evening between nine and ten o'clock-I saw her frock directly she
came in--it is not here--it had several holes about the back, the shoulders,
and the waist--there was a hole in her stays, and the shift was burnt-
there was a hole in it-no harm was done to her person- her back was
warm, I believe, as she complained-my husband put some soda-water to
L and that removed the heat immediately the skin was not burnt
through, and there was no scar-the prisoner and her mother had made
the frock about a fortnight or three weeks before, and there was a little
dispute about it-I thought it was much too small for her-I spoke to the
prisoner about it, and stopped a shilling towards what I thought was missing
of the stuff-she said all the stuff was there, and they had not any left,
that it was a mistake of mine, and she desired to have the shilling-I said I
would not give the shilling till she returned the stuff--a few words arose
about it, and she came three times, and said her mother wanted the shilling
I said, "I will pay it when you return the stuff" -I thought her saucy to me,
and put her out of the street-door, shut it, and went in-doors--she said, "I
u have the shilling and I will be revenged," or something to that purport
--but that was to me, and not to my daughter--my daughter and her
were the best of friends--they always went to church together.
Cross-examined. Q. She was a quiet, humane, kind sort of a girl?
Particularly so-we never had a word before this-there was a great
of grief on both sides respecting what happened.
