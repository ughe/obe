take to 9, Duke-street, Westminster-I took them there and asked for Mr.
Stanley-I saw Keeling- I gave him the cigars with the bill, and he said
Mr. Stanley had just gone away with his lady in the carriage into the
country to a dinner, and that if I did not leave the cigars he would be vert
much offended, and very likely would not have them at all I told him.
when I gave them to him, that I could not leave them without the money-

Shine came rushing out from a back room and told me that Mr. Stanley had

CHARLES JOHN OLIYT. I am assistant to Mr. Bartram, pawnbroker, at
51, Princes-street, Leicester-square-on the evening of 25th May, 3 lbs. of
cigars were pledged there between 7 and 8 in the evening by Shine-they
were in three boxes, tied up in three bundlesI advanced 31. on them the
prisoner gave me the name of Lee.Keeling came in and recognised him as
a friend-they spoke together-Keeling went out of the shop first; Shine
went out after he was served I had seen Shine before I had never known
him by the name of Stanley, I know him by the name of Shine.
Cross-examined. Q. You knew his name at the time he told you his name
was Lee A. He told me his name was Lee-I only knew him as having
pledged things before in the name of Shine--it is very seldom that the real
name is given when persons pledge-Keeling was in a different part of the
shop-I should say he would be there on business or he would not be there
at all-there are two others in the shop besides myself-those goods were
redeemed the next day, by whom I ean't say for certain, I was not present
