516 SAMUEL PIZZEY, stealing 1 bag, value 6d., and 9 shillings.; the
property of William Lovell, from his person.
WILLIAM LOVELL. On Saturday night, 19th Jan., I was at a beer-shop at
North Hyde--the prisoner was there; I had never seen him before-I had a
purse with ten or eleven shillings in it-I spent one or two shillings, and
had then nine shillings in the purse-I sat and drank, and the prisoner sat by
my side-he put his hand in my pocket-I said, “What are you doing with
your hand in my pocket?"-be drew it out and dropped a shilling and a hall-
penny on the floor- I left the beer-shop about eleven o'clock with Collis-
had my money then-after I had got some distance I was overtaken by the
prisoner and another-he said, “You have accused me wrong to-night"-I
said no, I had not-he began to push against me, and put his arm round me,
pat his hand in my pocket, and pulled my money all out-I said he had taken
my money-he walked a distance with me, and said he had not-I saw a
policeman, and the prisoner and the other man ran away-I never got my
money again.
John Collis. I was in the public-house that night with the prisoner
and Lovell-I did not see the prisoner do anything, but I heard some money
fall on the ground-Lovell said, “What business had you with your hand in
my pocket? you have picked out my money--the prisoner said, "No, I
have not”- Lovell picked up the shilling, and I picked up the halfpenny-
Lovell afterwards left the house; I went with him-two persons overlook
us, but I do not know who they were-it was quite dark-a man struck at
Lovell-he said, "I never picked your pocket"-Lovell said, “You did"
--the policeman came, and both the men ran away-we were rather in drink,
but we knew what we were doing.
DAVID ACRES (policeman, T 111). I came up and heard Lovell say, "If
you rob me, don't knock me about"-1 turned on my light, and a man, who
I believe was the prisoner, ran away-he jumped over a hedge, and we lost
him-Lovell had been drinking, but he knew what he was doing, andra
very well.
Prisoner's Defence. Lovell and the other man were playing; they were
very tipsy; I did not sit beside him; I sat on the other side of the table; he
never accused me of robbing him in the house at all; he was pulling out his
money and offering to toss anybody that came.
Witnesses for the Defence.
