EDWARD AMOS. I am the husband of the first witness—the prisoner has been in my service about three or four months—on Wednesday morning, 8th October, I called him up at about half-past 3 o'clock, and he started with me to go to the Metropolitan Meat Market—he had some coffee there at 4 o'clock—I did not see him after that—I left him to go back home; he merely took me to the market—he was perfectly sober when he left me—I saw him next day at the police-court—it took three-quarters of an hour to drive from home to. the market. SOLOMON GEORGE WATSON. I am a registered medical practitioner, of 7, Duncan Villas, Wood Green—on 8th October I was sent for to see the deceased child—I had it stripped and examined it carefully—it was then alive—there was a bruise on the left aide of the head—it was suffering from concussion of the brain—it rallied for a day or two, and subse quently had convulsions and died from fracture of the skull and injury to the brain. MARIA AMOS( Re-examined ). The prisoner had no rum at his break fast that I saw, nothing whatever—he told me he would have some ale if I did not object, and he took a can to fetch it, but he came back with it empty—I said "Did not you have the ale?"—he said "No," and I said "Come and have your breakfast." NOT GUILTY.
