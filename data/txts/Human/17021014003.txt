Henry Dodds, of the Parish of Enfield, was indicted for feloniously stealing 2208 Beech Billets, and other Wood, from Thomas Barnet, on the 6th of January last. It appeared to be a malicious Prosecution. The Jury   acquitted him, and the Court ordered him a Copy of the Indictment.
   Rachel Sampson, of the Parish of White-chappel, was indicted for a Burglary, for breaking the House of Susan Edwards in the Night time, on the 28 of September , and taking thence two Cheeses, a pair of Silk Gloves, and a pair of Worsted Stockings, and 7 s. in Money. It appeared that the Prisoner got in and hid her self till they were a Bed, and took the Goods, which she confest when taken. She denied the Fact upon her Trial, saying, she knew nothing of it. The Jury found her Guilty of Felony only, and acquitted her of the Burglary. [Branding. See summary.]
   Jane Housden alias    Newstead, of the Parish of St. Andrew's Holborn, was indicted for High Treason, for counterfeiting the current Coin of this Kingdom, on the 18th of August last. It appeared that there was an Information given, that the Prisoner followed the Trade of Coining, a Warrant was granted to apprehend her, and going in Holborn, by Turnstile, they met the Prisoner with false Money in her Hand, upon which she dropt upon her Knees, and would have thrust the Money into some Grates of a Window, but was prevented; an other Evidence said, that she had bought several quantities of false Money of the Prisoner, who confest to her, that she had made it. The Prisoner was an Old Offender in this kind, and had nothing to say, only denied the Fact: The Jury found her   Guilty. [Death -respited for pregnancy. See summary.]
   Thomas Parry, of the Parish of St. Andrews Holborn, was indicted for feloniously stealing 3 Brass Porridge-pots and a Brass Kettle from Rich Southey, on the 17th of July, in the 11th Year of the late King William. The Prisoner alledged he bought them 7 Years ago, and farther said, that he was Prosecuted out of Malice, by reason he had lived so long by the Prosecutor and was never taken up for the same till now. The Jury   acquitted him.
   Joseph Day, of the Parish of St. Clements Danes, was indicted for privately stealing 3 pair of Crimson Worsted Stockings, out of the Shop of Anthony Huslam, on the 7th of September Last. The Prosecutor said he was fitting in the Shop with his Wife, and on a sudden she saw the Prisoner come from behind the Counter with something under his Arm, and immediately he followed him, and took the Goods upon him. He denied the Fact, saying he did not touch them; but that did not avail him, the Jury found him Guilty to the value of 4 s. 10 d. [Branding. See summary.]
   Jane Bourne was indicted for a Misdemeanor for uttering false Money, she knowing it so to be. The Evidence deposed that she came into the Shop to buy some French Brandy, and offered a good Guinea to change, which was weighed; but she pretending that she must not give so much for it, and desired her Money again; at last she agreed to have it, and threw down a gilt Shilling instead of the Guinea, and they gave her her change out of it, and she went her way; there was another deposed that she bought a Shift of her, and she gave her a gilt Six-pence instead of a half Guinea; another said, that she came to buy 3 l. of Sugar, and she gave her a gilt Six-pence instead of a half Guinea. She having little to say for herself, the Jury found her   Guilty. [Fine. See summary.] [Pillory. See summary.]
   Joseph Bennet, of the Parish of St. Martins in the Fields, was indicted for stealing a Walnut-tree Dressing-box, and 29 Shillings in Money from David Hutchinson, on the 22d of September last. There was no Evidence against him, he was acquitted.
   Thomas Twist, Jane Williams and Mary Buck, of the Parish of Chelsea, were indicted for breaking the House of William Stubbington , in the day time, on the 18th of July last, and taking a Copper, a pair of stuff Curtains, and divers other Goods. It appeared that the Door was broke open whilst the Prosecutor was gone out early to Market, and the Goods gone. There was other Evidence who saw the Women near the House, and saw one of them carry the Copper upon her Head, and the other the Bundles, and the Man was not with them; which the Jury considering,   acquitted the Man, and found the two Women Guilty of Felony only. [Branding. See summary.]
   Lancelot Roberts, of the Parish of Stepney, was indicted for robbing his Lodgings, granted to him by Contract, on the 10th of January last. and taking 20 l. weight of Feathers, a blanket, Sheet and divers other Goods of Mark Hooks deceased. There was no Evidence against him, he was acquitted.
   Sarah Stephens alias    Manshty, of the Parish of Stepny, was indicted for picking the pocket of David Hughes , on the 13th of October last, and taking from him a Linnen-purse work'd with Cruel, and 20 s in Money, numbred. The Prosecutor said that he was drinking at a House where the Prisoner was, and going his way over Goodmans-fields, she overtook him again, and would have him to make her drink, which he refused, and on a suddain got her Hand into his Pocket, which he fealing, she snatched it out again with the Purse, and ran away; there was other Evidences who said, that she confest, where she had left the Purse, and accordingly found it by her Directions. She denied the Fact, saying, she found it in the Street, but could not prove it, or call any to her Reputation, the Jury found her   Guilty. [Death -respited for pregnancy. See summary.]
   Martha Lewin of the Parish of St. Giles without Cripplegate, was indicted for feloniously stealing an Indian Sattin Gown, and divers other Goods of Martha Roberts, and a silk Hood the Goods of John Mercer, on the 14th of September last. The Prosecutor deposed that the Prisoner was a Servant, and took the Goods, which she confest when taken and brought before the Justice.
