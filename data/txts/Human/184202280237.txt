On the 7th of February, the prisoner came for a pot of ale, and gave me a five-shilling piece—I gave her four shillings and eight pence and the ale—I put the crown-piece into the till—there was no other crown in the till, which was locked—I kept the key—it remained there till I was going to bed—I then took it out and put it into my pocket—I had no other crown in my pocket—next day I gave it to my husband, and he kept it in his pocket—I am sure it is the same, I had not another—on the Wednesday week the prisoner came to my shop again, had a pot of beer, and gave me a half-crown—I said it was bad—she said it was not—I sent my boy next door to the baker's shop with it, and he brought it back and said it was bad—I said to the prisoner, "I know you brought me a bad five shilling piece before, and now here is a bad half-crown; and I shall have you and the money together"—she said she was led into it, and would give me good money if I would let her go—I sent the boy for a policeman—she wanted as well as she could to get away, but I kept her.
   Prisoner.  The five shilling piece I do not know anything about—I said I would give her good money for the half-crown—I threw down the half-crown—I did not know it was bad—I said I would fetch the money for it; and while I was there a person came and brought me the fivepence to pay for the beer—she said she had a little drab hat she would sell me for my child, which was in my arms, for five shillings, and I said I had not the money. Witness.  No I did not. JAMES BALL. I am servant to Mr. Pearson, the beer-seller. On the 16th of February my mistress gave me a half-crown—I took it to the baker's next door but one—the baker's wife looked at it and gave it me back—she said it was bad—I brought back the same half-crown to ray mistress. JOHN PEARSON. I remember seeing a person in my shop on Monday, the 7th of February, between four and five o'clock, and the next day I received a crown from my wife, and some shillings, but only one crown—I bad not another crown—about two o'clock I was going to make a pay ment, and I took the crown out of my pocket—I thought it looked smooth and slippery—I bent it with the pincers to try, and it was bad—I put it into my pocket, and kept it till I gave it to the policeman—I am sure it was the same.
   Prisoner.  The witness charged another young woman with giving the five-shilling piece, and she was taken, and discharged. Witness.  No I did not. JAMES CUDDY( police-constable L  20.) On the 17th of February Bull came to me—I went to the house, and the prisoner was given into my custody—I received this half-crown from Mrs. Pearson, and this crown from Mr. Pearson—the prisoner was searched by a female—there was no money on her—she said she lived at No. 24, Hooper-street—I went there, and inquired, but could hear nothing of her—it is only a two-roomed house, and there is only one family living there. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint. This half-crown and crown are both counterfeit. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Six Months.
