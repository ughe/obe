1030. HENRY PIERSEY GARNSEY was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of March , 1 coat, value 30s., the goods of William Shearman.
 WILLIAM SHEARMAN. On the 23d of March, in the evening, I left a coat at the bar of the  Nelson's Head public-house, Hackney-road, while I took a pint of beer; I did not see it taken.
   JOSEPH THIMBLEBY. I am a pawnbroker. This coat was pawned with me on the 24th of March, by the prisoner.
   ELIZABETH HANNAH WRIGHT. I live at the Nelson's Head. On the 23d of March, about half-past ten o'clock at night, I was in the parlour, and saw the prisoner coming out of the bar - he turned by the counter; I went and looked at the till - that was all right; I did not think of the coat till the prosecutor applied for it.
   GEORGE SMITH. I took the prisoner, and found the duplicate on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never took the coat; I met a man who asked me to pawn it for him, as he did not like to go into a pawnbroker's; and he gave me the ticket.
     GUILTY. Aged 68. Transported for Seven Years.
 1031.  MARY DOBIE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of March , 1 lace cap, value 13s.; and 1 drinking glass, value 8d.; the goods of Joseph Warf.
   MARIA WARF. I am wife of Joseph Warf - we live at Wapping. On the 20th of March I met the prisoner - she said she was very much distressed; and I employed her to wash for me - on the 24th I missed these things, and charged her with it; she said, "You don't know what you have lost - if you won't give me in charge I will make it all right."
   JOHN HENRY HARVEY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner into custody; but the property has not been found.
 Prisoner. I never saw them.
     NOT GUILTY.
 FIFTH DAY. TUESDAY, APRIL 15.
 Third Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Serjeant.
 1032.  AMELIA LEVY was indicted for stealing, 4 pieces of ribbon, value 4s. the goods of William Petherbridge.
 The prisoner pleaded GUILTY. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1033.  JAMES ELLIS, WILLIAM BOYDEN, and THOMAS WILLIAMS, was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of February , 1 truck, value 4l., the goods of James Sholbred and Gregory Cook.
 JAMES SHOLBRED. I am in partnership with Gregory Cook, of Tottenham-court-road. We lent this truck to Maynard; I have since seen the front part of it, with our name on it.
   JAMES MAYNARD. I borrowed the truck on the 28th of February, and at half past nine o'clock at night put it into the mews - I missed it in the morning.
   FRANCIS KEYS. I am an officer. On the 29th of February, I found the front of this truck in the lost of a stable, in Great Garden-street, Whitechapel; Ellis stood at the top of the mews, the other two were in the stable - the front of the truck was smeared over with dirt; I cleaned it, and found the prosecutor's name on it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1034.  JAMES ELLIS, THOMAS WILLIAMS, and WILLIAM BOYDEN were again indicted for stealing, on the 29th of February , 1 cart, value 14l., the goods of James Prichard.
 JAMES PRICHARD. On the 28th of February, about eleven o'clock in the evening, I put my cart into my shed in  Paxton-place, Tottenham-court-road; I found it at Lambeth-street Office a good deal broken - I can identify it as mine; my name was nearly taken off the shaft, and the copse was gone.
   THOMAS LLOYD. On the 29th of February, about a quarter past seven o'clock in the morning, I saw Ellis, in Gun-square, Houndsditch, sawing the copse board of a cart - Boyden was unscrewing one side of the cart; I did not stay there above five minutes.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. This is a public place? A. Yes - I saw the name of "James Prichard, Fitzroy-market," on the cart.
 COURT. Q. Was that part sawed off? A. They were sawing it off at the time.
   GEORGE EWING. One day in February I was at my own door in Gun-square, and heard a cart coming up - I looked out, and saw Ellis; he asked if I knew anybody who wanted to buy a cart - I said, No; he said, "You don't want to buy it?" - I said No, and asked if he had had it long - he said No, that he took it of his brother for a debt.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see his brother? A. I saw a man looking down the street, who it was I do not know; I returned about eleven o'clock, and saw Ellis and another man at the King's Arms public-house door, talking about a debt - they asked me to drink with them, and I did.
   FRANCIS KEYS. I was on duty in Whitechapel on the 29th of February, between eleven and twelve o'clock in the morning, and saw Boyden and Phillips go out of Ellis's stable with a horse and an old cart, with the name of James Ellis on it; I followed them, and when they got to Aldgate church I lost them - I waited about Whitechapel church for nearly an hour, and saw Ellis driving down the cart in question - the other two were in the body of the cart; they drove down to the mews in Great Garden-street, and just before they got to the stable I saw Boyden get out; I got my brother officer - we went and found Ellis at the top of the mews; we took him to the stable - the other two were lying there - we handcuffed them together; I asked whose cart that was - neither of them would own it; I said to Ellis, "I suppose it is yours" - he said it was, and that the horse was his; I asked where he got the cart - he said he had it from his brother for a debt; they had gone out in an old cart, but came home in this one; which the prosecutor claims; the sideboard and spring were taken off, and the name on the shaft partly obliterated and covered with grease - I could just trace the name of James on it - the front board was off.
   JAMES FOWLER. I saw the prisoners driving up Whitechapel in the cart which Mr. Prichard claimed; part of the copse had been sawed off, the boards on each side taken off, the springs taken off, and a pair of wooden ones put on.
