 1940.  JAMES KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 1 hat, value 10s., the goods of Charles Hong ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
 CHARLES HONG. I live at Mr. Jones', the  Two Brewers', public-house, Leadenhall-market. On the 1st of August, about ten o'clock at night, the prisoner came in and called for a glass of porter; my hat hung by the yard door, in the passage - he asked me to show him backwards: he would pass my hat - he came out of the yard, drank part of his porter, and wanted me to take the rest; I saw some bulk under his coat, and suspected him - he left the house, and in half a minute I missed my hat; I went and overtook him about ten yards off - he ran about two hundred yards; I followed, and saw him stop, then walk on a few paces, and pull my hat from under his coat - I immediately collared him, and gave him in charge; he had his own hat on.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I took it by mistake, being intoxicated. Witness. He was perfectly sober.
   SAMUEL PECK. I am a constable. I received him in charge.
   WILLIAM GLEED. I am an officer. I have a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction - (read) - I was present in this Court when he was convicted, and know he is the man.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had come out of the House of Correction that morning, after eight months' confinement, and not having drank any beer there, the spirits I took had got effect on me; I do not know what occurred.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. - Transported for Fourteen Years.

 1941.  BENJAMIN PHILLIPS and JAMES REARDON were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , 1 half-sovereign, the money of Peter Poland.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   ANGELINA WILCOX. I am in the service of Mr. Peter Poland. On the 12th of July I was at the back of his shop, No. 351, in the Strand, between the hours of twelve and one o'clock - I had counted the money in the till about two hours before; there were eight half-sovereigns and some other money - no other person had been to the till; I saw Reardon come to the door, he appeared to be looking about - he then came into the shop, and asked for a situation; I said I did not know of one - he came about half way into the shop, and I answered him as I sat down - a woman then came into the shop; I got up to answer her, and found Phillips in the shop, with his hand in the till - that was a few minutes after Reardon had asked for a situation: I did not see Reardon when Phillips was at the till - I asked Phillips to give me the money he had taken from the till; he said he had none; I asked him again - he said he had not, and walked out; the officer took him, and took a half-sovereign from his hand.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not at first say you had lost three half-sovereigns? A. Yes, but I afterwards recollected I had given out two at the time I counted the money; there had been ten, before I gave out the two, and I left eight - there had been no customers in - the till was not locked; there was another person there who attended to the shop, but she had not been to the till - Phillips was laying across the counter, and I saw his hand in the till.
   JOHN KENT(Police-constable C 171). I was in the Strand, and observed the two prisoners on the other side of the way, not far from Mr. Warren's; I followed them - they were in company; they went, I suppose, into twenty different shops, and when they came out, I or my brother officer went and inquired what they had been for; when they came to Mr. Poland's, I saw Phillips go in, and Reardon stood at the window, and make several motions with his head and his fingers; Phillips then came out, and Reardon went in - he then came out, and went a little way off; a woman then went in, and we crossed - Phillips was just coming out, and Stone took him - I went and took Reardon, who was then running.
 GEORGE STONE. I was with Kent, and saw Phillips go into the shop - Reardon waited outside, and made motions to him; then Reardon went in, and came out again - a woman then went in; Phillips came out - I stopped him, and found a half-sovereign in his right hand.
 Cross-examined. Q. I believe you caught Phillips by the throat? A. No; we were both in plain clothes.
 Phillips' Defence. The young lady said she missed three half-sovereigns, and the officer seized my throat, put his fingers into my mouth, and nearly strangled me.
 Reardon's Defence. I went to get a situation, and when I came out, the officer took me.
 PHILLIPS -    GUILTY. Aged 18.
 REARDON -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
