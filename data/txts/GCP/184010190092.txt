MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH MATILDA GRAY. I am the wife of William Gray, and line
with him at Northampton. On the 6th of October I came with Ma
Hickson and her little boy, by the Gravesend steam-packet, to London-1
arrived at half-past five o'clock, and got to Fish-street-hill, andthere
took a hackney-coach, which the prisoner drove- I had a deal box among
my luggage, with a wrapper over the top and sides-it was placed on the
foot-board of the coach- I was driven to No. 20, West Smithfield-I
down, and my friend paid the coach-I supposed my luggage was out of the
coach, but I missed the box a very short time after the coach was gone-the
things stated in the indictment were in it, and amongst them a pair of mil-
tens, marked with gold and silver on the backs-1 have a pair here (or
ducing a pair) bought at the same time, and of the same pattern-there
were some things of Mrs. Hickson's with mine-her husband's name i
William-there was a cap, two yards of lace, and two collars belonging
her-I saw the prisoner again on the Thursday, two days after, in Nev
gate-street, and knew him immediately-he was driving a hackney-coach-
I took the number of it, and gave the number to Mr. Hickson, and he is
formed the police of it-the value of my things is about 13., and Mr.
Hickson's, about 31.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. What boat did you come by! 4
The Topaz—that left Gravesend about three o'clock ---we arrived about
hall-past five o'clock, and then took this coach-I cannot say how long
was getting to Smithfield-it was about six o'clock-I saw the prisoner
father in custody before the Justice, and the father was let off-I saw
man plainly-I believe the prisoner is the man.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Have you any doubt at all about it? A. No-
his father was an old man-I never said his father was the man.
