CAROLINE THOMPSON. I am the daughter of Jam's Thompson, who lives in Pleasant-row. On the night of the 10th of March I went for a pot of beer to Mr. Dodds's, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening—I saw the two prisoners and two young women talking together—I am sure the prisoners are the two men—I then went into Mr. Dodds's to get the beer, and there was a person there I knew—I stood there a good bit, as they were serving some more persons—I went to the door to see if a person was coming home to our house, and saw Coleman standing by the window, looking in—I kept my back to the counter, and saw him unhook the brass guards, and run down Effingham-place with them—I am sure Button was the other person who was standing with Coleman and the girls when I went in.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you go to the station-house? A.  I told Eli zabeth, the bar-maid, and Mr. Dodds's pot-boy came for me the next morning—I had never seen Button before—I did not say that I was not sure of him, nor that I could not be sure about him—I never said that, to my knowledge—I never said that I had a doubt about it—I did not know Lovell before this—he has not talked to me about this before I came here.
   Q.  Did you never talk to him at all about Button since you have been here? A.  I cannot swear to that—I do not know that he said any thing to me about Button—he might, or he might not—I do not think he asked me if Button was the man—I did not say to Lovell, or to any one, that I was not certain Button was the man—I am quite sure he is the man, because I saw him. JOSEPH TANNER. I am servant to Mr. Dodds. Caroline Thompson gave an alarm, and I heard a cracking, like the breaking of tobacco-pipes—I ran out and saw the two prisoners breaking up our brasses—I am quite certain of their persons—I seized Coleman, and got this brass from him—he dragged me down Effingham-place, and threw me over the wagon-shaft—my master came up, and he was taken—Button stopped in Effingham place, and came back—Coleman spoke to Button, and then Button got into the crowd—as they were taking Coleman to the station-house, I looked round and saw Button.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you were before the Magistrate did you say a single word about Button coming back to Coleman, and speaking to him? A.  Yes—I do not know whether it was taken down—I did not hear it read over—my master was present when the prisoners spoke to one another—no brass was found on Button—he said he knew nothing about it—Cole man said he would punch  my head if I did not let him go.
   JURY . Q.  When you went out and caught Coleman, was there light enough for you to see the other prisoner? A.  Yes; it was gas-light.
