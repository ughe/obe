 1696.  THOMAS McNAMARA was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 1s., and 1 curtain, value 2d., the goods of Robert Tims.
   JOHN WEATHERLEY. I am an officer. I met the prisoner on the 8th of July, in Regent's Park, with a bundle - I asked what it was; he said he had bought a coat that evening; that it was his own, and he would not shew it to anybody; he tripped me up and ran away; I followed and secured him; he still had the parcel - he tore the string, and threw the papers about - we found the curtain and handkerchief in his cap.
 ROBERT TIMS. I am a  carpenter, and live in Edward-street, Regent's Park. These things are mine - I cannot say when I had seen them last - I had been removing from No. 1 to No. 2, and am not certain where these things were - they were usually kept in a chest in my bed-room; the prisoner was working for me as a bricklayer's labourer- I paid him on the evening of the 8th of July - and in the morning, when I awoke, I found the chest wrenched open by a chisel - I had gone to bed without a light - there was more valuable property and some money left in the chest - the prisoner had access to the house - these papers were in the trunk - he bore a good character.
 Prisoner's Defence. I found the things a few yards from the public-house, where I was paid.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined Six Weeks.
 1697.  THOMAS PHILLIPS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of September , 1 half-crown, 1 shilling, and 5 sixpences, the monies of David Brinn , from his person.
 DAVID BRINN. I am a  stone-sawyer, and live in Fitzroy-place. On the 5th of September, about half-past eleven o'clock at night, I was returning from Covent Garden, where I had been to see my uncle; I met the prisoner by the Seven Dials - I was a little in liquor, and asked him to shew me the way to Tottenham-court-road, and I would give him 6d. to drink with; we went into a house and had something - when we came out, he asked if I knew the way; I said Yes; but asked him to go further - when we got beyond Tottenham-court chapel, we went into a house, and had a quartern of rum - when I came out I wished him good night - I felt his hand in my pocket - I put my hand to it; he pulled his hand out, and slapped my face, and said, "You are no use, and I am off;" I swear his hand was in my pocket - I missed a half-crown, a shilling, and five sixpences - I ran after him, calling Watch! I lost sight of him once in turning the corner; but I heard the watchman say "We have got him;" I went up and found him in charge - the money I lost was found on him.
   JOHN THOMPSON. About half-past eleven o'clock I heard Watch! called - I saw the prisoner running; I stopped him, and took him to the watch-house - I saw a half-crown, a shilling, and five sixpences found on him.
   JOHN JONES. I found this money on the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Life.
 1698.  RICHARD REEVES was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of April , 1 warrant, for payment of and value 52l. 10s., the property of Daniel Magniac. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1699.  THOMAS ROBERTS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , 1 silver cream-jug, value 30s., the goods of Andrew Luriottis.
   FRANCIS TITE. I am servant to Andrew Luriottis, he lives in Sackville-street. On the 26th of August, at a quarter before eleven o'clock, I saw the prisoner in the area - the gate was shut, but the people in the kitchen opened it for him - he said he came to know if there were any orders for fish - but he was quite a stranger - the bell rang, and I went away for about three minutes - on my return he was still there; but as he was going out, I saw some milk on his coal-sleeve - I then looked on the tray, and the silver cream ewer was gone - I ran out at the front-door, and overtook him running about the middle of the street - I found this milk ewer in his hat.
   GEORGE SMITH. I am the officer. I took charge of the prisoner, and have the property.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Transported for Seven Years.
 1700.  WILLIAM JOHN RICE was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of June , 4 pieces of glass, value 20s., the goods of Henry Bingham.
   JOHN CULLIFORD. I am a watchman. I met the prisoner in Lisle-street, Leicester-square, on the 1st of June, at half-past two o'clock in the morning, with a bag under his arm - he looked at me, and went on into Little Newport-street - I went up to him and said, "I think you have got something that is not all right;" he threw it at me - I caught it in my arms - it contained this glass; he ran off; but was not a quarter of an hour out of my sight - I gave the alarm, and he was taken.
   JOHN MASON. I heard the cry of Stop thief! I took the prisoner.
   ROBERT HOWARD. I am a constable. I was at the watch-house, and received him in charge.
 HENRY BINGHAM. I live in  Ryder's-court. These pieces of glass are mine, and worth about 1l.; they were in a cupboard, in a lumber-room.
 Prisoner's Defence. They were given to me by a man to carry - Mr. Bingham told my mother that if I wanted to get released, I had better say who he was - I did so; it was Phillips, who I went to to sell my coat - he sent a boy with me, who brought the property out.
