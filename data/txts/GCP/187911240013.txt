JOSEPH Dx CARLE. I am employed at the Camden Town goods station,
London and North-Western Railway Company-I produce a consign-
ment note in respect of leather goods dispatched to Northampton from
that station on 13th December, 1878; they left in due course, these are
my figures on this memorandum (Those toere consigned to L. Collins, Over
stone, Northampton, from W. Foley, 10, Claro Terrass, Brompton).
WILLIAM FOLEY. I did live in Blackfriars Road, and am now living at 10,
North Street, Holborn, and am a boot salesman-in December, 1878, I
was employed at Mrs. Ellis's, 10, Olaro Terrace--I knew Lordan seven or
eight years ago I only knew Mannion as a relation-I had no connec-
tion with him; I knew Watson only by seeing him at Worship Street
once or twice-I think I have seen him at Olaro Terrace once
twice-I knew Summers as living at Claro Terrace I saw him there
about 5th December, 1878_I believe he had a conversation with
Mr. Edwards there-ke told me that he expected & van; he did not say
where from a few minutes afterwards a van arrived-I asked him in a
general way how they were getting on at Worship Street; I understood
him to say that it belonged to a Mr. Hammersley when the van arrived
he asked permission of Mr. Edwards to unload it-Mr. Edwards had the
shop at that time; he had bought the business of Mrs. Ellis-permission
Was given, and I helped to unload the van; there were some bales of
leather skins and about 12 bales of butte Bummers said that he had
come from Worship Street-I think he said he had made himself answer
able for some money ; 250l. I think it was, and he had got these as
security from Hammersley- he was present while the van was being
unloaded—the leather was put in the garden at the baok of the house,
and in the afternoon it was removed from there to West's Repository
- about a fortnight afterwards I saw Summers, and went to West's
Repository, and sorted the leather there, and I believe it was taken to
the Camden Town Station to go to Northampton-Summers gave me the
address- I called his attention that there was no name that it came
from, and he said "Put your own, it will be all right”-some of the
goods remained at the Repository, and I took those to Watson Austin's
place in Earl's Court in accordance with Summers's instructions.
