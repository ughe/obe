   Q.  Is this your hand-writing?—( handing him an affidavit .) A.  Yes, it is—I recollect signing it—I read it—I might not have read the whole very particularly, but I swore to it, and signed it—it is the writing of Mr. Rumsey Williams—I am not aware that he got it made out and prepared—it was prepared in his office.
   Q.  Will you now swear that on the 18th of July you did not know that there was a will and other testamentary documents of the deceased in possession of Thomas Williams the prisoner? A.  I do not recollect—that is the only answer I can give, to the best of my knowledge and re membrance—till the day of the sale I had no idea whatever that my will was to be disputed—I have no idea of the value of the property in dispute between me and my brother-in-law—it may be from 50, 000 l . to 60, 000 l .—the real estate may be about the half of that—I do not believe that the personalty is worth more than 25, 000 l ., it may be more, I cannot say pre cisely.
   Q.  Do you recollect this paper ( showing him one ) coming down to the country? A.  Yes, it is a commission to take this affidavit—I saw it and read it before I swore the affidavit—I knew of no will of my father before his death, but this one which was made for him by Mr. Jones—I never heard of any other—I never heard of any will made by Mr. Thomas Williams, of Beaumaris—my father never mentioned that he had one prepared by Mr. Williams—he never mentioned that there had been two made by Mr. Williams—I never heard of it.
   Q.  Did you know of Mr. Williams having made the codicil in 1831? A.  I know my father went to Mr. Williams that year, but I do not know what it was about—it might be about the year 1830 or 1831, that I first began to show attention and pay my addresses to Mr. Rumsey Williams's daughter—it was before 1830—I had known the family some time—it was known to Mr. Williams before 1830, that I was paying attention to his daughter—I used to go to Penross to visit her—not very often—Mr. Williams sometimes saw me, and was aware of the object of my visit—that was before my father went to Mr. Williams in 1831—I had nothing to do with bringing Mr. Rumsey Williams and my father together—my father did not tell me of Mr. Williams having made a codicil to his will, nor did any one else—I do not know that my father went there to have his will made or altered—be told me the contents of the will under which I now claim, just before his death—he had told me that I should have the whole of his property.
   Q.  Did he not tell you so in 1826? A.  He told me so often, and often he told me he had made a will in my favour—he did not tell me so in the life of my brother Jones, but he did after his death—he said my sister, Mrs. Hamilton, was very well off, that she had married a gentleman of good property—he was not on very good terms with Mrs. Hamilton—there was no quarrel—my father used to pay a visit to Brynbras Castle once in the year perhaps—he staid over the night and returned—that was only once a year—he was not fond of being away—Mr. and Mrs. Williams used to come to Plasgwyn—they once stopped a night there—the prisoner was in the profession of the law
