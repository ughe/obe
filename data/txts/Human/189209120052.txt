   Cross-examined.  The doctor was present when we came, and saw the dead man lying there—I and the doctor went there first—it might have been about half-past one when we went on board the ship, and then De Ryter had three little beads of blood on the back of his hand—the blood was not dry—the bruise over the eye appeared to have been done quite recently—it seemed red and raw; I should think the skin was broken—so far as I could see it had been bleeding—De Ryter said, when I pointed out the dead man, "No me," not "Me know"—I said before the Coroner that when I first examined De Ryter he was in a dazed condition, covered in sweat—he may have been roused out of sleep and suffering from drink—he seemed to take no notice when I said that anything he said might be used against him—I was in uniform—I said, "Come along" before he got out of his bunk, and when he had got out I took his arm; he took his cap and came peaceably with me. THOMAS BARRETT(42 K R ).
