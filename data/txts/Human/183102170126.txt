ROGER RAGAN. I am a Police-constable. On the 22nd of January I was in Long-alley at two o'clock in the morning, and saw the two prisoners coming along with two sacks of soot; I questioned them as to where they got it - they said where they had been at work, at the London tavern; I said, "I can't believe you - I think you would not be at the London tavern at this time in the morning;" they said if I was not satisfied I might go with them, and I went to No. 10, Cock-alley, where they shot the soot out on the floor of an empty room - I asked if they occupied that room; they said they did, but one of the witnesses came down, and said, "Good God! what brought you here at this time in the morning?" I asked if they lived there, and she said No- they then said they had taken it of the landlady the day before; I gave an alarm, and Stephen Renew came up - I left him in charge of the property, and took Moore to the station-house - in going along he told me it was his own property; I could not see Jennings at the time I took Moore, for he had got up the chimney - I brought Moore back from the station-house to the room, and then I found Jennings there - that was half an hour afterwards, or rather more.
   STEPHEN RENEW. I live at No. 15, Great George-street, Mile-end, and am a watchman. Ragan called me into the house in Cock-alley; I saw Moore there, but I did not then see Jennings: after Moore had been taken to the station I saw some mortar fall down the chimney - a boy named Onion came in; I told him to look up the chimney - Jennings then came down; Moore was afterwards brought back, and then both the prisoners were taken.
 Jennings. I was not up the chimney - I sat on the stove.
   SAMUEL ONION. I went in, and looked up the chimney - I saw Jennings there; I had known him before - he went by the name of Ben Matthews; I said, "I know who it is - it is Ben Matthews- you may as well come down;" he made no answer: I found some shoes in the yard where Spearing's shed is, but I do not know of either of the prisoners wearing them.
   ROSANNA KEMP. I live at No. 10, Cock-alley, Nortonfalgate. I heard a noise about two o'clock in the morning - I went down, and saw the officer, the two prisoners, and a quantity of soot in a room; I had seen them there, but they had not lived there.
   JOHN BURNESS. I live at No. 2, Cock-alley. About half-past three o'clock that morning I went to No. 10 - I saw Renew there, and a quantity of soot; the officer afterwards brought Moore in; I got a lantern, and tracked some soot about a hundred yards from No. 10, Cock-alley, to about forty yards from the prosecutor's shed - the track ceased in Long-alley; I suppose the sacks had been opened there - there was no track at the door of Spearing's shed; the track ceased very near Smith's-buildings - I traced it through the passage of a double house, No. 14, both the doors of which were open; I saw the staple and lock were off the shed, and there was nothing to prevent a person then from going in - I found a piece of tin, which appeared to have been used as a shovel.
   ROGER RAGAN. I walked through the double house; when I first saw the prisoners they were in Long-alley, and appeared to be coming in a direction from the shed, which Spearing says had been broken - I think they were sixty yards off it.
   HENRY WRIGHT. I am foreman to Thomas Spearing. I compared the soot found at Cock-alley with that on my master's premises; I believe it had formed a part of it -I had mixed it for my master; I saw at Cock-alley about seven pecks shot out, and one sack which had not been shot out - it was worth 7 1/2d. a bushel; my master did not miss any sack - the soot might have been mixed by other persons - I did not find near as much soot as was missing from the shed; I suppose there were thirty sacks of soot in the shed.
 Jennings. We were doing work for ourselves, and we had been out to try to get a little soot - we put it down against the wall by the London tavern; that was what I told the officer, and that it belonged to me.
 JURY to ROGER RAGAN. Q. Did you say you saw two sacks? A. Yes - I went with them to the room; I then took a short turn round my beat and returned - part of the soot had been conveyed away; the sacks appeared full when I first got up to them.
 COURT. Q. How long were you absent? A. About five minutes - the prisoners went out, and said they were going to the London tavern for more.
     NOT GUILTY.
 647.  JOSEPH MOORE was again indicted for stealing, on the 15th of January , 36 bushels of soot, value 30s., the goods of Susannah Collins.
 SUSANNAH COLLINS. I live at No. 1, Sharp's-alley, and am a  widow. I have a shed near Grey Eagle-street, which was secured by an iron padlock - I went there about eight o'clock in the morning, on the 15th of January, found it had been broken open, and missed about twelve sacks of soot; I found a handkerchief, which I gave to the officer- I had seen my shed safe on the night before; the soot I missed was worth 30s., it is 2s. 6d. a sack - I traced some sooty foot-marks, from Grey Eagle-street to the top of Pearl-street, opposite Fleur-de-lis-court; it was a damp morning, and that was the reason I suppose.
   SAMUEL ONION. I am an apprentice to Mr. Peter Savage, Moore's-alley, Norton-falgate - he is a chimney-sweep. I have known the prisoner these five years; he was in the habit of bringing soot to my master, about six months before he was taken - when he was engaged in shooting the soot, I used to hold his cap, and I used to see a handkerchief in it; I observed it particularly - I noticed a tear in the corner, and three letters on it; I have had it twenty times in my hand - there was a cook-shop at the corner of Moore's-alley, which he used to frequent, and I have seen him use the handkerchief there; I remember on one occasion, we were standing at the corner of Cock-alley, and he had it in his hand - we used to call him by the nickname of Buss, and I said, "Buss, let me look at your handkerchief;" I looked at it, and said, "It is torn at the corner;" he said, "I know it is;" there were three letters on it in a straight line - this is the handkerchief, here is the tear; here are three letters in a straight line, and one below; I had not noticed the one below at that time.
 Prisoner. Q. When did I show you the handkerchief? A. I cannot rightly tell; it was about three days before this happened.
