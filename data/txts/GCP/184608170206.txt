Q. Suppose the prisoner, right or wrong, took coals for sale to his
friends at the same price as Moore, would these entries be right? A. No,
the pool price on the 13th of Jan. was 188. 6d. a ton--the price charged
in the book is 4s. 6d. extra, 238., but the price charged in the bill is
258.--that would make a difference of 68. 6d.- the entry at the trade
price would be right, all except 6d., if he was allowing 4s. 6d. it would
be right-he does not account for the last entry in the cash-book--the
pool price here was 168. then-48. 6d. added to that, would make 20s. 6d.
--three tons would be 31. 18. 6.- I had not quarrelled with him before
he left-Mr. Moore was constantly seeing the books—I have the ledger
here-the entries are Moore's writing-after the prisoner left I went to
Mr. Nash's public-house--1 do not know that the prisoner lodged there
- there was a removal of some of his goods—my wife had given his wife
some things—she was anxious that they should not appear in a public-house
among his wardrobe, and I went to Mr. Nash's—the prisoner called after
wards, and asked what I meant by overhauling his things I parted with him
under the best of feelings-this might be a fortnight or ten days after he left
- he had improved my business considered him an excellent servant-
after he left some of my customers told me he had solicited them for a new
master-he went into the service of Mr. Wright, who took a wharf 500 ar
600 yards from mine.
Q. Did Wright commence business there the very day you took the pr.
soner up? A. I cannot recollect, but as soon as I became acquainted with
those facts I went to my solicitor-a great many of my customers have gone
to Wright--some of them are now returning-they were hucksters who came
to the wharf for their coals—it was his usual business to treat with them, and
receive their money-I know nothing of Watts-he had not been a customer
of mine-I very likely saw Mr. Wright at the Coal exchange on the 21st of
May-I do not recollect saying to him, "I see you are going to be a neigh
bour of mine, and take my late clerk into your service"-I will not swear!
did not-he did not say, "Yes, is there anything against his honesty ?" ---
made no reference to character, nor did I say, "Not the slightest"-he mig
have said, "Have you anything to say against him?" and I studiously
avoided giving any answer.
Q. Did not you say "Not the slightest ?" A. Certainly not-it is im-
possible for a man to recollect everything he says--I cannot recollect whether
I did or not-it was after that I discovered this - the prisoner's brother called
on me on a Saturday.
