 259.  GEORGE PETERS was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of November , 1 batten, value 1s. 6d., the goods of William Reed.
   WILLIAM REED. I live in Hare-street, Bethnal-green, and am a weaver. I lost a batten on the 2nd of November, and found it at Mr. Cluff's: I had seen the prisoner before.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When had you seen it? A. I left it safe at a quarter before twelve o'clock, when I went out.
   WILLIAM CLUFF. I keep a broker's-shop in Cheshire-street. On the 2nd of November, about twenty minutes past twelve o'clock, the prisoner brought a pair of battens; he asked 1s. for them - I did not know they were stolen; I went out from two to four, and when I came home there were fifty people round my house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you deal in a great variety of articles? A. Yes; I bought the battens of the prisoner, in the name of William Jones - I pay ready money; William Cooper is my servant, and was by my side when I bought them: the prisoner was bailed by the Magistrate.
   WILLIAM COOPER. I am servant to Mr. Cluff. On the 2nd of November the prisoner sold the battens to my master - he asked 1s. for them, which my master gave him; I had not known him before; my master put it down in the book, "Jones 1s."
 Cross-examined. Q.Is the book here? A. No - this was about twenty minutes after twelve o'clock; I was not at home when they were claimed.
   SAMUEL MOORE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner about four o'clock on the 2nd of November.
 Cross-examined. Q.What day of the week was it? A. On Wednesday, Mr. Reed gave me charge of him - he was bailed by the Magistrate.
 Witness for the Defence.
   ELIZABETH MILLER. On the 2nd of November I was at work at No. 101, Green-street; the prisoner came there at half-past ten o'clock that morning - he brought home a bird-cage which I had lent him; he left my place from thirty to thirty-five minutes after eleven - Cheshire-street is about a mile and a quarter from there.
   HENRY BLUMSER. I am a silk-mercer, and live at No. 1, Bacon-street, Bethnal-green. On Wednesday, the 2nd of November, I saw the prisoner sitting at his father's table, at dinner, at ten minutes past twelve o'clock; his father lived at No. 3, Cheshire-street - I staid with him till five minutes past one; he did not leave the room the whole of that time, upon my oath.
     NOT GUILTY.
 260.  JOHN LODGE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November , 3 half-crowns, 4 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of Mary Ann Lodge ; and 1 pair of shoes, value 3s., the goods of Emma Lodge.
 MARY  ANN LODGE. I am the prisoner's sister.    EMMA LODGE. I am the prisoner's sister. These are my shoes, and I know the money was in the trunk.
   JAMES BUCKLE I am an officer. The witnesses sent for me, and gave the prisoner into custody; he had a new jacket on, which he said he bought in Golden-lane, and he said that he had bought a pair of trousers which he gave to another boy, and the rest of the money he had spent in a coffee shop - he said he might as well take his sisters money as any body's; he has been dreadfully neglected: the two young woman bear good characters, and one of them left her place, at Mr. Baker's, to attend to the prisoner.
 GUILTY. Aged 15. -    Judgment Respited.
