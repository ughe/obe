On Sunday, the 24th of March, the prosecutor called on me—he inquired about the prisoner's cha racter—he did not say one word about his having a charge against him for stealing handkerchiefs—he told me at the office that he would get the pri soner sent to Newgate, to prevent an action being brought; and that when he got him there he would try to get him off.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  I believe the prosecutor had a character of the prisoner from you? A.  Yes, he had—when he came to me on the Sunday, be came to know whether I had any suspicion of the prisoner, and said he had robbed him of 13 s . 6 d .—my wife and my wife's mother were present—he did not say that as they had threatened to bring an action against him, be must go on with the prosecution—I said, "Has not he robbed you of any goods?"—he said, "No, I am sorry to say he has not, because the most distressing thing of all is, the boy, who he says, gave him the money, has gone away, and his parents are distressed, as he has not returned"—he said to me, at the police-office, "It may appear harsh to you to go on in this case, but, unless I get the boy convicted, I shall be liable to an action." NOT GUILTY.
 1387. ELIZABATH MURPHY was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of April , 1 tongue, value 8 d  ., the goods of Daniel Lucas . DANIEL LUCAS. I live in Calmel-buildings, and am a grocer. About twelve o'clock on Saturday night, the 6th of April, the prisoner came to purchase some tea and sugar—she paid, and was about to leave the shop—she then said she had lost a thimble—my young man took the candle to go and look by the counter for it—I saw the prisoner take a tongue, and put it into her apron—I said, "What have you got in your apron?"—she said, nothing but what she had paid me for—I took down her apron, and found this tongue—she got outside—I called the officer, and gave her in charge.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Had you know her before? A.  I had—she was not intoxicated. GUILTY  .— Recommended to mercy. — Confined Nine Days.
