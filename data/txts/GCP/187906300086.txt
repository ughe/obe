rules where we can take proceedings against men who do not give up
books and documents-if he wanted to bank money he could have got the
book on May 31st, at the ordinary branch meeting-we were very glad to
get the 231. odd, for the prisoner's sake--I have heard that 331. 118. was put
on the table--the meeting lasted about half an hour-when I found out
about there being another 101. wrong the matter was out of my hands-I
don't know why Hinckston was not called at the police-court.
Re-examined. At the time of the meeting I had not the slightest idea
that the prisoner had forged the deposit book-the 231. 98. 11d. was the
balance shown by the books of the society-I should not expect at that
meeting that 331. odd would be paid-it appears from his book that he paid
two sums of 151., viz., one on 3rd September and the other on the 17th-
the one on the 17th is the one he says he paid on the 24th-credit was
given for both those sums in the book.
JOHN PRYOR. I live at 97, Brunswick Street, Hardwick Green,
Manchester, and am General Secretary of the Amalgamated Society of
Carpenters and Joiners--I produce a certified copy of the rules upon which
I instituted legal proceedings—there is a rule in reference to deficiencies by
the treasurer by which we can take summary proceedings.
Cross-examined. It would be under rule 47 that proceedings would be
taken.
JOHN JAGO. I am a member of the Paddington branch of the
Amalgamated Society of Carpenters and Joiners, and was formerly branch
secretary-the prisoner told me that he had banked two sums of 151, during
the September quarter that I was secretary-the two sums appear in that
book.
Cross-examined. I do not know that he has banked as much as 361. at a
time.
WILLIAM CRANE (Detective Sergeant X). I arrested the prisoner on 6th
June, and told him he would be charged with embezzling 101., the moneys
of the Amalgamated Society of Carpenters and Joiners-he made no reply,
but in the waiting room of the Marylebone Police-court he made a state-
ment to me, which I took down at the time---previous to his making the
statement I told him that whatever he told me I should tell the Magistrate-
he said: "I have been in very great trouble, that is why I took the money; I
was 331. 108. deficient in my accounts; Mr. Hinckston ought to have pald
the money when he had it; if he had done so I should not have been in
this trouble. I borrowed a cheque for the amount from Mr. Curtis, of 22,
Abbey Gardens, St. John's Wood, and gave it to Hinckston; he never paid
it in.
