BEFORE JUDGE LUMLEY SMITH.
(Monday, January 22.)
DAWES, Frederick (31, grocer), and TRUMAN, Richard (31,
grocer), forging and uttering an endorsement on an order for the pay:
ment of money, to wit, a banker's cheque for the sum of E50 with
intent to defraud.
Mr. C. G. Moran prosecuted; Mr. Tully-Christie defended Dawes.
Dr. HENRY WATSON TURNER, 59, Wimpole Street, W. Lowed 8 Mr.
Snook 2118. I sent him a cheque for E50 dated September 2 drawn
on Barelay and Co., Norwich. It was paid into my bank on Sep-
tember 7. I identify the cheque.
ALFRED SNOoK, 73, Liverpool Road, Islington. I did not receive
the cheque. The endorsement is not mine.
LAURA WEBsTer, jeweller, 18, Brook Street, W. In the beginning
of December Dawes called on me about buying some jewellery. He
gave the name of Brooks and said he same from Australia. He came
once or twice alone ; then he brought Truman. He purchased jewellery
to the value of E20 and gave me the cheque for E50. I said we could
not take it because it was not his own. He said it was a cheque of a
friend of his. I said, Why don't you pass it through your own
bank He said he did not want his father to know he was friends
with the man. He said his friend owed him money. Truman gave the
name of Sidney Lewis and brought a business card with that name on.
Cross-examined by Mr. Tully Christie. Dawes came about five
times before he brought the cheque. Truman had been in twice before
the jewellery was hought. I do not know how many days it took to
clear the cheque When it was cleared Dawes came in and
I handed him the goods and my cheque for E30. He said he was an
old customer, that he knew us when we were in Queen Victoria Street.
L looked up the books, but could not find the name Brooks. I could
not make it out, but then I do not know the customers. After he had
taken the goods he came back about another ring. We went to the
Cafe Koyal in a taxi-cab, where I had a sandwich and something to
drink. My brother goes there every Saturday and I was in hopes I
should see him there, as I was getting nervous about the cheque and
thought I could find out something At the police court they both
said they wwere the men who gave me the cheque.
Cross-examined by Truman. I think you were present when
Dawes presented the cheque. I saw the cheque was marked 4 Not
negotiable," or I should not have taken it. You were present and
helped Dawes to choose the chain.
