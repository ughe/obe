This concluding the case for the prosecution,
Mr. Justice Coleridge asked if there was any suggestion / motive.
Mr. Bickmore submitted that there was sufficient evidence of a quarrel
between prisoner and his wife.
Mt. Stewart submitted that there was no case to go to the jury.
Mr. Justice Coleridge declined to withdraw the case from the jury.
JORN MACINTOSH (prisoner, on oath). On the day in question
I went to see my wife, who was staying with her mother'; my wife was
* there. I told my mother-in-law (who is here to give evidence) that
I ww going round to our lodgings and would wait there for my wife.
I went home, went straight up to our room, and sat by the window,
from which I could see my wife as she came in. I took out &
garette and lit it; as I jerked my hand to put the match out it
stuck in the curtain, and the lighted part broke off in the curtain;
the timsy stuff caught light at once; in trying to put it out I dragged
the whole lot down. It fell all over me and burnt my hands and
arms. (I showed the policeman where I was burnt.) It fell in little
pieces, and these set fire to some other flimsy stuff round the dressing-
table and the bed (accounting for the appearances of separate fires).
Finding I could not put out the flames, because it all burnt so quick,
I ran out to go to the fire station. I was naturally very excited. If
I said to the officer "I know things look black against me,” it was
because I knew that people would imagine I had done it because of my
quarrel with my wife. I swear that the whole thing was an
doident.
Cross-examined. I did say to Mrs. Olney, "Good God, Mrs.
Oney, I have set the place on fire.”
Mr. Stewart was about to call prisoner's mother-in-law, when
The Jury stopped the case and returned a verdict of Not guilty.
Ir. Justice Coleridge intimated that he quite agreed with the verdict.
