 923.  THOMAS WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of April , 1 looking-glass, value 16s., the goods of William Smith.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I live in York-street, Westminster. I lost this glass on Saturday evening, the 11th of April, about ten minutes before seven o'clock; it had been about four feet inside the front of my shop.
   THOMAS TRENT. I am in the Coldstream Guards. I saw the prisoner reach into the shop and take this glass - I took him and brought him back - there was another young man with him who threw a sack over the glass when the prisoner put it down on the stones.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been to Pimlico and was returning - the witness came and said I had stolen the glass, but I had not seen it.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Whipped and Discharged.
 934.  JONAS SIMONS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 1 crown, and 1 half-crown, the monies of William Arnold Hadden Hows.
   JOHN COOK. I am foreman to Mr. Hows, a pawnbroker, of High-street, St. Giles'. On the 17th of February the prisoner came into one of the private boxes, and asked the shopman to give him a sovereign for a pounds worth of silver; the shopman is not here; I saw him give him the sovereign - he did not seem satisfied, and said, as well as he could speak in broken English, that he wanted it changed for two half-sovereigns; he gave him the two half-sovereigns and then he did not seem satisfied, but wanted one of the half-sovereigns changed for silver; the shopman pulled out the till to do so; the till had been out before - it was just under the counter, opposite the private boxes; he then put the half-sovereign on the counter, put his hand into the till, and said in broken English, how easily any body might help themselves; I saw him draw his hand from the till, and put it down by the side of his trousers pocket; he then took his hand from his side, and put it into the till a second time; I had not seen him put any thing into his pocket, but he was saying something which we could not understand; I watched him more sharply - I was standing on his right-hand side, and saw between his thumb and the palm of his right hand a crown-piece, two half-crowns, and some other silver, which he drew from the till, and put into his right-hand breeches pocket; the shopman and I together accused him of it - he said something that we could not understand, and shifted his hand from his breeches pocket to the side pocket of his coat, or to that part of his person - his hand was under his coat on his left side: Mr. Hows sent for an officer and had him taken - this half-sovereign and the 10s. were laid on the counter during the scuffle.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Have you always given the same account of this? A. Yes; I did not state the prisoner asked us to give him change for a sovereign; he wanted a sovereign for silver; he spoke English very imperfectly - we had some difficulty in understanding what he wanted; he pointed to the drawer, that he wanted 10s. for a half-sovereign; I saw the two half-crowns and the crown in his hand, and some more silver; I swear I saw shillings and sixpences in his hand - there were, I suppose, 5l., 6l., or 7l. in the till - it was not so very large; a female was on the same side of the counter as he was - he could see her if he looked round; she is not here, she has left her lodgings; the prisoner made great resistance; Mr. Hows jumped over the counter and seized him, and they were scuffling when I left; he put his hand across to show what sort of silver he wanted - the counter is about four feet high, and about two feet and a half wide - and the till was opposite him.
   THOMAS WRIGHT. I was sent for, and found the prisoner and Mr. Hows scuffling together; I took the prisoner into the back parlour, and from his left-hand waistcoat pocket I took 2l. 13s. in silver - there was one crownpiece, six half-crowns, and shillings and sixpences; I began to feel at his trousers pocket, and from his left-hand, trousers pocket he drew a purse which contained money -I took it to Marlborough street, where Mr. Plank opened it - it contained thirty-six sovereigns and a half; I do not know why the woman is not here; I went to her lodgings several times, and last Saturday the landlady told me that a Jewess had been there and given her money.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had not the prisoner a pack of goods near him? A. Yes, he had: I did not search his coat pockets till we got to Marlborough-street.
   WILLIAM ARNOLD HADDEN HOWS. I am the owner of the shop. The prisoner wade great resistance, and wished to get away.
 Cross-examined. Q. He is a foreigner - did you explain to him the object you had in view? A. I did not.
 Prisoner's Defence. (Through an interpreter.) I am innocent; I did not want any thing of the kind - I had plenty of money.
     NOT GUILTY.
