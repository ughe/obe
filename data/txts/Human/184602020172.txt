 637. SAMUEL OVERY was indicted for that he, on the   13th of Nov ., feloniously did assault Celia Overy , and strike her on the head, thereby causing her a bodily injury dangerous to life, with intent'to murder her.—Two other COUNTS  , for wounding, with intent to do grievous bodily harm.
   MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution. CELIA OVERY. I am about thirteen years old, and live with the prisoner, who is my father, at Dockhcad—I have a mother-in-law. On the day this happened I had my mother's baby—she went out, and left it at home—I cleaned the fire-place, and stirred the fire with the poker, and left it in the fire—it was in the morning—the baby was playing on the floor—it cannot walk, but can crawl—my father was in the room—he had not been out—he got the poker out of the fire, drove me into the middle of the room, and put my two hands together in his right hand—he hit me on the forehead with the poker, which was in his left hand, a good many times—he did not say a word to me—I had not spoken a word to him—he hit me on the head when he moved me into the middle of the room—he had been sitting on a chair by the fire before that—he works at home, at shoemaking, but not in that room—he has not been at work since Whit Monday, half a year ago—I do not know what hap pened after I was struck—when I came to my senses I was in the hospital—this is the poker now produced—I remember it well—here is the mark where I put it into the fire.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  Do you remember after your father struck you, whether he danced round about you? A.  No, he fell down on the top of the stairs—it is an up-stair room—my father was sometimes kind to me, and sometimes very cross—I ran into the yard, and exclaimed, "Oh my poor father,"—I remember that very well—I had not done anything to make him angry—he said nothing to me before he struck me—he has been very ill in the head since Whit-Monday.
   COURT . Q.  What do you mean by being very ill in the head? A.  I do not know—he did not always keep his bed, he sometimes got up and sat by the fire—I do not know whether he talked sensibly—I never saw a person mad or beside themselves. LOUISA JONES.
