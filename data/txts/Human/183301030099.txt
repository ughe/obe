 COURT. Q. Was any notice left on your premises that the table had been removed? A. No.
 Prisoner. Q. You state you searched my premises; do you know where I live? A. In Dean-street, I believe; the Magistrate told you to go about your business - he said he should know where to find you; I do not know that any young man came and said you were gone in search of the landlord - you did attend afterwards, and ran away again; I desired that the landlord should be produced - I did not hear you do it; I did not see the landlord the last time when Mr. Halls was sitting - there was a good deal of old rubbish in your warehouse; I did not see any thing like my table: my table was the only one that was covered up.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Are you quite sure that before you found it, he denied that he had it? A. Yes.
   RICHARD MOORE(Police-constable F 80). I had a search-warrant, and went to the premises in Bear-street - the prisoner was denied by a young man; I said I came to search for a table which had been lost from Mr. Simmonds', and if he would not let me in, I must make my way - I then went up stairs, and saw the prisoner; I told him I came to search for Mr. Simmonds' table - he said he knew nothing of it, and denied having it on his premises at all; I searched the place in which the prisoner was standing, and found this table under a lot of bed-furniture - it was completely concealed; the prisoner then said he had been authorised to make a distraint for the rent.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not find the outer door open? A. Yes, but you was denied; you were in your working-dress - I believe this case was three times before the Magistrate; I came the last time to give you notice to attend - I did not take you into custody.
   SARAH MARIA SIMMONDS. I am the prosecutor's wife, I went out with my husband; I returned before him - when I came back my door was open, and appeared to have been opened from the inside, and this table was gone; the chairs were set two by two, and the middle door, which had been nailed up, and which I had put paper round, was open.
   HENRY BAKER. I called on Mrs. Simmonds on the evening of the 31st of October, and in consequence of what she said I called on the prisoner - I saw him in a little bit of a counting-house, in his house in Dean-street; I asked if he had made any seizure on Mrs. Simmonds; he said he had not received any instructions - I said there was a table lost; he said he knew nothing about it.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not represent yourself as an attorney. A. Yes, because you have represent yourself as such many times; I told you I should get a warrant, as I had reason to believe you had it - I knew you was difficult to get at; you pointed up to your books, and asked, if I thought you were such a d-d fool as to do that - I wrote down my address and gave it you, and then I tore it up; the Magistrate said to the prosecutor "There is your table, you had better drop it" - I did not hear you offer the table to pay the rent and expenses - you did not tell me that if Mr. Simmonds had any thing to say, he should come and say it.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. Are you quite sure he denied knowing any thing about the table? Yes.
 The distress-warrant was here put in and read.
 Prisoner's Defence. I admit being denied, but I had a reason for it - I was looking over some dirty goods, and was not in a dress fit to see gentlemen, but when the officer said he had a warrant, I said, "Let him come up, be he who he may;" and when he came up and asked for a table, I said, "If there is any table you want, take it."
 WILLIAM KING. I know the warehouse in Bear-street; it belongs to the prisoner's father - it was the prisoner's before it was his - I have been in the habit of going there; I believe the signature to this inventory is his.
 EDWARD SEAL. I signed this paper - I signed it at the prisoner's request.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. What are you? A. A licensed appraiser; I was sworn on this occasion by Wilson, the constable, at the warehouse, in Bear-street, where this table was brought out to me to examine and value - I believe it to be worth about 16s.
 MR. GEORGE COOK. I am a furniture-broker, and am a juror in waiting - this table is worth 1l. 1s.; to sell again, it would fetch at an auction 24s. or 25s.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 426.  JOHN CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of December , 21 lbs. of coffee, value 30s., and 1 bag, value 4d., the goods of Bowen Dunn Meredith.
 BOWEN DUNN MEREDITH. I am a  grocer, and live in Whitechapel. On the afternoon of the 15th of December, I was standing about six yards inside my shop - the prisoner came in, and took a bag of coffee; I pursued him, but he carried it so dexterously that I could not discover he had it till I came up to him; I then collared him - he dropped the bag; it was picked up, and carried into a shop - I was surrounded by several more of his gang, who endeavoured to rescue him - this is the bag and coffee.
     GUILTY.* Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
