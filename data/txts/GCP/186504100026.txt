JOHN HENRY CRANE (re-examined). I recognise both these pistols pro-
duced by last witness—I recognise them all, the one pledged at Mr.
Hastings and the one at Mr. Byas—they are my property; the value of
then is about 71. and those three about 508. each--they were & portion of
the missing pistols which were safe on Saturday, 4th March, when I left.
MR. METCALPE Q. Had you seen them yourself? A. Decidedly; before
I left on that Saturday evening--they had been lying on my bench for a
long time; in fact they were my sample pistols – I recognise all these
produced as my property.
HENRY PARKBR. I am assistant to Mr. Folkard, pawnbroker, of 160,
Blackfriars-road-I know the prisoner White ; not by name-he pledged in
the name of Martin he came to the shop on Saturday night, 4th March,
about 9 o'clook, and offered these two revolvers to pledge-I advanced 21. 58.
on them--they were pledged in the name of Martin of Webber-row- he said
they belonged to a friend of his who had bonght very largely for the Federal
army, and this was superfluons stock that he had for sale on the same
evening, afterwards he bought five others which he wanted to dispose of; he
wanted 71. 108. for them-I said I had no time then to go into the particu.
lars of value, but if he felt disposed, I would let him have 51., and if he
would call agnin on Monday or Tuesday, I would ascertain the value and
give him the value of them-I think he called on the Monday or Tuesday;
in the meantime Hancock had called on me, and when White came again, I
said to bim, “These things are stolen: I expect you will refund me the
mouey "-he said, “They don't belong to me; they belong to a friend of
mine, at all events I will get you the money"-he went out, and in about
half an hour he came back and brought me 6l. 108. of the money-he said
he would give me the other in the morning, and I retained the pistols-1
have since given them up to Hancock-I have known him for some time as
pledging at the other shop-I always thought he was a commercial man,
trading and attending sales and so forth-he had always gone in the name
of Martin.
Richard EDMONDS (Police-inspector L). Between 9 and 10 o'clock on 17th
March, I found a letter and a brown-paper papel on my desk at the police
station in Tower-street-it contained this revolver- I gave instructions to a
constable, and the prisoner Collins was apprehended—I saw him at the
station at 2 o'clock the same day- I told him I should apprehend him for
being concerned with others in stealing a quantity of pistols in the city-be
said that he had sold one to the grocer at the corner by the Windmill-ho
also said that he had had a row with Jack about five, meaning Campbell --I
asked him who Jack was-he said " Jack Campbell, you know, and these
five were found in a dust heap at the back of the Windmill, and Jack had
them "--I showed him the pistol that was left at the station-he said it was
the one he had sold to Jones the grocer.
