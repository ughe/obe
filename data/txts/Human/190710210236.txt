The con  tract was: "We sell you on our usual terms five Rio Tin to shares at 93 1/4. I received the letter of February 5 from William Tyler and Co., saying that owing to a set-back in the market yesterday the cover became exhausted, and that it would be necessary to send another £5 to make 2 per cent, cover, etc. I next had a letter saying they had had no reply from me, and presuming I did not wish to continue the shares, enclosing contract. I replied, saying that I did not feel I could risk another £5, etc. I then received another letter from them advising me to send further money for the purpose of a fresh speculation. I sent them £10 more, which they acknowledged as being £5 on 2 percent, cover on purchase of Brighton "A's," and £5 deposit on Rio Tintos, also sending contracts at the day's prices. On February 21 I received another letter saying that, regarding Brighton "As my cover had been exhausted, and presuming I wished to continue had debited the Rio Tinto's, which had risen, with the £5 necessary to continue. I am not sure what I replied to that, but I got a further letter as follows: "With regard to Rio Tintos which you have opened, we shall be glad if you will let us know whether you will leave the closing in our hands. If you decide to leave it to us you might rely upon us doing he best for you in the matter." My answer was: "I will leave the closing in your hands; hoping you will do full justice to me. You do not mention the Brighton 'A.' Is it quite gone?" Further correspondence took place, in the course of which they asked for £4 7s. 6d. in order to keep my interest going. I replied, "I am unable to forward another sum of £4 7s. 6d. at pre sent. Would it be possible therefore to retain the Rio Tintos even if I forego the Brighton 'A,' or must I close up both accounts?" The account (produced) I received from Tyler and Co., showing a balance in my favour of 12s. 6d. I had not heard about contango before that account; I do not know what it arena. They wrote me again saying that unless I was prepared to pay the difference due they would have to close; and I wrote telling them to do so. They acknowledged, and said they had closed my account; afterwards send ing me their account, giving the purchases and prices, and showing amount necessary to balance £41 5s. I sent them £3 as instalment, saying I would send another £9 in a few days. On March 25 I had another letter from them asking for a further remittance as they had a heavy account to deal with. The acknowledgments (produced) are from Tyler and Co. for £3 15s., for 30s., March 28. and £20 on April 9, making the amount paid up £25 5s. I think that was the last I sent I got another letter on May 29 asking for more, but that is all I could manage. Some little time later I saw something in the news papers, and did not think it wise to send more. I remember getting a circular (produced) from the "Financial Guardian" last year, in consequence of which I sent £5 deposit on the purchase of five Rio Tintos, which was acknowledged the letter (produced).
