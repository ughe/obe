MR. CLARKSON. Q. Is it a part of your duty to go through the book of
each of the shopmen, for the purpose of seeing whether the tickets you have
corresponded with the books ? A. Either I or Mr. Hoskings do--it is mote

ARTHUR WOOLF HOSKINGS. I have compared the books and the sheets-I
count the money myself-31. 188. 6d. has not been received from the 10th to
the 16th--I have counted up the books, and find the 31. 188. 6d. has not been
paid-the sum of 1l. 133. bas not been entered on the 9th of Jan.if that
sum had been paid in the cash would not balance, and I find my cash right on
all occasions, with the exception of 48., 58., or 68.--we cannot balance to a 1d.
-it is the duty of the yonng men to compare their books with the sheet every
evening, but if a young man is out we have it done for him.
COURT. Q. When the prisoner pays in a sum, has he the means by his
own book and the sheet, of ascertaining whether they have given him credit
for it? A. No he would give a bill at the cashier's desk--these bills are
given to the customer--we see by the bill that the amount paid agrees with
the sum paid up at the desk-each one has a cheque, puts the number of bis
book on it, enters that amount into the book, and puts the book back into
its place-whatever he receives he must enter in his own book that is a book
that he had access to-in the evening the young man who is called comes and
adds his book up to see what has been taken in the course of the day--the
cashier says, "How much do you make your book ?"-he says, "So much"
-if it corresponds with the cheques it is passed if not, the cheques are all
called over one by one-the prisoner has to call it himself, to see where the
error may be.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. If he received money and it has escaped him of put-
ting it in bis book, what means have the owners of knowing he received it ?
A. Because he makes a cheque of it—if he does not make a cheque, it would
of course appear in the book-we never allow any one to go away without
making a cheque-if a man takes goods home be does not take the book with
him.
Q. If he took goods to show, and they were bought, suppose he received
the money and forgot it, what then? A. These goods are all entered before
they go out, and the length of the goods are put down in the entry-book, and
when he comes back they are compared to see that they are all right-the
party who keeps that book does not let it go out-I do not know whether
there is an entry in the approbation-book, of the goods sent to Mrs. Clark
whether it has been paid would not appear in that book, but he would have
his memory refreshed by the circumstance of the approbation-book.. lide
