BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT.
(Wednesday, September 15.)
BRETT, James (23, carman), and HEATH, Arthur (38, catsmeat
reador), both maliciously causing grievous bodily harm to Minnie
Slamovitz.
Mr. Allan Ramsay prosecuted; Mr. H. Warburton defended Brett,
Mr. Gervais Rentoul defended Heath.
AMAHAX BLANOVITZ, 349, Queen's Road, Upton Park. On July 26,
# 3p.m., I was standing at my stall with my daughter Minnie, who
was then 27 years old, when I heard a seream and saw her lying in
the road on her face. I picked her up, when I saw prisoners driving
away in a trap. My daughter's face was bleeding. After taking her
to a doctor I took her to the London Hospital, where she was
tramined. There were no bones broken. She was in bed for three
weeks and is still suffering from nervous attacks. There were a
number of people in the road screaming.
Cross-examined. I did not see the accident. The two prisoners
vere charged at the police court-Brett had a month's imprisonment
and Heath was fined. I have taken civil proceedings through a
wciety--the solicitor has written demanding damages. There was not
auch traffic in the road.
Re-examined. Prisoners were convicted at East Ham Police Court
of being drunk in charge of a vehicle.
JOHN PACKER, M.B., 336, Queen's Road, Upton Park. On July 26,
* 3 p.m., I examined the child Minnie. She was in a condition of
shock and moaning; there were no broken bones or bruises-there
T an abrasion across the face, apparently caused by the face rubbing
against the road or the wheel. I attended her until August 4. She
complained of pain in the back and was suffering from shock to the
bervous system. The injury to the face was quite superficial. I
vas informed that she starts up in her sleep.
Anxie BAUX, 341, Queen's Road, Upton Park. On July 26 I saw

prisoners were in the cart--they drove away. A great many people
