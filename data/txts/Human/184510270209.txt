MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Do you mean nothing was said about these goods from beginning to end? A.  No, there was nothing said about the furnace and chimneys—all the articles were placed in this outhouse—nothing was said to him about the articles that were placed in that out-house—there were some loose things, and them I fastened up in a box—I sent the box to lock them all up in, and they were not to be disturbed—he was not to let any other person have them.
   Q.  Were they not under his protection, though they were your pro perty? A.  When I lent him the key, I expected it to be returned; but when he had got the things locked up I did not ask him for it again—What Was there, was my property—he was not to disturb them—I did not tell him to take care of them—I did not at any time tell him they were to be taken care of for me—I did not tell him what was in the show him what was there—I did not remove the fixtures from the house to this place—I did not remove a single thing from the front house to this place—I do not know that they were put there by my orders—Was in the front shop was about a dozen and a half of pots and, some measures, that I allowed the prisoner to have to get the windows mended—I ask for the key afterwards when he had been a few days in possession of it—the key was in the door—I saw it in the door—I went in and saw all the things safe—when I came out I think I took the key—yes, I must have done so, or else how came he to ask me for it—after I had given him the key I asked him for it—I went and found it in the door—I left it in the door.
   Q.  You said you took the key? A.  In first first instance I took the key, and then I think he had it again; but I found it in the door—my son was backwards and forwards, looking after the things—the prisoner got the key from me—he said he wanted to put some things in, and I found was two crates—I cannot charge my memory whether he asked me for that key after that occasion—it passed backwards and forwards two or three times—I cannot say what was the last occasion on which I gave him the key—it was to move out what he had got in—I did not ask him for the key after that—I considered the things were safe, and therefore did not ask him for it—I considered he was taking care of them—these pre mises had formerly been used for a pewteier—the things in this indictment were articles for the purpose of pewtering—the prisoner was a beer-shop keeper—he put up some things for the purpose of carrying on the Alton ale business—there was not less than twenty squares of glass to be put in when he got into the house—he put some in—I agreed to allow him 1 s . a foot for the glass, and 6 d . a square for taking the old broken glass out—I did hot come after he had been in some time, ask him It he would dispose of any of the articles at the back of the shop—none at all—I know Mr. Barnett—he bought the things—I do not know Mr. Church or Mr. Paisley—I have seen Mr. Church within the last two or three days—I have seen Mr. paisley two or three days in Court—I have not told them that I authorised the prisoner to sell these articles for the best price he could get—to the best of my knowledge, I never saw them till I saw them in this Court—I did not call on the prisoner on the 2nd of May, to my know ledge—it might be on the 2nd of May—he did not tell me he could get 9 l . 10 s . for the things, if I approved of that sum, nor that if he were to sell these things I must allow him 3 l . 10 s . to paint the back and front windows, and to paint and grain the shutters—he did not paint the back
