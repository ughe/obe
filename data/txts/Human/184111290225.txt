On Sunday night, the 12th of September, my mistress gave me leave to go home—I left about eight o'clock, and met my sister Mar  garet and George Lartin, and went with them towards my father's house—just before we got into John-street, I saw the prisoner with his wife and a man and woman, on the other side of the way—the prisoner called after us, and made use of very improper names—he said, "You had better go home to such a house, or you will not be able to get up early enough in the morning"—I said he had better go about his business, and that there was another way to make him pay for what he had said—Lartin went up to him and said, "Are you aware that you are taking these young womens' character away?"—I said to Lartin, "You had better come away, don't have any conversation with him, there is another way to make him prove what he has said, besides having any words with him"—I went up to Lartin to tell him to come away, when the prisoner and his wife struck me both together—the prisoner struck me with his fist on the head, and tore my hair—part of my head is completely bare, and he tore the skirts of my gown off, and tore my collar—my mother came up, and he commenced beat ing her in the same manner, and tore her hair—I went to my father's, changed my dress, and came to the street-door to go home, and saw a crowd of people outside—as I went down the steps of the door, the prisoner came to the gate of the fore court and threw a stone, and said, "Take that," making use of bad words, and struck me on the forehead—I do not know what happened afterwards—I was taken to the surgeon's—I saw some flower-pots thrown down, but I cannot say who threw them—he threw a brick at me—I had not abused him, or offered him the slightest provo cation—I was at St. Thomas's Hospital from the 12th of September to the 1st of October.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Do you mean to state that it was done without the slightest provocation? A.  Not the least—I used no violence—I did nothing to the prisoner's wife—I believe Lartin has lived with his friends since the transaction—he has not lived in our family—he has not lived in my mother's house since the transaction—I did not see any of the prisoner's windows smashed—there was nothing whatever done to him by any of the party.
