WALTER SAXBY. On Monday, 31st Dec., I assisted Mr. Wilks in weigh.
ing the bales of leather--there were thirteen bales of light butts, and three
bales of heavy butts-Mr. Wilks took the weights dowti---these are the cor-
rect weights he took down--next morning I went with my master's cart to
the prisoner's house in Ann's place, about eight o'clock-I knocked at the
door-a man opened it-I told him I had brought the bales of leather from
Boutcher and Co.-- he told me to drive round to the back gates-I drove
round, and the gates were open-I stopped my cart outside the gates, and
two men who were there told me to bring the goods in-I said I could not
bring them in till I saw Mr. Tucker--the cart was outside, and I waited in
side-Mr Tucker came in a little while I showed him the invoice, and told
him I had brought the goods, and I was to have the money for them; I could
not leare them without-I put the invoice into his hand - I never received it
back again--he said, “Very well, that is all right, put them down here"
that was inside the gates--I put the goods down, and he said, "They must be
weighed"-I assisted in weighing them-be put down the weights on a slate
- he did not let me see it, he took it in-doors, and told me to drive round to
the front-I drove round to the front, and the prisoner came to the front and
asked me if I sbould like to have a pint of beer, or a glass of gin, it being a
cold morning - I said yes, and he gave me sixpence-that had not the effect
of getting me away from the door-I was at the door outside-he shut the
door, and said he should be ready for me in a minute or two- in two or three
minutes be opened the door, and said there was 2lbs, short weight-I believe
that would make a difference of 1s. 4d.-I told him I knew nothing about
the weight, my orders were to bring the money back, and I must have it or
the goods--1 said our scales were very correct, and his might make that dif-
ference-he said, “Oh, my good man, don't be alarmed, I will go with you
in the cart and settle with Mr. Wilks"-that threw me off my guard-he
went and got his hat, and got in the cart, and went with me--when we got to
the Swan, the prisoner said, "Which way are you going? I am going this
way," pointing down Swan-street, which leads to the Grange-road-as soon
as he said that he jumped out of the cart over the front asked him why
he did not come to settle with my master, and I whipped the horse to prevent
his getting out, but he got out and went down Swan-street-I went after him,
and overtook him about 200 yards off-I asked him why he did not come
with me and settle with Mr. Wilks-he turned round and told me not to
insult hin- I told him I did not insult bim, all I wanted was for him to come
and settle with Mr. Wilks as he promised-I followed him down as far as the
Grange-road, and then he said he would come and settle with Mr. Wilks in
an hour -- he went on, and I went and told Mr. Wilks - it was then a little
past nine I went to my breakfast, and on my return, by direction of one of
the partners, Mr. Wilks and I took the horse and cart and went to the pri-
soner's-we tried to get the leather-We got one bundle, containing five butts,
and then were told we should neither have money nor leather, and we came
home without money or goods.
