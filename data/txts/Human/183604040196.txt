ANN HEARD. I am servant to Mr. Lewis Stephens Lyne, of Brick's-buildings, Hoxton. He is a silk-broker—the prisoner was occasionally. employed as char-woman—she had access to the room in which I slept—I missed from there a pair of child's shoes—these are them—the child has been dead five years, and these were put with some other shoes—they are worth 2 s . 6 d .—I missed them three weeks ago last shoes—they was charged with the offence last Thursday week—she had been to the house in the mean time. GEORGE KEMP( police-constable N  82) I apprehended the prisoner, and told her it was on suspicion of receiving some property which her daughter had stolen—she declared she was innocent, and knew nothing at all about it—I asked if she had bought any tickets—she said, no, she left them at home—I said she had not, as I had been and searched her house—I took her to the station-house—a purse and some duplicates were founds on her—one to these relates to the shoes—she told me they were her child's master's property; that her daughter stole the shoes, gave them to here, and she pawned them—I made her no promise or threat.
   Prisoner . I had a very sick house—I took them myself, and pledged them myself, because I could not support my family. WILLIAM ASTELL. I am apprentice to Mr. Essex, of Islington, a pawn broker. I produce a pair of child's shoes, pledged on the 12th of March by a female, in the name of "Mary Palmer"—this duplicate, found on the prisoner is the one given for them. ANN HEARD. The prisoner daughter is nine years old—she is in ser vice at my master's—she was charged with stealing these things.
   Prisoner  They shut her in a room two policeman—she was so frightened, she confessed it, but it was me that took them. Witness . The prisoner worked four there, and bad four children, she had 3 s . a-day for washing; and half-a-crown for charring at our house, and her living.  1049. THEODORE CHANNELL and PHILLIP CORK were in dicted for stealing, on the   11th of January , 90lbs. weight of pork value 2 l  . the goods of James Morgan Strachan . HENRY BEARE. I am servant to Mr. James Strachan, of Teddington. His dairy was broken into in the month of January, 1834, and some legs of pork and other pieces cut for salting, were stolen—I know nothing of the particulars. WILLIAM ALLAWAY TEDDINGTON. I am a police-sergeant. I applied for a warrant for Phillip Cork and John Stuckey—I went to Cork's house, on Twickenham-common—I told him I wanted him on a charge of felony—I read my warrant to him—he said, "Why did you come at this late hour of the day—why did you not come in the morning?"—I was about to put the handcuffs on him—he said, "I will go without them"—I said, "You must have them on"—he then wanted to go into the next room for his jacket—I said his wife could get it—a constable who was with me gave it him—he seized this knife, and said the would not go, and he would stab the first man that come near him—I seized him by the collar and got him in the corner of the room—he kicked the other constable, and struck me—we got the handcuffs on one hand and took him—I went to get Stuckey—I could not find him—I returned to the lock-up place, and searched Cork—he said "You want another person, don't you?"—he said it was Channell—I looked him very bard in the face—he said, I am guilty—I mean to plead guilty before the magistrate'—I looked him up, and and left him.
