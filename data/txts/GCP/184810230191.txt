9442. SPENCER LINDFIELD, MARY ANN DRYDEN, and RICH.
ARD ORPIN were indicted for the wilful murder of Eliza Wilson, by pro
curing her miscarriage with the use of a catheter; they were also charged,
on the Coroner's inquisition with a like offence.
Messrs. RYLAND and LAURIE conducted the Prosecution.
SAMUEL ELYARD, Esq. I am one of Her Majesty's Justices of the Peace
for the county of Surrey. On Wednesday, 20th Sept., the deceased, Eliza
Wilson, made a statement in my presence and hearing, and signed it-I took
it down, but in consequence of her weak, dying state, I had to bend over her
in bed, and then I got the police-inspector to write part, in my presence-I
had seen her on the previous Sunday I was requested by the inspector to go
there on that day to take the declaration of a dying female--she was then
very ill, but she expected to recover, and I therefore went away on the
Wednesday the inspector came again to me, and I went again-I saw her
father, and sent for a Clergyman-I asked her how she found herself-she!
said, "Very ill"-I said, "Do you now think you will recover ?"-she said,
"No"-I said, “ Do you think you will die ?" - she said, “Yes”-I then
proceeded to take the statement.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. She was lying in her bed at the
time, I suppose? 4. Yes-the Clergyman, the inspector, the nurse, and
myself were in the room -I only addressed to her those observations I
have stated--I asked her age-she appeared clearly to understand wat she
stated to me, though she was extremely weak; and after each sentence the
nurse put some moisture to her lips--no one had to repeat what she said
she related the whole herself.
Cross-examined by Mr. Parry. Q. Did you send for the Clergyman?
4. Yes-before I went into the room I had not sent him to her to confer
with, as to whether she would recover or not-she wrote her name with very
great difficulty, indeed—I believe she died at half-past three o'clock on the
Friday morning-it might have been eleven or twelve on the Wednesday
that she made the statement.
