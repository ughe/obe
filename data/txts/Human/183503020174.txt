 868. JOHN ELDRIDGE and THOMAS GORKEN were indicted for stealing, on the   20th of December , at   St. Mary, Lambeth , 1 cow, value 11 l  ., the property of William Newman .
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution . MARY NEWMAN. I am the wife of William Newman. I live in King's Bench-walk, St. George's—we keep cows—on the 20th of December, the two prisoners called at my husband's house—he was not in town—it was between ten and eleven o'clock—they asked me if I had any cows to sell—I said I had three cows to sell, and I did not care which of them I sold—both asked me if they could see them—I said they were in the shed, and they could see them if they liked—I and my son went into the shed with them, and showed them eight cows—they examined two or three, and asked me the price of the first two—I told them 14 l . each—they were in calf—they said they could feel no calf in them—they left them, and walked to the cow in question—Gorken said, "I can feel a calf in this—a good big calf," and said to Eld ridge, " Jack , go you up and feel her"—I had seen Gorken in the market, for about six months before, but had never spoken to him before—Eldridge said he could feel the calf, and asked me the price—I told him I could not take less than 12 l . for it—Gorken called me out into the yard, and said, if I sold the cow to Eldridge I should sell her to a good customer, and I should be sure of the money when I delivered her—we walked back into the shed, and Eldridge said, "I will give you 11 l . "—I said I could not take 11 l . for her—they went aside, and talked together, then came back again and asked if I would take 11 l . for it—I said I would not—we all four walked out of the shed together—I and my son followed them out into the yard—I thought they were going into the street, and wished them good morning—I went in doors—my son called me out again, and Eldridge said he would givt me 11 l . 10 s .—I said I would take it—Gorken stood under the gate, about ten yards from him—I cannot say whether he could hear what Eldridge said—Eldridge asked if I could let my son take the cow up to his place—my son said, "I do not know where you live"—he asked him if he knew Kennington-lane—my son said he did—he told him to bring her to Ken nington-lane, at four o'clock, and he would meet him—he then went away—he told me three or four times that he would pay me—he said, when my son delivered the cow, he would return the money back to me for it at the time—he said that in the shed—he said he would send the money back—Eldridge then left the yard—he came back in about two minutes, knocked at the street door, and said he had altered his mind, and if I would let him bring the cow in the course of half an hour to Kenniogton-lane, he would meet him there—he said, "I will return the money back by your
