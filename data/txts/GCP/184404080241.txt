1320. SOPHIA HARMER was indicted for stealing 1 sovereign, the
monies of Edward Britcher; from his person.
Mr. Payne conducted the Prosecution.
EDWARD BRITCHER. I am organ-blower of Lewisham-church. On the
evening of 21st March, Mr. Southorn, the churchwarden, paid me 12 1s. 62.
- he put it into a piece of paper-I put it into my side breeches pocket
went to the Plough, and called for a pint of beer-I was sitting outside
the house the prisoner came by, and asked me to give her a drink of beer
-I let her have a drink, and she sat close by my side-I had my money
safe in my pocket when I sat down, and while she was there I looked, and
missed the paper all at once-she went into Mr. Griggs, then came and sat
down by me again, gave me the paper, and said she did not like to rob me
-she went away, and when I was going home, I looked in the paper, and
there was only one farthing there--no one else had been sitting by me who
could have taken it.
Cross-examined by Mr. Horry. Q. Did you speak to her about having
lost your money? A. No she was with me when I missed'it—I had not
accused another young woman before the prisoner was taken.
SIMON SOUTHORN. I am the churchwarden of Lewisham-I paid Britcher
a sovereign, a shilling-& sixpence, and the sovereign being dull, I went
to my cash-box, and took out a bright one I put it into & paper, gave it
to him, and he went away.
JANE Grigg. I am the daughter of the landlord of the Plough, at
Lewisham-Britcher was having a pint of beer--he took it outside, and in a
short time after the prisoner came in for two farthings for a halfpenny,
which my sister gave her--a short time afterwards Briteher came in, appa-
rently looking at something in his hand-he went out again in a very great!
hurry-I am certain it was the prisoner came in, I knew her before.
STEPHEN Brooks (police-constable R 50.). I took the prisoner in Mill
lane, on the morning after the robbery-I said to her, "Sophi, do you
recollect how many pints of beer you had with this man at the Plough
she said, "Only one"-I said, "Do you recolleet how he paid for it?"
she said, "No"-he had described her dress to me--he said she had a
light dress, a dark shawl, and a velvet bonnet; and that was the dress she
had on-there was another young woman shown to him-I asked him if the
girl had a black eye, because there was another young woman used to dress
like the prisoner, but she had a black eye-he did not express any doubt
about the prisoner.
