   Prisoner. Q.  Did you ever see me drunk, or ill-use her? A.  I have heard you ill-use her several times before—I was never in her room when you and she were there, but I have heard you ill-use her many a time with your tongue—I never saw you strike her. SUSAN MARY MATTHEWS. I lived in this house. On Sunday night, the 10th of July, about nine o'clock, I saw the prisoner come out of the deceased's room—she called him an infernal rogue, and said he had done  for her now—he had given her a death-blow—as he passed by the door. he made answer, "You infernal b—h, it serves you right."
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you hear me say so? A.  Yes, I did—I saw you go down stairs—my door was open.

On Sunday evening, the 10th of July, about eight o'clock, I was going down stairs, and met her on the stairs—she appeared to be as well as usual—the spoke to me—I do not know what age she was—she appeared about seventy—Mrs. Vincent came and told me something; that evening, in consequence of which, I went up into my own room, and heard the deceased crying bitterly, in her own room—I did not see her any more that night—about half-past eight in the morning, I heard her knocking in her room—I went out to see what it was, and then went up into her room—she was just sat down on the bed—she was alone—when she spoke she put her hand to her left side, and it was several seconds before she could speak—she showed me her bruises—I observed bruises on her side—she said she was sure her ribs were broken, and he had given her her death-blow—she had a bruise on each arm, a bruise on her breast, and a slight one on the leg, besides those on the side—I went up about one o'clock, and took her a little broth, thinking it might do her good—she seemed in more agonizing pain than before—she was in the habit of going into the streets with a basket, for the last eight months—she went out that afternoon, between three and four o'clock—I carried her basket down stairs for her—she seemed very bad, and still complained of being very ill, that he had given her her death blow, and she should never get over it—she returned between six and seven—I saw her next day, Tuesday, between three and four in the after noon—she appeared to be getting worse—I did not see her on Wednesday—I heard her in her room about half-past twelve at night, groaning very much indeed—on Thursday morning, about half-past seven, the prisoner opened my door, came in, and said, "She is gone;
