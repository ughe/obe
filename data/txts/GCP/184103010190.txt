-I did not tell Clothier that she was married to Huggins, I thought it
was not my business, I thought he was her husband's first cousin-
when he came he was shown into the front parlour- I used to leave him
there frequently I cannot say how long he staid.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Have you any reason to believe that Clothier
knew that Huggins was her husband ? A. I do not think he did, because
she always used to tell me to keep it a secret from Mr. Huggins that
Clothier came there I do not think that Huggins ever saw Clothier.
BARTHOLOMEW HERRING (police-constable P 187.) I have produced the
copy of the register here to-day-on the 1st of February I took the prisoner
into custody at No. 34, Manor-place, Walworth-it was Clothier's father who
fetched me I found the prisoner in an up-stairs room--Mr. Clothier, senior,
went into the room first-I told the prisoner she must come with me - I said
to Mr. Clothier, "My charge is for intermarrying with your son, her former
husband being alive"-I went down stairs while the prisoner dressed-I
went up again, and in about a second she got ransacking a box, and took
this paper out-I took it from her hand-she said, “This is my marriage
certificate, which I got from Mr. Clothier," and she said, "Mr. Clothier got
it at St. George's church"-on the following day, wherr I met her, I said,
“Who is Richard Bell and Frances Bell ?"-she said, "That is the man
and woman that belong to the church ; they gave me away when I was
married"-I said, “What, the clerk and his wife?"-she said, "Yes"
she said she was very sorry for what she had done, that Mr. Huggins was
always a good husband, and she was very sorry for ever getting married a
second time I see the words "Strah Johnson, widow," on this certificate
-I did not say any thing to her about it-I said, “Did Mr. Clothier
know you were a married woman"-she said, "Yes"---she said she repre-
sented herself to Mr. Clothier as a widow.
