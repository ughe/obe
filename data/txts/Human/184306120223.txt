ELIZABETH ANN HUBBERT and ELIZABETH HUBBERT were indicted for stealing, on the   16th of Jan ., 2 plates, value 2 s  .; 6 decan ters, 16 s  .; 5 glass rummers, 4 s  .; 3 blankets, 4 s  .; 1 counterpane, 3 s  .; 6 salt cellars, 6 s  .; 1 toilet-cover, 3 s  .; 2 salt cellars and stand, 3 s  .; 1 bottle-stand, 3 s  .; 4 trays, 9 s  .; 1 table-cover, 4 s  .; 3 knives, 2 s  .; 3 forks, 1 s  .; 1 stuffed bird sod case, 2 s  .; 3 candlesticks, 9 s  .; 1 looking-glass, 2 s  .; 1 writing-desk, 10 s  .; 4 yards of carpet, 5 s  .; 2 mugs and stands, 1 s  . 6 d  .; and sugar-basin, 3 s  .; 3 wine glasses, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 carpet, 4 s  .; 2 chairs, 6 s  .; 1 set of fire irons, 16 s  .; 1 standard, 4 s  .; 1 brush, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 2 images, 1 s  .; 3 vases, 7 s  . 6 d  .; 3 ornaments, 1 s  .; 3 cups, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 3 saucers, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 basin, 1 s  .; 1 printed book, 7 s  . 6 d  .; 1 table-cloth, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 2 aprons, 1 s  .; and 3 bottles, 2 s  .; the goods of Lewis Meyer ; to which ELIZABETH ANN HUBBERT  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 47.— Confined One Year . SHAH METER. I am the wife of Lewis Meyer, and live at Stepney, The prisoner Elizabeth Ann Hubbert came with her husband on the 11th of Jan., and took my apartments—she and the other prisoner were turned out doors by her brother, and I out of compassion took her to sleep there—I lost these articles—I can positively swear to the shawl—Elizabeth had an opportunity of taking them.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  When did you miss these? A.  On Saturday, the 29th of April—Elizabeth's father and two children continued living there after she left—they were not in distress—they lived on the best Kings—this shawl was kept in a box in the back kitchen, and the looking glass hung up in my bed-room, which I permitted the prisoner to sleep in. Chaeles James Mercer. This looking-glass was pawned with me by Elizabeth Hubbert.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have a distinct recollection of her? A.  Yet—pawned it once, and took it out again.
   ELIZABETH HUBBERT  — GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined One Year .

 1983. SUSAN RATTINGER was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of May , 1 shawl, value 19 s  .; the goods of Elizabeth Jay . ELIZABETH JAY. I am twenty years old. I get my own living, and live my father—the prisoner took lessons in dancing at my father's house—on the 15th of May, I had a shawl on a nail in the room—I missed it—this cross-examined by Mr. Ballantine. Q. You bought it with your own earnings? A. Yes—I believe she gave her direction, but my father did not recollect it.
   COURT . Q.  Did you meet her on Tuesday the 20th? A.  Yes—she said, "You accuse me of taking your shawl"—I said, "Yes"—she said, "I have not"--I said, "You have"—at last she said, she did, and would give it me if I would wait till the next day—I would not—she said it was in the country—she could not give it me then—it was found at the pawnbroker's. FRANCIS SPINE. I am in the employ of a pawnbroker, in Covent-garden.
