Q. Was not the bill on Morris, which induced you to send Mr. M. R.
James to the prisoner in gaol, due in October? A. That was for another
1001., which I never heard any more of.
Q. Then there seems to have been thousands according to your state-
ment? A. I cannot say-I did not send Mr. M. R. James to see if be
could get any admission from the prisoner-I sent him to know the extent
of the forgeries-he was examined as a witness for me on the trial last
summer, and he swore, in my presence, that the prisoner admitted the
forgery, and the Jury found for the plaintiff.
Q. Pray have you got any security besides that you have produced,
either from the prisoner, or his family? A. I have security from his
mother-I got that, I think, at the latter end of 1837—I have not brought
it to London-I received it, I think, in October, after I sent Mr. M. R.
James to the prison it is a mortgage-it does not state any amount-
took it after I found there was another mortgage, I mean Biddulph's mort-
gage-I do not know the amount of that I have heard it is 6001., but Mr.
Hulme, the clerk, said yesterday he thought it would cover the other 100l.
also-I know his mother's property lets for 55l. a-year-I bave a general
charge, without any amount stated there is no incumbrance on it-she
gave me that as security that I should not be injured by her son-I think
Mr. Gwynne was the prisoner's detaining creditor- I did not know he was
going to arrest him-he was examined on the trial as a witness--Mr.
Hulme was not-the case was tried before Mr. Baron Gurney.
