REBECCA LINDSEY re-examined.  The things had been in a clothes basket, but when I sent them up they were all in parcels—there were a dozen knives and forks tied in two papers—I have the paper here; and when I came to have the things down again, I said to her, "They are sadly out of condition," I missed two knives and two forks, and she said, "I cannot think what could possess me to take the things"—those were the words she used—she admitted taking the whole of the things.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you tell that of the Magistrate? A.  I do not think I did—she did not say so till the night I found them—when I missed them I asked her if she knew any thing about them, and she said, "No"—I did not tell the Magistrate she acknowledged taking them—I was too much frightened—I did not know I had need to say it—I did not know that the question was put to me—my next door neighbour was pre sent when the prisoner acknowledged taking them—I have never had any quarrel with the prisoner—when I had occasion to complain to her I did so. LUCY TRUSCOTT. I went into Mrs. Lindsey's house to order some things—Mrs. Lindsey said she had found out that her servant had robbed her—the prisoner was in the parlour, cleaning it, and could hear what passed—Mrs. Lindsey asked me to go up stairs to search her box—she called to the prisoner, and said, "We are going up, Susan, to search your box"—she said, "You are quite at liberty to do that"—we went up, and she said, "Now, Susan, let me look at your box"—she first produced a box with some rags in it—her mistress said, "Have not you another box, which I lent you?"—she produced it, and her mistress said, "Now, open that"—she opened it, and a pocket fell out—she said, "Now, take out the rest of the things"—she took out her gown, and the needles and pins fell out, and at the bottom were all the other things—the knives were loose, not in paper—they did not appear to have been used—we locked the door and came down—the officer was sent for; and after he had taken out all the things, her mistress said, "I suspect the comb you have in your head is the one I have lost"—she took off her cap, pulled it out of her hair, and said, "Yes it is"—when we were up stairs, and had found all the things I said, "Susan, you have done a very bad thing"—she wrung her hands and said she did not know what possessed her to do what she had done.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you find any stockings in the box? A.  No. REBECCA LINDSEY re-examined. Q.  Had the prisoner any hair-brush of her own? A.  I never saw one—the stockings had not been put away to be washed—I do not know how long she had had them—I am certain they have been taken out of my drawer, and the pocket also, and she has picked the letters out of my handkerchief—here is the L on it now, but part of the the R is taken out—she never bought a brush of me.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Might not the mark be rubbed out in the washing? A.  No—the things were left in the box till the officer came.
   JURY . Q.  What wages had she? A.  Eighteen-pence a week with board and lodging—the knives were safely packed up when I sent them up. NOT GUILTY.
