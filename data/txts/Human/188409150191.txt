Street, Bermondsey—I had a little girl four years old named Alice; I was out with her on Tuesday, 2nd September, just after 4 in the after  noon—they were doing some repairs at a school there as I was passing by—my little girl had hold of my hand; she was next to the wall—I heard a woman scream—I looked up and saw a pail falling close on my head—I dodged my head away—the next thing I saw was my little child on the ground insensible—she had been struck by the pail—it was a zinc pail, an empty one—the child was taken to the hospital, and she subsequently died—there was no scaffolding on the pathway, I did not notice any. WILLIAM STANLEY. I live at 19, St. John's Place, Bermondsey; I am a costermonger—on Tuesday I was at the corner of Webb Street at about twenty minutes to 5—I saw some buckets being drawn up and down at the Board School—I saw one bucket go up and down—it was fastened on to a hook—I only noticed one hook; the pail was hanging on that—I saw the pail start from the platform of the scaffolding—I saw the prisoner there—it was let down by a rope and a pulley; it came down very slowly—I did not see who was at the bottom—when it got about 10 yards it struck against a pole—the prisoner started it from the top—he put the pail on the hook and it struck the pole and tilted the pail off the hook—I saw it fall, and it touched the wall and struck the child on the head.
   By the COURT . If the pail had gone down all right it would have gone inside the school premises—there was no high wind blowing at the time that I noticed—it came down all right till it struck the piece of project ing scaffolding—I did not take notice afterwards whether the prisoner was lowering the bucket, I ran towards the child—I noticed afterwards that the buckets were properly lowered, with the chain properly fastened round them, properly hooked.
   Cross-examined.  There was no hoarding there except the wall—this building stands back in a recess, back from the wall—the prisoner was standing between sixty and seventy feet from the ground—I will not swear that there were not two hooks on the pail—I saw a bucket full of asphalte go up—I saw a man named Avery pulling it up; he was over the other side of the wall—I could see enough to see that it was being pulled up by a rope by some one from the bottom, but I could not see if the same man that pulled it up lowered it down—I saw the pail go up and the prisoner lowered it—I could not undertake to say that there was not a gust of wind. JEREMIAH GILHAM. I am a labourer employed by the Asphalte Com pany—I live at 33, Kell Street, Borough—on the day in question I was at work in the Board School at Webb Street—it is not my duty to fill the pails at the top, I stand close by the pails at the bottom—I make the stuff ready to put in the pails, and then Avery wound them up to the prisoner, and the prisoner then lets them down again—I saw the pail coming down and saw it come into collision with what we call a puddick, and immediately fell—I could not say from where I was standing whether the chain was round the handle or through the hook before it struck anything—it was coming down steadily.
