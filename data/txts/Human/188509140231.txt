MANY ANN DARWIN. I live at 28, Page's Walk; the prisoner occupied a room there on 20th July with Greeson as Mr. and Mrs. Martin—between 8 and 9 o'clock on 23rd July the prisoner asked me if I would lend him the street door key as his wife had gone out with the key on the ring—shortly afterwards the constable came and took him—the bag was locked up in the room till Sunday by the constable, who had my key I then saw the constable search the bag, and saw a piece of brown paper taken out—I had not seen the bag open before.
   Cross-examined.  I had seen you about 7 or 8 o'clock that evening in the back parlour—it was about 7.30 when you asked for the key—you went out with Greeson before that—about 7.30 I said your missus  had gone out, and you went out alter her—I gave you the key—about an hour after you came in, but 1 did not know you were in the house after 7.30—I had been out all day, and came home about 4.15—I found the place in a disgusting state of filth, and the woman drunk on the bed—I saw nothing of you between 5 and 7 o'clock—there is a factory clock outside the window which we look at. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. Mrs. Garrett's half-crown is bad—these five found in Greeson's hand are bad—none of them are from the same mould—these 11 half-crowns found in a packet in the waste ground are all bad; two are from the same mould as Mrs. Garrett's—counterfeits are wrapped up separately in tissue paper generally, after being rubbed over with lampblack to keep them bright—I see no marks on this paper.
