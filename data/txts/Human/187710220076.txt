   Cross-examined.  I found the offices of the Lichfield Chronicle.  Henry Maxwell. I live at Elm Hurst Hall, near Lichfield—I saw the prisoner on the 25th September, and asked him if he had the money for the haystack he had bought—he said yes, and gave me a cheque for 100 l .—I paid it into the National Provincial Bank—55 l . to go to my employer's account, and kept 3 l . for a small account the prisoner owed me, and returned the balance to the prisoner. WILLIAM NORMAN. I carry on business at Lichfield—I know the prisoner as steward for a Mr. Fox—he rented a house of my son as an office in Fore Street—I went there on 4th October, and found an office table, four chairs, a small book-case, and a small counter, the value of which would be about 25 s .—I went over the house, and found nothing else, except some loose papers.
   Cross-examined.  The prisoner had had the offices about two months—the Lichfield Chronicle  was bought as a local paper would be—I do not know what the circulation was—most of my friends took it—about four or five of them. WILLIAM HERNMAN. I am the superintendent of the Lichfield police—the last number of the Lichfield Chronicle  in circulation was that of the 21st September—the office was in Birt Street—I have seen the prisoner in Fore Street, but not since the 24th of September—I arrested him in Birmingham—he said "It is a most abominable proceeding—I was going to pay the young man to-morrow—I have written to him for that purpose"—I have seen a paper advertising a sale of furniture.
   Cross-examined.  I had known the prisoner in connection with the Lichfield Chronicle , and he has since held a respectable situation—I was not present at the sale—I have seen bills announcing that the paper would be continued. FREDERICK BROWN. I am a printer carrying on business at Lichfield—I entered into partnership with the prisoner on the 15th March, and continued till the 21st September, when the partnership was dissolved—the offices were in Birt Street—the machinery and plant and furniture belonged to myself—the prisoner was only entitled to an equal share of the profit—on the dissolution, this statement of account was made out, which showed that 4 l . 2 s . 10 d . was due to each partner—I was not present at the sale of household furniture.
   Cross-examined.  I gave the prisoner notice to dissolve the partnership—I determined to start a fresh paper—as far as I know the prisoner has Dot seen this balance sheet.
