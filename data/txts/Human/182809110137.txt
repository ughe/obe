 1799.  WILLIAM LEDGER was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 2 silver spoons, value 2l., the goods of the   Earl of Limerick.
   DANIEL DUTCH. I am an officer of Mary-le-bone. On the 28th of July I saw the prisoner come to Mr. Squires', the Devonshire Arms public-house, between seven and eight o'clock in the morning - I was in the house searching it because there had been a robbery; while I was in the cellar I saw a cupboard locked; I opened it, and found these two spoons in it; I asked Austin where he got them, he gave me some account, and I took the prisoner.
   JOHN AUSTIN. I had the care of the cupboard at the Devonshire Arms - I keep my cords and provisions there; I put those two spoons in on the Thursday before; I got them from the prisoner; I had lent him some money before - he wanted some more, and I said I would not lend him any more till he gave me some security; he then brought these spoons - I knew he was in the Earl of Limerick's service; I did not open the spoons to see if there was any coronet on them.
   BENJAMIN SCHOFIELD. I am a constable. I went to Lord Limerick's, in Mansfield-street, and the butler wished the prisoner to stop with me while he looked over the plate; I asked the prisoner what he had done; he said, nothing that he knew of - but in a few minutes he said he had given Austin two tea-spoons, as a security; the butler then returned, and was very very angry with him, but would not give him in charge; the prisoner, however, went with me to the office.
   WILLIAM SNOOKS. I was butler to the Earl of Limerick - the prisoner has been two years in his service, and had twenty-four guineas a year; I heard there were two spoons at the office, and I went, and knew them - I went back, and spoke to the prisoner about them; they have a crest on them - the prisoner had property in his care to the amount of 500l.; I always found him honest.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1800.  JOSEPH LEE was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , 1 hammer, value 1s. 2d., and 3 iron tips, value 1d., the goods of Edward Dodd.
 EDWARD DODD. I am an  ironmonger, and live in Moor-street, Soho. The constable came on the 6th of August, and gave me information; I suppose this hammer might be mine, but there are many alike - I do not really think it is mine; I do not think these tips are mine - they are odd ones. The prisoner was in my shop on the 6th of August, and asked to look at a hammer and some bristles; I showed him some hammers pretty near like this, but the iron of this hammer is more straighter than mine - he paid me 1d. for the bristles, and then came in and asked if I had lost anything - I had shown him seven or eight hammers.
   DANIEL HONE. I am an officer. On the 6th of August I was facing the prosecutor's window about nine o'clock in the evening, and saw a lad looking in the window - I crossed over, and he ran away: I saw the prisoner in the shop, looking at some hammers - Mr. Dodd turned round, and the prisoner put this hammer into his pocket; Mr. Dodd then came to the window - the prisoner put his hand into a bag, and put some tips into his pocket; he stood talking a minute or two, and was then coming out - I stopped him; and went in and asked Mr. Dodd if he had bought anything - he said only the bristles; I took the hammer out of his pocket, and showed it to Mr. Dodd.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence soliciting mercy, and received a good character.
   EDWARD DODD re-examined. All the articles in the shop are mine.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 1801.  CHARLES CHRISTMAS was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , 2 window sashes, value 24s., the goods of John Doyle , and others, his masters.
   WILLIAM DOYLE. I am partner with my father, John Doyle, he is a fan-light maker. We missed some sashes on the 16th of August - the prisoner was our apprentice- these sashes which are here, correspond with those in our stock; I measured them, and I know we had sashes like those; we missed sixteen of them; the circumstance of one sash being found with Baker, led us to look, and then we missed them. (see Page 838)
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you see the prisoner take any sashes? A. No.
   HENRY HERRING. I am a labourer. On the 16th of August, about nine o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came to our house at Somers'-town with these two sashes, and asked him to let him leave them for a short time; I let him leave them, as I thought he might have a job in the town; I afterwards gave them to the constable.
   SAMUEL SHUTTLEWORTH. I received these two sashes from Herring - Green took up the prisoner; Herring had been to Mrs. Baker's once or twice to know what was to be done with them, and Mrs. Baker went to Herring's, and said "For God's sake! break the glass and burn the frames.
 MR. PHILLIPS to HENRY HERRING. Q. We have heard that you went once or twice to Mrs. Baker's? A. Yes, I am sure it was not Baker who left them, but I went to know who Christmas' master was; I went there again, because she said her son could tell me.
 Prisoner's Defence. I do not know the young man at all.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months.
