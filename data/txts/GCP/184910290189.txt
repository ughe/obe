living in a state of prostitution, but was a married woman; she became desirous
of having a certificate to send to her friends; she said nothing could be done
to prosecute me without her, and the least I could do, was to give her the
opportunity of satisfying them, and I married her in the name of Richardson.
Guilty. Aged 26.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury.-Confined
Two Months.
2052. JOSIAH WILLIAM CHARLTON was indicted for bigamy.
ALICE MATTHEWS. I am the wife of Richard Matthews. I was present
at Marston Church, on 29th May, 1845, when the prisoner was married to
Susannah Hale-I knew the prisoner before I saw Susannah down below,
about ten minutes ago.
Prisoner. Q. Was I sober at the time? 4. No; very tipsy.
ALFRED WAKEFIELD (policeman). I produce two certificates, one I got
from Waterloo Church, Lambeth, and the -other from the Parish Church of
Marston, near Oxford-I compared them with the original; they are correct
copies (read)— I took the prisoner in custody at the Elephant and Castle, and
on the way to the station be asked me what I took him for I told him, and he
asked who the female was, walking on a-head-I said I did not know-he
said, " That is my wife, I believe" I said I did not know her-we passed her,
and he said, “That is my wife"-I said, "Your married wife!"-be said, "Yes;"|
and said he had been married twice--I have seen the same woman here to-day.
Prisoner. I said I had some knowledge of her, but I did not acknowledge
her to be my wife.
MARY ELIZABETH KNEBB. I am single-I was present at Waterloo
Church last Christmas day, and saw the prisoner married to Sarah Harriett
Perkins.
Prisoner's Defence. I was not aware what I was about at the time of my
first marriage; I was intoxicated.
ALICE MATTHEWS re-examined. He was very tipsy-he walked to Church,
but could hardly walk home-as soon as he came out of Church he went to
bed, and did not get up till the next morning-he lived with his wife after-
wards, and had some children.
Guilty. Aged 31.-Confined Twelve Months.
