I produce some trowsers and a frock, which were pawned on the 17th of Feb., for 2 s ., but I cannot say by whom—this duplicate was given to the person who pawned them—here is a counterpane and some other articles pawned by the prisoner in the name of Heathfield—I have known her some time—I took them in of her. JOHN CARPENTER( police-constable R  84.) I went to Mr. Tuck about nine o'clock at night, on the 30th of April—I waited till the prisoner came home, and Mr. Tuck charged her on suspicion of robbing him—she said, "Me rob him—what of?"—I asked her to give me the key of her box, she took it from her pocket and gave it to me—the box was searched in her presence, and a great number of things were found in it—amongst the rest these six towels, this necklace, these duplicates, and other things—I afterwards went to the house of a person at Lewisham, where a child of the prisoner's is, and there I found this wine-glass, basin, and egg-cup, and a handkerchief which Mr. Tuck identifies—after the prisoner had been searched at the station-house she said, "I am very sorry for what I have done; I hope my master will not come against me." SARAH TUCK. I am the wife of Barber Tuck. I know these children's clothes—I opened the prisoner's box with a common key of another trunk—almost any trunk key would open it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  My box was unlocked with a false key; I never put the things in it. GUILTY. Aged 27.

SUSANNAH NUGENT. I am the wife of Francis Armstrong Nugent. The prisoner was in my service about two months—she left about the 4th of Jan.—these towels, pillow-case, night-gowns, and other things are ours, I had them in use when the prisoner was in my service—I missed them, but I did not know who had got them. JOHN CARPENTER( police-constable R  84.) I searched tne prisoner's box in her presence at Mr. Tuck's—I found in it one towel, the pillow-case, night-gown, and some other things which Mrs. Nugent identifies—at the place where the prisoner's child was, I found this other towel and a wine-glass of Mrs. Nugent's—I asked the prisoner whose the things were, she made no answer—I asked if she had lived with a person at Greenwich, she said, "Yes, with Mr. Nugent."
   Prisoner.  They were all put into my box; I never put them there. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Transported for Seven Years . JOHN CARPENTER stated that other property was found in the prisoner's box belonging to four other persons with whom she had lived, and he had two certificates of character by which the prisoner obtained situations, but which turned out to be false.
