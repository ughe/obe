ABRAHAM BERLYN. In Nov. 1840, I was living in Back Church-lane,
Whitechapel, and carried on the business of a boot and shoemaker. After the
fire at Hoare's-wharf, I purchased some Hessian linen canvas, and other
things, at the sales--there were five successive sales-I saw a man named
Nathan at all the sales, and he saw me-on the 4th of Nov., Nathan came to
me at Goulston-street, where I have a warehouse-be said to me,"Well, Mr.
Berlyn, have you sold all your linen yet?"- I said, "No"-he said, "I think
I can sell a great quantity for you; what will you allow me!"-I said, "I
will allow you 5l. per cent. on all that you sell for me”-he said 5l. per cent.
would not pay him, and it was ultimately agreed that I should allow bim &
farthing a yard, which made it come to 611. per cent.-he asked me to give
him samples of the various sorts I had, and the quantity of each, which I did
--he came again the same day and said he had seen the person he wanted the
linen for, that he was captain of his own vessel, and a man of large property,
that the captain wished to see me himself to bargain for this linen, and if I
would go with him at half-past four that afternoon, after 'Change time, to
No. 15, Baker-street, Lloyd's-square, Captain Cornish would try to deal with
me-we arranged to go that afternoon at half-past four, after 'Change time-
