1795. ELIZA TERRELL was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of
May, 2 rings, value 11. 10s. 3 printed books, value 38.: 2 boxes, value
18.7 2 thimbles, Value 18. 6d. 1 bottle, value 6d. ; 7 waist-ribbons,
value 2s. 6d. 2 handkerchiefs, value 18. 3 cap-borders, value 1s. 1
box of dominos, value 6d. 1 shoe-horn, value 6d. 2 packs of playing-
cards, value 1s. and 2 baskets, value 6d. the goods of Martin Hatfield,
her master.
MARTIN HATFIELD. I am 8 Manchester warehouseman, and live in
Camberwell New-road. The prisoner was my servant for about thirteen
months--I missed a sovereign on the I7th of May, and got an officer to
search her box in my presence was not locked-it was in her bed-
room--th articles stated in the indictment were found in her box-these
are the articles now produced-these rings had been taken out of Mrs.
Hatfield's casket, where the sovereign was lost from-the prisoner was
present when her box was searched, and said she had found the rings, and
the other articles she meant to return before she left the situation-the
officer is ill, and not able to attend.
Prisoner's Defence. My mistress asked me to lend her a basket to put
these things in; not having one, I offered her the loan of my box she
made no answer; I thought she did not object to it, and I put the articles
there with intent to bring them safe when we removed to Camberwell
New-road. The reason of my box being searched was, Mrs. Hatfield said
she had lost money which she found she had not, and having had no time
to unpack after we moved, these things remained there; the smallest of
these rings I found in the high road five months ago I did not know my
mistress had lost one the other ring I found under the drawers in her
bed-room I intended to give it her, but she did not come home till late,
and I forgot it; the silver thimble she told me to work with, and take
care of it.
GUILTY. Aged 19.Recommended to merey--Confined Three
Months.
1796. ALEXANDER LAWSON was indicted for stealing, on the
19th of May, 1 handkerchief, value 28., the goods of Henry Whittaker,
from his person and that he had been before convicted of felony.
HENRY WHITTAKER. T live in Manor-street, Clapham. In the after-
noon of the 19th of May, I was in Gravel-lane, Southwark, about half-past
fee o'clockthere was a fire--some persons were standing there, but I was
passing on my business-] felt some one at my pocket- turned, and saw
the prisoner in the act of dropping my handkerchief from his hand-he was
close to my back-I took bim into custody-h said it was not him, but I
saw him drop it-this is my handkerchief which is now produced.
Prisoner. I said I was not the person-and there were persons there
who said, You have got your property, let him go." Witness. There
were three women who said it was not him, and those women followed him
down to the office each day he went there.
Prisoner's Defence. I am innocent. I made application to respectable
persons in the crowd, who said I was not the person.
