WILLIAM GEORGE HUDSON. I do not assist my father, but I came into the bar, saw the prisoner there, and followed him—another man joined him outside—I missed the other man near the Salisbury Arms, and saw the prisoner looking in at an optician's shop—the other man joined him there, and they went to Adelphi Terrace—one of them wore white trousers—I saw money passing between them down a side street—they were there about five minutes, and then the prisoner went into the Queen's Head. I went in and called for a glass of ale—I spoke to the barmaid, but the prisoner could not hear me—he had something to drink, but I did not see what took place. CHARLOTTE PICKERING. I am barmaid at the Queen's Head, 405, Strand—on the evening of 17th February I served the prisoner with a pint of half-and-half—he gave me a half-crown—I told him it was bad, but he doubted it—I asked him to give it to me again; he did so, and I tested it and broke it in half—he only had 1 d . left, so he only had a half-pint of beer.
   Cross-examined.  He stood near the door, and there was nothing to prevent his going out. THOMAS LANCASTER. I am landlord of the Queen's Head—my atten tion was called, and I went out and saw George Hudson, junior, who made a communication to me, and I gave the prisoner in custody, with the pieces of coin.
