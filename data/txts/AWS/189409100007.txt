673. HARRY LASSITER (27), Feloniously forging and uttering an
order for E6 1ls., with intent to defraud.
Mr. SELLs Prosecuted.
GEORGe THATCHER. I was shopman to Thomas and John Parry, of
924, Regent Street-on 5th February, about six o'clock, a man who I
failed to identify came and asked to be shown some goods-he bought a
small diamond searf pin and a small gold horse brooch-the things
amounted to E4 9s.-these (produced) are the things-he paid by this
cheque for E7 15s. it was not then endorsed-he endorsed it in my pre
sence, and I gave him in change E4 9s.-it was paid into the bank, and
was returned marked 46 No account.'
Cross-e.amined by the prisoner. The man said he was a jockey, or
rider, in the employ of General Owen Williams, and had ridden for him
many times-he was a clean-shaved man; he had no beard.
RICHARD BURGess (Police Inspector, Brighton). The prisoner was
arrested at Brighton on 9th February-I was present when he was
searched-these two articles were found on him-he was charged with
obtaining the goods-he was clean shaven, and had a moustache of about
a week's growth, and might have had a very slight whisker.
HENRY JoNes (Detective Sergeant B). I arrested the prisoner on a
warrant for obtaining E4 6s. 6d.--he made no reply.
Cross-eaamined I am aware that there is a warrant out for a man
named Campbell : not on this charge-about this time there were a good
many forged cheques about-I have seen the prisoner write-the body of
this cheque is something similar to his writing; I can't say more.
JOsEPH HENRy CANNING. I am cashier to Messrs. Ives and Allen,
bankers, of 99, Cannon Street-this cheque is one of ours-we have no
customer of the name of J. H. Williams-the cheque-book was issued to
a Mr. 0. C. Kendall in October, 1892-that account was closed in De-
cember, 1892-the book contained twenty-five cheques; four have been
used, which drew out all the money-the cheque-book was retained by
the customer-Genera Owen Williams has no account with us.
NOT GUITy.
674. HARRY LASSITER was again indicted for unlawfully obtaining
E4 15s. 6d. from Richard William Dodd by false pretences.
Mr. SELLS Prosecuted.
RICHARD WILLIAM DODD. I keep the Stanhope Arms, Little Chester
Street-on 4th January I changed this cheque for E4 15s. 6d. for the
prisoner-he was brought by a customer, Mr. Farrant, for whom I had
been in the habit of changing cheques-before this cheque was produced
Farrant asked, 46 Can you change me a cheque 1"I said, 44 Yes, if it is
not too big said to the prisoner, 66 Where is it 1"the prisoner put
his hand in his pocket and produced it-I said to Farrant, 46 I do not know
this man or anything about the cheque. Do you know if it is all right
-he said, 66 Yes ; it is all right, Mr. Dodd"_ then put E4 15s. 6d. on
the counter, and the prisoner took it and put it in his pocket-he said
nothing-I paid the cheque over with others to Mr. Poole, a general
dealer, a next-door neighbour, in settlement of some account-about three
days after it was returned to me by Mr. Poole, and I refunded the money
to him.
