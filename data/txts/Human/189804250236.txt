SARAH JANE WATSON. I am in the employ of the Welford Dairy Co., at Streatham—on April 4th, between five and six p.m., the youth Green wood ( see page  658) came in for two eggs, and put a five-shilling piece on the counter—I picked it up and did not like the look of it—I took it to the secretary, Mr. Renals—I did not notice anybody outside—I saw a man go by, but I cannot recognise him. ANNIE RENALS. I am the wife of the secretary of the Welford (Surrey) Dairy Company—on April 4th I saw Greenwood in the shop, and through the open door I saw Banks and recognised him—Greenwood was given into custody—I saw Banks again on April 7th with eight or nine others, and picked him out at once—I have no doubt he is the man.

JOHN WINDSOR( Detective V ). I received a description from Carter, and arrested the prisoner in Latimer Road—it was 10 a.m.—he was driving a pony in a costermonger's cart—I said, "I am going to search you," and told him to get out—I took some good money from his pocket—I said, "I shall take you to the station"—he said, "What for?"—I said, "On suspicion of uttering"—he was placed with others and identi fied—Carter had previously given a description, and I told him to go at seven o'clock—there is no truth in the statement that the lads were pointed out by the police or that the witnesses could see through a window. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to Her Majesty's Mint—these coins are both bad and from different moulds—it is the custom of people engaged in coining to wrap the coins up in paper.
