   Prisoner's Defence.  On the 19th of January I was in the employ of Mr. Scudds, at the Joiners' Arms, Camberwell—about half-past nine o'clock in the evening he brought this horse to me, and told me to take it to Mr. Heath, in the New-road, and ask him to let him put it up there for the night, and he dare say he should be there as soon as me—I asked Heath if Scudds had been there, and he said, "No"—I told him what I had come for, and he told me to put it into the stable. ARNOLD HEATH re-examined.  I had known Scudds about a month—it was him who took the horse away on Tuesday—the prisoner was not present then—he never brought a horse to my stables before—he is a horse clipper I bad known the prisoner about a fortnight before—I knew him in the employ of Scudds—he staid at the stable till Scudds came, and then went away with him—Scudds is about twenty-eight years old. THOMAS HUDSON. I am a fishmonger, and live at Camberwell I know the prisoner—on the 25th of January he came and asked if I did not want to buy a horse—I said I did—he brought a pony with him, which he asked 2 l . 5 s . for—I bid him 30 s .—he said he must go back and ask his partner Scudds if he would take the money—he brought the horse back that night, and said he had been to his partner, and his partner said he was to take the money—I am positive he used the word "partner"—I have seen Scudds and the prisoner together once or twice—Scudds is a great deal older than the prisoner. NOT GUILTY.

I aw the deceased, but I do not recollect on what day—I made no minute of the circumstance, but I think it was on a Monday, five or six weeks ago he called himself Hay wood—I do not know his Christian name. ROBERT AUSTIN. I am a labourer, and live at Norwood. The deceased, Emery Haywood, had lodged with me twelve months, and was twenty-five yean old—on Monday, the 23rd of January, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, the prisoner brought him home—they walked together, and did not seem in ill humour with each other—I went into the room, and Haywood's wife came down with a candle—the prisoner said, "Is this your husband?"—she said, "Yes"—the prisoner said, "Then I desire you to keep him at home"—he then went away, and just as he got out at the door, he called Haywood out, and said to him, "Now you mind, don't let me see you on my premises any more"—Haywood and his wife then went up stairs—I went up also—I picked two shots out of his back, but they were very small—he complained of having been wounded—I did not learn that any body had been present when he received the injury—there were some shots round his loins, but I could not get at them—he did not go to work that week—he went to work on the Monday, Tuesday, and Wednes day and on Wednesday night he was confined to his bed—he died on saturday morning, the 4th of February.
