 1727.  JOSHUA HILL was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Bellamy ( Susannah Hollinshead and others being therein) and stealing 2 sheets, value 6s.; 1 table-cloth, value 2s. and 1 piece of tick, value 2s., his property.
   SARAH BELLAMY. I am the wife of John Bellamy; we live in Gibralter-walk, Bethnal-green. On Sunday, the 6th of August, at three o'clock, I went to the hospital to my husband, who had a broken leg - I left the prisoner, who is my lodger, in the yard; I left Susannah Hollinshead and some other persons in the house; there was no one in my apartments, which are the two lower rooms - the prisoner took his meals in our kitchen - the front window was fastened by two falling drops - and when I went out I fastened the door of my apartment - I returned about nine o'clock in the evening, and found the prisoner on the stairs, very much in liquor; he said he had lost the key of his door, and could not get into his room - I put him to a bed, in another room, where he slept all night - he went out the next morning - I received information, and went in search of him, and found him at a public-house, at the corner, pretty much in liquor - I asked where my two shillings were, which he had taken out of the drawer in my room - he said "Here it is, take it and bed d - d;" I had missed the two shillings from a screw-box in the drawer, and the rest of the property - we usually fasten my backwindow with a gimblet.
   GEORGE SMITH. I am headborough. I went with Bellamy to her house, on the 7th of August; I found the prisoner there drunk - I took him to the watch-house - I said I must search him - and he gave me two duplicates of a sheet and a piece of cotton.
   GEORGE JOHN BOLTON. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet and a remnant, pawned by the prisoner, early in the morning of the 7th of July; I gave the duplicates the witness has just produced.
 COURT to Mrs. BELLAMY. Q. Is this your sheet? A. Yes; it was safe during the absence of my husband; but I cannot say it was safe on the 6th of August - I had seen the money locked into the drawer that day - the drawer, where the sheet was, was not locked - he had lived with us fourteen or fifteen years - he had access to all our property - we had a good opinion of him. GUILTY. Aged 77. Of stealing only.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1728.  SUSAN MELSOM was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Gibbons , about eleven o'clock in the morning of the 11th of July ( William Gibbons and Robert Gibbons being therein), and stealing 1 apron, value 6d., and 1 shirt, value 4d., his property.
   ANN GIBBONS. I am the wife of John Gibbons - we live in Type-court, St. Luke's. On the 11th of July I left the house at five o'clock in the morning, and left William and Robert Gibbons, my two children, in bed and asleep; the house is let out in tenements; I have only the lower part. I went to Spitalfields-market - I left the room door on the latch; my husband had gone out some time before; I returned at six o'clock, and was told I had been robbed; I went to the watch-house, and saw my apron and a shirt, which I had seen safe at five o'clock - the shirt was on the bed, and I think the apron was over the head of the bed; the prisoner was a stranger.
   ELIZABETH PEARES. I am the wife of Thomas Peares - we lodge in this house. On the morning of the 11th of July I was coming up from the cellar, and heard the room door shut; I said, "Is that Mrs. Gibbons," but no person answered; I went out at the street door, and saw the prisoner, who had a basket, with an apron or something in it I went and told her she did not belong to that place - she said she did; I brought her back, and said to her, "These things are not your's, for I saw the woman tying up onions in this apron, and this is the shirt the little sailor boy wore"- she then threw them down.
   WILLIAM RICE. I am a watchman. I took the prisoner; she said she was drunk, but she was not - she gave no account of the property.
   GEORGE GARROD. I am a watchman. I assisted in taking the prisoner - what Rice has said is true.
   JOHN BROWN. I am watch-house-keeper. I searched the prisoner, and found 3d. in her pocket. GUILTY. Aged 22. Of stealing only.
 Recommended to Mercy - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1729.  GEORGE SMITH was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Henry Carter , about six o'clock in the forenoon of the 14th of July (no person being therein), and stealing 1 gun, value 12s., and 1 gown, value 7s., his property.
 HENRY CARTER. I live in  Maiden-lane, Islington. - On the 14th of July I had my gun and this gown safe when I went out, at half-past four o'clock in the morning - my wife came and told me they were gone; I came and missed them; I saw them four days afterward, at Bow-street - I had left my wife in the house, and my child in bed.
   DOROTHY CARTER. I am the prosecutor's wife. On the morning of the 14th of July I came down stairs and saw my window open, and missed the gown; I applied to my husband to know if he had opened the window; my child and I had been in bed till I came down.
