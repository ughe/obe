The two men also got off the tram and Brown invited them in. We
were in there about three-quarters of an hour. The two men had
some drink which Brown paid for and he gave them another shilling
each. After leaving the Swan" we went to my house in Milford
Road, about a quarter of an hour's walk. The two men were behind
us in the Whitmore Road, about 100 yards. When we got to my
house Brown asked my permission to invite them in and I gave it.
They came in to supper. Before supper Brown asked prisoner to
get some beer; ae went out and got a gallon. Then prisoner went
with Brown to the corner shop to get the supper. When they were
on the point of leaving prisoner said to Brown, "Who is going to
start the game 1" The two men and Brown went into the passage
and closed the door. I could see what happened. Brown went to
shake hands, but prisoner struck him on the nose; then they
wrestled with him but could not get him down, I could see from the
light in the kitchen. I went to Brown's assistance, and got knocked
about. I had a black eye. I could not say whose hand struck me
Besides the souffle I could hear the jingling of money. The men
then went off. My daughter ran to get assistance and I attended to
Brown. My daughter is now very ill, and unable to attend here
I found Brown bleeding at the nose. He said he was injured in
ternally. He had to go to the hospital next day, and remained there
as an in-patient for a considerable time. Ho afterwards had to attend
the hospital for another seven or eight days.

four o'clock. On that day I was not at work. I do not recolleot what
time we left home on that day. We came out with the prosecutot.

went to cash the advance note at the "Peacock," and Smith was
there. We were there about three-quarters of an hour and had two
drinks, or it might have been three. We left there and went to the
"Swan." I am quite positive prisoner came into the "Swan." Brown
paid the fare of the two men and paid for the drink at the Pen
cock." I cannot say how long we stopped in the "Swan." It was
quite dark when we got home. They left my house at about
quarter to 10; they had been there about three hours. There is no
light in my passage, but when the kitchen door was open there was
full light from the kitchen, and I could see out there. The next time

Court. There were two other men coming out of the van. I did
not see an old man get out. I was standing in the yard outside the
