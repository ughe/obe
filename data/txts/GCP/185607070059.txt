WILLIAM LEONARD. I live at Caroline-street, Rateliffe, and have been in
the service of the Trinity Company twelve years, and am still. On 24th
June I was with Neil at the Türk's Head, about half past 2 o'clock in the
morning-we were talking about sailing, and the prisoner came in and
interfered with us-Neil went out, and went down our alley-I waited
while the prisoner went out, and walked out after him-I sat down on
the butcher's step, and saw Neil leaning on a post I had had & drop to
drink, but knew what I was about when the prisoner and prosecutor were
together, I caine up with my eyes as if I had just woke up, and ran down,
and saw the prisoner strike Neil with his hands, I thought, but I took the
prisoner by his hands, and he had a knife Neil was lying on the ground,
bloeding-I saw him strike Neil one blow, but what he gave him before I
cannot say-I never saw so much blood in a human being in my life I
shouted, "Police !" and in four or five minutes & policeman came I had
been drinking, but had a good deal of my senses about me_Cox, the police
constable, gave me a very nice rub with his elbow, and I got a very nice
character from the prisoner, but I am not a paramour of women, though
Mr. Pelham stated it in the Weekly Dispatch, and I may lose my character
through it.
