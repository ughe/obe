 1498.  MARY ANTHONY was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , a coat, value 8 s., the goods of Patrick Carmoady.
 PATRICK CARMOADY. I lost my coat on Monday, the 12th of September, from the tester of my bed - it was safe about ten o'clock, when I went to the Session House; I came back and said, I would take the prisoner to Bow-street - she said, "Do not take me, and I will tell."
   MARY CARMOADY. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw the coat at twelve o'clock that day, when the prisoner, who lives in the kitchen, took it to brush - I was in bed at the time, and thought she was going to take it to my husband - when he came home from his beat in the morning, she said she had pawned it at the corner of Blackmore-street, and torn the ticket.
   WILLIAM MAINWARING FENNER. I am shopman to a pawnbroker at the corner of Blackmore-street. I never saw the prisoner in our shop, nor is this article entered in our book.
     GUILTY. Aged 17.
 Judgment Respited.
 1499.  STEPHEN HARMER and WILLIAM CARTER was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of June , five quarts of gin, value 10 s.; two quarts of rum, value 7 s., and ten bottles, value 10 d., the goods of John Craddock Bettridge.
 MR. CRESWELL conducted the prosecution.
 JOHN CRADDOCK BETTRIDGE. I am a  victualler, and live in Ratcliff-highway. Harmer was in my employ, and Carter was there occasionally - in consequence of information, I went into my wash-house, and found some bottles which had recently had spirits in them - I left them there, and on the morning of the 29th, I saw Harmer and my son go down stairs - I followed them, and saw Harmer go back to the wash-house - I then heard Carter ask my son where Stephen was; he said, he was out backwards - I looked out at a window, and saw the two prisoners go out; I went down and looked about, and found a bottle of gin in the copper-hole - I went and asked my son, if Carter was gone - Carter heard me, and said, No, and asked me for a glass of gin, which I served him with - I then went and asked Harmer what was the cause of the bottle of gin being in the copper-hole - he said, he was very sorry for what he had done, but he had admitted Carter to sleep there, in consequence of his being locked out from home, and that Carter had gone into the bar, and drawn the spirits. I asked him if he knew of any more being concealed, he declared, he did not - but I looked about, and under some water-butts, and some saw-dust, I found six bottles more of gin, and three of rum - Harmer was present, and declared, in the most positive terms, that he knew not how they got there.
 Cross-examined by MR. LAW. Q. All you know of Carter is, that he went into the wash-house; and then called for a glass of gin? A. Yes, and all the rest I heard from the other prisoner in Carter's absence - I believe I had other lodgers in the house - Harmer and my son slept together, but my son is not here - I perhaps might have played at skittles with one of the prisoners for a pot of beer.
 MR. CRESWELL. Q. How many bottles did you put there to wash? A. I suppose about thirty - I suppose ten or twelve were left.
   WILLIAM SUMMERS. I am an officer. I took Harmer into custody on the 29th of June - he said, he was very sorry, but he had admitted Carter to be in the house two nights - that his master was very good, and he was extremely sorry for what he had done - Carter was taken in about a fortnight.
   FRANCIS JACKSON. I am an officer. I received charge of Carter, at the watch-house, on the 21st of July - he said, he knew nothing at all about the gin - I do not recollect his saying any thing further.
 HARMER'S Defence. When I go to bed, I cannot come down, except one of the family gets up, because the door is fastened.
 CARTER'S Defence. I am quite innocent - I have used the house as a customer, and I am very sorry I did, for that has brought me here.
 Two witnesses who gave Harmer a good character, engaged to find him employment.
 HARMER - GUILTY. Aged 21.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1 s., and Discharged.
 CARTER -    NOT GUILTY.
 Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1467.  LOUISA LLOYD was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , a hat, value 5 s.; two sheets, value 5 s.; a pillow-case, value 4 d.; two shirts, value 5 s.; a shift, value 2 s.; a cap, value 1 s.; a napkin, value 1 s.; a gown, value 3 s.; a set of bed curtains, value 3 s.; a pair of shoes, value 1 s., and a flat iron, value 6 d., the goods of William Billing.
   ANN BILLING. My husband's name is William Billing - we live in Ray-street, Clerkenwell. The prisoner lodged in my house for about two months. On the 27th of August I missed an umbrella; I asked her about it; she at first denied it, but afterwards took a sheet off the bed, and got the umbrella from the pawnbroker; I then searched and missed the articles stated in the indictment: she had no authority to pawn them - she had had the linen to wash.
   JOSEPH JOHN GOOD. I live with Mr. William Mote, pawnbroker, of Little Warner-street. I have some articles which the prisoner left at our house.
   THOMAS LINSCOTT. I live with Mr. Croft, pawnbroker, Turnmill-street, Clerkenwell. I have a number of articles pawned by the prisoner, at different times.
   WILLIAM REED. I am an officer. I took the prisoner and found some of the duplicates in her pocket.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I intended to have returned them on the following Monday.
     GUILTY. Aged 31. Transported for Seven Years.
