   Cross-examined by MR. CROUCH . Q.  You had Carrington in custody first? A.  Yes—Appleton came up, in a minute or two afterwards, with fourteen or fifteen more, and was picked out—I cannot say that he saw me previous to his coming near me—he came up at the back of the coaches—he had no op portunity of getting away—I called to him, and said, "I shall want you"—he went to the station willingly—when he was charged with the robbery he said he knew nothing at all about it—I bad seen the mob come out of the King's Arms there, and knew they had been there to what they call a Fifth of November supper, which they have had, to my knowledge, for the last ten years—I saw nothing of the fight—I heard of it afterwards—the prosecutor and his party appeared sober, but flurried—they did not appear to have been drinking—Appleton did not appear flurried, as if he had been chased—I searched them, but found nothing on them. NOT GUILTY.
 260. JAMES NICHOLLS and EDWARD CARRINGTON were again  indicted for assaulting Archibald Pennycook , with intent to rob him. ARCHIBALD PENNYCOOK. About one o'clock on the morning of the 8th of Nov., I was with Phillips and Isabella Hooper—there was a mob at the bottom of William-street—I went towards it—Appleton spoke to me, and after he left me I received a blow on the hat—I turned round, and saw Nicholls with his hand held up, which descended on my hat again—I re ceived a blow from a person in the mob which broke the crown of my hat—I did not see Carrington do anything—nothing was done to my person or pro perty to intimate that there was any desire to rob me. ISABELLA HOOPER. I saw both the prisoners in the mob—I did not see them do anything. ROBERT ROGER PHILLIPS. I believe that Carrington struck Pennycook, but I am not positive—I saw Nicholls strike him afterwards on his hat—my watch was stolen as soon as I received the blow from Appleton. NOT GUILTY.

I am the wife of Joseph Kendall, of Agar-cottage, Sid ney-place, Clapham-road, in the parish of Lambeth. The prisoner came into our service on the 5th of May, and left on the Monday following—on the 14th of Nov., a little after seven o'clock in the morning, I missed the articles stated, which I had seen safe on Sunday night—when I went down in the morning three doors were open, which had been fastened over night—they had been opened from within—the prisoner was not living with me at this time—I had not seen her on the Sunday—I do not know how she got there—I made the house fast over night, at a little after nine o'clock—I am sure it was after nine—every thing was safe—I cannot say that I particularly observed these articles after nine o'clock—I was in the kitchen, and did not miss anything.
