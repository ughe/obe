SARAH TORY. I am a widow-I keep a chandler's shop in Albert-place
Bermondsey. On Friday evening, the 19th of Feb., Haley came for half an
onnce of tea, which came to 14d.-she gave me a sixpence-I gave her 4d.
change-l put the sixpence in the till the policeman came in instantly, and
asked me what she had given me- I took it out and gave it him-he told
me to mark it, and I did there was no other sixpence in the till.
CATHERINE HASLER. I am the wife of George Hasler-he keeps the Ship
public-house at Horslydown. On Friday evening, the 19th of Feb., at a
quarter before nine o'clock, Haley came in for a pint of porter, and brought her
own jug-I served her, and she gave me a shilling--the policeman was there
- he came and told me the woman was offering me bad money-I had not
laid the shilling out of my hand—I gave it to him and he took her-I asked
where she got it and she told me at the Kent-road.
John WHITLAMB (police-constable M 89.) On the 19th of Feb. I was in
company with West, in plain clothes--I saw the prisoners in company toge-
ther in Long-lane, Bermondsey, proceeding in a direction to Mrs. Rook's
shop-I saw them pass the sbop, and at a few yards distance they stood talking
for about a minute-Haley went into the shop, and Peel looked in the shop
while Haley was in—when Haley came out she went down a turning and stood
in the dark till Peel joined her--they then proceeded till they came to Mrs.
Tory's shop-Haley went in, leaving Peel outsideI saw him pass and
repass the shop, and he stood in a dark turning-Haley came out of the shop,
they joined again, and went to the Ship-Haley left Peel and went to the
bar-she called for a pint of beer, and I saw her lay this shilling down-I
cautioned Mrs. Hasler, and said she was taking bad money-Haley said she
was in the habit of coming in there every night, and if it was bad she did not
know it; that she had taken it for some work she had done in the Kent-road
-while I was talking to her Peel came in, and was secured.
THOMAS WEST (police-constable M 249.) On the 19th of Feb. I was in
company with Whitlamb-I saw the two prisoners in company in Long-lane
--they went on to Mrs. Rook's shop-Haley went in, and when she came
out I went in and received a shilling I came out and found the prisoners
again in company--they went to Mrs. Tory's-Haley left Peel a little dis-
tance from the shop-she went into the shop, and as soon as she came out I
went in and received this sixpence-I came out and saw Peel with the loaf
of bread which Haley had brought out of Rook's shop-they then went to the
Ship at Shad Thames-Haley went in, leaving Peel outside-he then went in
-1 followed and took him-I found on him two sixpences, 7 d. in coppers,
some sugar, and a little tea- I told him I wanted him for uttering base coin
with the woman-he said he never saw her before-on the way to the station
Haley said, "What is this young man taken for?" I said for being con-
nected with her, in passing counterfeit coin-she said she never saw him
before.
Mr. John Field. These two shillings are both counterfeit, and from the
same mould-the sixpence is also counterfeit.
Haley's Defence. This man is a perfect stranger to me; what they say
against us is quite false ; he never was in company with me at all; I was not
aware the shilling I had was a bad one; the lady had not time to tell me
whether it was bad or good.
