1079. DANIEL BIGGS and HENRY JOYCE were indicted for stealing.
on the 16th of Feb., 1 log of wood, value 28. 6d., the goods of Christopher
Tyler,
MR. CURWOOD conducted the Prosecution.
CorrISTOPHER TYLER. I am a coal-merchant, and have a house at Tlford,
in Essex. I had a pear-tree growing in my garden a year and a half ago.it
was taken up by the roots, and carried to my yard-1 did not miss it-I was
told it was gone.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. You had this in 1841 ? A. Yes,
and it was taken into the yard to be put down as a pile, but it was left on my
wharf, and never put down.

WILLIAM PARSONS (police-constable K 63.) On the night of the 16th of
Feb. I was on duty near Ilford-I know Biggs-I saw him that evening.
with another person, whom I did not know-they were carrying a piece of a
tree-I called out, “Biggs, what are you at?"- they both ran away, and left
the piece of the tree behind them-I got assistance, and took it to the station
I took Biggs at his father's house the same night--I am sure he is the man.
Cross-examined. Q. He said he knew nothing about it, did he not? 4.
Yes-when I saw him with the tree he was about six feet from me, there was
an iron pallisade, about six feet high, which prevented my laying hold of him
-it was seven o'clock at night, but it was not dark-there were two gas-lights,
about fifty yards apart, and he had to run past one of them--the fence was
six feet below my feet-I was above it I could not get over it-I was about
sixteen yards from Biggs. oto

WILLIAM CURLE. I am a gardener, and live at Wanstead. I have worked
for Mr. Tyler-I remember the pear-tree that was grubbed up-I should
know the trunk of it well--this is it-(looking at it) from my knowledge of
the wood of that tree when it was growing, I can undertake to say that this is
part of that tree-I know it by cutting the branches off myself, and the marks
I made on it at the time, and here is the mark of a trellis fence that it laid
against-I am sure it is a part of Mr. Tyler's pear-tree.

Cross-examined. Q. How long is it since you had to do with it? 4.
Twelve months last October-I have not seen it since I have eut branches
off a great many trees, but I can swear to this, because I made marks on it
when I cut the branches off-I have possibly made marks on other trees, but
not the same as these.
John Wiggs. I am a brick-maker's labourer, and live near Ilford. I know
both the prisoners well-I remember, on the 16th of Feb., being at a beer-
shop, where the prisoners were we were very joyous and singing there
Joyce asked me if I would go with him after a piece of wood-I refused
my brother was outside the house he did not come in-I left the house at
the time the prisoner did we went together just outside the door we then
parted, and the prisoners went away together, towards Ilford, and towards
Mr. Tyler's-I bade them good night, and told them to mind they did not
get cotched.
