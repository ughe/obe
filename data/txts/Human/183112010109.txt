 248.  EDWARD UPCHURCH and MARY ALBERDINE were indicted for stealing, on the 21st of October , 1 sovereign, the money of John Smith , from his person.
 JOHN SMITH. I am a  Chelsea pensioner. On the 21st of October I had a sovereign, which was a part of my pension-money; I went to the public-house where I lodge- I was quite capable of taking care of myself; the two prisoners were sitting by me - I took my sovereign out of my pocket; it was in the corner of my handkerchief - I was going to get it changed; Alberdine took it from me - she gave it to Upchurch, and he went away immediately - Alberdine staid a little time, and I argued with her about the money; they had been sitting by my side, but I took no notice of them - I had not known them before; I did not follow Upchurch, because I had a bad leg - I cried out, and threatened Alberdine; I have never seen the sovereign since.
 GEORGE FARLEY. I am a labourer, and a pensioner. I was at the public-house where I lodge - I saw Upchurch, and three of his comrades with him; he went out for half an hour, and then returned with a shilling - the prosecutor was drinking there, and Upchurch said, "I will show as much money as you;" the prosecutor said, "If you will show a shilling, I will show a sovereign" - the prosecutor then showed the sovereign from his handkerchief; Alberdine took it and gave it to Upchurch, who started with it- I ran to the bar; the landlord was not at home - I told his daughter; she said, "What can I do? I can't stop him"- Alberdine then got up to go out; the prosecutor held her for a minute or two, but she got away; they were taken the next morning.
 Upchurch. Q. Why did not you accuse me of it? A. I did, and tried to stop you; I had heard you say in the morning where you lodged, and I gave information.
   WILLIAM BOWEN. I keep the Adam and Eve public-house, not the house where this robbery was committed, but Upchurch came to my house the same afternoon, between three and four o'clock - he changed a half-sovereign, and had two or three pots of beer.
   ROBERT DUFF. I am pay-serjeant of the company in which Upchurch is a private in the Scotch Guards. The last money I paid him was 2s. 6d. on the 18th of October - I paid him that for three days, he was under stoppages, and must have been very short of money; I have known him fifteen years in the regiment, his character has been irregular, but I know no dishonesty of him.
   HENRY WILLIAMS. I am a constable. I took Alberdine being informed by the landlord of the Three Crowns, that a sovereign had been lost in his house; when I took her, I asked her if she knew any thing about a soldier? she said No - a little boy, who was there, said, "Yes, he lives at the Royal Oak, at Knightsbridge;" I went there, and found he had been absent two days - I afterwards took him in the guard-room.
 Upchurch's Defence. I receive 9s. 4d. a week, but I am quartered, and sometimes it does not cost me 2s. a week for living, because I carry out beer for the landlord.
 Alberdine put in a written Defence, declaring her innocence.
 UPCHURCH -    GUILTY. Aged 39.
 ALBERDINE -    GUILTY. Aged 38. Confined Six Months.
 249.  EDWARD SWEENEY, JAMES HENESSEY, and DENNIS BUCKLEY were indicted for stealing, on the 14th of November , 1 watch, value 25l.; 2 keys, value 3s., and 1 seal, value 5s., the goods of William Hardy , from his person.
   WILLIAM HARDY. I am a carpenter. I and my brother were at a beer-shop in Essex-street, Whitechapel, on the 14th of November; we left the house at ten minutes before ten o'clock at night - the prisoners were in the room while I was; there were forty persons in the room -I was quite sober; when I was leaving the room, I put down my hand and felt my watch chain - as I was coming down stairs, Sweeney and Henessey stood one on each side the door; Buckley was a little before them, going down the stairs before me - I do not know that he did any thing to prevent my going down stairs then, but when he came down to the street door, he stopped the doorway up, to prevent my going out - the other two prisoners were then close by me; we at last got out of the house, and I immediately missed my watch - I did not know which way the prisoners had gone - I looked for them, when I missed my watch, and they were all gone; and the people about said it must be these three young men, as nobody else was a thief; I gave information of them - they were all about me, and hustling me at the time Buckley was stopping the doorway.
 Cross-examined by MR. STURGEON. Q. I believe this was a smoking-club? A. I believe it is called the Free and Easy club - I do not know whether the prisoners are members, but I saw them in the room; when I got up to go the other persons in the room followed very shortly; no one was near me in going down but the three prisoners - I felt my watch safe while I was in the room, and missed it when I got out at the street door; no one touched me on the stairs but the prisoners - the stairs are narrow; no one attempted to hustle me but the prisoners.
   JOHN HARDY. I was with my brother, and saw the three prisoners there; when my brother was going down Henessey and Sweeney got one on each side of the door, and on each side of my brother, and when they got to the end of the passage Buckley stood with his hand across the door, and they were all close to my brother; I had seen the chain of my brother's watch as he left the room, but the seal was out of sight - when we got into the street I asked him if he had lost his watch; he felt, and it was gone - the prisoners were all gone directly.
