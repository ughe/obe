497. THOMAS HENRY BENNETT (32), and HENRY KENZETT (40),
Unlawfully conspiring together to cheat and defraud James Westaway of
his goods.
MR. LILLEY conducted the Prosecution; MBSRS. GRIFFITHS and SMITH
defended Bennett; and MR. SIMs defended. Kensett.
JAMES WESTAWAY. I am a gardner at Ashburton, in Devonshire, and
also near Staverton-during the summer I had about fourteen tons of fruit
to dispose of, I have a step-son whose name is John Reddaway-about the
middle of July he came to London, and I authorised him to advertise in the
London papers for the purpose of finding purchasers--I subsequently
received a communication from Bennett and Co.-I have not been able to
find that letter-I received other letters from Bennett and Co., but I think
it was in consequence of the first letter that I sent a sample of the fruit-
afterwards, during the month of September, I sent fruit from time to time
-on 12th September I sent 3 tons 4 cwt. of apples and 41 cwt of pears,
the charge for which was 382, 58.-on 19th September 1 ton 5 cwt. of
apples, 16 cwt. of pears, 211, 158,—that includes the hampers and empties
- in consequence of a telegram I sent 38 lbs. of grapes at 18, 9d, per lb.,
and on the 22nd I sent 18 lbs.-on the 25th I sent 1 ton 10 cwt. of pears,
181. 58., and 3 tons of apples, 251. 158.-on 26th September 1 ton 5 ewt. of
apples, 101. 12s. 6d, 11 cwt. of pears 61. 178. 6d., and the total value of the
fruit sent was about 1301.—they were consigned to Bennett and Co., 74,
Little Britain-I authorised my step-son to receive the money for the fruit.
JAMES REDDAWAY, I have seen Bennett write and know his handwriting
perfeótly well—these letters are in his writing.
JOHN WESTAWAY (continued). These are the letters I received from time
to time in the progress of sending the fruit to London—there is one letter
missing, and it was in the same handwriting as these - it was concerning
the quantity of fruit I had to sell, asking for samples and the price-I sent
& sample and received a letter from Bennett to say he would give me 81. 108.
for apples and 121. 108. for pears all round, and after the receipt of the
goods he would send the cash within seven days—the 14th August was the
day of the first consignment-I must have received another letter before
that-I sent all the fruit and got none of the money—the statement made
in the letters was that H. Bennett and Co. were merchants at 74, Little
Britain-I received a letter from my step-son during the progress of the
matter stating that they were salesmen in Covent Garden Market-all the
consignments were addressed to 74, Little Britain.
