Jaffe gave me then a further order for 25 roubles. I said to him 'These are not the 25 fives which were in circulation,' but he informed me that the old ones had been drawn in and a new issue had come out. He gave me three samples of these, and he gave a deposit of 25 l . to see something done towards being made. When he saw that the plates were in hand he supplied more money, to the extent of nearly 200 l . Jaffe had all the goods that were manufactured, and they passed through his hands, and now seeing that they are out of date, and no one wants to buy any more, desired me to make some new ones, and he sent 29 l . to Nice, which can be proved by the lips of the landlady's daughter where I resided for seven years in Hamford Street, the Pickersgill; but she did not know what the money was for, Jaffe sent that money to have new 25-roubles made. The man being dead I could not do it; he died about three years ago. Jaffe is a native of Kurland and had a manufactory there. The man's name who made the plates in Kurland is Braim. That man stood in the same dock, and he told me all about Jaffe and his career. Bear this in mind: not only had Jaffe goods through me, but he was the promoter of several factories in London, which nearly all fell but one; I believe so, I do not know there are any more. He had 3 l . 5 s . 10 d ., and he sent them all abroad. He never had another business besides dealing in Russian rouble notes. I do not know Russia, and never was there in my life, and never negotiated nor offered to any one goods of that description. I can always make a living wherever I go. I am well known on the turf and am a general dealer. I am a good linguist and can speak six languages, and always earn a living wherever I go. Jaffe used frequently to visit me in Stamford Street. I positively assure you that in the event of getting my liberty I will render you all the assistance possible. I have the sources of that kind of information, and just because I am in trouble they all sympathise with me, and therefore I shall be able to fulfil my promise, and more so that the Russian business of that description shall never appear in this country.— N. "October 17, 1883.—As I omitted on the last occasion to state to the Treasury, I am in a position to prove in evidence that Jaffe not only gave the money for the purpose of manufacturing plates, but he had them also in his own possession for a considerable length of time for the purpose of getting money under the pretence that he had the manufacturer who makes the notes and required the means to carry it out He had the plates stored away by an old man, who will come forward as a witness, to prove that Jaffe asked him to take care of these for him, and removed them from time to time from his place so as to show the people, and thereby induce them to part with their money. The plates were shells, and Jaffe had them filled up by a foreigner whom I hope to be able to produce at my trial. Jaffe had also a printing press, and tried to take proofs from the plates, so as to endeavour and send them abroad to finish them. I shall be able to produce people to whom he showed the plates.— HOLCHESTBR ."
