Mary Ann BOYER. I am the wife of Henry Boyer, and am female
searcher at the Bishopsgate-street Police-station-I was directed to go to the
Saracen's Head, Northumberland-alley on Thursday morning 5th November
- I was there when the prisoner came in with a woman-they called for a
quartern of brandy--they then stepped a little distance from me, and I saw
& small parcel of something pass from the prisoner's hand to the female's,
he took it from his right-hand pocket-I afterwards saw the female before
the Magistrate-she is the prisoner's wife-Tilley had communicated with
me, and I was there expressly to watch.
GEORGE WAITNEY. I am a detective police officer-on this Tursday morn.
morning I was with Tilley in plain clothes, and saw the prisoner and the
woman go into the Saracen's Head-when they came out they separated at
the top of Northumberland-alley, and the prisoner went into a barber's shop
-I saw him an hour afterwards in London-street, after the female was
charged-he was outside his master's house-I told him I wished to examine
his boxes-he said, “Very well," and went back with me-be said, “Here
are the keys"-I then asked him if he had been out-he said, "Yes"-
I asked him if he had met any person-be at first declined to answer, saying
he would tell the Magistrate-as we were going to the police-court I told
him he need not answer my questions unless be liked-I then asked him if he
had met a female-- he said he had met his wife--I asked whether he had given
her a parcel-he said, "Yes"-I said, “How much 9"-he said, “Eighteen
shillings "-I asked where he got it from-he said, "It was paid to me last
Dight by a party who owed it me"-I asked him who that was-he said he
should tell the Magistrate-I searched his boxes, but found nothing-on the
way to the station he said that eight shillings of the money had been paid
to him on the previous night, and ten shillings that morning-I asked him
then by whom, and he declined to tell me-I was present when Mr. Forster
marked the money--I can identify three florins and two shillings,
Cross-examined. 2. Did you put another question to him then after he
had declined to answer ? A. I did-I think that is part of my duty.
MR. POLAND.Q. Were you before the Magistrate ? A. Yes-the prisoner
did not tell the Magistrate of whom he received this money.
