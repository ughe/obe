   MR. MOIR . Q.  Is there any pretence for suggesting that your son went to Chadwell Heath in order to get out of the way? A.  None whatever—he knew no more about the job when he came home than I did—I don't know why I was not called at the police-court.
   COURT . Q.  Why did you not tender yourself as a witness if you knew you could prove that your son was at home? A.  I did—I was at the police-court, but I was never called for—I entrusted my solicitor with all these matters—Mr. Addingham is my solicitor, there he is; Mr. Wells, I should say—I am not aware that any statement was made to the Magistrate as to what evidence I could give—the case was sent for trial, and there appeared to be an end of it, according to what I could understand. ANN REBECCA MOORE. I am fourteen years of age—I have a bed in the same room where my brother, the prisoner, sleeps; it is not shut off, there are curtains round—I remember Sunday, 24th November—my brother was at home all day and all night—I saw him at half-past six on Monday morning, and again about eight, when he got up to breakfast—I was at the Stratford police-court, but was not called.
