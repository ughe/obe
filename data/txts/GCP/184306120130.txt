1851. JOHN TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of Mar
4lbs. 12oz, weight of copper, value 18. 6d., the goods of Robert Glass the
younger, in a vessel in a port of entry of discharge.
EDWARD BARKER. I am second officer on board the Tecumtia, in the
West India Docks. On the 22nd of May I was on board—I saw the prisoner
- I asked him what he wanted-he said he was waiting to get employ-I saw
him leave the deck with something under his jacket I took him with this
copper—it belongs to the owner of the ship, Robert Glass, jun. ae
John Grant. The prisoner was given into my charge-he had part de
this copper under his jacket, and part was given to me by Barker, which the
prisoner had dropped.
Prisoner's Defence. I was waiting for a job; my master told me to go on
board, and he would give me one; the mate said, “ What are you doing
here?” I went away, and found this copper in the dirt-bin, on deck; I was
tipsy.
John Grant re-examined. He did not appear tipsy at the time, but on
the road he appeared very tipsy.

1852. EDWIN SHAY was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of May,
1 half-crown, 1 penny-piece, and 2 half-pence, the goods of Thomas Osborn,
from the person of Thomas Osborn the younger.
HARRIET OSBORN. I am the wife of Thomas Osborn-I have a little boy
named Thomas Osborn-on Saturday evening, the 13th of May, I sent him
out with a half-crown, one penny, and two half-pence--he came back with
the prisoner and a policeman, and said, in the prisoner's presence, that the
prisoner took the money, and gave it to the other boy, who gave it to him
again, and then ran away--this is the paper it was wrapped in, and here is
the piece I tore it from.
RICHARD Brown. I heard the little boy complain of the loss of his
money-I ran after the prisoner, and overtook bim-he pulled out this
money and paper, and said it was not him that took it, it was the other
boy.
Timothy Hayes (police-constable K 201.) I took the prisoner.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Guilty. Aged 14.- Confined Four Days, and Whipped.

11dwts. and 12grs. of gold, value 41., the goods of George Harrop and
another.

enwell—the prisoner was my apprentice-on the 25th of May, we looked into

14dwts. of gold cuttings--in the afternoon we asked for his cuttings, and said
we wanted to melt them up he gave up 3dwts. twenty odd grains, and said
that was all he had—when he left at night the boxes were all brought in-
the foreman asked me to look into the prisoner's box to see if these cuttings
were there they were not-I told him to look into his drawer, and they were
not there-I watched the prisoner out, and said, “John, I want you; where
are those cuttings out of your box ?"- he said, “I have got no cuttings"!
said, “ You may as well tell the truth, and pull them out of your pocket"
said he had none. I called the foreman-he said, “Where are the cuttings
and he pulled out this tin box, with the cuttings in it weighing 1oz. 11dwes.
12grs.--they had no business in his pocket.
