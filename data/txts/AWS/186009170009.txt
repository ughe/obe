746. ALFRED HOARE (24), Feloniously breaking and entering the
warehouse of Zacharias Finlay and another, and stealing 113 pieces of
ribbon, value 268L., 880 ostrich feathers, and other articles, their property.
Second Count, receiving the same.
MR. DICKIE conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM ARTHUR EADE (City-policeman, 146). On 24th August I saW
the prisoner wheeling a truek with a box and a bag on it, from Monkwell-
street into Cripplegate-buildings shortly afterwards stopped him and
asked him where he was going : he told me to the Fastern Counties Railway
LI asked him where he brought it from, he said 26, Wood-street," that was
not the way from Wood-street-I took him to the station and employed a
man to take the truck and box-I searched him at the station and found a
cotton handkerchief upon bim-I afterwards broke the chest open in his
presence, and found this jemmy at the top and 130 rolls of ribbon, 15 head
dresses, 2 pieces of crape, 30 yards, 14 bottles of wine, 24 ostrich feathers,
9 packages of silk, from 8 to 112 yards in each packet, and 7 remnants of silk,
average 12 yards each.
Cross-ezamined by MR. RIBTON. 2. What street Was he in when you saw
him 7 A. In Hart-street, which is quite a different way from Wood-streeta
I have not had any money from the prosecution I will swear I have not
been promised any money-he said he had been employed by a gentleman at
26, Wood-street-this is the bag (produced) that he had in the truck; the
box is not here-it was nailed down and corded with small oordathe bag
was under the box.
ZACHARIAS FINLAY. I am a manufacturer, of 35, Monkwell-street, close
to Hart-street-I left the premises about half-past seven on the 23d, the night
of the robbery, quite safe all the doors were shutI was the last person on
the premises that nightI returned next morning at about half-past 8I
tried to open the door, and after a great deal of difficulty I succeeded--] found
the inside door of the warehouse forced open, and the boxes which contained
these feathers and the papers containing these silks, lying open the boxes
which contained the ribbons were lying about on the floorI found three
empty bottles which had contained wine the previous nightI found the
counting-house door broken open and the desk, and 4l. 118. 3d. and four-
pence taken out of the desk-the papers were not thrown about much-a
new pair of boots were also taken, and an old pair left in their stead--the
boxes contained feathers, a great portion of them-two drawers upstairs
were also broken open, on the second and third floor, and some tiles on the roof
of the house-1 have samples of the property here the value altogether of
the property lost was about 5006- can swear to these goods they have
all my private mark on them-I had not sold them.
WILLAM FENNING (City-policeman 88). I went to the premises of Mr
Finley in Hart-street, and compared the jemmy!; it corresponded exactly
with the marks on the warehouse door, and also on the desks in the counting-
house.
The Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate toas here read as follows
I do not wish to say anything I was asked by a man in Wood-street to
take it to the Eastern Counties Railway, and he said he would give me a
shilling."
GUITY of receiving.-Confined Twelve Months.
