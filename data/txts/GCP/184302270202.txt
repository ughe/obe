JOSIAH ALLWORTH Pash. I am a shoemaker in the Old Kent-road-about
the 21st of October the prisoner called at my house ; he said he was about
to open a shop at Epsom, and wanted a supply of goods to the amount of
about 401.--I said I generally did business for ready money, but if he gave
good referenres and supplied half cash I would supply him, I was referred to
Mr. Hamor, of Bread-street-hill--I went, and found him at home--it seemed
a respectable wholesale warehouse—there were hampers, apparently full
goods, and the counting-house appeared so full that there was hardly room for
me to sit down I told him what I came for-he said Clark was a respech
able man-he had known him many years, and the whole of the family,
they were respectable, his relations were farmers, that his wife had some inde.
pendency, and I might safely trust him; he would prove a good customer-
the 28th of October the prisoner came again-I parted with some goods to
him, to the amount of something over 201.-he paid me 51.- he took them
away in a cart-he was to have come again, and taken part of them--on the
4th of November he came again, and I refused to let him have more I wrote
several letters to “Clark, Epsom," and they were returned.
