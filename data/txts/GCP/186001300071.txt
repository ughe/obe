232. WILLIAM FREDERICK KNOX (28), and JOHN SPOONER
(31), Stealing 1:0lbs. of rope, value 258., the property or William Brown,
in a vessel in a port. MR. O'CONNELL conducted the Prosecution.
RALPH FIELD THOMPSON (Thames police-inspector). On 23d January, I
was on the river in a galley at Blackwall Reach, and saw the prisoner,
Spooner, in a skiff laden with old rope-I asked him where he brought the
rope from he said, " From Victoria-dock"-I asked him what ship-he
suid, "The Lady Combermere "-I asked him if he had any note or receipt
to show that he had brought it from that ship-he said, “Yes," and
produced a receipt, sigued by the captain of the Lady Combermere, for 8cwt.
of old rope - there is no complaint in reference to that, but I observed these
two other coils (produced) in the fore part of the boat-I asked him if he
had bought them also as old rope-he said, " No, a man chucked them into
ny boat "- I doubted it, aud he said ; “Yes, it was in the captain's presence"
- I said, “I have a doubt, and I must certainly take the rope and you to
the station, and make further inquiries "- I took the rope into my galley-
he then said, "To tell you the truth, I bought it of a lighterman named
Brown, for 58."-I asked him where the lighterman was, or whether he
knew him-he said, "Yes, he has a barge now outside the Victoria-dock
eutrunce, and if you like I will go along with yon and sbow you the man"
-he went with me to the Victoria-dock entrance, got on to the dummy and
said, “ There, that is the barge be belongs to, that he came out with "-I
went on board the barge and found two Custom-honse officers in the
cabin--it was laden with old rope from Sebastopol, and one of them was in
charge of it to see after the duty-1 afterwards received information, and
went with Spooner to the Bell and Anchor at Plaistow-I met Knox near
Plaistuw, coming out of his own door, and asked him whether he had sold
any rope to anybody that day ; Spooner then was 180 yards from me
Knox said, “No, I have not " I said, " From information and description,
I have every reason to believe you have "- he said, “No, I have not, Mr.
Thompson, I have sold none"-I proceeded with him to where I had left
Spooner; and said, “Is this the man you bought the rope of!"-Spooner
said, “ Yes"-Knox said, “Well to tell you the truth I have sold him a.
little bit "-I told him I should have to take him in custody for it-he said
he hoped not-I asked him how he came in the possession of the rope-he
said, "It was in the barge cabin, but how it came there I do not know, and
