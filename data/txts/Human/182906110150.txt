 1329.  JOHN SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of June , 1 coat, value 20s., the goods of Timothy Roach.
   MARY ROACH. I am the wife of Timothy Roach: we live in a passage in Berwick-street. The prisoner lodged in the same room with us; last Monday week, I put my husband's coat into a box just before I went out, at twenty minutes after seven o'clock in the evening, and took out an apron - I left the prisoner sitting in a chair; he pretended to be asleep: I came home in a short time - he was there; my husband came in soon after, and the prisoner went into the privy - when he came in he had the coat wrapped up, but the skirt of it hung down; I was standing at the room door, and saw him go past - I called my husband, and said he had his coat; he would not believe it till he looked into the box, and missed it - he then pursued, but did not catch him; the coat has not been found.
 TIMOTHY ROACH. My wife said the prisoner had taken my coat - I looked into the box, and it was gone; I pursued the prisoner, but lost sight of him.
 Prisoner. Q. Is your name Roach? A. Yes; it is not Murphy.
 SAMUEL LINDLEY. I am a constable. I took the prisoner, and said it was for stealing a coat from the prosecutor - he said he was no thief, and would not be taken by any body; he showed fight, but I struck him with my staff - he gave the prosecutor some wounds, and he has not been able to work since.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 1330.  JOHN WHEELER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of June , 1 coat, value 5l. the goods of the   Marquis of Lansdown.
 JOHN GILPIN. I keep the  Mitre and Dove public-house, in King-street, Westminster. Lord Lansdown's carriage was at my door the day before yesterday, at a quarter-past ten at night - I saw a coat go off the box, and gave an alarm; the footman went round the carriage, and took the prisoner.
 JOHN HARRIS. I am a constable. The prisoner was given to me with this coat.
 JOSEPH MUSPRATT. I am footman to Lord Landsdown. I ran round the carriage, and saw the prisoner throw this coat on the wheel - I took him.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Did you not first see it on the wheel? A. No; he was not walking with his hands in his pockets; he had the coat - I saw him throw it on the nearest hind wheel - there were no other persons about; the landlord was talking to us; he said the coat was going - I ran round the carriage - on my oath I saw the coat thrown on the wheel.
 Prisoner's Defence. I came out of the corner door - I went round the carriage; the coat was on the near wheel; I was on the off side with my hands in my pockets; the coachman caught hold of me, and said,"Where is my coat?" - the footman went and got it, and said, "Here it is;" and then he said, "There is no one here but this man, we will give charge of him, in fact, I will swear I saw him throw it."
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Transported for Seven Years.
 1331.  JAMES WALLS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of June , 12 bushels of wheat, value 4l., the goods of Richard Booth Smith.
 RICHARD BOOTH SMITH. I live at  Uxley Farm, Edmonton. On the 12th of June, I detected the prisoner in my barn taking this wheat; he had taken it from a heap and put in another place - I had seen him before, but not employed him - I was called up by Hawtree, and saw a light in the barn - we went to put in a key to unlock the door, the light was then put out; we went in and found about three sacks of wheat, which were dressed, and laid separate from the heap which was not dressed.
   WILLIAM HAWTREE. On the Sunday night, at a quarter-past twelve o'clock, I went to get my horses to go to London - I saw a light in the barn, and called my master - we went, unlocked the barn, and found the prisoner there - we had waited outside watching him for half an hour; some corn had been made ready to put into sacks; it was thrown on the other heap at day-light.
   JOHN CAMP. I am a constable. I went about four o'clock in the morning to the prosecutor's kitchen, where I found the prisoner charged with having been in the barn; he said he was found there - I went to the barn, and near where they said he had stood, I found about half a candle, which had been lighted, and this stick which he had opened the door with.
     GUILTY. Aged 44. Transported for Seven Years.
 1332.  JOSEPH DUDLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 1 necklace, value 15s. the goods of William Spicer , from the person of Emily Spicer.
 MARGARET EASTON. I know Mr. Spicer; he is the father of Emily Spicer - she is sixteen months old. On the 1st of May I took her out; she had a row of beads round her neck; we were at the corner of  Gerrard-street, and three boys came behind me, two of them stood at a little distance; I then saw the beads go from the child's neck - I turned and asked the prisoner for them - he said he had not got them, and ran away; I ran, and called Stop thief! but he got away - they have not been found - no one was near enough to take them but him.
 ROBERT PRIDHAM. I was going along with a board of fish, and saw Easton running with the child - the prisoner ran down Porter-street, and looked to see if any body was running after him - he then turned down Newport-street towards St. Martin's-lane; I lost sight of him - I heard, when I came back, what had been lost.
 PHILIP RILEY. I was sent for about four or five o'clock, and found the prisoner in custody, from the girl's description.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was standing in St. Martin's-lane; the gentleman came and asked if I knew any thing of the beads - I said "No, I am innocent."
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Fourteen Years.
