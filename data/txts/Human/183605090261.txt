EDMUND NORTH. I am shopman to Messrs. Barbey and Jupp. I watched Messrs. Ruck's shop on the 4th of April—I saw Ash go in there—I had a view of the counters and tills—if any persons went to the tills I could see them—I watched from half-past eleven o'clock to three or four o'clock—I saw no person go to the tills besides Caulfield and the lad—I saw Ash go into the shop—I had seen him several times before that day—he did not go into the shop till about a quarter past two o'clock—I am quite positive he is the person—he went to the right hand side of the counter to look at some bacon—Caulfield came round the counter to serve him—Caulfield then returned to the till, and appeared to me to put some money is he drew out the till—and then Ash came out of the shop—I told the constable, and he was taken—I was at the shop door when he was taken—I did not perceive Caulfield hand any thing over to Ash.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  You speak of from half-past eleven o'clock? A.  Yes—Ash could not have got behind the counter without my seeing him. JOHN RUCK. This two shillings and sixpence is part of my marked money—I watched the shop from eleven till two o'clock.

   COURT to HENRY RUCK . Q.  Did you give in the course of that day two shillings and sixpence? A.  All I gave was 6 d . 2 d .
   Q.  You saw some persons come with a ship like that into the shop? A.  Yes—he purchased some goods of me—I think a 1/4 lb. of butter, and two gga—I do not recollect what they came to—I did not give two shillings a change—only 6 d . and 2 d .
   JURY  to JOHN RUCK . Q.  Was the money counted after Ash was apprehended? A.  It was—there was then 1 l . 11 s . 6 d . in the till—it was five half-crowns marked, (one less than I put in); two not marked; mine shillings, marked; five not marked—the increase of copper was not more the 5 s .—an hour and a half elapsed before I counted the money—I did not ount the copper.
