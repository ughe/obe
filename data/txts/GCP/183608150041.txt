Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When be entered your service,
was there not a stipulation between you that he was to be accountable for
all deficiencies ? A. Yes I believe he has a wife and family had no
conversation with him about the property-I wished to have the account
of my plate, but he hesitated, and I said several times, “Do, pray give it
to me" -he always had an inventory, but I desired him to count it over
with me, and he would not do it, I believe he was taken into custody near
my house-I had a very good character with him, the first time he was
with me, from Mrs. Lovegrove, a part of the family who keep the London
Coffee-house.
WILLIAM THOMAS Barton. I produce seven silver spoons, four silver
forks, and a silver butter-knife-they were pawned at different times be-

different times—I do not know which-91. 178. was advanced on them-
they were pledged by the prisoner, in the name of " Hughes, 21, Norfolk
street" -- I took in three of the table-spoons and the butter-knife articles
of the same description had been pawned before I cannot swear to their

Cross-examined. Q. You took from him spoons of the same pattern,
which were redeemed afterwards by him? A. Yes, and pawned again
he came over and over again there was no concealment about his name
and address.
JAMES DUPERE (police-constable C 90.) I apprehended the prisoner in
Tottenham-street, about half-past eleven o'clock, on Sunday night, the 31st
of July, I told him it was for stealing a quantity of plate from his late
mistress-he made no answer, but directed his conversation to the prose-
cutrix's brother, who was with me I searched him, and found some
duplicates, which I produce, and I found some napkins in his coat pooket-
I ascertained that his wife lived at No. 21, Norfolk-street, which is on the
duplicate.
WILLIAM THOMAS BARTON re-examined. These duplicates correspond
with the articles.
CATHERINE PIERCEY re-examined. These are my property, and were
intrusted to the prisoner as my servant.
