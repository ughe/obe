morning and said, "Is there anything I can do in your line!"_I
said, “No, I do not think there is anything I really want." He
said, "How are you off for sugar?" I said, "Well, I can do with
some sugar." He said, "Are you prepared to buy a small lot?” I
said, "What do you call a small lot?" He said, "I have a lot
damaged, which I will clear at a bad price." I said, "What do you
call a bad price?". He said he had some sugar he could supply me
with for £15 10s. down or on a month's credit or £15 ready cash.
told him I was not in the habit of buying such large quantities as I
had not got sale for it and had not sufficient money,
to be able to pay him £15 or £15 10. Thereupon
he said, "If you can show me satisfactory references you
can have it on a month's credit, and by that time you will be
having the holiday over, and you can turn the sugar over." We have a
big stall at one of the fairs- we would sell then about 5 owt. or 6 ewt.
I showed him some bills and he said, "Very good: your references
are good enough; most likely you will have it down either at the
beginning or middle of the following week." I never heard apy
more about it. On the Monday, expecting the sugar down, I put
these receptacles in the storeroom, which the police officers term
parlour, but the sugar never came down until the 25th. During that
time I ran short of sugar, practically buying only just enough to
go along with. I went out on Wednesday at our usual hours and
returned about 9.30 p.m., when Bradfield said to me, "The sugar
has come, guv'nor. I said, “What, at last? Where is it?" He
said, "When I came here the carman had been knocking here for
some time, not being able to get in, and so I told the man to take
it down to the stable that it was a little way off, and it would be
better to take it down there than all the way to Whitechapel." I
said, " Very good-where is the note?" He said, "I have been and
put the thing down somewhere in the flurry and I don't know what
I have done with it." I said, "He has left it down in the stablo"
He said, "Yes; it appears to be a bit damaged." I said, "Is it
damaged very much?” He said, "Not a great deal, but it should
be turned out." I then told him I wanted him up early next morn
ing to do a job. He had to fetch some ladders from some stables
and he must have brought some sugar up then, which he put into
the receptacles. I never saw the boxes of sugar until the detectives
went into the storeroom with me.
