JOHN THOMAS re-examined. Q.  How did you get admission to New-gate? A.  By passing as the father of Riley—I found I must pass as some relation in order to see them—I passed at the gate as Riley's father—I tried to get in as his uncle, and they would not let me in; and I tried as no relation at all, and they would not lot me in.
   J.H. MAYBANK re-examined. Q.  Describe exactly the direction the two persons, you saw took from your shop? A.  They went up Church-street, towards the Broadway—that would lead through the archway—the rail-road crosses Church-street—it was in Broadway I gave them in charge—they must have passed under the archway to get to where I saw them.
   JURY . Q.  How long was it before you got out and followed them? A.  About a quarter of an hour—there was no difficulty in going the dis tance in that time—I went twice that distance—I saw them distinctly run to the archway—Barregan ran straight on, and Riley stood at the corner of the arch, and looked back—I have no doubt of them whatever—they were taken, about a quarter of a mile from the archway—there was no man standing in the street at the time the robbery was committed—I have heard Thomas's evidence—there was no man standing near the spot.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  There could be no man on your side of the street without your seeing him? A.  Not at the distance I could see—I opened my door directly after, and there was nobody—the train of carriages comes down every half-hour I believe—it does not pass my door—I lost sight of the prisoners for about a quarter of an hour—I live about a mile and a quarter from Greenwich Hospital.
   JURY . Q.  Did you see the prisoners take the necklace from the child's neck? A.  Yes—I could not go out in my overalls. NOT GUILTY.
