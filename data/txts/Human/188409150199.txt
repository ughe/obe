GEORGE KEMP. On 21st August, at 2 o'clock, the prisoner came in—I saw Emma Hill take some coin and put it in the tester; I stood by her and saw it break—I said to the barmaid "Go and ask her where she got it from"—she said "I got it from a pawnshop"—I said "It is a lie, show me your ticket"—she said "I won't"—I said "I shall send for a con stable and give you in charge"—she said "You can do so, and then I won't show you the ticket"—I sent for a constable and gave her in charge—she said the pawnshop was in Union Street; that has been shut up for years. ABBAHAM SURREY( Policeman M  300). I was called to the Winchester Arms, and Mr. Kemp gave the prisoner into custody, saying the prisoner had come in for a glass of ale "Tendering me this half-crown," handing me a half-crown—I said to her "How did you come by it?"—she said "I got it at a pawnshop where I pawned my shawl"—I asked her for the ticket—she said "I shan't show it to you, now you know, do as you like"—she said the landlady had pawned the shawl in Union Street, Borough, and she had got the ticket—at the station in answer to the charge she said "The shawl was pawned in Union Street by the land lady, and she has got the ticket"—she gave her address at 5 l ., Little Surrey Street—there is no pawnshop in Union Street, it has been done away with for two and a half years—before the Magistrate she said she had got the half-crown by selling a bunch of flowers to a gentleman, and afterwards told the old story that the landlady had pawned the shawl. ELIZABETH REEVES. I live at 51, Little Surrey Street, Blackfriars Road—the prisoner lodged with me on 21st August—I have never pawned a shawl for her. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. This is a bad half-crown.
   The prisoner in her defence stated that she gave the shawl to a woman to pawn, and that the woman gave her the half-crown and a penny, but that the woman could not now be found, as she had gone to the hop country. GUILTY.
   She then  PLEADED GUILTY  ** to a conviction of felony for having a galvanic battery for the manufacture of counterfeit coin in her possession at this Court in January, 1881, in the name of Ellen Raynor.— Five Years' Penal Servitude.
