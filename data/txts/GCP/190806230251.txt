JAMES BEARD, recalled. In June, 1905, I was walking down the
Mile End Road and saw Calcutt standing outside the" Three Crowns
with a horse and trap. I went up and said to him, "Whose lot is!
this?" He said " It belongs to me. I have taken it for a debt from
friend at Southend. Will you buy it?" I said, "What do you want
for it? He said, "I will take 445 from you." I said, “You know I
have just sold mine off that cost me nearly £100 for half the money : 1
don't want it." Then he said, "Do you think Mr. Hirst would buy it?"
I said, "You had better ask him.” He said, "Well, I think I ought
to get a bit more off of him." I said, "You must do the best you
can." That ended the conversation. I was present at the Public
Inquiry when Calcutt gave his account of the sale of this pony and
governess car to Hirst. I understood Mr. Robb's suggestion to be
that Calcutt had bought this horse especially for Hirst, the car in one
place, the horse in another, and the harness in another. I got up
and said, “That is a he; Calcutt offered it to me for £45." I re-
member the election of Guardiars in 1904, but I do not remember
anything about an election in 1907. It is very likely that I should be
in the Three Crowns" on the night of the eleotion, but I know
nothing at all of any conversation between Hirst and Calcutt about
giving 5s. to some men.
