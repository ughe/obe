I am shopman to Mr. Castle, a pawnbroker, in High-street, Camden-town—on 12th March last I changed this note, No. 45,970, for the prisoner ( produced )—he gave the name of William Smith, No. 81, John-street-road, which I wrote on the note—he had pledged a Geneva watch and chain in the morning and redeemed it in the afternoon with this note.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did 700 know him before? A.  No—I saw him again about a month afterwards—I see about 200 or 250 persons a-day—I saw him twice in that one day.
   MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Is it a common occurrence for persons to pledge their watches in the morning and take them out in the afternoon? A.  Fre quently—I am sure the prisoner is the person who changed the note. ( The RECORDER . was of opinion that the evidence was too slight to go to the Jury. ) NOT GUILTY.

I am employed at the Western Railway at Paris—it is my duty to place tickets on the luggage that is registered to London. On 19th Jan. it was my duty to paste tickets on the luggage, and I did so—there was only me and the weigher to do so—I do not recollect pasting a ticket corresponding with this one ( pro  duced)—I might possibly have pasted it, but I am not sure—if this bag ( produced ) had been brought, and registered for London, it would have been my duty to have pasted a ticket on it—the remains of the ticket on this bag correspond with the ticket I should have put on—I put a mark of a red cross on registered luggage—I put the mark where it would show most, on the front—here is the mark of the red cross on this bag—this is the way in which I should make the cross—I cannot swear to this—this is the way bill of that day, No. 13—I should enter the luggage that day—if I were absent it would have been the weigher's duty to enter it—I do not know whether I was absent.
