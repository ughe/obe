HENRY STANLEY, coffee stall keeper. On October 2 I was at my!
stall outside Upton Park Station. I saw the three prisoners with
Mr. Thomas. I saw Hill come out of the station first of all, a few
minutes before the others. Then I saw Thomas come out with Stowe
and Hayes, and they were having an argument. Then I saw Thomas
try to get away on to a motor 'bus and Btowe hang on to him and
stopped him. While I was serving a customer they got back to the
station. Hill remained outside. I started to go to the station and
Hill come across. I thought something was the matter. Hill said,
"Don't put that b g flap on my head," so I put it up again and
went towards the station, but I turned round and saw Hill on the
counter trying to get inside, so I came back. Then the other prie
soners came up and stopped talking at the corner of the stall. Stowe
was getting on to Hill about not being in the station to help them.
Stowe said, “We missed a chance." There was a little con
versation, and they said, “We had better get away from here or we
shall hear more of this." Then they went off down Queen's Rond. My
coffee stall is about 40 yards from the station; I identified the three
men.
Cross-examined by Stowe. My stall is in Queen's Square, by the
fountain. I could see anybody coming out of the station from my
stall. It is facing the station. You were under the electric light
on this occasion, and were quite discernable. Mr. Thomas struck
you with his umbrella, in the middle of the road. (Btowe said he
hoped witness was not carrying out his threat made in September
about having his own back.) Mr. Justice Ridley cautioned prisoner
to be careful in making accusations.
Cross-examined by Hayes. When at the police station on Satur
day night I said I do not know your name. "I said I knew Hill and
Stowe from the first. I do know you well. I have not known you
to interfere with me. I did not see you with Thomas, but you came
out of the station after this last train and went round the square.
You did ask me for a hard-boiled egg.

tober 26, I was in Green Street with Detective Edwards and others,
and saw the three prisoners. I said, "We are police officers, and
we are going to arrest you on suspicion of being concerned together
in robbing Mr. Thomas, and assaulting him on the 2nd of this
month." Hill sid, "What are you going to take me for I had
nothing to do with it." Hayes made no reply. On the way to the
station Stowe said, "Highway robbery, governor 1 That sounds &
bis serious." At the station they were placed with other men. Hill
and Hayes were identified by the prosecutor. Stowe was identified
