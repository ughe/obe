I saw this tea in a bundle with the shirt-I said to him, " This is Mr.

ing and left-he said nothing- I did not know he was going away-he

Prisoner. Q. When I came home, did not you say, “Is that the shirt
that Hannah made for you?" and I said, “ Yes ?" A. It is false.
ROBERT BYLES. I am a policeman. In consequence of information!
apprehended the prisoner at the Ship and Half Moon public-house, at
Woolwich-he had this tea in his hat in a hat-box--he said he had bought
it with some more things to send down to his mother.
Prisoner's Defence. I have been acquainted with Hannah Robinson
about twelve months ; she was always asking me to her master's house
during his absence, and one Saturday, about three months ago, I stopped
with her all night ; on the Monday following she said, “I have got a
good balf-pound of tea to send to your mother, and have bought you two
shirts;" I refused taking them; I gave her 58., and said that when she
finished them I would give her the remainder of the money-about three
weeks ago she came to my house and said she was in the family way, that
she had told her master, and he had discharged her; she wanted me to
marry her, which I was not able to do, but said I would marry her in the
course of a month, and she has done this through spite : when I was a
Mrs. Kitchiner's, on Good Friday, she gave me this shirt, and the hand-
kerchief to take it home in ; when I got home my landlady said, "Hare
you got the sbirt Hannah made for you!" I said, “ Yes;" I paid the
landlady and went away, leaving the shirt on the bed; I went to Wool
wich to get employment, and was taken there ; is it feasable I should
steal the shirt and tea when I had 22s. about me at the time? she gave
it to me, and said she should not want the money, I should want it mysem
another time.
