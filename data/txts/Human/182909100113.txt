 NEW COURT, Fifth Day.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1762.  WILLIAM SNOW was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 9,500 needles, value 30s.; 8 ozs. weight of pins, value 1s. 6d.; 2 yards of ribbon, value 1s.; 2 ozs. weight of thread, value 6d.; 13 balls of worsted, value 3d.; 80 skeins of silk, value 7s.; 2 reels of cotton, value 5d.; 33 balls of cotton, value 1s. 3d.; 1 half-sovereign 1 crown, 2 half-crowns, and 2 shillings, the property of William Rotherham and another, his masters; and MARTHA SNOW was indicted for feloniously receiving part of the said goods, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   JOHN CAPEY. I am in the employ of William Rotherham and John Hill Grinsell- the prisoner, William Snow, was in their employ for two months previous to the 30th of July, as a cashier, to sit at the desk, and give change - he slept with me. On the Thursday morning, before the 30th of July, he got up first, and went down stairs; I got up about seven o'clock, and saw a bundle hanging on a nail in my bed-room - some of our shop-twine was hanging through a hole in it; I took it down, and found some skeins of silk, some cotton, a purse, a half-sovereign, a crown, two half-crowns, and a quantity of my master's property - there was a cheque-book, a number of billheads, and some paper - I called a young man, and we showed them to Mr. Rotherham; in about an hour I was called into the counting-house, and found the prisoner -Mr. Rotherham said to me, "This boy denies these things, and says they don't belong to him;" I said to the prisoner,"It is of no use your denying it, it must be your's, and you took them;" Mr. Rotherham said it was of no use his denying it, he would bottom the affair, and he had better tell the truth; he then said he took them, and he had taken some cotton, needles, and worsted home, and they were in a school-box, in the back-parlour - the officer and I went to his mother's, after he was gone to Worship-street; when we got to the house, the officer asked Mrs. Snow whether she had a son in the employ of Rotherham and Grinsell; she said, Yes; he asked if she had any of their property in her house; she said, No; she then said,"His aunt has been here, and I have been looking into the school-box, and found these two parcels" - Attfield said, "Have you got any more?" she said No; he said,"We must look," and we found two more lots in the parlour, in different places, in boxes and drawers - here is cotton, needles, tape, and silk, such as they have in their shop; the whole amount, I should think, is about 3l.; the cottons have our name on the label, and the needles have our private mark on the paper.
 Cross-exmained by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Who are your employers? A. William Rotherham and John Hill Grinsell - I have sold things out of the shop with these labels on them - I sometimes find needles and things about the shop, but these are done up in papers.
   WILLIAM ATTFIELD. I am an officer. I went to the house of the female prisoner, at No. 31, Gloucester-street, Commercial-road - it is about three quarters of a mile from the prosecutors' - she opened the door; I asked if her name was Snow; she said Yes; I asked if she had a son at Rotherham and Grinsell's; she said Yes; I asked if she had ever received any goods belonging to them - she said not to her knowledge - I took her into the back-parlour, and read the warrant - I asked if there were any goods there; she then opened the drawer, and gave out two parcels, which she said she had taken from the boy's box, and intended to take to the prosecutors' - I said, "Is this all;" "I believe it is," she said; I said I must search, and in different parts I found these papers of needles, cottons, and other things - this other parcel I got from Capey.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is the husband of this woman living? A. Yes, and I believe he sleeps in the front-room, in consequence of this woman's mother being there, and sleeping with her - her husband is coachman to a gentleman in the City.
 The prisoners received good characters.
   WILLIAM SNOW- GUILTY. Aged 13.
   MARTHA SNOW- GUILTY. Aged 44. Recommended to Mercy- Confined 6 Months.
 1763.  RALPH GEORGE MARTIN was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , 170 foreign dollars, value 35l., and 1 night-cap, value 6d., the property of Robert Willie Briggs.
   ROBERT WILLIE BRIGGS. I am chief officer of the  Catherine Stewart Forbes. I left my dollars in a trunk in my cabin - I left it on the 4th of August, and the last thing I took five dollars out of the bag, and gave them to a doctor to take to a man who was going to the hospital; there was no one present; next morning one of the men was searching my box, and said, "Here are a great many wax candles;" I said Yes; he said, "We burn a great many in our lantern, I will take one;" I said, "Do;" the prisoner was second officer of the ship - he was on board when I left; I missed the dollars about half-past five o'clock, on the 5th - the prisoner had gone on shore that day about nine o'clock in the morning, and returned about three; I did not know then that I had lost them; he came on board the next day, and said, "Is it true you have lost the dollars?"
