JAMES VERNON. I am a traveller, and live at 84, Westminster-road—I was in Union-street, Borough, on Saturday evening, 21st April, about five minutes to 12—I did not know any of the parties in this case—I saw the prosecutrix and Mrs. Mauler—they stepped on to the foot path and were passing immediately before me—I did not see the house they came from—they stepped on to the foot path as if they came across the road—I saw the male prisoner come up immediately—he assaulted the prosecutrix, and pulled off his coat, and said he would fight her husband or any other person she liked to bring, and immediately upon that he made a rush at her, struck her, and knocked her down, and I saw him make a grasp at one of her hands—it was in the face, I think, that he struck her—he laid hold of her hand—they both fell down, and money fell from the proseoutrix's hand—I did not see what became of it; it scattered on the ground—there were two others, I believe, with the prisoner; they were all together, and there was a scramble for the money—both the prisoners, and two or three others took part in the scramble—I cannot say whether the male prisoner suc ceeded in getting any of the money—I saw Mrs. Bellamy on the ground—I did not notice anything done to her by anybody while on the ground—she and the male prisoner got up almost together—the prisoner attempted to run away—he ran for perhaps four or five yards—Mrs. Bellamy pursued him and he immediately turned round, struck her in the face, and knocked her down—he then ran away—a crowd immediately collected; I did not go into the crowd, but I saw a policeman coming up, who went to them—when I first saw Mrs. Bellamy she had on a dark shawl which she took off, but under that she had a light shawl, apparently a silk one—I saw her after she had been knocked down the second time—she had no shawl at all on then—I should say three or four minutes elapsed between my losing sight of her, and seeing her the second time.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you see her also take her bonnet off? A.  Yes—I did not see her husband at all, till after the male prisoner was given in custody and it was all over—there was not a considerable crowd immediately—I do not know the Bed Lion.
   COURT . Q.  You stood on the pavement and looked on? A.  Yes—I should not think it lasted above five minutes—I do not know whether Mrs. Bel lamy's bonnet and shawl were loose, and she gave them to some one for safety; but she pursued the prisoner—she was knocked down the first time, and she immediately threw them off—I could form no opinion as to whether there had been any drinking—Mrs. Bellamy appeared sober—I did not see her husband knocked down at all—the male prisoner made a rush at Mrs. Bellamy's hand before she fell down. JOHN CLEMENTS IRELAND( Policeman, M  217).
