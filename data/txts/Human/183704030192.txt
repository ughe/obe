JOSEPH HARRIS. I am a jeweller. The prisoner came to my shop on Saturday, the 1st of April, between one and two o'clock in the day—she put a gold watch into my hand, and asked what I thought it was worth—I hesitated about giving her an answer—she said "Is it worth 10 l .?"—I said I don't know—she said "Yes; I know it is; for it has been pawned for that, and I am the woman that has pawned it"—I then went away, and left her; she came again, and my wife asked me to take it to pawn for her—I said I would not have any thing to do with it, she had better take it to where she was known—she said she had taken it to one or two, and they had not so much to lend—I said, perhaps they thought it was not her own—she said, "It is; I met Mr. Johnson, and told him I must have some money, and he took the watch from his guard, and told me to pawn it for 8 l ., and send him the duplicate in a letter."
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is the prisoner to you? A.  She married my wife's brother—I don't know Mr. Johnson—I have heard speak of him, as sending her money at various times—she is married—her husband is abroad. GEORGE WESTON. I took her into custody, and have had the watch ever since. JAMES MONKHOUSE. This is my property. GUILTY. Aged 23.— Transported for Seven Years.
