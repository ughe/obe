Winchester, Mayor.
Fourth Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
93. MARY DAVIS and ANN HAXTON were indicted for stealing, on
the 21st of November, 73 glass phials, value 108.; the goods of James
Richards and another, the masters of the said Mary Davis.
MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES RICHARDS. I am in partnership with my father, John Richards
Mary Davis was in our employ to purchase old phials, and to wash and
guage phials-she had been with us six or seven years on the 21st of
November, about half-past six o'clock in the evening, I placed myself in a
gateway, where I could have a view of the prisoner Haxton, who I saw
waiting about at the corner of Dorset-street, opposite our warehouse she
went to the warehouse door, and knocked three times-the door was imme-
diately opened by Davis, who handed out a bundle, which Haxton took
hold of-I jumped upon her, and said, “I have caught you at last" - she
quitted the bundle, and Davis took it back-I pushed Hoxton into the
room, and Davis fell on her knees and said, "Oh, my God! my God! Mr.
James ; I am very sorry"-Haxton said, "You cannot hurt me; you did
not see the phials in my hand"-the bundle had not then been opened she
afterwards asked me what I thought the magistrate would do to her-there
is a betl to that warehouse door, which should ring when it is spened, but it
did not ring--a person inside could prevent it.
Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. You were outside? A. Yes-
I cannot swear to these phials they have no mark on them they are
worth about 10s.--the bundle was tied with a bit of string, which corre-
sponded with some in our warehouse I was in my counting-house at half-
past four o'clock-no one came to the warehouse door then I was not
there at half-past five o'clock.
GEORGE SNEATH. I am a clerk to the prosecutor. I was there from five
till half-past six o'cloek-29 one called to leave this bundle there at that
time I could hear the betl of the door ring when it was opened in the usual
way, but I did not hear it ring,
ELIZABETH EDWARDS. I was in the prosecutor's employ. I know Da-
vis-I have seen her wear an apron like this in which the phials are, but
almost every one of them there wore a blue apron.
WILLIAM PARSONS (police-constable H 21.) I took the prisoners-
Haxton asked me what I thought the Magistrate would do to her.
Davis's Defence. This woman brought me the bundle about half-past
five o'clock-she asked me to buy them-I said I could not then she
asked me to let her leave it-when she came for it she asked me for six-
pennyworth of copper, and I had it in my hand when my master came-I
said, “I beg your pardon for taking the bottles at a wrong time"—the
bundle had stood on the floor the whole of the time.
DAVIS-GUILTY. Aged 26.-Confined Three Months.
HAXTON-GUILTY. Aged 60.-Confined Six Months.
