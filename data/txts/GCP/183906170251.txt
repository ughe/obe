THOMAS PHILLIPS, I am in the accountant's office in the Bank of Eng-
land. It is the duty of Hawkes and other pay clerks to make entries--I
produce extracts from the Bank books as to the £300 and two £5 notes I
took the extracts myself, and examined them with another clerk (reads)
-"3rd January, 1839, £300, No. 39,836" -- the ledger is headed Matthew
Marshall-it is entered to his credit-he is the chief cashier of the Bank
it is posted on the 2nd of April in the Y book, page 546_that is the
whole of the entry in that book in the cash-book there is an entry on the
2nd of April, 1839, under the head of Mr. Hawkes, the cashier I find
the note entered-(reads) " 3rd of January, 39,886, £800"_this is all
the entry in both the books on the subject of the £300 note--according to
the course of business at the Bank of England, that denotes that this note
of £300 came in through Mr. Hawkes, on the 2nd of April, 1839, and was
posted in the ledger to the credit of Matthew Marshall, the chief cashier,
on the 2nd of April with respect to the £5 notes, here is in Page 729, the
2nd of April, 1888, £5, No. 71,812 that is the date of the note it is
posted when it came into the Bank, on the 14th of May, 1889, 146,
this is headed “Matthew Marshall, chief cashier"-in the cash-book, on the
14th of May, 1889, in 146, there appears " 2nd of April, 1838, No.
71,342 £5," and at the end of it is the name of Mr. Warner, teller, which
signifies it came in through him-the other £5 note in the ledger is “Srd
of March, 1838, No. 81,486"- that is the date of the note, and it is
headed Matthew Marshall "18th of May, 1889, X 43," which denotes the
day on which it came in-in the cask-book, in page 43 , 13th of May, 1889,
is entered "3rd of March, 1838, 81,406 £5%-it came in by the London
Joint Stock Bank-according to the course of business at the Bank, the two
extracts I have read denote the same circumstances with respect to the £5
mote as are denoted of the £300 note-the date they come in, through
whose hands, the date of the note, the number of it, the value of the
mote, and that they were posted in the book according to the entry--the
extract denotes that the £300 note came in through the hands of Mr.
Hawkes-and of the two £5 notes, that one came in through the medium
of Warner, and the other through the Joint Stock Bank.
Mr. PHILLIPS to John Howard. Q. You stated you had been paid
for forty-four days, was it during these forty-four days you saw the two
Caspars ? A. Yes, I do not keep dates in my memory--I put down
nothing in books I had been employed the Wednesday previous to my
seeing the two Caspars in Mansel-street on the Monday, which was five or
six days I was employed on the 27th-this took place on the 1st of May.
