 2625. RICHARD ADAMS was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of May , 1 bed, value 3 l  ., the goods of Joseph Skinner . JOSEPH SKINNER. I am an upholsterer, and live in Whitechapel-road The prisoner was frequently in my employ—I employed him to take a bed, on the 29th of May, to Mr. Gilmore, in the Tower, and one was to hen turned, but he never brought it to me—the one he was to have brought was mine, and was worth 3 l .—he was not found till the 11th of Septem ber, when a person met him in Clerkenwell, and gave him in charge. SUSANNAH GILMORE. I live with my husband in the Tower. On the 29th of May the prisoner brought a bed, and took another back instead of it to Mr. Skinner. GUILTY  * Aged 28.— Recommended to mercy, — Transported for Seven Years.
 2626. GEORGE SPENCER was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of September , 1 hat, value 4 s  . 9 d  ., the goods of Mark Marks ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. DANIEL MOBBS. ( police-constable E  11) I fell in with the prisoner in Bainbridge-street, St. Giles's, on the 11th of September, at nine o'clock at night, with this hat—I asked what he had got there—he said, a hat which he was going to take to his father—I found the shop ticket on it, which led me to Mr. Marks. MARK MARKS. I am a hatter, and live in High-street, St. Giles. This is my hat, it has my ticket on it—I saw it a short time before I was fetched to the station-house—it was in my window.
   Prisoner's Defence.  A boy took it, and gave it to me to carry it for him. JAMES M'CORMACK. ( police-constable E  59.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—he is the same person. GUILTY Aged 18.— Transported for Seven Years.
 2627. EDWARD BOND was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of Sep tember , 1 coat, value 7 s  ., the goods of Edward Burton Hensley ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. GEORGE URIDOE. I am coachman to Mrs. Watts. This coat belonged to Edward Burton Hensley, her footman. On the 3rd of September, the carriage was standing in Chandos-street, Cavendish-square—the footman is coat was on the rumble behind—I was on the box, and felt a motion—I turned, and saw the prisoner with the coat under his arm—I turned, and drove the carriage after him—he put the coat on, and as soon as he saw me he pulled it off, and ran away—he was stopped—I asked him if he was not ashamed of himself, and he begged to be let go. EDMUND GREENING. I was passing through Mansfield-street, and heard a person call, "Stop him"—the coachman pointed to the prisoner, who was running with the coat under his arm—he threw it down, and I caught him.
