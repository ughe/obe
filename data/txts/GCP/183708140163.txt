been subpænaed here by the prosecutor-I know the prisoner Hill-I have

whatever-I think the whole of this other paper is Hills's writing.
Mr. Phillips. Q. Do you know Mr. Moule ? A. Yes I did not see
this order in his presence-1 might say something about it-I do not be-
lieve that I said I believed it to be Hill's handwriting, but I would not
like to be sworn I cannot recollect it, and I will not swear to it.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. When was it you saw Mr. Moule? A. I believe,
last Saturday week, and I was subpoenaed on Thursday- I do not think it
is Hill's writing-it is an altered hand.

JOHN WOOD. I am a coffee-dealer, and have a warehouse in French-

believe any part of this paper to be his writing-his general character has
been very good.

design; but I believe it was never written by the prisoner.

John ANDERSON. I am a sailor. I came to town on the 1st of August,
and went to lodge at John Smith's, who keeps a lodging-house-I stopped
in the house five days, till I lost my money-I went there two days after
I came from on board the ship-I had ten sovereigns, three half-sove-
reings, and three shillings-the prisoner was in the house-I had never seen
him before--he asked me if I could lend him a couple of shillings, as he
had lost a ship-I said I would look by and by, and see if I could give
him a dinner-as soon as I had my dinner I went to sleep-I had my
money safe in my waistcoat pocket-when I awoke it was gone, and the
prisoner was gone out of the house when I went to sleep the prisoner
and Mr. Smith were in the room--the money was taken out of my pocket
while I was asleep.
