937. FARLOW WILLIAM GREEN (22), Feloniously forging and
uttering a receipt for 51. 48., with intent to defraud.
"MR. PURCELL Prosecuted'; MR. FULTON Defended.
MARY JANE BATCHELOR. I am a widow, of 6, Grove Road, Ealing-
my husband, who was a painter, insured his life in the Liverpool Pro-
tective Insurance and Burial Society-he died in July this year, and I
then applied to the London head office of the Society, and sent this
certificate of death produced), according to the rules-I was entitled to
receive 51. 118.—this is my husband's policy (produced)-I sent it with
the certificate-I never received any money-this "M. J. Batchelor" is
not my signature, nor did I authorise any one to sign it.
Cross-examinod. Mr. Richardson, the collector, sent the notification of
death for me he is the agent who introduced us to the Society-he lives
in our neighbourhood-I never saw the prisoner, but I went to the office
on 11th August.
Ro-examined. After going there I did not write, I communicated with
the collector.
JAMES MOLLOY. I am president of this Society-the prisoner was
manager of the London district—it was his duty to collect the sums from
the various collectors, enter them in a weekly balance sheet, and transmit
them to the office in Liverpool--it was his duty to pay claims to the rela-
tives of persons insured in the Society, and obtain receipts from them and
enter their names, and send the balance sheet to me at the head office
with the certificate of death and the policy-I received this certificate of
Batchelor's death in the ordinary course, and this balance sheet (produced),
in which here is entered “Claim paid Batchelor, 51. 48."-I received this
receipt for 61, 48.-in consequence of a communication from Richardson,
& collector, two members of the committen came to London and saw the
prisoner-1 afterwards came up and saw him, and asked him if it was
true that the receipt was a forgery-I had it with me-he said "Unfor-
tunately, it is a forgery”-I asked him if there were any more of a simi-
lar nature-he said that there were two other cases that in one case
21. 108. should have been paid, and only 11, 106, was paid, and 11. in an-
other case--this produced, is the receipt of the person insuring for 21. 108.
- he ultimately made up this list and handed to me, and said that he had
received these money from the several collectors which he could not ac-
count for-I put a number of documents into his hands, but he gave me
no explanation.

for you if you make a clean breast of it, as you have given much trouble
