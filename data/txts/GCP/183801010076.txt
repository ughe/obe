JAMES Weston. I am landlord of the White Swan, Little St. Andrew
street, Seven-dials. On Tuesday, the 27th of December, the two prisonen
were in my tap-room-I could not get them out-I called for a polio
man, but he dared not come in, he said, except for robbery or murder-
they were too much for me, I could not get them out-I tried to get then
out, and they threw my table against me, as if they were going to murder
-I called the policeman at ten o'clock at night--I sat down till four o'clock
in the morning, and fell asleep-I then awoke, and saw Compton at my
till— Phillips was on the other side-Compton had some coppers in his
hand, and the till, and was going to put them into his pocket- I took him
by the collar-be put them back, and said, " I have got no money"
my wife was in the parlour- I said, “We will search you"--we searched
his right-hand pocket, and found this money--I charged him with it
being my money-that money was missing from the till-We have two
bowls in the till, and, as near as I can say, three minutes before, this silvet
was in the bowl, and one halfpenny laid on this bowl on the silver-the
halfpenny was found in his pocket-all this time Phillips was on the other
side, and he kept on telling Compton to cut my b-y head off, and best
out my brains-Compton was inside the bar, and Phillips on the other
side-he could see all that was done by Compton-he said, "You hare
three halfpence of Compton's money, give it him back, or I will pull you
up and make you sorry" - I told him to behave himself, or I would call
policeman-I was going to call a policeman, and he clung round my neck,
and held his hand over my mouth as I got along-our door was closed
he got his head against the door-I got the door open as well as I could
- he put his hand on my mouth, and Compton ran out of the house
a policeman met him and brought him back-1 had found the money, and
gave it to my wife-the policeman took them to the station, and the money
was left there.
