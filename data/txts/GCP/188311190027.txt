The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrato. "A couple of days!
hefore the case arrived here I received a letter from Philips and Graves,
enclosing the bill of lading of a case of bulbs. I took Konekemmer with
me to the Custom House. I went from there to the 8.8. Holland to see
about the case, and there I had the answer that my case could not be
found ; that very likely it was already delivered on shore. I went to
Messrs. Philips and Graves after this, and they told me that the case had
gone to a wharf, and I should have it the next day. The next day I went
t) Philips and Graves, but the case was nowhere to be found. When I
vame home I had a letter that my case had come ashore at Blackwall
with the passengers' luggage, and then I went to the place I bad the
letter from to fetch the case. I took the case away with Ronckem-
mer. I took the case in the presence of four witnesses, and instead of
bulbs being in it of the value of 201. they were not of the value of 101.
After that I went to Philips and Graves's office to claim the case. I made
the claim not for the loss of the case, but for the loss of the bulbs. I can
hring witnesses. I signed the claim to receive the money, but I did not
know what was written down. I call no witnesses here,
