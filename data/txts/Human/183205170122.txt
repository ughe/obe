 Prisoner's Defence. The gentleman I bought it of is here.
   JOSEPH BARNETT. I live in Field-lane, at the bottom of Holborn-bridge. The prisoner bought a hat of me last Monday week; I could point it out from a hundred - this is the hat; it is a very singular one - it is a silk hat, and narrow in the crown; the shape of a man's head has brought it to its present from - we generally have a mark in them, but when we sell them we take it out; I know this hat, by the white trimming; I am positive it is the one I sold the prisoner - it is a fashionable hat; it was quite flat when I sold it, such as a coachman or a cab-driver would swear, from the heat of the body, but a beaver hat would not - I bought it of a lad who was apprentice to a man who was a bankrupt, at the corner of Plumtree-court- he made it for himself five or six weeks ago; I sold it to the prisoner for a crown - I did not know him before.
 JAMES HILDRED. I live in Field-lane, next door to Mr. Barnett. I saw him sell the prisoner a hat between eleven and twelve o'clock last Monday week - I did not know the prisoner, but I happened to be present; it was sold in the lane - I believe this is the hat.
 JURY to  GEORGE DAY. Q. How long has your father lived in Gray's Inn-lane? A. About eight months - I live with him; I have been brought up to his business, which is the trunk business - I bought my hat this day fortnight, in Romford-market; there is no name in it but my own, which I put in at the office - I knew at the hat again when the prisoner came down the alley.
   MARY DAY. I am the prosecutor's mother. This is my son's hat, I believe; he came home in this old one, and I said, "Where is your hat?" he said, "Never mind, it is all right" - I scolded him for buying this hat; he said he gave 6s. for it, and bought it to save his bettermost one - his father said he had bought the hat too large, and he might have let him gone with him.

 1397.  PETER SHERIDAN was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of May , 1 coral necklace, value 15s., the goods of John Sulman , from the person of Harriet Sulman.
 ELIZABETH ULYET. I am the daughter of  Henry Ulyet, a carpenter, who lives in Crown-street, Old-street-road. On the evening of the 13th of May, I was looking out of our front room window, and saw the prisoner with another boy; he turned to Harriet Sulman, unsnapped her beads, rolled them up in his hand, and ran to the top of the street - I am sure he is the person; I got a fair look at him- he turned to see if any body was looking at him, but he did not see me.
 Cross-examined by MR. ROWE. Q. What hour was this? A. A little before eight o'clock in the evening; I was at our two-pair of stairs window; the little girl lives four doors from our house - I did not see several other persons passing; I had seen the prisoner before; he had a round cap on; I did not say any thing when he took them.
 COURT. Q. How soon was he taken afterwards? A. It might be five minutes.
   EMMA RIGGLESWORTH. I am the daughter of Isaac Rigglesworth- I am nearly ten years of age. I was playing with Harriet Sulman, in Crown-street; the prisoner came and turned her round, unsnaped the beads from her neck, and gave them to a bigger boy, who was with him, and the prisoner ran away.
 Q. Had you seen him before? A. No, I was next door; I could see what he did - I am quite sure he gave them to another boy when he got to the top of the street; I saw a gentleman ran after him; I did not make any noise - I told the gentleman, and he came out when he heard the child cry; my father came home from chapel, and I went in with him, but I saw the prisoner give the heads to the other boy before I went in.
 GEORGE SIMPSON. I live in Crown-street. This witness called my attention to the prisoner; I pursued, and caught him running through Hoxton-square - I took him, and asked what he had done with the beads which he took from the child's neck - he said he knew nothing about them; I brought him back; he was identified by both the witnesses, and I gave him in charge.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far do you live from the spot? A. Not above seven yards - I ran out as soon as I heard the child scream; it was about eight o'clock in the evening; there were a few boys in the square, but not running - there is a short turn from the street which I live in to the square; the beads were taken about thirty yards from the square - I saw the prisoner when he was about twenty yards from me; I did not lose sight of him.
   MARY SIMPSON. I am the wife of George Simpson. I have the care of Harriet Sulman - I put the necklace on her neck about three o'clock, and saw it safe about five minutes before I heard her cry out; I ran to the door, and saw my husband run after the boy - her father's name is John Sulman.
