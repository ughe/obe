About six o'clock int the evening of the 28th of Oct. I was called by a man, who said he had got a fare for me—he told me to go to the corner of Robert-street and George-street—he rode on the box with me—I went to ther corner of Robert-street—he rode were three more men—they put the box on the cab , two of them got in-side, and one rode with me—they ordered me to drive to the Borough, down Holland-street—I passed a little way, and went to turn down Edward-street—some inquiries were made of Jones—I was told there was no thoroughfare, and orders were given to me to drive up the Blackside—when, I drove to the street—I was afterwards told to go straight down Bankside—when I got half way, they said they were going down the New Kent-road—I drove to the corner of New-street and Lyon-street—I was told previously to drive in the direction of Blackfriars-bridge, which was from the Kent-read—when I got there they had no money to pay me—they took the box off, and one of the four stopped with me—I waited about three minutes till they brought me 2 s . 6 d .—my fare was 2 s ., but I carried three persons—they went down New-street—when I got there, there was a fourth man—I did not see what became of him—when we left Edward-street, I suppose he must have ridden behind the cab  —I found him when I arrived—I have only a slight recollection of Field being the man who rode outside—he sat on the roof—we did not get into con-versation—he sat above me—it was a dressed cab  —it was not Field who called the cab  —the man who called me was dreeeed in a fustian jacket and dark trowsers, and a hat—one of them had a cap—it was a black leather trunk, with brass nails, and in oval top, and a cord round it—the man who rode at the top assisted in putting it up, and staid with me while the others went—it was a very dark, dirty street where I pulled up—I think two of the men were in jackets, Field in a black frock-coat, and one in a Chesterfield wrapper. JOSEPH THOMAS WELLS, hair-dresser, Charlotte-street.  I live about three doors from the Queen's Head, on the same side of the street—on the evening of the 28th ef Oct. I saw a van oppoeite the Queen's Head in Charlotte-street—I saw Applegate—I went into the public house, and remained abou, half a minute—as I came from the door I saw the van loaded with trunks and a little boy—it attracted my attention—I saw some persons about of bed character—I went back, and spoke to Applegate, who was in the public-house—the child was looking towards Blackfriar-road over the boxes—I saw some persons about—I believe Field was one of them—I cannot speak to the others—I did not notice how many persons there were about, or how they were dressed—I saw Field come from a tobacconist's shop next door to the public-house, and speak to a short young man—I took notice of the van being laden—it was not laden up to the tail-board.
