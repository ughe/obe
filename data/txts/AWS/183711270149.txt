MARY ANN MILLeR. I live with my mother, in Stanhope-street, Clare-

box at Mr. Johnson's, in Clare-street-it contained the articles stated in
the indictment-] had seen them safe in it about a week or fortnight before
the 1lth of November, when I had the prisoner taken into eustody-
have seen her before, but did not know her-she charred at Johnson's
house-] had left the key in my box.
THOMAS DICKINSON. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Clare-market. I
produce an apron pawned on the Srd of November by the prisoner for 4d.
with another article--I knew her before.
MaRY ANN MILER. That is one of my articles, and was in my box.
Cross-ezamined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. How came you to leave it at
Johnson's? A. Beeause my mother was going to move her lodging.
CHARLES JOHNSON. The prosecutrix left her box in my care-it was
under my bed-I know it was safe on the 5th of November in my posses-
sion-the prisoner was a poor woman in great distress, and I took her in to
wash and char for me--I gave her shelter and food, but no money.
SARAH WRIOHT. The prisoner sold me the bonnet and pillow for 10d.
Cross-ezamined. Q. Did not she ask you to keep it a few days for her 2
A. She came next day and said she hoped I would not sell it, because she
would come and buy it again of me.
GUILTY. Aged 20-Recommended to merey by the Proseoutris.
Confined Three Months.

and that he had been before convicted of felony,

ner, drapers, in High-street, Marylebone, On the 30th of November, at

-I ran out, hallooing Stop thief," and never lost sight of him till he was
