WILLIAM GINBY and DOROTHY GINBY were indicted for feloniously receiving of a certain evil-disposed person, on the   15th of Ja nuary , 1 watch, value 10 l  .; 1 coat, value 2 l  .; 1 waistcoat, value 10 s  .; 1 pair of trowsers, value 1 l  .; 2 toilet covers, value 2 s  .; 1 shift, value 2 s  .; 1 petticoat, value 3 s  .; and 1 shirt, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of James Hunt Burgess ; well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. ELIZABETH BURGESS. I am the wife of James Hunt Burgess, a lighter man, of Grange-walk, Bermondsey. On Saturday, the 15th of January, I went out about six o'clock in the evening—nobody was left in the house—the windows and door were fastened—I returned in about an hour and a quarter by myself, unlocked the door, and fell over a candlestick in the passage—I got a light, and found the front-parlour very much disordered—I missed a table-cover and a brown mantle out of the front parlour, and from other parts of the house a gold watch, a coat, a pair of trowsers, a waistcoat, a shirt, seven shifts, two petticoats, three night-gowns, two toi let-covers, one black mantle, and an Orleans cloth dress—on Tuesday, the 18th, I saw them again at the police-office—I had seen them all in the house at six o'clock on Saturday night. JAMES HUNT BURGESS. On the 15th of January I came home shortly after eight o'clock, and missed the things—I had some bills left at Mr. Marchant's, the pawnbroker—on Monday evening I received a communi cation from him, went there, and saw my watch—while I was there the female prisoner came in to pledge a coat—I did not bear what she said, nor see what she brought—a policeman was sent for—I went to the station with her, and thence to her residence, with a policeman, (leaving her at the station)—we there found the male prisoner—it is a general dealer's shop—we searched the house—I found up stairs, in a drawer in a bed-room, a shift, a petticoat, a shirt, and a toilet-cover of mine, and on the drawers another toilet-cover—the male prisoner was taken into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did you hear the policeman tell him that he had come to search his house, and that his wife was in custody for felony? A.  No—he was admitted to bail by the Magistrate, and surrendered to-day. JOHN HENRY SYMES. I am in the employ of Thomas Marchant, a pawnbroker. On Saturday, the 15th of January, the female prisoner came about eight o'clock in the evening, and pawned a suit of clothes—about an hour after my employer took a gold watch in pawn from her, in my presence—on the 17th we received a printed bill respecting the property stolen, and finding the watch described, I sent our boy to inform Burgess, who came; and while I was showing him the watch, the female prisoner came to pawn a coat and handkerchief—I was told the prisoner was in the shop while I was speaking to Burgess, and I gave her into custody—I left the watch in the care of Mr. Marchant—I have brought it here.
