issue to the Construction Company £150,000 debenture stock, £100,000
54 per cent. preference shares, and £150,000 ordinary shares, fully paid,
except as to £50,000 of the shares, and in consideration of this issue the
Construction Company undertook to complete the purchase of the shares
and debentures of the old Company, paying off their liabilities and to
convert the line into electric traction, equip it with electric cars, and give
it £5,000 working capital the proceeds of the issue of the £150,000 de
benture stock were to be placed without deduction to a separate banking
account in the names of trustees--the exact words are set out in the fifth
paragraph of the agreement-there was to be a certificate given subject
to a decision of the engineer as to the sum required-there was a trust
deed of July 11th, 1901—1 procured three guarantors for the £150,000
on August 1st we completed our guarantee by putting in applications for
the exact balance of the money then subscribed, making up the £150,000
--the money was eventually all paid, and we performed our contract
absolutely-I remember meeting Lawson with Mr. Kitehin on August 3rd
--we paid our money in and made up our guarantee to the £150,000, and
we asked Lawson for the remuneration which under the contract we were
to get, and he said he was not in a position to pay it-1 had given notice
to the bank not to part with any of these subscription moneys either for
the debentures or the shares without consulting us, and on that very
Saturday morning I received a letter from the bank in London to the
effect that Lawson's solicitor had been to see them and that they
were unable to act upon the stop order which we had given him-I.com-
plained to Lawson about this, and he said that his company were entitled
to draw out the money if they wished—I do not think that Lawson ever
claimed in any way to touch the debenture stock money, but this was the
share money-as a matter of fact, both the debentures and the share
money were payable, under the contracts which you have, to the Electrio
Tramways Construction and Maintenance Company, Limited, and it was
their duty afterwards to transfer the debenture stock money to the credit
of the trustees, and this part I was afraid they would not carry out-the
matter was settled by an agreement dated August 9th, 1901, between the
Construction Company and the guarantors all the difficulties and errors
with regard to the banking account were after considerable discussion and
difficulty settled and the arrangement was incorporated in the deed which
was duly executed—there was a meeting held on August 30th, of the
directors of the Blackpool (South), at which I was present, and Lawson
was also there at 34, Victoria Street, Westminster-at that meeting the
statement made as to the number of shares sold to the public under the
prospectus was that it was between 95,000 and 100,000—these shares were
payable by instalments, so much on application, so much on deposit, and
so much on certain dates—the date was about half-way, so that substan-
tially there would be roughly about £60,000—eventually we obtained from
Lawson and his company £22,500, being part of the £30,000 which we
ought to have had, and through this they were entitled, on paying over
