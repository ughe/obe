has put my clothes 1" He said, "No," and with that he shifted his

into the gate, and went to where I had left my clothes at six o'clock
and they were not there. My wife stood against the gate. I said
to her, "Perhaps they have put them round the rick out of sight as
they were so near the road." I then went round the rick and came
to the waggon load of bay that had been drawn up for the night
ready to be unloaded. Not finding my clothes I walked towards
the shafts of the waggon intending to go to the farm to find out where
the gov'nor had put my clothes. I then lit my pipe and threw the
match down and went towards the gate. There was some loose hay
about from the waggon and rick. I did not notice the match was
alight. My wife shouted out that the hay was alight. I tried to
kick it out. It then caught the riek and Aared up and caught the

has got to be my master." I went five or six yards up the Uxbridge
Road when I saw my bag and jacket in the ditoh. I picked them up

policeman then overtook me in a trap. The policeman got hold of
me and said, "I charge you with setting fire to that riok." I said,
