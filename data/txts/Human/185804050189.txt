491. THOMAS BERGANCE  (27), CHARLES SMITH  (22), and JULIA SMITH  (17) , Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin, having other counterfeit coin in their possession.
   MESSRS. POLAND, THOMPSON , and LAXTON conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD THOMAS WEEKS. I keep the Oak, in High Street, Norwood. On 24th March, the prisoners came, and had a pint of porter—Berganoe paid me with a penny and two halfpence; they stayed some little time, conversing and drinking—I am sure they are the parties.
   Bergance. Q.  What time was it? A.  About 6 o'clock—I had never seen you before. JOHN BARBER. I am a labourer. I saw Berganece come in, followed by the others; they had a pint of porter, and drank and talked together. SELINA HALL. I keep the Robin Hood, at Lower Norwood. On 24th March, Bergance came, between 7 and 8 o'clock, and called for half a pint of porter; he tendered a shilling; I bent it, put told him it was bad—he said, "Is it? I suppose I have taken it this afternoon; I have been at Croydon to see my mother"—he felt in his pockets, and at last he found 1 d ., and I put the shilling on the counter for him to take, and he said, "You had better keep that; you may be able to detect somebody else—he went to the door, and said, "What is the next train to London Bridge?"—I said, "There is more than one to London Bridge, and if you make haste you will catch it"—I then went up the hill with James Collins, and saw Bergance standing with his back agatinst the wall—I went into a little shop; when I came out he crossed the road, and joined the two Smiths, who were standing about twenty yards off—they all went up the hill, and I follwed them, and gave information—Ikept the money in my pocket, and gave it to the sergeant. WILLIAM MARSH( Policeman, P  33). I was at the police station, Nor wood, when the last witness came and made a communication to me—I went out, and found the two Smiths standing with their backs to the railings, about twenty yards from the Windsor Castle, beer shop—I said, "I Want you to go with me to the station; I suspect you are connected with a man in passing bad coin; where is the other man?"—Julia replid, "He is at a beer shop, lower down"—as we went to the station, I saw Julia pass her hand to Charles, but did not see what it was—I searched Charles at the station, and found two bad shillings ( produced ) in a piece of blue paper in his waistcoat pocket—I asked him how he accounted for them—Julia said that she picked them up in front of a public house in London—I locked them up, and went to the Windsor Castle, and saw Bergance in front of the bar—I asked the landlady if she had taken any base coin, and when she opened the till to look, Bergance walked out—she found a base shilling, and gave it to me ( produced )—I stopped Bergance about 20 yards from the house, and said "You must come back with me; I suspect you have been passing bad coin;" he said "I will go with you; you need not take hold of me"—I took him to the Windsor Castle, and thence to the station, searched him, and found 31 s . in silver, and 10 d . in copper, all good, and these six new combs, which he said he bought on London Bridge. HENRY BIGGS. I keep the Windsor Castle.
