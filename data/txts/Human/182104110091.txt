 680.  SAMUEL STEVENS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of April , three spoons, value 10 s., the goods of John George Warigner.
   ELIZA CLARK. I am servant to Dr. J. G. Warigner, who lives at Kingsland. On the 3d of April a boy (not the prisoner) came about some potatoes; we ordered some - then the prisoner came with the boy; we had not seen him before - they carried the sack of potatoes down stairs together; my mistress had been to the door to look at them. The boys waited down stairs for me to take them the money; some silver spoons were on the kitchen shelf over the dresser; they took the potatoes into the back kitchen. I was cleaning knives while they waited for the money; they were in the kitchen about ten minutes - mistress paid them, and they went away about eleven o'clock. About an hour after they were gone, I missed two salt and a dessert spoon. I saw them at Worship-street last Monday fortnight.
 MRS.  MARIA WARIGNER. Another boy came first, then the prisoner came with him, to take the potatoes down into the back kitchen; they remained there about six minutes while they emptied them. I left them in the passage between the two kitchens. I was up stairs a few minutes; they said they would not have the money till I had tried the potatoes to see if they were good; but when they emptied them, they wanted their money directly: I paid them 18 d. short. After they were gone I missed the spoons, and found them afterwards at the office. The boy asked for the money, the prisoner did not speak; he heard what was said.
 WILLIAM ATTFIELD. I am a constable. I took the prisoner into custody on the day mentioned in the indictment. was coming from Walthamstow, and received information of some men who were selling potatoes about Hackney being suspected of a robbery. I met the two; I took the other, and left him in care of a gardener while I run after the prisoner. I caught him, and found the spoons wrapped in paper in his pocket.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I picked them up with three potatoes.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months and Whipped.

 681.  MICHAEL MACKIN and JOSEPH DOGHERTY were indicted for stealing, on the 23d of March , one bed, value 20 s.; two blankets, value 7 s.; one bolster, value 5 s.; and one saucepan lid, value 3 s., the goods of William Charles Fortescue, Viscount Claremont, of Ireland.
 STAIR STUART. I have the care of Lord Claremont's house in  Berkeley-square; his names are William Charles Fortescue. On the 23d of March I had a message from his lordship, who lodged in Duke-street, St. James's, desiring me to deliver a hamper to the prisoner Dogherty, whom his lordship permitted to live in the house in Berkeley-square out of humanity. These things were taken from the house in Berkeley-square. My wife gave me information, and I missed a bed, a bolster, and two blankets: they were taken while I went with a message to his lordship. I had left Dogherty and my wife in the house. The things were in the room where he slept. I had seen them some time before; he was out when I missed them. He returned in about an hour. I asked him what he had done with the bed and blankets? he said he had sold them; fell on his knees, and begged I would not inform his lordship of the transaction. He was in liquor; it was between one and two o'clock in the day time. He told us where he sold them. I found them at Marlborough-street. I suspected Mackin, as I had seen him three or four times at the house; he came to see Dogherty. Somebody gave me information. I went and found another bed had been taken out backwards; he owned to taking that, fell on his knees again, and begged for mercy.
