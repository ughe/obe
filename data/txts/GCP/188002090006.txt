ELLEN CRACKNELL I was serving in the shop on Sunday night when
the prisoner came in with two women and a man-she asked for tea, sugar.
and bacon, which came to 6d., and put down a half-crown--my father took
it up and gave her 28. change and then went out after her.
EDWARD BURDON (Policeman E 315). On 7th December I saw the
prisoner leave a public-house in New Compton Street-Mr. Cracknell
had spoken to me and taken me there--I said to her “This man accuses
you of giving him a bad half-crown”-she said "I did not know it was
bad," and asked her friends to give her some money, and said "Give him a
good one for it," but none of them had any money-she said that she got
it on the ice by selling oranges- I took her to the station and Cracknell gavo
me this half-crown-she was charged at Marlborough Street on the 8th-she
gave her own recognisance to appear on the 11th, but did not appear; I took
her on a warrant and she was charged on the 18th and discharged.
WILLIAM LEONARD (Policeman E 426). I took the prisoner on January
16th on information received from Mr. Schy and told her the charge-she
said " If you take me down I shall get seven years "-I said "I cannot help
that, you will have to go "-on the way to the station she said she did
not care as she had not been convicted since 1873–- Mr. Schy handed me these
two bad shillings.
Cross-examined. I did not say that I would make it as hot as I could
for you.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These two shillings are bad, and from the same mould.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. “It is a lie, I never was
in the shop in my life."
