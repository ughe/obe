to the power required, an effort of 12 pounds would be necessary, I

Cross examined. In my experience there are often cases of violence
being inflicted on a child before it is fully born, and therefore it comes
into the world dead; it may be born dead from violence, accidental or in-
tentional-the lungs being what is called crepitant simply shows that the
child has breathed, but not that it has had an independent circulation
I put the lungs in water-I admit that that is no certain sign of itself-I
know no cause but disease for the lungs of children who have not
breathed floating on water; I mean what is called putrefaction--the fact
of the child having the power of breathing before it is entirely born does
not render these tests certain-I believe it is not very common for a child
to breathe and yet never to live in the sense of that term-it does happen
that a child breathes and yet does not get an independent circulation-
the mere act of gasping in the act of being born would help to inflate the
lungs-there was blood in the conium and the venit oasieva; that does not
necessarily prove that the child was born alive-I do not know that in
cases where a woman delivers herself, the chances of a child coming into
the world alive are not so great as when a medical man attends her; the
operation is nature's own effort, but there might be a slight difference in
cutting the umbilical cord, which in this instance was severed an inch and
a half from the child ; that would be the place where a medical man would
sever it-it was not tied at all, and death might possibly have been
caused by bleeding from it-I considered the severance properly near to
the abdomen-by what is known as an independent circulation I wish
the Jury to understand that that means when no blood is flowing from
the mother to the child-I cannot positively state, as a medical man, that
this child ever had a separate existence.
Re-examined. There was no evidence, on my examination of the child,

or putrefaction of the lungs to account for crepitation ; there was nothing
but the child having breathed to account for it-the child contained very
