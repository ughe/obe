 Prisoner's Defence. I often found money, and gave it him; I took this copper, and said I would frighten him, because he should not put money in my way any more; there was no key in my room, but his niece said to me,"There is a key in my room" - I took a key from there, it would not open my door, and I put it into the cupboard - they often mislaid keys; I did not know what door it would open - I found this copper on the club-room table.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1884.  ROBERT FOLKS was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 2 sovereigns, 1 half-crown, and 7 shillings, the monies of Robert Peppin, his master.
 ROBERT PEPPIN. I am a  working silversmith, and live in Gray's Inn-lane. The prisoner was in my employ. On the 14th of April I gave him a bag of work to take to Goldsmiths'-hall, with 2l. 7s. 6d. in it, to pay the duty; he did not return, and was taken on the 17th of July - the work was taken to the hall.
   CHARLES FLINT. I am foreman to Mr. Peppin. I put two sovereigns and 7s. 6d. into the prisoner's bag to take to the hall; he never returned.
 Prisoner. Q. I did not see the money in the bag - Did you put it in? A. Yes; you know I did.
   HENRY DAVIS. I took up the prisoner in Cloth-fair; he said he was very sorry, and asked me to ask his master to forgive him - I said I could not, as I understood it was a second offence; he said if I would intercede for him, he would pay his master so much a week, as he had done in a former case.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, denying all knowledge of the money being in the bag; and stating his absenting himself was on account of the lowness of his wages.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Six Months.
 1885.  GEORGE WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 100lbs. of lead, value 15s., the goods of Thomas Miller , and fixed to a building.
 THOMAS MILLER. I have some buildings in  St. Luke's parish. I did not miss the lead from them myself.
   GEORGE TWEEDY. I am servant to Mr. Miller, and look after his houses, which are in St. Luke's parish. On the morning of the 17th of July the lead was missed; I had seen it all safe the day before - part of it was left in the house, but it was all cut from the roof; I was looking over the premises, and discovered it - I afterwards compared some which was found at the watch-house, and it fitted exactly.
   DANIEL NEWTON. I am a blacksmith. On the morning of the 26th of July I was at work in my shop, and can see Mr. Miller's premises through a hole in my wall - I looked through, and saw the prisoner coming down the stairs with a load under his arm; I went after him, found him with a large quantity of lead, covered with an apron - I took him in the court, and saw the lead matched with the roof, it fitted exactly; he was taken to the watch-house - he had about 30lbs. under his arm, and up stairs I found about three quarters of a cwt.; I had seen him there before.
   GEORGE WOODGATE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner to the watch-house, with the lead; I saw it compared to the top, it fitted.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never was in those houses; I was going to work - I ran into the privy, and there was this piece of lead.
     GUILTY. Aged 37. Transported for Seven Years.
 1886.  JOHN EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 75lbs. of lead, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Miller , and fixed to a certain building.
 THOMAS MILLER. The house in question is mine. I have lost two or three cwt. of lead at different times.
   GEORGE TWEEDY. I am servant to Mr. Miller. On the 4th of July I saw this lead safe; the prisoner was stopped next morning - I was sent for, and compared the lead, it fitted exactly, nail holes and all.
   RICHARD BURT. I was going to market that morning and saw a man come out of the court with a load; I went to Woodgate's door and told him.
   GEORGE WOODGATE. Burt gave me information; I went, and overtook the prisoner near Pitfield-street, with a soot bag, which he was resting on a post - I took hold of it, and it contained lead; I compared it with the roof of Mr. Miller's house, it fitted exactly.
   JOHN SHAW. I am a watchman. I was called to assist a man over the road; I went over to the George the Fourth public-house, and took the prisoner - I saw the lead fitted - it appeared quite right.
   DANIEL NEWTON. I was on the parapet on the 4th - the lead was all safe; next morning I saw this fitted.
   MARY STEWARD. I am bar-maid at the George the Fourth: the lead was left there - we let them have the same again.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going down the court, saw the lead lying down, and put into my bag.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
