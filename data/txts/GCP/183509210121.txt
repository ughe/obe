SAMUEL Porter, (police-constable C 35.) I went to Buckingham Pa-
lace with Dudley-I had before that been to Pimlicó—we went first to a
public-house, and then to several other houses-we went to No. 4, Cathe-
rine-street; a woman came to the door and answered-she afterwards
went with us to the Palace, and we took Nicholls into custody- we took
him to the station-house; the inspector there put some questions to him;
he told him not to say any thing unless he thought proper he asked him
if he had taken any lace from the Palace ; Nicholls said he did on several
occasions, and had sold it to Shadford—I was present when Shadford was
searched—I saw two pieces of silver-gilt lace found on him, and some
dust in his pocket.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How many different houses dia
you go into before you went to No. 4, Catherine-street ? A. From four-
teen to eighteen; they were public-houses—I believe the first was Tatter-
sall's tap; I was on duty-there is a regulation, respecting policemen not
drinking when on duty I did drink there that day-Shadford was going
to see for the other man--we went into other public-houses, but we did
not drink any more; we were asked, but refused I went from Marlbo-
rough-street Office to Mr. Smart's shop, to tell him to come, or send his
servant-he sent his man, but I have never seen Mr. Smart since.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. What was the first question
that was put to Nicholls when he came to the station-bouse? A. I can-
not exactly say--the inspector was standing there he does not always
take a note of what passes-on that occasion he made some sort of a me-
morandum, but he did not take it all; I saw him write.
