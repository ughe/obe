I was at the station-house on Sunday morning, the 6th of March, and about eleven o'clock, the prisoner Frost, who gave his name "Webber," came there in a state of in toxication—he said he had met with a man the day before who had robbed him of 2 l . 5 s  —I asked him if he knew the man—he said, no he had never seen him till the day before—that he had promised to get him work, and he had employed him to carry some lead for him, and he was to give him 5 s . for carrying it—I asked him if he knew where-the lend came from—he said he could point out the place—I went with him to the place, in Shadwell-market—it is a sort of sentit is an unoccupied place—the door was standing open, and, on looking up to the ceiling, I saw some lead had been taken off. THOMAS MATHER. That place is the property of Abraham Gole, Esq.—I have known it for twenty years.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is Mr. Gole alive? A.  Yes—it is in consequence of having seen some leases, and having the rent to receive, and the taxes and repairs to pay that—I know the premises well—they are in my pos session at this time.
   Q.  Was there any lead whatever, to your knowledge, on that building? A.  Not to my knowledge—and in the course of twenty years it must want repairing—I never had to repair any lead—I never say any lead on that building in my life—I never knew what it was covered with—it is only one room—the roof may be ten or twelve feet high—it was hardly ever used—I could not let it at all—I have never had to repair it—I saw it covered—I saw the tiles put on—these was a gutter, but I do not know what it was made of—I never saw the gutter.
