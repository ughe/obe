The prisoner came there some time in Oct.-- he had a watch on the table
he owed me 198., and I said to him, "Jackson, it is almost time for you to
pay me"-he said, "Tick the watch"- I took the watch, and hung it ap

Prisoner. Another boy gave it him, whose name is Thomas Coile; he is

Prisoner. Mr. Wilmott never gave me any money; he gave it into the
other boy's hand.

611. JAMES KEMBLE, stealing 1 atlas, value 58.; the goods of Alexander
James Edmund Cockburn, Esq., his master.
ALEXANDER JAMES EDMUND COCKBURN, Esq., Q.C. I live in the Temple.
The prisoner was my under-clerk and messenger-I lost such an atlas as this
produced I believe this to be mine
Cross-examined by MR, PAYNE. Q. You cannot say how long it is since
you saw it? 4. No--the prisoner was in my service nearly three years---|
have trusted him with considerable sums, and always found him honest-the
books were kept in a room to which he had access-sometimes I did not go
in there for a month together, unless I wanted a book.
· JAMES CRAWFORD. I live in Thames-street. About six o'clock one Saturday
night in Jan., I met the prisoner in Charles-street-he asked me if I would
sell two atlasses for him : this looks like one of them I sold it to Dr.
Williams, & schoolmaster, for 58., and gave the prisoner the money,
Cross-examined. Q. How do you know the book ? A. By the colors on
the back, and the binding.
THOMAS Durkin, (policeman.) This book was brought to me by Mr.
Williams's assistant--this is Mr. Henry's signature to these depositions
(read~"The prisoner says, 'I asked the boy to go to Mr. Williams, to sell
the book, because I did not like to go there myself, having been at school
there.'")

ELEANOR KEMBLE. I am tbe prisoner's mother. I cannot swear that I
have seen this book before, but I gave the prisoner a map-book like it, out of
my father's box, not more than six months ago I do not know whether it is
a French book-my father was not a French scholar-I did not examine it
particularly-the prisoner said, “This book is of no use to me, mother, I have
a great mind to take it to Dr. Williams."
Court. Q. Are you Mr. Cockburn's laundress? A. No-my husband
is a gentleman's servant-my father was porter at the Judges' Entrance to the
Queen's Bench-I have known gentlemens' bags left with him in the long
vacation.
