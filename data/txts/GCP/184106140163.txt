RICHARD WEBSTER. I am in partnership with my father, a chronometer
maker, in Birchin-lane, Cornbill. This gold watch has the name "R.
Webster, Cornbill," on it--we generally put the word "London" on ours,
which is omitted on this-it was never made by my father or myself we
had not made so high a number when we lived in Cornbill- it has appa-
rently the general appearance of one of our watches, but I can say it is
not-if it was one of our make it would be worth 20 guineas new, second
hand it would be perhaps 15l. or 161.- it depends on the time it has been
made I have taken it to pieces since I was before the Magistrate the
value I put on it now is 77. or 81. at the utmost-the case would cost about
51. 1 should imagine-the works are worth nothing at all.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Then I suppose it would not
go? 4. I should think not--we are liable to have articles imitated-we
frequently find our articles imitated-it is certainly not worth 81. I do
not know where I could get a pair of cases like these made-I did not
take it to pieces myself—it is a lever watch there is no compensating
balance--it goes on a pair of jewels, which are worth about 38.-it is cal-
culated to deceive an ordinary purchaser, and it did me; for before I took it
to pieces I said it was worth 121.-the jewels are garnets apparently, we
use rubies.
