Bana HIBBERT. I live in Bedford-place, Borough-road. On the 18th
of September I was walking in Blackfriars-road, near the New Cut, and
saw the prisoner there my sister Rose was there with mewe all three
want into a public-house-he accompanied us to our lodging, and went up
stairs with us-he remained in the room with me--my sister was con-
stantly in and out-he proposed to drink tea with us I said I could not
afford it, but if he would send half a crown down to the landlady, she
would send tes up-he pulled a parcel out of his pocket, which seemed to
me to contain half-sovereigns my sister was in the room at the time ho
gave me two half-crowns, which I took down to the landlady-I shortly
afterwards left him alone with my sister, for nearly half an hour, and when
I came back she had got half-8-sovereign-she showed it to me-I said it
was not a good one-she sounded it on the drawers, and said it was good
-the prisoner said he wished he had got a thousand of them, and that it was
good-I asked him to give me one, and, after considering, he said he could
not afford it, but at last said, if I would fetch the 58. back, he would give
me one-1 fetched him the 58., and gave them to him, and he gave
me what I supposed was half-a-sovereign-he left the room almost imme-
diately, and said he would be back in a quarter of an hour after he was
gone, I rubbed the half-sovereign between my fingers, and thought it was
not good I went down stairs and saw the landlady, and then went out in
pursuit of him-I found him about two doors below, on the opposite side
of the way--I told him he had given me bad money-he said, had he ?, he
would give me good, and asked me to give him that I had got in my hand
I said, "No" he said if I would give it to him, he would give me good,
and asked if I would have it all in gold, or all in silver-I said I did not
care what it was, so that it was good-he walked a good way I turned
round to look for a policeman--we walked a long distance--he asked me
if I would accompany him to his place, and then he would give it mo all
in silver-he went to snatch both half-sovereigns, and snatched one from
my hand-Wyborn and two policemen came up I saw him then take out
of bis right-hand pocket a brown paper parcel and throw it into the road-
it was found to contain several gilt sixpences- I gave the one I had in my
hand, to Corstorpham the constable.
