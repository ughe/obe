SURREY CASES .
   Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
 1962. FREDERICK COLE and CORNELIUS SHEEHAN , unlaw fully uttering a counterfeit shilling, having others in their possession: to which SHEEHAN  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Six Months .
   MESSRS. SCRIVEN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. ADELIA JONES. On 1st Oct. the prisoners came to the bar of the Russell Hotel, and asked for a pint of half and half—Cole gave me a shilling for it—I put it in the detector—it bent, and I gave it to my father. WILLIAM JONES. I am last witness's father. On 1st Oct. I saw the prisoners at my bar—my daughter brought me a bad shilling—I told them it was bad, and asked them for a good one—they said they had not got one—Cole then said he was a hardworking man, and wished me to give him back the shilling—I would not, but sent for a constable and gave them into custody and gave him the shilling—the prisoners were searched in my house—Shee han struggled very much, and I saw two other pieces of bad money fall from his mouth—three others were afterwards taken from him. HENRY COOMBER( policeman, P  302). I took the prisoner on 1st Oct. Another constable said Sheehan bad some more in his mouth—I caught him round the throat, and took three pieces from his mouth—I received (his one from Mr. Jones ( all produced )—I found is 3 s . 4 1/2 d . good money on Sheehan, but none on Cole. JAMES PLUMB( policeman, P  89). I produce the two shillings which fell from Sheehan's mouth. WILLIAM WEBSTER. These six shillings are all counterfeit, and all from the same mould.
   COLE  — GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined Six Months .
 1963. JONATHAN WILD , unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. SCRIVEN and CLERK conducted the Prosecution. MARY ANN MEAD. I am barmaid at the Bridge-house beer-shop, Old Kent-road. On 6th Sept., about half-past 8 o'clock in the evening, the pri soner came and asked for a glass of gin—I told him we did not sell it, and he then said a glass of ale—I served him with it, and he threw down a half-crown—I tried it, bent it, and told him it was bad—he said he had just taken it of an omnibus conductor—he did not pay for the ale, but left without drinking it—I gave the half-crown to Pocock. WILLIAM POCOCK. I am waster at the Briidge-house beer-shop. I remem ber the prisoner coming on 8th Sept, and saw him give the half-crown to Mead—she gave it to me—I said to the prisoner, "This is a regular duffer "—he said, "I was not aware of it; an omnibus conductor gave it me, a cousin of mine"—he wanted me to give it him back, but I would not; I gave it to Juniper—the prisoner left—I followed him, and saw him go to Mr. Simons's.
