 (  The prisoner read a long defence in which he stated that he had been en  gaged by the agents of the ship in which he first left England to manage the hotel at Port Philip—that his health obliging him to return to England, he with their consent transferred his licence to another party, who agreed to take the house at the rent of 700l. per annum, and he came over with the money he had obtained, intending to take back his poor relations to settle at Port Philip, where he had sufficient influence to procure them good situations. He denied ever representing to any one that he had to receive 700l. or 800l., or that Mr. Marklew gave him the money and goods stated. He declared that he had no fraudulent intention, but that being unable to obtain sufficient money to pay the remainder of the passage-money for which he had become security he was induced to leave the vessel with his wife, and proceed to Caen, in Normandy, where his step-father resided, in hopes of procuring it from him, but without success, and that his return to England was prevented by a severe attack of illness; had his intentions been not to have gone with the vessel, he should not have paid any deposit to the owners, nor have gone to any expense in fitting up the vessel for the accommodation of the passengers; that the contents of his trunks were not rubbish, but of much greater value and importance than was stated; he produced several papers, the inspection of which, he said, would corroborate the truth of his statement.) GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined Twelve Months.
