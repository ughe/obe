CAROLINE Fox. I am the wife of Joseph Fox. I formerly lived as servant
with the prisoner-he keeps a beer shop in the Waterloo-road-last Friday
week I met the prisoner, between 1 and 2 o'clock, and he asked me if í
would go for an errand for him, and he asked me to pledge these things-
they were lying before me, folded up-I pledged three pieces at Mr. Cole's
between 4 and 5 o'clock, and these two at Mr. Wharton's- I took two pieces
of silk to Mr. Vickers'; and while I was there an officer came and prevented
me-I handed the money and duplicates to my mistress, and she passed
them over to my master, in my presence I had taken the articles off the
table, in the presence of my master and my mistress; my master told me to
take them I have been in the habit of pawning things before-I received
118. on each of the three pieces of silk.
Cross-examined. Q. How far do you live from there ? A. Ten minutes'
walk at the most I was a servant there when I married—Mr. Cole lives
opposite, Mr. Duke lives close by, and Mr. White not far off-I had pawned
things there before for the prisoner.
JAMES HOBBES, I am assistant to Mr. Wharton, of Mount-street, Lam-
beth. I produce two pieces of silk, pledged on 2nd May, by the last
witness, for 158. each.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you know her! 4. I had never seen her before,
and I have been there twelve months.
SAMUEL HEZEKIAH COLE I am a pawnbroker, of the Waterloo-road. I
produce three pieces of silk, pledged by Cox, on 2nd May, two at 228., and
one at 11s., in the name of Chandler.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you know her? Yes; I have known her
some time.
GEORGE LEGG (City policeman, 440). On Friday afternoon, 2nd May, I
saw Cox at Mr. Dickers, the pawnbroker's, No. 82, Lambeth-marsh-these
two pieces of silk were rolled up on the counter; from information I got
there, I went to the Hit or Miss beer shop, Waterloo-road, saw the prisoner
there, and asked him if he had sent Fox with two pieces of silk to pledge-
he said, "Yes"-I asked him where he had got them from; he said that
he bought them of a tallyman on the Tuesday previous (this was on Friday)
-I asked him if he had got a bill—he said, “No, I paid ready money, 11. a
piece, to make my wife some dresses -I asked him if he knew the man,
he said that he did not-I told him that the silk would be detained, and left
him-I then went to the City, took Mr. Trim to the pawnbroker's to see
the silk; then went back to the prisoner's house, took him to Mr. Dickers,
and asked him whether the silk on the counter was his property-he said,
“Yes;" and that they were the two pieces he had sent his servant to pledge
for him-I told him that he must consider himself in custody for having
stolen property in his possession, and asked him if he had got any more-he
said that he had only those two pieces which he had bought of a tallyman on
the Tuesday previous, to make his wife a dress, and that it would be a lesson
to him--I searched him at the station, and found 31. 168. 6fd., a silver
watch and guard, a gold ring, and a purse.
