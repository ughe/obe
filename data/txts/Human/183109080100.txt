 Q. If the cab horse had jibbed towards the north side, might that not have drawn the shaft towards the dray wheel? A. It might - horses on the rank will shift sometimes, and the space of half an inch will bring them closer - the coal-waggon was going on at the time, and the farther it went on the narrower the road got - I cannot say it was not the prisoner who took the deceased from under the wheel; Hazlewood got there before me: my back was towards the dray - how the accident was caused I cannot say; Goodson the driver of the first dray came up, when I was taking the man up; two or three assisted, I cannot say whether the prisoner was one.
   ROBERT DARKETT WYATT. I am one of the housesurgeons at St. Bartholomew's hospital. The deceased was brought in between six and seven o'clock in the morning; he appeared to have received a very severe injury, and died almost immediately - several of the ribs of the right side were fractured and driven into the lungs, which were much lacerated; there is not a doubt the accident caused his death.
   BENJAMIN WELLS. I live in Mercer-alley, Bankside, and am carman to Mr. Elmer, a coal-merchant. I was driving his coal waggon towards the Mansion-house; my waggon was going on at the time of the alarm - it was on the Gutter-lane side; I was at my third horse's head on the foot path side, near the kennel - my wheels were in the kennel; I heard the alarm and stopped my horses immediately; the first thing I saw was their picking the man up - I did not notice whether any body was at the head of the horses of the dray; it had just past between my middle horse and the coach-stand - I did not observe the prisoner at all; when I first saw the cab-man, he was standing at the off side of his horse, on the side nearest to the dray; he and the cab were then quite safe.
 Q. Was the cab the first in the rank, or the middle of it? A. It was first on the rank; my waggon and the dray were going in the same direction - the first dray cleared my waggon, except rubbing a little against the chain as it passed; I cannot say whether it went between the cab and my waggon, for I was on my near side, near the shaft horse's head - I saw the first drayman pass me, he had two horses; I did not see the leading horse of the second dray coming, for hearing the alarm I had just time to pull up my horses - I did not see the horse of the first dray till it was stopped.
 COURT. Q. Was there a good road on the other side of the way? A. Yes; there was nothing passing just at that time.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q.Would that not have been his wrong side? A. Yes, unless there had been a great deal of room - whether the cab had moved after the first dray passed I cannot tell.
   JOHN COVENTRY. I am a coal-porter. I was in Cheapside, standing at the end of Paternoster-row, looking towards Watling-street; I heard a cry out, and saw a man laying before the wheel on the ground - the cab was in the rank, and the coal-waggon standing near; I saw two drays, one had passed the coal waggon, but not the other - I went up, the man was then turned on his back; and the second dray backed a little; several persons were putting the man into the cab - I had seen the drays turn out of St. Paul's churchyard, but not afterward; the drivers were with them then.
   CHARLES WEBB. I drive a cab, and live in Francis-street, Tottenham-court-road. I was talking to the deceased, by the side of his horse, and went from him to my own cab, leaving him standing between his own horse and the pavement, in the middle of the road; my cab was not on the stand - I was on the opposite side of the way; I heard a scream, and saw the deceased down - I did not see him knocked down; the wheel of the dray was on part of his clothes - they could not get him up till the dray was moved; the man driving the dray, to the best of my knowledge, backed the horse; it was the drayman who backed it - he was at the fore horse's head when I first saw him; the dray had passed the cab, and the coal waggon had just passed the cab; I cannot say whether the driver was with his horses at the time they passed the cab, but when I called to him to stop, when I took the man up, he was at his horse's head.
   BENJAMIN SIMS. I am in the employ of the Post-office. I was coming along Cheapside, and saw the coal waggon on the pavement side; I cannot say whether it was moving; the first dray passed on, and as the second approached, I saw the cab-man was in danger, as soon as the first horse passed - he appeared to me, to be leaning on the shaft of his cab, and not to see his danger, till the wheel of the second dray came close to him, nearly in contact with him - I saw his hands extended, as though he was attempting to save himself, by trying to catch hold of the planks which cross the dray; he was so near that the dray wheel was hid from me; I saw him go down - I cannot say where the driver of the dray was; I was some distance off, and before I got up he was released; the prisoner was with his horses when I got up - I believe he was on the off side of his horse, having passed round to where the accident happened; I saw him back his horse as I came up.
   MATTHEW IRENOUX. I am a butcher. I was in Cheapside, in my cart, following behind the two drays; there was a coal-waggon going on in front; I saw the cab on the stand - one dray passed the coal-waggon, the second followed it, but there was not sufficient room to pass - I saw the cab-man just before the second dray came up, standing with his back towards the dray, with his elbow on the shaft - I did not see the driver of the second dray; he was some yards off, on the pavement; the horses were going by themselves - the waggon was between him and his horses I am certain; I saw the front piece of wood which held the first butt of beer strike the man, who could not recover himself, and he was driven on before the wheel about six yards - it was pressing him on; if the driver had been at his horse's head I think he might have stopped the dray before it got on the man's body; I am sure he was on the pavement at the time; I helped to put the man into the cab - the prisoner came up; I told him he had killed the man - he said he was very sorry for it.
