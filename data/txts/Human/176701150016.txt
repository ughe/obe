 107. (M.)  Anne Neptune, spinster, was indicted for stealing a linen shirt, value 3 s. the property of Abraham Burage ; and one cotton gown and one linen shift, the property of Stephen Mercer, Jan. 7. ||
   Catharine Burage. I live at Cow-cross; my husband is named Abraham. I gave a shirt to Elizabeth Burage to wash on Twelfth-day; it was lost; I saw it since in the pawnbroker's hands.
   Frank Rochford. I am a pawnbroker; I took this shirt in of a woman named Sarah Silk; she said it was her husband's: I lent her half a crown upon it; (produced in court.)
 C. Burage. This is the shirt that was lost.
   George Fisher. I am constable; I took the prisoner up, and the things were delivered to me at Justice Girdler's; that is all I know of it.
   Elizabeth Burage. Catharine Burage delivered me this shirt to wash; it was washed, and taken away before it was ironed.
 Q. Why do you charge the prisoner?
 E. Burage. The prisoner is my sister-in-law; I took her in, she having no place to go to; she absconded; I suspected her, and found her three nights after, in Catharine-street in the Strand, in the night time. I took her before Justice Girdler; there she confessed she took it; I found the shift at Mr. Rochfort's.
 Rochfort. I took the shift in of, I believe, the prisoner at the bar, but she came in the name of  Mary Jones; (produced by the constable.)
   Sarah Mercer. I gave Elizabeth Burage my gown and shift to wash.
 Q. What is your husband's name?
 S. Mercer. His name is  Stephen Mercer; this is the shift here produced.
 The prisoner said nothing in her defence.
     Guilty. T.
