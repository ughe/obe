 1812.  ISAAC BEVEN and WILLIAM ADAMS were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , 50 lbs. weight of lead, belonging to Thompson Hankey , and fixed to a certain building, against the Statute.
 ANN BUMSTEAD. I am a widow, and live in Graham-place, Dalston. I was in my upper room on the 18th of August, and saw the two prisoners; Adams was assisting Beven in getting on the roof of Mr. Hankey's coach-house - it was only Beven got on the roof, and he stripped the lead from the roof; I sent my servant to give an alarm: I saw Adams go to the other side of the road, and return with a bag, which appeared to be empty - I did not see them more than ten minutes.
   WILLIAM PREST. I live near Mr. Hankey's coach-house at Dalston. On the 18th of August I saw the two prisoners near the coach-house gate, and a parcel of lead lay on the ground by them; I heard one of them say to the other, "D - n the fellow, why does not he come with a ladder?" I then walked across the road, and had a more full view of them; while I was there Bumstead sent her servant to ask if I thought they were Mr. Hankey's workmen - I said I thought not; I still watched them, and saw Adams pick up some slates; he marched off towards Hackney, and the other began to take up the lead; I walked up to him, and said, "What are you going to do with that lead?" he said, "What am I going to do? I am going after the slater to do a job in Hackney-road;" I said, "What are you going to take Mr. Hankey's lead? No, you shall not step a foot further;" he then threw the lead down, while I had hold of his collar; Mr. Hankey's servant then came out; Adams afterwards told me he lived in Bryan's-buildings, Kingsland-road - we have inquired since, and the address is correct.
   HENRY TROTT. I am gardener to Mr. Thomson Hankey. On the 18th of August I went out, and saw the lead - I compared it with the top of the coach-house; it fitted it in every part and every nail hole.
   JOHN KEEN. I am in Mr. Hankey's service. I saw the lead fitted - it matched exactly.
   BARNARD JEFFREY. I took up Adams on the 20th of August - he said he had done nothing that he knew of; he was near his own house, in Kingsland-road.
 ADAM'S Defence. I was going to my work - I never touched any lead; I had a load of slates on my shoulder, and that gentleman came and detained me, and looked at my property; I told him where I lived.
 MRS. BUMSTEAD re-examined. Q. Are you sure you saw this man help the other? A. I could not swear to his person, but Prest got sight of them before I came down - I saw him get up to them before I lost sight of them; Adams went across the road and got the bag, and then Prest went up to them.
 Beven received a good character.
 BEVEN -    GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Six Months.
 ADAMS -    GUILTY. Aged 28. Confined One Year.
 1813.  JOHN FOTHERINGHAM was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July , 1 quadrant, value 20s; 2 shirts, value 4s.: 2 pairs of trousers, value 6s.; 1 frock, value 1s., and 1 pair of drawers, value 1s., the goods of Bartholomew Crannell Longmuir.
   RUTH SARAH ESTALL. I keep a lodging-house for seamen at Bell's-wharf, Shadwell. The prisoner lodged at my house - he left me on the 29th of July, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, and did not return; I missed this property, which belonged to Bartholomew Crannell Longmuir - I had seen them safe the day before in his chest, in the back kitchen; he was at work on board a ship - this is one of his shirts; I had given it out to be washed several times.
   CATHERINE PORTER. I live in Dean-street, Shadwell. On Tuesday evening, the 29th of July, I saw the prisoner at near eight o'clock - he passed by and spoke to me, and I saw a quadrant in his bosom; I pulled his waistcoat aside, and saw it; I asked what he was going to do with it - he said he was going to take it to the young man it belonged to; he called him James.
   JOSEPH HAWES. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Ratcliffe-highway. On Tuesday, the 29th of July, the prisoner pawned a quadrant with me, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening; my house is about five minutes walk from Mrs. Estall's.
   EMANUEL SOLOMONS. I am a salesman at Rosemary-lane. I bought two duplicates of the prisoner - one was for a quadrant, and the other a shirt; I got the quadrant from Mr. Hawes, and sold it to Mr. Harris; I got the shirt - this is it.
   HENRY HARRIS. I bought the quadrant of Solomons - this is it.
   JAMES WHITE. I took up the prisoner - he said he took the quadrant, but not the clothes: but when he was before the Magistrate he said he took the quadrant and the shirt.
 GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to mercy. - Confined Six Months.
