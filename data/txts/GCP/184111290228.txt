GEORGE JONES. I am master-baker, and live in John-street. On the
night in question I was standing at my own door between eight and nine
o'clock-I noticed the prisoner, his wife, and two men coming up John
street-when they got opposite my door they stopped talking together-1
saw Lartin walk over to them--he said, “What is this you have got to
say concerning my friends ?"—the prisoner replied, "I do not know your
friends, I have no friends but my own here, who I am talking with" -- the
prosecutrix and her sister advanced towards the prisoner and made use of
most dreadful oaths, and said they would tear her liver out-they imme-
diately collared her, and had her to the ground-I saw Lartin strike the
prisoner-he brought him back some distance and struck him many blows
-at last he knocked him down and began to kick him-I saw a young
man go in among the few gathered together, and pick him up, and as he
picked him up I saw Lartin strike him again in his harness -I have no
connexion with the prisoner or his friends--a great crowd of people collected.
MR. DOANE. Q. Did you see the prosecutrix's gown torn off? A. No,
nor the flower-pots or flint thrown- I did not go off the step of my door
- I did not see either of the Mahoney's struck.
WILLIAM CHOUN. I am journeyman to Mr. Jones. I did not see the
commencement of this, but when the prisoner went down the court to his
own house I went down with him-he had all his things nearly torn off his
back at that time-we had to pass Mahoney's door, and saw three of them-
they were stripped to their stays to fight, waiting for the prisoner, and when
he came by the door they all three laid hold of him and beat him-Lartin
also took him by the neck and hit him three or four times I did not try
to assist the prisoner-I did not know him before-I told Lartin he ought
to be ashamed of himself—some man then took hold of the prisoner
and took him away, and I took him into his own house myself-the
women came hooting to him to come out again, and somebody came and
pushed the door-when I came out I met one of them with a stone and a
brick in her hand-somebody begged her not to throw it at the window,
and she said she would be d- if she would not-I heard the window
smash-- I could not see who did it.
MR. DOANE. Q. Were you there when the garden-pots were thrown!
A. No, nor the flint or brick.
JAMES BODDY. I am a master-carman, and live in Bond-street. On
Sunday the 12th of December, between eight and nine o'clock, as I and
my wife and family sat in the kitchen we heard violent screaming-
my wife went up, and I followed, and my children also— I opened my
door and saw two men fighting- I do not know them-there was a cry of
“ For G-sake let this good woman come in or she will be murdered"-
was the prisoner's wife--I saw her afterwards-she was then being dragged
on the curb of the pavement by females, who I did not know, but I have
since found one of them to be Bridget Mahoney-- I went to lift the prisoner's
wife and found she was grasped by Bridget Mahoney by her long hair
behind-I ungrasped her hand and she was taken away--she was down
at the time and her hair was entangled in Mahoney's hand.
