   Prisoner.  I deny being in the counting-house at all. WILLIAM HENRY MATTHAMS. I live at Southampton-street, Camberwell, and am a whip-maker—on 24th September the prisoner came to me and offered me this sample of grease ( produced ), saying there were twenty-four casks lying at the Nine Elms station—he asked me 31 s ., a cwt. for it, and I bought it—he said there would be charges to pay; would we pay them, or deduct it from the amount—he did not say what amount—I gave my carman 5 l .,—I am quite certain the prisoner is the man who came.
   Prisoner. Q.  Was there anyone else there at the time? A.  Yes; several persons about—the charges were to be paid out of the bulk of the money for the goods. EDWARD CALLUM. I live at 2, Havil-street, Camberwell, and am carman to the last witness—on 24th September I saw the prisoner at my master's premises—my master gave me 5 l ., to pay the charges on some grease at the Nine Elms station—I drove the prisoner there in the cart—he went into the booking-office and came out again with a paper in his hand—he said he had found the number and the truck, and they were down on the mid-line—he then said he would go and see what the charges came to—he went in, came out again, and said, "They come to 3 l ., 2 s ."—I gave him three sovereigns and a half-crown, and he went away—he went down on one side of the trucks and I went the other—he said he would go and fetch the porter who was to deliver the grease—he went away, and I never saw him again till I saw him at the Police-court—I am quite positive he is the man—I could swear to him from a hundred.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you see anything of the sample? A.  No; you par ticularly requested my master to take care of the empty casks, and he said, "I will guarantee they shall be quite safe"—you also said, going along, there were twenty-four casks, and that I could draw them away in three loads. JOSEPH LEWIS WORSFOLD. I am a butcher, at Fulham—on Saturday, 13th September, the prisoner came to my house and showed me a sample of grease in a box, and said he had got fourteen casks lying at the South Western Railway station, which he wanted to dispose of—I asked him about it—he gave satisfactory answers, and I agreed to buy it—he asked me if I could fetch them then—I said, "No; it was no good to go then, because the railway would be closed "—I saw no more of him till Monday morning, at 6 o'clock—I saw him go by my window—my man came to me about two minutes afterwards and I gave him 5 l ., to pay the railway charges on the goods—my man is here; his name is Henry Chambers.
   Prisoner. Q.  You are mistaken in the identity of the man? A.  No; I could swear to you in a thousand—my window was shut down when I saw you on the Monday morning; the shutters were open—there were no curtains—I advanced the money to my man to advance to you.
