the said John Baggallay and others 1,000 yards of silk, and other goods, value
801.; their goods; with intent to cheat, &c.
Messrs. CLARKSON and BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN BAGGALLAY. I am a member of the firm of Spence, Baggallay, and Com
warehousemen, of Wood-street, Cheapside; there are four partners. On Friday,
Joth May, the prisoner called on us, and again on the 11th-I saw him on the
11th-(i had heard what he had called about)-I requested him to go with me
to a private room-I put a great many questions to him which I cannot repeat
now-he told me I might go to Welsh and Margetson's, and I should hear such
an account as would lead me to let him have goods- I told him a person of his
name had been bankrupt or insolvent-he replied, that was his sister
asked him to write down the name of the present firm in Galway, and I
wrote in pencil from his dictation-it is almost obliterated now (produced
I wrote “Mortimer, Thomas and Michael ; " and " Kealey and Co., Galway
he wrote himself-he told me they had a balance of 2,0001. and upwards in
their favour-he stated that once or twice-he had previously looked out
goods to the amount of about 801.-while I was in communication with
him, Mr. White, jun. came in, and gave him into custody.
Cross-examined by MR. PARRY. Q. Did he, among others, refer you to
White and Co. ? A. No, he did not mention their names-he told me he

pay for the goods, if I would not let him have credit - I do not know whether
there was 601. found on him; there was some money,
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. What part of the conversation was it that he said

Court. Q. Did the prisoner see Mr. White ? A. He did -he came into
the room and called me out-all the rest had taken place before.
