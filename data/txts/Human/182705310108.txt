 1298.  JOHN ADAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of May , 2 window sashes, value 2s., and 16 panes of glass, value 10s., the goods of James Sherman.
 JAMES SHERMAN. I  deal in building materials. These sashes were in a house at St. Catharine's-dock, which had been sold; they were in the parlour, separated from the house - I had employed the prisoner that day - I had bought them.
   WILLIAM SIDGROVE. On the 1st of June, in the morning. I met the prisoner in White Hart-street, Aldgate, with these sashes; he said they belonged to Mr. Beard. I said he had bought nothing at the last sale, and I would go to his house - I went there and gave him in charge.
   ROBERT BURGESS. I took him in charge.
 GUILTY. Aged 21. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Two Months.

 1299.  THOMAS THOMAS was indicted for on the 7th of May, feloniously, willfully, and maliciously assaulting Rosetta Cohen , spinster , at St. Dunstan, Stepney, in a certain public street and highway there, with intent the garments and clothes, which she then wore, wilfully. &c. to deface against the statute the said garments, being the goods of Hyam Benjamin Cohen.
 ROSETTA COHEN. I was twelve years old last January. On the 7th of May I was in  York-street, Stepney, and stopped to look at Punch and Judy. I saw the prisoner following me, whichever way I went. I had seen him on the Friday night before, in Whitechapel, but had no quarrel with him - I said nothing to him; I did not see him do any thing - but Smith came and took me into a hairdresser's shop, and told me to feel my pocket and see if I had lost any thing. I found my pelisse was cut as if with a knife - I lost nothing; I had a silver thimble and pencil there.
 HYAM BENJAMIN COHEN. This child is my daughter, and resides with me - a gentleman called and took me to York-street; I found her in a hair-dresser's shop with her pelisse cut.
   HENRY WHITFIELD. On the 7th of May I was selling shrimps, and saw Rosetta Cohen in the street; I saw the prisoner go up to her, take a knife out of his pocket, and cut her pelisse one way - he then shut his knife, put it into his pocket, and appeared to be stooping to find her pocket-hole; he then took his knife out again and cut it the other way.
   GEORGE WATSON. I stood next to the prisoner, and saw him take the knife and cut the girl's pelisse across twice.
   RICHARD SMITH. I am a patrol. In consequence of what the witnesses told me I came up, and ask the prisoner into custody. I found no knife d
   HENRY WHITFIELD. I am sure he used of knife - it was a sort of square handled knife.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he was merely standing to look at the show; and was perfectly innocent.
 GUILTY. Aged 18.
     Judgment Respited.
 1300.  JAMES PINN was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of April , 2 coal-hods, value 2l. 2s.; 2 tea-kettles, value 30s., and 1 tea-pot, value 9s., the goods of Richard Levett.
 MR. BARRY conducted the prosecution.
 RICHARD LEVETT. I am an  ironmonger, and live in Broad-street, Ratcliff. On the 16th of April the prisoner called at my shop, and said he was directed by a lady in the neighbourhood to get a coal-scuttle, kettle, and teapot, and would I let her have two of each to show her, and she would return one of each in half an hour - he said"I expect you will give me a few shillings for my trouble." I knew him before; he took the goods, and never returned. I did not see him again till yesterday - I wrote down the prices by his desire - my man delivered them to him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I buy goods on commission - I sold them to a person who owes me 16l. - I cannot get the money for them.
 RICHARD LEVETT. I never dealt with him before.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Three Months.
 1301.  WILLIAM ADAMS and JOHN BARRATT were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of April , 1 watch, value 3l.; 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence, the property of William Perkins.
 WILLIAM PERKINS. On the 10th of April I was mending a fence by the road side, at  South Mimms- Adams came up, and asked if I had seen any beast go by that day - I said No - he said, "Well, I suppose they are not gone by yet - it is about five o'clock, is it not?" - I took out my watch, and said, "It is." Barratt then passed by, and dropped a needle-case out of his hat - Adams took it up, and asked if it was mine - I said, "No, it is that man's" - he said,"I wonder what he has got in it" - he shook it, then opened it, and there was a needle in it - he took it out, and said,"Now I'll have a little fun, and get a little beer of him" - he then called to Barratt, and said "Have you lost any thing?" he said "Not that I know of" - he then said "Yes, a needle-case;" he called him back, and gave it to him. Barratt took it, touched his hat, and said, "I am much obliged to you," and was going away - Adams said "What is in it?" - he said"A needle;" Adams said he would bet a pint of beer that there was not. Barrett said he would lay 5s. there was; they went on till they got up to a sovereign. Adams asked me to lay the sovereign - I refused, but through his persuasion I did, at last. I gave Adams my watch to hold against the sovereign - the case was opened, there was a needle and pin in it. Adams then gave him my watch - I said"It is a complete swindle, but if you will go home with me I'll give you the sovereign" - they went 40 or 50 yards.
