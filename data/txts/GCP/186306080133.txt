Cross-examined by Mr. OPPENHEIM, Q. Had you been in the habit of at-
tending this Chapel before. 4. Three years before I introduced him to my
mistress on the first occasion on which he came, and he said prayers in the
drawing-room, on that occasion he used to visit, after that, very frequently,
and remain all day and sleep all night, and have his meals there. I some-
times told my mistress, that the prisoner had tea, or was going to have tea
with them, and she made no objection--she said, that as my husband was
away, he would keep me from getting into barm, and therefore she had no
objection. I told her that I paid for his tea, and what he ate and drank-
she said that she had no objection to that, if I paid for it- she saw him
about two or three times she knew the times that I told her he was there,
but not other times my fellow seryant slept with my mistress, and slept
up stairs-after I had been there about a year, my mistress did not give
me a 5l. note to pay the taxes, nor did I use it to pay a bill.I deny that : I
did not do it-I never went to her and asked her to forgive me, for so doing,
por did she say that she would she never deducted the amount from my
wages--I had never taken it—the first time I robbed her, was when I took
the jewellery--that was nearly thirteen months before I took the notes, I
should think-I knew Mr. Wyeherley about fourteen months before I took
the notes-I do not know at all when the tea meeting was--I do
not know the month-up to this time, I have known him seventeen
months altogether--I pawned the gold chains and diamond ring, in
the Walworth-road, at Mr. Folkard's, and left the duplicate in Mrs.
Levy's drawers, before I went to Yarmouth-I had some time before
that told my mistress that I had a rich uncle---it might be two years from
the present time it was somewbere about the time that I went to the tes
meeting--she used to ask me where I had been to-I was in the habit of
going out very frequently, but more after I knew WyoherleyI used to go
to Chapel on Sunday- I went to one ball only during the time I lived with
Mrs. Levy; I will swear it was not more than one I did not enamel
myself; I used a little powder, that is all-I tried to look as well as the
rest, as well as I could changed one note at Mr. Hunt's, a draper, at
Stockwell; another at Mr. Simmons's, a chemist, in South Lambeth; and
another at Mr. Clement's, & brker, in Clapham-road-I took a 101. uote to
Yarmouth, not a bl. note; the rest was in gold—I did not change a 52.
note at a linen-draper's shop at Yarmouth; I will swear I did not take one
-I will swear I did not take this note (produced) to Yarmouth-I see
on it, “ Per post, East of England Bank, Yarmouth," and I swear I never
changed it at Yarmouth; it was a 101. note that I changed there. this is
Mr. Wycberley's writing at the corner of this note 73,091-I never wrote

Mrs. Newton- I did not give Wyoherley & second bl. note to go and change
for me at Morris and Kelsey's, 248, Regent-street-I did not go out with
the prisoner on that Thursday evening; he went to his preaching - I did not
