I have a handkerchief which was taken in by a person belonging to Mr. Reynolds, the pawnbroker, but he is ill.
 JOSEPH JOHN HARRAD re-examined. Q. What size was the chest of drawers? A. About a foot high. There were two bird cages and a sieve of currants stolen - I do not think they would all go in one bag, or that one man could have carried them.
 ROBINSON'S Defence. A young man was taken in company with me, who was. I believe, a nephew of the prosecutor - he was suspected of robbing his uncle; after the second examination they had him out privately, to see what they could make of him, and promised him his liberty. The prosecutor told him he was his uncle, and if he told where the property was he would not give charge of him; we were then remanded, and the young man's mother went to the grandfather, and they made up among their friends 21 l., and gave it to the prosecutor, not to say any thing against the young man. When we went up again Lady Minto, as she is generally called, came up half tipsy, and said she knew I was the person, because I was so very much pock-marked - the Magistrate asked if there was any charge against the young man - they said, No, and he was discharged.
 JOSEPH JOHN HARRAD re-examined. Q. Was there any other person taken up? A. Yes: a young man named Goat; my sister married his father's brother - he was taken up on suspicion of robbing me, but I do not know where he is now; I have seen him since - he passed the door on the Saturday following; there was no such thing as any money made up. I do not know who took him up.
 COLDECOTT'S Defence. I keep a lodging-house, and used to buy bones and rags, and who comes in or out I do not know. The chest of drawers was brought in for 2 s. - 8 d. of the money was for an old man's bed, and 1 s. 4 d. I let them have.
 Two witnesses gave Robinson a good character.
 ROBINSON -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 43.
 COLDECOTT -    NOT GUILTY.
 1520.  ELIZABETH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of July , at St. George , 11 lbs. of Cantharides, value 5 l., the goods of Samuel Foulger , in his dwelling-house.
 SAMUEL FOULGER. I am a  chemist, and live in Ratcliffe-highway. The prisoner used sometimes to come to my house, as an assistant to my servants. On the morning of Saturday, the 23d of July, I got up about six o'clock, for the purpose of watching what was done; I did not know whether the prisoner was in my house that morning - my family were out of town; I stopped on the landing-place, and saw the prisoner come out of the parlour, through the counting-house, with some mats in her hand, which she put down, and took some parcels off the cask of cantharides, which I had placed on it, and among the rest, a 14 lb. paper parcel - she then went back into the parlour, and came out again, with a bag, which she filled with Cantharides; she took it into the counting-house, and placed it under my desk; I went up to my son, and told him to get up, and go and tell Jackson, the officer, to watch the house, lest she should leave it. I then went into the shop and staid there till eight o'clock, and then left my son there while I went up to dress.
   SAMUEL FOULGER, JUN. I am the prosecutor's son. On the morning of the 23d of July my father desired me to watch the prisoner, and to follow her if she went out - she went away - I followed her, and found a bag under her clothes - I then went for the officer.
   THOMAS TRIPP ALLEN. On the 23d of July the last witness came for me, and gave me charge of the prisoner and the bag, which I delivered to the watch-house-keeper.
   FRANCIS JACKSON. I am watch-house-keeper. The prisoner was brought to me at the watch-house, with this bag.
 MR. FOULGER. It is mine - there are 11 lbs. - it cost 9 s. per pound.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have been many years in my prosecutor's family, and never touched any thing before, but distress induced me to do it. I throw myself on the mercy of the prosecutor and the Court.
 SAMUEL FOULGER re-examined. She has been many years in my family, and I believe always conducted herself well. GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 38.
 Strongly recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor.
 The prisoner pleaded pregnancy in stay of the execution of her sentence; a Jury of Matrons being impannelled, decided in the affirmative.
