WILLIAM CUMMING (police-sergeant D 3.) I took the prisoner into
custody on the 1st of March, and saw this coat fitted on him-it fitted
exactly.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you a good judge of the fit of coats ? A.
I can form an idea-it fitted him as far as I could see I did not observe
whether it was tight or loose-I considered it a good fit-it was not but-
toned-I should say it seemed to have been made for him-it is a frock
coat - I went to Mrs. Robins's-I believe it was before she was examined
at the office-I have a doubt of it-I believe it was after she went to the
office-I went there to tell here what time to be there the following day-I do
not think she was examined more than once-she came to the office, and
the prisoner was remanded on one charge, to be brought up on another,
and she was not examined I went to her before she was examined-she
had been told the prisoner was in custody before that I sent a man for
the clothes-the Magistrate did not tell me to be quiet, and not prompt
the witnesses, nor any thing of the kind-nor to go away from any wit-
ness- I took the prisoner in Great Marylebone-street.
Mr. Doane. Q. Walking the street? 4. No, in a public-house-I
was called in to take him.
MR. BALLANTINE called-
CATHERINE STEWART. I am single, and have lived with the prisoner
for the last nine months, at No. 7, Jeffries'-buildings, Westminster, as
his wife. I remember the night of Shrove Tuesday-he was at home with
me that very night reading the newspaper--we went to bed about half-
past eleven o'clock that night-he went to bed at that time-he had been
at home the whole evening-I got up about nine in the morning-he got
up after me--I left him in bed-he never slept away from me one night
from the 18th of January till he was apprehended-he was in good health,
on the 14th, and before that, and I was quite free from any disease.
