On the night of the 27th of February, about twelve o'clock, I was leaving a house in Ewer-street; I stood at the door and saw a female talking to the prosecutor in a very improper manner, because he would not go up the street with her—the prosecutor walked on towards the house, and the female stopped him against the door and began talking to him again—I heard her say some thing very improper—he said no, he would be d—d if he would—he was going from her—she said something about she would make somebody—I saw three young men standing at the corner of a court or gateway, and heard one of them say "She cannot get him in;" and when the prose cutor was leaving her, the three ran up the street—I saw one of them strike him—I called to my husband, "Come, here is a man will be killed"—we went up the street; the prosecutor and the prisoner were fighting as it were, and the other two struck him a blow—my husband tried to get the prisoner away—I hallooed "Police;" and one of them came up to me and said, "If you do not hold your tongue calling Police, I will kill you"—I ran to the top of the street, and called "Police" again; the police came down—I said several times that the prosecutor's hat was taken into the house, she was endeavouring to get him in too, calling watch; and it was at last thrown out of the door or window—he kept hallooing out, "My watch, my watch, mind my watch"—the female and the other two men ran away when the policeman ran down the street.
   Prisoner.  She did not come to the station that night. Witness.  I was there, but was so ill; the policeman said what I had to say I could say to-morrow.
   Q.  Did not he have me in the gutter? A.  You both fell—I never saw either of you before—it happened near Ewer-street, which is in Union street. ROBERT BRANFORD( police-constable M  101.) On the 27th of Feb ruary, between twelve and one o'clock, I heard the cry of "Police" in Ewer-street; I went up, and saw the prisoner holding the prosecutor's coat, and both struggling together—the prisoner was beating the prosecutor with his fist—there was several others about him—as soon as I made my appear ance they ran away—I knew the men who were about the prisoner by sight.
   Prisoner.  I was down in the gutter when he came up, and was kicked in my rib. Witness.  They were both standing up when I collared the prisoner—he resisted very much; if he fell it was before I came up. GUILTY.* Aged 20.— Confined Three Years.
