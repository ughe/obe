The articles sold in Mr. Adams's shop are many of them of very small value, such as memoranda-books, steel pens, a quire of paper, &c.; for such trifling articles many customers come in, in the day; sometimes several are in the shop together. The goods are bought, the customer pays, and perhaps wants change, and has it. For strict regularity, and to prevent the possibility of mistake, the entry of each sale should be made on the instant; if, however, a shopman were to go to the book to enter a sale to one customer, while another, or perhaps two or three others, were waiting in the shop to be served, they would become displeased at the delay, and leave the shop without purchasing at all; the consequence is, that it frequently happens that several different small articles are sold to different persons, and no entry made till the shop is clear; it then becomes difficult to recollect what article was bought by one person, and what by another; the money in the till and the memory are the only means by which the entries can be made. I had, on the morning in question, sold several different articles to a variety of people; I could not precisely recollect the articles sold to each individual, or the money paid for each kind of article; and I entered, to the best of my memory, the correct sums against particular parcels of goods, and I found the amount in the till and that in the book balance; and although I may not, and fear, as the evidence stands, have not made correct entries of the articles sold, and prices paid, I earnestly hope you will believe me when I say, that the gross amount of money received is correct, and not one farthing more was paid me than is put down. If I had intended to have embezzled my master's money, should I have placed it in the till? It is more reasonable to suppose that I should have put it in my pocket, and in my pocket the marked money would have been all found; but, gentlemen, only one shilling of that money was found on me, the whole of the rest was found in the till. If the sum which I am charged with embezzling had been found upon me, it would have operated against me; my own money was found by the officer, and amongst it one of the marked shillings, and that I can account for. I had two sixpences in my pocket. They are convenient for change, and I put them into the till, taking in lieu a shilling out.
 Mr. Smith, Mr. Moginie, Mr. Rouse, Mr. Kennett, Mrs. Matthew, and Mrs. Bertenshaw, gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 21. Recommended to mercy by the Jury on account of his character. - Confined for Three Months.

 Two other counts for uttering and putting off the same, with a like intent.
   EDWARD BENSLEY GARDNER. I am assistant to my father, Edward Gardner- he is a bookseller in Paternoster-row- on the 24th of December the prisoner sent a porter to us with this order, which is signed "E. Dalton, for J. Hatchard," who is a customer of ours; and I let him have the goods stated.
   JAMES LOFTUS. On the 24th of December the prisoner, who was a stranger to me, gave me the order, and I took it to Mr. Gardner, and they gave me the books - the prisoner said he would wait for me at the corner of Ivy-lane - I took the books to him there, and he told me to carry them on a little farther - he took me on to the Bell and Crown, and then took them of me; and he carried them to the first gate in Furnival's Inn - he then paid me one shilling and twopence - I am sure he is the man.
   EDWARD DALTON. I am in the service of Mr. Hatchard - I did not write this order, nor employ the prisoner to take it - I know his face - he used to live at Mr. Tilt's.(Order read.)
 "Piccadilly, December 24, 1833.

 273.  THOMAS COFFEY was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of December , 1 handkerchief, value 3s. 6d., the goods of William Edward Few , from his person.
   WILLIAM EDWARD FEW. I am clerk in a counting-house- on the 18th of December, about half-past five o'clock, I was in Fleet-street, and felt something at my pocket - I looked behind me, and saw the prisoner pulling out my pocket-handkerchief - he ran away - I pursued him - he was stopped in my sight.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Were there many persons there? A. Yes; it was opposite St. Dunstan's Church - when I turned, the prisoner was at a distance from me pulling the handkerchief - I perhaps might have caught the handkerchief, but I did not - the prisoner turned the corner to get into Chancery-lane - I did not observe any one running but the prisoner - I got into the road - he ran faster than I did - he had no handkerchief when he was taken - I saw my handkerchief at the station-house.
   JOSEPH REEVE. I was coming down Chancery-lane, and heard the cry of "Stop thief" - I picked up this handkerchief at a short distance from the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was running home when I was stopped.
 Daniel Hogan and Roger Matthew gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. - Confined for Three Months.
