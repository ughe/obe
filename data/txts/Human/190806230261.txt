I have no doubt I said at the Inquiry that I supported him partly because he was the son of Mr. Barker; I should think that probably would be so. It is very likely that the Guardians spent £17, 000 less last year than they did the year before, considering the large amount of work that had been done in the earlier year. I do not know what the estimate for this year is. I have not seen it. It is impossible that the rates can have been reduced by 1s. 1d. With regard to the governess car and cob, I should say there is nothing special about it. The first suggestion as to the sale of it came through Calcutt and not from me, and also the suggestion of an exchange for my big horse. If I said differently at the Inquiry it was wrong. I am on oath now, and I swear that the account I have given to-day is the right one, that Calcutt told me he had taken it for a debt. I did notice at the time that he had his monogram on the harness, but that is very easily put on. The harness was not new, but it was in very good condition. Calcutt did not say to me, "I would not sell it to anybody else but you for £50." I consider that I gave the full value for it. I daresay the cob now is worth £25 or £30; it has considerably improved since I had it, I think I said at the Inquiry it was worth £30. When I had it it was not worth £15. It is about seven or eight years old now. I was asked in Sep tember, 1907, whether I would, sell the lot for £50, and I said, "No." I think I paid £10 a little while ago to have it repaired. The value of the trap in September, 1907, would be about £20, and the harness £6 or £7.
 (Friday, July 31) ROWLAND HIRST(prisoner on oath), recalled. Further cross-examined I now produce the demand notes as to the reduction of rates. There is no reduction this quarter. I used to attend to this matter, but my memory is gone from me now. I was a supporter of Barker's contract for the Nurses' Homes, of December 22, 1904. He did not give a champagne dinner at my house that night. On January 12, 1905, I knew of some disturbance about the matter, and that young Barker offered to withdraw. I had not heard from old Barker anything about the Guardians deriving some benefit from that contract. I think I have some recollection of moving on February 9, 1905, the acceptance of the tender. I forget all about it now. On December 22 W. A. Barker got the contract. The next day a memorial was sent in to the Local Government Board. I was in favour of the building of the Nurses' Homes by anyone. I was chair man of the Infirmary Committee for years, and it was constantly before the Committee—the advisability of making suitable accommodation for the nurses. It did not matter to me who built it. I believe it was knocked on the head altogether; it has not been built up to now. I fancy I have some recollection of a reply of Barker's to Mr. Draper at the discussion. "It is the fact that all the plant upon the job belongs to me, and has my name and address upon it.
