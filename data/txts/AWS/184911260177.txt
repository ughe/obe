173. JOHN WILLIAMS, unlawfully breaking and entering the dwelling-
WILLIAM CoRY. I live at 11, Commercialyroad, Lambeth At half-past
seven o'clock in the evening of 28th Oet., r heard my front-door open-1
was sitting in my parlour, with the passage-door open-I went into the pas-
sage, and saw the prisonet, who had advanced two or three paces into the
passage--he asked me if a man named Williams lived there--I did not
answer him, but walked up and seized him by the wrist-he had put the door
to, but not shut it-I wheeled him round and called the police, and gave him
into custody--1 distinctly heard the door opened with a Bramah key-the
prisoner was searched the key which opened the door was in his hand, and
seven or eight other picklock keys were found on him.
Cross-examined by MR. PArNE. Q. Where were you A. In my par-
lour; the door which leads into the passage was wide open--I cannot recol-
lect whether I had shut the street-door myself-my wife and servants had
gone out to Church, about twenty minutes past six o'clock.
WILLIAM WEsToN (policeman, L 65). On 28th Oet. I was called, and
took the prisoner-I asked him for the key that opened the door: he gave
me this key, which opened it-I found on him seven other keys, two of which
are skeleton keys.
Cross-eramined. Q: Did he not say he found the door open A. Yes
