GEORGE HALL HALL. In the beginning of 1880 I was introduced to
Wood by Mr. Couthorpe with a view of negotiating a loan on my annuity
of 1001. a year with the Life Association of Scotland—the negotiations
were delayed some months by the difficulty of getting the documents
from the trustee; but eventually the papers were sent up to London, and
in August I was present at the payment at the solicitor's office of 6001.-the
second loan effected was 300l. on 10th or 11th April—there was a small inter-
mediate loan of 501.--the loan was 9001. altogether-inquiries were made, I
was told, as to the respectability of Mr. Couthorpe, and I was told that they
were satisfactory—there was no misrepresentation on Wood's part.
The prisoners each addressed the Jury at considerable longth, commenting
upon the ovidence, and entering into explanations as to the part which each of
them took in the different transactions, and contending that the evidence did not
show a conspiracy between them. (Loban and Wood received good characters.)
LOBAN-GUILTY-Eighteen Months' Hard Labour. WOOD.-GUILTY
on the first five cotints.-Troelve Months' Hard Labour. NOBLE.-GUILTY ON
the first five counts. Recommended to meroy by the Jury on account of his acting
as a servant-Four Months' Imprisonment.
121. ROBERT FREEMAN (21), FREDERICK WEBSTER (25),
and HENRY NATHAN (25), Stealing two horses, a van, 30 barrels of
grapes, and other articles, of John Alexander.
MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS Proseouted; MR. LEYEY appeared for Freeman, MR.
ROMIEU for Webster, and Mr. Fritt for Nathan.
JOHN ALEXANDER. I am a fruiterer, of 288, Edgware Road-on 31st
October I purchased of Keeling and Harvey 30 barrels of grapes branded
with a lion rampant No. 1-I saw one at the police-court marked in the
same way, and the grapes in it were the same kind as I purchased-the
delivery-note shows the brand-Fagg is my carman.
JAMES FAGG. I am carman to Mr. Alexander-on a Friday, I don't
know the date, I took my van and horses to Keeling and Hunt's ware-
house, Pudding Lane, with a delivery note from my master, and received
80 barrels of grapes marked with a lion rampant and seven cases of
royal apples-I covered them with a tarpaulin, fastened it down, and
went into a coffee shop in Botolph Lane I was away & quarter of an
hour, and when I came back the van was gone I next saw it in the
stable on the Saturday morning, but the contents had gone, except the
apples--I have seen one of the barrels since- the young chap who helped
me went into tea with me.
FREDERICK RABY. I am warehouseman to Keeling and Son-on 18th
November I delivered 30 barrels of grapes marked with a lion rampant
to Mr. Alexander's carman-I have since identified one of them.
Cross-examined by Mr. LEVEY. The grapes come packed in barrels
-we get thousands with the same mark, and sell them to all the
fruiterers in London-all I can say is that the barrel I saw had a simi-
lar mark.
