 758.  WILLIAM HALL was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of February , 1 yard of toilinett, value 3s., the goods of James Hollywell.
   WILLIAM EDWARDS. I am a constable, and live in Eight Feathers-court - the prosecutor's shop is in Holywell-street. On the 22d of February, at eight o'clock at night, I saw the prisoner and another person there; I passed them, and heard them say, "It is all right, go it;" I watched: they waited some time, and crossed - I saw the prisoner go in at the door, and take this piece of toilinett; I crossed over, and seized him with it - he struck me a violent blow; my hat and his own fell off - he ran; I pursued him into Angel-inn-yard, and lost sight of him there, as he got under the coaches; I had his hat - he asked me for it when he was taken, and I gave it to him.
   JAMES HOLLYWELL. I keep this shop - this toilinett is mine.
   JAMES STACK. I was standing at my shop door, and saw Edwards seize the prisoner; I am certain he is the man.
 The prisoner pleaded intoxication, and ignorance of the offence.
   WILLIAM EDWARDS. He was sober; he could run as fast as me.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 759.  ANN EAST was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of February , 1 coat, value 20s., and 6 yards of printed cotton, value 5s. the goods of Francis Tracey.
   MARTHA TRACEY. I am the wife of Francis Tracey; we live in King's-road, Chelsea. I take in washing - the prisoner lodged with us, with a man who took the room. I missed this property on the 14th of February, between ten and eleven o'clock; there was nobody in the house but her - my husband told them to leave next day, and they did so - I have not found my things; I was in my kitchen very ill, and saw the prisoner go out with something brown in her apron, between ten and eleven o'clock; I thought it was like my husband's coat - I found that the key of her room opened my parlour - nobody else was in the house but an independant old gentleman.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. She left because you gave her warning? A. Yes. I missed the coat at seven o'clock in the evening - I did not suspect it was gone, as I had the key - it was half under her apron; my daughter was in the kitchen with me, and said it looked like her father's coat - my husband missed it at one o'clock, but said nothing to me about it - I cannot say how many people came to the house that day - I told the prisoner of it at night; she said she had not been out all day.
   MARTHA TRACEY. I am ten years old. I saw the prisoner go out on Thursday with a bundle under her apron - I saw something like my father's coat - our parlour door is kept locked, and the street door is kept shut.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where were you? A. In the kitchen with my mother; I did not tell her of it, for she saw it herself. My father came home between two and three o'clock - he went to lay down, and the cotton and coat were missed.
 COURT. Q. Did you then say you thought you saw her take it out? A. No.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was not out all day.
     NOT GUILTY.

 WILLIAM WALDEGRAVE PACKER. I am an  undertaker, and live in Whitechapel-road. On the 12th of December, I had a funeral of Mr. James Bates.
   HANNAH PACKER. I am the prosecutor's wife. This funeral was performed on the 12th of December, about four o'clock in the afternoon - the corpse laid in our shop, and was to be buried about a quarter of a mile from our house; the prisoner came the day after the corpse came - he merely looked at it - appeared to shed tears over it, and he agreed with a man named Bone, who came with him, as to the orders he had given for the interment - Bone has not been seen since; Bone ordered ten cloaks in the prisoner's presence - he said thirty persons would come, but only ten would follow as mourners, and my husband was to prepare the cloaks; the prisoner came again next day, but only stood on the step, while the other man talked to me a little. On the day of the funeral, it had gone on with only two mourners - the prisoner and Bone came soon after, and said, "We have met your husband and the corpse, on the other side of the way, and he desires you will tie two cloaks on our shoulders; but we won't wait for hatbands; and he will wait in the street for us."
