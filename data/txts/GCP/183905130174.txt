NATHAN SMITH re-examined. I know the lad he names-I never saw
him at that house-I had lived there about three months, I have no
doubt that lad was along with the prisoner-I had met him one day
about three weeks previous to the robbery, and have not seen him
since I had employed him when I kept a broker's shop in Bermondsey-
street, but heard he was a bad character and parted with him-I know
nothing of the prisoner.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Confined One Year.
Before Mr. Recorder.
1705. JAMES CLARKE and RICHARD JACKSON were indicted
for stealing, on the 16th of May, 1 purse, value 1d.; 2 sovereigns, 4
half-crowns, 10 shillings, and 44 pence in copper, the goods and monies
of Sarah Emery, from her person.
M R. PAYNE conducted the prosecution.
SARAH EMERY. I live in Wellington-street, Newington-causeway. On
Wednesday last I went to Mitcham to see a friend it was the day of
Dorking-races--I left Mitcham between eleven and twelve o'clock at night,
and was the worse for liquor- I went into an inn at Mitcham-I had three
sovereigns in my purse when I went into the house-I had received them
from my friend-- 1 could not find a coach at the inn-I waited there about
a quarter of an hour, and had a glass of brandy-and-water-I changed a
sovereign to pay for it, and received 198. in change, which consisted of
half-crowns and shillings-I put them into the other end of my purse-I
also had one shilling in my pocket, and 4fd. in copper-not finding a coach,
walked on towards Tooting, and between Mitcham and Tooting I heard a
glass coach come by-I then took my purse out of my pocket and put it into
my stocking-when the coach came up I asked the man if he could convey
me to London-he said yes-it was the prisoner Clarke he got down and
let me inside the coach-there was somebody rather shorter than himself
on the box, who I cannot recollect- I found a little girl and boy inside-
the little girl was very anxious indeed to get home-after getting a little
way the horse would not go on-wegot out and walked a little way, as Clarke
asked me--I walked a very little way, being very ill and very sick, I was
obliged to get in again, and the children got in again soon after-soon after
a man came and took the children away--they said they must have some other
conveyance to get them home-1 do not know where this was they left me
in the coach, and I went to sleep-I was awoke by Clarke asking me if I
was cold- I said, yes, I was very cold-he covered me over with a great
coat-I do not think he stopped long inside, but I slept so soundly I do
not know-I went to sleep again directly -- I cannot say when I awoke again,
but I was sick and vomiting, and be opened the coach-door-at Vauxhall-
bridge he came to the door, and told me I must get out, which I did, and
