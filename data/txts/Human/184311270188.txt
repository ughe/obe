EDWARD COCK, surgeon, St. Thomas-street, Southwark.  On the night of the 24th of Oct. I was sent for to Guy's-hospital, where I am assistant-sur geon, and saw a child who was brought there—the woman who brought it said it was her child—I examined, and found its head very much swollen and mis-shapen—a great swelling on the right side, produced by extravasation of blood—I had no doubt the brain was injured, and that there was a fracture of the skull—it was alive then—I did all I could for it, and left it in care of the dresser and sister of the ward—I hardly expected it would live more than two hours—I saw it dead next morning—I attributed its death to the injury on the head—I made a post mortem examination, which entirely confirmed my opinion.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you find a fracture on both sides of the head? A.  Both.
   COURT . Q.  Could that both be produced by one blow? A.  I think so—it certainly would be a violent blow—it might be produced by any collision with a hard substance.
   JURY . Q.  Could it have happened if the child had fallen from a man's arms accidentally? A.  I think such an injury might have been inflicted by its falling directly on its head on the ground, from a certain height—if it fell directly on the vertex of its head, four or five feet, on a stone or hard floor, it is possible such a fracture might have been produced. GEORGE HAVILL re-examined.  The front of the bar is about three feet six inches from the ground.
   MR. WILKINS . Q.  Was Donovan by when these observations were made by the prisoner? A.  He stood by his side when he said he should be hung at the Old Bailey—they were all standing about.
   MR. COCK re-examined. Q.  Suppose it had fallen from a man's arms on to a place three feet six inches from the ground, on a substance harder than lead, do you think that would produce the fracture? A.  I cannot swear it would not, but consider it improbable.
   MR. WILKINS  to DANIEL DONOVAN . Q.  You was present immediately after the child's head had been on the metal; did the prisoner say he had said he would do it, he was always as good as his word, that he should be hung for it at the Old Bailey, and hoped every one would come to see him? A.  No, I did not hear any such words—he said, "It is done, now take me, "—he went into the tap-room, and laid his head on the table—I saw no more of the proceeding.
   JURY . Q.  But you was not at the bar at all? A.  No—I was not at the bar. GEORGE STEAD re-examined.  I did not see the child fall on the ground—I never saw it on the ground—I took it up before it got on the ground, after it had been on the counter—Spelling caught it and took it away, either by its legs or clothes—I never saw it drop from the counter on the ground, on its head.
