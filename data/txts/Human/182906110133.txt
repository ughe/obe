   WILLIAM CRAIG. I am a Bow-street patrol. I took this coat from the prisoner at a quarter before twelve o'clock at night, on the 30th of May; he was running from the carriage, and about three yards from it - I did not see him take it.
 Prisoner. Q.Where did you first see me? A. At the corner of Russell-street, with four or five others - I saw a man get on the wheel, take it, and give it to you; this is the coat - I called to stop the other man.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was returning home - there were seven or eight persons behind a carriage, and in a moment this coat was thrown upon my shoulder; the gentleman took me, and ran after another person, leaving me with the officer.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 1304.  JOHN CHARLESWORTH was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of April , 1 bell, value 4s., the goods of John Voyce.
 JOHN VOYCE. I lost a dustman's bell, on the 28th of April, from  Gun-square, Houndsditch; I left it on the stop of a door, while I took out some dust - I afterwards saw the prisoner (whom I had seen round the cart at the time I lost it) with it, tied up in a handkerchief.
 Prisoner. Q. Do you recollect any conversation between you and I? A. Yes; you came and asked me to have a glass of gin - you did not ask if I had lost my bell - I said I had lost it; you asked me what the value of it was, and I said 6s. or 7s.; you did not state that you lived in that square, not ask when I should be there again - I left you in the wine-vaults; my fellow-servants took the cart away.
 CHARLES CONSTABLE. I took the prisoner and the bell; he told me it was merely a joke.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been out and saw the bell at the door; when I came in I took it up stairs, merely as a joke - I put it on a stool, and went down to the yard till my landlord and landlady came in: she said you have been at some of your larks - I said "I will wash myself, you look out at the window, and see that the man don't go without the bell;" I said I would put a night-cap on it, and put this paper over it - I then went down and said to the prosecutor, "Have you lost any thing?" he said, "Yes, a bell of my master's, and I must make it good - it will cost 5s. or 6s.;" I said "I live at No. 13, when shall you be here again?" he said in a day or two - I said, "Don't be surprised if you find your bell again before night; will you have a drop of gin?" he said, "Yes, and thank you too;" he said no more about the bell, but went away - I went home and said, "I don't like this bell, it begins to be troublesome to me - I know where he shoots his carts, and I will go with it;" I followed him to White Lion-street, where I saw him with five or six more dustmen; one of them said, "He has got a bell;" I said Yes, and I untied it and gave it him - one of them said, "Why don't you give him in charge?"
 RICHARD PAPWORTH. The prisoner lodges with me in Gun-square; he came in with the bell under his arm, and said he was going to have a bit of fun with it - I wanted him to give it the man; he said, "Stop a bit, I will give it him presently;" he went down to the witness, but did not give it him then - he then took it from my house to give it him, I believe; he asked me to go with him.
 MRS. PAPWORTH. I heard the prisoner say he was going to play a lark - I said, "Where the goodness did you get that bell? go and give it the man;" he said, "I mean to give it him;" he went - the prosecutor and he had' some gin.    NOT GUILTY.
 1305.  WILLIAM CREAN was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of George Guyenetto , from his person.
 GEORGE GUVENETTO. On the 10th of June, I was coming down  Sydney's-alley, Mr. Carter, a friend of mine, said,"There is a fellow picked your pocket;" I turned, saw the prisoner and several others near me, and my handkerchief on the ground.
   ROBERT CARTER. I saw the prisoner throw the handkerchief on the ground; it was about ten o'clock at night- he ran, and the watchman took him; I had been drinking, but I was sober - I am not quite sure he had it in his hand.   NOT GUILTY.
 1306.  JOHN DEMPSEY was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June , 6 boards, value 18s., the goods of William Brown.
 WILLIAM BROWN. I am a  carpenter. I saw the prisoner on my premises in Warwick-place, Whitecross-street, at five o'clock, and saw him take from my shed some deal boards.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you recollect the prisoner? A. He had asked me for work some time before; he had not contracted with me about these timbers.
 MARGARET HOWARD. I live opposite the prosecutor's premises. On the morning of the 6th of June, I saw the prisoner get over a partition, put over three boards, and then put them on his shoulder; in a few minutes he came, and took three more boards - he was taken on the 10th of June.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the prosecutor for work- he said he could not employ me till that day week, as he had no materials ready; I went to a public-house, where Mr. Brown was, and begged him to be sent for - he came from the parlour, and said he was going to put most of his work out of doors, as he believed he had a parcel of thieves about him; he was a little elevated, and I left him - I came down to the place that morning, and saw the materials; I said to myself, "I will get about these doors," which he said he would let out to me - I took these boards, which are the regular thickness, for common pannel-doors, and I said "I will make them on my own bench, and let him allow me what he likes;" there were two officers, whom I knew, in the street, and I should have been mad to take them if I intended to steal them.
 MR. BROWN. I did not employ him to do any work; he said he was going to make some doors - I said it was not proper stuff for doors - I do not recollect seeing him at any public-house, but I might at the Black Boy publichouse, where I usually breakfast and dine.
 The prisoner received an excellent character. GUILTY. Aged 45.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
