WILLIAM THOMAS CHITTENDEN. I am a constable of Woolwich. On the 17th of November I went with Mr. Davis to the Earl of Chatham public-house, and was shown a room there—I found a bundle containing two shirts—the prisoner was sent for to—I opened the bundle, and took out the shirts—the prisoner produced the key of another box in the room—I found there another shirt, and detained him—I found on him a duplicate for a jacket and breeches. THOMAS PACY BIRT. I am a pawnbroker, in Hare-street, Woolwich. I produce a jacket and breeches—I do not know who they were pawned by, but I gave the party this duplicate—this note was left with the things—I did not know the prisoner was constantly in the prosecutor's employ—I knew he was a tailor.
 (  Note read .)—"Sir, please to lend on this fisher boy's suit 6 s . " SAMUEL BROOKS ." LEWIS DAVIS re-examined.  These articles are mine—they are not made for sale, but for the Navy.
   Prisoner's Defence . I took them home to get a stain out of the one found in my box—the others I took home to get altered, which it was my duty to do—I had five suits of the clothes, and did pledge them, as I wanted money—I put the shirts on a chair when I took them home—the one stained was left with my wife to get the stain out, and I placed it in my box—the other two remained loose in the room for fire days—I did not secrete them—I was going to give them to a person to alter. LEWIS DAVIS re-examined.  These are navy shirts—it was the prisoner's duty to receive them when made, and send a cheque round for payment——those which are not made right he was empowered to get altered—not to take to his private lodging, but to give them to work-people who came to the shop—his wife worked for us. GUILTY of stealing the jacket and breeches.—Aged 50. Confined Three Months.

I am the son of James Gilham, a shoemaker in Nelson-street, Greenwich. On the 1st of November, while I was attend ing to a customer, I received information which induced me to go towards the hospital—I overtook the prisoner, and asked what she had in her basket—she made no reply—I saw through the top of the basket two pain of pattens—I told her she had stolen them from the door—she said she had bought them of a man in the market-place—they were my pattens, and had hung by the side of the doorway outside—I had seen them five minutes before. MART POWELL. I am servant to Mr. Gilham. I was in the kitchen on the 1st of November, and saw the prisoner take the pattens off the hooks and put them into her basket—I told my master, he followed and took her.
