 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was an entire sheep found? A. I think almost the whole of it, but I cannot say, from the way it was cut - the whole sheep was not fitted to the skin, but a joint here and there; it certainly corresponded as I thought; Lovell lives about a mile and three-quarters from the field.
   WILLIAM HENRY HEWITT. I went with Webster and Cox to where we found Lovell - his evidence is correct; I observed all he has stated.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I am an officer. I apprehended Eyres on the Saturday morning - after Webster and Hewitt were gone, I waited in the front-room secondfloor of the house where Cox lodged in the back-room, and about a quarter-past nine o'clock I saw Eyres go to the door of Cox's room - he tried the door, but could not get in; I had the key in my pocket - I went out, and at the door found a female speaking to Eyres; I said to her, "Is this Cox," (meaning Eyres) - she said, "No, it is not, it is Smith;" Eyres was going away from the house towards Edgware-road; I walked by his side, and saw there was a great deal of wool on his waistcoat, and his shoes were all over fresh clay - I went up to him, and said, "Smith, are you out of work;" he said he was - I then said, "As you have been inquiring after Cox I shall expect you will go back with me to the house;" he said, "I may as well go back and sit with you as any body else" - we went into the front-room; I had some suspicion of him and searched him there, and in his right-hand jacket-pocket I found this knife, all covered with fresh blood, and very greasy, as if it had been cutting fat meat, and in his right-hand waistcoat-pocket I found this cord wet, and covered with wool; there was no blood on it - I then told him I should detain him on suspicion of being concerned with Cox; he made no reply - I did not tell him for what; I waited there about an hour or more, expecting Cox's husband would come in, but he did no:- she is a married woman; I sent to Paddington watch-house for a constable to stop in the room, while I took Eyres to the office - I was present on the Sunday morning, with Webster and another young man, at Hampstead, where the skin was found; I saw the meat applied to the skin and it tallied - there were holes in the skin, which corresponded with the meat.
   JAMES BEATTIE. I work for my father, who is a navigator, employed at Mr. Wright's, at Hampstead; we live about a quarter of a mile from this field. I was walking round Baker's field, where the sheep are kept, on Saturday, the 22d of November, about twelve o'clock in the morning, and saw a skin lying in the ditch; I went up to Mr. Baker and told him of it - I afterwards saw some mutton applied to the skin, and in my judgment it had come from that skin; I know the prisoners - I had not seen them about there; Eyres and Lovell worked for my father at Hampstead, but were not at work for a long time before this.
   JOSEPH TUTNER. I am a private watchman. About ten o'clock on the Friday night the two male prisoners went by me with another man - I knew their persons before; my station is on the Edgware-road - I do not know Baker's field; I was about two miles from Hampstead town - I wished them good night; one of them said, "I know you, old man;" I said, "Yes, and I know you."
 Cross-examined. Q. You were at least two miles from Hampstead-church? A. I cannot say.
   WILLIAM BAKER. I am a farmer, and owner of this field - it is about a mile and half from Tutner's beat. I went with Billen to Mary-le-bone-office on the Monday as the sheep was stolen out of my field - I saw the skin at the office, and some of the meat applied to it, but did not take sufficient notice to see if it fitted; Lovell had mowed for me for three or four years, and Eyres I have known twelve years.
 Cross-examined. Q. I suppose you had a good opinion of him? A. Yes. There was a good deal of meat at the office; I saw them apply part of the loins - it was on the Monday.
 Q. How many thousand sheep would this skin have fitted? Q. I cannot say: it was a common sized sheep.
   THOMAS KING. I am a butcher. I saw a skin, which Billen claimed, and saw some meat at the office on the Monday; I saw it applied to the skin - it was not cut as a butcher would cut it; I should think nearly the whole carcass was produced at the office - there were about twenty pieces, but it was not in joints; I saw one piece fitted - the head was taken off at the second joint, and the head left behind in the skin; I applied part of the neck to the head - it corresponded, and other parts, in my judgment, agreed with it; in my opinion, it was the meat that came from that skin; I have been a butcher from a child - my father was one.
 Cross-examined. Q. The necks of sheep are generally about a size? A. Yes.
 Q. It might have fitted hundreds of the same sized sheep? A. That is rather doubtful - it was cut in a particular manner; it appeared to have been killed within a day or two - it could not have been killed four or five days from the appearance of it; it is possible a man might be mistaken, but there are a great many people of the same opinion as myself - there are not many sheep of that size; it is a very large one - I will not swear there are not a thousand; it is possible to be mistaken as to the time it had been killed.
 COURT. Q.Did you ever see a sheep killed in that way before? A. No.
   SAMUEL RENSHAW. I am a butcher. I saw the sheepskin and the meat at the office on the Saturday; it did not appear to have been cut by a butcher - I saw it applied to the skin; I have been a butcher about three years, and in my judgment the meat fitted the skin; I saw the neck and loin compared - they corresponded with the marks in the skin, and, in my opinion, belonged to the same skin; I never saw a sheep slaughtered in that way before.
