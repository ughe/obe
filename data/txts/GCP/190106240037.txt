THE EARL OF KINGSTON. I am a member of the National Sporting
Club, and am very keen on boxing-I have seen contests and competitions
at the National Sporting Club-I know the conditions under which they
are carried out from what I have observed there, and I think they are
carried out with every possible care ; I have seen men box there six or
eight times--I was not there when the Brigade of Guards had their
boxing competition there; I know it was held there.
Cross-examined. By belonging to the club, members can see the con-
tests--I like watching them; that is why I joined—I think it is fairer
with the 10 seconds rule, and I like it better.
Not GUILTY.

THIRD COURT.-Friday, June 28th, 1901.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
480. WILLIAM JORDAN (31) and ALBERT WILLIAM WATSON
(27), Stealing a dress-basket and other articles, the property of Ella
Thornewill. Second Count : Feloniously receiving the same.
Mr. SIDNEY CLARKE, for the Prosecution, offered no evidence against
JORDAN PLEADED GUILTY to receiving. He received a good character.
Two months' hard labour.
**481. JAMES WILLIAM KNIGHT (33), Forging and uttering an
endorsement on an order for the payment of £5 98. 8d., with intent to
defraud.
MR. INNMAN Prosecuted, and Mr. FORDHAM Defended.
GEORGE WELLAND. I am a tin plate worker at Mare Street, Hackney
-4, West Street is my house-on May 3rd I was in the Abyssinia public
house with the prisoner and his nephew-I took this cheque from my
pocket and asked the beershop-keeper to change it, as I had not enough to
pay for the beer, ls. 4d -he refused, and I put it back with a lion
shilling and a Japanese coin--no writing was on the back—when I got
back to the shop, and my men were waiting for their sub-money, I found
I had no purse; it had been in my coat pocket--I had not given the
cheque to anybody, or authorised anyone to put my name on the back-
I had not bought a horse or a bicycle--the following morning, Saturday,
I saw the prisoner twice-no mention was made of a pony, nor of the
cheque-I had stopped it.
Cross-examined." I have known the prisoner for 15 or 18 years-I
bought a pony and van of him on November 2nd, 1897, for £10 10s.-I
did not try to get out of the bargain—I had had a glass, but was not
drunk-I had heen drinking with the prisoner since 11 a.m. in the Cat
and Mutton, the Lord Duncan, the Havelock and the Abyssinia—I did
not have much in either--I said at the Police-court that this man took
the lion shilling and seven Japanese coins—the purse was in my trousers
