Q. Did you say to him at the Southwark police-court, “I suppose I shall
hear again of what I said about your brother and Wright?". A. No, I do not

COURT. Q. Was it Moore's privilege to be charged 58. profit, taking the
larger profit himself? 4. Yes--the prisoner in this instance would have
rather a less profit.
Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Is there an account opened for Wicks in your ledger ?
4. Yes-my clerk opened it for his more immediate guidance this entry is
in Moore's writing in the ledger--the amount entered to Wicks on the 13th of
June is exactly the same as the prisoner has accounted for in the cash-book-
I had daily opportunities of seeing these entries.

Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. When the prisoner came into your employ, did he
claim the advantage of selling coals? 4. He did not-I should not have
allowed it-till I got to the Police-court I had no notion of any such
claim-he did not receive money from the customers generally, only from
the hucksters--I have no opportunity of knowing what customers he intro-
duced when Moore came to me I agreed to allow him this privilege, as he
had several connexions in the docks-the prisoner entered his disbursements
in a book I gave him into custody simply on discovering these defalcations
-when I had the conversation with Mr. Wright I had not made the dis-
covery--the entries in the ledger were made from his book--I have never
said that he was honest since I found out this the ledger is in Moore's
writing--if there was an error in the prisoner's book it would go through
the whole of the books.

three-he came into the counting-house and asked me to make out the bill
