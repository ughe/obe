 846. ELIZABETH JONES , stealing 1 watch, value 10 l  .; the goods of Robert Shipwright , from his person. ROBERT SHIPWRIGHT. On Tuesday night, 11th March, about half-past eleven o'clock, I went into the Mitre public-house—I saw the prisoner there—I had never seen her before—I am sure she is the person—there were two or three more girls with her—in consequence of something, I looked for my watch, and missed it, when I had been there about twenty minutes—I gave the prisoner into custody, in consequence of her being close to me at the time—this is my watch—I had looked at it when I went into the house.
   Crots-examined by MR. WOOLLETT . Q.  What are you? A.  A town-traveller; this public-house is in Chancery-lane—I was waiting there for an omnibus—I was at the bar—I suppose there might have been a dozen there, but not near us—I believe I had a glass of stout to drink, and a mall glass of brandy and water—I did not help the women to some; they took it without my leave; the prisoner took some; I cannot say whether the other women did—I did not look at my watch during the time I was in the house—I know I had it when I went in—I looked for it, in conse quence of my friend missing his. THOMAS LECHT( City policeman,  342). I was in Chancery-lane—Ship wright came out of the Mitre Tavern, and made a complaint—I went into the house, and saw the prisoner and other women—the prisoner put her arm out; I caught it, and took this watch out of her hand. GEORGE COOKMAN. I went into the Mitre Tavern with Shipwright and mother friend—we were there about twenty minutes when be missed hit watch—I know he had it when he went in—I saw him pull his guard out of his pocket, with the watch broken off—a policeman came, and I saw the prisoner put her arm out, as if to pass something to a female—the policeman took her arm, and found the watch. GUILTY.
