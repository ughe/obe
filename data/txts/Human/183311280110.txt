   ELIZABETH POLLARD. I am the wife of William Pollard - he is a newsman and an optician, in Rochester-row, Westminster. On Sunday, the 27th of October, at half-past six o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came to our house, and asked for a newspaper to read - I told him we did not lend papers to read, unless we had a deposit left; he laid down half-a-crown - I said, "I don't think this is a good one" - he said, "I wish I a had score of them" - I held the 2s. in my hand while I felt the half-crown - he took the 2s. very quickly out of my hand, and was going out, he dropped 1s., and asked for the light to get it; my boy went with the light, but the prisoner found the 1s. and ran off - my brother pursued and caught him.
 Prisoner. Did you not lay the 2s. on the table. Witness. No; you took them out of my hand, and went out as quick as you could; you dropped 1s. and picked it up by the light of the moon.
   GEORGE GLOVER. I am brother of this witness - I saw the prisoner come in - I received this half-crown from my sister - I followed the prisoner and caught him four or five hundred yards off, running as fast as he could - I gave him, and the half-crown to the officer.
   HENRY JAMES PITT(police-sergeant B 13). I took the prisoner, and have the half-crown.
   JOHN FIELD. These two half-crowns are counterfeits.
 Prisoner She did not mark the half-crown I gave her till it had been out of her possession; and when I came back, she said, she believed it was that; if it was bad why did she give me the change, which she laid on the counter.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Transported for Seven Years.
 148.  JOHN BURKE was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of November , 1 tripod, value 5l., the goods of Henry Nurse.
   HENRY NURSE. I am a carver and gilder, and live in Old Cavendish-street. This is my tripod - I did not miss it, till I was told it was taken on the 8th of November - I got some bills printed, and at three o'clock in the afternoon I saw it at Mr. Aldous - it is worth 5l., and had been on the step of my door - I had not seen it that day, but I saw it when we closed the shop the evening before.
   JAMES ALDOUS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Berwick-street, Soho. I have known the prisoner a long time - in the forenoon of the 8th of November, he brought this tripod to my house - I asked him who he brought it from; he said, from Mr. Newcombe, a respectable gentleman, and whom I have known some years; from whom he had brought things before - I asked him if he was quite sure; he said, "Yes," and he wanted 2l. on it, which I let him have; in the course of the day some bills were brought describing it as stolen, and I gave notice of it.
   EDWARD DAVIS(police-constable E 86). I apprehended the prisoner - I told him he was charged with stealing a tripod - I took him to the station-house, and he said, the next morning, that he had pawned it at Mr. Aldous for 2l.
 Prisoner's Defence (written). I am a porter, on the day mentioned in the indictment, I was at the corner of Rathbone-place, and was accosted by a man decently attired, who asked me to carry a parcel into Berwick-street, he went with me, when we arrived at the house of Mr. Aldons, the pawnbroker, he desired me to go in, and asked whether they would take it in as a pledge, saying, that it was for Mr. Newcombe. I went in, and asked the question, and was told that it would be taken in. I then went out, took the article from Mr. Newcombe, and pledged it for two sovereigns, I gave the duplicate, and the money to Mr. Newcombe, and receive 2s. for my work. I went away, and went to my brother's in Whitechapel, and received 19s. from my brother, for my mother (which he can prove); to pay a part of the funeral expenses of my father.
   ANN TUFT. I live opposite the prosecutor - I saw a man come to take this stand away, between eight and nine o'clock; I cannot swear to the man, but he was a short man similar to the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 149.  JOHN AXHAM was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of November , 1 watch, value 30s.; and 1 coat, value 2s., the goods of Joseph Axham.
   HANNAH AXHAM. I am mother-in-law to the prisoner, I have no children of my own - my husband is a smith, and lives in Phillip's-buildings, St. Pancras. On the 9th of November, my husband's watch was on the table in the bed-room, and on the Tuesday following I missed it - this is the watch.
   WILLIAM JOHN BIRD. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I have the watch, which I took in of an older lad than the prisoner for 10s.; in the name of Edward Hobbs, No. 10, Seymour-street.
   EDMUND COGNEY(police-constable S 169). I took the prisoner in the street - I asked what he had done with his father's coat and watch - he said, he and another lad had pawned the watch in Holborn; and the coat he had sold to a Jew in the street for 3d. - I found the watch - I did not tell him it would be better for him to confess.
   JOSEPH AXHAM. What my wife has stated is true; this is my watch, but my coat is lost - the prisoner was absent a week, and then came and took the coat while I was out.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
