WILLIAM HULME re-examined.  Messrs. Biddulph sent this bill to the Bank of England shortly after it was drawn—it would be in the Bank of England in September, 1837—we applied to Mr. James for it on the 20th of 21st of September, for the first time, it was due on the 17th of Sep tember—I am positive we did not apply before it became due—the prisoner has carried on a very large business as long as I have known him, and bore the character of an honest man. THOMAS GWYKNE. I am an attorney, living at Haverford-west In November, 1837, I went with Mr. James to Carmarthen prison, to see the prisoner—Mr. James said to him, "John, are you prepared to pay these crooked bills?"—the prisoner said he was not—Mr. James then said, "You must take the consequences"—allow me to explain that I was going to the gaol on my own business, to see Mr. Harris, and Mr. James asked me to accompany him—this occurred just as we left—a great deal passed before, but nothing more about the bills.
   MR. CLARKSON to MR. D. H. JAMES . Q.  Were the prisoner's goods sold in September? A.  There were two sales—I think the sale of his furni ture was about November—I do not think it was before I went to the prison, but I will not swear it—I swear it was not a month before I went to the prison—I cannot say whether the stock of the farm was sold in September—I do not think he was made a bankrupt till the spring of 1838—I do not know who struck the docket—I have not proved any debt under the commission—there was an application made for the balance of his goods in the hands of the Sheriff, but it was not made on my own account—I did it for him to come out of prison—I did not get it—I believe it was from 100 l . to 200 l .
