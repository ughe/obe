NEW COURT, (7th DAY.)
 Middlesex Cases, Third Jury, Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1505.  THOMAS DOWSE was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , at St. Andrew, Holborn , a coat, value 2 l. a waistcoat, value 5 s. a pair of trowsers, value 10 s. a handkerchief, value 4 s. and a shirt, value 4 s. the goods of Catharine Dowse , widow , in her dwelling-house.
   CATHARINE DOWSE. I live at No. 23, Cross-street, Hatton Garden, and am a widow. The prisoner is my son. On Tuesday, the 16th of August, about seven o'clock in the morning, I missed a coat, waistcoat, and trowsers, and a black silk handkerchief, belonging to another son of mine; they were safe the evening before. My cellar window was under repair; if any one had got in there, they might have got to the parlor, where these things were. The prisoner was brought in, and searched, in my presence. We found duplicates of the clothes, and 35 s. in money on him. He said nothing about the clothes; I got them back, and they are here now, The coat and waistcoat were in one place, and the trowsers in another.
   JOHN WHITE DENNING. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Great Surrey-street. These trowsers were pledged at my house on the 17th of August, in the name of John Good. I gave a duplicate, but do not know the person.
   JOHN LIMBRICK. I am an officer of Hatton Garden. I produce the duplicates.
   JOHN FLOWER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Shoe-lane. I produce a coat and waistcoat, which were pledged by a lad in court, (not the prisoner) on the 17th of August.
   EDWARD BILLETT. I pledged these articles at the last witness's shop. They were given to me by the prisoner.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   CATHERINE DOWSE, re-examined. I saw my son two days afterwards; he is employed in carrying milk for me.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 15.
 1506.  JAMES ARMSTRONG, was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , at St. George's, Hanover-square , two coach lamps, value 7 s.; two saddles, value 30 s.; three collars, value 30 s.; a pair of stirrup-irons, value 1 s.; a pair of pole-pieces, value 2 s.; a pair of collar braces, value 2 s.; a martingale, value 10 s.; a pair of pole braces, value 1 s.; a chaise pad, value 15 s.; a pair of traces value 10 s.; a pair of reins, value 5 s.; five bridles, value 3 l.; and a gig harness, value 50 s.; the property of David Peek , his master , in his dwelling house.
   DAVID PECK. I am a coach-maker, and live in Mount-street, Grosvenor-square, in the parish of St. George's, Hanover-square. The prisoner had been in my service as labourer for two or three years. On the 26th of August I enquired of him for some saddles and bridles; he said they were in the lumber-loft, he had put them there out of the way; I told him to go and look for them. I went up myself half an hour after, and forgetting what I had sent him for, I asked him what he did there. He said the things were not there, and he supposed they were down stairs. I came down, went out, and when I returned, I found he had gone out. He did not return. I searched the premises and missed the articles stated in the indictment; he had had them in his care. The gig harness is worth more than 40 s.; but it might have been taken separately, and the pieces would have been under that value.
 JONATHAN DOBSON. I got acquainted with the prisoner last May; he spoke to me about lamps, and he sent some by his little boy; I told him I would not buy of any children, and he came himself. I bought those articles of him, and hung them up in my shop, which is at No. 12, Francis-court, Wigmore-street.
   WILLIAM BALLARD. I did not apprehend the prisoner, but gave directions to a watchman, who apprehended him on the 25th of August.  I told him I had been looking after him for some time for stealing harness; he said he should wish to see his master. I went with him to his master's. I said,
 "Here is your master, will you tell him what you have done with the harness;" he then told me where the things were, and what he had got for them. Most of the articles are here: some were sold, and could not be got back again.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 GUILTY. Aged 46.
 Of stealing to the value of 39 s. only. Confined Six Months.
 1507.  MARY ANN COLLISON was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , three shirt bodies, value 18 s. and a pair of stays, value 3 s. the goods of Charlotte Hawthorn, widow.
 CHARLOTTE HAWTHORN. The prisoner slept at my house on the 8th of September, and had been there a week. I went out that day about two or three o'clock, and left her with my child; I returned in about a quarter of an hour; I searched my drawers, and missed three shirt bodies and a pair of stays: I had locked the drawer when I went out; I had seen them about a fortnight before. I saw the shirt bodies again at Marlborough Street.
   CHARLES WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker, and live in St. Martin's Lane. These things were pawned at my shop by a woman on the 8th of September.
   JOSEPH COLLINS. I am an officer. On the 8th of September the prisoner was brought to the watch-house. I asked her what she had done with the shirt-bodies and stays; she said the shirt-bodies were pledged in St. Martin's-lane, and the stays she knew nothing about.
     GUILTY. Aged 12. Judgment Respited.
 1508.  MARY BURN and ANN MALONY were indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , two pairs of boots, value 8 s., the goods of James Clough.
   WILLIAM MOSS. I am a tailor. On the 31st of July, I was in my shop in Great Crown-court, and saw the two prisoners facing my house, near Mr. Clough's shop. Burn went into the shop, and immediately returned, folding something up in her apron; I looked out and saw them running; I went over to the shop, and asked if they had lost any thing. I could not follow them, because I had no person in my own shop. I saw Malony distinctly, and am positive to her person.
   WILLIAM SABINE. Mr. Clough had these goods in his shop, and I found them afterwards. I cannot identify them.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1509.  JOSEPH MANNERVILLE was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , a waistcoat, value 6 s., the goods of Charles M'Nally. The prosecutor did not appear.
 NOT GUILTY.
