ROBERT MATTHEWS. I am a steel pen maker, and five in Baynes-row, Clerkenwell. On the 4th of March, I was coming down Baynes-row, towards Mount Pleasant, about nine o'clock—I saw the prisoner insulting every person they met—they stopped a seafaring man, and he pushed them away from him, and passed on—directly after that I met the prosecutor coming along Mount Pleasant—the two prisoners pushed him about ten yards—the prisoner Parr turned round, went up to the prosecutor, took him round the waist, threw him on the ground, and knelt on him, and drew his watch from his fob—when he saw me he let the watch fall on the pavement—he got up and staggered away, pretending to be intoxicated, and went round into Pool's-buildings—I brought him out of there, down to the prosecutor, who was standing in Mount Pleasant—there being no policeman present, we let him go, and followed him till was we went a police man in Laystall-street, and then gave him into custody—the prisoner Snell was ten yards off at the time Parr knocked the prosecutor down—Snell was staggering about at the time—he went to the workhouse, and we took him into custody at the workhouse, in bed, about an hour afterwards—when Parr was waiting with the prosecutor, after he had thrown him down, Snell called to him to come on.
