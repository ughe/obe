Third Session, 1905–1906.
657
Before Lumley Smith, Esq., K.C.
Third Court, January 16th, 1906.
195. GEORGE HERBERT (55), Unlawfully committing wilful and!
corrupt perjury.
MR. BOYD Prosecuted.
FREDERICK VERNEY (10 L.) Acting on instructions, I watched 1,
Rodney Place, Lambeth, from December 18th, till the 22nd, between
8.30 p.m. and 12.45 8.m.-on December 18th I saw the prisoner come out
of the house and go down Rodney Place; he returned in about fifteen
minutes, letting himself in with a key; he came out three times after
wards on December 19th I passed the house at 2.30 p.m. and saw him
cleaning the first floor windows-I saw him again at 10 p.m. that night-
on the 20th, between 10.30 and 12.30, he was outside the house, walking
up and down-he entered the house with a key-he came in and out several
times every night on the 21st I saw him about three times that night-
on the 22nd I saw him leave the house with a woman, and go with her to
public-house--the prisoner afterwards left the public-house, alone, and
returned, letting himself in with a key-he was charged with a woman
named Purkiss with assisting in the management of a brothel-I never
saw him outside the house with the woman I have seen them come to
the door together-I was present when the warrant was executed on the
22nd at 10.15—the prisoner was with the woman at the time I found
two keys on him, one a latchkey.
ALBERT MASTERS (375 L.) On instructions I kept watch on the house
in question from December 18th to the 22nd I have seen the prisoner
come out and fetch beer-I have seen him go in with a key and come out
again and chase boys away every day-I have seen him opening the door
for couples he was in the house with the woman Purkiss, but I have
nover seen them out together.
JOHN CUNDELL (Inspector L.) On December 21st, at night, I passed this
house and saw the prisoner walk up the steps and let himself in with a key.
ELLEN BRODIE. I am the wife of George Charles Brodie, and live at
10, Rodney Place, which faces No. 1-I know the prisoner by sight as
coming in and out of the house I have seen him cleaning the windows
I know the woman Purkiss by sight-I saw the prisoner once in her com-
pany--they left the house together-that was the morning before Christ-
mas, about 10.
WALTER PEOKOVER (Inspector L.) On December 22nd I arrested the
prisoner and the woman Purkiss-he said, “I have nothing to do with
the place”-I found a latchkey and an ordinary door key in his possession
--the latehkey fitted the street door-I was at the Lambeth Police Court
on December 23rd when the prisoner and Purkiss were charged with
managing a brothel at 1, Rodney Place-after evidence for the Prosecution,
the prisoner gave evidence on his own behalf and swore that he had never
been in the house before, and said when arrested that he had gone there
with a woman-he said he did not know Purkiss and had never seen her:
before that day.
GUILTY. Three months' hard labour.
