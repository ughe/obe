 195.  JOHN HUGHES, SAMUEL GILBERT, and WILLIAM STEWART, were indicted for burglariously and feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling house of William Brown , on the 1 last, about the hour of three in the night, with intent his goods, chattles, and monies, then and there being in the said dwelling house, burglariously and feloniously to steal.
 A Second Count, laying it to be the house of  Charles Brown and William Brown.
 The witnesses examined apart, at the request of Mr. Garrow, Council for the prisoners Hughes and Stewart.
   WILLIAM BROWN sworn.
 I live in  Pall-mall, the house is my brother's and mine, his name is Charles Browne we are seal engravers, I and my family live in the house, my brother does not live there, but he pays half the rent, we hold the house by a lease in our joint names, I sleep in the back room up two pair of stairs. On the 24th of October, a little after three, I was awaked by a noise, I directly heard people running out of the court, which is behind the house, I got up and threw up the sash, my window looks towards the back court; by the time I opened the window, the persons were got out of the court, so of course I saw nothing; it is Union-court, which leads to the Opera-house, there is a small paved yard which leads from my parlour to the court, and there is a door out of my yard into Union-court; in about half an hour or more, the watchman returned from the watch-house, who had secured some thieves, he gave no alarm by crying thieves, but I heard his rattle, and when the watchman and witness returned, they acquainted me with what had happened; I came down stairs, and found the parlour door, which looks into the back yard, bored through by the centre bit, this is a part of the pannel which I had taken out. (Produces it.) I was last in the room, and bolted the door myself, as I do every night, and take up the keys of the house; I locked the parlour door, which is next the passage, and took up the key.
 Court. Was the door next the yard unbolted? - No.
 Was that hole near one of the bolts? - It was, if they had been two minutes longer, they would have been in the house.
 Mr. Garrow. My Lord, I believe there is an end of the indictment.
 Court. Were the pieces of this door on the outside or inside? - I picked up this piece within the parlour.
