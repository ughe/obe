THIRD COURT.-Tuesday, April 6th, 1869.
Before R. Malcolm Kerr, Esq.
392. GEORGE SKINNER (17), Stealing 4lbs. of copper wire, of William
Hughes, his master; and WILLIAM HUBBARD (22), Feloniously receiving
the same.
Mr. Hunt conducted the Prosecution.
PHILIP HARRIS (Policeman F 169). On the afternoon of 16th March,
from information I received, I went to Mr. Hughes, a wire worker, at
39, Drury Lane-I found four bundles of wire ooncealed behind an earthen-
Ware pan, and I concealed myself in the cellar-about 12.55 Skinner came
to the place and took some of the wire away-he came three times, and
took some away each time-I was about five yards from the place, behind
the door of the coal cellar- at 1 o'clock the whistle blew for the men to
leave the works to go to dinner I left the cellar and went out of the front
entrance, into Drury Lane--I then went to Wilson Street, to the workman's
entrance, and saw Skinner leave-I followed him to 18, Paradise Street,
Lambeth Walk-he went into & marine store dealer's shop, kept by
Hubbard -I saw him take three bundles of wire from his coat, and put
them into the scale he took one bundle from his right-hand tail pooket-
Hubbard put the weights in the scale, weighed it, and gave Skinner some
coppers and a piece of silver, which I believed to be a shilling-he came out
of the shop-i stopped him and asked what he had taken there-he said,
