ELLEN DESMOND. I am the wife of Daniel Desmond, and live in Col lyer's-rents, Borough. The prisoner came to lodge at our house on Thursday evening the 13th of October—a woman, a neighbour, brought him—I was very ill in bed, having laid in only eight days, and I had a child lying dead—I agreed to let him the lodging at 2 s . a week—he said he might stop only a night or two, or a month—he slept there that night—we had a wake—I was in bed, and do not know what time he went to bed—he want away about seven o'clock in the morning, and these clothes were gone—I had several other lodgers—the prisoner was taken six weeks afterwards—he went away without paying for his lodging, and did not say he was going. DANIEL DESMOND. I know the clothes were all missed five minutes after the prisoner was gone—I had seen them the night before—they were all in my charge, and the lodgers look to me to pay for them. JOHN KEMP( police-constable M.  60.) I apprehended the prisoner last Wednesday in Bird-cage-alley, and found a pair my braces, on him. DANIEL DESMOND re-examined.  These are my braces, which I lost when he went away—the things were worth 2 l . GUILTY  of stealing the braces. — Confined Twelve Months.
 207. HENRY CHILDS was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of No vember , 2 shovels, value 4 s  ., and 1 brace, value 1 s  ., the goods of Robert Griffiths . JAMES GRIFFITHS. I keep the Queen Adelaide public-house, at Wands worth On Thursday, the 17th of November, the prisoner came to me, and said a barge of coals had come as far as the distillery, and he could not get it farther at that time—that he worked for the distillery and had some tools to dispose of—I said I was no judge of them—he went and brought Knight, who I employ, and he asked 5 s . for them—Knight said they were worth the money, and I gave him 5 s . for them,—I observed the initials "R G" on the shovels, and found they did not correspond with the prisoner's name—I then applied to the police, and he was taken up.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What is your business? A.  I do the lightering for a brewhouse—I did not ask the prisoner his name, nor where he lived—Knight knew him. JOHN BUSAIN. I am a police-inspector at Wandsworth. Griffiths applied to me on Saturday, respecting this, and left the tools in my pos session to find the owner—he described the prisoner, and I had him appre hended. WILLIAM REED( police-constable V.  95.) I apprehended the prisoner at the Ship at Wandsworth—he said at the station-house that he had bought the tools. ROBERT GRIFFITHS. I am a coal-merchant, and live at Belmont-place, Wandsworth-road. These tools are mine, and have my mark on them—I lost them from a wharf, three or four miles from the public-house they were sold at.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What did they cost you? A.  About 20 s  —I have known the prisoner some years as a lighterman—I believe I had seen the things the previous day. WILLIAM LAMBERT. I am a coal-porter. I used these tools on Wed nesday, the 16th of November on Mr. Griffiths's wharf—I saw nothing of the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 38.— Confined One Month.
