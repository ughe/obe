   The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he formed the society about Seven years ago; that his remuneration as treasurer was 12s. a quarter; that the members met from 7 to 9 p.m. each Saturday; that his son was appointed to assist him as secretary at 7s. 6d. a quarter; that there was a committee; that about four years ago he found the duties of treasurer rather onerous and difficult, so he handed the money over to his son, as he had the books; that he had full confidence in him, and did not know what he did with the money; that he retained none of the money that was received at the table; that after making inquiries he found that his son had left the neighbourhood, and then he went to the Romford Road police station, and stated the facts before the inspector in charge; that he meant when he answered the officer, "It is quite true" that it was true that he had entrusted the money to his son; that he handed over to the committee what money was in his hands, together with the books; and that he had no intention to defraud.
   Evidence for the Defence. JAMES RAWLINS. I have been for some time what is called the check secre tary of this club—I was also one of the trustees—I was so appointed by the secretary, whom I last saw at the Saturday night meeting on December 2nd—I had lived with him when I was a bachelor, but I am now married—I left him on May 7th—I was present at the weekly meetings, and saw what was done—on opening the meeting the prisoner's son initialled the members' books—the money was paid to the secretary, and not to the prisoner, and at the close of the meeting the secretary would hand what money he had over to the prisoner.
   Cross-examined.
