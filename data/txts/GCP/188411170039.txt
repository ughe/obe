DOBLE appeared for Jorvis; MR. Frith and MR. BLACKWEIL for Kent;
and MR. MOMORRAN for Garland.
HENRY PRICE. I was butler to Mr. Williams-in March last, in con-
sequence of an advertisement in a newspaper, I called at 64, Finsbury
Pavement, which is the Defence Society, and saw Jervis I told him that
I came in consequence of his letter-he told me I should have to perform
the duties of clerk and collector, that my salary would be 308. a week,
and I must deposit 25l, which would be returned to me in three months
-this agreement was then drawn up (This was dated 15th March, botroson the
witness and Morley Jervis, Secretary to the Defence Society, by which the
witness was engaged at a salary of 11. 10s, a week, he agreeing to deposit
252., to be returned at the end of three months, or on the termination of the
agreement, one month': notice of which was to be given on either side)
I sent up the 251. by post, and received this letter (Dated 2nd April, 1884,
requesting the witness to postpone coming till April 21st, as ho, Jervis, was
going for a holiday I went there on the 21st and saw & clerk named
Dean-I did nothing scarcely the first day or two-I first saw Jervis, I
think, on the Wednesday-I received my salary on the Saturday, I was
there four or five weeks and then could not get my wages, and saw Jervis
and asked him about it-he said he could not pay me then; I said that I
wanted to get some dinner, and he gave me a half-crown, and during the
day he gave me & sovereign; he said the business was at a standstill
through the solicitor leaving him, and he was obliged to dispose of it, and
he would pay me out of it and would pay me the 257. deposit out of the
money he was going to receive_I afterwards called at the office, and also
wrote for the return of the 251. I subsequently received this note (This
was dated June 6th, from Jervis, stating that he would send the money either
to-morrow or early next week) _I also received these letters of June 11th,
June 16th, and June 22nd (Apologising for not sonding the money, and
offering to pay 10 por cont. intorest) -I afterwards brought an action against
Jervis to recover my 257. deposit and the arrears of my salary, I recovered
151.-on August 6th I received this letter (This was from Torvis to the
vitness, stating that he had acted with surprising vindictiveness in suing
him for more than he ought, and offering him 157. 88, as the balanos, and
stating that if that wods not accepted he should apply to set aside the judg-
ment) while I was at the office I saw Kent about twice, but he did not
do any work there. I don't remember seeing Garland- & lot of men
called at the office and saw Jervis in a private room no one was em-
ployed in the office with me except Dean-I gave Jervis the 257. because
he told me that they were doing well, and he hoped to raise my wages as
the Society got on.
Cross-examined by Mr. HOPKINS. I read the agreement and know what
I was signing-I was paid 30s, for three weeks; I was there four weeks
and three days, I think I paid the 257. at his request by way of seourity
-if I gave notice I was at liberty to leave, and if he did not give me
notice I went on when I was leaving he said that he was going to
dispose of the business; I did not say that before the Magistrate, because
I did not think of it-I took the situation and paid the security because
thought it was a bond fide business—I did not hear that term at the
police court for the first time—the office was very nicely got up, but there
was no work going on when I first went-several people came every day,
and for aught I know they came to obtain the assistance of the Society
