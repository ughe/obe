JAMES HODSON. I am clerk to Mr. Prater, an army-clothier, at charing-cross; he occasionally acts as agent for some of his customers in the army. I paid the prisoner, on the 11th of March, 5 l . 2 s . 6 d . on account of Colonel William Ross, for Mr. Defoy.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Had you ever paid part of a bill to Hall, and part to the prisoner? A.  Yes, and received the prisoner's receipt for the whole—that was the case on the present occasion—I had paid Mr. Hall 5 l . 2 s . 5 d ., two or three days before—this is a receipt for 10 l . 4 s . 11 d ., the full amount of the two sums. ALFRED JOSEPH ALDERSON. I am clerk to Mr. Adland. On the 2nd of April I paid the prisoner 2 l . 15 s . 6 1/2 d . on account of Mr. Defoy—this is the receipt—the prisoner wrote it in my presence.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you give him the whole of the money? A.  Yes. JAMES ANTHONY VOLTEUR. On the 4th of April I paid the prisoner 3 l . 13 s . on account of Mr. Defoy—this is the receipt he gave—it was written in my presence.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was that sum paid in the whole? A.  Yes. HERBERT WILLIAM HALL. I am the prosecutor's clerk. The prisoner was authorized to receive a sum on account of Colonel Ross, and various sums on account of his master—he has never paid me any sum of money as received on account of Colonel Ross—he has not accounted for a sum received of Adland, and for only 3 l . received from Volteur on the 4th of April—he paid me no sum of money at all on the 2nd April, nor for any received on the 11th of March—he did not mention at the time he paid the 3 l . that he had received any other sum from Volteur—he said Mr. Volteur would not pay any more, our charges were too high.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How many times were you before the Lord Mayor? A.  Perhaps six—it may have been ten—the Lord Mayor said the prisoner might go out on bail—my depositions were not taken at the office—I signed none—it was not at my suggestion that I went before the Grand Jury without going before the Lord Mayor—our solicitor brought me here—I do not recol lect being told to come before the Lord Mayer again—I was not aware that I had to go again—I will swear that the Lord Mayor did not say my accounts were kept in a very suspicious and unsatisfactory manner—he said it would have been better if the prisoner had kept the books himself—he did not say "and not trusted so much to me"—the prisoner did not keep any books himself, he depended entirely on me—on one occasion he gave a receipt in full where I have only received a portion of the money—he was in the habit of paying money over to me and taking no voucher—I was to pay him 10 s . a week—I have the cash-book here, which will show that I paid him regularly—we were in the habit of going out in company together every evening—we have played twice at skittles—I borrowed 2 l . of him—I do not remember borrowing 15 s .—I paid him—it was two or three months ago—I do not owe him anything—my salary is 70 l . a year—I cannot tell when I returned the 2 l .—I did not talk about paying the night before his apprehension—I do not think anything passed with any one about this 2 l .—it may have happened, and I forgot it—he was generally the collecting-clerk—I have oftener sent him out than gone myself—I do not think he has paid me money above a dozen times since
