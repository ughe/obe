 1059. SUSAN ANN HABER , stealing a medal and 9 s  .; the property of Benjamin Bridges , from his person. BENJAMIN BRIDGES. I live at No. 3, Bond-street, Commercial-road. On 27th Sept., I met the prisoner in the road near the railway station, at Deptford—she accosted me, and asked where I was going—I told her I was going home by the New Cross railway—she asked me for money—I went with her up some bye-lane—during the time I was with her, she put her hand in my pocket—I did not lie down with her—I was walking with her when I missed my money out of my pocket—I gave her a shilling—I had connection with her; then I found her hand in my pocket—I had in my pocket about nine shillings and a pocket piece along with them, in my right hand pocket—it was all taken away but a halfpenny—I had a fourpenny piece and two or three halfpence—I asked her for it—I said, "I will be satisfied if you give me half of it"—she denied having it—two young chaps came up—I do not know who they were—when I saw a policeman I gave her in charge—I had had a little to drink—I knew what I was about—I was not the worse for liquor. AUBREY HAND. ( policeman , 417). I was on duty at New Cross. I saw the prisoner and the prosecutor together—I heard them talking together very loud, and I went up to them—the prosecutor gave the prisoner in charge—she said she had not the money—she produced one shilling, and said that was all she had about her—she was taken to the station house and searched—the prosecutor had been drinking a little, but seemed to know what he was about. ELIZABETH HOLMES. I am a searcher at the station house. I searched the prisoner, and found upon her a pocket piece and a fourpenny-piece—found them in her mouth. BENJAMIN BRIDGES. re-examined.  This is the pocket piece I had on the night in question. NOT GUILTY.
 1060. SAMUEL WRIGHT , stealing 12 s  . of George Cowthard . SARAH COWTHARD. I am the wife of George Cowthard, we keep a general shop. On the afternoon of Wednesday, the 8th instant, between 3 and 4 o'clock, I was sitting in my parlour adjoining the shop—I had my attention attracted to the ahop by the jingling of money, like silver—I went into the shop, and saw the prisoner with his hand in the till—he was leaning overihe counter, taking the money out of the till, and putting it into his pocket—he ran off with his hand in his pocket—he had money in his pocket—I had seen the till safe about two minutes before—there was then about twenty-five shillings in it—after he had gone away I found in it twelve shillings—I got my bonnet and ran after him—I am quite, con tain the prisoner is the person—I saw him again the next day. JOHN JAMES THOMASON. I live at Deptford. I was standing outside the prosecutor's door—I saw the prisoner run out of the shop—he had his hand in his pocket—I ran after him—I did not catch him—I am sure he is the person.
