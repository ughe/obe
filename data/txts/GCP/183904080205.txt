Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. How long was that after the
first pawning? A. The following Monday-I believe the first was on
the Friday--I said, “You had better leave me this piece to make up for
the one you left," and he made a jeer at me I said nothing about his
paying me the money for the piece he had left, and taking it back again-
some articles may be worth nearly the money we lend at the time, but
at the end of the year may not be worth half the money in taking an
article like flannel, we estimate the value as if it would remain in our
hands for twelve months, and then probably it may not have a good sale
we get fifteen per cent on the money lent-flannel at the end of the year
would be much less likely to sell than silver and other things it is liable
to moth and other things-this flannel would fetch more if sold in a shop
than at our sale I think this might fetch 18. 6d. a yard at the sale.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Are you obliged to sell these things by auction at
the end of a year? A. Yes- there is the auctioneer's commission to pay,
also moving, and other expenses, which we take into account.
