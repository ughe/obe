THOMAS EDWARD HENRY CLAY, Bank House, Chingford. The last
witness spoke to me about Tyler and Co., and in the beginning of
1907 I called on them at Pinner's Hall & goou many times.
Twice when I went there was somebody vere; at other times the
place was locked. I usually went about half past five. Afterwards.
I went between two and three, and found a voung lady, whom I took
to be a typist. I tried several times to find Tyler. There was no
sigu of business, no books, or papers. I afterwards wrote to him
about Mrs. Pittet's business, and received a reply, dated February
25, to the effect that the matter would be looked into. I wrote again
on March 4 and got this answer. “We are in receipt of your favour
of yesterday's date, and in reply beg to say we have looked into Mrs.
Pittet's account and find we had 15 Rio Tinto shares open for her,
which were closed last November as per instructions, when they were
quoted ex div. We also note that we have not settled with her, but
apparently the matter has been overlooked. We will make up the
account and forward it to her next pay day." That was the last
I heard from Wm. Tyler and Co.
Cross-examined by Mr. F. E. Smith: I never went into the inner
office. I did not go beyond the counter. I have an idea there was
a copying press there.
EDITH M. T. BUTCHER. I live at the Chestnuts, Godstone, with
my husband. I received a circular, dated February 14, 1906, headed
"Canadian Pacific Bonus," and dealing with an issue of Canadian
Pacifie shares. Believing the statements in the circular, I filled in
an application form, enclosed a cheque for £150, being at the rate
of 3 per cent. on £5,000 nominal stock, and sent it to the Financial
Guardian." The circular was signed "H. K. Ablett," and in due
course I received a contract note. Correspondence followed. I was
under the impression that after the stock was ex div. my account
was still in credit. On April 26 I went to Tokenhouse Buildings,
where I saw a person I now recognise as the prisoner Harris. I asked
if Mr. Ablett was in. He said "No, he is out of town." I asked
when he would be in, and Harris replied that he would be back on
Monday, it being then Thursday. I said I did not know whether
I should call again, and I came away. I gave him my name. I do
not recollect that he asked any other question. The same day I
consulted my solicitor, Mr. Hall. I never got back any part of the
£150. A writ was issued, but Mr. Ablett was said to be in Paris.
and Mr. Hall did not think it worth while to continue the action.
Cross-examined by Mr. Leyoester. I had had no experience of
Stock Exchange transactions. I have never invested money. I know!
that when people invest money they get certifiontes for their shares
or bonds. I understood these shares were held on the cover system,
3 per cent. cover being deposited. If the shares had dropped in
value more than 3 per cent. I should, of course, have lost my money,
