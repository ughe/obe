Re-examined. Q. Did you make them any promise or threat? A. No.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   GEORGE PARKER BIDDER. I am superintendent of the new works on the Commercial-road; Christopher Richardson, the elder, and a great many others are Commissioners; I know these to be ours, by the exact similarity; they are from a pattern made specially for the use of our road; Sullivan was employed on the road, but had left about a fortnight; he had been employed several months in pitching the road; he knew the road.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is there any private-mark on the property of the Commercial-road? A. Not on the grating- Messrs. Hunter and English, of Bow, are the manufacturers; it would be a breach of trust in the manufacturers to send the same sort of grating to other people; the pattern is not common.
 Re-examined. Q. Do you know of any road near the Commercial-road that has such articles on it? A. No; there is no other road undergoing any improvement.
 Sullivan's Defence. A man came and asked if we wanted a job - we said Yes, and we went down to where this grating laid.
   THOMAS FOWLER. I am a sailor. I was going down the Commercial-road, and about the Halfway-house I met these prisoners; I knew Sullivan, and stopped talking with him; a man came and said he would give him a job; he said he had a heavy load, and he must get a truck and another man; Kirby said, "I am here;" they went and got a truck, and he took them to where the grating was, and told them to take it to Back-church-lane.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Where do you live? A. In Essex-street; this was between three and four o'clock, on Friday, the 10th of September - I was in a public-house four days afterwards, and one of their friends came and asked the favour of me to come and say what I knew.
   JAMES BOWMAN. I am a letter-founder. I was with the last witness, and we met the prisoners; I know Sullivan was one - I am not certain of the other; a gentleman came up, dressed in black clothes - he asked if they wanted a job - Sullivan said, "Yes, if you will employ us;" he said, "If you will get a truck, I will give you a job to carry some grating down to Church-lane" - Sullivan said he could get a truck and he must pay for it; he said he should want another man, and the other said, "Here I am."
 SULLIVAN -    GUILTY. Aged 28.
 KIRBY -    GUILTY. Aged 35. Confined Six Months.
 1817.  JOSEPH MORRIA was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of September , 1 till, value 6d., and 5 shillings, the property of Sophia Miller.
 SOPHIA MILLER. I  keep an oil-shop in Spa-fields. Last Friday evening, between nine and ten o'clock, I was in the back parlour, and heard a noise of money; I went into the shop, and saw the prisoner going out with the till in his hand - it had 5s. and some halfpence in it; I am quite sure he is the boy.
   WILLIAM HAYNES. I am a shoemaker. I heard the cry of Stop thief! I saw the prosecutrix picking up the till - she said the boy had run down the walk; I ran and caught the prisoner.
   WILLIAM MARCH. I took the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1818.  ELLEN EDINGTON was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of August , 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s.; 1 crown-piece, and 2 shillings, the property of John Foster , from his person.
   ELIZABETH SALMON. I knew John Foster- he is gone to sea; I met him with the prisoner on the 10th of August, between ten and eleven o'clock; my sister was with me - he recognized me, and went to give us something to drink; he gave the prisoner a crown-piece to pay for the liquor - she put the change in her own pocket; we came out all together, and Foster said, "I have lost my money" - the prisoner then met another woman, and asked her to go into another public-house; while they were there the man said he had lost his money - I followed the prisoner, and saw her pay some money with some halfpence; I said, "You have robbed that man" - she made no reply; I went and told the prosecutor - I then went back; the prisoner was gone - I went and saw her; I said, "You have robbed that man" - she said it was of no consequence; I saw the Policeman, and gave her in charge - we went back to the prosecutor; he was gone to sleep.
 Prisoner. Q. How many times have you been here? A. Never in my life.
   CHARLES FIELDER. I took up the prisoner; I found 2s. 6d. in silver on her, and in her shoe a crown-piece - I found two handkerchiefs, which the prosecutor identified; he claimed them in her presence, and she owned before the Magistrate that she was guilty - she said nothing about the money.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not own to me that you heard this woman demand the money of me? A. No, I did not; on taking her to the station I said, "What money have you got belonging to Foster?" she said she had 2s.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met the prosecutor - he was very much in liquor; he desired me to go down Gravel-lane - he bought some fruit, and put it into this handkerchief; we then went to a public-house, and had five pints of gin - the witness and another woman were there; he desired me to go and get some oysters - this woman came and demanded part of the 5s. piece, and she said if I did not give it her she would say I had robbed him.
   JAMES LEA. I am an officer. Salmon has been tried here three times, and twice this year.
     NOT GUILTY.
