   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Did you receive directions from Mr. Bray to run against Trevett? A.  No.
   Cross-examined by MR. WHITEHEAD . Q.  Then your master, the first day, told you he left it to you to drive against who you liked? A.  Yes, to do as we thought proper—I never received instructions to drive against Trevett; but I did to run tin Bird and Johnson's time—that was about a fortnight afterwards—the "Albion" and "Emperor" were on the road at that time, I believe—I molested them at times before they started one be fore and one behind—I endeavoured to get all the passengers I could against them—I have not been driving for Johnson since—I drove for one of his men—I have been driving for Turnbull since.
   MR. CLARKSON . A.  How long have you been driving for Johnson's men? A.  Two or three journeys.
   MR. JONES . Q.  You say the first day you ran against Trevett? A.  Yes; I do not recollect that I told Mr. Bray of it—I do not recollect that he asked me against whom I ran.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  What means did you take to molest the other om nibuses, beside picking up passengers? A.  Sometimes I gallopped by them, trotted by them as they stopped to pick up passengers; and when I stop ped they came past me—when they have stopped for passengers sometimes I have pulled the pole against the door, and would not let the passengers get in.
   JURY . Q.  Had you instructions from Mr. Bray to do so? A.  No.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Had you instructions from him to do exactly as you liked? A.  Yes.
   COURT . Q.  When was it you began to pull the pole against their omni bus first? A.  In two or three days after they began running—I did that to do the best I could for my employer—I pulled the pole first against them—I did so because I thought I was doing the best I could to get pas sengers. WILLIAM BIRBECK. I am a time-keeper in Bartholomew-lane. I have seen Mr. Bray's pmnibus twice, and have seen Lee driving it—on the 20th of June I was going through the Poultry—I observed two omnibuses going together—I saw a lady wishing to get into the first—the one behind drove the pole agains the door, so as to prevent the lady getting in—I cannot tell who drove the one behind, but the name of Aaron Bray was on it—the omnibus drove on about three yards—the pole was again driven against the door, and she went away—there is a house in Bargholomew-lane where Trevett's men stop to get drink—I saw one omnibus with the name of "Corbett" on it, and one with "Aaron Bray" on it, stop there when he stopped, and went on when he went on.
