Pullip DORSET GOEPEL. I am a clerk in the Alliance Assurance-office,
and live at No. 16, Stonefield-street, Liverpool-road, Islington About five
dlock on Monday evening, the 22nd of March, I was in King William-
street-a horse had fallen down in the street-I stopped to look at it, and
perceived a pressure at my waistcoat pocket, as if of some one wrenching-
noticed the prisoner near there-he was in front of me-be passed round, and
appeared to be holding out his hand as if passing something to another per-
son, who leaned forward as if receiving something from him, and they ran
through the crowd I was not able to discern his features-he said words to
the purport of “Here it is," as he passed it to his companion-he was stand-
ing before me when I felt the pressure-I seized hold of him by bis arm, and
charged him with having my watch-be denied it it was while I had hold
of one of his arms that he appeared as if passing something--I felt as if
something was wrenched from me- the man who appeared to receive some-
thing from the prisoner ran through the crowd and got off.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINB, Q. Had you hold of him when he
held out the other arm? 4. Yes I felt his pockets afterwards, and the
inside of his great coat pocket, after he had handed the something to some-
body else-I felt in his waistcoat pocket-I do not remember that I told
anybody that he had handed anything to anybody else until I felt in his
pockets-1 do not remember saying anything to that effect-I may have told
the people standing by- I am not able to swear to the words he said to the
other person-I understood the words to be," Here it is"-there was a
crowd of people round-the man running through the crowd created a con-
fusion-I was laying bold of the prisoner, and did not notice.
EDWARD KINCH (City police-constable, No, 569.) On Monday afternoon,
the 22nd of March, I was on duty in King William-street, and noticed
Goepel having hold of the prisoner-he gave him into my custody, charged
with stealing his watch--the charge was stated in the prisoner's presence he
denied it, said he was innocent, and could prove his respectability I asked
if he was in employment, and he said he was-I asked how he was employed
-be said as a printer-I asked if he was then engaged as a journeyman-
he said he was I asked where-- he said at Snow-hill-at the station, in
answer to a question from the inspector on duty, he said he worked at the
printing business, on his own account, at Snow-hill, that the house was occu-
pied by his sister, and the printing apparatus was there-I went to the house,
and found his sister she said he was not a printer, and that there was
nothing there belonging to him--there were no printing things there-I found
41. 138. on him: he claimed it, and said he had saved it from his industry-
produce the ring which was banging on the chain round Mr. Goepel's
neck, which was attached to the watch-it appears to bave been wrenched
from it-it is the ring of the watch-it must have been severed by violence
I believe it is silver.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you see the chain? A. Yes, it was on Mr.
Goepel's neck.
MR. GOEPEL re-examined. I have not the chain here--this ring was
attached to it.
Cross-examined. Q. Were there any ladies in the crowd, who said they
had seen somebody else take it ? A. Some ladies said a man had run across
the road, who bad my watch, but not who had taken it-they did not say
they had seen him take it, or anything of the kind.
