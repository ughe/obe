-I said “Certainly not, the case is in the hands of the Lord Mayor and my

represent him, and the case was remanded until 3rd May-the prisoner was
not there then, and it was remanded until the 4th, when he again did not
appear, and the Lord Mayor granted a warrant for his apprehension-I had
in December deposited 1001. with my nephew Mr. Bale--the other cheque
for 881. has been returned paid.
Cross-examined by MR. WILLIAMS. I have known the prisoner for a con
siderable tine-I asked him to find a wine store for me the house was
mentioned the first time he had first called my attention to the Mitre in the
Temple, for the purchase of which, I believe, he had entered into negotiations
on my behalf-I became acquainted with the statistics of the Mitre, and
several letters passed between the prisoner and me about its purchase-I was
very anxious to get it I don't recollect the price--I had not got the money
ready for that, and it fell through they asked more for it than it was worth
-I then requested the prisoner to look out again for a house to suit me-I
don't know that he went and inspected many houses with a view to their
suiting me-my nephew, Mr. Bale, also undertook to look out for a house for
me, and in December I deposited 1001. with him-Bale and the prisoner
were great friends-Bale was not to use the 1001. if he found a house-the
prisoner was to buy the house, and Bale to have the money ready-Bale
recommended the prisoner to me-I went and saw the Horns, and went
thoroughly into the matter with the prisoner, and I believe I said the place
would suit me to a T-the price agreed on was 2,2751.—I was prepared to
pay, the deposit and 1,0001. on 1st February—the prisoner was to borrow the
rest-he did not press me earnestly not to attempt to take the house if I
could not carry it through, that I know of-I will not swear that he did not
-I said "I will manage it somehow, for I am sure I shall do well there"
- he told me that the former tenant was & man named Anderson, and that
the place was for sale by the trustee in bankruptcy, a Mr. Ashdown-I
believe he showed me some letters from him, and he told me the Horns was
already under offer to other persons I have no reason to doubt it-I never
handed the 1501. to the solicitor for the trustee-when I was in the outer
office I heard the prisoner talking about an agreement to be sent the next
morning, but I cannot tell you the exact words--when I sent up the 881. I
had not that day the 1501. to pay, but Bale would have found the balance
in a moment I wrote and told the prisoner to find the other 621-I don't
koow Mr. Ashdown-I know this case was to have come on last session, and
was postponed at the prisoner's request-he certainly never made a tender to
Ashdown of 1001. I did not see the lease of the Horns the agreement is
what the solicitor brought to Bow Lane on Thursday, 10th January--the
prisoner wrote to me after he had received the 100l. and the 881 with
regard to other houses I believe--nothing was mentioned besides a house in
the Strand to the best of my belief--the prisoner did not offer me 1001.
when he asked me to withdraw from the prosecution-I refused to accept
the offer of the 1001.--I never had an offer of the whole.
