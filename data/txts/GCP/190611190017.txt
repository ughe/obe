JARVIS, Joseph (28, labourer); feloniously throwing upon Ellen
Jarvis a certain corrosive fluid, oxalie weid, with intent to burn,
maim, and disfigure her, and to do her some grievous bodily harm.
Mr. Symmons prosecuted.
ELLEN JARVIS. I am prisoner's wife; we have been living together
eight years, and have five children s he works at an indiarubber works.
We have been living at 30, Royal Street, Lambeth. On the night
of October 9 I went into the bedroom at 9.30 or 10. In the next
room where prisoner was I heard a kind of hissing noise, I went
there and I saw prisoner had something in a little earthenware cup;
I asked him what it was; he said, "That is mine; taste it." I said,
No, I'm not fond of tasting things like that"; and then he threw
it all in my face (But, sir, he has been one of the best husbands
to me and the best of fathers to my children.) I felt my eyes burna
iug, and washed them under the tap; the woman upatairs helped
me; then I went to the hospital. We had had some words; he
thinks that my children do not belong to him, I want that cleared
up: I swear that there is not the slightest truth in his suspicions.
Apart from these suspicions, he has always been a good man to me
and my children. I do not remember seeing this cup in the place
before. I do not know of any brass stuff that he might have wanted
to do up. We had been having a few words, but I really do not
think he would have hurt me if he had thought for a moment; he
was too fond of me.
Prisoner stated, in reply to the Court, that he was very sorry he had

groundless.
