1057. SARAH FOX was again indicted for stealing, on the 24th of December , two sheets, value 4 s.; a blanket, value 3 s., and a caddy, value 2 s., the goods of Thomas Richard Read , in a lodging-room.
   MARY READ. I am the wife of Thomas Richard Read, who is a shoemaker, and lives in Colt-street, Limehouse- the prisoner took a furnished room of us about the end of August or beginning of September, and continued till December - she said she had a husband, but he was not with her - she was to pay 4 s. a week - she left in December, without giving warning - she owed 1 l. 5 s. 6 d. - she left the room locked we broke it open, and missed these articles - they were produced by the pawnbroker before the Magistrate.
   LOWTHER JACKSON. I am a pawnbroker. I produce two sheets and a tea-caddy, pawned by the prisoner in the name of Smith, on the 8th of October, 20th of November, and the 24th of December. I am certain of her person.
   THOMAS ADAMS. The prosecutrix gave me the duplicates of this property on Thursday.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. She has had the tickets these four months; and when she found the other woman was bringing an action, then she brought her action.
   MARY READ re-examined. Q. Did you ever summon the prisoner to the Court of Requests - A. No; we summoned the man, not her, some money has been paid.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.
 1058.  THOMAS CARR was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of June , 15 brass tubes, value 7 s.; 26 brass collars, value 1 s., and 43 brass rings, value 3 s., the goods of Thomas Brant, his master.
   THOMAS BRANT. I am a patent lamp manufacturer, and live in Charles-street, Hatton-garden. The prisoner was my weekly servant; in consequence of some information, I went with a publican, named Ranwell, to the prisoner's lodgings, to ascertain whether he had got a pint pot - he had given leave for us to go - the officer, who was with us, could not find the pot, but in the cupboard we found some brass goods, which I knew to be my property - when the officer asked how he came to take them, he said he did not know, and said they were his master's; they are worth about 11 s. - he has lived with me six or seven years, and I thought him honest.
   WILLIAM LEE. I am an officer - the last witness's statement is correct; the prisoner asked his forgiveness.
 Prisoner's Defence. The were not for myself, but for another person.
 GUILTY. Aged 26. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury and the Prosecutor. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 1059.  MARY COLVIN was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of June , 20 yards of ribbon, value 14 s., the goods of Thomas Rees , privately in his shop.
   RALPH ELDRIDGE. I am shopman to Mr. Thomas Rees, linendraper, Shoreditch. The prisoner came to our shop on Saturday evening last, about eight o'clock, and asked to look at some ribbons - I showed her some - she said she wanted narrow white ribbon - I placed the drawer on the counter, and she ordered one yard and a half of one, and two yards of another, to be cut off - she had not asked any price, which made me suspect she had taken some - there was another person in the shop, at a distance - I turned my head, and when I looked round I missed one of the ribbons, and charged her with it - she immediately pulled a piece from her pocket - I think I had said,
 "You have got a piece of ribbon in your pocket;" she had not gone from the shop - I then said,
 "How many more have you got;" she was putting her hand again to her pocket, and I got over the counter and took her to the end of the shop - she said she had laid two half-crowns on the counter - it is very unusual for women to come into a shop and take ribbons without asking the price - no other person served her - I saw her come in; and as soon as I took her to the end of the shop, she said she had put two half-crowns on the counter, which I had not seen before - I went and found one on the counter, and one in the drawer.
   JOSHUA ARMSTRONG. I was sent for, and when I came the prisoner said there were two half-crowns on the counter, which she had put down to pay for the ribbons.
   BARNARD LEE. I was with Joshua Armstrong- these two pieces of ribbon were taken from the pocket of the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the shop to buy one yard and a half of ribbon at 3 1/2 d. per yard, and two yards at 5 d. - I asked for some other - he laid hold of me, and shook my hand, and said I was going to steal the ribbon - one gentleman came in with one half-crown, and said it does not belong to her, it belongs to somebody else - I said I had given two half-crowns, and the officer sent him to look for the other, which he found.
   RALPH ELDRIDGE re-examined. Q. Had the prisoner the two pieces of ribbon in her pocket - A. Yes, both of them, she had not tendered any money.
     NOT GUILTY.
