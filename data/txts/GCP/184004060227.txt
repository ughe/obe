MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Do you remember his coming home wet-footed
one day? A. When he was out with baked potatoes he came home wet
in the feet, and I gave him a dirty stocking to put on, a dry one that is
the way they became mismatched directly I saw the stocking in which
the money was found, I said that was the one I had seen-I knew it be-
longed to Richard, and it was on the box on the Monday-I am quite
sure of that-I am quite sure it was the same stocking I had seen by the
side of the bed on Monday.
Toob-
Cross-examined. Q. Who washed for him when he returned to lodge
with you for the week ? A. I do not know-his dirty things were not
sent to the wash-I never saw any in the house they were not put up in
a bundle--none of his things were put up in a bundle I never said any
thing about a bundle - I never said I opened a bundle, to ascertain whe-
ther they were two stockings, or odd ones-- I have no recollection of say.
ing any thing about the stockings being in a bundle-they laid folded to
gether on the box, one turned over the other, rolled up, and the one pro-
duced to me was the one which laid outside they were rolled up in a
bundle, the same as a clean pair of stockings-I did not unrol the bundle
--they were rolled up in the ordinary form of a pair of stockings, one
doubled over the other the one the money was produced in was the
outer stocking I did not unrol them at all on the Monday- I took them in
my hand I do not know who washed for him then there was no mark on
the stockings—I do not think the prisoner had been out of place above a
fortnight when he came to lodge with us the last time he came and had
a night's lodging now and then I do not know how long he had left
Mr. Bartlett, of the Barnsbury Castle public-house, when he came the last
week-when he was in employment he paid me 2s, a week-we never made
any agreemnet this time I knew he was out of omyloyment and never
asked him for any thing--if he had got any employment 1 should have ex-
pected the same weekly sum he did not board with us it was the Sunday
morning after the Saturday that he came that Mrs. Jarvis brought him
some bacon and bread for his breakfast-it was on the Monday morn-
ing she came and called him to come to breakfast-he was in bed, and
she was outside the house--it was after eight o'clock, it might be ten
minutes after eight-he owed us some money for his former lodging-he
often asked me for an account, but as he had no money I never gave it
him I never cast it up-it might be 12 or more, if I cast it up, because
I often let him bave his dinner, and any thing of that sort-he told me,
several times during the week, that he had no money he told me he
was in great difficulties, and was without money many times he men-
tioned the subject
