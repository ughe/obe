1905, Willetts and a number of others were brought up before the
magistrates, and it was found that Clarke and Bokenham were going

ship then decided that as this was the first time that proceedings of a
criminal nature had been taken with regard to the printing, and a
great many persons had been engaged in the production of these cinco
blocks, the prosecution was not proceeded with, and the prisoners,
amongst them being Bokenham, were bound over not to offend in
future. With regard to Clarke, there was no doubt that from 1905
onwards he had been engaged in this business of illegal printing.
(Mr. Muir then referred his Lordship to the Sessions Paper report of
the oase.) Clarke was also engaged in the printing of Bank of En-
graving notes of superior finish.
Sergeant HALL, N Division, produced a certificate of the convie-
tion of Kidd at North London Sessions in 1900 for receiving, and
stated that during the last two or three years he had done no honest
work at all, but had been engaged in selling this pirated musie. On
being released from prison for a time he sold crockery in Chapel
Street. MeChrthy did no regular work, but occasionally sold asbbages

against Walsh, who also did no regular work.
EDMUND WALTER CHURCH, recalled. When Bokenham volunteered
to show the police where the blocks were kept I went with the police
to Eldon Street, and on the way asked Bokenbam who the litho printer
was. I suggested to him several names, but Boken ham said, "Well.
I cannot tell you that I will not tell you now, I will consider the
matter." A little later I approached him and he then wanted to know
whether a certain reward which had been issued for the printers of
the "Merry Widow" was still in existence. I told him it was not, but
had been withdrawn, and he then said he would consult with Kidd
and the others, which he did, but they would not disclose the name
of the litho printer, and we have not been able to find out the source
of the process of reprodueing the music.
