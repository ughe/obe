I am clerk to the Comptroller, in the circula tion department—it is ascertained whether the letters are over weight directly they come out of the bag, before they go to the sorters—they are marked if over weight, before they are taken to the sorters.
   COURT . Q.  I Does it not happen that sometimes they leave I letter that is over weight, and do not mark it? A.  I It may happen, but if it occurs to the letter carrier that it is over weight, he would take it to the examiner—neither of these letters would be over weight; both these would be covered by the stamps that are on them. MR. MAC ENTEER. Q.  I Is it not usual for letters to be weighed? A.  I Yes; those for the London district.
   COURT . Q.  I With respect to the one for Mrs. James, would that be weighed by the examiner? A.  I If it was thought to be overweight, but they are so used to it that they can generally judge.
