ELIZABETH MANLEY. This shoe is the property of my son-in-law, Thomas Evans—it was brought back by Garland—I had been to the win dow an hour before, and this shoe was then there. WILLIAM MILLERMAN( police-constable B  95.) I went after the pri soner, and took him—he denied it, and struck the boy, who was with me, and said the other took it as much as he did.
   Prisoner.  The other boy with a brown jacket took it, not me; I know nothing about it. EMMA GARLAND re-examined.  I did not see any other boy with him—there was a boy watching him, who cried, "Stop thief!"—I am sure I saw the prisoner drop the shoe. JOHN GILBERT. I am a baker, but am now out of place. On the 21st of Feb. I saw the prisoner with his hand in the window—he slipped the shoe down, dropped it, and ran away—I called, "Stop thief," another one came and struck me, and I ran into the baker's shop—I am sure the prisoner is the person who took the shoe and dropped it—I informed the officer.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you see me take it out of the window? A.  Yes, and you and your gang every time you met me knocked me down. WILLIAM MILLERMAN. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at the Guildhall, Westminster—( read—Convicted 27th March , 1843, and confined three months )—he is the person. GUILTY.  747. JOHN PARROTT was indicted for stealing 1 horse-cloth, value 5 s  ., the goods of William Savage ; and that he had been before convicted of felony. WILLIAM SAVAGE. I live in Little Albany-street, Regent's-park. On Monday afternoon, the 17th of Feb., I put my horse and cab  on the stand in Tottenham-court-road—I placed a horse-cloth on my horse, and went to get my dinner—I returned in about a quarter of an hour, and the cloth was gone. HENRY HART. I am a cab  proprietor. On the 17th of Feb., at half-past three o'clock in the afternoon, I was in Tottenham-court-road—I saw the prisoner walk to the prosecutor's horse, and take the cloth off it, double it up, put it under his arm, and run away—I knew him before, and watched him.
   Prisoner.  He never called after me. Witness.  I could not get after him fast enough—I was driving my cab, and before I could get off he was out of sight, down the ruins of the Rookery—I am sure he is the person. JOHN JAMES ALLEN( police-constable E  159.) I took the prisoner, from a description of him, on the following day.
