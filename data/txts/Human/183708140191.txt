ANN BROWN and ELIZABETH WHITE were indicted for stealing, on the   26th of July , 1 sock, value 1 d  .; 1 purse, value 3 d  .; 2 sovereigns, and 2 half-crowns; the goods and monies of John Drake Darby . JOHN DRAKE DARBY. On Wednesday night, the 26th of July, I came out of the Surrey Theatre, and went to the Alfred's Head, and met the two prisoners—I made a proposition to have some supper, and then proposed to go to bed with White—I laid down on the bed—the other prisoner was in the room—this was about ten o'clock—I can swear I had 2 l . 5 s . and more in my pocket—I put the two sovereigns into my watch fob, and then took them out and put them into my sock, and put it under the pillow—Brown shuffled up and took it from the pillow—I was not aware that she was coming to bed—she had laid down for about two minutes—I did not like my company, I turned round and they both went away, and the sock and money were gone.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Had you been drinking? A.  I had—I had been at the Westminster election, and drank a little there—I was at the Theatre before the performance began—I went in and out, and drank—I sat in the Two Shilling Gallery—I do not know what I paid—I forget now—I went in company with two young women, but neither of these—no gentleman was with me—I cannot tell how much I drank—I drank in small quantities—I was quite sober—I drank only once at the election—it was part of a pot of half-and-half—I had a pint of half-and-half, when I came out of the Theatre, with one of the women—I had one glass of ale at the Alfred's Head—I was in no employment at this time—I drank a little half-and-half at supper—I did not go to sleep—I was wide awake, and saw them go out of the door—I got up, and as soon at I got my clothes on, ran and called the policeman—I was quite sober at the time I lost the money—they were taken the next day, and I was examined on the 28th—I treated the other girls at the Theatre—I gave them no money besides—I had been one day out of place—there is no mark on this purse, but every honest man knows his own purse—there was 5 s . in it—I did not see Brown take the money, but it was missing from her side—I saw her scrambling at the pillow—I did not lay hold of her. JOSEPH JAMES( police-constable M  67.) I received information, and went after the prisoners, and found them—I found nothing on Brown—on White I found two half-crowns, 3 1/2 d ., and this purse. MARY ANN CLARK. I know White. I met her the next morning—she asked me to go and buy a frock with her—she bought one—it cost 4 s . 6 d .
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you taken up on this charge? A.  Yes—I have not been tried at the Surrey Sessions—I have been fourteen days at Brixton. NOT GUILTY.
