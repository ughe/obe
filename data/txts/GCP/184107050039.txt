Joan LAYTON. I am a victualler, and live in the Old Bailey. On the
17th of June, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, the prisoner
came to my house and asked for three-halfpennyworth of gin, and threw down
a half-crown I saw it was bad, and asked if she had got any more money
-she said no-I said, " Then don't drink the gin, for that won't do"-she
said she had a penny, and would have a pennyworth-I kept the half-
erown in my hand, and asked where she lived she said, " In Black Horse-
court"-I repeatedly asked if she had any more money, she said no-after &
few minutes she went away-I followed her into Farringdon-street, and
gave her into custody, and gave the half-crown to Harding -I knew her
before.
EDWARD HARDING (City police-constable, No. 274.) The prisoner was
given into my charge by Mr. Layton, who gave me this half-crown, which
1 produce-I found nothing on her.
John FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint-this half-crown and
shilling are counterfeit in all respects, and are both made of Britannia
metal.
Prisoner's Defence. I was very much intoxicated, and did not know
what I was doing.
EDWARD HARDING re-examined. She was not much in liquor.
JOHN LAYTON re-examined. She was not much in liquor-she seemed
to know what she was about.
