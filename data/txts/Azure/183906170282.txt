MARY ANN WHITE. I live in Mount-row, Westminster-road. On the
night in question, between ten and eleven o'clock, I was standing at the
coal-shed, next door but one to Daley's house, and next door to Shears's
—l saw the prisoner standing at Shears's door—I saw Godfrey come down
from the end of the place, go up to Daley, and strike him in the face—after
that I saw two or three men rush up, but what occurred afterwards I can-
not say—a mob collected, but I did not go from where I was standing—
Daley did not strike Godfrey at all, before Godfrey and the other men
struck him.
EDWARD SKELTON. I am apprentice to Mr. Shears, a silk hatter. On
this Saturday night, about half-past ten o'clock, I saw Daley and Godfrey
struggling together, about six yards from my master's door—there wereno
others there at that time, but two or three minutes afterwards, two or three
came from the corner, and a general struggle ensued—the confusion wasso
great that is all I saw.

RICHARD THOMAS RUSSELL.
I am a surgeon. On Saturday the
8th Of June, I was called in to attend Ann Dunn, about two
o'clock in the afternoon, she complained of a pain in her head—I did
not examine her head at that time—I found her tongue was foul, and the
state of the bowels confined—I sent her some medicine. and at one o'clock on
Sunday morning I was sent for again—I found her in a state of insensibi-
lity, and I applied leeches to the temple—I saw her again about ten
o'clock in the morning—she still remained insensible—she died on Thurs-
day, the 13th of June—she Was insensible till the Wednesday afternoon—
