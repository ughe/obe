FRANCIS EARL. I am a constable of Cripplegate. I heard the cry of "Stop thief" and saw the prisoner running down Phillip-lane—I endea voured to stop him, but missed my hold—I turned round and pursued him—another person, a stranger, caught hold of him—I came up almost imme diately and secured him—Kirby came to the station-house directly after wards—I did not notice him before.
   Cross-examined. Q.  He did not come up where the prisoner was stop ped? A.  I did notice—him—there were a great many people there. THOMAS EVAN. I am the employer of Bruce—the umbrellas are mine. Cross-examined. Q.  What are you? A.  A wholesale umbrella-manu facturer—these are not finished, but they are complete enough to answer the purpose of umbrellas.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was returning from my master's—I went to my mother's and on coming back, I was going to Billingsgate to assist my wife home, and on the road in Wood-street I heard a cry of "Stop thief"—I ran with the other people—a man in white trowsers was running, and that man let the bundle fall out of his arm—I turned one way, and suppose the man went the other—as soon as I turned the corner I was laid hold of—four or five people came to the station-house, and said I was not the man who dropped it, that it was a person in white trowsers; but they were all pushed away from the door—the door was shut in their faces—they pro mised to come forward, but I do not know whether they are here. MARY ANN FORD. I live in Phillip-lane, my husband works in Al dersgate-street. I was in Silver-street on the 12th of August, and as I came across from Silver-street, there was a young man with a bundle of umbrellas under his left arm—they were covered with green baize—the young man came across the road, and his right arm touched my left—an umbrella-maker living in our court, made me take notice, and I followed him down opposite the locksmith's—the people came running down the street—he turned his head, saw them running, and threw down the umbrellas in the gutter, a few yards from me—I did not go to the watch-house when the prisoner was taken—I do not know him—the man had dirty looking white trowsers on—the prisoner is not the young man I saw throw down the umbrellas—I cannot say it was not him—I saw no features because I was behind him, but I am sure he had dirty looking white trowsers—when they were thrown down there was a cry of "Stop thief"—there were several persons standing who might have taken him, but I could not see his face. JAMES BOLTON. I am an appraiser, and live in Addle-street. On the 12th of August, I was returning home from Aldermanbury—I had arrived near the end of Phillip-lane, when I heard the cry of "Stop thief", and immediately afterwards saw two men run past me, one in white trowsers, and the other in dark—the one in white trowsers was on the right hand side, and the other on the left—I turned after them, and went down to the end of the street—they then parted, the man in white trowsers to the right, and the other to the left—two girls were following them—I returned towards my own home, and when I got to Philip-lane, I found somebody in custody—I then went to the station-house, and was refused admission—I really cannot say whether the prisoner was either of those men.
