HUMPHERY, Mayor.
Thomas COLBORN (police-constable T 97.) I received the three prisoner
charge at Shepherd's-bush, nearly two miles and a half from Mr. Baynes's
received two half-crowns from Mr. Baynes-Adams was very hot, and said he
must have something to drink for himself and the others, it would do then
good after their run-Smith and another man had taken them-I inquired 2
the addresses of all three, and M'Kenzie was the only one which wa
right.
Mr. John Field. These are both counterfeit, and both cast in one
mould.
M'Kenzie's Defence. I met Adams, and we went into a public-house
came out, and turned to go to Hammersmith about a hundred yards, when
gentleman on horseback came and said we must stop--we walked back to
the public-house-we went in, and ten minutes afterwards they brought in
Wright.
Wright's Defence. I met two men; one of them asked me to go to Mr.
Baynes's shop for sixpennyworth of postage-stamps--he gave me a half-crowa;
they said it was bad; I came out, and could not see the man.
Adams's Defence. I never saw Wright before in my life.
M'KENZIE-GUILTY. Aged 22.
WRIGHT-GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Six Monthss.
ADAMS-GUILTY. Aged 23.
1281. JOHN SMITH was indicted for uttering counterfeit coin.
Messrs. BODKIN and DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH WRAKE. I was in my husband's shop, on the 25th of Feb., i
York-street, Westminster-the prisoner came, between seven and eight o'clock
in the evening, for a pennyworth of tobacco-he gave me a counterfeit shilling
-my daughter bent it double in my presence, and gave it back to him-
told me he got it at the turnpike, at the Marsh-gate-I am sure he is the
man.
CHARLOTTE Crow. I am Mrs. Wrake's daughter. The prisoner came on
the 25th of Feb.-my mother showed me a shilling-I bent it, and it was
turned to him-be is the man-he said he took it at the Marsh-gate-will
swear the shilling was not silver--I took it to a vice, and bent it, and then
bent it with my fingers.
Cross-examined by Mr. HORRY. Q. You never saw him before? A. No
-I saw him next at the police-office-when the policeman was taking him
along I was told what it was for, and then I said it was the person who had
been in the shop.
GEORGE SADLER. I am a dairyman, and live in Denby-street, Pimlico,
about half a mile from Mrs. Wrake's. About eight o'clock in the evening a
the 25th of Feb., the prisoner came into my shop for a quarter of a pound a
butter, which came to 3d.-he offered me a half-crown-I looked at it, and
found it was bad- I told him so-he asked me to give it him again--I said
should not, I should give it to a policeman-he used abusive language, a
said he would fight me for a crown the officer came and took himgar
the half-crown to the officer.
PATRICK M MAHON (police-constable B 138.) I was called in, and Sadier
gave me the prisoner and this half-crown-the prisoner said he got it
public-house near the Marsh-gate-I made inquiries, which led to
Wrake-he said he had never been in their shop.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you not hear him say he had got it at the match
manufactory, Lambeth ? A. No.
MR. JOHN FIELD. This half-crown is counterfeit.
Guilty.* Aged 21.-Confined One Year.
