I saw Leslie give Mrs. Smith a 5 l. Montgomery note, and told her to take part of the bill - she went out of the parlour, and came back with a 1 l. note, and asked Leslie if he knew what note he gave her; he said it was a 5 l. Montgomery note. Mr. Smith then asked what odd money there was - he said 2 s. 3 d. Mr. Smith then went out, and came in behind Mrs. Smith, and brought in the change of the 1 l. note, which he handed to Mr. Leslie - and Leslie said "Now I owe you even money, just 4 l." I am not positive about there being any paid for what we had that night by Leslie, but he lent me a 5 l. Montgomery note that evening.
 Cross-examined. Q. Who repaid this? A. I did that day week. I asked Leslie for it in Mr. Smith's presence. I had been intimate with Leslie before, but he had never lent me any money - he asked Mrs. Smith if she thought it was right, and she said "Yes." I suppose he meant, if it would be safe. Mrs. Smith said, she would lent me one, if I wanted it. I cannot remember that Mr. Leslie said, he regretted there had been so much trust about the beer.
 MR. ANDREWS. Q. Is that your book containing Mr. Leslie's account? A. Yes; It is entered by my wife and my son; this other book is one delivered by the Insolvent Debtor's Court to me, rendered up by Mr. Leslie.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is your daughter in London? A. No, she is married, and gone into Yorkshire two or three months ago. I attended before the arbitrators in this cause; I told my daughter there was no necessity for her coming here; I heard that Mr. Ford was inquiring after her, and my wife said she was at Pocklington.
   JOHN LEWIS FORD. I am father-in-law to Mr. Leslie. I remember a person named Wyard being in his service; he was discharged for receiving money and applying it to his own use; I am a freeholder of Middlesex; I heard of Mr. Leslie being in Mr. Smith's debt; when I called on Mr. Smith for a bill, he said "He owes me money."
 MR. ADOLPHUS to HENRY SMITH. Q. Where is your son John? A. He is ill in bed, with a typhus fever, at No. 15, Exmouth-street - he was at Grays, in Essex - he is confined to his own premises - my daughter is at Rockliffe, in Yorkshire - she has been there two months - she went first to Pocklington - this entry in the book is partly my wife's, and partly my son's - I delivered the bills to Mr. Leslie in my son's presence, when he came to my bar - these entries were made at the time they were cast up - I did not see Mr. Leslie give my wife the five pound note, but I saw my wife bring in the one pound note, and Mr. Leslie said to me, "I have given your wife a five pound Montgomery note;" I saw this balance struck in the book, and this one of March the 7th - I do not know how this, in 1822, came to be about the one of 1819 - it appears to have been scratched out again.

   JOHN BARLOW. I am a labourer. I was a coal-porter, and have delivered coals for Leslle, at several times, from 1821 to this time - on those occasions the beer was always brought down to us at the barge - either Mr. Leslie or his wife sent the money down to us for it - I worked for him sometimes once in a month, and sometimes once in two or three months - I never heard any charge given about credit - I heard Mr. Leslie say to Smith, "If you trust these men with any beer in my name, I will not pay you, by God" - he said this several times.
 JURY. Q. Was a man of the name of Wyard there? A. Yes; he has known that the money was sent for the beer, and that directions were given not to give credit for it - Mr. Leslie gave these directions in the public-house - he alluded to the beer we had in the barge, because the money was always brought to us.
   THOMAS WRAITH. I am a coal-porter. I have worked for Leslie occasionally - it is nearly four years since I first worked for him - it is customary to give us beer when we are at work - Mr. Leslie or his wife always gave us the money to pay, and we always paid it - if there was any more drank, we made up the money out of our own pockets - the beer was brought down to the barge, and we went to the public-house and paid it - Mr. Leslie used to tell Mr. Smith, if he trusted any thing on his account he would not be answerable for it - Wyard has seen the money paid by us at the public-house, and heard Mr. Leslie say he would not be answerable for any of us.
   JAMES BOWLER. I am a coal-porter. I have worked for Mr. Leslie - we used to have beer; the money was generally sent by him or his wife, or the girl, to pay for it: we sometimes had a pint, and never more then two pints a man; I never knew of our having been without paying for it when we came away at night; if there was a pot, or pipe of tobacco more, we settled it; I have never known of any beer being had on Mr. Leslie's account, and not paid for; I have often taken the money myself, and paid it to Mr. Smith, or his wife or daughter; his daughter is not here.
   WILLIAM REDLAKE. I am a coal-porter. I have worked for Mr. Leslie three years and a half; we used to have beer when we were at work; we used to pay for it; if we had any ourselves; I have heard Mr. Leslie tell Mr. Smith not to trust the coal-porters; if he did, he would not be answerable for it.
   SAMUEL MEAD. I am a coal-porter.
