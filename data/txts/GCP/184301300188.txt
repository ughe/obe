HENRY BERESFORD. I am an inspector of the C division of police. On the
afternoon of the 9th of January I saw the boy Lincoln come out, and the pri-
soner was standing at the corner of Compton-street-I went on the following
evening to the house--the prisoner came in-I searched him, and found on him
five shillings and some copper--the next morning he wanted to have some coffee
-I said, "No, I must keep this money"-he said, “It is my own money, I
brought out six shillings, and I changed one shilling"- when I first seized him
I found the money in his pocket-1 showed it to Mr. Davis, and he identified
four shillings of it as bis money-the prisoner had just got to the door, when
I seized him-he was not out.
Cross-examined. Q. And in fact he had got the money in his hand ? A.
It was in his pocket-he had his hand in his pocket- I took his hand
out and put my hand in, and took the money-I cannot say whether his hand
was closed- I told him to take his hand out-I watched him all the time-
when he received the money from the boy he closed his hand upon it, and put
it into his pocket-I found on him the five shillings and 6 d. in copper.
Mr. Davis re-examined. These are four of the shillings I marked and put
into the till with six others, which I have in my pocket.
Guilty. Aged 30.-Judgment Respited.

JANE ANN SHEPHERD. I am barmaid at the Dover Castle, in St. George's
in the East, which is kept by Mr. Abrabam Watson. The prisoner came
there on the 6th of Jan., about half-past eight o'clock (I never saw her be-
fore)-she said, "I want half a pint of gin and a pot of porter for Mr. Mace,
next door to the baker's shop"-I served her-she came again, and said she
wanted another half-pint of gin and a pot of porter for Mr. Mace, who was
out, and as soon as he came in he would send over the money, and she had it
---she came again for another half-pint of gin and another pot of beer-1 let
her have it-I saw Mr. Mace afterwards the next morning I saw the prisoner
come with the empty pots-I asked her how she came to come for the gin and
porter for Mr. Mace, when he did not send her-she said she knew Mr. Mace
did not send, but a gentleman whom he knew did.
