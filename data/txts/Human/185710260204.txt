LAYTON ASHTON. I am a clerk in the London and Westminster Bank. The British Engineers' Benefit Society keeps an account at that bank—these two cheques have not passed through that bank. THOMAS PARKER. I was one of the auditors of that society. The prisoner had authority to pay me money as an auditor—he had not authority to pay me cheques—it was his duty to pay them into our bankers.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you been an auditor? A.  I am not by profession an auditor—I acted as an auditor to this society—we were sitting about fifteen days—I was acting with Mr. Sprey and others—during that sitting the prisoner paid various sums of money—we commenced sitting on Monday, May 18th, and sat till the 3rd June.
   Q.  Were the sums which are mentioned on this side of this account paid to you, begin at the 26th May, and go on? A.  I have a perfect recollection of a portion of these being paid—I cannot say whether the others were paid—there were four of us sat, and we had 11 s . a day—Mr. Williamson was our chairman—I believe he had the same as we had—I believe the prisoner paid me—supposing cash were paid to us we did not inquire whether it was the result of cheques or not, but we were perfectly well aware where the cash came from—we were aware that we received moneys.
   COURT . Q.  Are the 6 l . 6 s . and the 6 l . 10 s . in this account? A.  No.
   MR. LILLEY . Q.  Will you look at this paper: was that submitted to you as an auditor? A.  No, Sir, these items were never submitted to the auditors—we were only auditing the accounts of last year, not the present year—I heard from the prisoner that he had received these cheques of 6 l . 6 s . and 6 l . 10 s . and paid them in—I have not heard from him that he had paid over the money in the payments he made to the auditors—I am not aware at all that he did—I could not undertake to swear that he did not—at the commencement of the audit, 30 l . odd was advanced for the expenses of the trustees—probably the 30 l . was exhausted about the 26th of May, but there was other money advanced from the various branches to pay the expenses—Mr. Williamson was the chairman—I do not recollect that he directed that these two cheques of 6 l . 6 s . or 6 l . 10 s . should be cashed—he might give such directions, but not in my presence, nor in the presence of the auditors.
   MR. GIFFARD . Q.  When was it the prisoner told you he had received these cheques and paid them in? A.  When he returned from Dartford he told us he had received the cheque, and also when he returned from Messrs. Hopkinson he told us he had received their cheque—I did not ask him what he had done with them—I thought he would pay them into our bankers. WILLIAM HOWE. ( Police sergeant, M  19). I took the prisoner on the 17th of September.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You told him the charge was embezzling money? A.  Yes; his reply was, "Not that I am aware of"—a smaller sum was mentioned, and he said, not that he was aware of—when at the station he said he could not enter it in his books, because his books were taken from him in March last—he said, "I have no doubt I entered it in the minute book, I generally enter it there."
