WILLIAN ARTHUR RUDD, divisional surgeon. On the morning of
August 27 I was called to the Cottage Hospital, where I saw William
O'Neill. He had an incised wound on the right arm over the biceps
about three inches in length. The wound was gaping very much.
The muscles had been cut and torn. I stitched the wound up. He
had lost a great deal of blood. His clothes were saturated with blood.
He was in the hospital about a fortnight. The wound must have
been caused by a knife or sharp instrument. There were two tiny
wounds at the point of the elbow which might have been caused by
the point of a knife, but could not have been caused by the fist
did not see the clothes which had been removed.
Inspector INSTANOE said the coat was in such a filthy condition that
