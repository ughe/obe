   Friedeburg. Q.  When did I leave the lodging? A.  To the best of my recollection, it was 4th Oct.—you did not go out in the clothes you are now wearing—you wore a large morning coat, a wrapper—I do not recollect seeing Kauffman in any different clothes to those he now has on—I did not see any preparation for a press when I went into your room to make the bed—I saw the press go out—I did not see the stones come—you said you ex pected some letters from France, and when you received them you would be obliged to leave directly—I said it was a rule to have a week's notice, and you said it was a rule you were ignorant of—that was on the Sunday night before you left on the Monday—you paid me the rent on the Monday morning—it was due that day—I said, as you were ignorant of the rules of the lodging. I did not wish you to pay it, as you wished to leave directly—Aaronson came to you several times, and a lady and gentleman also came to the door—I knocked at your door, but only a strange man came out—I saw about thirty people coming to you backwards and forwards.
