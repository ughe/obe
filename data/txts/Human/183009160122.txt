 1738.  HENRY LACEY was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , 6 shillings, and the sum of 8d., in copper monies, the monies of George Abledon , his master.
 GEORGE ABLEDON. I am a  baker- the prisoner was in my employ. On the 12th of August he took 6s. 8d. of Mrs. Bailey, of Hackney; I sent him out with bread that day - she pays on Monday, and had two quartern loaves the week before; there was a little girl in my shop when he returned; I was just opposite - I asked him if he had paid; she said No - he went through into the bake-house: on the 8th of July, I sent him with some bread, which he did not deliver - he had delivered some to Mr. Bailey on the 7th; here are five quartern loaves down in the book on the 8th, but he did not deliver them - he asked for the five loaves, and I delivered them to him.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Did you deliver the five loaves to him? A. Yes, on the Thursday; she never has bread on a Thursday, but he said she wanted eight; there are no names in this book, but he goes to four customers regularly in order, and we know by that who has them; he has entered the five on Thursday - mistakes have occurred with him before; he has come home short of bread sometimes - I thought at first it was a mistake, but afterwards I found he had sold them at 6d. a piece; I am obliged to attend in my shop, as my wife is ill - this young woman comes over an hour or two in the afternoon; the prisoner's book was on the shelf on the Friday where he always puts it - I had looked there before, and it was not there.
 COURT. Q. Did you ask on Friday for the money? A. No - I did not expect it till the following week.
   SARAH BAILEY. I am a customer of the prosecutor's; I have bread on the Monday, Wednesday, and Friday - I ordered none on the Thursday; I had paid the prisoner on the Monday; he did no charge for these five loaves - he did not come on the following Monday.
   DAVID DEARMAN. My mistress gave me the money to pay on the morning before, and I paid the prisoner.
   THOMAS VANN. I was sent for to the prosecutor's on the 17th of July; he charged the prisoner with robbing him of fifteen loaves; he said he had left ten at Bailey's, and put the money on the counter - two half-crowns, a shilling, a sixpence, and two pence; he said he knew nothing of the other five, but I said, "Try to recollect who you paid it to;" he said "The young woman was coming from the passage to the shop, and that was the very money I received from Bailey."
 Cross-examined. Q. He was in his master's place? A. Yes.
 COURT to GEORGE ABLEDON. Q. Did you charge him with fifteen loaves? A. Yes; the ten Mrs. Bailey had the week before, and the five on the 8th - he said he had paid  Martha Bright for the ten, but I did not ask about the five- I knew she had not had the money; I gave him in charge for having the five loaves, and not delivering them.
 Cross-examined. Q. He was still in your service? A. Yes; on the Saturday I sent for the officer - I had not told him before the officer arrived; I keep no books - it is a ready money business; the money is laid down - there is always some person within call to take it.
 Prisoner's Defence. My master states he is in the habit of paying attention to his business, but he is always in the Black Boy playing at cards - he is a pugifist; I was in the habit of taking five loaves on Monday, five on Wednesday, and five on Saturday to Mrs. Bailey, and if any other customer wanted them she had to go without till Saturday, as she knows.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1739.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 1 sovereign, the money of Richard Payne , from his person.
 RICHARD PAYNE. On the 17th of July I was in  Union-street, Hackney-road, at half-past eleven o'clock in the evening - I had been to a hard week's work, and took a little drop of drink; I had fallen asleep in the street- I had changed a sovereign just before.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I saw the prisoner in the White Bear gardens, at half-past eleven o'clock at night; the prosecutor was lying on the ground, and the prisoner persuaded him to get up, but he would not - there was another row; I went, saw the prisoner go over to the prosecutor, help him up, and they fell down together - the prisoner then got up again, took the sovereign out of the prosecutor's pocket, and put it into his own; he asked me what it was - I said a sovereign; he said it was better in his pocket than in the prosecutor's - he said so help him God he should have it to-morrow; the prosecutor said,"It is all over, it is all over," and then he went away.
   WILLIAM HARTGA. I was going by, and saw the prisoner take something from the prosecutor and put it into his left-hand pocket - the prosecutor said it was all over, and went away; the prisoner said so help him God he would give it him the next morning.
   HENRY SOMES. I am a publican. The prisoner came to change a sovereign with me, and the officer came and took him - he said he had picked it up in the street.
   GEORGE COLLYER. I took the prisoner at the Gentleman and Porter - he offered me the change; I said, "I want the sovereign;" the landlord said, "This is the sovereign" - I made him give the change back; he paid the 1 1/2d. for a glass of gin out of his own money.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming home, and the good man was laying down asleep, very much in liquor - the two boys were trying to lift him up; I tried to lift him, but could not - I took the sovereign, and said I would keep it till the morning; I told the boys where I had lived for thirty-five years.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 65. - Confined Fourteen Days.
