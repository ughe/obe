   COURT . Q.  Are you sure he said in the house? A.  In the house—it was at or in the house.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did he say why you were to come at half-past eight? A.  Yes, he said, "I know you Childs, and I should rather you had the job than any one else"—he said, "I told Shelswell to come at half-past nine o'clock, but if you come at half-past eight o'clock I will put you on them first"—I went next morning at half-past eight o'clock, and saw Toy—he said, "Well, it is all over"—I said, "What is all over?"—he said, "I have given the butters up to Mr. Norman, the Inspector of the H divi sion"—he said he had given him four casks—on the following Sunday I went with Shelswell and Taylor to take Northcote—we found him at George Pementer's in Waterloo-town, sitting up in a bed undressed—it was between six and seven o'clock in the morning—I searched him, but found nothing—as we were bringing him to the office, we stopped at a public-house—I was about to search him again, and saw him take his hat off, pull his handkerchief out of his hat, and taw a pocket-book in his hand—I pulled it out of his hand and gave it to Shelswell, who pointed out to me what was in it—I produce the book, after that I took a letter out of his hand—the book is now in the same state as when I took it from him—here is written in the pocket-book, "I M R, 1. 0. 6."—I handed it to Shelswell in the state I found it—Northcote was in Toy's tap-room when I was there on Friday night, at eleven o'clock.
