1917. ROBERT ESSEX was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of Jane,
1 watch, value 31. 105. ; 1 guard-chain, 108.; and i key, 1d.; the goods of
Samuel Demaid ; and FREDERICK WARREN for receiving the same, well
knowing them to have been stolen.
Mary EGLINTON. I am servant to Mr. Demaid, of Southampton-rov,
New-road. About half-past four o'clock in the afternoon, on the 5th of June,
Essex came down the area with some fish which had been ordered—he asked
for some water- there was some beer in a jug, and I gave him some-I had
occasion to turn round for it-he took the beer, and said he would wait for
orders—I said I was sure there was nothing wanted-he said he would wait
and see, till the lady came down stairs—a lady came down, and asked for a
glass of beer-I went away with ber, leaving Essex in the kitchen—I came
back in about two minutes—he then said he would go-I then missed o
watch, which I had seen on a nail on the dresser about half an hour before
he came in—no one else had been in-it belonged to Mr. Demaid.
Samuel DEMAID. I keep the bouse, No. 8, Southampton-row-I missed
the watch from the kitchen when I returned home on the 5th of June-1
went with a policeman directly to Essex's master, Mr. Rowell – I said to him,
in Essex's presence, “I accuse him of robbing my kitchen of a watch,
guard, and chain, and I give him in charge"-Essex said, “You may, and
welcome, if you like; I have not robbed your kitchen"—the policeman ui.
buttoned his jacket, and out fell a lobster, which was his master's—he then
found on him this guard-chain and key of my watch.
John BAINBRIDGE (police-constable D 105.) I went with Mr. Demaid to
Mr. Rowell—I found this guard-chain and key in Essex's waistcoat-pocket,
and a lobster in his jacket-I felt something in his pocket, which proved to
be a bag, with buttons in it-I said, “ Here is the watch"-he said, “No, i
is not; the other boy has got it; a boy named Warren, you will find him in
the mews"--I then watched for Warren, and Hooper brought me this watch-
I afterwards found Warren—I said, “Your name is Warren"— he said, “Yes"
- I said, “I apprehend you for a watch"-he said, “Yes, I know that”-I
said, “The charge against you is for being with Essex, and stealing a watch
- he said, “That is a lie; he gave it me to take care of; I was always told
I should be transported for seven years, and if I am I must serve it."
WILLIAM GEORGE HOOPER. I live in Davis's Mews with my father, and
Warren lodges at my fathers- I went home to breakfast one morning, and
Warren said to me, “ I have got something in my pocket worth 51."— I said,
“ What is that ?”—he said, “Oh, it is a watch”- I said, “Where did you get
it ?”—be said, “I did not get it anywhere ; Bob Essex gave it me to mind till
he came out"--I afterwards saw a boy named Whiffin, and he said to Warren,
“The policeman says, if you give up the watch, there will be no more to do
with you”-on that Warren gave me a watch-I took it over to the policeman
immediately.
Warren. Essex said he would give me a shilling if I would mind it for
