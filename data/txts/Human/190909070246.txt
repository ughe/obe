RAY MARKS, 260, Queens Road, Upton Park, wife of David Marks On July 26, at 3.5 p.m., I was walking home in Queens Road when I saw a cart and horse galloping along zig-zagging from side to side of the road. The horse knocked the child down. I screamed and shouted to stop the men but no notice was taken. I assisted the father to take the child to the doctor.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Rentoul. The men tried to get out of the way of the child. I saw two men in the cart, but I did not see what they were doing.
 Re-examined. The men made no effort to stop—they drove right away. One man 'held the whip and the other the reins. BEN FILDMAN, 337, Queen's Road, cycle maker. On July 26, at 3 p.m., I saw a man and a boy running and shouting after a light spring cart in which Brett was driving, Heath sitting beside him They were driving eight to ten miles an hour on the near side. I then saw the child lying in the road. I holloaed out to them to stop, jumped on to a bicycle which was standing there and rode after them about 1 £ miles. Heath seemed to be trying to get Brett to stop, but the cart seemed to increase its pace. I spoke to a Constable, who took my bicycle and rode after them. There was no other traffic in the road, but with the stalls all along one side they could not see anyone very well who stepped out from a stall.
 Police-constable  JOHN GOWING , 640 K. On July 26, at about 3.20, I was in Katharine Road, East Ham, when I saw a pony and trap containing the two prisoners. Brett was holding the reins. Heath sitting on the offside had the whip in his hand whipping the pony, which was galloping full speed. Fildman spoke to me. I mounted his bicycle and followed the cart through Forest Road. blowing my whistle at intervals. As I did so Heath kept looking round at me and urging the pony. I followed them about four miles to Fernside, Beckton, when Sergeant Gale also took up the chase I on a bicycle, and we succeeded in stopping them. The pony was very much exhausted, bleeding from the flanks, the blood running down its hind legs. Its hind shoes were worn and the hoofs broken The prisoners were taken to East Ham Police Station, Brett was charged with being drunk in charge of a vehicle, Heath with being drunk and concerned with Brett in driving to the common danger Brett was very drunk. Heath was not so drunk, but very much excited—he knew what he was doing; he was in liquor but not so badly as Brett. I was informed of the present charge and inter viewed the father, but the accident was in West Ham another juris diction, where they were charged with the offence.
 Cross-examined by Mr. Rentoul. Heath was not trying to get Brett to pull up; he was urging the pony on and lashing it cruelly The reins were taken from Brett and the whip from Heath. Heath was not so drunk but that he knew what he was doing he was very much excited. Brett was very drunk.
