1321. JOHN FROST, stealing 1 pair of boots, value 38. the goods of
the directors of the poor of the parish of St. Pancras: and MARY ANN
WARD, feloniously receiving the same.
SARAH ANTHONY. I am single. On 7th May, between four and five
o'cloek in the evening, I Was in St. Giles's burial-ground- portion of
St. Pancras' workhouse looks into the barial-ground-] saw Frost at the
workhouse windowwhe whistled, which attraeted Ward's attention, and she
came up-Frost got out of the window on the leads, and I saw him with a
pair of boots in bis hand-be threw them into the burial-ground, and Ward
took them upesome conversation passed between them--I did not hear what
it was-they conversed about ten minutes-] left the ground in Ward's com-
pany-I saw her take the boots away-she said she was going to take them
home--] did not see Frost afterwards.
WILLIAM M LACHLIN. I am a pawnbroker. I produce these boots, which
I took in from Ward.
GEORGE JOHN FARRANT. I am master of the stone-yard at St. Paneres'
workhouse. I know these boots, they are the property of the directors of
the poor of that parish-on 7th May, Frost was an inmate there-Ward had
been in the bouse, but she was then sleeping in the casual ward-] afterwards
took her at No. 10, hillips'-buildings-) told ber she was charged with
receiving a pair of boots of Frost-her mother gave me the duplicate of them.
WILLIAM JOHN Brown (policeman, $67). I took Frost at the workhouse
-I asked if he knew what he was charged with-h said he did; he took the
boots for the purpose of wearing them when he came out.
Frost. Q. Did I not say that the boots were given to me A. You did
say so afterwards, but not then.
Frost's Defence, On 24th April I was admitted into the infirmary for an
affection of the lungs, which I have been suffering from yearly, and which
incapacitates me for labour; I applied to Mr. Eaton for a pair of shoes; I
received these now produced from Mr. Dalton on the Wednesday following
Ward called to see me, and was refused admittance ; I saw her at the window,
and she was telling me of her distress; I gave ber the shoes, telling ber they were
mine, and as such I deemed them, having had another pair given me which I
could wear; if I have done wrong I am sorry for it.
STEPHEN BULMER DALTON. I did not give him any shoes at all.
Frost. I applied to Mr. Eaton for them his attention was called away by
the baker, and a gentleman said, 44 Frost wants a pair of boots,' and you took
these and gave them to me.
