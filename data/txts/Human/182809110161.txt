 1875.  SUSAN HIBBERT and ELIZABETH MEARS were indicted for stealing, on the 22d of August , 1 coat, value 25s.; 1 waistcoat, value 7s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 8s., and 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Richard Moltoff , from his person.
 RICHARD MOLTOFF. I am a  fur dyer, and live in Harris-square, Kingsland-road. On the night of the 22d of August, I was going home with a bundle containing these articles - I met Hibbert, who asked me to go home with her; I went to a house in Red-lion-court, and up one pair of stairs; I put my bundle down between the bed and the table - I did not intend to stop there all night, but I threw myself on the bed, and Hibbert threw herself upon me - there was no one in the room when we went in, but I presently saw a man come into the room on his hands and knees - Hibbert took hold of the back of my neck, and held my mouth; I recovered myself as soon as I could, and threw myself off the bed, but the man and the bundle were gone - both the prisoners were then in the room, and another woman - the prisoners instantly blinded and muzzled me - the third woman disappeared; I told the two prisoners I knew them both, and they had better give me the bundle; I got down into the court, and the street door was immediately shut against me - I knocked, but there was no answer - I got the watchman, and he knocked, but could not get in; I went to the watch-house, but could not get in there; I then went to Kingsland-road, and met the Bow-street patrol - they got in, searched that house and the next, but nothing was found.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where were you going? A. Home; it might be a quarter past twelve o'clock, or rather more - I had had part of a pot or two of beer at a shopmate's house: I am certain we had no more; I was not tipsy then - I never said that it was a woman that robbed me - the women muzzled me - no woman came in the room till I had sprung off the bed - I am married.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am a watchman. On the 22d of August, I was crying half-past twelve o'clock in Hackney-road - some one called me; the watchman said "This man has been robbed in the second court in Kingsland-road" - the prosecutor did not appear drunk - he spoke very well; when the Bow-street officer went, I saw the two prisoners - the prosecutor did not know them till they got up, and put their clothes on.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not Jefferies, the officer, say he would have nothing more to do with it? A. Yes, in coming out of the court he did say so; the prosecutor was there, and must have heard it; he was rambling on at a terrible rate - no property was found - not on any man; he said he had been robbed in the last house, and his property was brought and put into another house.
   WILLIAM TITHERTON. I am a Bow-street patrol. I went to the house about ten minutes past two o'clock - the prosecutor said he had been robbed of a suit of clothes - we knocked several times, but could get no admittance; when we got in, we found the two prisoners in bed with a young man; the prosecutor swore to Mears, but not to Hibbert till about half-past ten o'clock the next morning - they denied having anything to do with him: he appeared sober at two o'clock, when he spoke to me.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you search both the houses? A. We searched two houses - there was a third house we did not go into.
   JAMES HILL. I am a headborough. I produce the bundle which the prosecutor gave me.
   RICHARD MOLTOFF. A man and woman came to me at the office, and said if I would stop the prosecution they would tell me where the bundle was - I said I only wanted my property, and it was left at a public-house.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1876.  WILLIAM PYMM was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of July , an order for the payment of 20l. the property of William James Cleaver , his master.
 WILLIAM JAMES CLEAVER. I am a  bookseller, and live near Portman-square; the prisoner was in my employ - I gave him a cheque for 20l. upon Mr. Laurie, the army agent in the Adelphi, to go and get change on the 3d of July, but he never returned; he had been with me about seven weeks as a porter and assistant - I did not see him again till the 11th of August, when he surrendered.
 Prisoner. Q. Was the money your own? A. Yes, it was; it was not Mr. Maxwell's; I am the prisoner's master.
   PATRICK LAURIE. I paid the cheque on the 3d of August, I believe, to the prisoner - but when I paid the cheque, I did not observe his features.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I am an officer. The prisoner came to the Police-office on the 11th of August, just before eight o'clock - he said he wanted to give himself up; I said, why - he said, "Go to Mr. Cleaver, and he will tell you," and in going along, he said, "I may as well he prosecuted first as at last;" when I got to Mr. Cleaver's house, Mr. Cleaver gave him in charge for taking a cheque and not returning - he said, he did go away about six weeks ago; I asked where he slept last, he said seventy miles off.
 GUILTY. Aged 24. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury- Confined 4 Months.
