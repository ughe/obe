EDWARD SPENCER SCOTT. I am a Bachelor of Medicine and am
house surgeon at the London Hospital-about 7.15 on December 26th
the deceased was brought in in a collapsed condition--he was undressed
and put to bed-I examined him and found that he had a wound about
2 in. long about the level of the sixth and seventh costal cartilages where
they join the breast bone-it was not the bony part of the ribs, but the
cartilagethere was no visible bleeding, but there were signs of hemorrbage
going on internally-I came to the conclusion that it was necessary to
perform an operation sent for Mr. Perey Furnival, who, when he arrived,
stitched up the heart-I assisted him-that was an absolutely necessary
operation-we found that the wound had penetrated the heart wall-it
had not actually gone into the cavity of the heart-there was a great

deal of bleeding going on at the time-he improved after the operation,

28th, he died-I got to him about five minutes after he was dead-if we
had not performed the operation, in my opinion the injury would have
been fatal within a few hours; as it was he lived for about twenty-eight

the cause of death was due to the stab in the chest, the wound in the
