Court. Q. If you had bung on your red board, would not the Forest
gate man, when he saw the Ipswich train pass, come out and hold up his
flag? A. It would have been the case, but we do not do so with ordinary
trains, only with special, or extra trains-this Fire-fly engine was an
extra train, but we were not aware that it was coming until it approached
the station--that train and the Ipswich up-train were at the station together
--we did not hang a red board on it, because it is not the rule--I cannot say
why it is not the rule-if the board had been hung up, it would have certified
at Forest-gate that a train was following, the driver of the second train being
at the place at the same time as the first train, would know that there was a
train before him.

Mr. CHAMBERS. Q. Was it not the daty of the Romford station-master,
when the truck-train started from the station, to attach to the preceding train
a red board by day, or an extra lamp by night? 4. I am not aware of such
a regulation-I have the printed regulations given me by the station-master
-I am aware that there is such a regulation in the book-it was not obeyed
op this occasion-it is not customary, unless with special trains-it is fre-
quently done-I have seen them pass and repass with boards and extra
lights by night-the regulation is that the station must at all times be in charge
of some competent person to give the signal to each passing train—I should
say if the signal is not obeyed by the approaching train, it is the duty of the
signal-man to hold up his hand; but it was obeyed on this occasion--if it was
not obeyed, he would advance and hold up his hands—the windlass is a hun-
dred and fifty yards off - the station-man never advances towards the signal
to see whether it is obeyed-if I was at the windlass, and the train did not
obey the signal, I should walk into the middle of the line, and hold up my
hand-Forest-gate is about a mile from Stratford-we bave at Ilford two
porters and the station-clerk by day, and one by night I have the care of
the signals by day-I should go off my duty to unload the cattle-truck, if I
was required; and if I saw anything approaching either way, I should leave
my work and go to the signal - the windlass that works the fan is a handred
and twenty yards off it in a straight line--you can see the working of the fan
at the Ilford station from the windlass-in foggy weather, a red light hangs
below the bottom fan, and it can be observed from the station whether it is
in view or not--the red light is enough to show the extent of the dauger--if
you let a green fan down, that means caution—I do not know what the
yellow indicates-the first red is a caution to go slowly the one red below
the green is to notify that there is something in advance the two reds mean
to stop altogether; they denote danger-in foggy weather the lamp is sub-
pended from the centre of the bottom red fan; as the windlass winds the
lan up, the light is thrown up with it I know the Stratford station you
cannot see round the curve in foggy weather, nor in any weather I do not
