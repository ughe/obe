BENJAMIN BEAVIS. I am headborough. I heard the cry of Stop thief! and saw a person in a white great coat run down an alley; I said, he was gone down the alley, and they went and got him - when he returned there was no hat on his head.
   WILLIAM ANTHONY. I am a watchman. I went in company with Buck and Taylor - the prisoner was taken at a door at the bottom of the steps; I saw this hat on his head; I took it off, and gave it to Buck.
   JOSEPH BUCK. I heard an alarm, and saw the prisoner in the passage - he was taken to the watch-house, and this hat was given to me.
   GEORGE TAYLOR. I saw the prisoner, and helped to secure him; I saw the hat taken from him; a handkerchief was in it.
   CHARLES BRETT. I went down the alley, and saw the prisoner in a passage with a hat on his head.
 Prisoner. I saw the hat in the road; I took it up, and, as it rained, I put it on my head; I took my brother's supper, and was returning - they came and asked me what I had got there - I said a hat which I had found.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined Four Months.
 1558.  THOMAS HATWOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s.; 1 hat, value 5s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s.; 1 comb, value 1/2d., and 1 sovereign, the property of William Miles , from his person.
 WILLIAM MILES. I live in the City-road, and am a  Smith. On the night of the 29th of July I went to a beanfeast at Holloway; I was rather tipsy, and sat down by the side of the road to wait for my companions - the watchman came - and then two men came, who said they could take me to the cottage in the bric-kfield - one of them left me, and the other went with me to a high hill - I awoke at about six o'clock - I missed a sovereign, a coat, and 2 1/2d., which I am sure I had after I left the beanfeast - I also lost my shoes, my hat, and two handkerchiefs; I do not know who the man was, but he had a straw hat and a white jacket on - the hat and handkerchiefs were left in the field.
   JAMES MEDLEYCOT. I am a patrol. I took the prisoner on the 31st of July in Tenter-row, Shoreditch - I found these shoes on his feet, and on his person a comb, a knife, and a shilling.
 Prisoner's Defence. I bought the shoes on the Sunday morning, at Battle-bridge - I have had the coat six months.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     NOT GUILTY.
 1559.  MARGARET KEANE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of July , 1 watch, value 4l., and 4 shillings the property of Richard Heap , from his person.
 RICHARD HEAP. I am a  watchmaker. I met the prisoner on Friday night, the 7th of July, between eleven and twelve o'clock; she invited me to her apartment, Bennet-street, Westminster, and I went to bed there - I put my breeches under the pillow with my watch; I awoke at five o'clock, and missed her - I found my breeches on the pillow, but the watch gone - I have never seen it since.
   ALEXANDER CANNON. I live in Bennet-street. The prisoner hired a room in my house on this day, and said her husband was in the Coldstream Guards.
 Prisoner's Defence. He went home with me. I asked what money he had - he said he had but one shilling, and would give me no more till the morning - I went in the morning and got a watchman; but he said he would not take any man in the room; he had no watch that I saw.
 PROSECUTOR. I swear I had a watch, and 4s. in a small leather purse, which was likewise taken - she asked for no money that night, but said, in the morning, she would make all right - I was a little in liquor when I met her.
 JURY. Q. When did you see your watch last? A. About an hour before - it was then past eleven o'clock - I did not see any other young woman - she took the key inside and locked the door.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1560.  JOHN LAWRENCE was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the goods of Edward Pritchett , from his person.
 EDWARD PRITCHETT. On the 30th of July I was under an arch-way, leading to  Palace-yard- when I had been there half an hour the officer told me I had lost my handkerchief - I saw the prisoner then in custody - I had not seen him before, nor felt any pull at my pocket - I felt my handkerchief safe just before.
   WILLIAM WHEATLEY. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner following the prosecutor - he put his hand into his pocket, and then put it into his left breast - I ran after the gentleman, and Tyrrell took the handkerchief from the prisoner.
   ROBERT TYRRELL. I took this handkerchief from the prisoner's left breast-pocket - we had followed him, but I did not see him take it - he said "Don't hurt me, I will go quietly."
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1561.  MARY MORIARTY was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of July , 1 purse, value 2d.; 1 crown, 2 half-crowns, 5 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the property of John Hurst , from his person.
 JOHN HURST. I was in the tap-room of a public-house, in St. Giles'. On the 23d of July, in the evening, the prisoner came in with one or two more persons, who went out again - she came and sat down by my side a little while, and then went out; she had some of my beer when she returned, and sat by me again, and then we went to another house and had some porter; I then went to a room with her; I sat down on a bed; she turned herself, and put her hand into my trousers pocket, and took out my purse, which had one crown, two half-crowns, five shillings, and two sixpences in it; I felt her hand coming out of my pocket, and said "You hussy, you have robbed me of my purse;" she denied it, and went to quit the room; the people below asked what was the matter; they then sent for an officer, who searched her; but I have never got the purse again.
