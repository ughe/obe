1909. ELIZABETH FULLER was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 1 pillow, value 1s.; 1 bolster, value 2s.; 1 quilt, value 3s.; 1 frying-pan, value 10d.; 1 saucepan, value 9d.; 1 teaboard, value 6d., and 2 chairs, value 2s., the goods of Elizabeth Purland, widow.
 ELIZABETH PURLAND. I am a widow, and live in the  Curtain-road. The prisoner lodged with me one week - I did not know she was going, but she did go; I went to see if my property was safe, and missed the articles stated - these are them.
   REBECCA DAVIS. The prisoner brought a pillow and a bolster to me in July; she said she was in great distress and cried very much - I bought this saucepan and fryingpan of her.
   HENRY FREAKS. I am a pawnbroker. I have a rug pawned by the prisoner on the 26th of July for 1s.
   CHARLES CONSTABLE. I am an officer. I took up the prisoner, and found this duplicate of the rug on her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I throw myself on the mercy of the Court.
     GUILTY. Aged 50. Confined One Month.
 1910.  PATRICK GRAY was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , 3 lbs. weight of pork, value 2s. 6d. the goods of Anthony Hagmire.
 ANTHONY HAGMIRE. I am a  pork-butcher, and live in Ratcliff-highway. I lost a loin of pork on the 9th of August; I was just outside the door - I went after the prisoner and found the pork on him; he had got about ten yards - it was my pork; he said he would pay for it, and at the watch-house he pulled some money out - he said before the Magistrate that he had never been in the shop.
   ELIZABETH STALL. I am servant to Mr. Hagmire. I saw the prisoner come and take this pork from the window - he came into the shop, and stood a few minutes, as if to have it weighed; he then went out with it, and I told Mr. Hagmire, who followed him; it was about ten o'clock on Saturday night - when I saw him go out I got under the flap and followed him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never was in the shop.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1911.  DANIEL HOLLIS was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 1 coat, value 35s.; 1 pair of trousers, value 15s.; 3 waistcoats, value 18s.; 2 pairs of stockings, value 1s. 6d.; 1 shirt, value 5s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 6s.; 1 sovereign, and 1 half-sovereign, the property of James White.
   JAMES WHITE. I am a journeyman shoemaker, and live in New Tothill-street, Westminster; the prisoner lodged in the same house. On the 26th of June I went home about ten o'clock in the evening, and missed the shoes from a basket - I then found the lock of my box was wrenched; I then missed all these articles stated - I had seen them safe at seven o'clock, when I left work; the money was in the box, and that was gone - a shoulder-stick and some tools were taken from my bench; the prisoner did not return.
   JOSEPH HARRIS. I am a shoemaker, and live in the same house. I saw the prisoner in the course of this month - he sent for me to the One Tun public-house; I asked where he had been: he said at different places round the country, and that on the 26th of June he came home, found us all out, and he took a chisel off my seat, wrenched the lock off this box, and took out the money and clothes, and took the shoes out of the basket.
   JOHN KILBY. I am a constable, and took up the prisoner; I found this waistcoat on his person, and these shoes were at a pawnbroker's, where the prisoner said he had pawned them; I found this shoulder-stick and tools at his lodging.
 Prisoner's Defence. The man who has come against me is a bad character himself; the landlord of the Cock public-house will not suffer him to come into his house, for stealing some cloth from a hawker, and living with a girl whom he cannot support, except by prostitution or theft; he told me he had been chased out of the place he lived in, for stealing a tea-caddy.
   JOSEPH HARRIS. I was never charged with theft.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 1912.  WILLIAM HARRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , 1 watch, value 2l.; 4 spoons, value 30s., and 1 pair of sugar-tongs, value 10s., the goods of James Ralph.
 MR. JAMES RALPH. I lost a watch, four spoons, and a pair of sugar-tongs, on the 7th of August; the prisoner had been my  servant for about six weeks - he was a very good servant, and I was much surprised, as I had seen all these articles safe about half an hour before; they were taken from my kitchen; my servant came and said the prisoner was gone - he did not return at night: I had paid him part of his wages.
   HENRY REID. I apprehended the prisoner on the 12th of August, and asked how he came to do this; he said he did not know, but it came into his head all at once - I asked if he had taken any spoons; he said Yes, two, and had sold them and the watch for 30s.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never had them.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
