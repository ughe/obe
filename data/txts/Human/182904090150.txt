JOHN SWAIN. I am an officer. I went to Bull's Headcourt, but could find no such person as Lane.
   RICHARD FINCH. I took another bill on Lane from the prisoner - I paid that away, and have not seen it since.
 Prisoner's Defence. This business was a transaction of my brother's, John Green; it was he that brought orders to me, and requested me to send the goods, saying Mr. Lane was a responsible person, and the bill would be paid; I had sent one lot of goods, for which he gave me a bill; that was paid; in consequence of that he came to me for other goods, and I had them sent - he gave me the bill which is now in question; it was entirely his transaction, and I depended on him: I never saw Lane myself, nor knew whether any such person lived there or not - he took the goods from me, and brought the bill to me accepted.
   BARNARD BOYLE. I am clerk to a notary. I presented this bill for 15l. 6s. on James Lane, at No. 4, Bull's Head-court, Snow-hill; I do not recollect who gave the answer, but it was, "Pay to-morrow," and the money was paid the next day.
   RICHARD FINCH re-examined. When he gave me the bill, it had the acceptance of James Lane on it; the reason the other bill was for only 18l. was, he asked me if I would have an 18l. bill, I said, Yes, as I wanted it to pay my brassman.
 JURY to  JAMES JOHNSON. Q. Have you any remembrance of his giving you any orders to pay a bill that might be presented to your house? A. No, he never mentioned such a thing - I gave an answer to this bill, that Mr. Green was not there; I did not say when it would be paid - the prisoner asked me if there was a bill presented, and I said, Yes, and gave him the memorandum, and he said,"Tell them I will call to-morrow."
 COURT. Q. Did  John Green ever give you any orders? A. No.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. This conversation took place after the bill had been brought? A. Yes.
 JURY. Q. Had you any previous notice that a bill would be brought? A. No; he came and asked me if there had not been a bill brought - I am quite sure there has not been a person of the name of Lane lodging there these four years, since I have been in the house; I was in the habit of taking him letters, and several bills came from the bankers, and I gave answer he was not there, or something of that kind.
 GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 47. Recommended to mercy; the Jury believing he intended to pay the bill; and was greatly influenced by his brother.

THOMAS GEORGE HAWKINS and JOHN BALLARD were indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Smith Carr , on the 17th of March , at St. Andrews, Holborn, and stealing therein 1 chest, value 7s.; 1 tin-box, value 2s.; 50 sovereigns, 10 crowns, 6 half-crowns, 30 shillings, 4 sixpences, and three 20l. Bank-notes, his property, against the Statute; and GEORGE BALLARD and ANN (HIS WIFE) were indicted for feloniously receiving, on the same day, at the same parish, 22 sovereigns, part and parcel of the said monies, they well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute.
 MR. SMITH conducted the prosecution.
 MR.  JOHN SMITH CARR. I am a shopkeer, and live at No. 90, Charles-street, Hatton-garden, in the parish of St. Andrew, Holborn. I occupy the two shops and two attics, in which I have two persons put in to take care of the house; it is a corner house - I let the house to different lodgers; I let one kitchen to a Mr. Ewer, the other kitchen I keep for the accommodation of the lodgers - the cellar may be about ten yards from the kitchen-door - I leave my shop every night for my residence at Holloway; I left it on Tuesday evening, the 17th of March, as near half-past nine o'clock as I can recollect - before I left it, I took down my cash-box into my cellar, in which was enclosed three 20l. notes, I can swear, but I believe there were four, upwards of 50 sovereigns, from 5l. to 7l. in silver, and amongst the silver was a bad crown-piece, with a piece cut out of the edge of it nearly the eighth of an inch, and two bills of exchange - I locked my cash-box, put it into an iron chest in the cellar; I locked the chest and the cellar-door - my shop was shutting up at this time; I waited till I saw the shop-doors closed, and then set off for Holloway - the next morning I received some information, a little after nine o'clock, and went down into the cellar with a light; I found the cellar-door broken open, and the iron chest gone, and a chisel was lying on the ledge where the iron-chest had been placed - the chisel is in the possession of the constable; I should know it again- I found my iron-chest the same morning at the house of a chimney-sweeper, named Brown, in Feather's-court, Fox-court - I have no doubt it was my chest; the prisoner Hawkins came to live with me, I think in December, 1826, and left me last September; he must have seen me going down stairs to deposit my cash-box in the cellar, as as it has been my custom for the last six months - the kitchen is at the corner of Charles-street.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You sleep at Holloway? A. Yes. Mr. Ewer, Miss Wellyer and some other persons lodge at my house in Charles-street, some of them had servants; one of their servants is here- I always sleep at Holloway; it is there my family reside.
   JAMES OWEN. I am servant to Mr. Ewer. He lodges at Mr. Carr's; on the night of the 17th of March, I left the kitchen window shut down but not fastened, and one shutter shut - I cannot tell what time of night it was; I have been there a year and a quarter - and that was the usual mode of leaving the window and shutter; I went down the next morning, and saw the kitchen door open which had been fastened with a padlock; the padlock was hanging by the hasp, but the hasp had been forced out- the window sash was up, and the shutters both back; this was about nine o'clock - I did not observe the cellar door then, but I did afterwards, and saw a piece had been broken out of it; I did not go into the cellar.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How many servants were in the house altogether at that time? A. Four- some men and some women; I left the house at nine o'clock at night; I cannot say that I left the other servants all up - I suppose they would not be gone to bed; they are not here; I do not know whether they were up or not.
