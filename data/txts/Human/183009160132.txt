Yes - his name is William Brickley, the same as the prisoners; there are many of that name - he lived at the bottom of Cartwright-square; he went into the hospital - I have not seen him since; only us three were working on board the Mentor - it was ona Monday; it was not any particular Monday - I am a Roman Catholic; it was not a particuday, in the calendar - when we get a job we are not particular about days.
 Q. What makes you know it was the 7th of June? A. Because I heard the people coming after this man, and heard it was the 7th of June - the prisoner lived in Cartwright-street; he went to harvest work a few days after this - he had employed me to this job, and paid me my wages; the captain was to pay him - it was the 7th of June that he paid me, as the job was finished at eight o'clock on the 7th.
 Q. What is your reason for saying it was the 7th? A. Because I kept it in my head; I did not hear he was accused of this till a fortnight or three weeks ago - I knew it was the 7th of June, because we never worked together since - the Mentor has sailed; I do not know whether I went to Mass on Trinity-Sunday - I kept the 7th of June in my mind, seeing these people were after the man that night; I saw two officers looking for him about ten o'clock that night - they came to his apartment; he was not at home - I know I had been working with him that day, and thinking there might be something serious, I kept it in mind; I did not make a memorandum of it - I can neither read nor write; the Mentor sailed that night - I lived in the same house as the prisoner.
 HENRY DAVIS. I believe this man is the prisoner's brother.
 MR. PHILLIPS to Witness. Q. On your oath, did you ever pass as this man's brother? A. I never did - I did not go to see him in gaol; I was not present when he was taken.
 HENRY DAVIS. That man was standing at the corner when I gave the Policeman information, and he said that was his jacket - that is my reason for saying I thought he was his brother.
   JOHN HARRIGAN. I never owned the jacket - I never saw it.
   GEORGE ORGER. I am sure the prisoner said he was at the public-house on the day the man was hired.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1790.    WILLIAM ARMSTEAD was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 1 pair of trousers, value 4s., the goods of Hugh Foden.
   HUGH FODEN. I am a tailor, and lodge in Haberdasher-street, Hoxton, in the garret; the prisoner lodged in the next room. On a Sunday afternoon, in July, at four o'clock. I left my apprentice and the prisoner in the room; the trousers were there then - I returned on Tuesday, and they were gone; the prisoner had left at eight o'clock on the Sunday evening - he was taken into custody on the Friday, with them in his possession.
 Prisoner. Q. Have you not lent them to me several times? A. I never lent him these; I have lent him those I have on - he made no defence when I took him; I did not know he intended to take them.
   THOMAS BURNS. I was one of the new Police. I apprehended the prisoner on the 30th of July, and told him it was for stealing the trousers, with a hat and handkerchief; he acknowledged taking the trousers, but did not say they were lent to him.
 Prisoner's Defece. I took them without a felonious intent - I meant to return them in a few hours.
   HUGH FODEN. He worked with me for about six weeks down to this time.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1791.  MARGARET AMBROSE was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of August , 1 coat, value 2l.; 1 pair of trousers, value 18s.; 1 waistcoat, value 9s.; 1 handkerchief, value 6d., and 1 pair of braces, value 6d., the goods of John Duffey.
 JOHN DUFFEY. I am a  labourer, and live in Springgarden-place, Pimlico. On the 9th of August I was in Lisson-grove, in liquor - I met the prisoner, and went with her to No.3, Dean-street, Lisson-grove, between seven and eight o'clock in the morning; I recollect going into the house, and sitting on a chair in the first-floor back room; I recollect nothing after that till between two and three o'clock in the afternoon, when a woman came and awoke me - I found I was undressed in bed, and all my clothes gone; I swear she is the woman who was with me - I had seen her before, but never went with her before; I met her about five o'clock next morning, in John-street, and asked her where my clothes were - she denied knowing any thing of them; I gave her in charge - she then said she had pawned them; they were produced at the office.
   GEORGE THOMPSON. I am apprentice to Mr. Trail, of Chapel-street, Edgware-road. On Monday morning, between seven and eight o'clock, the prisoner pawned this coat, waistcoat, trousers, and handkerchief, in the name of Ellen Newman, of Cato-street, for 10s.; I am positive she is the woman.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   CHARLES CLARK. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner on the 10th of August; she told me she had given the braces to an apple-woman in the street, and I got them from the apple-woman.
 Prisoner's Defence. I met him very drunk - he asked me to take him home, and only had 6d.; which he paid for the room; he said he had no money, and sent me to pawn the things - I lost the duplicate, spent the money, and did not go back till next morning, when I was given in charge; I only got 10s. on them.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Transported for Seven Years.
