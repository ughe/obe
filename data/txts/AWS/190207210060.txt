DIMSDALE, Mayor.
-I have frequently noticed that when a man has been the worse for drink,
and has had a great shock, that he has partially recovered his senses.
HARRY STANLEY TURNER. I am a registered medical practitioner, and
live at 211, Great Road, Battersea-] was called to 3, Barmour Street on
June 28th at 2.15 p.m.-I saw the deceased there-ghe was lying on the
bed wearing a pink chemise, which was stained with blood-I examined
her-she had a wound on her left side near the hip bone-I came to the
conclusion that the wound was inflieted with a knife when the chemise
was doubled on itself-there were two cuts on the chemise-this knife
would be capable of inflicting the wound-I ordered her removal to the
hospital.
Cross-ezamined. I should describe the point of the knife as being fairly
sharp-if the chemise was doubled it would require some force to inflict
the wound-I did not know the depth of the wound at the time-it had
not broken any bone.
Re-ezamined. I do not think a very heavy blow would be required to
cause the wound.
BERTRAM JoNEs, M.R.O.S., L.R.O.P I am house surgeon at St. Thomas's
Hospital-the deceased was brought to the hospita about 4.30 p.m. on
June 28th-I found a wound penetrating the abdominal wallit was about
6 inches deep and 2 inch in breadth-we performed an operation on her at
6.30 that night-at 1 a.m. on Monday morning peritonitis set in, and
we performed another operation, but it had no beneficial effect, and she
died of acute peritonitis at 1 p.m. on Monday-this knife would be capable
of making that wound, and corresponds in breadth and depth with the
flesh wound-it must have been driven with some considerable distance
judging from the sharp point-I think a small amount of violence would
inflict the wound.
The prisoner's statement before magistrate. I had been drinking very
hard, and have no recolleetion of what happened, and I have no witnesses.
to call."
GUITy. Strongly recommended to mercy by the Jury. Death.
