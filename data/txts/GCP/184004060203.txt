MARY THORNTON. I am the mother of the last witness-she resides
with me in a cottage in Pocock's-fields. On Tuesday morning, the 17th of
March, I sent her to Mr. Templeman's cottage with some writing-paper-
she returned, and made a communication to me I then went to Mr. Temple
man's coltage, and knocked at the door no answer was returned-I then
went to the bed-room window-it was quite closed there was a curtain
and drawn-blind to it-I looked over the curtain, and saw the bed there
was no one in it-I saw Mr. Templeman on the floor, about a yard from
the bed, lying with his feet towards the fire-place, and his head towards the
parlour door-I then went round to the sitting-room window, and found it
was open-I had known Mr. Templeman nearly four years-three years
and a half full he had been in the neighbourhood for about two months
I had been in the habit of washing and charing for him, fetching his
errands, and such like the parlour window was always closed previous to
this morning I never saw it open--it used to be fastened with a little
button just below where it was broken- it had been broken a long time-it
was quite a small hole, nearly close to the button there was no difference
in the hole that morning, except that the paper was gone, and, I believe,
a bit of glass-- it had been fastened with a bit of glass, and paper on it
--when the paper was gone any body could put their hand in and open it
--the window is quite big enough for a man to get in when it is open-I
had been to the house the evening before, at a quarter before six o'clock,
and left about five minutes before six o'clock he usually went to bed
about six o'clock he never was up afterwards, unless any body was there
- he did not have any beer on Monday evening, to my knowledge I have
known the prisoner about four or five monthshe had been pot-man at the
Barnsbury Castle public-house I have seen him serve Mr. Templeman
with beer-I know the Jarvises- they live nearly close to Mr. Temple-
man's-it is one of the four cottages I have heard that the prisoner was
on visiting terms at Jarvis's, and I have seen him go in repeatedly I think
it must be nearly a fortnight before this affair that I saw him there I did
not see any thing of Mrs. Jarvis on the Monday evening I saw her in the
fore-part of the day at her own door, or else in the ground she had her
child with her.
Q. Did you see any money that day at Mr. Teinpleman's cottage
Yes, he showed me some, and told me had been receiving some rents-it
was silver, and was in a little mahogany box-I had never seen that box
before-he put it on the table where he took it from to show me the money
- the table stood in the midale of the room he had never shown me any
money before.

serve Mr. Templeman with beer is that quite correct? 4. Quite--I ne-

see him, and that will settle it-I might have said I thought, although I
was certain the prisoner used to come round to serve the neighbours with
beer-it was seldom I had any beer, but when I did he served me.
