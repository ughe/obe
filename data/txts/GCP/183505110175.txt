1291. NATHANIEL EBBS was indicted for embezzlement.
Mr. Clarkson conducted the Prosecution.Nuwa
ALFRED Rosling." "I am a timber-merchant and was proprietor of saw-
mills, at Hackney, but they have since been burnt down. The prisoner
was in my service about twelve months—his duty was to take orders, and
receive cash-it was his duty to pay over every evening the cash received
of London customers-Kewell was my principal clerk -the prisoner used
to give me the amounts he received, and I used to enter them in the cash-book
in his presence, on the day on which he received them from the customers
I gave the customers credit for the amounts-Thompson, of Carnaby-
street, was a customer-on the 26th of March, the prisoner did not account
to me for 21. due from Thompson-he has never accounted for it-he did
not account to me for 41. 78., on the 9th of April, from Batista, Moltine,
and Co., of Baldwin's-gardens-he has never accounted for it-he never ac-
counted for 178. received from Trayler, of Bloomsbury-market-in con-
sequence of certain discoveries which I made, I charged him with this
he was afterwards taken into custody- I have my books here,
Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. I suppose you ascertained that
he bore a good character ? 4. I did- I have no entry about Thompson on
the 26th of March the entry would be made by me or Kewell—the
prisoner was not called upon to put his initials to the book to signify that the
entries were correct-he used to add up the sum total with me, and if it
did not agree, reference was made to it he had his order-book, in wbich
he made entries—he had delivered that up to me--I have not brought it
here-I never thought it necessary--that is the book from which he made
his original entries, and communicated the result to me, there is an entry
on the 24th, of 21. from Thompson, and two days before, there is an entry
of Thompson's it was an amount paid by instalments he has not ac-
counted for 4l. at one time, from Thompson.
Q. Do you know that he has, in some instances, lent customers money
to pay your balances, in order to get a fresh order from them? 2. I have
ascertained, since he has been in custody, that he has lent my money
to them he has received my money of one customer, and lent it to another
to take up a bill, to my prejudice--he advanced 21. 10s. to Mr. Berkley, to
take up a bill of 221. 58. 11d.-I understand from the prisoner that that
was out of 41. received from Batista and Co.-the 178. from Trayler, is a
balance of an account of 91. 175.- I think he once paid me 14s. more than
he had received from a Mr. Salter.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Had he authority from you to apply money which
received from one customer to assist another in making payments ? A.
Certainly not; I should not have allowed it-he never paid 4l. from
Thompson in one payment.
