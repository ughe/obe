2025. RICHARD ILES , stealing 1 spoke-shave, value 2 s  .; the goods of Robert Fitch ; 1 hammer, 1 screw-driver, and 1 pair of compasses, value 4 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Samuel Browning . ROBERT FITCH. I am a carpenter, and was working at Berners-street, Greenwich. The prisoner was working there, in the employ of the con tractor—on 14th Aug. I lost this spoke-shave ( produced )—I am sure it is mine. SAMUEL BROWNING. I was working at the building—I lost a screw-driver and pair of compasses—these are them.
   Prisoner. Q . Were not you, and I, and the builder's son, drinking all day from eleven o'clock till we left off? A.  Part of the time we were—I was tipsy, and fell between the joists. JOHN CARPENTER( policeman , 84 R ). I received information, went to a pawnbrokers, and saw these tools handed to the prisoner—he paid money for them—he took part of them—I took the rest—I said, "These are the things I am in search of, and I am in search of you also for stealing them"—he said, "I am very sorry, I pawned them here on Monday; if you will allow me I will take them back."
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to work; they called me down to pay my footing; I had but 1 s . 6 d .; I spent that, and a young man said, "Can't we raise some money by taking some tools?"—Browning authorised me to pawn them—I said I would get them again. SAMUEL BROWNING re-examined.  I paid for part that I drank, but these things were pawned afterwards—I left a boy to take my tools in—the boy was moving the boards, and I slipped down—the prisoner denied having had the tools the next morning.
   The Prisoner called WILLIAM ELLIS. I called at the house about half-past two o'clock—the prisoner, and the plasterers and carpenters were all drinking together—Browning was so much intoxicated he could not stand. NOT GUILTY.
 2026. ARTHUR LEARY and RICHARD COOPER , unlawfully utter ing counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and DAWSON conducted the Prosecution. ELIZA PERRIAN. My husband keeps the Joiner's Arms, beer-shop, at Lewisham. On 20th July, about twelve o'clock in the day, the prisoner Cooper came and asked for half a pint of porter—it came to a penny—he gave me a bad half-crown—I looked at it, threw it down, and told him it was bad—he snatched it up instantly, and gave me a good one—he went out and I sent a boy to follow him—I saw Cooper join Leary and another—they went to Lee.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How far were the other persons from you? A.  About 100 yards—I had never seen Cooper before—he was at our place about two minutes. ELIZABETH BOOKER. My husband keeps the Highlander, at Lee, about a mile from the Joiner's Arms. On 26th July, Leary came there about half-past twelve o'clock, and asked for a pint of beer which came to twopence—he gave me a bad half-crown—I gave him the change—he left—I thought it was not good, and laid it on a shelf—there was no other money there—my brother was standing at the door—I called his attention to the half-crown, about ten minutes after Leary was gone—he took it and went after Leary.
