   COURT . Q.  Did you find any articles which were not sealed up? A.  Yes—these two pictures—one has the maiden name of Mrs. Borras on it—they hung up in the room—I told him he must go with me—he said Eliza Wright had brought them all there, and he did not know what the package contained—he did not say for what purpose she brought them there—I went to his room again on the Saturday afterwards, and found a gingham umbrella. ELIZA BORRAS( looking at the property. ) This picture is mine—it was my mother's, and has been in the family forty or fifty years—I had it at the house in Newington—it was put into the cupboard, the door of which was locked, and the key in it—I had it in my house after the girl came to live with me—the other picture is mine, and was in the same place—I have looked at the other things—they belong to Mr. Borras—this toilet-bottle and tooth-pick I know—Eliza Wright had not applied to me to purchase such things—the value of the paper found in the prisoner's box is 10 s . or 12 s .—it is very expensive, and all fancy paper—this umbrella is Mr. Borras's—I never authorised any body to take it.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Whose writing is this, "Thurs day, 27th of September, one umbrella 4 s . 9 1/2 d .,"in the book? A.  I do not see 4 s . 91/2 d .—"here is umbrella. 1 s . "—Miss Morton wrote that, I think, but that is umbrella mending, 1 s .—this was never in the shop, it was intended for sale, but had never been put into the shop—the value of it is 7 s . 6 d .—we never have umbrellas at 1 s . CHARLES BORRAS. This is my umbrella—I never authorised any body to move it from my house, or to sell it. WILLIAM HENNINGHAM re-examined.  The cupboard in which I found the parcel was not locked—it was the bed-room cupboard, and the pictures hung in the bed-room—the umbrella was down stairs, in the back room. NOT GUILTY.
