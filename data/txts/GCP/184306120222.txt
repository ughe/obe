Henry Smith. I am delivery-foreman at the London Docks. I remember
a chest of tea being delivered to Macklin.
THOMAS HOPKINS. I am in the service of Lynn and Gibson, fishmongers,
near Nicholson's wharf. On the 2nd of June I was in the shop, and heard a
something coming on the pavement-I looked out, and saw the prisoner Kif
up a square green chest of tea, and put it on the copse of his cart by itself-
not among any other chests—soon after I heard of the loss of this chest-I
saw Macklin crying-I afterwards saw the prisoner in the custody of King-
I went to the station, and the prisoner denied all knowledge of Macklin-I
turned round to him, and said, “Did you not pick up a box of tea that fell
to the ground ?"-"Yes," said he, “ I did, it fell from my cart"-I said I
did not see any vacancy where a chest of tea could fall from the body of his
cart-that is the fact, the cart was not filled high up.
Cross-examined. Q. It was the fall that attracted your attention ? A.
Yes-the truck was behind the cart—when I saw the chest lifting up again it
was beside the cart—whether it had fallen off any copse I could not tell—the
prisoner's face was towards me-I did not see him drive away--his horse's
head was turned in the direction of Martin's-lane.
Court. Q. Did you see the truck behind the cart ? A. I did not-
saw the truck after the cart was gone, just where the cart had been standing
-it had nothing on it then.
David King (City police-constable, No. 548.) I saw Macklin complain-
ing of the loss of his tea- I received an account from Hopkins--1 turned
from him in the direction of the horse's head, that led me to search in that
direction for the prisoner-as I was returning I met Macklin-he took me
to Martin's-lane, Cannon-street-I found the prisoner there unloading his
cart-I asked if he knew anything of the boy's tea-he said, no, he knew
nothing at all about the boy, and had not seen him-I took him into custody.
