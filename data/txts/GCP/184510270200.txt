GIBBS, Mayor.
HENRY SPARLING. I am shopman to William Buddry Elsden, of Bruns-
wick-place, Old Kent-road. On the 30th of Sept. this piece of printed
cotton was safe in the shop at ten minutes past eight o'clock-it was
missed at half-past eight-1 am quite sure this is it-it is my master's.
WILLIAM NOAKES (police-constable M 104.) On the evening of the
30th of Sept., at twenty minutes past eight o'clock, I stopped the prisoner
about 300 yards from the prosecutor's-I asked what he had got-be said,
“Nothing" - I opened his coat, and found this cotton under it-I said,
"Do you call this nothing ?"- he said, “That is what I just picked up."
Prisoner. I picked it up against the Railway station.
GEORGE WILDE (police-constable M 94.) I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction, at the Surrey Sessions-(read-Convicted
26th May, 1845; confined one month, and whipped)--the prisoner is the
person.
GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined One Year.
2169. MARGARET ROBERTS was indicted for stealing 1 watch,
value 21.; and 1 pair of ear-rings, 58.; the goods of Ann Naylor, her
mistress.
Ann NAYLOR. I am a widow. The prisoner was my charwoman--
about the 27th of Sept. I was moving some goods, and missed a watch-
afterwards missed some other things-the watch has not been found-she
said she had not seen it-here are some other things which are mine, but
they are not in this indictment.
Henry Nixon (police-constable L 85.) I was called by the prosecutrix,
and went after the prisoner-I asked if she knew anything of the watch-
she said she did not-I took her to the station, and asked if she lodged at
the house where I heard she did she said, “No"- I searched there, and
found thirty-eight duplicates-a bread-tray, a looking-glass, and copper,
were given me by Mrs. Reed-she is not here.
Prisoner. The duplicates under the bed were of my own property.
Not Guilty.
2170. MARGARET ROBERTS was again indicted for stealing 3
quilts, value 6s. ; 3 yards of crape, 18. ; 1 pair of snuffers and tray, 28. ;
and 1 table-cloth, 38.; the goods of Ann Naylor, her mistress.
ANN NAYLOR. About the 20th of Jan. Í missed some quilts, crape,
snuffers, table-cloth, and other things-this quilt, and these other things,
are mine--the prisoner was living with me as charwoman.
Prisoner. The two shifts belong to me. Wilness. No, they are mine-I
examined them at the pawnbroker's—I have no doubt about this counter-
pane-these towels are mine-they are not marked, but I have some simi-
lar to them the shirt is my son's-it is marked.
HENRY Nixon (police-constable L 85.) I found the duplicates of the
sheet and counterpane under the prisoner's bed, with other duplicates.
Christopher Dry. I am assistant to a pawnbroker-I have the sheet
and counterpane, which were pawned, I believe, by the prisoner-this is
the duplicate given for them.
Prisoner. The prosecutrix was moving, to deprive the landlord of his
rent; the shifts belong to me; the knives are mine, and one table-cloth ; she
allowed me to pawn some things.
ANN NAYLOR re-examined. I never allowed her to pawn this sheet or
table-cloth-she never pawned but once in her life for me.
Guilty. Aged 37.-Confined Four Months.
