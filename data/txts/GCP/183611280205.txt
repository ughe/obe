187. WILLIAM POOLE and THOMAS HAWKES were indicted
for stealing, on the 22nd of November, 1 sack, value 2s.; ana 20lbs.
weight of hay, value 18.; the goods of Alfred Courthope, the master of the
said William Poole.
ALFRED COURTHOPE. I am a stage-coach proprietor, and live at the
Globe in Deptford Lower-road. Poole was my horse-keeper-he was with
me about three months the last time, and was previously in my service in
consequence of suspicion I and Bradshaw watched, on the 22nd of Novem-
her, in an omnibus close to the stable door-Poole came and began his work,
and in about half an hour Hawkes came down--in two or three minutes
they came up the yard Poole said to him, “This is a pretty time to come
in the morning-it is time I had my dram long ago"- they went out,
and returned in about ten minutes, and went down to the stable-Poole
took a sack out of the loft, put some bay into it, and threw it down in the
yard-Hawkes put it on his back and came out of the yard-we watobed
him into his brother's shop, and caught him shooting the sack down-it
contained a mixture of hay and elover-I have every reason to believe it
was part of mine I had seen the sack empty in the stable the night be-
fore--the contents were worth a shilling.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Is this the sort of stuff which
would come from the sweeping of the loft? 4. No-it had been drawn
for trusses---Hawkes is the brother of my farrier--the sack has the same
of Pavit on it.
JOHN BRADSHAW (police-constable R 126.) I was with the prosecu-
tor, and saw Poole come in about five minutes to six o'clock-shortly
after, Hawkes came on the premises-they both went away for about five
minutes, and then returned-Poolo went up into the loft with a sack, put
the fodder in, and brought it down to the yard-Hawkes took it up-wo
followed him to his brother's premises, where be emptied it down.R
Cross-examined. Q. Is not his brother's a farrier's shop? 4. Yes-
there was a horse there ready to eat it up-it is not such stuff as would be
swept up from the loft-it is a mixture of rye grass and clover.
(The prisoner Hawkes received an excellent character.)
POOLE-GUILTY. Aged 36.Confined Six Months.
HAWKES GUILTY. Aged 18.- Recommended to mercy. Confined
Two Days.
188. MARY MCARTHY was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of
November, 28lbs. of cheese, value 158., the goods of Frederick Pearce.
FREDERICK PEARCE. I am a cheesemonger, and live in High-street,
Deptford. On Saturday evening, the 19th of November, about a quarter
to ten o'clock, the prisoner came to the shop with another woman and a
man-she looked at several articles in the window, and brought a piece of
bacon into the shop to be weighed, and parted, between her and the parties
with her--she paid for it-I suspected them, and as they were leaving the
shop, missed a Cheshire cheese-I ran round and found it on the prisoner,
Judged on her hip the other parties were concealing her, one walking on
each side of her as well as they could I gave her in charge.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you see her take any
cheese? 4. did not it was not found on the ground it was on her
hip, covered by her arms, under her shawl-there were seven or eight ous-
tomers in the shop.
