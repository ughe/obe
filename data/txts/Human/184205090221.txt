On Saturday night, the 16th of April, about ten minutes past eight o'clock, I was in Southampton New-road, Tidman's-fields—I heard the slates cracking—I stood opposite for some few minutes, and saw two persons pass through the sky-light on the top of the house No. 2—I fetched another constable and a labourer to my assistance—I sent a man to No. 4, to know what business they had there—I heard some lead thrown off from the roof against the back-door, where I stood, and the prisoners came down through the rafters from floor to floor, Marrott in No. 1, and Finch in No. 2—I took Finch into custody on the rafters—I have examined the lead thrown down, with the roofs, and find it had come from the sky-light—it fitted exactly, nail-holes and every thing.
   MARROTT— GUILTY. Aged 41.
   FINCH— GUILTY. Aged 42. Confined Six Month.

 1692. THOMAS RUMFITT was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of April , 4 1/2 lbs. weight of solder, value 2 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of John Words worth Robson , his master. JOHN WORDSWORTH ROBSON. I live in Jamaica-terrace—the prisoner was a plumber in my service. On Monday, the 25th of April, I sent him to do a job for me at the Bull Head Dock, on board the Bathurst  —he went on with the job till Friday, the 29th—about 18lbs. of solder was required to do the work—on Friday, the 29th, I went to the work, and found a much greater quantity of solder had been taken away from my premises than had been used—(I had seen him take away 1qr. 9Ibs. in the first instance)—I examined the pot, to see what was left in it, and found a great deal less than I imagined there ought to have been—there was scarcely sufficient left to make a joint with—I saw the prisoner's bag lying close by, and found in it three ladle cakes of solder—I immediately took up my ladle, tried the ladle cakes in it, and it fixed exactly, so that I had every reason to presume that the solder was mine—it had no right there—I left a person to watch the bag, and went outside, intending to intercept the prisoner as he left the premises—in about half-an-hour he came out, with out his bag, and I allowed him to pass—I then went, examined the bag, and the solder was gone—I immediately followed the prisoner, but he had got too far a-head of me—about ten o'clock next morning my book-keeper called, and told me something; in consequence of which I took a police man with me down to the job—the prisoner had gone to the ship—I fol lowed him there, and charged him with stealing the solder—he said it was all used in the job—I weighed the remainder, and found 21lbs, short—I then asked him what he had done with the three ladle cakes that were in his bag the night before—he said he had left them in the poter's lodge—I said, "What, in your bag?"—he said, I said, "Where did you find your bag this morning?"—he said, "In the porter's lodge"—I said,
