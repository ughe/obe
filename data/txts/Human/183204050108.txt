 1061.  MARY BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of January , 8 shillings, the monies of William Blackburn.
   CHARLES HORATIO AUGUSTUS KIRBY. On the 3rd of January the prisoner came to Mr. William Blackburn's, where I was shopman; she offered a pair of trousers in pawn, which I agreed to take for 8s. - she asked me to let her do them up herself; I did, and instead of giving me the trousers, she gave me these old breeches, not worth a farthing, pinned up in this wrapper, and I gave her the 8s. on them - I should not have given it her without I had supposed I had the trousers, which were worth the money I agreed to lend.
   JOHN MAYERS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on the 17th of February.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never had any breeches in my possession in my life.
 MR. KIRBY. I am certain she is the woman; she was a regular customer at our house.
     GUILTY. Aged 42.
 1062.  MARY BROWN was again indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of February , 14 shillings, the monies of William Blackburn.
   CHARLES HORATIO AUGUSTUS KIRBY. On the 3rd of February the prisoner brought a coat to Mr. Blackburn's, which she asked 14s. on; I looked at it, and agreed to lend it her - she asked to wrap it up herself, which I allowed her to do, and she left an old worthless blue coat(the other was an olive one), and I gave her the money; there were some shillings among it.
   JOHN CHRISTOPHER HEWITT. I saw Mr. Kirby examine the olive coat on the evening of the 3rd of February; the prisoner asked a price above what he offered her - after a cavil, she agreed to take it; she then asked whether he would allow her to wrap up the coat herself, as her husband was very particular; he gave her the duplicate and the money, and she left this coat.
     GUILTY, believing the prosecutor did not intend to part with his 14s. without having the olive coat. Aged 42.
 1063.  MARY BROWN was again indicted for stealing, on the 7th of December , 18 shillings, the monies of William Blackburn.
   CHARLES HORATIO AUGUSTUS KIRBY. I was shopman to Mr. William Blackburn; the money in each case was his. On the 7th of December, the prisoner came and offered me an olive coat for 18s., which I agreed to lend her; she wished to pin it up herself, and I gave it her for that purpose; while I was writing the duplicate she shifted the parcel, and left this worthless black coat, not worth 1s.; I had agreed to advance the money at the time, and I did advance it - I gave the coat to her merely for the purpose of pinning it up; I would not have advanced the money, without I supposed she had left the olive coat - I did not intend to part with the possession, or my claim on that coat.
 Prisoner. The coat I pawned that night, I redeemed in less than an hour. Witness. There was one night she redeemed a coat, and brought it back in less than five minutes, and this may be the coat; I cannot say that she did not redeem this one afterwards; she did not take a coat out that night.
 GUILTY. Aged 42. -    Judgment Respited.
 1064.  JOHN WILKINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of February , 1 pair of shoes, value 12s., the goods of William Worster.
 SECOND COUNT, stating it to be the goods of  William Rockingham.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM ROCKINGHAM. I am captain of a fly-boat, belonging to Messrs. Worster and Co.; it came to London. on the 1st of February. When we were at Buck by wharf, a brown-paper parcel was put on board by Mr. William Worster - it was placed under the tarpauling, and well secured down; I employed the prisoner to assist us from Paddington, through Islington-tunnel, to the warehouse in the City-road; I assisted in unloading the vessel, and missed the parcel in about an hour after we began to unload - on the 17th of February I received information, in consequence of which I saw the prisoner at the British Lion in Brick-lane, City-road - I said I understood he knew something about a parcel I had lost; he said he did not - I said, "If you do, and will produce the duplicate, I will be much obliged to you;" he then said, "If you will go to the Windsor Castle I will bring it you in an hour;" he said he knew it was pawned for 4s. - he went away, and we saw him no more till he was taken.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. Where did you take the prisoner on board? A. I was in bed at the time he came on board - here is a man who was at work when he came; I took him to do my work while I was asleep- I had engaged him on the Friday night before; he came on the 3rd of February; I did not see him on board till we got to the wharf at the City-road.
 COURT. Q. Is it a navigable canal? A. Yes; it came from Market Harborough to London.
 THOMAS ROCKINGHAM. I was a boatman on board the boat. I remember the parcel coming on board; I do not know what was in it; the prisoner came on board, about five or six miles from Paddington, about eleven or twelve o'clock at night - he came with me up to the basin, in the City-road.
 Cross-examined. Q. What night did he come on board? A. On the 3rd of February, five or six miles on the other side of Paddington; he stepped on board, but at no particular place.
