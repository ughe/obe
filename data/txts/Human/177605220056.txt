499. JAMES PRICE was indicted for stealing a silk handkerchief, value ten pence, the property of Benjamin Stevenson, May 23d.
   BENJAMIN STEVENSON. sworn.
 On Thursday I was in the procession with the charity children in  Newgate-street; I felt something at my pocket-book; I turned about and saw the prisoner; I missed my handkerchief; he ran away; I pursued him and took him, and found the handkerchief stuffed in an apron he had on.
 PRISONER's DEFENCE.
 I found the handkerchief on the ground.
     GUILTY.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. RECORDER. [Whipping. See summary.]
 500.  THOMAS COPPERTWHAITE was indicted for having in his possession a bill of exchange directed to John Pollard and William Preston, New Basinghall-street, for the payment of 43 l. to the order of Thomas Bradley , two months after date, that he unlawfully and falsely did pretend to Thomas Broughton that the said John Pollard was a good man, and was in partnership with a house in Bartholomew-lane, and by that false pretence obtained several quantities of goods, and 29 l. 15 s. 6 d. in money, against the statute, &c.
   THOMAS BROUGHTON sworn.
 I received from the defendant a note drawn in the same manner for 25 l. which note I passed off, and that note was paid; afterwards the defendant brought me this note, and when I looked at the note, he said it is upon the same person as the last note; it is drawn upon a good person, and that you know. I said if it is drawn upon Mr. Pollard, who is in partnership with the house in Bartholomew-lane; but this is not Bartholomew-lane, it is Basinghall-street. Copperthwaite said, It is that Mr. Pollard, and though the house is in Bartholomew-lane, he lives in Basinghall-street, and you know that to be as good a house as any in London. I delivered him the goods he wanted: four gallons of brandy shrub, seventeen bottles, five gallons of rum, five gallons of brandy, and two casks.
   THOMAS DODSON sworn.
 I came here to inform the court that that note was not upon the house of  Richard Holden and company, and that that note never made its appearance at our shop; I am clerk there, and accept all the bills that are drawn upon the house; we never had any bills drawn from such place and by such names; nor never had any connexion with such people.
 DEFENCE.
 I never mentioned Pollard's name at all. The first bill which I had, which was in August, for 26 l. 10 s. I left with Mr. Broughton; here is an evidence in court that left it with him; I never offered him the 43 l. bill in payment; I offered him a draft upon Sir  William Leman for 23 l. When Mr. Broughton came in with the bill, he and I were at Old Will's Coffee-house together, we talked the matter over in our conversation; he promised that he would take half cash, in the course of a week or ten days, if I would give him the remainder. I sent him up 22 l. to Mr. Leveridge, he was to go to pay a note; he did not come upon the Saturday night, and then the advertisement came in. When he found I could not get the money, he said he would prevent my joining the regiment, as he knew I was an officer in the militia.
     GUILTY.
 Tried by the London Jury before Mr. RECORDER. [Imprisonment. See summary.] [Pillory. See summary.]
