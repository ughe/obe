of money by false pretences.
MESSRS. POLAND and MONTAGU WILLIAMS Prosecuted ; Mr. Grain Defended,
EMILE DENON BOURG. I am a lace merchant, of 51, Great Portland
Street-I have known the prisoner 10 years in January or February he
told me that he was about to buy the International Music business in
Regent Street, and asked me to give him a bill for 501., which he would
discount, and buy the business, and I accepted this bill (Dated March 18,
1882, for 501. at 3 months, drawn by George Dimsdale, accepted by E.
Denonbourg, and endorsed George Dimsdale- I went to Paris, and did not
see him for some time-I met him in May at Bishop's Road railway
station, and asked him what he had done with the bill; he said he bel
got it at home, and would return it the same night-on June 3rd I
received this letter. (This was from the House of Detention, stating that M
had given the bill to Mr. Genese, who gave him 108. on account, and he
proceeded to draw cheques on him for the balance, and while doing 80 od
arrested for obtaining money by false pretences. He requested Mr. Genel
to appear next day at Marlborough Street, and state that it was a mere
accommodation bill, which he would have paid if he had been asked)
Another letter stated: “Never mind about to-morrow, I will square with
you when I get out of here, as my property under my mother's will will be
paid this month.” I paid the bill on presentation, but have received no
