 1238.  JOHN MANLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of March , fifteen dozen of willow squares, value 4 l.; and a bag, value 2 s., the goods of Walter Thomas.
   WALTER THOMAS. I am a willow weaver, and live at No. 6, New-street, Shoreditch. The prisoner lived with me three or four months. I sent him with these squares to Mr. Grape, Clifton-street, Finsbury, to be dyed, on the 11th of March, between nine and ten o'clock. I did not see him again until he was taken, which was on the 11th of September. I found they had not been delivered to the dyer - they were worth 4 l. 2 s. 6 d.
   JAMES GRAPE. I am a dyer; the prisoner never brought me these squares.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never received these. I had some to deliver to Mr. Welch, which I did.
   MARTHA BUCKLEY. I am servant to Mr. Thomas, and saw the squares delivered to the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined One Year.
 1259.  JOHN JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , a half-crown, the money of John Brown , his master.
 JOHN BROWN. I am a  linen-draper, and live at Shadwell; the prisoner was in my service about three months. On the 6th of August, a cutler marked all the money in the tills on the head side, opposite to the face. About eleven o'clock at night, I gave 17 s. to a witness to put in the till, having myself first counted it; there were five half-crowns among it. The shutters were then shut, but the drawers not closed - no more customers came. It was his duty to empty the till at night - he did not know that I had done it. He came and separated the halfpence from the silver as usual - and in three or four minutes I came from outside the door with the witness, and immediately counted the silver, and found a half-crown missing. I accused him of taking it - he denied it. I found it in his pocket with other silver, and after some time he acknowledged taking it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you know what the till contained - A. I might have left 1 s. in it, among the copper. I found only four half-crowns left. I said if his friends came forward, I would let him go. I wanted to know how much he had taken, as he acknowledged he had been robbing me ever since he came. I bought his friends might persuade him to tell me.
   THOMAS HART. I was fellow shopman with the prisoner. Between ten and eleven o'clock, Mr. Brown gave me some money, which he said was marked - I put it into the till, when the silver was taken out at night; the prisoner was not in the shop at the time. The 17 s. consisted of half-crowns, and other coin. The shop was shut, and no customer came afterwards. The prisoner came in about five minutes after, and in three or four minutes the half-crown was missed. My master searched him, and found it on him among other money.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did your master say he would let him go if his friends came forward - A. He said, if he would say where his friends were, he would send for them. I saw no violence used to him. I saw him laying on his back, he was handcuffed before the officer came.
   WILLIAM SPOONER. I am a constable, and was sent for, and found him handcuffed - the money was delivered to me.
 JOHN BROWN. Here is the half-crown, it is marked P. H.
   WILLIAM CATTEN. I am a cutler. I marked this halfcrown for Brown.
 Cross-examined. Q. You were present when this charge was made - A. Yes; Brown said, if he had friends, he would write to them - he said he had none.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined Three Weeks.

 1261.  WILLIAM HELLENDON was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , a handkerchief, value 5 s., the goods of James Riley , from his person.
   JAMES RILEY. I am a coal meter, and live in Lambeth-street. On the 9th of August, I was in Jerusalem-passage, Clerkenwell, between twelve and one o'clock; somebody spoke to me - I felt and missed my handkerchief; the person pointed to the prisoner; I seized him, he denied taking it. I took him to a constable, who searched him, but could not find it; it was a large blue one, with white spots.
   JOHN BRYAN. I am a lapidary, and was in Jerusalem passage, and saw the prisoner with another young man behind Riley; I saw the prisoner put his hand into Riley's pocket, and pull out a large blue silk handkerchief - he was about twenty yards from me; he gave it to his companion, who ran off with it immediately. I told Riley, he secured the prisoner, but the other got away.
   JOHN BALLARD. I am a constable. Riley gave charge of the prisoner, but I found nothing on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a man rush by me, a man came up and said, I was concerned with him, but I am totally innocent.
 GUILTY. Aged 18. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1 s. and Discharged.
