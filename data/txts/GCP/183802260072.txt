Mr. John FIELD. I have examined the stuff produced by the officer-
it appears to me to be parts of a plaster-of-Paris mould, for the purposed
casting half-crowns-on three of the pieces there remains a small portion of
the impression of the obverse side of a half-crown-I can trace some of the
letters-here is "E, J, G, R, A," on one of the pieces, and “R, G,"
another-I am satisfied it has been a mould for half-crowns-I have ex-
amined the five half-crowns, they are all counterfeit, and have all been cast
in one mould--they are Britannia metal, a similar kind of metal to the te
spoons produced the half-crowns are of the date 1818, and I believe this
portion of the mould has been made from the same coin-this powders
plaster-of-Paris-the tobacco-Dipe contains white metal of a similar descrip
tion--this tiri band is used to put the mould into shape-the roughness i
taken off the coin with a file, and the glass paper is used after the file--thx
