Witness for Bailey.
WILLIAM MORTIMER. I was with you on this evening-we went to the
Brockley Jack together after work-we saw Parker and another having
& dispute over their beer, and we followed them, as everybody else did,
I think between 200 and 300 people-I saw you interfere in no way in
the least-when the cry of “Police” was raised you rushed to Parker's
clothes and picked them up.
Cross-eramined. I know Parker of Deptford-I did not know the
fight was coming off-I did not see this newspaper: "In reply to the
challenge of Jack Parker of Deptford, Alfred Bruiser, now out of
employment, will box him for 101. or 201. & side at any place he likes to
name-I did not know of any regular fight being arranged-I don't
know the other man-both men were stripped ; regular rounds were
fought, each man trying to knock the other out of time, but nobody was
picking them up-there were no acknowledged seconds.

790. GEORGE DUNK (23), JOHN WARREN (87), and JAMES
OOLEMAN (21), Stealing two dozen spoons and other articles, the
property of Henry Hill and others. WAPREN PLEADED GUILTY.
MR. SCALES Proseouted.
HENRY PEROY HILL. I am a baker, of 6, Evelina Road, Peckham-I
have charge of the St. Catherine's Park Lawn Tennis Ground-I cater,
and supply an assistant, who looks after the ground on 24th June I
locked up the place securely at 8.30-early next morning my assistant
told me something, in consequence of which I went and found the place
had been broken open-there had been a bar, with a strong padlock to
fasten it; the padlock was broken-I had a clock there, and some spoons,
mineral waters, biscuits, and cakes—I have not seen them since--there
were some clothes there not my property.
Edgar Cook. I am the last witness's assistant-I went to the pavilion
op this Thursday morning-I found it had been broken open and the
door shut, and the bar pulled across again--the cupboard behind the
counter had been foroed.
GEORGE JOHN NORTH. I live at 21, Oulmore Road, Peckham, and am
& clerk in an insurance office-I am secretary to the St. Catherine's
Park Lawn Tennis Club-I produce this jacket in club colours, worth
11. 10., which I left with other articles and cricket things in the pavilion
on the night of the 24th, in a bag locked and strapped at each end-when
I saw it again the bag was still looked, but the ends had been forood open
and the things taken.
FRANK HILLS. I am a wholesale linen draper, of 3, St. Paul's Church-
yard, and a member of this club-these canvas shoes were stolen.
