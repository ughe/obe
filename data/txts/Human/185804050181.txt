ROBERT WELLS. I remember seeing Nichols on the morning of the trial of Ashton v. Steel—he spoke to me about some debts owing to him, and of Mrs. Spurway's—he asked me if I knew any one who could get them in—I told him, I knew the prisoner had done similar transactions for other parties—I met him on Tuesday morning, he said, he was going down to Mr. Mart's house; would I go with him—he said, "He has arranged with Mrs. Spurway, and he wants 15 s ., would it be right to give it?"—I said, I had no doubt it would be all right—I went down with him to the prisoner's house—he agreed to pay him 30 s ., and asked if that would be sufficient—the 30 s . was for any trouble he might have in getting Mrs. Spurway's money—hesaid, "I will give you 15 s . down to-day;" that was to be in part of the 30 s .—I did not hear any thing said about the Proctor's Court—on Friday I called on Nichols, I asked him how the affair was going on—he said, Mart had arranged with Mrs. Spurway, and he was perfectly satisfied with what had been done, and the money was to be paid over on the following Saturday.—Imet him on the Saturday afternoon, and he said, you had taken Mrs. Spurway's money—I said, "If there is anything wrong, Mr. Mart will make it all right; let us go together to him"—he said, "I have something better coming off on Wednesday; I shall not trouble myself"—he did not say, "I am done"—perhaps he might have said something to that effect; that he was dissatisfied—on Wednesday, I heard the prisoner was taken before the magistrate—I called on Nichols in the evening; I asked him how he was going on—he said, "Oh, it's all right, I have got him locked up; I shall want you to go to-morrow morning to swear that the 15 s . was paid to take out proceedings in the Proctor's Court"—I said "I shall do no such thing; I never heard any thing of the kind mentioned, the money was to be for Mr. Mart's trouble"—I never offered money to Nichols for Mr. Mart.
