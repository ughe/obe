201. JOHN CHAMBERS was indicted, for that he, well knowing that one James Witty , deceased, had lately served our Lord the King on board the Exeter, and that certain wages and pay were due to him, on the 23d of October last, feloniously did utter and publish as true, a certain false, forged, and counterfeited paper, purporting to be a last will, dated the 4th of December, devising the same to the said Thomas Chambers , and appointing him executor thereof, in order to receive the said wages and pay, due to the said James Witty.
 Mr. Garrow, Prisoner's Council. The will purports to be a last will.
 Mr. Silvester, Council for the Prosecution. The act says,
 "if any person shall forge a will."
 Court. But it must be the will of somebody.
 Court to Jury. Gentlemen, as we are of opinion, in this case, the indictment is defective, it is unnecessary to trouble you with evidence.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Tried by the second London Jury before Mr. RECORDER.
 202.  JOHN MILES was indicted for that he, on the 5th day of October , feloniously and falsely did make, forge, and counterfeit, a certain deed, purporting to be a bill of sale, of Robert M'Cormick, seaman, lately belonging to the East India ship, called the Earl of Talbot, in consideration of ten pounds eighteen shillings, paid by Daniel M'Cartey, purporting to be a bill of sale to the said Daniel M'Cartey, of all his wages and pay due to him, as his, and their own proper goods and chattles, and to his and their own proper use and uses for ever; which said bill of sale was dated the 5th of October, 1784, and sealed and delivered in the presence of Nathaniel Darby , and Keefe O Keefe , with intent to defraud the said Daniel M'Cartey.
 A second count for uttering the same, with a like intention.
 The prosecutor and one of the subscribing witnesses could not identify the prisoner, but the other subscribing witness swore positively to him, the prisoner in his defence said M'Cormick owed him five guineas, and gave him a note for that ship, which he sold to the prosecutor, as other young fellows had done, he went on board the next day to M'Cormick, who paid him what he owed him, and gave him half a guinea for his trouble.
 The Prisoner called one witness who gave him a good character.
 The Jury retired for a short time, and returned with a verdict
     NOT GUILTY.
 Tried by the first Middlesex Jury before Mr.  Baron HOTHAM.
