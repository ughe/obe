   THOMAS HILLDRUP. I am book-keeper at the Saracen's Head. I received the two parcels from Read on the 24th of July - I entered them to go by the Royal Blue Bath coach: I put them on the appropriate shelf belonging to that coach, and saw no more of them.
   JOSEPH MOUNTAIN. I am a book-keeper there. I handed the parcels to go into the coach; I remember two brown paper parcels, directed "Crutwell, Bath," and ticked them off in the book.
   JAMES HODSON. I am a porter. I put all the parcels that I received from Mountain into the coach - I have no recollection of them.
   JOHN HILL. I am clerk to Mr. Crutwell, of Bath - he is the distributor of stamps there; these requisitions came from our office: we received but one parcel, containing one skin of 130/., six skins of 10/., and six skins of 20l.; the parcel that did come was numbered 1028 - we received no other parcel.
   THOMAS CHESTER. I am book-keeper at the Grey Hound inn, Bath; the Royal Blue coach comes to our house - I remember the arrival of it on the 25th; it came about six o'clock in the morning - all the parcels that came by it were sent out to the respective persons to whom they were directed; I called over the parcels by the way-billone parcel was missing; on the way-bill was inserted two parcels for Crutwell - one parcel could not be found; the porter searched the coach a second and a third time, but it could not be found.
   WILLIAM BOND. I am porter at the Grey Hound, Bath. There was one parcel missing on this day.
   EDWARD KING BALCH. I am a bookseller. My shop is at No. 3, Great Ormond-street - my father is the principal turnkey at Giltspur-street Compter, and has been there eighteen years; I have had my shop in Great Ormond-street about three months, but I sleep at my sister's, No. 40, Great Alie-street. I first saw the prisoner about seven years ago, in the Giltspur-street Compter - I was living there at that time; when he got out I lost sight of him for some time, but saw him again three or four years ago - I saw him again about three months ago; his wife spoke to me about some books, and I saw him on my calling at his house - on Tuesday, the 4th of August, I met him against the dead wall in the King's-road; he asked if I could purchase any stamps - he took off his hat, and produced some stamps on parchment; I took them in my hand, and saw three 30l. stamps, and some that I concluded to be 10l., and some others at 10s. - they laid in my hand like a pack of cards, one over the head of the other; and the way they laid I was prevented from seeing them completely, but the three thirtys were at the top; I said I could not purchase them myself, but I would see some person about them - he said there were about 1400l. or 1500l. worth: that there were two of 140/. each, and the lot would he sold for 300/.; he said he was going to see a party about them, but they would not be gone by the next morning, if I would call at his house - we parted at that time, and I called at his house the next morning; he was not at home - I left word that I would call again at two o'clock; I went at that time and saw him - I had previously looked into the Times, and several other papers, to see if I could see any account of the stamps, as I concluded they were stolen from some law-stationer's; I saw an advertisement in the Times paper - I went to his house at two o'clock, and saw a list of the stamps which he said were to be sold; he then told me they were prigged, by which I understood they were stolen - having seen the list, I found it corresponded with the advertisement; what made me think it was the same, was the two of 140l. - he again told me the price would be 300/.; I left him and went to dinner, and then went to the Stamp-office - I received some directions there what to do, and went to the prisoner's house about nine o'clock in the evening; I left word I should want a copy of the list - he said if I could call in the morning I should have a copy of it; he then said the price would be reduced, and would be from 200l. to 250l., because a portion of the stamps, amounting to 280l., were not to be sold, and he added, that he should expect something for himself; I told him of course he would have something - I told him I had seen the party, and they would not give more than 170/., or there about - I treated him with a pint of porter; we then parted for the evening, and I agreed to see him again the next morning - I called again between ten and eleven o'clock the next morning; he showed me the list which he had shown me previously, but the stamps at the top were struck out; I made a copy of the list, omitting those which were struck out, by his direction, as he said they would not be included in the purchase - this is the list I made out; I believe it nearly tallies with the list he showed me, with the exception of those struck out at top- I of course cannot swear that it does correspond; he told me that the carriage-plate and pawnbroker's licenses were of no use without they were signed by a magistrate, and that made a reduction of 433/. 5s. - after I had taken the copy we parted; I never, on any occasion, asked to see the stamps - they were shown voluntarily; after I had taken the copy; I went again to his house, after that, because an alteration was made in the list - a nought was struck out in 140, and I went for the purpose of adding that; but in the mean time I had seen Ellis and Ruthven, who were then in the neighbourhood, and they had made some arrangements - I saw the prisoner when I went the last time; I said I wished to see the list again, to rectify an error - he allowed me to see it, and I made the alteration; he then placed it on a table, under the window, under some books or a tea-caddy - I then gave the officers information, but did not go to the house again; I brought the list away that I made, and gave it up to the solicitor of the stamps - I had made some figures on the list that I took this copy from.
