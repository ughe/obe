I apprehended Mary Mitchell, but she is not here. WILLIAM WARD. I am a watchman. I saw Guest that night at half past ten o'clock—that was previous to the robbery—he had been drinking—I did not consider him to be drunk—he was in a middling state—I may say he was sober—at a quarter before eleven o'clock, I received information of this, and went to the prisoner's lodging—she was not there—as I was going back, I fell in with her—I asked the prosecutor if that was the girl who robbed him—he said, "Yes"—she said she had been with Guest, but she had not got then money, but Mary Mitchell might have it—she had nothing about her.
   Prisoner's Defence . I was at the King's Arms talking to Mary Mitchell, the prosecutor came out and asked me to take a walk with him across the green—I refused to go with him, or to take my thing to drink—he gave me a shilling, and the purse—I gave the purse to Mary Mitchell—she threw it down in the passage—I said she might go with him if she liked but I would not, he was so very rough. JOHN MORTIER. I am a gunner in the Rocket Troop. I do not know the prisoner, but I and Guest were together—I know he had a purse and 5 s .—we went to the Red Lion, where I paid for a pot of half-and-half—I then went to another house—Guest came to me again, and paid for a pot of half-a-half—I left him there, and went on guard—I came back to him, and he had another pot of half-and-half, for which he paid 5 d ., and half an ounce of tobacco—we then went to the Abercrombie, and had another pot of half-and-half, which he paid 5 d . for—he then went away and shaved himself—I met him again at Long's hotel, after stabling—we then went and had three pots of half-and-half, which he paid for, and a quartern of gin—he then went after a woman who sells apples—she would not have any things to do with him—he was a very drunk—he came in again, and said he must go somewhere—I told him to go home—he then went out of the parlour. NOT GUILTY.
