MR. ORRIDGE. . Did the prisoner complain to you of your not having
credited him with monies, which you had not advised him A. Certainly
not-these letters (produced) are in his writing-these are in the nature of
private books as well--here is a ball ticket, and an opera ticket-these are
private and are in the books in which he took orders from the customers
he then writes to me what is contained in the books—he did not produce
these books to me again after he was apprehended, and after they were
given to him in blank-these are my last year's books- I did not request
him to produce books of this description which he had—I find in this
private book of his, and in the private ledger, the sums named—I had never
seen this book before-on his return from a journey the other cash-book
was produced as the correct return of the sums of money received by him-
if mistakes are made by anything being sent to the wrong party that would
make no difference whatever in the cash account Mr. Smithies bad nothing
to do with making out the accounts; the cash account was entirely under
my control-I received what I could get from him and then gave him in
custody.
MR. METOALFE. Q. Did you receive a bill about 10th November from a
person named Webb? 4. Certainly not; we received three bills of 1006
each from the prisoner, and he only accounts for two bills that is 2001. -
there is no indictment for them; we did not discover it until some time after
the indictments had been prepared.
MR. ORRIDGE. Q. Who remitted those bills ? A. The prisoner; and that
name occurs in his private book - this is the book that he keeps for the
journey—he has sent bills for 3001., and he takes credit in his remittances
for 3001.-on one side is 2001., and on the other he takes credit for 9002. 6.
10d., wbich includes 3001. to Webb, who is only credited 2001.--the loss
would fall on Webb.

JOHN TAYLOR. I am a plumber of Oxford, and a customer of Messrs
Robertson on 21st December, 1859, I was indebted to them 671. os. 90.-
I knew the prisoner as their traveller ; he was in the habit of calling upon
me, soliciting orders-I paid him 301. on account on 21st December, and he
signed this receipt, and gave it to me (Read: “21st December, 1859, John
Taylor debtor to W. Robertson and Co., balance 252. 178 R G. Dean.")
that is the prisoner's writing-I paid him in money.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known the prisoner1. Nine
or ten years ; he introduced me to the house-during the whole time I have
known him he has borne a very high character for honesty and respectability.
MR. SLEIGA. Q. Did you know him in any other way than calling on you
as traveller 4. No.
