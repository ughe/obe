Cross-examined by Mr. O'Brien. Q. Is it by this tear that you know
this 101.-note? A. By that, and by its general appearance-I cannot tell
the number of it-I know it began with a "3"-I saw this note at the Bank.
and recognised it from others, though I had not seen the name on the back
there were 220 men on board the vessel-no one had access to my berth, or
to receive orders from me but the prisoner-my wages were 101. a year,
besides the Government pay, which was 221. 128.--the prisoner's wages were
81. a year, besides the Government pay-I came to England with the prisoner
-there was a man named Gilbert on board-he was gun-room cook--he had
nothing whatever to do with me I never told any one that I suspected him
of robbing me-I had no idea he was the man when I missed this property
the bags of all the ship's company were searched, with the exception of the
three cooks-the mens' persons were not searched-the ship has not arrived,
but I and the prisoner were paid off at Portsmouth-I was out about nine
months altogether--the note had passed through the hands of many of the
officers, and came into my hands again by my cashing it for them.
Thomas SELVES. I keep a public-house, at Woolwich. In the begin-
ning of July, the prisoner came to me-be asked me to change a 51.-note for
him, which I did -I observed that he had a 101.-note, and as he was rather
the worse for liquor I asked him to leave it with me this is it-my son-in-
Jaw backed it, and put it into the cash-box-I was to keep it till he came
back from Portsmouth, but a letter came for me to send him down 5l, to
Portsea, and I sent it-I paid away the 101.-note to Mr. Tuck-I know the
note by the name on it, and by a tear on it.
Cross-examined. Q. I suppose you knew the prisoner? A. Yes, I have
known him two or three years-he lived with me twelvemonths.
HENRY Tuck. I am clerk to Combe, Delafield, and Co. On 4th July, I
received from Mr. Selves this 101., and this 5l.-note--I paid them into
Robarts' next morning.
RICHARD ADEY BAILEY. I am a clerk in the Bank of England. I produce
these potes-the 51. is No. 34,529, 13th Sept., 1849; and the 101. No.
36,088, 10th Aug., 1849—they were both paid in by Robarts and Co., on
8th July.
JOHN GRIPPIN ( policeman, 229 R). I apprehended the prisoner in Port-
sea, on 10th July-I told him he was charged with stealing 397. from Mr.
Rice, to whom he was cook--he said, “I am innocent, there are others who
are guilty"-I asked him who it was-be hesitated some time, and I said,
“Is it Gilbert ?"-be said he was the guilty party. GUILTY. Aged 25.-
Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor-Confined Six Months.
