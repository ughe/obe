883. JAMES LEDWICH was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 1 writing-desk, value 10s., the goods of Cornelius Wallace.
 REBECCA WALLACE. I am the wife of  Cornelius Wallace- he is a mariner, and lives in Collins'-place, High-street, Poplar. On the 17th of March the prisoner came to me for a writing-desk of my husband's, who was on a voyage to Quebec - I let him have it, to take it to my husband.
   JOHN CHUBB. I am a pawnbroker. I have a desk pawned by the prisoner on the 17th of March.
   JAMES FOGG. I took the prisoner on the 18th of March, at a public-house; the publican said, "This young man was sent for a desk for the mate of the Scott, but he has never got it;" I said to the prisoner, "Did you get the desk from the mate's wife?" he said, Yes; and he had sent it on board the Scott.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was with the person who pawned it, but I did not pawn it; we were going to the play, and the desk was heavy to carry - we left it there; when I was taken I thought it was gone on board.
 The prisoner received an excellent character.
 GUILTY. Aged 28. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. - Confined 1 Month.
 884.  JOHN ADAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 1 coat, value 3l., the goods of Eli Pethwist.
   WILLIAM CROOK. I am in the service of Mr. Linsall, of King-street, Holborn. Mr. Pethwist, a tailor, lived opposite to us. On the evening of the 28th of March, I saw the prisoner step into the shop, take a coat, and put it into his apron - he then gave it to another man; I went out and took the prisoner; my employer took the coat.
   ELI PETHWIST. This is my coat - I received it from Mr. Linsall.
   WILLIAM COTTERELL. I am beadle. I took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was passing the shop, and came up to this witness, who charged me with stealing the coat, which I did not.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Month.
 885.  HANNAH FLANNAGAN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of February , 1 half-crown, and 5s., the monies of John Redguard , from his person.
   JOHN REDGUARD. On Sunday night, the 15th of February, I met the prisoner in Bunhill-row; she asked me to go home with her; I said I wanted no connexion with her - she took hold of me by the collar, and dragged me into a house in Draper's-buildings; one of them knocked me about, and the other took my money from me; they wanted me to give them some money, but I would not - I got away, and then missed half a crown and 5s.; it was all marked, and was what my master had paid me.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not say at the office that you had a cut in your head, and was very much intoxicated? A. I was intoxicated, and was sick with their knocking me about; I did tell the officer I had had a cut in my head -I offered to stop the charge, because I was afraid of losing my place.
   THOMAS WALKER. I am a watch-house-keeper. I took the prisoner between one and two o'clock in the morning; I took half a crown and 5s. from her hand - she held it tight; the prosecutor wanted to make it up - he had been drinking, but not much; he offered me money to let the charge go - he said there was a half-crown and some shillings; he did not know exactly what, and that his master always marked the money.
 Prisoner's Defence. He gave me the money to sleep with him, and then when he was sick he got better; he asked for it back again, and I would not give it to him - he said he would swear a robbery against me.
   ANN HARFORD I live in Twister's-alley; I am an unfortunate girl, and was with the prisoner at the time; the prosecutor gave me the money to go with me first, and then I gave it to her; I take my oath that he gave me the money.
     NOT GUILTY.
 886.  BENJAMIN HAWKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 2 sovereigns, the monies of James Collard.
   JAMES COLLARD. On the 10th of August I employed the prisoner to take a horse to Galley-quay, to go to Sunderland; he had two sovereigns to pay the expences - he did not put the horse on board, but put it at livery, and I had the bill to pay for it: I did not see him again till the 21st of March; I put my hand on his shoulder, and said,"Halloo, my friend!" he looked up and said, "Will you have any gin" - I said, "I will gin you to the watchman;" he said he had spent some of the money, and the rest he had lost; I found the horse at livery the day after he took it.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not send me to No. 11, Catherine-court, Trinity-square, to ascertain where the vessel laid? A. No such thing; I told you to go and put the horse on board, and I would give you half a sovereign when you had done.
 Prisoner's Defence. He gave me this horse, and told me to go to the place and inquire; I saw two gentlemen, who said the vessel was not to sail for three days, but I had better wait to see if the gentleman belonging to it would come there; I walked the horse about for an hour, and then the gentlemen came and said, "You had better put that horse up," which I did; I met a shopmate, and got rather too much - I do not know what became of the money, nor myself either hardly; I have since then broken my arm, and have been three months in the hospital - I have not had an opportunity of returning the money; if I had meant to have made a theft of it I should not have left the saddle and bridle in the ostler's hands.
     GUILTY. Aged 38. Confined Six Months.
