the prisoner Christall came to my house between four and five o'clock in
the afternoon-I did not know him before-he said, be bad some butter to
sell, and that his name was Hancock-I asked him if he was brother to
Mr. Hancock of Great St. Helen's, as I knew Mr. Robinson had dealt
with Mr. Hancock-he said no, but he knew Mr. Robinson, and had had
dealings with him-I told him I had no recollection of him he said yes,
he had sold him a los of butter some time ago--I have no recollection of
any dealings whatever with him-Mr. Robinson is since dead he said he
lived in Great Alie-street, next door to the Black Horse-be asked me
to buy, and brought a sample in his pocket in paper-it was not left with
me-he said it was Kiel butter, and 848. was named as the price-there is
a large quantity of that butter in the market from time to time I looked
at the sample, and tasted it-it was not very good-it appeared Kiel but-
ter-after some conversation, he said he had got a cask outside if I would
allow him to bring it in- I gave him leave-(& man named Tripp had been
with me two days before, and I was aware of the robbery at Brewer's
Quay)--the butter was brought in, by the prisoner Hopkins, in a cask co-
vered with a sack-I told Hopkins to put it down at the end of my coun-
ter, but Christall told him to take it into the back warehouse, which he
did, and I and Christall then went into the back warehousemine is a whole-
sale and retail shop-there are folding doors between the back and front
shop--the sack was taken off in the back warehouse, and I found the
head and top hoops had been taken off the cask-it contained Kiel butter
--I tried the quality, and it was very good Kiel butter, worth about 104s.
in the market-I asked how long it had been landed he said from
thirty-six to forty hours asked where the head of the cask was he
said it was at home-I asked him what ship they came out of he said,
“You must not be too particular"- he gave no reason for that I then
asked if I could see the remainder of the butter, the bulk of the goods-
he said I could-be had said he could supply me with ten casks a week,
but he had only got five by him at present-I wished to see the other
four- he said, if I would go home with bim, I could see them I
went with him--the cask of butter was left at my warehouse-he took me
to Toy's house, the Angel and Crown, Wellelose-square-I asked him, as
we went along, what ship they came out of, and he said, “You must not
be too particular, not ask so many questions"-he did not give me any
account of the butter- I did not see any thing of Hopkins--I suppose be
walked behind, as I saw him at Toy's house, in the tap-roomwhen we
got to Toy's, Christall asked if I would take any thing to drink in front
of the bar-I said no—he asked me to walk into the parlour, which is a
back room behind the tap-room-- I went towards there, and found that
Christall had turned back, and gone into the tap-room-I turned back,
and followed him unperceived by him--he went up to one of the boxes in
the tap-room, and joined some person there, but who it was I cannot say
-Toy was not there then he came directly almost.
