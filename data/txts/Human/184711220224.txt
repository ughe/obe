GEORGE QUINNEAR( policeman, P  201.) I went to Hyman's house in consequence of what Walker said—I saw Hyman—I asked Walker in his presence if that was the man he sold a copper to on Saturday night, 6th Nov.—he said it was—Hyman denied it; he said, "I can assure you it in wrong, I did not buy a cooper of him"—Walker said, "I did sell the copper to you; you asked what I wanted; I said, 'Six shillings'; you weighted it, and gave us 6 s . 2 d . you asked where we lived, and we told you in John-street, Albany-road; you lit your pipe and was going with us, and we ran away"—the cooper has not been found. ELIZABETH RICE. I live in Southampton-street. I heard about a cooper, and on Saturday, 6th Nov., went to Hyman's house—his wife said he was not at home—I waited—he soon came home—I said to him, "I understand my boy brought a cooper here, and it is a very wrong thing to buy anything from children without their parents knowing it"—he came out from his shop—I said, "It is a very serious thing"—he said, "You keep your boy in your house, it will soon blow over if you do not let him go along with the rest." SAMUEL RICE. I live with my father, Thomas Rice, a blacksmith, in Southampton-street. I know Walker, Moore, and Morley—on Saturday evening I went with Moore and Walker to 3, Peckham-grove—they told me to get up to the window—I broke a square of glass and got in—there was a copper there—we took it away to a house up steps, and they would not buy it—we took it to Hyman, and he gave us 6 s . 2 d . for it, and chucked  it on the rag heap—he did not weigh it—my brother found it out, and told my mother, and I told my mother where I took it to.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN. Q.  Have you told us all that occurred? A.  I took my mother where I took the copper—I and Walker went into Hyman's shop—we told him the copper was our father's, and we lived at 4, Bath-street.
