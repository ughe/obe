I am a millwright in the employ of Mr. Watney—this bucket is his property. THOMAS EMERSON( police-sergeant V  16.) I was at the police-station at Clapham on the 12th of Jan., when the prisoner was brought in in custody—I told him what he was charged with—he said he was outside, While Richard Hosier and Thomas Hadley were taking these things—I read the warrant to him—he said nothing then—about a quarter of an hour after, when I went to the cell and gave him two sacks to cover over him, he said, "It was a bad job—I was with them, I shall get transported the same as they are," or "as well as they are" that he must take the rough with the smooth, he could not help it now—he said he had been living at all parts of London since that—I told him Hadley and Hosier were transported, one at Croydon, and one at the Central Criminal Court.
   Cross-examined. Q.  It appears there were two men tried for this crime? A.  Yes—I am not aware that there is a reward for the apprehension of any person who committed that robbery—I never asked the foreman any questions—I did not take down the words the prisoner repeated—I have a good memory—I was not at the former trial—I do not know that the prisoner had been drinking—he had not the appearance of it. HENRY JAMES ELLIOTT. On the 13th of Jan. I was locked up in the station—I was charged with spending some money, but I was not convicted—the Magistrate dismissed me by letting me pay the money—I was in the same cell where the prisoner was on the 13th—I said to him "What are you in here for?"—he said, "Stealing a pump"—he said, "There was a pump stolen from Mr. Watney's, at Wandsworth, twelve months ago"—I said, "Were you in the mess?"—he said, "Yes; the other two mates got trans ported ten years each: one was from Croydon, the other the Old Bailey," but, says he, "I hooked  it; I was a full-whiskered man then, but I shaved my whiskers all off"—he said it was a bad job, he had no doubt but what he should get lagged  for it, he must make his mind up to that; it was a bad job, he could not help it now—he said he should not have been taken if he had not come on that side of the water to help his sister move—that he had a bed on his head when the policemen came to him, but he gave the policemen a two hours' run, and then gave them an upper  and a downer  before they took him—I had no quarrel with him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Will you repeat exactly what this man stated on the 13th of Jan.? A.  I asked him what he was in there for—he asked me what I was in for—I told him I was never locked up in a gaol before—the money I spent was for work done at the Wandsworth Railroad—it belonged to me and some more men—they gave me orders to take it—the Magistrate allowed me my liberty on condition of repaying the money, because the men were agree able to it—I have been at work at the reservoirs in Battersea-fields—I worked before that for Mr. Thornton, at Nine Elms—I do not know what I am to have for my trouble in coming here—I asked Mr. Paynter whether I should be paid my expenses—he said, "Yes, when you go to the Old Bailey."
