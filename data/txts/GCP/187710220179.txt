WILLIAM WALTER KNAGGs. I am sub-manager of the Langham Hotel
- Benson was there on 18th August, 1875—he had a servant with him,
and he had the rooms Nos. 70, 71, and 98-I do not know whether he
had any visitors on that occasion I was away from the hotel during that
visit-he was there again from 14th February to 15th March, 1876, and
had rooms Nos. 68, 69, and 303—he had a servant with him again-
he was either wheeled about in a chair or carried he was there again
from 27th June, 1876, to 8th July, under the name of Yunge the tirst
time he signed the book “ W. Gitford, Shanklin" - I heard that he had
got an alias during his last visit, and then I heard of him as Count
Montagu-that was in July, 1876—the servants asked for letters for the
Count de Montagu-I remember between 27th June and 8th July, 1876,
his going out for one day in a brougham-I remember one morning
receiving a message fnom his servant, and Meiklejohn came in five or six
minutes afterwards-I asked Meiklejohn what he was doing therehe
said that he came to see Mr. Yonge I said that he could not see Mr.
Yonge that day because he was unwell; he said " He is sure to see me"
-I knew Meiklejohn perfectly one of the pages happened to be passing;
I told him to take Meiklejohn's name up to him, and he said "Tell him
Mr. John wishes to see him "—the boy took the message upstairs, and
Meiklejohn went up to Yonge's apartments-he did not give any name
he said to the boy "Say Mr. John wishes to see him," and the boy went
up with the message I cannot tell whether Meiklejohn waited till the
boy came down-I left bim standing there there was somebody with
Meiklejohn-I cannot swear to who he was it was during his last visit
of ten days-it would be between 27th June and 8th July, 1876 he had
no visitors while he was at the hotel except Meiklejohn STOR ada

WALTER LANGDON. I am cashier at the Holborn branch of the Union
Bank, and was so in April, 1876, when William Kurr kept an account
there—this is a cheque of his (produced)-it is made payable to H.
Collett, and dated 10th April, 1876, for 1001.- I paid it over the counter,
in two 201. notes, Nos. 35775 and 35776, dated 19th July, and six 101.
notes, Nos. 45582 to 45587 inclusive, dated 20th September.
Cross-examined by Mr. GRAIN. I was called for the defence in the
De Goncourt frauds from the Union Bank—man named Harry Street
had an account at that bank-these two letters (produced) were put into
my hands by Serjeant Parry at the trial, and I was asked if they were
written by William Kurr, the convict-to the best of my belief I answered
that they were not-they are very similar to Harry Street's writing, and
I still believe they are his.
