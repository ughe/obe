 2248. HARRIET COLLIER was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of September , 1 bag, value 1 d  .; 1 half-crown, 10 shillings, and 1 sixpence; the goods and monies of Joseph Parsons . JOSEPH PARSONS. I am a pensioner of Greenwich. I got drunk last "ale day," about four o'clock, I think—I had been sober before—I had seen my money before four o'clock—I had 15 s . in my little bag, a half-crown, and sixpence, and an old sixpence, which I picked up about four years ago—I know it again—this is it ( looking at it )—I found myself lying in a bed, and the policeman came and awoke me—my money was gone—I do not know that I had been with any woman that night.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How old are you? A.  Seventy nine—it was not my own bed I found myself in—I was put into the bed, as I was drunk—I cannot tell what I did. WILLIAM DYKE( police-constable R  100.) I received some information that induced me to go after the old man—I found the prisoner at the bar in bed with him—I left the house for some time, and about an hour after I met the prisoner going from Greenwich towards Deptford—I said, "Where are you going, my lass?"—"To Deptford," said she, "and I came from Woolwich"—I said, "You must have made a quick passage; I saw him in bed with the pensioner"—she said, "I left him; I did not like to stop with so old a man"—I said, "What money have you got about you?"—she said, "Only one shilling"—I said, "I am afraid you have been doing the old man, as the other one did before; I shall search"—I found another shilling, a sixpence, and a halfpenny—I said, "Where did you get this?"—said she, "To tell you the truth, I have taken it from the pensioner"—I told her I should take her to the station-house—in going along I saw she wanted to shift something—I saw her going to throw something into the road—I caught hold of her by the hand, and said, "What have you got here?"—she said, "I don't know"—I said, "It is a purse, or something like it; where did you get it?"—she said, "I took that out of the pensioner's pocket likewise"—there was a half-crown, ten shillings, a sixpence, and an old piece of silver in it, which the pensioner has identified.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time was it that you found these two in bed together? A.  Between twelve and one o'clock in the morning—the prosecutor was sitting at the side of the bed, wide awake—the woman was undressed, and laying on the bed—I did not know her at all—I never saw her, to my knowledge—I was in the room from a quarter of an hour to twenty minutes—I have been on that station these last seven years. NOT GUILTY.
