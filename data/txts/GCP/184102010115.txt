Strand, my shutter-box is in the foregate, St. Clement's, nearly opposite
the prosecutor's shop. On Monday morning, the 11th of January, I was
removing my shutters, and after putting up the second shutter, I heard
something fall, and found this crow-bar-it fell down on the ledge I gave
it to Saers.

734. JOHN PATTERSON was indicted for embezzlement.
John MILLER. I am a baker, in Broad-street, Rateliff. The prisoner
was in my service-he collected money, and carried out bread-- he has not
paid me 3s. from Mary Barton on the 18th of January, nor 6s. from Louisa
Bentley, nor 4s, 11d. from Elizabeth Payne--it was his duty to pay me
when he received the money.
Prisoner. Q. Did not my fellow-servant agree to make this money up
for me? A. No---you and he both absconded, and did not give me an
opportunity of paying you your wages.
MARY BARTON. I deal with Mr. Miller. I paid the prisoner 3s, on his
account on the 18th of January.
Louisa BENTLEY. I deal with Mr. Miller. On the 18th of January
I paid the prisoner 6s. for him.
ELIZABETH PAYNE. I deal with Mr. Miller. On the 18th of January
I paid the prisoner 4s. 11d. for him.
Prisoner's Defence. When I came home from work I went out with a
stale loaf to a customer- I went to look for my fellow-servant, but could
not see him; I spent a shilling or two of my master's money; I met my
fellow-servant, and he told me he and master had made it all up.
John MILLER re-examined. His wages were paid every Tuesday-this
happened on Monday-88. was due to him-I believe he is a very respect-
able young man--he conducted himself well before.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined Two Months.
