WILLIAM ROBINSON. I went up stairs, and saw Grant under the bed—Walker came in about a minute or two afterwards—I cannot tell from where—I called my son, and a neighbour ran up—he pulled Grant out, and these things, which were all in a bundle under the bed. HENRY MOORE. I was in the club-room—I heard "Harry"  called, and I went up, thinking it was myself—I saw Mr. William Robinson at the door, with a candle—he said, "There are thieves in the room"—I said, "I will find them"—I opened the door, and on looking under the bed I saw Grant—I pulled him out by the legs, and these clothes came out with him—I did not see Walker. THOMAS DEAN( police-constable G  37.) I took Grant, and gave him to another officer, while I got the property—Grant took out of his pocket this china image, blow-pipe, and pair of gloves—he did not say anything, but Walker said they should not go one without the other, if one went they should both go—Walker resisted.
   Walker. Q.  Where did you take me? A.  In the room. JAMES CHURCHYARD( police-constable G  174.) I saw Grant take these things out of his pocket.
   Grant's Defence.  Robinson was about to let his house; the new landlord came that evening to look at it; Robinson invited a lot of men to come and drink free of expense—I was invited by a friend; I had occasion to go down stairs; I did not know the way; I mistook the way up, and got into the room, I suppose.
   Walker's Defence.  I was not in the room; the policeman says he took me in the room, but it was not till I came down, and the landlord said, "Take him, he is an accomplice;" this woman came out of the room, and she must have seen me if I had come down stairs; I did not know this man before that night. HENRY ROBINSON re-examined.  I was about to let the house, but did not invite persons, and did not give them anything.
   GRANT ,— GUILTY.

HENRY WHITE, was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of April , 2 sheets, value 3 s  .; 5 shirts, 2 s  . 6 d  .; 3 waistcoats, 3 s  .; 1 pair of trowsers, 1 s  .; and 1 jacket, 1 s  .; the goods of Charles Spence . CHARLES SPENCE. I am a seaman, and live in Bromley-street, Stepney. On the 1st of April these things were hanging on a line in my mother's yard—I missed them—they are mine. CHARLES GILL( police-constable K  59.) I took the prisoner and another boy at the White Horse at Stepney at three in the morning on the 1st of April. WILLIAM HACKET SHAW( police-sergeant K  2.) About six o'clock in the morning I received these things from Kinche. I examined the premises and saw where they had got over by some remarkable shoe marks—I went to the station, took the prisoner's shoes off, and they fitted exactly with the marks, which all came from Ruston's towards the prosecutor's premises—these things were in a bag. THOMAS KINCHE. I live in Charles-place, Love-lane. I was at work at Mr. Easton's rope ground at Stepney—Mrs. Ruston asked me if I saw any thing—I turned and saw a large bundle full of clothes. NOT GUILTY.
