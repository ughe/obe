JAMBS POTTEN. My father was a carman; he is dead—this is his signature to this deposition. ( The deposition of James Potten was here read ). HORACE YBRWORTH. ( Cross-examination continued ). I was some 10 years in the employ of Saull, Godfrey, and Brown—I knew how the business was going on the whole of that time—the partnership expired on 31st December, 1871, and I left in May, 1872, because I found that the business was falling away, and I knew that Chancery suits were going on—I was perfectly free to leave if I chose, and I left and engaged myself to Brown at Worship Street—he commenced business there in May or June, '1872, about six months after the partnership expired—he did not instruct me to make out a list of customers and take it to Worship Street—I forget what I did with the list I made out; I must have destroyed it—I saw it at Worship Street—I never gave it to Brown, nor did I inform him that I had made it out—I know the wine trade well—I am in business now with Mr. Chitty, who was one of the travellers to Saull, Godfrey, and Brown—a traveller to a wine merchant has his own connection, and carries it with him when he leaves—the first transaction I had in my own name was when Brown was away; I sent to a man who had been a fellow-clerk, for 12 gallons of dark sherry to make a butt golden; it was for the business, but he entered it in my name, and it after wards came to my knowledge that it was entered in my name, and I protested against it—I do not know that it was still done after I protested—I remained in Brown's employment, and received my salary.
