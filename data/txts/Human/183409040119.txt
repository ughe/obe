 1445.  JAMES BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Edward Thompson , from his person.
 EDWARD THOMPSON. I am a  clerk in a solicitor's office, in Essex-street. On the 15th August, I was in Southampton-street, Strand, between three and four o'clock - I felt my handkerchief taken from my pocket - I turned round directly, saw it in the prisoner's hand, and secured him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been to Hungerford-market - I was walking up the Strand, looking in at the picture-shops - the gentleman turned round and accused me of stealing his handkerchief, which I am innocent of.
     GUILTY.* Aged 15. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1445.  THOMAS GUNTER was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , 1 sovereign, and 1 half-sovereign, the monies of Thomas Hurst , his master.
 THOMAS HURST. I am a  silk manufacturer. The prisoner has been my apprentice three years and a half - on the 28th of August, I and my wife went to bed in the first floor front room - the prisoner slept in the attic - I had ten sovereign and a half - in my trowsers pocket, which I put by the side of the bed - in the morning I got up, and missed a sovereign and a half - I told the prisoner I suspected him- he denied all knowledge of it - I sent for a policeman - I said, "You will not tell me where my money is; I dare say, you will tell the policeman" - they then went into the yard - the policeman afterwards showed me a sovereign, a shilling, and two sixpences - I asked the prisoner how he got it - he said, "I got up at four o'clock in the morning, and took your money, and then went to bed again" - my bed-room door was shut, but not locked.
   JOSEPH WORMALD. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner - I went into the yard with him, down into the cellar - he went down on his knees, and began searching for the money - I helped him, and found this money concealed under the dirt in the cellar - he picked up a sovereign; I found the silver - he said he went down at four o'clock in the morning, opened his master's door, and took the money out of his purse, in his pocket, and then went to bed - he said his mother told him to take all the money, for she had no tea nor sugar. GUILTY. Aged 19. - Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor. - Confined Three Months.
 1447.  ELIZABETH PERRY was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , 1 coat, value 5s.; 1 pair of trowsers, value 7s.; 1 hat, value 5s.; 2 aprons, value 6d.; 2 pairs of half-boots, value 1s.; 1 box, value 2d.; 1 handkerchief, value 3d.; 6 shoes, value 6d.; 2 shillings; 3 sixpences; and 6 1/2d., in copper monies; the goods and monies of Charles Wilkinson.
   SARAH WILKINSON. I am the wife of Charles Wilkinson; I sell things in the street. I have known the prisoner about a fortnight - I employed her to clean my room and wash my children - she did not live in the house - on Sunday afternoon, I was cleaning myself, and missed my husband's trowsers and hat; and in the morning, I missed his coat off my bed - she had left the place at a quarter past nine o'clock at night, and wished me good night - she said she would come soon in the morning - my pocket was taken from under my head while we were in bed, at one o'clock in the morning.
   JOSEPH RENSHAW. I live with Mrs. Wilkinson. I was taken ill at one o'clock in the morning, and came down stairs - I found the street door open, and about three yards from the door sat the prisoner, with the coat on her back, and the bonnet on her head - she had left the house - I said,"You have got Mr. Wilkinson's coat on" - she said Mr. Wilkinson had given her it to put on - I called the prosecutrix - the policeman came - there was no light in the room - the prosecutrix's husband is a milkman, and goes out at two o'clock in the morning - I found several of the prosecutor's things thrown upon the stairs, towards my room - the prosecutrix being called, missed the pocket from her bed.
   JAMES CLAPSON. I am a policeman. I was sent for, and found the prisoner in custody - I found the shoes tied up in an apron - she had taken them out of her pocket - I found 4s. 0 1/2d. on her person.
 SARAH WILKINSON re-examined. I lost 2s. 2 1/2d. from my pocket, and my husband lost 1s. 9 1/2d. from his pocket - this is my husband's tobacco box - the coat and all the property are ours - my husband had nothing to put on when he got up.
     GUILTY. Aged 49. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1448.  WILLIAM THOMPSON and GEORGE STONE were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 1 saw, value 4s., the goods of Richard Mason Wood.
   ALEXANDER WHITE. I was in Chiswell-street on the 27th of August, about half-past eight o'clock in the morning, and saw the prisoners in company together - I watched them, and saw them cross the street to Sowerby's, the pawnbrokers, and loiter about for some time - they then crossed over to Wood's shop - a saw laid in the passage - I saw stone go in, and when he came out he had the saw in his hand - Thompson was waiting outside for him - I directly went over to Mr. Wood, and saw Harrison, and told him - I pointed them out when they got to Bunhill-row - I had seen Stone hand the saw to Thompson - I called "Stop thief," we overtook them, and they were secured at the corner of Jewin-street.
   ROBERT HARRISON. I am errand boy to Mr. Wood - White gave me information - I went in pursuit - I overtook the prisoners, and they were secured - here is the saw.
   EDWARD McDONAL. I am a City policeman - I have had the saw ever since I took the prisoners into custody.
 RICHARD MASON WOOD. I am a  printer- this is my saw - it lay near the doorway.
 Thompson's Defence. I was passing along the street, somebody going by, with a fustian coat on, dropped something - I picked it up - it was the saw - I did not run away with it till after I got some distance - when I heard a cry of"Stop him with the saw" - I then turned back, and gave the saw to the witness.
