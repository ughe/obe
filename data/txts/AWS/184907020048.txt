WILLIaM SMITH ALLeN. I am a joiner, and have been engaged in the
business nearly all my life-I was employed by Mr. Gear, the foreman to
Mr. Pitcher, somewhere about last Oct., to work on board the Bombay-
continued to work at her till sometime in Feb. I think-she was lying in the
East India outward-bound dock-there were other persons employed at the
same time in doing work on board-there was a person on board who gave
orders what was to be done-I do not know who he was-they used to call
him the colonel-he was a short, darkish man, I never heard his name-the
vessel was fitted up in a very splendid manner-I should say it was more like
a ship-of-war than a merchant-vessel- never saw a merchant-vessel fitted up
like her-she was much stronger than any boat I had ever been on board
before-I know the mail-boats, I should think they have above 100 cabins on
board, I do not think there were twenty on board the Bombay-there was
metal down on the deck for the traversing of the guns, and there were fresh
carlins fore and aft-carlins are pieces of timber put in between the beams
