 SECOND COUNT, for uttering the same.
   THOMAS ALLAN. I am a warehouseman, and live in Friday-street, Cheapside, and have a partner; the prisoner was our porter; he carried goods to different wharfs and booking-offices. On the 16th of July our packer gave him a box to carry to the wharf - On the 23rd of July he produced to me two receipts for 1s. 6d. each, for wharfage and shipping charges; here is one signed "Beloe;" I paid him 1s. 6d. for that - I suspected it at the time.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. How long was he in your employ? A. About six weeks as porter; he gave me notice to quit on the Monday morning after I kept the note back - our porters occasionally employ journeymen carters as they pass along to take goods to the wharfs, but not by our order - that is the cheapest way, but I never saw him employ them; I believe our porters have done so, by their charging it in their books - they sometimes have a heavy load, and give a man 3d. or 6d. to assist if he is going their way; they frequently charge me for "a pitch;" they have no authority from me to employ chance carters - when I have found they had got a cart to assist, when they did not want it, I have objected to their charge, and they have said the load was too heavy.
   DANIEL BELOE. I am clerk to Mr. Wilson, of Smith's wharf, Tooley-street. Part of this receipt is in my writing; I wrote it for 1s., it has been altered to 1s. 6d. - I am positive it was only for 1s. when I signed it; the 6d. is not in my writing.
 Cross-examined. Q. How many of these do you sign in a day? A. I may sign fifty - I enter them in a book, which I have here; I cannot say whether the prisoner is the person who paid me 1s.
   JOSEPH RENSHAW. I am a Policeman. I received the prisoner in charge.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was a perfect stranger to the trade, and got a carter to take the goods to the wharf - I stood by the cart; they got the receipts from the wharf, and gave them to me as they are - if they were altered, it was before they came into my hands.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1931.  SAMUEL BARTLETT was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of July , 11 carved ivory knobs, value 36s.; 20 ivory hooks, value 23s., and 11 ivory fists, value 22s., the goods of Charles Houlden , his master.
 CHARLES HOULDEN. I am an  ivory-turner, and live in Fitchett-court, Noble-street- the prisoner had been my apprentice about eighteen months, I missed goods several times - I received information, and went to Kenn's umbrella-shop, Bagnigge-wells, and found these goods, which are worth about 3l. - I am quite certain they are mine; I took Kenn to my place, called the prisoner into his presence, and asked if he knew any thing of those goods; he said he did not - Kenn said he had bought them of him; the prisoner then asked me to forgive him.
   WILLIAM KENN. I keep an umbrella-shop, near Bagnigge-wells. On the 2nd of July the prisoner brought these goods to my shop, and I bought them of him for 2l. 5s. - he offered them for 2l. 15s., and said he made them - I had bought of him before; I saw Mr. Houlden on the 24th, who claimed them - I went to his house with him, and directly the prisoner was produced, I said he was the person - he denied it at first, but afterwards said it was his first offence, and hoped to be forgiven.
   WILLIAM COLYER. I received him in charge, and heard him ask for pardon.
 The prisoner received an excellent character.
     GUILTY. Aged 18.
 1932.  SAMUEL BARTLETT was again indicted for stealing, on the 19th of May , 60 ivory hooks, value 2l., the goods of Charles Houlden.
   CHARLES HOULDEN. I found these goods in possession of Clark, about the 19th of May, and am sure they are mine - there were one hundred and forty-three hooks; more than ninety-five of them are ivory.
   ANTHONY CLARK. I live in King's-road. About the 19th of May I bought some ivory hooks of the prisoner for 20s.; I bought another lot afterwards.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 18.
 1933.  SAMUEL BARTLETT was again indicted for stealing, on the 14th of May, 12 parasol handles, value 30s., the goods of Charles Houlden.
   WILLIAM KENN. About the 14th of May I bought these twelve parasol handles of the prisoner for 21s.
 CHARLES HOULDEN. They are mine, and worth 30s.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Twelve Months.
 1934.  JOSEPH MATHER was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , 18 1/2 yards of printed cotton, value 9s., the goods of Thomas Matthews.
 JOHN MALLET. I am shopman to  Thomas Matthews, a linen-draper, of Aldgate. This cotton hung about four feet within the door - I saw the prisoner come into the shop, and take it: I pursued, and took him about fifteen yards off, with it on his arm.
   JOHN FORRESTER. I am a constable. I received him in charge; he said he did not care any thing about it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I never had it in my possession.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. - Transported for Seven Years.
