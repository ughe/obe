Q. Did you give evidence in this case of your own accord, or were you
called on to do so? A. I had not time to come over--I sent word over
to Mr. Allen on the Wedaesday, about an hour after Squires made the
communication to me--I went before the Magistrate on the Saturday-
one of the officers called on me that was after I had made the communi-
cation to Allen-I stated at the office all of the circumstances as near as I
could recollect- I never had any quarrel with the prisoner-I had always
been on friendly terms with bim-Squires is living at this place now
messenger was here sent to fetch Squires.)
JOHN RICHARD JOHNSON. I live at No. 7, Dorset-street, Spitalfields,
and am a print-colourer-I colour caricatures, and principally religious
prints--that is the way in wbich I get my living--I have known the pri
soner between four and five years—I saw him on Friday evening the 13th
of March-I left off work about half-past seven o'clock that evening, and
when I came down stairs there was a person in the passage inquiring for
Jem-I did not see him I heard him, and when I came down in the passage
the woman below said Jem was not at home, and then I saw the prisoner
"Arthur Nicholson" that was the name he gave me a private in the
11th Light Dragoons, Canterbury”-the prisoner was present when I wrote
this paper I have in my hand that if ever he should be tried as a deserter,
I might know his right name he gave me this, as near as possible, twelve
months ago last December-he desired me to write down his name that I
might know if he was ever tried as a deserter, he was the man-it is my
handwriting-he saw me write itI put it into the drawer, and there it
remained till this occurrence took place when I saw him in the passage, I
said, “Dick, how do you get on ?"-he said he wanted to see Jent-
asked him what he wanted to see Jem for he said he wanted to see him,
for he wanted to borrow a screw-I asked him what forbe said he was
going to serve an old gentleman in a lonely cottage - I told him he had
better not do any thing of the kind, if he did he would be sure to get trans-
ported-he told me he might as well be transported for that, for if he was
taken as a deserter, he was sure to be transported at the end of that con-
versation, a fight took place at the bottom of our street, and I and the pri-
soner went down together to see it-seven or eight rounds were fought---
the police came up to disperse the mob, and I and the prisoner returned
back again about as far as our door, and I wished him good night-he said,
"You had better not go yet, for I want to see Jem very particularly"--
asked him what he wanted to see Jem so very particularly for he said he
wanted a darkey, that he had left there some time ago, and it might be
handy-I told him if he wanted to see Jem so very particularly, he had
better go over the way to the public-house-he said he could not go over
the way, for he had no money-he further stated, that if he served the old
gentleman, as was his intention on the Saturday, he was going over the
water to see his aunt or cousin, and would give me a call on Sunday, and
let me know.
Q. Let you know what? A. Whether he had robbed the old gentle-
man, I suppose--that is the inference I drew from what he said he did
not say he would rob him-he said he would call on Sunday and let me
know.
