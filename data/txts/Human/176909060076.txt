The Captain came in again: then the man with me said, I'll do you over for six-penny worth. The other said, Aye. Then he pulled out with his six-pence some guineas and some half guineas. We won six-pence at hiding under the hat. I put the halfpenny under, and my partner lifted up the hat and looked at the halfpenny. Then he said to the Captain, What do you cry, a man's head, or a woman's head? The other said a woman's head. It was not what he cried. Then we won. The Captain then made a great talking about whores, that it was a great expence, and he did not mind money, saying, he had been among a parcel of butchers and had lost 50 l. The breeches-maker said, Sir, if I had not been there, you would have been almost killed. Then the Captain kept swearing and talking to him, and said, Here, you was a good fellow, here is a guinea for you, and laid a guinea into the breeches-maker's hand. The breeches-maker said, Sir, I don't desire it. Said the Captain, You was a good fellow, when the butchers won my money, take and keep it. Then the man put it into his pocket. After that, the Captain went out: he had got his pocket filled with liquor and came in with it, and we took share of it. Then he said, Have you a mind to play for forty, or fifty, or one hundred pounds? Said my partner, Do you think you can do him over? No, said I; I have not got any such money to play. I have but seven guineas, and that I intend to lay out for this horse that I wait for. Said he, Let us have a stroke at him for about a five-and-three-pence or half a guinea; that will not hurt us. The horse will be worth the money. So I did. We put the halfpenny as before under the hat, and my partner lifted it up, and looked at it, and said, Captain, what do you cry, a man's head or a woman's head? Then the Captain with an oath cried one of them. The Captain lost. This was for half a guinea. After we drank up our liquor, the Captain went out and brought in half a pint of brandy, and sat down on his chair again. The man that was my partner takes the pot, and pours his liquor into our bowl, and said, Drink heartily. I took it and drank, and he drank after me, and gave it to me, and I drank again. I then found myself quite intoxicated. I said the horse will not come, and I should lose my market, as it grew tish. He said, No, no, the horse will soon be here; you have no occasion to be in a hurry. Then he said to me, How much money have you got? I said, I have got but seven guineas. Then he said, What do you think of this fellow? (meaning the prisoner.) I said, I think he is a foolish fellow. The prisoner kept swearing and damning, that he did not value money; for what was money to him, he could go on board a ship and get money. Then he threw his purse upon the table again, and his guineas scattered about. He said, There, you may take some if you will. I said, I do not want your money. My partner said, He does not mind his money; you and I may as well win twenty or thirty of him. I said I have no such money to play. Then my partner pulled out a pocket-book, and took out a note drawn upon some banker for twenty-nine pounds, odd money. They looked at it, and called it a banker's cheque. Said he, You fool, if you lose, you shall not want for money; I will go and change this note, and get cash for it. He said, It is all nonsense to be afraid; we are sure to win. I said, If I should lose, I should not be able to get the horse. He said, No, no, do not fear, for I will get cash for this note, and let us play for twenty. I said, No, I can't. Said he, You have got seven guineas, and your watch value at three pounds, that is ten: let us drink again, and have better heart. So I drank, and being confused, I laid my watch and money down: the prisoner pulled a handkerchief out of his pocket, laid his purse in the handkerchief, took my watch and money, and wrapped all up in the handkerchief, and laid it on the table: then my partner said, Do not be dishearented, take a halfpenny out of your pocket. I did, Said he, Lay it under your hat. Then he took up the hat every time, and looked at the halfpenny, and said to the prisoner, What do you cry now, a man's head, or a woman's head? The prisoner, with an oath, said as it happened to be. He was to guess three times out of four for the whole money and watch. He guessed three times right. Then he takes and sweeps the watch, money, and purse and all, into his pocket. I then said to my partner, You will change your note. He said, Stay a bit, and I will. (I had tied my horse to the rails in Smithfield.) I said I will go and see if my horse is safe. I hope you will be honourable enough to stay till I come.
