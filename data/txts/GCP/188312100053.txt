The prisoner, in her defence, stated that her husband was a master mariner
and had retired on a pension, and was killed in 1867, and that when she met
Lord Kilmorey she was in deep mourning ; that Mr. Lempriere was a gentle-
man, and had left her enough to keep her for life; that the Lord Chief Justice
had ordered her papers in the T'ichborne Trial to be given up to her ; that she
had claimed them seven times since 1873, and when she was going to apply
again this charge was brought against her; and that after her husband was
killed at Alexandria she came to England, and was here from May, 1881, to
September, 1882.
