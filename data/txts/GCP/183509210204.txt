Q. Did you not direct both Lee and Beales to run in opposition to Bin
and Johnson, and not to leave them till they had your orders, if they did
not earn one shilling? A. Never-I believe they have been in every one's
time, but I never gave them orders, except not to go in the women's time
(there are some female proprietors)--that was about a month or five
weeks before the prosecution commenced.
Q. Have you not sworn to Mr. Phillips that you never gave them any
specific directions at all, but that they ran when they pleased, and did
as they liked ? A. Yes, they did - I have never, on any occasion, directly
or indirectly, by myself or Mr. Norton, sought or agreed to settle this matter
if I could get money paid me-1 did not, after the 8th of June, give diree-
tions to Lee to run my omnibus against Bird and Johnson.
Cross-examined by Mr. KNOWLES. Q. Do you know Thomas Parker!
1. Yes; he is foreman to Mr. Aldridge-I might see him about the 5th
of this month, with the two Bardells, and there might be a conversation
about this indictment- I told them I had no animosity against them, but
their names must be in the indictment.
Re-examined. Q. You were a bankrupt the first time, about seven
years ago ? A. Yes; I obtained my certificate about two months after-
wards- I did not leave my premises--they took bills at certain dates,
which were afterwards paid-the second time I was a bankrupt, was about
ten months ago--I had lost 900l. in three weeks, by bad debts, which was
the cause of my being a bankrupt-I got my certificate on the 15th dl
January-every one of my creditors signed it, who had proved- I do not
know where Lee or Mann live-I live nearly opposite Day and Martin's,
within a few doors--I hardly know wbat the meaning of a time is-1
understand it is to start three minutes before another-if my omnibus
started from the Bank at ten o'clock in the morning, it would go to Padding-
ton, and it would stop there for half an hour perhaps-they went just a
they could that would necessarily interfere with different times. )
Court. Q. Tell us every thing that was said by you to John Bardell!
A. He asked me if I would go with him to get Mr. Norton to take the
bail for his brother, without some certificate, or something; I forget what;
and would I go and advise Mr. Norton to do so, without giving any
trouble; and I did so he did not ask me why the Bardells were made
defendants when I went with him he complained of Mr. Norton having
refused the bail, and asked whether, if he were there on Monday, prepared
with the bail, he would take it ; and I told Mr. Norton not to give them
any more trouble, but to take their bail - I did not want to be litigious
with them, or any one-I had Bates once before the Alderman for co-
ducting himself wrong to my buses.
