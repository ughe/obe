I told him she was at Noah's Ark in Stroud, where I had directions to send the money; he said he'd send me to prison till I did produce the woman. I begged for liberty, in order to find her ; he said no, but I must go before the justice: the justice examined me; and I told his worship the same I do here: and now I observe in Mr. Cotes's evidence, he says I said before the justice, that this woman was to be found in Ratcliff highway ; I said I had the paper from her there. The people of the house don't know her, they know me; and if your lordship pleases, I will call the woman who keeps a green stall; she will tell what passed between this last evidence and her in that house.
   Mary Gold. There came a gentleman, I don't know who he was, and asked after the prisoner at the bar; and also asked me, if I knew one Elizabeth Symonds? I told him I did; he asked me where she was? I told him, I did not know; her name was not Symonds now; she has been twice married since.
 Q. What time was this?
 M. Gold. I don't know the day of the month, it was the same day the prisoner was taken up. I told him I believed she was now in Suffolk; he said she had been with him within this week past; I knew it could not be the same; I made no answer and wondered what it could be upon, and he did not tell me what he came about; only he told me, if I could find her, there was money due to her from on board a ship; that her husband died in the king's service; I said I did not know where to send to her. I have known the prisoner near two years; he always behaved well in my house; he kept good hours, and spent his money with great conduct as the neighbours tell me.
   Robert Cofield. I have known the prisoner about 10 months, he always paid me very honestly for whatever I did for him.
     Acquitted.

 152.  Henry Lloyd, was indicted for stealing one silver watch, value 2 l. 2 s. the property of Thomas Cureton, in the dwelling-house of John Luke Landon. Dec. 8. ++
   Thomas Cureton. On the 7th of Dec. about eight at night, four men came to empty the necessary house; my master lives in White-lion-yard, Spittlefields; I sat up; between 9 and 10 o'clock my master came to me, and bid me go to bed. I pulled my watch out of my pocket, and hung it upon a nail in the kitchen, and went to bed, expecting to be called up about one, but was not called up till five; then I went into the kitchen to see for my watch, and it was gone. I went and told the person who sat up with those men, it was gone, and he said he knew who must have it; he went to the master's house of those men. We got a constable and searched, and found the watch in the hay-loft, where the prisoner and the other three men were lying. The watch was produced in court, and deposed to, the prisoner did not own to the taking it.
   Thomas Mumford. I am fellow servant to the prosecutor. About 12 o'clock on the said night, my master and the others in the house went to bed, and left me to take care of these men. I was either in the kitchen, or with them all the time till about four in the morning, when they had done, at which time I had orders to give them some bread and cheese and small beer: The prisoner had been down in the vault, and he was so weak when he came out he could not stand; I gave him a dram of gin when he was washing himself; he fell down upon the stones with cold; he asked me, if I had any fire below? I told him, no: he then asked me, if he might make a little fire of hay or straw? I told him, no. I seeing him so very cold, let him go down into the kitchen, and I went with him. I had a fire there, but did not tell him so at first; I gave him some bread and cheese and small beer there; he pulled off his shoes and stood by the fire and warmed himself; the others had victuals and drink in another place.
