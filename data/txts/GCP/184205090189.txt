JOHN WILLIAMS, I am the prisoner's brother-in-law. I embarked with
him on board the Pathfinder I was not present at the conversations be-
tween him and Mr. Marklew—I heard him say he would take his son out
with bim, and that he kept the Yarrow Steam Packet Hotel, at Port Philip
I did not bear him say any thing about receiving 7001. or 8001. I was
present at the Mansion-house when he was before the Lord Mayor-I do
not know whether what he said then was taken down in writing- I will
not be sure whether Mr. Marklew stated there in the prisoner's presence,
that he (the prisoner) had said he kept the Yarrow Steam Packet Hotel at
Port Philip-1 married the prisoner's sister-I think she was going on for
seventeen years of age-I never knew of his having to receive 7007, or
8001. in England—I have known him about four years, before he went to
Australia, and when he came back-he married my sister.
Prisoner. It is about two years since I first went out to Australia.
Witness. Yes, in 1840.
MR. MARKLEW re-examined. I was present at the prisoner's examin-
ation at the Mansion-house-I do not think what he said was taken
down-I stated there, in his hearing, that he had told me he came over
here to receive 7001. or 8001., and that he kept the Yarrow Steam Packet
Hotel at Port Philip-the prisoner replied, " It is false," and called me a
false perjured villain for stating that he had ever said any thing about the
7001. or 8001., for he never had stated such a thing, and that I should
suffer for it on my death bed-he said he never was the proprietor of the
Yarrow Steam Packet Hotel, and he had never stated to me that he was.
Prisoner. I stated that I was never the proprietor, but that I kept the
hotel. Witness. He said he never was the proprietor, but he was always
there to manage the business-I am sure he did tell me that he was the
proprietor in the first instance it was on Saturday, the 9th of October,
that I gave him the goods and money, and it was on that day that he told
me he had paid the deposit.
