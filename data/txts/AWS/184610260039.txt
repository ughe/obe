Cross-ezamined by Mr. BODKIN Q. Do you agree with Mr. Coulson, that
delirium tremens was the immediate cause of death A. It was-I saw him
during life| was ealled in to assist Mr. Coulson in the post mortem examina-
tion by his desire-I think a great distinetion should be drawn in cases where
you have delirium tremens arising from wounds-I should expect to meet
with a great degeneration of the ecchymosis, of the swelling externally--it
would be pulpy-there would be palpable evidence of the mischief going on
inside the brain-where delirium tremens is the result of the wounds, the
wounds themselve would indicate that things are not going on right within
the seull-if the wounds had healed rapidly I should not have expeeted that
they were the cause of delirium tremens-] have seen a great many cases of
delirium tremensin this country the general exciting cause is drink-if a
person who is not an habitual drunkard, but who is constantly sipping and
taking large quantities of porter, was engaged in a quarrel, or in a passion, it
would be likely to bring it on; but delirium tremens is sometimes caused
without drinking if a man had a predisposition to it excitement would be
very likely to produce itthe passions of the mind would produce it, totally
irrespective of any violence-the state of the liver indicated that, in some
degree, the deceased must have drank-the existence of delirium tremens in
this case is consistent with the fact of its having been induced by the exeite-
ment of the conflict, irrespective of any blow at all-it is my opinion that if
the blow had been at all an exciting cause of delirium tremens it would have
presented a different external appearance-i the blows had been the cause as
blows.
