   Cross-examined.  I instructed no solicitor to prosecute—I was shown into a room at the police-court after waiting a quarter of an hour—there were three or four average aged women there—the prisoner was not so old; she was the only person wearing a hat—I told the police the woman who came into my shop wore a hat; the others had bonnets on—I had never seen any of them before—I gave her the meat before she said it was for Mrs. Farley—I thought she was a respectable woman—a detective came for me to go to the station next day—I did not give a description to the police—Mrs. Farley's husband did—I did not interfere till the police called on me, and said they had got a woman in custody who answered the description, and wanted me to identify her—there are two Mrs. Farleys in Wellington Street—I first asked her if she was the photographer's wife after she had the mutton—I have not got the mutton back.
   Re-examined.  I would not have let her take the mutton out of the shop unless she had mentioned the person's name—she had a different hat at the station. FREDERICK ALEXANDER( Detective Officer ). I took the prisoner on 1st August, two days after—I read the warrant to her, and told her I should have to take her in custody on the charge in the warrant—she said she did not have the meat, neither had she been out of the house on the afternoon of the 29th.
   Cross-examined.  We received no description of the woman from the prosecutor—he picked out the prisoner from among two other women who had bonnets on, and were working men's wives; one was much older and the other about the same age, not over 40—I made no note of the conversation with the prisoner—I have given from my memory exactly what she said—I said at the police-court that she said at the station "I met Mrs. Farley; she got some steak and gave it to me"—I said before the Magistrate on the second occasion that she said she had not been out on that day—I signed my deposition knowing it appeared there. ( This did not appear in the deposition. ) Wellington Street is in the immediate neighbourhood of Mr. Gurney's shop. ELIZABETH FARLEY. I am the wife of Thomas Farley, a photographer of 74, Wellington Street, Woolwich—I know the prisoner—I did not send her on 24th July to purchase mutton for me—I had given her that morning 2 lb. of steak for her husband's dinner out of charity—I thought she wanted it; she is a tradesman's wife.
   Cross-examined.  She has lived in Woolwich some time; there is no difficulty in finding her.
   MR GEOGHEGAN submitted that the false pretences being subsequent to the meat having been obtained there was no case to go to the Jury. The COMMON SERJEANT considered it was a question for them. The Jury, without hearing MR. GEOGHEGAN , returned a verdict of NOT GUILTY.
