Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Does your master sell clothes
in a made up state ? 4. Yes—he gets his cloth direct from wholesale houses
in London, Mr. Vicat's in Wood-street, and Mr, Williams's in Aldermanbury,
and not in any other way that I know of-the 1st of Nov. was the last time I
saw these articles in my master's shop-I bought part of them on that day-I
sleep on the premises- I went to bed about eleven o'clock-the act must have
been committed after eleven.

JOHN PAWLEY, shopman to Mr. Attenboro, pawnbroker, Crown-street,
Finsbury. I produce six remnants of cloth and kerseymere pledged on the
2nd of Nov. by James Bradshaw, jun., for his father-I advanced altogether
101. 198.-it was worth about 141., or very likely not so much-there is about
thirty odd yards, I do not know exactly-part is beaver, part broad-cloth,
and part kerseymere--I had been in the habit of taking in pledges of the elder
Bradshaw for fifteen years—that was what induced me to advance money to
the young man-it is very common to pledge in the cloth trade, particularly
for tailors-on the next day the elder Bradshaw came to our shop to measure
me for a pair of trowsers—I asked him if he was aware that bis son had
brought some cloth to pledge yesterday he said yes, it belonged to a gentle-
man named Hodges, a master tailor, at Gravesend, whom he had worked for
for years-in consequence of something told me by a fellow-shopman, on
Monday morning, the 6th, I communicated something to the prosecutor
went with him, his foreman, and a constable, to the Bradshaws' house-I said
something to one of the Bradshaws, but forget what it was now.
