Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  I think Lee, Mann, and Beals were servants of Mr. Bray as well as yourself? A.  Yes.
   Q.  Have you not told Mr. Bird, when you were running your omni buses against his, that you should not have done it but you had received orders? A.  I certainly received orders to go in his time, as well as Mr. Trevett's, as it was a very good time.
   Q.  Now I give you notice that Lee and Beals are here; upon your solemn oath, have you not said that to Mr. Bird? A.  No, Sir; I have not said any thing to him in particular—I have not told him I was sorry to oppose him—I never had any any conversation with either of the Mr. Birds about going in their times.
   JURY . Q.  Had you orders to go in Mr. Bird's time? A.  I had.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did you not assign that as a reason to Mr. Bird? A.  No; Birds name was mentioined to me, and Trevett's and Johnson's was also mentioned by Mr. Bray.
   Q.  Then if Bray has sworn that he never directed any servant of his to run in opposition, or in the time of any body by name, has he not sworn what is wickedly false? A.  I do not know what Mr. Bray has sworn—I worked for Mr. Bray before he bought the third omnibus—I do not know who he bouoght it of—I have not known Mr. Norton till within the last month—I have seen him about Mr. Bray's premises since that time—I have never been in any trouble myself—I have not for a long period get my living by fighting—I have fought pitched battles for money—I know Lyons, he used to be an omnibus proprietor.
   Re-examined. Q.  You never saw Mr. Norton till the last month? A.  No—the directions I first received from Mr. Bray, were to go in what time I liked; as he did not know what time, he would leave it all to his men—about the first or second weeek after I went, he said that Bird's and Trevett's were very good times, we could not do better than to go in their times—their time was about nint o'clock in the morning, or a little after—It is just upon seven years since I fought my last pitched battle—I never killed a man in my life—I was present at a fight where a man was kille, and two men were transported for it. HENRY SWAITZER. I am a clerk in the Stamp Office. On the 15th of June, Thomas Bolton, in the name of John Bolton, obtained a supple ment license, in the name of Edmund Sylvester; and at the same time, Robert Trevett obtained a supplemental license, adding the name of Thomas Corbett to his own—the first was for the "Emperor," and the second for the "Albion;" and on the 6th of July, Bray obtained a sup plemental license, to go to Maida Hill—and on the same day, Bolton and Sylvester obtained a supplemental license, lengthening the distance for the "Emperor" to Maida Hill. WILLIAM NASH. On the 27th of July, I saw an omnibus and the horses had blue ribbons—I cannot tell the number of the omnibus, nor who drove it—I did not see either of the defendants there, to the best of my knowledge—I know Mr. Cowderoy—I do not recollect that I have drank with him—I saw him at the Feathers that day, I believe—I do not recollect seeing him in the box of the omnibus when the horses had blue ribbons—I cannot recollect who I saw—I am in the habit of standing at my gate, and seeing them go past.
