   MR. ATTORNEY-GENERAL . Q.  What sort of terms have you been on with Mr. and Mrs. Thomas Williams? A.  On no terms at all—I do not think I spoke half a dozen words to Mr. Thomas Williams in my life, at least since that marriage was in contemplation—there was no intimacy between us before the marriage—I received this letter in course of post—it is signed by my father:—"Plasgwyn, Feb. 22nd, 1836.
 "  DEAR PAUL ,—In closed I send you 75 l ., which you will please to ac knowledge by return of post. On Saturday I received your letter announcing the birth of your little boy—I congratulate you upon the event, and I am glad to hear that the mother and infant are doing well, and hope they will continue to do so. I remain, dear Paul, yours affectionately, " JONES PANTON ."

   Q.  Did you receive any answer? A.  Yes, signed by my brother Bar ton, but I destroyed it with a number of others—it stated that he had not thought fit to communicate my letter to my father, because he had rallied and was not conscious of the dangerous state he was in—I cannot ex actly say the date of that letter, but it was several days after I had written—my father died on the 26th of May.
   Q.  Now just look at this paper in pencil, (No, 7,) do you see any dif ference in the hand-writing of the whole of that; do you believe the whole of that to be your father's hand-writing? A.  To the best of my judg ment it is all my father's hand-writing. RICHARD MATTHIAS PRIEST, ESQ. I am cashier to the Carnarvon Old Bank, and am in the commission of the peace, for the borough of Car narvon. The deceased, Mr. Jones Panton, kept cash at our bank twelve years—at that time I inspected and paid cheques that he drew—I never saw him write, but they were always received right in account—I believe this letter, of the 22nd of April, 1834, to be Mr. Jones Pan ton's hand-writing—I believe this (scrip No. 9) to be his writing—I believe this (No. 7) to be his writing, the whole of it, both sides.
