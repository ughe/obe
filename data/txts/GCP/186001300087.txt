JOHN PETER PERRY. I am chief clerk to the Magistrate at Lambeth-
police-court-I took notes of the evidence given against Kinghorn on 17th
October—this is the note I took of the prosecutor's evidence-(This being read,
the prosecutor, after describing his meeting with Burns, proceeded as follows:
“We went to another public-house, where I became quite senseless, just as if
I was drunk, and I had only had two glasses of ale-I was put into a cab,
and I remember no more till I found myself at the Crown and Hope beer-
shop-I was not much better then-I found myself playing at skittles I
tound I had lost my money before I had played at skittles-after I had lost
my money I wanted to come out, but Kinghorn would not let me they got
hold of me, and then I remember playing at skittles the other two men
were there at the same time-I played at skittles with another man not here
--it was 5 o'clock when I met the first man, and about 7 when I missed the
money. Cross-examined. I don't remember what took place at the Hope
and Anchor, not exactly, till I was playing at 'skittles-the prisoner is the
man, I believe, to the best of my recollection."
Cross-examined by MR. GIFFARD.Q. When was the next examination
A. On the following Wednesday, I have no note here of that date-I have
only brought the book I was required to bring-I know as a fact that the
man was brought up on 19th October and was then discharged afterwards
another man was taken, and subsequently a third man, and then Mr. Norton
directed Kinghorn to be re-apprehended, and to be brought before him again.

245. RICHARD ELD (21), Feloniously assaulting Edward Yates, with
intent to rob him.
EDWARD YATES, I am a servant living at 4, Vauxhall-terrace, Vauxhall-
walk-on the night of 10th January I was walking along Dorey-street,
Lambeth ; it was about half-past ten as near as I can judge-two men came
behind me, I never heard them, they sprung on me and grasped me with
their hands round my throat-I placed my hand in my pocket to secure my
Swatch; one of them after that let my throat go and made & snatch at my
guard-I still kept hold of my watch as they turned round, one of them
came back and struck me in the eye-as they turned round to let my throat
go I recognised the prisoner to be the man that struck me I had never
seen him before he was brought back to me about five or ten minutes
afterwards I have not the least doubt that the person brought back to me
was one of the persons so engaged.
