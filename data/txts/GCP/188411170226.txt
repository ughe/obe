80. EMILY BUDD (42), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
MR. GOODRICH Prosecutod.
WILLIAM HUBBARD. I keep the Surrey Arms public-house, Wilcox
Road, Lambeth-on 18th October, about half past eleven a.m., I was
serving, when the prisoner came in for a pint of four ale, and tendered
a half-crown-I gave her 28. 4d. change, and then tested the half-crown
in my teeth, which sank into it-I told her it was bad, and asked where
she got it-she said at a butcher's on the terrace I said "What
butcher's ?" --she said “From Mr. Reed, or one of the other butchers" -
she then gave me a good shilling, and gave me back my change, and I
returned her the half-crown-I was quite sure it was bad, and quite sure
she was the woman-I did nothing besides biting it; two or three of
my customers handled it, and some of them followed her-she would
not let them damage it in any way because she said she wanted to take
it back.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I sent one of my customers to see where
you went, and you went to 7, Luscombe Street.
CHARLES LANGLEY. I am a butcher, of 3, Simpson Street, Wands-
worth Road-on the morning of 22nd October, between eleven and twelve,
the prisoner came to my stall in the Wandsworth Road and asked for &
pound of 4fd. pieces, and tendered a half-crown-I gave her 28. 1 d.change,
and she went away-I put the half-crown in my pocket-I had no other
half-crown there-I looked at it again when I got home, and found it
was bad-I put it in the tester and it broke-I put the smallest piece in
the fire, and this other piece (produoed) I gave the sergeant-I am quite
certain the prisoner is the woman.
ALFRED VAUGHAN. I am a butcher, of 281, Wandsworth Road-on
24th October, about 5.30 in the afternoon, the prisoner came to my stall
for half a pound of 41d. pieces and tendered this half-crown-I gave her
two separate shillings and 14d. change and put the half-crown in a jug
where there were only coppers-about half an hour after Langley com-
municated with me, and I then examined the half-crown and found it
was bad by indenting it with my teeth-I work at Charles Langley's
stall-on 25th October, the next morning. I and Mr. Hubbard saw the
prisoner in the Luscombe Road outside a public-house, I recognised her
and gave her into the custody of Detective Vagg-I had no conversation
with her, the constable had.
ALFRED Beach (Policeman W 52). On 25th October the two last
witnesses gave the prisoner into my charge for uttering two counterfeit
half-crowns on 22nd and 24th October-I said “You will be charged
with uttering two counterfeit half-crowns"-she said "I wasn't there, I
know nothing about it, I am a respectable married woman"-I took her to
the station and she was charged, I was not then present.
