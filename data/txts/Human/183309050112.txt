 1376.  ALFRED PEGG was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of August , 11 sovereigns, the monies of Joseph Saxon.
   ELIZABETH SAXON. I am the wife of Joseph Saxon. The prisoner was partly a servant of mine - I gave him a cheque for 11l., on Hankey's, on the 15th of August; this is the cheque - I never had the money in my possession.
   JOHN LENT. I am clerk to Messrs. Hankey's - I paid this cheque, but I cannot say to whom.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1377.  JOHN PATTEN and JOSEPH LEYLAND were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 watch, value 3l.; 1 seal, value 16s.; and 1 watch-key, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Seaman , from his person.
 THOMAS SEAMAN. I am a  letter-carrier, to the Two-penny Post-office, on the 29th of August, I was in Tabernacle-square, between nine and ten o'clock, I was intoxicated and did not know what I was about - I had a silver watch with me, with a gold key, and seal.
   JAMES MIELL. I am acquainted with the prosecutor, I met him that night, he was drunk, there was a mob of forty or fifty people in Tabernacle-square, he was obstinate and would go into the mob - the two prisoners were there close to each other; they endeavoured to pick a quarrel with the prosecutor - I got him out of the mob, and on towards Old-street - he had his watch at that time, the two prisoners followed us, and Patten ran against the prosecutor's shoulder, and pulled the watch out of his pocket - the other prisoner was close to him - I took Patten by the collar - the policeman came up and I gave them both in charge - the watch has not been found, there was still some persons about - I positively swear I saw Patten take the watch.
 Patten. How do you know the prosecutor had a watch? Witness. I saw it, and saw you draw it from him; I cannot tell what you did with it.
   WILLIAM HOLLAND(police-constable N 146). About eleven o'clock at night, I heard of a robbery, and went out, and saw Miell with the prisoner Patten, I took him, and he proved to be the person I was looking after for another robbery, but the parties would not prosecute - Miell had been drinking a little.
   WILLIAM MEDLICOTT. I am an officer. I took Leyland.
 Patten's Defence. I had been on an errand to Chiswell-street, I was coming home through Tabernacle-square, and the witness charged me with this.
 PATTEN -    GUILTY. Aged 19.
 LEYLAND -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1378.  EDWARD PARKER was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , 1 handkerchief, value 5s. the goods of Alexander James Burgess.
 2nd COUNT, stating it to be the goods of Alexander James Burgess and another.
   SAMUEL ROSE. I know the prosecutor's shop, it is nearly opposite my house, in Shoreditch- I saw the prisoner go into his shop on the 31st of August, and pull down a handkerchief, he came out with it, and was putting it into his bosom - I ran over and seized him with it, and he threw it down behind him.
   ALEXANDER JAMES BURGESS. I am a pawnbroker and have a partner - this is our handkerchief, it has our mark on it.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Six Weeks.
 1379.  JANE CORNWALL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , 1 gown, value 5s.; 1 sheet, value 5s.; 1 tea-spoon, value 2s.; and 2 waistcoats, value 3s., the goods of Henry Rutter , her master - and 15 shirts, value 4l.; 4 waistcoats, value 10s.; 1 hat, value 15s.; 5 coats, value 3l.; 4 pair of trousers, value 1l.; and 1 book, value 1s., the goods of John Jones.
   SARAH RUTTER. I am the wife of Henry Rutter. John Jones lives in our house, No. 19, Great Coram-street; the prisoner was my servant; she came on the 19th of September, in last year, and left me on the 31st of August last - I had given her a regular warning; after she was gone I missed the property stated - I sent an officer to the prisoner's lodging, who brought me a number of duplicates.
   WILLIAM TOOLE(police-constable C 132). I took the prisoner - I went with her to the prosecutor - she there acknowledged the robbery, and took me to her lodging, and delivered me up sixty duplicates, among which are the duplicates of this property.
   JAMES GILL. I am a pawnbroker. I have a great number of articles pawned by the prisoner at different times.
   SAMUEL WILLIAMS. I am a pawnbroker. I have several shirts and coats pawned by the prisoner.
 SARAH RUTTER. These are all my husband's and Mr.  John Jones's.
 Prisoner. I throw myself on the mercy of the Court.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Transported for Seven Years.
