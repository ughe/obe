1351. MARGARET CONNOR and JAMES CONNOR were indicted
feloniously receiving, on the 17th of March, 1 watch, value 21.; 1 watch
guard, 158.; 1 locket, ls. ; one pair of boots, 6d.; and 1 cap, 18.; the goods
of Thomas Spong; knowing them to have been stolen.
Ann SPONG. I am the wife of Thomas Spong, and live in Buckle's Cal.
tages, Upper Clapton. I found Elizabeth Loader, took her into the house.
and befriended her-she suddenly went away, on the 17th of March, while i
was gone out-I missed a watch and chain, and locket-she would have a
opportunity of taking them-I never saw either of the prisoners before
Loader never came back.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How do you know the watch ? A.
I have had it ten years-here is “Robert Stroud" on the back of it, and one
of the hands is turned up at the end I have the key I wind it up with.
WILLIAM HOWARD. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned on the
18th of March, by Margaret Connor-she first asked 10s., and afterwards
158.-I lent her 158.-she said it was her husband's-I asked what he gare
for it-she said he bought it before marriage, and she could not say-she wa
sorry she was obliged at last to part with it, in consequence of the illness of
her husband-this guard-chain was pawned by James Connor, on the 20th of
March--my brother asked him if it was silver--he said he bought it for
silver.
Cross-examined. Q. How long have you known Margaret? 4. Nearly
six months—the prisoners are husband and wife-they bear a very good cha
racter--James is very ill, and Margaret gets her living by keeping a coffee
stall.
JOSEPH NIFTON (police-constable D 118.) On Monday, the 20th of March,
I went to No. 20, Carlisle-street, where the prisoners lived-I saw Margaret
first-I said I came about a watch she had pledged-she said she had pledged
it; she found it in the Edgeware-road-she produced this cap, and said she
found this watch-chain and locket in the cap on the morning of the 18th,
close against ber coffee-stall-I asked if she knew a girl named Elizabeth
Larey, or Loader-she hesitated a few moments, and at last said, "No"-
James then said, “ We don't know anybody" Margaret produced the dupli-
cate of the property- I told her she had done very wrong in pledging them, I
she had found them I took her into custody-James being very ill, I did not
take him-after the examination the next morning I went back to the pa
soners' lodgings with my brother officer, and found this cap, bonnet, boots,
and shawl, which she said Loader wore when she absconded.nl
Cross-examined. Q. Was it after James said,“ We don't know any one," that
Margaret said she did not know? A. No, before-she made the reply first
(Property produced and sworn to.)
(The prisoners received a good character.)
MARGARET CONNOR-GUILTY. Aged 47.-Confined Three Months
