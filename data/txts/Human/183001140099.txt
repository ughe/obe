 MR. COLLINS conducted the prosecution.
   GEORGE FAIRBURN. I am a wool card maker, and live in Compton-street. On Saturday, the 2nd of January, I went to the King and Queen public-house, Norton Falgate, with a person named Smith; I had something to drink, and after some time, Smith left me and went backwards -I stopped there talking to Relf, who was a friend of Smith's; I then went into the tap-room, at his suggestion, as my feet were cold with standing at the bar - I had something more to drink; I saw Smith standing with his back to the fire talking to his friend - he went out, and one of his friends went with him, leaving me in the prisoner's company - there were ten or fifteen people in the room, besides the four that sat in my box, which were two females on the outside of Allen; I sat opposite Allen, and Relf, my left hand outside - it was a box with two seats, and a table in the middle; Relf was nearer the fire than me, and was opposite the females; we took part of a pint of porter in the tap-room - I had never seen either of the prisoners before; after having the porter, Allen said he was a cooper, and he had been lately married, and that was his wife, sitting further from him - that he had no work to do; Relf said the other woman was his wife - he was a weaver out of employ, and had earned nothing for several weeks; he then called for half a pint of gin, and wanted me to have a glass - I refused; he pressed me, and I said if I must have some I would have a glass of noyeau; after drinking tha', Relf said, "We are out of work and will thank you to pay for it," which I did, and in a few minutes the gas-light was turned off, and the room was in darkness - I do not think it was put out by any persons in our box; I immediately felt something like a handkerchief or thin cushion against my mouth - Relf instantly rushed his hand into my left side breeches pocket, he being on the left side of me -I caught hold of my watch outside, and some herbs which I had in my coat pocket, helped me to hold the arm of his between my arm and body - his hand was in my pocket, and he could not get it away, my coat being buttoned; I held it a considerable time - Allen then reached over the table, and caught hold of my watch-chain; I hit his hand down, and broke it away - he got part of my chain; when he found he had not got the watch he pulled me down by the head on the seat I was sitting; I called out Watch! and Murder! he put his left hand to my month to stop me, and I bit him; I twisted myself off the seat on the floor, and there Relf got his hand out of my pocket - in getting up there was a glimmer from the fire, and I saw the landlord come to the door with a candle in his hand; I am certain of that - the light was nearly knocked out, and they said "You b-r, we don't want you here;" a person behind the door said so - I firmly believe it was Relf going to escape; I then began to square about with my hands - Allen was then the only person in the box; when he found he had no chance against me he left me - my trousers were all pulled open with the force they used; my waistcoat was open, and my shirt torn to pieces; all the buttons of my trousers were pulled completely out of the fastenings - I got out of the room, met the landlord, said I had been robbed, and that was the way they had served me; I had felt my money go - it was in a black leather purse; I gave my watch to the landlord - the chain and three keys were missing; I saw Allen standing before the bar while I was telling the landlord how I had been robbed - the watchmen had forced themselves into the house, and I gave Allen in charge; a steel chain was found in his pocket, either at the house or watch-house - I claimed it, but the chain produced before the Magistrate was not mine, and was not the one which had been taken from him - I was perfectly sensible when I was robbed, and am certain the prisoners are the men who sat in the box with me.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Is Smith here? A. I have not seen him since; I was sober; I was not drunk - I did not strike the landlord at the watch-house; there was not a blow struck - I might push the watchman; the inspector of the night took the chain - I supposed the chain found on Allen to be mine when it was taken out of his pocket, it being a steel chain; I said it was mine, and that I would swear to it - I cannot say the constable changed it; I did not lay, any wagers that night - I threw down two sovereigns by mistake, and then put them into my purse; that was before I went into the tap-room - I had three glasses of warm wine, and one of noyean; no rum - I had another glass of noyean in the tap-room; I had only two glasses before I went there - I had four in the course of the day.
 Q. Was it after the light was extinguished that you saw the prisoner do this? A. There was a glimmering light from the fire; no man could get to me so quick as they did after the light was put out - Relf's hand was in my pocket directly; I said to Allen, "You villain, I have transported one man, and will transport you, if it costs 60l.;" a chain and a piece of one was found on him- all the persons in the tap-room fled like a swarm of bees on my crying Murder! I was sober at the watch-house.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Had you not at that house six half-pint glasses of hot wine? A. I had not; they were small glasses, and are 2d. each; I had five glasses of noyean during the day - I said I only had four at the King and Queen; I think there are two boxes in the tap-room - the right-hand side is open, with a table in the middle; the other persons were sitting round on the other side - I did not perceive any body in the next box.
 Q. Do you swear there was sufficient fire-light to enable you to discern the person of any one? A. Yes, if I was looking at them - but it was done so instantly; Allen was in the box when the landlord came, hustling with me; he got my handkerchief from me after the landlord came in.
 MR. COLLINS. Q. Are you quite sure the chain taken from Allen was part of your's? A. I thought so then, and said I could swear to it; Relf sat outside me.
 COURT. Q. Neither of the prisoners put out the light? A. I do not believe they did; I did not see the cock turned.
