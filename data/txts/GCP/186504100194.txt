Cross-examined. Q. Is there an express train which ought to leave Dart-
ford at 3.15 A. No; I do not know what time it leaves-the train left
Maidstone about half-past 2, or twenty minutes to 3—when I got to Charlton I
should have gone straight through without stopping, but for the signal against
me—the weight of the train might be 250 tons, I do not know-I heard
Mr. Cardwell, the engineer, give bis evidence-when so heavy a train as that
stopped it is difficult to start afresh--there is an incline and a curve in the
middle of the tunnel - I had quite power enough to drive the train ; if there
had been balf the number of carriages the wheels might have slipped all the
same—the engine would be more likely to have gone through with half the
number of trucks—it would carry ten trucks through ; in fact, I divided
the train, and then found myself able to go through-when we had got 200
yards or so into the tunnel the slipping commenced, and the guard got down
--we were then going almost at a walking pace, it was just previous to
stopping still-my fireman got down also- I did not see him kick ballast on
the line to make it less slippery-I felt the jar on the engine as if another
engine had come into us in front-when one engine backs to another there
is sometimes a bit of a jerk-it was not at all a violent shock; the guard
asked me what I came back for ; I said I had not come back--there was a
great deal of steam and noise in the tunnel--when I came out of the tunnel
with half the train, Jones, the signalman, was at the end of the platform by
the door of his box-the mouth of the tunnel is not very far from the Black-
heath Station; I afterwards saw Mr. Chapman, the station-master - the
signal-man was about 200 yards from the mouth of the tunnel, I do not
know exactly-when I got to the Blackbeath Station I passed along to a
siding, and then went back with the engine, having left half the ballast train
in the tunnel-I beld my arm up to the signal-man, and told him to hold
hard-I do not kuow whether he understood it-Lancaster was in charge of
the train ; we have no fixed time to run the ballast trains they are not in
the time service at all; it has occurred before in my own case, that ballast-
trains have had to divide, but I cannot speak of others; I have since heard
of one or two trains sticking in the same place.
Court. Q. Is the slipping in consequence of frost or not? A. From the
greasy state of the rails the engine-wheels do not adhere, as it is a very wet
tunnel, and such a great quantity of steam goes through which falls on the
rails, and there is always a greasiness ; if it had not been for the greasy state
of the rails the engine was abundantly powerful to draw the train, and more
than that, we are more liable to stopping in the winter season.
