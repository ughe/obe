975. WILLIAM WALLIKER (28), Stealing a watch and chain and
101. in money of Henry Clarkson, his master.
MR. BUCK Prosecuted.
HENRY CLARKSON. I live at 142, Newport Street, Lambeth Walk, and
am a contractor-on Bank Holiday, 4th August, about 11 a.m., I went
out with my wife and children, leaving a gold watch and chain of the
value of about 201., in the drawers in my bedroom, and also about 101. in
gold and silver in the same drawer; there was also a diamond ring there,
that was afterwards found on the floor-I got home about 10 or half-
past 10 at night and found the door of the room burst open-we had left it
barred up and then gone out of the front door and locked it, leaving nobody
in the house—the bedroom-door had been prised open-when we came
back we lit the lamp and saw the ring on the floor-the watch and chain
were gone and about 101. in gold and silver-this was on the ground floor
-the prisoner has lodged with me two or three years, sleeping in a room
on the ground floor, and was one of my carmen-on this morning he
washed the trap and cleaned the harness before I went out-up to Bank
Holiday night I think he had been in the habit of coming home regularly,
on that night he did not come home-next night, Tuesday, I saw him with
a woman at the Equestrian public-house at the side of the Surrey Theatre
- he was three-parts drunk he had not been to his work on the Tuesday
-Constable Jupe was with me-I asked the prisoner “What have you
done with the watch and chain?"-he made no answer, but stood there
- I asked him about the money and told him what I had lost, and said
“If you don't tell me where it is I shall lock you up "-he said nothing
for a minute or two, but turned all manner of colours- I told him I had
lost my missus's gold watch and chain that I gave 211. for and about
101. of my little children's gold and silver-I said nothing about the ring
-he stood there for five minutes and made no answer, and then he said
he did not know nothing about it and what he knew he was going to
keep to himself-I gave him into custody-the prisoner knew all the
ways of the house, as he had lived there-he had no business whatever
in the bedroom or parlour.
