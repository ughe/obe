Mr. Marshall Hall submitted that there was no evidence against Perryman on the counts for obtaining, and that such counts should be struck out; that there might be evidence of a common object, but there was no evidence to obtain by false pretences or of any obtaining by false pretences.
 Mr. F. E. Smith submitted that there was no evidence against Tyler with regard to the circulars of Chalmers and Cox, "Financial Guardian," or Herbert Smith. He referred to the case of Revs. Warren and others (page 1022), and submitted that the prosecution ought to be severed and the further indictments quashed, as was done in that case by the Common Serjeant.
 (Held. There being evidence of conspiracy involved that there was evidence of acts done in pursuance of that conspiracy.)
 (Defence.) CHARLES WILBRAHAM PERRYMAN(prisoner, on oath). I have known Herbert Smith 15 years. I have known Tyler since 1895 or 1896 when he was director of one or more companies with which I was connected. Harris became my clerk in about 1886, when the "Finan cial Observer" was being published. Hawkee was sub-editor of that paper. It ceased to exist in 1896. Herbert Smith was in the tea trade. I had not seen him for some 10 or 12 Years when I met him accidentally in 1900 in an inside stockbrokers office with whom I did business, where he came to look at the tape and get prices. Harris was in the employment of that firm, and I learnt that Smith was working with his brother, who was a stockbroker near Lombard Street. 43, Moorgate Street, was the office of two or three public companies, of which Tyler and Atkins (who is now dead) and Fardell were directors. Tyler was going to South Africa, and I wanted office room as I had large interests in companies, and I arranged with him to make my headquarters at 43, Moorgate Street. I was a director of Hannan's King Gold Mining Company and the Tinga Valley Gold Mining Company. In 1902 Smith proposed to start an outside stock broker's business, and I agreed to back him up. He than took offices at 80, Lombard Street, but as the offices at 43, Moorgate Street were larger it was arranged to transfer the company work to Lombard Street and conduct the stockbroker's business at 43, Moorgate Street Circular of August 1, 1902, has one or two words written upon it which I believe are in my handwriting, and an alteration of the date to the "16th" from the "l st" August. No circular of that date has been sent out. I may have trimmed up some circulars for Herbert Smith. I financed Smith at the start to a very small extant—he did not want any further help. I had a large Stock Exchange business and I introduced him to a broker to obtain cover; the runner of that broker introduced him to another firm also.
