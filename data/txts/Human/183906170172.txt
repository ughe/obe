   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  How long have you been a messen ger in the office? A.  I have been in the employ close upon seven years—I have known Lewin Caspar there about a year and a half—he was very attentive to business as far as I observed—he came between seven and eight o'clock that morning—I am sure it was before eight o'clock, but he went out prior to receiving the ship's box—I will say he came at a quarter or twenty minutes before eight o'clock—nine o'clock was his usual time, sometimes he used to come rather before nine o'clock, and sometimes after nine—I have not often known him come as early as eight o'clock, with the exception of mornings that a vessel was to sail at six or seven o'clock—he has not been there as early as six or seven o'clock, but I have heard him say he slept on board a vessel all night—it was very seldom vessels did sail so early—I have known him be at the office when vessels sailed so early, but to go away again—I have been there as early as six or seven o'clock on those mornings—I have not seen him there so early—when a vessel did sail so early, he has slept on board all night—I have seen him at the office as early as six or seven o'clock in the morning—I have not seen him there as early as seven or eight o'clock when no vessel was to sail—he has said be slept on board occasionally—I cannot tell whether he did or not—he did not usually do so when vessels were to be unloaded—I am not aware that it was part of his duty to superintend the delivery of cargoes—I have never seen him attending the delivery of a cargo.
   Q.  Have you not seen him there as early as seven or eight o'clock when vessels were not expected to sail? A.  He might be there as early, I can not say—I have not the least doubt I have seen him there at eight o'clock—not frequently—he might be there as often as twice or thrice at that hour—I mean only two or three times in the year and a half—I should be very sorry to swear I had not seen him there ten times—he might or might not—on the 25th of March Mr. Hartley was not in town—the whole management and superintendence of the business fell on Lewin Caspar during his absence—he was the confidential clerk or manager—he was the only man the business rested on—there was a second clerk—every body else there was under him—the business of the office generally commenced at nine o'clock—it opened between six and seven o'clock—I was one of the first there—I remember that it was the 25th of March that he was there. between seven and eight o'clock, because it was the day the robbery was committed—I should say he always showed the greatest anxiety to do what was proper in the business—I have brought him his breakfast to the office several times on mornings that he has been on board ships—I cannot re member that I have done so on other mornings—I will swear I cannot re member doing so except on those mornings I have named—I will swear I have not—I have done so when a vessel was sailing—those were the morn ings to the best of my recollection—I will not say I never did on other oc casions—I have brought him his breakfast time after time, and especially on those mornings when a vessel was to sail early, but I am not aware; of any other time—I will not swear I have not.
