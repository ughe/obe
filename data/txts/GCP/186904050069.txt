EDWIN JOHN WHITE. I am an auctioneer at Islington—the letters pro-
duced are in Spragg's writing, who I have known for some years—he was a

WILLIAM SOUDDER. I am the landlord of the Royal Victor, Old Ford Road
--Spraggs has never been the landlord of it—he has supplied me with
twelve chimney-pieces for the houses I am building-he introduced Sharman
to me, and I employed him as a carpenter.
JAMES THOMAS LOVEDAY. I am one of the surveyors of the Phoenix Fire
Insurance Company-in 1867 Sharman was insured for 3001.-2501. on his
furniture, and 501. on his stook-in-trade and utensils on 6th January, 1868,
the policy was endorsed in the ordinary form, making it effective for his
property at 7, Lefevre Road-on 6th February a olaim was made by him in
reference to a fire, and he saw me at my office on the 12th-Spraggs after
wards came with him-Sharman said he had brought him that he might
speak to me about the claim being correct-Spraggs said he had known
Sharman twenty years, and had had opportunities of seeing his furniture
and stock; that his property was kept on the first floor, and there was a
room for stowage on the basement, which he kept looked : that he had
several good prints in frames, mahogany chairs and sofas, chiffoniers, loo
tables, and other things, in fact his house was well furnished for a working-
man, and that he (Spraggs) knew nothing of the fire until 3 o'clock in the
morning, when Mrs. Sharman knooked him up-I read over some of the
items in the claim and asked him if it was correct, and he said it was
thoroughly true I had told him that I was not satisfied with Sharman's
statement--the trial at the Guildhall, when Sharman sued the company on
the claim, lasted two days, the result being a verdiet for the defendants
the claim has never been satisfied-in consequence of something which was
told me I gave instructions as to the salvage debris of the fire, and I directed
it to be sifted, and everything that was found to be carefully noted and pre-
served amongst other things were found thirty-six common bed-screws,
four head screws, and a number of iron casters, some small hinges and
locks, tools, clock-weights, &c.--the metal produced could not have been
reduced to its present oondition by passing through such a fire as that at
Sharman's—the casters do not look as if they had been screwed on furniture
--they did not correspond with any articles of furniture mentioned in the
claim-in my judgment they have been in a fire somewhere else, one other
fire at least there was nothing to indicate that anything like 120 work-
boxes had been destroyed in the fire-there were no screws, or looks or
hinges, to answer to such a pumber as that-if feather beds are burnt we
usually find something in the debris it is difficult to destroy feathers; they
clog together in lumps, and get crushed down by things falling upon them,
and by the water; at all events, they get together by some chemical powers
which I cannot explain if there had been any carpets we should have ex-
pected to find therein some portions of them left-in respect to books we
generally find the covers, or the books themselves with only the edges burnt
--we did not find amongst the debris, metals corresponding to anything like
the articles mentioned in the claim.
