684. HENRY CUMINE BURCHETT, stealing 228 lbs. of rope, value
188.; the goods of Richard Henry Maloney, in a vessel in a port, &c.
John SHEPPARD. I am a constable of the East and West India Docks-1
stopped Habgood at the gate on 31st Jan.-he had a truck laden with
228 lbs. of old rope-I would not let him go through-he had this pass, but
I told him to go and fetch the Captain-be went back and brought the pri-
soner, and said he got it from bim--the prisoner said, "It is all right "-I
said, "I won't take your word for it; I will go and see the Captain myself"
- I went on board the brig Betsy-(the pass describes it as having come
from that ship) -I met the Captain on the quay, and went on board again, in
consequence of what he said the prisoner had then absconded-he was after-
wards brought to the police office in the Docks—I told him he was charged
with stealing the rope-he acknowledged that he had altered the pass, and
said he was induced to do so by the apprentice-I saw the rope ; it was 228lbs.
John HABGOOD. I am a dealer in marine-stores; I live in Robin Hood.
lane, Poplar. On Wednesday, 31st Jan., I was stopped at the gate of the
Docks—I had been on board the Betsy-1 saw the prisoner, and no one else
-I asked him if he was the person that had some rope to sell -- he said he
was- I went to the forecastle with him, and he showed me this lot of rope-
he wanted 108. for it-I gave him 5s.6d.-1 asked bim for the Captain's pass
to take it out, and be gave me this pass- I was stopped-I went back to the
prisoner and said I was stopped at the gate-he went and told the gate-keeper
it was all right-he would not allow it to pass this is the rope-a ship-
keeper from another ship had come to me, by the prisoner's orders, and told
me there was some rope to sell.
RICHARD HENRY MALONEY. I am master of the Betsy—the prisoner was
put on board to take care of it-this rope belongs to me, I had seen it safe
on the evening previous to the sale- I told him to get it all collected, as
I intended to sell it the next morning-I left a pass for two empty casks
on the bamper.
Prisoner's Defence. On the 30th Jan., the apprentice came and said that
the Captain had given orders for me to sleep on board; he left in the morn-
ing, and said a man would come for this rope; he told me there was a pass
there, and to put the old rope on it; this man came on board, and I asked
whether he was the man to buy the rope, he said, “Yes ;" he was stopped
at the gate, and after that I went home: the next morning I went down
to the station
RICHARD HENRY MALONEY re-examined. There was an apprentice or
board who represented the prisoner as his cousin - he asked me to let the
prisoner be on board the ship to look after it for his victuals--I agreed to it-
this pass is my writing, but there is an alteration on it.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Recommended to mercy-Confined Six Months.
