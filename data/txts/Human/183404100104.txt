 723.  CAROLINE JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of February , 4 shirts, value 2l.; 6 cravats, value 5s.; 7 pair of socks, value 7s.; 2 flannel petticoats, value 4s.; 1 handkerchief, value 2s.; 3 gowns, value 2l.; 1 shift, value 3s.; 2 petticoats, value 10s.; 2 waistcoats, value 3s.; 4 pair of stockings, value 8s.; 4 collars, value 15s.; 1 waistcoat, value 1s.; 1 pair of drawers, value 1s., the goods of Theodore Zimmerman : 5 caps, value 3s.; 1 collar, value 6d.; and 2 pair of stockings, value 1s., the goods of Catherine Slater , and that she had been before convicted of felony; and WILLIAM JOHN JONES was indicted for feloniously receiving on the same day, 1 handkerchief, value 2s.; 1 waistcoat, value 1s. 6d.; one petticoat, value 8s.; 2 gowns, value 2s.; 3 pair of stockings, value 6s.; and 1 cap, value 6d.; being part and parcel of the said goods, well knowing the same to have been feloniously stolen, &c., against the Statute.
 CATHERINE SLATER. I live in  Norfolk-street, Strand. Mr. Theodore Zimmerman lodged in the house, but he is now abroad - on the 10th of February Caroline Jones came there, and said she came from Mrs. Farmer, our washerwoman, for the linen - I asked if she had any order from the washerwoman - she said "No," but she had begged of her to come and not to leave the house without the things - I told Mrs. Zimmerman - she looked up the articles stated for her - I gave them to her: and in coming down stairs, I told her to wait, and I got my own linen for her - she pushed them all into the basket, and took them away - I felt quite sure she came from there - the washerwoman is in the habit of coming herself.
   MARY PALMER. I wash for Mrs. Zimmerman and Catherine Slater. I never sent Caroline Jones for any of this linen - I never saw her till she was in custody.
   REYNA SYMONS. I live in Three-tun-alley, Petticoat-lane. On the 10th of February, Caroline Jones told me she had a few things to sell for a widow - I bought three shirts, four lace collars, one gown, one waistcoat, a pair of drawers, and one or two other things - I gave her 16s. in money, and 9s. worth of earthen-ware, out of my basket - I sold them the next day for 31s. to Mrs. Cheetham.
   MARY CHEETHAM. I bought these articles of Symons for 31s.
   ROBERT BROOKS(police-constable G 29). I apprehended the prisoners in Somers Town - my brother officer found some of the property, which is here - Caroline Jones wished us to be quiet, and not let the neighbours hear any thing of it - she asked us to call her husband down stairs - my brother officer called down the other prisoner, and when they were both in the room, my brother officer laid hold of this basket of linen - Caroline Jones said, "You need not trouble that: that is mine" - my brother officer then took up this handkerchief - she said, "You need not trouble that: that is my husband's;" and the male prisoner said,"That is mine" - my brother officer said, "If that is yours, that is the first article I shall take" - we found the rest of these articles in a basket in the house - I had before been to Mrs. Cheetham's, in St. John-street, and found these articles which she had bought.
   EDMUND LANCASTER(police-constable G 197). I went with Brooks to the house - I called the male prisoner down - he said the handkerchief was his.
   THOMAS YAPP. The prisoners rent a house of me, No. 7, Smith's-place, Somers Town - I believe they took it together - they represented themselves as man and wife - the man said he was a ladies' shoemaker.
 COURT to E. LANCASTER. Q. Did you ask this woman if her husband was at home? A. Yes, and she said he was up stairs - I went up to him, and saw him at work with two lads - I told him I wanted him about some boots; and when he came down, I told him my business.
   JAMES GLIBBERY(police-serjeant N 21). I produce a certificate of the conviction of Caroline Jones, which I got from Mr. Clark's office - I know her to be the person.(The prisoner, Caroline Jones, put in a written defence, stating that her husband and a Jewess had induced her to commit the offence; and that her husband had treated her with great brutality, in consequence of her refusing to support him by dishonest acts.)
 William John Jones. It is utterly false.
   JAMES SMITH. I live with my mother in Brewer-street, Somers Town - I was employed in sewing shoes at the prisoner's house - I have heard him tell his wife to go out, and if she said "No," he said, "Do go this time, there is 10s. to pay for rent, and I have had a very short week" - I heard him say so on the Monday before the 10th of February - Caroline Jones is my sister.
 CAROLINE JONES -    GUILTY.* Aged 20. Confined Ten Months.
 WILLIAM JOHN JONES -    NOT GUILTY.
