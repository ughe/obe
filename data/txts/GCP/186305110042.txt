Rose, Mayor.
MARY FLETCHER. I serve in Mr. Jobbin's, a baker's shop in Brompton-
on 21st April Townsend came in and asked either for a penny bun or two
halfpenny loaves, I can't say which, and gave me a bad half-crown-I told
him it was bad-what the last witness has stated is correct-I gave the half-
crown to the policeman.
Townsend. Q. You did not band me the half-crown back? 4. I was
going to, and the policeman came in.
DANIEL MCLEOD (Policeman, B 18). My attention was called to the two
prisoners by Mr. Godfrey, and we followed them to the Brompton road-I
spoke to 274 B, and gave him some instructions--I took Townsend in the
baker's shop and received this half-crown (produced) - at that time I saw
Druce and the other policeman standing together-Mr. Godfrey beckoned me
to the shop-door-I looked at Druce at that time, and noticed he was basten-
ing his pace, and going between a run and a good walk towards Lloyd's-
place-I went there next morning, and on searching I found one bad half-
crown and two bad florins, and a workman there picked up another bad
florin and handed it to me-a very low fenoe separates Lloyd's-place from
where the money was—when we took the prisoners to the station they said
they were strangers to each other we found 2d. on Druce, and 2jd. on
Townsend in a bundle which he had.
RICHARD NEWTON (Policeman, B 874). Druce was pointed out to me by
the last witness-I followed him, and saw him go down Lloyd's-place, lost
sight of him for a few seconds, and then saw him again coming from under-
neath the wood fence-there is no thoroughfarehe was obliged to come
back I heard Townsend say at the station that the half-crown which he
offered to the man at the public-house was the same which he tendered at
the baker's shop.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These coins are all bad.
The prisoners' statements before the Magistrate were here read as follows :-
Townsend says—"I had not the slightest idea of this man. I did not know
him before. I met him in the park, and asked him for a pipe of tobacco. I
bad nothing to do, so I walked to Brompton with him, and am happy to find
that I am made richer than I really was." Druce says_“I have nothing to
say."
Druce's Defence, I was coming across Hyde-park, and Townsend came up
and asked me the way to Knightsbridge. I told him I was going that way,
and we went together. I saw him go into the baker's shop, and they took
me because I was in his company. I know nothing of this man:
Townsend's Defence. I know nothing of this man, neither does be know
anything about me. GUILTY. (See next case).

687. ROBERT DRUCE was again indicted for feloniously uttering coun-
terfeit coin. MESSRS. Cooks and CRAWFORD conducted the Prosecution.
The evidence of James West, Henry Godfrey, Mary Fletcher, Daniel McLeod,
Richard Newton, and William Webster, as given in the last case, was read over
to them, to which they assented.
Prisoner. I have nothing more to say. Guilty.
