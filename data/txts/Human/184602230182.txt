   COURT . Q.  What money did you advance? A.  5 l .—I was to have 5 l . 5 s . for it for one month—I do not know what that is per cent.—I am not scholar enough for that—we do things off-hand at once, and do not calculate the interest—I was told in Sept. last that this was a forgery.
   MR. PARRY . Q.  If this money had been paid he never would have been indicted? A.  No, there would have been an end of the transaction—whether there would be an end of it if he would pay me now, I must leave to others.
   MR. PARRY called SOPHIA OLPIN. I am thirteen years old—I am the prisoner's daughter. I know Mr. Coney—he and my father have been acquainted long—in June or July I saw Coney at our house—it was just before we had our dinner, or directly after—I do not remember the day of the month when he came in—a bit of paper was lying on the table—my father was writing when Mr. Coney came in, and said, "Olpin, I can't write, but you write my name; I will put my cross, that will do just the same," and he did so—it was a strip of paper like this.
   MR. WILDE . Q.  Do you write yourself? A.  Yes—I was standing in the room against the door—I do not know whether it was before dinner or directly after—no one dined with us that day—the first time I saw the paper, it was on the table—I saw Coney come into the room—neither the dinner thingsnor the tea-things were on the table—the door was open, and my father and Mr. Coney walked in together—they took chairs, and sat down by the side of one another, and then the paper was lying on the table—my father laid it on the table—I cannot exactly say where he brought it from—it was lying on the top of the books—I did not see what was on it. NOT GUILTY.
