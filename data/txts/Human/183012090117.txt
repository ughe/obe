On the 9th of December this hearth-rug was taken from the further end of the shop- I saw it safe half a minute before, but did not see it taken; I sent my boy one way, and I went the other - it was about five o'clock in the afternoon, and about eight I found it at a pawnbroker's in Old-street-road; while I was in the shop, claiming it, the prisoner came in to pawn a carpet - the pawnbroker said he was the lad who had pawned my rug.
   WILLIAM BIRD. I am shopman to Mr. Burgess, a pawnbroker, in Old-street-road. The prisoner pawned the rug at near eight o'clock last Thursday night.
 Prisoner. Please your worship, it was six o'clock when I took the rug.
 Witness. I think it was about eight - I lent him 5s. on it; he said he brought it from his sister, and gave me the direction, No. 4, Bath-street, in the name of  George Turner he came again while the prosecutor was there, to pawn a carpet, which has not been claimed; I think he brought the rug later than six o'clock - it was near eight that he came the second time.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN BOLT. I am a Police-constable. The prisoner was given into my charge for stealing the hearth-rug - I found no duplicate, or any thing on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going along Kingsland-road, at a quarter before six o'clock, and met a man, who gave me the rug to pawn at Miller's for 6s. - I thought he said 3s., which I asked; but when I went to him and gave him the 3s., he said I was to have had 6s. - he went with me to get it out again, and sent me to Burgess'; they would only lend 5s. - I came out and gave the man the ticket and money; he then sent me to pawn the carpet - the prosecutor said the rug bad a string tied to it.
 WILLIAM BIRD. There was a string tied round it when the prisoner brought it - the carpet is now at our shop.
   JAMES ABSELL. I am sure the rug is mine.

 240.  JOHN COOKSEY was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of December , 2 pairs of stockings, value 20s.; 3 pairs of stockings, value 14s., and 1 pair of mittens, value 3s., the goods of James Robertshaw , from his master.
 JAMES ROBERTSHAW. I am a  hosier, haberdasher, and linen-draper, and keep a shop in Oxford-street. The prisoner was in my employ for seven months and twenty-one days - I had a good character with him from a respectable house in Bond-street. On the 6th of December I had discharged him, and was about to pay his salary - he said he would take his trunk, and call in half an hour for his salary; I had suspicion of him, and called him up into the drawing-room - I put rather forcible question to him; I certainly said he had better state all about it - I found I could get nothing from him, and called in a Policeman; the key of his box was found; after a short time, the Policeman opened it in his presence - a pair of ladies silk mittens were found, which I conceived to be mine, but cannot positively swear to them; they are worth 3s. - I thought it useless to search the box further at the house, and sent it to the Police-station; I was present there when it was searched; the two pair of silk stockings, and three odds ones were found in it - they are black, spotted with white; I have three stockings corresponding with the three odd ones; they are a peculiar stocking, and I can say decidedly they are mine - the pair are very fine, and not general in the trade, but I will not swear to them; the odd one I will swear to.
