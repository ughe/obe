half-crowns, and other moneys; the property of Charlotte Lapham, from
her person.

CHARLOTTE LAPHAM. I live in Aldersgate-street. I was on Snow-bill
about one o'clock, on 22nd May-I stopped to look in a window about two
minutes-Munro stood by my side-Cullen was three or four yards from her,
and said to Munro, “I am going"-at that instant Munro drew ber hand
from my pocket-I missed my purse, with two balf-crowns, three shillings,
and a sixpence in it--they went into a public-house--I spoke to a policeman,
who took them-38. 6d. was found on Munro, but no purse,
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. On which side of you was Munro ? A.
On my right, where my pocket was- as she left she bad her bandkerchief in
her hand I had never seen her before I could not swear to Cullen.
RICHARD HUMPHREY (City-policeman, 289.) I was on duty on Snow-hill
at a quarter past one o'clock - I took Munro coming out of the public-house
-I took her to the station, and went back and took Cullen--she called on
several of her companions to split my head with a quart pot-she had a key
in her hand—I saw ber try to pass it to her husband- I caught her hand-
she had a half-crown and some halfpence in it-I tried to get it from her-
the threw me down I had to call another officer to get the money from her.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you search for the purse in the public-house?
A. No--there were ten or twelve Irish tailors, and I was afraid.
