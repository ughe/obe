   Re-examined.  The wife's statement that the articles were purchased for her use related to the silver, which was in a box with a glass lid to it, with deeds relating to property and wearing apparel—the silver is described in this police list of May 16th—Hewlett had a file of these lists, but there were a number missing—there had been a furnace there.
   By the COURT . We make up the lists and put the descriptions on them—here are on one list 138 articles—not one per cent, of those are reported by jewellers, but occasionally we get information—people can go into a public room and buy plate. JOHN WINDSOR( Re-examined ). I found the silver locked in a box in what was designated the kitchen; I asked for the key and removed some clothes, and found this box with a glass lid, and the silver in it.
   GILL ( Police Sergeant  8 B ). It is part of my duty to deliver the police list to jewellers and pawnbrokers—I delivered one to the prisoner Hewlett on 16th May, the last of the date.
   Cross-examined by MR. GILL . I delivered it about 9.30; I put it on the counter. CHARLES SMITH(185 B ). On 22nd and 23rd May I called at Hewlett's workshop and asked him if he had had any property offered for sale—I had a written list with me, and said, "Have you had any of these offered for sale? Some of them have bull's heads on them"—he read the list down, and said, "No. "
   Cross-examined by MR. GILL . The list is destroyed—I knew Hewlett, and have been at his shop—Harwood had the shop before him; I never spoke to him. THOMAS ANNEST. I am servant to Mr. Gwin Jones, of 8, Trevor Terrace, Knightsbridge—on the night of May 14th his house was broken into, and a quantity of silver stolen, value about £100—I had had charge of it eighteen years, and cleaned it myself, and knew it well—this silver is Mr. Jones's property, but there is not a quarter of it here—it had a crest on it—these spoons are not in the same condition now—I can see the crest on these sugar-tongs, but it has been smoothed down a good bit.
   Cross-examined.  The salvers were not silver—the plate has been in use a long time; it was his father's—the crest was quite plain—it is an ox's head. ALICE SMITH. I live at 13, Rutford Gardens, Kensington—between 3rd and 4th July my house was entered by a key while it was being repaired—I missed a carriage clock and other articles—I have identified some of them—the total value is about £40—I have recovered about £12 worth, about a quarter of the whole.
