THOMAS WILLIAMS. I am a butcher, at Walworth. I had seen the pri soner once before, and bought hay of him—he came to me that Wednesday, brought the hay to the door, and asked me if I would buy half a load, or a quarter of a load—I agreed to buy a quarter for 13 s . 6 d .; and it was delivered, and one truss over; I paid the prisoner 1 s . 6 d . for that—I did not under stand him to say that it was his own hay. THOMAS LOCKYER( policeman, L  135). I took the prisoner, and told him it was for stealing a load of hay—he said he could only make a debt of it.
   The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he intended paying for the hay, but was unable to get sufficient money to do so; that he offered the nan  2 l . 10 s ., which he refused to take, and before he could make up the money he was given in charge.  182. MICHAEL WHALE , stealing 43 pairs of boots and 12 pairs of shoes, value 9 l  . 11 s  . 6 d  .; the property of Abraham Jacob Lion , and another. ABRAHAM JACOB LION. I keep a shoe-warehouse, in Crown-street, Fins-bury. In the beginning of July, the prisoner called and gave an order to my brother, who is my partner, to send some boots and shoes to 34, Lisle-street, Kent-road—we said we could not send them unless he paid or lefts deposit—he promised that he would call again in the afternoon, but he did not—in a fortnight afterwards he called again, and gave us a fresh order, and we were to send the goods next day—he left ten shillings deposit—we sent them, and desired the porter not to leave them without the balance of the money—we did not consider the deposit as part payment—we did not like to send the goods at all—we gave the porter directions several times not to leave them without the money; and when he was gone, 1 sent after him to say so.
   Prisoner.  I left a deposit of ten shillings, and took a receipt of him; he did not send them all, only a part, and part of them were wrong.
