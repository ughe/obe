   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Now Miss Constable, I ask you distinctly, whether you ever settled an account with Mr. Newhall? A.  I did not—no ac connt of any kind was ever prepared by him in my presence, more than some memorandum which he made, which I understood was two sums of 400 l . each, and nothing more that I was aware of—I have no doubt this ( the letter marked  C) is my father's handwriting, and this also (D)—these letters have been picked out from hundreds of others, which have been stolen from our private papers, at the house at Lewisham—boxes, trunks, and drawers full—these letters are some of them.
   COURT . Q.  In saying, that the letter marked "D" is genuine, do you speak from the signature alone, or from the body of the letter? A.  I have looked at them both, one is a letter written to Mr. Stanley in Wales.—( One letter was dated  1838, the other  1839.)
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  When your father and you first became acquainted with Stanley, he was a merchant of opulence in London? A.  He was—in the year 1837, he occasionally called at our house at Kennington, but he did not become an inmate of our house till we went to Lewisham which was about June, 1838—he came to us with the intention of paying 2 l . a week for his board—that was his own proposal—he was very delicate about it—he said he would not come on any other terms—he never paid a farthing from the time he came, till he quitted—he wanted assistance constantly—he seemed to be straitened in his circumstances at that time, but he showed us a document, which would entitle him in a short time to 80,000 l .—it was a skin, which had much the apperance of one of these deeds—we never got any produce from that security.
