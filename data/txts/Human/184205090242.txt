ROBERT TEE. I am a constable of Roehampton. I went to Mr. Shiell's stables on Thursday, the 7th of April, and saw in one of the stalls the body of a woman—I found these keys on the premises, and brought them away, and have brought them here to-day—I found them on the corn-bin in the stable opposite the stable the body was found in, not in the same stable—I have had them in my possession ever since—the body was buried on the next day week.
   Cross-examined. Q.  It was buried on the 15th? A.  Yes—the keys laid on the lid of the bin, rolled up, with this guard round them, not on the floor. JOSIAH TYE( police-constable V  199.) On Wednesday night, the 6th of April, I was on duty at Wandsworth, I went to Mr. Shiell's stables, and saw the body of a woman found—I found Gardiner there—on going into the harness-room I noticed a very bad, fulsome, sickly smell—I never smelt such before, but it appeared to me like rind of bacon—after stop ping some time it was so bad I went out to the sergeant, and said, "Here is a very bad smell here, something must be wrong"—I searched the fire-place, and found two pieces of bone in the cinders among the coals in the furnace of the fire-place—I afterwards took them to the sur geon—on the 12th of April, the Tuesday following, I went there again, and searched the coach-house—I went to the four-wheeled chaise, and opened the gig-box lid, I saw nothing in the box, but smelt a very bad smell, like the smell in the room—after taking off the cushions, and taking the box out of the chaise, I found a small piece of flesh on the outside—it was so very small I could not keep it—I found it here—( pointing to the box )—I found another set of bones in the left-hand side of the furnace where the fire burns—I found them all at the same time, and in the fur nace.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What you call a small piece of flesh, was so small you could not keep it, it was not hidden by the cushion? A.  No, it was about half the size of a pea—I have kept the two sets of bones all together—I have a skull-bone—all I found that night are in this paper—I did not find bones in more places than one—Palmer was with me when I found them—he found the first piece in the cinder place—I found these in the grate where the fuel was laid—there was nothing burning then—we took out two pieces of skull bone directly to Mr. Allen, a medical gen tleman, who was waiting there.
