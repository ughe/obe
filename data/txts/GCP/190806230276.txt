seconded Hirst's motion that a letter be sent to the Local Government
Board stating all the facts, and was put on the deputation. I
wished to show the Local Government Board we were acting
fairly in taking Barker's tender, who was not the highest but
who was a local man, employing local labour, and paying Trade Union
wages. I protested against the way the Board had been stigmatised by
Draper and said, "Are we to take this lying down?" The Board had
been said by Draper to be corrupt and rotten. I was present at the Board
on November 5, 1904; December 2, 1904 (two contracts for Calcutt);
December 22, 1904 (three contracts for Calcutt); April 6, 1905 (Calcutt's
teuder the highest); September 7, 1905, Calcutt's tender was 5s, higher
on one contract and on another £300 lower; on May 24, 1906; on July
12, 1906: February 14, 1907. I know I have voted against Calcutt on
two or three occasions, but I cannot give the dates. I swear I was to
puy Calcutt the £10 if he had settled the bill of Smith and Hill, and that
I afterwards paid Barker the £15. I voted against the motion of Boites
that contracts should not be given to relatives of Guardians. I did not
see why the children should be punished because the father did public
work. Barker, junior, lived at Forest Gate and his business address was
Settle Street, New Road, at his father's place; I live about a dozen doors
off on the opposite side of the road. I inquired, and he and his father
showed me the books showing that the accounts were kept separately, and
I wae satisfied that it was so. I understand they have also satisfied the
Local Government Board that it was a separate account. Towards the
end of my time I learnt that most of the houses used for Scattered Homes
belonged to Guardians or their relatives; they did with one exception
house bought from Chiswick. He was a friend of mine shortly after I was
on the Board. I did not know until the Inquiry that he had sold a pro-
perty to the Board. I did not know Calcutt well before I went on the
Board. I have visited his office frequently and been in his house perhaps
12 times since. A day or two previous to seeing Mr. and Mrs. Calcutt,
after his arrest, I asked Heward whether Clancy had read my letter to
Calcutt. He gave me Calcutt's address at Poplar. I went there. MR.
Sunith answered the door, told me they were not in, and I met Calcutt and
Mr. and Mrs. Smith. I prevailed on them to come and have a drink,
including Smith, as I understood Smith knew a lot of Calcutt's business
I said to Calcutt, “I am thoroughly dissatisfied with Clancy's working of
these rents, his expenditure, expenses, etc When you are over your
trouble I want you and me to have & thorough investigation into these
accounts, because I do not think we are being treated rightly. Has be
read that letter to you?" He said, “ Yes." I said, "Then you know all
about it." Then we got to general conversation. I said, "I suppose you
have heard the rumour that is about?" He said, "There are lots of
rumoursabout, are not there?" I said, "Yes." He said, “What one do you
refer to?" I said, “Mrs. Calcutt said you are going to give the whole
show away." There was no secret in the matter; it was ordinary con-
versation. Then he pulled out some papers and said, "If I was going to
do that should I retain these papers?" I said, "No." I did not think
