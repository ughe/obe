 ----  MARTIN . I am the attesting witness to this deed; I saw Miss Constable execute it.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Were you in the room all the time, or merely called in to witness it? A.  Merely called in; I know nothing of what passed.
   COURT  to MR. BOYS . Was this deed with the blanks in it, with the signa tures and the attestation, ever drawn to Miss Constable's attention? A.  I am not aware that it was—there were two other deeds in her favour, exe cuted, I think, in Feb.—her attention was called generally to deeds—I never called her attention to the fact, that as this was a deed executed by her father on the day before, or the very day of his death, whether she recollected the transaction, or who were present—I never anticipated any inquiry of this kind—I cannot say exactly which told me, but I collected from them that it was the 30th—in the case of a will I should ask whether it was executed on the very day of the man's death—the only question about this deed was the date of it—it was in blank—it had no date; and of course that was the subject of conversation, and the effect of that deed with the two others—I do not re collect that I asked whether the prisoner had ever seen either of the subscrib ing parties at the time—before this second deed was executed I had reason to believe that the prisoner had seen Miss Constable and her father before the beginning of the year 1841—it did not occur to me that I was making at deed in favour of the prisoner, who was the attesting witness of the other deed, in favour of Stanley, because he was interested under Stanley's will.
 (  The Deed of Confirmation was here read. It recited the whole of the former deed, with the addition of the blanks being filled up, it stated that there (was a debt of 1109 l. 7 s . due to Stanley from Mr. Constable; and finally conveyed the property mentioned in the first deed to the prisoner and Phoebe Blackwell, for the purpose of meeting the demands upon the estate, including the above sum of  1109 l . 7 s .)
