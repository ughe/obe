JAMES TURNER( examined by MR. RYLAND .) I am twenty-three years of age—I have been married to the last witness nine months—I first heard of the loss of the money on the 14th of November—I knew very little about the money myself—my wife was very careful of it indeed—we had very little squabbling about it—she never charged me with stealing it—I swear that—I have seen some of the lodgers here—my wife never complained that I tried to break open the drawer where the money was, and take it away against her will—I did break open a drawer—I did not expect to find the money there—I broke it open to aggravate her—the money was gone—I did not know the money was in the drawer that I broke open—I expected it was—I did not break it open for the purpose of taking the money—if I had found it there I intended to have taken it to her for her to give me some out of it—I smashed a glass one day—it was my own property—I had not been quarrelling with my wife about the money—we had had a few words and I revenged myself on the glass—I never told the lodgers when the money was missed that I had got it, and if it was all right I would have a flare  up, and if the old girl  behaved herself well I would give her back part of it, nor anything of the sort—I deny the whole of it—I did not say the money was all right, and I had got it—I never did take any of it against her will, nor with her will—I helped to count it on the 14th of November—there was ten 5 l . notes, three 10 l . notes, and 37 l . in gold—I had never seen it counted before. EDWARD M'KEANE. I am clerk at Messrs. Glynn's bank. I know the prosecutrix—I cannot say that I saw her on the 17th of September last—I have an extract from our book here, not the book itself—I made the extract last Thursday—I made the original entry in the book, and made the extract from the original entry.
   COURT . Q.  Have you refreshed your memory by the original entry to-day? A.  Not to-day—I have a recollection of the note in question from my reference to the entry the other day, I bore it in mind.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When did you look at the book? A.  Last Thursday, when I made the extract—I am quite certain the two agree—I compared them with particular care that there should be no mistake—there is never more than one note of the same amount, number, and date in cir culation—that is a rule at the Bank.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Can you tell whether you paid away any 5 l . note on the 17th of September? A.
