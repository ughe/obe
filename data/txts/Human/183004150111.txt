 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. What room were you in? A. The first floor, and they on the second - I heard their door shut and locked, and heard them go out.
 COURT. Q. Have you any other lodger? A. No -Tanner had a key of the street door, and could let himself in after we went to bed.
 Peacock's Defence (written). I have witnesses to prove I was not in the neighbourhood at the time, and under circumstances which render it impossible for me to be a party, as White wishes you to believe - the robbery was committed on Saturday night, between seven and eight o'clock; I can prove I was at a public-house, went home, and remained at home all night - the robbery was not discovered till Sunday night; I can prove I was at home all that evening too, and nobody pretends to have seen me about the premises on Sunday, except White, who is utterly uncorroborated - it is true I had the cheque in my possession, and gave it to my son to get paid, which was wrong; but I was in pecuniary difficulties, and had an execution in my house for King's taxes - my furniture was about to be sold; when I found the cheque by St. Bennett's church the temptation was too great to resist; I gave it to my son, which I admitted at once, and when I heard he was in custody I gave myself up without hesitation - it is not likely, if concerned in the robbery, I should have sent my son with the cheque.
 Simpson's Defence. I content myself with declaring my perfect innocence; as to White, I declare solemnly I never saw him till the Tuesday we were under examination, when he was one of the accused; I trust you will pause before you return a verdict to banish me from this country, and leave a wife and six children destitute.
   HENRY JAGO. I am Mr. Whitehead's (the broker) assistant, and live in Philip-lane, London-wall. In March last I was in possession at Peacock's house for, I think, eighteen days - I had my meals with the family; he tead with with me on Saturday evening. the 6th of March - it was from six to eight o'clock; I will not say within half an hour - his wife was with him, and I smoked a pipe; he went out after tea - that might be about eight or nine - I will not say within an hour; he came home about ten or eleven o'clock - I saw him go to his bed-room; I sat up in a chair in the middle room, which he must go through - I saw him, I think, from six to eight in the morning; I saw him on Sunday evening about dusk, I suppose between six and seven o'clock; he went out again, and came home I suppose about eleven or twelve, and went to bed.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. When you say you drank tea from six to eight o'clock, do you mean that you were two hours at tea, or that some time between those hours you were at tea? A. We went to tea about dusk - he was at the table for a good hour; I think we had a candle when we began tea, but I will not say; we were in a first floor room in Thames-street - here is my distress-warrant: there are two warrants - I was in possession six days on the first warrant; we had a consent not to distrain from day to day - he was to pay the expenses, and let the warrant stand; he signs his name to the consent - my master has got that; there were two or three consents - I did not go out before I received the second warrant; they were both for King's taxes; here is the stamped inventory - it is not a list of goods, but it is stamped; the list of goods on the premises is with the consent - this stamped paper is the condemnation.
 The warrants were here read; one was for 5l. 11s. 4 1/2d. taxes, due at Michaclmas, dated the 19th of February; the other dated the 8th of March, for 2l. 15s. 8 1/4d.
   HENRY JAGO. I charge 2s. 6d. a day for remaining in possession from the 19th of February to the 18th of March, when the second warrant came; I received the money by distraining - there were sufficient goods on the premises; I think it is dusk in Thames-street from six to half-past six o'clock, on the 6th of March, and for a full hour after that I am sure the prisoner was there - after that I do not know what became of him, till he came in again, about ten or eleven o'clock in the morning - I saw him again on Sunday evening, about seven or eight; it might be six, I will not say: perhaps it was five - and about half-past eight, on Monday morning, he had a cup of tea with me; about seven or eight, or half-past - I did not see him send his boy to the bankers, or hear any thing of his having picked up a cheque - I saw him send his boy out to school, about half-past eight or nine o'clock; the officer brought him back about ten - I cannot recollect whether any body came to tea on Saturday; I saw no skeleton keys about the premises.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When you are in possession, people seldom entertain much company? A. No; I could have taken the goods away at any time.
   WILLIAM OWERS. I have the management of the Sun and Last public-house, Newgate-market; I have known Peacock nine years. I saw him at our house on Saturday evening the 6th of March - he came after eight o'clock; I served him with a glass of gin; I do not know when he left.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How much after eight o'clock was it? A. Not exceeding half an hour - I know it was after eight; it might be half-past eight when I served him- I remember it, because the book binders always come in every Saturday; I have no particular reason for saying it was on the 6th of March, further than applying to Mr. Harmer to know what I was subpoenaed for; I know it was the 6th of March from my subpoenae - the person, who subpoenaed me, mentioned the 6th of March, and I know it by going to the Times newspaper, which I had.
 COURT. Q. The Times did not tell you when you served a man with gin? A. I looked about to see when he was taken before the Lord Mayor - I cannot recollect when he was before the Lord Mayor, but I served him with a glass of gin on the 6th of March, and served him another on Sunday morning, between eight and nine o'clock - I am quite positive I served him between eight and nine on Sunday morning, with a glass of gin: and, between seven and eight on the Monday morning, I served him with a glass of beer; he said nothing of having found a cheque - his little boy was not with him; I can run from my house to Doctors' - commons in three minutes.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. On the Saturday night did he appear to have been running? A. No; it might be more or less than half-past eight o'clock that night when I served him - he lives about a quarter of a mile from me; Jones was in my tap-room.
