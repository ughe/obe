 Cross Examination.
 Did not she tell you that  Ann Charlotte, Mary Gardiner, and Esther Kelly, brought the things? - No; but I was told something of that was mentioned at the office. Ann Charlotte, I believe, was taken up, but it was for something else. She was put to the bar for somebody to look at her, and she went away again.
   GEORGE WILKINSON sworn.
 I am servant to Lord Mansfield; I believe all these things are my lord's property.
 Was you present when the prisoner was examined at the justice's? - Yes; she said that she had had some of them from Ireland, and others a great while, but I cannot say the time; they were in Lord Mansfield's house on Tuesday the 6th; they are plates that are made use of every day.
   HENRY RICHARDS sworn.
 I am cook to Lord Mansfield. I know the two sallad dishes, and two of the plates, to be my lord's property; they were in the house on the Tuesday night; the prisoner, upon her examination before the justice, said, she had had some many years in Ireland, and some from Bristol.
   HENRIETTA CASSMAJOR sworn.
 I am Miss Murray's maid. I know the linen to be the property of Lord Mansfield, by having often seen it there. I am certain of it.
 Was you present when the prisoner was examined before the justice? - I was. I understood her in general to say, that they had been in her possession some time, I do not know how long, some were given her by a relation, and sent from Ireland. They were in Lord Mansfield's house on the 6th of June.
 PRISONER'S DEFENCE.
 When I was taken up I told them Ann Charlotte and Mary Gardiner brought the things to me, and desired they would go and take them, and I would pay them for bringing them the next morning to me, but they did not bring them. When I went to Tothill-fields there were two men; we sat on the bench; I was telling them how I was brought in by those people; they said if I brought them, and swore against them, I should be cast myself. I said what must I do; they said, say you had them from Ireland, or any where; so I did, and they laughed at me.
   WILLIAM TILCOCK sworn.
 I am a driver belonging to the Artillery. I lodged at the prisoner's house in June.
 Do you remember the night Lord Mansfield's house was burnt? - Yes When I went to bed at ten o'clock she was at home.
 Cross Examination.
 How came you to recollect that night? - Because the next morning I went off early to camp.
 Did you see any thing of Mrs. Tilcock after you went to bed? - Yes, between twelve and one she came into my room; I heard the lodgers come in.
 Does she lie in the same room? - No, in the next room.
 Does any body lie with you? - Yes, Perry.
 Does any body else lie in the same room? - Yes, another woman; Perry lodges there.
   SARAH PERRY sworn.
 I lodge in this house. Tilcock lodges there. The prisoner was at home all that evening, all night, and next morning between twelve and one o'clock. Ann Charlotte, Poll Gardiner, and  Esther Kelly came in and asked the woman where I lodged if t hey should leave the things; there was a sheet, some china, and some trifling things, I cannot recollect what, I was in bed there; she said they should not leave them then. They said where should they leave them but in their lodgings? that they came honestly by them, and would leave them there.
 What sort of china was it? - Long dishes, two soup plates, and brown plates all full of colours; it was burnt-in china I believe.
