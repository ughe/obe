M. BALDWIN to JOHN NEWLING. Q. Are there any means by which
you can tell that this Alpaca dress was ever your property ? A. I believe
it is, and I have a piece to match it-there has been a large quantity of it
made lately, but they are not of like shades--the one I have is of a shade
like this dress-I missed ten yards out of my shop off a new piece,
which had not been in the house above three weeks-it was similar to this,
and this perfectly matches with the bit I produce as a sample- bought
it of a draper--there is no reason to believe he had not plenty more of it.
WILLIAM HACKET Shaw (police-sergeant K 2.) I took Henry Jolly
into custody- I was present when the prisoner's house was searched, and
Mary Ann Jolly was taken-I found a number of buttons in a drawer of
the dressing-case, which Henry Jolly had the key of, and two duplicates
-one was of a gown which Phæbe Jolly said was her own gown-it was
not shown to her-she only said it from the description.
JAMES CHARLES BENNETT. I am a pawnbroker. I produce the brown
gown and a black Polka dress - I took them both in of Phoebe Jolly.
JOHN NEWLING re-examined. I have lost six yards of black gambroon
precisely like this Polka dress.

1371. ARTHUR HARVEY was indicted for stealing 1 bed, value
21. 108. ; 2 blankets, 78.; and 1 rug, 38.; the goods of William Fearn;
and that he had been before convicted of felony.
WILLIAM FBARN. I keep the White Lion beer-shop, White Lion-
street, Pentonville; the prisoner lodged there. I was told of something,
and missed from his room a bed, two blankets, and a counterpane-they
are here I can positively swear to them.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How long had he lodged with
you? A. From the 14th of May-his father and mother lodged there
from Oct.-I missed these things on Monday, the 19th of May, about
half-past eight o'clock.
SAMUEL Frost. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned those
things with me on the 19th of May.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you sure it was him? A. Yes I had seen
him before.
