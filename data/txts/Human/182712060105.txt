 Q. How long have you known his wife? A. Which way do you mean? I have known her as a woman, apparently for about a year and a half.
 Q. Had not this man forbid you his house? A. I forgot to state that, upon the 6th of October, in a public-house, he threatened to stab me with a dagger; and on the Sunday night previous to this, I heard him threaten my life in his own house.
 COURT. Q. What did he say? A. He said in the public-house, that he had bought a dagger on purpose to run through my heart.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Before the day he struck you with a knife, had he not forbid you his house? A. Yes; on the 8th of October - he struck me on the 19th of November; I had been to his house frequently in his absence, and in his presence, for my washing.
 Q. Had you not frequently drank tea, and breakfasted with his wife in his absence? A. Yes; and dined there too; she dressed it for me and my family - I have no wife, I have some boys. I dined there in person - the dinner was sent to my house for them.
 Q. Upon your solemn oath, did you ever introduce this man's wife as Mrs. Williams, to any one? A. I never did I never called her Mrs. Williams in any body's presence, I swear that. I know Kearsley and his wife; I was at the Red Lion public-house, Shoe-lane, in their company, in the Bartholomew fair week. I did not introduce the prisoner's wife to them as my wife, nor call her Mrs. Williams in their presence. I know Renshaw, she is a married woman; I never called the prisoner's wife Mrs. Williams in her company, nor introduce her as Mrs. Williams. I know a woman named Bosley; I never introduced the prisoner's wife to her as Mrs. Williams - they were all in company.
 Q. Did you at any public-house, in the presence of each and all these people, call her Mrs. Williams; in the Bartholomew fair week? A. I never did.
 Q. How late did you stay at the prisoner's house in his absence? A. Perhaps till ten or eleven o'clock at night, for little odd things I might want. I am not a follower of Mr. Carlile. I believe the book I have been sworn on. I have been to the prisoner's house since he has forbidden me; in hisabsence.
 Q. Did he not forbid you the house, in consequence of the gross improprieties he had detected, between you and his wife? A. He said he was jealous of me, and his wife - I have known him about a year and a half.
 COURT. Q. Were you at Bartholomew fair with the prisoner's wife? A. Yes; the prisoner was not there - I was there about three hours the first day, and about four hours the second - I do not recollect being there more than two days; we went afterwards to a large gin-shop, at the top of Shoe-lane - I do not know the sign; the prisoner did not know we were going there; his wife told him that week, or the next week, when she went to see him, where he was in possession for a Sheriff's officer; she told me so.
 Q. Did she say he took it very much amiss? A. No; he was very satisfied.
 JURY. Q. Do you know whether you went more than twice? A. I cannot swear - I might have gone three times.
 COURT. Q. If you did go the third day, how long were you with her? A. I cannot say that I did go that day; if I knew the truth, I would tell you.
   ANN WOODS. I live at No. 2, Mark's-place. On the 19th of November I heard a scuffle outside my door - I took a candle to see what was the matter, and saw Grover stabbing at Mr. Williams, and heard him say, "D-n you, I will kill you, I will, by G-d" - I then shut my door.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he not appear to be in a violent rage? A. He did not appear so to me; he did not seem in the least passion - I have known Grover and his wife four years; Williams has lived in the court about half a year.
 Q. Until he came into the court, did they not live very comfortable together? A. I have heard his wife say, she could not live with him - I never quarrelled with Grover.
 COURT. Q. You understood they did not live very happily before he came? A. She told me they did not, I cannot say further; they have not lived worse together since, that I know of - I heard a great quarrelling between them, on the night before.
 Q. When was it that the wife told you they did not live well together? A. When she was in the court one day - I cannot say how long before; it was repeatedly, and before Bartholomew fair.
   JOHN JONES. I am a surgeon. I went on the 19th of November, and found the prosecutor stabbed in the upper surface of the arm; it is not severe, nor dangerous; it was in the fleshy part of the arm.
 MR. PHILLIPS to JOHN WILLIAMS. Q. Were you looking at the execution in the Old Bailey, on Thursday, the 23d of November? A. No, Sir, I was not.
 Q. Were you in the Old Bailey, near the execution, upon that morning? A. No, Sir, I was not.
 Q. Then you was not there with the prisoner's wife, while the prisoner was laying in prison? A. I was not, before God and my country.
   ABRAHAM COLEY. I am a constable. I went to take up the prisoner, who lived on the other side of the court; he was in an agitated state; he said Williams had destroyed his peace of mind, and his wife was taking away her clothes, and was going to live with Mr. Williams; he pulled out the drawers, and showed me that they were empty - I asked if he was aware he had wounded him in that dreadful manner; he said No, he was so agitated he was not aware that he had wounded him, if he had, he could have made his escape.
 Q. What, you thought Williams' life in danger? A. Yes - I saw the wound; it appeared to me to be a dreadful one; the prisoner went with me without any resistance.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he not appear in great agitation of mind, and distress? A. Yes; he showed me some empty drawers.
