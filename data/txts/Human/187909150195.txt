   Cross-examined.  I did not hear Inspector Shaw caution her that what she said would be used in evidence against her—I believe there have been two bodies of children found besides this one; not in the exact neighbour hood, some distance off, one at Barnes and one at Kew; that would be a distance of about a mile or a mile and a half—I know the ditch in question—I believe it is not a tidal ditch; the water runs one way, but not very strongly.
   Re-examined.  It is a continual steady flow, not sufficient to move any thing the size of this child—I cannot tell where the other bodies were found—I had nothing to do with those cases. EDWARD SHAW( Police Inspector ). On 4th August, between 1 and 2 in the afternoon, I went with Sampson to the residence of the prisoner's father at Mortlake—I knocked at the door, the prisoner answered it—I asked her her name—she said, "Charlotte Cutler"—I told her who I was—I was in plain clothes—I said I wished to speak to her—she said, "Come outside"—I said, "No, I would rather see you iuside the house—she took me into the back part of the house; there was a young woman there—I then asked the prisoner if she could tell me where her child was that she had been confined of—she said, "My child is all right"—I said, "Where?"—at this time I was interrupted by a gentleman who came into the wash-house and said he was a solicitor—I told him not to interrupt me in the execution of my duty—he got out a piece of paper and a pencil, and told the prisoner not to say anything—I then spoke to her again about the child—she said, "If my sister goes out I will tell you"—the sister went out and the gentleman also—the prisoner then said, "I will tell you now all about it; I left Kingston Union on the 2nd or 3rd of last month, I went into a public-house and had some refreshment; I took the train to Kingston, at St. Margaret's I got out and I walked to Mortlake, and while I was on the bridge I met a woman with a child, and I gave her a shilling to take the child away"—she said she did not want to disgrace her family by taking the child home—she did not at that time say where the woman was to take the child—I told her I should take her into custody on suspi cion of having murdered her child—I then conveyed her to the railway station at Mortlake, and while standing on the platform she said, "There has been three or four children found lately, has there not?"—I said, "Yes"—I then conveyed her to Richmond police-station—I afterwards heard her
