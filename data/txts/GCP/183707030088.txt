WILLIAM STUBBING. I am shopman to Mr. Harding, of York-street.
Westminster, a pawnbroker. I produce this watch-the prisoner brought
it to me about eight o'clock in the evening, on the 27th of June--I had had
a description of it, and detained it.
JOHN WILDS (police-constable B 25.) I was called, and the prisoner and
watch were given to me asked him how he came by the watch-he said
he won it at a raffle at Bermondsey-I asked him where he said he did
not know the place again.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he not tell you something more? A. He said
he threw forty-four-I did not forget that I should have mentioned it!
asked him whether he was tipsy at the time he had been rafling-he said
he had had a little drop to drink--I told that to the Magistrate- I mean
to swear that I cannot say whether it was taken down in writing-what
I said was read over to me--I firmly believe that was among it-he told
me on what night he had won it.

1734. WILLIAM GLANVILLE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th
of June, 560lbs, weight of printed paper, value 101., the goods of Robert
Wilson, his master.
Messrs. CLARKSON and HORRY conducted the Prosecution.de
ROBERT WILSON. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Regent-street la
May, 1836, I was in possession of the copyright of "Pinnock's Guide to
Knowledge"-the prisoner came into my employ at Christmas, 1835, and was
there till 1836- he was the publisher of this work--he was engaged by
young Mr. Pinnock, who was the editor-I ceased to employ the prisoner
at Christmas, 1836—he has since been in my employ for about a month,
arranging the work for the purpose of sale--this paper was principally w
Charles-street, Covent-garden, and the remainder at a wharf, in Thames-
street--the prisoner had the key of it-he had two porters to assist him-
we did not agree on any salary, but I said I would satisfy him when it was
over, which he said would be in a fortnight or three weeks--he used to
send every Saturday night for me to pay the porters, and I was to pay him
at the end of the time I received this note from his for money-read
-"17th June, 1837. SIR, Have the goodness to send by bearer ll. for
the men for the five days this week, and at the same time have the kindness
to let me have a trifle on account, and you will much oblige. I am, Sir,
your obedient servant, Willian GLANVILLE."
MR. WILSON re-examined. I had given him authority to dispose of the
damaged part-he returned me 51. 143. for that I took the £5 pote and
left the silver with him for other things-he said he had sold it pretty well
-I did not see the prisoner any time after I received the letter-in conse-
quence of that note I gave the porter the money, and he took it away
I saw within two or three days that the bulk was not so large as it had been
-I heard some of it had been sold at two different places in the City.
