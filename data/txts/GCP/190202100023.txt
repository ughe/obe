By the Court. I have never known of a case where a fit lasts during
the period while a man is running a mile-there are some cases on record
of excitement follo'ving a fit-in the severe forms the person falls down,

shows there was an element of drunkenness, but I cannot say there was no
epilepsy.

NEW COURT.-Wednesday, February 12th, 1902.
Before Mr. Recorder.
172. GEORGE CLUBB, Feloniously wounding Albert Tarratt with
intent to do him grievous bodily harm.
The prisoner stated that he was guilty of unlawfully wounding, and the
Jury found that verdict. He received a good character. Discharged on his
father's recognizances,
173. ALFRED MOORE, Stealing a bicycle the property of James
Thompson. Second Count, Receiving the same.
MR. TURNER Prosecuted, Mr. LBYCESTER Defended.
The prisoner having been in business as a dealer in the same neighbour-
hood for 30 years, and having paid a fair price for the bicycle, the Court
considered that there was not sufficient evidence that he knew that the bicycle
was stolen.
Nor GUILTY.
174. ALFRED MOORE was again indicted for stealing a watch, the
property of Walter Edward Freeman.
No evidence was offered.
Nor Guilty.
