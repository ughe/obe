Before Mr. Recorder.
1688. MARTHA ROGERS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th di
April, 1 sixpence, and 9 groats; the monies of William Hollands, her master.
WILLIAM HOLLANDS. I am clerk to Jones, Loyd, and Co., and live in
Beresford-street, Newington-the prisoner was eight days in my service-
consequence of some suspicion, I marked some fourpenny pieces, and other
silver, put it into my wife's purse, which I put into her reticule, and left it
on the parlour table, as we were in the habit of doing- I locked the par.
lour door, and the key was put on the prisoner's mantel-piece for her to
get the room ready in the morning-next morning I found the reticule lying
on the table as usual, but missed some fourpenny pieces and a 6d.--I sent
for a constable, and gave the prisoner in charge.
John HUNTLEY (police-constable P 143.) I took the prisoner in custody,
and took her to the station.
SARAH EVEREST. I am the wife of David Everest, a policeman, and live
at the station-I searched the prisoner on the 20th of April, and found on her
these nine fourpenny-pieces, a fivepenny-piece, another small piece, and
sixpence.
MR. HOLLANDS re-examined. These are what I marked, and put into the
purse,
Prisoner. I had some clothes in pawn, which I was very much in want ol,
and seeing the bag lying on the table three or four days, I was tempted to take
the money, which I should not have done, if it had not been lying there,
Guilty. Aged 17.- Recommended to mercy by the prosecutor.
Judgment Respited.

1689. WILLIAM GOY was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of Dec.,
2 pewter pots, value 28.; the goods of Thomas Roberts.
CHARLES MILLER. I live in St. Ann's-street, Kennington-the prisoner
was in my employ, as a chimney-sweeper, about seven months-in conto
quence of something I heard, I said, "William, where are those pots ? "-be
went to the soot yard, and brought three or four, such as publicans use-I
asked how he came by them-be said they came from No. 4, Bedford-terrace
-I asked what he was going to do with them he said, “I do not know;"
and then said, smilingly, “To sell them"--I said, “ Are you aware what you
are about to do? you are liable to be sent to Brixton ; do you know who
they belong to ?"-be said "No"-I said, “You can read what is on them"
-I could read it-one part over the inscription had been cleaned-on the
27th of April, a lad in my employ, named Lovejoy, brought me two pint
pots-I gave information to the police that afternoon, and gave him the
two pots--the others could not be found—I had asked the prisoner if he had
taken the pots back-he said he had I asked what the people said he said,
“Nothing"-I asked what they gave him-he said, “Only something to
drink."

window-ledge of the soot-shed, about four months ago I heard master tell
the prisoner to take them home-he did not say where he was to take them-
I saw them that same afternoon wrapped up in a handkerchief-last Thurs-
day week, in cleaning out the loft, I found the same two pots under the tiles
- took them to my master-these are the pots.
