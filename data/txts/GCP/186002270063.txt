DANIEL LEWTHWAITE. I liveat Mr. Harris's, a pawnbroker, of 5, St. Mary's-

Prisoner. I gave a gold chain; I did not give a brass one.
ROBERT BELL (Policeman, P 39). I received this chain from Mrs. Ingram,
this other I received fom Mrs. Baker- I took the prisoner-I spoke to him
in English; he answered me in Italian and it was interpreted to me by De
Grussa-I charged him with bawking chains about as gold and giving
fictitious ones—he threatened to punch De Grussa's head and to kick his
head-he said, that if I attempted to take him along the street he would
lie down,
NICHOLAS Rossi. I am a waiter, of 27, Frith-street, Soho-on 31st
January I saw the prisoner at the London Bullion-tavern, Titchborne-street,
where I am employed-he spoke to some one else- & communication was
made to me and he went with one of the other waiters into a pawn-shop
-I did not go with them; the waiter came and spoke to me and I went
out to see who was going to sell the chain-I saw the prisoner-ho would
not show it to me-I spoke to him in Italian and said, “This man has no
money to buy, but I will-he took the chain out of his pocket in a piece of
paper-I only just looked at it because the other waiters told me they had
been to a pawn-shop and tried it-I said, “I will give you 41. 158. for it,"
and told him to come at 12 o'clock when the place shut up, as I had not the
money in my pocket-he said, “Yes, but I am hungry and have not got a
farthing; I want to go and eat"-I gave him half-a-crown.
COURT. Q. Can you say whether the chain you ultimately got was the
one you first saw or a different one? A. I cannot-I gave him 41. 155. all
but a few pence.
DANIEL LEWTHWAITE (re-examined). This is a brass chain, the same as the
other-it is worth about 38.
Prisoner's Defence. The chain passed this man's hands. I gave it as
