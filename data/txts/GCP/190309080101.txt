Eleventh Session, 1902–1903.
1116
ERNEST WILLIAM HART Cox. I am a clerk in the London Provident
Institution Savings Bank-I opened the account when the money was
first paid in- I remember Lichtenstein coming with another woman
about the withdrawal of the amount.
CHARLES RUSSELL LEE. I am cashier in the London Provident Bank-
I entered the payment of £13 when the account was opened the “ £13"
has been altered since.
PHILIP SILBERSTEIN. I am a watchmaker, of 41, Mansell Street-the
prisoner Eva is my sister and lives in the same house-one night she came
and said Lena Lichtenstein had come erying to her and said that she had
money in the bank she wanted to take out, and that she had been with
Lena to the bank for some money-she did not say she signed the name
"Lichtenstein,”-she said she went as a witness.
Cross-examined by Lichtenstein. You left a watch at my brother's
shop to be repaired-it was to be 38. hd.-you said you had £160 in the
bank-another day you brought three £5 notes-you said you were going
to pay it into the bank and that would make £175–1 asked you to lend
me £5---you said you would only lend £2-afterwards I asked you to let
me have a £5 note and I would give you the change, keeping £2, and you
said no, you would bring the £2 back when you paid the money into the
bank—then another day you did lend me £2, and when you had the watch
back you paid ls. 6d. and had 28. as part of the £2 you had lent.
By the Court. When I gave her 28. she gave me a little receipt, as I
said, “We shall know how much I give you every week," and she signed
her name here it says, “ From Mr. Silberstein the sum of 25.; balance,
£l 189. ; Lena Lichtenstein."
Lena Lichtenstein, in her defence on oath, stated that she took a watch to
be repaired to Mr. Silberstein, 29, Middlesex Street, and asked where the bank
was ; that he asked her to shore him the money she had ; that at first she refused:
that she ultimately did so ; that he then asked her to lend him £5; that she
did not want to, but he would not allow her to go until he had got £2; that she
returned to the shop orying, and wanted the money back as there was only £13
banked, and then he said he knew what to do in the matter : that he took the
book, kept it about five minutes, and then gave it baek and said, " Now you
can go home quietly," that when she went to the bank and they told her to fetek
her sister, Philip Silberstein sent his sister instead, and that Mr. Silberstein
asked her to draw the £13 out.
LENA LICHTENSTEIN, GUILTY, but under intimidation. She received
a good character. Discharged on recognisances. EVA SILBERSTEIN,
Nor GUILTY.
