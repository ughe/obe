RICHARD FIELD. I am a waterman, employed by the Tunnel-pier Com pany at the Lower Tunnel-pier. On the 25th of Oct. I saw the Waterman, No . 6, coming up before ten o'clock in the morning—I saw a skiff with persons in it—it was about forty or fifty yards from the steamer when I first saw it—I saw some man in the boat, get up and wave his hand to the steamer.
   Q.  From the position of the steamer and the skiff at that time, could the steamer, in your opinion, have avoided it? A.  Yes, if he had altered his helm, by starboarding the helm, or stopping the engine, might have saved it—there was nothing to prevent them taking a left-hand course—I saw a man on the paddle-box—if he had been looking out he must have seen the boat—he sung out to them, "Hoy, hoy"—he did not stop the steamer at all—he kept on his course—I saw an eddy from the schooner that was lying aground—that eddy could not have affected the skiff at all—she was away from it—she was in the tide's way—if she had been in the eddy, the steamer could not have got at her at all.
   Cross-examined by . MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What are you at the Lower Tunnel-pier? A.  A waterman—I act for the Company—I am on duty there—these boats go to the Upper Tunnel-pier—when a steamer is going with the tide, by merely stopping her engine, and not turning it astern, she would go a long distance before she stopped still—perhaps she would go half a mile before she would stop by herself, so as to have only tide-way upon her, unless she was stopped by the engine being reversed—they could stop her by reversing the engine in her own length, that is my judg ment—the length of the Waterman, No . 6, is eighty or ninety feet—if she was going at eight or nine knots, and the engine were reversed, I think she might be brought up in that distance—our steam-boats go at the rate of eight or ten miles an hour on the river.
   Q.  It is a rival company, is it? A.  Yes; they are in conjunction now—they go to different piers—there is no opposition now—the two com panies have agreed together.
   JURY . Q.  Were the companies in conjunction at the time of the acci dent? A.  I think they were.
   COURT . Q.  Do you mean that these steamers go at ten miles an hour in the Pool? A.  Yes; a steamer obeys the helm the better the quicker she is going—if she was only going with the tide she would not obey it at all.
