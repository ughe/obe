OLD COURT.-Friday, March 11th, and Saturday, March 12th, 1892.
Before Mr. Justice A. L. Smith.
370. GEORGE WOODYATT HASTINGS (66) was indicted for that
he being trustee for a sum of £15,000 did unlawfully and with intent to
defraud, convert the same to his own use and benefit. In eight other
Counts he was charged with appropriating certain securities to his own
use, which he had received as a trustee. To this indictment he PLEADED
GUILTY.
Messrs. CHARLES MATHEWS and HORACE Avory Prosecuted, and Mr.
LOCKWOOD, Q.C., with Hon. ALFRED LYTTLETON, appeared for the prisoner,
who received the highest character from Lord Coventry, Lord Aberdare, Lord
Cobham, and others.--Five Years' Penal Servitude.
371. MAITLAND FRANCIS MORLAND (65), Feloniously sending
to Earl Russell a letter demanding money with menaces, and without
any reasonable or probable cause-to this he PLEADED GUILTY.
MESSRS. G. F. GILL and HORACE AVORY Prosecutod, MR. PAUL TAYLOR
appeared for the prisoner.-Ten Years' Penal Servitude.
There were other indictments against the prisoner.
372. JOHN STAMP (a police-constable), For a rape on Mary Earl.
MR. C. F. GILL Prosecuted, and MR. HUTTON Defended.
Not Guilty.
373. LOUIS ALLARD (28), For unlawfully procuring Mary Allard,
otherwise Lawler, to become a common prostitute.
MR. C. F. GILL Prossouted, and MR. ABINGER Defended.
GUILTY-Twenty Months' Hard Labour.
374. ALEXIS RUDET (44), Unlawfully conspiring with Louis
Allard to demand money from William Leybourn Popham; troo other
Counts, for threatening to publish certain matters and things, and for
proposing to abstain from publishing
MR. A. GILL Prosecuted, and MR. ABINGER Defended.
MR. ABINGER submitted that a difficulty arose in this case, from the fact
that the principal witness would be the wife of the man Allard, who had just
been convicted ; although under the circumstances of that charge she was a con-
petent witness against her husband, she could not be examined against the
present defendant, whose conspiracy if with anyone was with Allard. MR.
GILL contended that her evidence was admissible to prove that Rudet conspired
with Allard ; although not for tke purpose of establishing Allard's conspiracy
with Rudet. After argumont MR. GILL, at the suggestion of MR. JUSTICE
SMITH, abandoned the first Count, alleging conspiracy, and the case on the troo
other Counts was left to the Jury, who found the prisoner GUILTY.-
Fifteen Months' Hard Labour.
