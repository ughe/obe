DANIEL GARDINER. I am shopman to Mr. Smedley, a pawnbroker, of
Somer's-town. I have two rings, which the prisoner offered to pawn on
Friday, about half past two o'clock-I stopped her with them.
WILLIAM SPENCER. I am shopman to Mr. Cassiday, a pawnbroker,
living in Warren-place, Camden-town. I have a gold watch and ring-
the ring was pawned on Wednesday morning, the 10th of December, and
the watch in the afternoon between four and five o'clock, by the prisoner
-I asked who she brought it from; she said her aunt-I asked her to send
her aunt, but the aunt never came- I kept the watch, and could not find
out the place she gave as her direction-I lent her no money on it-I
did not take in the first pledge.
EDWARD LAMBERT. About half-past two o'clock on Friday, Gardiner,
the pawnbroker, brought the prisoner to the watch-house, saying she had
offered to pawn some shoes, which he had stopped ; and that she had
offered two rings before I found out her master, but found no property
on her.
