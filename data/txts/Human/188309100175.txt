   Cross-examined.  I gave a promise of 50 gross to the prisoner as soon as I got through the boxes which had been supplied to me—he pressed me for an order for the new firm which he was travelling for—that was about the end of July, two or three weeks before he was arrested—he mentioned Mr. Fraser's name and said that he would have to send to Wales to get the printing done, and it would take some time to get them ready, as they did not do their own printing—I had not given him a positive order for 50 gross in October, but by this time I should have wanted them, and I should have had no objection to confirm the order if he had not been charged—I never heard anything against his respecta bility—I became bail for him, and I will give him employment to morrow if he is clear—I said so at the police-court before the Public Prosecutor was introduced—I should be very glad of the tins at the price.
   Re-examined.  I said that I was willing to take them—Mr. Fraser offered them to me and I refused to receive them and said that it was a forgery, but at that time they were not made—I have only lost a lot of time; I have not since become bankrupt. RICHARD KIMBER( Police Sergeant M ). On Sunday morning, 12th August, I went to 42, Lambeth Road, saw the prisoner, and read the warrant to him for obtaining 5 l . from Mr. Fraser by false pretences—he said "It is a very bad job; I was in want of money, can I see Mr. Fraser?"—I said "No, I think you cannot; my duty is to take you to the station and take you before a Magistrate"—he said "I can pay the money"—I said "I have nothing to do with that."
