 893.  MAURICE SHEEDY was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of March , 4 iron stove-bottoms, value 3s., and 7 iron turn-buckles, value 6d., the goods of Margaret Nash.
   MARGARET NASH. I am a widow, and deal in ironmongery. On the 3d of March these things were within four inches of my door; I had put them out myself - I was at my dinner, and a witness called out "Mrs. Nash, do you know the person who has taken these things from your door?" I said No, went out, and gave an alarm; when the prisoner was brought back he said it was the first time, and he hoped I would be merciful.
   RICHARD BURTON. I saw the prisoner take these articles - he walked very fast by me, and I went and called to know if Nash knew the young man - I heard a voice say No- the prosecutrix then came out; the prisoner carried them in front of him: I followed him, and saw him drop the things.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined One Month.
 894.  SAMUEL TASKER was indicted for embezzlement.
   CHARLOTTE PHILLIPS. I am the wife of Horatio Nathaniel Phillips, a publican- the prisoner was in our service as waiter; my husband is not here.
   JOHN WEATHERBY. I am an officer, and took the prisoner - he asked if I had a warrant against him; I said I had not - he said he would go and see Mr. Phillips, and hear what he would say.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Then he went with you? A. Yes; Mr. Phillips asked if he had got any friends who would come forward to see the money paid that he owed him - I believe he owed him about 21l.; he said he had got nobody to come forward - Mr. Phillips ordered me to take him into custody, but he did not appear before the Magistrate; he said nothing to him about the 6s.
   ANDREW WATSON. On the 25th of March, between one and two o'clock, I paid the prisoner 6s.
 Cross-examined. Q. What was it for? A. For lodging; I took no receipt - I made a memorandum of it myself.
   CHARLOTTE PHILLIPS re-examined. The prisoner had been in our service two years; he ought to account on Saturday night to me or my husband for monies received- he absconded on the Saturday night.
 Cross-examined. Q. If the 6s. had been accounted for to Mr. Phillips it would have been just the same as if it had been paid to you? A. Yes.
     NOT GUILTY.
 895.  SAMUEL TASKER was again indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 15 shillings and 10 sixpences, the monies of Horatio Nathaniel Phillips.
   CHARLOTTE PHILLIPS. The prisoner was in our service, and was in the habit of receiving 1l. in silver to go to Mr. Gurney's factory to receive scores; he had it on the night of the 28th of March, and did not return.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the bar and Mrs. Phillips gave me fifteen shillings, eight sixpences, and 1s. in copper, which I never did account for certainly; but she never gave me 1l. worth of silver.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Confined Six Months.
 896.  HENRY WISKER was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 1 mill wright's tool, called a square, value 6s., the goods of William Lockie.
   WILLIAM LOCKIE. I am a millwright. I lost a square on the 25th of February, from the back room of my house- the prisoner lodged in my house two or three days; he had no right in that room.
   RICHARD ROPE. I am a pawnbroker. I took in this square on the 25th of February; I do not know from whom.
   THOMAS WATERLOW. I am an officer. I took up the prisoner and charged him with stealing this square; he said there were other persons in the house beside him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written defence, denying the charge.
     NOT GUILTY.
 897.  ANN ANDREWS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of March , 1 shawl, value 7s.; 3 aprons, value 2s., and 1 handkerchief, value 6d., the goods of Betty Barnes ; 1 shawl, value 15s., and 1 table-cloth, value 5s., the goods of Robert Back.
   ROBERT BACK. I lost a shawl and table-cloth previous to the 25th of March, from a house where I resided; the prisoner lived there as servant to Mr. Jenkins - these are them; the table-cloth has my initials, and the shawl is like my wife's, but I will not swear positively to that.
   BETTY BARNES. I lost a shawl, five aprons, and a handkerchief, from the same house; the prisoner was servant there - these are the articles.
   GEORGE SHEPPERD. I am a pawnbroker. This red shawl was pawned by a woman - I cannot say by whom.
   GEORGE UNDERWOOD. I am a pawnbroker. I have three aprons and a silk handkerchief; I cannot say by whom they were pawned.
   RICHARD ROPE. This shawl was pawned with me by a woman; I do not know who.
   SAMUEL SHARWOOD. I am a pawnbroker. This tablecloth was pawned with me - I believe by the prisoner.
   ROBERT DUKE. I took up the prisoner at the house, and desired her to get me a light to look for some other property; while she was looking for the candle she went to the bottom of the kitchen stairs - I saw her throw her hand back; I went to the place and found between twenty and thirty duplicates - I said "You have just thrown these away, what did you do that for?" she said, because she did not want me to know she had them; they were for this property.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Judgment Respited.
