1631. HANNAH DENNY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of June , 6 sovereigns, the monies of Robert John Palk , her master.
 MR. ADOLPHUS, on behalf of the prosecution, declined offering any evidence.    NOT GUILTY.
 1632.  JAMES DAY was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of June , 13 handkerchiefs, value 3l., the goods of Samuel Morgan ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   SAMUEL MORGAN LEWIS. I am shopman to Mr. Samuel Morgan, a glover and hosier, in the Strand. On the 26th of June the prisoner came to purchase a pair of brown cotten stockings; I showed him two parcels - he selected a pair at 1s. 6d. - he gave me 2s., and while I was getting a sixpence from the till, he snatched a parcel of handkerchiefs from the counter, and concealed them under a parcel in his arms; I came round the counter - he saw that I noticed him, and he ran out; I pursued: the officer saw and pursued him - he threw the handkerchiefs down and the stockings which he had bought.
   MORRIS FREELAND(Police-constable F 85.) I pursued, and took the prisoner; I saw him throw away these articles, and I took them up.
   JOHN RATTERY(Police-constable F 54). I have a certificate of the former conviction of the prisoner, which I got from Mr. Clark's office; I was a witness, and know he is the person.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1633.  CHARLES CLEMMENS was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of June , 2 coats, value 3l., the goods of Charles Christian.
   MANWARING CHITTY. I am in the service of Mr. Charles Christian, a land-surveyor, who lives in Lisson-grove; I was in his office on the 18th of June, about half-past twelve o'clock, when these coats were taken from his passage - the door was open, and I saw the prisoner go out with them; I followed, and brought him back - I had never seen him before; he had got over the garden gate, which was locked.
   JOHN TAYLOR(Police-constable S 17). I took the prisoner, and have the coats.
 Prisoner's Defence. A well dressed man asked me to earn 1s. - he went and got the coats, and put them on my arm; soon after this gentleman came and took me - as soon as I recovered myself I saw the man who gave them to me, running off.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1634.  ANTHONY CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of June , 1 watch, value 6l.; 1 gold chain, value 3l.; 1 steel chain, value 2s.; 2 seals, value 10s.; 1 watch-key, value 1d., and 5 rings, value 5l., the goods of Catherine Twamley.
   CATHERINE TWAMLEY. I lost a great number of articles at the fire in Oxford-street; I have seen the articles stated - they are mine.
   JAMES WALSH(Police-constable C 4.) I have a watch-movement, which I got from Mr. Burgess - this medal I found on the prisoner at the station; this gold chain I received from Mr. Smart - this gold watch, four rings, six sovereigns, and a brooch, I found at Mr. Sharp's, a publican, in Green-court.
   SIMON GEISMAR. I am a jeweller. This watch-movement is mine - I lodged at the house which was burnt down.
   HENRY BURGESS. I am a builder. The prisoner gave me this watch-movement on the Thursday night after the fire; I had spoken to him, in consequence of having heard that some things had been taken, and he brought this watch-movement, and stated it was all he had got, except a ring, which appeared more like a brass ring than any thing else - I had employed him to remove the rubbish of the fire, and what was found he was to give to me, as I was in possession for the Westminster Fire-office.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. Did you give him instructions to give all he found to you? A. Yes: I desired all who were there to put all they found in a fireplace-I do not know that I desired the prisoner in particular.
   WILLIAM LYNCH. I am a workman in the Five-fields, Chelsea - the prisoner lodged in my house. I saw the prisoner with this gold chain, a silver chain, and this watch, which he took out of his pocket in Mr. Smart's shop: I work for Mr. Burgess, but I was not at this job - the prisoner told me he had picked up these things in Soho-square, where Mr. Burgess' cart shot the rubbish; Mr. Smart objected to buy the watch, and he took that and some other things to Mr. Sharp's, and delivered them at the bar.
 Cross-examined. Q. What was it you saw in possession of the prisoner? A. This chain and the case of this movement, which Mr. Smart bought; I believe this to be the watch which Mr. Smart would not buy - he said he picked them up in Soho-square, among the rubbish; I am a labourer - I have been in Newgate, but was not tried.
   KNELLER SMART. I am assistant to Mr. Benjamin Smart. He is a refiner, and lives in Princes-street. I remember the prisoner and Lynch coming there on the 4th of June, about six o'clock in the evening; the prisoner laid down this chain, this watch, and several small silver articles; he said he picked them up in Soho-square; the chain came to 2l. 2s. 7d. and the silver to 12s. 4d. - I paid him, and Lynch gave me back 1d. - they left the shop, and soon after came back, pulled out a paper, and laid down this watch - I said that did not come from the shoots; he said No, from the ruins; I said he must take that back, and give it to the firemen in possession - he said there was no one - I told him then to take it to Mr. Burgess, and he could tell him where to return it to - he hesitated, but I said I should insist upon his going to take it back - he then left the shop.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had he given you his name and address? A. Yes, the first time.
   ANN HIGGINS. I live with Mr. Sharp. The prisoner called and asked me for the money that he left with Mr. Sharp; I fetched the parcel from my master, and gave it him in the passage; he was forward in drink - I followed him; he went into another public-house, and I told him he had better leave it with the girl there - I gave it her, and she gave it me again afterwards - I do not know what was in it.
   MARY HIGGINS. I am sister of this witness - she gave me the parcel, and I gave it her again.
