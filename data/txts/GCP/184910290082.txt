1923. WILLIAM JOHNSON, and WILLIAM SMITH, stealing 161bs.
weight of rope, value 4s. ; the goods of John Kelk.
WILLIAM BUTLER (police-sergeant, B 3). I was on duty in Vauxhall-
bridge-road on 28th Sept., at half-past nine, and saw the prisoners, each car-
rying a piece of rope-Smith was about two or three yards in front-I
stopped him, and asked him where he got the rope-he said he picked it up at
Hyde-park-corner.
Thomas Pronger (policeman, A 237). On 28th Sept., I was in Vaux.
hall-bridge-road, and stopped Johnson; he had parted from Smith-I had
seen them together first-I found a scaffold cord in his possession ; I asked
how he came by it-be said he found it at Hyde-park-corner-I took him to
the station.
SAMUEL WARWICK. I am carman to Mr. Kelk. This rope is his, and
was on his cart-I know it by this end, I cannot positively swear it is his
I left it safe with the cart in the yard. -
THOMAS LYNCH. I am labourer to Mr. Kelk. This scaffold-cord is his;
there is a mark on it-it was brought from Prince's-gate before it was lost.
Smith. Q. Can you swear to the big piece? A. Not positively.
Smith's Defence. I saw the rope lying on the ground, and was going to
see if I could make anything by it.
Not GUILTY.
