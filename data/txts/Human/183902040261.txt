CHARLES PTE. I am a constable of the rail-road. Foley works on the railway—they were both given into my custody—I went to the place, and found the pump cut in pieces—I knew it to be the Company's pump—I had seen it within a week—Holland works for the contractor. JAMES KENNERLY. I am a constable of the Southampton railway. The prisoners were brought into the station-house—I found a hammer at Holland's lodging, in Vauxhall-road—Foley gave his address in South Lambeth-buildings—I could find no such place, but I found he lived in Alpha-court—I there found an axe and hammer—when I came back I asked him why he gave me a wrong address—he said he did not know the name of the place—I asked if it was in Alpha-court—he said he believed it was. JAMES WALKER. I work on the Southampton railway, and am a car penter. This is my chopper. I left it safe in the basket on the works, on the 1st of January, in a building which I was roofing. WILLIAM HAMMERSTON. I am a carpenter on the Southampton rail way. This is my axe—the last time I saw it was on the 30th of October—I had met with an accident, and did not go to work after that for five weeks. CHARLES CONSTABLE. I am a carpenter on the railway. This hammer is mine—I left it on the works—I was away for a month. GEORGE WILLIAM EDGECOMB. My father is a carpenter on the railway. This small hammer is mine—I left it in my father's counting-house.
 (The prisoner Foley put in a written defence, stating that he had found the things in the field.)
   Holland's Defence.  As I was coming home, at four o'clock, I picked up the hammer on the road—I did not know who it belonged to—I thought it not worth advertising, and left it at my house—I know nothing at all about the pump.
 (The prisoners received good characters.)
   FOLEY  — GUILTY. Aged 40.
   HOLLAND  — GUILTY. Aged 39. Confined Six Months.
