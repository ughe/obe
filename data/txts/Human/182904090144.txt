 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. When it was discovered to be an improper seizure - a good many things were put back into the house? A. Yes; I was not present then - I did not know Martin before; he was not taken into custody - he went to Worship street of his own accord, and attended twice.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Do you know Petit? A. Yes; I had not seen him before; a little boy let him in at the street door - he told Butler he had shewn his authority to so many, he was afraid it would he torn up; I think the whole of the things were not put into the van at the time he was taken into custody.
 Q. Did he make the least resistance? A. Yes; he would not go to the office for some time; he did not say he had shown his authority to the proper person, and they had no right to take him.
 Prisoner WHITE. Q. Was I at all violent in my behaviour in the house? A. No; I only saw you standing still in the passage.
   MARY ANN LOADER. I am single, and live with Miss Howorth. I was at home on the 3d of April; she was called out of the room I was in, about half-past one; and while she was out I heard a noise on the stairs - I went into the passage to see what it was, and saw a man in top boots, and Pettet and White on the stairs; while I was in the passage I heard a noise of a padlock breaking, and saw Miss Howorth come down; I saw Petit a very few minutes afterwards - he came into my room - I told him I hoped he had not come to strip us of all our things; he said, I might take a few things out, if I could get them out, but I must be very quick in doing it, for he had got a van, and his men coming directly to clear the house; I lodge in the same room as Miss Howorth - I took some things out, into a court by the side of the house, and then he bolted me out; I was let in again by Mrs. Mackey, who had been at the house for a few nights: when I got in I went into my own room, and into Gardner's room - that was nearly empty - and the chairs, tables, and candlesticks, were taken out of Miss Howorth's room; I saw the same things brought back again; I afterwards went to Bennet's room, and saw Butler there - Petter was there- I did not notice any thing else: I heard Butler ask Pettet for his authority, and he refused to shew it him: Butler asked me, in Petter's hearing, who had put in the seizure? I said Smith, by which name Pettet had appeared) had put it in, in the name of one Chapman - I think he had said William Chapman; Petter was desired to cease- he was stopped with a table, and he insisted on taking it out, saying he was acting under the authority of the King: Hawthorne was sent for - he came, and took Pettet into custody; I saw Pettet take some chairs and tables out of Miss Howorth's room - and he took some candlesticks out of my hand; the things in the room were her's, but I had the use of them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Who brought the things in? A. I brought some in - some persons at the door took them off the van - some were in the passage.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did not Pettet shew an authority at the time this took place? A. I saw him show some sort of paper at one time to some of the persons, but what it was I do not know - he afterwards refused to shew it to Butler.
 Q. Did he not say you might take some things, if you could get them out before some persons came to the house? A. If I could do it before the van came: I never heard him say he was agent to Smith; the things were brought back after he was in custody; some of the other lodgers' things were also brought back - but not till after he was in custody.
   JOHN PATTEN. I am a fishing-tackle maker, and lodge at Miss Howorth's, on the ground-floor. About half-past one o'clock on this day I was in Miss Howorth's room, and heard somebody call out Miss Howorth - I opened her room door, and saw Pettet and White, and a man in top boots, standing in the passage; I said, "Miss Howorth, you are wanted," and she went out - in about five minutes I went into Gardner's room; I found nobody there but Gardner and his brother - Pettet came into the room; I asked him who authorised him to come and make a seizure in that room; he told me to mind my own business, or else he would charge a constable with me, and said he was acting under the King - he said he wanted 120l. for six years' back-rent; I said, "This young man don't owe any rent, he has got three or four weeks good yet" - he told me again to mind my own business, or else he would charge a constable with me; I said, "You won't be so hard - this poor young man has got but one hand, let him have a thing or two that he gets his living by." I asked him for his nets, his fishing canns, and gun; he said, be quick, for directly the van came he should clear the house immediately - I went up stairs into Miss Howorth's bed-room, and got two or three small things out, and when I came down the van was at the door, partly loaded - Martin was in the van; I saw Hillick bringing a trunk down stairs; he took it to the van - Pettet at that time was giving the men directions to be quick; and I saw Pettet with a table in the passage just before Butler came - it had been taken from Miss Howorth's room; after this Pettet was taken into custody - I afterwards assisted in unloading the van; Watts, the street-keeper, was there - I was in the van; Martin asked who authorised me to move the goods from the van - Watts showed his staff and said, "I authorised him." Martin said,"Where am I to get my money from?" I said, "You must look to those who employed you." Martin assisted me in moving the heaviest things directly Watts showed, his staff.
