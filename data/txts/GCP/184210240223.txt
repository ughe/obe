SARAH MORLEY. At the time I was examined I lived in Union-street,
Borough; I knew the deceased by living in the same house for seventeen
weeks. On the Sunday before the Saturday on which he died, the pri-
soner was in my room, and our conversation began about the newspaper
I told her a lady and gentleman and their servant had been poisoned with
arsenic, by their daughter-she asked me whether I knew how they sold it,
whether in liquid or powder-nothing was said about the name it was called
by-I saw the deceased on the morning he died-I cannot tell what time,
it was before dinner he was sick when I first went into the room-te
brought something up, but what I do not know-the prisoner was there at
that time-she said he brought up every thing she gave him-I asked
her why she did not have a doctor-she said, “We owe money"-I said,
" Why, there are parish doctors and dispensary doctors ; why not have a
doctor!"- the deceased said, “Why not me have a doctor?"-she said,
“You don't want to be physicked and tortured abɔut"-he had his hand on
his chest, I did not hear him complain of anything at that time-on the
Monday after the Saturday on which he died I was conversing with Mrs.
Parfitt, whom Mrs. Bowler calls her mother--Mrs. Bowler was present
I told her that he had said, "Why not have a docto ?"-she said, “He did
not say so; and if you say that, you will hang me, for you will be called
on the Jury"--on the Sunday morning the day after his death, I was clean-
ing the room, after laying him out, and brought a half-pint mug off the
drawers in the room, and took it into the kitchen-there was some tea in
it-I emptied it in the yard, on the stones by the water-closet-I saw a
sort of sediment in the bottom of the mug, of a dirty white, like magnesia
-I then took the mug up stairs to my husband, to show him-he told me.
to take it down and place it in the kitchen, and I did 80-I did not wash
it out-I never saw it afterwards-a man named Dunster lodged in the
house-Mrs. Bowler told me at one time, just before Mr. Bowler's death,
that if he (Dunster) got married to the young woman he was paying his
addresses to, she would destroy their happiness on one occasion, previous
to Mr. Bowler's death, she told me that Dunster would stop five years for
her-I never saw her and Dunster go out together.
Cross-examined. Q. The prisoner was of a very cheerful and lively
disposition, was she not? A. Yes I cannot say whether the observa-
tions about Dunster were made in a joking way--she was not joking
at the time, she was speaking very serious I did not attach any im-
portance to them I had no conversation with her husband about it
cannot say whether the prisoner was left an orphan, and brought up by

We came together as witnesses we have had no conversation as to what
We were to say I cannot remember any conversation at all, either before we
went before the Coroner, or since--my husband had read in the newspaper
about the lady and gentleman being poisoned, and had told me of it-the
newspaper was not before me-I told the prisoner what my husband had
