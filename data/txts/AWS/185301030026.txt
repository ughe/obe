178. ELIZABETH MIDDLETON, unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin
MR. ELLis conducted the Prosecution
ELIZABETH MILLs. I am the daughter of Frances Mills, who keeps the
Duke of Wellington, in Coleshill-street On 16th Dec., a woman came,
about half past 1 o'clock in the day- am not certain that it was the prisoner
-she asked for a pint of porter-] served her, and she. offered me a half
erown- tried it-it was bad-I told her it was bad, and returned it to her
-she said she would fetch another, and said she lived in Caroline-street-
she appeared to be a servant.
FRANCES MILLs. I am the mother of the last witness- keep the Duke
of Wellington-I recollect the prisoner coming that day- am sure she is the
person - called my servant to follow her, and she was brought back by the
officer- asked her what she had done with the half crown-she said she
had broken it up, and thrown it away as she was going down Caroline-street
JOHN DAvEY. I am waiter at the Duke of Wellington. I was told by
Mrs. Mills to follow the prisoner- followed her down Caroline-street, and
several other streets, to Grosvenor-road, where I gave her in custody-she
said she had thrown the money away, and she had not got anything about
her-nothing was found on her.
ROBERT ALLEN (policeman, B 151) I took the prisoner on 16th Dec,
-she said a gentleman gave the money to her, and she threw it away in
Caroline-street
MARY JANE PETHERIDGE. I am barmaid at the Trevor Arms, Knights-
bridge. I saw the prisoner on 18th Dec.-she came about 10 o'clock in the
evening for a pint of porter, in a jug-it eame to 2d.-she gave me a half.
crown-I tried it, found it was bad, and told her so-she said she lived in
Trevor-square-] gave the half crown to Mr. Street, my master's brother.
ROGER STREET. I was called by the last witness on 18th Dec., and got
bad half crown from her-the prisoner was present- asked her where she
came from-she said, 46 From No. 8, Trevor-square"'- told her I would go
and inquire whether she came from there, as I did not believe it-she said it
was no use to go there a gentleman gave it her, and she was obliged to get
rid of it.
JOHN CITTING (policeman, B 235). I took the prisoner, and got this ball
crown from the last witness-the prisoner said a gentleman gave it her, and
she was obliged to get rid of it-she said she lived in Old Pye-street-) went
there, and found some prostitutes, who said she slept with them.
