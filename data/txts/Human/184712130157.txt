 397. WILLIAM MADDOCKS , stealing 6 sovereigns, 4 half-crowns, 2 shillings, and 2 pence; the moneys of John James Sharplin , his master. JOHN JAMES SHARPLIN. I am a waterman, at Putney. I have known the prisoner from his childhood—on 1st Dec., about eleven o'clock in the morn ing, I employed him to do my work—I gave him six sovereigns and the other money stated, to pay Mr. Philips—he was to take a butter flat, and bring butter back—he did not come back. SUSANNAH BENNETT. I am the wife of Charles Bennett, of 4, New gate-street. Mr. Phillips is landlord of the premises—we occupy the upper part—the prisoner came there about six o'clock in the evening with an empty butter flat for Mr. Phillips—I asked if he had anything more to leave—he said, "No"—he gave me no money. JEREMIAH M'MAHON( policeman, V  182.) I took the prisoner—he said, in going to the station, that he had lost the money, and Mr. Sharplin could not hurt him. WILLIAM HOARE( policeman, B  93.) The prisoner told me, on 2nd Dec., that he had lost the money. JOHN JAMES SHARPLIN re-examined.  He told me next day that he had lost the money—I found him at a booking-office at Queenhithe—he said he had been robbed.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was to have brought the flat of butter back, but Mr. Phillips was from home—in going home I fell in with a young woman, who took me home, and picked my pockets of all the money, and 5 s . more which I had in my pocket—I met a policeman, who told me to go to the station. WILLIAM HOARE re-examined.  He came to me on the morning of 2nd Dec., and said he had been robbed by a female who he went home with, that she got up in the night, and left him in bed. SUSANNAH BENNETT re-examined.  He could not have had the butter that night—if he had paid the money, I should have received it—I do not know whether he was aware of that. NOT GUILTY .
