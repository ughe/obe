1597. JOSEPH PATTERSON was again indicted for the like offence.
   ROBERT GOVAN. The prisoner was in my employ, and authorised to receive money - he never accounted to me for five sovereigns received of Miss Rivet, for Mr. Dallimore, of the Globe, in Fleet-street - I was present when the officer searched him on the 28th of July, and found five sovereigns in his coat, the officer asked him where he got the money, and he confessed, it was part of Mr. Dallimore's bill; he ought to have accounted to me every day, at five o'clock, for what money he received.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you make this charge before the Magistrates? A. No, because the person could not appear - I had found it out that day, but the Magistrates committed him for the 3 s. 11 d. - I stated this case, but the Magistrate said he could not hear it in the absence of the witness. I told the Magistrate we had found part of Mr. Dallimore's bill, but the whole of it was 6 l. 12 s. - the prisoner did not say he had lost part of this money - he said, he had lost a part of a bill of 13 l. 5 s., which he had received about two months before - he stated he had lost ten guineas, and paid me 4 s. a week.
   JANE RIVET. I am in the employ of Mr. Dallimore. On the 27th of July, I paid the prisoner, on account of Mr. Govan, 6 l. 12 s. - I gave him six sovereigns and a guinea, he went out and got change, he signed the book and the bills.
   WILLIAM PAYNE. I went to Mr. Govan's on the 28th of July; I searched the prisoner, and found five sovereigns in the button-hole of his jacket - he began to cry and said, it was part of Mr. Dallimore's bill - there was no guinea found on him.
 ROBERT GOVAN re-examined. Q. Had you asked the prisoner on the 27th of July, if he had received Mr. Dallimore's bill? A. Yes, and he said No, he paid me 6 s. 8 d. that day, which was all - I went on the 28th to inquire and found it had been paid.
 JANE RIVET. I am certain I paid him on the 27th.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1598.  JOHN SCHMIDT was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of August , a watch, value 30 s.; a seal, value 8 s.; a key, value 4 s., and a ribbon, value 1 d., the goods of John Clark , from his person.
 JOHN CLARK. I am the  mate of a vessel called The Times, which lay opposite the Custom-house, in the same tier with the Sarah and Mary - it is called the Yarmouth tier. When I went to bed on Saturday night, the 27th of July, I put my watch, seal, and key by the side of my bed. I awoke about six o'clock next morning, they were gone.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   DANIEL BLIGH. I am a Thames Police Surveyor. I was on duty about two o'clock in the morning of the 28th of August, and heard a call for the police from the Sarah and Mary. I went on board, and found the prisoner in custody of the crew. I took him, but only found a small piece of money on him - he had no hat on; I asked him where it was, one of the ship's company took up a hat from the deck, and said "Here is a hat;" the prisoner at first said it was his; but I looked into it, and found this watch - then he said it was not his. I put it on his head, and said "It is your's no doubt." I took him in the police-boat, and asked where he got the hat; he said he bought it on shore for 1 s. 6 d. I then said "Where did you get the watch?" he said I should hear about that next morning. I then called to the people on board the vessels in that tier, and said, if any person had lost a watch, they might apply to the office - the Times was one of the vessels. The prisoner then said, he had taken it from one of the vessels - he had only come out of prison on the Thursday, and he must go there again. I asked him how he found it in the dark; he said he heard the b - y thing tick, and took it through distress.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was three months in the House of Correction. I applied to the Prussian Consul, and he said "You have been in confinement, and shall go there again;" So I was obliged to go on board the ship, and take this watch to support me.
     GUILTY. Aged 24.
 Judgment Respited.
 1599.  HENRY MACK and JOHN BAPTIST were indicted for stealing, on the 5th of August , eight gold seals, value 5 l., the goods of Thomas Lewis , from his person.
 THOMAS LEWIS. I am a  jeweller. On the morning of the 5th of August, I lost these seals - they had no stones in them, and these in Court have.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1600.  ISAAC MURRAY was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , two hats, value 2 l. 2 s., the goods of John Okey , his master, in his dwelling-house.
 JOHN OAKEY. I live in  London-wall and am a hatter. The prisoner was in my employ on the 20th of August. These hats are my property - I know them by the stamp inside, and the mark on the paper.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Did you miss them yourself? A. No, I did not.
 COURT. Q. After these hats were found I did you look to see if any were missing? A. Yes, and I missed one which was made for a particular customer; I did not see it after it was finished.
   WILLIAM SHARP. I am an apprentice to Mr. Reeve, the pawnbroker. On the 22d of August, between seven and eight o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came to our shop, and offered these two hats, which I have had in my care ever since. I saw the name of Oakey and Co. inside and asked how he got them; he said his father worked for Mr. Oakey, and desired him to pawn them - I kept the hats and sent him to fetch his father - but no person came.
   JOHN ELLIS. I was sent for to take the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.
