799. RICHARD STRATTON and SARAH BURGESS were indicted
for a misdemeanor.
MARY ANN DUCKWORTH. I am the daughter of John Duckworth. On the
20th of February the male prisoner came into the shop for a black pudding
he offered me a shilling-I said I did not like it-I showed it to me
father, and then returned it to Stratton-he went out, and I saw him walk
ing with Burgess and a young man the next time I saw him was in
custody.
GEORGE WALLIS. I am the son of James Wallis, a gardener, at Totten
ham. On a Friday I saw the two prisoners just by my father's house
Stratton then came in for two pennyworth of nuts, and offered me a shilling
I sent my sister to get change-it turned out to be bad-Stratton the
took it and went out.
John FOWLER. I am a constable. I met the prisoners in Tottenham,
just before they got to Wallis's—I watched them, and saw Stratton
into Wallis's-he afterwards came out and joined Burgess and Nicholls-
they went on to Edmonton I followed them with Foster, and we took
them all—I found nothing on Nicholls but a good sixpence and some
copper.
JOSEPH Foster. I took the two prisoners, and searched them both-
found in Burgess's left-hand six counterfeit shillings, and in Stratton'
right-hand jacket pocket, one counterfeit shilling he said it was the first
time he had ever done any thing of the sort.
JOHN FIELD. The whole of these are counterfeit.
STRATTON-GUILTY. Aged 17.-Confined Six Months.
BURGESS-GUILTY. Aged 16.-Confined Three Months.
