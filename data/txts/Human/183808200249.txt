JOHN RYAN , DANIEL DALEY, and JOHN JUDEN, were indicted for stealing on the   19th of July , 4 gowns, value 3 l  .; 10 yards of merino value 10 s  .; I shawl, value 4 s  .; and 1 petticoat, value 1 s  .; the goods of Jane Rands ; and CAROLINE JUDEN was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the statute, &c.—3rd COUNT  , for feloniously receiving the same of a certain evil-disposed person. JANE RANDS. I live in Grafton-place, Kentish-town, with Mr. Keghill, and am single—this is my property—I missed it on the 19th of July, between one and two o'clock in the day, from the middle parlour of the house—I had left the window open—it looks into a mews—I saw them all safe at one o'clock—I saw three persons sitting down when I was there, I could not identify them—I saw my property at Union Hall—I know this to be mine. RICHARD DEYKIN. I am a constable of Kentish-town. Between eleven and said twelve o'clock on the 19th of July, these three lads came into the town—I followed them a little way, but a gentleman stopped me, which caused me to lose them—they came in by the Castle, at Kentish-town—they were all in company together. EDMUND NORTH. I am an assistant to a pawnbroker, and live at No. Stone's-end, Borough. I produce one gown, which was pawned by the female prisoner on the 19th of July, I think in the afternoon, I am not quit certain, in the name of Ann Jones. WILLIAM REEVE. I am a pawnbroker, in Blackman-street, Borough. I produce one silk gown pawned by the female prisoner in the name of Mary Butler. JOHN CRESWELL. I am a pawnbroker, in Blackman-street, Borough. I produce a gown and shawl pawned by the female prisoner on the 19th and 21st of July—one in the name of Ann Juden, and one in the name of Ann Jones-when she pledged the gown she said it did not belong to her. JAMES BROOKS( police-constable L  118.) On the 29th of July, I appre hended the three boys for a robbery at Vauxhall, and from further information I took the female prisoner into custody on the 31st—I told her to take off the petticoat which she was wearing at the station-house, which she did—I asked her who sent her to pledge the four gowns—he said her brother and others—I found a duplicate of a shawl and this piece of merino at her two other's mother's lodgings—she said she got them from her brother—he was not present. CHARLES BURGESS GOFF( police-constable L  63.) The female prisoner had this bonnet on at Union Hall—she said her brother gave her the merino to make the bonnet, for pawning the four gowns.
   John Juden's Defence . I went home and saw my sister with a young lad—she said he had brought the things there for her to pledge.
