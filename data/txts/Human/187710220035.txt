GEORGE BLYTH. I am a ticket-collector at Gloucester Road, in the service of the Metropolitan District Railway Company, and was on duty on the 17th July last, when my attention was called to Tilke, who entered the station by the usual exit and passed direct to the platform without showing his ticket to a collector—I followed him on to the Hammersmith platform, and asked to see his ticket, which he showed me—it was a return half from Sloane Square to Earl's Court already cancelled—it should be cancelled at Sloane Square, that is snipping it—the wrong half might be cut by mistake—I cannot say what it would convey to my mind—I ex amined his ticket and gave it to him back—when I went back I made a com munication to Osborne—Finch had drawn my attention to him—on the 30th I again saw Tilke at Gloucester Road, when he arrived by the 11.38 train with the first half of a return ticket from Hammersmith to Gloucester Road—I did not see the return half of the. ticket on that day—on the 31st July he arrived by the 1.1 North-Western train, and gave me a ticket from Hammersmith to Gloucester Road, first half—I did not see him use the return half—on the 30th August something similar happened—I have seen him frequently at Gloucester Road, and have had my attention called to irregularities connected with him.
   Cross-examined.  I have seen him at the station in his butcher's clothes—the clipper may in a hurry clip the wrong  half of a ticket—it does not happen very often. JAMES HICKS OSBORNE. I am inquiry inspector in the service of the Metropolitan District Railway Company—on the 17th July from informa tion that came to me I watched Tilke, who is employed by Mr. Scarlett, a butcher, of the Earl's Court Road, from his master's shop about 12.5—he was carrying some meat in a butcher's tray—when he arrived at Earl's Court Brooks was the collector on duty—Tilke went up to him and put his tray down on the desk alongside of him, and entered into conversation with him—I could not hear what was said, but I was sufficiently near to see Brooks and to be seen by him—I am known to Brooks—I saw him look towards me, and make signs with his eyes to Tilke, and saw his lips moving, though I could not hear what was said—Tilke became confused directly, and rushed from Brooks towards where I was standing, passed me, and went to the urinal—I could see a small paper parcel in one of his hands as he passed me—he first went inside the door of the urinal, but was not there long enough for any necessary purpose, and returned again immediately, again going past me, still carrying the parcel—the tray had in the meantime remained on the table—he again went to Brooks and passed him, and went outside the station out of my sight for Half a minute, or perhaps a minute, and returned again—I could not see the parcel then—he had had some private conversation with Brooks—from there he went to
