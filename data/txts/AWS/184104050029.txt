1017. WILLIAM SPARKES was indicted for stealing, on the 21st
of March, 1 half-sovereign, 10 shillings, and 3 sixpences, the monies of
Joseph Hawkrigg.
JOsEPH HAWKRIGG. I keep a coffee-shop in Barbican. The prisoner
came to my house on Saturday night, the 20th of March, and asked for a bed
for the night-he went to bed about half-past eleven o'clock- had eleven
or twelve shillings and a half-sovereign in my trowsers' pocket when I went
to bed-my room door was not locked-] got up at a quarter before six
next morning, and missed my money from my pocket--the prisoner being
the only strange person in the house, I went to his room-I found the door
open, the key taken out, and put on a chest of drawers-one of the drawers
was taken out and placed a few yards off, where it had no business to be
Lwhen he came down at half-past six, I told him what had happened, and
said he could have no objection to be searched-he said, 48 No"I asked
what money he had in his pocket-he said 118. 6d.-I asked if that was
all-he said, $6 Yes"-g policeman was sent forI told him what money
I had lost, and went up to the room with him and the prisonerhe was
searched, and 118. 6d. produced-he was asked if that was all the money
he had about him-he said, 46 Yes"-, not being satisfied, requested him
to pull off his shoes, which he did-in pulling off his left shoe I perceived
a bit of cotton rag tied round his leg-I asked what it was-he did not
speak at first-I spoke rather sharp, took hold of him, and endeavoured to
pull his stoeking off-he endeavoured to prevent me, by putting his foot
on the floor, and said he could do it himself-I said I would do it for him
-I pulled his stocking off, and found a hard substance in it-I gave it to
the policeman-he put his hand in and found a half-sovereign-on my
return from the station I missed a pair of shoes from a closet in the room
where the prisoner slept, and which I had seen safe the night before-I
found them under the seat where he had sat while I sent for the policeman,
with a small bundle of patent leather, and four stiffeners for stocks, which
had been on a shelf on the landing, close to his room door the night before
-the piece of cotton which was on the prisoner's leg had been taken from
the closet where the shoes -there was nothing the matter with his
leg.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Have you been an officer A. I
have-I should not like to swear to the piece of cotton, though I know it
is my property-the prisoner said he had ten shillings and three sixpences
about him, and I found it was so-the silver I lost was all shillings except
two sixpences, or there might have been three.
