I was with Steele and Richardson all the morning and did not hear
any conversation between them which showed guilty intention on
Steele's part. I have always known Richardson as being straight-
forward and honourable. I know him as a good son and he keeps
his mother. I have never been connected with any affair of this
sort before.
Cross-examined by Mr. MacMahon. On September 25 I met
Richardson at Atlantic Road, Brixton, having just left & customer.
We had not completed our business; we had to call back later be-
tween five and six o'clock. When Steele asked me to go into 137
Cheapside, he showed me no samples. I was not in a fit condition
to ask properly for what was wanted, so Richardson went in. Steele
said, “Mention Felgate's; say they are for Felgate's." He did not
mention where they carried on business. Richardson walked so
hurriedly towards the General Post Office that we could not catch
him up before he had almost got to Sweetings. I did not return his
bat because he did not ask for it.
Verdict, Guilty, with a recommendation to mercy.
It was stated that Steele had been out of regular employment for
some time and had tried to earn a living by selling goods on commis-
sion, and that he would have returned the money in this case had
he been able to sell the goods; also that he had been offered employ.
ment on his release.
Steele was released on the recognisances of his mother in the sum
of £30 and his own in £10; Richardson on his own recognisances in
the sum of £10, to come up for judgment if called upon.
