JANE SIMESTER. I am servant to the prosecutor. On the morning of 10th
Feb, I got up about six, or a quarter-past-it was not later--the two inner
shop-doors were broken open, and stuck wide open-I had stood in the
passage the night before, while my master locked them--the window was
pulled down at top and bottom-I gave my master information.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. Is there any room built out over the
parlour A. No-my master always sees to the fastenings--we went up
stairs together-the street-door was on the jar in the morning.
WILLIAM JARVIS (City policeman, 614.) On 10th Feb., about half-past
nine o'clock in the morning, I was in Bishopsgate-street-without, and saw a
cob stop between Artillery-lane and Union-street, and the two prisoners got
out-I observed the bandle of a silver spoon sticking out of Smith's pocket,
behind-I took hold of them, and told them they must go to the station with
me-Clark turned round, puehed his hand out of mine, and said, “Oh, wait
a bit," and ran away-I had plenty of opportunity of seeing him-I stood
opposite the cab-door, and saw him get out-at first I thought he was ill from
the way in which he got ont-I took Smith to the station, and found on him
sixteen silver spoons, forks, a musical-box, and other articles-he said I
ought to bave had the other man, but as I had got him, he supposed he most
pay for all-last Wednesday I again saw Clark at the Nag's Head, detained by
constable of the E division, and recognised him-I did not know him previ-

knowing him.
