 1764.  HENRY NORRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 waistcoat, value 10s.; 4 sovereigns, and 1 half-sovereign, the property of James Picknell.
   JAMES PICKNELL. I lodge at Mr. Featherstone's, in St. George's in the East; the prisoner slept in the same bed for eight days - he was there before me. On the morning of the 8th of September I employed him to take my chest down to the Hull packet - it was locked, and I had the key of it; I took a bag, and another young man took a hammock - I opened my chest to get the money to pay the prisoner, and this money and the waistcoat were gone; the prisoner seemed quite unconcerned, and pressed me to go back and see if it was there - I went home and told the landlord - he seemed quite surprised; when I got back to the packet, the prisoner was gone - I did not see him again till eight o'clock in the evening, in Jewin-street, at the sign of the Woolpack; he had the waistcoat on, and 19s. 2d. in his hat - he said he had bought the waistcoat, with the other things he had on; I am certain it is mine - it has two gores under the arms to make it larger.
 Prisoner. Q. When had you these pieces put in? A. The beginning of this summer - I had not worn it above three times; they were put in at Sunderland.
   JOHN COVERLY. I am landlord of the Woolpack. The prisoner came to my house on the 8th of September, about nine o'clock in the morning - he gave me a bundle to take care of; he came again in the evening - I gave the bundle to Repton.
   ANTHONY REPTON. I am an officer. I have the bundle, which Coverly gave me, and the waistcoat which the prisoner had on; the prosecutor described the pieces in the waistcoat before I took it off - these other things, which were in the bundle, appear to be new.
   JAMES PICKNELL. I went to the docks about seven o'clock - my chest was locked, and the key was in my hat by the side of the bed; I had seen the money safe the night before - the prisoner saw me put my clothes away, and take the money out.
 Prisoner. There were eight other men in the room Witness. No, there were three or four.
 Prisoner's Defence. Another young man slept in the same bed with us - there were three beds in the room, and three slept in each; in the morning he asked me to lend him a hand - we went down to the Dock, and he missed his money; I told him he had better go back and see if he had left it - I then went to a ship going to the Swan River, but it was not going off then; I went to my father, and then to Jewin-street, where I was taken in the evening.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 1765.  GEORGE SAMUEL BALL was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of February , certain fixtures, (i.e.) 2 pairs of window sashes, value 30s., and 2 locks, value 3s., the goods of Anthony Pratt , and fixed to a certain building; against the Statute, &c.
   EMANUEL PLAYER. I am a house agent for Mr. Anthony Pratt, who has a house in Raven's-buildings, Whitechapel. On the 6th of February there were two sashes taken from the front parlour, and two from the back - I had seen them safe the evening before, and missed them at nine o'clock in the morning; I had opened the house the evening before, and put the other keys in the front parlour, and took the parlour and street door keys away - when I went in the morning the parlour door was open, and the sash frames gone; the officer showed me the frames the next day - I compared them with the window places; they fitted exactly, and I have no doubt they belonged there - two locks, which had been taken off, were fitted to their places.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Were not the sashes burnt? A. Yes, a little; but the tops and bottoms fitted exactly - there was sufficient left to fit in; it was not a common size - they had but six squares, and there were eight squares in the sashes of the other houses in that street - all frames, having the same number of squares, are not of the same size; I had seen them hundreds of times - a man named Bailey was transported for this very offence; I distinctly swear that I saw the frames the same day as I missed them.
   THOMAS GREEALY. One morning in February last, about seven o'clock, I saw a man opposite my door, in Parsey's-gardens, which is less than a quarter of a mile from where they were taken - he had two sashes, which laid up against the wall; I do not know who he was - in about a second I saw the prisoner going into a house in North-street with two sashes; I went on to my place of work, and saw the man I had first seen go into the same place as the prisoner did - it was the day the other man was taken up.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen the prisoner before? A. Yes, and knew him - he was just at the door, but I saw him quite sufficiently to know him.
