SAMUEL, Mayor.
I have known Hyman and Philip Bernstein sixteen or seventeen years-
Philip has been resident in London most of that time-Hyman was away
for about eleven years in Australia-he returned to this country about
1900, when I used to see him frequently-I saw a man named Mayer
in Hyman's company-in June, 1901, I was living with my father at
25, Broad Lane, Tottenham-I was friendly with the Bernsteins-
eventually I met a man called Johann Schmidt-Philip Bernstein had a
jeweller's shop at 120, High Street, Whitechapel-it had not long been
opened- always thought it belonged to both the brothers-i was
conducted as Bernstein and Co.-it was doing a bad business-we got
talking about various things, and Philip Bernstein asked me if I could
not look up that forger"-he did not know his name-I said I would try
and look him up, which I did-I saw Hyman at this time, but I would not
swear whether he was present at that particular interview-he was no
doubt present at others about this time, when the matter of the employ:
ment of Schmidt was discussed, and as to what should be done if I could
find him, and if anything could be forged-that is all Schmidt could be
employed for-it was not diseussed what he should forge-I saw my
father about it, and had some conversation with him, in the course of which
Sehmidt's name was mentioned-I was not present at any meeting between
my father and Schmidt-I met Schmidt and had a conversation with him
-I then went and 8aw the Bernsteins-I saw them both, and told them
I had looked up Sehmidt and had had a conversation with him, and
that he had suggested to me that Bank of England notes would be the
cheapest and easiest things to make-I said that Schmidt had mentioned
f50 as the probable cost of making them-we discussed the matter,
and they wanted to see a sample-I saw Sehmidt again, and told him I
should want a sample made-I gave him instructions to make a sample
watermark of a fiver-Schmidt took that work in hand at our house,
35, Broad Lane-he had a genuine f5 note to work from-Philip Bernstein
give me that-it took Schmidt about a week to make the sample, and
when it was finished I went to the Bernsteins, taking two samples of
the watermarks of E5 notes-they both examined them and found
something wrong with them-I do not remember what it was-it was
some slight fault in the watermark-they did not consider the fault was
so grave as to make the notes useless-it was easily repairable, and they
were put on the fire in their room-I think it was next day that Hyman
Bernstein came to me and brought four genuine E5 notes and pointed out
what was wrong and what they wanted altered in the forgeries-I showed
them to Sehmidt and had a conversation with him-I do not think Sohmidt
worked on them-I think I handed them back to Bernstein-Schmid had
got his first genuine note all this time, but they brought me the four notes
so as to show me that the sample was different to each of them-Schmidt
had a plate of the samples he had produced-the Bernsteins objected to
the work being carried on from the original f5 note, and I gave it back
to them-no further samples were made then, but Schmidt was to give an
estimate how much it wouid cost exactly to make so many pieces of E5
and f10 notes-I do not know how many pieces, but I know it was 212,000
