GEORGE HAWKES. I am a letter-carrier in the post-office. On the evening
of the 17th of April, I paid Cross 191. in three five pound notes and four
sovereigns- there were two Bank of England notes and one country note
- this Winburn note, No. 4030, is the country one-I received it from Mrs."
Willis at the King's Head, Newgate-street-I afterwards went to Mr. Cross to
ask to see the note-I wrote my name on it-it has on it the name of “Samuel
Day Whilmore"—that was on it when I first had it I know the prisoner's
writing, and believe it to be his-I have frequently seen him write.
Cross-examined. Q. Is that the only one you have of these notes? A.
The only country note-I am in the sporting line, on a very small scale, if
any-I bave had a bet of a shilling or so-my betting has not been limited
to a shilling-perhaps two shillings or half-a-crown-I have never gone
higher than that I have lost between twenty and thirty shillings to William
Cross, brother of the witness--I was at the post-office, on my usual duty, on
the morning of the 3rd of April-I was present at the time the post came in
from Hampshire-I am regularly on duty.
Court. Q. What walk is yours? A. Milé-end-191. was paid for a 201.
prize that was won by Bob Cross-I was commissioned by Cross to call for
it for him-I have received it of Mrs. Willis.
MR. BALLANTINE. Q. I believe you have been intimate with the prisoner?
A. I have been intimate with him up to the time of his being in custody
I was a good deal about with him-we never had any money transactions
together-I may have advanced bim half-a-crown-I am still in the em-
ploy of the post-office-I cannot say whether he has or has not lent me
money-I have not bad bets with him over and over again I think I recollect
having a bet of five shillings with him once--my wages are 11. 58. a-week.
Court. Q. You had nothing to do with the sweep; you went to get 191,
for Cross ? A. Nothing whatever.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. How long have you been in the post-office ? A.
seven years; I am on the Mile-end walk-on my solemn oath, no letter ad-
dressed to Rogers, Olding, and Co., ever came into my possession.
THOMAS LAURIE. I am one of the partners in the firm of Rogers,
Laurie, and Co., formerly Rogers Nephews--we have a pigeon-hole in the
post-office, in which our letters are deposited-we send for them-no letter
containing this remittance, addressed to Rogers, Olding, and Co., came
into our hands on the 3rd of April.
COURT. You have had occasional mistakes with Rogers, Olding, and
Co.? 4. Frequently-their letters have come with our letters, when we
sent for them, and we immediately send them to Rogers, Olding, and Co.,
and they have sent us letters in like manner.

- 1 paid it to Hawkes the same day-these tickets produced are for sweep-
