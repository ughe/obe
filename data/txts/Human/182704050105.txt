 SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, APRIL 11.
 Second London Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1004.  WILLIAM JEFFERIES was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of March , 30lbs. weight of waste paper, value 5s., the goods of John Thompson.
 2d COUNT, stating it to be the goods of the said John Thompson, one of the executors of  Joseph Birket Jackson, deceased.
 3d COUNT, stating it to be the goods of  Charles Cooper and the said John Thompson, executors of the said Joseph Birket Jackson, deceased.
 JOHN THOMPSON. I am a  warehouseman, and live at No. 94, Watling-street. On the 5th of March some waste paper was in my possession; a great many volumes of waste books were taken away; they were in my custody, as executor. I saw them at Guildhall, and swore to them.
   CHARLES HERBSFIELD. On the 15th of March, between nine and ten o'clock, I met the prisoner with a bag of books; I asked him what he had got; he said, a little waste paper; he said it was given to him by a person in Old'Change, but he afterwards said Watling-street, and he took me to the place; I produce seventeen account books, which I took from the prisoner.
   GEORGE HUTCHINS. I was porter to the late Mr. Jackson, who lived at No. 94, Watling-street - Mr. Thompson has the care of his property since his death. On the morning in question I gave the prisoner a lighted candle to go into the cellar to take the dust - I went into a back cellar to clean boots - the books laid in the corner near where the dust was - I missed them afterwards; I am sure I did not tell him to take them; I did not see him take them away.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did you not say any thing to him about the books? A. No; the cellar is a dark place; there were boxes and other things there - I cannot say how many books there were; he is the regular dustman, I suppose; I did not watch him, because I had no idea he would take the books; I did not miss the books for an hour.
 Prisoner's Defence. The books were covered among the dust.
     GUILTY. Aged 48. Confined Six Months.
 1005.  ROBERT EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 1 pewter pint pot, value 1s., the goods of George Bird.
 GEORGE BIRD. I am a  publican, and keep the Three Colts public-house, London-wall. The pot produced is mine - it was taken from the tap-room by the prisoner;
   JAMES THORN. I am an officer. I took this pot from a person in Whitecross-street on the 17th of February - I took the prisoner also, and took him to the Compter; he gave no account of how he came by it.
   JAMES HEWITT. On the 17th of February I was in the tap-room of the Three Colts, and saw the prisoner there - he put this pot in the breast of his coat, and went away - I told the landlord, and we followed him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in great distress at the time, and had been relieved by the Refuge for the Houseless Poor.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined One Month.
 1006.  CHARLES BROADBIN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of February , 1 great coat, value 5s., the goods of George Chives.
 GEORGE CHIVES. I am a  carman. On the 27th of March I was driving my waggon in Smithfield- I had a coat in it, and missed it about nine o'clock - it was produced to me afterwards by an officer - it is here.
   GEORGE HASLEWOOD WORRALL. I was sent for from the Compter to take charge of the prisoner and this coat, about a quarter-past nine o'clock; a person came afterwards, who said he saw him take it out of the waggon - he is not here - the prisoner was present, and said he did it through distress - when he got to Guildhall, he said he was in a place and received 18s. a week.
 Prisoner's Defence. The coat was lying on the ground in the mud, and I picked it up.
 G. CHIVES re-examined. It could not have fallen out, but it might have been pulled out.
     NOT GUILTY.
