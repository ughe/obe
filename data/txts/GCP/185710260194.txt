MARY MOORE. I am cook at the Coach and Horses. I saw Seers served
with half a pint of gin, on 22nd Sept.-he paid with a sovereign--the ber-
maid hesitated and said, "I do not know that I can give you change"_be
said, “You must find change, or how am I to pay you for the gin ?"_I SAW
change given out of a piece of newspaper—the principal part of it was in
half crowns.
Seers. Q. Where were you? A. Close by the parlour door-I was not
coming up out of the kitchen.
JANE HUNTLEY, I am the wife of Reuben Huntley, who keeps the
Coach and Horses, at Kew. On 22nd Sept., between 9 and 10 o'clock, a boy
brought what appeared to be a sovereign, from Warden and Fletcher, the
butchers, nearly opposite, for change—I did not lift it off the counter, but
said that it was bad, and the boy took it back-about a quarter of an hour
afterwards I went to a drawer in my bureau, and found a counterfeit
sovereign-in consequence of what my niece, Miss Edwards, said, I gave
my husband the sovereign I found in the box, and went with him to Mr.
Ripley's building to look for Seers, whom I knew by sight-I afterwards saw
him on an omnibus in the afternoon-my husband was with him, and my
niece was called out to identify him I have seen Welch at the house,
generally with others—the prisoners have had breakfast at our house, I
cannot say when.
REUBEN JAMES HUNTLEY. I am the landlord of the Coach and Horses
I went with my barmaid to Ridley's building after Seers, but did not find
him, and then went to the Waggon and Horses, at Kew, which is just across
the bridge, and saw Hawkins, the ostler-in consequence of what I said to
him, he got on horseback, and went towards London-I was at Kew between
2 and 3 o'clock, and Seers was brought there on an omnibus by an officer-I
called my niece out, and she recognised him-I then got on the omnibus, and
went to the police station, at Richmond-I recognised Seers as a man to whom
I had given a pint of beer the day before, without paying for it, for taking care
of some things—I said, “You are the man to whom I gave a pint of beer
yesterday"-he said, "I am"-I said, "You gave me a bad sovereign; you did
not ought to have served me in that way"-be made no answer for some time,
and then said that it was not a sovereign he gave, it was half a crown-I asked
him how he came to leave his work-he said, "A man will do anything
when he is tight, or three sheets "—he had been drinking--my wife gave me
this counterfeit sovereign-I marked it with a penknife, with a cross on the
head side, and gave it to the officer.
Seers. Q. Did not you ask another man, who was at work on the build-
ing, whether he had not been to your house after gin? A. No-I did not
ask Welch, but I saw him on the scaffold, turning his back to me.
CHARLES HAWKINS. I am ostler at the Waggon and Horses, Kew
Bridge, the prisoners lodged there for eight nights before 22nd Sept.--they
occupied a small room to themselves- I remember Mr. Huntley coming over
the bridge to me, at a little before 10 o'clock that morning-I had seen Seers
about half past nine o'clock-he was then in liquor, and told me that he was
going to the hospital to see his father, who was ill-Í told Mr. Huntley where
I had seen Seers go, and I got on horseback, and overtook him on an omnibus,
going towards London-he said, “Halloo, Charley, what are you after
I said, "Nothing particular"-I stopped the omnibus, called a policeman,
and gave him into custody- I did not then know what the charge was.
