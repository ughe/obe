 Prisoner's defence. Mr. Penton sent for me about five o'clock, and gave me two-pence for a basket of sand; at the same time, he knew I never paid more than three-halfpence for it; I fetched it, and pitched it in the yard; I went to look for him to give him the odd halfpenny; and, as I was coming back, I went into the necessary, and kicked my foot against this brass, and I picked it up out of the dust; I never saw the brass before in my life; I put it under my apron, and the last evidence stopped me with it; he took one pig from me, and I gave him the other.
 The prisoner called three witnesses, who gave him a good character.

 Tried by the London Jury, before Mr. Baron THOMPSON. [Transportation. See summary.]
 152.  CHARLES PILKINGTON was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 15th of January , two pounds, and three quarters, of black pepper, value 4s. the property of Abraham Newman, and William Thwaites.
   WILSON JACKSON sworn.
 I am warehouseman to Messrs. Newman and Thwaites,  grocers; I was informed, by William Rose, that the prisoner had stole some pepper; I went after him to the George Inn, in the Borough, and asked him what he had got in his apron? and he said, a little sugar; I told him I knew it was a falsity, he had got pepper; and then he owned it was pepper; we came along to Fish-street-hill, and then I took from him two pounds three quarters of pepper; and then I took him home to his master's house, and gave him in charge to the constable.
 Prisoner. He says he took the pepper from me; I gave it to him, and told him I had bought it.
 Jackson. He first said it was given him, and then he said he gave sixpence for it.
 Prisoner. I said three shillings and sixpence.
   WILLIAM ROSE sworn.
 I am shopman to Messrs.  Abraham Newman and William Thwaites; all that I know of it is, that the prisoner had that pepper, and that it is Mr. Thwaites's property; I had it in my hand before it went out in the bag that it is in now; the constable has got it.
 THOMAS PERRY sworn.
 I am a constable; last Friday I was sent for to take charge of the prisoner; I asked him where he got it from; he said, he bought it at a porter's pitching-block, near London-bridge; that he gave sixpence for it. Mr. Rose gave it to me, and charged the prisoner with stealing it out of the cellar; it has been in my custody ever since, (produces it).
 Rose. This is the prosecutor's property; there is a hole at one corner of the bag, and it is tore.
 Jury. Q. Had it that hole before it went out? - A. Yes, it had.
 Jury. Q. Can you swear to the pepper? - A. Yes.
 Q. By what means? - A. I compared it with what we have at home, and I am equailly certain to the pepper as to the bag; I had it in my hand in the bag for upwards of half an hour.
 Jury. (To Perry.) Q. Did you write upon it? - A. No.
 Prisoner. Wilson put that mark upon it after he took it from me.
 Jury. (To Rose.) Q. How long did you see that bag before it was taken from the prisoner? - A. It might be half an hour before, in the cellar; there was a hole at the bottom of the bag, and I was afraid the pepper would be lost; and I turned the bag underneath, that it might not be lost.
   WILLIAM THWAITES sworn.
 Q. Look at that pepper; can you identify it? - A. I cannot; it was a confirmation of it, that when it was compared with my other pepper, it was like it; there are different qualities of pepper and different colours.
 Q. Can you say any thing to the bag? - A. No; I know nothing of it; the prisoner had been with me about three weeks.
 Prisoner's defence. My Lord, the cellar that this Mr. Rose says he saw that bag in, lies at the back of the shop, and there is ten or eleven men go backwards and forwards into it; Mr. Rose ordered me to take a hamper to the George Inn, in the Borough; I pitched it at the bulk at London-bridge, and a man came up with that bag of pepper, and asked me if I would buy it; I told him I did not want pepper; he asked me 3s. 9d.; I told him I had not so much; I would give him 3s. 6d. which I did, and I lapped it up in my apron; then I went on the George Inn with my hamper, and as I was coming away, I met Wilson; he said I had got sugar in my apron; No, Sir, says I, I have not, it is pepper; he said he met our Frank upon the Hill, and he was sure he gave it me; we went on, and, upon Fish-street Hill, he said, I must give him the pepper; I said, if he would give me what I gave for it, he should have it; he said, I was his prisoner, and I gave him the pepper out of my apron; when I came back, Mr. Thwaites said, I will have every one of you to the Compter.
