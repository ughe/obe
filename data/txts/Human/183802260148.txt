   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Was Ridgway taken into custody? A.  He was, and I should say he is indicted for receiving this silk—I believe he stands, in the list to be tried for receiving silk—I went to Ridgway's place, and found a bale of silk, with a card on it, "Mr. James Ridgway, 3, Foster lane, Cheapside"—there were marks on it, as if it had been through a car rier's hands, and on the other side a direction to Macclesfield—nobody went to Macclesfield—an officer brought a bale of silk from Macclesfield—he is a Macclesfield officer—I do not know whether goods from Macclesfield come to Pickford's wharf. JOSEPH BRIDGETT. I am a silk dealer, in Aldermanbury. I recollect telling some silk to Mr. Fellowes—I sold about 1401bs. of organzine on the 12th of December, and another 140lbs. on the 10th of January, both from the same bale—I afterwards saw the silk in the black bag—it was the silk I had sold to Mr. Fellowes—I have no doubt of it whatever—I have some reasons for being positive—it was thrown at a mill I have at Derby—we have not thrown a bale of Italian for about three years like it, and have not since—it is thrown in a peculiar manner. EDWARD FELLOWES. The silk in the warehouse in Broad-street on the 15th of January all belonged to me—I have examined carefully the differ ent parcels produced from Gilbert's, and found at the prisoner's house—I have compared them with my own silk, and can say most decidedly that it is my silk, and what I lost from my house—I knew nothing of Ridgway previous to this discovery—I have seen the silk found at Ridgway's—it is perfectly different altogether to this—it is tram—it is silk which I claim. NOT GUILTY.
