FOURTH COURT .— Tuesday, November  23 rd , 1875.
   Before Robert Malcolm, Kerr , Esq.
 26. JAMES BROUGHTON  (19), PLEADED GUILTY to stealing 26 handker-chiefs and other goods of Samuel Lewis , his master— Judgment respited.
 27. SIDNEY MARSHALL  (40) [Pleaded guilty: see original trial image] , to unlawfully forging and uttering an order for the payment of 9 l  . 16 s  .; also an order for 9 l  . 5 s  ., having been before convicted of felony— Ten Years' Penal Servitude.
 28. FREDERICK MATTHEWS  (17) [Pleaded guilty: see original trial image] , to stealing a chest of tea of George Roper and another, having been before convicted of felony— Eighteen Months' Imprisonment.
 29. JOHN WHITTEN  (30) [Pleaded guilty: see original trial image] , to stealing 12 skirts, of the value of 2 l  . 7 s  ., of William R. Sutton , having been before convicted of felony— Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
 30. SARAH BARTLETT  (55) [Pleaded guilty: see original trial image] , to unlawfully keeping a dis orderly house— To enter into recognizances to appear. And
 31. WILLIAM CRAWFORD SMITH  (42) , to feloniously forging and uttering an order for four guineas with intent to defraud. Recommended to mercy by the Proacutor— [Pleaded guilty: see original trial image] Judgment respited.
 32.  In the case of RICHARD WHEELER  (40) , indicted for unlawfully inflicting grievous bodily harm upon William Holditch Stephens , the jury found him insane upon arraignment — Ordered to be Detained till Her Majesty's pleasure be Known .
 33. WILLIAM HOLLAND  (26) , stealing a purse containing 14 l  . and a receipt for goods of Hope Duff , from his person. GUILTY — Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
 34. WILLIAM KELLY  (18) and ALEXANDER LAWSON  (20) , stealing fourteen pieces of skirting, value of 12 l  . 12 s  . 11 d  ., of Theodore Crewdson and another.
   KELLY PLEADED GUILTY.
   MR. THORPE COLE conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD POTTERILL( City Policeman  116). On the 5th November, about half-past four, I was on duty in Wood Street with Westwood—I saw the two prisoners and two others not in custody, one of the latter wheeling a barrow he up-ended the barrow, and the two prisoners and another went into the pas sage of 39, rolled a sale of goods into the barrow, and Kelly wheeled it away into Silver Street, accompanied by Lawson and the others—I had known both the prisoners before—when they got into Silver Street an alarm was given; Kelly instantly dropped the barrow, and all of them ran away, Kelly running down Wood Street, where I apprehehded him—the other men escaped—I saw Samson after that on the 15th November about 1.30 in the daytime in St. Paul's Churchyard—I told him I was a police-constable, and I should take him in charge for being concerned in stealing a sale of goods from a doorway in Wood Street—he said "You have made a mistake this time; I was not there"—I took him to the station-house—he was asked his name and address, and he said "I have no fixed place of residence." GEORGE WESTWOOD( City Policeman  707). I was with Potterill—his evidence is perfectly correct. HENRY HOOPER. I am a warehouseman at Mr. Crewdson's—on the 5th November a sale of goods was produced to me containing fourteen pieces of skirting—it belonged to Theodore Crewdson, and was valued at something like 12 l . 12 s .—it was placed in the passage on the ground floor on the the morning in question; and it was missed about 4.45 in the afternoon.
