1370. MARY OWEN was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of September , one watch, value 1 l., and one key, value 6 d., the property of James Gillman.
   MARY LOWE. I am servant to Mr. James Gillman, who lives at Highgate; the prisoner came to the door, and asked if we wanted a cook? I told her Yes; she said she came from Kentish-town, and had lived two years and a half in a Scotch family there, who were going to Scotland, and that Mr. Stringer told her of the situation. She asked me for some water, I said if she came into the kitchen I would give her some table beer, which I did. She said she would come after the situation next day, as my mistress was at Ramsgate. I thought she looked ill, and left her for a minute or two to get her some wine. As I returned, she met me on the kitchen stairs, drank it, and ran up. I went into the kitchen, to see what o'clock it was, and missed the watch. I and York followed and secured her in a field. I saw the watch drop from her.
   EDWARD YORK. I am servant to the prosecutor. I secured the prisoner in a field - I told her a watch had been missed - she told me not to accuse her of it. I saw it drop from her in the field.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I have three children, and my husband is out of employ.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined One Month.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1371.  THOMAS PEELE was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , one counterpane, value 7 s.; one blanket, value 5 s., and one sheet, value 5 s., the property of Thomas Barrow , in a lodging-room.
   JANE BARROW. I am the wife of Thomas Barrow, who lives in Middleton-street, Spitalfields. On the 9th of September, the prisoner came and took a furnished bedroom at 3 s. per week. He slept there that night, and did not come again until the Tuesday following; he then said he had been into the country to see his father, who was dangerously ill - he went out about half-past six o'clock next morning. As soon as he was gone, I went up stairs, and missed these things, which I am certain were in the room when he went to bed the overnight. I have never found them.
   THOMAS BARROW. I heard the prisoner go out about six o'clock in the morning, and I am sure he is the man.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1372.  SAMUEL ROBINS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , one ounce of silk, value 3 s., the property of William Groves.
   WILLIAM GROVES. I am a haberdasher, and live in Chalton-street, Somers'-town. On the 7th of August, about eight o'clock in the morning, I was called down, and found my daughter had been serving the prisoner with silk - she had lost a quantity; the prisoner had just left. I went with the constable to his house; he denied it, and said we might search - he pretended to turn his pockets out, but did not produce the silk. I asked him to produce that which he had bought? He said he had lost it. He afterwards produced some, which he said he had bought. He is a tailor.
 Cross-examined by MR. WALFORD. Q. I believe he had no breeches-pocket - A. No, he had a fob.
   MARY FLEETWOOD. I went to the prosecutor's shop. While the prisoner was being served, I saw him take up a handful of silk, and put it into his pocket - he was served and went out. I also saw him open another bundle, and take some silk, which I thought he put into his pocket. I told a woman in the area of it - I am sure I saw him take it. It was raven-grey silk.
   ELIZA GROVES. I was serving the prisoner; he bought a skein of olive silk, and asked for some ravengrey. While I was serving him, Fleetwood made a motion to me, but I did not know what to do. I let him go as I knew him before - he always came to our shop before breakfast.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have dealt there five years. I paid for two skeins, and took nothing else. The prosecutor said I was seen to put some silk into my breeches-pocket, but I shewed him that I had no pockets,
     NOT GUILTY.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1373.  JAMES RYAN was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of July , one apron, value 6 d.; one handkerchief, value 6 d; one frock, value 6 d., and one shirt, value 6 d., the property of George Horne.
   GEORGE HORNE. I am a labourer. On the 11th of July, about half-past three o'clock in the afternoon, I was sitting in my chair with my handkerchief over my head. I heard a noise, and saw the prisoner putting these things into his hat, which he had taken off the table. He asked me for some water, and ran out; I followed and took them from him. He made a great resistance.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went into his house after a woman, whom I was told had gone in there. He pushed me out, and gave me in charge.
     GUILTY. Aged 47. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1374.  MARY SHENTON was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , three night-gowns, value 4 s. 6 d.; two aprons, value 1 s.; two petticoats, value 3 s.; two shifts, value 4 s.; two pair of stockings, value 2 s., and one trunk, value 2 s., the property of Philip Mackey.
   PHILIP MACKEY. On the 25th of July I lost my trunk, containing these things, from Dorset-mews,
   CHARLES RILEY. On the 26th of June, about half-past eleven o'clock at night, I was in the mews, and heard the prosecutor's window open. I met the prisoner in the yard, and found the trunk just by where she stood.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
