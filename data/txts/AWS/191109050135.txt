Before JUDGE RENTOUL.
(Friday, September 8.)
PAGE, Charles (34, labourer), stealing one loaf of bread, the goods
of Emma Seriven.
The prisoner pretended to be deaf and dumb.
a
EMMA SORIVEN, 15, Hill Road, Richmond, baker and confectioner.
e
On August 15 prisoner came into my shop. He put his hand over the
rail and took up a loaf of bread. He walked out of the shop. I
e
followed him and accused him of taking the loaf of bread, saying,
e
You have not paid for it." He made no answer. I called a police-
man and gave him in charge.
Sergeant JAMEs CAWLEY. I was called to last witness's shop. When
I got there she was holding prisoner with one hand and in the other
m
she had part of a loaf of bread which she said she had taken from
underneath his coat. I said to prisoner, 44 You hear what the lady
says1" He said, 44 Yes." I asked her if she wished to charge him.
She said, 41 Yes." I took him into custody. He made no reply
to the charge. When searched the other part of the loaf was found in
his pocket. When he was being placed in the cell he said, took
it because I was hungry. I have only had a piece of bread and an
s
apple the last two or three days. I am absolutely certain he spoke to
me.
Verdict, Guilty.
Numerous convictions were proved.
Dr. DYER, Medical Superintendent, Brixton Prison. This man has
been under my observation during the whole time he has been at
Brixton. He is perfectly well able to speak. I have had several con-
versations with him and I have had another conversation with him
tiis morning. There is nothing wrong with him.
Sentence, Six months' hard labour.
