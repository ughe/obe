Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1448. THOMAS HOY was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of April , 4 pairs of boots, value 1 l  . 2 s  ., the goods of William Freeman . WILLIAM FREEMAN. I am a shoemaker, and live in Greenwich-road. I was sitting in my back parlour on the 10th of April, and in consequence of something ray wife told me I ran out of doors—I saw the prisoner rather running—I pursued him, and took hold of his collar—I said, what have you been taking? and at that moment I saw a boot fall from his person—I took it up, and with the boot and the prisoner I proceeded towards the station-house—Mr. Judson my neighbour came up—I wished him to go and see if there were not some more boots—be said, "If you will go I will hold the boy"—I went back, and close to where the prisoner had been standing when I took him, I found seven more boots—they were all mine. JOHN JUDSON. I am a licensed victualler. T was standing at my door cleaning a bird-cage—I saw the prisoner running along—I ran after him with the prosecutor—I saw one boot drop from the prisoner—I took him to the station-house.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went up the gateway, and the gentleman came and said, "Have yon stolen any thing?" I said, "No"—he took me op the gateway and found the boot. GUILTY. * Aged 17.— Transported for Seven Years.

 1449. MARY RHODA JONES was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution. ELEANOR MART ELIZABETH LOCKER. I am the wife of Edward William Locker; he is one of the Commissioners of Greenwich Hospital. On the 31st of December the prisoner called at my house—she was quite a stran ger—she stated that her husband was transported, and she was very anxious to join him—that a ship was lying at Gravesend, and her children were on board, and she was very anxious to raise money to go there—she showed me this card, which she said had been given her by the owners of the ship, and it was to sail very shortly—seeing the card I believed her story, and gave her a crown and some trifling articles—she did not give me the card, bat laid it down and forgot it—I found it after she left.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Have you kept the card ever since? A.  No, I sent it to Mr. Gordon—if this is not the card she produced, it is so like it that I cannot tell the difference—I read it while she was there-rand after she left, and kept it till I sent it to Mr. Gordon—I gave the prisoner a crown-piece as an act of charity and humanity, supposing what she said was correct.
