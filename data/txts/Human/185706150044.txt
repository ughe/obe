   Cross-examined. Q.  You saw Scott again, you say, at 2 o'clock in the morning? A.  Yes—he was standing in Gifford Street alone at that time, about twenty yards from the house where I saw the prisoner—I had some conversation with him at that time—he asked me if I had seen the old vagabond that was talking to him—I said, "Yes"—he said, "He is one of my lodgers, and he owes me some rent"—he said he intended to wait till he came home, and wait there till the morning, and then put the brokers in for his rent—that was about three quarters of an hour before I heard the alarm of "Murder!"—the other time that I saw Scott and the prisoner together was about half past 11 or 12 o'clock; I did not notice the time exactly—I had been on that beat, but he had been home, I believe; I did not see him about there all the time—they were wrangling together about half past 12 o'clock—they did not appear to be excited, either of them, particularly—they were both talking together—I did not know Tripp before—he might have had a little to drink; he was not drunk—I did not see Mr. Scott after he was stabbed—Tripp did not speak to me at any time that evening about Scott obstructing or molesting him. GEORGE BECKLEY. ( Police sergeant, N  12).
