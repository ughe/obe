• John STEEL. I am book-keeper at the Queen's Head Inn in the Borough
-the prisoner is ostler there we have a stable called the Nag's stable-the
prisoner had the control of it-Wiggins called my attention to a sack there, on
Tuesday, the 8th of Sept.-it was standing by the corn-bin-a man named
Moore was there-I took a sample of corn from the sack and gave it to Goff.
COURT. Q. How was the prisoner employed ? A. By Mr. Walker, my
employer-my master has no horses-we call it the ostler's stable because he
has his corn-bin there, and gentlemen's horses come there-they pay the
ostler, and he is to account to me every night-he could not make a profit of
any corn in that stable without he sold it to other carriers in the stable-
would be of no use to him-be charges for baits- I saw Edwards and the
prisoner together in that stable, that day, three or four hours afterwards the
sack was still there.
Cross-examined. Q. It would be the prisoner's duty to be in that stable!
A. Yes, but not always about six horses could stand there--no one else
came in there to my knowledge.
JEREMIAH MOORE. I am a labourer, I was in the stable at the Queen's
Head on Tuesday, the 8th of Sept.--I had come from Epsom that morning, I
arrived about six o'clock, and put my horse in the prisoner's stable-I went
into the stable to go to sleep, I laid down on the corn-bin-I believe that is
the bin he serves his corn from I have not seen him go to it-after I had
been there some time, I saw the prisoner bring in a sack on his shoulder--he
put it down by the side of the corn-bin-he went out-Mr. Steel came in
afterwards, and went to the same sack as the prisoner brought in.
Cross-examined. Q. How long had you slept? 4. I do not know-
when the prisoner came in with the corn he awoke me-I remained awake
till Mr. Steel came in-I had had a hard day's work before-I had one
horse and a van--I brought up corn with me I carried it into the Queen's
Head stable, which is the ordinary course--the door of the stable was open,
anybody may come in and out-it is common for people to lie down there,
and go to sleep.
MR. CHARNOCK. Q. I suppose you do not take your master's corn into
Mr. Moon's stables ? A. No, into our own stables.
