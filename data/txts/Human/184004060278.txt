THOMAS WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of March , 3 shirts, value 6 s  .; 1 flannel-shirt, value 2 s  .; 1 pair of drawers, value 2 s  .; 2 handkerchiefs, value 4 s  .; and 1 stocking, value 9 d  .; the goods of Charles Civil . CHARLES CIVILL. I belong to the ship Felicity,  which was lying in Gould's Dock—the prisoner, who was mate, was paid off in February—before he left he went with me, to take the articles stated in this indict ment to be washed—soon after, he left the ship, and when I went for my clothes they were gone—from what the washerwoman told me, I suspected him, and about six weeks after I saw him on board a vessel, as we were going down the river—he told me he had got some clothes of mine—I said I knew he had, and collared him—he went down below, and pulled my clothes out of his box, all but one stocking, which he could not find—I gave him in charge—these are my clothes—I never gave him authority to get them from the washerwoman—they were clean when he got them, and he has worn them. JOSHUA JUDGE. I am a Thames police officer. I took the prisoner—I asked him how he got the clothes—he said he got them from the washer-woman, and he had done wrong, but was drunk at the time—he said the prosecutor did not allow him to get them.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went with him, and left some things of my own to be washed, and when I went for them the washerwoman asked if I would take his things, as we both belonged to one vessel. I took them. I got tipsy, and the vessel he was on board of left the dock that night. I then went on board another vessel, and when I returned I saw the pro secutor going by in a boat; I hailed him to come and fetch his things. I said I had worn them, as I was short of linen; but I would make him any recompence; he would not take less than half-a-sovereign; that was more than they were worth, and be gave charge of me. NOT GUILTY.
   Sixth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
