   MR. BODKIN . Q.  At what pace was Leopard going when you saw him? A.  At a slow place, being lame—I walked much faster—I think he must have entered Wellhall-lane by the time this occurred—there are a few houses in the lane, but the greater part is only hedges—there was nothing but the hedge to prevent any body getting out of the high-road if they thought proper. HENRY LEOPARD. Hive at Eltham. I was going there from Wolwich-common, on the night of the 6th of June last year—I remember seeing Mr. Watson pass me—I was then about three hundred yards from the Dover-road, but I cannot say exactly—I crossed the Dover-road and entered Well hall-lane—after I entered the lane a little distance, I met the witness Nor ton—he was in uniform—I have known him from his infancy—he was going in the direction of Woolwich—he was walking at a steady pace—he was alone—he said, "Good night," and I bid him the same—we called each other by name—I had got four or five hundred yards or more up the lane when I met him—I described to the constable the place where I met him, and he had the ground measured—Norton would not have an opportunity of seeing me coming some time before we met—as the road is zig-zag, winding—when I got further up the lane I got to Mr. Watson—I had not seen any other person in the interval—I found him hurt very much and assisted him to Eltham—I spoke of having seen Norton—his father and all his relations live at Eltham—I had left Eltham that day, and was returning home. JAMES WATTS. I am high constable of Woolwich. The witnesses pointed out the places to me which they speak of—I measured the distances, and they are accurately stated in this plan ( produced )—I first saw Norton the morning after the robbery—he made a communication to me on the subject—in consequence of which the prisoner was taken into custody a few days afterwards—he was not in the barracks that day—I was present before Sir Thomas Matthew Wilson and Sir John Webb at the examination—he made a statement before them, which was taken down in writing—I saw him put his mark to it—( looking at it )—this is the hand-writing of the two Magis trates—he called a witness named Thomas Baker, who also made a state ment—this is it—I cannot swear whether it was made in Perceval's pre sence or not, but he was called as a witness for Perceval—I cannot charge my memory whether he was present, because at one period the wit nesses were out of the room.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were not other witnesses called by Perceval be sides Baker? A.  There were witnesses called, but whether by Perceval I cannot say—I know several were called, and examined one by one out of the room—I do not recollect that Perceval called other witnesses besides Baker—I was present in December last when Baker was convicted of this offence—he was the man who was examined at the office. CHARLES STEWART WARDEN. I was present when Baker was called as a witness before the Magistrate when Perceval was charged with this offence—he was examined in Perceval's presence.
