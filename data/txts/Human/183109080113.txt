   JOHN ROBERTSON. I found that box in Cross-street, Islington, at Atkins' brother's, which is an oil-shop; Mr. Marchant saw the contents, and identified part of them.
   WILLIAM COX. I am an officer. I went to Mr. Marchant's house on the 12th of July, and saw Atkins; I assisted Robertson in apprehending the prisoner - I went up to a room in Marchant's house with Atkins; he pointed out a box as his own - paper and starch was found in it, which Mr. Marchant claimed; Alkins said it was his own, and Marchant spoke to a mark on the paper.
   JOSEPH BUTLER. I am the husband of Charlotte Butler. About the 24th of June I took a pair of boots home to Atkins at Mr. Marchant's; he asked me if I would take a jar home to my house - I did so, and have a sample of the contents; he afterwards called at my house with an oil can and a painted ornament - he told me to give it to a person who would call for it; that he was a man with big whiskers - Richard Roper, who answered that description, called for it; Atkins afterwards told me that man was going into partnership with him -I saw my wife give Richard Roper the oil can and the ornament; both are here now.
 The prosecutor here examined several of the articles produced, and identified most of them by marks on the papers, and labels upon several jars of pickles contained in the box.
   WILLIAM ATKINS. The prisoner Atkins is my brother; the two boxes were left at my house - he afterwards saw them and opened them; I saw a bottle in one of them - the Policeman took them away in the same state as they came.
 Cross-examined. Q.Should you know any of the boxes again? A. Yes; they came to my house between January and February - here is one of the boxes; I know it, because I am a painter and glazier, and I have mixed putty upon it.
 Richard Roper's Defence. Part of the things I confess to; but the rest. I solemnly declare, I bought out of my weekly earnings - the things found at my brother's I engaged him to carry to my father's house, he being a porter; as respects the other things I throw myself on the mercy of the Court.
 Atkins' Defence. I throw myself on the mercy of the Court.
 WILLIAM HOPKINS. I keep a public-house at Wimbledon. Atkins was apprentice to me for three years - but I was unfortunate.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q.Was he not in the habit of coming to you while in the prosecutor's employ? A. He was; we bought some candles of him, also starch, mustard, blacklead, and salt - we asked him to buy them, thinking he could get them cheaper in town; I knew he was living with Mr. Marchant.
 COURT. Q. Did you never swear that you supposed he was selling them on his master's account? A. I do not think I did; (looking at his deposition) I very possibly might say so. R. ROPER -    GUILTY. Aged 25.
 ATKINS -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 F. ROPER -    NOT GUILTY.
 1777.  TIMOTHY DONOVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 2 pictures and frames, value 20s., the goods of Wallis Drew.
 WALLIS DREW. I  keep the  White Lion public-house, in White's-alley, Chancery-lane. In August I had two pictures hanging in my back parlour; on the 23rd I missed one, and on the 24th the other - I have since found them; the glass of one has been taken out.
   JULIA MATTHEWS. I am servant to Mr. Drew. I saw the prisoner in the tap-room on the 24th of August, in the afternoon; master and mistress went out that evening, and about eight o'clock, I saw a man go out at the back door with a picture: I could not see him clearly, and do not know who he was - I went into the back parlour, and missed a picture.
   THOMAS LAW. I bought a picture of the prisoner on a Monday, about the middle of August, for 1s.
 Witness. I gave him one shilling.
 Prisoner. You only gave me, 10d.
   SARAH HAND. On the 24th of October I bought a picture of the prisoner for 9d.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. When I took the large one there was no glass to it; I was in distress, and had warning to quit my lodgings.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1778.  SAMUEL BUTLER and PHILLIP WILDEY were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of August , 4 lbs. of beef, value 2s., and 2 lbs. of lamb, value 1s., the goods of John Owen, their master.
 JOHN OWEN. I am a  butcher- the prisoners were in my service; I had taken Wildey in out of charity, as a jobbing-man. On the 18th of August I shut up my shop, and they were going home to their supper; they live opposite; I stopped Wildey, and asked what he had got in that cloth - he said a piece of beef, which Butler gave him to take - Butler ran across, I followed and brought him back, and the Policeman took a breast of lamb from him: both joints were in my shop two minutes before, when I left the prisoners there; Wildey said,"Master I had nothing to do with it, Butler gave it me to hold, till he came from supper:" Wildey had told me a fortnight before to look out - the cloth this meat was in is mine.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q.Wildey was only an occasional man? A. Yes - when I took Wildey, Butler was crossing the street, and turned to go into the privy; he had been a long time with me, and I suspected he had been robbing me all the while: I stopped Wildey as soon as he got out of the shop.
 Butler's Defence. I never saw the beef - I picked the lamb up going through the market.
 Wildey received a good character.
 BUTLER -    GUILTY. Aged 40. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 WILDEY -    GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined One Year.
