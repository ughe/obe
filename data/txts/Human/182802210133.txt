 739.  EDWARD WELCH was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of December , 1 piece of wood, called quartering, value 2s., the goods of our Lord   the King.
 SECOND COUNT, of the Hon.  William Sturges Bourne, William Dacres Adams, Esq., and Henry Dawkins, Esq.
 MR. ALLEY conducted the prosecution.
   PETER COOKE BARRAND. I am a carpenter, and live at the Castle, in Moor's-yard. About 7 o'clock in the morning, about two months ago, there was some paling there; I saw the prisoner there; he pulled a piece of quartering out of the ground, and walked away with it on his shoulder; I followed him into Vere-street, and then said, "That is the way the wood goes from Moor's-yard:" he turned round and said, "I may as well take it away as another, my master put it up." I said he did not authorize you to take it away - I saw him take it into a house in Vine-street. I then gave information.
   WILLIAM OXLEY. I am a constable. I was sent for, and took the prisoner at a public-house - I got this piece of timber from Andrews.
   WILLIAM ANDREWS. I bought this wood of the prisoner; I am in the employ of Mr. M'Pherson, a publican, in Chandos-street - I did not ask where he got it; I bought it for firewood: part has been burned.
   JAMES GILBERT. This wood is the property of the Commissioners of Woods and Forests; it was in Moor's-yard under my care.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that there were three other persons of his name residing in the street, and that he had not committed the robbery.
     GUILTY. Aged 41. Confined Three Months.
 740.  JOHN PHILPOT was indicted for embezzlement.
   WILLIAM SHEARMAN. I am a carpenter, and live in Little Anchor-street, Church-street, Bethnal-green; the prisoner worked for me occasionally for about three years; he was in my employ on the 14th of July, 1824; I occasionally employed him to collect money; I told him on that day to receive 1l. 16s. 6d. from Tucker - he should have brought me the money the same morning; I never saw him afterwards till he was taken, and have never received the money.
   JOSEPH VERISON TUCKER, In July, 1824, I owed Shearman 1l. 16s. 6d., which I paid to a man - I cannot say whether the prisoner is the man: here is the receipt the person gave me.
 WILLIAM SHEARMAN. When I took the prisoner he said, voluntarily, that he had spent Tucker's money, and I ought to have had it.
     GUILTY. Aged 35. Confined Three Months.
 741.  SAMUEL WARD was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of November , 1 bushel of mixed corn, called horse-corn, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Deborah Dixon, widow.
 MR. COOPER conducted the prosecution.
   THOMAS GREENFIELD. I am coachman to Mrs. Deborah Dixon, widow; I drove the Henley coach for her from there to London. On the 3d of November, I stopped with the coach at some private stables at Hounslow, which she rents of Stuckbury; before I changed horses I saw the prisoner at the back of the stable; I got off my coach, and went round to the back stable - I had seen the prisoner there before, and desired him not to remain there - it is a private yard: we have two rest-horses and a corn-bin in the back stable; I saw the prisoner go to the bin, and fill a bag, which he hid under the dunghill - he did not see me; I went up, and said, "Mr. Ward, what have you got there?" he said, "D - n it, here is a great rat;" he took up a brick, and ran round the stable; I went after him, but he crossed the field, and ran away; I went to the dung-heap, and found this corn in the bag - it is called horse-corn; all the corn there belongs to Mrs. Dixon - here is nearly a bushel of it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was never in the stable; I found it under the granary, and threw it on the ground.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 742.  JEREMIAH DONOVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of January , 1 cloak, value 30s., the goods of Thomas Larkman.
 THOMAS LARKMAN. I  keep the  Wheatsheaf public-house, Holywell-street, Strand. On the 18th of January this cloak hung in my club-room; I went up about seven o'clock, and saw the prisoner and two young men - the young men went away soon after, but the prisoner seemed loath to go; I said he must go; he went out, but soon returned, and staid in the tap-room; I went out, and returned in ten minutes - he was then gone; I went up stairs, and missed my cloak; I afterwards apprehended him; Berkley gave me the duplicate.
   MARIA BERKLEY. I saw the prisoner a few days after this; he gave me the duplicate of a cloak - I gave it up.
   JAMES TAGUS SHOOT. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Fetter-lane. On the 19th of January this cloak was pawned by a young woman, not Berkley, in the name of Jesse Moriencourt.
