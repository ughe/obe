JOAN THOMAS EDWARDS. I am manager of the London and County Bank
at Greenwich I have been twenty-one years in the banking business—(MR.
POLAND proposed asking this witness the practice of the Bank of England as
to keeping notes, but upon objection taken, at the suggestion of the Court, it
was not pressed).
JAMES SPENCER. I am a solicitor, at 14, South Street, Greenwich-I am
the attorney conducting this prosecution-I ascertained from the bank
books the numbers of the notes that had been paid for this 4401 cheque--
I went to the Bank of England and made enquiry with reference to those
notes: a general statement was made to me the notes were not produced,
I could not get to see them-after what I heard at the bank I did not
trouble myself to endeavour to trace them- I went there for the express
purpose of tracing them.
MED
SAMUEL HOBBS. I live at New Cross Road, and am a contractor-in 1861
I did work for the Board of Works of the Greenwich district-I did the
work mentioned in these four accounts-I made out this account for the
Greenwich parish myself—- the work was done by my people at Lady Day in
that year, this was the account for that quarter-I added it up myself --
when I sent it in to the Board, it was an account for 831. 168. 6d.; that,
with the other accounts, made a total of 3402 46. 8d. due to me on the
morning of 30th May I called at the clerk's office for payment of those
accounts; I had a memorandum book with me, I have it here now-I saw
the prisoner; it was a little after 12 o'clock when I went into the office-I
anid," Mr. Chapman, I have called for my accounts for the quarter ending
Lady Day" he said, “I must trouble you to call again" I said, “For
why?"-he said, “I am sorry to say that I have made an error in drawing
the cheque for your amount, 1001. in excess, call again in half-an-hour and
I will get the cheque cashed and pay you in cash, and pay the excess over
to the credit of the Board" he begged me not to say anything to any
member of the Board, it would make no difference, every one was liable to
mistake—I did not see the accounts then I went away and called again in
half-an-bour, he then paid me 3401. in bank-notes, three 1001. and two 20%.
-I put stamps on the bills and receipted them, all four—these are my
signatures-before receipting them I looked at my memorandum-book to
see whether the accounts agreed—this is my memorandum: "St. Paul,
Deptford, account 2471 48. 4d., cartage account 31. 38., Greenwich account
831. 168. 6;d., St. Nicholas, Deptford, 61. 08. 104d.”_I am certain that the
Greenwich account was 831. when I receipted it as soon as I received the
notes and signed the receipts I went and paid them in to my credit at the
London and County Bank at Greenwich, together with a little cheque of
131. 188.; the 48. 8d. I put into my pocket-I have my pass-book here, and
I am credited with that amount-I entered the payment in my memorandum-
book as "Cash 3401. 46. 8d." -I entered that at the time the St. Paul,
Deptford, account was 2471. 2s. 7d. when I sent it in; it is altered to
2471. 2s. 4d.--I altered my book at the time to make it agree : the amount
that I received was 3401. 48. 8d. --before this I had always been paid by
cheque-Mr. Chapman paid me I have been paid since by cheque_this
was the only time I was paid in money that account included all that was
due to me up to Lady Day.
