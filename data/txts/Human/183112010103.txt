I lived at a baker's about three months ago - I am now looking for a situation; it was rather dark when this happened - I stood at the corner of Tothill-street, and could see which way they went; the man turned up Fludyer-court once, and the next time up Dean-street; I sometimes looked at him and somethimes at the prisoner.
 Q. Did you not first charge the prisoner's brother with doing this? A.When he stood up I said it was not him.
   DANIEL REARDON. I am a Policeman. King gave me information on the 29th of November, and I took the prisoner in bed with another man; he said he knew nothing about any stones - I asked if he had got any in a court, at the back of his house; he said, "No, I have no stones there;" I said, "Well, this boy says he saw you take some there;" he said, "Oh, they are my stones;" that he had bought them of one person and another - he could not exactly say who; I went with Mr. Howell, and brought the stones from the court to Newway watch-house; King pointed out where they were taken from - they are large flag-stones - we compared them with the place, and four exactly fitted the place; we found fourteen - after he gave me information I went to a court in the Almonry, and found some large flagstones; I watched, and the prisoner and another man came out of the house; King immediately said, "These are the two men;" they went towards the stones - I afterwards went and found them in bed, as I have stated.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he go up to the stones? A. No, because he saw me - I am certain he faced and saw me; they were regular square stones - one in particular fitted in the joint; King at first charged the prisoner's brother with this.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did the prisoner claim the stones, and say he had bought them? A. Yes; I told him there were fourteen; he said he had bought them all - no stone would fit, unless it was the size of the one taken up -King pointed out a small stone to me, which fitted exactly.
   JOSEPH KING re-examined. The man only carried one stone at a time - I saw him come three times; the man who came out with the prisoner was not his brother.
   GEORGE HOWELL. I am an inspector of the Police. -Reardon's evidence is correct; the prisoner told me he had bought the stones of a man named Sweeney, at 2d. a foot.
 The Act of Parliament, vesting the property as  James Mitchell's and others, was put in.
 Prisoner's Defence. I have a witness - it is impossible I could pull a stone out of the ground, I was so ill.
   EDWARD CORMACK. I am the prisoner's brother. I was charged with taking these stones, but did not - I live at No. 4, Little Dean-street.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You was not at your brother's house that night? A. I was at my own house; we live together.
   DANIEL REARDON. I have seen the prisoner about the street with a stick - I believe he is capable of taking a stone up to put on another man's shoulder.
   JAMES DOWIE. I know the prisoner has had a paralytic attack, and been in two hospitals; he is paralysed on his right side, and always walked with a stick - I cannot say whether he was incapable of lifting a stone.
     NOT GUILTY.
 228.  WILLIAM STRICKLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of November , 13 watch-cases, value 12l.; 5 watches, value 17l., and 2 spoons, value 20s., the goods of Edward Keat, his master; to which he pleaded GUILTY.
 Strongly recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor, having given immediate information to lead to the recovery of the property - he received a good character. Confined Six Months.
 229.  WILLIAM SYKES was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of September , 22 yards of woollen cloth, value 20l., and 40 yards of kerseymere, value 16l., the goods of Samuel Johnston.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
 SAMUEL JOHNSTON. I live in Addle-street. On the 20th of September the prisoner came to my house, and said he wanted a piece of black cloth, and black kerseymere for a cash customer of his westward; I sent them by a porter - the prisoner and I met the porter at Dimsdale's, St. Ann's, chop-house; the goods were delivered to him, in my presence: we dismissed the porter there, and the prisoner took the goods out under his arm - he did not tell me where they were going, but said it was a cash customer in that neighbourhood; he said he was going to sell them for cash to a customer of his; he came back with the goods, and said he had failed in selling them; he proposed as we had dismissed the porter, that he should leave them at Dimsdale's all night - I agreed; I saw him two or three days after, and proposed sending for them - he said I was not known at the house, and they would not deliver them to me; I saw him again in a few days, and said they ought to be sent for - he said he would send for them; I saw him again in a day or two - he told me he had sent Cleasby for them with a written order; on seeing him again, he said he had sent Cleasby with a note for them, but they refused to deliver them, not knowing his hand-writing - I called at Dimdale's the next day but one; I saw the prisoner in the City afterwards, and told him he had told me a falsehood, for Dimsdale had told me they had been gone this ten days or a fortnight, and that he, Sykes, had taken them away - he said I was mistaken, that he had taken them from Dimsdale's, and left them with Burrows, a tailor, for cash: I inquired who Burrows was - he said he was an intimate friend and companion of  Peter Thompson's, who I knew; I said, "If so he must be a highly respectable man" - I saw him again in a day or two afterwards, and urged the necessity of his calling on Burrows, for the cash or telling me where he lived, that I might call myself, and get it - he said Burrows was gone over to Boulogne on an excursion with Mr. Thompson, and would not be back for a week;
