John Swan FLOWER. I am a surgeon, of Camberwell-green-I attended
Luscombe, I found him in bed-he had a clean-cut wound about an inch or
rather larger, penetrating to the bone-it had been given with considerable
force, and much against the bone, and if it had not met with an interposition
of that kind it would have been very serious-he was otherwise bruised on
the shoulder, such a bruise might arise from a fall, but the other was a clean
cut, and must in my judgment have been caused by some such instrument
as this-he has been under my care ever since, and is still unable to use his
arm.
Cross-examined. 0. How are you able to know that the bone was pene-
trated ? A. It did not heal kindly, and an effusion of blood followed-I
do not think falling on the shoulder immediately after or at the time would
aggravate the appearances--if a man received a cut and fell on it the
surrounding muscles might be affected—I do not think a flat stone would
bave done it-the macadamized roads with stones with projecting points
could not do it.
MR. METCALFE. Q. Did you see the clothes ? A. Yes, they were cut
right through clean.
MR. LILLEY to J. CRUTCHER. Q. Did you see a person named Lowes after
you had been before the police Magistrate ? A. I never heard the name,
and do not know any such person-I never spoke to a person on 25th April
and said, “Good morning, Sir, you had your journey for nothing the other
morning"-I do not know what you are alluding to.
