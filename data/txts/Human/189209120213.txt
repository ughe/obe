MARY CHURCHER. I live at Worthing House, Hill Street, Hastings, about a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes from Hastings Station—it is a lodging house—I have been in Hastings two years—Mr. and Mrs. Furness came to lodge with me on July 9th, and occupied a sitting-room and two bedrooms—my sister and I attended to them till they left—Mr. Furness never absented himself, to my knowledge, from July 9th to the 21st—I attended them at breakfast from 8.20 to 8.30—I never remember Mr. Furness having breakfast by himself as late as a few minutes before eleven; breakfast was always out of the room by nine o'clock.
   Cross-examined.  I keep no servant—they paid £1 1s. a week for their rooms—I had a deposit when they first came—I have walked to the station lots of times—my sister or I cleared away the breakfast, whichever was more ready—I do not remember whether I went on the Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday, but Mrs. Furness was speaking to me on the Wednesday morning as I was clearing the breakfast about going to the benefit; they were going to book tickets—I cannot swear that Mr. Furness was in the room at that conversation, but he was there when I took the breakfast in—they were the only lodgers I had—my sister is not here.
   Re-examined.  I did not see the benefit announced in the paper before it took place—Mr. Hunter is very well known there—I think it is an annual benefit. JOSEPH CLARK( Re-examined ). I know Hastings; I have been there year after year—I know Hill Street—you cannot walk from there to the railway station under a quarter of a hour very sharp walking, and ordinary walking it would take twenty minutes. ALFRED HENRY SAVAGE. I live at Clyde Villas, Hastings, and have retired from business—I am the uncle of Mrs. Furness—I remember their coming to Hastings on July 9th; they stayed till the 21st—Mr. Hunter took his benefit on Wednesday, July 13th, and I met Mr. and Mrs. Furness that morning at eleven o'clock on the sea-front about six minutes' walk from Hill Street; farther from my house, but nearer to the station—we strolled about the parade and had lunch at one of the restaurants, and afterwards went on the pier with a view of attending Mr. Hunter's benefit; it was very crowded, and we went on board one of the boats and went to Eastbourne, and returned at five o'clock—Mr. Furness was in my company all that time—on the Tuesday we went to the front; they were in the place at six o'clock at tea, and about nine o'clock we left, and I went home with my wife—I never heard anything said while I was there about a visit by Mr. Furness to London.
