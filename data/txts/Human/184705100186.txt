   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRAEN . Q.  You did not know till you found it, how much had lost? A.  I knew by what I had taken from it, what I had left—I have been living with the prisoner between four and five years—I have kept the beer-shop about three years—the prisoner is a labourer he works at one thing or another—he was employed in a cow-shed when I first knew him—he had 18 s . a-week—he did not bring his wages home—what he brought home was very little—it went in support of house—he worked, at times, after the beer-house was taken—after he came from work he might have attended a little to the business—he never paid money, he might take have money—I had lodgers in the house—I sometimes received 7 s .✗ or 8 s . a-week from them, sometimes less—I could not save any of that—I took some from the money I had saved—I never added any to it for four years—I could not tell to a sovereign or two what I had there—the money was in the purse four years ago—this bag is one that I have had about two years—I look some money from one, and put it into the other—on the Friday, after the charge was dismissed, he held up a bag tome and said, "I have got your money"—he said, "You had a spree  last night, I will have one to night"—I had no means of living except what I took from the lodgers, and the proceeds of the beer-shop—I paid 27 l . a-year rent, and the license was 3 l . 6 s . a-year—the prisoner paid the license—I gave him the money to pay it with—I paid the rent—the receipts were not in my name.
   MR. HUDDLESTONE . Q.  You say when he came back he held up a bag to you, and said he had got your money? A.  Yes; that was after he had been to the inspector and had the charge dismissed—in the morning I had said to him, "Will you have the kindness to clean the pots to-day"—he said, "No; I will never clean another pot as long as I live—my name is Walker "—he said, "I have got plenty of money, I am going to enjoy my self"—that was before I had missed the money—when I charged him with taking my money, he said he knew nothing at all of it, nor yet of his clothes—he said he did not know I was possessed of a farthing, and when he came back from the inspector he pulled out a small bag in which he carried his own money, and thrust it at me and said, "There is your b——y money, you got it out of b——y old Scott"—I had all this money before I went to live with the prisoner.
   COURT . Q.  Do you swear positively that you had all these sovereigns before you lived with the prisoner? A.  I do; and none of them were got in our joint concern after we were living together.
