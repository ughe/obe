   MR. HORRY . Q.  When did you first make an agreement with Wiggins about getting counsel? A.  After the prisoner said he had lost the original—I was present when Moore was tried—the witnesses did not appear for him.
   JURY . Q.  When were you first acquainted with the prisoner? A.  Down at Lambeth-street—but I had seen him at the King William before—I have always treated him at the public-houses I have been at with him. SAMUEL WIGGINS. I am a tailor, and live at No. 6, Sidney-place, Sidney-street, City-road—I was in the George on Thursday, the 18th of October, and saw the prosecutrix, the prisoner, and another young woman—I saw the prisoner come in, stand at the comer of the box, and ask for 4 s . 6 d ., to get a subpoena, in the room of the one he had lost the day before—she gave him two half-crowns—he said, "You may as well give me a crown," as she had only got the two half-crowns—they both went out—he came back in ten minutes or a quarter of an hour—the friend who was with the prosecutrix asked him if he had got it—he said yes, it was all right—he threw it on the table, and the prosecutrix said, "In my opinion this is the one that I folded up last night"—she said she thought it was the same she had given him the night before—I said, "What writing is on the back of this?"—he said, "You b——y fool, cannot you read?"—I said, "No, nor any body else."
 Cross-examined.  Q.  How came you to be there? I had made a pair of trowsers for a gentleman—I do not interfere in the defence of prisoners, my brother does, and when he is not here, I sometimes give answers and sometimes go down to the George—the prosecutrix did not give the sub poena to me—I have not had a quarrel with the prisoner—I never saw him before then—I did not tell the Magistrate this—I was not asked—I looked at the back of the paper—I have worked for Mr. Berdoe, of Gos well-road since last April—I made part of a great coat with another man—we work at home, because he has not convenience on his premises. PHOEBE FORDHAM. I am bar-maid at the Mahogany Bar public-house, in Wellclose-square—before the last Old Bailey Sessions the prisoner called and asked if I remembered seeing two men, and serving a glass of rum-and-water the week before, and whether the old man was drunk—I said "Yes, he was drunk"—he asked if I would attend at the Sessions—I said no, I could not—he said, if I would not attend by fair means he would make me, and in the evening he brought down a subpoena, and left it—this is the subpoena—I then said I could not attend—he said if he could do without me he would, but he would let me know—this subpoena is the same with respect to the writing as when I received it—it has got dirty since.
