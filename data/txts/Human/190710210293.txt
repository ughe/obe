I also discussed it with Perryman, who suggested some Exchange as a name, "The London Exchange," or something like that; but I did not like Exchanges. I should imagine the discussion took place at Moorgate Street; I should think that would be right. My memory about that time is pretty indistinct; I can remember things better 20 years ago. I drafted the circulars for Chalmers and Cox, and I rather think Perryman has altered them sometimes. I had an assis  tant in the business, Mr. Beteon, and I had a good deal of assistance from Harris. Whenever I was in any difficulty I frequently consulted Perryman. I think I have the credit of being able to write, in every sense of the word. If I remember rightly I signed the letters with a rubber stamp; my signature is inclined; to vary; not through bad habits. I signed the cheques as they were required; possibly some times in advance, because I was away very frequently. Betson would have the cheque book, and he had instructions to hand it to Harris whenever he wanted it. Harris supervised my business when I was ill. I think he has been working with Perryman all the time I have known him. I could hardly say that handing blank cheques to Harris was the same thing as handing them to Perryman. I think there were one or two interregnums when Harris left Perry man for a short time because of a tiff. I borrowed money from Perryman when I was short; I do not know that it would be £2 a week; more than that; I used to draw a certain sum per week. It was a loose arrangement I had with him. I had some money of my own when I started this business, which I lost; I paid a good deal out to clients. I should think I got into difficulties five or six months after I started, soon alter my illness. Watson and I had the moneys that came into the business.
 To Mr. Justice Lawrence. I should think I put about £100 into the business; I had it in the bank; I had £300 or £400 about that time.
 To Mr. Muir. In regard to the phrases in the letter, "complete angler," etc., and "playing croupier," a croupier is a man who rakes in the money; I feel a little clearer now about it. The letter to Mrs. A. E. R. Brush is in my handwriting. It would appear by that that all my splendid prophecies had come untrue. In regard to the letter, "We are sorry to learn you have been let in, and fear you have fallen across some black sheep. Your orders and instructions shall have our immediate attention; you will always find us prompt in our settlements. As you will have observed from our circular, our inte  rests and our clients' are identical," the broker does not necessarily lose if the client is successful, unless he is running the stock against the client; for the most part we were doing so. That letter would hardly bear much explanation. The words in the circular, "Our commission is 10 per cent. of the profits only, so that our clients' interests and our own are identical,"' would seem to suggest we were mere brokers.
