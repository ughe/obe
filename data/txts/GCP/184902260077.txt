CHARLES SAYER. I saw 10s. 6d. paid to the prosecutor I saw him put
it into his pocket--he then got tipsy-I took him out of the house and walked
a little way home with him-bext morning I heard he was ill.
JOHN LEE. I am twelve years old, my father is a tailor, My father sent
me out on an errand on 27th Jan., and as I was coming along Brick-lane it
Struck half-past twelve o'clock at night--when I was near Wentworth-street
saw the prosecutor at the corner-I never knew him before he put his hand
into his right trowsers-pocket, and said he had not a shilling in his pocket-he
was talking to himself-he went from the corner and came back again-the
prisoner and three or four men were at the corner-one of them caught
the prosecutor by the collar, and the prisoner turned his pockets inside out-I
am sure he is the man that did it was three or four yards off him-he
then caught him by the arm, brought him four or five yards down Went-
worth-street, and there he threw him down on his head-I did not see him
take anything out of bis pocket4I do not think that they saw me--the pro-
secutor was quite drunk-the others had gone up Brick-lane, and after the
prisoner bad thrown the prosecutor down, he ran up Brick-lane I saw the
prosecutor's head bleed-I stayed there till a policeman came, and then there
came three or four more-the one that came first ordered a stretcher to be
brought, and he held up the prosecutor on his knees till it was brought.
JOSEPH SADLER (policeman, H 68). I found the prosecutor lying down
bleeding --Lee gave a very accurate description of the prisoner-I knew who
he was--I gave a description to my sergeant, and he was taken the same day,
Tuomas Hoons (polioeman, H 52). I went to a lodging-house in George
street, Spitalfields I saw the prisoner there-- I told him I wanted him for rob:
bing a man in Spitalfields--- he said I was mistaken; he was at home and in bed
by ten o'clock on Saturday night, and he did not get up till ten o'clock on
Sunday morning-I told him I knew it was false, for I searched his lodging at
seven o'clock in the morning, and every bed in the house; and more than
that I saw him out at three o'clock in the morning,
Prisoner. Q. Did you see me? A. Yes, at the door, and you sat smok-

Prisoner. I came out of a public-house a few minutes after twelve o'clock,
