1433. ELIZABETH WOOTTEN was indicted for stealing, on the   8th of April , 1 gown, value 3 s  .; and 1 blanket, value 2 s  .; the goods of Tho mas Wootten . MARIA WOOTTEN. I am the wife of Thomas Wootten, a Greenwich pensioner—the prisoner is my husband's son's wife, and lived with us for nothing—her husband did not live with her—he allowed her 3 s . a week About half-past five o'clock last Monday morning I went out to work leaving my gown and blanket at home—I returned at half-past seven o'clock in the evening, and missed them—I never allowed the prisoner to pawn them. EDWARD CARTER. I am a pawnbroker at Greenwich. On Monday, the 8th of April, the prisoner pledged this gown and blanket with me—not both at the same time—I believe she pledged the blanket first, the gown next, and she came a third time for more money on the gown—she pledged them in her own name—I knew her very well—I believe a woman named Cook was with her when she pledged the gown. ROBERT DUNN( police-constable R  9.) I received information last Monday, and found the prisoner at a house in Queen-street, Greenwich—I told her I took her on suspicion of stealing a gown and blanket—she said she knew nothing at all about it, it was the first she had heard of it—on our way to the station-house I told her I knew where the gown was—she said, "I know where it is too, but the other woman knew nothing at all about it, for I pawned it myself'—I said, "If you pawned them you must know where the duplicates are"—she said, "I have got them here, here they are"—they were pinned to her gown under her shawl—I took them from her, and produce them.
 (  Property produced and sworn to. )
   Prisoner's Defence.  I did not pledge the gown—it was the prosecntrix's son, because his mother had locked up the tea and sugar, and he could not get any breakfast—he pledged it for 8 d ., and I went and got 10 d . more on it afterwards—I pledged the blanket myself, and the boy was with me at the time. EDWARD CARTER re-examined.  I am sure it was the prisoner pledged them—the boy had pledged the gown before—I have no recollection of seeing him that day—I will not positively say he was not there—I am po sitive I saw the prisoner twice about the gown, and she had two separate payments on it—the last payment was in the evening—I cannot say the boy did not pledge it—I am positive the prisoner pledged the blanket—she has been with the boy when he has pledged the gown before.
   MRS. WOOTTEN re-examined.  I have sent my son to pawn the gown several times before, but I never knew that the prisoner went with him. NOT GUILTY.
   Before Mr. Recorder.
