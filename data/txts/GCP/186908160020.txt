EDWARD DILLON (Police Sergeant K 19). I received a purse from
Sergeant Austin when I took charge of the police station after him-I
produce the purse and the shilling found in it-Smith came to me and said
that the Magistrate had ordered the money to be given up, and he counted
out 77. 3d, to the wife, and she signed the book as a receipt-Smith then
said, “I should like to count the money in the purse"-he took it up and
turned the money out-he took this shilling, handed it to me, and I bent
it with my teeth.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you give the money up! 4. No-Smith gare
it out of his own pocket--we always give up a corresponding amount, not
the identical amount--I had not examined the money at all before--I
suppose Sergeant Austin did—when Smith gave the money to Race's wife
he wanted to see whether there was the same amount in the purse.
GEORGE SMITH. I was at the station when Whiffen searched Race I
saw him take the purse from him, containing 78. 31d.-on the following
morning I went to the station and saw Dillon there, and also the purse-I
emptied the money on the table and looked it over-I picked up a shilling
and handed it to Dillon--he put it to his teeth and bent it-I had previously
received an order to give up the money to Mrs. Race-I gave her a corre-
sponding sum-I afterwards got a shilling back from her.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen the money on 20th July ! A. I SAW
it on the desk-Austin put it in the purse again and placed it at the back
of the desk-I saw it again next morning about 12 o'clock-Sergeant Dillon
was acting inspector--no one had access to the desk.
