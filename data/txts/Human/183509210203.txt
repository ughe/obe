   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  About how many witnesses have been subpœnaed by Mr. Norton in this case? A.  A great many—I have heard fifty or sixty—there were only four witnesses went before the Grand Jury and only two of them were heard—the amount of debts at my first bankruptey was about 5000 l . or 6000 l ., and the second time not 1500 l .—I have not paid fifteen shillings in the pound on either commission.
   Q.  Are you aware that, not having paid 15 s ., you are not in point of law, entitled to any property? A.  No, Sir—I succeeded to my stable in about three months after my first commission—I never left it the second time—It was mortgaged to my bankers, and they let me remain.
   Q.  Was Thomas Bird a creditor under your second commission? A.  Not that I know of—I do not know the man—I have no recollection of his name for a penny—I never got into his debt—I had some bills of exchange out, but very few—I have no recollection of Bird's name in either of my bankrupteies, or in any kind of dealing—I did not enter a bill of Bird's for a backney-coach—I have no recollection of it—I hired my omni buses of Mr. Boswell, in Aldgate High-street—I set them up on the 8th of June—I paid part of the hire of them 17 l ., having had them twelve weeks—I had my buses  ordered before Alexander had any idea of coming out—he then told me he was coming out—I did not agree with him to bring out omnibuses.
   Q.  What did you mean by saying just now, if he had come out as he agreed? A.  No, Sir, "as he said he would," I did expect him to come out with me, and I thought he was coming out on that day, but I heard after wards the Paddington committee had given him 100 l ., to stop him—I consulted with Mr. Norton a very little time after I started, I believe about three weeks—he had been my attorney before—I have heard that forty or fifty omnibuses ran from the Bank to Paddington before I set up; but I do not know.
   Q.  Did you not state in your own yard, to Mr. John Bardell, that you did not wish to indict the Bardells at all, but your lawyer said they must be in the indictment as he had a reason for it? A.  I said I had no animosity against them—I did not say I did not wish to indict them at all—I told them Mr. Norton said they must be in the indictment, he had a cause for putting them in—I did not say I did not know the rea son, but if he would meet me at Mr. Norton's I would go and see if I could not get them out—I did not go to Mr. Norton's for any such pur pose—I went to Mr. Norton's on a Sunday, with Mr. John Bardell—I do not recollect his asking Mr. Norton why he had put his brothers in the in dictment—I will swear it was not said in my presence—I do not recollect Mr. Norton being questioned as to whether they could not get out of it—there was very little said—we were not there ten minutes—I will swear I did not hear Mr. Norton say their names must remain in the indict ment his object being to preven their being witnesses for the defence—I will swear I never heard it; nor that their evidence against them would be of importance.
