454. JEREMIAH LEARY (33), Stealing a watch, the property of
James William Benson.
MR. LAWLESS Prosecuted.
RAYMOND JOHN BRASIER. I am assistant to James William Benson, of
Ludgate Hill—on May 18th, about 2.30, I saw the prisoner throw a
portion of a brick at our window, and put his hand in—I went out, and
found his hand in the window, drew him back, and ho dropped a gold
watch, value twelve guineas—I gave him in charge.
Cross-examined. He was excited—I did not see the watch in his hand,
but it was the only watch outside—there was no jewellery nearer the hole
nethe watch was about eighteen inches from the hole—the watches hung
on hooks,and to take off one you must take them all off—we have a window
which might have been broken, where there was o quantity of jewellery.
Re-examined. He threw the brick through the glass ; it left his hand.
NICHOLAS LAVERS (480, City). On May 18th, about 3.30, I was on
duty, heard a smash of glass, and saw the prisoner at the window ; per-
sons said that he had broken it; I asked him why ho broke it—he said,
"Because I am disgusted with everybody in this country except Her'
Majesty "—he said that I should not take him—he was taken to the
hospital, and then to the station—he said that he should do a similar
thing "hen he came out—he was a little excited ; I think he bad had a
little drop.
Cross-examined. He threW himself on the ground, and said that I
should not take him ; he would go for it—I do not know that he at-
tempted to commit suicide.

breaking a window at Mr. Benson's, the jeweller•s, and stealing a watch.
I wish to plead guilty to breaking the window, but not to stealing the
watch. I have been charged several times before, but never with felony.
I have had so many wounds on my head, and am not responsible for m

the Thames, and twice with attempting to drown myself, and with cutting
my throat in Holloway Prison. I am not responsible for my actions.

brethren before next Session.
