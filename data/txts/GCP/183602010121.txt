498. JOHN COLLINS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of Ja-
nuary, 1 cloak, value 12s., the goods of Joseph Goodey.
JOHN Wooron. I live with Joseph Goodey, at Holloway: he is a linen-
draper. On the 28th of January, about five o'clock, a little boy told me
some property had been taken away-I went out of the shop, and came up
with the prisoner- he had another with him the policeman stopped him
- the prisoner was carrying the cloak-the other was taken, but the bill
against him was thrown out-this cloak was hanging at the door about five
o'clock in the evening—they had got about five or six hundred yards from
the shop, (about five minutes' walk.)
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. You immediately missed it ?
4. Yes.
HENRY GOODIN. I saw the prisoner near the shop-he was alone--he
took a cloak-1 went and told of it-the prisoner is the person.
Cross-examined. Q. This was at dusk ? A. Yes there were two men
- they were not together when they stole it only the prisoner took the
doak I was about twenty yards from the shop, on the opposite side
swear positively to the prisoner--there was a person with him when he took
it the other was nine or ten yards from him.
(Mr. Case, carpenter, White-hart-street, Drury-lane, and Mr. Spraggs,
of Holloway, gave the prisoner a good character.)

499. ANN M'INTYRE was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of Ja-
nuary, 1 watch, value 17. 58.; 1 watch-chain, value 6d. ; 4 seals, value
16 48. ; 3 watch-keys, value 1s. 6d.; and 1 split-ring, value 88.; the goods
Samuel Hudson, from his person.
SAMUEL Hudson. I am a baker, and live in Little Earl-street, Seven
Dials. On the 21st of January, at a quarter before one o'clock, I had been
to a small party, and was sober-I was coming out of St. Andrew-street-
I felt myself unwell, and leaned against a post-the prisoner came and put
her arms round my waist, and wanted me to go home with her-I never
opened my mouth to ber-the policeman came up and asked what she
wanted with me-she said I was her brother I went home, only just over
the way, I went up stairs, took of my jacket, and missed my watch-
went down again, and by that time the same policeman had returned round
his beat-I told him of it, and he apprehended the prisoner the next even-
ing--I had no conversation whatever with her—this is the watch, seals,
chain, and key--the ribbon does not belong to me.
Prisoner. Q. You know me? 4. I have known you some years, by your
coming to my shop-I did not say, "I shall fall down"-I never opened
my lips to you at all-I know nothing of her, further than her coming to
my shop to buy bread.
ARTHUR JAMES Jones. The prisoner pledged the watch and seals with
me, at Mr. Samuel Warman's, the same morning, the 21st.
