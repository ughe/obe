COURT . Q.  What time did you leave London to go to Deptford? A.  About three o'clock, as near as I can recollect—we were on the right-hand side of the archway—they were apprehended at the end of Church-street about a quarter of a mile from Maybank's. and about twenty minutes after five o'clock—it was ten minutes after five o'clock when we were on the rail-road, when we gave our tickets up—we did not go within 100 yards of Maybank's shop—we did not stop any where—we walked on the right-hand side of the rail-road, and Maybank's is one the left-hand, through the archway—the prisoners were taken on the right-hand side of Church-street—one part of the railway goes very close to Maybank's house—we came on the railway to go to see Jones, at Greenwich college—we never went under the archway—we could not be near Maybank's—we were at the corner of Church-street, in the Broadway—I came from Holborn—one of the prisoner is a shoemaker, and comes from Saffron-hill, and the other is a printer, and comes from very close to the spot—we had a holiday that day. JOHN THOMAS. I am a boot and shoe maker, and live in Peter-street, Clerkenwell. On Friday in Easter week I happened to go down to Dock head, and at the top of Church-street I saw two boys looking at a bill—it was near about five o'clock—I saw a little child—one of the boys was con siderably taller than the other—I saw the tall one snatch something from the child's neck, which I considered was a necklace, and run away with it, and the other soon followed—it was near the station-house—I did not follow them, being lame from an accident I have received—I afterwards heard of two boys being taken up for this robbery, and came to Newgate to identify them—I saw the prisoners—they are not the boys who committed the theft.
   COURT . Q.  How came you to Newgate? A.  Because I saw this robbery committed, and not being able to follow them, I went to several streets, thinking to see them, to have them taken, but I could not, and returned home—I did not know Barregan before this transaction—I first saw him in Newgate—I did not know Riley before—I do not know whether either of them live in Clerkenwell—I never heard it—I went to Deptford by the rail-way that day—I got there about half-past four o'clock—I got into Church-street just before five o'clock, as near as I can guess—I had no watch with me—I was never in Deptford before, and walked into Church-street just for a ramble round the place—I did not tell any of the shopkeeper in the street what I had seen—I should know the persons again who took the necklace—they went under the arch-way towards the rail road—they went down Church-street in front of me—I gave no alarm—there was no one by at the time—I went to Newgate of my own accord, to give evidence against them if they were the right people—the prisoners did not ask me to go.
   Q.  How did you know the prisoners were in Newgate? A.  I was in company, and said I saw this robbery committed, and one of my acquaint ancee, named Phillips, who lives at Chelsea, said, "There are two taken up for the robbery, by the name of Barregan and Riley, and committed to Newgatee"—I put down the names, and went to Newgate and saw the prison ers, and to my surprise found they were not the two who committed the robbery—I had not seen the two who committed the robbery before—they had not come down by the rail-road with me—one of the parties had a drab coat on, and the other a corduroy jacket—the tall one was nearly five feet seven inches—I cannot say how tail the short one was—I did not notice what coloured neckcloth the tall one had on.
   Q.  How came you here as a witness, how did you know the trail was coming on? A.  I found the father of Riley out after I saw them in New gate, in Saffron-street—I do not know the witness Russell—I have known him since the 24th of April—on the Sunday I called on Riley—I did not go there with him—I never saw Russel at Riley's—I have only seen him in Court, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday—I never saw him before Monday—I was never at his house, nor he at mine—I never met him at any place—I found out Riley's father by his son's directions.
