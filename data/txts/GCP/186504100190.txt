WILLIAM MOUBRAY. I am storeholder in the Royal Arsenal - I have
charge of the condemned stores; I have the superintending of the sales of all
of the o-I have seen the leather produced bere to-day; this large piece is
seat-leather, to cover cloaks, and for other purposes-leather of this kind is
never sold; it is new serviceable leather, unconverted--this hide is Govern-
ment leather blacked; that is also new, serviceable leather-this is some
more of the same not coloured-I have seen it all-I find the broad arrow
on some of it, and the War Department mark.
Cross-examined. Q. What stores pass through your hands ? A. Leather
and blankets, chiefly the stores from foreign stations-blankets are not
always torn in four before they are sold-those that I sell are chiefly horse-
blankets, and I have sold the others as borse-blankets-if a skin of leather
became damaged, it would be made convertible for the repairs of the De.
partment work-sales take place once a quarter ; up to two years back we
used to have them more frequently-the last sale I had was on 12th Decem-
ber-I have a foreman to assist me, but under my superintendence- I see
everything as it is laid out.
MR. POLAND. Q. Do you find that this leather is damaged, so that it
could not be used ? A. No; it is fit for use.

483. THOMAS COOK (20), and CHARLES WALTERS (20), Felo-
niously cutting and wounding Richard Henry Dennis, with intent to do him
some grierous bodily barm.
MR. DALEY conducted the Prosecution, and MR, TAYLOR defended Walters.
RICHARD HENRY DENNIS. I am a sawyer, and live at Princes road, Plum-
stead-on Monday evening, 20th March, I was in the Royal Oak, Plumstead,
with my wife, my two daughters, and my little son-I went out at the back,
and the two prisoners followed my son into the room and insulted my daugh-
ters-Cook pulled them about, and tried to kiss them-Walters did nothing
to my daughters; he tried to get Cook out-they were got out with the
landlord's assistance Cook then went to the bar, and accused my daughter
of taking a watch-they were got out of the house twice, and about a
quarter of an hour after that we left - I had the best part of a quart of ale
in my hand, which was for supper-rome one, I cannot say who, knocked
me down, and then Cook knocked me down a second time-I got up, and
was going up the road ; Walters then turned round, knocked me down, knelt
on me, and Cook stabbed me with a knife on the top of my head-I saw the
blade shine---my little daughter came up and pushed them off-I got up,
and was knocked down again by Walters-my son came up, and they ran
away-with the second blow the knife closed-I am very weak still, and
not able to work-I was also struck with the knife when it was half shut.
Cross examined. Q. Had you had some drink? A. A little was not
more excited with drink than I am now; I had only had three pints and a
half in the day-I bad three parts of a quart in my hand, but that was spilt
- I do not think I struck with the pot, but I might have, without being
aware of it.
