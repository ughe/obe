   WILLIAM PAYNE. I am clerk to the Magistrates at Guildhall. On the 18th of January I took a deposition against Lowe for having bad money in his possession; it was read to Scott and Prothero, and they signed it - there were afterwards two or three examinations, in which Lowe and Rose were made witnesses; what they said is in the depositions.
 MR. ALLEY. Q. After the depositions, were made did you hear the prisoners say any thing? A. I have two or three different statements at different times; on the 2nd of February, after the deposition was read over, Prothero and  Scott  both said they had been led into it by Rose - Evans then said "I know nothing of it; I never had any dealings with the man in my life" - Perkins was there, but he did not say any thing; on the 28th of January Murray only was in custody, and Scott made a deposition against him - Scott said, "I know the prisoner, he came to fetch me to take Lowe into custody; he said, there were two men on Holborn-hill that he suspected had bad money in their possession - I went with him, and he pointed out Lowe, who was standing against the lamp-post; I took him to the station, searched him, and found the bad money in his hat - I have seen Prothero the officer and Murray frequently together; he went with us to Kennington the same evening, when we went to try to find out what sort of a character Lowe was - I never saw the prisoner and Perkins together" - on the 30th of January Perkins was in custody, and Mr. and Mrs. Lowe gave their evidence against him; after the evidence Perkins said -
 COURT. Q. Has there been any statement of Perkins' which has been reduced to writing, and signed by him? A. No.
 Q. Can you, by looking at that paper, so far refresh your memory as to state that was what he said? A. Yes, my Lord, he said at first he had no question to ask of the witnesses; he then said, that on Saturday Lowe had been tutoring his wife - he said they had been employed by Mr. Powell in several cases, and had received about 30l. from him - he said that  George Roberts and George Bailey were convicted on their testimony, and he knew a man. named Myers, a tobacconist, at Newington-causeway - Thomas Prothero was then called in, and said that he had seen Murray a day or two before he took Lowe into custody; he did not know Murray's business, but he had seen him with letters cut in iron or steel - he had seen Scott and Murray speak together several times; that they had come together to the Old Bailey - he first saw Murray when he apprehended a man named Carter, in St. Paul's-church-yard - he said further, that he took Lowe soon after he was dismissed, at seven o'clock, when he was going away from duty; he had not seen Murray before he was discharged that day, to the best of his knowledge - that Murray came running up the hill; he said he sometimes had the key of the watch-house, and his partner had it at other times.
   WILLIAM ROSE. I am a butcher, and live at No. 6, Charles-street, Westminster-road. I recollect when Lowe was taken on this charge - I had seen Evans about two months, or nine weeks before Lowe's business - Evans came to my house one afternoon, and said,"Are you doing any business now for the Mint" - I said, No, I was not - he said, "Have you any objection to doing business along with two or three friends of mine over the water, who are two City officers?"- I consented, and said, "When can I see them?" - he said, "If you will be in the way to-morrow evening, from six to seven o'clock, I will call for you" - he called the next evening, and I accompanied him to the Horse and Groom, in Blackfriars-road - he had told me that his friends over the water had plenty of money to go to work with - we got to the Horse and Groom between seven and eight at night, and he introduced me to Scott, Prothero, and Murray - after sitting a little while, he said to Scott, "This is my friend," (meaning me) - Scott said to me, "You have done business for the Mint before, I understand?" - I said, Yes - he then said, "Have you any objection to go into business along with us?" - I said not - he said, "Can you get me a man or two over the water for possession (I understood he meant to have counterfeit money on their persons) - I said I could not get them, I did not know where to find them; but I told him I knew two or three informers on our side the water whom I would introduce to him - that was all that passed then on that subject - I afterwards went to the Horse and Groom again, and I introduced James Hinton to all the prisoners, except Perkins - that might be a week or ten days after I first saw them - I went there again just after Twelfth-Day - I saw all the prisoners but Perkins - it must have been between seven and eight o'clock in the evening - the landlady made us a present of two glasses of rum and water, and some cake - I was very often in company with some of the prisoners after that; we had meetings at the Blue Lion - on the Wednesday week before Lowe was apprehended on the Thursday, Hinton, and all the prisoners but Perkins, met there - Evans took me from there about nine o'clock down the London-road, opposite St. George's-market, and told me where to go to call on Perkins; I went there by myself, singly, leaving Evans in the road - I found Perkins at home, without his coat - I asked him to take a walk out with me - he put on his coat, and came out; and I made an appointment to meet him the next afternoon at a public-house at the foot of Westminster-bridge, which he did;
