   MR. CROUCH . Q.  Was it covered with blood? A.  Yes, and Trenate's hands also—there are the remains of blood, exactly as I found it—after I apprehended Jones my hat fell off, in consequence of a violent struggle which ensued—he threw me, and the knife fell out—in going down the road I saw Mr. Hinton, a saddler, and I gave the knife to him, as Jones was very violent.
   MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Whereabouts was Jones in the coal-cellar? A.  At the far side, as far as he could get—he was on his face—he had no appear ance of being in liquor—I did not smell his breath. WILLIAM SANDFORD. I am foreman to Mr. Beard, and live in Red Lion-yard, at the foot of Westminster-bridge—it is the yard behind the prosecu tor's premises—the back gates were open on the night in question, as my master was out—my attention was attracted by the policeman running about—he told me to stand back, which I did, till he had taken two of the pri soners—he called for a light, and I brought one—I picked up in the yard three spoons, a constable's staff, and some cigars—I gave them to the ser geant No. 3. WILLIAM SIMONS( police-sergeant L  3.) I received some articles from Sand ford, which I produce—I found some cigars, and 1 found a jemmy  in the coal-cellar—I dug it out of the coals—I found two german silver forks and a spoon in the bar-parlour, and a pair of plated sugar-tongs—the life-preserver was found on the cellar steps—I tried the jemmy  to the bar window, and it fitted it.
   Cross-examined by MR. HUDDLESTON . Q.  Where in the cellar did you find the crow-bar? A.  Under the coals, nearly in the centre of the cellar—I found it about twenty minutes after I first came up—I did not go into the house the first time—I went through the kitchen, down the stairs, into the coal-cellar—there might be, perhaps, a ton of coals in the cellar. MATTHEW FRENCH WAGSTAFF. I am surgeon to the police-force—I saw Mr. Mackintosh at the station on the morning, I was sent for—he appeared to have a contused wound on the temple—it was done by some heavy instru ment—this life-preserver would have done it—there is danger attending a blow on that part—the eye has been discoloured and swollen—it is very slightly so now.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Was it not such a blow as might have been given by a man's fist? A.  I think not—it must have been some heavy instrument—the skin was not broken, but there was that appearance to lead me to suppose it was some heavy instrument—it is possible a violent blow with a fist might do it.
   MR. MACKINTOSH re-examined. Q.  What was the length of time between your receiving the blow and what took place outside in the yard? A.  I be lieve that must have taken place at the same time—I heard no sound of violence outside—I never heard more than one foot in the house.

   JONES  — GUILTY. Aged 23.— Death Recorded .
