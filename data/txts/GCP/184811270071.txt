109. ELIZABETH WILLIAMS and LYDIA LEE, stealing 5 yards of
fringe, value 28.; the goods of Elizabeth Clarke, from the person of Mary
Watkins.
MARY WATKINS. I am in the service of Mrs. Clarke. On 21st Nov., at
half-past ten in the day, I was in a lane at Kensington with a parcel contain-
ing a visite trimmed with fur, and some fringe by the side of it-I met the
prisoners in the lane-Williams asked me if I would have my fortune told
I made no reply, but was going on, when Lee snatched at my parcel—the
paper gave way, and she took the fringe-two young men at the top of the
lane turned and looked-she threw it down and ran off-the constable ran
after them.
Lee. I picked it up, said, "Here is your fringe," shook the dirt off it, and
gave it you, and you said, “Thank you !" Witness. It is not true.
ELIZABETH CLARKE, I am single. Watkins is my servant- sent her
with a parcel containing a visite and some fringe-it belonged to Mrs.
A'Becket, a customer.
HENRY COLLIER (policeman). I took the prisoners at the Admiral Kep-
il. WILLIAMS-GUILTY. Aged 10. Confined Six Months.
LEE-Guilty, Aged 15.
110. GEORGE WILSON was indicted for embezzlement.
ROBERT CLARKE. I am a licensed-victoaller, of Pimlico. The prisoner
was my potman six or seven weeks-he absconded on the 28th-four shil
lings were due to him-it was his duty to receive money and settle for it on
Saturday evenings-he did not settle before he left for any money for that
week-be paid me no money received from Mr. Jones. S o bow
WILLIAM JONES. I live at Denham-road, Chelsea, and dealt with Mr.
Clarke for beer-the prisoner brought it-the last money I paid him for Mr.
Clarke was 78. 2d.-I usually paid on Saturday night.
Prisoner. Q. Did I give you any bill? 4. No; I dealt with Mr. Clarke.
not with you.
JOHN SMITH (policeman). I took the prisoner.
Prisoner's Defence. I sold the beer on commission; I was not able to make
up what I had received; I intended to pay Mr. Clarke as soon as I could.
GUILTY. Aged 27.-Confined Four Months.
