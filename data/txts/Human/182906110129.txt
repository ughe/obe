Since I have been in prison I have heard the heart-reading intelligence of the death of my mother; let all these circumstances be considered, and let my youth and the miseries I have experienced in my short career, plead in my behalf, and I trust you will not consign me to further disgrace on the accusation of one more experienced in crime than myself.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1294.  ROBERT WATTE was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of May , 1 child's cart, value 8s., the goods of David Field.
 DAVID FIELD. I am a  labourer. I bought a little cart to draw my children out - I saw it about six o'clock in the evening in my kitchen; it was missed next morning at seven o'clock - it is a lodging-house, and we cannot keep the door shut - the prisoner is a stranger.
 Prisoner. Q. Was it in use on the Sunday? A. No; it was sometimes in the yard.
   WILLIAM JAMES. I am a labourer - I live in Meard'scourt; the prosecutor lives at No. 1 Kemp's-court. On Monday the 25th of May, I saw two young men with this cart, in Berwick-street - I cannot say whether the prisoner was one - I went to Kemp's-court, saw Mrs. Smith standing at the door, and she asked me if I thought David had lent his chaise to any body - I went into the brewhouse, and saw the prosecutor's brother, John, who told me it was not lent; and I then went in pursuit of it - I did not come up with it till I got to Panton-street, Haymarket, Where I saw the prisoner drawing it; two other persons were behind him - I asked what he was going to do with it; he said he was a spring-maker, and he was going to repair one of the springs - I saw it had no springs - I told him if he came by it honestly, he would have no objection to go to the person it belonged to - he turned, and drew it as far as Rupert-street; the other two walked behind; one of them called out,"Bob, what a fool you are to draw it;" he made no answer, but walked on till he got to the corner of Archer-street. when he dropped the handle and ran off - I cried, Stop thief! a person got in the middle of the road, he turned back towards me, and I collared him.
 Prisoner. Q. Look at me, and say whether I was one of the persons you first saw with it? A. I did not take notice - you did not ask a young man if he did not give it you - there were three of you; one had an iron footman in his hand.
 Q. Was not there a person you had a communication with yesterday, and you said you were very sorry you had any thing to do with this business? A. I said it was a great deal of trouble - I was very sorry, as I was very near losing my situation - I suspect it was a person who had followed the cart - I do not think I told him to cut his stick, and say he was one of them.
 THOMAS GOOK. I am a constable. I came up about seven o'clock, and James had hold of the chaise in one hand, and the prisoner in the other; I took the prisoner.
   ELIZABETH SMITH. I live in the parlour of the same house as the prosecutor - I saw the cart in the kitchen on Sunday, about eight o'clock; on the Monday, about seven o'clock, I saw two men taking it through the passage and out at the street-door - I looked out and saw James coming up the court - I told him to ask Mr. Field if he had lent the chaise; I did not notice the persons - I saw it again about eleven o'clock before the Magistrate.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was turning Coventry-street, and saw two young men drawing the cart - I knew one of them, and told them where I had been; we walked down the Haymarket, and one of them who had a white handkerchief and a snuff-coloured coat, asked me to take hold of the cart, while he gave his parcels to the other men- I had not had it two minutes when Mr. James came up - I turned back, and asked the young man whether he did not give it me - I might have run away; but knowing I received it innocently, I scorned to run till I saw the other two running, and then I did run a little way.
 Three witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined One Month.
 1295.  JOHN WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of April , 2 china jugs, value 4s. the goods of William Butler.
   WILLIAM BUTLER. I keep an earthenware-shop, in Silver-place, New Road. On the 10th of April I saw my jugs safe at half-past eight o'clock in the evening, and was showing them to a person - in a quarter of an hour I missed them, but did not see them taken - in about half an hour the watchman brought them - he asked if I had lost any thing - I told him I had lost two jugs; he then pulled them from under his coat.
