 MRS. CALLENDAR. I was at that house on the 4th of August, from nine o'clock in the morning till four o'clock in the afternoon; my laundress man came with the cart - he was the only person who called on my account.
   HENRY BUCKEREDGE. I am an officer. The prisoner was brought to the office; he said he lived in York-street. which I found to be the case: I went into the room in York-street, he said he had been to the Leith and Berwick wharf - that he had been on board the Sir William Wallace packet to see if a letter had been brought up for McKenzie, and he had seen the steward and the cook - I was then sent out, and returned about two o'clock; the prisoner then stated that he had eleven shillings or twelve shillings in his pocket - he said he had got nineteen shillings for a Scotch not which he had exchanged near the Haymarket, and bought a bottle of whiskey, paid some money to a green grocer, and a score to a man who lived in the Edgware-road; I calculated the whole together, and it was twentyfour or twenty-five shillings.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You say he told you where he slept? A. Yes; the robbery was committed on the Monday; he was on the wharf on the Tuesday - I searched his trunk, but found nothing belonging to the prosecutrix.
   GEORGE MOSS. I am clerk to the Leith and Berwick Smack Company. On the 5th of August, the prisoner came with another man to enquire if they could get Scotch notes for gold - I said the clerk who had the Scotch notes were not in the way, but just then Mr. Thompson came in, and I referred to him; the prisoner's companion went to him - the prisoner said nothing.
 Cross-examined. Q. It was not asking gold for notes? A. No; Scotch notes for gold.
 COURT. Q. You saw neither gold nor notes? A. No, I recommended them where they could get notes.
 MR. PHILLIPS to MR. GULL. Q. Do you remember that woman telling you she was about to allow the prisoner and McKenzie to sleep in the house? A. She proposed to me that they should come to the house, and I said"That is a thing I by no means approve of;" I did not mumble that, she perfectly understood it, because the answer came immediately, "Very well, Sir," and on the same afternoon my house-maid went to ask whether the female servant whom I wanted to sleep with Jane Thompson, could not come - I expressed my disapprobation in a tone that she could not have avoided understanding, and she said, "Very well, Sir."

 1817.  WILLIAM BENNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Joseph Bailey , from his person.
 JOSEPH BAILEY. I am a  warehouseman, and live in Lad-lane. On the 30th of July, about four o'clock, I was in Goswell-street; I felt a pull at my pocket - turned round, and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his coat - I had used it about two minutes before; I took hold of him, and he dropped it down; I took it up - I know it to be mine - it has my mark on it - I have made enquiry respecting his friends, and find they are very respectable.
   JAMES CLIFFORD. I am an officer. I was sent for and took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. There was a caravan at the corner of Sutton-street; the horse would not go on - there was a mob; I stopped where this gentleman turned round and accused me of taking his handkerchief, which I never had.
 One witness gave the prisoner a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 14.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury -    Judgment Respited.
 1818.  HENRY BARNES was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of Henry Henman , from his person.
 HENRY HENMAN. I was sitting at the back of the Stratford coach, on the 6th of September, coming to town, when the officer called to me that I had lost my handkerchief - I felt my pocket, and it was gone; I did not see the prisoner till he was secured, in three or four minutes.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   FRANCIS KEYS. I am an officer. I saw the prosecutor riding on the coach - the prisoner got up behind, and I saw him put his left hand into his pocket, and take out the handkerchief; my brother officer went and stopped the coach - I took the prisoner to the watch-house with the handkerchief.
 The prisoner handed in a petition, soliciting the clemency of the Court, and regretting the consequences of evil associates.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Whipped and Discharged.
 1819.  WILLIAM RICKWOOD and JOSEPH HOPKINS were indicted for stealing, on the 3d of August , 9 lbs. of mutton, value 5s. the goods of Amelia Calder Stewart.
   CHARLES CROGGETT. I am a watchman of Lissongrove. On the morning of the 3d of August, I was on duty, and saw the two prisoners about half-past five o'clock, standing together, opposite No. 13. Dorset-street - I watched them. and saw something pass into Rickwood's hands over the area rails, and they turned into a stable-yard which leads to Crawford-street; I pursued, and saw Rickwood throw down a piece of meat; I cried Stop thief! - they were stopped at the top of the mews by two men; I brought them back, and took up the meat, which was a leg of mutton and a small piece - I went to the area, and the person who had been there had made his escape - there was a ladder down the area.
