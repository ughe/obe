 1318. WALTER GRIGG and GEORGE AUSTIN were indicted for stealing two loaves of bread, value 10 d  .; the good of Richard Trew , from the person of Charlotte Trew .—2nd COUNT  , stating them to be the property of Charlotte Trew .
   GRIGG  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 17.— confined one month . CHARLOTTE TREW. I am the daughter of Richard Trew. I live at Wim bledon, with my father and mother—on the 6th of March, I was at the bottom of the White houses, at Wimbledon, about half-past twelve o'clock—I had my little sister Eliza with me—I was carrying four half-quartern loaves home—the prisoner Grigg came up to me—Austin was about twenty yards behind me—he was near enough to hear what Grigg said—Grigg said, "what have you got?" he pulled my apron open, and snatched out two loaves—I said, "Grigg, I know you"—Austin said, "Do not take it, she knows you"—Grigg ran to the back of the white houses—the groom ran after him. JAMES ROOTS. I am groom to Mr. Wilson. I came up, and Charlotte Trew was crying—Austin was standing there—Grigg ran down a turning—I ran to take him, but he got off—he had no bread then—the policeman came up—showed him which way the boy ran, and he saw the prisoners and another sitting in valley—we went and brought Austin—Grigg ran away—the policeman brought him off the farm, and he showed us where they had put the bread. EMANUEL STOCKLEY( police-constable V  291.) I found the prisoners—Austin said, he never touched the girl, and had nothing to do with it.
   AUSTIN  — NOT GUILTY.
