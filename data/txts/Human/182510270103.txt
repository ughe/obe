Re-examined. Q. Whose hand-writing do you believe it to be? A. I believe it to be Mr. Leslie's.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. If you had not seen Mr. Leslie's name, should you have believed it to be his hand-writing? A. No.
 COURT. Q. From the recollection you have of the character of Mr. Leslie's writing, do you believe that to be his independently of its being his name? A. Yes, my Lord, I believe it to be his writing - I lived with him nearly twelve months.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. How often did you see him write? A. I won't swear to above twice. I have seen him set down coals. I will not swear I saw him write his name above twice.

 HENRY SMITH. I am a  publican, and live at the King's Arms - I did keep the Lord Exmouth public-house, in Catherine-street; I have known Mr. Leslie ever since 1817, or 1818 - he ran a bill with me of 8 l. 2 s. 3 d., and some time in 1819 he paid part of it to my wife, and reduced the debt to 4 l.; it was paid in my parlour, and in my presence - I did not see him give her the 5 l. note - she gave him 1 l.; he said, "What is due now?" I said, "You have the note in your hand" - he said, "It is 2 s. 3 d.," which he paid, leaving the debt just 4 l. - he said, "Now I owe you 4 l." He began to be a coal-merchant some time in 1821, and I received directions from him about supplying beer - he contracted a bill for beer and other things at his house, between 3 l. and 4 l.; I knew of the supply of these things; he said he was going into the coal line, and he expected some money from the West Indies, and he would pay me altogether - we used to keep his account chalked up - he used to come to my house to spend his evenings, and was frequently looking to the place where his account was chalked up; he had an account of it rendered to him from time to time - he sometimes came and ordered the beer, and sometimes it was sent by my lad; I am sure he had the account delivered to him - there was no dispute between us at that time. He brought the bill one day to my wife in 1821, and said to her, in my presence, that he would not have his wife and his father-in-law to know it for a good deal - this was the bill for between 3 l. and 4 l.; my wife wrote it out, and my son delivered it to him - this bill was entered in the book I have here; it is partly my wife's writing, and partly my son's. After this I delivered him a second bill in 1822 - part of that was for beer for the coal-heavers - he came himself, and ordered us to let the men have sometimes one gallon, and sometimes two gallons of beer; there was none delivered to them but one gallon without his orders. The beer delivered to him in 1822 was by his order - it was sent down to the water-side to the men - when it was carried out I sometimes marked it up myself, the boy used to come back and say, "There is so much to set down to Mr. Leslie," and it was entered in the book from time to time - he used to come very frequently, and say, "That account of mine is getting very long. I must have it cast up." He used to come two or three times a week; he never called upon me to pay me any money, or said I owed him any till his affairs became embarrassed. There was an account of 15 l. 1 s. 8 1/2 d., which I delivered myself, after I was served with a copy of a writ, but I had delivered a similar account about October 1823 - after I left my house; that I can swear on my oath; he came evening or two after, and said it was all right but one gallon of porter; he never in his life said that it was not right till the action was brought against me - I owed him 14 l. 13 s. for coals, labour, and carting - so that there was 8 s. 8 d. owing to me. He has since been discharged under the Insolvent Debtors' Act - I was the assignee; this book was all that was handed to me.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you deal with him for any bushels of coal? A. Yes; but I always paid for them regularly. I never set off the account for the beer against these coals; when he had been at my house we never made any sort of balance - he never paid me any money but that 4 l. 2 s. 3 d. - I can swear I never stopped it out of the coals - I never was answerable to a person of the name of Best; nor did the defendant ever lend me any money to pay Best - I never requested him to pay Best any money; there was a 5 l. note given to Best, one night in my house.
