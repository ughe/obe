BENJAMIN PRIOR. I live at 28, Artillery-place, Woolwich, and am an eating house keeper—on 17th February I saw the prisoner standing at Mendez's door—I went next door to speak to a party, and presently I saw the prisoner come away from the shop into the road putting a coat on—that coat has not been found—it was not this one—I think it was a frock coat—I could not see, because it was dark—I saw him ran away, and I went to the shop—I am positive the prisoner is the man. MARTHA BORER. I am the wife of James Borer, of 90, High-street, Woolwich—on 17th February the prisoner came to my shop just upon 6 in the evening, with this coat on, and asked me if I would purchase it—I said, you must give me your name and address—he said, "My name is Thomas Sutton, and I have just come in from Gravesend, and I am going to lodge at Uncle Tom's, I have bought my bed there"—I then bought the coat of him for 4 s .—about a fortnight afterwards the prisoner came and said, "Have you got a coat to sell, I should like to buy that pilot coat I sold to you some time ago"—I said by way of excuse, "You shall have the coat, "and I sent for Mr. Mendez—I saw a policeman passing and called him in—I had received some information—I afterwards showed him this coat and he identified it.
   Prisoner. Q.  Can you swear I was the man who came to your shop? A.  Yes, positively. ROBERT HICKMAN( Policeman, R  172). I was called in, and took the prisoner into custody—I asked him where he got the coat from—he said he had bought it—I asked him who from—he said he did not know—he said, "Don't take me into custody, I will pay the 4 s . back"—he gave his name as John Smith—I found a discharge from the army on him, with the name of Sutton on it—I asked him if that was his name and he said, "Yes" GUILTY.— Confined Two Months .
