LUCY TATE. I am servant to Mr. John Powell, of Crosby-row. We dealt with Mr. Hayes, but do not now—I took in the bread—I always paid the prisoner regularly every day that he brought the bread—I do not re member any particular date, but it was always paid regularly daily—if we had a quartern loaf on the 25th of January I paid for it—he was in the habit of bringing bread from the 25th to the 28th of January, and I am quite certain I paid him on every occasion—I generally took in a quartern loaf.
   MR. HATES re-examined . It appears by the account furnished by the prisoner to me, that Mr. Powell owed money, not as paying the money daily.
   Prisoner . I used to pay him sometimes every other day, and at other times he let it run for a week. Witness . He paid me money perhaps every day—some days he would say there was nothing paid—I looked through my accounts, and he has not paid me this—he has made Mr. Powell a weekly customer—these entries are made in his presence—I could not prosecute him before, as I never saw him after he absconded. GUILTY.— Confined Two Months.

GEORGE WOOD and HENRY BLAGDON were indicted for stealing, on the   30th of June , 8 sacks, value 6 s  .; 5 bushels of flour, value 2 l  . 15 s  .; 4 bushels of meal, called middling, value 20 s  .; and 6 bushels of pollard, value 6 s  .; the goods of Thomas Andrews : and JOHN SAVAGE , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to be stolen; against the Statute, &c.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS ANDREWS. I am a miller, and live at East Moulsey. I have a brother, George William Andrews, who is a corn and flour dealer, and lives at Brentford—I grind his corn for him.
