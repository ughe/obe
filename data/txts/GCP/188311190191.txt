WILLIAM STEVENS. I am a smith, of 24, Wilson Street, York Road
-the police sent me to 28, Knox Road, and I saw a printing press which
works by hand- I took it to pieces; there was fresh oil on it, and I
should say it had been recently used.
Cross-examined by MR. FRITH. There were tympans in it, but it was
not fixed; it was out of gear, merely a bolt taken out, but it was all
there.

RICHARD RAINISH. I am a compositor and pressman to Messrs.
McOorquodale-on 10th October I went to Scotland Yard, and was shown
& printing press and some blocks and plates--I made these impressions
from them (produced these 25-rouble notes are what I worked off-I had
to get the ink and colouring matter the press had been recently used; the
oil was fresh and the runners bright-I also printed some five-rouble
notes from the articles found, but they were very imperfect.
Cross-examined by Mr. Besley. I am not a copper-plate printer, only
letter-press; they are quite different-I simply used the blocks; it could
be done with the printing press it was an Albion press, and stood on
the floor, not on a stand if a tall man used it he would put a block
under it, but it was just the height for me it weighed about two-
hundredweight-it seemed to me to have been regularly worked; it might
have been kept bright by rubbing, though no form had been used the
worked portions were quite bright, and I should say that it had been
used I worked without the parchment in, I had to put a pad in and
lay the plates with my eye-that was a slow and awkward way to do it.
Cross-examined by Mr. FRITH. The tympan plate was there an im
pression pin was missing-these are impressions pulled off the plate-
they have to be printed in six or seven colours.
Ro-examined. We required no pin blocks the sheets are perforated so
as to get them perfect.
JOSEPH TEÁK. I am manager of a department of Messrs. Spottis-
woode's-Hill has been employed there as a compositor since 1876-on
the day he was taken in custody he came at 8 p.m.--he has always borne
the character of a steady, honest, respectable workman.
Cross-examined by MR. FRITH. He has never worked at the press that
I know of, but in the country they combine the two branches.
Eliza CHARLOTTE BANKS. I am a widow, and live at 16, Lavender
Road-Holchester came there in the name of Chestor on 14th October,
1882-he occupied a front and back room-he brought two boxes, and
left two at the station-I did not see the boxes which the police took
away from his rooms, but they did not belong to me--the female
prisoner is my daughter-she lived with her husband, Hill, at 28, Knox
Road-he has lived there some years.
