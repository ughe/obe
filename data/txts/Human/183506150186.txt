   B. G. GILL re-examined . These are my seals and chain—there is very nearly half the chain gone—the men were first examined on Wednesday morning—there was only me and the constables before the Magistrates then.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you tell the Magistrate that Glannon said he hoped you would not press the charge against him? A.  Yes, I did—a constable who is not here took Glannon to the station-house—he was so deaf that they would not bind him over—I do not mean to say that the Magistrate said that was the reason—he might hear what Glannon said—I cannot tell.
   Q.  Have you asked for any money not to prosecute? A.  Not since they were committed—one party came to me before, and I said I did not wish to press the charge, knowing his father, if they would make good my watch and trowsers.
   Chappell. Q . You say you took the seals out of my hand? A.  I say they were in your hand—whether you dropped them, or I took them out of your hand, I cannot say—I could not see them in your hand, as I was lying on my back; but they were in it, as I had hold of it when you first made the attempt.
   Glannon's Defence.  I was at the public-house with two females, and two young men, handing the glass to the females—Gill came behind me, and snatched the glass out of my hand—he shook hands with me, and had share of five half-pints of gin with me at the bar—he went out, and came in again, and said a man had called him an informer, would I come out—I came out, and he began fighting with a man—I pulled him away, and he immediately charged this young man and me with stealing his watch—I said, "Have you lost your watch?"—he said, "Yes"—the policeman re  fused to search him—I took him into the road, and said he should go with me to the station-house—the policeman said that I was not to do that, but I insisted on holding him, and took him with me to the station-house, and insisted on his being searched—he knew very well where to find me at any time, if I had robbed him—he has been for three days before the bill was found, trying to get money to make it up—my sister said she had money to fee counsel, and he said if she would give it to him instead, he would make it up. EMMA GLANNON. I am the prisoner's sister. I saw Gill on the Wed nesday my brother was examined—he said he was very sorry this had hap pened, and he cried, and said it was a shame to rob a man who got his living by thieving; and if he had 3 l ., he would not appear against my brother—I said I had not got the money—he said he would get them re manded, and would make an assault of it—he used some very bad lan guage at the time—I saw him again last Thursday, as I was coming out of the New Court—he asked where I had been all day—I said I had been about the place—he said he had been looking for me, and had sent over the water for me—he said, "If you will give me the money you are going to give the counsel, I will throw the bill out—I will say I was very drunk"—he asked me to give him something to drink—I said I would—I had no money, and returned into the Court, and went out and treated him with a pot of half-and-half—I had no one with me.
