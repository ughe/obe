MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. JAMES MEAD. I am beadle and engineer of the parish of St. Olave, South wark—I have charge of the engine—after the fire at St. Olave's it was kept in the arch No. 9, at the bottom of King's Head-yard, Tooley-street—I kept the key of the arch. On the 23rd of Jan. I had my attention called to the archway—the evening before, the carpenter, whom I brought, secured the arch-door with a padlock—the stand-pipe and branch were there safe at eleven o'clock that night—my attention was called to it in the morning by a policeman, saying the place was broken open—I went about half-past seven o'clock—I then missed the stand cock, pipe, and branch—the prisoner keeps a marine-store and rag shop in Bermondsey-street—on Friday, the 26th, I saw the stand cock, and branch, which I missed, in the possession of the policeman who brought it from Mr. Paine's, in the lane—the prisoner's shop is about two or three hun dred yards from Tooley-street.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Was that all the property which was produced? A.  No, here is more—it was entire when I saw it the night before, and was worth between 3 l .; and 4 l .; when new—the prisoner's shop is in a very bad neighbourhood—there are plenty of bad persons about where it was stolen from. JAMES MILLERY. I am in the employ of Mr. Paine, brass-founder. Shoe-lane. I know the prisoner—between ten and eleven in the morning of the 24th of Jan. I purchased these thirty-nine pieces of metal of him—they would make a stand-cock and stand-branch of a fire-engine if they were entirev—it was broken when I bought it—I gave him 5 d . a pound for the brass, and 8 d . for the copper—I paid him 7 s . 5 d . altogether—there was a short man with him—about half an hour after, I delivered the metal to the officer.
   Cross-examined. Q.  There was nothing to attract your attention at being suspicious? A.  Nothing—I only took about a minute to buy it—I had often bought of the prisoner before—I went with the policeman to his shop on the Thursday. GEORGE WARDLE( City police-constable, No.  325.) Itook the prisoner on Thursday, the 25th of Jan., at his own house, in Bermondsey-street—I told him I came respecting some metal he bad sold to Mr. Paine, in Shoe-lane, on the Wednesday—he said, "I have sold some metal to Mr. Paine"—I said I was a policeman, and the metal be sold belonged to a fire-engine—he said he bad sold no such metal as that to Mr. Paine—I said I must take him. THOMAS BARNES'( City police-constable, No.  334.) From information I received, I went to Mr. Paine's, on Wednesday, the 24th—I there found this metal, with the name of the parish on—it has been indented, but the name is very distinct—it was by Mr. Paine's request that I looked at it. RICHARD FULLER( police-constable M  120.) Iwas on duty at seven o'clock in the morning of the 24th of Jan., and noticed the arch in which the engine was kept—at a quarter past seven I found the archway was broken open, and part of the engine gone—I found the padlock in the mud. JOHN HIOHWOOD( police-constable M  102.) Iwas on duty by this arch way on the night of the 23rd, and morning of the 24th—the lock was on safe at twenty minutes past six o'clock. JAMES MEAD re-examined.  These are the pieces which, when entire, would form the stand-cock and stand-branch of our engine. NOT GUILTY.
