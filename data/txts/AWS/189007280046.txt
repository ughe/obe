THIRD COURT.-Thursday, July 318t, 1890.
Before Mr Common Serjeant.
594. GEORGE THORNE (30), Stealing twelve watehes and two
cabinets, the goods of William Charles McEntee, his master. Other
Counts, charging him with larceny as a bailee.
Mr. ORMSBY Prosecuted, and Mr. TURRELL Defended.
WILLIAM CHARLEs McENTEE. I am a merchant, of 11, Queen Victoria
Street-the prisoner was traveller and agent for me-he was paid partly
by salary of 10s. a week, and partly by commission of 5 per cent.-on
21st May I gave him twelve watches, value 3 15s., which he was to take
to Robert Tueker and Co., merehants, of 12, Coleman Street-he said
they were wanted to buy ; I asked him if he had a written order; he
said, said, 46 Well, you may take the watohes, and bring me
back a written order "-he did not bring one back, but said the principal
would call that evening or next morning, and bring a written order-I
was satisfied-no written order came; I sent him again for it, and he
made some excuses-the watehes did not come back, nor did the money
come for them-on 31st May I gave him a liquor-stand with a cigar
cabinet at the bottom, value E2 brought the order for it from
Mappin and Webb-I told him to take it to them-I have never seen
the cabinet since, nor have I been paid for it-on 5th June I gave
him a similar article of a new pattern, value E2 3s.; he was to take
it to Mappin and Webb, and see if they would take
it came back, and said they had kept it have not
been paid for it - I did not see him after 5th June till I
had a warrant for him-I received this post-card from him on 6th June
(This stated that as he had a fow days' toork, preparing for stooktaking, he
should not be in till Tuesday or Wednesday.)-he did not come on the Tuesday
or Wednesday-he had no authority to take the watches anywhere else
except to Tucker, nor the cabinets anywhere except to Mappin and
Webb.
Cross-aramined. I first arranged to pay the prisoner commission only,
then he said he wanted something for expenses, and I agreed to give him
10s. a week for expenses till the Ist July, and if he did good I would
make a steady agreement with him-he travelled yery little-there was
no arrangement that he was to give me the whole of his time-I left
entirely to his discretion where he should get orders from-he might not
have shown the cabinet for the order from Mappin and Webb to any
other customer-the terms of eredit with Mappin and Webb were a
month-the prisoner was arrested a few days after 6th June, I think-he
never made claims, nor asked me for a commission, nor has he asked me
to give him an account of the orders he obtained for me, nor for a list of
