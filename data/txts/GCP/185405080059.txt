GEORGE BOND (policeman, D 265). On 19th April I went to the Abbey
Tavern, St. John's-wood, and went with Reed into the pleasure ground I
told the prisoner that he had been endeavouring to utter a forged 101. note
- he said he was not aware it was a bad one I took him into custody on
the way to the station, I asked him where he had got the note he said at the
hotel at which he was stopping, in Leicester-square-as we were going along
he made a snatch from me, and ran away-I ran after him, and caught him, he
then said he was a Frenchman - at the station inspector Chambers asked
him his name and address; he said, " Henry Fuchs, No. 77, Castle-street,
Leicester-square" he was asked his occupation--he said he was a tobacco
nist-the inspector asked him where he got the note he said he got it of a
man who came to buy a few cigars of him—he was then locked up that
same evening I went to No. 77, Castle-street, Leicester-square ; it was a
small tobacconist's shop, with the name of " H. Fuchs" over the doors 1
saw a few cigars there the shop was searched by another constable.

WILLIAM WYBURD. I am an inspector of Bank notes, at the Bank of
England. This note produced is a forgery—the paper is not the same as a
Bank of England note; there is an imitation of the watermark.
(The prisoner not being able to speak English with ease, made his defenos
in German, which was interpreted by one of the Jury, as follows: "Some one
that lived at an hotel in Leicester-square came to my shop, which is a cigar
shop, and purchased 28. worth of cigars, and tendered this note in payment;
I gave him the change, not knowing that it was a forged note; and two
days afterwards I went to this pleasure ground, where I was taken into
custody.") Guilty. Aged 24.- Transported for Fifteen Years.
