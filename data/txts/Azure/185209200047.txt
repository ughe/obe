905. JOHN PALMER and JOHN SHEEN, feloniously assaulting James
William Dowse, and stealing from his person 2 duplicates, a half sovereign,
and other monies ; his property.
MR. CAARTEEN conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES WILLIAM DowsE. I am a labourer, and live at 3, Paul-street,
Portman-market. On Wednesday night, 18th Aug., I was at Marylebone
Theatre with a friend, John Place—we left at about twenty minutes to 10
o'clock, and went to the Champion public house, about 150 yards off—when
we got to the public house I saw the prisoners there—I bad seen them about
there frequently before—I had known them by sight about two yean—I re.
cognized them at the Champion as persons with whom I was acquainted—
asked me if I was going to stand any beer—I stood some, of which
be partook, and Palmer. and my friend—we remained there five or ten
minutes, and then me, Place, and Sheen returned to the theatre, leaving
Palmer sitting in the public house—we all three returned to the public house
in about three quarters of an boar, aud found Palmer there—we had some
and all four remained five or ten minutes, and then Place, Sheen, and
me went back to the theatre—we remained there about an hour and a half.
•bea we three left together, and went again to the Champion—Palmer was
there—we remained about ten minutes, and then me and Place left to
go home—Sheen came out with us as well—I do not know what became of
Palmer; we left him in the public house—it was about ten minutesto 1 when
ve left—we all three went up Salisbury-stree in which street the Champion
is—Sheen left us in Salisbury-street, and me and Place went up Little
Church-street, which is a continuation Of Salisbury-street—when we had got
a 'bort distance Sheen returned, and asked me if I was coming—I do not
know what he meant by that—I and Place continued along Church-street,
nd Sheen returned a second time and said the same—I parted from Place at
the corner of Exeter-street and Little Church-street, and he went down
Exeter-street, and I turned back iti Little (hurch-street towards my home—e
I had been going away from home before—I saw Sheen at the corner
Exeter-street and Little Church-street—I was on one side and they were on
the other—Sheen did not follow me—he caught hold of me as soon u I
turned round—he was about five or six yards from me when I first saw him
Ifur I had left Place—he came right in front of me, and put both his bands,
in my neckcloth—l fell on my back on the ground from the effect Of his
strangling me, and while I was on the ground I saw Palmer—I had seen
them both together shortly before at the corner of Little Exeter-street—
Palmer was about five or six yards off when Sheen assaulted me—while I
on the ground Palmer took my purse and money out of my right hand
trousers pocket—it was a leather purse, and contained 108. in gold, a 58.-piece,
and two duplicates—while Palmer took my purse out Sheen was holding
me down by the neck—they then ran away—I got up. walked into Earl-
Street, and there met Clark the policeman, and returned with him into
ChUrch-street to the Sailor public house, where we found Sheen in the road,
and I gave him into custody—there was a horse and chaise there.
