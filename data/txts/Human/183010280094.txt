 2063.  THOMAS HISSEY was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of October , 1 coat, value 30s.; 1 waistcoat, value 4s.; 1 shirt, value 3s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 4s., and 3 handkerchiefs, value 4s., the property of George Edser.
   GEORGE EDSER. I am groom to Mr. Raine, of Uxbridge, On Sunday, the 24th of October, I lost my clothes out of my box, in the hay-loft, over the stable; I had seen them safe on the Sunday fortnight - I have seen two handkerchiefs which are a part of what I lost; I know them by one side being run and the other hemmed; the other things have not been found.
 Cross-examined by MR. J. ALLEY. Q. Then all you can speak to are the handkerchiefs? A. Yes; one of them has holes in the middle of it.
   HANNAH CHEESEWRIGHT. I made these handkerchiefs for the prosecutor about four months ago; I am sure of them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is there any mark? A. No; but I speak from the work - one side is hemmed, and one run in a loose manner; I swear it is my work.
   WILLIAM FAIR. I am a horse-patrol of Bow-street. I went in search of the prisoner at Harmondsworth, and left word with the headborough to send him to me; he brought him to me, with these handkerchiefs, on the 26th of October.
   ROBERT TOMKINS. I am the headborough. I took the prisoner yesterday week, between six and seven o'clock in the morning; my son said to me, "Here comes Tom Hissey;" I called to him as he had got by me, and said, "Young man I want you;" he set off running - my son pursued him about a mile, and then lost him; we went after him to Windsor fair, and in returning back we met him in Windsor Park - I said to him, "You are my prisoner;" he said, "Let me go, I will give you something;" I said, "No, I never did such a thing in my life, and I will not now;" he said to my son, "Take these handkerchiefs, and don't let the patrol see them."
 Prisoner's Defence. The new handkerchief I bought of Mr. Holloway, at Uxbridge.
     NOT GUILTY.
 2064.  THOMAS HISSEY was again indicted for stealing, on the 19th of October , 1 umbrella, value 5s., the goods of Matthew Newman.
 MATTHEW NEWMAN. I went to  Hayes, on a visit to my brother, on the 19th of October; I had an umbrella in my gig - I went to the gig at half-past eight o'clock in the evening, and the umbrella was gone; I had left it safe between three and four - I saw it again in possession of the prisoner, who was walking with it opposite my house; I let him go past about two hundred yards - I then ran to him, and said he had property which did not belong to him; he gave it me, and said he had picked it up - I am quite sure it is my umbrella, by two marks which appear as though they had been cut with a knife, and by a mark on the handle.
 Cross-examined by MR. J. ALLEY. Q. Were you out with your gig the day before? A. Yes; I swear it was in the gig when I arrived at my brother's; when I was about to return it could not be found - I had cautioned my brother's young man not to take it out of the gig; I put the gig in the gig-house; I saw it in possession of the prisoner the next day, at six o'clock in the morning - he passed close by me; he saw me but did not know me - he got on about two hundred yards and I walked after him; I had been speaking to a neighbour when he went along.
 JURY. Q. Did you see the marks you speak of in the umbrella as he went along? A. No; I knew it by its general appearance.
   HENRY FRIEND. I saw the prisoner about three or four hundred yards from Mr. Charles Newman's, between four and five o'clock in the afternoon; I think it was the 19th of October - he had nothing then.
   THOMAS CLEMENTS. I am servant to Mr. Charles Newman. The prosecutor came with the gig, between three and four o'clock, to see my master; I put the gig into the cart-house with the umbrella in it - when he was going to return, about half-past eight o'clock, the umbrella was gone - it was of this size and general appearance.
 Prisoner's Defence. I found it on the road.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Transported for Seven Years.

 2065.  JOB TAYLOR BAILEY was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of October , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of George Kearsley Davison , from his person.
 GEORGE KEARSLEY DAVISON. I am a  printing-ink-maker. I was passing in Holborn on the 15th of October, about eleven o'clock, near Chancery-lane, walking with my father, who is very lame; he was leaning on my arm- I felt my handkerchief taken from my pocket; I turned and saw the prisoner and another - I collared them; the other got away - the prisoner got from me; I caught him by the tail of his coat, and the tail of his coat came off - he crossed the way, and some gentleman caught him; this handkerchief was taken from behind his back- it is mine.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you see it taken from him? A. Yes; I do not think it possible for another person to have put it up his back.
 Prisoner's Defence. There were three lads following the gentleman; I got before them and some person called Stop thief! - the gentleman seized another lad, but he got away; knowing I was innocent I went to clear myself, and tried to get the other - I never had the handkerchief.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 20. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Confined Three Months.
