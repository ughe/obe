828. WILLIAM MANN (26) and DAVID PUGH (28), Unlawfully
obtaining 21. 108. from Ellen Brent, and attempting to obtain 501. from
Earah Peseean, by false pretences.
MR. MONTAGU WILLIAMS Prosecuted ; MR. GEOGHEGAN appeared for Pugh.
ELLEN BRENT. I live at 5, Bryanston Square-I saw an advertisement
in the Daily Telegraph for a lady to join a gentleman in a light profitable
business-the address was "É. C., care of Mr. Webb, 219, Liverpool
Road North--this is my answer (Found upon the prisoner, asking for full
partioulars)--on 10th August Mann called on me-he gave the name of
Harding, and said "I am replying to your letter in person. Do you want
this for yourself?”-I said "No, it is for an old friend of mine. What
business is it?"-he said "Drapery”-I said "That will not suit my
friend”-he called again on the i3th, and said "I think you will be
glad to hear that I have met with a lady partner who has a capital
of 9001., and I have 4001., will you kindly allow me the use of
sitting-room, as the lady lives at Hastings, and wishes to come up to
morrow morning to meet me in town to see the landlord, and it can
not be done at a restaurant or at any of the railway stations ?”-I said
that my dining-room was quite at his disposal for half an hour or so, and
he left-the appointment was for 11 a.m., and a little before 11 on the
14th Pugh came and said "I am going to wait for Mr. Harding and a lady"
-shortly afterwards Mann and Harding appeared, but no lady-I heard
Pugh talking very loudly, apparently vexed that the lady was not coming,
and Mann came out of the room and said to me "I have been down to
the station, and the lady has sent me a telegram saying that she could
not come up till Thursday, and the landlord had had another offer for
the house, and is going straight there to see the party, and unless I can
pay a deposit I shall lose the house, and the lady will be so disappointed;
I don't want to break faith with her, can you lend me just sufficient to
pay the deposit P”-I said "What does your landlord want?"-he said
i Well, I think he will be satisfied with 308. or so; I will go and ask him
how little he will take"-he went into the room where Pugh was-the
door was not open, but I was in the hall, and Pugh spoke very loud, and
said "I won't take less than 21. 108.”-Mann came back, and said "The
landlord won't take less than 21. 108.”-I wrote this cheque for 21. 10.,
and gave it to him, believing his statement. (Endorsed "Charles Harding")
-I believed Pugh to be the landlord of the house-Mann said that he had
only to go to his lodgings, and would bring me the 21. 108. immediately
-I told him I would wait till 3 o'clock for him, and did so, but he did
