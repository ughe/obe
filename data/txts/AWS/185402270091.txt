413. GEORGE JONES and GEORGE SMITH, breaking and entering
the dwelling house of John Richard Cornock, and stealing 50 shirts and
other articles, value 9L. 48. 5 the goods of William Henry Cornock.
Mr. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution
CLARA SMTTH. I am single, I am the niece of Mr John Richard Cor-
nock, of No. 6, Sussex-place, Kingsland his brother was liying there at
this time. On Friday, 17th Feb., I was the last up in the house-I made
the house safe--the back kitchen window was safe, it was secured by a
catch-there were some shirts and other clothes in that kitchen belonging
to my unele's brother went to bed at 12 O'clock, and got up in the
morning about 7 o'elock I went into the back kitchen, the window Was a
little way open, and a piece of glass taken outwhen that was out any one
could put in their hand and arm, undo the cateh and open the window-
the shirts, trowsers, socks, blankets, and a bag, were all gone.
HARRIETT FLAGG. On 17th Feb., I was living as Servant at Mr. Cor
nock's came down at 7 o'clock on the morning of the 18th-I found some
snow on the floor in the back kitchen, a chisel under the window, and a
fracture in the glass, the window was openI know the elothes had been
in the kitchen on the over night, and they were gone in the morning-the
had been in a bag together, I had not heard anything in the night.
JOHN HARvEY (police sergeant, G 14). On Monday, 20th Feb., I was
in Bishopsgate-street about 2 oclock I saw both the prisoners in company
-they were each carrying a bundle-I stopped them, and aaked what they
had got-they said some shirts and trowsers, and they had bought them of
a Jew in Golden-lane, and given 148. for them-I took them to the station,
and found these goods which were claimed by Mr. Cornock.
WILLIAM HENRY CORNoCK. I am the brother of the prosecutor, and
am a seaman. On the night of 17th Feb., I WaS staying at my brother's-
there was some of my property in the back kitchen, and it was gone in the
morning I have seen part of it since, what I have seen is part of what
was stolen that night-the value of all I lost, was about 121-here are
seventeen shirts, seven pairs of trowsers, and seven pairs of stockings, I
lost about fifty shirts.
