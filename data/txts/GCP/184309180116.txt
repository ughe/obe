John LANE. I am a cab-man. On the evening of the 14th of July I went
to the Wheat Sheaves, at Islington-I saw the prisoner and Soder there
they were drinking-1 joined them--she was rather intoxicated-she said it
she went to Dock-head she should be at home-I assisted the prisoner to put
her into my cab-he paid for it, and I went off to Dock-head with her."
Cross-examined. Q. Did she give her directions quite clear? 4. Yer
-she wished to be put into the cab—the prisoner was sitting down on the
seat alongside of her--we were all three drinking-at the time she left she
made no complaint, every thing appeared quite right-I did not know she
had any money, nor tell her to put it into her bosom-she was not sick.
JOHN RAMPLING. I am a yold-beater. On the 3rd of July I got a cheque
for 61. 168. cashed at Rogers's banking-house-I received a 5l. note and
some money-I do not remember the note the cheque was drawn by Mr.
George Donney-I gave the bank-note to Soder, with two sovereigns and a
half-sovereign, to go and purchase some fine gold-she left my house, and I
did not see her again till she was in custody.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you give her in charge ? A. My wife did – 1
gave her the money on the Tuesday- I had an excellent character with her
-she was with me six weeks or two months.

the publican-I gave the note to him-he changed it took the change

Cross-examined. Q. Did Wilkins, the policemen, come and inquire about

the truth-I might on that occasion have told a falsehood- I did not serve
the prisoner-I can remember a little about it-I took notice of the prisoner,
because he was in the shop such a time I had never seen him before I was
at the shop door- I went to the public house after he bought the things
remember he was the man that bought the things.
