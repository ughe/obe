Detective DIGBY, recalled. Cross-examined by Campbell. The
people living in the same house as you have spoken of yourself and
Rose Campbell as being quiet and respectable during the time you
were there. I did not know that Rose Campoell was keeping a fried
fish shop and looking after it. (To the Jury.) The cheques which I
found were in a locked box in the front room at 61, Lyttleton Street,
Earlsfield-Campbell's house. Those were National Provincial Bank
cheques.
Detective-inspector FRANK HALLAX, recalled. (To Mr. Holden.) 1
was present at the Bridewell Police Station when Childs was charged.
On September 5 I was in the Deodar Road, Putney, with other
officers. I there saw the prisoner Redgrave at about 11.30 at night,
I also saw McQueen. Redgrave entered the side door of MoQueen's
house ; McQueen remained at the garden gate. I went past MoQueen,
and just as Redgrave was closing the side, door I pushed it open and
said to him, "I am a police officer; I believe your name is Redgrave,
and I wish to speak to you outside. He said, "All right." MoQueen
was still at the garden gate; I think he must have heard it. I said
to Redgrave, “Let us walk down the street, we may see Davenport
Redgrave said, "I don't want to speak to Davenport--who is Daven
port-I do not know him." He closed the garden gate and held it. We
were both inside. A uniformed officer was passing by and he came
to my assistance. I said to Redgrave, "Now I am going to arrest
you." I took himn further down the street, where I saw Detective
Loakes and Detective Betteridge. I said to Loakes, "Is this Red
grave?" He said, "Yes." I said to the prisoner, "Shall I tell you
the charges now or will you wait until we get into the train." He
said, “In the train." I then took him to the train and there I told
him the charges--that he would be charged with being concerned with
a man named Herbert Arthur Childs, then in custody, with forging
and uttering an order form for a cheque book. I referred to the
other charges and took him to Bridewell Police Station, where he
Wits detained. When I saw Childs at the Bridewell Police Station in
September, I did not see any of the prisoners near or about the
station. Detective Harris saw McQueen. At two p.m. on Saturday,
Oetober 5, I was at the London Road Terminus Railway Station,
Manchester--the London and North-Western line. I was on the
arrival platform. I saw the prisoner Coleman, who was standing as
if he was apparently waiting for someone. At 2.15 I saw a train
arrive from London--the 10.30. I saw Campbell alight from the
train and speak to Coleman. Stollery was with Campbell. I spoke
to them and said, "I am a police officer, and you will both be arrested
for forgeries in London. I will tell you the charges when you arrive
at the police offices at the Town Hall." I conveyed them there. I
told both Coleman and Campbell what they would be charged with,
