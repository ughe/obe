 2002.  JOHN GAWTHORNE and JOHN SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of October , 7 pieces of leather, value 1l. 15s., the goods of George Waite.
   CHARLES HERDSFIELD. I am an officer. On the 7th of October, between-nine and ten o'clock, I was in Whitecross-street; I saw the two prisoners together - Gawthorne said to Smith, "To-morrow, mind; ten o'clock;" I said to Gawthorne, "What have you got there?" he said,"Some leather" - I asked him where he got it; he said, from Mr. Allen, of Green-arbour-court, Old Bailey, and that Mr. Armstrong had given him 30s. to fetch it and pay for it - I said, "You are not fetching it for yourself;" he said, No. I brought him to Giltspur-street.
   DANIEL FORRESTER. I was with Herdsfield, and took Smith.
   JOHN GOODWIN. I am in the employ of Mr. George Waite, a currier, of No. 16, Gray's-inn-lane. This is the same leather I saw at the examination, but I have seen so many pieces, I should not like to swear to them - I did swear to this piece, but there is no mark upon it; there was a piece of cordovan missing like this, but all shoulders are alike; I said this piece was my master's property, but I had no more reason for knowing it then than I have now - I have seen so many pieces like it since, that it makes me doubtful. Here is a mark on those other six pieces; I have no doubt but they were my master's; but the same person who marked these works for other shops.
 COURT. Q. You say in your deposition, "One of these seven pieces of leather, being a neck of cordovan, I saw on my master's premises, and I know it to be his property, and form the marks on the other pieces, I believe them to be his property" - what is your belief now? A. I believe it is.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You have stated that you swore to one piece of leather; is there your master's mark on it? A. No, my master never marks leather - here is the tanner's mark on it; there was a finger-mark on this piece which I swore to, but it is not here now - I only believe them to be his, and cannot say they have not been sold.
   GEORGE WAITE. I believe these seven pieces of leather to be mine; I have no doubt of it - I cannot say I never sold them, but I believe not. The prisoner was my apprentice, and had access to it (looking at his deposition) - I swore positively to them before the Magistrate, as that was my impression; there are marks on it made by a person who is now dead.
     NOT GUILTY.

 MARY EDWARDS. I live in Fashion-court, Spitafields. On the 23d of September my daughter gave me a 5l. note to redeem some pledges - I went to the  Golden Anchor public-house, Golden-lane, for change, as the pawnbroker had none - Mrs. Jones, the landlady, returned me the note, as she had no change; I went away with it, and returned again soon after, and held it up for change at the bar, when some person behind me twisted it out of my hand - I was frightened, and cannot swear to any one - it was the same note my daughter had given me.
   MARGARET TOMLINSON. I gave Edwards, who is my mother, a 5l. note, which my brother had given me - it was a new note - he had been paid off the day before; I have not seen it since.
   SOPHIA FORDHAM. I live in Golden-lane.
