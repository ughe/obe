Skinner pleaded guilty of all the charges. Miles pleaded guilty of obtaining the two cheques, and of the conspiracy.
 The two persons with whom Skinner had conspired, John Joseph Walker and Harry Miles the other prisoner, were meat and fish con  tractors respectively, and contracts had been placed in their hands by Skinner for supplying such goods to the asylum. The system adopted of falsifying the ledger was an ingenious one. It was stated on behalf of Skinner that he had previously borne a good character, and that he had been tempted by reason of insufficient supervision and auditing of the accounts. Miles was also stated to have borne a good character.
 Sentences, Skinner, 18 months' hard labour; Miles, Nine months' hard labour.
