GEORGE SONLEY VENUS. I am clerk to Messrs. Linklaters--they are
solicitors to the trustee under the defendant's bankruptcy-I produce the
proceedings in his bankruptcy under the seal of the Court--he is described
as of the Sun" public-house, Blackheath Hill-the petition was presented
on 4th August, and the trustee Mr. Blackie, was appointed on 6th Septem-
ber-on 23rd September, the defendant was examined in the Court of
Bankruptcy-I took down his evidence-it was afterwards read over to him
and he signed it, and swore to it-the amount of his debts to unsecured
creditors was 6371. 10s. 9d., rent and taxes 381. 138. 4d., and the assets nil.
-Taylor, Walker & Co., are creditors to the amount of 2801. and a further
gum of 201. for rent—that is also unsecured-Sewell, Blackie & Co. are
put down as creditors for 2321., but it was proved not to be so much.
Cross-examined. They afterwards proved for 1821.-the account of
Taylor, Walker & Co. is 801. for beer and 2001. loan-the account of
Cockerell & Co. for coals is not proved--the other accounts filed by the
bankrupt are, Slee & Co. 21. 28. 6d., Hall & Co. 261. 188., Dingwell & Co.
41. 88., Lyell & Glyn 51. 68. 6d., Henley & Co. 61. 98. 6d., Stevens & Sons
461.it does not appear what the amounts were for-he became bankrupt
on the petition of Messrs. Sewell & Blackie. (The bankrupt's examination
was put in and read).
HARRY JONES. I am a public-house broker, and reside at 4, Brunswick
Terrace, Commercial Road-I was employed by Taylor, Walker & Co., the
brewers, to let the “Sun," public-house, at Blackheath--I saw the defen-
dant on that subject, in October, 1869–he had been down previously
to the brewery, and had seen Mr. Sewell, the manager, and expressed
a desire to take the house, and Mr. Sewell referred bim to me we met the
next morning, at the brewery, and an agreement was prepared, and Mr. Fair-
child paid a deposit of 501. into my hands-he agreed at that time to pay
2001., and the firm was to lend the other 2001.- he signed this agreement
in my presence. This was a memorandum of agreement, dated 26th October,
1869, between William Henry Sewell and George Emberson Fairchild, and
witnessed by Harry Jones, to assign over the "Sun," tavern, at Greenwich,
in consideration of the sum of 4001. to be paid, and as tenant to Messrs.
Taylor, Walker & Co.-Fairchild to pay 801. per year, and deliver up posses-
sion of house and such premises as might be under-let, also to make good, and
repair the premises-possession to commence on 3rd November, 1869–501. to
be now paid, in part payment of the purchase money –He was able to write
at that time, but with difficulty-his arm was affected; he was not
aware at that time it was broken-1 revious to 3rd November, I made an
inventory, and on that day I saw the defendant at my office, for the
purpose of completing the arrangement-there was a person with him
whom he introduced as his brother, or brother-in-law -- he stated
that he was prepared to complete the agreement, and to pay the balance
of the money, and I prepared the equitable mortgage - 2001. was to
be lent by the brewers, and he gave an equitable mortgage for that
on 3rd November, he put his mark to that agreement- This was
an agreement to become tenant at 801. a year, of house and fixtures, with
power to the landlord to re-enter if half a year's rent should be in arrears,
