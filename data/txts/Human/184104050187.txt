 1237. MARY ANN COX was indicted for a misdemeanor. ELIZABETH TUCKETT. I conduct the National School in Little Guildford-street, Bloomsbury. About one o'clock on the 10th of March, I was at the school—the prisoner came to me and stated that she met two ladies in the square, and one of them was Miss Powell, who was a teacher in the school—she said that Miss Powell met another lady, who was a teacher in the school, but she did not know her name, and described her dress—that one of these ladies bad bought a bottle-jack of her, and requested it to be brought to me, to be left with me, till she should send her servant to fetch it—she said I was to pay her 5 s . for it, and this lady's servant should bring me the money—the bottle-jack was worth 10 d . ort 1 s .—believing she came from the lady, I gave her the 5 s .—I knew Miss Powell very well—I wondered they had not paid for it.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You had some doubt about the truth of her story? A.  I cannot say that I doubted, but that remark came into my mind—Little Guildford-street leads out of Great Guildford-street—when she first entered the room, she asked for Miss Tuckett—I said I was Miss Tuckett—she said she had met two ladies in the square, that one was called Miss Powell, that she was a teacher in the school, that she wore a merino dress with two flounces, a scarf, shawl, and a black velvet bonnet, and that was her dress (Miss Powell had been with me not five minutes before,) and that she had met another lady who wore a black shawl and a black silk dress, and that they had purchased the jack of her.
   Q.  If she had come and used the name of Miss Powell, without describ ing her dress, should you have let her have the 5 s .? A.  I very likely should, but the dress confirmed to me that she had evidently seen Miss Powell. CATHERINE POWELL. I live in Great Coram-street, Brunswick-square. I did not send the prisoner with the bottle-jack—I never saw her till she was before the Magistrate—I did not authorize her to go to Miss Tucket and get two half-crowns.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know this other lady by the description of her dress? A.  Yes, Miss Harper, of Henrietta-street—she is a teach in the school with myself—I did not see her that day. EMMA GAY. I was at the school that day—the prisoner came a bottle-jack, and got 5 s . GEORGE BOTT( police-constable E  26.) I took the prisoner, and have the bottle-jack—it is worth 10 d . GUILTY.
