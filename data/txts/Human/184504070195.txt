Q.  Who told you it was the 4th of March? A.  I heard some young men talking about the 4th of March—they were not of our party—when they said it was 4th of March, I began to recollect all about the 4th of March—I did not recollect the day of the week—I recollected that it was the 4th of March when'the sheep were taken—I was not there when they were taken, only I heard them say about it on the Monday—I then recollected Hack was in the room with me—I am sure that was the day.
   COURT . Q.  What day of the month was Monday? A.  I am no scholar, I cannot think of it now. GEORGE ARGENT. I am a gentleman's senrant, and work in the garden of Mr. Drake, at Ley tonstone. I can take my oath that Hack was in bed on the night of the 4th of March—it was on the Tuesday—I heard of his being taken up on the Monday following—there were two beds in the room where we slept that night—I slept with John Faulkener—I was in bed first—he sat up by the fire taking his supper—I had just gone to bed before he came in—I do not recollect what he had for supper—he was in the other room—the living room is not the bed-room—he was sitting by the fire when I went to bed—Hack was in bed before I went to bed—Charles Baron slept with him—I cannot tell where Baron is—I was awake when Faulkener came to bed—I cannot tell whether he smoked his pipe before he came to bed—he is in the habit of smoking, and so was I after supper—I had my supper before he came in—I had some pickled pork—I left none for him—I did not finish it—we board ourselves. NOT GUILTY .

 980. ALFRED CALLOW and GEORGE MOUNTJOY were indicted for stealing, on the   5th of April , 1 mare, price 2 l  ., the property of Thomas Dyer . THOMAS DYER. I am a carpenter, and live at Forest-gate, in Essex. I had a grey mare on the forest on the 5th of April—I saw her last at two o'clock that day—she is a very quiet mare—I went to look for her in the evening, finding she did not come home at her usual time, and at half-past ten I found her at the station—she was dose against Forest-gate, in West Ham parish, on the Wanstead-flats. EDWIN DIVEB( policeman. ) I met the two prisoners about half-past seven o'clock in the evening, against Can Hall-gate, about a mile and a half from Wanstead-flats—it was not part of the forest—Callow was riding the mare—he had on a jacket and collar—Mountjoy was walking at the head of the mare—this is the bridle they had—( producing a boy's red comforter )—the toll-man stopped them, and demanded his fare, which was 1 1/2 d .—Mountjoy said, "Wont it do when we come back?"—I said to the toll-man, "I don't think this is right, I will question the boys"—I asked them who the mare belonged to—they said, "To Mr. Davis, an ironmonger, of Stratford"—knowing there was no such person in Stratford, I said, "Does he keep a shop!"—Callow said, "Yes, he keeps an ironmonger's shop, it has been lately opened"—I said, "How long has it been opened?"—at last Mountjoy said, "I will tell no more lies about it, we took the mare off the forest; I took this off my neck, and Callow put it on the horse's neck, and rode it away;" and they meant to take it to their uncle's stables, in Beer, or Vera-yard, I did not properly understand them at the time.
