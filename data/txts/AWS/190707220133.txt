WILLIAX NORTH TBARL.E, assistant to Stanley Caney, 44, Cheapside,
jeweller. On May 14, 1907, at 1 to 1.30 p.m., a man, who I identify
as the prisoner, came into my shop. I showed him some diamond
rings on a tray; he selected a five-stone half-hoop lady's ring at E22.
He looked at it several times and said he would like to know the
weight of the stones in it, that he would come'baok again in an hour,
as he had not suflieient money, and was going to the bank. I put the
ring aside, he returned, and again handed it to him. He took it
to the door and then asked me if I would let him know the weight
of the stones. I said I would refer, and went to the end of the shop
to turn up the stoekbook. I heard a sound, looked up hurriedly, and
found the shop empty, and that the prisoner had gone with the ring.
Mr. Caney, who was attending to something else in the shop, ran out
after him. I remained in the shop. On June 11 prisoner was put
up with other peoplean I identified him at once. I have not the
slightest doubt he is the man.
