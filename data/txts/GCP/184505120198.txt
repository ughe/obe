NICHOLAS BUTLER (police-constable P 314.) On Sunday night, or Mon-
day morning, I was on duty in Camberwell-road at a quarter after one o'clock,
and about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's house, I met the prisoner
and two others coming in a direction from the prosecutor's house towards
London-I observed that the prisoner had no shoes on, and no cap or hat
-- I took particular notice of him-I carry a light with me and used it-
as they passed I heard the prisoner say that they had malleted the b-
which means beat a constable or somebody-I followed them about ten
yards—they were walking three abreast, very fast-the prisoner and the
one nearest to me were smoking pipes--I have no doubt about the prisoner
-I described him to my fellow-constable in the morning.
Cross-examined. Q. They walked pretty freely? A. They walked
very fast, right by me--the prisoner spoke loud-I have heard the word
malleting used before in the West of England, and in London as well, and
different places, before I was in the police-I was then a bricklayer-it is
used among bricklayers as well as others, and means the same thing-I
had no grounds for stopping them then.
GEORGE Rous (police-constable P 219.) On the morning of the 5th of
May, about ten minutes after one, I was on duty in Addington-square, which
opens into the road, half-way between Camberwell-gate and the George-
I was turning to go into the square, about mid-way from Southampton
street to where my brother constable was, which was nearer the gate, and
saw three men pass me in a direction for London--I saw one of them
without shoes--I did not see his face-I observed his feet when he had
passed- I looked after him sufficient to see he was without shoes-1
observed his height and general appearance, and have no doubt the prisoner
is the man- I did not observe his head-he was outside the other two,
and about half a step before them.
Cross-examined. Q. Was anybody with you? A. No-I did not
observe that he was talking-I never remember having seen him before-
I did not observe whether he had a hat or cap on-1 could hear him
slouching as if without shoes-that induced me to turn my attention to
him-he made a different noise to what he would if he had shoes--I could
hear the noise-Butler was nearer to London than I was.
