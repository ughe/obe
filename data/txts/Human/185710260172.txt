   Cross-examined by MR. TINDAL ATKINSON . Q.  Are you not an agent? A.  Yes, for Schofield and Co., of Rochdale—they do not manufacture blankets, only flannels—Knight is a party who was formerly in the employ of Whitesell and Co.—he was my town traveller at this time—the reference the prisoner gave me was to Mullett and Co., of Fenchurch Street—I went there, and was satisfied with what they told me—that was the foundation of my credit with him—I drew on him at first in two bills for 113 l . 2 s . 2 d . and 144 l . 4 s . 6 d .—the 113 l . bill was honoured, and the 144 l . was not—I never borrowed 20 l . of him—I went and got money on account, and gave him an I O U—I suppose that was after I had taken the bills, but it was not before he had got goods worth a great deal more than that from, me—I had got two bills on the 21st—I did not borrow 20 l . of him, that I am aware of, on an I O U, on 19th May—I had a paper cancelling all the I O U's, but have torn it up—I should not enter 20 l . borrowed in my cash book; I did not ask him to lend me the money; I asked him if he would give me so much on account—there is a small sum on the 20th, of 7 l . 10 s ., for goods supplied to him—I cannot tell the date, or whether I got 5 l . on the 29th—I have had several small sums—I could not take upon myself to say that I have not—I may have had money from him on the 29th May—I cannot go into dates—I am not aware that I had money from him when it was not due for goods—I might have had money from him two or three times during a week—he told me that he could not find the I O U's, and gave me a paper cancelling them—they were not given up—they amounted to 100 l ., a little over or a little under—I cannot say whether I left a blue covered sample book of blankets on 9th June for his inspection—I do recollect the fact of taking it over there, and after it had been there some time I may have seen him on the subject of the purchase of the blankets—I recollect his giving me an order for 200 samples, and 1, 200 pairs at my con venience—the order for the 1200 pairs was not given to me at the same time—they were ordered perhaps three or four days afterwards—they were to be delivered as soon as possible—I stated, in Mr. Schofield's presence, that I had an interest in the concern—I was not in any way interested as a partner—the 1, 200 blankets were to be paid for, fourteen days after their delivery—this is the order for them ( produced )—this large writing is the defendant's—I began to deliver the blankets upon that order on 27th July—I have had several amounts of money on I O U's—on 8th July I went to him and got a settlement—that was nothing further than an I O U—I told him that my friends were anxious for me to get money for them, and that I wanted to balance the account—he said, "Very well, we will go into if"—that was about 5th July, and on 8th July I told him what was due to me—I cannot say what it was, but it was a large amount—he gave me some money on that day, and it was added to the I O U to make up the balance, and I drew on him—I told him that I should draw bills on him—I got bills from him for the balance—I found that, on 8th July the amount he owed me was 518 l ., after deducting 100 l ., the amount of I O U's—that did not take in the 113 l . which had been met—that was crossed off the two first transactions—we cancelled the I O U's and began with a clean slate—just before 24th July, when the 113 l . bill became due, I began to make a little inquiry—I did not make inquiries of his housekeeper—she was brought before me in his presence to say that a party in Manchester, one of my friends, had been making
