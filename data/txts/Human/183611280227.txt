   Cross-examined. Q.  Which hand did he strike with? A.  His right hand, I am sure of that—I cannot say whether the blow reached Lett—if it had, it would have struck him from the water—the blow was made at his head or shoulder—they were standing near each other—Lott was abaft  —I did not notice whether he staggered before he fell—his face was turned towards the head of the ship—I was about half tipsy—the party were all fresh.
   Q.  Is it not very easy, when a bulwark is unshipped, for the mere motion of a man's body to cause him to slip off vessel? A.  Yes—this was a frosty night—the decks were frosty and slippery—the vessel was lying quite still and quiet—there was no wind—I cannot undertake to say whether the man fell in consequence of a blow or push or not—I went to look for the body, and two or three more came after me in a boat—I cannot say who they were—I saw the body after it was found—it was the body of John Lott. JOHN MACHIN. I am Captain, of the brig Barnsley.  I was in bed—I heard an alarm, and came on deck—I saw the deceased swimming in the water, and made an attempt top save him—he had got hold of our cable, and he pulled himself partly out of the water—I go into a boat, but he was lost—the prisoner was in our boat trying to pick him up.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he appear very eager to save the deceased? A.  Yes—the decks were very slippery—the ship was down about eighteen inches, slant with her head—she was afloat, but he cargo laid forwards—the prisoner was very much affected at this—I have known him from a boy—I never saw him out of temper with any one—I never saw the de ceased swim—the tide was very strong, and drew him under the vessel's bottom. JOHN WEST. I am a fisherman. I found the body two tiers beyond where he was lost, and took him ashore.
