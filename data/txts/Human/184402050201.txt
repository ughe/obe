 774. WILLIAM BRACEY was indicted for stealing, on the   19th of Jan ., 55 lbs.  weight of hay, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; and three bushels of a mixture of chaff and bran, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Samuel Ball , his master. SAMUEL BALL. The prisoner was in my employ at East Ham. On the 18th of Jan. I was with him in the stable, and ordered him to come to town with a load of dung with three horses and a cart, and told him to fill three nose-bags, which contain nearly a bushel a piece of chaff and bran mixed—the boy filled them—he was to start at three o'clock in the morning—I told Stacey to look out when he left.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Had you had any quarrel with the prisoner the night before? A.  I have had a few words with him at different times—he did not upset a cart of dung into a ditch—he got wrong, and I was angry with him—I was called up about half-past three o'clock in the morning. GEORGE STACEY( police-constable K  269.) I stopped the prisoner with Mr. Ball's cart about half-past three o'clock in the morning, about a hundred yards from Mr. Ball's—I asked what he had in the cart—he said, two baskets, and a little bundle of hay for his horses—I called my brother constable, who got up into the cart, and told me there was a truss of hay there—I then took the prisoner back, called Mr. Balls ip, and asked if he had allowed the prisoner to take any hay—he said, "No"—the prisoner beard that—on examining the cart again, I found hanging on one of the shafts, a bag, containing bran and chaff—the nose-bags which were on the fore ladder of the cart were full—I asked Mr. Ball to go up in his loft, and see if he missed any hay—he did so, and said the truss he had seen there overnight was gone.
