HARRIET SMOOTHY JARMAN. I am assistant to Mr. Hunt, a draper, in
Hammersmith-I know both the prisoners in the early part of April Julia
came and purchased a dress, which amounted to 148.--I am not quite certain
whether she paid with gold or silver, but both the prisoners came the next
day and had bonnets and trimmings for each of them--they came to 223. or
238., and Julia paid with two sovereigns, and received the change-they after-
wards bought two print dresses, one for each of them, which came to 78. or
88.-on the Wednesday in the following week Julia came and changed a note
in the hurry of business I could not say whether it was a bl. or a 101. note
-my impression is that it was a 10l. note.I took 2s. 7. of her she
bought some rafling to cover boxes, and a pair of worsted stockings, and for
that she changed the note- I did not take the No. of it I wrote the address
on it, No. 27, Phillimore-place, Kensington.

JAMES GODDARD ( policeman, 1 80.) In consequence of information T
proceeded to Ireland with a warrant to apprehend the two prisonen-I found
them at No. 7, Connaught-street, Merchant's-quay, Cork, on the 6th May
they were both in the room-I read the warrant to them-Ellen said she
knew nothing about any notes-I asked her if she had changed any note
previous to leaving England-she said she had one at Mr. Cooper's, the Adam
and Eve, for Mr. Conron ; that was all she had ever changed, and she believed
that was a 51. note-I asked her to put out her keys and other things that she
had in her poeket, and show me what she had in her box-she gave me a

apparel, and those articles which I produce, which Miss Sandilands identifies,
Court. Q. What was the new wearing apparel that you found ? 4.
One chemise, two plaid shawls, two shot poplin dresses, one gauze scarf, two
