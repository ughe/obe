 1090. LOUISA MORTON was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of February , 3 work-boxes, value 7 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Edwin Crowder . EDWIN CROWDER. I am a perfumer, living in High-street, Woolwich. On Wednesday evening, the 23rd of February, about six o'clock, the pri soner came to my shop—she made some excuse—I had had some work boxes on my counter immediately before—I missed them an hour after wards—no one else had been in the shop in the meantime—these pro duced are mine. EDMUND NORTH. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a box pawned by the prisoner on the 23rd of February, in the name of Louisa Morton, for Ann Read, Red Lion-street. BARTHOLOMEW KING( police-constable R  176.) I took the prisoner—as we were going to the station, she said, "Another girl gave me the boxes, and said if I pawned them, she would give me one box"—she showed me where they were pawned.
   Prisoner's Defence.  A young woman gave them me to pledge. GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined Three Months.

CHARLES MAY. I am steward of the Abercrombie Robertson,  lying at Deptford—Mr. Selby is the owner—every thing on board is the property of the captain, Robert Laurence Frazer—it is a transport, and the Crowe provides the stores. On the night of the 12th of January I was called by the captain to go into the Trinity  barge, which was lying alongside the ship, in consequence of the lighterman having called for assistance—when I got into the lighter I found Connolly and the prisoner—the third officer then went into the cabin of the lighter, and found a bottle of wine con cealed—it was the same sort as had been placed in the barge Nelson,  which was lying outside the Trinity  barge—I had placed seven hampers of wine in that barge, which was to return them to the Victualling-oflice—it corresponded with the bottles placed in the hamper—there was one hamper missing, containing twenty-five bottles.
