 1071.  GEORGE CLEAVER was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 2 crowns, 8 half-crowns, 40 shillings, and 20 sixpences, the monies of William Spratt , his master.
 WILLIAM SPRATT. The prisoner was my  servant. On the 12th of August, 1826, or thereabouts, he absconded; I then kept the  Hole in the Wall public-house, in Fleet-street- this money was given to him one Saturday evening, to get his money in from the customers; he was to account to me on the Sunday morning - I never saw him again till the 3d of March last, when I saw him in Fleet-street, and gave charge of him.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Are you in any business now? A. No; I have retired for awhile; I left the Hole-in-the-wall last February; a short time before this happened I was indicted with the prisoner through a troublesome neighbour; I did not tell the prisoner that he was to bear the charge of the indictment; I paid 12s. for Mr. Thompson, who had bailed him; while I was gone to the Nore the prisoner absconded from his bail; I did not stop one farthing of his money for that indictment; I have not treated this as a matter of account; I did receive different sums of money to the amount of 1l. 9s., but I did it with a view to apprehend the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. Did you receive these sums as part of payment? A. Yes; in part of the 4l. of silver.
   SARAH SPRATT. I am the wife of William Spratt. I gave the prisoner this money on the 12th of August, 1826, to the best of my recollection; he returned in about an hour, and looked round in the tap-room; at nine o'clock I asked for him to take out his beer, but he was gone.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had there not been an indictment against him? A. Yes; he did not abscond on account of that; we went down to the Nore - we heard he had been taken up, and that Mr. Thompson had bailed him.
   THOMAS LIGHTFOOT. I am an officer, and took the prisoner into custody.
 GUILTY. Aged 28. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury on account of his master's improper conduct. Confined Three Months.
 1072.  JAMES BARRETT, SAMUEL ROBINSON and JOHN BROWN, were indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of James Nicholas Waylett , about two o'clock in the night of the 8th of April , at St. Leonard, Eastcheap, with intent to steal, and stealing therein. 1 desk, value 2l., his property.
 JAMES NICHOLAS WAYLETT. I live at  No. 45, Fish-street, in the parish of St. Leonard, Eastcheap. I am a boot and shoe maker; I sleep in Crooked-lane, about one hundred yards off - I was awoke about a quarter past two o'clock in the morning of the 8th of April, by the watchman, who said my boy wanted me at Fish-street-hill: I have a boy sleeps in my shop there - I went there and unlocked the door: I saw my boy standing inside with a mallet in his hand; he said "Sir, there are thieves in the house;" I said "Impossible, you are frightened, the cats have got in:" he said No - I said to the watchman "Give me your staff, and go and get what spare men you have - they came immediately; I lighted several candles, and requested they would go into the cellar and turn on the gas and search the cellar - they did so: I said "Now search this house;" and in the mean time I was watching the adjoining house, which is empty; they were up stairs, they went into the adjoining house through an internal communication which I have, but I was not with them, and in five or six minutes the prisoners were brought out - I did not see anything taken from them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. There is an internal communication between your house and the other? A. Yes, in the party-wall - I do not know whether it was open or not.
 COURT. Q. What did you find done? A. When I went into the parlour I found the window open and broken, and the desk was on the floor, which I had seen some time the day before.
