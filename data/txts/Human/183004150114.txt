 902.  GEORGE RAYNER was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Henry Evers , from his person.
   HENRY EVERS. I am a cabinet-maker. I was in Piccadilly on the evening of the 25th of February - I felt a tug at my pocket, turned, and saw the prisoner near me; my handkerchief was then gone; it was found in his breeches pocket - this is it.
   WILLIAM BARNETT. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner try several gentlemen's pockets, and at last the prosecutor came up - part of his handkerchief was out of his pocket; the prisoner fixed his eyes upon him, and took it out of his pocket - he was in company with another boy. who ran away; I took the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 14. - Transported for Seven Years.
 903.  JOHN DAY was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of March , 1 watch, value 30s.; 3 seals, value 4l., and 1 watch-chain, value 6d., the goods of Henry Eames , from his person.
 HENRY EAMES. On the 29th of March I was in  Bunhill-row, at a quarter before twelve at night - I had been drinking part of two pots of ale with a man and his wife, but was quite sober - I happened to kick my foot against a step and fell down; as I was rising up the prisoner drew my watch from my fob - I jumped up and ran ten or twelve yards, I caught him, and called the Policeman, who came and took him with my watch in his hand.
   CHARLES LEWIS At a quarter before twelve o'clock I heard a voice saying, "You rogue where is my watch?" -I ran and saw the prisoner in the prosecutor's hands; I took the watch from the prisoner's hand - the prosecutor said he did not know him, but the prisoner said it was given him to take care of; the prosecutor seemed quite sober - he said at the section-house that he took the watch from his pocket to take care of.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never said I took it to take care of; I met the prosecutor, who was rather in liquor and fell down - I assisted him up, and the watch fell down; I took it up, and was in the act of giving it him, when the Policeman came up; he had the seals in his hand.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Confined Six Months.
 904.  MARY BANKS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of March , 1 watch, value 3l.; 1 watch-chain, value 1s.; 2 seals, value 1l., and 1 watch-key, value 6d., the goods of John Agness Johnson , from his person.
   JOHN AGNESS JOHNSON. I am a baker, and live at Stepney. I was at the end of Redman's-row, leading to Mile-end, on the 21st of March, between one and two o'clock in the morning; I had been at my brother's on the Saturday evening - he had just lost a child - I met the prisoner just by Mile-end-gate; she asked for some gin - I said the shops were all shut, we could get none, and I was going home - she followed me to the end of Mutton-lane, and solicited me to go down there with her; we then stood talking together, and she put her arm round my neck; I saw the watch in her hand - I did not feel it go from my fob, but I saw it the moment she was drawing it away - I seized her, and called the watchman - she endeavoured to get away, and slipped into a ditch with one foot; the watch was found in the ditch next morning, just where she slipped in.
   JOHN CHAMBERLAIN. I am a Police-officer. I was on duty, and came up when the prosecutor called - he accused the prisoner of robbing him of his watch; she said she had no watch - I searched her, but found nothing; I could not find any thing that night - it was dark.
   THOMAS DREW. The watch was brought to the section-house on the end of a garden rake.
   GEORGE FAIRBRASS. I took it out of the ditch and carried it there.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. The first thing he said was that, he was not certain whether he had left it at home, or where he had been drinking - he was quite intoxicated.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Life.
 905.  ANN RIVERS was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of April , 1 half-pint of rum, value 2s., and 1 bedgown, value 6d., the goods of George Hammerstone , her master.
 GEORGE HAMMERSTONE. I  keep the  White Horse, in Orchard-street- the prisoner lived with me about three months, and was in the habit of going to my bed-room, where I keep most of my liquors; in one corner was a large cask of gin, and I saw the bung had been removed; I replaced it, and made a mark on it - in a day or two I saw it had been removed again; I called her and asked her to let me look into her box - she refused; I sent for the officer - she then said she had lost her key, but it was opened, and better than half a pint of rum was found in a bottle wrapped up in one of my child's pinafores; I cannot swear to the rum.   NOT GUILTY.
