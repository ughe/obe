Spooner, and is addressed to Rowton House—an identity certificate was posted at the same time in a separate envelope—it was returned the next day through the post office. HERBERT JOHN COOPER. I am a clerk in the Parliament Street Post Office—this money order was presented to me on October 4th—the identity certificate was presented at the same time—the order is for £1 4s., and is properly signed—I cannot recognise to whom I paid it. FRANK PIKE Detective L. ). On October 31st I found the prisoner detained at Rowton House, Newington Butts—I told him I should arrest him for forging and uttering a money order for a reservist named Spooner—he said, "All right,"—I conveyed him to the police station—in the presence of the Inspector he said;"This is how it happened; Spooner and I were out for a walk together; outside St. George's hospital he asked me to wait; a few minutes afterwards a policeman brought him out, and he was taken to Gerrard Road Police Station; the following morning I endorsed the order in his name; he asked me twice."
   The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said the prosecutor had been about with him at his expense, and had asked him to get the money, and had given him two letters and the identity certificate, and the next morning after the prosecutor was taken into custody he cashed the order at Parliament Street Post Office. GUILTY. Six months hard labour.
   ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, DECEMBER  12 TH , 1904.
