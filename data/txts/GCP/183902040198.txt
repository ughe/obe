790. SAMUEL HARVEY was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of
February, 1 dead fowl, value 28. 6d., the goods of James Morris.
JAMES MORRIS. I am a poulterer and cheesemonger, and live in
Church-street, Hackney. On the 2nd of February, in consequence of
information from the witness Ogan, I looked at the poultry that was
exposed for sale, and missed a fowl - I went with Ogan to the Old Mer-
maid-he there pointed out the prisoner to me, and told me his name, and
said, "That is the person that stole the fowl"-I said, “Come, Mr. Harvey,
give me the fowl, let us have no bother about it, all I want is my fowls-
he denied all knowledge of it-I saw a fowl afterwards taken out from
between some water-butts.
Prisoner. Q. Will you swear it was your fowl? A. I will not swear
to it, but I believe it to be mine.
WILLIAM HENRY OGAN. I am a shoemaker, and live in Church-street,
Hackney. I was standing at my father's door on Saturday evening, the
2nd of February, directly opposite the prosecutor's—I saw the prisoner
pass with three others, and as he passed, he took a fowl from the prose-
cutor's window, passed it along to the other three, and turned up the old
Mermaid-yard, which is close by-I immediately ran and informed the
prosecutor, told him the prisoner's name, and went with him into the
Mermaid public-house, where I found the prisoner, with others, in the
parlour—the water-butts, where the fom. was taken from, are up the yard,
a little distance from the door-the prisoner would have an opportunity of
putting it there before he went into the house.
THOMAS OAKES. I am a policeman, I took the prisoner into custody
at the Mermaid, and found the fowl between two water-butts in the back
yard.
