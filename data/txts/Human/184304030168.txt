1344. ROBERT BAKER, was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of April , 1 pair of boots, value 6 s  .; 2lbs. weight of bread, 3 d  .; 8 shillings, 2 pence, 4 halfpence, and 1 farthing, the property of James Gifford . JAMES GIFFORD. I live in Cross-street, Huntingdon-street, Hoxton. On the 4th of April I left my boots safe—the next morning I found ray breeches, trowsers, waistcoat, and boots behind the door on the bannister—my father sleeps with me—the money stated was in my pockets—some one had come into my room and taken these things—the prisoner did live in the house for some time—there is a man in the house who plays instruments—he is out late, so we leave the street-door unfastened, and on that night I had forgotten to bolt the roomdoor—I had seen the prisoner at six and half-past ten o'clock, before I lost the property, and saw him in the Hackney-road the next morn ing—I told a policeman—he took him, and found eight shillings and 2 1/2 d . on him, and one shilling I could swear to—this is it—he had told me on the Saturday that the worst had come to the worst, as he had gone to the relieving officer—he pulled out a paper to go and break the stones.
   Prisoner. Q.  Is it not as likely that the shilling might have got chafed against my tobacco-box as yours? A.  No, there is a black mark on its edge, and there are three scratches on the other shilling I had in my bag—the money found on him exactly corresponds with what I had, all but 1 1/2 d . WILLIAM JACKSON( police-constable N  190.) I took the prisoner, and found on him eight shillings and 2 3/4 d ., and some papers and books—the pro secutor said he lost eight shillings and 4 3/4 d .—he tried to keep his money back, and gave me a shilling, but I found a purse on him, containing seven shillings—he had got the purse up the sleeve of his shirt—the prosecutor immediately identified this shilling, as being chafed and marked—the prisoner had a quan tity of bread.
   Prisoner.  I don't know anything about it. GUILTY. * Aged 29.— Confined One Year .
 1345. THOMAS JARVIS, was indicted for stealing, on the   20th of March , 200lbs. weight of lead, value 1 l  ., the goods of Robert Ford Hayles ; and fixed to a building.—2nd COUNT  , not stating it to be fixed.—4 other COUNTS  , stating it to be the property of William Hubbard , and William and Sophia Hubbard .
   MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution. JOSEPH BUCK( police-constable F  124.) On Sunday evening, the 19th of March, my attention was attracted to a house, No. 2, Bedfordbury, in the parish of St. Martin-in-the-fields—some bricks had fallen from the cornice-work—on the following Monday I went and examined the state of the lead, it was coiled up when I first saw it, but would require cutting before it could be taken away, and the place where the lead had been was vacant—I saw the prisoner at the window—he was within reach of this lead—I went round my beat, and returned about twenty minutes past six o'clock—I then noticed that the lead was beaten out, and flattened down to its place—I came again at half-past seven o'clock—I saw a truck, and the prisoner brought a barrel out of the house, put it into the truck, and pushed the truck round to King William-street, from the front of the house, and left it there—I looked at it, and found the barrel contained some lead—it was much heavier lead than that which I had noticed before—I then saw the prisoner at the barrel—I asked him what he had there—he said "Nothing, it is my own pro perty"—I was not satisfied, and took him to the station—I went back to the house, went on the roof, and found the dormer had been stripped of lead—the cornice was left as it was—there were four nail-holes in the lead.
