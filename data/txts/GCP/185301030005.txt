Cross-examined by Mr. ROBINSON. Q. Where was Wynn's shop? 4.
No. 4, Sidney-street; I had known him before-I called on him about four
years ago, when he was a broker in the New-road-he never did any business
for me before.
Mr. REED. Q. You say that Paxton told you that Mr. Knight had said,
"Take no notice of the notice ?" A. He did;. I afterwards saw Mr.
Knight, and after that I saw Paxton; I did not tell him what Mr. Knight
had told me--the brokers valued the whole of the property I had received of
Paxton-the spirits that were there were all paid for that was not in the
1552., the spirits were taken by our distiller, transferred from Paxton over to
me, and the beer the same by the brewer-the beer and spirits were not
included in the 1551.-before I paid the money Mr. Paxton showed me the
brewer's book, and the distiller's book-I afterwards wished to dispose of the
business because I could not get support in it; I could not get my salt at it,
it was the most miserable place for business & man ever put bis toe in-
Paxton represented that the business was from 122. to 15l. a week, never less
than 121.--the first week I was in it I only took about 6l., and that was the
reason I wished to get rid of it.
CHARLES Smith. I am clerk to Messrs. Drew; they are solicitors for
Mr. Linder and Mr. Knight— I was directed by Messrs. Drew to leave a
notice, of which this is a copy (produced), at the Golden Horse, Theobald's-
road - I left it there on 24th June.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was that after you had conveyed
these premises to Paxton! 4. I do not know anything about that I know
nothing about the money paid by Paxton for the lease-this paper pra-
duced ) is not Mr. Drew's writing, it is written by one of his late clerks,
one of the persons who acted for bim-Mr. George Henry Drew, jun., is
one of the firm.
HENRY HOLMER. I am a wheelwright. On 1st Dec., 1852, I purchased
the inn called the Golden Horse, and other premises- I was to have posses-
sion on Christmas-day.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you take them by any engagement in writing ?
4. I had a memorandum for the deposit--the agreement was a verbal one.
I had no written engagement-I bought the premises of Mr. Linder.
Court. Q. How much did you agree to give? A. 1207. : that was for
a lease of the public-house and private house; there was a lease of sixteen
and a half years from the freeholder--the parties will not let me have the
house, they want me to have my deposit back again I paid the 101. to Mr.
Linder; it has not been repaid me-I have not had the house, because the
executors could not agree-I never was in possession.
