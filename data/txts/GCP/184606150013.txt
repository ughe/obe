Messrs. Clarkson and HuddLESTON conducted the Prosecution.
John RATCLIFF. I am a victualler, and live at Harefield, four or five
miles from Uxbridge. On Saturday, the 3rd of Jan,, I had in a box in my
bed-room, (which I saw safe under my bed between four and five o'clock in
the evening, when I took some money from it,) about four 201. notes of the
Uxbridge Bank, a 201. Bank of England note, nine or ten 101. notes, some of the
Uxbridge, and some of the Bank of England, and about fifteen 51. notes of
the Bank of England and Uxbridge, some title deeds, and other articles
there was a bag, but, I believe, no gold in it-there are two beds in the
room--the box was under a bed close to my own--I locked the box, and left
it there--my attention was called about ten o'clock that evening, and the
box was gone-I gave information to the police, and offered a reward -I had
a club at the house that day; I was short of hands, and was very busy
there were a great many people in all the rooms the bed-room door is not
always fastened there is an out-house adjoining my house, in which I keep
a butcher's-shop-I am in and out there several times a day, and was so be
tween six and seven o'clock-I afterwards found the title deeds, and saw the
201. note in the possession of Lee, the constable I had received it from Mr.
Collins, and am confident it was the same the male prisoner was in the habit
of being at my house--I have employed him as a labourer, and he knew the
localities of the house-(looking at the 201. note -I know this by the name
of “William Jupp" on it, and it being a little defaced with black ink.
John JAMES RATCLIFE. I am the prosecutor's son, and assist in his busi-
ness. On this Saturday evening, between seven and eight o'clock, I went
into the back house, and found the back door open-I closed it--I had been
there about an hoar before it was shut then the bed-room is on the first
floor--I was at home all the evening--my father gave an alarm at ten o'cloek
-- on Sunday morning, the 4th of Jan., about nine, I saw the male prisoner
and Charles Lamb go up a road opposite our house, in a direction towards
Banyard's wood-I heard nothing of the property till the 17th of March, when
Joseph Lacey gave me information - I accompanied him to a furze-field about
two miles from our premises--I saw Lacey's son in the field, and my father's
box in some furze--it was pointed out to me as the place where Lacey had
found it-I opened it, and found the title deeds and a check for 1001.- there
were no notes in it--the title-deeds and other property amounted to 7,000l. or
8,000l. value to him--there was a promissory note for 6001.-all the property,
except the notes and money, were in it, also the bag and purse the lock of
the box was broken.
Joseph LACEY. On the 17th of March I was eutting furze in a field at
Rickmansworth, and found a box, which I gave to Mr. Rateliff, jun., in the
state I found it-this is it-it was closed, but the lock broken.
