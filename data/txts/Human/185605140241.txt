I could not do it—in going along, he said, "I will give you 5 l ., I will give you anything in the world"—he was taken to the station, and while the charge was being booked, he took this hammer out of his pocket under his waistcoat, and said, "This is what I opened the box with"—I found the salmon secreted in the straw under the tarpaulin.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Where were they secreted? A.  In the middle of the van—there were some baskets of fish in the van which were to be delivered, according to the address—there was straw in the body of the van—there was nothing done to the other box—the first I saw was, the prisoner was in a stooping position, that attracted my attention—he was before me—I could see his face and the box—I did not Intend that he should see me—I got in a doorway—I will swear he could not see me—I examined the box at the station—I found the lid closely nailed down—I could not swear whether it was an old lid—the prisoner said the nails were loose; the lid was old, and the fish were coming out—he said he would give me a pound, a sovereign; nay, he would give me 5 l ., if I would not take him to the station—he said, "I shall be a ruined man; I hare a wife and six chil dren; I will give you 5 l ." HILTON JOHNSON. I am a porter at the Great Northern Railway. On 1st May, the prisoner drove a van from there—I checked the goods that he took out that morning—he took three boxes of salmon—they were in a good state when he left the station—they were for Boulogne.
   Q.  I believe the drivers take hammers and nails with them, in case it should be necessary in the passage of the goods to use them? A.  I never knew it—I do not know that it is done—I do not know that the lids some times get loose, and ttfe nails come out on the way—they are always well examined before they leave—it might be the case that the boxes might get loose and they might repair them at the instant, but I do not know it.
