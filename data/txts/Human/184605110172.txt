JOHN ASKEW. On the 24th of April Baker came about four o'clock—I saw her served by Thompson with some calico—I saw her tender him a shil ling—I took it from him, and cut it—Baker said she did not know it was bad—I told her I could not let her have it again—she put down a good shilling to pay for the calico.
   Baker.  I gave a half-crown. Witness.  1 did not take it—I thought it was a shilling—the officer! West, came in a little afterwards—I gave him the bad shilling—this is it.
   Baker.  My husband gave me a half-crown and a shilling; the shilling happened to be bad; I gave it to the shopman; he handed it to  his master, and he said he should not give it back: I said I did not mind, it was no use to me if it was bad; I gave the half-crown, and he gave me two shillings and 8 halfpenny. GEORGE THOMAS BLAKE. I am barman to Mr. Garratt, of the Ordnance Arms, in York-road, Lambeth. On the afternoon of the 24th of April the two prisoners came together—Moran asked for a pint of beerr—I drew it, and be gave me a half-crown—I put it into the till, and gave him 2 s . 4 d . change—I observed the half-crown he gave me—it was a King George III—both the prisoners drank the beer, and went away—West came in afterwards, and I looked at the half-crown I had received from Moran—it was bad—there I was only one other half-crown in. the till—that was under the shillings and six pences, and that was a Victoria half-crown—between the time of Moran giving me the half-crown arid my looking at it again, I had not put any money io the till, and no one had interfered with the till at all—I marked the half-crown—thia is it. THOMAS WEST( police-constable M  249.) On the 24th of April. on duty about two o'clock in the day at Newington—causeway—I saw the two prisoners—Moran walked a little ahead of Baker—I followed them—Baker joined him, and they conversed together—they west on  about half-mile beyond the Elephant and Castle—they returned in the same direction—I saw Baker go into Askew's shop, and put down the half-crown—Moran stopped six or eight yards from the door, on the same side of the way—he could not command a view of the shop—he could only see the door.
   COURT . Q.  Did he linger at all about the shop? A.  He stopped at the door about a minute—Baker went in just as he passed on—he stopped six or eight yards past the door—I went into the shop, aad when I came out I saw them in company together, walking away, about twenty yards from the shop.
   MR. BALDWIN . Q.  Where did they go? A.  To the Elephant and Castle aad to the London-road, and they went to the Ordnance Arms—I followed them to the door—I saw them come out—I wtntin and saw Blake, he looked in the till and saw the half-crown—I told him to mark it—I went after the prisoners and came up with them at the foot of  Westminster-bridge—they were together—I took the man—the woman left, and I sent another officer to take her—I told the man what I took him for, he said he knew nothing about it—he resisted me.
