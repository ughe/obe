   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKON. Q.  Who were present on that occa sion? A.  Holliday, Cusell, Loup, Mr. Crauch, and myself.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q.  How long have you known Holiday? A.  About three years—I then had one transaction with him—that was an honourable one—he was then in a respectable station, and the owner of a ship as I heard—I had heard nothing of him since. GEORGE WILLIAMS re-examined.  On 30th July I received some tools at Custom-house-quay—about 3rd Aug. an order came for their removal, is consequence of which they were given to Mr. Needle's carman—I did not observe anybody besides the carman attending to the removal. WILLIAM NEEDLE. I was employed to cart some tools from Custom-house-quay on 3rd Aug. by a stranger—it was neither of the prisoners—I did not go myself, I sent a man who was jobbing for me. JOHN CATON. I am in Mr. Needle's employ. On 3rd Aug. I carted three packages of tools from Custom-house-quay to Mr. Booth's, in Budge-row—one of the gents  went with me—I do not recollect which it was—I do not know his name—I saw him before the Lord Mayor—I cannot say that I see him here—he went in to Mr. Booth's—I had not known him before—he went to the quay with me, and saw the goods loaded, and then took me to Mr. Booth's. ROBERT BOOTH re-examined.  Last Aug. Holliday came to my warehouse about some tools—they were brought by Holliday in two casks and a case, and I advanced him 40 l . on them in two checks—on 10th Aug. Holliday and Cusell came, took away the tools, and paid me 2 l . for warehouse-room—they were to have been sold by public auction, but they said they did not wish that as they could make a better price—Holliday repaid me the 40 l . GEORGE ROBERTS. On 10th Aug. I carted two casks and a case of tools from Mr. Booth's to Johnson and Day's, in Eldon-street—Cusell went with me. JOHN JOHNSON. I am a packer, in partnership with Mr. Day, in Eldon-street, Finsbury-circus—I know Cusell—on 10th Aug. he came with a van of carpenters' tools and other ironmongery to our warehouse—they were brought loose, some in a crate and some in a hamper—they were all new—they were left with me I expect to be packed; he did not say what for, I did not ask—he said he wanted me to examine them that night, to see that they were right—we took and laid them out=they were to be sold—they were not offered to me—persons were to come and look at them—that is not part of my usual business—I did not advance anything on them—Cusell was no acquaintance of mine, further than coming backwards and forwards with other people for two and three years—I arranged to show these goods—they re mained at my place till 21st Aug., spread out for inspection—the morning after they came a person called by appointment to see them, and several per sons came to see them—not many, perhaps three or four—I know Loup—he came the same evening that Cusell brought the tools—he looked at them, and told us to take care of them and keep them dry, so that they should not get rusty, and he thought the best place for them would be up stairs—he brought a person next morning to examine them, at least I cannot say he brought him, they were there together—when he first came they were not ready, and we appointed for him to come when they would be ready—Loup
