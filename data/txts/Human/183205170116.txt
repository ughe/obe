1377. CHARLOTTE HOLLAND was indicted for a misdemeanor.
 MESSRS. PRENDERGAST and BARRY conducted the prosecution.
   THOMAS WATSON. I am overseer of St. Mary, Aldermanbury. I commenced giving the prisoner parochial relief on the 6th of April, 1831, and continued to do so till the 28th of March, when I went out of office - I paid her 3s. a week at first, and increased it on the 30th of November to 4s.; I have paid her altogether 10l. 8s., believing her to be chargeable on our parish - she presented herself as a pauper of that parish at the vestry, weekly - her name was in the book which was handed to me by my predecessor.
 Cross-examined by MR. ROWE. Q. How are the entries made in that book? A.Weekly - the casual poor are entered in a different account; she made no representation to me, except coming with the other paupers, and taking the weekly allowance.
 MR. BARRY. Q. Was she entered as a weekly pauper before you came into office? A. Yes, and I find, in 1830, an instrument was bought for her to go to work with.
 BENJAMIN BROWN. I am one of the parish officers of St. Mary, the Virgin, Aldermanbury - I came into office on the 4th of April. The prisoner applied to me that day for her weekly allowance; I paid her 6s.; Mr. Watson had given me the list, and had reduced her allowance to 3s. a week, which she refused to take, saying she had a great deal of illness in her family, and I gave her 6s.
 Cross-examined. Q. You know nothing of her settlement? A. No.
   JOSEPH WEST. I am master of the workhouse of St. James', Clerkenwell. I have paid the prisoner 5s. a week from the 3rd of June 1829, to the 14th of March 1832.
 JAMES HILL. I am clerk to the vestry-clerk of Clerkenwell. I took and examination of the prisoner's husband, and at his death, of herself - she claimed a settlement on her husband's apprenticeship in 1829.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was there ever any question about her settlement? A.Never; she said her husband died at sea. GUILTY. Aged 25.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.

 Fifth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1378.  THOMAS BAKER and HENRY McGEE were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 1 necklace, value 8s., the goods of Charles Barnes , from Maria Barnes ; and that Henry McGee had been before convicted of felony.
 MARY BARNES. I am the wife of  Charles Barnes; my daughter Maria is eight years old; I put a necklace on her neck on the 1st of May; I afterwards saw her without it.
 MARIA BARNES. I am the daughter of Charles Barnes. On the 1st of May I went out with Mrs. Lathan, to look at the chimney-sweepers; I had a necklace on -Baker snatched it off my neck, and gave it to the other prisoner, who ran away towards the market; I laid hold of Baker's coat, and some persons came to my assistance.
 JOHN MEARS. I am the son of  William Mears. I was in Bath-street on the 1st of May, looking at the sweepers; I saw Baker snatch the necklace off Maria Barnes' neck; he turned his hand round, and gave it to the other prisoner; they both ran through the mob, but Baker was stopped; Maria Barnes said, "You thief, give me my necklace;" he said, "I have not seen it;" I said, "Yes, you have, I saw you."
 McGee. I was in the country at the time; this witness's word is not to be taken - he is nothing but a bad character. Witness. I am sure it was you; I know you perfectly well.
 HENRY BERESFORD (Police-serjeant G 8). I was at the station when Baker was brought in; I found nothing on him - the other prisoner was described, and I took him a week afterwards.
 THOMAS HOBBS KING(Police-constable N 248). I produce a certificate from Mr. Clark's office of the former conviction of McGee; I was present, and know he is the person - he was convicted by the name of McGinn, and whipped.
 BAKER -    GUILTY. Aged 14. Whipped and Discharged.
 McGEE -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1379.  WILLIAM TAMWELL MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 3 yards of carpet, value 5s.; 1 chaise-cushion, value 3s.; 1 blanket, value 2s.; 3 half-blankets, value 4s.; 2 quilts, value,.; 4 sheets, value, 10s.; 1 pillow, value 4s., and 1 pillow case, value 1s., the goods of Joseph Parker.
 JOSEPH PARKER. I live in  Ann-street, Globe-fields, and am a painter. I was in the country; I returned, in consequence of a letter from my wife - I found my house stripped, the children laying on a mattress, and all the furniture gone; it was represented by my wife that it was by a distress warrant; she gave me this paper - I went to the landlord, and have no reason to believe there was any authority for what was done; the prisoner called on the Tuesday morning following, and appointed to meet me at the landlord's house at eleven o'clock, previous to the goods being condemned - he failed in his appointment; there was no address on the inventory which he had left, and the landlord could not tell me where to go to him; I found him on the following morning at the sale-room - I had made an arrangement with the landlord on the Wednesday morning, and procured from him a written order for the delivery of my goods; I tendered it to the prisoner who refused, to give them up and said he should sell them - I attended the sale, and took an account of what each lot sold for - they fetched 5l. 6s.; the goods I charge him with taking, were not included in the inventory he left, but some of them I believe were sold; my rent was 2l. 8s, and was two or three days over due.
