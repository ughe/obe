I am a solicitor, living in Stone-buildings, Lin coln's Inn. I was acting for Mr. De Horsey at the last Newcastle election, is a friend—I arrived at Newcastle on Saturday, the nomination day, and went to the Castle—I dined there—Mr. Miller dined with me—we sat down about a Quarter or ten minutes after five o'clock—Mr. Miller and Mr. De Horsey went out to finish their canvass that evening—they left the tables of the best of my recollection, about half-past six o'clock—I remained at the Castle that evening—there were no speeches whatever made that even ing from the Castle windows—Mr. De Horsey returned to the Castle that evening, I should say from half-past eight to nine o'clock, about half-past eight o'clock—Mr. Miller did not return with him—I did not see Mr. Miller again at the Castle that night, after he left at half-past six o'clock.
