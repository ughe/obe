Q. I believe you know that in the hurry of business, it has frequently happened that persons omit to put down a letter in the money book? - Yes, it has occurred to me.
 Q. I believe you can tell us that a letter in going from here to Bath, must pass through a vast number of hands, and may likewise be lost by accident? - It may.
 Q. Now I would ask you, supposing for instance it had occurred to you not to have a letter put down in the money book, and you was obliged to pay the money, would you, or would you not, have thought it disagreeable to have paid more than the money? - I would have returned the money contained in the letter, and the postage; I never knew any thing further.
 Q. Would you, or would you not, have thought it a suspicious thing to have returned more than the usual money? - I should.
 Q. You hold the post of a letter carrier, it is esteemed generally a valuable post? Is it a post that interest is made to obtain? would you part with it for a hundred pounds? - No.
 Q. Would you part with it for half a guinea? - I would not.
 Q. Nor nine shillings and seven-pence? - I would not.
 Court. You know it is capital offence to steal money out of the letters? - Yes.
 Q. You know that men has been hung for it? - Yes.
 The prisoner called two witnesses who gave him a good character.
 - CABAIN sworn.
 On the morning of the 22d of August, the prisoner informed me that he had omitted to put such a letter in the evening before, that he had received it, I believe, in Oxford-road; that he had received the postage of that letter, and was informed that it contained money, that in consequence of that he brought that letter to the Post office, with the other letters for which he had received postage, that he had paid for the postage of the letter in the proper office, but that in consequence of the celerity with which business was obliged to be done with us, he had neglected to have it entered in the usual money book.
 Q. If he had paid the postage of it, it must have found its way into the Post office at the same time? - It must. some days afterwards, I cannot tell the distance of time, about six or seven days, he again came to me, and told me that that letter had not found its situation to the place where it was directed, he was apprehensive it must be lost: I told him if that was the case, as he had neglected to have it entered in the proper book, he would be answerable for the amount that the letter contained. The man acquiesced in that demand.
 Q. Do not these things frequently happen? - It is unpleasant to say that it does frequently happen.
 Q. How many hands do you think the letter must pass through between this and Bath? - At least five or six; I don't think it can pass through a great many more.
 Jury. It does not pass through any more hands after the bag is sealed up on the road to Bath? - It does not; but at Bath it goes through several hands.
 Mr. Knowlys. What character has he borne in the office? - Always borne a very fair character, nor have I observed any impropriety in his conduct whatever.
 Court. Where is this money book kept? - It is kept in the paid letter office at the Post office.
 Q. When the  bellman brings his letters, who separates the money letters from the others? - He should do it himself.
