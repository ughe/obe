RICHARD PHILIP WILDY. I am a member of the Salvage Corps-in
June, 1887, I was stationed at Commercial Road East-on 1st June I
was called to a fire at the coffee-shop, 228, East India Dock Road, about
half-past two in the morning-I remained on the premises-in my opinion
the fire originated in the middle room of three rooms on the second floor,
which looked like a lumber-room--a lot of furniture, boxes, and so on
appeared to have been stacked there-after I had been there a little time
Harry Cove came there with another lad, who I have not seen, but
neither of the prisoners.
Cross-examined by Mr. TAYLOR. I went over the premises afterwards
on the first floor there was a front room, then a gallery with two billiard
tables in the centre, and then a back store-room-I examined the top
floor; that was where the fire broke out--no one stated that the fire.
was from a two-jointed bracket coming in contact with one of the
partitions.
WILLIAM LEACH. I am a member of the Salvage Corps-I was at
this fire after it oocurred, taking charge of the premises from eight a.m.
on 2nd June-I looked about in order to see where the fire originated-1
think it was in the middle room second floor-I saw nothing whatever to
explain the fire-while I was there Cove came to the premises--I saw
young Adams, and old Adams when he came back from where he was
staying at the time-he was there with Cove-that was some days
afterwards.
NOTREBE
EDWARD ERNEST HARDING. I am a member of the firm of Toplis and
Harding, St. Paul's Churchyard-we are assessors to the Queen Insur-
ance Company from a communication from them I went to 228, East
India Dock Road, the day after the fire-I saw Cove there-I examined
the premises--I agree that the fire originated in the room on the second
floor-I was not able to discover any cause for the fire-I subsequently
examined the claim made by Adams—I made an offer of £185 to settle
it, and advised the company to cancel the policy.
Cross-examined by MR. TAYLOR. I did not take the claim with me, and
check it-my partner, Mr. Rees, attended to the fire at Newington Butts.
W. A. REES (Re-examined). I made an examination of the wooden lift
at Newington Butts--there was no gas bracket attached to it-I know
about the other fire; I could not account for that I have heard it
accounted for by a swing bracket-we cannot account for every fire.
G. D. WOODCOCK (Recalled by Mr. K18oH). I know Edward Eyre-
the fire at Hastings took place on Wednesday morning-I do not
remember Fouraker calling on me at Blackfriars Road on the Tuesday,
and asking for his brother-in-law, Mr. Eyre, who was out-Eyre was
lodging at No. 11 at that time, for a week or two after I took possession
of the place—I do not remember his leaving and going to Sheffield-I
believe Fouraker called on the Saturday morning previous to the fire,
saying he had travelled from Manchester-I do not remember his
calling on the Tuesday, asking for Eyre; I could not say either way-I
do not remember Eyre going away on that day-I know Hastings pretty
well; I do not know such a place as Pearce's coffee-house there.
