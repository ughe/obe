 1012.  JOSEPH CANNON and JOHN LANE were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of April , 2 lbs. weight of cheese, value 1s. 4d., the goods of Christopher Tutin.
 WILLIAM WHITTINGHAM. I am an officer. On the 1st of April I saw the two prisoners together in the Strand, they passed the window of Mr. Christopher Tutin's shop, in  the Strand- he is a cheesemonger. I saw Cannon take a piece of cheese, which he gave to Lane; he passed the shop, turned short round, and took another piece, which he gave to Lane, who put them both into his apron, and wrapped it round his body - I took them both.
 Prisoner LANE. He took us into the shop, and asked how many pieces of cheese they had lost, they said one. Witness. They looked afterwards, and said there were two.
 CANNON -    GUILTY. Aged 16.
 LANE -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 1013.  JAMES WILKINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of March , certain fixtures, viz. 2 sash-frames, value 4s.; 3 sash-weights, value 1s., and 24 panes of glass, value 10s., of, and belonging to Daniel Dyball , and fixed to a certain building of his; against the Statute.
 DANIEL DYBALL. I am owner of a house in  Type-court, Type-street, Grub-street; I saw it on Sunday morning the 9th of March I think - it was all safe, and the sashes were all right; I had not been there before for some time - on the Monday or Tuesday night two officers came and gave me information; I went down and missed the sashes, here are some parts of them - I have fitted them and they appear to be part of the sashes; I had employed the prisoner occasionally, and he was a tenant of mine.
   THOMAS BREEDON. I am a carman. On Tuesday night the 11th of March, I was in bed, and was knocked up by some one who said there were thieves in Mr. Dyball's house next door to me; I went with Mr. Bee and examined the premises - we saw a parcel of old sashes broken, which had been taken from the second floor - we went round to Dyball's; he came with us and searched the premises; on coming away, I said, I had seen the prisoner on the Sunday, taking out some at the back premises - Dyball said, "He lives up here;" we went up to his apartment - he was in bed; they went in and I staid outside - they brought out this old frame and glass which we took to the watch-house; I did not hear the prisoner say anything.
 Prisoner. Q. Is there not a thoroughfare through the empty house to mine? A. It ought to be kept fastened, but I have seen it open.
   JOHN BEE. I went to the prisoner's bed-room and found these parts of sash-frames; he said nothing about them; I took him to the watch-house - I have matched the pieces to the house, they all correspond - I had information, and got up, and saw a light.
 DANIEL DYBALL. I swear these are my property; they were in the fore part of the house.
 Prisoner. Q. Was there a fire in my room? A. Very little, if any.
 Prisoner's Defence. There is no yard to my house; I went to bed that night very soon after nine - my wife staid up; she had to go to the cellar, and seeing these pieces thrown down, she brought them up in her apron, and put some on the fire.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1014.  WILLIAM SAMUELS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of February , 75lbs. of lead, value 5s., the goods of Isaac Dark , and fixed to a building of his.
 ISAAC DARK. I have a house at  Hammersmith, in the parish of Fulham. On the 20th of February, between three and four o'clock in the morning, a person gave me information: I went to the house and missed this lead: I found one piece of lead about eighteen yards from the premises, and the rest was turned up.
   RICHARD HEDGES. On the morning of the 20th of February, I was at Hammersmith and saw the prisoner and another man taking the lead off the shop front of Dark's house, I went up and spoke to the prisoner, who I knew, and they both ran away - I called, Dark, who got up and went to the premises: he got over, and went and took the prisoner up - some of the lead was torn up and chopped; I prevented them taking it away - it was about five o'clock.
   WILLIAM HALL. I found this piece of lead in the road to the creek, about seven o'clock in the morning.
   EDWARD EDGESON. I apprehended the prisoner, and matched part of lead to the premises - it fitted exactly.
 Prisoner's Defence. I can prove I was in bed at the time.
 - PRICE. I know the prisoner. On the night before the robbery he was about with me, and went to bed at half-past nine o'clock - we slept in the same bed at Mrs. Salmon's, near the water side; I left him in bed between six and seven in the morning, when I got up Mrs. Salmon saw us there - she is not able to come here; her daughter also saw us - it was on Tuesday night the, 20th of February.
 RICHARD HEDGES re-examined. I swear I saw him at the house. I was close to him, and spoke to him; he goes by the name of Doggey: I said "Master Doggey, I have caught you at last" and he ran away - I told Mr. Dark who it was - there is a gas-light opposite.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
