786. JOHN GRANT (30), Unlawfully conspiring with one John
Draper, not in custody, to rob Robert Johnson of divers of his moneys.
MR. BRINDLEY conducted the Prosecution; and MR. WARNER SLEIGH
the Defence.
ROBERT JOHNSON. I live at Lugar, in Ayrshire, and am a surgeon in
practice there on the 16th August I came up to London for a holiday
and on the 24th I went down to Croydon to see the races—when I went
on the course Grant, turning to me, asked if I knew the names of the
horses walking about I told him I did not, and immediately after-
wards I turned to go away-he came after me and said "I think you are
a Scotchman; I am a Scotchman too"-he said "I am here just now
for holidays"-he asked me first if I was, and I said “Yes”-he told
me his name was Grant-that he belonged to Paisley, his father was &
shawl manufacturer there, and that he had a brother in the firm-soon
after Draper came up, and Grant turned to me and said "Here is a
friend of mine coming who does some betting for me," and he told me he
was brother to the great jockey, Cannon-he introduced him to me as
"Cannon," and they spoke aside to each other for a minute or two-he
turned to go away, and when he had gone eight or ten yards, Grant
came after me and said to him, “You might see what you can do for my
friend here”-he turned round and held up his hand and said "AL
right”-I went on I turned round to Grant and said "I didn't come
here to bet; I am not going to bet at all, I know nothing about it"-
short time afterwards Draper came back, put a ticket for a bet made in
the hand of Grant, and handed one to me I said I didn't authorise him
to make any bets for me and refused to take the ticket at first-Draper
said “Oh, I understood I was to make a bet for you "-I objected again,
and Grant said “Oh, it is all right”-Draper said, when I objected to
pay the money, “Oh, but I have paid the money for you "-I think it
was 21.--that having been represented to me, although I had not
authorised him to do so, I gave him the money-Draper then went away
- I thought I was in duty bound to pay the money-I took them to be
gentlemen-Draper came back-I think it was after & race was run-he
said there had been a bet on, and that I had won 61. 108.-I thought he was
going to hand me the money, but he said "I have made another bet for
101." - I objected again, and Grant said “It is all right”-I was asked
for more money-I had only a 5l. note in my pocket at the time, and I
was induced to give it him-Draper said I was owing him something for
what he had paid for me, I cannot tell how much the next time he
came running back after the next race, and said " Oh, I have lost you
101.," but before the race finished he had made another bet for me,
whereby I had won 401.-he said he had risked 201., but won 401.-I
said "I am not going to have any more of this "-I told him I wished
to get away-when they saw I was anxious to get away one of them said
he would go and see if he could find the bookmaker, and let him come
out and see if he could make arrangements for me to go away-he repre-
sented that I had won some money-Draper went over to where the
crowd was, and came back and said " The bookmaker will be here after
the next race to see you"-he went away again and left Grant and
myself together, and came back and said that he had backed a horse for
151. for me I think I got a ticket twice-it seemed just like a visiting
card, with a man's name on it-I objected again to the man betting on
