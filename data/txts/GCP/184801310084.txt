Court. Q. How far is the place where the shovels are kept from the
gate? A. Close to it; there is just room for a cart to go out-I am sure the
prisoners bad nothing to do with the shovels that day.
JEREMIAH LOCKERBY (policeman, S 130.) I apprehended Jennings on
16th Jan.--I told him he must go with me, for being concerned with Busbell
in stealing a shovel-he said, "I know nothing at all about it"-he said alter-
wards, he had a shovel to clear some small stones in the barge, and he had
returned it-I took Bushell-I said it was on suspicion of stealing a shovel
- he said, “Very well, I will go with you"-in going along, he said, "I
have been expecting this all day"-I said, “Do you know Jennings I"-he
said, “I do not know his name; I lent a man a shovel, he brought it back
-when he was locked up, he said, "Go to the gate, and ask the porter if
he recollects bringing Is. 6d. on Sunday for the shovel; and if you will go to
Bullinger's you will find the shovel "--the mark has been erased, and there is
a black mark, about the size of a halfpenny, where it has been burnt out-
found on him a button, about the size of a halfpenny, that had been in the
fire-he said the 1s. 6d. had been divided between four of them-this shovel
was produced at the Police-court by Tolliday, who lived in the King's-road,
but he has absconded.
Bushell. Q. Did I not tell you to go to the man that worked for Mr.
Bullinger, and you would find the shovel? A. Yes--I went there, and
went to Mr. Bullinger's also.
