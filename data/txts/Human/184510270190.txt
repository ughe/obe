JAMES BAKER( policeman. ) I took the prisoner and told her the charge—she said it was all spite—next day, when locked up at the station I heard her say that she did give her sister the pepper-castor—she made a statement before the Magistrate, which was taken down—this is Mr. Henry's handwriting—( read )—"The prisoner says,—I lent the book to my father to read; my sister took it to him, and I intended her to bring it back, but I never could get it back, my father thinking it was my own; the other things I gave Eleanor to buy bread for herself and father, as she said they were in want of it."
   Prisoner Defence.  My sister came to me at Mr. Cole's one morning before Mr. Cole was up, and asked for some money, and something else to make away with; I said I had nothing; she went to the cupboard and saw this pepper-box and salt-cellar, and asked me for them; she laid them on the table; I said she might take them if she thought proper; she pledged them for 9 d ., and made away with the money; four months afterwards she owed me a little spite, and she took them out of pledge, and took them to Mr. Cole. ELEANOR STEWART re-examined.  She gave them to me at home, at her mother's house—I will not be sure whether she had left her place or not.
   MR. COLS re-examined.  The witness in her former examination stated that she did come to my house, and took them from there. ELEANOR STEWART re-examined.  I signed this examination, but they had a solicitor, and he stupified me so that I did not know what I was saying—( The witness's deposition being read, stated ,—"About four months ago I went to see my sister, the prisoner, when she was living with Mr. Cole, and she gave me a knife, pepper-castor, and salt-cellar, which I carried away.") NOT GUILTY.
