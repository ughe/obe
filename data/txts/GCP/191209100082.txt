September, 1912
HARTOP, Frederick (42, glass silverer), was indicted for and
charged on Coroner's inquisition with the manslaughter of Ellen
Hartop.
Prisoner pleaded guilty.
On September 1 prisoner, during a drunken quarrel, struck deceased
(with whom he had lived for 18 years and who had eight children by
him) a violent blow; she had three ribs fractured and died two days
afterwards. The post mortem examination showed that all the woman's
organs were healthy exoept the lungs; the ribs having been broken had
penetrated into the pleura; there was blood in the pleural cavity; this
had set up an acute stage of bronchial pneumonia; death had no doubt
been accelerated by the fracture of the ribs. Prisoner bore an excellent
general character and had been kind to his wife except when in drink.
Sentence: Four months' imprisonment, second division.

certain corrosive Auid upon Ellen Barrett, with intent to burn and dis-
figure her, or with intent to do her some grievous bodily harm.
Mr. L. A. Lucas prosecuted.
ELLEN BARRETT. I am a married woman. My husband came out of
prison this morning after doing six months' imprisonment. I have
been living with prisoner's husband for about eight weeks ; I knew that
he was marr ed. On July 29 I had been out with him and my little
boy; we were returning home to Campbell Road, Holloway. I had
almost reached home when I felt the fluid in my face. I saw prisoner
with a small brown jug in her hand. The fluid injured my right eye
and my mouth and lips. I am now quite recovered.
Cross-examined, I am on good terms now with prisoner and it is
my plea that she be dealt with as leniently as possible. I can quite
understand her feelings. I might have done the same thing myself
if I had been put in her shoes.
Police-constable WILLIAM SPENCER, 616 Y. On July 29, about
11 p.m., I heard a police whistle in Boven Sisters Rond. On going
up I saw prisoner and prosecutrix; the latter said, "She has thrown
something in my face." Prisoner, who seemed in a very excited state,
said, "It is spirits of salts." I took prisoner to the station. On the
way she said, " She won't ruin another woman's home." She is a per:
feotly respectable woman.
Cross-examined. She made no attempt to escape, but she was strug.
gling very violently with her husband when I arrived.
