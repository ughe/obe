 494. FREDERICK PRICE and HENRY PRICE , Robbery with violence on Charles Blatcher , and stealing a watch and chain, his property.
   MR. HUGGINS Prosecuted ; MR. BROUN Defended. CHARLES BLATCHER. I live at 25, Commercial Street, Commercial Road, E.—on Saturday night, about 20 minutes to 7, I was walking along Lancaster Street, and as I was crossing from Southwood Street, the taller prisoner gave me two blows in the neck and knocked me down, and as I was getting up, Frederick Price took my watch and chain from me—I drew this rule out of my pocket to strike him, and he gave them to me back, and they both made off—I went up the street and saw two friends home with whom I was with, and then saw two little boys, and in consequence of what they said, I went to 4, St. George's Court, London Market—I took a constable with me—he knocked a rat-tat  at the door—one of the prisoners came up and then went back again—the constable then walked upstairs—I went with him—he knocked at the front-room door, but no one answered, and we walked in and saw the two prisoners there—the constable asked me whether I recognised anyone there, and I turned to the small one and said, "Put on your clothes"—he was in his shirt-sleeves—he didn't do so, and I called on the policeman to make him, which he did, and I then said, "I shall charge him with stealing my watch and knocking me down," and gave them in charge—I have had abscesses in my throat ever since the blow, and have had to attend St. Thomas's Hospital—there is a hoarseness in the throat now—the value of my watch was 1 l .
   Cross-examined.  I only showed the rule to the short prisoner, the other one ran off—I had passed them previously in Lane Boad—it is an ordinary two-foot rule, and as soon as I showed him it he made off—I got to this house directly after the robbery, about 10 minutes to 7—they were behind me when this occurred—I was perfectly sober—I saw Louisa Smith as I was coming from the station—she spoke to me first—I ob served no one else in the street at the time.
