JAMES SAUNDERS ( policeman). About half past 3 o'clock on 17th July 1
heard a cry of “Police !” in St. Ann's-street, Westminster-it had been day.
light nearly half an hour-1 saw Ashby and another young man running out
of James-court, and Bakewell running after them, who said he had been
robbed, and I seized Ashby and asked him what he was running for-he said
he was running after his mate, who had robbed a man-I found this hand-
kerchief on him-before I showed it to Bakewell, I asked him, in Ashby
presence, what colour his handkerchief was-he said it was an old dark-bron
silk one-I showed it to him and he identified it-he was quite sober
Ashby said he supposed it would only be a sixer-Bakewell described the other
man to me, and I took Manning on suspicion twelve days afterwards--I have
known him for the last five years--I believe he has not had any fixed reni
dence lately, but I have seen him in St. Ann's-street, Westminster, and Duck-
lane, but could not see him between 17th and 29th July, though I wa
specially employed to look for him-I found him at the Portland Baths, 71,
Oxford-street, and told him the charge-he denied being the man-Bakewel
saw him at the station, and said he was the second person that came in at the
window after Ashby.
