THOMAS Mears. I am surgeon to the H division. On the 19th of April,
about twelve o'clock at night, I was sent for to the station the policeman,
Francis White, was there, I found two very serious wounds on his head, one on
the right side, and the other on the forehead, which was not quite so severe as
the other-it was a contused wound, about three inches long, and extended
to the bone-it must have been a very violent blow done by a blunt instru-
ment-he remained ill for three weeks--he was in considerable danger at
first-a good deal of head-ache and symptoms of internal injury.
Cross-examined. Q. Was it such a blow as might be caused by a police-
man's staff? A. It was that on the side of the head was more extensive
than the other that might have been caused by a policeman's staff.
Court to CHARLES ANDREW AMES. Q. You saw the man throw the staff
over the rails, what sort of rails were they? 4. A fence to keep the people
from some houses--the place behind the rails was five or six feet deep-we
went afterwards to look for the staff, but it was gone.
