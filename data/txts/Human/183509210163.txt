 2077. THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of Au gust , a quarter of a yard of woollen cloth, value 8 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Abra ham Russell , and another, his masters; and JOHN WAITE was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well-knowing it to have been stolen, against the Statue, &c. ABRAHAM RUSSELL. I have one partner. The prisoner Jones lived er rand-boy with us for about twleve months—on the 13th of Auguest, I missed a quarter of a yard of fine woollwn cloth—I went with the officer to Waite's house, after I had questioned Jones.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Had you not therntened Jones, and told him to tell the truth? A.  Yes; I thrashed him with the cane.
   COURT . Q.  When you arrived at Waite's what passed? A.  We asked Waite if he had bought a piece of black cloth, of a little boy, on the Wednesday (this was on Friday)—he said he had not bought a piece of block of any body—but he had bought a piece of green, which he had sold again on the Thursday—we then asked if he had bought any black of any boy—he said he had bought some that week—I asked him to produce a piece, which I described as about a quarter of a yard or rather more, and cut across the piece—he said, he had no such piece in his shop—the officer turned to the counter where a boundle of black laid—he opened it, and I identified a piece, as very much like the one I had lost—I compared it with the piece that it was cut off, and it fitted exactly.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did not waite point out the bun dle in which you found this? A.  No; he brought it after the officer saw it—I then went home to compare it, and when I returned Waite was still there. NOT GUILTY.
