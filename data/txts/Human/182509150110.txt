I took him after he had got round two or three turnings - he immediately took a bitter oath, that he had not seen the lace, nor any thing of it, or any thing else, nor touched it. I then went back to the prosecutor's house, and took him, and Locke the officer, to the public-house where I left the prisoner - but he was gone. Locke took us to the house in Bridgewater-square - we met the prisoner and some others coming out - they all ran away - but the prisoner fell down, and was taken; he then confessed that had taken the lace, and sold it.

 OLD COURT.
 SEVENTH DAY. THURSDAY, SEPTEMBER 22.
 Middlesex Cases, First Jury.
 Before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1524.  ANN WILKING was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , two pelisses, value 5 s.; two shawls, value 8 s.; nine sheets, value 6 l.; five shirts, value 30 s.; a shift, value 6 s.; five neckerchiefs, value 5 s.; two caps, value 10 s.; two spoons, value 25 s.; three towels, value 5 s.; an umbrella, value 2 s.; five pieces of linen, value 5 s.; a pair of clogs, value 1 s.; two table cloths, value 10 s.; two pairs of stockings, value 2 s.; five gowns, value 20 s.; two napkins, value 2 s.; a pair of stays, value 2 s.; a blanket, value 4 s.; a frock, value 1 s., and three flat irons, value 1 s., the goods of George Thomas Dale , her master, in his dwelling-house; and ANN BLISS was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
 GEORGE THOMAS DALE. I am a  surgeon, and live in King's-place, Commercial-road. Wilking was in my service. On the 10th of August, a table-cloth was missing and that led to this other property being found.
   JAMES LEA. I am an officer. On the 10th of August, I took Wilking into custody in Mr. Dale's kitchen, and asked what she had done with a table-cloth, if she had pawned it - she said No; but her mother had, and she believed it was at Mr. Aaron's, in Houndsditch. I went there with Mr. Dale, and found it - Bliss is her mother. I went to her house two or three times before she came home, and saw another daughter of her's who gave me a pocket-book, containing forty or fifty duplicates of property, which Mr. Dale identified. I took Bliss about four o'clock, and charged her with pawning property of Mr. Dale's; she said she was very sorry - she had done it through distress, and meant to redeem them again. I searched her, and found another pocket-book, with eight or nine more duplicates of Mr. Dale's property.
   JOHN AARON. I am a pawnbroker. I have a shirt pawned for 2 s. 6 d., and a towel for 9 d., both in September, 1824, a gown in November; some stockings in December; a shirt in May; a cap, a coat, frock, table-spoon, table-cloth, and gown, all at different times, and all by Bliss.
   EBENEZER HODGES. I am shopman to Mr. Baker, of Aldgate. I have a pelisse, shirt, gown, and stockings pawned at different times by Bliss.
   GEORGE GILES. I am a pawnbroker. I have a towel, two handkerchiefs, and a table-cloth, pawned by Bliss.
 MR. DALE. The property is all mine. Wilking lived two years with me.
 WILKING'S Defence. My mother is innocent; she knew not but they were my own. Sickness and poverty induced me to do it.
 BLISS'S Defence. I always understood they were her's.
 WILKING - GUILTY. Aged 35. Of stealing to the value of 39 s. only. Transported for Seven Years.
 BLISS -    GUILTY. Aged 65. Confined Two Years.
 Before Mr. Justice Littledale.
 1525.  JAMES COLLINS was indicted for the wilful murder of Nicholas Dawkins.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND and BRODRICK conducted the prosecution.
 THE RIGHT HONOURABLE  LORD WESTMORELAND. On the 23d of May I was one of a party at Lord George Cavendish's, and had occasion, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, to go out with my servant, and when I came to the gate two carriages were jammed in together; no person could pass - several people were about. I called an officer, desired him to do his duty and clear the way; I suppose the officer to be the deceased, but I did not know him then; he spoke to one of the coachmen, then went in front of the prisoner's carriage, and was speaking to him; the other carriage gave way a little, and the prisoner's carriage rushed with great velocity up the yard; I cannot say that he whipped his horses - I did not see him do any thing - I thought there must be a good deal of mischief done, but I was so struck with his conduct that I immediately followed the carriage, nearly to the door, and asked the owner's name - he said Lady Lambert; I immediately went into the house, and told her Ladyship. I then came out, and found the Police officer against the wall, with his legs broken - I did not observe the man sufficiently to say whether he is the one I had told to do his duty, but I suppose he was; when he went up he was in front of the carriage - I saw him use no violence.
