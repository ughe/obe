 639. WILLIAM HAWKINS was indicted for stealing; on the   15th of January , 1 cheese, value 2 s  ., the goods of William Waghorn . WILLIAM WAGHORN. I am a cheesemonger, and life at Woolwich. On the 15th of January, at half-past eight o'clock in the evening, I was in my shop—I saw the prisoner come to the window, and take this cheese from a pile of twenty-eight cheeses—he ran away—I followed, and overtook him about a hundred yards off—this is the cheese—the prisoner is a marine, and is quartered at Woolwich. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Six Weeks.
 640. SAMUEL BIRD was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of January , 1 pair of trowsers, value 16 s  ., the goods of William Oliver Henderson . WILLIAM OLIVER HENDERSON. I am a clothier, and live at Wool wich. I lost a pair of trowsers on the 10th of January, which had been hanging on an iron rod, which goes into my shop and round the door-post. JAMES CHANDLER. I am shopman to Mr. Davis, a pawnbroker. On the evening of the 10th of January, the prisoner brought these trowsers to me to pawn. I saw the legs were pinned together—I sent for the officer, and took the prisoner. WILLIAM CHITTENDLE. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and produce the trowsers. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined Three Months.
 641. WILLIAM BESSER was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of January , 1 pair of boots, value 3 s  ., the goods of Andrew Donohue . ANDREW DONOHUE. I am in the artillery, and am quartered in Wool wich. On the 29th of January I got intoxicated, and lost a pair of boots which were on my feet—I cannot tell how they came off, or whether I took them off myself. CHARLES DAWSON. I am a watchman it Woolwich. On the morning of the 29th of January, at two o'clock, I saw the prosecutor lying under the batchers'shambles in the old market-place—he had only one boot on, and no cap—I searched about and found his cap, but could not find his boot—I saw him again in a short time after, when I had been round my beat, and then his other boot was gone, and he was turned over—I took him to the watch-house, but could not find the boots. WILLIAM DAVIS. I am a police officer. I saw the prisoner on the 29th of January, about twelve o'clock in the night, with these boots under his arm—I followed him about two hundred yards, and asked him what he had got—he said, "A pair of old boots"—I took them, and examined them—they appeared to be military boots—I took him to the watch-house, and there found the prosecutor—the prisoner said he found them in the market. NOT GUILTY.
