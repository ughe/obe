   MR. BAYLEY conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM NICHOLS. I am a tailor, residing in the Old Kent Road. On Monday, 15th Feb., I saw the prisoner in Gnildball Yard—at that time a woman of the name of Spurway was indebted to me in the sum of 4 l . 5 s . 3 d .—Iattended as a witness—the prisoner subpoenaed me on the previous Satur day, in an action which was to be tried there—he was recommended to me as an attorney—I had some conversation about this debt which was due—I told him a person owed me a small amount, and she had said she would pay me if it ever lay in her power, but I could not take any proceedings against her, because I had taken some goods back, reducing the amount of the bill from 9 l . 14 s . to 4 l . 5 s . 3 d .—the prisoner said, "Oh yes, that is to be got easily enough; that is all right enough"—he said, "I will call upon you this evening, to see what the amount is"—he did call; he then said, "Pro ceedings must be taken in the Proctor's Court to recover the debt," as the debtor was a widow, and had not administered—I afterwards saw him at the Five Bells public house in the evening—he said he had been to see Mrs. Spurway—he said she had stated that she could pay the debt, but would not pay it without giving me further trouble—I said, "That is very strange;"and he said I should have to take proceedings in the Proctor's Court, and that immediately, because he said she had stated, she had paid everybody else, but would not pay me—he had previously told me he should require 30 s . to take these proceedings, and that would be the expense of the Court—he asked me if I could advance him 15 s . towards those pro ceedings—Itold him I would let him know in the morning, and he left me his address;
