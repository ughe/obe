   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What sort of a shop was it? A.  My work was there, and I was going to take it to another place, and somebody said that I was going to take it away—it was not Mr. Cox's work—I was never tried before that time, and then I was sent by the Justice for a month.
   COURT . Q.  Were you tried in any Court, or committed by the Ma gistrate? A.  I do not know—I was before some persons—I was tried at a bar something similar to this—I cannot say what place they called it—I had never been in a Court of Justice before that time—I believe there was a Jury—it was several years ago—I cannot say exactly how long, it may be six, seven, or eight years.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Have you been working for Mr. Mason since that time? A.  Yes. CHARLES FRANCIS. I am a trunkmaker, living in Lower-street, New castle. I know the Royal Express beer-shop, or public-house—I was there on Saturday, the nomination evening before the last election, between eight and nine o'clock—I know Mr. Miller, the member—he came into the Royal Express beer-shop—I do not know Mr. Mason—there were several gen tlemen with him—the name of the landlord is Mr. Boot—some gentleman with Mr. Miller asked him for his vote in his hearing—I was coming out of the house at the same time—I afterwards saw Mr. Miller canvassing the voters in Lower-street, and I saw him at the Castle hotel—before that I saw him at the bottom of Church-lane—I was at the Castle about half-past nine o'clock, or a quarter to ten—I went there to hear Mr. Miller address the people—while I was standing there, I saw Mr. Miller—he went into the Castle hotel, and a gentleman had hold of his arm—when he got in he came to the window, and made a short speech—I heard that.
   Cross-examined by MR. KELLY . Q.  What time was this? A.  Nearer ten o'clock than nine—a young man of the name of Thomas Patmore was with me, he is a shoemaker—I have lived some years in Newcastle—I am not a voter—I am no relation or connexion of Gallimore's—my wife is no relation of him, nor his wife—we did not marry two sisters—there is no con nexion, or kindred, between my wife and his—my wife's maiden name was Amelia Downs—I do not know who the prisoner married—his brother married a sister of my wife's.
   Q.  What is the reason you did not tell me that? A.  That is no con nexion of mine—I never thought about it—it was getting dusk, but quite light enough for anybody to distinguish a man's face—I swear that.
