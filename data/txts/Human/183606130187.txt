I pray you leave me alone—I don't want to play with you"—they laughed, and said many disagreeable words, and squeezed my legs—I complained many times, but it was useless—they did not understand—during that time we had passed one gate, where the carriage paid the duty—they stopped at that time, and then went on again—I called the carter to stop the cart—he said nothing—he smoked his pipe, and I knocked him—he was sleepy all the night—he says, "That is very bad; you make very bad manners with that gentlemen"—then I get over the cart to go and get my portmanteau—he would not let me have it—I said, "You must let me have it"—I cannot tell the precise time when I lost my skewers—it was about one o'clock in the morning—the two prisoners were by me when I lost them—they did not push me before they left—I suppose they came to London—they stopped till I got down, but I stopped about half-road—I got my hat and port manteau, and went to find the policeman—Stone's hand was on any pocket to get my watch out, but the skewers taken before that—I was sensible they were taken from me—Stone took them, I am certain—when they were out I was much more comfortable—I was not so squeezed—the other prisoner knocked my head to give him facility—I was not knocked at all before the skewers were taken—I was squeezed before I felt them go.
