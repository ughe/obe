I only come among you as a visitor, but I wish to ask you one or two questions, and I take the opportunity of seconding the resolution to ask to ask you—I wish to know, has organization done its duty among you?—are you satis  fied, Englishmen, that the troops of Britain are sent to slaughter your fellow men in Ireland I  Are you satisfied? We are not satisfied, and we are come to the determination, and let the reporter hear me, that sooner than any shall leave London for Ireland, they must leave it across our corpses (cheers); I made a vow a month ago that I would never say anything at a meeting again; but you must pardon me to-night, for the future nothing but action is required for men; I beg to second the resolution"—there were some words at the con clusion which escaped me—Mr. Counsell's note is fuller than mine—after that the defendant spoke again—he said, "I rise for the purpose of answering the two questions Mr. Rooney has required to have answered. One was, whe ther we were organized or not? You are aware we are organized, and that all the acknowledged Chartists belonging to this Hall are organized, and under the proper officers; but every individual who comes into this Hall is not under organization, therefore we have no control over them. The other ques tion was, whether you were satisfied with the present abuse towards Ireland.? '(cries of 'No!') and whether you would co-operate, and whether you are willing, that is, the organized part of you, because the others are of no use to any party, whether those who belong to the organization are willing to co operate with the confederates (cries of 'Yes! ') It bas been said some time ago, that the confederates would not come over to us, and that we must go over to them; but this evening is a contradiction to that statement, for the confederates have come to you, and they are willing to co-operate; and I trust Mr. Rooney, when he returns to his club, will inform them that the organized Chartists of the South Lambeth locality, are willing to go hand in hand with any scheme that the confederates have for the amelioration of Ireland"—immediately after that Rooney said, "I beg to tender the best gratitude of the John Mitchell Club, for your sympathy with their unfortunate countryman, and I hope"—there was an interruption there—something was said about a nod being as good as a wink to a blind horse, but I did not write it down—Mr. Counsell has it.
   Prisoner. Q . You have mentioned that I took the chair without any cere mony? A.  I did not hear you proposed into the chair—you seemed to me to take it as a matter of course, as if there had been some previous arrangement—no one interrupted me in taking my report—I asked for accommodation as soon as I entered the room, and stated the purpose for which I came—I took, the whole of the meeting verbatim, not merely the heads—there was so much confusion, that if any proposition was made for you to take the chair, I did not take it.
