Cross-examined by the Prisoner. I did not pick out a prisoner on the

ANNIE PENNYMOULD. I am the wife of Henry Pennymould, of 27,
Porter Street, I saw the prisoner and four others come out of a public-
house—they lounged upon our barrow, which was standing outside our
shop-my husband told them to go away; the prisoner said he would
knock his head off-I saw them go up Titchfield Street, and stand
against the railings at No. 7 for a minute or two, and they walked along,
the prisoner and another being in front of the prosecutrix--the prisoner
struck her on her arm--they pushed her back against the house-one
man took her bag and ran round a little turning--my house is No. 6,
and this occurred at 8 or 9, on the same side of the way I have no
doubt the prisoner was one of the men-I went to the station on 19th
May, and picked him out directly from six or eight others.
THOMAS BOWDEN (Detective Officer). The prisoner was given into cus-
tody on 19th May-I saw Mrs. Botto and Mrs. Pennymould pick him
out-Mrs. Botto referred to a short man as the one who ran away-I
charged the prisoner; he made no answer.
GEORGE STONE (Policeman C 193). I was on duty with another con-
stable in Porter Street-Mrs. Pennymould called our attention to these
men, and I went to the back of Prince's Row; that is a court leading
into Titchfield Street-I went into a public-house to see the prisoner; I
found nobody-that was about 4 o'clock-I afterwards found the bag
(producod); in it was a miniature letter-box made of wood; I took it to
the lady-I afterwards found a bunch of keys at No. 9; I do not know
who lives there.
