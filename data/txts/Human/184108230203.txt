BRIDGET IVES. I am the wife of Isaac Ives, and the sister of Dalton. I know the prisoner, and have seen his wife twice. On the 25th of June I was with my sister, coming down King-street, and just before we got op posite the King's Arms public-house I saw the prisoner and his wife stand ing on the pavement—I saw him hit her on the left side of the head—she fell on her right side into the road—I had not heard them talking together before, but they seemed to be talking—she got up, and went to the other side of the road, and he walked in the middle—when they got about twenty yards, she went to him—he had only hit her once, but he made an offer to hit her then—she put her hand into her pocket, and he went into the Cheroot public-house—we went to where he knocked her down, and she showed us the mark where he had hit her in the head, and the scratch where she fell—I here is a pavement there—I saw her next Thursday coining from the doctor's—she looked very bad, and had her head bound up in a white cloth—I saw nothing more of the prisoner—I cannot say whe ther he was sober or drunk.
