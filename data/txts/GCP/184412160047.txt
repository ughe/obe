WILLIAM Davis (police-constable G 143.) On Monday morning, the
95th of Nov., I was on duty in Bath-street, City-road, and about a quarter
past one I found a hat nearly opposite the Red Lion, under the madhouse
Will-I also found this case of instruments lying close to the hat-I took
the hat to the station immediately, and it was claimed by Sutters as being
his property-I gave it to him-Jenkinson afterwards went with me and
pointed out a spot near the madhouse wall-it was the exact spot where
I had found the hat and the instruments.
Cross-examined. Q. About how many yards from the hat was the case
of instruments? A. Close to it--they were lying close together under the
wall.
MR. MELLOR called
Tuomas Suez. I reside in Paul-street, Finsbury, and am a dealer in
building materials. My sister keeps a beer-shop, and I look after it for
her-on Sunday evening, the 24th of Nov. last, the prisoner Butters came
to our house about hall-past five o'clock, and stopped till about eleven
o'clock--I remember the time he came, because we usually open at five
o'clock; and as soon as we had opened, and got ready for tea, he came
in-he had used the house for some time I know he stopped till eleven
o'clock, because we usually close the room at eleven o'clock on Sunday
evenings, and get to bed soon-I am certain he did not quit the house
between those hours- I was in the house mysell-I serve up stairs, and
no one else serves there but me--I will swear he did not quit my sight
between half-past five and eleven o'clock-be might just have gone down
stairs.

Mr. COBBETT. Q. You live at this beer-shop with your sister? 4.
Yes--we have kept it about seventeen months Sutters has used the house
about nine or ten months-he has come there of a night to have a pint of
beer-I have never seen Noon there, to my knowledge-Sutters has been
there at times with persons-I never take particular notice who parties
come in with—it is not my business, as long as they conduct theniselves
properly while they are in my place-he has been there with other per-
sons—I cannot say whether they were his companions-I catinot say
whether he had been there on the day before this-I am generally out all
day on Saturday, in fact every day-I am only at home a little of a night
-- he was in the up-stairs room on this evening-it is a sitting-room,
where we have a little bit of a concert of a night, and on a Sunday even-
ing a few friends meet there to spend their time-I do not know about
their being his friends-they get acquainted with one another there we
do not have music on a Sunday evening, nor any dancing—there are men
and women there of course-a man may come with his wife, and have half
a pint of beer- I do not allow prostitutes to come to the bouse-if I see a
bad character, man or woman, come to my place, I tell them to walk out,
or I will give them in charge-I do not exactly inquire into their cha-
racters, but as soon as I find they are bad characters it is my duty to turn
them out-I never discovered that Sutters was a person of bad character
I believe he is not-I do not exactly say he is a good character, or a bad
character--I do not know what he is I do not know anything of his cha-
racter.
