868. THOMAS WILLIAMS (18), Stealing a metal box, the property
of Richard Cummings.
MESSRS. MONTAGU WILLIAMS and TORR Prosecuted; MR. HOPKINS Defended.
Annie CUMMINGS, I am the wife of Richard Cummings, a corn dealer,
2. West Dale Street, Forest Hill-on the 28th August, at about 2.30
in the afternoon, I was in the washhouse at the back of the premises
behind the parlour-I heard a noise in the parlour, and looked and saw
the prisoner in the parlour-he had hold of this tin box, which stood on
the mantelpiece-he had moved it from the mantelpiece, and had it in
his hands--I screamed and ran into the parlour, and caught hold of him
by the arm-he said "I don't want to do any harm," and he pushed me
of-I still held him-lie said "I only want to buy two quarts of chickens
corn, will this box bold it?"--my brother-in-law came into the shop
from the front of the house-the prisoner asked him to let him go her
said he wanted to buy two quarts of chickens' corn, for his landlady
would let him keep chickens-he was then given into custody.
Cross-examined. I had never seen him before the box was a biscuit-
box of no particular value he did not try to run away.
FREDERICK GEORGE CUMMINGS. On 28th August, about 1 o'clock, &
man came to buy half a peck of oats-he tendered half & sovereiga, and
I gave him him the change-about 2 o'clock a man drove up in a trap
and called me outside, and as I was speaking to him about supplying
him with forage I heard my sister-in-law scream out-I ran into the
abop, and found my sister-in-law holding the prisoner she said she had
found him in the back room-I asked him what he was doing there
-- he said he had come into tbere not to do any harm, and he wanted to
buy two quarts of chicken's corn, and not seeing anybody in the shop, he
went through-the parlour is our private apartment, we never allow
customers there-I asked him his name, he said " Tom Jones "--I gave
him into custods—the man in the trap drove away.
Cross-examined. The prisoner must have seen me in front of the shop; he
had to open the door to go into the parlour, and he had fastened it back.
George Moss (Poljooman P 608). I was ealled and took the prisoner
in custody-he said he was very sorry for what he had done.
WILLIAM Hodak (Policeman P 899). On taking the prisoner to the
police-court on the 29th of August, I asked him if he had given his correct
name and address-he said he had—he further said he went there with
the intention of stealing the cash-box, but he took the wrong one.
Guilty. He also PLEADED Guilty to having been convicted at Wandsworth
Police-court on 27th December, 1877.
