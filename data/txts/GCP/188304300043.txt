Cross-examined. The book did not remain in Emith's possession, but it
remained in bis department, and he was able to get at it-Mr. Flatow
usually paid the workpeople-it was not necessary for Smith to be present
-I am always present; I tick the wages book-a little wicket opens into
an office, and the men are paid through it-I saw Coben paid these
amounts-workmen sometimes come and ask for other persons' wages
besides their own, and if they bring the book they are paid-Oohen did
not bring a book- he came himself.
STANFORD SMITH (The prisoner). I have pleaded guilty to falsifying
these entries-I was in Mr. Flatow's service I saw Oohen at the
Cambridge Music Hall in January last-he said "Trade is very bad, and
I have been wanting to see you to ask you if you can make some money
for me every week in the account-book,” or “the outdoor work-book”-I
said "I don't think I can, as you are only a common finisher"-he said
“Yes, you can; this time last year I was taking 81. or Ul. a week
I said "I will tell you to-morrow whether I can," and next day I said "I
have put down 11. for the first week more than you have really earned"
- he told me to make erasures in the wages book-I made alterations
every week down to 17th March, and always let him know what I had
put en by holding up as many fingers as I had altered the pounds-I
never altered the shillings and pence.
JOHN NATHANIEL FLATOW. I am one of this firm-we are boot and
shoe manufacturers and leather merchants-I was present on these dates,
and paid Cohen wages as King called them out to me I received a letter
from Smith when he was in the House of Detention, and went there, and
he made a communication to me.
Cross-examined. It was after that that I commenced proceedings
against Cohen-I paid Cohen myself-he always asked for the sum
which was in the book, and if it corresponded King would say “ All
right," and I paid it.
COHEN-GUILTY. (See next case.)
