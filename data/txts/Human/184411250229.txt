   COURT . Q.  Are you sure that the things you found at John Perfect's are yours? A.  Yes—I have brought another spring to match this one—I know this pillow, and believe it to be mine—this is one of my sacks—I claimed two at John Perfect's—this one was cut open and laid upon the ground—this has the maker's name on it. SUSANNAH FRANKLIN. I went to the station with Mr. Skinner—I know this pillow and tongs. THOMAS BENJAMIN BAKER SKINNER. I went to Rose Cottage, and the prisoners were loading a cart—I could not discover what things were in it—I did not stop—John Perfect drove it—when I went back the things at the bottom were all covered over, and then they put their own things on the top—they had got some of their own things there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were present during the whole time they loaded the cart? A.  No—I went after they had got a good many things in the bottom of the cart—I then went home and told my mother, and then went back—John Perfect saw me—I do not know whether the things he carried away were his own or not.
   Henry Perfect's Defence . I could take my solemn oath that my brother did not take them away.
   MR. SKINNER re-examined . John Perfect occupied the cottage near Gravesend, and Henry was at Rose-cottage.
   MR. DOANE called BETSY WALKER. I live at Northfleet, and get my living by labour. I know John Perfect—I have been working with him there for four months—this pillow they have had ever since I knew them—here is one particu lar mark that I know it by, a piece of black thread at one corner. JOSEPH SKINNER I wish to know what did you say to me in reference to the pillow when I was at the house.
   Witness . I said one was fuller than the other, and this one they had when they first came down—I was there when they came—the cart only came once—he and his wife both came.
   MR. SKINNER re-examined . It was on the Tuesday after the 1st. of Nov. that I took the goods at Gravesend—Henry Perfect's goods were not mixed with mine—I put mine in a room separate. NOT GUILTY.
