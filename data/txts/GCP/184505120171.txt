Cross-examined. Q. They stood up and fought together; was that after
the table was thrown over? A. Yes--they did not fall—they went out to
fight-it all happened in the front yard-there was no interval between
the table being thrown over and their going out to fight-it was all one
transaction-the deceased and his companions were certainly laughing at
the prisoner, what is commonly termed chaffing that was what Bates
was doing, not all of them the prisoner seemed annoyed at it-I heard
him say he was not lousy-I think that was in answer to the deceased,
who said he was anything but a respectable man, because he wore a
corduroy jacket-I should say the prisoner would not have spoken to him
-I have known him six years he has been a well-condueted person
from what I have seen of him-I merely employed him to cart things to
the Docks.

Robert MouseLY GLENNY. I live at Ilford. I was sitting in the
yard of the Rabbits public-house with Bates on the 23rd of April–the
prisoner came and sat by the side of Bates-he said, “I suppose you will
not like me to eit by the side of you with this old coat on" - I do not know
what answer Bates made-I was engaged talking to a cow-keeper, and when
I listened again the prisoner was talking about Ireland-Bates said, “You
never were in Ireland in your life "--the prisoner said he was in Ireland
several times, he was there not a great while ago--this was all in good
humour-Bates said, "I will lay you a sovereign of it"- the prisoner
said, “I will lay you a sovereign"-he put down two sovereigns and a
half upon the table, and said, “ Put down yours"-Bates said, "No"-
after that the prisoner said, “I am a Roman Catholic; I am a Roman
Catholic to the backbone"-Bates said, "If you are, why do you not do
