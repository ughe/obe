Cross-examined. Q. You live in the same house, and that little girl
played with you that afternoon? A. No; I only saw her go out at the
back-yard--I hallooed out, "There is a noise at the window"-I saw her
once before go to fill her kettle, after her parents went out-she had to go
about twenty yards to get water.
HENRY TURNER. I know all the prisoners. On the 27th of January
was coming home from work, about twenty minutes past six o'clock-1
passed the prosecutor's house-I saw Farringdon up against the wall, and
Lavell stooping down, about ten feet from their yard--they were in the
street- I thought there was something up, and when I got to the top,
looked about half a minute, and then they were on the same spot-I then
went home, and returned about half-past six o'clock-I saw Lavell pass
with a box on his head, and three or four other men-he went straight
down the street-one of the other men was Farringdon.
Cross-examined. Q. Lavell and Farringdon were near this yard ? A.
Yes, it is; Mr. Hines yard, he lodges there. I call it his yard-Farringdon
lives about 200 feet off-Lavell had a frock coat and white trousers-Far
ringdon bad white mole-skin clothes- I am sure of that I cannot tell
who the others were.
SAMUEL Lock. I produce the box-1 found it within two doors of my
house, No, 4, Church-terrace, under the church wall-that is a quarter of
a mile from the prosecutor's-it was in this broken state.
John HINES. This trunk is mine—it was not broken--when I left it,
it was locked-I had in it at two o'clock, when I left the house, a watch
two coats, two pairs of trowsers, and a waistcoat--the rest of the pro
perty belongs to my wife-I came home at eight o'clock-I had been bome
to dinner-the two Cranstons live in the same house - I work at Me
Maltwood's factory at Lambeth.
Cross-examined. Q. Mrs. Cranston is the person to whom this house
belongs ? A. Yes-the prisoners lived with their mother-I was twice
before the Magistrate-I bave not spoken to my girl about what she should
say, nor threatened her-I left the house in her care.
MARY SNELL. I live in the same house with Mrs. Cranston-I wa
in her room when the sons came home to tea that night-they were in
hurry for it-George said to his mother, "I think I shall go down to Hina
and boil my kettle"-Charles was in a hurry to go-George swore, and
said, "What a hurry you are in."
Cross-eramined. Were you present when the little girl called out
to Mrs. Cranston, and the boy Hawkins ? A. Yes-Mrs. Cranston want
down stairs, and asked what was the matter-I followed her-Mrs. Cranston
talked about going for her father--the girl was afraid for her father to be
sent for, as she said he would murder her.
THOMAS William BEDFORD (police-sergeant L 17.) I took Farringdon
into custody.

but did not take it in--we gave it back-two young men brought it, but
I cannot swear to them.
