 (Friday, November 8.)
 Paying in book produced shows amounts paid into my account with the London City and Midland Bank, Queen Victoria Street, from January 25 to June 28, 1906; three slips are in my writing, four in Harris's, and the remainder are in Mabel Long's and some other clerk's handwriting. On the slip of February 2, 1906, written by Long, there are resemblances in the writing to the signature of "A. R. Fraser" in the Economic Bank signature book. Cheque-book No. 344 of A. R. Fraser has eight counterfoils in my writing. I could not tell you who filled the cheques in. I was not on confidential re  lations with Harris and he did not tell me who filled those cheques up. I do not pretend to remember, but it is probable the money was wanted for certain orders when I was doing the accounts at Smith's, and I wrote the counterfoil to indicate how much was wanted from Smith's banking account, and it was drawn from the Fraser ac count. I have never seen Fraser. On June 29, 1904, the business of Herbert Smith was closed down—cheques of £50 and £210 in July would be the settling of old business. Of £100 drawn from Fraser's account in the Economic Bank as "Stereos," £20 in notes is paid into H. Smith's account (Harris); £20 to Chalmers and Cox (Hawkee); £20 to my account at London City and Midland Bank, and £30 to my account at Farnborough. Whatever I got I got from Mr. Harris trading as Herbert Smith. (Witness admitted that a large number of accounts and several letters in Herbert Smith's letter-books were in his writing.) I was helping Smith as much as I could, and when I was there all day I have no doubt I wrote one or two letters out of those many thou sands. He was busy on account day, and I have helped him. On December 17, 1902, cheque Montgomery £10 was paid into my ac count; in Fraser's letter-book, p. 91, is a letter acknowledging receipt of £10 signed A. R. Fraser, with a stamp. On December 18 "Price £10" is on my paying-in slip; on the same date is letter from A. R Fraser acknowledging receipt of £10 to Price. On December 19 in my paying-in book is "S. P. Bowles £4"—letter from Fraser acknow ledging receipt of same. "H. D. English £5" is the same. There is no, concealment about it—the names are put in my slip. I got the cheques from H. Smith, whatever they were, and they were paid into my account and marked accordingly. There is nothing to show it comes from A. R. Fraser. January 28, 1903, Price £40; February 27, S. Hynd £10; September 14, 1903, Foster Knowles £206 7s., are amounts dealt in in the same way and the paying-in slips are in Harris's writing. I accept it as much as you like.
