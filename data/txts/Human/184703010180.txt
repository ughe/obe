 848. RICHARD FARRELL was indicted for stealing 30lbs. weight of twine, value 1 l  ., the goods of William Bennett ; and SAMUEL MORRIS for feloniously receiving part of the same, well knowing, it to be stolen. WILLIAM BENNETT. I live in Worcester-street, Union-street, Southwark, I am a twine-manufacturer. On the 12th of Feb. I sent twelve bundles of twine by my servant, to Abney-street, Horsleydown—I saw one of them in possession of the officer the same evening—this is one of the twelve bundles, and is mine. JOHN HOLLAND. I am in Mr. Bennett's employ. I was sent by him on the 12th of Feb., to Abney-street, with twelve bundles of twine, in a truck—this is one of the bundles—I went to Mr. Edgington's factory, and left my truck, in order to get one of the men to open the gates—there were twelve bundles in the truck when I got to the gates, and when I came out of Mr. Edgington's factory I missed two—I had been absent about a minute, and then two bundles were gone—I had seen Farrell in Collingwood-street—I asked him to give me a lift with my truck through the snow, and he helped me to push it as far as Edgington's—he then asked me for 1 d . for half-a pint of beer, and I said I had not a farthing. PHILIP DAVIES. I saw Holland drawing the truck, assisted by Farrell—I saw the truck stop at Edgington's gate—Farrell went from the truck to Three Oak-lane, opposite my house—Holland went into Edgington's—I saw Farrell return to the truck and take two bundles of twine out, and run off with them—I sent my servant to give information to Edgington. JOSEPH ROYLE KNOWLES. I am a marine-store dealer, and live in Tooley-street. Between six and seven o'clock in the evening, on the 12th of Feb., Morris brought me a bundle of twine to sell—I asked him what he wanted for it—he said a groat a pound—I should have to give about 10 d . a pound for it—I gave my boy 1 s ., with a pretence of getting change, and sent for a policeman, and gave Morris in charge—I had heard there was twine stolen.
   Morris.  A man came and asked me to sell it—he said he would give me a pint of beer—he said, "Ask 4 d . a pound for it"—I said I was led into an error. Witness , No, he brought it in a small bag—he left the twine on the counter, and he went out and gave the bag to a boy, who, I believe, was the other prisoner.
