 Q. How then did he owe him 25l.? A. It was a bill on Mr. Tibbins for 15l., and the costs amounted to 25l. - he was party to that bill - Mr. Tibbins was a bankrupt, and he endorsed the bill.
 Q. Was not the bill given to receive 25l., to pay 15l. which was the debt, and the rest for the watch? A. No; it was 25l. for the old debt - my father did not give any receipt on account of the old debt - directly my father ascertained it was all a trick, he went before a Justice - that was about three weeks afterwards, as far as I can recollect - I believe the interview at the Bank Coffee-house was on the 27th of June, and the latter end of July or the beginning of August applied to before the magistrate - he had given full value for the bill in money and the old debt - I saw the prisoner about a week afterwards, and said, "Where is my watch?" and he pulled it out of his pocket - my father went several times to the house, and he could not be found, and he sent him a letter - my father appeared at Lambeth-street, and made a charge against the prisoner - I do not exactly know on what day - I went to the Mansion-house after that about this charge - I wanted to prefer the charge at Lambeth-street, but they said I must go the Mansion-house - my father's charge was not dismissed - I believe it was a charge of obtaining money, under false pretences - it was not a felony, but a fraud - the prisoner was ordered to be taken to the Mansion-house, the money being obtained in the City - I went before the Lord Mayor respecting the watch after I had been to Lambeth-street, but I do not recollect the date - Mr. Norton was the attorney at Lambeth-street - my father went to Lambeth-street of his own accord - I was only at one examination at Lambeth-street - on my oath I was only there once - I went to the Mansion-house after that - I then went to Guildhall, as the Lord Mayor did not sit that day - I appeared at the Mansion-house about the beginning of August - I know it was in August.
 Q. Did you not go to Guildhall on the 20th of August, and to the Mansion-house on the 26th of August? A. I do not know the dates - I think it was about five weeks before I went to either place - I will not swear it was less than seven - I will swear it was less than eight weeks from the day the watch was obtained, till I went before the Lord Mayor at the Mansion-house - when he showed me the watch, he said he had not seen Mr. Alexander - I did not demand it of him - I did not tell Mr. Norton about it - I have never seen the prisoner between June and August - I did not go to look for him - my father did - and my father had such confidence in him, seeing him dressed all in black, and he gave him a paper, which my father has got.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You expected he would return the watch in two or three days? A. I did; I never gave him authority to keep it - the Bill of Exchange forms the subject of another charge against him.
   JOSEPH MILLINGEN. I am the father of Charles Millingen. I remember the prisoner coming to me at the coffee-house - he represented Mr. Alexander as an independent gentleman, living on his estate at Maidstone, who was executor to his aunt, Mrs. Ann Bradshaw- and after the business had been transacted about a bill, he said Mr. Alexander was a very good friend, and he should like to make him a present of a watch - my son happened to take his watch out, and he said, "I should like one something like that," and said he should like something similar to present to Mr. Alexander - my son seemed to look at me, to know if he should allow him to show it, as he said he should like to show it to Mr. Alexander, and if he liked it, he should like to give him one similar to it - he took the watch away - he was to return it in a few days, I am certain - my son has never got it, to my knowledge.
 Cross-examined. Q. I do not know whether you and I are old acquaintances? A. I have had the pleasure of seeing you before - the watch was merely seen by the prisoner by accident - my son might have taken it out, and Wilkins see it and say, "I should like to make a similar present to Alexander" - he said he should like a watch like my son's, which he had seen before - my son might have taken it out, whether by accident I do not know - he never saw my son before.
 Q. How often have you been a bankrupt, Mr. Millingen? A. I have been a bankrupt, and am proud to boast I have paid 20s. in the pound - I was a bankrupt twenty-two years ago - I was one but a year ago, and lost 22,000l. - I have been a bankrupt four times, but always paid 20s. in the pound - I have never compounded with my creditors or taken the benefit of the Act - I never superseded either of the commissions - I have been plundered of too much money - there was 3,000l. to pay 1,000l. - I have been convicted of a conspiracy - I beg to state it was through the attorney who is now conducting the defence, Saul Yates, of Bury-street - it was in consequence of his swearing he saw placards in the hands of myself, and the other party, when we had ten respectable witnesses - we have a rule nisi granted for a new trial, on the perjury of Mr. Yates.
 Q. Among the rest of your misfortunes, how often have you been burnt out and recovered the money from the offices? A. I never recovered money but once - when my wife was nearly burnt to death, I received 70l., and my costs were 200l. - I have had fires in my factory when I was not insured for a farthing, and lost 1,000l. - I think fires have occurred accidentally at my manufactory three times, but I was not insured for 1s. - I never swore that I once received 70l., and at another time 100l. - I think about eighteen years ago there was a sum of 110l., and 70l. when my wife was nearly burnt to death - it was not 200l. that I said I recovered - to my knowledge I never said so - I cannot tell what I said three or four years ago.
 Q. Did you not say that you recovered 90l. once, and 200l. at another time? A. I cannot say that I did - I will not say I did not.
 Q. Pray did you ever indict a gentleman named Jessop, or something, for perjury? A. Yes; there have been indictments and cross indictments and informations, and 1,000l. has been spent in law expenses.
