 2033.  FREDERICK alias    WILLIAM DOWELL was indicted for feloniously, wilfully, and maliciously assaulting Paul William White , and with a certain sharp instrument, cutting and wounding him, with intent to kill, and murder him.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating his intent to be, to disable, or to do him some grevious bodily harm.
   PAUL WILLIAM WHITE. I keep the  Three Compasses, Mile-end-road. On the 3rd of September, I had a large dinner at my house, and in the evening, about nine o'clock, the prisoner came in, and went up to the club-room, where the persons had dined; I was in the bar - I sent him up three glasses of brandy and water, one after the other; I took up the last myself, and told him there were three to pay for - he pulled out sixpence, and threw down; I told him that would not pay for three, that they were 1s. each - he seemed to take no notice of what I said; I told him to put the sixpence into his pocket, and we would settle it when he came down - when he came down I was in the parlour; my wife called me, and said he was going away without paying - I went out and said," Sir, you have not paid for these three glasses of brandy and water; pay for them before you go" - he made no answer; I repeated it - he paid no attention to me, but gave Parnell a glass of gin, and had one himself; he paid for both, and directly took a penknife out of his pocket, opened it, and put it into his pocket again - my wife told Parnell of it, and he said,"Mr. Dowell, you have put the knife into your pocket, open;" he took it out, shut it, and put it in again, then opened the door, and went out - I went after him, tapped him on the shoulder, and asked him to pay for the three glasses of brandy and water; he then turned round, struck me in the throat, and knocked me down, with his fist - I went up, touched him on the shoulder, and collared him, to prevent his going away; he struck me again, a scuffle ensued, and we both fell together - I got up first, and when he was sitting on the ground, he said, "How much do you want?" I said, "Three shillings for the brandy and water," he put his hand into his pocket, and I thought he was going to pay me - instead of which be ran at me directly he got up and struck me with the knife - I put up my arm and received the cut in it; I fell from the blow, but did not know he had stuck me; he ran across the road and hallooed Watch! it was about eleven o'clock at night; the watchman was going to take me, till he saw the blood all over me, and then took him to the watch-house; I was taken to my doctor's, who stopped the blood - I lost about three quarts, and was taken home for dead - my vein was nearly cut through; the knife was found on him with a blade broken; it was a two-bladed knife - I do not know whether that was the blade that cut me.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Had he any thing besides the three glasses of brandy and water? A. Not at my house; there was no dispute about the price of the brandy - he was about half drunk when this took place, and seemed the same when he came in; the scuffle was about thirty yards from my house - I went to the office next day, about four o'clock in the afternoon, not earlier; I there charged him with a common assault - the prisoner was not there; the Magistrate did not refuse me a warrant; the prisoner surrendered here yesterday - I went and preferred this bill because I could not find him any where -I heard he was in Paris; he is an ironmonger, living near me - I did not look for him at his shop, but the officer did; I never told Mitchell, or any body, that I would make this up for 200l.; I did not seize him - I touched him on the shoulder in a civil manner.
 JOHN BRADLEY. I am a watchman. I heard a call of Watch! and on turning round, saw White and the prisoner running across the road; I went to them - they were struggling together, each having hold of the other; I parted them - White instantly gave Dowell in charge, desiring me to take him to the watch-house - I walked with him a few steps, turned round to my right, and saw blood running from White's arm: he became very wet - some people said he was stabbed; I took the prisoner to the watch-house.
 Cross-examined. Q. You saw them having hold of each other? A. Yes; they did not fall in my presence - at what time he got the wound I cannot say; I took the prisoner to Worship-street the next morning - White did not appear: he had been sent for - the prisoner was detained about twenty minutes.

   GEORGE DUNSTERVILLE. I am apprenticed to Mr. Kay, a surgeon. I was called up between eleven and twelve o'clock to Mr. White, who came to our house - there was a wound in the upper part of his arm, and a great effusion of blood; it appeared such a wound as a penknife would give - it was not likely to occasion death.
 Cross-examined. Q.How long did you attend him? A.Perhaps for a week or a fortnight.
 Prisoner's Defence. I spent the evening at another house, and went to inquire for Mr. Birch at this house - they said they expected him there; I had a glass of brandy and water, which I paid the lad 8d. for - I had two more glasses, and handed the landlord 2s.; I said I had paid the lad 8d. for the first, and should pay no more - when I went down, he said there was a mistake in my reckoning; it was 1s. instead of 8d. - I said I would pay no more; I was eating a pear up stairs, and came down with the pen-knife in my hand, and went away; he followed, and collared me sixty yards from the house - he said I must come back; I refused - he knocked me down, and kicked me; we had three or four scuffles, and I suppose the knife must have cut his arm and broke the blade - I called the watchman, and gave him in charge; he found his arm bleeding, and gave me in charge - the Magistrate took my word to appear when wanted; White got a surgeon to make him out too bad to attend the office - the Magistrate told me to take a warrant out at Lambeth-street, and indict his house for being open late; but he went, and took out this warrant to prevent me.
