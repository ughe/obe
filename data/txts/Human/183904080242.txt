Up in his bed-room—it was locked—the key was taken from him by the officer—it is a horse-brush, but it will brush clothes—I never knew him to brush his clothes up stairs—he generally came down into the kitchen to do that—he had been in my service about twelve months. JAMES WILLIAM BURRIDGE( police-constable V  160.) I was called in—I found a shilling on the prisoner, and this tea and this brush in his box.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did he give you the key of his box? A.  Yes—he said nothing about the brush or the tea. NOT GUILTY.
 1461. WILLIAM EVANS was again  indicted for embezzlement. HENRY CRAVEN. I live in Britannia-place, Wands worth road. On the 11th of March, I marked three shillings, and gave them to Mr. Young, a draper close by, to bring to my shop at half past seven o'clock the next morning, and in ten minutes or a quarter of an hour after Mr. Young was gone, I went to the till, and there was only one of the marked shillings in it—this is one of the shillings—( examining one. )
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  How is it marked? A.  In three places with small dots made with a brad-awl—if the prisoner had to give change for any thing, be gave it out of the till—he had no right to give change out of his own money, and I never knew him to do it—in my absence if he could not get change from the till, he might give change from his pocket, but on that morning there was no larger money in the till then shillings—I usually take the money out of the till at night—I cannot tell how many customers had been there—there were five or six shillings be side the marked shilling—there is 14 s . due to him for two weeks' wages—I boarded him. CHARLES YOUNG. On the 11th of March, the prosecutor delivered me three shillings, which were all marked, and the next morning about half-past seven o'clock, I laid out two of them at his shop—I gave them to the prisoner—they were both marked alike—this is one of the shillings.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What became of the other shilling? A.  I have left it at home—I gave the prosecutor a shilling for it—I had left the pro secutor's shop before the policeman was called in—there was one woman  inthe shop when I was there—I purchased a piece, of bacon. JAMES WILLIAM BURRIDGE( police-constable V  160.) I searched the prisoner, and found the tea—this shilling he produced himself, I believe from his coat pocket.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How many shillings had he? A.  Three, and one half-crown, besides this shilling—he took them all out, and his master looked over them, and found the marked shilling among them—this is it—he made no difficulty in producing his money.
 (The prisoner received a goad character.) GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined One Year.
