 1939.  JOHN DOWNES was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of July , 4 books, value 8s., the goods of Cephas Cox.
 CEPHAS COX. I am a  bookseller and live in Gray'sinn-road. I lost four books from a box at the side of my door about two o'clock in the afternoon on the 24th of July; I saw the prisoner take them and run away; I went after him and saw him stopped by another person.
   JAMES GOLDSMITH. I heard a cry, Stop thief! I looked out and saw the prisoner; he threw down these books at the corner of a street; a man took them up and gave them to me; I looked at them till the prisoner was brought back, and then I gave them up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   SAMUEL MOUNTSTEPHENS. I am an officer. The prisoner was given to me; I took him to the office; he said it was the first time he had ever been guilty of such an act, and he hoped the prosecutor would forgive him.
 Prisoner's Defence. Another young man and I were walking down Gray's Inn-lane, and he wanted to go home to dinner; I said if he would stop I would pay for his dinner; he ran away and I after him; I suppose some other person took the books.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined One Months.
 1940.  HENRY BURTON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , 2 caps, value 12s., and 1 pricker, value 8d., the goods of Francis Edmond.
 FRANCIS EDMOND. I am a  ship-chandler and slopseller. I lost a pricker used for sail-making, on the 11th of September, from the counter in my shop, No. 141 High-street, Wapping; I did not see it taken; the officer brought it in shortly after I missed it, with two caps.
   THOMAS CURRANT. I am a Bow-street patrol. On the 11th of September I stopped the prisoner in Petticoat-lane offering these two caps for sale to a Jew; I asked whose they were; he said they belonged to his father; I went to the prisoner's father who said he did not deal in caps; when I came out the prisoner said he should give me no more trouble; that they had gone to the prosecutor's to buy a needle, and the other one took the caps and pricker.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went with the other boy to buy a needle; he took the caps and pricker and gave them to me to sell.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 17.

 1942.  WILLIAM LANE was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , 4 lbs. weight of bacon, value 2s., the goods of William Softley.
 WILLIAM SOFTLEY. I am a  cheesemonger, and live at Old Brentford. On the 8th of July, between nine and ten o'clock in the morning, I was at the door putting some goods into the cart, and when I returned I missed a piece of bacon - I saw it again in ten minutes under the prisoner's coat; I knew him before, but he was not in my employ; he had not left the shop; I took it from him; when I first asked him, he said he had not got it - I asked him again, he denied it; I opened his coat and took it from there.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Did he not say anything about it? A. No; he said he wanted 1 oz. of tea, and he put down a shilling to pay for the tea - the bacon was not in his hand; his coat was buttoned, and his hand against it; he did not put the bacon on the counter and the money to pay for it - I did not put it under his coat again, and call my man to see where it was, and say,"Now you can swear you saw it under his coat;" my man came before the bacon was put down at all; I served him with the tea, and then charged him with the bacon - he did not say he had no notion of taking it without paying for it - but he said afterwards, he would do anything if I would forgive him, he did not offer to pay for it till I found it on him - he then said he did it for want.
   HENRY LINKFORD, I am in the prosecutor's employ. I saw the prisoner come into the shop as I was putting some bacon into the cart; I went into the shop just after he was served with the tea - my master said, "What have you got under your coat?" he said, Nothing; my master said, "You must have something, let me see what;" I forced his coat open, and there was this piece of bacon; my master told us to fetch a constable - he said he would do anything if he would forgive him.
   JOSEPH DICKINSON. I am an officer. I went to the shop, and saw the prisoner in the prosecutor's parlour - I knew him, and said, "I am very sorry to see you here Lane;" he denied his name several times, but I knew him very well; I said to the prosecutor, "What is your intention;" he said, "I give you charge of him, take him to a Magistrate;" the prisoner then went on his knees and begged for mercy, and said, "You don't know my circumstances;" I found near 16l. on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a piece of bacon which I thought would suit my family; I took it up and put it under my arm; I then had some tea, and paid for it; the prosecutor said, "What have you got here?" I said, "A piece of bacon, which I intend to pay you for;" I put it on the counter and half a crown to pay for it - he then took it up, put it under my coat, and called the man, and said,"Now you can swear."
