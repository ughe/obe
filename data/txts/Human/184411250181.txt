 165. GEORGE REYNOLDS LEGGATT was indicted for stealing 1 watch, value 1 l  . 10 s  ., the goods of William Piggott . WILLIAM PIGGOTT. I am waiter at the White Swan at Greenwich-the prisoner slept in the next bed to me—I had a watch safe in my chest, in Oct.—I went to my chest on the 26th of Oct., and missed the watch—I had seen it safe about a fortnight before—I spoke to the prisoner about it—he said he knew nothing about it—he denied it strongly—this is my watch, and the one I lost from my chest in my room. BENJAMIN HASELDINE. I am a pawnbroker—I took in this watch of the prisoner on the 15th of Oct.
   Prisoner . I do not intend to deny taking it, but I deny that it was with the intention of stealing it. I did it to get rid of a temporary difficulty in which I was placed. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Six Months.

 166. GEORGE STOCKEY was indicted for stealing 30 sovereigns, and 20 half-sovereigns; the monies of Harriet Arnold .
   MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution. HARRIET ARNOLD. I am a widow—my husband died in March last—I kept a shop in Dartford for nineteen years—I had 218 l . in the Bank, which I drew out four or five months ago—in Oct. last I had 106 l . left in gold, and a few shillings—on the 19th of Oct. I was going to Greenwich, to take a shop there—I went into the Castle, at Woolwich—I had my money about me—I had 40 l . in a nightcap, and I lost all that—I had the rest of my money in a blue handkerchief—I was in the tap-room, at the Castle, and got the worse for what I had there—I saw the prisoner sitting by my side—I took out the nightcap, for the purpose of looking for some silver—the prisoner could see the money—I was rather intoxicated, and I turned the money out of the nightcap into my lap—the prisoner shook my apron, and it all went on the floor—I tried to pick some of it up, but the prisoner picked it up, and put it into his pocket.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  Where did you find yourself the next morning? A.  I was taken to the police-office after 1 was turned into the yard—the prisoner tried to get some of the money out of my hand—it was past eleven o'clock at night—I do not recollect whether there were many other persons in the tap-room—I observed other parties besides the prisoner, but they were not sitting by the side of me—the prisoner was sitting by my side—I do not recollect the prisoner offering my sovereigns to me, and my saying, "No, keep them till morning"—he forced a glass of rum on me—I went into the tap-room while it was daylight—I sat there till it was time to go to bed.
