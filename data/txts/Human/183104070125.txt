   GEORGE DOSSON. I am a Police-constable. On Thursday evening, the 31st of March, I was in Chiswell-street; I saw the prisoner about twenty minutes after eight o'clock in the evening, with something bulky in his jacket - I followed him a few paces, and asked what he had there; he said Nothing, but I opened his jacket and found the property - I was taking him to the prosecutor, on suspicion, and he said, "If you will let me go this time I will never do the like again."
   FRANCES GANSDEN. I am the daughter of William Gansden- he lives in Beech-street, and is a tailor- I am eleven years of age. On Thursday night, the 31st of March, between eight and nine o'clock, I was in my father's shop and saw the prisoner and another boy come in- the other boy asked me the price of some pearl buttons, which were in the window - I sent my sister to ask my father; she came back and said they were 1s. a dozen - the boy looked at them, and said they were flat, not round, and he would tell his mother the price - they then went out, and in ten minutes the officer came - I looked into the window and missed the parcel of pearl buttons - I did not miss the garters - the buttons had been safe till they came in, and the officer brought them back.
   WILLIAM GANSDEN. These gaiters are mine; one pair has a ticket on them of my writing - I know I had such buttons as these, and this is the very number that was missed out of the gross - my house is about seven doors from where he was taken.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 15.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 984.  JOHN JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of March , 1 stove, value 14s., the goods of Stephen Horncastle Geldard.
   STEPHEN HORNCASTLE GELDARD. I live in Pullen's-row, Chelsea - I have the care of a house called Ranelagh cottage; it is unoccupied; there are stoves and other fixtures in it. I was there about twelve o'clock in the day on the 5th of March; I fastened the doors when I left; I received some information in about half an hour, and returned; I looked through the parlour window, and saw the prisoner in the parlour, with a stove in his hand; I put the key into the lock, and heard a bolt-shot on the other side - I broke a pane of glass, unfastened the window, and got in; I saw him going out at the back door; he went down the garden - some persons met him, and he jumped into the common sewer; I followed him; he got out, went over a wall, and across a nursery-ground - he was afterwards taken - the stove had been taken out of its place, and set behind the parlour door; I had lost a grate from the same house on the Saturday before - a key was given to me by one of the witnesses, which opened the door very easily.
   JAMES CLARK. I am a Police-officer. I received the prisoner about twelve o'clock in the morning of the 5th of March; I found a pad of rags on his shoulder, under his coat.
   CHARLES KIDD. I live in Hertford-street. On the 5th of March I was passing the Five-fields, and saw the prisoner running; I stopped him, and said, "My friend, where are you running? what is all this hue and cry?" he said, "It is only a parcel of boys hunting me" - I said, "It is very curions, wait till they come up;" he said,"Oh! no, let me go" - I said No! and stopped him; he threw out his right hand, and threw away a key - I pointed out the place where it was thrown.
   EDWARD ROSER. I live in Ebury-square, Pimlico - I am a carrier. On the 5th of March I was in Union-street, I saw the prisoner come running down the street, and some persons were calling Stop theif! I saw the prisoner stopped by a person a few yards from me; I saw him throw something away - I took it up, and it was this key.
 MR. GELDARD. This key will open the room door. and I found this piece of iron in the passage just by the back door - I had lost eight stoves before.
   JAMES CLARK. I saw the prisoner go into the sewer, up to his knees.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going down the lane, and was taken had in my inside; I got over the rails, and heard a cry of Stop thief! I crossed the ditch, and went into the privy; I then got over a wall into the road, and a person stopped me, but I can take my solemn oath I threw nothing out of my hand - I never was in the house.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Transported for Seven Years.
 985.  JAMES HEATH and JOHN BAILES were indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of February , 3 pairs of shoes, value 3s., and 1 brush, value 6d., the goods of John Dickenson.
   BENJAMIN FOSTER. I live in Paradise-street, St. Luke's, and am a labourer. On the 22nd of February I was coming up Paradise-place, about three o'clock; I saw three lads; the prisoners were two of them; they were standing altogether - Heath had some shoes in the bosom of his coat; I saw the heels of them sticking out; I went to the prosecutor's shop, which is in Tabernacle-walk, close by, and spoke to his wife; she said there were three pairs of shoes gone - Mr. Dickenson came up, and he got me to go with him in pursuit of the boys, who had all run away; I came up to the prisoners in Pitfield-street - the prosecutor and the Police-officer had then secured them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You did not see Heath do any thing? A. No - the boy who is not in custody was the oldest; Heath did not say the other gave them to him to hold - he seemed frightened; we lost sight of the other all at once.
   ROBERT RODMAN. I was servant to Mr. Woodward, a cabinet-maker, who lives in Old-street-road. On the 22nd of February I was standing at his door, and saw the two prisoners run by - Heath threw three pairs of shoes and a bottle-brush at me; they were running by as fast as they could - I took the shoes and brush to the back of the shop, placed them under one of the tables, and the Police-officer came and had them.
