1787, EMMA BISHOP was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of May,
2 shawls, value 158. ; 1 waistcoat, value 78.; and 1 shirt, value 28. 6d.;
the goods of Samuel Hargood ; and that she had been before convicted of
felony.
John WILLIAM Dopuson. I am in the service of Samuel Hargood, a
pawnbroker in Surrey-place, Kent-road. I put these articles in front of
the shop for sale on Monday the 10th of May, and saw them safe at one
o'clock-I saw the prisoner in custody on the Friday following, and the
goods were produced.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How do you know they have not
been sold? 4. I can swear the shawl and waistcoat have not, but I will
not positively swear the shirt has not been sold-I have seen the prisoner
at times--I saw her in the shop shortly before one o'clock on the day of
the robbery-I had seen her several times before-she never bought any
thing to my knowledge-I do not know where she lived-she has pledged
at our shop-I left the shop at one o'clock-these goods were then safe-
I saw her as I passed through the shop- there are five shopmen altogether.
CHARLES WHARTON. I am in the service of Mr. Wharton & pawn-
broker in Crosby-row. Walworth. On the 10th of May the prisoner came
and offered a shawl and waistcoat in pawn-I asked where she got them
-she said she had bought the shawl of Mr. Blay, of East-lane, and the
waistcoat was bought in the New-cut-I do not recollect whether she said
she bought it herself, or her husband bought it, I sent round to Blay, and
before the person came back, the prisoner was gone.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen her before ? A. Frequently-I
knew her well-she came to me about half-past one o'clock, as near as I
can recollect-I sent my salesman to Blay's-he is not here-she remained
in the shop about five minutes—she asked me if the young man was gone
to Blay's to inquire-I said, Yes, if she waited a few minutes he would
be back, bat she left-I got a policeman about a quarter to two-I gave
the things to him, and described the prisoner, and he brought her to me
the same evening.
ROBERT DARLEY. I am a policeman-in consequence of information
from Wharton I apprehended the prisoner in Walworth-road, on the
10th of May, at half-past eight o'clock in the evening-I asked if her name
was Bishop-she said it was-I asked if she offered a shawl in pawn at
Wharton's that morning-she said she did -I asked if it was her own
property-she said it was, she bought it at Blay's, in East-lane-I took
her to Wharton, and he identified her—as I took her to the station she
said she did not buy the shawl in East-lane, but of three girls in Kent-street,
and before the Magistrate she said her husband had bought the waistcoat.
Cross-examined. Q. She answered to her name directly? 4. Yes-
found out the owner for the things next day-the shirt was found in her
lap when I took her, with other articles which are not identified.
CHARLES WHARTON re-examined. Here is the shawl and waistcoat she
brought to the shop.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you keep it till you gave it to the officer ?
1. Yes, except giving it to the young man to take to Blay-I know
the waistcoat by the collar, and by this string-I positively swear it is
the same shawl.
J. W. Dodson re-examined. I know this shawl and waistcoat as Mr,
hargood's property-neither of them were sold-I also identify this shawl
and shirt.
