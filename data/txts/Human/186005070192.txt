SAMUEL WOOLLETT. I am the son of the last witness—on the night of 29th March my father gave me a shilling to fetch some corn—I gave it to Mrs. Wood—it was not out of my sight—she returned it, and I took the same shilling back to my father. ANN IRONS. I am the wife of John Irons—we keep a china shop in Camberwell-lane—on 29th March the prisoner and Leary came to my shop—they bought a jug; the price was 5 d .—the prisoner gave me a shilling, I gave 7 d . change—they then left and took the jug—I laid the shilling on the counter apart from other money, and directly they were gone I found it was bad—I went after them and found them both together in the street—I told them they gave me a bad shilling—I took my jug and the change and gave them back the shilling—I am perfectly sure it was a bad one. CHARLES ROBINSON. I am assistant to Mr. Wrangham, a chemist at Camberwell—on Thursday evening, 29th March, Leary came to the shop, bought an ounce of Epsom Salts and gave me a shilling in payment—I gave her change, a sixpence, a 4 d . piece and a penny—I put the shilling in the till with others—the constable came soon afterwards, and I looked in the till and found a bad shilling, but I don't know that it was the one that Leary gave me. WILLIAM HARDING( Policeman, 54 P ). On Thursday evening, 29th March, I watched the prisoner and Leary and William Groves—they were all three in company—the prisoner and Leary went into the Green Man public-house, and William Groves stayed outside—the prisoner and Leary then went into Mr. Woollett's, and William Groves stayed outside—when the prisoner and Leary came out they joined William Groves again—they went to Mr. Wrangham's, and Leary went in there—the prisoner and William Groves waited outside—Leary came out; she joined the other two—they walked on together—I went into Mr. Wrangham's—I then went after the prisoner and the others—they were all together—I told them I should take them into custody, for I believed they had been passing bad money—they said they knew nothing about it—I said I should take them to the station—I did, and they were all locked up—I got this shilling from Mr. Insell, this from Mr. Woollett, and this from Mr. Robinson. ANN GIDDINGS. I am the female searcher at the Brixton Station—I remember the prisoner and Leary being brought in there—I searched them—I found on the prisoner 1 1/2 d .; on Leary a sixpence, a 4 d . piece, and a penny piece, a small portion of salts and some beef steak. WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint—these shillings are all counterfeit, and the one produced from Mr. Insell's and that produced from Mr. Robinson are from the same mould.
