   Cross-examined. Q.  Is it not the usual practice to examine the boats, whether empty or full? A.  Yes—we do not let any empty boats go out without—that is well known. JAMES HESELTINE. I am an officer of the Docks. On the morning that the Orton was stopped I went on board her about seven o'clock, and found 43 bladders of brandy, measuring about 38 gallons and 1 quart—there were 13 gallons of dark brandy and 25 gallons and 1 quart of pale—I went on board the Honduras , which was in the Dock—her cargo was brandy—I sounded some casks—one was "N. B. 957," and one other after—I found they were not full—three of the largest casks had been pierced with a centre bit, and the centre-bit was found in the prisoner's boat—it would have made the holes—I tried it to the holes, and they fitted exactly—the holes were filled up with a cork—I have samples of each of the brandies here. CHARLES COPPOCK. I am an officer of the Customs, and am searcher at the West India Docks. On the 5th of Aug. I went on board the Honduras , and found part of her cargo was brandy—I examined all the brandy there, and found 5 casks deficient—one of them was marked "N. B., 957," and was deficient 9 gallons, and there were 2 gallons deficient from one marked "D. G." with an "O." under it—there were 35 gallons deficient in the whole, some pale and some brown—I have tested the brandy that was in the casks and that in the bladders, and believe them to be the same. WILLIAM DAVIS. I am night-watchman in the Dock. I was in the Dock on the night of the 3rd of Aug.—the Honduras  was lying there—I saw the prisoner crossing the dock—I asked what he was doing—he said looking after his craft—it was after the men had knocked off work—I saw him there again at half-past seven in the evening.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you see any craft near the Honduras ? A.  There were several there lying alongside—I saw three or four. RICHARD SUMMERS. I am one of the coopers of the London Docks. I examine all casks before they leave the London Docks on the Brandy Quay—I examined all the brandies that passed from the London Docks on the 3rd of Aug., and on the 31st of July, I did not observe any tap-holes in any of them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know how many left the Docks on that day? A.  No—I do not know on what day this brandy was put on board the Honduras. SAMPSON CAPPS. I am a dipping-officer. When these brandies were in the St. Katherine's Dock I re-dipped them all, and delivered six casks—I have got the marks here—"D. G." with "O." under, from No. 1 to 6—they were what we call "quarter casks"—they were all right in point of quantity when I examined them.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you see them put on board the Honduras ? A.  No. NATHANIEL STEVENS. I am a re-dipping officer of the London Docks.
