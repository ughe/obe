I have two remnants of merino, pawned by a female—I cannot say who. SARLE WHITLOW. I have a child's petticoat and a napkin, pawned by Elizabeth Adams—not by the prisoner. GEORGE WORLEY. I have a knife, fork, two tea-spoons, and a brooch, pawned by a female—I cannot say whether it was the prisoner. MARYAN JENNER. I am the prosecutor's wife. I found this duplicate under the bolster of the prisoner's best—this property is mine, and there was a great deal more, which we cannot find—I never gave her leave to pawn any thing.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you had any thing to pledge, you did it yourself? A.  Yes, always—I have pawned when we wanted money—the prisoner was sometimes out till nine or ten, or sometimes twelve o'clock at night—she has brought home money to me—I never asked her where she got it—my husband had five houses in King-street.
   COURT . Q.  Do not you know that the prisoner went with men? A.  Yes—she came to us, and said she would get binding or umbrella-making—we always kept her—sometimes she gave me a shilling—the largest sum was 6 s ., and part of that was my husband's money, that she had of him. NOT GUILTY.

 1031. HENRY HOOPER was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of April , 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  ., the goods of John Middleton Hare , from his person. JOHN MIDDLETON HARE. I am an editor of a newspaper. On Good Friday evening, I was in Church-street, Bethnal-green—I felt something at my pocket—I turned round and saw the prisoner drop my handkerchief from his hand—I took it up, secured him, and gave him into custody di rectly.
 (  Property produced and sworn to .) Prisoner . Three or four boys ran across the road—the gentleman came and collared me directly. Witness. There was another boy, but the pri soner had the handkerchief. GUILTY. Aged 14.— Confined Three Months.
