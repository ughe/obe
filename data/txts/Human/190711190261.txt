Mrs. Smith, Mr. Read, and his daughter were stand  ing together in the yard. I am positive Mrs. Smith was there. I heard some whispering; between them but could not hear what was said. I then walked into the Court and was afterwards brought out and placed with a number of other men for identification, men between 20 and 40 years of age, one man with a high hat, four or five with hard hats, and one with a cloth cap like myself. I was the only one with a neck handkerchief Mrs. Smith came and identified me, and when I came up to the Court the same man who saw me get out of the van came and identified me. He picked me out, and then stood a little way off while his daughter packed me out. Every time the girl got near me the detective kept saying, "Do not be frightened," and ultimately she picked out me. Afterwards prosecutor was brought up to identify me. I was brought up in a private cab that time. He walked up and touched another man. I said, "You have touched the wrong man."
 Cross-examined. I only knew Maxwell as being in the shipping offices. I knew he lived in Newton Street. I will take my oath I was not in the cupboard when he was taken into custody. I may have been in the duck at that time, or I may have been at the shipping offices. I do not know where I was. I admit I was with Brown in the "Peacock." Maxwell and I had drinks with him and he gave us a shilling each. We were on the tram afterwards and got down at the "Swan." Then I left then to go to the "Abbey Arms" with my friends. I cannot say whether any  body else took my place with Maxwell when I left. I swear I was not there after we got off the tram. It was two or three days afterwards I heard the detective had been asking for me. I shipped at Tilbury on the 22nd. The little girl Read picked me out. She had not been in the "Peacock," I was not asked whether I would have all the men with hats on, or off. I say that is a deliberate untruth on the part of the Inspector.
 Re-examined. On the day this is alleged to have occurred, I went to look for a ship as usual and the next day as well; when I came home my mother made a statement. Then I went to Tilbury and got a ship on the 22nd. JOHN CHASEY. I am a stevedore. The prisoner is my son. On November 13, 1905, I met with an accident and broke my arm, and in consequence of that remained at home. My son came home the worse for drink, with two seafaring men, between nine o'clock and half-past. I was in the kitchen having supper. He sat in the arm-chair and had supper with me, then went to bed and remained there. He was brought home intoxicated. I do not know where Brown lives.
 Cross-examined. I did not know Maxwell and did not know my son had been about with him. It was not by my advice my son went to Tilbury. He went before the detective called. I knew he was going because he had been previously. My wife might have told the detective that he had gone to Tilbury and was going to sea. MRS.
