2775. ELIZABETH HODGES was indicted for unlawfully, malis
ously, and feloniously assaulting Philip Hodges, on the 13th of October.
and cutting and wounding him upon the left side of the head and let
cheek, with intent to maim and disable him.--2nd Count, stating her in
tent to be to do him some grievous bodily harm.
PHILIP HODGES. The prisoner is my wife. On Saturday night, the
12th of October, I came home between five and six o'clock-I had some
words with her that night-we had an altercation for some time-se pet
herself into a great rage, took up the poker, and said she would dash my
brains out-she struck me with it several times, and made my arm very
bad- I went out, thinking her passion would cool - I afterwards came
home and went to bed - I got up before seven o'clock in the morning, and
as I was dressing I thought she was asleep-(I slept in the same bed with
her, but after I got out of the room she came out in a rage, and swore she
would murder me-she took up the chamber-pot with the contents, came
after me in a rage like a bear, and struck me with it on the head and side
of my face-it cut me very much-I bled profusely--she only struck
once-it broke the pot all to pieces, and cut me on the cheek, just below
the eye-it stunned me for a few minutes, and then I recovered myself
one of the lodgers came down and endeavoured to stop the blood pre
liceman was sent for, and she was given in charge-I became an out pe
tient of the London Hospital - I am not quite recovered now I lost
deal of blood.
EDWARD THOMAS Roe. I am a surgeon at the London Hospital.
saw the prosecutor on the 13th of October-he had a severe wound on the
left cheek, which was bleeding considerably when he came-it required
ligature to be put on the artery, which was divided he must have lost i
considerable quantity of blood before I saw him, which was between two
and three o'clock in the afternoon I considered him in danger.
DAVID EVANS. I am a policeman. I was sent for about two o'clock
on the Sunday to take the prisoner into custody-she was using very abusive
threatening language then towards her husband, and swore she would
break his old head, and also his legs before she slept-I took her to
the station-house, with the assistance of another constable--she was a
violent, and was not sober at the time.
(The prisoner, in a written defence, complained of her husband having /
quently ill-used her ; that on the night in question he had cut her face, as
the morning thrown a pail of water over her in bed.)
PHILIP HODGES re-examined. I did not strike her-she had preparcu

when I came to myself, I threw that over her-I had done nothing belot
she struck me with the pot-I never spoke to her-I was afraid to a

(The prisoner received a good character.)
