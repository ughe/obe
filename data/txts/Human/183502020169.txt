I watched from the Saturday till the Sunday night, and the prisoner came between five and six o'clock, and took the sack out—I followed him—I then heard a cart coming—I drew back, and laid down under the hedge—the carter saw me, and he passed by without stopping—the prisoner came again at six o'clock and took the sack, (which he had before removed to a gate-way near the road,) and I collared him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Must not the prisoner have seen you the first time? A.  I do not know that he did—the carter saw me, and went past—I had no occasion to take the prisoner then, for I expected he would come back, and he did—I knew him—he did not see me the second time, till I took hold of him—I said, "George, I have got you"—he said he knew had had done wrong, and he was sorry for it—I did not say any thing to him about a pot of beer—he said he was going to take it to his master's horses, but he was taking it the wrong way to go to the stable. CHARLES SMITH. I received charge of the prisoner and this sack—when I was searching him at the watch-house, I said, "George, it is a bad job for you"—he said, "Yes, I know I have done wrong, but I must go through it now"—he said he took the corn out of the bin, and it had been there for a week. JOHN WRIGHT. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's office ( read )—I took him on that occassion, and I know he is the same man. GUILTY. For receiving them of an evil-disposed person. GEORGE MORGAN. I am a hair-dresser, and live at No. 6, Broom-field's, in the parish of St. Paul, Deptford, Kent. I occupy the house entirely myself. On the morning of the 10th of January, I met the prisoners White and Jones, in High-street, Deptford—my brother was with me at the time—I took them home to my house, and gave them some supper—I went into the shop with Jones, and while I was there my brother came into the shop and said something to me—he wished me to give him some money—I refused—Jones and I returned to the parlour—White and my brother were there—the women went away, and after they were gone I missed a watch which had hung over the mantel-piece in the parlour—I gave infor mation to the police—I have since seen it in the possession of the policeman Collings—the prisoner Moor was never in the house, to my knowledge.
