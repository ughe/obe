   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  How long had Hearn and Allen been employed there? A.  Allen only the day previous, Hearn several times for about two months—there was nothing found on Hearn, or Allen, or at their lodging.
   Cross-examined by MR. CAARTEEN. Q.  Has Mr. Herring any partner? A.  No—I have been there about eight months—it is called the "British and Foreign Alkali Company"—Mr. Herring centres in himself the whole of that body—he has told me so—I reside on the premises—Mr. Herring re sides at Wandsworth—he attends to the business himself, as I do—I have never seen any partner but him. JAMES MOORE SMART. I took at Mr. Herring's manufactory. On Friday evening, the 10th of Sept., I watched the three prisoners—I went down stairs about ten minutes before five, and about five minutes before five o'clock Hearn came down—he took something off the right side of the bench in the plumbers' workshop, which appeared to be solder—he doubled it up, and put it into his left waistcoat pocket—he went away, and returned a second time—he bent up some piping, or some sheet lead, I cannot swear which, and secreted that about his person—he went away, and come back the third time—he than beat up some sheet lead, and placed it under his waistcoat—while he was so doing Taylor came down—he beat up three pieces of sheet lead, and placed them under his waistcoat—then Allen came down—he beat up two pieces of sheet lead, and placed them under his waistcoat—they then went up stairs—I went up as quick as I could, and ordered the people in the warehouse to shut the gates, but the prisoners were gone out—I and Bake followed Taylor—I saw him taken in the yard by Baker—I saw Baker pick up one piece of lead—I saw Taylor give Baker one piece, and one piece to Lomas—of course it was lead taken from my master's premises—he went to no other place, because Mr. Baker was after him—I took Allen; he said he knew nothing of it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  Where were you? A.  In the wine-cellar, about twenty yards from the workshop—it is under the warehouse, but in a line with the plumbers' shop—I looked through the open—work of the door—it is a kind of grating—there is about two inches space between each bar—I saw Hearn take something that seemed to he solder, but I cannot say what—Taylor come down while Hearn was down the third time—he beat up three pieces of lead, and placed them under his waistcoat—the prisoners were all together before they went away, but they came in one at a time—Allen was taken the same evening at his own door—no property was found on him, or at his house.
   COURT. Q.  Did you see Allen do anything? A.  Yes, beat up two pieces of lead, and place them under his waistcoat—Hearn was in there three separate times, and the last time he was in, Taylor came, and directly after wards Allen came in—they could see one another take it.
