JOSEPH GAMBLE. My father keeps the Fountain Abbey, Market
street, and I manage the business-it is the house to which Trevett's omni-
bus drivers usually come. On Whit-Monday I was there, and two persons
who did not usually come, came there with Mr. Bray's omnibus-I canno
say what they said they would do, but they had refreshment at our house
--they asked for tea, and that not being ready, they called for a pint of ale
and a screw of tobacco-the tea was got ready for them-I brought it; while
that was done, Trevett's omnibus started-they left their tea behind, and
started off.
DEDMAN. I live in Market-street, and am a piano-forte-maker.
On Whit-Monday I went into the Fountain Abbey, a little before five o'clock
-I saw Bray's omnibus stopping there--two of Bray's men called for te
they called for ale, and while it was getting ready, I was sitting in the front
parlour-the forms were out at the window-they saw Trevett's bus go, and
they left their tea and went after it I was there on the Wednesday
-I saw Bray's omnibus in Market-street-it passed Mr. Trevett's stable-
the men were huzzaing, and said, “You are not game to follow us" and
“We will run the b--y cobbler down" or "off"--I have seen Bray's omnibus
occasionally, and observed its proceedings--when people were going into
Trevett's omnibus, the conductor of Bray's omnibus has got down from
Bray's, and laid hold of the people that were going into Trevett's I have
seen that several times I have seen nothing else done in particular as it
regards one running before the other, that has been a common thing-some-
times Bray's bus before the “Emperor," sometimes the "Emperor" be-
fore that.
