JEREMIAH BROWN. I am a plasterer, and live in Mill-street, Lambeth walk. I was employed whitewashing and repairing Mr. Lankester's pre mises—on the 31st of March I arrived there precisely at six o'clock—the clock was just about striking—I pulled my watch out—that is my time for going to work—it struck six a minute or two after I got there—I dare say my watch was correct—it is what I always go by—I was at work at the house next door—not at this house—I had then to go up the court belonging to Mr. Lankester, and found the back gate open—that was exactly at ten mi nutes past six—I went to the water-closet, and on coming out saw Mr. Lankester's back-parlour shutters down, and also his back-door open—I did not suspect anything—I thought Mr. Lankester was up and about, as he had had soldiers billetted there two days before—the window was open—I saw nobody go in or out.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You did not hear the clock strike at all, did yon? A.  No—I go by my watch—I looked at it—it might want a minute or two to six—I was there just before six—it was not after six—I could not say it was precisely six, or two minutes before—I regulate my watch by my landlord's clock, sometimes, not by any other clock—it was not till ten minutes after six I found the gate open—I did not give any alarm then. MARIA PARKER. I am single, and live at No. 9, Whiting-street, Lam beth. JOSEPH BROOK. I am a policeman. I took the prisoner into custody on the morning of the 5th of April, within fifty yards of No. 9, Whiting-street—I found this handkerchief in his pocket—I went to No. 9, Whit ing-street, and found the twopenny-piece produced.
