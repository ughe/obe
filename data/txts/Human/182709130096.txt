ROBERT COATS. I am a carpenter, and live in Webber-street, Lambeth. I know the prisoner had taken a house, and there was an agreement between him and me, for me to put it in repair; this was about the 16th or 20th of June; I went there twice to see what repairs would be wanted: he said there were very old stoves on the premises, and I told him that I could recommend him to a person, who would let him have some modern stoves cheap; I had intended to have taken the old stoves myself, and made him an allowance: I have nineteen houses myself, and Lewis, who collects rents, was with me when this agreement was making; he made no secret at all about his taking the house, or of there being old stoves there.
 MR. BRODRICK to  BENJAMIN BLABY. Q. Did you go to give any countermand of orders? A. No; I went to a house where I understood he had given orders for some brushes, and while I was there, he came to urge the completion of an order, and I took him.

 1748.  ELIZABETH MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of July , 1 watch, value 1l.; 1 ribbon, value 1d., and 1 watch-key, value 1d., the goods of Nicholas Murphy , from his person.
   NICHOLAS MURPHY. I am a fisherman, and live at Limehouse; I have frequently seen the prisoner at her mother's house; I met her on the Monday morning, about ten o'clock, in the Bull's Head, public-house; I was with her all the rest of the day, in several public-houses; when it came on bed time, I asked her where she was going to sleep; she said she had no where in particular: I said I would go and pay for a bed for her; we went into the Match-walk, and I paid for a bed for her; I sat down by the bed side, and went to sleep; in the morning I missed my watch, and asked her about it; she said she knew nothing about it: I let her go, but she was taken afterwards. This is my watch - it was pawned at a pawnbroker's; but I have made two voyages since it was taken.
   JAMES JEFFERY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner at the Three Compasses, public-house, in Shadwell-market, about a month after the watch was taken; she said she did not steal it, but the old woman stole it, and gave it to her to pawn.
   JOHN GRINSHAW. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned with me, on the 17th of July, by the prisoner at the bar.
 Prisoner's Defence. It was given to me by a woman, to pawn for herself: I went and took her the money; she took 1s. of it, and told me to keep the rest: I met her three days after in the street - she asked me for the duplicate; I said I had it not in my pocket, but I would give it her when I got home.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1749.  SARAH OWENS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of September, 1 purse, value 1d., and 9 shillings., the property of Richard Gee , from his person.
   RICHARD GEE. I met the prisoner in Whitechapel, about half-past two o'clock in the afternoon of the 5th of September. I went with her up a court in Wentworth-street- she said she did not want money; but she asked me to give her something to drink. I went up stairs, gave her 4d. to get a quartern of gin - she fetched it, gave me a glass of it, and took the rest herself; she put the measure on the mantel-piece, and in a minute some one tapped at the door - she said "They want the measure;" she took it off the mantle-piece, and snatched my purse containing nine shillings, and ran down stairs. I went down and asked the mistress where she was; the woman told me to get out, and said she did not keep a bad house. I said I was sure she was there, but the woman would not let me see. I went and got an officer, and he found her at the next house.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was she searched at the watch-house? A. Yes - there was half a crown and a silver pencil-case found on her - this was about half an hour after. She did appear to be in ill health. I am a pensioner, and served his Majesty thirty years. I think I had seen her before in Whitechapel. I was quite sober I had only had a pint of beer.
   ROBERT CRAIG. I am an officer. The prosecutor came to me, and said he had been robbed. I searched for the prisoner at the house, which is a brothel in Wentworth-street. I found her in the corner of the room in the next house, the door was locked.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you not threaten to send her over the herring-pond, as you call it? A. No - I did not - nor did the prosecutor. She had been standing in the corner of the room. I went as soon as the prosecutor gave me information, which was soon after three o'clock; she had had time to get away - the prosecutor appeared quite sober.
 Prisoner's Defence. I know nothing of the prosecutor. I had been put to bed but three weeks, and had not been out of the room; he came to the door and said I was the woman. He then begged my pardon, and said I was not; he then returned with the officer, and said I was the woman.
   RICHARD GEE re-examined. Q. Did you say she was not the woman who robbed you, and beg her pardon? A. No - I never did.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1750.  WILLIAM PLUCKROSE was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , 1 umbrella, value 1s.,; 1 snuff-box, value 1s.; 1 pair of spectacles, value 7s., and 1 pocket-book, value 6d., the goods of John Roberts , from his person.
   JOHN ROBERTS. I lost the articles stated, on the 25th of July. I had been dining with some friends in the City, I had drank rather freely. I went on to Holborn, and got into a hackney-coach - the coachman took my case in hand, and drove me home; there was a stranger followed me into the coach. I do not exactly know what happened there.
 Prisoner. Q. You recollect that you introduced yourself first to me to take a pinch of snuff, and told me to tell the coachman to take you to Covent-garden, and in going along, you took the pocket-book out of your pocket, to see if I could find a card of your residence? A. I recollect nothing about it - I might do so. The next morning the coachman called on me, and asked if I had lost any thing. I said I had, and he desired me to attend at Marlborough-street.
