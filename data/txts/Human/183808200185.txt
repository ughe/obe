FIANCIS HILL. I am an officer of the Customs at the tea warehouse. I saw the prisoner on the 4th of August, in the tea-warehouse—he asked A.  No he was for Mr. Avery—I told him he was in the building, and if he would go on, he would find him—the tea produced is worth about 10 s .—the duty is 2 s . 11 d . per pound—the teas in bond are the property of the merchants—they are in the custody of the London Dock Company. JOHN TREEMER. I am in the employ of the London Dock Company. I saw the prisoner in the warehouse No. 2, on Saturday, the 4th of Au gust—I know Avery—I had left him a few minutes before in the ware-house, on business—I saw the prisoner in Avery's room—I believe he was acquainted with him—I had seen him in company with him before that day—this paper which is round the tea, found on the prisoner, is si milar to what is used in the Dock warehouse for papering green tea—we had chests of tea opened in No. 2 warehouse the day before for tareing—the officers of the establishment had access to that warehouse while the tea was tareing, and so had Avery—he was superintendent of that ware-house—I cannot say how many chests of tea were opened, but of the de scription of tea found on the prisoner, we might have perhaps twenty opened on the Thursday and the Friday—this is precisely of the same de scription as that in the chests-when the tea is tared, the chests are en tirely emptied—the tea is weighed and put in again, and the chests are nailed down-Avery was present—there were no chests of this particular description of tea opened on Saturday the 4th of August—the value of this tea found on the prisoner is about 10 s ., including the duty, which is 2 s . 1 d . a pound—it is green tea.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Is not that a good price for it? A.  It is what I think it would fetch in the trade—I heard the officer say, there was about 2lbs. of it—it is young hyson—there are two sorts of tea which are more common, twankay and hyson-skin—it is perhaps two months since we had any tea of this sort open before in that warehouse—I cannot tell what had been opened in the other warehouse—there is only one other warehouse that has tea in it. JOHN COOK. I am a foreman in the London Docks. Some tortoise-shell was opened for public sale on the 25th and 27th of July—some part of it was left at the Docks—this piece found on the prisoner is of the same kind, but it ran of various shapes and sizes—the officers of the Company had access to the place where this tortoiseshell was kept-Avery came there sometimes, but very seldom-tea and tortoiseshell are sometimes left on board the ships, and not taken to the warehouses directly—after the sale, the tortoiseshell was packed into the cases, weighed, and put away-no person could have got at that tortoiseshell in any private man ner on the 4th of August—they must have broken a chest open of con siderable size.
