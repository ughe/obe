 First London Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1324.  HENRY GREENLEY was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Maxwell Windle , Esq. on the 4th of January , at the Middle Temple , and stealing therein, 1 cape of a great coat, value 6d.; 6 pairs of trousers, value 36s.; 5 coats, value 30s.; 6 waistcoats, value 6s.; 1 great coat, value 5s.; 3 handkerchiefs, value 6s.; 2 pairs of gloves, value 6d.; 1 pair of socks, value 3s.; 1 wooden box, value 1s.; 1 syringe, value 5s.; 1 tea-caddy, value 6s.; 2 tea-ladles, value 4s.; 1 tea-spoon, value 4s., and 1 canvass-bag, value 1s., his property.
 MR. EVANS conducted the prosecution.
   MARY BARKER. I am laundress to Mr. Windle. I locked up his chambers, which are in the Middle Temple, on Friday the 4th of January; he had gone out of town a few days before - I had gone there every day, except Sunday; I went there again on the 5th of January, about four o'clock in the afternoon - I found the outer door only on one lock, and the other door open, the blinds were drawn down, and the lamp was on the floor; it was in a very different state to what I had left it on the 4th - I looked round and was rather frightened, but did not know whether Mr. Windle had been there or not; I locked the chambers and went away - I went again on the next day, which was Sunday, a little after three o'clock, to look more particularly - the first thing I then saw was one of the drawers of the office table was broken open; I looked farther, and found all the drawers were broken open but two, which had not been locked, and the cellaret was broken open; I missed a tea-caddy, a brown silk umbrella, a clothes-bag, and a key, which were left at the chambers; I have since seen the caddy in Mr. Windle's possession, and the clothes-bag at Worship-street - I found a crow-bar and a chisel in the chambers; I gave them to the porter, and a gentleman who I fetched to the chambers- the outer door had not been broken; the key went in as easily as when I left it, but was only locked once - I had locked it twice.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you positive of that? A. Yes; I remarked, as I did not mean to go again that day that I had locked it safe - I found it singly locked on the Saturday; I double locked it on the Friday - I found the cupboards and drawers open, and the foul linen on the floor; I did not examine minutely that day, being alarmed and thinking Mr. Windle might have been there unknown to me - I made no more inquiry that day; I noticed it more on Sunday, when I went there to see if I could find Mr. Windle; I did not look for the key on Saturday - I locked the door when I came out; I went on Sunday to Mr. Tattersal's in Bloomsbury-square, and told him of it - I never saw the prisoner till he was taken up some months after; I heard he had been taken and was liberated - I have been a laundress four or five years.
 MR. EVANS. Q. Did you send to Mr. Tattersal on the Saturday? A. Yes, but he was not at his chambers; I went to his house on Sunday, and found him - he is a friend of Mr. Windle's; I attend to three other gentlemen's chambers.
   ROBERT LOCK. I am a constable. On Friday the 4th of January, between five and six o'clock in the afternoon, I was going up Whitecross-street, and saw the prisoner crossing the road; he crossed Whitecross-street, went up a turning, and knocked at a backdoor; I followed him, as he seemed in a hurry, and passed him two or three yards - I then saw him turn back, and go into Whitecross-street again; I lost sight of him for about a minute and a half. I then saw him come across, and go into the front door of a house with this bag - I followed him in directly, and asked what he had got there; he said his father's clothes - I said that would not satisfy me, he must go with me to the watch-house: I took him there, and then said, "How did you say you got these things?" He said, "I met a gentleman in Holborn, who employed me to carry this bag:" I asked if he knew the gentleman; he said, "No:" I said, "Do you know what is in the bag;" he said No; I said "I must know;" I looked in and found all these articles - I have kept most of them ever since, but not all; I searched him and found these keys on him, and thinking one was not a right key, I said I should detain him - I looked into his hat and found some handkerchiefs; he was taken to Worship-street and examined three times, and then discharged as no owner was found.
