276. JAMES HARRIS (38), Stealing one gelding, the property of
William Palmer.
MR. CROOME Prosecuted.
WILLIAM PALMER. I live at 24, Ottaway Road, Lower Clapton, and
am a greengrocer-on the 22nd December I bought a horse for 111.-I
turned it out on Hackney Downs about 8 p.m.-I saw the prisoner on
the downs when I turned it out-I missed it about 8 p.m.-I next saw it
on the 8th January
JOHN BAXTER. I am a horge-dealer, of 1, Seven Sisters Road, Hollo-
way-on 4th January I was at Aldridge's, in St. Martin's Lave a man
brought the prisoner to me and said he had a pony for sale, that he was
in a little bit of trouble, and was a builder-I went to Lambeth and sa
the pony-he asked 61. for it-I said it was too cheap, and I should go
to the station-I sent for the police, and Goddard came and took the
prisoner into custody.
Cross-examined by the Prisoner. Ponies fetch less when the snow is on
the ground.
WILLIAM GODDARD (Poliooman). I was called to Mr. Wilson's stables
in Carlisle Street, Lambeth, about 10.40 on 4th January-I saw Baxter
-he said the prisoner had offered him a pony for 51. and it was worth
101., but he should not buy it unless the prisoner went to the station-I
asked the prisoner how he came possessed of the pony-he said he bought
it in Barbican for 41., of a milkman, who lived in Camden Town, that
the brokers were in the house, that he brought it there because they
should not have it-I told him I should take him to the station and make
inquiries at the station he said it was no use my going to the address
he had given me, but he lived in Prince's Court-I went there and found
the address correct.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrats. "I have nothing to say
now, I will call witnesses at the trial."
The prisoner put in a long written statement to the effect that he bought the
pony of a man at Aldridgo's for 4l., that the man would not give him a receipt,
and finding the pony was a kicker, he tried to sell it.
GUILTY.-Eighteen Months' Hard Labour.
