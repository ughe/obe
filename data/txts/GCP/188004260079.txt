HENRY GROVE BADGROVE. I am an auctioneer at Forest Hill—I have
& business at Upper Norwood and in the City-William Smeeton was in
my service in the year 1876 for about six weeks-the business was then my
father's, Frederick Sadgrove's—I managed it-my father had an account
at the London and South-Western Bank, Upper Norwood branch-I used
to sign orders for cheque books for my father-our offices were then in
Belvedere Road-I believe the writing on this order to be William
Smeeton's, the signatures to the cheques as well—I gave the prisoner
no authority to write the order-I have not seen him since he left my
employment till lately-Edith Smeeton came to meet him at my office
I took them to be man and wife
Cross-examined. I employ a boy-I had other clerks-I will not pledge
my oath to the prisoner's writing-I must have seen him write-- he kept
my books—I did not overlook him when he was writing--this letter is not
filled up in the way I used to do it.
FREDERICK MOLEAN. I live at 55, Furley Street, Peckham-I have
known the male prisoner seven years, and the female five years they
pass by repute as man and wife - about last October I saw them at 33,
Henslow Road, Dulwich-William Smeeton showed me a telegram, and
asked me to write an answer to it-he said, “Enclose 21. on account of
the defence of Miss Denham”-I wrote the letter, and addressed it to
Mr. Roberts, solicitor, Stowmarket-I subsequently asked him why he
wished me to write it-he said, “Miss Denham might be searched, and
I do not want my writing to be seen," and that there was a cheque-book
found on the girl—I said, "It is funny thing how a girl in her position
could get a cheque-book”—then he said, "It was obtained by Sadgrove, a
auctioneer, and you know I was clerk to Sadgrove”-I knew that Denham
had been servant and companion of a lady-she had lived at 45, Palace
Square, Upper Norwood, and other places-I remember William Smeeton
going to Pontypool in February-Edith came down the evening he went
away, to my house-she said, “I have had a great fright. There has
been a man to look after the house. I think something will come of
this"-she seemed very much frightened—I told her I would come and
see her during the ensuing week she said she had seen a man talking
to two detectives-I saw her subsequently, and asked her what she was
frightened about-she said, “It is about that cheque-book that was
found on Lizzie, and if that beastly Roberts goes to the manager of the
bank it will be all up with Billy and I”-I afterwards wrote a letter to
Mr. Roberts I received an answer I gave information to the manager
of the South-Western Bank-on 26th March the male prisoner came to
my house-he said, “We have had a bother this morning. Edith has
