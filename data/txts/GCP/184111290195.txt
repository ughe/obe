Cross-examined. Q. I am afraid you are not very well, or is there any
thing the matter with you? 4. I am very well, thank you I was very
middling indeed yesterday.
Q. Very middling, I believe, when you were in the middle of the
road? A. I was not very well-I had been to the public-house a short
time before-I do not know the sign of the house-I cannot say
what time I got there yesterday morning-I had nothing to drink there-
I will swear that I had not any thing to drink at the public-house-I did
not bring any drink with me- I do not say I had nothing to drink all day
I had some porter at the public-house-nothing else--I swear that I had
nothing to drink any where else- I was very well when I had drunk the
porter-I cannot say to a few moments how soon after I became middling
-I am occasionally in the habit of taking a little something when I am at
home- I consider every body requires a little refreshment-I take a little
spirits occasionally-not in the morning, unless I am very unwell-some-
times I may be very well of a morning, and sometimes unwell--when unwell
of a morning I have taken a little spirits-I have quarrelled with my mother
occasionally, not very often-she has left our house-the prisoner never
gave me notice to quit nor notice to leave at all-she never gave me any
warning whatever to leave the house-I never knew that my husband was
in the habit of kissing her he never kissed her in my presence-I should
certainly have been very angry if he had-these jokes have not taken place
in my presence.
Q. Do you remember your husband wanting her to be bar-maid ? A.
He spoke of it-I said it should not be- I would not have it on
any account, I did not think we required a bar-maid- I believe my
mother has said she should never like the girl to come into her
house--as she was very disagreeable to my mother-I did not like her
much, because she made herself unpleasant to the family- I do not say she
was unkind to the little boy-she bebaved as a servant ought to the child
-I never noticed any particular fondness to the child-the prisoner was
sometimes called Elizabeth, and sometimes Betsy-no other name by me,
nor in my presence or hearing—the little boy never said, in my presence,
"Papa, kiss me, and now kiss Lillabella'- I should certainly have been
very angry if it had.
Q. Do you remember ever hearing of a lock of hair that was put into a
brooch ? A. I have heard speak of it-I cannot positively say who I have
heard speak of it-I have heard it pass as a joke-I cannot say how long
it was before the prisoner was taken up-I should think it was a week be-
fore-it was before she was taken up-I cannot tell who talked about it
-my husband was there.
COURT. Q. Whose hair was put into the brooch ? A. It was supposed
to be hers.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. You supposed so, did you not? A. It was sup-
posed to be hers—I did not suppose so--they were talking about a brooch
-I supposed it to be my own, because I had given my husband such a
one before I was married-I do not believe it to be her hair-I believed it
to be hers then, and said so.
