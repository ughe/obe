 1000. THOMAS BURTON was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Elizabeth Bradley , on the   10th of Febru ary , and stealing therein 1 watch, value 12 l  .; 1 coat, value 5 l  .; 2 pairs of stays, value 2 l  .; 2 pocket-books, value 3 s  .; 2 pairs of stockings, value 3 s  .; and one shawl, value 4 s  ., her property. ELIZABETH BRADLEY. I am a widow, and live at Peckham Rye Com mon. On Monday, the 10th of February, I left my house about a quarter past two o'clock, leaving my daughter and three grandchildren in the house—I returned at about a quarter to nine in the evening, and missed a gold watch, and the other property stated, from a drawer in my bed-room—I had put the watch there about half-past twelve o'clock—it has not been found—I value all the articles stolen at about 20 l .—I know nothing of the prisoner. MARGARET EMILY BRADLEY. I was left behind with the three children when my mother went out on the 10th of February—there was no one else in the house—a lady called about half-past four, but only staid a few minutes—between six and seven o'clock I found the street-door open, and the front parlour window about a foot and a half up—I had seen it at a quarter to five o'clock—it was shut down then, but not fastened—the door was then bolted and locked—the persons must have got in at the window, and let themselves out at the door—I was below in the kitchen with the children—I did not miss any thing till my mother returned. THOMAS EDWARD MERRITT. I am a general dealer, and live in Pomroy-street. On the 10th of February, from seven to half-past seven o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner running across the Old Kent-road and Mr. Collins after him, hallooing "Stop thief"—I went after him, and when I was about ten yards from him, I saw him throw down some clothes from under his coat—I picked them up—I never lost sight of him till he was taken—as we were taking him to the station-house he went into a watering place, and while there, I saw him drop this pocket-book from his trowsers, which a young man picked up, and gave to the policeman—this is it—( looking at it. )
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  What distance were you from Peckham when you saw the prisoner? A.  About a quarter of a mile—I saw two men about ten yards from him—they came by me—I did not see them throw anything away. GEORGE GREEN. I work for Mr. Collins at New Peckham. On the 11th of February, about half-past eight o'clock in the morning, I found these two pairs of stays under a cart about one hundred yards from my master's house. MORRIS GRAHAM. ( police-constable R  57.) I received this pocket-book at Greenwich station-house from a young man who is a butcher in Whitechapel—Merritt was present at the time—I found these eight skel eton keys and a latch key and a flute on the prisoner.
   MRS. BRADLEY re-examined.  I have no hesitation in saying that this is my pocket-book—it has no name in it—I have one of this kind every year, but I had not taken to it this year—I left one in my drawer, and it was gone—there is no memorandum or any mark in it. NOT GUILTY.
