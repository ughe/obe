Joun Seti ALLEN (policeman, 4 350). I was on duty, io plain clothes,
and saw the three prisoners together-I watched, and saw them go up the
Edgware-road together--they stopped at the corner of Queen-street, where
Bailey put his hand on Ricks' shoulder, and put bis other hand to Ricks' hand.
and appeared to pass something-he pointed towards Green's shop-I did not
see Ricks go into that shop; I was watching Bailey and Jones, who remained
there till Ricks came back and joined them, and they walked up to the corner
of Bell-street-I then saw Bailey put his hand on Ricks' shoulder, and point
towards Webb's shop--I saw Ricks go into that shop-I went in after him, and
saw the foreman hand him some butter-Ricks tendered a sixpence, and after
the foreman had bent it I took possession of it-I called in another officer,
and gave Ricks into his custody-I then went to the corner, towards Bailey and
Jones--they moved away-I took Bailey, and told him I charged him with
being in company with a boy, passing counterfeit coin--he said he did not
know what I meant; he did not know any boy-I took him to the station,
and Ricks was brought there afterwards, and Bailey directly said, "I do not
know that boy at all; I never saw him before"- that was before anything
had been said about him, or any remark made-Ricks was taken close to Bailey
in the dock, and Bailey stooped down and whispered something to him-
Ricks then said, “I never saw this man before; I don't know him; it was a
man with a black coat gave me the sixpence."
WILLIAM JAYCOCKS policeman, D 233). I was called to Mr. Webb's shop,

ELIZABETH GREEN re-examined. This tea resembles the tea I sold to Ricks,

WILLIAM WEBSTER. These sixpences are both counterfeit, and from the
same mould.
