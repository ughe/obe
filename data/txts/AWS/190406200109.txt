WILLIAM BECKTON, M.R.C.S., L.R.C.P. I am at present attached to
the Seamen's Hospitalon May 26th I made a post mortem examination
on the deceased, in conjunction with Dr. Rose, who is now seriously ill
and unable to attend to his workthe external appearances of the
deceased's body were those of a well nourished man there were no injuries
except a slight abrasion on the upper part of the cheek, which wa8 pro:
bably caused after death, probably in the dragging-the air passages
contained water and frothy mueus, which would be from the drowning-
the heart was quite normal-the stomach contained water and a very
large amount of food--all the appearances coincided with drowning-
there was no smell of liquor- large amount of water would destroy the
signs of spirits-the cause of death was drowning-Dr, Rose agrees with
me in that opinion.
