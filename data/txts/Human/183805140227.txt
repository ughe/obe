   COURT . Q.  Before they shook hands, did not the prisoner say, "I am rather passionate, but let it all drop?" A.  I did not hear that—I am sure of that—I did not hear the words, "Let it all drop"—I never heard him say so.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  May that expression have taken place, and you forget it? A.  Yes, it is a good while ago—he may have said so—after the prisoner struck the deceased, he fell from the last blow—the deceased sent for the doctor—I had not drank anything myself that night—the man that came in with the deceased (Harwood) went for the doctor—nobody sent for him but the deceased—he came and cut the deceased's hair off—he came in it; or six minutes after the blow was struck—he lives next door—the de ceased said to him, "Now, do what is necessary, and you shall be paid for it; you have no occasion to be afraid, you will be paid for what you do;" and the doctor said, "You go home and get a poultice, and come to me in the morning"—I cannot say how long he staid in the public-house, for I left myself about nine o'clock.
   COURT . Q.  When he shoved  him away, he shoved  him in the breast, did not he? A.  Yes.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did he get his head dressed at Guy's-hospital? A.  I do not know—I do not remember his returning to the tap-room when his head was dressed—I was not there.
