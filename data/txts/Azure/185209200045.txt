JOHN BARTLETT. I am employed in the General Post Offce, and live at
Dalston. On Thursday evening, 2nd Sept., I was in Tabernacle-walk,
Horton—I saw a boy running, pursued by a man in a butcher's dress—I do
not recognize the prisoner as being the man, Only by his height and appear-
nee—I could not see his features—the boy ran about six or eight yards before
the man overtook him—he then slid himself on the pavement close to the
wall, and the man came up and gave him a {lap on the head—the boy said
ømetbing, and he gave him another, and the boy laid himself on his side—
the man hit him first on the left side of his head with his open hand, and
then on the other side—several people came round, spoke to the butcher, and
requested him not to go away, as it appeared he had hurt the boy, but he went
—the policeman earne up, and J, supposing there could be nothing the matter,
vent on—I did say to a person standing by, You had better have him re-
moved to the other side, for I believe the boy is merely imposing on tbe
public, to get the sympathy Of the passers by "—that was my impression, or
I should have stopped to see the result; I thought the boy was shamming.
Cross-examined by ME. BALLANTINE. Q. You speak of a blow, it was
box on the ears, was it not? A. Yes; the first was on the head, and the
Other a little lower—his band was open ; it was not a violent blow—be did
not appear at all excited, and I did not hear any bad language used—the boy
did not stumble before the prisoner boxed his ears, but he sat down on
purpose—he did not sit down suddenly, in a way to shake himself—I did
not see his head knock against the wall—I cannot say whether it did or not
—it was rather a dark place.
SARAH HIGGINS. On the evening of 2nd Sept. I was in Tabernacle-walk.
and saw the boy running, and the prisoner after him—I saw him follow him
across the road for about twenty yards, as near as I can say ; at the end of
those twenty yards the prisoner overtook the boy, and hit him on the right
side Of the head, and with that the boy leaned towards the wall, and laid him-
self down on his left side—I only saw one blow—I should say that hit him
at the side of the ear—I did not think anything about it, and went away—I
have known the prisoner by sight.
Cross-examined. Q. It was a very slight blow? A. Very slight indeed.
