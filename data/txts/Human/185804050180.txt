MR. BAYLEY . Q.  Did you ever see the prisoner before Saturday 13th Feb.? A.  No, I did not—between the Saturday evening when he said, he had arranged with Mrs. Spurway and the following Wednesday when I gave him into charge I had only seen him once—I never agreed to give him 30 s . for what he was to do. SUSANNAH NICHOLS. I am the wife of the last witness. I was present when the 15 s . were paid—no one else was present besides Wells—my husband said when he gave the 15 s ., that was towards taking proceedings against Mrs. Spurway in the "Proctor's Court"—the prisoner said he would soon get the money; he said the expense would be 30 s . to recover the debt—theprisoner used the term "Proctor's Court," and said it must be recovered there.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did I use the words, "Proctor's Court?" A.  Yes; you said, as she, had not administered, you must take the proceedings. JANE SARAH SPURWAY. I am a widow, living near New Cross. On Mon day,15th Feb., or Tuesday, the prisoner came about a debt that was owing by my late husband to Mr. Nichols—he said he called for the debt—I said he had sent two or three men before, and I had been so recently left I could not pay then, that he must wait; I said also, he sent in his bill and demanded it while my husband lay dead and uncoffined, and threatened he would take pro ceedings, and that if he were cruel, enough to do so he must have the trouble, for I had been left without auything—there were two persons who came before the prisoner—I saw the prisoner on the following Thursday, I paid him 1 l .; I afterwards paid him 2 l .—he read to me a Judge's order, with Mr. Nichols' name signed at the bottom—I do not know his handwriting—the prisoner told me he had signed it—he did not say proceedings were begun, but they would be—I did not authorize anybody to give my con sent to a Judge's order; I did not know what it meant—except that piece of paper, I was not served with any proceedings—I never made a pro posal to pay 1 l . in fourteen days, and the rest at 5 s . a week—I expressed a wish to have the proceedings stopped, and would settle if they would take less—I never mentioned any terms.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did not Mr. Nichols send two or three men to you? A.  Yes—Ideclined to pay or make any arrangement—they were very impertinent—Mr. Nichols was present when one of them made an insulting speech—I said to you I had not administered, and he could not maker me pay—you said if I did not pay he would, take proceedings against me—I said I would see my father, and if you would call to-morrow I would consider it—you said on Wednesday evening, it would be wiser to pay by instalments; you men tioned the terms—when you next called I told you I had not seen my father—on Thursday you read a paper to me, it was a Judge's order—I did not know that you said if I paid 1 l . down, and 5 s . a week, and signed that paper, a Judge's order could be got—I said "If he will reduce the debt I will pay it at once"—the next morning when I paid you the money, I saw you go over to Mr. Nichols; I had then paid you 2 l .—I remember your saying Mr. Nichols depended upon the bill to pay Pawsons—you did not say you were Pawsons' attorney—I asked you, and you said you were not. JOHN ALLEN( Policeman, R  140). I apprehended the prisoner on 24th Feb.—he was in the County Court—the plaintiff was there, and he charged him in my presence with obtaining money by false pretences—he turned round, and said, "Now you have begun; by G—, I will serve you out"—he said at the police station, he collected debts for people.
