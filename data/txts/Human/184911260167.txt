   Cross-examined by MR. WOOLLET . Q.  Were not you very drunk? A , No; I could walk—my friend keeps the boarding-house where I live—I had only been to one public-house since my tea—I remained there till it closed, about three hours—I did not drink above two glasses of brandy and water—my friend was not drunk or sober—we met the prisoners very near the Grapes—we had some brandy afterwards at the house; I paid two shillings for it—I saw no other girls there—we remained there half or three-quarters of an hour—I was told that I had dropped my purse there—whether I did or not I cannot say, but I had it when I left. MARIA BERRY. I am a widow, of 2, St. Margaret's-court, Borough; my house receives temporary lodgers for accommodation. On 2d Nov., between two and three o'clock, the prisoners came with Rougier and another man—Rougier sent me out for some brandy, and gave me the money for it—they staid three-quarters of an hour, to the best of my recollection—as he went out of the parlour be dropped his purse, it was a small spring one—Sheen picked it up and gave it to him, and he put it in his left trowsers pocket—I heard the money chink—I requested to look at the purse; and he handed it to me—the spring was tight—I returned it to him—he had it all right when he left my house.
   Cross-examined, Q.  How many rooms have you? A.  Four bed-rooms and two parlours—I am only housekeeper there—the establishment consists of myself and a maid-servant—I fetched in a quartern of brandy and a quartern of rum—the purse was dropped in the back parlour—the captain did not go up-stairs; his friend did with Sheen, but they could not agree, and came down immediately—he was very drunk, but the captain was not so tipsy HENRY THOMAS( policeman, M  64). Last Thursday morning, between two and three o'clock, I saw Sheen come out of Mrs. Berry's house with the captain, they went into a dark corner of the court, twelve or fourteen yards from the door—Hurley went after them; they remained about three minutes—I saw them go away from him—he came and told me something, and I went after the prisoners, and stopped them about two hundred yards off in Redcross-street—I said they were charged with robbing a man in the alley—they said, "Very well," they would come back with me—Sheen dropped half-a-sovereign in Fishmonger-alley; my brother constable picked it up and gave it to me ( produced )—I found this, sovereign and two shillings ( produced ) in the pocket of Sheen's gown—I took them to the station. ANN LONGHURST. I am one of the female searchers of Newgate. Sheen was brought here last Saturday evening—I searched her and found a sovereign at the bottom of her dress, wrapped up in a little bit of leather, between the dress and the lining—it was not fastened, it could not have dropped there—I gave it to the turnkey.
