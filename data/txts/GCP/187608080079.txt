I find that in October and November there was a considerable run upon
the bank-I see on 4th December, 1875, there is 1,0081. to the credit of
Mr. Adderley, and on 19th January, 1876, it was reduced to 3771, the
chief reduction was by a transfer, a payment to Mr. Oakley himself for
Gilbert and Chaudiere shares-it was a simple transfer, no payment a
debit on account of shares bought by him—the account of Mr. Henry
Bryce, on October 22th, was 721., and on 20th January, it was 251., he had
drawn out the difference; Mr. J. Dupré's balance on September 6th, I was
1401., and on December 31st it was 741; Mr. D. W. Dowling's balance on

July was closed; on July 16th Mr. M. G. Clingender's balance was 1501.
the account was then opened, on 1st October it was reduced to 88. 3d, at
which it still stands--the total balance of cash in the bank on October 1st
was 4,7161., and it became reduced by the end of November to 3431,-
bill-book was kept at the head office, and there were bill transactions at a
few of the branches--the total amount in the head office bill-book is
9,382., and at the branches there might be perhaps another thousand or
two, speaking roughly with regard to the drawing account at|Bosanquets,
believe the usual practice was for Mr. Oakley to draw a choque everyday
for a sum that was given to Mr. Beddy to pay the depositors with, and the
balance was repaid into Bosanquet's again the tellers in the bank were
fed by cheques drawn on this account, while it lasted, by Mr. Oakley, in his
own name; then money was paid back from the bank and deposited at
Bosanquet's to Mr. Oakley's account, not to the amount of the cheque, no
specified sum, no specifio balance daily-I find the sums paid in are very
often round sums, not exact balances at the end of the day, whatever
there was to pay was paid to Bosanquet's--the business was not all done.
through Bosanquet's, a portion of it was, from March to June, 1875-the
furniture of his house was paid for out of his private account the furniture was
Bold and fetched about 3001, at an auction, under his bankruptcy, siezed
and sold for the creditors- I believe I heard that up to sometime in the
autumn Mr. Oakley lived in lodgings--when I took possession I saw two
Women servants in the house, it was a house of about 70% a year--we
have made inquiries to see if any of those charges against Mr. Oakley in
