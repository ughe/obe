Smithfield, and was accosted by Williams, who asked me to go home with her, which I refused—while I was talking with her, Johnson came up, and said, "Will you go home with me?"—I said no; I wanted to go to Long-lane—they said they would go with me—they both laid hold of me, one on each side—I tried to get away—Williams altered her position, and got a little in front—at that moment I felt a hand come out of my right  hand trowsers pocket, in which I had had a bag, with four sovereigns and the silver stated—they both had hold of me at that time, and I saw Williams pass something to Johnson—I then felt, and my bag was gone—I said I was robbed—they both declared I was not—I said, "I am sure I am, "and I saw my bag in Johnson's hand—Williams said, "Give the b----his bag; "which Johnson did; but I fancied I had been robbed—I still held them, and called the police—I saw Johnson put her hand to her mouth, and I heard something rattle in her mouth—I said, "She is swallowing something"—she said she was not—the policeman came and took her—I missed three sovereigns from the bag at the station.
   Johnson.  He asked us the way to Long-lane—I said I would show him—he gave both his arms to us, and told us to accept his arms. Witness. I did not; they came and seized me. ESTHER WOOLLIDGE. I searched the prisoners—I found on Williams a sovereign, a 1 s ., and a 6 d ., and 2 1/2 d .—she said she had nothing but a 6 d . about her.
   Johnson's Defence.  He said he had lost his bag—I took it off the ground and gave it him—I had a sovereign, and was afraid he would own it—that was why I said I had only a 6 d .

 1662. SUSAN JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of May , 1 shawl, value 4 d  .; 1 box, 1 s  .; 1 pair of stockings, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 1 cap, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 2 pillow cases, 1 s  . 6 d  .; 3 towels, 4 d  .; and 2 table cloths, 6 d  .; the goods of Elizabeth Sullivan . ELIZABETH SULLIVAN. I live in Twine-court, Shad well. On the 5th of May the prisoner came out of the workhouse, and told me she had neither home nor habitation—I said if she could make shift with me and my boy, she might come—on Wednesday she went out, between eleven and twelve o'clock, and came back between seven and eight in the evening, with a man—I went to my next door neighbour for about ten minutes—when I came back, the man was still there; but I missed my box and a shawl which had been on it—on the Friday after I met her in the street, with my shawl on—I said, "For goodness' sake give me what was in the box, or acknowledge it; I won't hurt you"—she said she would not—I gave her in charge—this is my shawl.
   Prisoner.  You sold my shawl to get tea, and said, "You can have the loan of my old shawl, "and I said, "Very well. "Witness. I did not—I did not sell her shawl to Emma Perkins—she left a few things with me for board and lodging—she never paid me anything. JOHN BUTT( police-constable K  197.) The prisoner was given in charge—she refused to give up the shawl, and said she would sooner break it in pieces than the prosecutrix should have it—she came to the station without an officer.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The prosecutrix lent me the shawl, when she had sold mine to Emma Perkins. GUILTY. Aged 48.— Confined Two Months .
