On the 15th of April the prisoner brought me this pair of wheels—he said his truck was run over and broken, and he could not sell his articles—I bought the wheels of him for half-a-crown—I am sure he is the man. JAMES EASTON. I am shop-boy to Mr. Matthews. I remember the pri soner bringing these wheels—I am sure he is the person—I went with the policeman and pointed him out. HENRY WOOD( police-constable K  44.) Easton pointed out the prisoner to me—I asked where he got those wheels—he said he never sold any wheels to him; but at the station he said, "I will pay the money sooner than go before the Magistrate."
   Prisoner's Defence.  From last August I was at work; I know nothing about the wheels; if I had wanted to sell them, I should not have gone close to my own home; there are plenty of men dressed like me. GUILTY  . Aged 23.— Recommended to mercy. — Confined One Month .

ELLEN REGAN was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of June , 1 coat, value 1 l  .; 3 pairs of trowsers, 1 l  . 10 s  .; 1 jacket, 10 s  .; 4 shirts, 10 s  .; 4 sheets, 1 l  .; and 1 pair of shoes, 5 s  .; the goods of Edmond Pennington ; and that she had been before convicted of felony. EDMOND PENNINGTON. I live in King-street, Kensington, and am a tailor. On the 30th of May I missed the articles stated, from my room—the prisoner was in the habit of visiting me—when I leave my room I put my key into a knife-box which hangs outside the door—sometimes under the mat, and sometimes in another place—when I left it on that day, at six o'clock in the morning, it was in the knife-box—I came home at twelve—when I re turned I had some suspicions, and went after the prisoner, and found her in John-street, St. Marylebone—two days after, I went into the prisoner's room—I told her a sister of hers had come from Liverpool, and if she would come to Kensington, she would see her at my room—the prisoner said she did not think her sister was there—I said she was—she said again she did not think she was, that she knew what brought me there, that she took the things, or stole them, I cannot say which, and burnt the duplicates—she took 31 s . 6 d . from her pocket, and said it was part of the produce of my pro perty—most of my things had been in the drawers, which were not locked. CHARLES REEVES. I am assistant to a pawnbroker. On the 30th of May the prisoner pawned this coat, three pairs of trowsers, three waistcoats, four shirts, three sheets, and a pair of shoes, for 2 l . 14 s .—I am sure it was her. HENRY MOUNT( police-constable T  126.) On the 1st of June the prisoner was given into my charge—the prosecutor brought this money, and stated how he got it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  He found me in Little Park-lane; I was not dressed; he told me to put my things on, and that my sister had come from Liverpool; he sent for drink, and then said, "I have been robbed, if you will tell me what you have done with the things, I will not hurt a hair of your head;" and he being my sister's husband, I thought he would not; I went and told him where the things were. JOHN BARTON. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from the Clerk of the Peace of Westminster—( read )—the pri soner is the person. Prisoner. Q.  Can you take your oath of that? A.  Yes, I have done it.
