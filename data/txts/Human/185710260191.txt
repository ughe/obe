   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you known her some little time before? A.  A year and a quarter—I should say his conduct was generally that of a kind and affectionate husband to her—they have two babies—there are five chil dren—two have died—they are all young—the eldest is nearly fourteen, another eleven, and the others very small.
   COURT . Q.  Did she say what passed when she insulted the man? A.  No, she did not—she could not recollect that properly. HONOR STAFFORD. I am a widow, and live at No. 4, Model Buildings, Vauxhall, in the same house and on the same floor as the prisoner and his wife; our doors are just facing one another. On Sunday night, 20th Sept., between 12 and 1 o'clock, I heard Mrs. Welch screaming, and saying, "You will kill me"—I jumped out of bed, and went to the door in my night dress—I saw Welch just within his own door, making, as I thought, towards the landing—I returned into my own apartment, locked the door, and went to bed—I heard no more—I did not see Mrs. Welch—I heard no screams after that.
   Cross-examined. Q.  It lasted a very short time? A.  A very short time, what I noticed of it—I have known the prisoner about twelve months—I believe his conduct was affectionate to his wife—I am no acquaintance of theirs. WILLIAM ERTHWAITE THOMPSON.
