Cross-examined by Mr. Phillips. Q. How long had the prisoner been
in your master's employment? A. A fortnight-be had very likely been
gaining his master's good will very fast-I know he had-I am not in the
employment now, I was discharged---my master did not direct me to
watch the prisoner-I told Johnson, on Saturday morning, of my intention
of doing 80-I had not heard that any money was lost-I had
reason to watch him, for on the Wednesday or Thursday before, I saw him
drop some money from his hand, and look very much confused-that wa
the reason I watched him—the 1s. 9d. is entered in the book, but very
likely that was put down afterwards-he did enter it-he asked me to go
with him to the desk, and look behind, to see if the shilling was there, but
I thought he would put it there, I went directly after, by myself—Johnson
was serving a customer in the shop at the time-I had had no words with
the prisoner-I was by the side of him when he put the 9d. on the desk-
I went directly after and saw no shilling.
- Q. If there had been one, you had the opportunity of removing it?
A. Yes, but I did not-I left a month after this, because I was ill-I wa
out one night-two gentlemen assaulted me, and had to pay 5l. each for
it-I slept Saturday night in the station-house-I was with my sister and
another young lady, in George-street, Strand, and they wanted to take the
inside of the pavement--I was eight or nine months in the prosecutor's
employ-I was discharged about a fortnight ago, on a Tuesday night-the
assault was on the Saturday I was ill on Saturday, and did not go back
till Tuesday- I went to the theatre on Monday night, instead of returning
to my master's it was very foolish of me I was out Saturday night,
Sunday night, and Monday night, without giving him notice-I told my
master I had been ill-I was discharged for staying away.
THOMAS JOHNSON. I am in the employ of Mr. Moyce. On Saturday
evening, the 27th of October, I counted the money on the desk, after Bryant
-there was three half-crowns, a sixpence, a fourpence, and some coppers--
Bryant told me to count it-the prisoner was serving a customer while I did
80—he took 18. 9d. of the customer, and went and put some money on the
desk-Bryant went and counted it, I went afterwards, and found there was
one shilling short of what the prisoner had taken-I did not remove any
thing-I am quite sure there was no shilling there when I went-Bryant
might, if so disposed, have taken the shilling off before I went.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you not very much surprised at Bryant ask-
ing you to go and count the money? A. Yes; he told me on Saturday
morning that he suspected him-I had a slight suspicion myself, as he
several times dropped money, and said it was very slippery-he said so in
Bryant's presence-Bryant was discharged for being out two or three
nights-Bryant asked the prisoner what he had taken from the customer,
and he said, " 18. 9d."- I did not look to see whether he entered it in the
book-he ought to have done so. but it is sometimes not done when we
are very busy--my master had not desired me to watch the prisoner-he
never intimated any suspicion about him, or complained of money being
deficient.
