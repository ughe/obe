   Cross-examined by Patterson.  The amount last Friday was consider ably over 1,000 l .—it ranged from 800 l . to 2,000 l ., according to the number of men—we have had nearly 2,000 men during the last 12 months. WALTER HENRY MAUDSLEY. I am a member of the firm of Maudsley and Co., Engineers—I have heard our premises described to-day and that description is accurate.
   Cross-examined by Patterson.  You have been in our employ about four and a half years—I believe we received a good character with you, but Mr. Trimme can speak to that—you have been during the last three years in the office and I have seen you almost daily and have never seen anything in your conduct to lead me to suspect you were connected with such men as the other prisoners—you used to fetch the money on Fridays, you and another messenger together, in bags from the Bank—I cannot say if you had an opportunity of seeing the cheque drawn every Friday—there are two cheques drawn, the larger amount on Friday and the other on Saturday morning—it has not been so low as 400 l .—I cannot speak as to the number of men we had.
   Re-eximined.  I did not know when we took Patterson into our service that he had been sentenced to penal servitude—I have since ascertained it.
   By the Prisoner.  I believe that was 15 years ago—I have seen the papers. WILLIAM CHAMBERLAIN( Re-examined ). I had a conversation with Patterson when he was taken with Workman—he said, "It was through him partly that I got into trouble before—it is some years ago now since I had the five years"—he volunteered the statement—I had these papers with me and showed him his photograph ( produced ) and he said, "Yes, it's quite right" ( certificate produced )—he had a ticket of leave and failed to report himself, and ought to have been apprehended.
   Cross-examined by Patterson.  You freely acknowledged this, and told me the date, and where you had been—you gave me three written characters from where you had been employed—you had been at one place nine months; at another place they could not find you in their books, and at Smith and Taylor's, Pimlico, they admitted that you were employed there as a labourer, but did not know how long.
   Patterson produced a written defence, denying the charge, and assenting that the witnesses' statements were wicked fabrications.
   Arthur Workman, in his defence, stated that the police had not told the truth, and had been working the case up solely on the letter they had received from Harding, and that Harding's statement at the Police Court was a tissue of falsehoods, and that there was no evidence to support that of the convicts. He also stated that during the last thirty years he had been occupied as a grocer, a photographer, and as a police-constable, and was never a housebreaker, except once with Rogers, when in great poverty. Emma Workman, in her defence, stated that she knew nothing of the charge; that she never saw the keys, that she had been annoyed by the police every time her husband got into trouble, and that the evidence was perfectly false.
