(Defence.)
FRED BARTLETT (prisoner, on oath). On November 26, at 10 pm.. I met
my wife at “The Star" public house and we left about 11 p.m. with
Schofield, Stanton and his wife and another friend and his wife, and we all
went to my home, No. 2, Grove Road. I had my pony and art,
which I left outside. I afterwards walked up the mews and saw
Detective Hyde and a policeman. I asked them what was the
matter. The constable said I had no light on my barrow. I then lit the
lamp. Schofield came out with a jug and a glass and offered the constable
and Hyde a drink of beer. The officers walked down the road and we went
in. We afterwards pushed the barrow up the mews and put the pony in
the seullery, The pony is generally kept at Stepney, but it was too late to
take it there. We had about five or six friends and were having a sing-bong
I had a very valuable dogand it barked. I and Schofield went out to see what
the noise was, as we thought there was someboby about in the mews
I had no hat or cont on. I told Sebofield to get over the wall, which
he did, and the constable got hold of him. I then climbed over
the wall and jumped into a van, and the constable got hold of
me by the shirt and collar. I said, "What are you doing. You
have made a mistake." He said, "No I have not-I will give you
bit of Wonderland "-he told me he had been boxing up at * Wonderland,
then struck out at me, and as he went to hit me he hit the rail of the van
and hurt his hand. I never attempted to bite him. All the vans were
by the side of my garden wall. I never went inside the stable or got on
the roof or into the loft of No. 710. It would be impossible for anyone to
jump 12 ft. off the roof on to a van on the other side of the mews.
ADELAIDE BARTLETT, wife of the prisoner. When my husband went
out he had no cont and no hat on. The constable came the next morning
and I gave him the cost.
WILLIAM FARLEY, Winnington Road, Leytonstone, tesselated tile fitter,
On the night of November 26 I was with Bartlett, Schofield, and three or
four others having a sing-song, and we had sat down to supper When the
dog started barking. Bartlett went out in his shirt sleeves. I did not
take notice whether he was wearing a hat.

Verdict, both Guilty.
Bartlett admitted having been convicted at Maidstone, on October 22,
1903, of horse stealing. Three other convictions were proved.
Schofield admitted being convicted November 11, 1902, receiving five
years' penal servitude for stealing lead. A large number of convictions
were proved, commencing in 1888, the sentences amounting to twenty
years. Sentence, Schofield, six years' penal servitude; Bartlett, three
years' penal servitude.
