WILLIAM Esden. I let out a horse and cart, and live in Vinegar-yard.
Bermondsey. On the 7th of February, Mendes engaged my cart for eight
o'clock in the morning of the 8th of February-we got three rolls of oil-
cloth at the corner of Snow's-fields, where I found Mendes with my cart,
and took it to Mr. Levy's in Finsbury-market-all but one little piece,
which we left at Mr. Weeks', in Wormwood-street-1 drove the cart.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you leave Mendes with the cart? A. I left
it against his door-he drove it himself the first thing in the morning-1
saw the oil-cloth delivered - I am not acquainted with any men in Mr.
Rolls' employ.

Thomas HOUGHTON. I am porter to Mr. Levy, of Finsbury-market.
On Tuesday morning, the 8th of February, Mendes brought three rolls of
door-cloth in a cart-there were 145 yards—he cut off four yards,
which he sent back--he sold the rest to my master, for 1s. 80. a yard
they came to 121. 2s. 6d. after we measured them—they were in five
pieces.
Jour WOODHOUSE (police-sergeant M 13.) I took the prisoner on Sa-
turday, the 12th of February, at his master's I asked if he knew what
be was charged with-he said“Yes," he was aware of it, one of the clerks
had mentioned it to bim-he said he was innocent-in consequence of
information I went with Thomas Rolls to the house of Mr. Levy, in Fins-
bury-market, and received from Mr. Houghton five pieces of oil-cloth-
they are identified by Mr. Humble I went to Mr. Weeks' house, who is a
pork-butcher in Bermondsey-street, and obtained four yards, which had
been cut from one of the other pieces the whole measured 146 yards..
WILLIAM PLATT, I am in the employ of Messrs. Pickford, in Wood-
street. About nine o'clock on Tuesday morning, the 8th of February, the
prisoner brought thirteen packages of floor-cloth in the prosecutor's cart
I saw the name of Rolls on it-it was a tilted cart-I had seen it before
I gave him a receipt in his cart-book-this is it-he did not bring any-
thing on Monday that I am aware of.
Cross-examined. Q. In what part of the premises do you receive the
goods? 4. In the yard-I always go down and receive the goods-I am
quite clear about that I went down myself that morning-I had no con-
versation with the prisoner-I had known him before-Mr. Rolls' is a
large heavy cart, drawn by one horse.
