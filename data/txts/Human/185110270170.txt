   Cross-examined by MR. CAARTEEN . Q. Who lives in the house? A.  No one but his father and mother, and brother, and a young woman, named Flaharty, who is now in her confinement, who attends his mother, who is afflicted—the mother and the young woman sleep on the floor—the father and the two brothers sleep in the room—I am no relation to any of them—my husband is a labouring man—he works for Mr. North—he was at home that night—he saw the prisoner, and gave him a glass of porter—he saw him go out of the room—I got up from the table and gave him the candlestick—my husband was sitting at the table—when the prisoner received the candlestick he wished us good night, and went up stairs—I got up and shut the door when he went out—I did not go in their room, but J am well aware that his father and mother, Flaharty and himself, and his brother were there—Flaharty is married—her husband is a seafaring man—half-past 12 o'clock is not a late hour for me to be up on Saturday night—I have many little odds and ends to do—on Saturday night I am very often up till 1—on Monday morning my husband goes to work at 6—we sometimes go to bed at 10, and some times at 11—we never go to bed leaving the street-door open—we shut it—if I do not bolt it, it has a spring-lock—I remember the night the policeman came to our house—he knocked at the door, and then shoved  the door, and I got up—the policeman did not enter the house without my knowledge he knocked and shoved  the door—the door did open when he shoved  against it, and I momentarily got up—he had knocked at my bed-room door before I got up—he was inside the street-door before I opened my bed-room door—to the best of my knowledge that was between 10 and 11—the prisoner has been in my place going on for three months—my husband is not here, he could not leave his situation to be here—the prisoner's father and his younger brother are here.
   Q.  How do you know that this was on 28th Sept.? A.  I do not exactly know the day of the month—I heard the policeman say it was 28th Sept.—it was the Saturday before they came to search my house—the prisoner was mostly in the habit of coming into my room—he used to come down for a light or a pipe of tobacco.
   MR. CHARNOCH . Q.  Your husband is at work? A.  Yes, as a slater, for Mr. North, in the Palace, New-road—it was on a Thursday night the police man came to my house—he knocked at my door, and shoved  the door against my parlour-door—he must have shoved  with great force to get it open—I did not prevent his searching my place, but he did not offer to search my apart ment—Ellen Flaherty is in her confinement.
