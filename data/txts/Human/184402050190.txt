   Q.  Is it in your knowledge that a prisoner is entitled to a copy of the deposition? A.  Yes, but not of his examination, I believe—if the prisoner had come and asked for a copy of any depositions that I have, he would have had them—my practice is, when I furnish a copy of the depositions, (though I believe I am not required to do it) to give the attorney a copy of the ex amination also, unless I had directions not to do it.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Those that you read first are not part of the depositions returned to this Court? A.  No, because there are numerous cases where the prisoners are afterwards discharged, and not sent for trial—the depositions were taken on the 29th—I believe that is the course of business pursued at every Police-court. WILLIAM STILES. I contracted with the parish, and was engaged in repair ing the pavement in Baker's-row, by order of the Commissioners, on the 23rd of Jan. last—it was my duty to take the old stones to the green-yard—I see that all the stones go there as far as I can—they are kept there for various uses to repair with—stones which are too much worn for some pavements, are good enough for others—many stones taken to the green-yard are subse quently used at other places—Tiediman was engaged by me, amongst other workmen at Baker's-row—it was not his duty to take these stones to the green-yard—the work was not then completed—when it was, my carts would have taken them—Tiediman had been employed by me nine years—he would know what the practice was—that was known to all the men—Tiediman had no authority to sell them or to give them away—on the 24th of Jan. he did not come to work, nor on the 25th—he had no leave of absence from me—I did not see him again till he was at the Police-court.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I suppose Tiediman wai your servant to work on the stones belonging to the Commissioners of Pavements? A.  He was working under my directions upon the pavements—when the stones are sent to the green-yard it is for the purpose of putting them in possession of the parish for various uses—they do not become my property—these are not what I should call useless pieces.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Have you seen the stones produced? A.  Yes—I can not swear to them, but they are like the stones that were there—when they are taken up they lie in the street till the work is complete—I have a contract with the Commissioners—Mr. East was the attesting witness to it.
   JURY . Q.  Do not the Commissioners hold you accountable for the old stones? A.  My property was there at the same time—if the stones were lost, Ishould say it would be the Commissioners' loss. WILLIAM FREDERICK EAST. I am surveyor to the Commissioners of Pave ments, of St. James's, Clerkenwell.
