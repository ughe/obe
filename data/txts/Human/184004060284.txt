I and my mother keep these barges. I was at Nash's-wharf on the Friday morning when these tares were delivering—there were about ten quarters in the barge when I came down at the end of the delivery—Nash said to me, in the presence of Long, "William, there is a sack short, there ought to be thirty-nine quarters and a half"—I asked Long how it was—he said, "I have got the sack, it is all right, I will go and fetch it"—he jumped into a lug-boat, went ahead of the road, and Mr. Nash told me to row him off in a skiff, which I did—we rowed round the stern of Vandefvorde's vessel, and Long went ahead—I observed him go and take the sack of tares from abaft the mast, and Mr. Nash walked forward into the sailing barge—I saw him go up to Vander vorde—the sack was put into the lug-boat—I heard Vandervorde say, at I was going to the police-office, be hoped Mr. Nash would not go any fur ther with it.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Long has been in your service a great many years? A.  Yes, and conducted himself Well. JOHN ROBERT THORP. I am a corn-meter, and live in Red cross-square, Tower-street—I was on board Vandervorde's vessel on Friday morning, the 13th of March, I had to master some corn there—I got, there about seven o'clock in the morning—I saw Long come on board about half-past seven—Vandervorde and I were speaking together relative to the corn that I was about to deliver—Long came and said, "I have got the sack of tares," or "brought the sack," or "a sack of tares"—I saw nothing particular in his manner—Vandervorde replied, but I did not hear what—Long did not remain on board more than a few seconds—about nine o'clock in the morning I saw Vandervorde on boarde Long's barge, which was lying alongside Vandervorde's vessel—he was in the act of up-ending a sack of tares which was on Long's barge—Vandervorde said to me, "Brother Fellowship, tend me a hand," meaning me to assist him in get ting the sack on board his own barge from Long's—I was on board Van dervorde's barge from the lime of Long's coming, and saying he had brought a sack—I did not see any sack removed from Vandervorde's barge to Long's—I was measuring corn below, and a sack of tares could have been removed without my seeing, it—a man assisted Vandervorde with a kind of pulley, and I assisted in getting it on board Vandervorde's barge—I hooked it on—there was a small rent in the bottom of the sack—I can swear to the sack—soon after the sack was hoisted on board Vandervorde's vessel, I heard him call for a sack, and one of his men replied to it—the man asked him what sack—he said, "Any one of ours"—then one of his own was brought to him—he held it up, and I said it was as bad as the one they were going to shoot it out of—they were shot out of one sack into the other—Vandervorde was standing by the side, assisting in holding up the sack—the sack they were in before had the name of Nash on it—about an hour after Long came on board, and inquired for the sack of tares—he said, "Where is that sack of tares? it is wrong; for God's sake let me have them, or there will be such a piece-of-work about it"—I do not know to whom he said it—Vandervorde was there—I did not hear him ask for any thing—that sack could not be found at first, it was afterwards found and brought—I did not see the re-shooting of them—I went down soon after—I heard Mr. Nash on board.
