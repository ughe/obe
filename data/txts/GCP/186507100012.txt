CAROLINS JAMES. I am single, and live in the same house with the
prisoper and prosecutrix--on 9th May, about half-past 9 o'clock, the prisoner
came up to my mother's door and knocked, and told us to go dowu stairs
for he thought he had done it this time I went down, and saw Mrs. Banks
in one corner of the room, by the fire-place-her hair was all down over her
shoulders, and she was smothered in blood-I asked her if I could do any.
thing for her, and so did the prisoner-she did not answer for a minute
she then said I could do nothing for her-I got some water, but she would
not let me touch her head-I asked the prisoner what he had done it for-
he said he did not know-I asked what be had done it with-be picked up
the poker and gave it to me, and said, “This is what I have done it with "-I
then went with Mrs. Banks to the hospital - this is the poker (produced)
he said as he threw it down it broke.
WILLIAM SALTER EOCLES. I am house-surgeon at St. Bartholomew's
hospital --on 9th May last the prosecutrix was brought there-her face was
covered with blood, streaming down from wounds on the head-I examined
her head, and found eight severe wounds-at the bottom of three the skull
was broken, and in one the skull was driven in; one of her fingers was very
badly bruised, and a ring on the finger was bent in this poker would be
just the sort of instrument to produce the wounds-- she had no other bruises
-she has been in the hospital since 9th May, and she is not now out of
danger-she still bas some dead bove in her head.
MAXWELL ALLINGHAM (Policeman, G 15). On 9th May, about ten minutes
past 11, & woman came up to me in Whitecross-street-in consequence of
what she said I went to the prisover's house--I found Mrs. Banks lying on
the floor with her head against the cupboard, bleeding very much from the
head-I took her to the hospital, and sent the prisoner to the station-be
said he did it, and he was very sorry for it; if she died he supposed he should
be hanged-he did pot appear to be the worse for liquor at that time.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : "I have this statement to
make, that I was not conscious of what I was doing this night, and I had
not been right for some days; occasionally I am not to say answerable for
my actions. On the Sunday afternoon I told iny wife not to leave me, as
I thought something would happen, and I cannot be left by myself, such
awful things come into my mind since I tried to make away with myself,
especially in the room where I committed the rash act. I had a fall four or
five years ago, and the least excitement I am not conscious of what I am
doing ; I am obliged to take liquor before I can go on with my work; I am
very nervous; my mother was in an asylum, and my father made away

notice of what I say; my son has very often found me crying, and he has
