1950. EBENEZER WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the
30th of April, 3 decanters, value 58., the goods of John Curtis.
Joux CURTIS. The prisoner has been paying his addresses to my daugh-
ter for some time. In consequence of what she told me, and having
some duplicates put into my hand, I looked over my stock, and missed
some decanters—these are them.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Is your daughter here ? A. I
believe she is—she has not resided with me for several days-I have not
spoken to her since last Friday.
Louisa Curtis. The prisoner has been paying his addresses to me for
some months--I received a letter from him, a week ago, containing some
duplicates, which I produced to my father-I had authorized the prisoner
to take these decanters, and I knew they were out of the house I was so
agitated when I was before the Magistrate, I did not know what I said-I
have some recollection of his taking the decanters.
NOT GUILTY.
1951. AMBROSE BALL was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of
May, 1 sack, value 28., the goods of John Tralock and another, his
masters ; and SAMUEL WRIGHT HILL, for feloniously receiving the
same, well knowing it to have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.

MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAS TRULOCK, I am in partnership with my brother John ; we
are corn-merchants and factors; at Albion-wharf, Old Ford. Ball
was our carman--we had no other regular carman-by chance we might
send out another-I have seen a corn-sack produced-it is our property, I
am certain of it-it is one of some I bought in December-Ball had no
authority to deliver it to Hill or to any person whatever he had no
business with this sack on the 27th of May-he might have taken sacks of
this description in his business--he had to deliver beans that day, which
he did, and they might be in sacks of this kind-he had no right to take
anything but beans that day.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. How many sacks had you of this
description ? A. A thousand.
