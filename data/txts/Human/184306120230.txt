THOMAS WHITE. About ten o'clock, on the night of the 6th of June, I went to bed in a barge on the canal at West Drayton, Hillingdon—my things were safe in my cupboard a quarter of an hour before I went to bed—I awoke be tween three and four and missed them—the key of the cupboard was in my trowsers pocket at the back of my head—I have seen the prisoner working on our canal—these are my trowsers—I had a ring like this in my pocket—I be lieve this to be mine.
   Cross-examined  by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Have you any doubt about them.
   A.  Not any—I worked hard for the sovereigns—Soldier is a boatman who works about. ALFRED HUGHES( police-constable D  174.) I found the prisoner outside the Red Lion, in the Edgeware-road, on the night of the 8th June—I said I should take him on suspicion of stealing 11 l . and a quantity of clothes of Thomas White—he said, "Where?"—I said at West Drayton—he said, well, we could have a drop of beer and settle it—another boatman came out, and the prisoner said, " Soldier , give him a shilling, and say you have not been able to see me"—I said he must go to the station—he said he would not—he sat down—another officer was opposite—I called him, and we took him—I found on him this ring, and part of the property—I then went to No. 7, Union-place, and asked for Mrs. Buck—I said I had come for a bundle that Williams had left her—she produced this bundle in the room on the first floor.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not the prisoner address Soldier  particularly about this matter? A.  Not in my hearing— Soldier  is the nick-name of the boatman. MARY BUCK. I live with my mother, in Union-place. The prisoner brought this bundle on Wednesdy morning, the 7th of June.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know this boatman they call Soldier? A.  Yes, I have known him for years—he worked where my father does. GUILTY  . Aged 18.— Recommended to mercy  .— Judgment Respited.
 1999. GEORGE GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of June , 1 half-sovereign and 3 sixpences, the monies of John Connor , from his person. JOHN CONNOR. I am a pensioner of the East India-house, and live in Lon don-street, Ratcliffe. On the 3rd of June I had been to get my pension—I received a half-sovereign and three sixpences—I got tipsy—when I cane to my recollection I missed my money. GEORGE REEVES. I am apprentice to William Williams, of Cannon-street. On the 3rd of June I saw the prisoner go across Tower-hill, and take the pro secutor, who was drunk, into the Old George—he put his hand under the prosecutor's smock, took something out, and put into his own pocket—I told the policeman, who took him. WILLIAM DONOVAN. I live in Rosemary-lane. I was on Tower-hill—I saw the prosecutor drunk—the prisoner and five or six more knocked him down, and felt his pockets—they said it was not in that pocket, it was in his other—they then took him to the Old George, and the prisoner took something out of his pocket, and put it into his own. JOHN DOWDEN( police-constable H  27.) I found the prosecutor lying on the pavement, opposite the Old George—I asked him if he had lost anything—he said he had, a half-sovereign and two sixpences—I went after the prisoner, and found a half-sovereign and two sixpences in his pocket.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined Nine Months .
