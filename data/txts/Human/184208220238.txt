SARAH MARY CARPENTER. I am single, and live in East-street, Mary lebone—the prisoner came to lodge with me on the 7th of June. On the 31st of July, I went out, and returned on the 1st of August—I then missed my gown and shawl—these now produced are mine. SARAH PAVEY. I live in Barretts-court—the prisoner brought this shawl to me on Sunday, the 31st of July, and asked if I would lend her 1 s . 6 d . on it, which I did. WILLIAM JONES. I am a pawnbroker—the prisoner pawned this gown with me on the 1st of August.
   Prisoner's Defence . I had very little work to do, and I intended to redeem the property again. GUILTY. Aged 33.— Confined Three Months.

 2508. WILLIAM SADD , GEORGE HENRY LYNE, and JAMES WRIGHT  , alias    James Fenables, were indicted for stealing, on the   27th of July , 1 half-crown, and 2 half-pence, the monies of Charles Realff , from the person of Charlotte Realff ; and that Sadd and Lyne had been before convicted of felony. CHARLOTTE REALFF. I am the wife of Charles Realff, a coach-painter, of Charlotte-street, Whitechapel. About three o'clock in the afternoon on the 27th of July, I was walking along Greenfield-street, and heard a cry of "Stop thief!"—I put my hand in my pocket, and found I had lost a half-crown and two halfpence.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How lately before had you felt your money? A.  About ten minutes—I was then in Fieldgate-street. WILLIAM BRADLEY. I live at Seal, in Leicestershire. I was in Field-gate-street that afternoon—I felt the prisoners at my pocket—in conse quence of that I watched and saw Sadd and Lyne go to the prosecutrix—Sadd put his hand into her pocket—Wright was on the other side of the way—when I saw Sadd put his hand into the prosecutrix's pocket, I crossed over and seized him—Wright then crossed the road and seized his hand, and tried to get some money from him—I seized Sadd and Wright—Sadd dropped a half-crown and two halfpence.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you living in town? A.  I arrived the day before—Mrs. Harrison was with me—I saw the prisoners for about ten minutes before the prosecutrix was robbed—I am quite sure I am not mistaken as to Wright being one of the persons I had seen before—Mrs. Harrison was not examined at Lambeth-street—to the best of my know ledge she had the same opportunity of seeing this as I had—she was a little before me—we were going to her husband.
   Lyne. Q.  Are you quite sure you saw me behind Sadd when it was done? A.  Yes, and you shielded him with your coat.
   Sadd's Defence . I have no wish to criminate others, but, had it not been for persons working on my youth, and holding out temptations which I had not the sense to withstand, I should not be placed at this bar; I beg for mercy.
