ELIZABETH ARNOLD. I am the wife of John Arnold, of No. 27, Great
Portland-street. I let lodgings in the course of last year--1 had lodgers
there in June--the prisoner lodged there--I have no doubt of his being the
person-his appearance was not different to what it is now-he wore hair on
his upper lip- he lived there about twenty weeks—the last time I saw him
was Friday, the 30th of June, about four o'clock in the afternoon--I know
Lieutenant Monro by sight-he breakfasted with him that morning-he was
in the habit of coming backwards and forwards to Mr. Grant, I think, for
weeks I cannot tell how I learnt his name, but I have heard it mentioned
-I believe he was there again on the same afternoon-I did not see him-1
heard he was there-I did not hear his voice-Mr. Grant came home again
that Friday evening, I should say between nine and ten o'clock--I did not
see him-I never saw him after four or five o'clock in the afternoon--the last
time was when he was outside, passing the window--his portmanteau and
things were left, and were taken away some months after he had given Do
notice of going away-I had no other gentleman lodger in June last.
SARAW LONGMAN. I lived as housemaid to Mrs. Arnold, in Great Port-
land-street. Lieutenant Grant, the prisoner, lodged there--I have no doubt
of his person-he wore mustachios-I know Mr. Monro-he was at my mis.

his apartments -I know he did not sleep there that night, because his bed was
not tumbled in the morning-he did not return again that I know of till the
