   KENT CASES .,
 1372. JAMES WOODER, was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering a building within the curtilage of the dwelling-house of Walter Allen Meriton , on the   20th of March , at Lee, and stealing therein 1 pair of carriage-lamps, value 1 l  .; 1 box, 6 s  .; and 1 whip, 4 s  .; his property. GEORGE SWAIN. I am groom to Mr. Walter Allen Meriton, who lives in the parish of Lee. On the 20th of March, early in the morning, I went into the garden adjoining master's stable, and saw the prisoner standing close by the wall, which separates our garden from another—I saw a pair of carriage-lamps and the driving-box at his feet, and a pair of my boots on the top of the wall—I went towards him—he drew a knife, and made an attempt to come at me—I went back, and called "Master"—the prisoner got over the wall into an adjoining garden, and made his escape across the road—I looked stedfastly at him, and am sure he is the man—I went to the coach-house, and found master's carriage-lamps and driving-box gone, which had been safe the night before—the stable is in the yard between the two houses—there is a wall right round, enclosing the garden, coach-house, and dwell ing-house—I had locked the front door of the coach-house over night—it was about five minutes before six o'clock when I found the prisoner in the garden.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRICE . Q.  Was it not much earlier? A.  No—I am quite sure it was before six o'clock—I did not look at the clock, but I heard the clock strike directly after—I had never seen the prisoner before—he was dressed in a cloth cap, with a leather front to it, and a white frock on—that is a common dress for a bricklayer. WALTER ALLEN MERITON. I was formerly a captain in the merchant service, and live at Lee. On Monday, the 20th of March, I saw a person running away from my premises, across the high road—he appeared to have a white short frock on, very clean—I saw the prisoner before the Magistrate next day—he appeared to be dressed the same, but the frock was much darker than when I saw him about 100 feet from my house.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You never saw him before? A.  No—I could not swear to him—I only saw him behind. CHARLES NINTMO. I am a policeman. I examined the prosecutor's premises, on the 20th of March, at half-past eight o'clock in the morning—I traced some steps in that garden and the adjoining one, and found part of a whip in the adjoining garden.
