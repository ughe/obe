2230. JOHN NOBLE was indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of William Gilpin , about one in the night of the   1st of Sept ., at St. George the Martyr, Suothwark ; with intent to steal.
   MR. PRENDREGAST conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM GILPIN. I live in High-street, Borough, in the parish of St. George the Martyr, Southwark. About eleven o'clock, on the 1st of Sept., I went to bed—I fastened up my premises—the shop-door was bolted and locked—about one o'clock I was awoke by the door-bell ringing—I dressed, went down, and found two constable with the prisoner in custody—I looked round my pre mises with one of them, and found the first floor back-room window open—there is a court by the side of the house—the window goes out on the leads—the shop extends beyond the house—there is a skylight with leads, and whoever got into the house must have climbed up the water-spout, got on the leads, and then lifted up the back-room window—that could have been done—I found foot-marks on the leads, as if a person had had nails in his boots—it was a scraping on the leads—I had a very large stock—nothing was taken—a pair of steps had been placed against the shop-door at night—I found them two yards from the door, placed against the shutter-box—the window had been shut quite down, but not fastened—I saw the officer pick up a dark lantern in the shop—it was not on my premises before.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q.  Is your dwelling-house at the shop? A.  Yes; it is one building—there is only one door in the front, and no door at the back—there was no means of getting in at the back but by the window—when I came down the shop-door was shut, and the policemen were in the shop—there was quite a mob outside, and the police men closed the door against them—I do not know the prisoner—I always close the window myself—it has a shutter—I had not shut the shutter for some time, but have done so since—I am quite certain I had fastened the window that night—there are gas-light near my shop—the street is very light all through. DONALD THAIN( policemen, M  246.) On this night I was passing along the Borough, and tried Mr. Gilpin's door to see if it was safe—it opened, and the prisoner rushed out of the shop into the street, and turned to the right toward Union-street—I ran after him—I saw him stopped just as he turned Union-street—I am quite sure the man that was stopped was the man I was in pur suit of, and I am quite sure the prisoner is the man who came out of the house—I produced a black bag, which I got from Apthorp.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How far is it from the prosecutor's house to when the prisoner was taken? A.  About 130 yards—I lost sight of him for a moment as he turned into Union-street, and then found him in custody— —there was a cry of, "Stop thief!"—people were not running after him—there was a crowd after he was brought back in custody—I saw nobody run after him but myself and Bray—I took him—some more people might have been there—no one was close to him when I took him but Bray—I can not say whether there were fifteen people within a few yards of him—I saw marks on the lead and window-cill—there was only one mark on the window-ledge—there were a good many on the wall outside—I only saw one at the place where the nails were visible—the wall is a about eleven feet high—the water-spout runs up it—I did not pursue anybody at the back.
   MR. PRENDERGAST. Q.  Could anybody have been substituted for the prisoner between the time of your missing him for the moment, and turning the corner, and seeing him in the witness's custody? A.  No, there was no body else that could have been mistaken for him.
