RICHARD LEE. I am a stockjobber, of Charlotte-street, Camberwell—I
have known Burgess eight or nine years, Pierce about eight years, and
Tester four years perhaps—I was formerly in the employment of the South
Eastern Company, as clerk-at that time Pierce was ticket porter to the
Company, and Burgess was guard—I have been in the habit of using Stearn's
· public house, and have seen them there occasionally at the beginning of
Feb., 1856, Burgess saw me at Mr. Stearn's, and asked me if I knew anything
about Turkish bonds I said, "Yes"—he asked me the price-I referred to
the paper, and found that it was 817 or something like that, and he gave
me instructions to buy 5001. stock - I went to Hutchinsons', the brokers,
and asked them to do it for me, and they did—I took a note of the purchase
to Burgess at Stearn's I bought for a day or two on, because being 5002, it
was not so easily got as 1,0001.--the consideration altogether was 4072 108.
-I went to New Cross, and got the money from Mrs. Burgess---Mr. Burgess
asked me when I should want the money; I said, “It does not matter for &
day or two," and he said, “I will leave it at home; ask Mrs. Burgess for
it ;" and I did so-4051. of it was in notes, and 21. 10s. in gold-I paid the
same notes to Hutchinsons; I wrote the numbers of them, but left it at
New Cross, at Mrs. Burgess's, for safety-I wrote my name on the notes
these three 1001. notes, and eight 102 notes, are part of what I received from
her, and paid to Hutchinsons—my writing is on them (the notes were Nos.
45421, 45423, and 45424 for 1001. each, 9th Jan., 1855, and eight 101.
notes, No8. 21560 to 21567, all dated 5th June, 1855).
Cross-examined by MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE 0. You say that you
saw the prisoner at Stearn's, do you mean the whole of these three men
A. No-I never saw Tester there I never said that I saw them all three
there, I know nothing of Tester being employed on the railway, excepting
that he was clerk.

MR. SERJEANT BALLANTINE to J. P. KNIGHT. Q. What were Tester's
hours of work ! A. From 9 o'clock in the morning to 5 in the afternoon
-it would occasionally happen that he would stay after that time, but those
were his regular hours.
MR. LEE, cro88-examined by MR. GIFFARD. Q. You say that you have known
Burgess seven or eight years! A. Yes—he has always borne the character
of an honest man I had 4057. in notes from Mrs. Burgess-4071. 10s. I
obtained from her in all-I paid the whole at that time--you have had
the notes from the banker's, and you will find my endorsement on them-
I remember five weeks after the Turkish bonds were purchased, that I sold
them again for him for 4641. 78. 6d.-he obtained a profit of 572. 108. on
that transaction--at the time he bought them, he said that he understood
that they would rise 20 per cent, and they did rise 22-I sold them at 931.
MR. BODKIN.Q. When was it you sold them? A. Some time in the
beginning of Feb.-I had & cheque for them on 15th Feb.--I employed
Hutchinsons to sell them, and I had half commission—they gave me a cheque
on Curtis's, and I got it cashed there, put the notes in an envelope, and
went over the water and left them at Mr. Stearn's, for Burgess-I gave them
to the young woman, Thompson, the same notes.
