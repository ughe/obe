   ROBERT WILD. I am shopman to Mr. Sowerby, a pawnbroker, of Chiswell-street. I received this watch in pawn from the prisoner on the 30th of September, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, and lent him 30s. on it; I never saw him before, but am quite certain of his person - I gave him what I supposed amounted to 30s.; he came next morning, and said I had given him a 7s. piece instead of a half-sovereign - I said, "I gave it you for the watch you pawned last night, did not I?" he said Yes, and I gave him half a sovereign for it - I am quite certain of him.
   JOSHUA ARMSTRONG. I am an officer of Worship-street. I went to Portsmouth, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner. Armstrong knows my character. Witness. His father has been forty-two years in His Majesty's dockyard at Portsmouth, and has been a very respectable man.
 Prisoner. My friends are all at Portsmouth.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 22.

 2071.  WILLIAM CLEGG was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of September , at Paddington, 1 watch, value 20l; 1 seal, value 1l.; 1 brooch, value 10s.; 4 rings, value 2l.; 1 pair of sugar-tongs, value 10s.; 1 spoon, value 10s., and 40 herrings, value 5s., the goods of Alexander Cabbell Mann , in his dwelling-house.
   CAROLINE COURTNEY MANN. I am the wife of Alexander Cabbell Mann, who is a fishmonger- we live in Connaught-terrace, Edgware road, in the parish of Paddington. The prisoner was about three weeks in our employ - he had been in service before he came to us; he did not sleep in our house, but came in the day time. On the 4th of September he came as usual a little after seven o'clock; I let him in at the private door, and desired him to go down into the kitchen and light the fire - I went down into the kitchen with him, and the watch was hanging over the dresser; it was a gold watch - I had hung it there a quarter of an hour before - I left him in the kitchen, and went down again to see the time at twenty minutes to eight o'clock, and the watch hung there then, and he was there; I went up stairs to call my husband and children, and when I came down he had opened the shop door, and was gone; I had told him not to open the shop door - I missed the watch, also a gold brooch from up stairs in the bed-room; he could not have taken that that morning, but it was safe the morning before; I also missed four rings from a drawer in the bedroom, and a silver table-spoon and sugar-tongs from the same place; there was a seal to the watch: about forty herrings were taken from the shop - I had seen them that morning: I have never seen the things since - I inquired about at the pawnbrokers in the neighbourhood. I never saw the prisoner again till the 23rd of October, when I found him in a cart at Kensington gravel-pits; my husband was with me, and took him.
   GEORGE WATTS. I know the prisoner - I have seen him about Kensington gravel-pits; I first saw him about the 7th of September, and have played about there with him - I have seen him with two or three sovereigns; we used to ask him how he got them, and he said he lived at a bonnet-shop in Regent-street three years - that his master never paid him regularly, and when he did he paid him as much as seven sovereigns.
 MRS. MANN re-examined. He came into our service from Mr. Plummer, a hosier, of Regent-street, but we took him on his mother's recommendation, and did not see Plummer. The watch was gold, and worth 20l.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 15.
 Before Mr.  Baron Garrow.
 2072.  JOSEPH MOGGERIDGE was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of James Ward , on the 14th of October , and stealing 2 jugs, value 1s. 6d.; 2 dishes, value 9d.; 4 plates, value 8d.; 2 saucers, value 2s., and 21 penny-pieces, his property.
 MARGARET WARD. I am nine years old, and live with my father, James Ward, in  Whitecross-street. On the 14th of October I was alone in the house, I shut the street door, left it on the latch, and went out into the back yard; I remained there about a quarter of an hour, then came back, and saw a little boy run out - I did not see whether he had any thing, now hear any noise; I had time to see him; I had never seen him before - I missed some Delphware from the cupboard, and some halfpence in a little bag, which was in a mug - I went and told my mother almost directly; she was in Whitecross-street, selling fruit: I then got a constable, and went to Moggeridge's father's house, which is next door but one to us - the constable knocked at the door; it was not opened - he got in at the window, and let me in; this was within ten minutes of the time, but nothing was found - the prisoner is not the boy who ran out; he was much less than him: he told the Magistrate the door was kept fast because his father expected an execution; I am quite sure he is not the boy, for I knew the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.
