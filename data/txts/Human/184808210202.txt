2025. GEORGE HUGHES , stealing 1 coat, value 3 s  ., the goods of William Charles Dewick . WILLIAM CHARLES DEWICK. I am a coachman of Blackhead). I was in the service of the proprietor of the Green Man. On 16th Aug. last year, I put my coat in a carriage in the coach-house about half-past one or two o'clock in the clay—I missed it about seven at night—I found it next morn ing at Mr. Sharp's, a pawnd-oker, at Deptford—I got it out,. and. I have it on now—it is mine—I know the prisoner by sight—I cannot swear that I had seen him that day.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Was there anything particular going on at Blackheath that day? A.  Yes, a foot-race—there were many people about, and many people came in the yard no doubt—I fetched the tailor to swear to my coat before I took it out, but I had no doubt of its being mine—he said he made it for me—there was an old silk handkerchief in the pocket, and the coat was tied up in that handkerchief, which I knew, and had had a long time. JOHN ACKLAND. I am a coachman. I was in the employ of the pro prietor of the Green Man—I recollect the foot-race Last Aug.—the prisoner came there about five o'clock—I heard Dewick speak about this business that day.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What makes you recollect that the prisoner came that day? A.  He was a fellow-servant of mine, and came to see me—I did not see him go away—I left him to go to see the race—I know he came down on the day of the race to fetch some little things he had left at the Green Man with me—I have seen him once since. JAMES SHARP. I am an assistant to Joseph Sharp, a pawnbroker at Deptford. On 16th Aug. last year, a coachman's blue coat, of this descrip tion, was pawned with me, I do not recollect by whom—the person had this ticket given him—I let Dewick have it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you give it up next day? A.  My employer did; I was present—I am quite sure it was the coat that was pawned the day before—I did not see the duplicate again till it was brought on 30th June this year—I had not the coat to produce then. RICHARD MINOR. I am in the service of Mr. Riches, of Clapham-com mon. I saw the prisoner at a public-house three or four months ago—he asked if I wanted a coat—I said I did not know that I did, what sort of coat was it—he said a blue frock coat, with velvet collar, and pockets at the sides—I said, "How much do you want for it?"—he said, "I don't want to sell it, it is in pawn at Deptford, but I will give you the duplicate, provided you will et it out"—I said, "How do you know it will fit me"—he said, "Because it fits me, and you are about the same stamp"  —I said, "Then you knew the man it belonged to?"—he said, "Yes, he was a groom, who left service, and took a cab , and is now gone to Australia "—he gave me this duplicate—I kept it till 30th June—I sent it to the pawnbroker by my nephew. JAMES MINOR. I took the duplicate to the pawnbroker. JOHN CARPENTER( policeman, R  84). I took the prisoner—I asked him if he pawned a coat at Mr. Sharp's—he said he had not—I asked him if he had had the ticket of a coat pawned at Mr. Sharp's—he said, "No"—I said, "Did you not gave a ticket of a coat to a man named Minor, living at Clap ham?"—he said, "Yes, I did "—he said he had the duplicate of a man he did not know at a public-house in the Kent-road for a pot of beer. NOT GUILTY.
