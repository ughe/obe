 303. THOMAS PENNY , embezzlement. RICHARD RANDALL. I live in Norton-street, Marylebone—the prisoner was in my employ—it was his duty to pay money to me as soon as he re ceived it—if he received 3 l . 2 s . 6 d ., or 2 l . 15 s ., he has not paid me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  Were there any books in which he was to enter what he received? A.  There was no regular book, nothing more than giving up the money—the book is not here—he was not to enter such sums as these—I know he never entered them—I keep a book in which the accounts are, but he never entered them—it is not a regular book—I have the bills that he took. THOMAS HARRIS. I am clerk to Mr. Saunders, in Wimpole-street—I saw my master pay the prisoner, on 3rd July, 3 l . 2 s . 6 d . for his master. JOHN BLACKSTOCK. I am clerk to Mr. Jones, of New-square—I paid the prisoner 2 l . 15 s . for his master—I produce the receipt.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY.  Aged 23.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury. — Confined Three Months .
