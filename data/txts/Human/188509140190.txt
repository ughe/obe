   Cross-examined by MR. PARKES . To the best of my belief Isetts was one of the men—I took in the newspapers, and looked carefully for my advertisement. FREDERICK CHURCH. I am manager to the Herman Loog Company, Limited, sewing machine manufacturers, of North End, Fulham—I received from a canvasser an order of 10th March—I afterwards went to 51, Sutherland Street—I believe Isett opened the door—I asked for Robertson—Isett said he was not in—I called the next day with Coulson—I was shown upstairs to Robertson or Mountford—I said "You have been introduced by Mr. Coulson, who says you require a sewing machine"—he said he required one—I asked him if he required it for private or business purposes—he said he required it for business purposes—he was a jobbing tailor, or words to that effect—I filled up this order, and read it over to him, and explained it—he paid 10 s . deposit on a seven guinea sewing machine—on 14th March I sent the sewing machine—I received the second 10 s . or first instalment of 10 s . after a little pressure—on 11th June I went with Morley to 111, Allen Street, Lambeth, and recognised the same machine by its number—on 6th April Isett brought me an order from Isett—on 18th April I went to 51, Sutherland Street again; I saw Mr. and Mrs. Isett—I told them I called in reference to their application for a perambulator—after inquiries they paid 7 s . 6 d . deposit—I read the promissory note over to them—Isett said he was a salesman in Covent Garden Market; he signed the note in my presence ( produced )—the value of the perambulator was five guineas—I sent it on the 19th April—I never received any instalment—I paid Coulson 7 s . 6 d . for the introduc tion of the order—in June I called at 51, Sutherland Street, but the people had gone—I recognised our perambulator at the police-court—about 7th April I got another order from a person named Cramer, of 47, Sutherland Street—on 7th or 8th I called, and could not see him—on the 10th I called, and saw Isett—I asked if Cramer was in; he said he was at No. 51—I asked Isett "Can I trust Mr. Cramer with a perambulator to the same amount as your own?"—he said "You can trust him to that amount," and that he was a respectable lodging-house keeper, and had lived there two or three years—during the conversation Cramer came to No. 51—I said I wished to see him about the order he had given to Coulson, and asked him to sign the promissory note produced—he paid me 7 s . 6 d .—I paid my canvasser salary and commission—on 15th the per ambulator was sent to No. 47—on 20th April Cramer came to the depot—he said he called in reference to introducing another gentleman; that he had an appointment at Walham Green, and would go and see if he was there—he left fur half an hour, and came back; the gentleman had not turned up—he came again in a day or two with Sidney—Sidney said he wanted a perambulator for his daughter's children, and chose a seven guinea one—he said he was a master mariner, of 40, Nelson Square, Blackfriars Road—I asked Cramer if I could trust him for the amount, and Cramer said "Yes"—I then drew up this promissory note ( produced )
