WALTER HALCOMB ROBERTSON re-examined.  I examined the deceased. In my opinion, his death was caused by the wounds in the back, produced by the shot—I do not attribute it to his having worked after being wounded—that might aggravate the mischief or not.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How deep did any of the shots go; did they go much below the skin? A.  They went beneath the trot skin—I suppose they were about one-third of an inch deep—the immediate cause of his death was a locked-jaw—whether that was brought on by his working, I cannot say—a lock-jaw is very often produced by such an injury as I saw—I examined his hands at the post-mortem examination, and all his body except the brain—I never knew pressure on the brain cause a lock-jaw—I cannot swear that it would not cause it.
 (The prisoner received an excellent character.) GUILTY.— Strongly recommended to mercy.
   COURT  to JOHN BASS . Q.  Now the verdict is given you may state to us what he said before the Magistrate? A.  He said the deceased was in the act of stealing coals, that he was some distance off, watching the property of the Company, and supposed the deceased heard his footsteps, as he ran away—he called to him three or four times to stop, but as he would Not, he fired his pistol at him. Fined 2 l., and Discharged.

JOHN RISLEY was indicted for stealing, on the   18th of November , 1 looking-glass and frame, value 4 l  ., the goods of John Hassell —2nd COUNT  , stating it to be the goods of Mary Ann Sewell . WILLIAM HUTCHINSON MOISTER. I live in Fulwood's-rents, Holbom. On the 18th of November I was employed to conduct a sale of furniture in the Old Kent-road—the prisoner was standing about there, and I delivered him a dining table to take to a place—he returned, and said he was to take a looking-glass to the same place—I said I could not attend to him—I went up stairs, and when I came down, he had taken the glass down, I asked him where the bill was—he said it was all right, that he had given it to me—he had given the bill of the table, but not of the glass—it was the property of Mr. Hassell—it was sent in there from him for another person, and he has since paid for it—the prisoner took it out, and asked me to lend him a board to put it on, and said he should return in a quarter of an hour, but he never came back at all—I did not see him again till last Monday week, as he came out of the Compter.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did you not take the glass down for me, and help me outside with it, and lend me a hammer? A.  No; you took it down your self with a screw-driver—I lent you a board, which I have not had back.
   Prisoner.  I took the glass to the Bricklayers' Arm—a lady gave me 18 d . for taking it, and I put it on an omnibus, and she gave me 6 d ., for doing so. GUILTY.*— Transported for Seven Years.
