Henry Clark. I am the husband of Sarah Clark.
Mr. Bopkin. Q. Do you remember any occasion on which the
deceased made an attempt on his life? 4. Yes--I was with him at the
time---it was between four and five years ago I have known him ten or
twelve years, and worked with bim-I was with him and his brother-in-
law at his mother's, at Lambeth-I forget his brother-in-law's name--he
lives at Dartford-my wife and Mrs. Bowler were there we were all
together-his brother-in-law came to the shop, and he went out to drink
with him--we went from there to his mother's at Lambeth--I saw him
outside the door, and he said, "I shall go and jump into the Thames"
he ran very hard from me, direct to the Thames-I ran very hard after
him, and stopped him about twenty feet from the water-I said, “ Joe,
what are you at?"-he said, "I shall go and jump into the Thames, and
drown myself"-I brought him away my wife was there, but not when
I stopped him--she was along with Mrs. Bowler--we were all five to-
gether in company outside when he started-we were, it might be, 400
yards from the Thames--I know of no attempt he made since that.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Had you been with him all the day? A. No only
three or four hours--nothing had happened to make him do this he was
very tipsy at the time-he did not get nearer the water than twenty feet
-I think he would have gone into the Thames that night if I had not
stopped him.
Court. Q. Was he melancholy, or dissatisfied about anything? 4.
No, only he was in liquor.
