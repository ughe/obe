   Q.  Did you crib  the draft of it from under his portfolio? A.  I did—he wrote it and kept a copy of it—that is the fact—it began as this letter does—Mr. Rundle is foreman of the vault, and I am casting clerk-when I cribbed  this letter, I showed it to Mr. Mitchel, a superior officer, but to no one else—I have not had it since—I do not know Edwards—I know Pearson—he is a common man—I had some conversation with him about the letter-everybody in the Docks knew it—I do not know how soon after an officer was desired to be in attendance to see if he could find any thing on a gentleman wearing grey breeches—( looking at a letter .)I saw this at the police-office—I cannot say whether Pearson wrote it—it is dis guised—I think some of the letters are very like his writing—it is a long time since I saw his writing.
   Q.  There is a secret bottom to this drawer or top to the cupboard, how long has it been there? A.  I cannot say—I have known it there two years, ever since I have been there—that may be a nice place to put a bottle in, to get a man into a scrape, that writes in the Town  —I do not know whether Pearson knew of that place, or whether the superior officer did—I never told them of it—I mentioned it at the Thames police, and to the constables—I do not know of any other secret place—I have not got one. NOT GUILTY.

JOHN M'CORMACK , WILLIAM SLOANE, and GEORGE SMITH, were indicted for stealing, on the   6th of July , 1 sheet, value 5 s  .; 6 spoons, value 12 s  .; 1 tea-pot, value 5 l  ., the goods of John Soy , in a vessel, called the   Royal Adelaide , on the navigable river Thames.-2nd COUNT  ,. stating them to be the goods of Mary Coatsworth .
   MESSRS. CLARKSON . and BALLANTINE . conducted the Prosecution . THOMAS LAWSON. I was a seaman on board the Royal Adelaide steam vessel. She was lying in the river, off St. Katharine's Docks—the there prisoners were employed as porters, in stowing the cargo—they were all three working in the hold with me—I was called up to get tea, at half-past five o'clock, and after that I sat down to smoke on the grating-Sloane came up with a sheet under his arm—I asked what he had got—he said some washed linen—I took it from him—it was a sheet marked "C. R."—I said there was something going on wrong—I went down into the hold, and saw M'Cormack with something, and asked what he had—he said he had nothing—I said he had something he ought not to have—he put it in his pocket—I took it out of his right-hand pocket, and it was half a dozen silver spoons—I called the mate and found a tea-pot lying by two cases, by M'Cormack's feet-Smith was on deck at that time, at his duty-Coyle and I examined the hold, and found a chest which had been broken—I did not observe any thing on the top of the chest—there appeared to have been things taken out—there was a space in the chest.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Had you given either of these men any thing to drink? A.  No-no rum, nor wine, nor any sugar to eat-M'Cormack did not tell me he was going to give these spoons to the mate—he put them in his pocket, before my face—I cannot say how many. persons were on board-no one was in the hold but M'Cormack.
