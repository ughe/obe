FRANCIS HALL (Detective Sergeant Y.) I was in charge of the prisoner on
August 2nd-when he was waiting at Highgate Police Station in the cell
passage, he said, " The Judge cannot look me up for stealing that watch
and chain, I would sooner go to prison; that watch and chain belongs to
my girl Jenny; she lives somewhere in Glasgow, I do not know the
address; I gave her the money which she bought them with, and earned
it on my last voyage.
The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he admitted doing everything
except stealing the watch and chain and shilling ; that he had been at High-
gate for a couple of days on the drink, that he was going back to London
when he saw Mr. Olive and Miss Curzon lying in the grass in a com-
promising position, that he had a small walking stick in his hand and
struck into the grass, but did not intend to hurt them, because he was half
drunk , that they jumped up and ran away; that he then saw a watch and
chain and handkerchief lying on the grass, which he pioked up, that he
went back to the path and saw another young lady and a man in the gra88
that he hit the man, and then went on to the road.
GUILTY. Ten years' penal servitude. Mr. MATHEWS stated that the
doctor's examination of Miss Curzon made it clear that what the prisoner
had said in reference to misconduct was dearly false.
