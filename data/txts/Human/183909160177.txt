   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How long have you been in your brother's service? A.  Twenty years, and the prisoner about five years—I missed both the springs on the 1st of August—I took the pri soner into custody on the Thursday following—I am quite sure I missed them both at the same time—the prisoner occupies some rooms over the coach-house, in the mews adjoining the premises—Prince's spring was kept in the smiths' shop, not in the dwelling-house—the prisoner rented the rooms of Mr. Lines, and paid for them out of his wages—these springs had not been sold to any body while I was in the shop—I was in the shop when I in quired about Prince's spring—the other men were about—the prisoner did not follow me about the shop—I asked him in particular what had become of Prince's spring, had he moved it—he was taken up before the search—the value of this spring is 35 l . or 2 l .—it is one of a set—I wrote upon it in chalk the name Prince—when I took it to the Magistrate I could still see my mark on it quite plain. CHARLES LINES, Jun. I live with my father. On the day the spring was lost, the prisoner said it was a d—d strange thing where the spring had got to, and he supposed Jackman had taken it, or the foreman must have lent it to one of the customers.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was this the only time you asked him? A.  It was the only time the prisoner was present—the smith's shop is not locked—I heard the prisoner make a statement before the Magistrate—I was not in the habit of going to and fro to the place he occupied. SIDNEY LARKIN. I work for Mr. Lines. I saw the prisoner at work clean ing an axletree—it was one belonging to Prince's carriage—I said it was very strange where Prince's spring was gone—he said the foreman had lent it to Shepherd, of Camberwell, or it was on one of the Camberwell busses.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Repeat, if you please, the words that were used? A. ( The witness did so )—This was not the same conversation at which Jospeh Lines was present—he did not say it was "most likely," I am quite sure of that—this is my writing to this deposition—I go into the coach-house to work, that is in the same place as the prisoner's room—I do not know the place as described by Joseph Lines as not being locked—the Prisoner said, "Perhaps the foreman had forgotten it"—he did not say "Most likely."
