On Wednesday, the 25th of July, about twelve o'clock at noon, the prisoner brought this horse to our repository - I entered it in the day book - I asked him what name; he said, "Linton, Church-street, Bethnal-green;" he booked it, "Quiet to ride, and quiet in harness, to be sold to the highest bidder" - it was sold to the highest bidder, at the Friday's sale for 1l. 10s. - it was small and very lean - it was delivered to the purchaser, who paid me for it; he is not here, but I understand he took it to Smithfield - it was knocked down to a Mr. Hedges, but afterwards transferred to Mr. Clansey, who paid me, and took it away.
   THOMAS BARKER. I shewed the prosecutor the same horse, and also shewed it to Seares.
   THOMAS SEARES. I saw it - it was the same horse; the prisoner gave his name as Thomas Brown - he called on Monday for the money, and I detained him.
   JOHN STEVENS. The horse they showed me was mine, and the one I lost from Chelmsford.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming from Islington, down the City-road - this man was coming down Old-street-road with the horse; he beckoned to me, and asked me to take the horse to Dixon's, which I did - and asked the boy if that was the sale day - he said No. I asked if I should take it home; he said I might leave it, which I did, to be sold on Friday. I went and told the man, and he asked if I should be that way on the Monday - I said, Yes; he told me to meet him, and he would pay me for my trouble - I went and met him, and then went to get the money - he said he would give me 5s.; when I got there, I told them the name I booked it in.
   THOMAS BARKER. I went to Church-street, Bethnalgreen; next day, and was about there some hours, but no such person as Linton could be found or heard of.
 GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 23. Recommended to mercy by the prosecutor, understanding him to bear a good character.
 1945.  JOSEPH MACHEE was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of September , 1 gold pin, value 5s., the goods of Charles Gibson.
 JAMES YOUNG. I am in the employ of Mr. Charles Gibson, a  jeweller, of Bishopsgate-street. On the afternoon of Tuesday, the 11th of September, the prisoner came into the shop, and asked to look at some gold pins; I showed him some, which did not please; he then asked for some brooches, and while I was reaching them I saw him take a pin from the card, (with the two fingers of his right hand,) and put it into his pocket - he was then about twenty minutes bargaining for another pin, for which I asked him 6s., and he offered me 3s. 6d. - he went out, without buying any thing - I followed and brought him back, before he got out of sight; I made him produce the pin, which was in his watch pocket - I could not understand half he said, being a foreigner; I said, "Produce that pin you took away;" "Here is that pin," said he - he appeared to understand me, though I could not understand him. I then sent for the officer. He did not appear at all intoxicated.
   THOMAS TUNNELL. I am an officer. Mr. Young sent for me, about a quarter after four o'clock, to take charge of the prisoner - he gave me this pin, and said he had stolen it; I took him to the Compter; I found 11s. in his possession, and in a bundle which he had, was a new pair of stockings, some butter, bacon, and pork - he offered me the 11s. if I would let him go - he spoke plain.
 Prisoner's Defence (through an Interpreter.) I was intoxicated - I know nothing about it.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. Confined Six Months.
 1946.  JOHN GRAHAM was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 1 bag, value 1d.; 2 tobacco-boxes, value 1s. 6d.; 1 snuff-box, value 6d.; 1 knife, value 2s.; 2 hooks, value 1s.; 1 key-ring, value 6d.; 4 keys, value 1s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 8s.; 2 half-crowns, 1 sixpence, 3 pence, and 3 halfpence, the property of Joseph Baker , from his person.
 JOSEPH BAKER. I am a  carman. On the 14th of August I was coming home from my club, to the Maze, in the Borough, where I lodge. I was going across Newgate-market, about a quarter before one o'clock; John Woods, one of my club-mates, had gone with me as far as there, and then left me; when he was gone I sat down on some boards, and fell asleep; the prisoner came and sat by me, and took my property; my jacket and side pockets were buttoned up- he awoke me, by untying my apron, and getting his hand into my pockets: he took out my purse and a bag, with my money in it. I had seen it all secure a quarter of an hour before. I was sober, but being tired I fell asleep.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Were you quite in your sober senses? A. Yes; I had only drank a little beer; this man came and sat down by me: I had dropped asleep, and was awoke by his fumbling about; he was about five minutes in doing it - I did not seize him, because it did not directly strike me that there was any thing wrong- it was not possible for me to drop these things from my pocket; I was not off my legs five minutes; the clock had struck one before I sat down.
   JOHN SPEECHLEY. I am an officer. The watchman brought the prisoner to me, and gave me this bag, containing a knife and some buttons - I found this tobacco-box on him.
   SAMUEL BOND. I am the watchman. At half-past one o'clock the prosecutor called Stop thief! I saw the prisoner running and two men after him; I stopped him, and he gave me this bag and knife.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did not the prisoner tell you that what he had, he had picked up? A. Yes; the prisoner pulled the bag out of his pocket, and gave it me, and then the prosecutor gave charge of him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined Three Months.
