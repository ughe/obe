JOHN PURSER. I was present when the pigs came from the cellar, and there were a number of tools found, which a poor man had lost.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not take them with an intent of stealing them - they came into my house, and I kept them in my back room; they came at half-past six o'clock in the evening, while I went to get a pail of water - I inquired if any body had lost them, and could not find any one who had; on the Sunday the prosecutor came, and I shewed him one - he said it was not his: he went to Davis', and I followed him - the officer, who was there, asked him if he had seen his pigs; he said No - I said I had another pig; he went to see it, and then identified them.

 2042.  ANN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of October , 3 half-sovereigns, 3 half-crowns, 5 shillings, and 4 sixpences, the monies of James Gravenor.
 JAMES GRAVENOR. I am a  weaver. On the 17th of October, between one and two o'clock in the day, I met the prisoner and another in Whitechapel - I was sober; they asked me to treat them with something to drink - we went to a wine-vaults at the corner of George-street, and had a quartern of gin; they then invited me to go home -I went home with the other girl, not the prisoner; we went up stairs into a room - I had then three half-sovereigns, three half-crowns, and some shillings, which I shifted into my coat pocket, and pulled my coat off; the prisoner was not there then, but in a few minutes I turned round, and saw the prisoner with my coat in her hand, going down stairs - I went to go after her, and the other girl who was in the room took hold of me, shut the door, and kept me till the prisoner got out; I then got down, and went into the street in my shirt-sleeves - I went about twenty yards; the prisoner called me, put my coat on the step of a door, and ran away laughing; I took the coat, and missed all the money but 6d.; I went to Lambeth-street, described her, and she was taken that day week - I am positive she is the girl; I have not got any of the money.
   THOMAS GOODING. I am an officer. Gravenor de-described the prisoner to me; I had seen her about Whitechapel before - I met her in Shoreditch, and took her to the office; I put her with several women, and the prosecutor pointed her out - I had looked for her where I had been accustomed to see her, and could not find her.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had only just come out of fourteen days' imprisonment, and had not been in Whitechapel at all.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 2043.  GEORGE PALMER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of October , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of George William Jennings , from his person.
 MR.  GEORGE WILLIAM JENNINGS. I am of no profession. I was going to the Haymarket-theatre on the 15th of October, with two friends - the constable came up to me, and asked if I had lost a handkerchief; I felt, and said I had: he said, "Is this yours?" I said, "It is certainly the colour of mine, but I cannot be certain of it."
   JESSE PHILLIPS. I am a parish-constable. On the 15th of October I saw the prisoner, whom I knew, and another person with him - they followed the prosecutor and two gentlemen who were with him for some distance; the prisoner took this yellow handkerchief from the prosecutor's pocket, and was putting it into his flap; he came round the coach-stand, and I took him; I went to the prosecutor, who said he had lost a handkerchief of this description.
   WILLIAM WHITTINGHAM. I am a commission-agent. I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief from the prosecutor; I took it off the ground.
 Prisoner's Defence. It is my own - I took it out of my pocket, and used it - the officer took me; they said they would not take me to Bow-street, or they should get blown up.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Life.
 2044.  WILLIAM WEST and GEORGE LILLEY were indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 1 pair of shoes, value 10s., the goods of John Creasey.
   JOHN CREASEY. I am a shoemaker, and live in Seymour-street. On the 10th of September I left my boy in the shop - while I was out these shoes were taken.
   FRANCIS KEYS. On the 10th of September I was with Rierdon - we watched the two prisoners for a few minutes - they stopped at a pawnbroker's; West left the other, and went into the shop - I followed him, and saw him with these shoes on the counter; I went to the door, and told Rierdon to go on - he went and took Lilley, who was then going away; I went and asked West where he got the shoes - he said a young man at the door gave them to him to pawn; I asked if he ever saw that young man before - he said No: I took him to the door, and said, "Is this the young man?" he said No; I said,"I saw you in conversation with him" - I took them to the office; I did not know who they belonged to, but I saw the name of E. Driver, Esq. on them, looked in the Court Guide, and found him.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. You saw the shoes in the shop? A. Yes - that was the first I saw of them; he denied that Lilley was the person who gave them to him.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I was outside, and took Lilley - I found nothing on him.
 West's Defence. There was nothing but "Driver" on the shoes; I told the officer the person was waiting at the corner of the street, and if he would allow me to go to the corner I should be able to clear myself - he said, "No, I have got you;" in going to the watch-house he said,"Why don't you, like a good lad, say he gave them to you?" I said, "I will not bring an innocent lad into trouble;" the person asked me where the nearest pawnbroker's was - I said I would pawn them; he said pawn them for 5s. in the name of  William Ring, Pancras-street.
 JURY to FRANCIS KEYS. Q. Did he ask you to go to Pancras-street? A. Yes, but I did not, because I had seen him with the other.
 WEST -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 LILLEY -    NOT GUILTY.
