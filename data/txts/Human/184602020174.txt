No—I think the discharge from his bowels took place the day after he came in—I had given him no medicine—the discharge indicated great debility, accompanied by the low fever which would undoubted ly he accompanied by irregular circulation—if that took a direction upward, delirium might come on at once. WILLIAM BAKER. I live in Red-cross-street. I am secretary to the Old Friends Benefit Society—the prisoner was a member, and is now—I have been paying him sick money for about six months, weekly, and visited him often—he has mentioned to me several times that he has been very bad in his head. JOHN OSBORNE. I live in Harper-street, New Kent-road. I have known the prisoner about ten years—he was ill about four years ago of a fever, some thing of a brain fever—he was rather delirious—I recollect Dr. Baldwin of the Dispensary ordering a blister on the back of his head—my wife fetched it. NOT GUILTY, being insane at the time.
