MR. HUDDLESTON. Q. Who was the first that spoke to the prisoner ?
A. Mr. Bone-he was speaking to me,I then called the prisoner in, and Mr.
Bone said to him he had lost some catalogues, and he mentioned the number
- I heard him say it would be better for him to confess.
GEORGE PALMER. I am a chandler, and live in Bedfordbury. The prisoner
has been to my shop during the last three or four months-he has brought
paper similar to this now produced—the paper I received from the prisoner i
gave up to Mr. Bone and the officer-1 cannot say how often I have pur-
chased paper of the prisoner-I paid him 2d. a pound for the greater part;
but he brought so much that I told him I was almost stocked, and for the latter
part of it I only gave him 1 d.—it did not occur to me that these were cata-
logues, and might be of value to the party-I considered them as waste paper,
which I purchase a great quantity of—I did not know the prisoner, but he told
me he was a waste-paper dealer-I had about Icwt. they were not perfect
catalogues, not the whole book-they were just as they are here apparently— :
I pointed out to him, on one occasion, that they seemed to have a great deal of
these catalogues-he said, “ We are regular waste-paper dealers; we bought
as much as 5 cwt. yesterday”—I bought some music-paper of him--the largest
quantity he brought at one time was from 17lbs. to 191bs.- from the begin-
ning of this year to the latter end of April he used to come sometimes once or
twice a week, sometimes once a fortnight.
