Cross-examined by MR. METCALF. Q.  You tested the half-sovereign! A.  Yes, on my finger, a rung it on the counter—I have been ten years in business, and never took a bad one before. FREDERICK MITCHELL. On 2nd Nov. I saw the prisoners at my sister's shop—I gave them four half-crowns for a bad half-sovereign. SOLOMON ISAAC LEE. I was at Mrs. Ashton's on 2nd Nov. A man came and gave Mrs. Ashton a half-sovereign—the moment he had paid that the prisoners threw down a sovereign for what they had taken—Mrs. Ashton gave them the half-sovereign she had taken of the man, and went to get the silver—I heard Mrs. Ashton ring the half-sovereign—it sounded like a good one. THOMAS GARFORTH( policeman, L  151.) I went to Mrs. Ashton's on 3rd Nov.—she gave me this half-sovereign—I have kept it ever since—she made this cross on it. JAMES RUSHALL. I keep the Rose and Crown in Torrington-street, Surrey. On 2nd Nov., about five o'clock, a tall man came in and asked for a glass of gin—before I could serve him the prisoners came in—the man put down a half-sovereign in their presence—Howard asked for a glass of shrub and a glass of porter—before I served her I took the half-sovereign of the man, and knowing they had been there before, I looked at it very particularly, and am able to swear it was a goof one—I put it down in the receas, expecting Howard was going to ask for change for a sovereign—the prisoners then asked for change for a sovereign—I took the half-sovereign, looked at it parti cularly again, and gave it them with the rest of the change—as soon as I had turned my back Howard said, "This half-sovereign feels very light; I will thank you to change it"—Page said, "Oh my dear! I think it is good; do not give the gentleman the trouble to change it"—Howard said, "I think it is light; I would rather have silver"—I took the half-sovereign, went to the station, got a policeman, and gave the prisoners in charge—Howard wanted to take the half-sovereign out of my hand—she said, "Let me look at it"—I said, "No, you have seen it before"—I had know the man who came in before, and I should know him again.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How did you test the half-sovereign? A.  I looked at it very particularly—I have taken many hundred pounds in half-sovereigns. JOHN DAVIES( policeman, L  46.) I was called to Mr. Rushall, on 2nd Nov., about five o'clock—I received from him this half-sovereign, and Howard attempted to take it out of his hand—she said, "Give me the half-sovereign; I will be at the loss of it rather than have any further brother about it."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you search the prisoners? A.  No—they were taken directly to the station, and female searched then—only 9 s . 6 d ., and 1 1/2 d . were found on Howard—they had to go a quarter of a mile from Mr. Rushall's to the station. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. These half-sovereigns are both counterfeit, and cast in the same mould.
   HOWARD  — GUILTY . Aged 22.
