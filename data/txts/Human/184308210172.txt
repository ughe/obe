 2445. FRANCIS SEYMOUR was again indicted for stealing, on the   15th of June , 4 sauce-ladles, value 2 l  . 12 s  ., the goods of George Cowie , his master; and ELIZA SEYMOUR for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. JOHN POOLE. I am shopman to Mr. Bassett, of Great Queen-street. On the 15th of June, the prisoner Eliza pawned a pair of silver sauce-ladles for 15 s ., and said they were her husband's. RICHARD SAYER. I am in the service of James Sayer, a pawnbroker, in Drury-lane. On the 20th of July Eliza Seymour brought this pair of sauce ladles to our house.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  These are a common pattern, are they not? A.  I suppose so. ALFRED HARRISON. I am in the service of Mr. George Cowie. I do not know these ladles—I do not know whether my master had such. THOMAS HARRISON. I was in Mr. Cowie's employ. I have examined these ladles—they are part of his stock—the prisoner Francis had access to where these were.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Has Mr. Cowie any partner? A.  No—these are a common pattern—I have left Mr. Cowie six years ago—I know these ladles were in his stock ten years ago. NOT GUILTY.
