2530. STEPHEN ROBINSON was indicted for a misdemeanor.
Thomas HALFHIDE. I live in Thames-street; the prisoner is my
nephew; he has professed of late to deal in horses. On the 31st of May be
came to me about some horses, and I gave him some money to buy a horse
for my son-in-law-he came on the 4th of June, and said he had bought
a horse, and he had left 31. 10s. as deposit for it, that the difference was
31. 108., and he wanted 31. 108. more to make up the price for it-in con-
sequence of that I let him have three sovereigns and one half-sovereign-
he gave it to a person whom he brought with him to receive the difference,
because the proprietor would not let the horse go without it.
Prisoner. He lent me the money; I was to pay it back, and pay him
interest. Witness. No.
Henry Sumner. I am the prosecutor's son-in-law. The horse was to
be bought for me, but it never came to me, I went to the prisoner's house,
and saw him, after waiting half an hour, at the Ship public-house--be came
to me there-I told him I came for the horse--he said the horse did not
turn out to his expectation, and he had given him a ball and turned him
out to grass ; if I would meet him at seven o'clock the next morning, at
Whitechapel, he would bring me a horse-I went five minutes before
seven, and waited till half-past eight-I never saw him, nor the horse-he
had promised to meet me to go to Romford to buy a horse.
WILLIAM Nicol (police-constable K 177.) I took the prisoner- I told
him it was for getting 31. 10s. of Mr. Halfhide under false pretences -- he
said he gave it him to do as he liked with.
THOMAS HALFHIDE re-examined. When he came to me he brought
person with him, and said he had paid a deposit for a horse which came to
77., and he wanted 31. 108. more before he could have it.
Prisoner. I was to pay him interest; I could not make it up so soon
as I could wish for.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
