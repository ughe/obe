money of Henry Barnett
MARY BARNEY. I live at 39, Enor Road, Plaistow-on the morning!
of February 9th the prisoner knocked at my door and asked if I would
have a pane of glass put in--I had a broken window-I asked him what
the price would be ha said Is. 8d. in broken English, and then ls. 6d.,
and then 1s. 4d.-I had £2 10s. in a box and a watch and chain, and
took them into the noxt room and laid them on the bed - I left the
prisoner for ten minutes --he oame down and I asked him for change
he said that he had no change and went and got some-I went upstairs
to shift the box into the bedroom again, and the money was gone I saw
the prisoner at the Police-court on the 17th, with ten or twelve others,
and picked him out at once-I left the watch in a little flannel bag in a
box on the bed.
AR
AMELIA BARNETT. I am the daughter of the last witness-on Feb-
ruary 9th the prisoner came to mend the window—he is the man. I
picked him out from a lot of men shabbily dressed I went to & shop and
got change, and paid him for the work—my mother gave the money into
my hand, and I put it into a little box, and when I came back, two or
three minutes after the prisoner left, it was not there.
Cross-examined. I picked you out at once.
JOHN WHITTAKER (21 K). After the charge was taken at the station,
the prisoner was placed with other men and told to place himself in any
position he liked-he did so, and the last witness and her mother picked
him out-they were all working men out of the street,
Prisoner's Defence. I do not know whether I mended the witness's win.
dow or not, because I mend ten or twelve in a day-I am innocent-I
have stolen nothing.
