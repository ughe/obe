WILLIAM STEPHENS. I am office lad to Mrs. Spencer-I remember a
cheque for £23 58. coming back from the bank, and I sent it, with a
message, by post-I tried to get it back-I saw the prisoner, and said I
should get the sack if I did not get the cheque-he said that he would
come and see Mr. Spencer-he did not come to the office-Mr. Spencer
was very ill-a long time afterwards the prisoner came and had a dis-
cussion, and I helped them, and the prisoner wrote out the bills I did
not hear any reference made to the cheque that I was sent about the
prisoner made his statement, which was rectified at the interview,
By the Court. Before these bills were given nothing was said about
the cheque for £23 odd - I did not see it again I do not remember when
the last time was that I went for it.
Evidence in reply.
CHARLES BOYLE. I produce the file of proceedings in the Bankruptey
Court-the prisoner has never applied for his discharge-the adjudica
tion was in November, 1896.
Cross-examined. The account shows a deficiency of £132 2s. 7d.
FREDERICK Dixon. I am manager to the Trustee in bankruptcy--the
prisoner is the person referred to in the proceedings it realised
£29 odd.
MARTHA ANN SPENCER. I am the owner of this business of Spencer
and Co.--I had no dealings with the prisoner; he never told me that he
was an undischarged bankrupt.
The Prisoner, in his defence, on oath Bated that he had never applied
for his discharge in bankruptcy; that his business was carried on by his
wife, who had a separate estate ; that he had known Mr. Spencer seven years,
and told him that as he was undischarged, any business between them must
be paid for every week, and that he signed the two bills himself, having his
wife's authoriiy to do so, that Mr. Spencer said, " Well, we have been
friends for some years, and you are in the same position as myself, you
cannot claim your own name "; that his real name was Charles Johnson
Mathew Mayhoe, and that I. M. Mayhoe was his wife, but that he signed
the bills in question, and that though they were not paid, Spencer had the
money afterwards, and the cheque was honoured at the bank ; that he
liquidated the dishonoured cheque for £23 58., by paying cash to Spencer
in March, but had not brought a single book into Court to show his pay
ments, and that it was Spencer who said to him, " You are an undischarged
bankrupt."
Guilty.-- Judgment Respited,
