   Q.  Are any of those persons here who were present when you had this conversation with the prisoner? A.  No; there was no one present when he said he took it out of a lark , but there were two or three present when he said I might be d—and cook it in my pea—soup—Mrs. Munyard was one, the wife of Munyard, whom I have mentioned, and I believe his daughter—there may have been the two daughters, but I will not swear positively—I have not seen either of them here—I saw Mr. Munyard yes terday I believe, he was drinking with the prisoner's father.
   MR. JONES . Q.  What is Mr. Munyard? A.  He seems to deal is horses—he keeps vans and flys, and lives in Spring-gardens, Greenwich—he has only one arm—he has been very intimate with the prisoner for years—Mr. Jones, senior, has bailed him, and he has bailed Mr. Janes—Mr. Munyard has told me so—he has spoken to me several times about this charge—Mr. Jones, the solicitor, has not been saying any thing parti cular to me about it—I have never had any quarrel or unpleasantness with the prisoner—we were always the best of friends, and are to this moment. JAMES WILD( police-constable R  141.) I apprehended the prisoner on Tuesday, the 1st of May, at Charlton, in Kent—he was in a horse and chaise, with another man—I told him I had a warrant against him, on suspicion of stealing a snuff-box, and read the warrant to him—he said "You have done all you can about that box, you can make nothing of it—I attended as a witness before the Grand Jury, when the bill was ignored—the box was not then produced before the Grand Jury—I have found it since.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know the prosecutor of this indictment? A.  I did not know him before this time—I have seen him a few times—I do not know who has employed counsel to prosecute this man—I under stood there was one employed—I believe the person who drew out the brief employed counsel—that was Mr. Blakely—Mr. Clark told Mr. Jonas to let him have the depositions—Mr. Clark asked me if anybody was employed, and I said, "No"—I do not know who employed Mr. Jones—I did not em ploy him—I mean to swear that—Mr. Blakely did—I do not know who he is—I do not see him in Court—I mean I do not know what he is—I have seen him many times about here since I have been attending this Court, which is eight years—I have not been speaking to the prosecutor in this case—I never told him who employed counsel—I kept myself as far from him as I could.
