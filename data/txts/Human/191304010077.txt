 (Thursday, April 10.) CHARLES HENRY HENNY, hall-keeper, Strand Theatre, said that he received the two letters addressed to Miss Dane and forwarded them to her private address.
 Mr. Bryan submitted that there was no case to go to the jury. There was no direct evidence of the sending of the letters; the only evidence as to handwriting was that of a police officer. In R. v. Harvey (11 Cox) a police officer was held to be not qualified as an expert.
 Mr. Grain cited R. v. Smith (3 Cr. App. R., 87) where the Appeal Court Judges took the responsibility upon themselves of looking at the documents and forming their own judgment.
 Mr. Justice Lush held that under the terms of Denman's Act he was entitled, without the assistance of any expert witness, to look at a document, and, being satisfied that the document was genuine, he could compare it with the handwriting of the document in dispute. Here, however, there were marked differences in the handwriting of the documents, and, as there was no other evidence against the prisoner, it would not be right for the jury to convict upon such evidence. The jury, under the direction of his Lordship, returned a verdict of Not guilty.
