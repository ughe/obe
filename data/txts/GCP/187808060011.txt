The Prisoner in his defence stated that he was a County Magistrate, and
would not make any statement which he did not believe to be true. He com-
plained of the absence of his solicitor with some most material papers, and
that he had not subpoenaed Mr. Pigeon, and contended that by the evidence he
was justified in his conduct, believing that Messrs. Randall and Angier were
cognisant of all that Mr. Crawley had done, and were therefore his accom-
plices; that what he had written under the belief that he had been grievously
wronged was not a libel; brut, having been told that Randall and Angier were
innocent, he would withdraw the accusation against them, which he believed to
be true when he made it. He further stated that he had been charged with
lunacy, being a spiritualist and planchette writer.
