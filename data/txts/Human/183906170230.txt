   Q.  Had you given an account of the transaction before that to any body? A.  My father's solicitor came to me, and desired me to tell him all the facts that were in my knowledge—my brother was present at the time—it was at our apartment in the Haymarket—we had taken apartments there to be there—we certainly had reasons for doing so—we left our home by the advice of our solicitor, and went to the Haymarket, in order to be out of the way—I made the statement to the solicitor before I went before the Magistrate—I do not know whether it was for the purpose of being handed to the solicitor for the prosecution—I was not told that at all—he told me he had hopes per haps of my father being admitted as a witness, and then I made a statement which was taken down—I suppose that was for the purpose of seeing whe ther my father could be made as a witness—I believe it was—my brother and John Dean were present when I made my statement—we had all kept out of the way together—I and my brother have lived in Coventry-street since we went before the Magistrate, and have carried on business there.
   Q.  Did you receive, about a week ago, a copy of your deposition? A.  I have had my deposition—I had it about a fortnight or three weeks ago—my father's solicitor gave it to me—I really do not know who was present—I have no recollection—I have had it ever since—perhaps I have looked at it—I do not know, I am sure.
   COURT . Q.  Do you know whether you have looked at it or not? A.  I think I did once or——
   MR. SERGEANT BOMPAS . Q.  Finish your sentence. A.  Once—I am not sure whether I looked at it once or twice—it was the statement that I made to Mr. Abrahams—it is just the same as my deposition before the Magis trate—just the same as I have got—it is a copy of the deposition, but it is exactly word for word the same as the statement—I am not sure that I looked at it more than once—I am positive that I looked at it once—that was the day he gave it me—I am not positive sure that I have looked at it from that time to this—I might have looked at it once after that—I have not looked at it to-day—I have not looked at it within this week—I do not know whether it is a fortnight or three weeks ago—I am sure I have not looked at it this week—I have not got it in my pocket now, nor have I left it over at the New Inn—I have not looked at it to notice whether the date of my examination was put there.
   Q.  Did Mr. Abrahams tell you why he gave it to you? A.  He told me to keep in my recollection what I had stated before the Magistrate—my father did not tell me where Moses lived when I went there—I knew the house myself—I do not know whether it was always ten or eleven o'clock at night when I went there—it was generally at night that I went, but I do not know what time exactly—I spoke to Moses at the bar—the house was generally empty when I was there—he never spoke to me while persons were there—he never addressed me while any one was there—it seems he knew me—I knew him by seeing him about—the house was generally empty when I was there—now and then there may have been a person there—it is right opposite Drury Lane theatre—I saw his wile at the house, but that is all.
