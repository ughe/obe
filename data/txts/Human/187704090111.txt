   By THE COURT . The object of getting the assignee was to buy up the bankruptcy—the debt was 1,350 l .—I expect the 900 l . was part of the 1,000 l .—I ultimately gave Street back the 850 l . which I had left of the 900 l .—Benson instructed me that Street would become the assignee—and I paid' Street my cheque for 850 l . the balance I had, and he handed me a cheque for 1,050 l .—I assumed that Street was acting as one of the friends of Benson at that time—in fact it was the bankrupt buying up that particular debt—he or his friends were going to buy up the whole of his debts—Street had been sent to me by Benson at the first meeting to make proof of a debt due by him to Street—I don't know who Street was, except that he gave me his name—I can give you his address from the draft assignment—I had previously sent the 50 l . to Kurr at Brighton—I did not make out any bill of costs—I have been paid by Benson; that 50 l . I asked for costs—I was paid fifty guineas in a lump sum—I paid 1,050 l . for the purchase money—the 50 l . was for my costs and I was afterwards paid two guineas for my disbursements by Benson—I really can't say that the money came out of Benson's pocket—the 50 l . I dare say did—but I really have no knowledge of it—I suppose the fact is that it did; but I have no knowledge about it—I expect that Street and William Kurr were nominees of Benson.
