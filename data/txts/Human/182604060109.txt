 856.  JOHN ERBY was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of March , 8 bushels of oats, chaff, and beans, mixed, value 30s., the goods of William Waterhouse and Joseph Waterhouse , his masters.
 JOSEPH WATERHOUSE. I am in partnership with my father, William Waterhouse - we  keep the Swan with Two Necks, Lad-lane. The prisoner was driver of one of our corn vans - he was sent on the 8th of March with this property. I have a list of where he was to leave it.
   JAMES CONSTABLE. I was employed by Messrs. Waterhouse, at Brook-street, near Brentford. On the 8th of March the prisoner left me four sacks of mixed corn - he should have left me five; I told him there was one short; he said his master had no more corn in the yard, and he would bring six on the Wednesday afterwards, but on that day he brought but four; I told him I should send his master word of it - he said I might send and be d-d; he appeared to be drunk on the 15th, but on the 8th he said it was all right enough about the corn.
   JOHN FIELDING. I am horse-keeper to Messrs. Waterhouse, at Brentford. The prisoner should have brought me four sacks on the 8th of March, and he brought but three.
   JOHN PROSSER. I am landlord of the Harry public-house, Mile-end. The prisoner called at my house several times.
   SAMUEL JOHNSON. I am granary-man in the service of Messrs. Waterhouse. I gave the prisoner twenty-seven sacks on the 8th of March - he was to take eighteen to Roam's stable, near Romford, five to Brook-street, and four to Brentwood.
   WILLIAM MORGAN. I am an officer. I took up the prisoner, and as he was going along Holborn he said he was afraid he should be sent to Newgate, for William Middleton had enticed him to do it, and he had but 3s. out of the 12s., and he was a great fool for doing it - that he had sold the two sacks for 6s. a sack.
 Prisoner. I was drawn into it by the horse-keeper at the Harry; he asked me a great many times before I would do it, and said he would let me in at the back gate.
 GUILTY. Aged 33. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor, having had a good character. Confined Three Months.
 857.  WILLIAM SEARLE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of January , 1 bed, value 25s.; 2 pillows, value 3s.; 1 pillow-case, value 1s., and 1 sheet, value 2s., the goods of Mary Poulton , widow , in a lodging-room.
 MARY POULTON. I am a widow, and live in  Union-buildings, Leather-lane. The prisoner came at the latter end of October, to lodge with me, and left on the 21st of January, without notice - I went into the room, and found a note, with some duplicates of the property stated in the indictment.
   FRANCIS RAMSAY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a feather bed and blanket, pawned by a female.
   MICHAEL OSBOURNE. I am a pawnbroker. I have some articles pawned by a female.
   WILLIAM JORDAN. I apprehended the prisoner about a month ago. I asked if he knew a person of the name of Poulton - he said, "I know what it is about - it is about the bed and other things - it was poverty made me do it, but I have now got a service, and shall soon be able to redeem them" - he had hardly a thing to cover him.
 GUILTY. Aged 22. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 858.  GEORGE PAYNE was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of March , 5 handkerchiefs, value 20s., and 1 shawl, value 20s., the goods of Charles Cleverly and James Hutcheson , his masters.
 CHARLES CLEVERLY. I am in partnership with James Hutcheson - we live in Chiswell-street; the prisoner was in our service.
   WILLIAM HAYES. I am a pawnbroker. These handkerchiefs were brought to me by the prisoner, on the 11th of March - he said he bought them at Gravesend of Mr. Hurley - I detained him - he made great resistance.
   THOMAS GARDNER. I am a constable, and took him in charge. I found the shawl in his coat pocket.(Property produced and sworn to).
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 22. Strongly recommended to Mercy. - Confined Eight Days.
 859.  JOHN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , 99 yards of Canvass, value 3l. 10s., the goods of Benjamin Gawthorne.
   HENRY CLAYTON. I am an apprentice to Benjamin Gawthorne, who lives in Great Portman-street. About a quarter before eight o'clock, last Friday night, I was told a person was walking up and down, and I supposed they were after the canvass - I stopped, and watched the prisoner, who was at the shop, and saw him take this canvass, and walk off with it - I pursued him - a person met him and turned him round - I then took him.
   HENRY STOWELL. I took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. It was laying against a baker's shop - the witness came out - I asked if the property belonged to her - "Yes," says he - "Then take it," says I.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined Two Months.
