2231. HENRY BENNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of
October, 1 bed, value 178.; 1 bolster, value 4s. ; 6 sheets, value 48:31
blankets, value 38. ; 1 quilt, value 1s. ; 2 rugs, value 18. 6d.; I chest
value 2s. 6d.; 6 chairs, value 128.; 1 table, value 58.; 1 tea-board, value
60. ; 1 clock, value 38.; 1 fender, value 1s. ; 1 set of fire irons, value
1s. 60.; 1 tub, value Is. 6d.; and 4 salt cellars, value 18., the goods of
Jonathan Nash.
JONATHAN Nash. I live at Hillingdon, and am a farmer--the prisoner
rented a house of me-I distrained on him for rent for 61. 28. 6d., and the
broker made terms with him.
WILLIAM STRANSON. I am the broker-I distrained on the prisoner
for 61. 2s. 6d.-he offered to sell Mr. Nash the goods for 41. 108., and
agreed to rent them of him-Mr. Nash allowed him to continue in the
house, and he was to pay 6d. a week for the use of the goods-I saw the
paper signed by Mr. Nash, to agree to let the prisoner have the use of the
goods after I had taken possession of them.
SAMUEL GRANGER. I live at Uxbridge-I was employed by the
broker, and made the seizure-the things were all there, and all right
then-I went to the place on the 12th of October, the prisoner and all the
things were then gone-I do not know who took them.
FRANCIS WEEDON. I am a constable of Hillingdon-I apprehended
the prisoner at Hackney-I charged him with taking Mr. Nash's goods
away-he said he had made a bad job of it, and was sorry for it--that he
sold the bed and the table to get bread for himself and family.
NOT GUILTY.
