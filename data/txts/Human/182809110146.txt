JAMES HUNT. I am housekeeper to Amelia Calder Stewart, of No. 13. Dorset-street. I heard the cry of Stop thief! and looked out at the window - I saw the witness and the two prisoners come up to the house - I saw the ladder in the area, and missed a leg and a piece of mutton from the area.
   ALFRED CRITCHER. I am a coachman - I live exactly opposite the mews. I heard a cry of Stop thief! While I was washing a carriage, and saw the two prisoners coming along together - Rickwood was a little first, and Hopkins near him.
   THOMAS CHINA. I am a labourer. I heard a cry of Stop thief! - I stopped Rickwood, but did not see him have anything; he said "Let me go, it is not me that has done it, it is the other chap."
 HOPKINS' Defence. They did not see me take anything - I ran after the other, and when there was a cry of Stop thief! I stopped to be taken.
 RICKWOOD'S Defence. I heard the cry of Stop thief! and ran to see what was the matter.
 RICKWOOD -    GUILTY. Aged 18.
 HOPKINS -    GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Fourteen Days.

 1820.  HENRY CLAYTON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of August , 1 watch, value 4l., 1 seal, value 6d., and 1 key, value 1d., the good of William Gray , from his person.
 WILLIAM GRAY. I am a  ship-carpenter. On the 1st of August I was standing at the end of Field-alley, Shadwell-market, speaking to a ship-mate; I found the prisoner before me, who said some words which were unintelligible - he then made a grasp and took my watch; I seized him, but he got away - we pursued, and about fifteen yards before we came up with him, I heard something fall on the ground; we did not stop, but pursued and took him - we brought him back to where I heard something fall, and it was my watch.
   JOHN SKUES. I am a watchman. On the morning of the 1st of August I took the prisoner from Gray; he took me to the place, and there was this watch, I saw it picked up.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming up Cox's-lane, and heard a cry of Stop thief! this person caught hold of me.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1821.  THOMAS JOY was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of August , 1 pocket-book, value 6d., 3 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the property of Ann Davis , from her person.
 ANN DAVIS. I am a  widow. On the 26th of August, I came to a public-house in Hornsey-road with the prisoner and another person; we drank together at one table, and in the course of an hour I came away - the prisoner followed me out, and said he was coming my way; we walked a little way together, and he thought to take liberties in an improper way with me - I refused two or three times; he then threw me down on the ground, and took my pocket-book out of my pocket, with 3s. 6d. - there was a washing-bill in the pocket-book, and a bad shilling; he thrust his hand into my pocket, took them and got away - I pursued him to the public-house again, and he was sitting looking at some silver in his hand; I asked him for the money and pocket-book, he denied it several times, and out he rushed in a moment - I charged the watchman with him.
   JAMES DENNIS. On the 26th of August I was on duty in the Hornsey-road. I was called, and saw the watchman search the prisoner - he found 4 1/2d. in one of his pockets; I followed them to the watch-house, and saw the prisoner put his left hand behind him, take out this pocket-book, and throw into the road - I took it up, and asked the prisoner what he threw it there for - he said"He knew he threw it away; and not to ask him that foolish question;" I asked the prosecutrix if that was her's, and she said it was, and one of the strings she had sewn on it - I then asked the prisoner what he had done with the money; he said 1s. 6d. he had spent in the public-house - he did not appear tipsy.
   FREDERICK SMITH. I saw the prisoner throw something away, James Dennis picked it up, and the prosecutrix owned it: they were both sober.(Pocket-book produced sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1822.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , 1 watch, value 6l.; 1 seal, value 18s.; 1 key, value 8s.; 1 ring, value 5s.; 1 slide, value 3s.; and 1 ribbon, value 6d., the goods of John Dargie , from his person.
 JOHN DARGIE. I am a  carpenter of the ship St. George; she was in the West India-dock. On the 27th of August I was in a street near Glass-house-fields; I was not drunk, but I fell down - a man came and took hold of my arm, with a pretence to assist me; I told him I did not want any help - we walked about three paces, and he took his right hand from my left side, whipped the watch out of my pocket, and ran away immediately; I ran after him a little way, but soon lost sight of him, and soon after I saw the prisoner in custody - he is the man that was detained, and he is the size of the man that took my watch - I could swear to the colour of his coat; I cannot tell how long it was before I saw him in custody.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Were you not quite drunk? A. I was as sensible as I am now; I felt the watch go out of my pocket - it was half-past ten o'clock: I had been at a public-house at Limehouse - I went there about four o'clock, but I did not stay there all the time; I was not exactly drinking from four till ten - I was not in any house of ill-fame, as far as I know; I was only in two houses - I cannot say what I drank or what I paid.
     NOT GUILTY.
