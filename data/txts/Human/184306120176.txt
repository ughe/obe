ELIZABETH CURZON. I am in the service of Mr. Lough. On the 17th of May I fastened up the house—I went to the attic that evening a few minutes before twelve o'clock—there was a lock on that door, and I had seen it safe some time before—I found the door open, and the lock taken off—it was safe at ten o'clock that morning, which was the last time I had been in the room—the window was open—the key was in the door, as I had left it, but the lock was off—I had seen the prisoner about the premises about a month before that.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was the prisoner remanded, that a shoemaker might speak to him as a man who had been seen about the premises? A.  No, I recollect nothing of that kind. JOHN THOMAS CRESWELL. I am shopman to Mr. Wood, a pawnbroker, in St. John-street, Clerkenwell. On the 19th of May, about half-past eleven o'clock, the prisoner came and produced these razors to pledge—I had re ceived information, and asked him if they were his own—he said, "Yet," and asked me to advance 5 s . on them—I detained him under frivolous pretences, and said I would send to ascertain the value of them, while I sent for a po liceman—when the policeman came, I asked the prisoner again if they were his own—he said no, they were given to him by a man with whom he had been playing at skittles at the Sun Dials—he went away with the policeman.
   Cross-examined. Q.  He did not offer to go away? A.  No.
   MR. RYLAND . Q.  Would you have let him go if he had tried? A.  I think not—there were two young men in the shop. JOSEPH PRIOR( police-constable G  65.) I was called to the pawnbroker's—I found the prisoner there—Mr. Creswell said he had brought these razors to pledge—I asked where he got them—he said he had been playing at skittles with a man at the Sun Dials, and he had given them him to get some money on them—I took him to the station, and there he told the same story—he re peated it before the Magistrate, and the Magistrate said I had better go there—I proposed to the prisoner to go, but he would not—I went to the publican to make inquiries, and he had not seen him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you quite certain that he did not say he would not go handcuffed, and he would not go without a cab ? A.  He said he would not go unless he was carried—I was in the office when a shoemaker was sent for, and the prisoner was put with some other persons—the shoe maker pointed out another man, who he said was very like the man he had seen on the premises—there is a public-house in Sutton-street—I do not know that it is the Sun—I know the Sun Dials—it is about 200 yards from the pawnbroker's, and about the same distance from the public-house in Sutton-street.
   MR. RYLAND . Q.  What house did the prisoner say he had been at? A.  The Sun Dials—he repeated it two or three times—he said he had just left it with these razors to get some money on them, for the man he had been play ing at skittles with.
