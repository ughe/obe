 1392  WILLIAM ELLIS, was indicted for embezzlement.
   THOMAS HORNBLOW. I am a trunkmaker, and live in the City-road- the prisoner was in my employ for fourteen years; he has received monies for me - something has been entered in the book, but it is now dashed out.
   JOHN HORNBLOW. I am son of the prosecutor, on the 6th of August I had been out, and when I came home I went to the book, and saw a bonnet-box had been sold - I went to the prisoner, and asked him for the money, he said "Let's halve it" - I said "Very well," and he gave me 3s. out of his pocket.
   CHARLOTTE SANDELL. I am a bookseller. I was at my door near the prosecutor's; I saw a box sold to two ladies in a carriage - I don't know what was paid for it, but I saw some money.
   JOHN BROWN. I am a constable. I took the prisoner; he said, as he was going to the watch-house, that he had given the boy half of it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1393.  ELIZA JONES and MARTHA BENNETT were indicted for stealing on the 15th of June , 9 yards of linen, value 13s.; 3 pair of shoes, value 12s.; 1 comb, value 6d.; 5 skeins of twist, value 10d.; and 2 yards of galloon, value 2d., the goods of William Newman.
   MARY NEWMAN. I am the wife of William Newman- he is a shoemaker, and lives at Uxbridge- Jones had lived servant with us, but had left us - I lost this property at different times; I could not tell when, but I was continually losing property while she was with me - the gentleman to whose service she went from me was not satisfied, he came to me and gave me some information - Bennett washed for me about three months ago; this property was found at Bennett's; Jones was afraid to take it home.
   JOHN BIRCH. I am a constable of Uxbridge. I was employed to look after this property; I went to Jones, who gave me an apron, which she said she had taken from Mr. Newman, and said that was all she had in the house; but Mrs. Newman had been to Jones before me.
 MARY NEWMAN re-examined. Q. Did you make Jones any promise? A. Yes; I told her, if she would tell me where she had taken my things I would forgive her, but she would not; I then took the officer with me - we found part of my property at Bennett's.
   JOHN BIRCH re-examined. Q. What else did Jones say? A. She said she had taken a cut of Irish to make some aprons, and then another cut of Irish, to make three aprons, and three pairs of shoes - I found this piece of Irish at Bennett's and this pair of shoes - Bennett said this was one pair the girl gave her, that one pair she had worn out, and one pair she had lost; and this comb was also found.
   MARY NEWMAN. The Irish has been washed, but I believe it to be mine - I can swear to this comb by a mark on it.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. I believe Jones' friends are respectable? A. Yes, truly so; and I believe this is her first crime.
 JONES - GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Three Months. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor.
 BENNETT -    NOT GUILTY.
 1394.  JOHN LANGLEY CROWLEY was indicted for, feloniously altering a receipt for money, with intent to defraud William Frederick Jones and another. against the statute, &c.
 2nd COUNT, for uttering the said altered receipt.
 2 OTHER COUNTS, for forging a receipt, and uttering the same.
   JOSEPH WILLIAM PERRY NEWSOM. I am clerk to Mr. William Frederick Jones and another; they are wharfinger s, at St. George's steam wharf - the prisoner was porter there; on the 28th of August I sent him out with two parcels, one was to Mr. Capron, and one for Mr. Wilson; I gave him this book, which is signed by the persons who receive the parcels and pay the money; I gave him two receipts for 1s. 6d. each, which was the charge for wharfage - these receipts in the book purport to be for 2s. 6d., he never paid them to me.
   SAMUEL BLINKS. I live with George Capron, Esq. solicitor, in Saville-place- on the 28th of August the prisoner brought a parcel, I paid him 2s. 6d. for it; I signed the book; this is the receipt the prisoner gave me.
   WILLIAM FREDERICK JONES. The money was never paid to me.
   JOSEPH CLEMENTS. I took the prisoner.
 GEORGE GARNER. The prisoner lodged with me - I sent the book to the wharf when the prisoner was apprehended.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Transported for Seven Years.
