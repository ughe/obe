WILLIAM PALMER. I paid the prisoner half-a-crown on or about the 20th of Sept. for his master. I employed the prisoner every Sunday for months to take me to church or chapel—he always charged half-a-crown.
   Prisoner. Q.  You never paid me but once, and that was 1 s . 6 d . Witness.  Yes, I paid a great many more times—when my wife had not money I paid—it was half-a-crown. JOHN NEAL( police-constable P  30.) I took the prisoner—he told his master he was very sorry for what he had done, and he would make it up to him if he would allow him time. GUILTY.— Confined One Year .
 2197. WILLIAM MARLER was indicted for stealing 5lbs. 2oz. of bristles, value 10 s  .; 30 brushes, 10 s  .; 2 wool rugs, 10 s  .; 1 tray, 6 d  .; and 2 yards of webbing, 4 d  .; the goods of Thomas Dutton Templar Sparrow , his master; and MARIA MARLER was indicted for feloniously receiving 30 brushes, part of the same, knowing them to have been stolen; and that William Marler had been before convicted of felony. THOMAS DUTTON TEMPLAR SPARROW. I am a brush-maker, aria live in  Stamford-street, Blackfriars-road. William Marler was in my service—in consequence of suspicion, one evening, as he was going out, I said to him, "John, (which was the name I called him by,) you have been robbing me"—he said I had been a kind master, he should not requite me so—I searched him—I found some bristles in his pocket, and some in his hat—I sent for an officer, and gave him into custody—I have lost a great many bristles—I found these brushes at a pawnbroker's—I can identify some of them, and believe they are all mine—I had such, and lost such—I have heard nothing against Maria Marler—every one speaks favourably of her.
   William Marler.  He said at the Magistrate's office that lie missed two bundles of these bristles, and these only amount to one; there was a man at work there who brought three bundles down, and dressed them himself, and he brought another and gave them to  me to work. ROBERT DAVIS( police-constable L  120.) I went to Maria Marler's, and asked her for some duplicates—she went to a watch-stand on the mantel-piece—she took some papers, and selected two or three, and dropped them into the fire—I jumped by her, and picked some duplicates for brushes out of the fire—they led me to Mr. Mackie's, and there I found these brushes. ALFRED MACKIE. I am a pawnbroker. Maria Marler pawned these brushes with me last Saturday fortnight.
   William Marler's Defence.  I made the brushes, and took them to my mother's; she said if they belonged to me she would take them to pawn; I told her they did, and she took them; the bristles I bought at different times.
   Maria Marler's Defence.  I am innocent of knowing they came from his master's. JOHN CRAWFORD( police-constable K  101.) I produce a certificate of William Marler's former conviction at this Court—( read Convicted  24 th Nov. , 1845, and confined one month )—he is the person.
   WILLIAM MARLER  — GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Nine Months .
   MARIA MARLER  — NOT GUILTY.
