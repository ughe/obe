HENRy CLARKE. I have lived at 65, Brook Street, Kennington Road,
about a year, with my mother-I have been employed by Humphreys and
Grigg, military tailors, Haymarket, for six yearson October 25th, about
7.30 p.m., I called upon Richard James, 42, Peacoek Street, and went
with him and Jinks round to see the illuminations-I parted from them
about 12.30 a.m., in Leicester Squarethe whole time James was perfectly
sober.
WILLIAM ENRIGHT. I live at 40, Turner's Buildings, Erasmus Street,
Westminster, and am a packer at the Army Stores, Grosvenor Road,
Pimlico, in the day time, and am employed at the Empire Theatre stage
entrance in the evening-I am an old soldierI saw Richard James on
the evening of October 25th at the stage door of the Empire about 8.50
he
was quite sober-about 11.20 p.m. James came and complained that
he had lost his friends-he waited till about 11.40-we went to the Prineess
Head, where I purchased a small brandy for my wife, and he had a lemon
and bitter, we walked on to Guest's, and then through Whitehall to the
bottom of Parliament Street, where we stood talking outside the London
and North Western Railway Company's offices till 12.15, when I left him
opposite the Parliament Houses-he was quite sober.
