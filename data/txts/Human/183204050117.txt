   MARY KING. I am a widow, and live in Fox-court. I saw two men in Feather's-court; Sculthorpe was one of them, and he had part of a glass in his hand - the other man was in dark clothes, but I cannot be certain of his person.

   SUSANNAH MARIA BOONE. I am the wife of William Crawford Boone. On the 20th of March a dressing-glass was taken from our shop; I saw it safe about three o'clock; it was cut from a string in the shop - Bennett told me of it, and told me who the parties were.
 Levy. Q. Was there not a young man who told you before Bennett? A. Yes, but he did not know who you were.
   RICHARD BAYLIS(Police-constable G 67.) I apprehended the prisoners on the 27th of March, at the Three Tuns in Brook-street; they beat and kicked me about very much, and said if they were at liberty they would knife me - there were between twenty and thirty very bad characters in the house; my hat was twice cut through with a poker - we were obliged to send to the station for assistance before they could be got out, and this knife was thrown open into the crowd.
   MOSES DAVIS. I am a constable. I was present at the trial of Levy in this Court, and I produce a copy of his former conviction - (read) - I know he is the man.

 LEVY -    GUILTY. Aged 26. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1095.  CHARLES SMITH and THOMAS HORTON were indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of February , 1 cloak, value 28s., the goods of John Fenwick.
   HENRY HENMAN. I am shopman to Mr. John Fenwick, linen-draper, Regent-circus. On the 22nd of February Mr. Coxhead informed us, that he saw two persons near our shop; I looked, and saw Horton, and a boy in a flannel jacket, pass our window once or twice; they appeared to be looking if any body watched them; I laid down behind the counter, and watched - there was a cloak hanging at our door - in about ten minutes I missed it; Horton and the other were gone - I then ran out, and saw Horton on the right side of the door; he had before been on the left -I then saw Smith, whom I had not seen before, rolling up his apron; I secured him, and found this cloak in his apron - Horton had ran off.
   SAMUEL WOOD. I am shopman to the prosecutor. I came into the shop about ten o'clock, and saw Horton was the window several times; I got into a pulpit desk, and saw him go across the road - I got down and crept to the counter, behind some goods; I looked towards the door -I saw the cloak raised from a book, but I could not see who took it; I followed Horton down several streets - I called Stop thief! some persons secured him, and gave him to me; I took him to the shop, and there saw Smith.
   WILLIAM DAWES. (Police-constable T 35) I received the prisoners from the two witnesses; I had before that seen them watching the shop, and when I returned back, they were given to me - Smith had the cloak in his apron, but denied knowing the other prisoner; he said he had committed the offence, and he must suffer for it.
 Smith put in a written Defence, pleading distress - a witness, who gave him a good character, engaged to take him into his service.
 SMITH - GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 HORTON -    NOT GUILTY.
 1096.  ELIZA RILEY was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of March , 1 watch, value 2l., the goods of Benjamin Cornish.
 BENJAMIN CORNISH. I live in Manor-street, Chelsea, and am a  private watchman at Carlton-terrace - I have known the prisoner about three months; I met her in the Broadway, Westminster, on Monday fortnight; I went home with her to Union-court- we went to bed; I had put my watch under the pillow - I did not then know that the prisoner was a soldier's wife; I fell asleep, and when I awoke the prisoner was gone; I missed my watch - I had my dog tied up in the room; I am quite sure he would not let any one have come into the room - he would not let the Policeman in without me; I had given the prisoner 1s., which was all she asked me - my watch was a silver one; I had paid 3l. 13s. 6d. for it the Saturday before.
 Prisoner. There was another girl in the room, and it was her he spoke to. Witness. No, there was no other girl in the room - I will swear you had the watch; I did not go home with the other girl.
   WILLIAM CRUSH. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Museum-street. On the 29th of March a silver watch was pawned with me by Thompson, in company with another female, who I believe was the prisoner - it was pawned in the name of Fitzmorris, in which name Thompson had been in the habit of pawning.
   ELLEN THOMPSON. My husband is a labourer, and I sell things in the street. I met the prisoner in the street last Monday fortnight, and took her with me to the pawnbroker's, where I was then going; we there pawned the watch, and the prisoner bought a shawl, which is here - I gave the duplicate to her.
