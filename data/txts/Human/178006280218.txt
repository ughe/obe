He was not out at day break? - No; he generally gets up between eight and nine o'clock.
 Cross Examination.
 What time did he go out that morning? - About that time I suppose; I do not know.
 Did you see him go out? - I just got a sight of him.
 Did you see him over night? - Yes, he went into the coffee-room, and had some rum and water, and then went to bed.
   SAMUEL SHEEN sworn.
 I believe you saw Mockford on the evening that Newgate was burnt? - I did. I met him in the Old Bailey about nine o'clock.
 Was he peaceable or did he appear to have any thing to do with the rioters? - Nothing that I saw; I went to look at the fire, I met him; he asked me if I came to look at the fire; I told him I did. He did not appear to have any thing to do with the rioters; he walked away with me about a quarter before ten o'clock, and left me at my lodging in Great Russel-street, Covent-garden, at about a quarter after ten o'clock, and went towards his home.
 Cross Examination.
 Newgate was burnt down then and the prisoners were out? - They were coming out then.
   CALEB SMITH sworn.
 I have known the prisoner about five years; he is a very honest, careful, sober young fellow, and a very good servant.
 Sir  WILLIAM MOORE sworn.
 I have known the prisoner I suppose five years.
 What character has he borne? - I can speak no farther of him than as the waiter who attended upon me at the tavern; he appeared to be sober, clean, and civil.
   JOHN KALENDAR sworn.
 I have known the prisoner these eight years; he was a fellow-servant with me; he has a very good character, he always conducted himself very well when I lived with him.
 Mr. Alderman WOOLDRIGE sworn.
 You was I believe, at the time the riots happened, at Newgate? - I was in the Old Bailey from eight o'clock at night till half after two in the morning. I recollect the prisoner's face and some others who made way for me as I came into the Old Bailey from Ludgate I was at the other end of the town, and heard of the fire at Newgate, I immediately drove to the Old Bailey; as soon as I came to the end of it I saw the prisoner with some other very peaceable, decent looking men. Some said that is Alderman Wooldridge. I desired them to make way for me to come up to Newgate; I remember the prisoner with others taking hold of persons who had sticks in their hands, and making way for me to come up to the prison. Some of the mob, among whom was the prisoner, Desired me to ask the keepers if the rioters were out, that the engine might play against the building. I said I will ask that question and will give you such information as I receive. Then the prisoner called out, Let the engines play, let the engines play against the building. The prisoner and some other men, whose faces I knew, were the persons who made way for me up to Newgate, and walked before me up to the prison door.
 Court. Let me understand you about the enquiry for the engines to play? - One of them, I believe the prisoner, called out That is Alderman Wooldridge, and desired they would make way for me. When I got facing the door of the lodge of Newgate, the prisoner with some others who had conducted me up to the gate, desired the mob to suffer the engines to play.
 They made some enquiry about the rioters being out? - When those persons were conducting me to the prison door, there was a general voice, Let the Alderman go and desire to know whether the rioters are out. I understood by that, that they wanted to know whether the persons were in Newgate; I promised the mob I would go in and come out again, and give them a fair answer; upon that the prisoner with two or three others said that if I would go in they would guard the door (the door at that time was fast) and see that no mischief should happen to me.
