JAMES JOHN ILOTT. I am medical officer of the Whitechapel In-
firmary-on 16th January, about 3 p.m., I saw the child there, it was
covered with cinders, dust, and ashes, and was cold and exhausted-I
had it put into a warm bath, and while there we took some cinders out
of its mouth, about six pieces altogether; one larger than the others was
at the back of the tongue; it was as large as a small bead-there were
two exooriations, one at the back of the head and one on the back-those
might have been done by the child falling on the stones—I do not think
that a child recently born could have taken in its mouth the cinders I
found, I think they must have been put there--the baby could not have
been in the dusthole more than a few minutes; the cord was broken-I
had the child attended to and it is now doing well--if it had remained in
the dastheap it would have been dead in ten minutes-I saw the prisoner
about half-past 6, and examined her-she had been lately confined-she
was then intoxicated-if the child had opened its mouth the cinders
might have fallen in.

I saw the prisoner in Baker Row with two other women, about a quarter
of a mile from Old Montague Street-I asked her where she lived, she
said "In Whitechapel" I said “Whitechapel is a large place, can't
you tell me the name of the street ?”-she said “If you want any in-
formation you must find out"-she was the worse for drink-I took her
to the station; on the way she tried to get into several public-houses-I
took her to the Infirmary that she might be attended to.
