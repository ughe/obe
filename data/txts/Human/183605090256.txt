   COURT . Q.  Was he left in charge of the things when your father went out of town? A.  All the things except the carpet, which was locked up in my father's room.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Were you present when your father delivered then things into his care? A.  No. HENRY BEDFORD. I am solicitor to Mr. Herbert. He has taken out an administration to the effects of the deceased—here are the letters of administration with the will annexed, it is dated, 1827—there in no bequest to the prisoner in the will—I went with Mr. Herbert to the deceased house, in October, 1834—we were shown two rooms belonging to the de ceased by Mrs. Hill—I do not think the prisoner was present—the rooms were both locked—the keys could not be found—it was supposed that the deceased had them with him—but we at last found a key which would open the front room, and in that room we found the will, and took an in ventory of the things—we left the back room locked as it was—on the 9th of October we went again, the prisoner and his wife were there then—I said he had not got any key, that the deceased had got it with him, as he supposed, and we burst the door of the back room open—the key we got on the 6th was either found about the house, or Mr. Herbert had it with him, having found it in the deceased's pocket—the rooms were locked up—the prisoner did not pretend to have the control of the things at all—the carpet was not there on the 9th of October—I never saw it till the 2nd of May this year. NOT GUILTY
