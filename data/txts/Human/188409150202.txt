   Witness for the Defence. JOSEPH COLLINS. I am a labourer, living at 11, Elpherton Street, Old Kent Road—I left you at half-past 8 p.m. on the night of 29th July—I was working for Mowlem and Burt at the back of St. George's Barracks—the job was stopped and I was out of work for a week, and I went down to your house on 22nd July about half-past 4 o'clock and asked you to come for a walk to St. George's Barracks to see if they had started—we went all rouud the park and round and back by the Strand—coming back you asked me to lend you a shilling, you said your hand was better and you were going to start work on Monday and would give it back to me on Saturday—I did so, and we went into a public-house at the corner of the Westminster Bridge Road—then we came out and I asked you to go to Mr. King's to get a trowel, and you left me then at about half-past 8 o'clock—you said you had been up to Walworth; you said you had earned something and had something to pay away, and you showed me a half-crown.
   The prisoner in his defence stated that on this day he pulled a barrow to Camberwell Green and that the gentleman he did it for gave him a half-crown, that he then had dinner when Collins came in, and that they went for a walk till half-past 8 o'clock, and that he offered the half-crown without knowing it was bad. GUILTY †— Six Months' Hard Labour.
 984. THOMAS GROVES  (22) , Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin and having another in his possession.
   MR. GOODRICH Prosecuted. SARAH GOODALL. I am Thomas Henry Goodall's wife—we keep a general shop at 289, St. James's Road, Bermondsey—at 11 o'clock on 8th August the prisoner and another man came in—the other man asked for two bottles of ginger-beer and laid down this half-crown—I looked at it and said "This is a bad one"—I bent it—he said "Don't break it, I may get another in place of it, I took three yesterday for my week's wages, all the money I have in my possession is a penny"—I said "Never mind, that will do"—I gave him the half-crown back—he put it in his pocket and said to his friend "Come along," mentioning some name, and they both went out and up St. James's Road—I spoke to a neighbour about a quarter of an hour afterwards—the prisoner was brought back to my shop—I identified the half-crown at the station.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  You drank all the ginger-beer—when you came out of the shop with the detective you did not say "Did I give you this half-crown?"—I took a good half-crown out of my till and said "You can feel the difference between the two"—he felt them, and said "I think this," which was the one he had tendered, "is a bad half-crown."
