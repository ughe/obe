WILLIAM REEVES. I am the busband of the last witness-we lived in
Somers-street six or seven months before the child died-its name was John
William Coupland-it was nearly five years old I used frequently to see it
-I occupy the front parlour, and it was generally kept in the room over my
head, and generally alone-the mother used at times to run up; she would
leave it for hours at a time; she used the girl as an angel compared with this
child-the boy appeared wasting away for six months—the girl was not so by
any means-Mrs. Parker, the aunt, who lives opposite, has frequently thrown
bread up to the child- I have frequently heard the child screaming, and heard
the father say he would stamp his guts out, and then I have heard the child
being beat, and thrown down—that would happen frequently-I was not
away from home from morning to night.
Cross-examined. Q. You have never had any children? 1. Not by this
wife, but by my former wife-I am the father of nineteen, but only two
are living.
MARY CAVANNAGH. I am the wife of Edward Cavannagh. Last Jan. We
lived in the parlour at 8, Somers-street-we were there two months--the pri-
soners lived in the room above us—the child died there--I have seen it come
down--it was in a very bad state, much declined about five weeks before the
inquest I noticed that its face was black from the forehead to the chin-1
went to ask it a question, but the mother put her head out at the window, and
it did not answer me- I have heard the mother lock it in, and go out without
him, and when she has come in I have heard a noise as if the child was
beaten, and knocked against the floor-I asked her what was the matter with
him-she said he was perfectly well, but it was his own bad temper and dirt
left him in the state he was in the other child was very healthy, and was
treated very well, as far as I could see.
Cross-examined. Q. You were never in the room? A. No, only when
the child was burnt.
