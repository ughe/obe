Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY. I came to England to ascertain the
position of Wilson's business—I was suspicious that they did not account
in full for the goods—I also came to improve my knowledge of the
English language; I knew something about it when I came, but I had
never been here before-young Wilson is supposed to be very deai-I
am capable of suggesting that he affects to be deal when he is not-
there have been dealings between Borneman and Wilson since 1881-this
3811. was not due bofore the new agreement-when the agreement was
entered into there was a balance of 1351., and that stood over-that is
quite a different acoount--all the accounts under this were consignment
8000unts-I knew of this agreement & few days or & week after it was
entered into there is nothing in it about keeping books of account-
when I came over here I went to their place of business every day, and
saw the business going on-I asked to see the books I was not told
that the books referring to their general business were not proper for me
to see I did not come over to find out the names of their customers; my
desire was not to improve my knowledge of English and to set up in
business and get the business of their firm--- it was not my scheme with
Borneman that I should learn English and set up here and get the
defendant's customers—I am Mr. Borneman's cousin-I know enough of
English to know that an acceptance at three months, and not drawn till
two months, is a five months' credit—he did not cut off two months' credit;
it is not a fact that instead of giving five months' credit Borneman cut
bim down to three, because the agreement was that we were to draw in
one month; it says: “At the end of every sucoessive period of two
months the principal shall draw on the agent," and I am sure we have
never drawn the 20 per cent. -10 accounts were ever settled between us,
we could not get him to do so-the goods were silks and satins, cheap
ones and dear ones—they had to go through the English workman's
hands, for which Mr. Borneman paid in the first instance, but there was
8 balance on the old account which we could not get, or we would dozens
of times have rettled it, and instead of that he paid the workmen-we
paid 911. for finishing the goods, and the old debt was wiped off-he did
not pay from day to day what we required for finishing the goods-he
did not complain that the silks and satins were of inferior quality, and
not fit for the trade; on the contrary, he was very pleased and satisfied
with them-I was in Court when George Pulley was called on my behalf
-I have sent goods on many occasions to Sergeant to be finished, and
Mr. Borneman paid for them-young Wilson complained that the goods
were not up to the mark for the London market on account of the finish-
