SANUEL PARK. I was a boy on board the Eaperiment. I was taken on
board at Searborough, about 16th or 17th Jan.w left there that same
morning, and went on the fishing ground-I think we caught nine pots and
seventeen or eighteen baskets-they were sold at Sunderland to men and
women, who came on board-] saw some money paid to Cooper-we staid
at Sunderland a week-the prisoners were ashore during that time, sometimes
they were aboard at night, and sometimes not-a day or two before we left
Sunderland, Swale went away--he told me something before he left--] did
not intend to leave, as I did not know where to go to-the vessel left Sun-
derland on Monday morning; we went along the coast that day, it came on
to blow, and we were brought up in Whitby-roads--we staid there two days
and two nights-we then started and went past Girimsby; we had fine wea-
ther all that day, and ran up with a fair wind, there was nothing to prevent
our going into Grimsby-we could have gone in with a nice fair wind; we
were sailing all that night, and did not see anything of the light boats: next
day it was rather hazy, and we made the Lemon lightwe took our course
from there across to Holland- caught some fish before we went there-we
sold it at New Dieppe-at least not sold it a man came off to take the rope
to haal us in, and he took it ashore; we had a bottle of gin and a pound of
tobacco for it-some chain was taken ashore there, a large bower anchor, a
compass, and two kedge anchors I do not know what else, a Dutehman took
them away-the three prisoners were present when they were taken-the
Dutchman could not have taken them away without they had let bim.
Cooper. He states we were all present when the anchor and chains were
taken out of the vessel; we were not; I was on board, but these two men
were not. Witness. They were all there when I went down, but when the
anchor went, only the skipper was there-] cannot say how the wind was
vben we went past Grimsby, bat T know it was a fair wind-T have been at
sea nearly three years, and ought to know when the wind is fair.
JAMES VINCENT West. I was chief officer of the Lion steam ship, which
trades between Dieppe and London. I was at Dieppe in Jan. last-there is
a fine harbour there, the sea flows into ir; and ships go in-I saw the
Experiment there-I received a communication from a Mr. Saunders, and
returned to London in the Lion, and was employed by Mr. Howard to feteh
the Erperiment over-I arrived again at Dieppe on 22nd Feb.-th prisoners
were then on board the Dutch guard ship, by order of the British Consul-I
took charge of the Eaperiment-I found her in rather a deplorable state, very
thort of stores, and of very nearly every description of things-I found 120
fathom of chain cable gone, three anchors, a big jib, a two-hour glass, a
compass, and pair of boat grips-I saw Cooper on board the guard ship, and
he told me as far as he knew about the thingsTI went where be told me to
go-at a Jew's I found sixty fathom of cable, a best bower anchor, a jib, and
a pair of boat grips-it appeared that the Jew had also purchased the kedge
anchors, but I could not find them in his place-I was obliged to buy other
things to supply their place-I engaged two men, and with their assistance
and the boys brought the vessel to Grimsby-we sailed from Dieppe on
23rd Feb. at 11 o'cloek, and arrived at Grimsby at 3 next day--I saw the
prisoners on board the Lion in custody-| consider the Experiment was a
very fast sailer ; she averaged eight knots an hour, she was seaworthy, or
I should not bave come home in her.
