   Q.  Do you remember the Saturday night after the prisoner had agreed to work at 36 s . a week, his coming to be paid? A.  Yes; and he was paid—my master said to him, "George, what time are you going to allow me?"—he said he could not allow him any, and then he had his full wages paid—on the following Saturday he was paid again—I did not see my master produce the paper again—the prisoner demanded his indentures at twelve o'clock on the 6th of June—my master refused to deliver them till he was satisfied—the indentures were demanded several times—I cannot say How many—I cannot tell bow long I had seen Skipper before the prisoner was taken—I had been at home three days—I was not well—I was sent for—I saw Skipper on the day the prisoner was taken—after he was taken, not before—I saw him at Union-street, outside the hall—I am certain I had not seen him the day before—I am certain I had seen him on my mas ter's premises, talking to his brother-in-law.
   Q.  Have you not acknowledged to Cook, Lewis, and Newland, or to either of them, that you were aware of the prisoner having had taps and dies and several other tools for the last three years—that you had often lent them to him, and he had brought them back, that you knew of his having some knife-shanks—and that the other apprentices and journey men frequently had tools lent to them? A.  Never.
   Q.  Now attend to these words, "But if we don't get Batchelor trans ported for fourteen years, I shall be out of employment, and Sadler will have nothing to do"—upon your solemn oath, did you ever say to any of these persons any thing of that kind? A.  Never—they spoke to me on the subject of his being in prison—I said I was extremely sorry, that it was all his own fault, he had no occasion to do so, he had a good master and good employment—that was the principal conversation—I might have said something more, but not what you said—I might have said as far as this, that I was afraid he would be transported—I believe that was all I said—I believe that to be the whole conversation—I have seen the prisoner buy tools of a man—I cannot recollect what sort—I never saw him buy castings—it was merely tools for his own use—it is a long time
