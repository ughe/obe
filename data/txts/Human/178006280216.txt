   JOHN HALEY BRAZIER sworn.
 I keep a wine vault in Market-lane. The prisoner lodged at my house.
 Did you see him on Tuesday evening? - On the night the fire was at Newgate he came to my house in an hackney coach and brought something in his pocket, between six and eight o'clock I believe.
 Are you positive as to the time? - It was about that time, it could not be later than that.
 Was it dark or light? - It was light; he rattled them in his pocket when he came in and said I have got the keys of Newgate; he put them down upon the top of the table, and after that they were given to the servant, and she carried them up stairs into his own room; what became of them afterwards I do not know. I said, I did not like to have any thing to do with the keys; he desired me to lock them up; he said it was very possible that they might be advertised. He said, Mr. Smith, Mr. Akerman's brother, gave him the keys; I think that was the name.
 You are not sure as to the name? - No.
 Are you sure he said he received them from Mr. Akerman's brother? - Yes.
 Jury. Are you certain to the time he came to your house? - Yes; it was between the hours of six and eight; he was very dirty about half way up his leg; he went up into his own room, put boots on, and went out again; the keys were carried up into his own room; I do not know what became of them afterwards.
 Jury. How long did he stay at home? - Not above ten minutes I suppose.
 Did he go out in the coach again? - No, on foot.
 What part of the town is your house in? - Market-lane, the back part of the Opera-house.
 Cross Examination.
 You seem to say he came home dirty? - Yes.
 Did curiosity never induce you to go out in the time of these riots? - Yes.
 Could you go out without getting your shoes dirty? - There was no need to dirt one's shoes unless one was amongst some water, where one ought not to be.
 Did he lie at your house that night? - Yes.
 What time did he come in? - I cannot tell, I was a-bed.
 What time did he get up in the morning? - I cannot tell, I was not up; the servant can tell you.
 Did not you think it strange of this man if he was gone to burn Newgate, that he should tell you he had the keys of Newgate? - I did think it strange.
 Did not he shew these keys to other people? - Yes, I believe he did.
 Did he not want you to keep the keys till they were advertised? - Yes.
 Did not you think it strange if he had been concerned in burning Newgate that he should want a person to keep the keys for him? - Yes.
 What size were they? - They appeared to me to be large keys.
 You would not touch them for fear they would contaminate you? - I would not come near them.
 Counsel for the Crown. Was it after you would have nothing to do with the keys that he said he would advertise them? - No, before.
   CHARLES SANDERS sworn.
 I am cook at the St. Alban's Tavern. The prisoner was waiter there.
 Do you recollect seeing him the evening of the 6th of June? - Yes; at about seven o'clock, I saw him opposite the Old Bailey.
 Had you seen him before that evening? - Not in the city of London; I had seen him in the street where Justice Hyde's house was; and I saw him in the street afterwards, with  Thomas Haycock.
 Was any body with them? - They were walking sometimes by themselves sometimes with other people. They seemed to walk singly by themselves before the mob.
 Jury. Leading the mob? - No, far from it.
