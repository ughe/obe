JOHN HIGHAM. I am master of the West Ham Union Workhouse-
the four prisoners were discharged from there on Thursday morning, the
24th of March-in eonsequence of cireumstances, I went into the laundry
on Friday morning, the 25th of March, between ten and eleven o'clock,
and missed from there three shirts, a pair of stoekings, and a handkerchief
-I traced some footmarks over the back fence into the Infirmary-yard,
and from there into the laundry-none of the articles were found-] gave
information to the police-Aller was brought in in the afternoon--I ordered
him to be locked up till we traced the footmarks-he made a statement
about others, but nothing about himself.
MICHAEL CAsEY. I am a policeman, I took Allen-in taking him
before the master of the Union, he said that he received the goods out of
the laundry from some one-that there were three sheets, a handkerchief,
and a pair of stockings--h said he stood outside the laundry window-he
did not say how far he was from the laundry-he said he accompanied others
to London to dispose of them-they have not been found.
GEORGE SITH. I am a police-sergeant. I went to Windley's house,
and found him concealed under the stairs-I took him to the station-]
took off his, Allen's, and Parker's shoes-the next morning I went to the
Union, and saw a great many marks which correspond with Allen's and
Parker's marks-I could not see any of Windley' Hennesey was not
in custody then- put the shoes into the holes, and they exactly
fitted.
GEORGE CARTER. I am a policeman. About half-past nine o'clock
on Friday morning, I was on duty at Low Leyton, and saw the four pri-
soners coming in the direction from the Union-Parker had a bundle
Parker, Windley, and Hennesey took the road leading to Temple Mills-
Allen went towards Stratford-afer going some short distance, Hennesey
called to Allen, and told him to wait at Stratford, and they would meet
him there-] afterwards had suspicion, and followed, but did not overtake
them.
HENRy Miles. I am a policeman. I apprehended Hennesey on Sa-
turday morning, the 26th of March-I told him he was wanted for the
robbery at the Union about the sheets-he asked me who we had locked
mentioned Allen's, Parker's, and Windley's names-he said, It
will be a gooser with some of us"-I then took him to the station.
ROBERT REYNOLDs. Between eight and nine o'clock on the 24th of
March, Windley and another came to my house, and called for a pint of
porter, they stopped a few minutes, and went out into the street.
Allen's Defence. All Casey says against me is false I was not near
the place.
Parker's Defence. My shoes were taken off my feet; they could not
correspond, for I was not on the premises from the time I left.
NOT GUILTY.
