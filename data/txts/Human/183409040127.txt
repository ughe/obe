   THOMAS BIRKETT. I am in the service of a pawnbroker- I have some silk stockings and handkerchiefs, pawned on the 23rd of July, for 32s., by a female - I cannot say it was the prisoner.

 Prisoner's Defence. My husband's disgraceful conduct was the cause of my pawning these things - I was privately married to him, but his conduct in going with other females was such that I could not live with him - the man Varley, now in Court, persuaded his son to abscond and leave me destitute; and, on my demanding a maintenance, he promised, on my retiring into Wales, to allow me the vast sum of 20l. a year - I went there; but on my applying to a bank, through which it was to be remitted, he, to my great surprise, refused this 20l. a year, pretending it was not due - my friends then persuaded me to return, and reside near his country seat, till he felt inclined to perform his promise, and send me this handsome settlement of 20l. a year - I went and resided at a little cottage, near his country seat - but finding he would do nothing for me, I went to Mrs. Porter, who let me have the things; but as she had given me twelve months' credit before, I thought I should have time to pay her - I thought, as I had the good had a right to do what I pleased with them - my chief prosecutor would not send me what he promised, though his son got in debt with other women, and it was all laid on my shoulders; and so destitute has my husband left me, that when I go out, I should not know where to turn for a home, nor where to go for a sixpence.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined Nine Months.

   GEORGE NEWLAND. The prisoner was in my service, and left on the 7th of August - I then missed these articles.
   GEORGE FREDERICK SHEPHERD. I am a pawnbroker - I produce these articles, which were pawned by the prisoner, between the 7th of May and the 7th of August.
 Prisoner. The prosecutor said, if the duplicates were delivered up, he would not proceed against me.
 GEORGE NEWLAND. I said so, but I had only missed three spoons then.

 1467.  GEORGE ANSON was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July , 1 hat, value 4s.; and two gloves, value 1s.; the goods of Frederick Bennell.
 FREDERICK BENNELL. I am a  law stationer- on the 29th of July I was at the Bedford Arms, playing at skittles - I then went into the billiard-room, leaving my hat and gloves in the skittle ground - the prisoner was afterwards taken with them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is the skittle ground covered? A. The floor of the billiard room covers it - I went up stairs without my hat - all the lights were put out but one.
   GEORGE BROWN. I was at the skittle ground - I did not see the prosecutor put his hat down - I did not tell the magistrate that I did, but I saw the prisoner take a white hat, and try to put it inside his own, but he could not - he then put his own hat on, and put the white hat under the front of his coat - I pursued him with a friend - I said to him, "Young man, it is no use your attempting to make off with that hat; I know what you have got" - he said, "It is nothing but a boy's hat" - I said, "It belongs to a gentleman in the Bedford Arms, you had better take it back" - he said he would not - we went about one hundred yards further, and one of the young men who was with him said,"If you take it back, you are a b - y fool" - the prisoner said he would take it back, provided one of his own party would go back with him - a policeman came by, and I gave him in charge.
 Cross-examined. Q. You were looking at him when he was putting the hat under his own? A. Yes, I was looking at him - Matthews saw it - I did not call out, "You have got a white topper" - I do not know what it means.
   DANIEL MADDEN(police-constable S 186). I took the prisoner with the hat - I heard the witness say, "I insist upon the gloves being returned to the owner" - I found the gloves in his pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. The hat was thrown at me - I walked about one hundred yards - the witness came, and said it was a gentleman's hat - I then said I would take it back, but he wanted to take it back himself.
   JAMES MATTHEWS. I am a wheelwright - I was there that night - these gentlemen came in - they asked me to get them a light, and set up their skittles, which I did - we then went out - Brown came after us and said, "You have got a white topper;" and he said he would take it back - but the prisoner said he would.
     NOT GUILTY.
