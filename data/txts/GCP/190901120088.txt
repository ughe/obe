JOHN WARRINER (prisoner, on oath). I go to Billingsgate Market
to buy my fish. On December 11 I arose at 5.30, lit three fires,
washed, and went to market at six. I went on board one of the
"Red-Cross" cutters-a fish-carrier running to the North Sea from
Hall to collect fish-and saw an old shipmate. On board the cutter
I got a donen cigars, which are some of the cigars produced, and
tobacco also, from the old sailors. Coming ashore with my friend I
met another friend and we went into the only saloon in Billingsgate
Almost anything you can mention can be bought in Billingsgate,
being kept for the use of sailors. While in the saloon I was accosted
by a man with this whisky and brandy in two baskets, who asked me
to buy the last eight bottles he had. Being near Christmas I bought
them, after a little argument. I did not ask him any question, for I
had bought of him three bottles of spirits on the first of the month.
He had told me he was a traveller commissioned to sell this liquor.
I bought what I thought were eight bottles of spirits; but one of the
bcttles was a dummy got up for sale. I was cheated. I paid with
money borrowed from my wife. When I bought the bottles the sailor
took & cloth off the empty baskets and wrapped the spirits in its
that I should not break them. I had a market-bag containing fah.
I took the fish out, put the liquor in, and bought another bag for 2d.
to hold the fish. I asked a friend who had nothing to do to go over
London Bridge with me. He carried the fish and I carried the liquor.
We caught a tram at the Borough, got off at Lambeth New Baths or
Lambeth Road, walked about 200 yards up Kennington Road to
Walnut Tree Walk, and knocked at the door of No. 62, asking for
man named Fitzgerald. One of the lodgers appeared and I said I
had come for a box which I had left there a fortnight earlier. He
took the box off the landing and gave it to me. I opened it and
placed in it the bag of fish and the bag containing liquor. This was
about 7.30 in the morning. My friend and I carried the box to
Kennington Road, where the cabman picked us up. I went home
My friend went part of the way with me and paid half the cab fara
Cross-examined. The friend who came with me in the cab is, I
think, at sea. His name is John Williams, not William Quick. I
know William Quick. As to the cigars, I knew, without inquiring,
where they came from. I did not buy them. They were given to me
by a friend.
