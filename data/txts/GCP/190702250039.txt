The Common Serjeant said that having considered the case he had come to the
conclusion there was no case to go to the jury. The question was a diffoult one,
but so far as he had to deal with it it was a mere question of law. Prisoner being
indieted for converting money to his own use, he had received the money under
written agreement by whiob ho and the prosecutor agreed to act for considerations
of mutual advantage in trading." The case was complicated by the fact that
Norris, the prosecutor, was for the time being entitled to the style of the firm
of "W. Coleman and Co.," and under that prosecutor supplied the coal to the
customers, but these people did not know him at all. They knew Bouth by the
Name of Coleman and Co., he was the only person who had any bargain with them,
and they paid him not because be represented Norris but because he was the person
who made the bargain with them, the only bargain to whish they were parties, they
giving it to him for himself. Then there was a contract between him and Norris that he
should pay the money hereceived over to Norris without any deduction for commission.
Taking the case as it stood without having heard the defence there was plenty of
ground for saying that when prisoner omitted the names of people from whom he had
received money and did not pay over the money and kept more than he was entitled
to be was acting dishonestly. But that was not the chargo. The charge was that
be kept back money dishonestly, omitted to make an entry or mado falso entries,
having received the money for Norris, but be thought that the effect of the agree-
ment was that as between prisoner and those who paid him he received the money
absolutely for himselt. They knew nothing of Norris and the whole contract was
between the customers and prisoner. As to the clause that prisoner was to pay the
whole of the money to Norris, the legal effect of it was not that he received the
money for Norris but that there was a contract that wben be received it be should
pay it over. A contract that a person should pay money over did not in itself imply
that the person receiving the money received it for the other but was a separato
contract, a civil contract. In this case where prisoner was the only person known
to the customers and received from them, be did not think that Amounted to
receiving for Norris. Therefore, without saying that the defendant Bouth might
not possibly be indicted for something or other, he did not think he was guilty of this
charge.
Verdict, Not guilty
