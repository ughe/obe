608. ELIZABETH TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of February , 10 lbs. of pork, value 3s., the goods of Benjamin Hands.
   MARY WILDING. I live with Mr. Benjamin Hands, a surgeon, near Crouch-end, Hornsey. On the 9th of February, about half-past twelve o'clock, we lost part of a dressed loin and a leg of pork, from the safe, which is attached to the house, in the garden - I told my master, and he went to the gate; I saw the pork again in about ten minutes - I have seen the prisoner come as far as the kitchen door to offer matches for sale; this is the leg of pork, which was not dressed.
   JOHN THOMAS DAWRENCE. I was at the prosecutor's. I ran out, and saw this girl with the pork in a basket, which was her own, about a hundred yards from the gate.
 GUILTY. Aged 12. -    Judgment Respited.
 609.  MARY McLEAN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of January , 4 yards of carpet, value 10s., the goods of Jessaint McCall Knapp and Frederick Augustus Knapp.
   JOHN BAGULEY. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Oxford-street. On the 15th of January, about ten o'clock in the evening. the prisoner offered this piece of carpet to pledge, and stated it was the property of a person in Wardour-street - I suspected her from her rude behaviour, and sent for an officer.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Are you quite sure it was on the 15th of January? A. It was on a Saturday evening, the 15th of January.
 JESSAINT McCALL KNAPP. I am in partnership with my brother  Frederick August us Knapp; we are upholsterer s. The prisoner first came to work at our place in September, and she has worked there almost continually ever since - she received her wages between six and seven o'clock on the 15th of January, but she was not discharged; I expected her to return on Monday: this is our carpet - there are four yards of it; it is worth 14s. - I did not permit her to take it away.
 Cross-examined. Q. She was not regularly employed by you? A. She was almost - I paid her her wages in the parlour on the Saturday night - I did not see her leave the house; I had seen this carpet in the course of that week -I cannot say what day; we had more of this pattern in the house, and some of it had been sold - I will not say positively when I had seen this piece; I did not miss it till the Tuesday, when I received information.
 GUILTY. Aged 24. -    Judgment Respited.
 610.  LEONARD JAMES MITCHELL was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of January , 50 yards of cotton, value 4l., the goods of George Washington England , his master; and ELIZABETH MITCHELL was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen.
   GEORGE WASHINGTON ENGLAND. I am an upholsterer, and live in Welbeck-street. Leonard James Mitchell was my errand-boy for about three weeks previous to the 15th of January - I discharged him on a Monday, I suppose about the 12th or the 14th, having missed various little trifling articles; after he was gone I missed fifty yards of cotton, which are in whole pieces, with the exception of one, which has had a yard or two taken off by the maker.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you any partner? A. No; this young woman had come to my house after the boy had left - I had found fault with some part of his conduct; I did tap him on the head with a child's boot - he did not threaten to take out a warrant for me.
 COURT. Q. Do you know whether his sister visited him while he was there? A. She came once or twice to the shop, and she came after he was gone to know why he had left, and wished he might return.
   JAMES STRINGER. I live with Mr. Gofton, a pawnbroker. I have some cotton pawned by the female prisoner on the 7th of January.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you known her before? A. She had pawned some things, and released them again.
   EBENEZER SOPER. I am a dealer in these articles - I have a mark on this cotton; I sold it to the prosecutor.
 MR. ENGLAND. I believe this to be mine, but I have no mark on it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 611.  JOHN KIFT was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of February , 1 saddle, value 20s., the goods of Giles Bell Hutton.
   GILES BELL HUTTON. I am a saddler, and live at Uxbridge. I lost this saddle on the 3rd of February from a wooden horse at the door of my shop - the door stands in about six inches; it was market-day - I had the saddle in my possession at half-past ten o'clock, and missed it about twelve; I saw it again on the Saturday.
   JAMES CHURCH. I am a foot-patrol. I apprehended the prisoner on the Windsor road, about seven miles from Uxbridge, about half-past four o'clock, on the 3rd of February - he went into a harness-maker's shop, at Slough, and offered this saddle for sale; he said he would give me the saddle if I would let him go - as we were going back he threw down the saddle, and again said he would give it me to let him go - I took him to the Red Lion, and sent for the officer to take him.
 JAMES BACON. I was sent for by Mr. Church, about half-past five o'clock, to take the prisoner - this is the saddle.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 55. - Confined Six Months.
