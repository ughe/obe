DAVID PEROY LAWRENCE. I am a clerk to James William Vickers, ad-
vertising agent and contractor, of 5, Nicholas Lane, Lombard Street --
our firm had instructions to have a report taken of a dinner at the
Hotel Cecil, on September 20th, 1901, and to cause it to be published
I think the instructions came over the 'phone-we carried out those
instructions, and sent instructions to the papers to send their reporters
there we received this cheque on account on November 25th, 1901,
drawn by H. J. Lawson, payable to our firm for £200—-We had several
items out against the Electric Tramways Construction Company that
paid nothing in particular--it was in respect of the dinner, and also
in respect of advertising work done for the Construction Company--the
figure in respect of advertising the speech at the dinner was £108 88. 3d.
Cross-examined by Lawson. It is quite a usual matter for us to report
dinners and shareholders' meetings- I was given to understand the
message came on the telephone, I do not know if they could tell whose
voice it was I cannot remember if the Construction Company had
reports of other meetings--there is nothing unusual in it-I cannot say,
but I should say there was more than £200 owing at that time besides
the amount for the dinner-I am not sure
CHARLES Edwin FILBY. I am a journalist, of 7, Brakespears Road, St.
John's, and am on the staff of the Financial News "-under instructions
I attended a dinner at the Hotel Cecil, on September 20th, 1901-I .
heard a speech there made by Sir Kenneth Mackenzie I did not take a
note of the speech itself, I think it was supplied to me afterwards - I
cannot remember at this time who supplied it to me--I handed it in
at the office of the “ Financial News" - I produce & copy of the issue of
the paper of the following Monday, September 23rd, Exhibit 125.
JAMES WATSON MILLS. I am a member of the staff of the “Financial
Times,” living at 13, Cheverton Road, Hornsey Lane -I attended a
dinner at the Hotel Cecil on September 20th, 1901, and reported a
speech then made by Sir Kenneth Mackenzie-that report appeared in
the issue of the paper on the following Monday, September 23rd-I pro-
duce a copy.
CHARLES WILLIAM Coop. I am a journalist on the staff of "The
Statist" newspaper, and produce a copy of the issue of “The Statist”
newspaper of September 28th, 1901—it contains a report of a speech
delivered by Sir Kenneth Mackenzie at the Hotel Cecil, on September
20th-I received instructions with regard to that matter from Lawson
-I received the first instructions from the secretary of the company, Mr.
Osborne; the payment for that insertion was 20 guineas, which was.
arranged with Lawson; it was a five column report at four guineas
a column-I produce the order under which this was done, Exhibit 142,
dated September 26th, 1901: "Dear Sirs, -Please insert full report of Sir
Kenneth Mackenzie's speech at the dinner of the Electric Tramway Con-
struction, etc., Company, Limited, in your next issue, to occupy columns
at the rate of £4 48. per column ”-it is initialled by Lawson--the
first order is signed "C. Osborne "; that is one column at four guineas,
and after that the order was extended to 20 guineas, and initialled by
Lawson.
