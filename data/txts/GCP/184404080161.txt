1209. ANN SHEHAN was indicted for stealing 10lbs. weight of flour,
value 38., the goods of Mary Carswell, her mistress.
MARY CARSWELL. I am a baker, living in Halfmoon-street, Piccadilly.
The prisoner was my servant for eight months, up to the 24th of March-in
consequence of suspicions I went to her box in the kitchen it was unlocked
-- she was not present-I found a large bag of flour in it, weighing ten pounds
and a half-1 let it remain-I had not missed any flour--the foreman and
my two sons compared it with what was in the sack which we were using-I
found the quality was equal.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Was any one with you at the
time you found this flour? 4. Not any one--I have five lodgers when my
house is full, and had one servant, and my two sons-she had to cook for
them and for my family, and to wait on the rest of the house-I gave her 91.
a year wages- I paid her weekly, 1s. a week-I mean to swear that I paid
her that, the place was as good as 161, a year-she had clothes-if I chose
to give them her she would be very thankful-I cannot recall to mind when
I have given her anything I have paid her 1s. a week-1 have my book to
prove it, and she has receipted the book-I will swear I have paid her 58.
and 10s.-T have not got the dates just at my recollection--she made no
demand for wages-I owe her 28. or 3s. - I did not refuse to pay her wages
-she gave me a week's notice to quit-I found the flour on Saturday night,
and she was to go away on the Monday-I only wished her to stay a few
days till I could get a servant-she did not refuse I do not know whether
Mrs. Ponsonby was to have got ber a better place--she has not said so.
ROBERT OXER policeman.) I was sent for to the prosecutrix's, and took
the prisoner-I saw these two bags of flour in the box-the prosecutrix said
she would give her into custody--the prisoner then said it was her own pro-
perty, that she bought it of a man in Oxford-street-she did not know his
Dame, or where he lived.
