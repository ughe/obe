MR. CLARKSON to Mr. Tidy. Q. In consequence of any request made to
you by the Grand Jury, with reference to a blue flame coming from a paper
in which there was arsenic, did you try that experiment only last night A.
1 did I tried it with white arsenic, and yellow arsenic or sesquisulphurate
of arsenie, both-no blue flame came from either--I suspect that the sesqui-
sulphurate of arsenie was not very pure, from having been in my pocket 86
long I wrapped the white arsenic in paper and put it to a live coal, but
there was no blue flame-the arsenic was sublimed on the coal by the in-
strument with which I held it to the fire.
Mr. Bopkin. Q. Are you much of a practical chemist? 4. Not at all
- know what is meant by the term “arsenical flame"-metallio arsenie

Court. Q. You tried white, and yellow arsenic, each wrapped up in
white paper? A. Yes, and it produced no blue flame whatever--it did not
colour the flame at all.
DR. ANTHONY THOMPSON. I am a Professor of materia medica and forensia
medicine at the London University I have heard Dr. Letheby's account of
having found arsenic in the contents of the stomach of the deceased, and
have seen the tubes in which the metallic arsenic is contained-the pro-
bability is that it was given in successive doses for a long time-when arsenie
I found in the liver it generally arises from accumulations there it is
eliminated by the liver, but remains a considerable time there I am not
acquainted with what Dr. Letheby has mentioned as the arsenical flame-
have never seen arsenic thrown into the fire in paper-I have seen it thrown
into the fire without paper, and seen it sublimed from blasts, and other in-
struments--the quantity of arsenic obtained by Dr. Letheby would be suffi
cient to cause death.
