   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Did Speed go with you from Wandsworth? A.  Yes—I knew him before—Dagnal is in Mr. Colling bourn's employ—it was a quarter to nine o'clock when I left Wandsworth—I got to the stable about half-past nine—it is about two miles from Wands worth—I examined the stable by my lamp which I had—the blood in the fourth stall, where I found the body, looked dry—I did not make any fur ther inquiry about it—I had not left the stable before I found the mattress—I stopped there till about one o'clock—I left sergeant Palmer and Tye, a policeman, there then.
   COURT . Q.  What quantity of blood do you suppose there was? A.  I cannot exactly say—it was about as large as that paper ( half a sheet offoolscap )—it was in the straw—it began eight or ten inches from the body, not under it.
   JURY . Q.  It was on the ground? A.  On the hay and litter, in the straw—I removed that, and saw the stain on the bricks—they were a lighter colour than the part which was stained—I could see plainly where the blood was—there was no appearance of the blood having run over the rest of the bricks—there was no stream of blood—there was some loose hay laying in the stall, not as if a horse had been there—it was loose hay from the truss—there is no hay-loft there—you go out of the stable to get to the loft. ROBERT SPEED. I am eighteen years old, and live at Wands worth. On Wednesday, the 6th of April, I went with Gardiner to Mr. Shiell's, at Roehampton-lane—we went to the house first, and afterwards to the stable, to a small private gate—I rang the bell—the prisoner answered it, and the young woman, the daughter of the gar dener—they came to the gate—when we were at the gate, Gardiner told the prisoner he had come for a pair of trowsers he had stolen from Mr. Collingbourn—he said he had no black trowsers—when we got inside the premises Gardiner told me to take care of the prisoner—we were then in side the premises, not far from the stable—we afterwards went into the stable—the prisoner went first—I saw him open the stable-door with a key—I saw him turn the key which he produced from his pocket before he got to the door—I saw him turn the key—we all went in, and I kept near him—Gardiner commenced searching, and after searching some time the prisoner went into the place where the body was found—Gardiner told him to stand back—before that, I saw the prisoner begin taking the hay from one side of the stall, and putting it over where the body lay—it was on that Gardiner told him to stand back, he must make a more diligent search—he stood back nearer to me—we were about two yards from the stall Gardiner was searching—while Gardener was searching he said, "What is this?" loud enough for the prisoner to hear—I stepped forward to see what it was, and the prisoner rushed back towards the stable-door to escape—I saw him as he had the door in his hand going out, and imme diately heard the door locked—I heard the sound of the key turning—I afterwards saw the body.
