 (  The witness's deposition was here read. )—"I am a shoemaker. I called upon Mr. Nichols on Friday—he told me he was perfectly satisfied with what Mart had done; that the money was to be paid on the following week—Iwas present at Nichols' when he paid the prisoner 15 s . he was to have 15 s . more—I did not hear that Mart was to have this for his own trouble, or whether it was for legal expenses."
   Q.  How do you account for that statement; is that true that you swore? A.  I say the 30 s . was to be paid—I never heard about any proceedings—I said that before the magistrate, and I say so now.
   COURT . Q.  What was it to be for? A.  I don't know, definitely; I don't know what the 30 s . was for, only for labour—Nichols said, "I shall give you 15 s . down, part of the 30 s .—I cannot swear positively, that the 15 s . was in part—I am quite certain it is true that Nichols said, "I will give you 15 s . in part of the 30 s .—I do not recollect telling him that Mart had a great many things in hand; and was short of money—I have seen the prisoner two or three times a week. WILLAM NICHOLS re-examined by MR. BAYLEY ). Q.  You saw Wells at Guildhall? A.  I did—I had conversation with him; he told me Mart was a solicitor; he afterwards told me, Mairt had a great many things in hand, and was short of cash—he said he had known him a great many years, and it would be all right.
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you know that the magistrate took this prosecution out of Fogo's hands? A.  He said I had got into bad hands, and he would take me out of them. GUILTY.— Judgement Respited.
