JOHN JENNINGS. I am in partnership with my brother, at 10, London-
mad—we are gold-beaters—the prisoner has been in our employ three or
four montbs. On 16th Dec. I gave bim 2 ozs. 9 dwts. of gold, worth
101., to beat into leaf—none of the men were allowed to take the gold of
the premises—be worked that day ; and the next day, Tuesday, he was the
worse for liquor—I think I saw him on the Wednesday, but am not quite
certain—on the 19th Thursday, at five o'clock, finding the work was
neglected, I went and iooked, and found the gold gone, box and all, and the
prisoner also—I found bim the same evening, at ten o'clock, at the Seckford
Arms, Clerkenwell-green, and said, Gilson, what have you done with
the gold ? "—he said, It i' in the shop ' '—I said, It is not there; and
if you do not come with me I shall give you in wanted to
have some more to drink—I said I should give him in charge if he did not
come, and he came—on going down St. John-street, Green, who is in our
employ, and was with me, spoke him, and the prisoner pulled the box
from his pocket w%th the gold in it, and Green instantly handed it to me——
it was the box he usually kept gold in—not a grain of the gold was removed

Cross-examined by MR. WOOLLETT. Q. Was he irregular in his work
d. Yes; he was a very drunken man—previous to being in our service,

what was found in it—the remainder would quite fill it—there is 'bout

TIMOTHY JENNINGS. On 19th Dec. J saw the prisoner in our shop,
about ten minutes or a quarter of an hour—I saw the box there on his
bench, at nine o'clock in the morning—I bad seen the gold it at nine
the evening before—I did not weigh it, but from my knowledge of the
material, and from the fulness of the box, I conclude it was all there—he
was tipsy on the 18th, but not on the 19th.
Cross-examined. Q. He was not given into custody till the Friday
night ? A. No; he was to come home, and see how much it weighed, and
he slipped away at Blackfriars-bridge—we weighed the gold, and on the
Friday found him in bed, and gave him into custody, and the gold-dust
was then found in bis pocket at the station.
