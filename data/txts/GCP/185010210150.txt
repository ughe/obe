1896. WILLIAM BROOK, unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
Messrs. Clerk and Scriven conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH SHEARS. I am the wife of John Shears, a chandler in the
Borough. On 2nd Oct. the prisoner came between three and five o'clock for
four red herrings, they came to 2.d.-he refused to take them-he then said
he would have them and gave me a half-crown-I gave him the change and
the herrings, and he left-1 put the half-crown to my teeth, and found
it was bad- I put it on the mantel-piece, and then gave it to my husband-
he took it to the station--this is it; I know it by the marks I made on it.
Prisoner. Q. Did not you say to the policeman on Wednesday that I was
not the man? A. No; I said I could not identify you till I saw you by
day-light.
MARY Ross. I am barmaid at the King's Arms. On 2nd Oct. the
prisoner came to the bar with two men, and asked for a half-quartern of gin and
some cold water-he paid for it with a bad shillingas I was counting the
change, one of his companions asked me for a glass of porter-I served him-
he put down another shilling--I had the change in my hand-I put it down.
and took the two shillings- I tried them by the detector, they were bad; they
bent- I told the men so, and they all three rushed out of the house-they took
the change for the first shilling- I kept the two shillings in my hand till the
constable came-I marked them, and gave them to him-these are them-I
had not parted with them.
John SPILLAN policeman, M 200). I took the prisoner- I received
these two shillings from Ross.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint-this
half-crown is counterfeit-these two shillings are both counterfeit, and from
the same mould.
Prisoner. The money I had about me was taken from me, and has been
kept from me; I have not had the means of corresponding with my friends.
GUILTY.* Aged 31.-Confined Twelve Months.

1897. MARY ANN JOHNSON was indicted for a like offence.
Messrs. CLERK and Scriven conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZA ANN Peet. On 9th Oct. the prisoner came to my shop to buy
some lace-she gave me a bad half-crown-1 put it in the test and bent it-
I said, “This is a bad half-crown, and you know it"-she said she would not
have passed it if she had known it was bad- I gave it to my brother.
JAMES HILTON LAWSON. I am the brother of the last witness. On 9th
Oct. I saw the prisoner at her shop-my sister gave me a half-crown, and
said in the prisoner's presence that she gave it her-the prisoner said she
would not have given it her if she had known it was bad-I sent for an officer
and gave him the half-crown.
DANIEL FREEMAN policeman, P 56). I took the prisoner-Mr. Lawson
gave me this half-crown which I have kept ever since.
CAROLINE BAKER. I am the wife of John Baker, a policeman-I searched
the prisoner, and found two shillings on her-when I first went into the room,
she said, "I have some more about me, for God's sake let me go"-I gave
the two shillings to Sergeant Kelly.
JOHN KELLY (police-sergeant, P 22). I received these two shillings from
Baker.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This half-crown and these two shillings are counterfeit.
Prisoner's Defence. I had no idea it was bad money.
GUILTY. Aged 20.-Confined Six Months.
