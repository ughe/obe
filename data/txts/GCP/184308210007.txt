ALICE WOODING. I am the wife of James Wooding, a chandler, in Frith-
street. About a fortnight before the 14th of June, the prisoner, who was a
stranger, called, and asked if I would buy some butter--he said it was very
excellent Dorset butter-I told him to call again, which he did in a week or
fortnight, and on the 14th he brought a pan of butter into the shop- the top of it
looked very good I was busy, and did not examine it further I bought it on
the strength of his assurance, and paid him 108. 2d. for it-I cut it next morn-
ing, and found the top was very good Dorset, but underneath was very bad-I
cannot call it any thing but cart grease--we made no use of it-the good butter
did not extend above half an inch–I was obliged to throw it away-it smelt
very had, and was quite a different colour.
Cross-examined by Mr. Horey. Q. You saw him, and told him to call again?
4. Yes-it was the first time he said it was Dorset butter-I asked him the
first and second time if it was good- I did not weigh it, being busy-he said
he would warrant it good-if it had been good I should not have cared where it
came from.
Court. Q. Did he say who he was ? A. He said on each occasion that

asked who he was travelling for he had represented himself as a traveller
this conversation was not on the 14th–I did not know Mr. Christopher-he
