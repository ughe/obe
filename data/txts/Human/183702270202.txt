   Prisoner.  On what business did I come? Witness.  To have your trowsers mended—my husband said he would not be paid for the trowsers as it was so small a trifle, and then you said you would send for gin. ISABELLA GROVE. I live with my brother, in Lower Marsh, Lambeth. I was in the shop last, about half-past nine o'clock—I left the shop about ten minutes, and the prisoner was left alone—I left with Mr. Cartwright—I went up stairs, and he went out—I returned in about ten minutes—I then remained all the evening—the prisoner was alone in the shop when I returned—no one entered the shop during the ten minutes that I was up stairs—I was there when Cartwright counted the money at half-past nine o'clock—it was all right.
   Prisoner. Q.  When you returned was I in the shop? Witness.  Yes; I left you in the shop—it was your duty to be outside that night, being Saturday, but you were in the shop—I do not recollect your being sent to the other shop to borrow a coat for a customer—I am aware that you bunt the knee of your trowsers—I saw you attempt to mend them yourself—you went up stairs to do so—you came down in a few minutes, and said you could not do them properly, and went to this woman's husband to get them mended. JOHN CARTWRIGHT re-examined.  I counted the money in the till when the prisoner was up stairs—I did not stop him before he went out, because I was not certain whether it was 5 s . or half-a-crown.
   Prisoner. Q.  When you left the shop was not I away? Witness.  Yes; you were at Sennis's—there was a customer for whom you had to go and borrow a coat—another shopman was there, and attended to the cus tomer. NOT GUILTY.

ROBERT PHILLIPS and CHARLES GELLART were indicted for stealing, on the   27th of January , 1 waistocat, value 10 s  ., the goods of Richard Scott , the master of the said Robert Phillips ; and JOHN STEDMAN for feloniously receiving the said goods, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c. RICHARD SCOTT. I live in Westminster-bridge-road. Phillips was an errand boy in my service—he left on the 23rd of January—I did not miss any thing till the Thursday—I missed one waistcoat—I am certain that is mine. WILLIAM BRYMER PRATT. I am a shopman to John Richards of Westminster-bridge-road, a pawnbroker. A waistcoat similar to this was pledged on the 27th of January, and taken out on the 30th—it was pledged in the name of John Stedman, by the prisoner Stedman.
   Cross-examined by MR. JONES . Q.  Did you know him before? A.  Yes; I have known him some years—his parents are respectable people—I have never known any thing against him before—he did not tell me he had received it from a boy named Gilbert—Mr. Scott lives nearly opposite our shop.
