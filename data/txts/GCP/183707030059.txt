MR. PHILLIPS called
Thomas BIRD. I am the prisoner's son. I know Mr. Partridge--I was
at the Black Horse with my father on the day in question-I sold goods
to Mr. Partridge for my father to the amount of 161. 8s. in the latter end
of last year-about October-it was four ends of striped, two brown and
two black-he paid me 81. for two of them, which I receipted-two he did
not pay for-he owed me 81. 88.-I remember being at the Black Horse
with him-I saw this kerseymere produced-Mr. Partridge wanted 58. 6d.
a yard for it-my father wanted it for 58.; and after a little conversation,
Mr. Partridge agreed he should have it at 5s.6d., and delivered it to him;
and he took it off the table and put it on the seat-my father went on
board the packet the same evening-I went with him on my oath, at
that time Partridge owed my father 88 88.

MR. BALLANTINE. Q. How old are you? 4. Near seventeen - I
accounted to my father for the 8l. paid by Mr. Partridge, on the same day
at Bristol - I cannot tell whether my father saw Mr. Partridge after the
bill was made-I do not think he did-I went into the room with them
at the Black Horse-I cannot say how long I was there_1 was there
when my father left-I left with him-I was there when Mr. Partridge
left to fetch the cloth--I saw all that passed- remained in the room after
Mr. Partridge was gone-I went with my father to the vessel - we did not
go straight there—I did not lose sight of him the whole time he left the
cloth with a person to sell, and the person went away with it I do not
know him-I never saw him before I do not know where we met him
it was in the street, and afterwards we went to a house I cannot say what
house-- I do not know where it was—we stopped there for some time,
waiting for the person who took the cloth to return when my father met
this person he appeared to know him I have been in Court during the
examination of the witnesses for the prosecution when my father went
out he told the waiter he should return in about half an hour he met this
man very shortly, and then began the conversation about selling it - I can.
not tell what became of the cloth-my father did not return to the Black
Horse—the ship was to go off the same evening--this was Thesday even-
ing-my father was taken on the following Monday morning-he was not
on board the ship the whole of the time.
MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I suppose you are not very conversant with Ame-
rican Vessels? A. No I have been in London a fortnight-I am a
Gloucestershire boy- I cannot tell the names of the streets and houses
the man did not return to my father with the cloth the cloth came to
about 71., and Mr. Partridge owed my father 81. 8.-I did not know
Where to find him to get the money-it was about eight or nine months
after I sold him the cloth that I met with him in London-I am pre-
pared to say that 161. 8s. was the price of the cloth, and that he paid
only 81.
Prisoner. The cause of my waiting to see Partridge was to see the
other kerseymeres which he expected from Balham-bill-I am a stranger,
and have no friends in London.
GUILTY. Aged 38.--Transported for Seven Years.
