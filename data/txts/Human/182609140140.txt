   JOSEPH SAWYER. I am a horse-patrol. At half-past nine o'clock, on the day in question, I was on the Bath-road - Mr. Cox came and informed me of the robbery - I went in pursuit, and overtook Trindell in the road to Staines - I asked him what had happened, and where the girls were who he had been in company with; he said they were gone down the lane with three gipsies; I went down the lane, and found them with the gipsies; I turned back, and brought Trindell back to the place; the landlord said,"Here is a man here who owns to taking the box, and giving it to another man;" Hazell then came out and said he had given it to Faris; one Goodman, who was bound over, but is not here, brought me the box in the morning.
   WILLIAM COURTNEY. I keep the Punch Bowl at Colnbrook. The young women came to my house - the three prisoners came in, and when the waggon was coming they brought out the box - I went and asked them if they had got all right; they said, Yes.
   MARY ROBINSON. I live at the Punch Bowl. I saw Faris come out with the box, and go through the turnpike-gate - it was a small deal trunk.
 Cross-examined by Mr. BRODRICK. Q. How far was the turnpike? A. Close by.
 MARY ANN HUMPHRIES. I was with Williams, and had 15s. in the box; she has stated the truth. I saw Hazell bring the box to Faris - we went home with the horse-patrol; and the next morning the box was brought there by  William Goodman.

 1688.  WILLIAM SANDERS was indicted for embezzling, on the 27th of July , 5 shillings and a sixpence.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   WILLIAM JOHN HADDON. I keep the  Bell brew-house, at Tottenham; the prisoner was in my employ in June last; it was his duty to deliver beer and receive money for me. On the 27th of June he was sent out with some beer; there were fourteen firkins, but I cannot tell how many casks - I had no customer of the name of Parker, to my knowledge, he never gave me any account of any money received from a Mrs. Parker.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ANDREWS. Q. Did you, with your own hand, measure the fourteen firkins of beer he took out? A. No; it was the duty of my clerk to do it; it is my clerk's business to count the casks, and when he returns, he gives an account of it - I took him before the Justice, about this 5s. 6d., without asking him about the matter; but this 5s. 6d. was not the only ground of complaint I had against him - I had received a letter before I went to the Magistrate.
   JAMES SHARP. I am clerk to Mr. Haddon. I remember the prisoner going out on the 27th of June; I can tell by the book what beer he took out - it is put down every morning, and he signs it, and gives an account when he returns. On that day he took out twenty-five casks of different dimensions, containing beer and ale; there was some of it be-spoke; he brought back an account at night - but there was a deficiency of nine gallons of table ale - he did not account for any thing delivered to Mrs. Parker, her name is not mentioned, she had never been a customer of ours.
 Cross-examined. Q. You take an account when he goes out and when he comes in? A. Yes; it was not till a letter came from Mrs. Paker that we found there was some deficiency - he was authorized to sell beer to any body, if he brought the money; but he gave no account of this.
 Mr. ADOLPHUS. Q. He accounted for the whole of the casks, but did he account for all the money? A. No, there was a deficiency of 5s. 6d.; it was a nine gallon cask of table-ale; which I afterwards saw at Mrs. Parker's - he accounted for all but that - he had accounted for the casks sent out, so that it was a deficiency, in the value of the beer, of 5s. 6d.; I did not discover this till the letter came a fortnight afterwards.
   ELIZABETH ANN PARKER. I live at Hackney. On the 27th of June I purchased, of the prisoner, a nine gallon cask of beer, for which I paid him 5s. 6d. in silver - I believe it was five shillings and a sixpence; there were some shillings - this is the receipt he gave me (read) - I had never before bought any beer of Mr. Haddon or of the prisoner.
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you certain you did not give him a piece of gold, and receive the change? A. Yes; we were out of beer; my servant saw him, and we bought of him; I heard him tell the Magistrate it must have arisen from mistake my being an accidental customer.
