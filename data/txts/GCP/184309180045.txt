Prisoner's Defence. I changed the cheque for Mrs. Smith; she told me to
pledge the portmanteau, and bring her the ticket and money, because she wanted
in make up 61. that very day, to prepare for Mr. Smith's trial the next day.
I did so, and brought the 88. and duplicate to her ; she said she had not then
got enough. I could not change the next cheque that day; she and Henry
Bush met me in the evening, near the place where I lived once before, the cof-
for-shop where I had my clothes ; she said she did not care about losing 10s.
of 1l. by the three cheques, because she wanted the money directly; she told
me she must have money, and she would pay me very handsomely for it, if I
could get the money, and make up the amount for her, so I went to the person
in Oxford-street for change, and brought her the two silver watches, and il.
12s.6d., which made up the amount; she said, when I brought her the other.
31. she would give me 108. for my trouble; that is the cheque for 81. which
has been produced. I was not to go to the bankers', as it is dated the 29th,
and they would not pay it till it was due. I was taken into custody on the
29th, 80 I could not go. Immediately I was taken into custody I sent to
her to justify me; they would not let me go*; they brought word that she
was out; the Magistrate, Mr. Coombe, sent a summons down to lock her
up. Next day the landlord where she resided, in Great Ormond-street, came
up, and said she did not sleep there that night, that she had left the two
watches, and run away without paying her rent. I did not know the cheques
were forged. I have received a good many cheques since I have been here.
I was commissioned from various merchants in Paris, and had various kinds
of cheques. I am the loser. I gave her the balance. I believe she and Henry
Bush made it up between them to say that they had better give it to me,
because they knew I was a foreigner, and easily deceived. I brought them
their money directly. (See the case of E. A. Taylor, page 873.)
