MUSGROVE, Mayor.
John Allnutt and another, his masters. 2nd Count, stealing a bank post-bil
for 151. 10.; the moneys of John Allnutt and another, his masters.
MR. PARRY conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM LUKE Evans. I am a clerk in the public drawing-office of the
Bank of England. On 9th July the prisoner came and presented this bank
post-bill for payment produced)-it is a seven-days' bill, and was due when
presented there was a crowd round the counter, and I requested him in a
general way to receipt the bill with his name and address--he went to a desk
which is there for that purpose, and a few minutes after presented it again
with the address on it-it now has, "Received the within amount; A.
Churchill, 3, Park-place, New North-road"- the bill is endorsed payable to
Allnutt and Arbouin, and that is signed, "Henry Bolton”-it occurred to
me that the endorsement was not correct—the word "Bearer" is not distinct,
and knowing Messrs. Allnutt's to be a London firm, my impression was that
it should have come through a banker-I conferred with Mr. Hodgkinson.
the principal of the office, and he came forward and asked the prisoner if it
was his bill-he said he was sent by a party to receive the money-Mt.
Hodgkinson asked him by whom he was sent-be said by a person in a coffee
house, in Bishopsgate-street, and that he was desired to write that person's
name on it-Mr. Hodgkinson sent for an officer, and the prisoner immediately
fainted-the officer came, took him to a private room, and I left him in his
charge- I had not looked at the bill when I told him to receipt it-when I
received it again, the receipt was apparently recently written-it is payable
to Henry Bolton, or order.
Cross-examined by Mr. Bodkin. Q. There were several persons about?
A. Yes; and I gave a general direction to them all, “Be so good as to write
your name and address"-I did not see him write the address, his back was
towards me-1 did not notice anything about him which betokened ignorance
of the mode of proceeding.
George RUSSELL (City-policeman). I am occasionally stationed at the
Bank. On 9th July, about 2 o'clock, I was called to the public drawing.
office, and found the prisoner there he had fainted-I received this bill from
a gentleman in the Bank, and when the prisoner came to, I asked him bow
he came possessed of the bank post-bill he had just presented for payment-
he said a person had given it him outside a coffee-shop in Bishopsgate-street,
who he had never seen before, and did not know, and he was to meet that
party in the evening, and give him the 15l. and retain the 108. himself, and
he was to write the name of "A. Churchill, 3, Park-cottages, Islington," on
the bill-I saw the bill was payable to Allnutt and Arbouin, and I asked him
whether he was not in their employ-he hesitated at first, and then said be
was- I asked whether he did not see when he wrote the name on the back
of the bill, that it was payable to his masters-be said not before he got to
the Bank, and that was the reason he fainted-I took him to the station.
HENRY BOLTON. I am a licensed-victualler, at Answorth, near Birming.
ham. In June, I owed Allnutt and Arbouin 151. 10s.- I received this letter
from them produced), and in consequence sent up the Bank Post-bill which
has been produced this endorsement. “Henry Bolton," is mine- am
credited for it in my bank-book (produced), on 2nd July-I bank at the
Birmingham Branch Bank-I sent it in a letter to London, and have never
received any acknowledgment of it.
Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. Did you post it yourself? A. NO;
I gave it to my daughter to post.
