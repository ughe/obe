176. WILLIAM HEATH was indicted for assaulting Mary Ann Has-
sel, on the 21st of October, at Charlton, near Greenwich.
MARY ANN HASSELL. I am a single woman, and live with my parents
in Bell-street, Greenwich. On the 21st of October, I went with a friend
to Charlton fair-I saw the prisoner there he asked me to go into a show
- he is an artillery-man I went into the show, and, when we came out,
he asked me to go and have something to drink-we had some rum and
water at a public-house-he was quite a stranger to me, but he said he
would see me home, and we walked arm-in-arm till we got to Blackheath
--he then threw me down-he had kissed me before he threw me down-
I did not resist when he kissed me, but I resisted when he threw me down
- I called to him to let me get up, but he would not I called for assis-
tance-he did not take any liberties with me of any kind, but he threw me
down, and put his hand on my mouth-he was taken immediately, and
has been in custody ever since-Mr. Pershouse came to my assistance--I
did not know him before he came up, but he knew me I had been thrown
down against my consent.
Prisoner. Q. Were we not dancing and waltzing together, and humming
a tune as people do when they dance without mu sic? 4. I was
not; but he was singing to himself as he walked along with me we were
not dancing as we came along-it was not by accident that I fell-be
took me round the neck, twisted me round, and threw me down-he
proposed to go the most private way, but I thought I had a friend in
him.
WILLIAM PERSHOUSE. I am a cow-keeper and farmer, and live at
Greenwich. I did not know this witness till she recognized me I know
her father, I was coming home in my gig, and saw two persons come
across the road, making to this spot-I looked back and saw the prisoner
and this witness on the ground-she screamed out, “For God's sake, come
to my assistance"-I must have passed just by them, within half a dozen
vards, as I had just come out of the main road under the fence, but my
horse had shyed at something at the time I saw the persons running to
the place : had it not been for that, I could not have passed without
seeing the prisoner and the woman-she might have screamed at the time
I passed without my hearing her, as my gig made a noise-the moment !
stopped I heard her-I went to her, and found her dress very disorderly,
her belt was undone, and the back of her clothes very disorderly-the
prisoner's dress was not at all undone.
WILLIAM YEOMAN. I am a hair-dresser. I was on the heath with my
wife and a friend when we heard the screams I ran across with my
friend, and found the prisoner and the female on the ground.
Prisoner's Defence. I never had any intention of acting in the way
that is alleged against me- I considered, by the way she conducted her-
self, that she was a very imprudent woman.
GUILTY. Aged 31.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury.
Judgment Respited,
