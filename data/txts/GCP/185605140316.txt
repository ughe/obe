SALOMONS, Mayor.
551. JOHN FENNELL and SARAH HOWES, stealing 2 blankets,
4 sheets, and other articles, value 188., and 3506. in money; the property
of William Horne.
Messrs. SLEIGH and THOMPSON conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM HORNE. I am an excavator, and live in North Woolwich-road.
I resided there with my wife in April last I have been married about
eleven years, I had some money, which I bad saved up-it was in a box at
the foot of the bed, in the bedroom up stairs, where I slept-I cannot say
exactly the amount, I believe nearly 4001., it was all in gold, in sovereigns
and half sovereigns-on 21st April I left home between 8 and 9 o'clock in
the morning; I went to Chatham to seek for employment-I returned
between 9 and 10 o'clock in the evening-I found my wife was not at
home, and I missed my money and my clothes there was some little money
left, about 301. - the box was always kept locked, and my wife kept the key
of it-I missed all my wife's wearing apparel, and some blankets, and sheets
and other things, and five shirts, these articles which were brought from
Liverpool - I can identify this patchwork quilt, and this towel I know
these sheets and blankets are mine I can speak to them as being my pro
perty--my wife did not return that night. I went over to the Borough,
where I expected to find her.--I never saw Howes but once before, which
was at my wife's father's honse at Maidstone I knew Fennell before-he
had lodged with me for two years I dare say, but he had left me a good
while, for more than a year-I saw Howes the next day in the Borough-
I asked if she knew where my wife was; she said she did, and she said she
would bring her to me, and I should see her, which she did not do-she
told me times and times over that she would bring her to me, but she did
not do it- she said the same again till I got tired of it-I saw her seven or
eight days, and she always repeated the same words to me-I offered her
5l. if she would show me the house where my wife was, that I could see
her-I asked her if there was a man with my wife-she said no, there was
no man with her, and she asked whether I thought there was I said I did
not know; I could not say exactly whether there was or not, but I had a
suspicion the last conversation I had with Howes was on the Monday
before I gave her into custody-she then told me if I would come the next
morning she would take me to her on the Tuesday I saw her again, and
gave her in charge of the police when she was given in charge I and the
policeman went with her to a house in Brook-street, West-square- the first
house we went to we did not go in-when we did get into a house, there
was a great deal of conversation-Howes said that my wife was staying
with Mrs. Hughes, and likewise Fennell—we could not find them—Mrs.
Hughes's husband was there_Mrs. Hughes said that Howes had done all
the mischief, and wanted to get all the trouble off her, and Mrs. Hughes's
husband said if Howes came there any more he would throw her out of the
window-Howes said she did not want to come there, she did not know
that she had done anything wrong after that I made further search for
my wifeI did not find her-I never saw her-I gave the case into the
policeman's hands--this gold watch and chain are mine.
