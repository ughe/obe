HENRY STANLEY, coffee stall keeper. On October 2 I was at my stall outside Upton Park Station. I saw the three prisoners with Mr. Thomas. I saw Hill come out of the station first of all, a few minutes before the others. Then I saw Thomas come out with Stowe and Hayes, and they were having an argument. Then I saw Thomas try to get away on to a motor bus and Stowe hang on to him and stopped him. While I was serving a customer they got back to the station. Hill remained outside. I started to go to the station and Hill come across. I thought something was the matter. Hill said, "Don't put that b——g flap on my head," so I put it up again and went towards the station, bat I turned round and saw Hill on the counter trying to gat inside, so I came back. Then the other pri soners came up and stopped talking at the corner of the stall. Stowe was getting on to Hill about not being in the station to help them. Stowe said, "We misted a f—g chance." There was a little con versation, and they said, "We had better set away from here or we shall hear more of this." Then they went off down Queen's Road. My coffee stall is about 40 yards from the station; I identified the three man.
 Cross-examined by Stowe. My stall is in Queen's. Square, by the fountain. I could see anybody coming out of the station from my stall. It is facing the station. You were under the electric light on this occasion, and were quite discernable. Mr. Thomas struck you with his umbrella, in the middle of the road. (Stowe said he hoped witness was not carrying out his threat made in September about having his own back.) Mr. Justice Ridley cautioned prisoner to be careful in making accusations.
 Cross-examined by Hayes. When at the police station on Satur  day night I said I do not know your name. I said I knew Hill and Stowe from the first. I do know you well. I have not known you to interfere with me. I did not see you with Thomas, but you came out of the station after this last train and went round the square. You did ask me for a herd-boiled egg.
 Detective-sergeant FREDERICK STEVENS, Plaistow. At 8.30, on Oc tober 26, I was in Green Street with Detective Edwards and others, and saw the three prisoners. I said, "We are police officers, and we are going to arrest you on suspicion of being concerned together in robbing Mr. Thomas, and assaulting him on the 2nd of this month." Hill said, "What are you going to take me for? I had nothing to do with it." Hayes made no reply. On the way to the station Stowe said, "Highway robbery, governor? That sounds a bit serious." At the station they were placed with other men. Hill and Hayes were identified by the prosecutor. Stowe was identified by the railway porter. They made no replay to the charge. Imme diately after, while waiting to be put into the cells, Stowe said, "If that man lost 13s. that night we ought to get 10 years' each; if not, directly he steps outside this station he ought to meet with the severest accident that he ever had in his life."
