WILLIAM JOHNSON. I had the misfortune of being convicted two years ago
next October for a misdemeanor respecting a check-I suffered three months
for that I have known the prisoner by the names of Pool, Ford, and Pitch-
ford-on the 2nd of July he engaged my son to ferment some molasses-I
have been at his house, No. 22, Wellington-street, many times--I have seen
sacks there several times--the first I saw was about four months ago they
were in the back parlour, and were marked, “Truman and Hanbury," and
about six weeks ago I saw some sacks in the loft at the back of the house in
Wellington-street--they were marked “Truman and Hanbury" - the prisoner
offered to sell me them for 1s. 6d. a piece- I would not buy them-he after-
wards offered me 6d. a piece to take the names out of some sacks that were
in the yard I have seen him at a house in Playhouse-yard-I never saw
him at work there-I have left him to work at the still there-I took his horse
and cart home one day while he worked there when I saw the sacks, about
four months ago, I said I thought it very imprudent to have such sacks lying
on the floor, as he was liable to be detected by the police or the excise-he
said he bought them for 1s. a piece-I said there was a society for prosecuting
persons having sacks, and he would be liable to be called up to say who he
bought them of.

a measure, but I pleaded guilty-it was about getting some pigs and giving
a false check-Mr. Hill's partner died suddenly, and he owed me a good deal

WILLIAM JOHNSON, Jun. I am the witness's son ; I am a blacking-maker,
and live at No. 7, Wellington-street, Kingsland-road. I know the prisoner
by the names of Pool and Ford I have worked for him to ferment molasses
for the still in his work in Playhouse-yard—I continued to work for him till
Saturday, the 4th of July-on the Monday afterwards I went with the prisoner
with a horse and cart to Playhouse-yard, but I did not go into the premises
while I was at work at the prisoner's I saw two sacks with the name of “Tru-
man and Hanbury" on them, brought from the cart into the premises, with
yeast in them I was with my father at the prisoner's residence, No. 22,
Wellington-street-I saw the prisoner there, with some sacks in his hand with
the name of " Truman and Hanbury" on them-he said to my father, “I will
give you 6d. a sack if you can take the names out of these"- that was about
six weeks or two months since.
