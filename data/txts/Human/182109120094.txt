   JANE ELCOM. On the 6th of August I was stopping at a friend's, who keeps the Hare and Hounds, public-house, Baltick-street. About half-past eight o'clock in the morning, the room door was broken open, and my box containing this property taken out; the box was brought home empty that night, I suspected the prisoners. They did not belong to the house.
   JOHN BARTLETT. I was sent for, and went to a house in Ivy-street, St. Giles's, and found these things carelessly thrown down. I took Mary Maloney and Lyons in custody, they were admitted as evidence. I then took Maroney, he told me that he planned to rob the house, that he and three more boys took the box to Finnerty's lodgings. Mrs. Maloney lives in the parlour where I found the things. Finnerty was taken a few days after.
   JOHN DAVIS. On the morning of the robbery, I accompanied Bartlett to the house and found the property.
   MARY MALONEY. I live in Ivy-street, St. Giles's, near George-street, I live in the cellar. Lyon's called me to give her a light in the passage, I came up, there was a barrow in the passage, and a blue box between the handles of it; whether Maroney or Lyons took it into Lyons's room, I cannot say. The box was opened, and Maroney took the things out, tied them up, and took them away; he said, he got them from the Hare and Hounds, public-house. I told Lyons they were stolen, and it was a shame, and told the neighbours. She took the box to Elcom's after it was emptied - they took all the things out, and took them away. Finnerty was in the passage, but not in the room; next morning, the articles stated in the indictment were found in the passage where the barrow stood.
   LEWIS ROBERTS. I apprehended Finnerty between twelve and one o'clock at night, on the 9th.
 FINNERTY'S Defence. Mrs. Maloney said, she would have nothing to do with it, but she got half a pint of gin for it.
 MARONEY. -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 FINNERTY. -    NOT GUILTY.

 1222.  MARY JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of September , 10 s. 11 d., in monies numbered, the property of Job Mulcock , from his person.
   JOB MULCOCK. On the 2d of September, about two o'clock in the morning, I picked the prisoner up by Westminster-abbey; she asked me to go home with her, I said, I had no objection. We went to No. 8, Pye-street, on the first floor. I staid there about three hours; she called me - I said I was not going to get up - she left the room. I awoke afterwards, and missed my money from my coat pocket - I gave her 2 s. - I was sober - I live at Knightsbridge. I found I was locked in the room, and could not get out for near an hour, when a girl let me out. I told the landlord of it - he made very light of it. I stopped there till eight o'clock. When I was going out, she came up the street as drunk as possible. I told her I should get a constable - I fetched one, and we met her coming out of the house. I am sure she is the woman, nobody but her was in the room.
   BENJAMIN TIMBRELL. Between seven and eight o'clock on Sunday, the prosecutor fetched me to Pye-street. I saw the prisoner coming out of the house; he said she was the woman. I took her, and found one shilling and some halfpence on her; he said he could swear to one penny piece among them.
 JOB MULCOCK. I know this penny-piece was in my pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. I took the penny-piece of Mr. Thompson, of Charing-cross.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1223.  THOMAS JOHNSON was indicted for stealing on the 4th of August , one copper, value 18 s., the goods of Samuel Shelly.
   JOHN SMITH. I am foreman to Mr. Shelly. This copper stood at his door - I know it to be his.
   JOHN BARNARD. I was left in care of the shop, and saw the prisoner two doors off with it on his back, I stopped him; he said a man gave him sixpence to carry it.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I was employed by another person to take it to Great Queen-street, and he would give me sixpence. I did not consider what I was doing.
     GUILTY. Aged 50. Transported for Seven Years.
 Second Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1224.  HENRY JOHNSON was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of August , one shirt, value 3 s.; one coat, value 2 s., and one handkerchief, value 6 d., the goods of John Kelchea.
   JOHN KELCHEA. I am a labourer. I was looking for work, and went to a public-house in Charles-street, St. Giles's, to ask for a lodging; the prisoner came up and said he knew where I could get one at an honest place, he took me to Church-street, St. Giles's- the woman of the house was out. I thought I would shave myself, put my bundle on the table while I shaved, and he went out with my shirt, coat, and handkerchief. He was stopped and the duplicates found on him.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me take them out - A. I missed them the moment he was gone.
   HENRY WILLIAM POWELL. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Bridge-road, Lambeth. On the 11th of August, the prisoner pawned a shirt for 3 s. - my duplicate was found on him.
   PHILIP RILEY. On the 11th of August, I took him in charge, and found three shillings and the duplicate on him of a waistcoat, shirt, and jacket, all pawned that day.
 (Shirt produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
