WILLIAM HENRY WARRE. I am a pawnbroker, of Skinner-street, Snow.
hill. I have known the prisoner five or six years--I do not particularly
recollect seeing him at my shop on 29th Oct.; but on that day two gold
watches were pledged at my shop, to which this ticket ( produced) refers it is
in the name of John Purey, for two gold watches, pledgedfor 51.-the prisoner
was there on 3rd Nov., and pawned three silver watches for 41. 108., and on
24th Nov. a silver watch for 17. 58., and on 7th Jan, five silver watches for
71.--the watch pawned on 7th Jan. had been previously pawned-I do not
recollect seeing the prisoner at my shop on 7th Jan.; he merely paid the
interest on the watches, and got a fresh ticket.
Cross-examined. Q. What is the value of the watehes ! A. They are
pledged for 301.; the selling price in the market in England would be about
351., including the duty, as near as I can say there are a vast number of
these watches in this country-the prisoner is a watchmaker, and I always
considered him a respectable man he produced his card at the first pledging,
which I have lost; but it stated that he was a dealer or importer-I only knew
him in the course of business.

on 20th Sep.--I have not the slightest doubt of him—this produced) is the
