   Cross-examined.  In all diseases there must be a beginning, which is de veloped in course of time, there must be a beginning of everything—it does not always follow that in the case of a person affected with epilepsy that the fits increase in violence after a time; a man may be seized with a violent epileptic seizure without any premonitory symptoms; there is no rule even as to that; he may be seized with a violent convulsive attack without any premonitory symptoms—a person having an epileptic seizure and a week passing, there may be no trace in his countenance to show that he had had an epileptic attack; and another attack may not come on for six months—the attacks are very irregular and uncertain, and in the interval there may be no indication whatever of the person being subject to that disease.
