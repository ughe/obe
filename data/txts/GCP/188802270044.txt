Re-examined. When we found a counterfoil in our clerk's writing, it was
not usual to write to persons all over London-he was guaranteed for
1001.--that will not cover his defalcations; we have applied to the society,
but we can only recover a year and a half-Mr. Sandy was quite broken
down; he did not keep the books himself, three or four people kept them
-the prisoner kept his books-Mr. Dean and Mr. M.Marne were the
auditors--this is a parish cemetery of eight acres, it was opened in 1861
--I should know the price paid for a grave in perpetuity--21. 28. would
be the price for a parishioner, and when I saw 21. 28. down in the counter-
foil it would be assumed that it was a parishioner's grave, but being a
non-parishioner's it was 61. 68.-being entered at 21. 28. made me believe
it was a parishioner's grave.
CHARLES WILSHIRE. I am clerk to the Ealing Burial Board - I pro-
duce the counterfoil receipt-book and cash-book, and the prisoner's private
memorandum-book of payments into the bank, and the weekly banking
book-I have been carefully through them all, and have compared the
entries one with the other, and they all three agree-the entry on the
counterfoil receipt of April 11th, 1887, is Mrs. Slaymaker, 21. 28., and the
cash-book is 21. 28. he has entered that he received it on the 11th I do
not know his writing on April 7th he has put down 21. 118. 6d. as
received from Mrs. Gale, and the cash-book is the same-71. 38. would be
the charge to a non-parishioner-11. 108. is entered in the receipt-book
from Benjamin Myring, and it is the same in the cash-book-on October
15th, 118. is put down as received from George William Reeves, 116.
would be the charge for an ordinary grave, not for a parishioner, and
the cash-book has 118.-he had to keep a register of graves, a cash-book,
and a receipt book.
Cross-examined. In addition to that he had to make up the weekly
account and send it in to the bank on these slips-until I filled in the
21. 28. there was nothing to show that J. Slaymaker was & parishioner,
because the price of the grave was left blank-I kept that book-my
accounts have not been audited - I don't know whether it was the
auditor's duty to ask for this book - the prisoner did not call on me
and say that he had heard something was up, and offer to go through
the books with me, nor did he ask me to let him look at the books-he
has never had an opportunity of going through them with me.
By the COURT. I asked him to make entries in the case of Annie
Elizabeth Gale, on 7th April, and I filled in the amount which he had
received I did not fill in anything in the case of Benjamin Myring on
May 7th in the book, nor did I make any alteration in the case of George
William Reeves; the entry is 118., there is no 21. 88. there-it was his
business to make the entries so as to make the Board know whether the
persons were parishioners or not-they could not find out by the book
that the person had paid a perpetuity fee, and not only 21. 28.
