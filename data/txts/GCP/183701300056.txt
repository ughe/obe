538. AARON LEVY was indicted for stealing on the 9th of January.
1 pair of half-boots, value 48., the goods of George Green.
JAMES Cocklin. On the 9th of January I saw the prisoner by the shop
of Mr. George Green, in Shoreditch-he went to the side of the door, took
a pair of boots, and ran away-I pursued him, I lost sight of him once, but
caught sight of him again with the boots in his possession--he threw them
down-I took them up, pursued and took him.
DAVID Evans. I am a policeman. I was in Shoreditch, and took the
prisoner, with these boots, from Cocklin.
GEORGE Green. These are my boots--they have my initials stamped
on them.
GUILTY. Aged 12.-Confined Six Weeks; Fourteen Days Solitary.

539. ELIZA ROBINS was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of Novem-
ber, 2 rings, value 123. ; 1 brooch, value 58.; 2 ear-ring drops, value 38.;
1 ear-ring, value 1s. ; 1 piece of cornelian, value ls. ; 1 necklace, value
4d.; and 1 piece of copper coin called a twopence; the goods and monies
of John Samuel Hittinger.
MARTHA ANN HITTINGER. I am the wife of John Samuel Hittinger.
We keep wine vaults in the Broadway, Westminster - the prisoner
lived with me seven months, to nurse the children-I only had her for a
time-I paid her weekly, and boarded her-I kept this property in a
drawer, in my own bed-room, in a box, which was always locked-I gave
her my keys to give to her master, when I was going out; and when I came
back she said she had not given them to her master, as he was busy--this
key was on the bunch with others; and that is when I think she took
the things-she left me on the 29th of November-I missed the property
on the 7th of December--this ring and necklace were brought to me by :
witness-they are mine the prisoner's brother brought her to me with
Mrs. Plumbley-the prisoner denied taking them; and desired Mrs.
Plumbley to go and fetch them to me-I accused Mrs. Plumbley of en
couraging her; and she said all she had seen were the necklace and ring.
LYDIA PLUMBLEY. I am married. I have known the prisoner four
years—she lost her mother, and I took her in out of charity, when she left
her place I went to look at her box for her dirty things, and saw this neck-
Jace laying there—when she came in I said, " What do you do with these
beads ?"-she said, " The housekeeper in Frith-street gave me them"-she
brought me this ring--I laid them on the shelf, and said I would throw them
behind the fire, because they were not proper for a young girl like her to wear.
JANE SEWELL. The prisoner came to live servant with me; and she
came down one day and said, “Here is a ring the boy wants me to give
him"- I took it and put it down my stays, and lost it: and the next day,
said I had lost it-she came down and gave me a brooch-I said, “Where
did you get it?"-she said, “It was my mother's, but I will sell it to
you"-I said " If it was my mother's I would not sell it"-I delivered it
to the policeman.
WILLIAM CLIPTON. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner, and
inquired of her about a brooch and mourning ring-she said she sold the
brooch to Jane Sewell for 1s. 60.--I went to Craven-street, and found Sewell,
who delivered up the brooch which the prosecutrix swears to-I have the
necklace and one ring-Sewell lost the ring which she had.
(Property produced and sworn to.)
