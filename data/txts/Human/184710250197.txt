 2494. WILLIAM LEE was indicted for unlawfully obtaining, by false pretences, 480 lbs. weight of white of white lead, value 6 l  .; 12 gallons of varnish, 8 l  .; and other articles, the goods of Augustus Ratcliffe, the younger ; with intent to cheat and defraud him of the same.
   MR. PARNELL conducted the Prosecution. AUGUSTUS RATCLIFFE, JUN. I am a window-glass and lead-merchant, in Chichester-place, Gray's-Inn-road. On the 7th of Aug. the prisoner came to my shop, and said he wanted to see Mr. Soar, my traveller—I said, "He is not in; shall I do as well"—he said he knew Mr. Soar from his calling on his former master, Mr. O. Donnel, who lived at Chelsea; that he had left him, through his doing some business for Mr. Richard Day, and had now started on his own account; that he had got some houses now to paint, I think he said five, and he wanted a lot of colour and turps  to do them; would I supply them—he presented to me this bill ( produced )—he left it with me—it is drawn by Marson upon Richard Day, Esq., of Eltham—I said I would take the bill, if he would let me take the particulars, and let me inquire at the Bank if it was all right—I took the particulars of the bill and made inquiries; and in consequence saw him again about the 12th—he then gave me the order, which was for a quantity of white lead and other things—this is the invoice ( producing it ), I took the bill—he said, "Be sure and send them over directly, because the workmen are standing still"—the bill is for 24 l . 1 s . 9 d .
   COURT. Q.  It was the bill that induced you to send the things? A  Yes—I parted with a little varnish afterwards on another pretence.
   MR. PARNELL. Q.  How long after did you see him again? A.  About the 24th—he then brought a small phial containing some varnish—he said that was part of the first quantity I had sold him, that he had tried it on some clean white paper, and found it completely spoilt the whole of it—it was too dark for it, and would I let him have a lot more, much lighter—I let him have it—there was no invoice for that—he said be sure and send them directly, as his men were standing still—I sent part of it by the Parcels' Delivery Company for the men to go on with—the bill of exchange was more than covered by the first order—the second time I trusted to his representation of having a good job, and also that he wanted it in exchange for this other varnish—he represented that he had five or six houses to paint, and had five or six men on—the whole of the varnish was worth about 5 l . 5 s .—part was sent by the Parcels' Delivery Company, and part the Tasker—I have seen it since at Mr. Scott's, an oilman's, in the Marlboro'-road, Chelsea—the bill was returned to me—I have never been able to get the money—I went to where the prisoner lived—it was a small sort of chandler's-shop, at Chelsea—I have been down to Eltham to look for Mr. Day—there were two or three policeman after him then, I could not find him at all—I found the varnish at Mr. Scott's, on the 12th or 13th of October, the day after we look the prisoner into custody—the cans are here—( produced ).
