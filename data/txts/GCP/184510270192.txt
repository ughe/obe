SIMON BENNETT. I am the prosecutor's son-he is on the Continent-
in consequence of what I heard from Harris, I had some conversation with
the prisoner-I asked him what he had received from Mr. Hay-he said,
"A cheque for 111. 58."--I said “How much did you give my mother?"
he said 41. 7s., and he gave Mr. Hay's man 5l. 188.--I said, “How much
do you make that ?"- he said, “111. 58."--I said, “How do you make
that out? it is only 10l. 58."-he did not know what to say-he seemed
very much confused, and did not seem to know how to account for the
other 11.-his father was going by-I called him in and told him of it-
he said, “I am very sorry, Mr. Bennett, but I am not surprised at it;
he is a very bad boy; I could have got him into the Post Office"-the
prisoner said he did not know how to account for the 1l.; he must have lost
it, and had reckoned it up wrong-I said I did not know what to do in it
myself, as my father was on the Continent, and I was not in the business
I consulted with Mr. Harris, and we sent the prisoner away--next
morning he entered an action against us-I believe the action was not
brought by the prisoner's present attorney-I do not know.
JAMES LEWIS (police-constable L 86). I apprehended the prisoner on
the 11th of Aug-he was given into custody by Harris-he said it was
no use, he intended to bring an action against him. On the following
day, in going to the police-office, he was met by an acquaintance of his
own, who asked him what he had been doing, that he was locked up-he
said Bennett's people had locked him up last night-he told him to go
and get a solicitor-when he got a little farther, he met another person,
who asked him what he was locked up for-he said he had made a mistake
in a sovereign, and Bennett's people had locked him up.
