 1496.  JOHN CLARKE was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of June , 70 lbs. of iron, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Murray.
   CHARLES CRUTCHLEY. I live in High-street, St. Giles', and am a baker. On the 17th of June I was sitting in my shop, with the door open, about two o'clock in the morning; I saw the prisoner come out of Mr. Murray's passage, next door to me, with a flat bar and a bolt on his shoulder - I followed, and gave him in charge.
 Prisoner. Q.Did you see me go in? A. No; I asked where you was going - you said to No. 21, High-street, and I followed you; when you got to Lawrence-lane you wanted to go down; I got before you, and called the officer.
   THOMAS MURRAY. I found my side door open when I was called up, and missed articles of this description; I have bolts here to match them - I could not identify it at first, as I had not the fellow.
 Prisoner's Defence. He said he could not swear to it, and I was liberated - I met a man going up Holborn; he asked where I was going - I said I had no money to pay for a lodging; he said if I would carry these bars he would give me a pint of beer - this gentleman asked where I was going; I was so confused I did not know what I said.
     GUILTY. Aged 58. - Confined Three Months.
 1497.  JANE CLEMENTS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of June , 43 knives, value 7l.; 2 saucepans, value 10s., and 1 candlestick, value 5s., the goods of John Bennington and another.
 CHARLES ROBERTSON. I am servant to Mr.  John Bennington and Son, furnishing ironmongers. I went to a nobleman's house in Curzon-street, and received these articles to be repaired - I went to a public-house, and got tipsy; I put down my basket at the step of a door - I felt myself getting sick and ill; I laid down, and was taken by the constable - I did not see the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1498.  ELIZABETH BLACKMORE was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of June , 1 bed, value 3l.; 1 sheet, value 2s., and 1 piece of calico, value 1s., the goods of Valentine Rathbone.
   REBECCA RATHBONE. I am the wife of Valentine Rathbone; we live in John-street, St. George's. The prisoner lived next door to me - I lost the duplicates of these articles; I went to the pawnbroker's, and found they were taken out.
   WILLIAM BUTLER. I am a Police-constable. I went to the prisoner's house; I asked if she had got a bed out of pawn that morning - she said Yes; I asked her how she came to do it when she knew it did not belong to her; she said she was not aware of the consequences - I found this sheet and calico on the drawers, and on the table I found a bag; the bed was in the yard, tied up in a bit of carpet.
 Cross-examined by MR. STURGEON. Q. Did she in any way attempt to conceal them? A. No, she said she found the duplicates.
 MRS. RATHBONE. This is my bag - here is my marriage certificate in it, and thirteen duplicates.
   EDWARD EDMOND CHILD. I am a shopman to Mr. Reynold's, a pawnbroker, in High-street, Shadwell. The prisoner came to his shop on the 9th of June; she produced a duplicate of a bed, and said she wished to take it out - I got a man to take it home for her; Mrs. Rathbone, who had pawned it, came afterwards to stop it, and I produced the man who took it to the prisoner's.
 MRS. RATHBONE. These were all pawned in my name before I was married, and the marriage certificate has the same on it; I had only lived next door to her for three weeks - I suppose I dropped the bag in the street the night before these were taken out; here is only one duplicate of my ring, which is in my present name.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1499.  THOMAS BROWN was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 25th of May , 2 pairs of breeches, value 5s.; 2 pairs of trousers, value 5s.; 1 pair of drawers, value 1s.; 1 coat, value 3s.; 2 waistcoats, value 1s.; 2 shawls, value 2s.; 1 gown, value 1s.; 4 petticoats, value 1s., and 2 frocks, value 1s., the goods of Hugh Cox , which had lately before been stolen, he well knowing the same to have been stolen; against the Statute.
   BRYNE COX. I am eleven years of age. On the 25th of May I lost a 6d.; I stood crying at my mother's door - the prisoner came up, and asked what was the matter; I said I had lost 6d., which my mother gave me to get some potatoes - he said, "Never mind - bring me 4lbs. of rags, and I will give you 2d. for them;" he came into my father's passage, and I gave him my own coat and two waistcoats - he said that was not enough; I then brought my father's two pairs of breeches and two pairs of trousers; he gave me 2d. for them - I then brought him down one pair of drawers, two frocks and a gown, and he gave me 2d. more; I then brought him two petticoats and another frock, and he gave me 1 1/2d. for them - he asked me if I should have any more to-morrow; I said No - I am sure the prisoner is the man.
 HUGH COX.
