THIRD COURT.-Friday, February 14th, 1902.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
186. JOHN MAHONEY (35), Breaking and entering the dwellings
house of Ernest George Smith, and stealing an umbrella, a watch, and I
other articles, his property.
MR. JENKINS Prosecuted.
ERNEST GEORGE SMITH. I live at 86, Hewitt Road, Haringay-on
January 19th, at 3 o'clock, I left my house thoroughly secure-I returned
about 7.30, and found the front door forced open-I missed a silver
watch and chain, a gold chain without a watch, a watch stand, a silver
mug, and three silver labels of a spirit stand, value £11 108. 6d., and an
umbrella from the hall stand—I informed the police within half an hour.
WILLIAM HALL (62, Y.) On January 19th, about 9.30 p.m., I examined
No. 86, Hewitt Road, and found a dent on the door and door post, the
top screw of the hasp was broken, and the hasp inside.
FREDERICK WATTS (489, Y.) On January 19th, about 11.15, I went
with other officers and arrested the prisoner in Tarragona Road, in the
street-I took him to the station, and found on him five pawn tickets
which do not relate to this case-he was charged, but said nothing about
this case.
Cross-excamined by the prisoner. It was 9.30 when I saw you first-T
followed you to a public house about 9.40, and never lost sight of you-I
remained outside, and you went up Caledonian Road, and tried another
door-I arrested you about 11.15, when you were showing a man a chain,
in Caledonian Road.
By the COURT. I asked him what chain he was showing the man-he
said "You have made a mistake, Governor, I have got no chain."
ARTHUR HALL (631, Y.) I was present when the prisoner was
arrested-be threw this umbrella away-I picked it up and took it to the
station.
Cross-excamined. I had you in custody for five or six yards before you
Gropped the umbrella.
The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate. "I am innocent; I
bought the umbrella, three pawn tickets, and a silk handkerchief, for 268.,
of a man I had seen before. He handed in a written statement to the
same effect.
Guilty. He then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction at North London
Sessions on July 19th, 1898, in the name of George Edwards. Several
other convictions were proved against him, and there was a further
indictment for housebreaking. Five years penal servitude.
The Jury commended the police.
