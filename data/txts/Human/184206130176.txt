WILLIAM LEWIS KETCHER. I lived in Acorn-street, Bishops gate. I was at the Nag's Head on the 14th of May, about five minutes before five in the morning—I saw Chatter don there—he went into a part called the " bottle department"—Davis came in two or three minutes afterwards—they had some gin to drink—Davis was at the bar playing with some girls—Chatter don was in the bottle department, and I saw him put his left arm across the bar, and with his left hand take the decanter of wine—Davis was at that time playing with the girls, attracting the bar-man's attention to the right-hand part of the bar—when Chatter don took the de canter, Davis immediately went to him, and closed the door of the bottle department—Davis left first, and Chatter don afterwards—as he left I saw part of the decanter protruding from his pocket—I pursued him down Hart-street—he looked round, saw me, and ran off.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was Chatter don tipsy? A.  I should think he was rather the worse for liquor—he was not decidedly drunk—he reeled into the place. JOHN MAN WOOD( police-constable F  65.)
