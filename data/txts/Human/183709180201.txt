I am a policeman, I took the prisoners into custody. Mrs. Collins said, to Glenister before I took her, "If you hold your mouth it will be a good job for you—if you don't it will be a bad job, and you shall have your head smashed , if you split  you will suffer for it—if you had had your regulars  you would not have split "—I understand that to mean dividing the money between them.
   Mary Collins Defence , I said, "Do you mean to say I robbed the woman?"—she said "No, I don't mean to say you did, but you took good care your husband and this man should"—I said, "If you say any thing to me I will smash  your face"—it was because she did not have drink enough, that the words occurred between her and me. MARY ANN LANE. I am a hat and bonnet box-maker, and live in Queen-street. I was in the room when the prisoner and prosecutrix were drinking—they were dancing, and singing, and at times acting part of a play—they seemed very much intoxicated, and had both gin and beer they asked me to sit down—I had not been there more than half an hour before a quarrel took place between the prosecutrix and Glenister—she charged Glenister with robbing her, and called her several names which I not think proper to mention—she said, "We have walked the streets many years together, do as you have done for the money and don't stairs and she would give her the money—the prosecutirx put her hand into her packet, took out some money, and scattered it all about the room—Mrs. Glenister took it up and put it into her pocket again—I came away, and left them quarreling—I don't know who took the money out afterwards. GEORGE KIRTLAND. I am the prisoner's father—I saw these parties quarrelling about their money—the prosecutrix charged Glenister with robbing her—Glenister said, "Come down to the door and I will give you your money very speedily"—I thought it all a drunken spree , and walked away—I do not know what happened after—they were all drunk together.
 (The prisoner Kirtland received a good character.)
   JAMES COLLINS  — GUILTY. Aged 33.
   MARY COLLINS  — GUILTY. Aged 29. Confined Three Months.
   KIRTLAND  — GUILTY. Aged 29.

HENRY WARE was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of April , 9 hats, value 4 l  . 10 s  .; and 9 hat-boxes, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of John Quincey Harris , his master; and WILLIAM CLARKE for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c., to which Ware pleaded GUILTY. JOHN BOOKER. I am servant to Mr. Dempster, a pawnbroker, in Union-street, borough. I produce a hat which was pawned by the prisoner Clark. On the 22nd of June. JOHN QUINCY HARRIS. I know this hat by a private mark inside—it was stolen out of my warehouse, and not sold—I did not miss it till the 31st of August, when Ware was detected, and we searched the pawn-brokers shops—I know nothing of Clarke—it is a new hat—Ware had an opportunity of taking it—we considered him trust worthy—he has been in our employ four years.
