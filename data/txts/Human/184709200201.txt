LOUISA WILCOXON. I am the wife of Henry Wilcoxon, a brush-maker, living at 1, Wood's-court, John-street, Newington—I knew the deceased Mrs. Olds and her husband, they lived in the next house to me. On the Saturday night the 21st Aug., I was in my own back room, and was disturbed by a fall, and a cry of murder—I heard the fall first—the deceased and her husband slept in the up-stairs front room—the fall appeared to be in their back room—I heard Mrs. Olds cry "Murder"—I then ran to my door and opened it—the cries were still going on—they then appeared to come from the front room—I went up stairs into my front room, and opened the window, and then heard Mr. Olds say something to his wife, but what it was I could not understand—I heard her crying—I did not hear her say anything—it appeared to me as though he was still beating her—I did not see him—I judged by her cries that he was beating her—I did not hear the sound of blows just then—I saw their son William pass my door as I opened it—he came from his father's house—I saw him come back with a policeman—after the policeman was gone, I heard Mr. Olds beating his wife again—I heard the blows—the policeman stayed, I should think, about five minutes—I heard Mrs. Olds say, "You brute," you brute," several times, that was after I heard the sound of blows—there was no one there but the boy William, Mr. Olds, and his wife—I only heard the one fall, the other was hitting—I saw the deceased about four o'clock on the afternoon of the next day (the Sunday) in the back yard—she was very ill then—I saw her again on the Monday in the back yard, and saw marks on her throat—I did not see them on Sunday—I was nearer to her on Monday than I was on the Sunday—she had a bruise on the left temple—I had seen her about the middle of the day on the satur day—she had not any bruise on the left temple then—I was her on the Wednesday and Thursday—she was very ill each day—each day she got worse—I saw her at the sink vomiting blood—she called me to see it—I had not seen her spit blood before the Saturday—she had not been well for some time before—after the Saturday night I observed a very great change in her—it continued up to Thursday, when she died—I saw her on the morning she died, at nine o'clock, not after that—she died at half-past two—I ran in when she was dying.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were you intimate with her? A.  Yes—I generally saw her every day—she used to go out washing I believe—she did not go out to wash after this, and had not been for some weeks before, in consequence of the state of her heath—she said she had no strength—she appeared not well—she had a very bad cough—it was a very teasing cough, lasting for some minutes at a time—it appeared to strain her and try her very much—I did not see that she spat blood when she coughed—I have seen her spit after the coughing, but have not noticed whether there was—blood or not—it did not seem to be matter—she was a very sober woman—I never saw her the worse for liquor—I heard the prisoner say something to her, but what it was I could not understand—he was tipsy—when he was tipsy I could not understand him—he was tipsy on Saturday night.
