Court. Q. Were they both holding the poker when they went on the
floor together? A. Yes-as soon as Mrs. Burns stamped on him he let go
-she tried to take the poker from him, and dragged him off his chair on to
the ground, he keeping hold of the poker.
Q. While she was trying for it, did he slip off the chair on the ground!
A. She dragged him off the chair on the ground, he keeping hold of the
poker.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Were Mrs. Burns' gown-hooks torn off her back!
A. I cannot say, but she went out of the room, came back again, and said,
“Look at my gown, how it is all torn"-I saw the hooks off-I was not
drunk-there was another row afterwards, after Bridges went to sleep in the
chair-the doctor came, and said he did not think there was anything the
matter with him-I held Stacey back to prevent his attacking Mrs. Burns-
to keep him from getting into the row-I was afraid if he went near her she
would hit him I told him to keep away from Mrs. Burns- I would not let
him go near her he was not drunk, for he knew what he was doing-he had
had a drop of drink-I kept him back, as I was frightened there would be
more row-I did not keep him back after the row was over, and say I had
been the saving of Mrs. Burns' life from the violence of Bridges and others-
nothing of the sort--when I asked Mrs. Burns if she had got her thick clogs
on, she held up her foot, and said “ Yes," she had just come from the country.
Court, Q. Was Stacey taken into custody that night? A. Yes, because
they had a spite on him for fetching the doctor.
WILLIAM HAYNES. I am a painter, and am the husband of Charlotte
Haynes, and live in Essex-street. I was at the Artichoke-a contention
began by a few words mentioned to one party, and he went and told another
party-Mrs. Burns came into the room and told the deceased she wished him
to depart out of the house-he said why should he go more than any one else,
and we all said so-with that she took up the poker, had it in a striking posis
tion, and Mr. Bridges got off the chair, and took it out of her hands-Mrs.
Burns laid hold of the shovel, and put it down again-she made no attempt
with it at all-I turned round to light my pipe, and saw nothing more till
Bridges was on the ground, and Neale lifting him up.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you one of the party who had words with
Bridges and Stacey ? A. Yes-it was about betraying a secret, how a man
could keep a secret at single-stick-it was stated that Mrs. Holmes, the pri-
soner's mother, bad gone to bed ill-when Mrs. Burns came down, she
inquired what that row was about-she could not get a satisfactory answer-
she desired Bridges to go out-he was not making a disturbance particularly
-he was talking a little-she took up the poker--the deceased took it from
her-then she took up the shovel, and put it down-I did not see her attempt
to put the deceased out by the collar of his coat-I did not see her attempt
to put him out-I was lighting my pipe at the time I swear I was sober-
I was not so intoxicated as not to know what I was about-Bridges did not
call the prisoner an abominable name in my hearing-I heard Stacey call her
a b , and they retaliated one with the other-I cannot swear who used the
word first-he did, as far as I could judge.
