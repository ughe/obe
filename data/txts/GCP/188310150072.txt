946. WILLIAM HENRY GOLDSMITH (27) Unlawfully falsifying a
checkbook, the property of the Army and Navy Cooperative Stores, his
masters. Other Counts charging him with other misdemeanours.
Messrs. FULTON and GOODRICH Prosecuted; MESSRS. Besley and BURNIN
Defended.
HENRY JAMES YELDHAM. I am chief cashier at the Army and Navy
Stores, Victoria Street the prisoner was a check clerk in the grocery do
partment from 27th October, 1879, to 2nd July, 1883, when he was taken
into custody--the customer makes out the bill himself in most causes-the
duties of a check clerk ar to check the items, and see if they are properly
entered on the slip, cast up the bill, enter the ticket number of the member,
the name and the amount of the bill in the check book, stamp the bill with
& blue stamp, number it, and pass it through a slit in the partition to the
cashier who sits next to him; thin the cashier enters the number of the bill,
the name of the customer, and the amount of the bill in the cash-book, and
receives the money from the customer, and stamps the bill with a red
stamp--if the goods are to be taken away the bill is returned to the member,
if the goods are to be sent the bill is filed at the end of the day the check
clerk adds up his book, the cashier does the same, and the two should
agree-they compared them with one another at that time the course of
business has been altered since-the che k clerk then initials the cashier's
book for the amount of his total for the day, the cashier makes up his
money and passes it over to me--I compared the totals at that time, and
saw that they were signed for by the check clerk in the cash-book-I did
not see whether the totals of the two books corresponded ; that was done
by the accountants next day-I compared the totals in the cashier's book
.--the next day the accountant compared the two bills-it was his duty to
add up the accounts and see if the totals were correct-one book was the
counterpart of the other, and if right there would be no suspicion of any.
