ROBERT LARKIN, sen. I am the witness's father. On the 5th of Aug.,
about six o'clock in the afternoon, I had been to the police-court, and Mrs.
Truebody was bound to keep the peace-when I went to Stamford-place,
Mrs. Hart abused me-my son went for a policeman, and when he returned
with one, the prisoner and Finn came up-Finn struck my son, and then the
prisoner struck him-I saw them struggling together afterwards, and there
was fighting and quarrelling going on with the others--I was assaulted, but
not by the prisoner.
Cross-examined. Q. You had a stick ? A. Yes I shook the stick at
Mrs. Hart when she had scratched my daughter-in-law and pulled her hair,
and she served me so I did not give her a gentle tap with the stick-I dare
say it was two feet from her.
GEORGE KIDNEY (police-constable L 179.) I was on duty on the 5th of
Aug.- Robert Larkin fetched me I went with him--when I got to the
corner of Stamford-place, Finn and the prisoner came running round the
corner—they came up to Larkin, and both of them pitched into him-I was
standing there--they both struck me-Finn struck me first, and I instantly
laid hold of Finn-I saw Parsons I called him to assist in holding Finn,
and Parsons was thrown down by Finn, I believe-when I took hold of
Fion, I said to Larkin, "Get hold of the other," or something to that effect
-I did not see the knife used at all-there was fighting going on I was bit
in the arm by one of the women.
Cross-examined. Q. Was not the first thing you saw Mrs. Hart running
away? 4. I saw her running from the place where old Mr. Larkin was-
she seemed to be going to fetch somebody to ber assistance--she went in the
direction in which I afterwards saw Finn and the prisoner come.
