 Then your examination was reduced into writing, and signed by you? - Yes.
 In that examination I see you enumerated several reasons which induced you to suspect, (for it goes no further than that you had great reason to suspect) upon several accounts, that one  Michael Druit was concerned in this forgery; now, amongst those several reasons, though so close on the time, there is not one single mention of the person of the man that tendered you the note; the person, whoever he was, who received the payment, appears by your books to have taken it in cash? - Yes.
 No notes composed that payment at all? - None at all.
 You was some time examining the draughts, and comparing? - Perhaps a minute.
 And there was nothing in the conduct of the prisoner or the draught, that induced you to stop the draught? - No.
 The person who received the money returned two of the shillings as bad? - Yes.
 Did not you before the Magistrate say you did not recollect him, and could not swear to him? - I might say I could not swear to him.
 That was in July; now, in September, having clapped together a variety of circumstances, you believe he is the man; the draught was tendered in April, and you did not see him till July, and then he was in custody for forgery? - He was in custody then.
 Did you go to the house of the prisoner on the 25th of July? - No.
 Do you know, however, that there was an application made to him on this subject on the 25th? - I have heard so.
 And you have reason to believe it, it was made by your house? - Yes.
 He was found in his own house the 26th? - Yes.
 At what time do you begin paying in a morning? - Nine.
 What is your high time? - Between one and five; we have but little to do of a morning.
 Mr. Fielding. Mr. Wood keeps money in your house? - Yes.
 Do you know any thing of the account between Mr. Wood and Hankey? - Yes.
 Mr. Garrow. Is the book here? - Yes.
 Court. Is the book in your hand-writing? - I have signed it.
 Mr. Garrow. It is only an attested copy of better evidence; you may as well hear my friend's brief.
 Mr. Fielding. Your Lordship very well knows my friend would object to Mr. Wood, and I shall not call him without a release; I shall call others who know his hand-writing perfectly well.
   Joseph Wood, junior, called, but did not appear.
   EDWARD BULLINGER sworn.
 Do you know Mr.  Joseph Wood the elder? - Yes.
 Do you know his hand-writing? - Yes.
 Have you seen him write often? - Yes.
 Are you able to say when you see a piece of writing, whether it is his hand-writing? - Yes.
 Look at that? - I am positive sure that is not his hand-writing; I have seen him write so often.
 Mr. James. Why, Mr. Bullinger, your manner of swearing appears pretty confident? - I am sure it is not his hand-writing.
 In short, you are just as sure as you are of your own hand-writing? - More so.
 Who may you be? - I have lived with Mr.  Wood these seven years; I am a carpenter and joiner.
 Court. Have you ever seen Mr.  Joseph Wood the younger write? - Yes, often.
 Is that like his hand-writing? - I do not believe it is, I do not think it is.
 You are not quite so sure of that as the other? - I am quite sure, I could almost swear it.
