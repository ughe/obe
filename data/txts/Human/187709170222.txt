   Cross-examined by MR. W. SLEIGH . The first occasion when I saw the card given was February 3rd—I paid the 5 s . about the 10th—the first time I saw them was at the Amersham Arms on the 15th—my husband and I both went in, I am sure of that—my husband did not remain outside—I saw all the business that was transacted that day at the Amersham Arms—I did not go inside the Amersham Arms to fetch my husband—we both walked into the compartment and found them there—what they said was "Law, Mrs. Ballard, you are not afraid to trust us with a guinea, are you?" and then I paid'him the guinea—I said at the police-court "A guinea for the writ"—they said that the writ would cost him a guinea; that is in my depositions.
   Cross-examined by MR. SIMMS . The first time I went to the police-court I was not examined; my husband was examined and I was examined ten days afterwards. GEORGE POWELL. I am an officer in the Mayor's Court, London—I pro duce an affidavit sworn there on 15th February, 1877, at the same time as the issue of a precept; I did not see it sworn, it is signed "John Dobson;" it is an action between Samuel Ballard and R. Harriett to recover 21 l .—the attorney's name is William Hicks, 30, Strickland Street, Deptford—that affidavit was made for the purpose of serving the plaint out of the jurisdic tion; it is necessary for the person who makes that to be a solicitor—judg ment of non pros  was entered on 26th March in consequence of the plaintiff not proceeding with the action; upon that the defendant would be entitled to judgment for his costs, and the plaintiff, in order to get the action renewed, would have to get that judgment set aside.
   Cross-examined by MR. W. SLEIGH . The person swearing that affidavit need not be a solicitor or agent—in a case over 20 l . a summons would cost 7 s .—there would be a fee of 1 s . on this affidavit, 2 s . for the oath, and 5 s . for the action—the oficer who serves the summons is entitled to 3 s . 6 d ., that would make half a guinea, which would be altogether the cost of this summons, but if issued in Lincoln's Inn Fields, the cost would be 1 l . 17 s . 2 d . if paid within eight days without the cab fares if served within three miles—the whole sum paid in this case was 7 s .—the rest is what the defendant would have had to pay if he had appeared.
