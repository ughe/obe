2171. JOHN WIGLEY was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of July , 1 ring, value 15 s  .; and 1 snuff-box, value 5 s  .; the goods of Thomas Wells .—2nd COUNT  , stating them to be the goods of Mary Wells .
   MR. PHILLIPS conducted the Prosecution. MARY WELLS. I am separated from my husband. Early in July, the prisoner came to my shop, which is in Chancery-lane—I had been wash ing my hands—I placed my ring over my knuckle—he caught it Quickly off my finger—I requested him to return it—he would not—he went very quickly out of the shop—I saw it when it was produced by the pawnbroker—I repeatedly asked the prisoner for it after he took it—he said he had left it with a friend.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  How long have you been in the shop? A.  Not more than three weeks—I have been separated from my husband fifteen years—he is alive—my last child is between thirteen and fourteen—I do not know the prisoner extraordinarily well—I never drank with him—he once brought a coffee-biggen over to me, and told me how I should make coffee—I did not have any—he has been in the habit of coming in and sitting down, though he was not asked—it was on law business that he should have been employed, but I found he was no lawyer—I never trusted him with any other articles—I allowed him to take some cigar-cases to sell for me—he did not return them—I never gave him the ring—I never said he borrowed an opal-ring before that—there was a blue ring he asked me to lend him, laying he was going to a gentleman's party, and wished to make a dash with it—I let him take it on proviso  that he let me have it the same evening—he brought it the next morning—he took this one so suddenly I could not prevent it—I written to his sister—the letter produced is my hand-writing—he borrowed the other a few days before this—if I sent this letter it was after he was taken into custody—he did not borrow this ring—I have not said he bor rowed it—I have said, if he did not return it it would be the worse for him—I should not have taken these proceedings if he had returned it—the ring was given me by the father of my child.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did you give him leave to pawn your property? A.  No. ARTHUR JAMES JONES. I am assistant to a pawnbroker. I produce this ring, which was pledged for 7 s . by a person very much like the pri soner —I am not sure it was he that pledged it—I gave him this duplicate.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What is the date of the pledge 1 A.  The 29th of July. JAMES DAVIES( police-constable T  112.) I took the prisoner on the 3rd of August—I did not search him—he gave me two duplicates—one is for this ring. NOT GUILTY.
 2172. WILLIAM BROAD was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of August , 7 1/2 lbs. weight of mutton, value 5 s  ., the goods of John Townsend , his master. GEORGE WEBB. I am beadle of Hungerford-market. I saw the pri soner, on the 17th of August, in a cart opposite the market—I watched him—he had something bulky—I asked him what he had got—he said, "A waistcoat and some other things"—I said, "You have got something more than that"—he then admitted he had got a leg of mutton, which was Mr. Townsend's.
