1798. JOSEPH CANNON and DANIEL GARRARD were indicted
for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
MESSRS. CHAMBERS and SCRIVEN conducted the Prosecution.
CHRISTOPHER JOHN HILDYARD, draper, Kennington-cross. Between twelve
and one o'clock on the 23rd of May, Cannon came into my shop, and asked if
I could give him change for a sovereign—I said Yes

them silver—I said, not unless he took all sixpences, which be did chiefly,
and I received a counterfeit half-sovereign from one of the prisoners—I could
not say which, because the money was laid on the counter—it is our custom
to weigh gold—I could not speak to having weighed the half-sovereign I gave
them myself—I put the half sovereign they gave me into my pocket, and passed
it away to my sister—I saw her give it to the policeman.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did you know the prisoners at all?
A. No, I never saw them to my knowledge—they were not in my shop many
minutes—I know them—they were dressed precisely the same when I saw
them again, except Cannon not having the pins in his stock, and a silver•
headed cane.
MB. SCRIVEN. Q. When did the officer come into your shop? A. In
a minute or two—I have not the slightest doubt that the prisoners are
the persons.
