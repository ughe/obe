545. WILLIAM WALTON WILKINSON was indicted for stealing,
on the 29th of January, 5 half-crowns, the monies of Ralph Needham, his
master.
RALPH NEEDHAM. I am a linen-draper, and live in Shoreditch. The
prisoner was my shopman--I had some suspicion, and marked half-crowns,
shillings, and sixpences, to the amount of 31.-on the 29th of January and
put them in the till, about nine o'clock-I then went upon a lead-tilat,
where there is a sky-light, through which I could see into the shop-I saw
the prisoner go round the counter, pull the till out, and put his hand in,
what he took out I cannot tell I came down, and counted the money,
and found one half-crown short-I sent for a policeman, and gave the pri-
soner in charge--this half-crown is mine-it is marked with a cross behind
the head.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Where were they taken from?
A. Four were found in his box-three of them were marked-this is one
that was in the till that night--I missed this half-crown.
Q. Can you take upon yourself to swear positively that there have not
been shillings and sixpences to an equivalent amount put into your till?
4. I can; the other four half-crowns I had not missed, but I can swear to
these being mine-three of them are marked with my initials behind the
head-1 know the prisoner had a particular fancy for half-crowns. 1
Thomas SEAL (police constable G 16.) I took the prisoner into cus-
tody- I took him to the station and received from him the key of his box
-I went and found in it forty half-crowns, one of which was marked, and
Mr. Needham identified it as his--they were sealed up in a white paper-
I searched further, and in another part of the box I found in a leathern purse
41. 15s. in half-crowns, three amongst which were marked--the prisoner
declared that all he had taken was 178. 6d., all in half-crowns.
