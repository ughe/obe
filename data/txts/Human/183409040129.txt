 1472.  GEORGE FRANCIS and MATILDA FRANCIS were indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 1 chaise, value 1l., the goods of John Allen.
   JOHN ALLEN, jun. I am the son of John Allen, of Hackney-road. Matilda Francis came on the 20th of August, I let her a child's chaise at 2d. an hour - I am sure she is the person - she never brought it back - this is it.
   GEORGE KEMP(police-constable N 82). I found this chaise at Mr. Gurney's - Matilda Francis was given to me first - she said her brother gave her 2d. to go and get the chaise, and he then took it from her - I then took George Francis - he said he would tell the truth, and he took us to Mr. Gurney's.
   GEORGE GURNEY. I live at No. 1, Middle-row, Goswell-street. This chaise was brought to me by a girl and boy, but I believe not the prisoners - they said they had no mother, and their father was ill in bed - I said I would go to him, and they ran away - I kept the chaise for two days, and intended to advertise it, but the officer came and had it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1473.  GEORGE FRANCIS and MATILDA FRANCIS were again indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 1 chaise, value 30s., the goods of John Corben.
   JEMIMA AUSTIN. The prisoner, George Francis, came to me on the 14th of August, and asked me to hire a chaise- he said if I did, the baby I had in my arms should ride - he went with me as far as Mr. Corben's door - he put 2d. into my hand, and said, "Mind you have the chaise that I point out" - I hired the chaise, put my baby into it, and went to Pancras fields - he then told me to take my baby out, and he would put his sister in - he then ran away with it.
   SUSAN CORBEN. I am the wife of John Corben. Austin hired the chaise of me - it has not been found.
   GEORGE KEMP, When I took the prisoner, he said he got a chaise from this shop, and sold it for 3s. 6d.; but I could not find where - he sent me to one place, but that was not right.
 GEORGE FRANCIS - GUILTY. Aged 11.    Judgment Respited.
 There was another indictment against the prisoner.
 MATILDA FRANCIS -    NOT GUILTY.
 1474.  GEORGE OAKLEY was indicted for a misdemeanour.
   FRANCIS COURTNEY. I am a cheesemonger, and live at the corner of Clare-court, Drury-lane. The prisoner had lived in my employ - on the morning of the 15th of July, I heard a noise - I heard some one jump as if from the landing, and I heard footsteps - I opened the window, and saw the officer with the prisoner in his arms - my windows had all been shut the last thing the night before - there was a cupboard in the kitchen - I had fastened the door of it, and in the morning I found the cupboard door open, and some spoons were at the edge of the shelf, which were not there the night before - it was clear some one had broken in.
   WILLIAM WILCOX. I live in Drury-lane. I heard a noise, and looked out at my window that morning - I saw the blinds of Mr. Courtney's first-floor window were flying about, and I saw a person on his hands and knees on the leads, pushing up the window to get in - I called the officer.
   EDMUND SWEENEY(police-constable F 130.) I was on duty, and heard an alarm - I ran, and saw some persons at the corner of Clare-court - the prisoner was running round on the leads, and trying to make his escape, and I took him.
 Prisoner. I was inebriated. Witness. No; he was not.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined for Nine Months.
