ABRAHAM MILLER. I am a furrier, of 30, Church Street, Spitalfields
-about 20th May last the prisoner came to my warehouse, and asked
if I had some goods which would suit him to sell - I showed him some
skunk goods-he said he could do with them he said he had a good con-
nection in Scotland and the North of England, and he could sell a lot of
stuff like bread and cheese--he selected the goods, and I told him the
price, and he put it down on paper; it came to £107 88.- this is the in-
voice-at last he said, “My friend, come downstairs, and I will talk to
you"--I went downstairs, and treated him to a cigar, I think, and he
smoked it, and then commenced to tell me he could not pay cash for the
goods, that he always commenced to do business in bills, and he said,
I cannot pay you cash, but my name is Brydon; I have not robbed
anybody, and I won't rob you. I could pay you cash. I have some
money with me now, which I have to meet a bill on Saturday," and he
took out about £150 in notes and showed me - he said he
always paid his bills at the time-"My bills never come
back" — when I would not give him the stuff so 'quickly
