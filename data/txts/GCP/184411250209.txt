GEORGE THORNTON. I was the engineer on board the Waterman,
No. 6, on the 25th of Oct., when this accident happened. I had been on
deck previous to arriving at Lower Tunnel-pier-I went below, and in
quite two minutes after I got below I received the orders, "Ease her,
stop ber, turn her astern "--as well as I can judge, we were about the
Lower Tunnel-pier at that time—those orders were given rapidly, almost as
quick as they could be given-I obeyed those orders.
WILLIAM SALVAGE. I am a clerk in Somerset-house. I was a pas
senger on board the Waterman steamer on the 25th of Oct. I saw
schooner lying athwart the tide, between the two Tannel piers-shortly
after, I heard the captain cry out in one breath, "Ease her, stop her, turn
her astern"-I got on to the gangway, and saw a skiff, with five or six men
in her-she shot out, and came in contact with the fore part of the right
hand paddle-box of the steamer--the boat was upset, and the persons all
immersed in the water-in my judgment the accident decidedly could not
have been avoided.
