   Cross-examined by MR. JONES Q.  Were you employed as a police-officer? A.  Yes, I was only employed at the police-office in conse quence of the excitement that this examination of the prisoners caused—I was there to prevent confusion at that time—the prisoners were sometimes confined in the cell where I heard the conversation, and sometimes in the adjoining cell—they were brought from the prison there—I believe they had been brought from the prison that morning—I had not seen them come—I did not see them put into that cell that morn ing—I saw them brought out after when they went into the office after that conversation—I was present at the cell when they were brought out, and followed them into the office—at the time of the conversation there were no more prisoners in that cell—after that the door was opened, and one or two of Moses' daughters came—the door was opened by the jailor two or three times prior to my hearing the conversation, for the purpose of taking something in to the prisoners, I then saw who was in the cell—he took them in a pipe and tobacco, and twice he took in a light—I believe that Solomon and Moss, who were admitted as witnesses, were in the further cell at the time of the conversation—there were no prisoners confined on night charges—there were not four other prisoners confined in the same cell at the time I heard this conversation, nor after till I saw the two young women let in by the jailor—I will swear that the prisoners were the only persons there at the time I heard the conversation—I cannot say if it was two or three times that I had looked into the cell and seen who was there, before J heard this conversation—I will not say from ten minutes to half an hour How long it was before—I had remained in the yard from the time of my looking in the cell till I heard the conversation—I was in the yard all the time, so as to be able to say who went in—there was not a drunken woman confined in that cell at that time, there might be afterwards—there was a drunken woman in the yard—I cannot say How long after, it might have been an hour, or it might have been two—she fainted away in the open yard—I cannot say if four of Moses' children came to see him that morn ing—I saw two I think—the jailor kept the key of that cell all that time—I did not at all—it was part of my duty to keep watch over the cells at the police-office—I was stationed in the yard to keep the prisoners and to keep people
