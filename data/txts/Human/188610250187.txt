I live at 14, Vauxhall Street—on 2nd October I was on this tramcar, sitting next to the prisoner—I heard the prosecutor say he was stabbed—I turned round and assisted his brother in preventing the prisoner from doing anything more—the prisoner was stabbing him—I held the prisoner back—I had not noticed any quarrel between them.
   The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate. "He started it himself. We were singing, and because I would not leave off he started hitting me, and that is how the bother started."
   The prisoner stated in his defence that if he had used the knife he must have done so under the influence of fit, to which he was subject. GUILTY*— Eighteen Months' Hard Labour.
 1118. EDWARD DAVIS and SAMUEL CHAPMAN , Feloniously diverting certain points on the London, Chatham and Dover Railway , with intent to destroy certain engines and carriages.
   MR. LYON Prosecuted. THOMAS LEONARD. I am an engine driver in the employ of the Lon don, Chatham and Dover Rail way—on Sunday, 19th September, I was in the coal shed at the locomotive depot, between Queen's Road and Stewart's Lane, with Hawkshaw, a Dover driver, and Cook, who was on my engine—we were about 50 or 60 yards from my turntable—I saw the prisoners coming together from the direction of Queen's Road, Bat tersea; they came across to the turntable which we turn the engines on, they took the fastenings out at both ends of the table—they are the only things that hold it from moving when we go on and off with our engines, and with the fastenings off most probably the engine would run off the line and go into the hole, which is from 5 ft. 6 in to 6 ft. deep—the prisoners went on in the direction of Stewart's Lane—I and the Dover driver followed, telling two firemen we were going, and they also followed—I got up to the prisoners first—I asked Chapman if they knew the mischief they had been up to; he drew a spanner from under his coat, (he had taken it from the Company's premises), and swung it round and hit me on the ribs as hard as he could, I think—Davis struck me on the head with this stone bottle in a basket—I was incapacitated for some time. CHARLES COOK. I am a fireman in the employ of the London, Chatham and Dover Railway Company—I was with Leonard at the locomotive depot, Stewart's Lane, and saw the two prisoners together—Chapman, who is nicknamed Sambo , came and pulled out the first catch, and Davis walked across the table and pulled the other catch out—I was on the engine at the time—they passed about 15 yards from my engine, and as they did so Sambo  picked up this spanner from a heap of broken chains and fish plates and walked away with it—Leonard came to me, and I followed with him—Leonard asked Chapman what he thought he was doing there—he turned and abused him, and hit him with the spanner across the ribs—Leonard hit him down, but he flourished the spanner about and ran away—Davis hit Leonard with a stone bottle and ran away—I and another witness followed him down Stewart's Lane until
