SOREN SAMUEL PALM. I live in High-street, Shadwell. On Saturday night, the 21st of August, about a quarter before ten o'clock, this cloth was at my door—I saw it move, ran immediately to the door, and saw the prisoner with it on his shoulder—I followed him across the road, laid hold of him, and brought him back into the shop with it—it is worth 5 l .
   Prisoner.  As I was going across the road from his door, a man gave it me to carry; I went along; the prosecutor came and knocked me down and collared me; he would not go after the man. Witness.  He had got across the road when I took him—I first saw him about five yards from the door—this is the cloth. GEORGE ROBINSON( police-constable K  370.) I took the prisoner in charge—he said he was carrying it for a man behind—I saw no man behind—I came up about ten minutes after it happened. GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Six Months.

   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  What was the prisoner in the house? A.  A workman—he had been there six or seven weeks—the boots are worth 3 s . 6 d .—people coming into the employment have 1 s . footing to pay. JOHN JAMES LLOYD. I am a pawnbroker, and live at No. 18, Strutton-ground. I have a pair of boots, pawned by the prisoner for 1 s . 6 d ., on the 20th of August, in the after part of the day.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you seen him before? A.  Frequently. SAMUEL BRYAN. I am a policeman. I took him in charge—he said he knew nothing of it—I found the duplicate in the cell he was locked up in. JOHN JAMES LLOYD re-examined.  This is the duplicate of them.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 30.— Confined Three Months.
