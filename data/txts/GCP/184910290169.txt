ESSEX CASES.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
2015. JOHN DIRKS, stealing 1 live fowl, price 1l.; the property of
Thomas Easton.
Thomas Easton. I live at Woodford-I had my fowl safe on 17th Oct.;
I missed it-I went to the Spanish Cock and found it, one hour after I had
it safe on my premises--this is it (produced.)
Joan TURPIN (policeman, K 289). I saw the prisoner pick up something,
I went and searched his basket, there was nothing in it but some feathers
I asked him what he had got in his basket, he said, "Nothing now"-I
said, “I shall find the fowl in the bushes, dead he said, “No, you will
not find it dead”-this is the cock, it is alive.
William Vowles (policeman, K 275), I found this fowl near the spot
where Turpin met the prisoner--the feathers in the basket correspond with
the feathers of the fowl.
Not GUILTY.
2016. WILLIAM CRANBY, stealing 51lbs. weight of lead, value 5s. ;
the goods of Nicholas Charrington and others.
MR. COCKLE conducted the Proseoution.
JOHN WILLIAM MANNING (policeman, K 5). In consequence of informa-
tion, I went to the house of Moses Johnson, at Stratford, at half-past seven
o'clock in the evening, on the 12th Oct.-he produced this lead, and from
what he said, I went to "The Pigeons," and saw the prisoner - I asked him if
he knew anything of any lead-he said, "No"-I repeated the question, and
he said, “What lead do you mean, the lead in the stable ?"-I said, " Have
you sold any lead ?"-he said, “No" two or three times- I took him to
Bow station, and he looked down at this lead in the charge-room, and said,
"It is no use saying I did not take the lead, I did; I took it off the roof of
the stable at the back of Mrs. Laythorpe's"-I compared this lead with
some in the stable, and it corresponded exactly with the lead of the scullery,
and with the lead of the gutter called "the drip."
