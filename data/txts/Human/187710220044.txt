Cross-examined.  I never saw the prisoner before—I caught him with the knife in his hand—I said, "You stabbed me"—I called him a rogue and vagabond, or something to that effect—I said, "Joe, this rogue has stabbed me"—the struggle was going on the kerb—I should say there were a dozen or fifteen men there at the time—they were all in a ring round—I did not see the prisoner in the Sea Lion. JOSEPH COS. I am the brother of the last witness—I live at 8, Great Windmill Street, and am a French polisher—I know the prisoner, and have seen him at the Sea Lion—on the night in question I was fighting with a man and the prisoner wanted me to fight, and said, "Come out side and we will have a fight"—I said nothing to him—I fought a second man and fell—my brother picked me up—I didn't see what happened then, but I see blood streaming down his face—he called out, "Joe, I am stabbed"—he was standing over the prisoner, and reeled round and fell on the ground—he fainted and was taken to the hospital—I saw a knife in my brother's hand, and he told me he had taken it away from the prisoner—he and I caught hold of the prisoner and gave him  to the constable.
   Cross-examined.  I had had no quarrel with the prisoner—he said to me, "I will have a go with you"—where I was having this fight was not far away from the kerb—there were about fifteen or twenty persons outside—not particular in a ring—some on the pavement and some out of the public-house, and the mates, I expect, of the prisoner also. FREDERICK JEFFS( Policeman  51 L ). I took the prisoner in charge and told him I was taking him for stabbing Cox in the forehead—he said nothing—Cox said nothing in the prisoner's presence—the prisoner was not drunk—he seemed very excited. WILLIAM HENRY ROBERTS. I live at 19, Collingwood Street, Black friars—I received a knife from Cox—I handed it to another person stand ing by. GEORGE PIKE( Policeman  95 L ). I went to the Sea Lion after assisting to take the prisoner to the station—the landlord gave me this knife—it was covered with blood the same as it is now. WILLIAM HENRY BATTLE. I am house-surgeon at St. Thomas's Hos pital—Cox was brought in on the night in question—I found a punctured wound over the right side of the forehead—it measured between two and three inches in depth horizontally, and had an entrance of about three-quarters of an inch—it was such a wound as could be caused by this knife—it would require a great amount of force to inflict such a wound—it was not a dangerous wound—the wound has healed—he remained in the hospital from the Saturday till the Monday. JAMES COX( re-examined ). I do not know Henry Darling—I do not remember seeing my brother twisting a handkerchief round the neck of the prisoner and a man saying, "Let him go"—when I was on the ground there was a dozen trying to rescue him from my brother—I was at the police-court—a man said he lent the prisoner a knife—his name was not Darling. JOSEPH COX( re-examined ). I do not know Darling—the prisoner was not in such a condition that he could not speak—he asked me to let him go—a man did not come up and loose his handkerchief—I would not let go of his collar.
