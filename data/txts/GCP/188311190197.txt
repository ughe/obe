MESSRS. POLAND and HORÁCE AVORY Prosecuted; MR. AUSTIN METCALFE
defended at the request of the Court.
MARY ANN PHILLIPSON. I am the wife of Frederick Phillipson, a night
watchman, of 7, Milton House, John Street, Old Kent Road-the house
is let out in floors--the prisoner lived there with her husband two floors
above me I went to live there about three months ago-the prisoner had
been there about three weeks before her last child was about three
weeks old-when I first saw it it was a clean, healthy child-I think
there were four children-I never saw the husband til the night the
baby died; he was away all day-I saw the prisoner's sister there for
some time after the child was born-she left about four or five weeks ago
I did not see the child for three weeks after that; I then saw a great
difference in it-it was very dirty and neglected--that was only a week
before its deaih-it looked as if it was being literally starved to death
it was very emaciated and thin and not clean as a baby should be kept--
when I first saw it it was very nice and clean, very small, but very plump
-when I saw it a week before its death it was in its mother's arms-she
asked me what I thought of it-I said I thought it was very ill and
neglected-she said she was going to a doctor's—I saw her when she
returned-she said she had been to the doctor's, and he told her not to
feed the baby, that the breast would be sufficient-I told her I thought
she was seldom at home to feed the baby or suckle it either-she told me
to mind my own business-I asked her once or twice afterwards how the
baby was, and she said it was a little better-I was never inside her
room till the baby died-I have met the prisoner out in the street or on
the stairs at times, and I have seen her at the Bricklayers' Arms, and I have
asked her two or three times to come home to the children- I went there to
look for her because the children were crying-I went there for her three
or four times just lately before the baby's death-the children were some
times in the room; the elder one could reach the latch of the door and get
out, and leave the two little ones in--when I saw the prisoner at the public-
house she was under the influence of drink--I seldom saw her sober till I
saw her in Court-she would stand on the stairs and say she had a
husband that could allow her 11. 158. a week; she told that to all in the
house, not only to me-it was on 23rd October that I saw the child dead
-I was standing outside the door, and I heard some one say "Good God,
the baby is dead "—the husband asked me to come inside, and I went in,
and saw the baby dead on the bed that was about a quarter to 11 at
night-I had seen the prisoner come home that evening a little after 7;
she had gone out about 11-when I went into the room the child was
dirty and the bed was dirty-it had on a little calico nightgown, no flannel
or anything else--the prisoner only said what a bad job it was the baby
was dead I asked her if she had anything I could lay it out in-she
said “No," and we borrowed a sheet to lay it out in- I asked the husband
to fetch a doctor, and he went and fetched him-I don't think the prisoner
said anything to him except asking him for a certificate, and after he had
seen the baby he said he could not give one-the husband said he wanted
a post-mortem examination and an inquiry to show the cause of the
child's death-I told him to look at his wife and see the condition she
was in-she was under the influence of drink then, I believe the husband
to be a hard-working, industrious man, indulgent to a fault to her.
