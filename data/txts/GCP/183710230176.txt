in it.
Jury. Q. Do you keep a book with the numbers of the watches in it!

identifying them if there was not something particular in them-this
has the Hall mark, and the only one I had answering to that description.

2381. ELIZA SUMMERS was again indicted for stealing, on the 10th
of September, 1 watch, value 81., the goods of William Ebben, her master;
and JAMES WELLS for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it
to have been stolen ; against the Statute, &c.
WILLIAM EBBEN. The prisoner Summers was in my service and quitted
me about a fortnight before her apprehension--I lost three watches from
the cupboard- I did not miss them till after her apprehension-I had lost
the key of the cupboard while she was in my service-she quitted of her
own accord-I have no evidence of her being in possession of them.
HENRY CHENIE. I am assistant to Mr. Ridpath, a pawobroker,
and live in Upper Seymour-street, Euston-square A watch was
pawned at my shop on the 14th of September, I do not know who
by, in the name of John Brown, 1, College-street, Camden-town—it wass
man-the constable has the counterpart of my duplicate-it is what we
gave the person who pawned it.
John RAE. I am a policeman. I took Wells into custody-he did not
say who pawned the watches.
Louis KEYzor. The prisoner Wells, in his confession, said he had
pawned two watches--one at Mr. Ridpath's, Somers-town, and the other
at Camden-town, but he did not know the name.
NOT GUILTY.
