 1701. MARY BRITTEN was indicted for feloniously receiving, of an evil-disposed person, on the   7th of May , 168 pence, 816 halfpence, and 240 farthings, the monies of Thomas Beardshaw , well knowing them to have been stolen. THOMAS BEARDSHAW. I am a chandler, and live in Gravel-lane—on Friday night, the 7th of May, I went to bed about ten o'clock, and the next morning, a little before four I got up—when I got to the bottom of the stairs I saw a light—I found a wooden pane removed—I went into that shop and saw nothing amiss—I returned into my room and all my money was gone, which was 4 l . 10 s . in 5 s . packets of pence, half-pence, and farthings—I can swear to a portion of the money by the tying up and the paper—this is the parcel—the papers were old sugar bags, and they are wrapped up in a slovenly manner—I did it on Friday night.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Do you often tie up your parcels in this way? A.  Yes—I take a good deal of copper—I keep do account—I put them up every other day—if any body comes in for change I have plenty in my till to give without these parcels—if people come for change I give all silver, no copper—they will not take it—I swear these are the parcels I lost—they were safe on the 7th. JOHANNA MAHONET. I am servant to the prosecutor—I went to bed on Friday night at one o'clock—the place was all safe then—I observed the pane next morning—it was big enough for a boy to get through—t woman could not get in—I could not get in myself. JOHN MILLS. I am a policeman. Mr. Beardshaw called me a few minutes after four o'clock on Saturday morning, and gave me information—I examined the place—I am positive no woman could have got over the wall—it is a back yard where they got in from the adjoining yard—a boy could get in at the bole, but it was not big enough for any body else—when I was coining off duty at six I saw the prisoner coming out at the door of a coffee-shop which is about 200 yards from the prosecutor's house—a boy was with her—they seemed confused—the boy went one way, and the woman another—I asked the prisoner what she had in her basket she said she had nothing—I told her I thought she had—she said, "Yes, I have got some money in it; I am going to market with it"—I said, "Let me look at it; you are the very person I want "—she said, "That money I found in the Grove"—I took her to the station—I found nine packages of 5 s . papers, and two broken ones which I produce.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where is the Grove? A.  About three minutes walk from the coffee-shop—it is a very lonesome place—the robbery hap pened on my beat—it is a beat that requires two of our men to go round of a night—it was the prisoner's son that came out of the coffee-shop with her—I have always said that since the first examination—I did not know it then—he is between 16 and 17 years old.
