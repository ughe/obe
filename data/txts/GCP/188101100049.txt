JAMES ARMSTRONG (Police Sergeant N). The prisoner came to Dalston
Station and I said “What about this cheque?"-he said “Well, I am
all right, I will tell you all I know about it. A man named Evans
called at my house, 3, Half Moon Passage, about 3 o'clock this afternoon;
he came up to my bedroom and said Harry, get up and come along
with me and get this cheque cashed, and I will pay you what I owe
you.' I had lent him 48. and 2s. when we were out in the evening. I
got up and came downstairs; we both went out together to the Adam
and Eve, Aldersgate, and asked the landlord if he would cash it; he
would not do so, and we went from there to Kingsland, to a public-house.
We went in there and he tried there to get it cashed; the woman said
No, I don't cash cheques? We then went up High Street; Evans
went to a cornchandler's, he could not get it there, and he said Here,
Harry, you take it over to the corner and see whether you can get it."
That was Mr. Merton's shop, where the cheque came from. Evans said
Don't go there, I shall lose my work. I went to the pawnbroker's,
and when I came out I could not find Bill anywhere "-I said "Who is
Bill?”-he said " Bill Evans "-I said "Where does he live he
said "I don't know we made inquiries, but could not find Evans's
address, and have not been able to find it since the pawnbroker'e is at
the corner, close to Mr. Merton's.
Cross-examined. I have not found out anything against the prisoner
- I believe there is a person named Evans--the prisoner said that Evans
worked for Mr. Merton's brother, and while he was out on bail he went
with me to try to find him-he told me that Mr. Olark introduced Evans
to him--Cane, a detective, was called for the prisoner at the police.
court.
CHARLES BOWRING (Re-examined by MR. FILLAN). I went to Merton's
shop on the Sunday after this-I don't think I told him that the
prisoner was not the man who gave me the chèque for 921-I did
not tell him that the man was a fair man; I said that he was rather
tall, without whiskers, and with a small moustache -I did not tell him
that the prisoner was not the man-when I saw Mr. Merton, it was
after the prisoner was brought up at the police-courtI did not
recognise any prisoner at the station, but I saw the prisoner there ; that
was on Sunday, the next day, not a week afterwards.be
EDWARD BROOKS (Re-examined). There was a man looking over the
door, and I should say that he was beckoning—there were several persons
outside on Saturday night-it is not uncommon for persons to wait if
