 2488. ALFRED PRATT was indicted for unlawfully breaking and en tering the dwelling-house of Jane Johnson , with intent to steal.
   MR. PLATT conducted the Prosecution. HENRY JOHNSON. I am a clerk, and live with my mother at St. George's-row, New Kent-road, St. Mary, Newington. On the 9th of Oct. I left my home about half-past six in the morning—I am quite sure I left the door quite close after me—it has a common spring lock—my mother and brothers were in the house—no one was up—I do not know the prisoner. THOMAS HEATH. I am a warehouseman at 20, St. George's-row, opposite Mrs. Johnson's—on the 9th of Oct., about a quarter before seven o'clock—I saw the prisoner make several attempts to enter the house at the door—it was some time before he could do so, as people were frequently passing—I saw him trying the key-hole several times—he saw some one coming along and came to the front of the house, went to the door again, put his hand to the lock, and appeared to be forcing an entrance—I saw him go in—I went down stairs and went over—the door was put too but not fastened—I just went over the threshold, and saw the prisoner in the passage close the par lour-door, I laid hold of him, sent got a constable, and gave him in charge. GEORGE BASS( policeman. ) I received information, and went to the passage, found the prisoner, and took him in charge—Health had got him by the collar—I took these two chisels from him—I took him in the station—as he went he said he went to make water against the door.
   COURT. Q.  Was he found inside? A.  Yes. GUILTY of Entering with intent to Steal.  Aged 24.— Confined Six Months .

 2489. THOMAS HARVEY and THOMAS BLACKMAN were indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Thomas Crisp , about one in the night of the   11th of Oct ., at Bermondsey , with intent to steal,
   MR. PARNELL conducted the Prosecution WILLIAM NOAKES( policeman, M  104.) I know Mr. Crisp's premises in Bermondsey-wall, in the parish of Bermondsey—I was on duty there on Tuesday morning, the 12th of Oct., about one o'clock, or a little later, I tried the gate of the premises, it opens into a yard which leads to the back of the counting-house—I saw a pair of steps places against the counting-house win dow, which was open—I went up the steps, looked in, and saw everything strewed about in confusion—a cheat of drawers was drawn out close to the door, so that nobody could get in at the door—it is a door leading from the counting-house to the dwelling house—I came down the steps, turned on my light, and saw the two prisoners lying down on a hen-coop beneath some shrubs, striving to conceal themselves—I had known them both well before—they both jumped up and ran into another corner of the yard—I turned my light on them both again—they found they could not get away, and I secured Harvey—Blackman escaped—I called him by name as he was getting over the fence—Harvey said, "It is not much of the case, the window was open before I came"—I took him to the station-house—I afterwards examined the counting-house window, and found there had been some small instrument put underneath to force it up—there were no sash-lines to it, so that it required some force to get it up—next morning I searched the yard and found this letter lying in the direction in which Blackman had escaped over the fence—it was within a few yards of the fence.
