1179. FRANCES JELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of
March, 1 petticoat, value 18.; and 1 flat-iron, value 6d.; the goods of
Sarah Alexander.

SARAH ALEXANDER. I live in Brook Green-lane, Hammersmith. I
asked the prisoner, on Sunday afternoon, the 30th of March, to take care
of my place till the morning when I came back I missed a petticoat and
iron-I went down Hammersmith, and there I met her-I asked where
they were, and she led me to the pawnbroker's-she was very saucy, and
said she would get one out.
George Coward. I am assistant to Mr. Aldous, a pawnbroker at
Hammersmith. This iron was pledged by the prisoner, and the petticoat
I believe by her.
THOMAS MORTLEY (police-constable F 112.). I took the prisoner, and
asked her for the duplicates-she produced them from her pocket.
Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutrix went out, leaving me with not
more than a pennyworth of bread, and enough tea to make one cup; she
did not return until Tuesday morning, at half-past one o'clock : on the
Monday morning I took these things to buy what I wanted
SARAH ALEXANDER re-examined. I left her with plenty to eat-there
was bread and butter and meat enough to last her till Tuesday-there was
some left when I came home-she might have taken things of greater value.
Prisoner. That was part of what I bought with the money.

1181. RACHEL ROBERTS was indicted for feloniously receiving, on
the 6th of March, of an evil-disposed person, 1 coat, value 10s., the goods
of Charles Wentworth, well knowing the same to have been stolen ; against
the Statute, &c.
CHARLES WENTWORTH. I live with my father in Park-terrace. On the
3rd of March I threw my coat down in a play-ground in Camden-town-I
was away about an hour, and my coat was gone-this is it.
GEORGE GRANGE. I am a pawnbroker. This coat was pawned by the
prisoner on the 6th of March.
