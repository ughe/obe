 1443. ANN CARR was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of April , 121/2lbs. weight of butter, value 10 s  .; 1 1/2 lb. weight of bacon, value 1 s  .; 111/2lbs. weight of cheese, value 2 s  . 2 d  .; and 9 eggs, value 6 d  .; the goods of John Nelson , her master: and JOHN MOORE , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Sta tute, &c.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM CROWLEY( police-constable R  178.) In consequence of in formation, I went, on the 5th of April, about a quarter-past six o'clock in the morning, and placed myself near Mr. Nelson's house—I stood at the top of New King-street, Deptford—I saw the prisoner Moore come down Broomfield-place with a basket in his hand—he turned down Old Kings street, and stopped at the back door of Mr. Nelson's premises—he re mained there about a minute or two—I saw the gate opened, and he went into the yard—I went and stood two or three yards below the gate—I could not see into the yard—I remained two or three minutes, when I saw the gate opened—Moore came out with a basket in one band, and a small bundle in the other—(the front of Mr. Nelson's shop was not open at that time)—Moore saw me, and appeared very much confused—he turned round, and walked into the yard again quickly—I followed, and about t yard inside the gate I took him by the arm, and told him, from informa tion I had received, I should take him into custody on suspicion of rob bing Mr. Nelson—the female prisoner was standing about a yard from him at the time—they both appeared very much confused—on the way to the station-house, Moore repeatedly begged me to let him go,  if not, it would ruin him—he said the things were given him by a female—the bas ket contained twelve pounds and a half of butter, and the bundle a Dutch cheese, a piece of Gloucester cheese about half a pound, a piece of bacon weighing about a pound and a half, and in his great-coat pocket were nine eggs, which he took out—I went to Mr. Nelson's house—Mr. Nelson and and I went to Moore's lodgings, and, in a cupboard in a room occupied by Moore, I found a small piece of Cheshire cheese, which Mr. Nelson identified—about nine o'clock the same morning I took Carr in Pleasant-row—she told me she had given the things to Moore.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Carr is sister to the prose cutor, is not she? A.  I believe she is his sister-in-law—the prosecutor's back gate is in Old King-street—it opens into the yard which is connected with the houses—I saw Moore go into the yard, and he remained there two or three minutes at furthest—I cannot say whether he entered the house—I could not see him in the yard, as the gate was shut.
