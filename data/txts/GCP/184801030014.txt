ness prevented me- I was in a delicate state of health at this time-l gene-

was opened some time in March-I was confined a month ago-we did not

penses were 21. a week, and the stock was diminishing-I wished to take
stock, and because I should not do that, he destroyed the books and said he
would show me a trick-he did not say that the trick was that he was a
partner-1 found I was sol. or 401. deficient, I told him he ought to be
ashamed of himself-his time was not up till the Saturday, but he went away
before that I went before the Magistrate-I kept witnesses back that I
might not convict him of felony, as they begged of me not to do it, and be
laughed at me afterwards I did not wish to prosecute him.
M. BALLANTINE. Q. What did he say to you? A. He said nothing to
me-Mr. Read went up, and he said Mr. Read was a good-natured fool; that it
was my doing, and I must put up with the consequences.
GEORGE LANGRIDGE. I work for a man in possession. I was sent for by
Mr. Pachin to 1, Wellington-place, near the railway on the Friday--I found
Mr. Pachin, Mrs. Read, and Usher there I remained there until the Sunday
-on Sunday afternoon Mr. Read came, and brought three men to protect the
premises-Usher, Partridge, and myself were there-directly after Mr. Read
came, Usher went out-he returned in seven or eight minutes, with twenty-
four or twenty-five navigators-they began fighting directly-I did not see
anybody hit-I saw a blow aimed at Coney-I got away immediately- I did
not remain in possession any longer-I saw Partridge in the row, but did not
see him strike anybody.
Cross-examined. Q. You did not see either of the prisoners take any part
in it? A. No; Partridge was smoking and drinking with me and Usher all
day-we were very quiet, and were having our tea when Mr. Read came upon
us-be gave Partridge a shilling, and told him to go and drink his health
-be went away, and returned with the mob-Usher ran out the back way-
he did not say a word-I saw nobody speak to him I suppose he was
frightened-I saw Coney-he had not a large rail in his hand, nor anything-1
did not see a stick or anything in the others' hands-I do not know the
names of the others--I saw Partridge standing against the counter-I did not
see any of the other prisoners.
