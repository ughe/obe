1719. JOHN WILSON, MARY ANN JONES, and JAMES LLOYD
were indicted for a like offence.
Messrs. Scriven and CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
MARY LEARY. I am a fruit-seller, and live in New-court, White-street,
Southwark. On Friday evening, the 10th of July, between nine and ten o'clock,
I was selling fruit in the Dover-road, and saw the prisoner Wilson and another
man, who I believe to be Lloyd-Wilson wore a cap, and the other a hat-
Wilson said, “What have you got there, old lady?" - I said, "Gooseberries'
- he said, “Will you give me a pennyworth?" which I did, and he gave me
a shilling- I said, “Is that a good shilling ?"-he said, "Oh yes, it is "-
said, “If it is not, why do you give it to an old woman like me?"--Lloyd was
standing by the stall near him at the time, I gave them 11d. and they went
away- I put the shilling between my teeth, took it out, and had it in my hand
when West, the policeman, came up I gave it him, and he took it away.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did not you say at first that Wilson
was not the man? A. No- I am sure of that I was asked if I knew the
man-I looked at him, and said, “Why, he wore a cap "-I swore to him
when he had a cap on I did not when he had a hat on-he wore a cap at my
stall-when I saw him again be had nothing on the policeman put a cap on
him, and I said, "That is the man ”-the shilling had not been out of my
hand till the policeman had it.
