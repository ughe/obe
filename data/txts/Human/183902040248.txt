No—the prisoner was afterwards found in bed—I was not told he had been in bed ever since a little after seven o'clock—I went to the house of one Col lins that night—I did not tell him that it was a lad of the name of Smith—I have never said so—I have been accused of forging a Magistrate's name to an order for payment of money—I am not aware that any boys were ever taken up, and committed on my oath, and that the Magistrate afterwards discharged them on its being proved that I had sworn falsely—I do not know that there is a warrant now against me for forgery—I believe there is.  845. ROBERT GEE was indicted for stealing, on the   8th of January , 1 box, value 5 s  . 6 d  . the goods of Lewis Davis . JOSEPH WEIGHT. I am a constable of Woolwich. On the 8th of January, about six o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner take a box out of Mr. Davis's shop, and run away—I pursued, and brought him back—he had to reach inside to get it. JAMES LAMB. I am assistant to Mr. Lewis Davis, of Tower-street, Woolwich, a pawnbroker. This box is his—we had some like this standing inside the door—I could not swear whether it was sold or stolen. JAMES HENRY WATTS. I am an ironmonger. I was with Weight, and saw the prisoner take the box from inside Mr. Davis's shop.
   Prisoner. Q.  Had I any thing with me? A.  Yes, a bundle containing a gown.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to that shop on some business of my own—I cannot say how I got the box, but I must have paid for it—I had the fever very bad in Jamaica, and since that I sometimes do not know what I am doing. GUILTY. Aged 30.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury. Confined Four Days.
   Before Mr. Recorder.
