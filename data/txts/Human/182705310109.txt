   THOMAS HUNTLEY. I was in the road, and heard a great noise. I went up and saw Perkins and Barratt struggling in the road. Adams kicked him several times. I ran up and said "Are you going to murder him?" they made no reply; he said they had swindled him out of his watch.
   FREDERICK PROPSTRING. I am a constable, and apprehended Barratt. I found some cups and balls, and needle cases on him.
 ADAMS Defence. I gave the watch to the man as soon as he had won it - he caught hold of him and said he would have it back. Barratt called Murder! and I helped him.
 BARRATT's Defence. On my way to Barnet I saw the prosecutor in conversation with this man, who is a stranger. I accidentally dropped a case, containing two needles, but did not know it; the prosecutor betted his watch, and 1s. 6d. that it did not contain two needles - it was found to contain one pin and one needle, which he must have placed there - this caused the dispute; he refused to comply, struck me violently, and injured me; after recovering, I returned his watch and money. He demanded 5s. for the insult which I gave him, and went away, but he had me apprehended.
   WILLIAM PERKINS. I did take 5s. but was so stunned with the kicks and blows, I did not know what I was about, or I would not have done it.
 ADAMS -    GUILTY. Aged 38.
 BARRATT -    GUILTY. Aged 28. Transported for Seven Years.

 1302.  RICHARD TAYLOR was indicted for feloniously assaulting William Helder , on the King's highway, on the 2d of April , at St. Giles-in-the-Fields, putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 watch, value 20l.; 1 chain, value 5l.; 1 ring, value 15s., and 3 seals, value 10l., his property.
 MR. ALLEY conducted the prosecution.
 WILLIAM HELDER. I am an  attorney of Clifford's-inn. On Monday, the 2d of April, between five and six o'clock in the evening, I was going up George-street, St. Giles, and on arriving at the corner of a street which leads to Meux's brewhouse, I received a quantity of mud in my face, eyes, and mouth, from some person, but I do not know who; it got so much in my eyes that I could not see; I was then struck in my stomach, which I drew in, and my watch was immediately taken from my fob; the blow was given by a fist I am certain; it was so sudden; and the watch was taken suddenly. The blow did not hurt me much; I felt my watch go - it was a gold one, and had a gold chain and three seals - it might be worth 40l. or 50l. together; my watch-chain hung out. I saw the person who took the watch, and followed him; it was after the mud was thrown in my eyes that he took it, and whether he came in front of me I cannot say, but I saw him, followed him, and seized him by the shoulder, and am certain that was the person who took it - he had ran about fifteen yards before I took him; he then turned, struck me a violent blow in my mouth, and disengaged himself by struggling; and left a piece of his coat in my hand; I am certain he left it in my hand - I dropped it, pursued, and caught him again, about fifteen or twenty yards further - I then struck him - we had a struggle; he struck me, and he fell from a blow of mine; I fell upon him(this was in one of the narrow streets in St. Giles', I do not know the name) - he then disengaged himself again, and ran off.
 Q. How did he get away at that time? A. By struggling on the ground, and crawling on his chest; I followed him again a very little way, and then caught him by the skirt of his coat, and that came off in my hand; I dropped it, and pursued again; he ran about ten yards, and entered a house - I was about to go in, but was surrounded by an immense number of men and women, who had collected; they said if I did I should be most probably murdered, and I declined it; I am not able to say whether the prisoner is the man - I cannot form an opinion about it. I do not know what became of my watch.
   WILLIAM SUTTON. I am a cooper, in the employ of Mr. Ladd, a wine-merchant, and live opposite to where this robbery was committed; it was in George-street, a most notorious place; I was in the street, going to the permit-office; I saw the prosecutor coming up the street; there was a young woman following him, soliciting him to give her something to drink - that made me notice him; he put his hand into his right-hand pocket, and gave her a shilling - I was three or four yards off; I noticed, at the same time, that the chain and seals hung a long way down Mr. Helder's thigh; Mr. Helder then passed on, and the prisoner was waiting at the corner of a little street, called Ivy-street; immediately on Mr. Helder's coming up, the prisoner drew his watch from his fob; I saw his hand go up; Mr. Helder said, "You d-nation villian!" and immediately pursued him; he ran round the corner; I am quite sure the prisoner is the man - his face was familiar to me; he and his friends live in the same street as me; I lost sight of him for a moment, while they turned the corner; I ran up, immediately, and when I got to the corner Mr. Helder had hold of him, against the wall, endeavouring to take him into custody; the prisoner struggled with him for half a minute I suppose - they both fell on the ground, and Mr. Helder came nearly between my legs; the contention continued altogether about ten minutes; the prisoner's coat was torn almost to pieces in the contention; they came on to the end of Church-lane, and there Mr. Helder knocked him down; he got up again, and ran up the lane; Mr. Helder pursued him, and I saw no more of them at that time; I returned to my own place, because I had left my door open. I attended at Marlborough-street, nearly five weeks afterwards, and saw the prisoner; I was quite certain of his person. I had seen him walk up the street one evening, about three days after the robbery - he merely walked past.
 COURT. Q. You said he lived in the street? A. Yes, with his father and mother; I do not know that they are his father and mother - I did not assist the prosecutor, because I had just recovered from a violent fit of illness - I saw no blow struck previous to taking the watch - I was then five or six yards from the prisoner, and behind Mr. Helder; the prisoner's face was towards me; a blow or push might have been given without my seeing it.
