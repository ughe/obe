WILLIAM HENRY HILDITCH. I am a corn-meter-1 was delivering
tares on board the Giraffe steamer. On Wednesday, the 11th of March,
Long came alongside with a lighter-I emptied thirty-nine quarters and
four bushels of tares into it I gave him a certificate, which the officer has
--this is it-(looking at it)-I am correct in saying it was thirty-nine
quarters and four bushels.

Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Did you mete it yourself? A.
Yes--the fellowship porters put them into the lighter-I was down in the
hold-I cannot swear that that quantity was delivered, but I measured
them-I saw them emptied, but I did not see them leave the vessel - they
were taken to the deck of the Giraffe-there were no more tares on board,
nor any thing in the shape of grain- I have had great experience--I never
had one mistake-it very often happens that a lighterman will try to
defraud a meter--mistakes have occurred among lightermen in the
tallying.

FRANCIS NASH. I have a partner of the name of Hicks-we are
wharfingers at Pickle Herring lower wharf-Mrs. Betts does our lightering
work--- Long was employed by her occasionally. On Wednesday, the 11th
of March, I ordered some tares to be lightered by her-on the Friday
morning I saw them landed at my wharf between ten and eleven o'clock-
when the last sack was landed I spoke to Mr. Scott and examined the
tares, and found one sack deficient-I said to Mr. Betts, in Long's pre-
sence, “ There is a sack of tares short, how is this, William?" he turned
to Long, and said, “How is this, Bill?"-I saw he wanted to say some-
thing to him in an under tone, and I turned away I did not hear what
passed—I looked round almost immediately, and observed Long shoving
off in a lug boat towards the road where Vandervorde's barge was lying
I got into a skiff and desired William Betts to row me under the
stern of the road, and observed Long in the act of having a sack of
something hove on his back-I saw him carry it from abaft the rigging of
the barge, and throw it off his back-by this time I was on board Vander-
vorde's barge- I went to the fore part and asked Long what he had done
with that sack of tares-he said, “I have it here, it is all right;" and I saw
the sack, which I supposed was the tares, lying in the lug boat, which he had
shoved off–he had hove the sack over the head of Vandervorde's barge into
