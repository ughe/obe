841. WILLIAM GINBY and DOROTHY GINBY were indicted for
feloniously receiving of a certain evil-disposed person, on the 15th of Ja-
nuary, 1 watch, value 101. ; 1 coat, value 21. ; 1 waistcoat, value 108.;
1 pair of trowsers, value 1l.; 2 toilet covers, value 28.; 1 shift, value 28. ;
1 petticoat, value 38.; and 1 shirt, value 1s. 6d.; the goods of James
Hunt Burgess ; well knowing them to have been stolen; against the
Statute, &c.
ELIZABETH Burgess. I am the wife of James Hunt Burgess, a lighter-
man, of Grange-walk, Bermondsey. On Saturday, the 15th of January, I
went out about six o'clock in the evening-nobody was left in the house
the windows and door were fastened, I returned in about an hour and a
quarter by myself, unlocked the door, and fell over a candlestick in the
passage-I got a light, and found the front-parlour very much disordered
-I missed a table-cover and a brown mantle out of the front parlour, and
from other parts of the house a gold watch, a coat, a pair of trowsers, &
waistcoat, a shirt, seven shifts, two petticoats, three night-gowns, two tol-
let-covers, one black mantle, and an Orleans cloth dress-on Tuesday,
the 18th, I saw them again at the police-office-I had seen them all in the
house at six o'clock on Saturday night.
James Hunt Burgess. On the 15th of January I came home shortly
after eight o'clock, and missed the things--I had some bills left at Mr.
Marchant's, the pawnbroker-on Monday evening I received a communi-
cation from him, went there, and saw my watch-while I was there the
female prisoner came in to pledge a coat-I did not hear what she said,
nor see what she brought-a policeman was sent for-I went to the station
with her, and thence to her residence, with a policeman, (leaving her at the
station)-we there found the male prisoner-it is a general dealer's shop
-We searched the house-I found up stairs, in a drawer in a bed-room, a
shift, a petticoat, a shirt, and a toilet-cover of mine, and on the drawers
agother toilet-cover--the male prisoner was taken into custody.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you hear the policeman
tell him that he had come to search his house, and that his wife was in
custody for felony ? A. No-he was admitted to bail by the Magistrate,
and surrendered to-day.
John Henry Symes. I am in the employ of Thomas Marchant, a
pawnbroker. Ori Saturday, the 15th of January, the female prisoner
came about eight o'clock in the evening, and pawned a suit of clothes---
about an hour after my employer took a gold watch in pawn from her, in
my presence-on the 17th we received a printed bill respecting the property
stolen, and finding the watch described, I sent our boy to inform Burgess,
who came ; and while I was showing him the watch, the female prisoner
came to pawn a coat and handkerchief-I was told the prisoner was in the
shop while I was speaking to Burgess, and I gave her into custody-I
left the watch in the care of Mr. Marchant-I have brought it here.
