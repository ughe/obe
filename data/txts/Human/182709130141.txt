In a short time he fell asleep in the house; I immediately sent for a coach; when it came I awoke him, and did the best I could to get him into it, and then I missed his watch, which I had seen safe in the house ten minutes before. I took him back into the house directly, and an alarm was given - the prisoner was not in the house then, but the other females were; the watchman went out, and in a very few minutes brought the prisoner in with the watch.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were you quite sober? A. I do not mean to say that I was - I knew what I was about; I do not know where we had dined. We had left Westminster between seven and eight o'clock, and had not been wandering about - we were at a public-house, and had some ale after that; I do not know how much we drank - we might be in that house two hours; I swear we did not drink two quarts of ale; we had three glasses of gin and water at Westminster; the prosecutor was not very tipsy; he treated some people with drink in Smithfield; I do not think be drank himself there - I did not see all he did there; he might have given the watch to somebody - I did not see him take it out.
   GEORGE BUSH. I am a watchman. I was in Smithfield, and saw a hackney-coach near the Bull and Butcher public-house, and a mob about it; I saw the prosecutor come out of the house. Wilkinson and the prisoner were with him - one had hold of each arm; the prosecutor was stooping, and all in a minute I saw the prisoner start round the coach and run across the street - Wilkinson said,"Where is your watch, Dyson?" - Dyson said, "I do'nt know." Hawes said something, and I pursued the prisoner; we found a person dressed very much like her, and took her first by mistake - we went down to the bottom of West-street, and took the prisoner in a court there; I said, "What was it you took from the man at the Bull and Butcher?" she pulled out the watch and gave it to me - I am certain she is the girl whom I saw close to Dyson. I saw her every night; I took her within ten minutes.
 Cross-examined. Q. She had ten minutes to get away with it? A. Yes - Wilkinson was rather gone, but not so drunk as Dyson; he was unable to walk; I thought the other was the girl at first.
   WILLIAM GLOVER. I am a watchman. I was in West-street. Bush came and asked if I had seen Pinfold - I said No - we went and found her in a court - I said I wanted her; she said, "I know what you want, it is here, take it, this is the watch."
   JAMES HAWES. I keep the Bull and Butcher public-house. I saw the witnesses at my house - Dyson appeared in liquor, but not Mr. Wilkinson - I recollect seeing Mr. Dyson's seal hanging out, as he left the house; there were three girls with him when he first came in; they brought in two girls, and the prisoner was the third - I assisted him into the coach; there was a great mob round the door, and the girls were very officious - one carrying his hat, and the other helping him along - I did not see the prisoner there after that.
 Cross-examined. Q. Mr. Wilkinson was rather sober by your account? A. Yes; he ordered brandy and water, and paid for it, and shared it with the girls.
   EVAN JONES. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house, with the watch.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1935  JOSEPH VALENTINE was indicted for feloniously assaulting Charles Ireland , on the King's highway, on the 12th of August , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 watch, value 2l.; 1 chain, value 6d., and 2 seals, value 9s., his property.
 CHARLES IRELAND. I am a  ladies' shoe-maker. On Sunday, the 12th of August, I had been to the Blue Coat Boy public-house, to get a glass of gin and noyeau. I was then going home, and was stopped close to my house, by the prisoner and a female. I had been treating them at the public-house, and we came out together; they wanted me to go further, but I would not; they then came, and each took hold of my arm, and the female made a catch at my watch; the prisoner seized me at the back of my neck, and held me while the woman took my watch - I seized fast hold of each of them, and saw her give the watch to the prisoner, when her things began to tear - I let go of her then; he then gave the watch to her, and she ran away - I detained him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you told the whole story? A. Yes - when the woman was near, the prisoner tried to throw me down, but I kept hold of him; they came into the house, and asked me to treat them at the Blue Coat Boy, and then we went to the Hoop and Grapes public-house - I might drink a drop of gin and water, and some beer - I did not go into any other public-house.
 Q. Did you not state, at the Mansion-house, that another person was the man? A. I was asked if that was the man, and said Yes, thinking it was the prisoner - I remember the Lord Mayor telling me to come sober next time.
   NICHOLAS BERRY. I am a watchman. I was coming up Petticoat-lane, calling twelve o'clock, and saw a woman dart off as quick as possible, and when I got to the corner, I saw the prisoner in the prosecutor's custody; he dragged him a considerable way - I took him in charge - he said the prisoner had held him while he had been robbed of his watch.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was not the prosecutor rather drunk? A. He had been drinking; he was not very drunk - I saw him at the Mansion-house; he was not sober then.
   JAMES MITCHELL. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house; the prosecutor was drunk, and said the prisoner had received the watch from the girl, as he has stated.
     NOT GUILTY.
