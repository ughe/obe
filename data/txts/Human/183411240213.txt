(This document was here put in and read, in which the prisoner deposed that he was not married—that he had never served either in the army, marines, ordnance, or navy—that he was enlisted in the Royal Artillery on the 12th of June, 1834, by Adjutant Kendall.) CAPTAIN KENDALL. I am captain of the eighth batalion of the Royal Ar tillery. The prisoner was brought before me to enlist on the 12th, to the best of my recollection—I do not remember at what time, but I think it was in the morning—I was not before Mr. Stace when he was taken to him to be attested—he continued in the service about two months, when an ap plication was made by a female to see him—I saw her, in his presence, about a fortnight after her first application—I sent for him, to know whether the woman who had made application at the barracks was his wife—I heard from the colonel of an application, made by the woman, for her husband to live out of the barracks—it is customary to allow married men to live out of the barracks—the woman came to ask that permission, and I sent for him, to ask if he was married—he said "Yes"—being married, and having sworn he was not, I said I should not allow him to go out—he made no observation in reply.
   Prisoner. Q.  Where did you see me on the 12th of June? A.  I really cannot call to mind—I do not know that he slept in the Tower of London on the 12th of June—I did not see the prisoner and the woman together at first—I saw them together between two and three months after he was in the regiment—what she said about it was not in the prisoner's presence—I cannot of my own knowledge say whether he was married on the 14th of June, or afterwards—I have not directed any inquiry to be made at St. Mary, Newmarket, where he said he was born. THOMAS CORBETT. I am a corporal in the 40th company of the Royal Marines. I became acquainted with the prisoner in January, 1831—he embarked with me at Portsmouth, in his Majesty's ship, Ganges , as a pri vate marine—he mustered and did duty in the marines as one of the pri vates—he belonged to the Portsmouth division—he received allowance as a marine, and continued on board the Ganges  with me about fourteen months—he was all that time subject to discipline and doing duty as ma rine—he wore the uniform—he was never punished, to my knowledge. WILLIAM LASSON. I belong to the 32nd company of marines. I was serving on board the Ganges , two or three years ago—I saw the prisoner on board that ship, in 1831, and knew him on board for about fourteen months—he was a private in the marines during all that time—after he left the ship, he was flogged at Portsmouth—he was then a private—I do not know whether he remained in the service after he was flogged. WILLIAM HASKET. I am quarter master Serjeant of the 8th battalion of marines. I saw the prisoner write in the month of July last—I believe the signature "Robert Franks" to this attestation to be his handwriting.
 (This attestation was here read. It was one made by the prisoner on the 12th of December, 1829, on his enlisting in the Royal Marines.)
   Prisoner's Defence . I was not aware of the questions being in the attes tation. WILLIAM STACE, ESQ. re-examined.  I read the questions and answers to him distinctly. He did not take the paper in his hand—I put the ques tions to him, and read him the answers also—the paper comes to me with the answers written, and he swore to the truth of it. GUILTY  , as to the previous service only. Aged 23.— Recommended to
   mercy .— Confined One Month.
