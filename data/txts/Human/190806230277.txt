I know nothing about it." This was after April 3. (Letter to Clancy of February 15, 1908, produced.) The letter I refer to is of later date.) I wrote to Calcutt asking him to call upon me." Give the show away" meant give all the Guardians away; there were many rumours. I did not think he was going to give me away—there was nothing to give me away for. I did not say it would be all right and that my wife would not say anything about the £10. As I went away I understood him to say, "How is your wife?" and I said, "She is all right." I never said to him, "Stick tight, Jim; you will be all right," or anything of the kind. I have been often in the "Three Crowns." Calcutt never stood me cham  pagne, nor Maitland. I do not know that Maitland was a contractor for drugs. Miss Auckland's evidence is untrue. I have never drunk cham pagne at the "Three Crowns" on contract nights. I always left for home about 8.30. Calcutt worked on my houses in Granby Street to the amount of £80. I have not paid him yet—he is collecting the rents and paying him self; that was the arrangement. During the Inquiry I asked him to buy the property—when I got a new lease. My offer is in the letter of Sep tember 17, 1907: "I beg to accept your offer of £650 and think it will be to your interest to call on me to close the matter on Thursday morn ing." "To your interest" is certainly not meant as a threat. I have had no quarrel with Knight. Knight charged me with blackmailing at the Inquiry, for which there will be other proceedings presently. There were some steel drums as to which he had wasted £400 of the ratepayers' money, and I moved that he should repay it. I went to see Knight before the meeting. He afterwards said I was blackmailing him. I never said, "You can stop my mouth if you like by paying." What I said was, "Are you prepared to pay half the cost of the drums (about £200) to the Guar dians?" He said he would not pay the Guardians a brass farthing. I said, "We must see what we can do in the matter." He said, "I will be equal to the occasion and I will not spare you." We shook hands and met a few hours afterwards at the Board meeting. Knight did not accuse me of blackmailing him. The charge on my property of January 22, 1907, is for £190 for the dilapidations. £70 worth of the addi tional bill for £80 was done before that. It was not put into the charge because Calcutt instructed his own lawyer. I have had a copy of the charge. I know nothing about Trott and Grimes' visit to Symes before the Inquiry came on. I will not deny I received Warren's circular—these little things escape my memory. I do not mean that is a little thing. When I went to the meeting I found it was a very serious thing. I did not know Trott and Grimes were the people implicated until the Inquiry. I did not telephone to Symes. I did not know him. I never asked him who the people implicated were.
 Re-examined. I have never seen Symes. I know now that he is a builder. There was a waste of £400 on the two steel drums, and I pro  posed Knight should be held responsible. At the meeting Knight said I had threatened him and that I intended to move a resolution to get him dis missed, which was untrue. I had seven houses altogether.
