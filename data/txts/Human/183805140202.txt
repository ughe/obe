   Q.  Who were the persons you saw at the Globe? A.  They were some of Mr. Badnall's friends—I went before the Committee of the House of Commons with Mr. B—in a cab  —I believe he had not given me any thing to drink—I will not swear he had not—I never saw him before that day—I met him in the road as I was coming down to the House of com mons—he was in a cab  —he stopped, and took me into it—I had never spoken to him before on that day, nor he to me.
   Q.  Had you ever spoken to him? A.  Yes, many times, but not on that day—Ashley was with me, and a person named Renham—they were wit nesses—he did not take them.
   Q.  About how wide did they leave this door open? A.  Two or three inches—there was a handle to the door—there was only us three together—when we rushed in Mr. Miller threw himself on the bed, and Mason rushed out and offered us half a crown—they were very much surprised at us—I think they did not say anything—I will swear they did not—Mr. Miller did not say anything to me. JOHN HAWKINS. I live at Newcastle-under-Lyne, and am an engraver, in a branch of the pottery business. I know Gallimore and Wall—I cannot say that I know Plant very well—I remember the nomination day at the last election—in the course of that evening I was at the Globe public-house—I remember seeing Gallimore there, I should think it was half-past ten o'clock, or thereabouts—Gallimore mentioned something to me as having occurred at the Castle that evening. JOHN BROSTOCK. I am a grocer, at Newcastle-under-Lyne, and have carried on business there twenty years. I know the prisoner Gallimore—I remember the nomination day of the last election—I remember being at the Globe public-house that evening—I saw Gallimore there—it was after ten o'clock in the evening, I cannot say exactly—I heard him make a com munication there of something that had taken place at the Castle that evening.
   Cross-examined by MR. KELLY . Q.  What time of the night was it when he arrived there? A.  It was after ten o'clock—I cannot speak po sitively—there were a great many persons there—I cannot say exactly how many—eight or ten perhaps—it is a house in the interest of Mr. Badnall—I was not examined before the Committee. LYDIA HAYES. I am the wife of Samuel Hayes—he is a hair-dresser, at Newcastle-under-Lyne. I remember Saturday, the day of the nomination for the borough at the last election—I was at the front of the Castle hotel between the hours of nine and ten o'clock that night—I went on purpose to hear Mr. Miller—I saw him at the window—he addressed the people out side—it was a few words, to the best of my recollection—I know the Roe buck public-house—I had heard Mr. Badnall that night before I heard Mr. Miller.
   Cross-examined by MR. KELLT . Q.  Were you examined before the Committee of the House of Commons? A.
