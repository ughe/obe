 641.  WILLIAM KINGHAM was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of December , 1 gold chain, value 3l.; 2 diamond rings, value 5l., and 1 diamond brooch, value 2l., the goods of Jane Fenwick Tuccio , widow , in her dwelling-house; and JOHN BARNES was indicted for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
   JOHN AVERY. I am in the service of Jane Fenwick Toccio; she lives in George-street, Portman-square, and is a confectioner- Kingham was her porter, and slept in the front garret. I was left in charge of the property in her house some months ago, when she went out of town; when she had left about a fortnight, I found she had left two diamond rings, a gold chain, a gold brooch with diamonds in it, and two gold watches, in a cupboard in her bed-room on the second floor, in a small paper box - I left them there; the cupboard was not locked - I afterwards missed the rings, the gold chain, and brooch; I spoke to Kingham and to every one in the house about them - Kingham said he knew nothing about them, he wished he did; he left without notice about a month afterwards.
   MICHAEL SWIFT(Police-constable D 126). I went to the prosecutrix on the 3rd of January; in consequence of what I heard, I went to Manchester - mews, and there apprehended Barnes - he said he had done nothing; I said he knew something about some jewellery - he said he knew nothing; I took him, and went and fetched Kingham to him - Kingham then told Barnes that he gave him the things to make sale of for him; Barnes acknowledged it, and said, if an officer went with him he would show the places where he sold the articles.
 Barnes. I asked him if I knew they were stolen - he said, "No you did not;" I kept them from one Monday till another, before I sold them.
   HENRY WYATT, I am a working jeweller, and live in South Audley-street, Grosvenor-square. Barnes came to my shop on the evening of the 10th of December, between six and seven o'clock, and asked if I would purchase a ring, which he brought - I looked at it, and asked how he came by; he said he found it twelve months ago, and had had it by him, but he was then out of a situation, and wished to sell it to raise some money, and he said he had just come from Mr. Neat's, the pawnbroker, who had offered him 15s. for it, and said it was a diamond - I said I was not certain whether it was or not, as it was candle-light, but I would give him a sovereign for it; he wanted 30s. for it, and then said I should have it for 28s. - I refused to give it; he then said I should have it for 25s. - I refused; he left the shop, but returned and took the sovereign, and asked for a drop of ale, which I gave him; he came again the next morning, and asked if I would buy a gold chain - I took it and weighed it, and asked how he got it; he said of a friend of his - I refused to buy it, and he asked if I knew any refuers; I said Yes, and named two persons - he then left the shop; the diamond in the ring was a rose diamond, worth about 3l. - I kept it for a fortnight, and then being in want of a bit of gold. I melted the ring.
 Barnes. I asked him how much it was worth - he looked at it for ten minutes or a quarter of an hour, and said he would give me 1l.; I said it must be worth 30s. - I at last took the 1l.; I took the chain next morning - he said it was worth 2l. or 3l. but he could not buy it as he had no small change. Witness. I did not, I had money enough to pay for it.
   FRANCIS GARRAWAY. I am a porter at the Green Man and Still, in Oxford-street, and live in Queen-street. I have known Barues four years; he came to me about the middle of December, and showed me a ring and a brooch; there were three stones in the ring, and I think three small ones and one large one in the brooch - he said he had found them or knew somebody who had, and he must dispose of them; I took them to Dobree's, to ascertain the value of them - they asked if I was going to buy them; I said Yes, for 10s. - they said I could not be hurt at that, and I bought them; I sold them to a Jew the next day for 4s., as I did not know what they were.
   KNELLER SMART. I live in Princes-street, and am a refiner. On the 11th of December Barnes brought this gold chain to me; I asked where he got it - he said it belonged to his sister, who was nursemaid to a lady; I asked what his father was - he said a shoemaker, at No. 15, Weston-street, Somers-town; I was not satisfied, and told him I should not buy it - I detained it, and gave him a voucher to show his father; I went in the evening with an officer to find him, but I could not - this is the chain; it is worth about 3l.; I gave it up at the station.
 Kingham's Defence. Barnes is innocent; I told him they were my own.
 Barnes' Defence. Mrs. Tuccio told me if I would tell where the property was, he would not prosecute me.
   JOHN AVERY. Yes she did.
     NOT GUILTY.
