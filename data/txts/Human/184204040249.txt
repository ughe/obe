None what ever, to my knowledge—none that I have had in my possession have—I have had these two brown owes—these two red ones have been in Mr. Bell's possession—I saw them when found—this one is the subject of another charge—these are the rings which were on it when found, to the best of my belief—it has been in Mr. Bell's pos session—I will swear they were exactly like these—I am quite certain these on one purse were not on the other—they have not been changed to my knowledge, and they have all the appearance of the same rings as when I found them—I perfectly recollect finding them, and the other rings were rather broader—I have not been to the Bazaar to see if such purses are sold there—we had been about ten minutes in the house when the pri soner came in, and were searching then—it was stated to the mother that I was an officer—I do not know whether it was named to the prisoner, but she saw me searching—the sister never told me the box was hers, I am quite sure.
   MR. ATTORNEY GENERAL . Q.  Did you put a mark on this purse when you found it? A.  Yes, an ink-mark just at the tassel, on the silk—the rings have not been changed—I think I found about a dozen cambric handkerchiefs, altogether—they are three or four different patterns—one or two are marked—I have not examined to see what the marks are—I was directed to look for cambric handkerchiefs by Mr. Bell.
