   Q.  Before this had you been to the referees? A.  Yes, on the 18th, after Shackleford called—I went first to Mr. Bachelard—I found him at a house in George-street—I asked him if he knew Mr. Beaumont—he said, yes, he had known him the last ten or twelve years, and more particularly during the last five years—he said Mr. Beaumont had lately married a lady of property, and Mr. Beaumont himself had house property at Chelsea, and 700 l,  in cash, and he had also a friend who was ready and willing to back him with 1000 l,  or more—he said he had not the least doubt that he was a strictly honourable and persevering young man, and he had no doubt he would do very well—this was in the Cork Stuffing Office—I told my master, and the goods were sent by Thomas Huddle, our porter, to the Swan-with-two-Necks—on the 20th of May Shackleford called again, and bought some more goods, and on that day he took a por tion of the goods away with him in a cab,  saying he was going over the water, to see the goods properly packed on Larkin's van to Maidstone, and he would save our porter the trouble, that he was going to Maidsfone to get a fresh list of goods that would be necessary to open the shop—the rest of the goods we sent—we saw saw him again afterwards, and he took more goods—I cannot tell what was the whole amount, but the books are here—( referring to them )—the amount of the first parcel, on the 19th of May, which went to the Swan-with-two-Necks, was 11 l . 1 s . 2 d .;
