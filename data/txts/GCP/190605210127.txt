EDWARD PARKE, examiner in the department of the Official
Receiver in Bankruptcy. I have personally had the conduct
of the bankruptcy proceedings. In the statements of affairs,
both original and supplemental, the liabilities were returned at
£1,591 58. 2d., and the assets at £3,750 18s. 60.,
leaving & surplus of £2,159 13s. 4d. The list of
creditors includes 35 creditors, to whom prisoner is liable
in the amount of £1,202, money which he has received on their
account and not paid over. So far as the cash-book is con-
cerned, the receipt of these moneys is contemporaneously re-
corded, but in the ledger the accounts have not been written
up since February, 1905, but that has been done since the date
of the receiving order by the bankrupt himself. On March 22,
1905, when Mr. P. Parry's money was paid into prisoner's
banking account, he was owing to persons on whose behalf he
had received money £683 Os. 4d., and at that date £144 13s. 2d.
was standing to the credit of his banking account, leaving him
deficient to the extent of £538 78, 2d. in respect of transactions
such as those which have been investigated. On May 3 he
owed to clients £581 5s. 8d., and had a balance of £269 19s. 6d.,
leaving a deficiency of £311 6s. 2d. On May 25 he owed
£745 12s. 8d. to clients and had £176 to credit, a deficiency of
£569 12s. 8d. On June 16 he owed to clients £953 10s. 9d.,
and had a balance of £170 128. 7d., leaving a deficiency of
£782 18s. 2d. On June 19 he owed to clients £987 45. 9d., and
had a balance of £169 4s. 7d., leaving a deficiency of £809 Os. 2d.
On July 28 he owed to clients £1,128 10s. 11d., with £63 15s, 5d.
to the credit of his account, leaving a deficiency of
£1,064 15s, 6d. On August 1 he owed to clients £1,181 126. 9d..
with a debit to the banking account of £23 48. 3d., a total defi
ciency of £1,204 17s. In the course of the investigation pri.
soner produced nine writs and one summons on which his name
appeared as defendant of various dates between January 17 and
July 12, 1905, the total amount claimed being £526 14s. 9d.
None of these writs were in respect of Stock Exchange transac-
tions. They were mostly in respect of rents received for
clients. Miss Rogers's writ was amongst them. Three of them,
amounting to about £150, were satisfied in money. Prisoner
returned the expenses of himself, wife, and two daughters from
August 10, 1902, to August 10, 1905, at £1,974 11s. 9d. in the
amended statement, being at the rate of about £650 a year.

£2,274 11s. 9d. On the other side there was an estimate of
