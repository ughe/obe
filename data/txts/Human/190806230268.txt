 Cross-examined. Calcutt got nothing from us at Christmas, 1901. The bill at Christmas, 1902, was £30. I keep the weekly account, but not the stock book; my husband keeps that. Perhaps six or seven of the Guardians would come into the "Three Crowns" every Board meeting day, or perhaps only two or three. I do not think any of the defendants. I have Been Loftus, Mr. Gunter, and Trott. I do not know much of the other gentlemen. There was not much drinking going on when they came in after the meeting. There was not any more jollification on contract nights than on other nights—just a little. I would know it was contract night by there being more Guardians there. I did not always know the contractors. I know Haseltine. He usually came there on contract nights. I know Cordy, he was not always there on contract nights. Lloyd did not use the house so much while I was there. I never opened more than two quart bottles of champagne for Haseltine while I was there. That was on a contract night. None for Lloyd. The two bottles would be shared amongst the Guardians. I have never served Calcutt. He never came in with the Guardians. In my time he only came in in the mornings, and very little with the Guardians. He might have stood beer to the Guardians in the mornings. I could not swear he was not there on contract nights. He usually had two boxes of cigars, 18s. the two. Perhaps once in two months and perhaps it would go for six months. December, 1902, contract read," 18 bottles Irish whisky, 18 bottles sloe gin, 12 bottles port, six of champagne, six of brandy, six of gin, 100 Murias, 600 Rothschilds, 150 separate cigars. "I do not know about them being served loose, we always served them out in boxes. We did not supply goods to Haseltine to take away from the premises, or to Lloyd or Cordy. I was at the "Coborn" in King's Road, Chelsea (one of Hirst's houses) from October, 1903, till the Inquiry. We have been at the "Coborn" with Hirst for seven years. I took my sister and sister's young man to Calcutt's house on the Christmas night. I never saw the champagne. EDWARD STIMPSON LONG, "Freemason's Hotel," Station Road, Wool wich. I was manager of the "Three Crowns" from October, 1903, to August, 1905, and after that from November, 1906, to September, 1907. I knew Calcutt very well. He generally came to the house on Board nights, not much during the week. I saw most of him on Tuesday evenings and Thursdays. Most of the Guardians came there on those evenings and used the saloon bar. Calcutt's usual drink in the evening was a 'glass of beer. I have served them about half a dozen times with champagne—quart bottles. They do not hold quite a quart. I have served him at the most with three. They would be drunk by the people in the saloon at the time. They pretty well always stood champagne in their turn. We charge 10s. a quart bottle. Hirst was not with the general company much, he generally went away early in the evening—he would call in about seven.
