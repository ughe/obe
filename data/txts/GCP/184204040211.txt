in Upper Manor-place, Chelsea. I have the charge of the vaults under
the New Church at Chelsea, and keep the keys-there are three public
vaults-a person named Hillyer is the constant grave-digger-the prisoner
assisted him occasionally-Hillyer was often intrusted with the keys to
get matting out, to cover the graves over, when he was going to dig a deep
erate-he had only to go to a place just at the entry of the vaults, which
was separated by an iron railing from the vaults-on the 23rd of March,
there was to be a funeral in one of the public vaults at two o'clock-all
the arrangements were made for it-the place had been previously selected
where the coffin was to be placed-there was nothing to do, as to looking for
the coffins of the relatives or anything of that sort-I gave the key to John-
son, the gate-keeper, that day-he went down to sweep the place up, about
an hour or so before the funeral took place--the prisoner had no business in
the vault at all — he was not employed by me-- there was nothing for him to
do there-after the funeral, in consequence of something that transpired a
search was made in the vaults, and we missed some handles and plates
from the coffins--there was a brass plate gone from Mrs. Lucy Goddard's
coffin.
DANIEL JAMES JOHNson. I am son of the sexton. About one o'clock
on the 23rd of March, my father sent me to the vault where the funeral
was to take place--I did not find any one in the vault the first time the
second time I went, about half-past one, I found the prisoner there in
among the coffins, with a candle out of a candlestick-I asked what busi-
ness he had there--he said he was looking at the names on the coffins-he
did not say what for the candle then touched something, and went out-
I do not know whether it was done by accident or for the purpose-the
place was then dark, and I could not see what was going on-I went to
fetch a light, and was gone five or ten minutes-he got out, and went
to a flue to warm himself-he was not in the vault when I came

JOHN CLARKSON. I am a dealer in marine stores, and live in Col-
lege-street, Chelsea. On the 23rd of March, the prisoner came to my
shop about the middle of the day, and brought 14lbs. of lead, and 2lbs.
and a half of brass-I paid him 10d. for the plates-I did not examine it
in his presence--when I first saw it it had the appearance of brass bent and
beat up in a roll, and doubled again I opened it after he was gone, and
found an inscription on it, with the name of "Lucy Goddard"-I should
say it was a coffin-plate- I sold it again to Gatfield-I asked him his
name and address I had no conversation with him on that occasion
on Monday, the 28th March, after I had seen a bill, I stated what I had.
seen to the churchwardens.
Cross-eramined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You saw the bill offering the re-
ward of 201.? A. Yes I expected the bill implied that the person who
gave information was not to be punishedI do not know whether I should
bave stirred in the matter but for that--as far as I am concerned the re-
ward is but 51.--they have not promised me 51. I do not expect to get
the whole 201.--I sell Gatfield all my brass-he was at my place all that
day-I keep the brass by me as long as it suits me to do so, sometimes a
week, and sometimes not five minutes--it depends on when I am going to
sell it, I did not keep this long-I did not deny to any one that I had
received any coffin-plates, before the bill came out-I swear that-no ono
came to make any inquiries about coffin-plates before the bill came out-
