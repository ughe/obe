 687.  ALFRED WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of January , 1 pelisse, value 18s., the goods of Susan Williams, widow.
 SUSAN WILLIAMS. I am a widow - the prisoner is my son; I go out to needle-work. On the 31st of January I went out at nine o'clock in the morning, returned in the evening, and missed my pelisse: the prisoner lived at home - he was never allowed to pawn my things.
   BENJAMIN BARRETT. I am an officer, and took the prisoner; his mother told him, in my presence, that it was for taking her pelisse - and as we went to Hatton-garden, he said if it was not his own wish, his mother would not prosecute; but it was, or if she did not, very likely he should do something worse.
   JOHN ABETHELL. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned this pelisse with me on the 31st of January.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 14. Judgment Respited.
 688.  EDWARD TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of January , 2 quarts of brandy, value 12s., the goods of Joseph Bennett.
 JOSEPH BENNETT. I  keep the  Bay Moulton public-house, in Gibson-street- the prisoner was my pot-boy- On the 29th of January I saw some wet on the floor in my cellar, and on the partition of the liquor cellar; I put my finger, and tasted it - it was brandy; I found part of the partition had been forced, and made to move up and down- it divides the liquor from the beer cellar; the prisoner had access to the beer cellar - I found a bottle of brandy in a copper; I kept a strict watch, but had occasion to go out; I returned in five minutes, went to the copper, and found nearly two quarts more of brandy, in a can; I called the street-keeper, and told him - we watched all night; by some means the prisoner laid very late that morning; I soon after received information from my brother - I went down, and caught the prisoner in the act of moving the bottle of brandy into the dust-hole; I saw him at the door of the dust-hole, and found the brandy in the hole - I said,
 "I have caught you;" he made no reply, but told the Magistrate that he did not force the partition, but stole the brandy, and he thought the partition was broken by the brewers.
   THOMAS BENNETT. I live with the prosecutor, and sat up with him in the morning. I was looking through the partition, and saw the prisoner moving the brandy from the copper; I was in the inner cellar; he crossed the kitchen, and took it to the dust-hole, where it was found.
   JAMES MCINTOSH. I sat up with Bennett, and in the morning I was called, and took the prisoner; I heard him acknowledge to taking the brandy.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I am an officer. The prisoner was brought to the office with the brandy.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Confined Six Months.
 689.  WILLIAM TRIGG was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of February , 1 tea-pot, value 2s., the goods of William Henry Royer.
 WILLIAM HENRY ROYER. I am a  broker, and live in Old-street. On the 11th of February I was coming home, and saw the prisoner draw his hand from my shop window; I ran up and missed this tea-pot - I ran and took him with it under his apron, walking away.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 40. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 690.  FRANCES WRIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of February , 1 umbrella, value 5s., and 2 books, value 10s., the goods of William Parrington.
 WILLIAM PARRINGTON. I  keep the  Horse and Groom public-house at Hackney; the prisoner was my servant. On the 18th of January when I came down in the morning, my children gave me information; I missed a silk umbrella from its place, and the prisoner was out; she came back without it - I told her the children had seen her take it out - she denied it - I sent for an officer, who took her.
   JOHN GODDARD. I am an officer, and was fatched. The prosecutor said his children had seen the prisoner take an umbrella out; she pointed to one which hung up, and said that was the one she took out, and had brought home again; The prosecutrix said that was there while she was out - I afterwards found this umbrella and two books at Bristow's.
   JANE BRISTOW. The prisoner brought me an umbrella to repair on the 18th; and said she would call for it at twelve o'clock, as she had taken her place to go to Paddington; she asked if she might leave a small parcel, which she did, and it contained two books - I gave them all to the officer.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, declaring that the articles were given her by Mrs. Perrington, instead of money, which she had agreed to allow her as chambermaid: but that the prosecutrix was frequently intoxicated and did not know what she was about; that the prosecutor had beaten his wife, and compelled her to deny giving them to her; it being done anknown to him, as they were constantly fighting.
   JOHN GODDARD. She said nothing about the things being given to ber till her second examination at Worship-street - the case was remanded for Mrs. Perrington to confront her.
 MRS. PERRINGTON. I did not give her the things; or allow her to take them or anything else.
