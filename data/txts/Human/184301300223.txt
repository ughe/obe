   Q.  When have you since seen it? A.  At Union-hall—I do not know who showed it to me—I saw it at Union-hall before the Magistrate—I can not recollect who showed it to me—the room was full of gentlemen—some body showed it to me.
   Q.  Then first of all you doubted, then you put on your spectacles, and still doubted, and you have since seen it, and you do not doubt? A.  Yes—it is not at all like my father's signature, nor is this signature at all like mine—it is of course an attempt, but it is not mine—they are both attempts—I am con vinced it is an attempt, because it is a forged deed—it may bear a resemblance to my signature, but it is not mine—I have seen the deed, and examined it—( looking at a deed of confirmation )—this is my signature—I have seen this before—I executed this at Mr. Boys', in Ely-place—I signed it—I said at Union-hall that I did not recollect signing it—I could not till one from the other—I have always recollected signing a deed at Mr. Boys' office—I was always perfectly conscious that I bad done so—I signed it on the 14th of Dec., 1841—the prisoner had taken me to Mr. Boys', but he had not at that time a retainer from me as my solicitor—in the March following he wrote me a letter, to require that I would give him a few lines, appointing him my solicitor, a written retainer—at the time this deed was executed, the prisoner, Mr. Boys, (Mr. Martin, his clerk, I think,) and Mr. Austin, his partner, were present—Mr. Austin was, I am sure, and I think Mr. Martin was—I went to their office for the purpose of executing that deed—I cannot say that I knew the nature of the deed before I was called upon to execute it—I did not execute it without some hesitation—I asked Mr. Boys to explain it to me—it was not read to me, and he said it was necessary that it should be read, and he read a part of it, which included the name of Phœbe Blackwell—I remarked to him that I had never beard of Phœbe Blackwell before, and it was a very extraordi nary thing to give a benefit to a person I had never seen or heard of—he said she was Mr. Blackwell's sister, and was so mixed up with her uncle's affairs, that it would make great confusion to separate them—(this was before I was charged with violently breaking into the house and taking the furniture)—I inquired of Mr. Boys if he knew this person, I having never seen her—he said he did not know much of her, or he did not know her; that he under stood she was a lame person, and resided in Derbyshire—I had asked to have the contents of the deed explained, and they were explained so far—I did not know what I was signing.
