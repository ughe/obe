 747.  EDMUND HILLYER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of January , 1 yard of cloth, value 7s., the goods of Thomas Wallis.
   GEORGE POWELL. I am a Policeman. On the evening of the 16th of January I saw the prisoner at Mr. Wallis' door in Holborn - I saw him pull the cloths, and a piece dropped; he picked it up, and ran away - I secured him with it in his hand, and in bringing him back he dropped this pair of scissors.
   THOMAS WALLIS. I keep a woolen-draper's shop, in High Holborn. The officer brought the prisoner and this cloth to me - it was hanging at the side of the door.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Three Months.
 748.  GEORGE GILL was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of December , 2 tea-boards, value 2s.; 2 pokers, value 1s. 6d.; 1 shovel, value 1s.; 1 pair of tongs, value 1s.; 1 looking-glass, value 2s.; 6 pictures, value 2s.; 2 dishes, value 1s.; 1 tea-pot, value 6d.; 3 glasses, value 9d., and 1 work-box, value 6d., the goods of Charlotte Gaffney.
   CHARLOTTE GAFFNEY. I lived on Christmas-eve in Caroline-court, Whitechapel; I had some friends that night, and the prisoner came to see a young man and woman, who lived in the house. On the 26th of December I left my house at eight o'clock in the evening; my property was then safe - a young woman lived in the house at that time - I returned between eleven and twelve, the young woman was at home; I missed my things.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had the prisoner been often at your house? A. No, the first time was on Christmas-eve - I do not know that he was there after that - he never slept with me - I never knew him to sleep in the house - I did not know that he was in the robbery till I found some of my things; Mr. Duncot used to come backwards and forwards, from knowing Mr. Gill; Duncot was there for a week once, but I did not know him at the time these things were taken; he slept in the same room that I did - I have seen the prisoner since the robbery; he lived at a public-house near where I lived - it is hard for me to tell how often I have been in that public-house with him - Duncot has absconded; I did not know that Gill was in the robbery till Duncot told me so; he never was in my place till Christmas-eve.
 Q. Do you mean to swear he never, to your knowledge, slept in your house? A. I never saw him about; he might come to Duncot one night, but I knew nothing about it; he never slept with Duncot to my knowledge; I slept in my own bed - I do not know that Gill and Duncot slept in the same bed.
 COURT. Q. Did Gill pass the night in the same room with you and Duncot? A. No; I do not recollect Gill being there on any night.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Upon your oath, did not Gill sleep one night in the same bed with you and Duncot? A I never slept with Gill; I do not recollect his sleeping in the same bed with me and Duncot - I have three beds, one on the other; I have slept on them for nine years - Duncot and I lived together, as man and wife, while he was there; I do not recollect whether I was in the same bed with Gill and Duncot - it is a good bit ago; to my knowledge I never was.
   HENRY TONGMAN. On the 26th of December, the prisoner, Duncot, and three other young men brought a tea-board, a poker, a shovel, and a pair of tongs to my house between eight and nine o'clock in the evening - I could identify the other three, but I do not know their names - Duncot told me that he and his wife were in great distress, and asked if I would buy two or three things of him; he asked 3s. for them, which I gave him; the prisoner did not speak at all.
 Cross-examined. Q. Duncot in gone off? A. Yes, Sir; I did not know that he was living with the prosecutrix; I know Gill by his working nearly opposite where I did; he bore an honest character - I suppose they came to me to buy them, because I was lately married.
   HENRY THOMAS DALLY. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner in the Cannon public-house, Cannon-street-road. he asked me if I wanted him for striking this young woman; I said No, for something done before that; he said he was not in it, that it was Duncot.
 Cross-examined. Q. How came you to go to Tongman's house? A. I had been at his house before, with Mrs. Gaffney, and he told me Gill was one of the party; I cannot find Duncot.
     NOT GUILTY.
 749.  THOMAS LODGE was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of February , 1 purse, value 6d.; 6 shillings, 1 sixpence, and 1 1/2d, the property of William Robert Cheeseman , from the person of Mary Cheeseman.
   JOHN STANDFAST PICKFORD. I live in Cross-street, Britannia-street, City-road. On the 4th of February I was in Collingwood-street, at near nine o'clock in the evening, there was a show; I heard a female scream Stop thief! I saw the prisoner draw his hand from Mrs. Cheeseman's side, make a stride, and run off; he was stopped by a neighbour, and given into my hands.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Are you sure you are not mistaken in his person? A. Yes, there was a crowd near him, but he was out of the crowd.
