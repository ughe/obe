1755. GEORGE WILLIS, stealing 3 coats, 8 handkerchiefs, 2 pairs of
gloves, and I cap, value 41. 198. 6d.; the goods of Joseph Del la Torre: and
1 coat, 158.; the goods of Joseph Del la Torre, junior; in the dwelling-house
of Joseph Del la Torre: having been before convicted.
Emily CowARD. I am servant to Mr. Del la Torre, of Lamb's-conduit.
street. On 18th Sept., about a quarter to seven o'clock in the morning. I
heard a gentle knock at the street door-I went, and found the prisoner
there-he gave me a note for my master, and wanted an answer-I told him
my master was not up-he said he must have an answer, as he had to be in
the City by eight o'clock-I went up-stairs, leaving him in the passage, and
as I went up I heard a step, looked over the banister, and saw the prisoner
taking the coats off the pegs, where they were banging-I ran back-he ran
across the road-I called, "Stop thief!"-he threw down the coats, and I
picked them up-these are them produced).
JOSEPH DELLA TORRE. I live at 70, Lamb's Conduit-street; it is my dwell-
ing-house, and in the parish of St. George-the-Martyr. The coats produced
are mine, and worth 51.
CHARLES RUMMEY. On 8th Sept., about seven o'clock, I was in Lamb's
Conduit-street, with a donkey and cart, and saw the prisoner run out from
No. 70, with some coats on his arm-I followed him, and saw him drop them
- he was stopped, and I saw some papers picked up in the direction in which
he had run-they were given to the constable.
HENRY PALMER (policeman, E 11). I took the prisoner in Judd-street,
and received the coats from Coward.
Prisoner's Defence. I was not the party that actually stole the coats.
John SMITA policeman, G 171). I produce & certificate of the prisoner's
conviction, at Clerkenwell-(read-Convicted, March, 1850, of stealing coals,
and confined six months)- I was present at the trial- he is the same person.
GUILTY. Aged 22.- Transported for Seven Years.
