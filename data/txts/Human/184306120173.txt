 1923. THOMAS BOWLER was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of June , 2 pairs of shoes, value 5 s  ., the goods of John Perkins . JOHN PERKINS. I live in Field-lane. The prisoner lodged in the same house with me—on the 12th of June I lost a pair of my wife's boots, and a pair my own—these are them—they are called high-lows, or shoes—I saw them the night before, when I went to bed. ELLEN BARRY. I live in Field-lane. On the 12th of June I saw the pri soner at the pawnbroker's, offering some boots there for 3 s .—I told the pawn broker to detain the boots, as I thought I knew them—I fetched the prose cutor's wife, and while I was gone the prisoner went away.
   Prisoner.  I am quite positive I was not in the shop. Witness.  I am sure it was you—these are the boots he offered—I took very great notice of them, from the toes to the heels, when I had seen the prosecutor's wife wear them for about ten days. WILLIAM LEE. I slept in the next bed to the prisoner, at the lodging in Fieid-lane—I saw him on the Wednesday morning after this in Newgate-street—he had a pair of boots on his feet—I gave information to the officer who took him. BOHLE HARME( City police-constable. No.  288.) I stopped the prisoner in Newgate-street, about eleven o'clock in the morning, on the 14th—he had these shoes on his feet. JOHN PERKINS re-examined.  These shoes, found on the prisoner's feet, are mine.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I purchased the shoes on my feet. I did not offer the others to pawn. GUILTY. * Aged 18.— Transported for Seven Years .
 1924. ANN PHILLIPS was indicted for stealing, on the   5th of June , 1 carriage, value 1 l  ., the goods of George Pipe . GEORGE PIPE. I live in Great Chapel-street I lost this child's chaise on the afternoon of the 5th of June—it had been kept in the back kitchen of the house where I lodge—the prisoner is a stranger. CHARLES MANSELL WHITROE. I live in Strutton-ground, and am a broker. On the 6th of June, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner and another woman at my place—they had nothing with them, but the prisoner asked if I would buy a child's chaise—she said she had borrowed 7 s . on it of a Mr. Marchant, a neighbour just by—her face was disfigured, and I said to her, "What have you been at?"—she said her bro ther had ill used her, and she wished to get a warrant against him with the money—I went to Marcbant's, in Old Pye-street—the prisoner took me up into the one-pair back room—I saw the chaise, and paid him 7 s ., and gave the prisoner 12 s . 6 d . more—Marchant is a pig-dealer chiefly—the chaise might fetch me, perhaps, 18 s .—I know the woman who was with the prisoner—she lives in Orchard-street. JOHN HUDSON( police-constable B  80.) I took the prisoner—I told her the charge—she said she knew all about the chaise, but did not mean to give me any information—Whitroe gave it up.
   Prisoner.  I did not know it was stolen, or I should not have taken it where I was known. CHARLES MANSELL WHITROE re-examined. She said she had it built for her little girl, and that she had been in better circumstances. GUILTY. Aged 28.— Confined Three Months .
