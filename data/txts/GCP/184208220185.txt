JAMES CROSIER. I am an oil and colourman, in Brewer-street, Somers-
town. On the 23rd of August, between three and six o'clock in the even-
ing, the prisoner came to my shop with this order--he said he had come
from a gentleman named Barham, who I had served before-(he had been to
me three months before, and those things were sent and paid for)-I asked
what things he wanted-he said, "White lead, oil, umber, and ochre, var-
nish, and Brunswick green"-I did not let him have the goods-I made out a
bill of the things he proposed to have-to the best of my recollection they
came to 11. 138.- he asked me to give him the bill enclosed in the order-I
refused at first, but I thought he might probably want to take it back again,
and I wafered it up and gave it bim-he said he would call again at six
o'clock in the evening, and go with my boy to take the things to Mr. Bar-
ham, and my boy was to bring back the money-he said the gentleman was
a very nice young man, and had got plenty of money-I told him I gave
no credit-after I had given him the bill he asked me to let him have half-
a-crown, as he had got some things to get for the gentleman – he owed me
68. before, and he said, "When the things are sent up to the gentleman's, your
boy shall bring back the money, and I will pay you a trifle of what I owe you,
I shall have some money then"-he came back in an hour or an hour and a
hall, not sober, to borrow another half-crown-I said, "Gerard, what have
you done with the other half-crown, I am afraid you have been spending it!
in drink?"-he said, “No, I bave not, I am as sober as you are" - I asked
him for the bill-he said he had given it to the gentleman at four o'clock
- he took his hat off, and the order and the bill fell out-I told him to
hand it to me-he refused at first, but I said, “You had better let me have
it, and I shall have something to show"-he then gave it me--I followed
my lad with the goods, and found Mr. Barbam at No. 45, Devonshire-
street, instead of No. 28--No. 28 is where he formerly resided-I have
not got the bill here it was given to a gentleman in Mr. Barbam's
house--the policeman has a similar one which was made out I should
have acted on the mere production of the order alone-I should have sent
white lead, oil, and turps, nothing more, had he not given me the
order for more-- this is a common form of order in the trade-it is
in the same form as one I had had previously-he did not ask me to give
him the goods, but to make out a list for the gentleman I called on Mr.
Barham the same evening-I did not know him-- I saw a gentleman who
I thought was Mr. Barbam, who said it was not his writing - I called next!
morning, and saw Mr. Barham-I had the goods close by his door waiting
for him, to see they were delivered, on the same evening I have not got the
paper which contained the list of the goods-I made it out-the prisoner
was to call for the goods in the evening, but after he called the second
time I had some suspicion, and went with them myself-he did not go with
me-I saw nothing more of him till he was in custody-he did not pro-
pose to take the goods in the first instance-he described what goods he
wanted-he said nothing about my giving him anything for bringing the
order-I generally gave him something.
