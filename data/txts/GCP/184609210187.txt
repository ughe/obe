Court to WILLIAM WIGGINS. Q. Did you see any sacks put in or taken
out of the wagon ? A. No-there were three sacks of corn in and one sack
of chaff-I did not see Edwards load them, but he did load them most likely
- they were in the wagon when it got to London.
WILLIAM Moon re-examined. In consequence of losing corn, I put a second
lock on my granary-there were three lots of corn in my granary on Monday
morning, and as soon as the wagon was gone the corn was gone-Edwards
had keys to go to my granary when he liked.
Not GUILTY.

1980. JOHN ROBINSON and CHARLES WATSON were indicted for
stealing 2 lbs. weight of beef, value 1s. 3d., the goods of Daniel Harling; to
which
ROBINSON pleaded Guilty. Aged 25.-Confined Three Months.
HENRY WHITTAL. I was at Clapham on the 23rd of Sept., and saw the
prisoners together—they then separated, and Robinson went to the butcher's
shop, and took a piece of beef-he put it into his sack-he then looked round.
and took another piece-Watson was at some distance, looking in at a win-
dow-they then joined, and Robinson gave one piece of beef out of his bag to
Watson.
Watson. I gave this prisoner 1s. to go and buy some meat; he brought
me one piece, and said it cost 10 d. ; I could not tell whether he bought it
or not.
EDWIN COLLINS. I am servant to Daniel Harling. I was in the stable
-I came out, and missed the beef-it was produced, and the prisoners were
taken-it was my master's.
ROBERT EMERSON (police-sergeant V-9.) I took the prisoners—there were
two half-crowns, a sixpence, and 6d. worth of halfpence found on Robinson,
but no shilling.
WATSON -Guilty. Aged 33.-Confined Nine Months.
1981. GEORGE GILES and TIMOTHY SULLIVAN were indicted
for stealing 2 bottles, value 2d. ; 1 quart of stout, 10d. ; and 6 cheroots, 15.;
the goods of William Benjamin Powell.
MARY ANN POWELL. I am the wife of William Benjamin Powell-we
keep a beer-shop at Battersea. The prisoners came there on the 21st of Sept.,
about one o'clock-they had some dinner and some porter-after they left I
missed some cheroots out of a glass- I suspected the prisoners, as no one
else had been in the house in a little time my husband came in and asked
if I had sold some stout-I said, "No"-he said the prisoners were drinking
some stout in the lane-I missed some stout, but I cannot say what-two
bottles were brought back-they are mine.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Who paid for what they had at
your house? A. Sullivan-the stout was kept in the bar, and the cheroots
were further in the bar, near the parlour-door-they are worth 2d. a-piece.
WILLIAM BENJAMIN POWELL. I keep the beer-shop. I gave information
to the policeman, and he took the prisoners-he took the cheroots from Giles
-one bottle of the stout was between the prisoners as they were lying in the
road in Falcon-lane I have examined the cheroots and these bottles of stout
--they are mine to the best of my belief-the prisoners were a little the
worse for liquor.
