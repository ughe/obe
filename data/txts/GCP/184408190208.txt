ELIZABETH CONNELLY. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw my husband's
cash-box on Thursday the 7th of March, between twelve and half-past, in a
large trunk in our bed-room--the trunk was not locked—the cash-box was-
there must have been 1801. in it, I cannot say to 51.---you go through our
sitting-room into the bed-room--there is a passage door for the use of the
house-I was at home on Friday evening, the 8th of March, but I was down
in the kitchen some hours-we were lately in the house, and I was busy a
ranging-a person might go up stairs, go through our sitting-room into the
bed-room, and take the cash-box out of the trunk-I am quite sure it was there
--I know Buckley was in the greatest distress-she knew exactly where I
kept the cash-box-she has seen it there in my presence-Mr. Connelly has
put money into it in her presence.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHARNOCK. Q. Has he put money into it in other
people's presence ? 4. Not that I am aware of-Mrs. Thompson, her daughter
and son, and servant, lodged in the house at that time--no one else, but my
husband, myself, and Brown, the servant-Buckley used to come to visit her
--Brown slept on a sofa, and had plenty of good clothes to cover her-I have
never pledged things myself, or sent Brown, or Buckley to do so, but Buckley
took a black satin shawl of mine-Brown one day pledged a shawl of Buckley's,
about a month before the robbery-my husband had gone out, and I bad for
gotten to ask him for any silver-I had none at home, and said, “Dear me,
what shall I do?"-- she said, "I will go and pledge my shawl"--I said, "O
dear, no, I do not require anything of the sort," but she went, and gave me
the money - I gave her back the money, and redeemed the shawl soon after
the robbery was found out-she never pledged anything else for me.
