WILLIAM TAIT sworn.
 You are clerk to Mr. Hankey? - Yes.
 What is the firm of your house? -  Thomas Hankey, Joseph Chaplin Hankey, Stephen Hall, Robert Hankey, and Richard Hankey; I speak of the partnership in April last.
 Lock at the prisoner, have you a recollection of him? - Yes, Sir, I think I have a very strong one?
 Did you see him in April last, and where? - On the 26th of July, at Hoxton.
 Did you see him on the 20th of April? - It struck me very forcibly that he was the same I saw on the 20th of April.
 On the 20th of April a man came to your house? - Yes.
 At what time of the day? - About ten in the morning, as far as I can gather from my own book, this is the draught I have in my band; I looked at the writing, and I saw a sensible difference to Mr. Wood's usual way of writing; I took the opinion of my fellow tellers at the counter, and they told me it certainly must be his writing, they thought it was; I was not perfectly satisfied, but referred to a drawer, in which Mr. Wood's draughts lay, I looked at the draught, and found it the same hand-writing, I paid sixteen guineas and a half, and six shillings, and the gentleman returned me two shillings as bad.
 Mr. Garrow, one of the prisoner's counsel. In what shape did you pay the draught? - Sixteen guineas and a half, and six shillings in silver.
 Have you a memory of the person that so presented the draught to whom you paid the money? - I had a very strong recollection then, I had rather speak to the time that I saw him on the 26th of July.
 Court. Did you ask the person who presented the draught any questions? - None.
 Mr. Fielding. Did you take any observation of the person? - Yes, I looked at his person, and I apprehended in myself he was a relation of Mr. Wood's, because I thought there was a kind of family likeness in the fullness of his eyes to Mr. Wood's son, but I found it was quite different.
 On what occasion was it that you saw him at Hoxton? - He was taken into custody by a constable.
 Look again at the prisoner at the bar, is he or is he not the person that presented the note; what is your opinion of him now, that you can warrantably say upon your oath? - I believe him to be the same, on recollecting that it struck me so forcibly then.
 Mr. Fielding. Does your memory serve you to say whether that was the man or not? - I will not say, but upon that recollection.
 Do you believe him to be the man? - I belive him to be the man that presented the notes, but I only belive it upon the strength of that recollection.
 Court. That is a very fair and candid evidence.
 Mr. Fielding. Do you know Mr. Wood? - Yes.
 Mr. Garrow. Now, Mr. Tait, I am disposed to hope, as the court does, that you mean to give a very fair and candid account; it is that hope that induces me to give you a caution, because I have here your information before a Magistrate; now, you have told my friend, with some difficulty, (which certainly is in favour of your testimony) that after all the corroborating circumstances that you have put together, you do believe that the person at the bar is the person that presented the note.
 Court. He says he does not speak from his knowledge now, from his recollection.
 Mr. Garrow. Now I ask you, abstracted from all you have heard, from all you have done at the bank, and all that has been told you on the subject, I ask you, without these corroborating circumstances, will you swear the prisoner is the person that presented the note? - I hesitated, as you remember before, about that question, nor can I answer it now; I say I am uncertain.
