 260.  ELEANOR DWYER was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of October , 7 shillings, the monies of William Harrington.
   FRANCES HARRINGTON. I am the wife of William Harrington; he is an umbrella-maker, and keeps a chandler's-shop, in Orris-street, Edgware-road. On the 31st of October, between eleven and twelve o'clock. I saw the prisoner's hand in my till; I was at the door, serving some potatoes - the prisoner had come to see me; she had lived with me three years before: I went into the shop, and she had fled - I pursued, and found her with another girl; she handed something to the other girl: I caught hold of them both, and demanded what she had got; she gave me 3s. 6d.; I found but 6s. 6d. in my till: I had seen 14s. there half an hour before, but I took a good deal while the prisoner was there; the prisoner ran to a hay-lost, and the officer took her there; I had been looking for her an hour or two in the street: she threw me down and struck me; she was a good girl when she lived with me, but she has turned out a horrid bad girl.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 267.  EDWARD DEATH was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 stove, value 7s. 6d., the goods of John Northfield.
   JOHN NORTHFIELD. I am an ironmonger. I lost a stove from my door in the City-road, between two and three o'clock, on the 26th of November; I had seen it two or three hours before: a witness brought the prisoner back with it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   HENRY BLYTH. I took the prisoner with the stove, bout eighty yards from the house; I saw him take it.
   JOHN FELLS. I am an officer, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was out looking for a place: I did it for want, and am sorry for it.
 GUILTY. Aged 13. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury. Whipped and Discharged.
 262.  JOSEPH CHAPP and JOHN BAKER were indicted for stealing, on the 4th of December , 1 bottle, value 2s. and 1 quart of Dantzic-spruce, value 2s. the goods of William Greenwood.
 WILLIAM GREENWOOD. I am a  licensed-victualler, and live in Bagnigge-wells-road. I lost a spruce-beer bottle from my counter last Thursday; I did not see who took it, nor know it was gone, till the officer brought it back, about twelve o'clock; I had seen it in the morning: I noticed the two prisoners come in, and served them with a pint of porter, but I did not see them go out.
   JOHN FOSTER. I took this bottle from Baker's pocket, about a quarter of a mile from the prosecutor's, it was about a quarter-past ten o'clock; he said he had bought it for 1s.: Chapp was with him.
 CHAPP'S Defence. I bought it of a brick-maker, (from his appearance,) down by Clerkenwell workhouse.
 BAKER'S Defence. I saw Chapp buy it.
 CHAPP -    GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
 BAKER -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Month.
 260.  SARAH CLEMSON was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of November , 1 Bible, value 2s., the goods of John Cunningham.
   JOHN CUNNINGHAM. I am a baker, and sleep in a room in Tooley-street. On the 26th of November, the prisoner lodged in the same house; I missed my Bible from my room on the Wednesday at six o'clock in the evening - I had seen it that day.
   ALEXANDER RUTTER. I am a constable. On the 1st of December, I was sent for - the landlord told me he had lost a quilt; we found the prisoner - she tore up a duplicate of the Bible, and threw it on the coals.
   MICHAEL COMERFORD. I am a pawnbroker. I have a Bible pawned by a woman, not the prisoner, on the 27th of November; I gave a duplicate for it.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I am an officer. There was an application for a search-warrant; I went to the house - the prisoner had been taken by Rutter; we went back, and searched for the duplicate.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not pawn the Bible; I had the duplicate in my possession, which, in my flurry I tore, and threw it into the fire-place.    NOT GUILTY.

   ELIZABETH HINE. I am the wife of John Hine; the prisoner lodged at our house. I missed a quilt on the Monday; we sent for an officer - Rutter came, and found the duplicate in the grate - the prisoner said she did not put it there.
   ALEXANDER RUTTER. I went on the 1st December, and saw the prisoner - she gave me thirty-seven duplicates out of a tea-caddy; I then saw her hand under her arm, and when I said I must search her; she tore the duplicate in three or four pieces, and threw it under the hearth - this is it.
 Prisoner. I did not give you thirty-seven duplicates out of the tea caddy? A. Yes, you did - here they are; you were in liquor.
