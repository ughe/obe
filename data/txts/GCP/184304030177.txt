JAMES Cook. I am the prosecutor's shopman-on the 22nd of March I

MARY BECK. I live in Harrold's-place, John's-row, St. Luke's, which is
next door to Wilson's stable-between nine and ten o'clock on Friday morn-
ing, the 24th of March, I saw Wilson go into the stable-shortly after I saw
Singleton-I afterwards saw a cart and horse go out with some bags-Single-
ton was driving-Wilson had gone on a few minutes before.
Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. What was in these bags you can-
not say? A. No.
THOMAS WELLER. I live in New-street, Borough-road—I let a cart to
Singleton on Thursday, the 9th of March-he returned it on the 24th of
March, from a quarter to ten minutes to one-my cart had my address on it
-I did not let him a horse-he took the horse away with him.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. When did he pay you? A. He
gave me 3s. when he hired it, and 38. more on the 22nd.
JOHN HALES. I am collector of the City tolls at Moorgate-on the 24th
of March, about a quarter past eleven o'clock, there was a horse and cart
driven through my gate by Wilson-I gave him a ticket that was marked
"Moorgate; City toils, 248"_I did not take any notice of the load.
GEORGE EDINGTON. I am collector of the City tolls in Jewry-street, Mino-

on it-the cart appeared to be laden at the top with coffee- I did not see how
many bags- I cannot say who was driving. U OD

ELIZABETH FULLERTON. I am landlady of the house No. 14, Pearl-row,
Blackfriars-road. I know Singleton by sight-between twelve and one
o'clock, on Friday, the 24th of March, I saw him come with a horse and cart
to my door, to Aburrow, who has my front room-be carried up something,
I do not know what he came down, and drove off-soon after he came back
with the horse, took off the harness, and went up stairs-it would take him
five or ten minutes to go to the Borough-road.
