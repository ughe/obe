 "41, Devonshire-street, Queen-square, September 23rd, 1836.
 "Sir,—I beg to acquaint you I have seen my patron this day, and as I have some incidental expenses to incur previous to the completion of the transaction, I will thank you to forward by my clerk the sum of 2  l . 10 s ., if convenient to yourself, expedition appearing to be an object to you; the appointment will be forwarded to you, on or before Tuesday next, in due time to afford you the opportunity of making the necessary arrangements. I am, Sir, your obedient servant, G S. MUTCH . "—" I acknowledge to have received 50 s ., being the balance of 10 l ., paid to me by Mr. Baker, for the purpose mentioned on the other side. I met the prisoner the same evening—I told him I had received a letter by his clerk—I told him that Mr. Edge had called upon me—he said, "Yes, I sent him with a note for an advance of 50 s "—I told him I hoped he was not deceiving me in any way, and he declared to his God that he was not—I told him, under these circumstances, I could depend on him as a man of honour, and I would advance the 50 s ., on the following Monday morning—I did so, by way of loan, at this office—I lent it—I told the clerk so at the time—it was so understood between the prisoner and myself—he offered, on a subsequent occasion, to give me an I O U for the money—I cannot exactly say the date—I had several interviews with the prisoner—I was to pay him 100 l . by instalments—that was said wen I signed the agreement—the prisoner gave me this copy of it on the 31st of October as a substitute—he gave me this as a copy of the other, as near as his clerk could draw it out—I saw it signed by the prisoner himself—I saw his clerk draw of the body—I had lost the former one ( read )—
