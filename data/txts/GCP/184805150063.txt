JAMES FELL. I am chief clerk at Marylebone police-court. I attended
the prisoner's examination on 17th April-he made a statement I wrote it
down-(reads“I have nothing to say; I have been in the Company's
employ nearly two years, and never was reported") on that being read, it
was stated that he used the words, "I am sorry it happened" I did not
hear him say so—the Magistrate asked him if he made use of those words-
he said, “Yes”-The Magistrate said, " That amounts to a confession ; do
you wish that to be taken down ?"-be said, “ Yes," and I took it down.
GUILTY. Aged 23.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined Two Months.
1279. HENRY LEONARD, stealing llb. of bread, value 2d. ; the goods
of Edward William Thompson, his master.
EDWARD WILLIAN THOMPSON. I am a baker, at Lisson-grove, the pri-
soner was in my service. On 21st April, about seven o'clock in the evening,
he came up from the bakehouse, and through the shop into the street
he was not going on my business-I went after him, and asked what he
bad got that did not belong to him-he said, “Nothing" - I said, “That
will not do-his pocket bulged out-I said, “What have you got here ?"
- he had a piece of bread in each pocket-he said he was going to give it to
a boy in the street, whom he described--I left him in the shop with a police
man, and went and found the boy, who had been working for me that morni
ing, and then went to the bakehouse and found four or five ball-quartern
loaves in a basket in which they put the bread which they consume it was
mine it had no business there the men are allowed to eat what they like
on the premises, but not to have five loaves-it would not have to be carried
out to my customers—those baskets were in the shop-I have two other
men-1 allow one of them, who is married, bread to take home he sends
one of his children to fetch it.
