whom I have known many years- I went to a doctor's shop to purchase
a plaster to cure corns- I put it into my pocket in a paper-I had two 5l.
Bank-notes, and 101. in gold, in a purse in my right hand trowsers pocket-
there were sovereigns and half-sovereigns-I had some silver in my other
pocket-I felt my purse safe when I was in the doctor's shop, in Milpond-
street--after I parted with Mr. Bryan I came straight along Milpond-street,
in a direction towards my own house-as I was coming along I had to pass
the end of a road or street-I think it is called the Neckinger--there is
a public-bouse very convenient to it, called Lillyput-ball-at the corner
of the street I saw two women and a man standing still-I passed them,
and as I passed I heard a woman's voice say, " There is Connor, from the
Borough"-I walked on towards the railway- I heard a woman run after
me-she came up to me, and got hold of my arm- it was the prisoner, Cun-
ningham-she said, "Are you not going to stand something to drink?"--I
said, "No," or something to that effect--we walked on about twenty yards,
and got to a railway arch-we went round the arch up out of sight-she
began to handle me, and she had an opportunity of putting her hand into my
pocket-she did not say anything, but ran away with great swiftness-I
missed my purse, and ran after her, and called, "Stop thief"-I fell down,
and my hat fell off-I left my hat, and ran after her- I did not go far before
a man came up, and said, “Connor, did she rob you of your money?" I said,
"Yes"-that man came in a direction from Lilliput-ball, where I had seen
a man and two women-to the best of my belief, that man was the prisoner,
Williams-he seized my gold guard-chain, but my waistcoat being tight he did
not succeed in getting it-the chain broke--I said, “You villain, do you mean
to rob me of my watch?" and I hit him in the mouth-Bryan came up imme-
diately, and he said to me, "Don't strike the man, Connor"-I said, "Why,
he intended to rob me of my watch"-Bryan said, “You villain, did you mean
to rob the man ?" and took hold of him--I said, "Keep him till I sing out
'Police!'"-I went singing out “Police !" as loud as I could-we could not
find one directly-in the course of some time after I saw Bryan again-I said,
"You do not mean to say you have let the prisoner go?"-he said yes, he was
obliged to let him go-1 afterwards saw a constable, and gave him informa-
tion-he said, "I know who it is"-I know I had my purse when I went up
the archway, and it was gone when the woman was running away-there was
nobody that could have taken it but her- gave a description of the purse
and money-I said there was a small half-sovereign of William the Fourth's
reign--you may have 1001. in gold, and not have one like that.
