RICHARD IMRAY. I live at Saville-toad, Mile-end-road. On 14th Oet.,
1848, about half-past three in the morning, I was walking to town, and as I
passed the Fountain public-house, id the Mile-end-road, I saw a wagon with
carrots in it, and saw the prisoner and two other persons unloading the
carrots into the Fountain.stable prisoner was on the wagon, and the two
others carrying them into the stable- afterwards saw the prisoner go on with
the wagon-about three-quarters of an hour afterwards I went with a police-
man to the stable, and saw the carrots there-he took charge of them, and I
gave information to Mr. Gregory.
Prisoner. Q. How could you tell me frorn the other men by that light ?
A. The shutter was down at the Fountain, and the gas shone on you.
EDWARD ABRAMS. I am foreman to Mr. Gregory, a salesman of Spitalfields-
market. On the morning of the 15th the prisoner brought a wagon of carrots
there-there were six or seven wagons same that morning from Mr. Gregory,
and one man brought a note of the whole-the prisoner left his wagon, took
an empty one, and went to load some dung-Mr, Imray then gave me infor-
mation- examined the wagon,-a and found the carrots had been disturbed, and
there was a space where some had been taken from-I then went to the stable
at the Fountain, and found three dozen and two, bunches of carrots, which I
compared with the others and found them to be the same-Mr Gregory sells
entirely for Mr. Adams-] can swear they were Mr. Adams's carrots-w told
over the remainder of the carrots, and there were three dozen and three
bunches short-1 went after the prisoner to Theobald's-road, where he was to
load the dung, and found the wagon and four horses there, and no driver
I did not see him again till this Oct.
CHARLBS ROWBURY (policeman, K 347). I received information, and
looked after the prisoner, but did not find him till 19th last Sept., when I
saw him on top of a wagon in Stratford-] took him into custody--he said if
he had done anything wrong he must suffer for it.
