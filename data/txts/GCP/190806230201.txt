Cross-examined by Mr. Fitch. Loftus came once or twice. I
think it is wrong that I said before the magistrate, "I do not remem-
ber seeing him at the office." I cannot fix the dates. I was a con-
fidential friend of Calcutt's and visited him at his private house.
I knew Mrs. Calcutt and have seen her since his arrest and done
what I could to help her.
Cross-examined by Mr. Vachell. I never habitually used the
* Three Crowns." I used to go there with Caloutt soon after I
entered his employment. It was usually in office hours. I am
builder's estimating clerk. I did not inspect the repairs after they
were done. I was in Caloutt's employment in 1904. In regard to
eroavation, the longer the drain the more exoavation. We do not, as
. rule, charge anything else than 1s. a piece for kitchen paper unless
specified by the customers. This item refers to 25, Bowden Street,
October 5, 1907. It is leading out of Bow Road. It is 6d. piece
for paper and 6d. a piece for hanging. To an ordinary customer I
would charge about 10s. 6d. I do not know what the paper cost. It
would be necessary in many of these cases to see the work and what
bad been done in order to see whether the charge was usual or not.
I have had no experience in buying, selling, or valuing horses, carts,
or harness. The cob I took to be in good condition and the
governess car was practically new. I made no deduction for wear
and tear. Calcutt bought another vehicle soon after he sold the cob
and governess oar.
Cross-examined by Mr. Edwards. Trott at one time supplied On-
outt with sand.
Cross-examined by Mr. Muir. I have gone to Southend every
wook to see Calcutt since he has been in trouble and spent a week
there last August. I have heard that Warren has gone to live
there. I made alterations in the bill book of the same characters
those in the rough book-adding on to items. The bills sent into
the Guardians were exact copies of the bill book. If the bills con-
tained entries of materials as being supplied which were never in
fact supplied, such entries must have been taken from the bill books.
The bill books were made out by me through Caloutt's instructions.
There were no entries, to my knowledge, of materials not supplied. I
must have known it. The books were destroyed because Osloutt
said, owing to alterations made from time to time in them, it was
best for them not to be in evidence, and I destroyed them on in
structions, knowing the reason for doing so.
Cross-examined by Mr. Mallinson. Gilder spoke to me on occasions
about his account, which may have been early in 1906. When Knight
went round measuring he had not a copy of my bill book to check.
My account was rendered quarterly.
Re-examined. The alterations in the books were dishonest. Calcutt
gave me a reason for making the alterations.
