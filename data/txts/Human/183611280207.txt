   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Are you a single man? A.  I am—I have known Alexander about a month, and Tyer about three years—Tyer has continually called at my quarters, but Alexander only once—they had been there together about five days before the 8th of November—they were in the habit of visiting me together—Alexander has been twice—Tyer had been in the habit of visiting me before my return—I had recently returned from abroad—when they came together one staid half an hour, and the other about two hours, and drank—Tyer was in the habit of drinking when she came—we were on terms of very intimate familiarity—sometimes she has staid four or five hours, and sometimes more or less—I generally treated her with liquor when she came—she introduced Alexander to me.
   Q.  Now, I ask you, were not these persons on terms of such familiarity with you that they might be induced to think they had some title to make free with your property? A.  Certainly not in my ab sence—Tyer has been in the habit of sitting with me in my quarters for three or four hours together drinking—they generally took wine, and occa sionally spirits and water—I first got acquainted with Tyer in Woolwich—I think she was in service at that time—she has visited me a great many times. CHARLES HAYES. I am a sergeant in the Royal Marines. On the 8th of November I went to the prosecutor's quarters about ten o'clock and found the prisoner Tyer lying on the top of the area, against the front door, on her back, very drunk indeed—one part of her body was through the gate, partly in the area of the next house, and partly out, with three table-cloths and a pair of trowsers lying within a foot of her, and some might be two feet from her—the front door and private door of the prosecutor's house were both wide open—Tyer was so drunk that she had no senses—I took a light to see whether she was dead or alive—she was frothing at the mouth just as if she was mad—I thought at first she was dead—I looked at her for some time, and then went into the prosecutor's quarters, and on the table there was a small basket with several articles in it—there was a pillow-case marked with his name on it—I left it there, locked the door, and took the key with me, and put a sentry there—when the pro secutor returned I gave him the key and his property—I went with him to his quarters about a quarter before eleven o'clock, and found the door as I had left it—I had Tyer taken to the guard room—Alexander had come crying to me about ten o'clock, saying, that Tyer had been in Lieutenant Varlo's quarters, and taken out some property, and she went back with me to the prosecutor's quarters—she had been drinking, as she smelt strong of liquor.
