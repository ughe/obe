   WILLIAM MALONEY. I am a labourer, and live in Poplar-place, St. Pancras.  Prisoner. That is the truth; it was my clock: I was forced to pawn it for 2s.
   HUGH SAVAGE. I am a labourer, and live in Harcourt-street. On the 23rd of March I was at the prosecutor's, and saw the prisoner go up stairs with a scuttle of coke and a tea-kettle swung round his waist - he then left the premises with the kettle.
   JAMES SMITH. I am a labourer, and live in Suffolk-street. I was at the prosecutor's on the 23rd of March, and saw the prisoner go up stairs with the scuttle of coke, and a tea-kettle swung round his waist - he left the house with the tea-kettle - his jacket hung over it.

 872  GEORGE WILLIAMS and JAMES ROGERS were indicted for feloniously assaulting Elizabeth Lower , and with a certain sharp instrument striking and cutting her, with intent to kill and murder her.
 TWO OTHER COUNTS, stating his intent to be to disable or do her some grievous bodily harm.
   ELIZABETH LOWER. I am the wife of John Lower, a tailor; we live at No. 12, Edden-street. On the evening of the 22nd of February I was sitting in my parlour between six and seven o'clock; I heard a knock at the door; I got up, opened the door, and saw the two prisoners - they both asked if Mr. Lower was at home - I said, No, he was not - Williams then asked what time I expected him - I said it was rather uncertain, as he never came home at one hour - he said, "He is a tailor" - I said, Yes - he said he wanted to be measured for a suit of clothes - I asked him if Mr. Lower knew he was coming - he said No- he then said, "If you will give me pen, ink, and paper, I will write the direction down" - I went into the back parlour to get pen, ink, and paper - they both came into the room - they had at first knocked at my front parlour door, which opens very near to the street door - the street door was shut - they came in some how - I do not know how, but I saw a latch-key afterwards found on Williams - I do not know of any other means they had of getting in but by that key - when they got into the back parlour I had got the pen in my hand, and Williams struck me on the head with a crow-bar, and then Rogers put all his fingers into my mouth, to stop my breath; and said if I made any noise he would murder me with the crow-bar - there were two knocks at the street door the prisoners left me in the back parlour, and went out - they opened the street door, and I heard them say, Go in to the old woman, for she has been murdered" - they said that to Reuben Wheeler, who had given the two knocks at the door - I came out, and cried, "Murder; it is them that came to murder me; there they are; there they go" - Wheeler ran after them, and Williams was brought back by the Police - I said, "You are the man who came to murder me" - my mouth was all pouring out with blood, and my head, where I had received the blow; the blood from my mouth was occasioned by Rogers forcing his fingers into my mouth, to try to strangle me - it was done by main force; it gave me great pain, and I could not eat any thing but what was cold for some time, my mouth was so sore - I feel the effects of the blow on my head to this day; I went to a surgeon, and Elizabeth Hedges dressed my head for me - I did not fall quite down, I went down on my side, but not quite to the ground; I stooped with the blow, but recovered my position, and was able to observe them go to the door, and followed them with the blood running down my mouth - the wound in my head bled very much; the skin was a good deal cut - I saw the latch-key taken from Williams in my room, also a knife, and the crow-bar; it is a large knife, which shuts in - Rogers' hand was in my mouth when the knock came at the door; it was almost instantaneously that the knock came to the door.
