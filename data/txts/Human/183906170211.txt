   Q.  Do you remember in the course of the transaction, whether there was any portion of gold that did not come into the account? A.  Yes, after all the gold was brought, there were some small pieces of gold which Mrs. Abrahams produced from her pocket, some was out of a little leather bag, not much bigger than a watch-pocket—she gave that to my servant Dean to melt for her, and I weighed it—she said I was not to say any thing to her father about that—to the best of my recollection that weighed between four and five ounces—I paid her 13 l . for it, and that was not put down in the ac count—she took it out of her pocket, and said she had got this out of the shakings out of the bag, or something of that description—I do not ex actly recollect what, but not to say any thing to her father—she particularly pressed that on me—I put the gold in my iron chest for that evening—I re-melted it on the Wednesday morning—my servant Dean assisted me—I put some Russian copper coin with it, but I had previously re-melted on the Tuesday evening one of the bars with about seventy ounces of silver—I put the Russian coin in that I melted on the Wednesday to alter the colour—that is the truth—it is no use to disguise it, and I put the silver in what I melted on the Tuesday for the same purpose—that would have the effect of altering the colour and preventing detection—after I had al loyed the gold with the copper coin on the Wednesday, it was melted into bars—I sent two of those bars to Messrs. Bult, of Cheapside, by my son Selim—on his return he gave me eleven 100 l . notes and one hundred sove reigns—Mrs. Abrahams came in soon after, and I gave her six of the 100 l . notes and one hundred sovereigns—I put that payment down on the me morandum—that reduced my debt with them to 1800/. and some odd pounds—the I O U I had first given her was then destroyed, and I gave her this one—( read  —I O U 1807 l ., H. S.)—my son Selim was present at the time I paid the £600 notes and one hundred sovereigns, to the best of my recollection—upon my giving Mrs. Abrahams the last I O U she begged of me not to destroy, the memorandum, for her father would be very angry with her if she did not return it—she said, "Pray do not de stroy it, sir, for if you do, my father will be very angry with me"—I gave it her back—some part of it was destroyed—I was in the act of doing it at the time, and part of it was torn off—to the best of my recollection, the amounts remained entire, and so did the sums paid her—there was a ba lance due to me from Bult and Co. for the two bars I sent them—I have not received it—I had dealings before with Messrs. Bult for some years.
