   STEPHEN STILLWELL sworn.
 I live with squire Holmes, at Little Ealing; I am his gardener; I never saw the prisoner till the 25th of this month; and I was at the public house at Acton, and Baker and the prisoner went home with me; it was near eleven when we set off; I got home about eleven or a little after; they staid about half an hour with me, and went out into the main road, that leads to Acton, a little before twelve; my house is about two miles from the prisoner's.
 The Constable. My Lord, there were two or three came yesterday, to give the prisoner a character; as the trial did not come on, they went away; Mr. Preddy, his master, was one, with whom he worked a good while.
     NOT GUILTY.

RICHARD ALLEN was indicted for burglariously and feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling house of John Marshall , about the hour of seven in the night, on the 7th of October , and burglariously stealing therein, a silver watch, value 3 l. a silk ribbon, value 1 d. a stone seal, set in base metal, value 1 d. a key, value 1 d. his property.
   JOHN MARSHALL sworn.
 I am a  watch and clock maker; I live at Wapping New-stairs; I lost my watch on the 7th of October; it was hanging on a post at the window, on the inside of the shop, within the glass considerably.
 What time of the day? - About seven in the evening.
 Was the glass broke? - There was two panes broke at once, and another cracked.
 Did you hear the crash of it? - Yes; we were at tea; I was much alarmed at the noise; I jumped up directly, and made to the door; and called the apprentice to mind the watches; before I got the door open, I missed one of the watches, and I immediately ran out and enquired of the neighbours which way the man had gone; I could hardly hear, the people called out so; at last I understood he had taken up the Orchard; it is a near turning; but little frequented of nights; I ran up there as hard as I could, and in the middle I met two women; I asked them if they met a man running that way; they said, he had just passed on; I still run on, and took hold of the prisoner by the collar; says I, it is you that broke my window and robbed my shop; says he, you are mistaken, it is not me; says I, it is; he said, he was going home, he lived at Hoxton; says he, I have some business in Rosemary-lane, and from thence to Hoxton; says I, come along with me, I will see whether you are the man or no; and I brought him round, and asked him where he had been, and he said, he had been to Deptford, and he walked there; I brought him into the shop; there was a crowd of people; says I, it is you, I am positive; have you got the watch about you? he said, no, it was not him; but he did not care, I might send him on board a man of war, he had no objections; so I sent for an officer; my apprentice had ran out after me, and missed me; I had the prisoner in the house; when he came home again, I opened the door to let him in; and my apprentice said, that is the man that broke the window and stole the watch; the man fell a crying; the officer came, and he was searched; we could not find the watch, or any thing, but a piece of a razor; we got a lanthorn to look for the watch, but could not find any such thing; I came back again, and said to the officer, what can we do about this affair, the apprence still persists in it; he was taken to the watch-house, and was locked up; and in about three or four minutes there came a girl of the town to him, and called him out, and said to him, Dick, have you got in the Watch-house! he said, yes; he told her to go
