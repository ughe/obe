(Tuesday, November 22.)
GRACE LILIAN Cox, 16, Cronshaw Road, West Ealing. From
June, 1909, to September, 1910, I was employed by prisoner as a
shorthand typist and book-keeper. I used to help to address the
green circulars. We sent out upwards of 20 a day. When the
slides were returned we formerly sent out a printed circular stating
the number of defective slides. This was afterwards altered into a
printed typed circular. Prisoner stated that it was altered because
people complained of the printed form as showing that it was made up
beforehand. I kept the invoice book for goods sold, the ledger and
day book, also a book showing people who had slides for home employ.
ment in quantities of 60 dozen. There are about 60 names, amongst
which I remember Rowe, Miss York, Mrs. Hyrons, Russell, and
Gillam as having had their deposit back. Other people who called
personally received their deposit from Miss Woods. The letters that
came from people who paid the 58. deposit were counted up into
hundreds and sold to Windusa and Co., of Hamburg. The letter I
wrote to prisoner was included with those ; I afterwards received a
circular about a lottery.
Cross-examined. There was considerable business done in photo-
graphic implements, mechanical toys, etc. The names of people who
called at the works and received their 5s. were not entered. I do
not remember anyone except those I have mentioned who received
