BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT.
(Friday, July 5.)
DOLAND, Henry (33, agent), forging and uttering, knowing the
same to be forged, the endorsements on certain orders for the payment
of £15 17s., £31 12s., and £6 9s. 9d, respectively, in each case with in-
tent to defraud; having received certain property, to wit, the said
cheques for £15 17., £31 12s., and £6 9s. 9d. for and on account of
Ernest Noeh, unlawfully did fraudulently convert the same to his own
use.
Mr. Eustace Fulton prosecuted; Mr. Daniel Warde defended.
Ernest Noer, trimming manufacturer, Annenberg, Saxony, On
October 1, 1911, I entered into agreement with prisoner (produced)
to act as agent for me in London, obtain orders, transmit them to me,
and render accounts furnished by me to the customers, which were
to be paid direct to me. Among the customers introduced was
Bususto, of 29, Barbican. Produced is my monthly statement dated
January 21, 1911, for £16 5s. 5d. less 24 per cent.-£15 17s. It bears
printed on the side “All payments to be made direct to the firm.” It
was sent by me to prisoner. Similar statements are produced for
goods delivered in February amounting, after deducting 37 per cent.,
to £31 12s. 9d., and of March 22, 1912, for £6 9s. 9d. On this state-
ment the words "Payments to be made direct to the firm" have been
cut off. Three cheques produced for £15 17s., £31 12s., and
£6 9s. 9d. are made payable to Ernest Noeh or order, they are endorsed
in my name, not by me and without any authority given by me. I
wrote to prisoner about Bususto's account and received letters from
