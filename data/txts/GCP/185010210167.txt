time this happened, and Costello was so about three months before Taylor
worked at the printing-office at Brixton, about 300 yards from the dwelling
house-two or three weeks or a month before the robbery, Taylor told me he
had put Costello on to Mr. Martin's-Mr. Martin is Mr. Stiff's foreman the
day after that, he told me that they were disturbed by a cat, and all cats ought
to be stuck with a knife-about a week before this robbery, he told me he

away-he said he was going to the Crown and Sceptre, and would wait for

and Sceptre, about thirty yards off, and saw Taylor there we had some
porter, and stayed till about half-past six-Taylor then went down Brixton
bill with his father-I heard of the robbery next morning, and recollect a mari
named Haydon telling Taylor that Mr. Stiff had had his house broken open-
Taylor laughed, and nodded his head, and turned quite red the same
morning Taylor told me he was going to Drury-lane to get his shack of the
money--the same evening he came to me, and said, “Be on your guard;
you will have the pigs on you all down the road”-I did not exactly know
what he meant, and said, "I have nothing to fear or care " I have heard him
say that pigs means “policemen" he said he should disguise himself, and
take the other road; and before he went I saw him tie a bandkerchief round
his neck, which was contrary to his usual dress-it went round twice, and
came over his chin-I did not make any communication to Mr. Martin or
Mr. Stiff thought he was joking

Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How long was it before you told
Mr. Stiff anything? A. About a fortnight-Taylor is the son of a respectable
carpenter and joiner employed by large brewers in London—there was no
arrangement that I and Taylor should quit the premises of an evening before
the other people Mr. Martin was there when Taylor left-it was usual for
him and us to go to the Crown and Sceptre-I found him there when I got there
--there were none of our hands there-there were two or three people; I do
not know who they were exactly-one was Dellar--the waiters at the Crown
and Sceptre are Nash and Bardell-I got there about a quarter to six-there
is a clock there, which I looked at-I saw Taylor from that time till he went
away with his father-we had some rum and water together--I went away
with him at half-past six-he met his father at the nursery and parted from
me, and they kept ahead all down the road saw him with bis father all
the way to Oakley-street, which is about four miles from Mr. Stiff's-his
father lives in the Commercial-road, near Waterloo-bridge-I have had three
situations- I was not discharged for passing bad money-I took some bad
