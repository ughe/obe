 221.  ANN MOORE was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 7 pieces of ribbon, containing 82 yards, value 14s., the goods of James Morrison and others.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
   ROBERT LUSHER. I am shopman to James Morrison and Co. On the 9th of November the prisoner came to the shop, and purchased some ribbons; she had several lengths cut off, but paid for none. I saw her take two pieces of ribbon from the drawers, and put them into her pocket; I informed the person who has the care of the shop; I saw her searched, and these two pieces of black ribbon found on her - they are my employers' property, and worth about 3s.
 MR.  JOHN DILLON. I am one of the managing partners of James Morrison and Co's concern. I saw these two pieces of black ribbon produced by the prisoner; Lusher then left the room, but it struck me there might be more upon the prisoner, and I insisted upon her emptying her pocket; she then produced seven other pieces, five of which I can identify, by having our private mark on them - she acknowledged she had taken them; I had not promised to forgive her.
   DANIEL FORRESTER. I was sent for, and took the prisoner; I have made inquiries, and find her friends are very respectable.
 Prisoner. I hope you will have mercy upon me; it is the first time I ever did such a thing.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Two Months.
 222.  THOMAS WELLER, alias    WHEELER, was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of November , 1 great coat, value 30s., the goods of George Weller.
 GEORGE WELLER. I am a  coachman, and live in Mr. Gray's yard. The prisoner is my own brother. On the 13th of November I had occasion to go out at four o'clock, and did not return till four in the morning; my child gave me information, and I missed the coat from where it had hung when I left it - the prisoner did not live in the family, and I had not sent him for it; I have not spoken to him these four months.
   MARY ANN WELLER. I am the daughter of George Weller. I shall be nine years of age in January - my uncle came and asked if my father was at home - I said he was gone out - he asked if my mother was at home - I said No - he said he saw my father go into the yard with a gig; he then went away, and came again and knocked at the door as hard as he could, and rang the bell, and said my father wanted his great coat, as he was going out with a chariot; I said my father was gone out post - he said his job was altered - he was going out with a chairot - he then ran up stairs, took the coat, and ran down.
   JOHN NORRIS. I received information, and took the prisoner on the Friday following; I asked if he knew any thing about a robbery at Mr. Gray's - I did not then know it was his brother; he laughed at me, and said, "Now I shall go to Newgate again for it, but I don't care; I shall get over it." I found three duplicates on him, but none relating to this coat; I then went down to Mr. Gray's, and found it was his brother.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am not guilty of the charge - the words were put into the child's month at the office.
   JOHN NORRIS. I did not say a word to her - the coat has not been found.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Transported for Seven Years.
 223.  THOMAS BRIND was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of November , 2 ozs. of silver turnings, value 10s., the goods of John Bridge and others, his masters.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   CATO SHARP. I superintend the manufactory of Mr. John Bridge and others, at my house in Dean-street, Soho; their firm is Rundle and Bridge; the prisoner worked at my house, and had two guineas a week; I had him apprehended in consequence of some information, at the house of Mr. Sirrell, a refiner and dealer in bullion, on the 13th of November - I found him there, and asked what he did there, as his work was so much wanted - he said, "I came here on a little business of my own;" I said, "You can have no business here;" Mr. Sirrell's young man pointed to some silver turnings in a paper, and said he came to sell this; I took the paper and held it up, and said, "You know, Brind, this is not your property - you know you have taken it from Dean-street;" he said, "Yes, I have;" he had been turning silver in the warehouse that morning; he has been in our employ about two years - he is married, and had a good character - here is 2 ozs. of silver, worth 10s. - it cannot be sworn to.
   LAWRENCE CHILD. I am shopman to Mr. Sirrell. The prisoner brought the silver turnings to our shop on the 13th of November; I told him to call in an hour, and went to Mr. Sharp; I have heard what he has stated, it is correct.
   JOHN BARNES. I am an officer. I took the prisoner.
 GUILTY. Aged 33. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Six Months.
