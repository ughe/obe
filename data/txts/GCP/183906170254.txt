JOHN HARDWICK, Esq. I am one of the police Magistrates at the office
in Lambeth-street. I have been so about seventeen years—the examina-
tion of the prisoners and the witnesses in this case, was conducted before
me-I am the committing Magistrate- I remember some papers being pre-
sented to me, which I took into my private room-I had had no communi-
cation with the parties to whom the statement alluded before they were put
into my hands--they were put in publicly in the office in the presence of the
counsel and attorney-I took these statements into my private room at the
request of the counsel, which was made publicly-it was publicly stated to
be for the purpose of enabling me to ascertain whether, in my discretion,
I should receive the testimony of Solomon as a witness for the Crown-
the prisoners were present, and the counsels and attorneys on all sides, and
the office full of people-I desired the clerk to accompany me to the pri-
vate room-the attorney of Solomon was present in my private room--
the statements were in the hands of the clerk, and I believe were
read by the clerk in the presence of the young Solomon and Solomon's
attorney-each of the persons who had made statements were brought
into the room to me by themselves, and each of the statements were read
to me without the interference of any body-the statements were read
to each separately, and the others were not in the room at the time-after
that had been done, and I had come to my conclusion as to the course to
be taken, the persons left the private room, and were taken into the office,
and there a viva voce examination by counsel took place publicly.
