   Cross-examined. Q.  Do not you know it was bought by him for the prisoner? A.  I do not know it—the conveyances were made out to Mr. John Trevethen—we should not have had them made out to a man who was a bankrupt, and had not received his certificate—I have known the pri soner ten or twelve years, living at Bury St. Edmunds—he left very shortly after he took the benefit of the Act—I believed him respectable up to the time of his bankruptcy. JAMES SMITH( police-constable H  113.) On the 30th of October I re ceived the prisoner in custody from the prosecutor—on our way to the sta tion-house he asked me with what he was charged—I told him, for obtaining wine by false pretences from Mr. Rarasbotham—he said he wished to have the business settled, and he had thrown himself in the way on purpose—he gave up some papers, among which were these three warrants, for a hogshead of wine each.
   MR. RAMSBOTHAM re-examined.  This is the deed the prisoner showed me, and this was the part he particularly pointed out—( reads )—"to be vested, &c, for the benefit of Thomas Trevethen, his heirs and assigns, as his private and absolute property"—it was believing what he stated about that which induced me to part with the warrants—he never told me it had been sold to his brother, or that his brother held it—he never mentioned his brother—he said he was heir to his father—he did not mention his mother at all.
