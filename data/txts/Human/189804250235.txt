CAROLINE DUCHAL. I am the mother of the last witness—she showed me a five-shilling piece—I took it in my hand—it was light and greasy, and I thought it was bad—I did not identity the prisoner. WILLIAM HEAD( Detective Officer ). I received some information and a description—on the morning of April 7th I saw the prisoner—these two shops are some two miles apart—I said "I am a police officer, and I shall take you for passing counterfeit coin"—I searched him and found nineteen shillings in silver, and three pence in copper, but no bad money—there were three half-crowns and three sixpences—I took him to the station—he was identified in the evening by the two witnesses—he made no reply to the other charge.
   Cross-examined.  There is a window in the inspectors office, but the witnesses did not go in there. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to Her Majesty's Mint—this crown is counterfeit. GUILTY to the uttering on March 25th. — Judgment respited.
 344. RICHARD BANKS  (28) , Feloniously uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. PARTRIDGE Prosecuted. JOHN CARTER(234 V ). On March 26th I was on point duty in Battersea about 6.30 and saw the prisoner looking into the shops in a suspicious manner with Webb ( see page  658)—I kept them under observation—they got to the corner of Cairns Road, when Blinks pulled something like paper, about the size of his fist, out of his pocket, took something from it and handed it to Webb—I walked along on the other side—they turned up a road in the dark and I lost sight of them—I walked along the Northgate Road and received some information—I saw Banks looking into Mr. Piper's shop window at 19, Northgate Road—I went to the shop door and said, "Who wants me?"—Banks looked at me and ran away as hard as he could—Mr. Piper and Webb were struggling in the shop—I arrested Webb and gave Banks' description to Serjeant Windsor—I picked him out from twelve others on the night of Good Friday—I have no doubt about him.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I did not see you through the small window of the inspector's office—that is Webb ( pointing to him ).
   Re-examined.  I received a counterfeit coin from Piper and one shilling from Webb—there in a yard at the station—I never saw the prisoner from the time I first saw him till I picked him out. ETHEL PIPER. I am a fancy draper with my father, at 19, Northgate Road, Battersea—on March 26th Webb came in "and gave me a bad five-shilling piece—I gave it to my father, who locked the door—he had a struggle with Webb, who was handed over to the police—that is Webb ( pointing to the prisoner )—I did not see the prisoner. GEORGE PIPER. I am father of the last witness—on March 26th Webb came in and put down a five-shilling piece—I locked the door and gave a signal—Webb asked me to look outside to see his father—I looked out and saw the prisoner Banks, to the best of my belief, and I afterwards picked him out from five others at the station—I believe he is the man, but I was struggling with Webb at the time.
