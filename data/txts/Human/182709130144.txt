 1942.  JOHN HAWKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , 1 watch, value 6l., the goods of William Light.
   WILLIAM JONES. I live with Mr. Light, of Skinner-street; he keeps a Birmingham warehouse- the prisoner came with two others to buy a pin-cushion; they went out, came in again, and wanted another like it, which we had not got - we said we would change the first one - the prisoner then bought a comb, and went away; they came again with a fourth person, who wanted a knife, and while my mistress was gone into the parlour, I saw the prisoner with a gold watch in his hand; he was shutting down the glass-case from which he had taken it; he ran out; I pursued - the others ran after me, and I was tripped up. I do not know what became of the watch - the glass-case was on the counter.
 Cross-examined by MR. McDOWELL. Q. Was it not a dark night? A. Yes; I never lost sight of him - my master told me to make quite sure of him.
 COURT. Q. Do you swear your master told you to swear to him at all events? A. No.
   WILLIAM LIGHT. I keep this house, it is in the parish of St. Sepulchre. I told my boy he might be asked if he was positive of the prisoner, and to speak accurately to what he knew. I was out at the time - I had seen a gold watch in the case about an hour before.
 Cross-examined. Q. What was the lowest value of it? A. The selling price is 7l. 10s. I paid about 6l. 10s. for it.
   GEORGE BUSH. On the 13th of September, between eight and nine o'clock at night, I was in Skinner-street - I saw the prisoner come out of this shop; he was running towards King-street, and Jones running after him - three more came out, and ran right before him. Jones fell down - I ran and caught the prisoner - we brought him back to Mr. Light's. Mrs. Light said, "That is the boy who took the watch out of the case."
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it not dark? A. There was plenty of gas-light. I found 2s. on him.
   GEORGE GODFREY. I am an officer. I took the prisoner in charge.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 12.
 Of stealing to the value of 99s. only. Whipped and Discharged.
 1943.  THOMAS BECKETT was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of September , 1 basket, value 1.s, and 5 gallons of damsons, value 2s., the goods of Samuel Jacobs.
 SAMUEL JACOBS. I am a  fruit-salesman, of Fleet-market. I lost a basket of damsons; this basket is mine, and stood under my window.
   GEORGE GODFREY. On the 6th of September, at night, I went up Wheatsheaf-yard, and found the prisoner asleep in a cart, with this basket of damsons under him. I asked where he got it - he said "I wanted money, and intended to sell it."
   GEORGE STILLING. I am a watchman. I was with Godfrey, and took the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was led into this by others.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Confined for Fourteen Days.
 Third London Jury - before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 1944.  THOMAS HEFFIELD, alias    BROWN, was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , at St. Giles without, Cripplegate, 1 gelding, price 6l., the property of John Stevens.
 JOHN STEVENS. I am a  higler, and live at Chingfordgreen, Essex. On Tuesday, the 24th of July, I was at Chelmsford races, where I had a drinking-booth. I saw my gelding safe at ten o'clock that night, when I sent my man with it - it was a black gelding - it was brought down to be fed at the booth; it was turned out to grass on Galley-common, adjoining the race-course - there were other stall-keepers' horses there. I sent my man for it next morning and it was gone - my man is not here. I sent him nearly twenty miles to look for it; my wife's brother found it in Smithfield. I saw it there myself, at the Ram Inn-yard, on the following Saturday - I know nothing of the prisoner.
   THOMAS BARKER. I am an officer of Smithfield. On Friday the 27th of July, I was on duty in Smithfield - it was horse market day; some man came to me, and asked if I would come to the Ram-yard, and take possession of a horse which they had found, belonging to somebody in the country. I went and found this gelding there, in possession of a man named Clinsey; and from what he said, I went to Dixon's Repository, and from what I heard there, I went there on the Monday at nine o'clock - I left, and went again at ten; I then found the prisoner in custody. I asked him where he got the horse; he said a man met him in the street leading the horse, and asked him to take it and sell it for him, and he would give him 5s. if he would sell it, and bring him the money. I asked the prisoner his name, he said it was Thomas Brown, and that the man who employed him was John Linton, of Church-street, Bethnal-green.
