I feel assured that you are already convinced that the contents of the indictment are nothing more than a tissue of falsehoods, and that this is only intended for the purpose of putting down public opinion—of destroying the right of public meeting, and making victims of honest men. Do not lend yourselves as the tool of such a Government; but let your manly and consistent verdict this day tell the tyrants of misrule and oppression, that Englishmen shall inherit the ancient rights of their forefathers, and the gratitude of a grateful people will reverence your memories in ages yet to come. On Friday even ing, 28th July, I was present at a meeting at the Chartist-hall, Webber-street, Blackfriars-road—I got there a few minutes before eight—about 150 persons were then assembled—the number afterwards increased to the extent of between four and five hundred—they consisted of mechanics—the defend ant presided as chairman—he was not in the chair before I got into the room, but a few minutes afterwards, as the numbers gradually increased, the chair man and other parties appeared on the platform—he took the chair without being moved in—he addressed the meeting in the first instance—I took a note of his speech, and have it here—( reads )—"Ladies and Gentlemen, we have met this evening to give utterance to sentiments of sympathy in behalf of a people who have suffered the first shock of the tyrant's vengeance. Whether the intelligence that was received, and generally circulated yesterday, is correct or incorrect it still requires some time to elucidate; but it is very evident that the excuse, and the lame story told by Sir George Grey last night in the House of Commons, tallies very ill with what appears this morning in the papers. A newspaper when it receives intelligence from its correspondent at a distance, know whether to place confidence in the man or not; and it is not likely that so many correspondents would put their heads together to send one and the same story to this country by express, and then the next day contradict every word of it. They must. have stated, the correspondents themselves must have acknowledged, this morning, that they were gross liars, and it is not very likely that they are going to do that for their employers; or else they must have stated that they fell asleep from over fatigue, and dreamt all about it.
