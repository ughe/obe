1978. JOHN ELSWORTHY and WILLIAM HAYNES were indicted
for stealing 32lbs. weight of beef, value 188.; the goods of Henry Caswell.
RICHARD CASWELL. I am in the service of my brother, Henry Caswell.
butcher, in Belvidere-place, Southwark. On Saturday afternoon last I wa
outside the shop, about three o'clock, and saw the prisoners standing at the end
of the window-board-there was a rump of beef on the end of the board
was called to attend in the shop, and when I came back the beef was gone
it was about 32lbs. weight-it was worth about 188.--we sell it at 7d. a lb.
- I gave information, and afterwards saw the prisoners in custody.
Cross-examined by Mr. Meller. Q. Were there many persons about the
shop? A. Not a great many, ten or a dozen perhaps.
SARAH JANE HARRISON. I am the wife of James Harrison-I live in
Flint-street, Southwark. On Saturday, the 19th of Sept., I was in King.
street-I saw the prisoners come from Friar-street into King-street, in
direction from the prosecutor's shop-as they passed I saw Elsworthy had a
large rump of beef under his left arm-I was speaking to a friend-it was
covered with a dirty handkerchief, but the tail of the beef hung out-I went
on and was passing the prosecutor's shop-Richard Caswell said to me, "I
have lost a rump of beef" - I told him what I had seen, and gave information
to the police--the prisoners appeared to be in company-Haynes was walk-
ing by the left side of Elsworthy-I am quite sure they are the persons.
Cross-examined. Q. King-street is rather crowded? A. No-it leads
into Friar-street, and up into Union-street- I did not notice any person
but the person I was speaking to-I had never seen either of the prisoners
before-- it was about five minutes walk from the prosecutor's-both the
prisoners had caps on, nearly alike, close caps to their heads-one of them
was dressed in fustian-it was a few minutes after three o'clock.
Q. Did you not express some doubt whether Elsworthy was the person!
A. When I saw him I said I was not sure, but when he stood up I was sure
- I now know that he is respectably connected.
WILLIAM ALFRED HEADINGTON. I am in the service of Mr. Elwood,
who keeps the Friar public-house, in Friar-street. Last Saturday I was in
the back washhouse, cleaning my pots-the prisoners came in, and Elsworthy
produced a dirty handkerchief with about 1} lb. of rump steak in it-I noticed
that it was cut some part thick and some thin-it was not equally cut-he
asked if I would cook that steak for him-I said, “Yes"-I said, "Have
you got some cow-beef ?" seeing it was in a dirty handkerchief-he said, "No,
it is good rump-steak, it cost me 10d. a pound"-they had the steak and eat
it, and left the house-they seemed in very great agitation, and came to me
three times while I was cooking it-it was about four o'clock-my master!
is not more than two minutes walk from the prosecutor's.
Cross-examined. Q. Were the prisoners dressed in fustian? A. One of
them I think had a corduroy jacket on-I did not notice their caps-1 never
saw a steak cut as that was.
