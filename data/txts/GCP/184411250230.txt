HENRY HOLTON (policeman.) I went to this place at Peckham, and
found this property-the prisoner said nothing to me-he made his
escape.
Prisoner's Defence. A gentleman I was at work for put me into this
house ; the prosecutor came and asked the rent of the house ; I told him;
he said he should want it, that he should sow the ground with grass, and
would give me the job, and that he would give me 28. 6d. a week, and
coals and candles ; I said I was obliged, and would stay; he then brought a
horse, and said might he be put into the stable; I said I did not know, he
must ask Mr. Hudson, the proprietor; he then brought these things, and
soon after went into the country: Mr. Hudson came, and said what busi-
ness had I to let Mr. Skinner bave possession of the house; then Mr.
Skinner came, and said what should he give me to take care of the horse;
I said 2s, a week ; I worked all day, till twelve o'clock at night, and got
no recompense; he then came and found fault with the things he asked
my wife for a screw-driver, got her into the shed, fastened the door, and
would not let her pass; he said, "You had better leave, I am going to
leave." I said, “When do you move?" he said, “This week." I said,
"You had better take these things." My wife gave him a bill, but he did
not pay it ; he came and said, "I want you to go and seize on a shoe-
maker;" I refused; I came back in the evening, and he said, “ Your wife
has given me another bill, when are you going to move ?”. I went and
found all my things packed up, and he said he would lend me a horse and
cart to take them in; we put the things in one corner of the room, and when
I opened them I found I had got these things; I thought he might have
put them in for the money he owed me; I did not see them packed up.
Guilty. Aged 25.-Confined One Year.
