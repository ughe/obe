JAMES MEARA. I am brother to the last witness and the deceased--T
was in the public-house-I heard Mr. Farrow, the landlord, order the men
out-they went out, I went out last, and saw my brother on the ground,
and the prisoner a top of him-I got hold of the prisoner by the collar
and pulled him off-my brother rose and the prisoner struck him a blow
with his clenched fist behind the ear-he staggered and fell insensible to
the ground, while he lay there the prisoner said, “You take me for
looby, but I will let you know what I can do when I start"--my brother
John and Prendergast assisted him up-he stood on the pavement a short
time till he got round a bit, and after he was dressed I carried him home.
Cross-e.camined. I was in the bar at first, and don't know what took
place.
CHARLES BROCKLEY. I am a bricklayer, and live at 23, Malcolm Road,
Upper Holloway-I was at the Junction Arms on September 19th-I
saw the prisoner strike the deceased twice.
Cross-examined. That was inside; I saw nothing that went on outside
CHARLES PRENDERGAST. I am a bricklayer; I was at the Junction
Arms on the evening of September 19th-I saw the prisoner and deceased
fight the prisoner struck him in the face twice; they fell together; they
got up again and sparred—I saw the prisoner strike him a second time,
and he fell on the corner of the table and then to the ground-I then saw
them outside-I saw the deceased lying in the gutter unconscious, and I
helped to pick him up-I saw no blow struck.
