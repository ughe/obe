I saw the prisoner come out of Mrs. Bennett's shop—she pursued him—I collared him and brought him back; he had no bread with him—I took him to the shop—he had a white coat on. BARTHOLOMEW HERRING. I am an officer. I received the prisoner, and this shilling, from Mrs. Bennett—the prisoner denied having been in her shop at all.

 1080. ELIZABETH JONES was indicted for a misdemeanor. ELIZABETH DEAR. I keep a baker's shop, in Covent Garden. On the 11th of March, the prisoner came for a two-pound loaf—I served her, and she put down a shilling—my son said it was bad—the policeman came by, and the prisoner was given in charge. THOMAS DEAR. I saw the prisoner at my mother's house—I took the shilling off the counter, it was bad—I called the constable, and gave the prisoner in charge—I gave the officer the shilling. JAMES ADAMS. ( police-constable F  62.) I took the prisoner, and have the shilling. ELIZABETH WELLINGTON. I am niece to Mr. Ward, who keeps the White Horse, in Gilbert-street, Clare-market. On the 15th of March, the prisoner came for three-halfpenny worth of gin—I served her—she give me a shilling, which I gave my uncle. WILLIAM WARD. I received the shilling—I saw it was bad, and gave the prisoner into custody. EDMUND SWEENEY( police-constable F  130.) I was called, and took the prisoner—I received this shilling from Mr. Ward. JOHN FIELD. These are both counterfeit, but not cast in the same mould. The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that she had never been to Dear's shop, and that she did not know the other shilling was had. GUILTY. Aged 29.— Confined one Year.
