2040. CHARLES M'PHERSON, feloniously wounding Henry Decimus
Ilderton, with intent to do him grievons bodily harm.
MR. METCALPE conducted the Prosecution.
Henry Decimus ILDERTON. I am a solicitor, and live at Grove-cottage,
St. James's-street, Old Kent-road. On 15th Aug. I was angling in the
Surrey Canal-the prisoner and three or four other boys were on the canal in
boats-be was alone in a boat, and was splashing about the water with his
oars, evidently to annoy those who were fishing--we asked him and the
others to go away-after some time they did so, but came back again-the
prisoner broke the top of my fishing-rod-I jumped into his boat and gave
him a slap over the head -- he jumped ashore and said, "I will serve you out
for this" - he got hold of the rope and began dragging the boat along the
canal with me in it-he picked up a stone-I jumped out of the boat and
ran after him to make him give it up, and just as I got up to him his father
got out of a barge and came up to me with his fists up to strike me the
prisoner stumbled over a dog and fell--the father came up in an attitude to
strike me, and just at that moment I received a severe blow from a stone on
the right temple-it must have come from the prisoner who was on my right
side-1 bled very much-my hat was cut through the brimlining, and all
with the assistance of one of the bargemen I went to a surgeon, and was under
his care for a week.

EDWARD JACKMAN. I was angling in the canal--the boys were annoying me
by frightening away the fish I did not see Mr. Ilderton get into the boat, but
hearing a laugh among the men I turned round, and saw him in it, and the
prisoner palling him along-I saw him pick up part of a brick-Mr. Ilderton
ran after him the father ran up to strike him the boy fell, got up again,
and after dodging about two or three times I saw him throw a brick, which
struck Mr. Ilderton on the temple--be was only about four yards off at the
time.
RICHARD WILLIAM KBY (policeman, R 339). I took the prisoner, and
told him it was for throwing a stone at a gentleman-he said he had nothing
to say to it-he could not deny it.
