 COURT. Q. When he returned and said he had not met with the cash customers, why did you not have the goods back? A. He proposed that they should be left at the house, and we should send the porter for them next day, as he was gone; I conceived he was then bound to send them home - if he had sold them for cash the next day, I should not have found any fault.
 Q. You would have given him credit for them if he had sold them? A. No; he was my agent only - he was bound to sell only in my name, and with my invoice; that was our general agreement - he never sold on his own account, to my knowledge; I did not furnish him with an invoice, because I expected the goods back - if he had met with a customer he was to make out an invoice in my name, and give a receipt in my name, as my agent.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Do you keep a warehouse in Addle-street? A. It is a warehouse for me and others; a general deposit for two or three different people - Wilkinson keeps the warehouse; I have been there about six months - I took the benefit of the Insolvent Act about eighteen months ago; I seldom have so little as 50l. worth of goods at the warehouse - I dare say I have that amount now, but will not swear it, as I have been so engaged in this business; the prisoner was in my employ- his employment ceased when I discovered this; it commenced about the 10th or 12th of August.
 Q. Was there not some dissolution of a connexion between you before? A. I trusted him with some small commissions, and gave him credit for some small sums; I never employed him to pawn goods for me, or to get them pawned - there was a written agreement between us when I dismissed him, which was about the 8th or 10th of November; and after I made a charge against him at Worship-street, I destroyed that agreement, because I dismissed him; he had lost my confidence, and I tore it up in his presence - the charge at Worship-street was about two other cloths; I think that was on the 2nd of November -(looking at three papers) these are my hand-writing; I believe this invoice is all my writing, except the signature of Sykes - here is my own signature; the prisoner came to Worship-street of his own accord one day, and I agreed to let him go, on the 2nd of November, without bail, and he was to come on the Monday - he came again in the meantime, and was detained on another charge; he came one day when I did not attend; I had told him I would not, in consequence of promises - I preferred this charge at the Mansion-house, on the 24th of November, I think; I had my suspicions about this cloth when at Worship-street, but had not seen Thompson himself - I knew a duplicate had been found on the 5th of November, but did not know it was for my cloth; I received the duplicate from Cleasby, about the 8th or 9th of November - 9l. 10s. had been obtained on the cloth: the prisoner was not present when I received the duplicate - I got a duplicate for 10l. from Sykes, about the 8th of November; it was after I had got the first from Cleasby, and after he was at Worship-street- it is dated the 29th of October; I had both these duplicates when I agreed not to appear at Worship-street - I got the one dated in October after he was discharged.
 Q. Did you not get that ticket from him the very morning of the day you was to appear against him? A. No, I am certain I did not get it till after I was to appear against him at Worship-street; I will not swear it was not on the same day.
 COURT. Q. Who did you receive it from? A.From the prisoner, about the 8th; I did not prefer this charge till the 24th - I had seen him several times between the 8th and 24th, and never took any steps.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you not receive from him, 4l. in money? A. I did after his discharge - I mean after the time I had appeared against him; I received it on the 3rd or 4th; he was discharged on the 7th: I advanced him 10s. 6d. when he was discharged - I do not know that it was to pay his fees; I knew my cloth was gone, but I had not seen Thompson to ascertain if he knew Burrows.
     NOT GUILTY.
 230.  CHARLES KENNELL was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of October , 1 saw, value 1s.; 1 stock and bit, value 2s.; 1 holdfast, value 18d.; 1 bed-wrench, value 6d.; 2 chisels, value 1s., and 1 screw-driver, value 6d., the goods of Thomas Harris.
 THOMAS HARRIS. I am a  bedstead-maker, and live in Bagnigge-wells-road. The prisoner was my journeyman; he left on the 1st of October, and I missed all these tools - he had no authority to pawn them; I met him on the 21st of October, in Gower-street, and he ran away - I called a Policeman, who took him.
   GEORGE CHAPMAN. I am an apprentice to Mr. Nichols, pawnbroker, Gray's Inn-lane. I produce all these tools which were pawned by the prisoner, in three pledges; I am certain of him.
   ROBERT TURNER. I am a Policeman. I apprehended the prisoner, and found the duplicates of the tools on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Confined Three Months.
 231.  JOHN EVANS was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of November , 9 lbs. of bacon, value 5s., the property of Patrick Neal.
   FREDERICK MILES. I am a Policeman. On the 25th of November, between eight and nine o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner and another lad in John's-row; the lad went into Neal's shop, took the bacon off the counter, and gave it to the prisoner in the passage - they both ran away; I followed, and took the prisoner with it under his arm.
   MARY NEAL. I am the wife of Patrick Neal, of John's-row. I heard the scales rattle in the shop, I ran out, and the bacon was gone; the Policeman brought the prisoner in with it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was not in the passage; a boy betrayed me from work, and put the bacon into my arms.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 17.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Week.
