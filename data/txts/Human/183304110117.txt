   JOHN BUCHANNAN. I am shopman to a pawnbroker in Roe-buck-terrace, Dover-road.    JOSEPH CLOTHIER. I live at Mr. Mullen's, a pawnbroker in Staverton-row. I have a silver watch I took in of the female prisoner, on the 21st of February - I lent two guineas and a half on it - she said it was her own, and gave her name Ann Hardy, No. 3, Salisbury-place.
   JOHN WATKINS. I live in Great Sutton-street, and am a watch-gilder. I know most of these watches to be mine - I pawned them at the prosecutor's.
   THOMAS ARMSTRONG. I have missed the whole of these from our stock - Charles Harding had access to the stock; his mother had not.
   JOSEPH PRATT. I live on Great Saffron-hill - I keep a stall, and sell fruit and oysters near to Mr. Armstrong's. I know both the prisoners - Charles Harding lived with Mr. Armstrong, and I have been employed by him; the first time was to take a letter to No. 3, Salisbury-street, Old Kent-road, to Mrs. Harding - I went there, and saw the other prisoner - I afterwards took small parcels, four or five times - I received them from Charles Harding - I did not know what they contained - they were directed for his mother, and I took them to her - I never formed the least judgment of what they contained - I received 1s. each time.
   ROBERT DUKE re-examined. I took the female prisoner at No. 3, Salisbury-street - found some duplicates there- I asked her if she had a son who lives at a pawnbroker's- she said she had - I then asked her if she had been in the habit of pawning watches for him - she said she pawned one which was his own - I asked her if she had not pawned more - she at last said she had pawned a good many.
   Charles Harding put in a written Defence, acknowledging the crime; but stating that his mother was unconscious of the manner he had obtained the property, he having stated to her that he received them from a young man, who was a watch-maker.
 Jane Harding put in a written Defence, declaring herself perfectly unconscious of the goods being feloniously obtained.
   CHARLES HARDING- GUILTY of stealing to the value of 99s. only. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
   JANE HARDING-   GUILTY. Aged 32. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 OLD COURT. THURSDAY, APRIL 18TH.
 Second Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Sergeant  Arabin .
 880.  JOHN MERRELL was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying Mary Rocher Latter.
   JOSEPH CURTIS. I am a surgeon, and live at No. 12, Union-terrace, Camden-town. I saw the body of the deceased at No. 28, University-street, on Tuesday, the 2nd of April- the beadle of the parish produced a bottle to me; I smelt it, tasted it, and satisfied myself that it contained prussic acid - there was still some left; the bottle would hold about half a pint - Hutchins, the beadle, produced two other bottles; one of them contained tincture of myrrh - I do not know what the other contained; I saw a-day book produced from Mr. Clapham's, a surgeon, and in that was the entry of a prescription for Mrs. Latter - it was in Latin; it contains no directions for prussic acid - it is a very deadly poison - in the prescription immediately preceding that, there is one containing three drops of hydrocianic acid, which is prussic acid; I believe the mistake arose from there being a dram and a half of hydrosiamus in the prescription for Mrs. Latter, and they are very much a like in writing; they are not written at full length in the prescription - they appear nearly the same, written short, but the difference would be very apparent to a person who examined it minutely; a dram and a half of prussic acid would be deadly poison - prussic acid is like water; hydrosiamus is something of an olive colour - the brother and mother of the deceased described the symptoms, and I consider she died from taking prussic acid, in mistake for hydrosiamus; I did not examine the body internally.
