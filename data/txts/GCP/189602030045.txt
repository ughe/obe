William Hearne, in his defence, stated that when the mob came he had
only a foot rule, with which he was measuring a coat, that they burst in
the door, and Newton must have been fallen on, and so received his injuries;
and that when he was found in bnd he was suffering from a kick.
Driscoll stated that he was helping to make a coat when 80 or 100 people
came, and he denied that he had injured Newton.
The COMMON SERJEANT considered that there was not sufficient evidence
to go to the Jury as against ANDREN HEARNE.-Not Guilty.
WILLIAM HEARNE and DRISCOLL Guilty.

August, 1893. Nine other convictions were proved against him-Thres
Years' Penal Servitute. DRISCOLL-Twelve Mowhs' Hard Labour.
