JOSEPH BUTTERFILL. I am a constable of Woolwich. On Sunday morn ing, the 17th of March, the prosecutor came to me—I went with him to the Duke on Horseback, but the prisoner was not there—we went to the Marquis of Gran by, and there found him—I said, "Fowler, you are my prisoner"—he said, "Your prisoner, for what?"—I said, "I will tell you when we get to the door"—when we got to the door, I told him it was for robbing a man of some money—he denied it, and said, "Let go of me, Butterfill, don't lug  me through the streets in this sort of way"—I said, "Well, Dick,  if you will go quietly I will let you"—I let go of him, and he walked by my side twenty or thirty yards towards the cage—be then buttoned up hi coat, stepped back, and put himself into an attitude to fight—I had great difficulty in keeping his blows off—the prosecutor who was with me, came to my assistance, and the prisoner began sparring with him—we took him to the cage, searched him, and in his jacket pocket found two half-crowns, two penny-pieces, two halfpence, a knife, and a tobacco-box—be told me that Davidson had given him the purse and money to take care of, but seeing him so free in spending the money, he (Davidson) wished it back again—Davidson appeared to have been drinking, but knew what he was about.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did I not say he asked me for some of it again? A.  No, not some of it—you said he asked for it again. JAMES DAVIDSON re-examined.  I have every reason to Believe this money is mine—I know the prisoner had no money while I was in his company, for he asked me to lend him a shilling.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did I not tell you I had 18 d . of my own? A.  No—I lent you the tobacco-box to take a pipe of tobacco from—I do not accuse you of stealing that.
 (  The prisoner in his defence stated that the prosecutor had been to a great number of public-houses with him, drinking; and being very much intoxi  cated, had given him his money to take care of for him.) NOT GUILTY.

 1432. JAMES PEW and HENRY JONES were indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of April , 1 handkerchief, value 6 d  ., the goods of James Clamp , from his person. JAMES CLAMP. I was at Greenwich fair on Tuesday, the 2nd of April, standing, looking at a show—a witness told me my pocket was picked—I turned round and saw both the prisoners in custody of Dyke—I went op to them, and saw my handkerchief drop from under Pew's coat, which was buttoned up—it fell down in front of him—I saw his hand up at his coat at the time it fell—Dyke took it up—I had felt in my pocket, and missed it as soon as the man spoke to me—I knew it to be mine directly I saw it Pew. Q.  Did you see my hand in your pocket? A.  No, but I saw the hand-kerchief drop from your coat—I did not tell the Magistrate I saw it in your hand—I said, in the officer's hand—I said it was in the prisoner's hand, but immediately corrected myself, and said it was in the officer's hand.
