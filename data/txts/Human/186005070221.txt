   MR. LILLEY . Q.  Have you any doubt that this was one of the persons who enquired the way to the Kent-road? A.  No doubt at all. ALFRED CASILEMAN( Police-Sergeant P , 38). In consequence of informa tion I received on the afternoon of 5th April, I went to the Rosemary Branch—I there received charge of the prisoner—I told her the charge, and she said she was innocent—she said she lived at 34, Norland-square, Notting-hill—I went there—she said she had been to 34, Wellington-street, Peckham—there is no such street as that—I have been resident there between seven and eight years—I know every inch of the ground.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I suppose the prisoner was searched? A.  She was searched at Camberwell-station—I did not search her—she was taken in Camberwell—I have been in Peckham about eight years—there is not a Wellington-street in the whole of Peckham—I know all the new streets—there was not a great crowd, not more than half a dozen people—it is about 200 yards from where the old lady lives, to the Rosemary Branch. GUILTY.

 498. THOMAS CHEESEMAN  (20) , Robbery with violence on Dennis Bryan , and stealing from bis person 9 s  . his monies.
   MR. LILLEY conducted the Prosecution , DENNIS BRYAN, the prosecutor, was called on his recognisance, but did not appear. ANN JONES. I live at 17, William-street, New Kent-road—I know Dennis Bryan; he used to live at the same place; he did on Sunday, 29th April—on that day, about 2 o'clock in the afternoon, I saw him at the door of the house standing in the passage; some females came up; one of them tied a stone in her handkerchief and struck Bryan on the head—I saw the prisoner about ten minutes after—he rushed into the passage, knocked Bryan down, and kicked him violently while down he then got up and ran out, and the prisoner ran after him—I saw nothing more—Bryan left that house on the following Thursday; he had been to the police-court on Wednesday the day before—I was present when he was examined before the Magistrate—the prisoner was at that time charged.
   Prisoner. Q.  What time was it when Bryan was standing at his door? A.  Just about 2 o'clock—I live down stairs; he told me he had been rubbed about half-an-hour after—he did not tell me he was coming round to your house with a policeman—the disturbance began by Harriet Cross coming round and challenging the other young woman—Bryan pushed her away from the door—they had not been drinking in our house—I did not see you take anything.
