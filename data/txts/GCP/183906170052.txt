John WILLIAM LANE. I am the prosecutor's son-my father gave the

Cross-examined. Q. How long ago is it? A. About six years-1

started off that morning for Brentford-I could not tell where these things
were to go to--I had fenders and fire-irons in the cart-I am certain the
prisoner is the man-he has grown stouter since then-I did not have
above five minutes' conversation with him.

CHARLES BARKER. I am a varnish-maker. On the 5th of April I was
at my cousin's house, Mr. William Day, the younger, in Edward-street,
St. Pancras-my cousin brought the prisoner home, about half-past one
o'clock in the morning-I was sitting up to let my cousin in- I had never
seen the prisoner before; but he and I having been to sea, we sat,
talking, till about four o'clock--I then said I was going to bed, and went
up stairs, but previous to that I had made up the prisoner as comfortable a
bed as I could in the kitchen, where we had been sitting-1 got up about
half-past six o'clock in the morning, and the door was open--the prisoner
and the articles stated were gone-I found this strange knife lying in
front of my trunk, which had been broken open, and these pincers, which
are mine, are, I believe, what the trunk was broken open with-I looked
round, and missed a pistol from the mantel-piece, which was my cousin's->
