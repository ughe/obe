JAMES CHARLES WOLLEN. I live in New-street, City-road About
half-past ten o'clock in the morning of the 12th of September, I was in
Cornhill, sitting in Laithwaite's newspaper-office, (where I am shopman,)op-
posite 'Change-alley, Cornhill, under the Royal Exchange I had a full
view of the street-I heard a number of people calling out to a cart to stop
-I immediately looked, and saw a man, whom I knew as a porter, under
a cart-I did not see the cart before the wheels had gone over him
-I cannot tell at what rate it was going previous to the accident-whes
I saw it, the wheel had gone over the man-it was going at a fast rate, and
had only one horse in it it was a particularly heavy cart, and had only
two wheels there was nobody in the office, and I could not leave it--thie
man was taken up, and taken into Change-alley-I did not go over-
should say, at a guess, the cart was going at the rate of five or six miles as
hour after it had gone over the man.
Cross-examined by MR, WALESBY, Q. He was down on the ground
when you first saw him? 4. He was; whether he slipped down, of
was knocked down, I cannot say-the cart continued to go fast till it was
stopped, which I should say was twenty-five or thirty yards -- that might
take two or three minutes- I could not see any body who was in the cart-
there was nothing near till the accident occurred the cart was in the road
by itself -- I saw nothing but that vehicle, till two or three minutes after
wards.
