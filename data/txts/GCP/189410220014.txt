Tyler, Mayor.
JAMES WOODHOUSE. I am assistant to James Woodhouse, a pawnbroker,
of 332, North End Road--the prisoner pledged some shirts, socks, and
other articles with me.
Cross-examined by the prisoner. I was present when you pledged them
-Isaw you go into the shop with a bundle; I cannot tell what was in it,
but I saw the articles afterwards.
Prisoner's defence. The chair was against the wall because it was
always there. The son was standing on one of the chairs, and my young
brother got on it, and put some bottles on the wall, and forgot to bring
the chair in. The constant dripping outside made the mould sodden, and
it took the footmarks, but there were no footmarks traced from our house.
The blanket was out in the garden.
Guilty.*-Twelve Months' Hard Labour.

OLD COURT, Wednesday and Thursday, October 24th and 25th, 1894.
Before Mr. Justice Collins.
794. HENRY LLEWELLYN WINTER (42) was indicted for
wilful and corrupt perjury in a certain affidavit before the Court of Pro-
bate, in a petition then pending, to which he PLEADED GUILTY.
Mr. C. Mathews Prosecuted, and MR. BESLEY Defended.
Counsel were heard in part, and the case was postponed to the next
Session.
795. BETSY WEBB (24) was charged, on the Coroner's Inquisition,
with the manslaughter of a male child.
MR. HORACE AVORY, for the prosecution, offered no evidence upon the
Inquisition. The GRAND JURY had ignored the bill for murder.
Nor GUILTY.
796. ANDREW SKINNER was charged, on the Coroner's
Inquisition, with the manslaughter of George James Newman.
In this case the GRAND JURY had ignored the bill for manslaughter, and
MR. KERSHAW offered no evidence upon the Inquisition. Not GUILTY.
797. JAMES CROWTHER (35), charged with murder, was, on the
evidence of George Edward Walker, surgeon of Her Majesty's Prison,
Holloway, found to be insane and unable to plead.-Ordered to be detained
until Her Majesty's pleasure be known.
For other cases tried on these two days, see Kent and Surrey Cases.
