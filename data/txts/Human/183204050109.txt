Cross-examined. Q. Who did you give them to? A. To Miss Worster.
 MISS  ELIZABETH WORSTER. I received the pair of shoes from him, and gave them to Joseph Lee to take to the wharf.
   JOSEPH LEE. This lady gave me the parcel - I took it to the wharf, and delivered it to Mr. Bland.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was there any direction on it? A. I did not see any.
   THOMAS BLAND. I received a pair of shoes in a parcel from Lee - I directed them to "Mr. William Worster, City-road, London," and delivered them to Shaw to put on board the boat.
   JAMES SHAW. I received the parcel, and gave it to Thomas Rockingham.
 Cross-examined. Q. You cannot say what was in the parcel? A. I cannot say, but from the feel of it I believe it was shoes; it was directed to Mr. William Worster, the son of one of our partners, in London.
   WILLIAM WEBB. I am servant to Mr. Joseph Cooper; they call me by the nick-name of Oxford. On Friday, the 10th of February, I was at the British Lion - the prisoner came there, and said he had a ticket of a pair of shoes; he let me look at it - he said they were in pawn for 4s.; I was to see if they would fit me - I was to give him 1s. 6d.; he said he had them made by Barker, in Macclesfield-street, and gave 11s. for them; I did not go for them - I gave up the ticket to Noah Elwell.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you ask the prisoner any questions? A. No - I did not see the shoes.
   NOAH ELWELL. I am captain of a boat, in the service of Messrs Worster. On the 17th of February I was in the tap-room of the Windsor Castle - I saw the prisoner there that morning; he asked me where Rockingham was - I said on board; he said he had been giving him a pretty character - I said, "Great cause he had;" he said it was about producing a parcel; I said, "If he don't produce that parcel he will be discharged, and his men and all;" he said if they were in pawn he could get the ticket - I told Rockingham of it; I went with him the same day to a pawnbroker's, near St. Luke's church, and got the shoes there; Mr. Holt went, and saw the shoes, but he did not take them - we got them by means of the ticket, which we got from Webb.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you work in that boat? A. No; he works one boat, and I another - we went to the pawnbroker's, and inquired for a pair of cork-bottomed shoes, pawned on Saturday or Friday for 4s.; I did not mention the prisoner's name or any other.
   THOMAS CASSIDY(Police-constable N 71.) On the 21st of February I apprehended the prisoner, and received the duplicate from Holt.
   GEORGE HOLT. I received the duplicate from Elwell, and gave it to the officer.
   CHARLES WORLEY. I am foreman to Mr. Cassel, a pawnbroker. I had the shoes; they were pawned with me by a bargeman, but I do not remember his face; I gave this duplicate of them.
 Cross-examined. Q. What do you mean by a bargeman? A. A man in the dress of a bargeman; he gave his name as Thomas Cocktail, and said he worked at Pickford's wharf; the men frequently live on board the barges - it was on the 3rd of February, and I think in the morning - it was day-light.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. I believe, when you speak of daylight, it was as much light as comes into a little box? A. We have plenty of light in our shop - nothing is more common than for persons to pawn in a name that is not their own.
 MR. DONNE to THOMAS ROCKINGHAM. Q. How many persons were on board the boat when it came to the City basin? A. Four, altogether - the prisoner, another man, my brother, who was in bed, and myself; the other man who was on board, was dressed like a bargeman.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Confined Six Months.
 1065.  ELIZA BALDWINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of March , 1 gown, value 14s., the goods of Daniel Brice.
   DANIEL BRICE. I live in Brownlow-street, Drury-lane, and am an organ-builder.    ROBERT HARRIS. I am shopman to Mr. Newberry, a pawnbroker, in Drury-lane. I have a black velvet dress, pawned by the prisoner, on the 23rd of March, in the name of Ann Bennett, No. 6, Brownlow-street; the prosecutor lives at No. 8; I think I had seen the prisoner before, and I saw her two or three days afterwards.
 DANIEL BRICE. This is my dress - I do not know what rent the prisoner paid; I think I was out of town when she took the lodging, but her money being spent, and having a dissipated mother-in-law, and but few clothes, I said she might stay and do needle-work - she left me on the Saturday, to go to a place in Oxford-street, as she told us - on the Monday we missed a gown, and on further search we missed this dress; her mother is a kind of charwoman at the Olympic theatre, and her father is a servant there.
   ROBERT SUTTLE(Police-constable B 97.) I took the prisoner.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
