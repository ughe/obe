Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Did you never see any glue at Davis's shop? A.  Yes; but never in a small quantity—I saw the 5 cwt. standing three or four days in front of the counter—the bags were sewed up close, so that they could not see what was in them—they are a coarse bag made I believe on purpose—the first bags I saw brought was on the 2nd or 3rd of November in the evening—I believe the gas was lighted—I was sitting in the yard during Clark's trial—I was not examined.
   COURT . Q.  Did the prosecutors know that you were waiting? A.  They saw me, but did not know me.
   MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Do you know the prosecutor? A.  I know Mr. Harrison by sight—I communicated to him that I could give him some information when the trial commenced—I was here the day the bill was found, and three days before the trial was on—I am quite sure it was before Clark was put to the bar to be tried that I spoke to Mr. Harrison—I gave him my address, 15, Somers-street, Back-hill, and be sent for me—I live there now—I will swear the gas was lighted when the man brought the glue out of the cart on the 2nd of November—there were about six or seven different persons in the shop, men and women—Mr. Davis is in a very large way as a chandler—you seldom or ever go into the shop but there is somebody in it—Mr. and Mrs. Davis were serving in the shop—nobody else ever served in the shop that I saw—I am generally there morning or evening—sometimes they have got things to sell, or want work done, and I did it—the glue was brought in as quick as the man could put down the tail-board and bring it in—the customers were in the shop all the time—Clark was in the shop in front of the counter when the glue, was put down—Mr. and Mrs. Davis were both talking to him—there are two counters—I was between the two—this occupied about five mi nutes—the carman came to the door—Clark paid him and he went away—Mrs. Davis might go round two or three times serving the customers—there was no whispering—it could be heard by all the customers—the conversation about the yellow jaundice was the next morning at the public-house—I forget the landlord's name—he was there—I have been at work at Mr. Martin's, the pork-butcher at Shore ditch for nine months, till within the last six weeks, when an alteration took place—the last, six weeks the solicitor wished me to go and see if I could find Bonnell and Clark, and told me he would satisfy me—I have some little coming in extra, besides what the solicitor supplied me with, of my own money—I have never less than 18 s . every Saturday night—I have a family of two boys who have 18 s . a week, and a daughter—I have been supplied with money by the solicitor; when I have gone into the country I did not go at my own expense—I cannot say how much I have had—perhaps 3 l . or 4 l . during the last six weeks—I have had as much as 15 l .—I have been to Southend and Margate, and other places—I have not made as much as 50 l .—I have gone with an officer, and he has had the money and paid all my expenses; but that I do not consider I had—at the time I saw them in the shop, I lived by selling goods to different persons—I had lived five or six years in that way—I never kept a shop for five years—I do portering work or beat carpets—I have bought goods and sold them on my own credit, at times—on the 2nd of November I was living in Somers-street, Back-hill, and have for two years—before that 1 lived in Spring-street, Spafields, about 150 yards from where I live now, for more than two years, I think, and before that in Church-row, St. Luke's—I might be there twelve
