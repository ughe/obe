Fourth Jury, before Mr. Baron Rolfe.
2392. JOHN JONES was indicted for feloniously killing and slaying
Thomas Caulfield.
MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES WINSLOW. I am a smith, and live in Little Sutton-street, Cler-
kenwell. On the 2nd of July I was standing at my shop-window in
Allen-street, in the afternoon-the prisoner, who keeps the Rose and
Crown public-house in Allen-street, was talking with the deceased--the
deceased pointed to me and said, “This is the man for cheek," meaning
impudence-after that Jones and one Roberts were standing at the door
of the Rose and Crown for a long while-Roberts did not appear to be
sober-Roberts came to my shop-door and attempted to pull it open to
pull me out--Caulfield was not there then, but hearing the row he came
back-the prisoner was there, and had a little discourse with him—the
first I saw was the prisoner, shove Caulfield twice-then there was a sort
of scuffle or fight--I do not know what to term it-I then went into my
shop to work I came out again after hearing Caulfield's leg was broken,
and found him lying down on the curb-stone by my shop-door.
Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS. Q. Your shop is opposite the
door of the Rose and Crown? A. Yes I do not know what Roberts
Wanted, but he left Jones and came over to pull me out of my shop-he
went away from my door just after the scuffle began between the prisoner
and the deceased, but when it first began he was annoying me I happened
to look up and saw Jones give the deceased a push twice the deceased
was a small man-I do not know whether he was powerful-I only knew
him by his passing my shop-Jones had summoned me for not paying a
Deer score, but it was a very unlegal thing I consider more was booked
to me than I had I was not always abusing him I never heard Caulfield
abuse him.
