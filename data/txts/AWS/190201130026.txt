EMILY JANE IDENDEN. I live at 5, Wellington Road, Peckham-
formerly lived at St. Donot's Road, Brockley-I was Mrs. Bullock, and
became a widow in 1899-I have since married and again becoine a
widow- have a daughter named Emily Maud Bullock-in 1899 I was
possessed of some leasehold property at Greenwich and Brockley-Emily
Maud assisted me in collecting the rents-in the early summer of 1899
Miss Amy Candy lived with us and occasionally went with my daughter
to collect the rents-through Miss Candy I was introduced to the
prisoner Francis, as Mr. Raycott--I understood that he was engaged to
Miss Candy, that he was a comedian without an engagement, and that
Brooks was a gentleman of independent means-Brooks paid attentions to
my daughter-he said he was living at home with his mother at
Coldharbour Lane, Brixton-that he had an allowance of E7 a month
from herthat his uncle, a Mr. Davis, who had lived at Finchley
Road, St. John's Wood, had lately died, and had left him a fortune
of E14,000 to E16,000; that E3,000 was in houses, and the rest in
money and plate in the Bank of England--he said that he should be
21 years of age on February 14th, 1900, and that he would then receive
the property that it was to be sold, and that Mr. Harding, a solicitor, of
Chancery Lane, was arranging the uncle's affairs; and that he wanted
E200 to pay for the probate of his uncle's will-Francis said that he had
lent Brooks E500 for the same purpose- said it was inconvenient for
ine to lend it, and suggested that his mother should advance itBrooks
said that he could not ask her as she was very much opposed to the will
as she thought it ought to have been left to her instead of to him-I said
I would think about ita few days later I again 8aW the prisonersI
had turned the matter over in my mind-I agreed to lend E100-Francis
said several times that he had lent Brooks E300 without security, because

had Alhambra Music Hall shares which he could not sell as he would
be the loser by it understood him to say that he had E800 worth
of them-I entirely believed what the prisoners said-for the purpose
of raising the money I deposited some of my leases with Messrs. Mumford
LI handed E100 to Brooks on June 17th--he thanked me,

Rayeott put it in my box-later on I moved to other lodgings, after
