MR. CAARTEEN conducted the Prosecution.
JOHN Cox (policeman, N 369). I met the prisoner, about a quarter
before 6 o'clock in the morning, on 11th April, at the top of Canonbury
street, Lower-road, Islington-he was carrying a birdcage with two birds
in it-I followed him as far as Queen-street, about 200 or 300 yards, stopped
him, and asked where he brought the birdcage from—he said, “If you
will come with me, I will show you "-I went with him about two yards,
when he dropped the cage at my feet, and ran away-I pursued him about
& dozen yards, when he turned round, drew a knife from his pocket, and
said, “You bas if you come nigh me, you will have it "<he then con-
tinued running till I stopped him again, about a dozen yards further
before I secured him he turned round several times, and attempted to stab
me time after time—at the corner of George-street he took two tablecloths
from his waistcoathe threw one on a wall, with intent to throw it into a
garden, and the other he threw in the middle of the road—he then ran
about fifty yards further-he then dropped the knife, and I secured him
another policeman came to my assistance, and we searched the prisoner, and
found this piece of ribbon in his waistcoat pocket-I got one of the table-
cloths-a butcher came by, and took one, and ran away with it-I have not
the least doubt that the prisoner is the man I saw with the birdcage-1
never lost sight of him.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE Q. How far is the place where you saw
him with the birdcage from where he was taken? A. About half a mile-
when he threw the cloths away, he buttoned up his clothes, and said, “Now
come on," and he ran some distance, and dropped the knife, and I took
him-it was fifty or sixty yards from where the things were thrown down
to where he was taken-I did not lose sight of him till I took him-Atkins
saw the whole of it-I never saw Atkins before I believe he has been &
witness in one case before-he is not an amateur policeman.
