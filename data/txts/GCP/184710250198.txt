EDWARD SCOTT. I live in Marlboro'-road, Chelsea. On the 13th of
Aug., about the middle of the day, Marson, who was tried just now, came to
me with a broker, named Smith, in the Fulham-road-in consequence of
what Marson said, I saw the prisoner that same day in Turk's-row, Chelsea.
where he was living-I purchased of him a number of goods, some of which
are here, and bave been identified by the prosecutor-here are two cans which
contained paper varnish, for which I paid the prisoner 30s.-- he gave me a
receipt for the money-I forget the date, I think it was after the 24th of
Aug.- this is the receipt-it is dated the 1st of Sept., and is for 21. 48,--this
includes some of the varnish-I had before that purchased oil and varnish of
him to the amount of 77. 158.-I had not known him before.
JAMES Tasker. I am carman to the prosecutor-I delivered some varnish
and brushes soon after the 24th of Aug., it might be four or five days after
I delivered the first lot of goods, which was on the 13th of Aug.-
I delivered them at Turk's-row, Chelsea - there were several persons there-
when I first went I could not find the prisoner-once when I went, the
prisoner said, had the varnish been there before, the rooms would have been
varnished and dried, but he was not going to be humbugged by Mr. Ratcliffe,
or anybody else, if he did not send it down, he must do something else
with it, but did not say what.
JAMES Morris. I live in Turk's-row, Chelsea, opposite the prisoner.
In the middle of Aug. I saw a great quantity of white lead and oil at the
prisoner's- I was employed by him to roll it into the back yard-about a
week afterwards, I saw some varnish brought there, and the prisoner employed
me next day to take it to Mr. Scott's-I never saw the prisoner at work, be
is a carpenter by trade, but I have seen bim about Turk's-row all day long,
backwards and forwards- I never saw any men employed by him, or any
carts going from his premises-he has been living there nearly two years
he was at work seven or eight months ago, for a Mr. O'Donnell-have not
known him at work for anybody since I am at work for a cowkeeper, and
have been for the last nine years, on and off-my father is a pensioner.
CHARLES WALKER. I keep the house, 7, Turk's-row, Chelsea. The
prisoner has lodged there upwards of two years-he did work for Mr.
O'Donnell, and left him about three months ago I do not think he has been
in any employment since- recollect the paint and varnish being brought to
the house, it remained there two days I saw the cart take it away the second
evening--the prisoner hired the cart to take it to Mr. Scott's-none of it
was sent to use at any house that I know of the prisoner was not employed
in painting or papering any houses at that time that I know of-he was
generally about the premises- I never saw any workmen come to him.
