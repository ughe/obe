morning of the 28th the prisoner's labourer, Charles Dove, brought some
solder--the prisoner was present-it was put into the scale to be weighed -
the moment he put it into the scale he came to me and reported himself as
being correct to time I walked to the scale and saw three of the bars which
I had marked, among the eight which were there the prisoner came to me
in the evening and asked me to take the weight of the solder--I asked him
twice over how many bars there were-he said, "Eight"--I said, "Are
you sure there are eight?"-he said, “Yes," counting them-1 then
ordered them to be taken to the counting-house, and saw the three that I
had marked, and recognised in the morning-I called two men to weigh
them, and about three-quarters of an hour after, I saw the prisoner, and
told him I had a very unpleasant charge to make against him-I told him he
was seiling me solder twice over-he denied it, I told him it was no use
to deny it-he said, "I hope you will deal leniently with me, and not give
me in charge," as he would appear to-morrow morning-I said I could not
do such a thing, as I was answerable for the property to the company-I
gave him into custody, and delivered the three marked bars to the officer
these are them-(looking at them.)
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is this your hand-writing to
this deposition ? A. Yes I did not tell the Magistrate that the prisoner
said he hoped I would deal leniently with him, because the question was
not asked me I first saw these three bars at seven minutes past seven
o'clock-I afterwards saw the prisoner in the factory, standing close to
Dove-I remarked it in my own mind-I mentioned it at a quarter to
six o'clock in the evening-1 and Dove have never had any conversation
with respect to Dove's wages-I had understood by a man in the factory
that the prisoner charged 188. a week for Dove's wages, and gave him
only 15s.
MR. PAYNE. Q. Do you know what wages were allowed to the pri-
soner for Dove? A. 188.
CHARLES Dove. I am a labourer, and worked for the prisoner. On
the 27th of February, in the evening, he gave me directions to call at his
house on the following morning-I did so, at twenty minutes before seven
o'clock, and found some bars of solder in the passage-he had left bis
house, and gone to the factory- I took the bars there, and saw him there
- he told me to put the eight bars of solder into the scale, and I did so-
it was weighed in Mr. Munro's presence.
Cross-examined. Q. Were there any other bars of solder lying there
besides the eight? A. Now I cannot swear there were not-Mr. Munro
did not make any observation to me about them--I told him I had 158. a
week from the prisoner on the Saturday morning, the 29th, Munro said
it was a great shame that I should only have 155. a week, when the pri-
soner had 18s. from the firm I was never taken up for any robbery-1
was accused of one by a person who is here to give the prisoner a cha-
racter-I considered myself innocent-I was sent from his employ, and
taken in by the prisoner out of charity.
NOT GUILTY.
