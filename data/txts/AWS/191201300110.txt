me in sheep ; I estimate altogether E3,149. It is not true to suggest
that it was wholly against my interest to complete the Thorney pur:
chase. I did intend to complete. It is true if I had carried out the
purchase I should have had to pay 237,000, the purchase price, and
Carter's bonus, E3,750; I should not necessarily have had to pay
Bond's 24,500 from Thorney because he had only a collateral charge on
Thorney he had other securities. I had also contracted to pay him
21,050 bonus and 623 per cent. he would not entertain it unless he
had those terms; he would not have charged anything like $1,500 for
interest. I agree that adding all these figures and Bourner's 8500
together the amount I would have to pay would be E48,300, and leav.
ing Bond out the figure would be 241,250, but I am not satisfied that
that is the way to reckon it up. Comparing that with the 239,760,
the sub-sales and Tweedale's 26,000, I should sustain a loss, but that
is not a fair way of putting it. If Risley had fetched on my reselling
it E16,000, which it was worth, I should have had E9,000 credited to
me by Tweedale on what I owed him for the Hall which I had bought
back from him for E12,000; he would have the balance of the profit
$7,000, E6,000 being the money he had advanced and 21,000 the
guaranteed profit; I never intended to sell the balance to him out
and out for such a low price as $6,000. I do not agree I should
have sustained a loss on the Thorney completion; deducting deposits
received and adding Tweedale's 24,000 I had to receive E34,572,
which would leave me a balance in hand of E1,250 after paying Mrs.
Herrick: I could have satisfied Carter from what I made out of the
Risley deal. If the sale to Thorney had been an out and out sale I
