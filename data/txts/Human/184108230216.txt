   JURY . Q.  I think you said you went on shore from the morning till the afternoon? A.  Yes, we lunched at Mr. Lewis's house—we drank a glass of wine at lunch, but nothing more.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Do you mean you only drank one? A.  I cannot say—I only drank one as far as I remember—I had drank no more than was rational to drink on such occasions—I had been taking a glass of wine and some ale—no more than my proper quantity—it was in the skiff I rowed about to see if I could find the body.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  You have said you were athwart the stream, was that the usual way? A.  It was the way I have always been—I have fished twice this year, and have been accustomed to go down there every year—I have seen other punts moored in that way—I was not in the least af fected by what I took at Mr. Lewis's, nor were any of my friends.
   JURY . Q.  What did Undey say or do, which made you suspect him to be in liquor? A.  His manner was as if he had been drinking, and his breath smelt very much of liquor—he did not make any low remark, but it was his demeanor altogether which induced me to think so. SAMUEL WEST.
