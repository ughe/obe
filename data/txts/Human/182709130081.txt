MARY ANN SHERRIFF. I was coming down Cockspur-street. The coachman accosted me; the butcher and the other man got out of the coach, and solicited me to get into it - I said I had no objection to a ride - I stepped in, and they all three got on the box together; they drove furiously on through several streets, and when I sat down, I saw the other man had got his hand in the prosecutor's pocket; he took out his money; he and the coachman shared the money between them, behind the prosecutor's back; when I got down I saw the traces of the coach were broken, in consequence of their furious driving.
 Cross-examined. Q. What are you? I work at the braid trade - I did not know the prosecutor before; it was folly in me to get into the coach: no one was inside with me. I heard the butcher say he had got a fowl in his pocket - I had been taking my work home - I did not hear him say he was robbed of 5l.; he said it was between 2l. and 3l. I live in Bennett-street - I was married fourteen years ago. I dare say I told them I lived in Bennett-street - I do not know whether I did not; the coachman took me imprudently round the waist, and then the other man asked me to go in, and I did; the coachman was tipsy. I do not think he knew what he was about.
 COURT. Q. Who drove the coach? A. The butcher first, and then the other man; they drove furiously, and tried to upset the coach - I was in the coach when I saw the other man take the money; it was a charriot - I put out my head, and said, I saw what they had done - the man put out his fist, and said he would put my two eyes into one, and knock me out of the window.
   JOSEPH SALT. I am a japanner, and live in Horseferry-road. I was going down Princes-street, Westminster, and saw the coach stop - I saw a young man get off the box, and run away; the coachman said, "Cut, you b - g - r; cut as quick as possible!" the coachman was at that time off the box; the butcher found he was robbed - I said,"Take his number, and you will find him in the morning;" we went into a public-house; the coachman came in about three quarters of an hour, with two other men; he made a noise, and said, he would punch our heads about if we said he had robbed him; he then pulled out his money, which was only two or three sixpences; but he had been absent three quarters of an hour; the coach was No. 19.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was the coachman so drunk as not to know what he was doing? A. He was not sober, but he knew what he was doing; he was drunk.
 Mr. PHILLIPS called -
   JOHN LITHGO. I am a watchman, and live in Perkins'-rents, Westminster. I remember seeing the prosecutor on the night in question; he said at first that he had been robbed of 5l. I did not hear him say it was 2l. 10s. - the first word he said was, he had been robbed of 2l. I was calling the hour, and then went up to the coachman, and said he was my prisoner; the prosecutor then said, "You scoundrel, you have robbed me of 5l.; take charge of him." I said I could not take him from his horses; the prosecutor then said he was robbed of 35s.; he was in liquor, but knew what he was doing. I looked at the coach minutely; no trace was broken - I know Sherriff to be a common prostitute in Bennett-street; this is my beat - I have seen her there with men, like other prostitutes.
   MARY ANN SHERRIFF re-examined. I have lived three years at Mrs. Smith's, No. 10. Little George-street, Hampstead-road, and was there till last Saturday fortnight. I have lived at No. 4, Bennett-street, only for the last three weeks, and have never been out after ten o'clock at night - I am not in the habit of getting into coaches - I thought no harm of going in this coach. I had been carrying home a large bundle - I never got my living as a prostitute, or picked up men in the street. I am to return to Mrs. Smith's next Saturday, or to No. 10, Bowling-green-lane.
   JOSEPH SALT. I have been to this woman's house, and always found her hard at work at her needle.
     GUILTY. Aged 46. Transported for Seven Years.
 1692.  GEORGE YOUNG was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 2 silver tea-spoons, value 8s., the goods of Collingwood Marshall. The prisoner pleaded GUILTY. Aged 51. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1693.  ELIZA EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , 1 handkerchief, value 6d.; 1 key, value 2d.; 1 tobacco-stopper, value 6d., and 3 shillings, the property of John Bex , from his person.
   JOHN BEX. I am a collar-maker. On the 1st of September, between nine and eleven o'clock in the day time, I met the prisoner and another woman in the street; we went to a public-house, and had some liquor and some coffee. I was not quite sober; I laid my head on the table, and went to sleep. I awoke in about an hour, and the prisoner was then gone; I missed my handkerchief out of my hat; also a key, a tobacco-stopper, and 3s., from my pocket. I saw the prisoner again on the 3d of September, in Tottenham-court-road, with the handkerchief round her neck; it has not my name on it, but I knew it by this mark, which had been made in my presence.
 Prisoner. I met this man with another woman; we went to a public-house at the corner of Charles-street, and had five half-pints of gin between three of us. I was perfectly sober, but the other woman was very sick, and she went out; he lent her that blue handkerchief to wipe her face with - we then went to another house, and when I got out I found the handkerchief in my hand, but I was in liquor before I parted with him - he went to the bar to change a sovereign, but the landlady said he had got silver in his hand.
   THOMAS SIMONS. I am a watchman. I took up the prisoner on the 3d of September, and found this handkerchief about her neck.
   JOHN BEX. I did go to change a sovereign, and the landlady could not give me change; she said she had seen some silver in my hand - there had been no one in the room but us two. I had my handkerchief in my hat, by my side, on the table.
     NOT GUILTY.
