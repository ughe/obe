THOMAS WALTER WILSON was indicted for stealing 1 chest, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; and 1152 boxes of lucifer-matches, 2 l  .; 12 s  .; the goods of Samuel Elcock Molyneux , his master; and JOHN DYKE and SAMUEL SAVAGE , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. SAMUEL ELCOCK MOLYNEUX. I am an engineer and lucifer-match maker, in the City-road. Wilson was my clerk—it was his duty to enter the orders in the order-book, then in the delivery-book, and then send them out by Brook, the carman, who would have to take them—the delivery-book was always placed in Wilson's possession, to be sent out with the carman, where his goods were sent—there is no entry in the order-book kept by Wilson of any goods sold or delivered to Mr. Carter, of Croydon—there is an entry in the delivery-book—there is no corresponding entry without the name that could be applied to these goods—we have no customer of the name of Carter, and never had—these are the two chests of matches—supposing each chest to contain eight gross of match-boxes, their value would be 2 l .; 12 s .—I have never been paid for either of these chests of matches.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How do you know that they belong to you? A.  By the chests, and the nature of the matches—lam away about eight months out of twelve—Wilson is not authorised to execute orders on his own account, or on mine, unless they are orders that I send up, or customers who are known to us—I was away when this charge was made—if a person were to come into the shop, and apply for goods to be sent, the pri soner would not have authority to send them, unless the customers were known, or he had consulted me or Mr. Riding—I do not know that the pri soner has made bad debts in sending out the goods—he might have made bad debts, but it might have been my orders—I have called on him to pay money when he has collected accounts and not accounted for them, but never for bad debts—there was not one of 6 l .; near Southampton—my mother is not liable for the debts of the business—she might have paid accounts for me in London—she has the charge of the money—I keep a ledger, and we post it from the order-book.
   Cross-examined by MR. O'BRIEN . Q.  How Jong is it since Dyke left your employ? A.  Last December.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  Is there any entry of an order, on the 17th of July, to Carter, of Croydon? A.  There is not, nor in any part of my books. JOHN BROOK. I am carman and packer to the prosecutor. On the 17th of July I saw two chests of congreves directed by Wilson to Mr. Carter, of Croydon—one chest contained eight gross, and the other ten gross—they were directed to be taken to the Talbot-inn, Borough, and left there till called for—I can swear to this being one of the directions that Wilson put on the cart, and I believe the other to be the same—the day before I took them, Wilson told me he would give me a pot or 1 s . if I would get them out by ten or eleven—he told me not to tell the cooper anything about it—I took them to the Talbot in my master's van.
