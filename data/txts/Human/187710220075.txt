 801. HENRY KIRKE SWANN  (38) , Unlawfully obtaining by false pretences from Timothy Arthur Symonds , an order for the payment of 100 l  ., with intent to cheat and defraud.
   MR. D. METCALFE conducted the Prosecution; and MR. M. WILLIAMS the Defence. TIMOTHY ARTHUR SYMOUDS. I am a booksellers' assistant—I saw an advertisement in the Daily Telegraph  on September 12th, and I wrote to "C. A., Post Office, West Bromwich"—I saw the prisoner at my lodgings in Stanley Street, Pimlico, on Saturday, 15th September—he said he was the gentleman who had advertised—( Read : "Cashier and clerk wanted in the office of an established country newspaper: salary 30 s . to commence, security 100 l ., apply C. L., "West Bromwich, near Birmingham")—he also said he was the proprietor and editor of the Lichfield Chronicle , and that it was being carried on in partnership with Mr. Brown, who did the printing and publishing; that a disagreement had arisen, and a dis solution of partnership had been drawn up, and he was going to carry on the paper alone—he said he should want some one to do general office work, and to act as cashier, and he would give  me 2 s . 6 d . more than the salary advertised—ultimately I accepted the terms, and I was to com mence at once, but I was to draw up an agreement on the Saturday, and forward it to him, and he would let me know on the Monday, which I did—I did not hear from him that week, but on the following Friday I received a post-card from Mr. Swann, and on Saturday the 22nd I had this telegram "Meet me at Charing Cross Hotel at 2.30, if not there, shall drive to Pimlico"—I did not go to Charing Cross—it was 2.30 when I got to Pimlico—I waited for the prisoner till four o'clock, but did not see him—I then started to Pulbourne, near Cambridge—I re ceived a letter from Mr. Swann the following morning—( This letter was dated 22nd September , 1877, Charing Cross Hotel, and stated that it was absolutely necessary to arrange with the prisoner or someone else at once, as the arrangements had been made for publishing )—in answer to that letter I sent this telegram—""Will come to you on Tuesday next to settle agreement on Wednesday"—the prisoner came to me at Pulbourne on Monday the 24th, and said the matter must be settled at once, and he was considerably put about—we came back to London, and at the Charing Cross Hotel he told me that all was ready; that I had good security; that his wife's relations were worth 30,000 l .; that he was well acquainted with Messrs. Swindle and Collis, great coal-mine proprietors; that he would settle at once, and I could have a week's holiday, and commence on the next Monday—I believed his representations, and handed him the cheque—he gave me this receipt "24th September, 1877, received the sum of 100 l . deposited with me this day by Timothy Arthur Symonds, to be held as security during his engagement with me, and at the termination of the said engagement to be returned to him.
