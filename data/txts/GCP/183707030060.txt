KELLY, Mayor.
OLD COURT.-Thursday July 6th, 1837.
Second Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
1694. ROBERT ARNELL, alias Robert Arnold Arnell, was indicted
for bigamy.
NANCY SMITH. I am a widow, and live in Princes street, Red Lion-
square. I have known the prisoner for eighteen years-be was married to
my sister in 1818, at old Pancras church-I was a witness, and signed the
register--my sister had no occupation-my father was formerly a mer-
chant, and the prisoner had been a lieutenant in the navy, but was not
any thing at the time he married my sister--they lived together a great
many years, and lived at St. Katharine Docks--they had no family!
saw my sister alive last Thursday.
Prisoner. Q. You remember the time I kept the Reading Tavern, in
Broad-court? A, Yes--there was a deed of separation between you and
your wife-a Mr. Tattersal and my sister lived together as man and
wife after the deed of separation-I know Mr. Tattersal's handwriting-1
am confident this is his handwriting, and this is my sisters-(looking at a
paper.)
ALGERON SMITH (policeman.) I took the prisoner into custody in
Westminster on the 16th of June.
MARY WEBB. I was a witness to the second marriage, when the pri-
soner was married to Catherine Marshall, on the 8th of October, 1831, at
the church of Stoke Damerel, Devon.
James Milward. I am an attorney. I produce a register of the first
marriage-I examined it with the original (read) I also produce a certi-
ficate of the second marriage, but I have not examined that
CATHARINE MARSHALL. I was married to the prisoner on the 8th of
October, 1831. I was a widow at the time, and had two children, one eight
and the other fourteen years old-he courted me about three months, and
represented himself as a gentleman-I was living on my means I had my
widow's pension of 701. a year, and some freehold property- I had nearly
2001, a year altogether-he represented himself as a single man-he con-
tinued to live with me, and I have had four children by him-I discovered
that he was married in December, but could not leave my house till March,
in consequence of illness-I had discovered the deed of separation some time
previous, but could not find out whether his wife was alive or dead-the
prisoner has not wasted my property in any way this is my prosecution.
Prisoner's Defence. I have been separated from my first wife since
1829, by a deed of separation-a party named in the deed of sepe-
ration is a gentleman of the name of Tattersal; and the deed will
show that there was a connexion existing between her and that gentleman
before my marriage: and immediately after the deed was signed, this gen-
tleman and my first wife lived openly together as man and wife in more
places than one-the deed likewise carries on the face of it that my wife
has no manner of claim on me--my present wife will do me the justice to
say that my conduct has been strictly correct, both in a moral and social
point of view I have not squandered away her property, but a succession
of unfortunate circumstances have placed me in this situation-I considered
I was placed in a situation to marry again.
GUILTY. Aged 11.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutria.
Confined One Year.
