 186. HENRY BROWN was indicted for feloniously assaulting Jenkin Stradling , on the   29th of August , at   St. Paul's, Deptford , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, 1 watch, value 2 l  .; 6 seals, value 4 l  .; and 1 watch chain, value 1 s  .; his property.
   MR. CHAMBERS conducted the Prosecution. JENKIN STRADLING. I live on my property, in the Vauxhall-road, Pimlico. On the night of the 22nd of August, I was returning from Blackheath to my residence—I got to New-cross about a quarter or ten minutes to ten o'clock—I had drank more than I should have done—when I got near New-cross, I found myself Very ill in the stomach, and sat down on the bank by the side of the road—I found my watch was gone, and my chain, and six gold seals—afterwards I got up from there, and walked on; and met a policeman, named Redwood, whose father I had known very well—I told him of it—I had discovered I had lost my watch, before I met him—I discovered that before ten o'clock—I felt it safe about the turnpike gate, on this side of Deptford—I do not think the bank it above one hundred yards from the turnpike-gate—I felt in my pocket, and found the guard-chain, which was round my neck under my waistcoat, was broken, and the watch gone—I had not felt any body tugging at my watch—I was either knocked down, or pushed down fin the road, I think—I did not feel any body near me—I felt a knock or push when I got up from the bank—it was after that that I found my chain broken—a man pushed me down—that is the person standing there, (the prisoner)—I gave a description of him—it was after that man knocked or pushed me down, that I missed my watch—after that I met the policeman, and told him some thing—to the best of my recollection, it was the prisoner—I did not know whether I was pushed or knocked down, but the more I have looked at the prisoner these three or four days, I am the more convinced—I got into a hackney coach, and went home without speaking that night—when the witness looked for me that night, I was gone off in the coach—I did not see the prisoner before that night—I have no recollection of drinking with him—he was shown to me the morning after the robbery, and I swore he was the man who was ill using me that night; and I asked him what he had done with my watch and seals—he said he had never seen them—I do not recollect feeling any thing done to my watch or watch chain, at the time I was pushed or knocked down—my watch was in my fob, and the guard was round my neck—that was broken, and I look upon it it was broken with a pair of pinchers—it was a very strong one—I put it under my waistcoat—it was made of strong steel—I do not know what parish the place is in where I received the push—I believe they call it St. Paul's, Deptford—it is in Surrey—I had six gold seals to my watch, and a steel chain—the watch was a silver hunter.
