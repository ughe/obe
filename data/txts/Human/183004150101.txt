 887.  SARAH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of April , 6 sovereigns and 11s., the monies of William Roberts , from his person.
   WILLIAM ROBERTS. I am a German. I met the prisoner on the 11th of April; she asked me to go with her to Wentworth-street- I did not like to go into a house with her; it was about four o'clock in the morning - we went down a court, and she told me to give her a 1s.; I took out my purse, which had 12s. and six sovereigns in it, and gave her 1s. out of it - I put it into my pocket; we were on the ground together for about five minutes - I felt her take my purse from me; I said, "Give it back to me, I don't want to make a noise, and you shall keep 1l. if you will give me 5l. back;" two Policemen were going by - I said,"Now give me my money;" but she would not, and the Police took her.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What did you give her? A. Two sixpences - she did put her arms round me; I went back with the Policeman to the yard where we had been - they found nothing on her the first time, but when I went back again, the Policeman found a sovereign in her hand; she forced me to go to the yard with her.
   JOHN ROBINSON. I am a Police-officer. I was on duty in George-street, and met the prisoner and prosecutor - he said he had been robbed of six sovereigns and 11s.; the prisoner denied it, and said she had only 1s. 6d. about her, but on searching her I found 13s. and 1s. 1 1/2d. in copper - I am quite certain she denied having any money but 1s. 6d.; I went back to the spot with the prosecutor in hopes of finding the money, and when I returned I got permission to search the prisoner again - I opened the cellar-door and found a sovereign in her hand.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you search her at first attentively? A. No, sir, I was in hopes of finding the money, and I repaired back directly - it was dark; I found no money at the place the prosecutor said they had been at - the prisoner did at the latter end of my search request that a female might search her, but I had used no indecencies to her; when she asked that a woman might search her I left her and took her word - I did not say"Ding it to me and it will be all right;" the prosecutor pointed out the spot where he had been with her - my brother officer took it from her, but I saw it in her hand; the prosecutor did say he had lost a purse, but I saw none - I searched the cell, but found no sovereigns there.
 COURT. Q. What was the distance from the spot they had been at, to the watch-house? A. About half a mile.
   MICHAEL BERGIN. I was on duty at the watch-house when Robinson brought the prisoner in, a little after four o'clock in the morning; he searched her and found 13s. on her - he put that down, but did not search her very particularly; he was ordered to go back with the prosecutor to search where they had been, and see if he could find any gold - the prisoner positively denied that she had any more money about her; she was then locked up - when the prosecutor and Robinson returned, I was ordered to bring her up; I saw she was a little confused with her hands, and she asked for a drink of water - I said, "Let me see what is in your hand;" she opened her hand, and there was a sovereign.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did the prosecutor tell you that he had told her she might keep one sovereign? A. No, not at that time - I was not present at the last search; I know nothing of the prosecutor.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never saw any money except the 1s. he gave me; he held me from the time he charged me, till he gave me to the Police - I was used in a most inhuman manner, but I had no more money.
 MR. PHILLIPS to ROBINSON. Q. You took her on a charge of robbing the prosecutor? A. Yes - she walked by my side, and I kept my eyes upon her; she had time to have opened the purse and taken a sovereign out, between the first search and the second - she was left in charge of the inspector, who is not here.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Life.
 888.  WILLIAM JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of February , 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Alexander Bailey Richmond , from his person.
   ALEXANDER BAILEY RICHMOND. I am engaged at the periodical press. I was walking along the Strand with two friends, on the 27th of February - we turned up Catherine-street; it was about six o'clock in the evening - the Police-constable came and asked if any of us had lost our pocket-handkerchiefs; I felt, and said mine was gone - he requested us to wait, and, in about a minute and a half or two minutes, he brought the prisoner, and asked if I should know my handkerchief; I said Yes - we went to the watch-house, and I was asked if I had any mark on it; I said my family were very much in the habit of marking my things, and, on examining it, it had my mark and a number on it.
   JAMES VICKERY. I was in Brydges-street, and saw the prisoner walking up Catherine-street, with two others; a gentleman desired me to look sharp after them - they were following the prosecutor and his two friends; I went on the other side, and in a few minutes I saw the prisoner with this handkerchief in his hand; I desired them to wait- I went and took this handkerchief from the prisoner's pocket.
