JABEZ MOORE. I live in Bond-street. I have known the prisoner many
vears by the name of Anderson I did not endorse any promissory note for
him in Aug., 1846.--the endorsement to this note is not my writing-I never
ve him, or any other person, authority to use my name to this bill. in
way-be never asked me for my authority to this bill-I never gave him
lave to refer Mr. Allen to me, nor ever recommended him to Mr. Allen.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you had business transactions with the pri-
soner? A. Yes; as a customer twenty years ago I am a breeches-maker
-I have never endorsed bills for him-nor ever authorised him to use my
Dame-I saw him repeatedly about July, 1846-I know Mr. Allen as a
neighbour--the prisoner came to me, and asked if I knew whether Mr. Allen
was in the habit of discounting-I said I did not-be said, a gentleman,
named Platt, in Maddox-street, had told him that Mr. Allen was a great dis-
counter of bills-I said, if he was I did not know it-be wanted to get a bill
discounted by Mr. Allen-I did not see him on the matter afterwards, to my
recollection-T rather think he told me that Mr. Allen would not discount
the bill on his credit alone-I did not understand that he wanted me to
endorse it I would not endorse a bill for any body-I should not think he
could have imagined, from what I said, that I authorised him to use my name
-he had not dealt with me for many years-he told me he was in the wine
trade-I did not consider myself under any obligation to him at this time-
he was always friendly, giving friendly advice, and so on-I expressed myself
very much obliged to him for some services he had performed-it was after that
that the conversation took place about Allen's discounting the bill-I never
expressed myself so as to lead him to believe that he had my authority to
sign my name I had no idea of anything of the kind-I might have said I
should be happy to serve him-I do not know that I did I was very ill at
that time, and bad been for seven years.
MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did he at any time show you any bill or pro-
missory note! 4. He said he had sold some person a pipe of wine, and had
got a bill for it-I am quite sure I gave him no authority to use my name
be never asked me I did not see him after that till he was in custody-I
never endorsed a bill for him, or be for me--I saw the bill in his hand, but
not so as to read it--that was at the time he spoke about Mr. Allen being a
discounter.
JOSEPH RACKSTRAW (policeman D 169.) I apprehended the prisoner-I
was waiting at the door of the chop-house, in Woodstock-street, and saw him
come down stairs with Mr. Allen-Mr. Allen said he should give him into
custody for uttering the note, knowing it to be forged-I took him to the
station-he asked Mr. Allen if he could say something to him-Mr. Allen
said, no, he had nothing to do with it-he said, in the station, that the endorse-
ment of Jabez Moore was not on the note when he gave it to Mr. Allen, and
he did not know but what any other person, or Mr. Allen might have put it
on himself.
Cross-examined. Q. You were not examined before the Magistrate! A.
I was-he was taken on this charge, but Mr. Moore did not attend, and no
deposition was taken.
