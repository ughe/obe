ELLIS, Mayor.
-I was first introduced to this champagne by Salomons-I afterwarda
found out that it came from a person named WilkieI heard afterwarda
that it was forged--I did not know it at first; I think I knew it about
six months after I had been at Basinghall Street; that would be in the
summer of 1880-we then began to manufacture it, and for that purpose
employed Bond, who I had known as working at Bevis Court for Bass
he got the brands from Salomons; he suggested that we should do it on our
own account the difficulty was in getting the brands-Salomons informed
me that he knew of a party near where he was residing at Islington, who
would manufacture the brands, and that was Maddeu-the brands were
then made by Madden, both for Heidseick and Moet and Chandon-he
did both about the same time the labels and wrappers were printed by
Norris; I went to Norris and ordered them-Salomons had previously
had some plates made by some friend of his who I did not know, and
they were printed from them-we got a bottling machine from Farran
and Jacksoy's—those were got previous to employing Bond-we were
under a difficulty; we did not know how to proceed in the matter, aud
Bond was employed to teach us--at this time we were intimate with Bass,
and he was a constant visitor at 64, Basinghall Street when we had the
apparatus there-Salomons brought the brands from Madden’s to Basing.
hall Street, and they were eventually taken on to Finsbury Squere--the
operation of changing the labels and the corks was performed at
Finsbury Square-we got the wine from Hills and Underwood, and when
the labels aud corks were altered we delivered the wine from Finsbury
Square-I sold the first parcel to Bass- I told him that we had portions
of this wine-we did not actually inform bim how it was got up; we took
it for granted that he knew; it was palpable; the cases were so different;
and the landlord of the Windmill, after he had tried it, told him what it
was-I do not know the landlord personally I have been to the house-
I think it was about July, 1880, that we sold the first parcel to Bass; we
sold it at something near 508. a dozen, 415. odd; in fact it was less,
because we did not get paid for it--we eventually got payment of it from
Bass, 308. odd a dozen; we got it by pounds at a time, extending over a
considerable period—be declined to pay-he sent a carman for the wine-
Bass told me that he knew really what it was; he told me it was not
Moet's champagne, and the landlord wanted him to take it back---We
laughed at him, I and my partner did-Bass wanted some more of it-
we offered to let him have it at a certain price, provided we were allowed
to deliver it and receive the money and hand him the difference we
declined to let bim have it without the money being paid right down or
being paid to us, and we to hand him the price it realised-I think we
offered it to him at 448. or 458.; I can't exactly say to a shilling; it was
Moet's wine-we found it was not good enough, and we stopped manu-
facturing Moet, and we then commenced manufacturing Beidseick's-
that is, we began to use Heidseick's corks and lubels, and a different wine,
a better class; it cost 448. a dozen-Bass came, and wanted 100 cases of
this new wine-we informed him that we bad stopped the Moet's and had
commenced with Heidseiek's—we told him we could not let him bave 100
