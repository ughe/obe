   Cross-examined. Q.  When was it you received the last? A.  On Easter Tuesday—I only serve in the evening—my husband does in the day—we never allow our servants to serve—my husband puts money into the bowl as well as I—there were no half-sovereigns put into the bowl but what I put in—it was Connor's duty to hand the half-sovereigas from Welham to me, and the change to Welham. JOHN SONNEX. I am husband to Ann Sonnex.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you receive any half-sovereigns from this Thurs day to the Tuesday? A.  None—I serve in the morning—I did not change a half-sovereign for anybody—I had not the key of the desk—my wife serves in the evening, and day time too—I have other business to attend to—I do hot serve above an hour every day. EDWARD PALMER( police-constable R  231.) On the 20th of April I went to Mr. Sonnex—some half-sovereigns were produced—Mrs. Sonnex marked them, and they were given to me—these are them—I took Connor—he said, he knew nothing about it, he took the money from Dick, the marine—I af terwards apprehended Welham—I told him it was for passing bad money—I put him into a coach, and when we were half-way, he said, "Have you ap prehended me for passing bad half-sovereigns? "—I said, "Yes, "and he im mediately went into a fit. GEORGE HORSLEY. I keep the Cricketers—my wife is no relation of Wel ham's—she could not, as his sister, have given him half-sovereigns occasion ally. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—I have examined all these coins—they are all counterfeit half-sovereigns—they have all been struck from one die—the two black marks on this one arise from the gilt having been worn off, and the base metal appearing underneath—the general appearance of them is tolerably good—they are made of brass—they will not ring.
