First London Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1779.  HENRY LLOYD was indicted for a misdemennor.
 MR. GURNEY conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN HOWELL. I am a hosier, and live in Aldersgate-street. On the 13th of August, between five and six o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came in and bought two pairs of stockings, which came to 3s. 6d.; he gave me a 5s. piece, which the officer (Harrison) has - I saw him searched; another crown-piece was found in the lining of his hat, and a good shilling in his trousers pocket.
 JOHN HARRISON. I am a constable. On the 13th of August I was fetched to Mr. Howell's and searched the prisoner; Howell gave me a crown-piece, I found another in the lining of his hat, and a good shilling in his fob.
 JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of counterfeit-coin. Both these crown-pieces are counterfeits, and cast from the same mould.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had gone to a pablic-house with a person and fallen asleep, during which time somebody must have changed his hat - and that upon putting the hat on a crown-piece fell out, which he thought was good.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Twelve Months, and to find sureties.
 1780.  JEREMIAH MULLENS was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   THOMAS TYRELL. I keep  the Farm and Castle, Little Trinity-lane. On the 26th of July the prisoner came in, and ordered a pint of beer - he offered my wife a crown-piece, and she called me; she gave me the crown, and I gave it to Oldis.
   HARRIET TYRRELL. I am the prosecutor's wife. The prisoner gave me the crown-piece; I knew him before, and told him to sit down and wait for his change; I gave it to my husband, suspecting it was not good - there was an officer in the house, to whom we gave it.
 JOHN OLDIS. I am a constable. I was in the house, and saw the prisoner there - I took the prisoner in charge; he was discharged before the Magistrate - I received a crown-piece from Tyrrell, which I marked, and when he was discharged I gave it to Mr. Field; Mr. Tyrrell was before the Magistrate, but not Mrs. Tyrrell.
   JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of counterfeit coin. I received this crown-piece from Oldis on the 29th of July, at Guildhall.
   MORY SAVAGE. I am the wife of Robert Savage, and keep an oyster-shop in Harp-alley. On Thursday, the 5th of August, about half-past twelve o'clock at night, the prisoner bought 1d. worth of oysters, which he paid 1d. for and went away; he came again in ten minutes with another man, had 4d. worth of oysters and 1d. of bread - the prisoner paid me a shilling; I did not examine it - I asked my brother-in-law to examine it; he thought it good, and put it on the counter, but did not mix it with any other - after he left the watchman came up; I then sounded it on the counter - the watchman said, "You have given that man changed altbough I cautioned you:" the prisoner had new been gone a minute - my brother-in-law went in search of him I saw him in custody on the Sunday.
   WILLIAM HENRY HART. I was in Savage's shop - I did not notice the prisoner; I received a shilling from my sister-in-law - I thought it good, and gave it back to her; the watchman came up - I then looked at it, and pronounced it bad; I went with the watchman to look for the prisoner, but could not find him - I saw the shilling at Guildhall, and knew it by a mark which I put on it; I had given it to the officer on the previous night.
 Prisoner. I saw him put the shilling into his pocket. Witness. I did not.
   MARK ADAMS. I am a watchman. I was by Savage's shop on the 5th of August, between twelve and one o'clock at night, and saw the prisoner give her 1d. - as soon as he was gone I spoke to Savage; I afterwards endeavoured to apprehend him, and took him on Sunday morning, in Black Horse-court - I am certain of him.
   JOHN BADDEN. I am an officer. I was on duty in Harp-alley, and received a shilling from Hart, which I have now.
 MR. FIELD. This crown-piece and shilling are both counterfeits, and both cast in a mould - I was before the Magistrate on the first charge; it was understood to be the first he had uttered.
     GUILTY. Aged 28. - Confined Six Months.
 1781.  JAMES CLARKE was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   CAROLINE NINER. I live in Fore-street, with my brother-in-law, who is a hosier and glover. On the 16th of July, about four o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came for a pair of stockings which hung at the door, and came to 93/4d. - while I was folding them up he put his hand into his pocket, and said he did not think he had sufficient without changing a half-crown - he put a half-crown on the counter, which I took up and told him it was bad; he said it was not, and said, "Whether good or bad I must have change for it;" I was alone in the shop - I went to the door, and beckoned to Pudley, a neighbour; I gave him the half-crown - he desired the prisoner to pay for the stockings, and told him it was a bad one; he objected to pay; a mob gathered, and the officer came and took him - he was not out of my sight; Pudley took the half-crown to his own shop (next door), cut it in two, and brought it back; the prisoner was searched, and 7d. found on him.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I not offer to pay for the stockings, if you would return me the half-crown, to return the person I had it from? A. He said if I would give him the halfcrown he would go back and change it, and pay for the stockings - I said I should not part with it, for it was bad - he did not tell me where he took it.
   CHARLES PUDLEY. I am a porkman, and live next door to Miss Niner - I went into the shop, seeing a mob, and Miss Niner said, "Is this a good half-crown?" I said No; she said, "This man insists on my taking it;" he said he had no more money - I asked where he got it; he said he took it in change at the Elephant and Castle, at Newington, on his road from Chatham - I asked if he had sufficient to pay for them without changing the half-crown; he said he had not - he was searched, and two shillings, a sixpence, and 7d. in halfpence were found on him; he insisted at first that it was the coin of the Realm, and on having change for it - he never offered any money for the stockings.
