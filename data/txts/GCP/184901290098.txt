565. HENRY OLDFIELD and DIANA PARSONS, stealing 28. 2d.;
the moneys of Arthur Saunders, the master of Oldfield.
ARTHUR SAUNDERS. I am a grocer of Woolwich. On Saturday, 22nd
Dec., I engaged Oldfield to assist in my shop-about eight o'clock that even-
ing, I heard a rattling in the till, and saw him putting his hand into Parson's
hand-in half an hour Parsons came again, went up to the counter, and with-
out her asking anything, he put down llb. of sugar to her-she put 18. on
the counter-he took it up, and put down 2s. 2d, change-I stopped her,
took the money from her, and gave them in charge.
Cross-examined by Mr. HORRY. Q. Who was serving besides you and
him? A. Baxter; we both left off serving as soon as Parsons came in-the
prisoner had just finished serving a customer-I wont swear he had not given
her change-he said Parsons had given him ball-a-crown.
RICHARD BAXTER. I saw Parsons come in-she placed 18. on the coun-
ter, Oldfield took it up, and gave her 23. 2d.
Nor Guilty.
566. ELIZABETH COUCHMAN, stealing 1 tame goose, and 2 tame
fowls, price 108.; the goods of Edward Munn: and 1 bag, value 6d. ; the
goods of Joseph Hillier.
SEPTIMUS FENN (police-sergeant, R 31). On 10th Jan., about half-past
nine o'clock in the morning, I was on duty in Black heath Road, and saw the
prisoner with something bulky- I took her to the station, and found on her a
goose, a live tame rabbit in a bag, and two chickens in her pockets-she said
she found them, and the rabbit was given her by a man.
ELEANOR MUNN. I am the wife of James Munn-this goose and two
chickens are my brother's, Edward Munn- I saw them safe about four o'clock
in the afternoon, on the 9th of January.
CHARLOTTE SARGEANT. I searched the prisoner at the station-I found
two chickens in her pocket-these are the heads of them.
Prisoner's Defence. I hope you wont be hard with me; I have three
children, and my husband is in Maidstone Gaol.
GUILTY. Aged 38.-Confined One Year.
567. BRIDGET LLOYD, stealing 1 gown and 1 frock, value 58.; the
goods of Thomas Pacey Birt; having been before convicted.
THOMAS BORDERS. I am shopman to Mr. Thomas Pacey Birt, a pawn-
broker, at Woolwich. On 4th Jan., he bad a gown and a frock hanging in
the shop, just inside the door-I saw them safe about eleven o'clock-I did
not miss them till the policeman told me of it, about six in the evening-I
can swear these are my master's-(produced.)
EDWARD GLASSCOCK (policeman, R 25). I found the prisoner in Mr.
Dracot's shop, with this gown and frock in her possession-she said they were
her own.
Prisoner's Defence. They were given to me.
WILLIAM HARDING (policeman, R 78). I produce a certificate of the
prisoner's former conviction-(read-Convicted Aug. 1848, and confined four
months)—the prisoner is the person-she has been summarily convicted nine
or ten times. GUILTY. Aged 27.- Transported for Seven Years.
(There were two other indictments against the prisoner.)
