778. MARY LANE was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of Feb
ruary, 1 shift, value 38.; 1 boa, value 28. ; 1 night-gown, value 1s.; 1
collar, value 18. ; 1 trinket-box, value 1s. ; 1 breast-pin, value 28;8
yards of ribbon, value 1s. 6d.; and 1 pair of ear-rings, value 88.; the
goods of William Colson.
ELIZABETH Colson. I am the wife of William Colson, and live at Na
7, Barnsbury-row, Islington. I do not live with my husband-the poi
soner came to lodge with me a fortnight ago on Monday, saying she wa
out of place-in consequence of missing articles on my premises, I asked
to see her box, which was corded-she uneorded it for me, and I found in
it a boa, a shift, and night gown belonging to me--I sent for a policeman-
the prisoner had a bonnet-box, which was afterwards searched, and in that
I found a collar-I had missed some ear-rings, which I have since seen in
possession of Liddle, the policeman-I also missed a gold pin, some rib-
bon, and a small box-I had not given any of the articles to ber-they
are worth 11. altogether-the shift and bed-gown were taken out of my
drawer, which was kept locked-she represented herself as a servant-
had lent her the boa once..
EZEKIEL GUMMY (police-constable N 88.) I received a shift, night
gown, and boa from the prosecutrix.
JOSEPH LIDDLE (police-constable N 270.) I produce a collar, two drops
and a snap--I found the collar in the prisoner's band-box, and the ear-ringo
I found on the witness Elizabeth Saunders, at No. 4, Charles-court,
Strand.
ELIZABETH SAUNDERS. I know these ear-rings-(looking at them)--the
prisoner gave them to me-I went into a public-house, and sat down by
the side of her, and she gave them to me, saying she had found them in
the street-I kept them till Tuesday night, when I was taken into custody
--I had gone into the public-house to look for a young man who I live
with, and was talking with her-I had seen her once before.
