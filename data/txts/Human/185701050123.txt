he could look round the corner at him; he did so—I saw him again on the following day, on the pier, I think between 11 and 12 o'clock—he was with Tester—the boat was then getting ready to start for Boulogne—nobody was with him when I first saw him, but a very short time afterwards I saw him with Tester—I knew Tester perfectly well—I saw them go from the pier in the direction of the Pavilion Hotel—I did not watch them much beyond the station—the Pavilion Hotel is close to the station—I should think they must have been in my sight altogether for twenty minutes on that occasion—I believe Mr. Ledger was away at that time; his marriage was about that time—I saw Tester again that day at the harbour station—he came and went up by the train which left Folkestone harbour at half past 7 o'clock in the evening.
