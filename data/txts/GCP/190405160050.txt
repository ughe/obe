EDWARD Parsons (Police Inspector.) On March 30th, with another
officer, I arrested the prisoner-I said, “We are police officers ; I hold a
warrant for your apprehension for the misappropriation on September 5th,

he said, "I have a complete answer to the charge "-shortly after I read

the said Edith Mary Mitchell "-he said, “Yes, I understand "-he was
formally charged at the station, and repeated that he had an answer to
the charge.
MR. JUSTICE WALTON said that he did not consider that there had been
any entrusting, and that the evidence did not support Counts 1 and 2, which
he would, therefore, withdraw from the jury.
The prisoner, in his defence on oath, said that he was admitted as a solicitor
in 1875; that, apart from the family matters which had not been claimed for
and the £650, his first statement of affairs showed solvency; that he made a
claim for £250 against the Mitchells as having been advanced to them; that
when he received the money from Moore's estate he paid it into his own
account ; that Mr. Mitchell came and asked him not to pay it into Court,
as he and his wife had been talking the matter over and wanted him
(the prisoner) to use it in his business, and pay them 3 per cent, interest;
that he gave Mitchell £2; that he came time after time toith his wife and had
more money ; that Mitchell said he was entitled to money on the death of his
jather, and any sum rohich he had had would be recovered; that they had at
least £250 from him, that while Mitchell was ill he (the prisoner) advanced
them from £5 to £5 weekly for some weeks ; that he had not kept an account of
the money, as he trusted them as they trusted him ; that he let Mitchell have
money from time to time to pay the Furnishing Company; that the rest of the
money was lost in his business as he had made a lot of bad debts, but that he
had not benefited by it.
Guilty on Counts 3, 4 and 5. Not GUILTY on Counts 1 and 2, Eighteen
months' hard labour.
