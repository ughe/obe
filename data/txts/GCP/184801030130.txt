JOSEPH BALL. I keep the Queen's Arms Tavern, Newgate-street. These
eight spoons are my property-they were given out to the waiter on the
morning of the 21st, as is the custom-- I have no recollection of seeing the
prisoner there, but a person could slip in and out again-our house is up a
passage-there are two coffee-rooms.
Cross-examined. Q. You do not know him? A. No; one or two of my
friends have given him a good character.
WILLIAM WATERS. I am waiter at Mr. Ball's I know these eight spoons
--they are my master's I had them under my charge on 21st Dec., at ten
o'clock in the morning, and missed them at nine at night-when the officer
came I cannot say how lately before they had been taken - they were on
the table in the coffee-room-a person coming in could put them in his
pocket-persons come in and go out again, without taking any refreshment,
sometimes I placed the spoons off the table on the sideboard, about four
o'clock-they might have been taken between four and five.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you in the coffee-room? A. No; I was
engaged up stairs at a dinner until about five o'clock.
Prisoner's Defence. I can prove I was at Garraway's till after five o'clock.
WILLIAM ANDREWS. I was with the prisoner on 21st Dec., at Garraway's
-I saw him there as late as four o'clock-it might have been half-past-
that is about ten minutes' walk from Mr. Ball's.
Guilty. Aged 32.-Transported for Seven Years.
