ROBERT GOSLING. I live in Meeting House Lane, Peckham, at the back
of the King's Head. On Tuesday, 1st Sept., I went out about 8 o'clock or
half past, and saw Richardson in the passage leading from my house to Mr.
Coleman's, coming out of his back gatethere is a gas lamp four or five
yards from the gate I went into my house, and stopped about a quarter of
an hour, then came out, and saw Peach, I believe, standing there, with a
red shawl on, leaning against an iron fence-I cannot swear to her being the
person-I stood there for about a quarter of an hour, waiting for a little boy
of mine, and then saw Brooks in the passage---the roadway and the path
there measures eighteen feet.
Cross-examined. Q. Were you examined more than once! A. Only
once; it was on Brooks's trial-I was not examined when he was first in
custody-a policeman came on a Saturday night, to ask me to give evidence-
he said that he had got a man in custody, and asked me if I did not think
there was a woman concerned, and I said. “ Yes - he asked me if I thought
I should be able to identify the man - I said I thought so a sergeant
gave me a summons to appear, and I went, and saw him brought before the
bar; he was alone, and I told the Magistrate that, to the best of my
belief, he was the man-I was not certain, because he had a different dres
on--that was some days after he was taken-I believe he has the same
clothes on now that he had when he came out of Mr. Coleman's house.
Cross-examined by MR. SHARPE. Q. Did you say before the Magistrate that

was nearer the half hour-I know it was lst Sept., because I had a letter
from my wife; my daughter brought it to me when we met Richardson-
was afterwards taken to the station, and saw her there, there were two
other women present.
