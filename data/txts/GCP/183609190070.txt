CHARLES WILLIAMSON. I was at the Rosemary Branch-in consequence
of what Medcalf said I went after the prisoner, and found him on the
bridge, about 100 yards off, with a parcel under his arm-I asked
him what he had got there-he said he had nothing, at first, but afterwards
gave me a pot, and began to cry-I said it was very foolish to do any
thing of the kind, and he had better go bome- I took the pot from him,
took it back into the tap-room, and put it on the table in consequence of
what was said to me I went after him again in about two minutes, and
found him in Branch-place, by the side of the canal-he had two pint pots
in his hand, and one quart he gave me out of his pocket--I felt about his
clothes and found nothing-the quart pot was bruised and flattened which
he had in his pocket-I brought them back to the Rosemary Branch,
took them through the tap-room, and put them on the table in the pot
shed.
Cross-examined. Q. Did any thing more take place ? A. Yes.

JOHN PARROCK. I am a constable. In consequence of what I heard !
went to the prisoner's house, and found him in bed- I put my hand on
his shoulder and said I wanted him-he said "Very well, I will get up and
go with you "-he did not attempt to do so till he saw me looking round
the room-I expected to find more pots there-he said " I know what you
want with me now, I will dress myself and go with you directly," which
he did I have the pots, which I received from M'Pherson.
Cross-examined. Q. What time was it when you took him? A. About
quarter after nine o'clock in the evening-I hurried him and said, "Why
don't you get up?"-he put his foot out of bed and put it in again.
