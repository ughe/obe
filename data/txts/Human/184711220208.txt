   MR. HUDDLESTON. Q.  Had you at that time discovered that Haynes and Son had not given the delivery order? A.  Not at that time—I went to Mr. Haynes on the Tuesday after the day of payment, and ascertained that he had never given the delivery-order for the seven pockets—the prisoner came the day after, and said he had sold one pocket to Haynes—I had seen him be tween the time that he got the order from me, and the time I went to Haynes and Son—he did not then say anything about the sale of the one pocket—when he first came he said that Haynes and Son would purchase the seven pockets of hops—I made out the invoice, and gave it him, to give it to Haynes and Son, for me to call with him on the Saturday to receive the money—I think that was on the 11th, the Monday after—they were delivered on the Saturday—it was before the money became due—he said he wanted the invoice, to leave at Messrs. Haynes'—I learned from Mr. Nash that the hops had not been delivered.
   COURT. Q.  Did no conversation pass between you and him on Monday, the 11th? A.  Yes—he asked me for the invoice, to take to Mr. Haynes, and said that he had sold them for 78 s ., not for 72 s ., he wished me to make out the invoice to Mr. Haynes for the 78 s ., and to go with him on the Satur day to receive the money—I did not see any more of him till the following week, the Wednesday after the day of payment—no other names besides Messrs. Haynes and Son were mentioned when I gave him the invoice. MARY DAVIS. I am the wife of Nathaniel Davis. The prisoner came to our house on 8th Oct., and left a paper for my husband—he was out, and I gave him the paper at night. NATHANIEL DAVIS. I am a town-carman. On the evening of 8th Oct.
