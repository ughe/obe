254. EDWARD HUSSEY was indicted for stealing, on the   9th of November , 1 boiler, value 10 s  ., the goods of Charles Wright .
   MR. PHILLIPS conducted the Prosecution . BENJAMIN EAGLES. I am in the employment of Mr. Charles Wright. I saw the boiler safe on the 8th of November—I missed it the next morn ing—it is here.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Does Mr! Wright keep the Red House? A.  Yes—the boiler was in the shooting-ground. JAMES WILLIAM BURRIDGE( police-constable V  160.) I saw the prisoner with a boiler on the morning of the 9th of November, in Battersea-fields—he put it down and walked away, and when I went towards him he ran—I took him in Tooley-street, "Westminster, at the Adam and Eve public-house—I told him I wanted him for that copper boiler—he said, "It is not copper, it is iron."
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 21.— Recommended to mercy .— Confined One Month.
 255. WILLIAM JACKSON was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of November , 2 3/4 lbs. weight of wool, value 7 s  ., the goods of Mark Brown . JOHN THOMAS CAMPBELL. I am foreman of Mark Brown, a wharfin ger, in Potter's-fields—the prisoner was my extra man. On the 3rd of November I was in the counting-house, and saw him coming along—I went and took him by the collar into the counting-house, and he took some of this wool out of his breast—he was then taken—part of this was found about him, and this was on his person—( producing it .) DANIEL JOSEPH HURLEY. I am in the employ of Mr. Brown—I saw part of this taken from the prisoner's person.
   Prisoner . It was lying outside the gate, and I picked it up. JOHN THOMAS CAMPBELL re-examined.  No, it was in a bale—I exa mined the bales, and found one was cut—it was the same sort of wool as this. GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined Three Months.
 256. BRIDGET TOOMEY was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of October , 1 crown-piece, the monies of Thomas Bryant . THOMAS BRYANT. I am in the employ of Mr. Sherwood on the rail I went into a shop in Southwark to buy some muffins, and put down a crown-piece—the man had no change—the prisoner followed me in, and wanted me to give her a muffin—she snatched up the five shilling piece, and said she would go and get change—I ran after her, and caught her—I asked her to give it me—she would not—I held her till the people came up.
   Prisoner . Did you not drag me into the shop, and say you would give me a muffin? Witness . No. HENRY SMITH. I am an apprentice to John Green, in Kent-street, a shoemaker. I saw the prosecutor go into the muffin shop in King-street—he put the money down on the counter—she took it up and ran out—the man said, "You go after it, or you will lose it"—he did, and she said she would not give it him.—he went to get a policeman—I and another young man watched the prisoner, and she went up some stairs—the money has not been found.
