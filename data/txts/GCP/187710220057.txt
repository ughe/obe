WILLIAM CAIN (Detective C Division). At about 1 o'clock on the morn-
ing of the 9th October I saw Butcher in Jermyn Street, at the corner of
York Street-in consequence of what he told me I stood there with him
for a few minutes I saw Poulton on the opposite side of the way just
against the rails of St. James's Church-after staying there a few minutes
he gave a shrill whistle, not very loud-then he crossed over to the
corner of York Street, and a few minutes after I saw two men jump out
of the area from Wood's Hotel over the rails I have no doubt that
Wilcox was one I followed Wilcox and the other man not in custody,
and then lost sight of them up Eagle Place I returned with Butcher
and saw Poulton in Piccadilly-we followed him up Piccadilly-he was
about 100 yards from the hotel—Sergeant Butcher stopped him and took
him to the station.
Cross-examined by MR. A. Jones. I made some inquiry about Wilcox-
I found he had a very good character for the eight months he had been
with his present employers, Novello, the music publishers—I also found
he had been formerly in the employ of Woodfall's for two and a half
yearshe has been out on bail.
JANE GOODALL. I am the book-keeper at Wood's Hotel, Jermyn Street-
I put the chain up at about twenty-five minutes or & quarter to one on
the morning in question I left the door open to admit air-when the
constable aroused me we went down in the area and found the wooden
steps up against the railings when we went to bed they were lying down
the side.
Cross-examined by MR. A. JONES. I cannot say how far the street lamp
is from where the man got over-I think it is not the next house, but
I am not certain.
Poulton's Statement before the Magistrato. “I have been keeping company
with a young woman for four years, and last night I was at the Oxford
Music Hall, and went to Jermyn Street to meet a young chap who used
to work with me I stopped twenty minutes, and could see nothing of
him, and turned to go home, and I was stopped in Great Windmill Street
-I told him yes I had been in Jermyn Street-I never saw Wilcox in my
life." Wilcox says. “At twenty-five minutes to one I was in Crown Street
talking to a friend I went down St. Martin's with him as far as Palace
Yard, and came across St. James's Square I stopped at & post to get
a light-this gentleman came up and asked me what I was doing-I
said I was only lighting my pipe, and he took me-I don't know this
man or this house."
