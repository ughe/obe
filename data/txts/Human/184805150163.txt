 1436. ANN PUMPHERY and ANN MILLER , for a like offence.
   MESSRS. ROBINSON  and SCRIVEN conducted the Prosecution. MARY ANN POWELL. I am apprentice to Mr. Kingsman, a linen-draper of Westminster-bridge-road. On 5th May, the prisoners came to the shop—Pumphery asked for a pennyworth of black tape—I served her—she gave me a sixpence—I gave it to the cashier—he took it away—I saw no more of it. THOMAS GROOBY. I am cashier at Mr. Kingsman's. I took the six pence from Miss Powell—I gave it to Mr. Kingsman—a policeman was sent for—he took two other sixpences from pumphery's hand—she tried to swallow them. WILLIAM KINGSMAN. My cashier brought me a bad sixpence, on 5th May—I took it, and walked down to the end of the shop, where the prisoners stood side by side—Miss Powell said pumphery gave it her—I asked Pum phery where she got it, and whether she had any more—she said, "No." and she did not know it was bad—Miller said, "If I had known you wasted a penny I could have lent it you"—a policeman was sent for—he said to Pumphery, "What have you got in your hand"—she was trying to get it to her mouth—he seized her hand—she used very abusive language, and struggled very much—Miller was about to pull the policeman away—another policeman came in, and got two sixpences from Pumphery's hand—I marked the sixpence I had, and gave it to the officer. DENNIS MURPHY( policeman, P  102). I produce two sixpences which I took from Pumphery's hand, and one which I got from Mr. Kingsman. MARY HEARN. I searched the prisoners at the station—Pumphery asked to go to the water-closet—I saw something like money in her hand—she threw it down—I pointed it out to the officer.
