 1261. JAMES BARTON was indicted for stealing, on the   8th of April , 1 time-piece, value 18 s  ., the goods of William Sparrowe . WILLIAM SPARROWE. I keep an auction-room, and live in Smith's-row, New-road—I had a time-piece on the 8th of April—this is it—it had been with me several days for sale—I had seen it about a quarter of an hour before it was missed. ELIZABETH SAXON. I saw this time-piece at Mr. Sparrowe's on the 8th of April—I saw the prisoner take it up and put it under the left flap of his coat, and go out—I made an alarm—he was brought back with it. GEORGE DUDNEY. I was at the auction-room on this evening—I heard Saxon call, "That man has stolen a time-piece"—I went, and saw him throw it into a piece of land—I took it up, and gave him into custody.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I came out with the rush of people, I did not take it. GUILTY.— Confined Six Months.
 1262. JOSEPH HANNEY was indicted for embezzlement. CHARLES WYMAN. I keep a public-house in Curzon-street, May-fair. The prisoner was my postman—it was his duty to receive money paid on my account—if he received 1 s . 3 d . on the 22nd of March, he has not paid it to me, which he ought to have done that day. MARY FOSBURY. I live in Spring-square, and deal with the prosecutor. The prisoner brought me a pint of beer, and half-a-pint of rum on the 22nd of March—I paid him 1 s . 3 d . for his master.
   Prisoner.  I own taking the money, but it only came to 1 s . 2 d .; the whole money was 5 s . 1 d .—I used to account to his wife twice a day, and I accounted to her for this. NOT GUILTY.
