10th of February , 1 watch, value 1l., 1 seal, value 5s.; 1 watch-key, value 3s., and 1 ring, value 2s., the goods of Michael Dibly , from his person.
 MICHAEL DIBLY. I work for Messrs. Stringer and Co. On the 10th of February, about ten o'clock at night, I was at the  Three Crows public-house, City Road; I cannot say whether the prisoner was there: I had been there about two hours; six or seven people were in the taproom; I pulled out my watch about eight o'clock, and Eliza Morris, who was there, wanted to look at it; she took it, and in a few minutes I enquired for it; she said she had given it me back; she afterwards went out - I went out and gave her in charge - I had not noticed what she did with it: she was tried here last Session; I received a blow from Cooper, who has been tried.
   JOHN KETT. I belong to the Guards. I was at the house, and saw Dibley, Morris, and Harding there; Cooper was not there - the prisoner sat next to Morris, who had the watch some time in her hand, and then laid it down by the prisoner, who took it up and gave it to Harding; he put it into his bosom, and went out; Davis followed; Dibley then went to the bar for an officer to take the girl; the prisoner was taken to the watch-house; I am sure he is the man who had the watch.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you see me in the room before ten o'clock? A. Yes: I had come from Ware that day.
   GEORGE WILMOT. I was at the watch-house when the complaint was made - I knew the prisoner, and on the Tuesday following I went and took him; I told him it was for a watch; he said, "I may thank Cooper for this" - he afterward said, "I admit I was in the room; but I had nothing to do with the watch" - the watch was returned to the prosecutor at the office by some girls.
   THOMAS STOCKS. I was with Wilmot - the prisoner was discharged, but we were ordered to take him again.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was not in the tap-room, but in the parlour, and heard a scuffle; I went out, but knew nothing of it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 982.  ROBERT DIXON was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of March , 1 half-crown, 1 shilling, and 2 farthings, the monies of James Squires , from his person.
 JAMES SQUIRES. I live with a tailor in New Kent-road. On the 2d of March, between three and four o'clock; I was looking at the ruins of the  Brunswick Theatre; there were a great many people there; I felt the prisoner's hand coming out of my waistcoat pocket, where I had this money; I caught hold of him, and asked him for my money: the officer got it from him. I lost a half-crown, a shilling, and two farthings: the shilling dropped; I took it up, and gave it to the officer.
   HENRY HILLIER. I was at the ruins of the Theatre, and saw the prosecutor there; he accused the prisoner, who was close to him, of stealing his money - I saw the prisoner put his hand behind him, and put some money into his coat pocket; I could not see what but something dropped.
   THOMAS JOSEPH WEST. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and found a half-crown in his pocket.
   JAMES GLEDBURY. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner attempt several pockets before he went up to Squires - I went up, seeing a scuffle - Squires picked up a shilling and a farthing, and gave them to me.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had this half-crown and three halfpence in my pocket - this gentleman put his hand into his pocket, he let his money fall, and charged me with it.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Life.
 983.  JAMES MALONEY was indicted for embezzlement.
   PETER GRAHAM. I am assistant to John Graham, of High Holborn. The prisoner was his porter, and had a yearly salary; he was authorised to receive money when he delivered goods; he never accounted for 2l. paid by Mr. Whitehouse; which he should have done when he returned, or at all events on the same day. When he returned he said Mr. Whitehouse had sent out for change, but fearing he might be detained too long he was to call again for it.
 Cross-examined by MR. CHURCHILL. Q. Did he not say he had paid Welch? A. Yes; he said so the next morning.
   HENRY HOLLAND. I am a clerk to Mr. Whitehouse, of Thavies Inn. I was out when the parcel was brought, but the prisoner called about ten the next morning, and I paid him 2l. on Mr. Graham's account.
   JOHN BURRELL WELCH. I am cashier to Mr. Graham. The prisoner never paid me this money; it is my duty to receive it - when it was enquired about he said he had given it to me, but I am sure he did not.
 Cross-examined. Q. How many errors have you made in the last year? A. I cannot say; I have made a great many - the prisoner left in consequence of this accusation - he came back next morning, saying he was uneasy about it, and desired to have it investigated - he went before the Magistrate.
   JOSEPH HEALEY. I am in Mr. Graham's employ; it is my duty to receive money in the absence of the cashier; the prisoner never paid me this 2l.
   WILLIAM HOTSON. I am a clerk to Mr. Graham; the cashier accounts to me every evening, but never gave me this 2l. - the prisoner wished this to be fully investigated.
     NOT GUILTY.
 984.  THOMAS BUNN was indicted for embezzlement. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 985.  WILLIAM ATTERSLEY was indicted for embezzlement.
   GEORGE AVELING. I am an oilman, and live in Gloucester-street, Queen-square. The prisoner was my porter and received money for me; Mr. Harrison is a customer of mine; he lives in Spring-garden-terrace.
   CATHERINE ROBERTS. I live with Mr. Harrison; the prisoner used to bring goods from Mr. Aveling; I paid him 19s. 8d. on the 12th of March, on account of his master, and he gave me this receipt. (read.)
 MR. AVELING. He should have given me the money directly he came home; I never received this - Mr. Harrison pays his bills every Wednesday, and as it had stood over two or three weeks, I said to the prisoner, "How is it, Mr. Harrison has not paid his bill?" he said he was out of town - I am certain he never paid me the money.
   THOMAS BENTLEY. I am shopman to Mr. Aveling.
