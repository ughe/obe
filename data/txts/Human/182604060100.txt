 826.  JOHN BARNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 1 sovereign, the money of James Wright.
 JAMES WRIGHT. I am a  sailor, and live in Hall's-rents, St. Catherine's. I went to a shop in Ratcliff-highway, on the 28th of March; I saw the prisoner at the door, who asked me if I wanted to buy a suit of clothes or a jacket; I went in and bargained for one for 12s.; I put down a sovereign on the counter - he took it, put it towards his pocket, and kept it there a few minutes, when he threw down another, and said I had given him a counterfeit, and asked where I got it; I told him to put the jacket by, and I would call again when I got out - I looked at the sovereign, and it was not the one I gave him - it was whiter and lighter; I had taken two sovereigns of my Captain that day, and had changed one at Mr. Farmer's shop - they were both alike and of the same colour.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. How many shops had you been to? A. Only one; there was a young man with me, who is gone to sea; the prisoner came down the next morning after the officer had been for him; my Captain was before the Magistrate; there was a man and a woman in the shop; a man gave my shipmate the jacket, and he was putting it up.
   WILLIAM WILSON. I am an officer. The prisoner is shopman to Mr. Hart; he is not the owner of the shop; I went down to the shop, and the prisoner came to the office immediately.
     NOT GUILTY.
 827.  PATRICK JOSEPH CASHMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of March , 1 brass fender, value 1l. 15s., the goods of Joseph Adams.
   JAMES McGUIRE. I am journeyman to Joseph Adams, ironmonger, St. Giles'. I was in the back of the shop on the 22d of March; the prisoner came in and asked if a person named Hobhouse worked there; I said No, and as he was going out he took this brass fender and put it on his shoulder; I took him and called my master down.(Property produced and sworn to).
 Prisoner's Defence. A man asked me to carry a fender; I was very much intoxicated.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Three Months.
 828.  JOHN COWLEY and THOMAS FOSTER were indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 75lbs. weight of flour, value 13s., the goods of William Aldridge.
 WILLIAM ALDRIDGE. I am a  baker, and live in Coppice-row, Clerkenwell. The prisoner Foster was in my service; on the 28th of March two persons came from the watch-house, and asked if I had lost any flour - they had some in a bag; the bag was not mine, but the flour was; it had been in my flour-house which opens by a flap into the street; I missed 80lbs. of flour out of a sack which was full the night before.
 Prisoner FOSTER. My master told me to put some fresh and some old flour into the bin; I had not enough to make my dough, and I took that out of the sack. Witness. No, there was enough without opening that at all; he wanted about 80lbs. to make his dough - there was enough of the other flour which I saw in the trough; I can tell within a peck; I saw the flour at the watch-house, and it was precisely the same as that in the sack.
   JOSEPH ROOT. I am a watchman of Clerkenwell. I was going to get a light of the next watchman, and met Cowley coming up Pear-tree-court, about three hundred yards from Mr. Aldridge's, with a bag on his shoulder - I called twice to know what he had got, but he gave no answer - I went up and said, "What have you got here?" he put it down, and said he found it; I said, "You must go to the watch-house, and take the flour;" he said he would not - I called another watchman - we secured him.
 Prisoner COWLEY. Q. Did I not say I had picked it up? A. No; you did not stop till I put my hand on it - you then pointed to a corner which I had passed two or three times just before, and said you had found it there.
   THOMAS GARNER. I am the watch-house keeper. Cowley was brought there about one o'clock on the 28th of March, with a bag of flour, and when I went out I knocked at Mr. Aldridge's cellar, and asked if he was there; I was told by Foster, (to the best of my belief,) that he was not - I left word that some flour was found, and was at the watch-house, and if he came there he might see it; I think the answer I received was, "Very well;" after Mr. Aldridge was up I went to his house again - he came and saw the flour, which he said he believed was his - he then searched over his flour and missed it; he inquired what house Cowley belonged to, and he said the Marquis of Gransby, which was the same house his man belonged to.
 COURT to  WILLIAM ALDRIDGE. Q. Who did you leave in the cellar? A. Only Foster - he was to remain there, and call me up at three o'clock, which he did, but did not say any one had been inquiring for me; I have a sample from the sack, and it corresponds with this.
   JAMES PERRIN. I am a baker. I have compared the flour that was found with that in the sack, and they appear to me to be both the same.
   JOHN BOWYER. I received the sack of flour from the witness and gave it to Mr. Garner.
 FOSTER's Defence. This man is quite a stranger to me; he does not belong to the house of call to which I do; Mr. Aldridge is not the only gentleman who has the same sort of flour.
 COURT to WILLIAM ALDRIDGE. Q. Do you know any thing about Cowley? A. They both used one house, and I have heard that he and my man were about together; I do not think Foster would have wanted any flour to make up the dough, but if he did, it could not be near the quantity missing; I am certain there was nearly, if not quite, enough - I can tell within a peck.
 COWLEY -    GUILTY. Aged 43.
 FOSTER.    GUILTY. Aged 30. Transported for Seven Years.
