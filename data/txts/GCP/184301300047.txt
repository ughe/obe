Q. Did he not say to you, “Read that, and see what confidence a lady.
that I never saw in my life, pots in me?" A. He said that he bad never seen
Miss Bailey-I do not recollect his saying, “Read that, and see what con
fidence a lady, that I never saw, puts in me," or any thing of the sort-he
advised me to be as saving as Miss Bailey-he did not add, and then I should
soon get as much money of my own-he did not say he was obliging Miss
Bailey much more than himself, by taking charge of her money, and that
neither she nor her father would ever have had a farthing, only for him-I did
not read the paper.
Q. Was it not an acknowledgment for 28001. odd ? A. I do not know
what it was--he told me that he had money of Miss Bailey's in his posses-
sion, I cannot tell the sum-he said he had been to Dr. Bailey in the morn-
ing, and had made an appointment to be at his chapel that evening--he told
me he was going there on business concerning the money he told me
he wished particularly to go to the chapel, because the doctor, in the
morning, had told him, that that evening was to be the last Thursday evening
service, and he jokingly remarked, that the doctor had lately got a large pro-
perty by his father's death, and was getting tired of preaching-I went to
the chapel that evening, but I was a little late-1 did not see Mr. Smith and
the clergyman that preached sitting in the same pew, in the middle of the
chapel-they were not in the middle of the chapel, it was a side-pewy by the
pulpit-Mr. Smith, a lady, and the clergyman, were in the same pew, at one
side by the pulpit--there was only one lady-there were not "just two ladies"
- I was not to say that I was sure there were just two ladies-Dr. Bailey
only read prayers-he was expected to preach-I felt very much disappointed
that he did not preach-I did not wait to speak to Mr. Smith, because I
