1161. JOHN SAXBY, stealing 9 lbs. weight of flour, value 25. 3d.; the
goods of Eliza Tucker, his mistress.
SARAH TUCKER, I live with my mother, Eliza Tucker, corn and coal-
dealer, in Spital-fields. On 23rd April, about five o'clock, I found this bag
of flour under some trusses of hay in the coal-cellar--it had been taken from
the flour-hatch-the prisoner weighs coals for us.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How many hours does he work ?
A. From seven in the morning to nine at night-he has 78. a week, but no
victuals--I have had no quarrel with him-I once asked him for some coals,
and he said if I did not go out of the place he would kick me out.
MICHAEL GIFFORD. I was told to watch, and saw the prisoner take the
flour from the bay and put it under some straw in the passage I went for
a policeman.
Cross-examined. Q. You took it away and gave it to your brother, who
is a policeman ? A. Yes.
Thomas KELLY (police-serjeant, H 2). I employed Gifford to watch, and
took the prisoner--he asked if I had seen him steal it--I said, " No, but there
was a boy who did."
ROBERT GIFFORD (policeman, H 89). I produce the flour-I searched the
prisoner's place, and found other flour.
(The prisoner received a good character).
Guilty. Aged 62. --Recommended to meroy.-- Confined Two Months.
