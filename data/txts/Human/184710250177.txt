   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q.  Had you seen the prisoner at work that day? A.  No—the Government employ him—there is a foreman over him in the shop he worked in, whose name I think is Hall—I do not know what the prisoner had been working at that day—I am not a judge of wood—I should say this is mahogany, I will not swear it—the prisoner was employed as a carpenter, as other persons about there are—I do not know whether they make such things as this in the carpenter's department. DAVID ROY. I am a clerk in the Dock-yard at Woolwich. This is what they call mahogany in the yard—the prisoner was a carpenter there, and he has been in the employ seven or eight years—it was his duty to lock up the carpenter's place of work—it was no part of his duty to take any of the wood away—he had no leave or licence to make any bracket and take it away—such a thing might be made for the use of the yard; but what he makes in the yard is by the order of those above him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Supposing it were not quite finished, had he no business to take it home? A.  No—the carpenters and persons employed there do not, from time to time, take work home to finish it there—they are never detected doing it—if they do, it is against order—I cannot swear they do not—the store-keeper pays the prisoner—he is employed under Captain Beacbey—he had 3 s . or 3 s . 6 d . a day.—there is extra work from time to time, and they are paid additionally for it—I should rather say the carpen ters are not paid by the job—I cannot say whether Mr. Hall or Captain Beachey hired the prisoner—he was immediately under Captain Beachey's orders. GUILTY .
