JOSEPH FOLKS. I am in the employ of Messrs. Tallis as an errand-boy.
On Saturday, the 15th of Nov., I was present when the money was given to
the prisoner, but I do not know what it was-there was a parcel-book, which
I took with me to the inns, and had the parcels booked-I paid the money,
and the prisoner minded the track-he gave me 38., and said, “I suppose
that will be enough"-I paid 2s. 2d. for booking a parcel at the Swan with
Two Necks, and I puid 2d. at the Belle Sauvage--I paid 28. 4d. in all, and
gave him a sixpence and 2d. back-he would have to pay 2d. for the truck;
that would make 28. 60. spent--I parted with him just by the Post-office-I
did not see him again till he was at the police office.
David EDWIN STAton (City police-constable, No. 248.). I took the pri-

deliver parcels amounting to 2s. 6d., and he ought to have returned to work

street. I had the prisoner in my service from March till July-he called on me

of my shop door and my side door—(if those doors were locked, no one could
get into my shop without false keys) - I did not get those keys--they were
