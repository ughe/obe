"Donald FRASER. I am a policeman. On the 18th of February, be-
tween four and five o'clock in the afternoon, I went with the prosecutor
into the King's Arms public-house, and inquired of the landlord if he had
seen the prosecutor there the night before-- he said, "Yes"-I asked if
he had seen any person in company with the prosecutor-he said, no; he
recollected his speaking to somebody outside-we then went to the Green
Man and to the Swan public-houses, but not inside-we then went to
the Bricklayer's Arms public-house-I told the prosecutor to go in and
see if he could see the person who robbed him-he came out, pointed to
the prisoner, who stood at a little distance, and said, “I think that is the
man that robbed me"--I told him he must be very certain he was the
right person before I could take him, and told him to go and look at him
- he went to him, and spoke to him-I did not bear what be said-I
went near him--he looked round, and then he said he was the man that
robbed him—1 asked him again if he was certain he was the person
who robbed him-he said, “Yes, I am certain that is the one that robbed
me; I know his voice"-I then took the prisoner to the landlord of the
King's Arms to see if he recollected seeing him in the prosecutor's com-
pany the night before--the landlord said no, he did not see him--the pro-
secutor had previously come to our station that same day, and given a
description of the person who robbed him-he described him as a person
about twenty years of age, rather short, and stout-he was asked whether
he was pock-marked or not-he said at first he was not sure, but after
ttat he said he was pock-marked, and that he was dressed in a black coat
-I believe he said he was of a light complexion, but I cannot be sure-I
do not believe he said anything about his eyes I searched the prisoner,
and found 4s. 3d, on him.
