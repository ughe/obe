JOHNSON, Mayor.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Did you observe anything either said or done,
tween the man who first came in and the prisoner? A. The man who was
looking at the watches was playing with a guard-chain, and when the prisoner
was by him, I saw the man who was looking at the watches, put his hand
down, and just before his hand was on the glass case—he slipped his left
hand down behind him, as if to pass something to the prisoner—the prisoner
was just behind him.
COURT. Q. Was the prisoner standing behind him, so that anything
could be delivered to him by the other man ? A. Yes.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. How long did the prisoner stay after that action?
A. Not a minute—I do not think he took but one step to get out, and the
other man followed him—he went out directly, and said he would call again
in three weeks—when he was gone, I missed a gold watch, and a chain—I
alarmed my father.
COURT. Q. Was the watch and ehain that was missed the watch the first
man was handling, and the chain he was plaving with ? A. Yes.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. How soon afterward' did you see the prisoner again?
A. I think it was on the Monday, at the Mansion-house—he was not pointed
out to me—directly I saw him I knew him—I said that was the man.
Cross.examined. Q. Where were you standing? A. On the side of the
counter, with my father—I was further from the door than my father was—
the man who was looking at the watches was exactly opposite to me—my
father was serving him, but his attention was attracted by Miss Harding"
she was opposite my father—when the prisoner came in at the door, he wai
by the side of Miss Harding—she was in the middle, and then he
himself, and came past Miss Harding, and opposite to where I was—I was
looking all the while—I saw the man who was standing by the glass playing
with a chain, twisting it round his finger—I saw him put his hand down—I
did not see him remove the watch and chain—he had his handkerchief in hii
right hand—his left hand was playing with the chain, and his left hand be
put down—I was noticing he was playing with the chain with his left hand—
I did not see him take it off—I was taking such particular notice of the pri-
goner at that time—I noticed the other man had a pocket-handkerchief in one
hand, and a guard-chain in the other, and he put his left hand down towards
the prisoner—I noticed that he had been with that hand playing with the
guard just before—I did not notice that he took his hand off the case-4
was looking at the prisoner—the lady who was there was a customer—there
was nobody else in the shop.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was there anybody else came into the shop from
time your father produced the watches to the first man, till the
came? A. No one but Miss Harding—I missed the watch almost directly
after the first man went out.
COURT. Q. Have we taken you correctly, that your attention was in the
first instance particularly drawn to the first man, so as to notice that he
playing with the chain in his left hand ? A. Yes—after that my attention
was drawn to the prisoner, and after that I saw the left hand of the first man
pass behind him in a position, that if he had anything in his hand it might
be delivered to the prisoner.
