GEORGE FRANKLIN. I am a labourer at Deptford-on 8th August I was
in company with my young woman in Ploughbridge, Rotherhithen I saw
five foreigners come along, one or more of them interfered with my young
woman, after a bit of a scuffle I ran over the bridge-while I was standing
there I saw Miller and Pressland on the bridge with three foreigners, the
prisoner was one of them-I did not see him do anything—I saw Miller
after he had been home and come back again-I did not see anything the
matter with his hand or arm-about half an hour afterwards I saw his
father-in-law take him to the doctor's; he had his arm bound up,
THOMAS BANNISTER (Police Inspector R). Shortly after 1 o'clock in the
morning.of 9th August I saw Miller at the station-he showed me his hand
and arm--there was a wound on the left arm above the elbow, and one on
the back of the left hand, both bleeding-I sent for the divisional surgeon,
who dressed his wounds—he gave a description of the person who had
wounded him, in consequence of which I went in search of the prisoner-I
met him coming along with a constable-he answered the description given
-he was placed with several others in the charge room, seven or eight,
some of his own countrymen, and Miller picked him out at once-he was
charged with cutting and wounding Miller-I afterwards went to Plough-
bridge and saw a large quantity of blood on the pavement from there down
to the entrance of the docks, about 300 yards--that was not in the direction
in which Miller went, but the direction in which the other Italians had
gone—the prisoner had no hat on when I met him-I saw the other man
who was wounded at the hospital.
Cross examined. I found a few pieces of broken pipe on the bridge
man named Calose was charged with being concerned in this--he was subse-
quently discharged
BENJAMIN BROWNING, M.R.C.S. I live at Rotherhithe-I am divisional
surgeon to the police on 9th August, between 1 and 2 in the morning, I
was called to the Rotherhithe Police-station to examino Miller he was
bleeding from two incised wounds, one on the left knuckle of the little
finger, the other at the back of the same arm, below the shoulder-that was
nearly four inches long; it was not very deep; it had exposed the muscle
both wounds were bleeding very much I dressed them-he was porfectly
sober.

Cross-examined. I saw a knife afterwards at the police-court-such a
knife as this (produced) would produce the wounds, but it would also
produce much more severe wounds-from such a formidable weapon as this
I should expect to find more severe wounds-I never saw a foreign sailor
carry such a knife as this.
CHARLES ELLIOT NASH. I am a deal porter in the Surrey Commercial
Docks--I found this knife and this dagger there when I was at work on the
morning of 9th August--the dagger was sticking in a deal, and the sheath
was lying in another place—there were marks on the dagger the colour of
blood-it appeared as if it had been thrown over the fence, which is nine or
ten feet high.
