REUBEN ALFRED Gray, husband of last witness. On May 17 I re-
turned home about 11 p.m. Going along the passage, I heard a row
going on in the downstairs front room. I went to bed. About
quarter to 12 my wife woke me up. I went downstairs, where I saw
Mrs. Cook in flames; also I saw a lamp in flames on the floor near
the bedroom window. I beat out the flames. Then I went to pri-
soner's bedroom; my wife holloaed out, and there was no answer, and
I broke open the door. Prisoner was in bed; he appeared to be just
woke up as we got to the bed. I did not hear what he said.
Cross-examined. Prisoner is a very sound sleeper; he usually
sleeps with his bedroom door locked and generally with a lamp burn-
ing. I have no doubt he was ssleep when we tried to get into his
room. He and his mother lived happily together, except for rowing
when they were not sober.
Mrs. DALLWYN. I lived at 934, Acacia Road, just opposite No 60.
I have known prisoner and his mother many years. On the night of
May 17 I heard screams from No. 60 and on going out I saw Mrs.
Cook with her clothes all alight. I helped to beat out the flames;
then I took her to Mrs. Biggs's house, and I stayed there till the
old lady was taken away in the ambulance. While I was there 8
constable same in with the prieoner Mrs. Cook said, Take him
away; he set light to me." Prisoner said, "I did not do it; you
be better in the morning." Later on I heard prisoner say, It
was a good job if she was dead," and he broke out laughing. I told
him not to laughit was a solemn thing; it was a death chamber.
Cross-examined. I have a pretty good memory. I was not
muddled when I gave evidence before the magistrate. There I did
say first of all that it was at the gate that prisoner said, "A good job
if she was dead." I contradicted it immediately; he said that at
Mrs. Biggs's. E also said at the police court chat Mrs. Cook said
"My son has thrown a lamp at me and set me alight; lock him up.
That is right. She kept on saying, Take him away." Prisoner was
laughing in a moeking, sneering manner; I never heard such a
laugh. When he said, A good job if you was dead," a constable was
standing near him. I do not know whether the constable eould have
heard it ; there was a great noise inl the room.
Mrs. BIGGs, 58, Acacia Road. Mrs. Cook was brought to my
house by last witness aPH U attended to her. When prisoner was
brought in by the constable I said, Georgey come and look at what
