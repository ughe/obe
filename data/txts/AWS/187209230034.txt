MARIA BLANKS. I am a nurse at the receiving ward at Bethnal-Gree
Workhouse-on 20th August, the child was brought there by Mrs. Gibbe
accompanied by the male prisoner; it was very thin and emaciated-
immediately called the doctor's attention to it, and be ordered it wine, beet
tea, and arrowroot, which I fed it with all night-I weighed the child;
was 171 lbs.-it afterwards went out of my charge into the infirmary.
ELIABETH BOOTLE. I am a nurse in the infirmary-the child was placed
under my care on the 21st-it was very thin and emaciated, and appeared
to be dying- gave it some warm milk at first, and it seemed to take it very
greedily, as if it were hungry-I gave it a little rice boiled in milkit ate
that as if it wished it, and it would have eaten more if I had given it-it
had nothing else the matter with it then, but about an hour after it W8S
taken with relaxation of the bowels-it was not able to retain the food it
took it threw it up once or twice, but it was more relaxation than sickness
-it lived until the 3lst, and then died-it did not improve after the first
two or three days-it never rallied-when I received it it was dressed in
night-gown, it came in that from the receiving ward.

EDMUND JoHN ADAMS. I am medical officer at Bethnal Green Work.
house-on 20th August I received the child it was very much emaciated
and looked very ill indeed-I treated it; it died on 31st-I heard the
prisoner say when it was admitted that it was two years and a-half old;
but I afterwards came to the conclusion that it was four years old; I judged
by the teeth it weighed 171 lbs.-if it were four years old it should weigh
from 33 lbs. to 39 lbs.-the immediate cause of death was diarrhcea-In
a post-mortem examination-in my judgment the diarrhcea was caused by
improper and insufficient food, and exposure to the outer air-I can hardly
say improper food; I would rather say insufficient in quantity- mesenterio
glands were not diseased-th weather was not very cold at the time-the
child was fairly clothed it had no flannels on I ordered it flannels directly
it was admitted- the probabilities are it would have lived if it had bad
proper treatment ; there was no disease to cause death when a child is in
a weakly state the being taken into the open air and kept there for some
time would accelerate its death.
