Certainly--the business we do with Bult's is notes and coin and a few
bars--it was quite an extraordinary thing to send two or three bars to this
extent at one time to Bult's when I returned from Messrs. Bult's, on 2nd
of April, Mrs. Abrahams was in the workshop-Mr. Solomon put the gold
in in my presence-not in both the crucibles, only in one I saw that it
was gold dust- I had heard of the loss I did not suspect what it was at
first-I did not suspect till after it was all brought, when I saw the
quantity-I did not know what weight there was I could see it was large
bars--I could tell there was more than thirty ounces when I saw it con-
tinually coming—when I saw there was such a quantity I suspected it was
the gold that was lost, when I saw it come so many times and there was
such a large bulk--I had no suspicion when I saw Mrs. Abrahams with the
one hundred and ten ounces- I thought it was the ordinary course of bu-
siness--I had never seen Mrs. Abrahams before I staid in the workshop
all the day from time to time when she came I saw her come from time
to time-I went out of the workshop after it was all done it might be
about six or half-past six o'clock--Mr. Solomon was with me all the after-
noon-he was in and out of the workshop all the afternoon-he was not in
the workshop all the afternoon- I do not recollect seeing Isaac in the work-
shop-I saw him in the front shop-he generally attended in the front shop,
but he went into the city, and Mr. Solomon was obliged to attend when he
was in the city.
Q. How often was Mr. Solomon in the workshop with you? A.
Every time Mrs. Abrahams came Mr. Solomon came into the work-
shop and put the gold into the crucible-he did not always do
that-he did that afternoon-I never melted any thing of the kind
before-I never melted gold dust-I never melt any thing but for
respectable dealers whom I know-when I saw Moses in the shop,
he was standing with his back against the wainscot, looking across
the counter-I was at the furnace at the time-there was a crucible in the
fire, but I was not looking in the furnace I was looking towards the door
-it was my business to attend to the furnace at that time--I had seen
him once before-he was not known to Mr. Solomon-he came there with
a bottle of spirits once-Mr. Solomon did not deal with him that I know
of-I do not think I saw him in the shop more than half a minute or a
minute at the outside- I do not mean when he came with the spirits, but
when he came on the Tuesday-there were two doors between me and
where he was standing—there is a door to the workshop, and between the
workshop and passage there is a little parlour—there is a door to that par.
lour-there were three doors between us, but neither of them were shut-
there were two doors, and one besides—if I had told you there was only
one and there were two, I should have said false 1 did not remember at
the moment-we leave all the doors open--that was the usual course, except
when the chimneys smoked, and then we closed them-I should think it is
nearly 90 feet from the further end of the workshop to the door in the
Strand-you can see the door in the Strand from the furnace-I swear that
-a person in the Strand could not see wbat was going on at the furnace,
for the furnace is not so high as we are, but they could see the furnace
alight-the shop-door leading from the street is generally kept shut-the
back parlour is not used for any thing particular -any lumber is put in it
--there is a counter there, on which we brush clothes or so sometimes-I
never knew Mr. Solomon take a person in there for private conversation-
I do not recollect that there is any fire in the workshop, except the furnaces
