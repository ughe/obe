 1732.  GEORGE HENRY CLEMENT was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , 1 coat, value 2l.; 1 hat, value 5s.; 1 pair of breeches, value 10s.; 1 waistcoat,5s.; 1 gold pin, value 1s.; 3, handkerchiefs, value 5s.; and 2 towels, value 6d., the goods of John Evison , in the dwelling-house of Thomas Young.
 JOHN EVEION. I lodge at Thomas Young's, in  Duke-court, St. Martin's-lane. I lost this property on Saturday morning, the 22d of July they were locked up in my box at nine o'clock that morning, and I had the key - the coat cost me four guineas a month or two before; I had not worn it six times.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Is your landlord here? A. No - his name is Thomas Young; he never told me so - I only know that is the name he goes by; I will swear he mentioned Thomas Young as his name - I know the prisoner, and frequently drank with him.
   JAMES HAYWARD. I am a boot-closer. On Saturday night, the 22d of July, I called on Mrs. Young - I do not know her husband's Christian name - I went up stairs, by her desire, and found the prisoner in the second floor room, where Evison slept; he was walking from the cupboard, with the hat in one hand, and the bundle in the other - I asked him what brought him there - he said Evison let him in to sleep with him, as it was a wet night - I was rather rash with him, and Mrs. Young desired me not to be so violent - I went with him to the public-house where Evison was - I found Evison was gone - the prisoner then went away with the property.
   WILLIAM WILCOX. I am shopman to Messrs. Gray, pawnbrokers, Fleet-street. The prisoner pawned this suit of clothes with me, on the 24th of July, in the name of Jones, for 25s. - I am certain of his person.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long was he in the shop? A. Ten minutes - I think 25s. a fair price to advance.
   SAMUEL LACK. I am an officer. I took the prisoner at a public-house; I found this handkerchief on his neck; this towel in his pocket, and a key which opens the street-door of the house, a piece of wax-candle, four duplicates, a hat, a towel, handkerchief, and gold pin, were brought to him by a young woman while I was there - I have had them ever since; I found a sovereign and 5s. on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Six witnesses gave the prisoner a good character. GUILTY. Aged 22. Of stealing to the value of 39s. only. Confined Twelve Months.
 1734.  JOSEPH HARDING was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of June , 4 shirts, value 2l., and 2 shirt collars, value 2s., the goods of James Lovell , in his dwelling-house.
   MARY LOVELL. I am the wife of James Lovell - we live at Islington- I am a laundress. On the 21st of June I lost four shirts and two collars - they were quite new - they were valued to me to pay 2l. for them - I had brought them in a bundle from town to wash, and put them down in the middle of my room: between nine and ten o'clock I took one parcel out to wash, and when I had washed them, I returned back for this parcel about twelve - they were then gone.
   JOHN OAKLY. I am a brush-maker, and live at Hoxton. I was at Winkworth-place on the 21st of June, and saw the prisoner with two other persons - he had some linen in his apron; I got a constable - he said it was his own linen, and he was going to take it to wash; he struggled, and got away - his hat fell off, and there was one shirt in his hat, and two in his apron - I gave them to the officer; one of the others threw away a sheet.
   GEORGE WILMOT. I am a headborough - the prisoner was given into my custody with these shirts; I asked where he got them - he said they were his own, and he was going to get them washed; as we were going to the watch-house, he said he had found them; I found two collars in his pocket.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he had found the articles in a brick-field.
 One Witness gave him a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1734.  THOMAS LEATHER was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of July , 1 watch, value 50s., the goods of William Singleton , in the dwelling-house of James Blake.
 WILLIAM SINGLETON. I  keep a shop, at Mr. James Blakes, in Grafton-street. On the night of the 3d of August I went into the yard about seven o'clock, and on my return I missed a watch from hook, which I had seen not five minutes before - it cost me between 2l. 10s. and 3l. about a month before; I had made an exchange with a person, who is not here, and it was never valued at that - I have not seen it since.
 JAMES BLAKE. On the 3d of August, at ten minutes past seven o'clock, I passed by Singleton's shop door, and saw the prisoner run out, about a yard before me - a boy called Stop thief! - finding a watch was missing, I followed calling Stop thief! - I lost sight of him while he turned the corner - he was secured.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Will you swear he was above a minute in your sight? A. I cannot say.
   JOSEPH BULLOCK. I saw some people running, and crying Stop thief! - I saw the prisoner, and ran up Bateman's-buildings, and into Rose-street; he turned into a watch-maker's shop, where I secured him.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see him searched? A. I searched him, but found nothing - I saw nothing thrown away.
   ALFRED SINGLETON. I serve in my brother's shop. On this evening the prisoner and another person came in, and asked for some tobacco - the prisoner said, "Dick, we shall want two pipes;" he immediately reached over the counter, took the watch, and ran out; Blake asked me what he had taken - I told him - the prisoner was secured afterwards; I had seen him before, walking about the street; the watch has not been found.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you never said that the other person came in first? A. Yes - he did; I was serving him with tobacco when the prisoner came in - he was about a minute in the shop.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
