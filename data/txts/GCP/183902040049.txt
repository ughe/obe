JAMES WILCOCK. I live in Little York-place, New-road, and am a shoe-
maker. About ten o'clock at night on the 1st of January, I heard a large
table knocked over in the cellar, and a dish broken-I went down with a
lodger of mine, and found the prisoner behind a flour-bin-he bad with
him a shift of Mrs. Morgan's--I asked where the other things were, and
he produced this bundle from behind the bin-it contained these other ar-
ticles--they had been hung in the cellar to dry-I had seen them safe in
the day.
CHARLOTTE MORGAN. I lodge in the prosecutor's house. I followed
him down into the cellar, and saw my shift brought from under the pri-

in the bundle.

599. WILLIAM MINTO was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of
January, 8 plates, value 28. 4d.; and 2 tea-pots, value Is. 5d., the goods
of Elizabeth Stevens.
GEORGE FREDERICK DUCKETT. I live in High-street, Marylebone, with
my aunt, Elizabeth Stevens, who keeps a chandler's shop. On the 10th
of January, about six o'clock at night, I heard a noise outside- I went to
the door, and missed eight soup-plates and two tea-pots--I found a
foreign boy at the door, who pointed to where the things had been taken
from, and beckoned me to follow him I did so, and saw the prisoner in
Great Marylebone-street, and found the plates on him-I took him back,
and met the policeman- I gave the prisoner into custody--these are my
aunt's plates-the tea-pots have never been found.
GUILTY. Aged 15.-Confined One Month.c h
600. JAMES PATMORE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
January, 7 handkerchiefs, value 12s., the goods of Thomas Vesper and
another.
GEORGE MARKHAM ( police-constable K 96.) On the 5th of January, a
httle after eight o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner near some hand-
kerchiefs, which were outside Mr. Vesper's shop, in the Commercial-road-
he drew some handkerchiefs out of the bundle I went and asked what be
had in his pocket-he said, “Nothing"-I said, "Let me feel "-he then
said, “Only some handkerchiefs I have just picked up "-I took these
seven handkerchiefs from his pocket.
ROBERT MORRILL. I am in the service of Thomas and William Vesper,
pawnbrokers. These handkerchiefs are my masters'.
Prisoner's Defence. I saw a boy throw down these handkerchiefs-1
took them up, and ran after him-I could not find him, and put them
into my pocket.
