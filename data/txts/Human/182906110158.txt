 JOSEPH BURGESS. I am a bookbinder, and work at present with Mr. List, No. 4, Prujean-square. On Tuesday evening, the 5th of May, I saw the prisoner - I knew him before; I was sitting in the house of call, the Swan and Sugar Loaf public-house, Fetter-lane - a person came in and asked me whether I would carry a box; that person went by the name of Rushlight Tom, but I have since found his name is Thomas Gall; he asked me at first if I would earn a shilling, and said it was to carry a box for a gentleman - I immediately went out with him down to the bottom of Bartlett's-buildings, and saw Jones standing with the box; it was the best part of a quarter of a mile from Greville-street - the box was as Bird has described it; his is an accurate description, according to the best of my memory, both as to the size, appearance, and every thing: I asked Tom if that was the box, and the prisoner said Yes; I asked Jones whether it was all right, he said Yes. and told me to take it into Chancery-lane; he preceded me - I went into Cursitor-street; he was a few paces before, and went up White's-alley; I took the box to a house in Kingsbury-place, White's-alley - it was Tom's house: I suppose Tom had been walking behind me, as I did not see him till I got to the house: I took the box to the foot of the stairs, and was helped up by one of them up to the second floor - they were both there; when I got up to the second floor, the prisoner Jones paid me 5d., and stated that he would send me the remainder, which was sent; I ought to have had 7d., but 6d. came down in the course of the evening.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you observe the box particularly? A. I was not many minutes with it, so that I did not particularly notice it; I do not know what has become of Tom - I knew him by his frequenting a house I was obliged to wait at while out of employ, as it is a house of call; I did not know his house, but I saw him go into it, and heard afterwards that it it was his - I did not know it before; I cannot say he had not placed the trunk in the prisoner's care while he fetched me.
   MARY ANN DAVIE. I live at No. 7, Hole-in-the-Wallpassage; the prisoner Jones lived at No. 1 - he occupied the house. On the Tuesday evening in question, about a quarter before seven o'clock, I saw the prisoner coming through the court as I was waiting at my door - he passed close to me, and had a bundle in his arm on his hip; it was a large bundle tied up very tight, but I could see the soles of shoes through the cloth, it being tied so tight - I am in the habit of binding shoes; at seven o'clock I heard of the robbery, and gave information to Mr. Bird - I have seen Rushlight Tom at Jones' frequently; they were well acquainted - I saw Rushlight Tom at Mrs. Jones' house next morning, but the prisoner was then in custody; they were conversing together - I saw them at the window, but did not overhear any thing then; Sophia Hawkins was there: I have since seen her, and Jones' wife together.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you quarrelled with Mrs. Jones? A. She brought a parcel of low game to kill me nearly - the day after the robbery; I did not strike her - I went and fetched an officer; I did not swear at the first examination that I could not form an opinion what was in the bundle.
   SARAH IVES. I live at No. 8, Bream's-buildings, Kingsbury-place, and am the wife of William Ives. I know Rushlight Tom; he and Hawkins came to lodge with us a little before last Christmas - I saw a trunk brought there on the 5th of May, between six and seven o'clock in the evening; it was brought to my house - I did not see it go up stairs; it was taken out of my house by two men the same evening - one was a very tall man, and the other a short man; it was between six and seven o'clock - it was a dark coloured trunk, with nails in it; I took very little notice of it, and do not know whether it was covered with leather.
   MARY MORANT. I live in the same house as Ives. I remember a trunk coming in; it was carried out about five minutes after it was brought in - Rushlight Tom has absconded.
   ROBERT DOWNES. I deal in shoes, and live at No. 4, Portpool-lane; the prisoner came to my shop between six and seven o'clock on the 5th of May, with two pairs of shoes, and asked me to purchase them; I took them out of his hand, and told him they would not suit me - he said he would sell them cheap; I said No, they would not suit me - he said could I tell him where he could sell them, for he could put them in at a good bargain, them and a hundred pairs more - I said I had no money to buy them.
 Cross-examined. Q. Are you not very deaf? A. I am, and was deaf that day, but he made me hear what he said - when a man speaks in my ear I can hear better than when he is at a distance, and sometimes I hear better than at others; he did not say he wanted to know the price as a person had offered to sell him some; I have made no mistake - he asked me to buy them; my wife stood outside the door - whether she heard him I do not know; she asked me what he came about when he was gone; my shop is about ten feet wide.
   MARIA BROWN. My husband deals in marine-stores -I manage the business, as he is a drayman; I know Jones. On the 5th of May he came in, my boy called me into the shop; Jones asked if my brother-in-law was there - I said No; he said he had some shoes which he thought would suit him as he is a shoemaker - I said he was not within; he said, "Well, will you purchase them;" I said No; I saw some in his hand, they appeared like men's shoes.
   ROBERT DUKE. I am a constable. I apprehended Jones on the 5th of May, between eight and nine o'clock at night; I found him in the public-house in Brookes-market; I had seen the thirteen pairs of shoes found at his house, and I found two pairs more there; when I apprehended him I called him into a parlour - he asked what I wanted; I told him I had come about the shoes we had found at his house - he asked, "What shoes?" I told him he knew very well what shoes - he said he did not, for he had not been at home since four o'clock that afternoon, and if I had found any, somebody else must have left them there, for he knew nothing about them.
