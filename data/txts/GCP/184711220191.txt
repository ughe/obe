Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Whether Loup had advanced
money on them or not, you did not know, but he was interested in their dis-
position ? A. Yes--I have known him about twenty-seven years he was
formerly principal clerk to Tait and Co., Manchester warehousemen, of 8, Ola
Jewry, for two, three, or four years, till Mr. Tait died - he was then in busi-
ness for two or three years, in Cateaton-street--the firm was Loup, Godby,
and Co.--they became bankrupts, or something of the sort, in 1840-he was
afterwards in the house of Pearman and Co., of Glasgow, and managed a
house of theirs at 8, Old Jewry, for about eighteen months, up to just before
last March-I have known him down to the time of this change-I never
knew or heard of his being charged with dishonesty-as far as I knew he bore
s respectable, upright character-he bas latterly been a commission-agent,
selling for any persons who employed him-he was so up to the time of this
charge.

FREDERICK TUORER. I went to Loup and told him I had heard that he
had some tools for sale-be went with me to Johnson and Day's, and I saw
some tools there-I asked where he had got them from he did not say, but
said be merely had them for sale-I asked him once or twice where they
came from, and he evaded giving an answer every time.
Cross-examined by Ms. CLARKSON. Q. I suppose by your knowing where
to find him, you knew where he lived and carried on business? 2. Not be-
fore that day—I was referred to him by a Mr. Buck, over the water-I am a
tool-maker-Loup came and bought a few tools of me a week or so after this
--he paid for them-it was a trilling transaction-I knew these tools to be
my father's make directly I saw them, and they had my father's name on
them--my father is a plane-maker-I went to look at them, understanding
they were for sale--they were readily sold to me.
Mr. Bodkin. Q. Where was it you bad the conversation with him ? A.
Going from his house to Johnson and Day's- I did not tell him who I was
I did not ask him who was the maker of the tools.

CHARLES PROCTOR. I have been in Mr. Loup's employ as porter, on and
off, for four or five months, for four or five days a week-he told me in Aug.
he had some tools to sell, and said they were at Johnson and Day's-he
wanted to know if I knew anybody who would purchase them I was living
in the Waterloo-road-I said there was a person named Buck living close by,
and I would call and leave the invoice with him as I went home-he had
given me an invoice, which I left at Mr. Bucks, at the corner of Oakley-street,
Waterloo-road-I saw his son, and told him the tools were for sale, that was in
pursuance of directions given me by Loup--I said I hoped he would come to
look at them as soon as possible, as there was a Spanish gentleman about to
look at the next day--next day I went to Aldermanbury, to Loup's office,
saw him, and told him what had passed-I used to go there every day-I
never saw the other defendants, except at the Mansion-house-Seager I
never saw till yesterday. 70
