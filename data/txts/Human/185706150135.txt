 754. WILLIAM SCAINES was again  indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling house of Elizabeth Coate , and stealing 1 flute and case, value 3 l  .; the goods of Alfred William Ray .
   MR. SLEIGH . conducted the Prosecution. ELIZABETH GROVES. I am servant to Mrs. Coate, No. 7, Burton Crescent On the morning after 3rd April I came down, and found the parlour window open, and some things had been taken out of the house—I had on the pre vious night fastened the doors and window—I missed, amongst other things, a flute and a case, which I had seen safe the night before. SAMUEL COPPIN. ( Police sergeant, P  15). On 8th May, I took the prisoner into custody—I found this flute and case standing on a table in his bar—I asked him how he accounted for this—he said he did not know anything about it.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE . Q.  Did he say he did not know it was there? A.  I believe he said afterwards he did not know how it came there—this was not in the common bar which was accessible to all, only to his own family; it was shut off from the public—I do not know whether this is an old flute; the gentleman valued it at 10 l . I think—the key was given to me some days afterwards by the prosecutrix; it was not in the public bar—there is a little door which separates that from the public bar—it was in a place where the cupboard and the table were, it was entirely a private part of the establishment—it had nothing to do with the public—no per son would go there, only the family—the prisoner owned the case—I asked him if he had the key, and he said, "No." ELIZABETH COATE. I live with my mother at No. 7, Burton Crescent This flute is the property of Mr. Alfred William Ray, who resides in the house—I saw it safe on the 3rd April. NOT GUILTY.
