   GEORGE GROVE. I am a licensed victualler, and live in Shoreditch. I have known the prisoner from five to six months, by his coming to my house - he is a labourer and a watchman at the Excise-office. Between nine and ten o'clock one evening, he came to me, and brought twelve half-crowns, and said "Mr. Grove, I wish you would take care of these for me;" "Very well (said I) do you know how many there are?" he said "Never mind, I will be back in ten minutes:" when he came back I said "Do you know how many there are?" "Not exactly" said he; said I there is 30s.: I cannot form any judgment when this was- I think I heard of this woman being robbed the next morning, but there was no inquiry about the half-crowns for several days; I had never received any money from him before - the prosecutrix lives about half a mile from me; I do not know what the prisoner's wages are - I have heard from him that he is paid every half quarter, but I do not know.
   DANIEL RIERDON. I am a Police-constable of Bow-street. I have two gowns, a petticoat, and a cloak, which I got from the prosecutrix; I believe on the 22d of July, at her house - I was sent down there, with Brown from Worship-street, in consequence of the robbery; I broke into a room, which was said to be the prisoner's, and there I saw two little children, seven or eight years old; one of them began to cry, and the other said something, but the prisoner was not present - I then went up to the prosecutrix's room, and got these clothes; I saw some marks on her door and on the door-post, but did not look at her box- this was between seven and eight o'clock in the evening.
   THOMAS BROWN. I am a Police-officer of Worship-street. I went to the house, with Rierdon, on the night of the 22d of July; we broke open the lower room door - there was nothing found there: Rierdon went up stairs, but I staid about the door, in case the prisoner should be concealed there - I went again the next day, and found this screw-driver in the prisoner's room, and this old trowel; the prosecutrix's son was with me at that time - I have tried this trowel and screw-driver with the impression on the door and door-post of the prosecutrix's room, and they correspond exactly; I found a small trunk in the room, and this trowel exactly corresponded with the impression on the front of it - the prisoner was taken on the night of the 22d, and delivered to me on the 23d; I believe he was taken about ten o'clock that night, but I was not present - I did not see these things in the room the first night, but the room was in a shocking state with filth and rubbish.
 FRANCES CUSHION re-examined. These things are all mine; these are my gowns and cleak - I gave them to the officer; he asked me for them - he asked if these were the things that were taken; I said they were - I am sure they were in my room when I went out to carry my work home- they were in the box, and the box was locked; I saw them in the box when I went to put on my things to go to the warehouse - I went to the Police-office the next day; I could not just then tell how much money I lost, but I look upon it I had about 2l. very near, but in my fright I could not tell exactly what I had - I had at the rate of 2l., but I am no scholar and cannot reckon.
   THOMAS BROWN re-examined. Q. How did you get the information that night? A. Her son and daughter came and told us.
   GEORGE GROVE re-examined. Q. How soon after did you hear of the prisoner's being in custody? A. I have a letter which I received from him, (I believe about a week after I received the money,) saying that he had been taken up, and desired me to send him 10s. by his friend Broadhurst, which I did, and here are the other half-crowns - I believe he has two children.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was at work at the Londondocks on the 22d of July - about half-past six o'clock I came home; I had not been in the house a minute when the prosecutrix came down, and said "I have been robbed" I said "God bless me, who could rob you:" "O dear," said she, "I am robbed" - "Let us go up stairs and see," said I; we went up, and I said "God bless me, you have been robbed," but I did not notice the door - we came down to the middle room, where there are two old women- I asked them if they saw any man go up stairs, they said No - I said "It is very odd you sit here, close to the door, and not hear anybody go up stairs:" my little girl looked out at my room door and said "Father, there has been three men up stairs," and I said to the old ladies, in the middle room, "There, don't you hear what Hannah says, that there has been three men here" - she said she did not hear it; I came down, and said to my girl "Go and fetch West, the officer, and I will have my room searched"- as soon as she was gone, my youngest daughter, who is six years old, said to me "Father, Hannah has brought down a red cloak, and it is under the bed;" I said "Get it out," she brought out the cloak and the gowns from under the bed, and gave them to Mrs. Cushion; and then she said she wanted some money; I asked what money - she said she had some money in a leather bag in a box, but that I never saw; I said "Don't make yourself uneasy, my pay-day is coming, and I will pay you every farthing she has taken from you."
 COURT to FRANCES CUSHION. Q. Did you point out the prisoner's room to the officer? A. Yes - that was the room he lived in; the little girl did not bring in any cloak while I was there - she took it from under the bed.
