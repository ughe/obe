2631. ANN MARIA EDGSON was indicted for embezzlement. FRANCES BURCHER. I am the wife of George Burcher. The prisoner was in our service, to take milk out, and receive money, which she was to pay to me—I spoke to her about some money received from Mrs. Luxmore—she said it was not paid—she asked me for the bill for Mrs. Web ster—I made it out, and sent it, when the prisoner came back, I asked if she had paid it, and she said she had not—I have not received it. ELIZABETH MANN. I live with Mrs. Luxmore—I paid the prisoner 2 s ., 4 1/2 d ., on the 21st of September, and 4 d . on the 29th. SARAH WEBSTER On the 27th of September I paid the prisoner 3 s . 2 d . for her mistress—I have the bill here.
   Prisoner.  I am very sorry; I did it to pay some bills for myself; I meant to pay again. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Three Months.
 2632. MARY ANN WHITBREAD was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of October , 1 gown, value 10 s  .; 1 bonnet, value 5 s  .; 1 cap, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; and 2 handkerchiefs, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of Joseph Whit-bread . JOSEPH WHITBREAD. I am a gas-fitter—the prisoner is my daughter; I have a second wife. On the 1st of October she gave me information, I did not know where the prisoner was—I then found her in Hyde-park, look ing at the soldiers, and she had these clothes on. HANNAH WHITBREAD. I am the wife of Joseph Whitbread. On the 1st of this month I went out to work, and locked the prisoner in the room—I returned at night, and found every drawer and cupboard broken—the prisoner and these things were gone.
   Prisoner.  She has locked me in a great many times, and beat me dreadfully. NOT GUILTY
 2633. WILLIAM PRATT was indicted for stealing, on the   12th of October , 2lbs. weight of leather, value 8 s  . the goods of Edward Taylor , his master. EDWARD TAYLOR. I keep a bootmaker's shop in Lamb's Conduit-street—the prisoner was in my service. On the 12th of October I lost these pieces of leather—here is a private mark on some of them—I spoke to the prisoner about it—he said, "It is the first time, pray forgive me."
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Was the policeman present when he said that? A.  Yes—the prisoner was with me about four years—he has a family. JAMES DUNN. I keep a shoemaker's shop in Well-street, Oxford-street—the prisoner brought this leather to me on the evening of the 12th of October—I said I had not money to purchase it—I doubted whether he came by it honestly, for I knew this could not be cabbage  —I informed the policeman, and he came the next morning, when the prisoner brought the leather again.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you detain the leather? A.  No—he took it away, and brought it the next morning—the policeman took him with it. GUILTY. Aged 30.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Three Months.
