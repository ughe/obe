WILLIAM DEAN. I am a solicitor. I was concerned in the case of
Budd against Armistead, and I have seen the prisoner repeatedly during
the progress of this business-he gave me one receipt, signed, " W. Willis
-I paid him 101. on the 20th of April, 1833-1 did not take his receipt
-on the 29th of the same month, I paid him 121.
Cross-examined. Q. On whose account did you pay it? A. On behalf
of the defendant-the case was then in progress-we had agreed to give a
cognovit-the 10l. was paid on account of it, and the 121. a week after.
wards-it was agreed to be 221. in all-the cognovit was executed at the
same moment as I paid him the 101.
John Woop. I keep the Bag of Nails public-house, Pimlico. In
August, 1833, I owed Mr. Willis some money for costs--the prisoner
called, and I paid him 101.— I have his receipt.
Cross-examined by Mr. Bopkin. Q. I believe you have been a good
deal acquainted with the prisoner? A. Yes: I was introduced to Mr.
Willis's office by him-he left Mr. Willis about three years ago, to the
best of my recollection-I went with Willis to prevail on the prisoner to
return-Mr. Willis said that Mr. Millet should not be the loser of the
money he was bolding of Mrs. White's-it was in consequence of that he
returned.
Court to J. R. Dudley. Q. Was the prisoner in the habit of making dis-
bursements between 1831 and 1833 ? A. He was they must have con-
siderably exceeded 301, or 401. ; I should say it was some hundreds-he was
intrusted to pay such things as might be required out of any funds in his
possession ; fees, or any thing of that kind.
(The prisoner, in a long address to the Court, stated, that he had the
exclusive management of the prosecutor's business for eleven years, during
which he received and paid various sums; the account of his disbursements
were on slips of paper, which were in the prosecutor's office, and he
could not get access to them; that he was engaged as "managing
agent," and was to have half the profits of the business he procured;
and that the prosecutor had promised him 2641., arising from the will
of Mrs. White, none of which he had received ; and that his brother
had discounted various acceptances of the prosecutor's; some of which
were dishonoured.)
WILLIAM Willis re-examined. Q. How came you to allow the dis-
bursements to remain open ? A. He would not make them up-I have not
here the means of showing that he did not expend a larger sum than be
received
