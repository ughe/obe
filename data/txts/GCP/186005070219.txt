ELIZABETH GRAY. I live in Pomeroy-street, Hatcham-the prisoner has
lodged with me since a week before last Christmas-I knew nothing of him
before further than seeing him pass to go to work-his character has been
very good-he has been a sober industrious young man-I never saw this

ELIZABETH COMIS. On 11th April I had been in the Bath tavern with a
young woman, a friend of mine, having gin and peppermint--the prisoner
was at the private bar, but I did not see him at first-he had this young
man with him (Barraclough)-as I was coming out the prisoner was at the
door with a glass of ale in his hand, and asked us to drink it; we refused-
his friend caught hold of my friend and the prisoner caught hold of me by
the arm, and pushed me back in at the private entrance, and asked us both
what we would have to drink; we said that we had had quite enough-my
friend said, "If we haye anything we will have some gin and peppermint"
we had some and called for two biscuitg—and then the young men went
behind the door with my friend, talking to her—I was standing by myself,
and went out to see the time, I found it was twenty-five minutes to 10-1
said, “Come, Sarah, it is time we got home, your mother will wonder where
we are" she came running out and the prisoner got hold of her in a shock-
ing manner-I said, “You, Jeave her alone, and let her come on - he
instantly caught hold of me behind and got my head partly doubled to my
feet-I said, "You good for nothing creature, you had better let me alone"
- he said, “Come back and have some drink"-I tried to get away, but
could not, and he struck ine on the jaw and dug his nail into my hand ;
here is the mark now-he went away in at the private entrance, and I went
in and stamped my foot and said that he should not strike me like that I
struck at him and he struck me two blows, one was over the eye, and I
instantly fell senseless the people ran round and got me in the parlour,
ordered the company to go out, and begged me not to let the prisoner go
away-the policeman brought some water, and my friend washed my
face—I sent my friend out to see if there was a policeman-she never
returned, and I found the two men walking away-I called, "Police,"
but no assistance came-I afterwards gave the prisoner in charge the
blows were struck violently with his full force ; here is blood on my shawl
now, but it has dried in.
Cross-examined by Mr. Lilley. Q. Is Sarah here ? A. No; I did not
beckon to the men, or speak to them at all-nor did Sarah in my presence
-We drank with them-I do not get my living in any other way besides

- he did not show the policeman the mark on his face, he said, "She struck

and over my eye-I mean to say that the blood trickled down from my nose
-I have not washed it since I had not passed half a dozen words with the
prisoner, and I gave him no cause I went with him to the station, and
heard that I might take out a summons- I went to take one out, but did
not, because there was a misunderstanding ; Mr. Morton did not under-
stand my words.
