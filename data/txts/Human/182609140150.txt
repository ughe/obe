 1701.  JOSEPH SCOTT was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , 7 yards of silk, value 1l., the goods of George Benjamin Ford , privately in his shop.
   JAMES ROBERT FIGG. I am shopman to George Benjamin Ford, a mans mercer, of Southampton-street, Covent-garden. On the 19th of July, at half-past seven o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came to the shop for some patterns of blue cloth - he then asked for some silk - I shewed him the pattern-book; he went away without buying any thing - I missed seven yards of silk which I had shewn him.
   JOHN KNOWLES. I am shopman to a pawnbroker in Drury-lane. The prisoner came to pawn the silk, about eight o'clock in the morning, of the 19th of July, for 26s.; I had received information, and detained him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. A friend sent me to pawn this, to make up his rent. GUILTY. Aged 23.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Year.
 1702.  JOHN SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , 1 watch-chain, value 6d., and 1 key, value 4s., the goods of Robert Coleberd , from his person.
 ROBERT COLEBERD. I am a  wine-merchant. On the 15th of September I was in Soho-square, at a quarter to one o'clock, a man ran up and took my watch-chain and two keys; I had a bottle of gin on my back - he snatched it under my arm; I cannot tell who he was; I called Stop thief! the officer pursued, and brought the prisoner back in two minutes; the person was about his size.
   JOHN WILLIAM BEAN. I am a silver chaser. I was in Swallow-street, going towards Soho-square, and heard a cry of Stop thief! I met the prisoner running, and stopped him; he used a violent oath, and put himself in a fighting attitude, and made a blow at me; I struck him again; he ran off; I pursued with others, and caught him in a court - he got from me again; I still pursued, and secured him.
   JOHN MASON. I am an officer. I was in Soho-square, and saw the prisoner and two others coming from Charles-street - the prisoner left the other two, and went into the road; I followed him till we met the prosecutor; the prisoner went up to him on the side on which the bottle was on his shoulder; he snatched at his watch, and ran off immediately; Coleberd called Stop thief! I pursued, and lost sight of him, as he turned into Sutton-street; I came up, and took him from Bean; I knew him before.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 1703.  JOHN TURNER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , 1 pair of shoes, value 3s., the goods of George Owen.
   ROBERT EVANS. Owen's shop is in Cross-street, opposite my father's. On the 15th of September I saw the prisoner pass the shop several times, about eleven o'clock in the morning - he at last went in; I saw him take a pair of shoes from the window; I ran out and took him - he gave them to me; he said he was in distress.
 GEORGE OWEN. I am a  shoe-maker. I was out at this time; the shoes are mine.
   WILLIAM WESTCOAT. I took him in charge. GUILTY. Aged 28.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury, being in distress. Confined Six Weeks.
 1704.  GEORGE TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , 10 sovereigns; 1 half-crown; 1 shilling; 1 sixpence, and 2d., the monies of Thomas Robinson , his master.
 THOMAS ROBINSON. I am a  tallow-chandler, and live in Drury-lane- the prisoner was my servant. On the 12th of August, about a quarter-past eleven o'clock, I sent him to the Excise-office to pay some duty, with ten sovereigns, four shillings, and two penny-pieces; he had the charge-paper with him; he never returned; I found him on board a ship in the river, and had him secured; I could not get a word out of him; the Captain said, in his presence, he had paid his passage with one sovereign and a shilling, for Yorkshire; he had eight sovereigns and 17s. 6d. in his pocket, which he gave up.
   WILLIAM WILSON. I am an officer, and took him in charge; he said he had paid the Captain 1l. 1s. of the money; I was obliged to handcuff him - he said he had changed one sovereign.
 Prisoner. I beg for mercy. GUILTY. Aged 17.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 1705.  THOMAS TAPP and HENRY DAVIS were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of September , 7 live tame fowls, price 14s.; 1 basket, value 6d.; 1 cage, value 6d.; 1 pair of shoes, value 2d., and 1 pair of boots, value 2d., the property of Thomas Nash.
 THOMAS NASH. I am a  labourer, and live in Copenhagen-fields. I kept my fowls in a shed; I lost them on the morning of the 16th of September - they were safe when I went to bed - in the morning, about a quarter to six o'clock, I found the shed open, and the fowls gone; I missed the other property from two different sheds in my yard; the feathers lay about - we traced them across the fields for a mile, to the New Kentish-town road - we there saw the prisoners who I had seen loitering about the premises the night before; they were walking about the hedges - this was a quarter-past six o'clock; Harmon, who was with me, secured them; I have received seven fowls, the boots, shoes, cage, and basket, back.
   EDWARD HARMON. I live near Nash, and went with him on this morning. I had seen the prisoners loitering about the place, the evening before, we found them, by the road; I secured them - they tried to escape; I said,"Where are the fowls?" Davis took the basket off his back, and said, "Here!" Tapp made no answer; there were five fowls in the basket, dead; Tapp had the boots; I delivered them to Nash.
   WILLIAM REYNOLDS. I am an officer, and took them in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 DAVIS's Defence. I met this man in the fields; he asked me to buy the basket.
 TAPP -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 DAVIS -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
