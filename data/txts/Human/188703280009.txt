The last witness gave the prisoner into my custody in the open street, about 200 yards from the shop, for passing a bad sixpence—she said, "If the sixpence is bad it was a mistake, I did not know it"—she said at the police-court that she received it from a man she met at Cambridge Heath Bridge, who gave her 1 s . 6 d ., and the sixpence was in her purse and 3/4 d . in bronze—she said, "That lady said I was in her shop a fortnight ago; I was not in her shop that day, but I was last Sunday."
   Cross-examined.  Mrs. Burroughs told me she believed the man was carrying the money and that you were passing it—you did not say that you were living with the man who gave you the 1 s . 6 d .—you said you were living in Broad Street at a common lodging-house.
   Re-examined.  She said nothing about the man being her husband—she said a man who she was living with. WILLIAM JOHN WEBSTER. These two coins are counterfeit and from the same mould.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was not in the shop on the 13th. I did not leave the house all day long of a lady who I wash for, but she is ill and cannot come. Here is a letter which I have received from her. I called at the shop for the eggs but am innocent of the coin being bad. GUILTY.— Judgment respited.
