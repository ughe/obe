and represented himself as the person—the Judge inquired the attorney's name—Humphreys said, "Mr. Delamere, of Lincoln's Inn-fields"—the Judge asked if he was Mr. Delamere—he said no, he was his clerk, that Mr. Delamere was generally to be found at the County Court, in Berner's-street, or at his chambers, 47, Lincoln's Inn-fields—the Judge then said, "This is a most extraordinary proceeding, that a gentleman should travel all the way from Bow-common; I should very much like to see the Bow-common Pheasant"—Humphreys said he was gone home—the Serjeant said, "No; he is not; you know where to find him; I dare say you could find him very soon if you liked"—he said, no; on his honour he was gone home—the Serjeant said that would not do, he must have a recognizance, or an affi  davit, or something of that kind—I then told the Serjeant that I knew Mr. Delamere perfectly well, and I did not really think he had lent himself to a thing of that kind—Humphreys said that Mr. Delamere had instructed him in the prosecution, or words to that effect—I then went to Mr. Delamere, saw him, and returned to the Court, where Humphreys had been detained in custody—I told the Court, in Humphreys' hearing that Mr. Delamere knew nothing whatever of the prosecution, that it was an abuse of his name, that he begged of me to convince the Court that he was no cognizant of it, and that he would have come himself, but that he had three or four cases on, and could no leave the Court, and that Humphreys never was his clerk—the Serjeant said that he should not allow process to issue, and he would teach him a lesson for treating the Court with contempt, and he ordered him to be locked up—it was on the bail being brought in, that Humphreys said the Pheasant had gone away.
   Humphreys. Q.  Do you know Mr. Delamere's handwriting? A. , No I do not think I do—I have known him for years, but I do not think I ever saw him write—( looking at a letter produced by Humphreys )—I do not know this to be his writing.
   Humphreys.  That letter would satisfy you that I am his clerk.
   COURT. Q.  I think he ought to be here—it concerns his own reputation—will you go for him? A.  No, I will not—I have had great deal of trouble in this matter, and am brought here very much against my inclination—I was called upon by one of the parties who were prosecuted to ask if I would reduce the circumstances I had stated at the Sessions, into an affidavit—I said I would not do so without seeing Mr. Delamere—I saw Mr. Delamere, and he then desired me to repeat that Humphreys had been acting without his sanction, and was not his clerk—I made an affidavit of that, and the parties got it, and subpoenaed me. HENRY CORNELIUS WRIGHT.
