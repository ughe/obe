But not quite? - I think I could.
 Look at it, and make yourself sure? - I never saw him write like it.
 Court. I observe, however, that you do not speak with the same certainty to that as to the other? - I am quite positive it is not the elder's hand-writing.
 Mr. Fielding. Is the younger Wood here?
 Prosecutor. He was obliged to step home; I told him I believed he might be spared for a few minutes.
 Mr. Fielding. Then do once more enquire at the door if he is there?
 Mr. Garrow. He says he is gone home; do not let us lose time.
 Wood. He cannot be gone above five or six minutes.
 Mr. Fielding. I have done, my Lord.
 Court. If young Wood was here, I should think it incumbent on Mr. Fielding to call him, especially after the manner of the last witness giving his evidence.
 Mr. Fielding. I have no further evidence in this case.
 Court. Gentlemen of the Jury, it is impossible to call on the prisoner for a defence in this case; it stands merely that a person brought a draught to Hankey's and Co. which one witness swears is not the hand-writing of the person whose writing it purports to be, for payment; no questions were asked that person, payment was made; and now one witness swears, that he believes the prisoner to be the man, but is not sure of it: in the first place there is no reason to believe that that person, whoever he was, knew it to be forged, his conduct in returning two bad shillings is reverse to that, and if the mere taking money for a draught would make a man guilty of forgery, a man would never take a draught; at the same time it is but justice to the prosecution to say, that though the recollection of Mr. Tait is very uncertain, and that uncertainty is greatly increased nearer the time of presenting the draught, yet such recollection is a proper reason for commencing this enquiry.
     NOT GUILTY.
 (The moment the verdict was given, Wood, junior. came into Court.
 Mr. Garrow moved the Court to have the prisoner's property returned to him, which was opposed by Mr. Fielding, the Court made no order, but observed, that there was but a very slight ground to justify the detention of the prisoner's person, and no ground at all to justify the detention of his property.
