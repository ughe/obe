270. ELEANOR ATTWOOD was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of
November, 1 watch, value 11, 108.; 1 watch-chain, value id.; 2 rings,
value Id.; and 3 watch-keys, value 3d.; the goods of John Connor.
John CONNOR. I am a Greenwich pensioner. I saw my watch safe on
Wednesday, the 3rd of November, in my chest under my bed at Green-
wich-I missed it on the Saturday morning-I had sent the prisoner to my
chest on Friday, the 5th, to get some bread for tea, about six o'clock--I had
seen my watch in the chest about two o'clock that day-she is a capmaker
-she drank tea with me on the 4th and on the 5th, but did not drink
tea regularly with me.
Prisoner. He told me to pawn the watch—he was drunk. Witness.
I did not- I was sober, and was capable of walking as well as at any other
time-I had no liquor that day--we had had the beer served out in the
College, and had a pot or two of beer, which I gave the prisoner the money
to buy-I suppose she must have taken it when she went for the bread-
she passed that night with me, I lived at Chatham, but I never gave her the
watch to pawn, either at Chatham, or at Greenwich.
John EVERDEN. I am shopman to Mr. Sharpe, a pawnbroker. The
prisoner pawned this watch with me in the morning, on the 6th of Novem-
ber, for 158., in the name of Ellen Connor, a lodger, No. 15, Queen-street,
Greenwich-she said it belonged to her husband, who had recently been
admitted into the College- I did not know her before.
EDWARD VINCENT (police-constable No. 131.) I was with the prosecutor
on the Monday after this happened the prisoner came up in a state of
inebriation, and gave herself up to me, she said she was the woman who
had stolen the watch on Friday, and she had pledged it on Saturday, at
Mr. Sharpe's, for 15s.
