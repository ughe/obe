NEW COURT .— Monday, March  9, 1835.
   Sixth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant .
 845. JANE HAINSWORTH was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of October , 2 shirts, value 16 s  .; and 1 table-cloth, value 3 s  .; the goods of George Hazlewood . MARY HAZLEWOOD. I am the wife of George Hazlewood, and live in Edward-street, Regent's-park. The prisoner was employed to work for me. In February I missed a shirt, and about the 11th of February, I spoke to her about it; and told her if she had any duplicates belonging to me, I would get the linen out, rather than lose my work, and would never say any thing at all about it—this is the shirt.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  I believe she was in great dis tress? A.  Yes. JOHN ROSE. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I took in this shirt of the prisoner on the 11th of February—I have a table-cloth, a napkin, and some other articles, all pawned by her. GUILTY. Aged 28.— Recommended to mercy .— Confined One Months.
 846. ANN JAMES was indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of March , 2 jugs, value 3 s  ., the goods of James Bodman . JAMES BODMAN. I keep an earthenware-shop in Church-street, Lisson-grove. On the 2nd of March I was in my back parlour—I saw the pri soner come to my door—she stooped down, and I thought she had taken something—I and my daughter followed her—I saw my daughter take the two jugs from her, which are mine.
   Prisoner . I took them up, scarcely knowing what I was about. Wit  ness. She had got into another street, and had the jugs concealed under her shawl. MARY BODMAN. I saw the prisoner come to our door and take these two jugs—I ran after her, and found them on her. GUILTY. Aged 35.— Recommended to mercy .— Confined One Month.
 847. CHARLES CHURCH was indicted for stealing, on the   20th of February , 1 truck, value 30 s  ., the goods of Thomas Lloyd Wardell and another. MATTHEW PEEK( police-constable C  198.) On the 20th of February, about eight o'clock at night, I was in Finsbury-market, and saw Nowland come for the loan of a truck—I asked him some questions, and followed him—he took it to Finsbury-square; and I saw in a dark place another truck, broken down, and a hogshead on it—the hogshead was taken off that, and put on another truck, which I followed. JOHN NOWLAND. About eight o'clock that night I saw two men with great-coats on—they had a truck broken down—they asked me to get a truck, which I did, from Finsbury-market; and when I got it, they had got a truck, and got the hogshead of juice on it—I asked who was to pay me—they said they had no money, and I returned home.
