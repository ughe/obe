 Prisoner's Defence (written). I called upon the prosecutor's son - he asked me to sell the harness; the prosecutor himself gave me the articles laid in the indictment, to show to hackneymen, to see if I could dispose of them, but being extremely old, although I had tried to sell them, I could not succeed; being very much distressed and fatigued, I called at Mr. Aldous, in Berwick-street, Soho, and pledged them for 1s. 6d., fully intending to redeem them, and return them to the owner - after I had received them from the prosecutor he wished me good-day, saying, that he had no money to treat me, and I said neither had I any.
 - WILLIE. The prisoner sent for me the evening he was taken, and I saw Mr. James, who said to me,"God bless my soul! I don't think the man has taken it with intent to steal it;" I said, "I believe he has not;" he said, "Will you call on me?" I called that evening at his place - he then said he did not think he took it with intent to steal it and not bring it back, but he did not bring it back for a week, and he was afraid his son would make him pay for it; I said I would make a bridle and pad to match rather than he should be taken - he said, "Well, I think I would take 8s. and drop it, if my expenses are paid;" I said as I had known Charters so long I did not mind that.
   ABRAHAM JAMES. I never saw the witness but once till he was before the Magistrate; I did not tell him I believed the prisoner did not intend to steal it, nor offer to make it up for 8s. and my expences - he followed me about from time to time, and wanted me to go and drink with him; since I have been here he has also followed me about.
     NOT GUILTY.
 First London Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1662.  HENRY BRANDT was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   JOHN THOMAS. I am a mariner. I came home on the 20th of November - Alexander Ross, of Pennington-street, owed me money for wages; I went for it on the 20th of February, and he offered me 20l. - the prisoner came to my lodging at No. 27, Brook-street, Ratcliff, about that time; I did not know him before - a woman, who lived in the house, was telling him how I was served by Ross, and the prisoner sent for me up to his room, which was on the second floor - he said he would take it in hand to get the money for me, if I would give him the money to pay the law-fees; I thanked him, and told him I would well reward him for it; we went the next day to what he called the Crown Court - I do not know where he it is; it was nearer to St. Paul's than to Whitechapel - I there gave him three half-crowns and 2s. 6d. to pay the law charges; I saw him again the same night, and a good while after; he said he was going on, and would soon bring Mr. Ross too and make him pay the money; I went with him to a place where there were a great many people, and gentlemen with whigs on, but I always waited outside - he always desired me to keep outside while he went in; he was to recover 26l. for me - he said he was an Excise-officer; I never saw him again for a long time after I gave him 2s. to buy a stamp: I have not seen Ross since - the prisoner showed me a Bank-warrant, and said he had 500l. in the Bank - he went into the Commercial-road to buy the stamp, and told me he would bring me the money next day, but I saw no more of him.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I not lodge in the same house as you? A. Yes; we both lodged at the house in Brook-street - I never lent you any money, and never said you had paid me 1l. 4s. 6d. of the money I lent you; I never sent a note to you in Newgate - I cannot write.
   BOYD SILVESTER. I am an officer of Thames-police. The prosecutor made a complaint at our office against Ross, for detaining his money and clothes, and complained of the prisoner about the beginning of June: I was sent with the prosecutor to see where he gave the prisoner the money; what he calls the Crown-office, where he paid 10s. is the Excise-office, which is in the City.
   JANE EVANS. I live in Joseph-street, St. George's East: Thomas lost a good deal of money which he had left with a publican; I heard the prisoner say he was an Excise-officer, and would forfeit 500l. if he did not get him the money from Ross: that was three or four months ago; he said he could not get it without going to different lawyers in the City, and every time he went he wanted money from Thomas - I saw Thomas give him some money in my room; I then kept the house in Brook-street.
 Prisoner's Defence (written) I was an English prisoner in France, and belonged to the ship Prosenjine - I took a lodging in the same house as the prosecutor; I paid some money to him off the debt I owed him - when the officer took me to the Thames Police-office, he stated that he heard the prosecutor say I had paid him part of the money back; when the clerks heard this, they discharged the Policeman, and gave me into the charge of a Thames Police-officer.
   JOHN THOMAS. I showed Silvester where I paid him the 10s.
     GUILTY. Aged 45. - Transported for Seven Years.

 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1664.  FREDERICK BENNETT was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of July , 1 pair of of trousers, value 18s., the goods of Thomas Sharpe and others.
   WILLIAM TALBOT. On the 7th of July, a little before twelve o'clock, I was in the shop of Thomas Sharpe and others, tailor s, Worship-street, and saw the prisoner snatch these trousers down from about a yard within the shop - they were pinned on an iron; he ran away with them in his apron - I pursued and took him with them, without losing sight of him.
   WILLIAM HOMES. I am a Policeman. I received him in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years.
