2500. ALFRED DAWSON was indicted for a like offence.
   MESSRS. HORRY and WILDE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM DAVIES. I keep the Queen's Head, at Mortlake. On the 22nd of Sept. I served the prisoner some brandy—he tendered me a half-crown in payment—I bit it, and found it was bad—it was put into the fire by the pri soner's request—he went away, and I followed him, and saw him in Mr. Willis's shop.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q.  You said you believed it to be Alfred Dawson? A.  Yes—he was much surprised when I told him the half-crown was bad—he gave me shillings, and said he was sorry such a thing should happen, and was not aware he had it about him—he did not stay more than five minutes at my house—he staid till the half-crown was melted—I showed him the pieces.
   MR. WILDE. Q.  Were you sent for by Mr. Day? A.  Yes—I saw the prisoner, and said he was the same person. WILLIAM LANGRIDGE. I keep a beer-shop. On the 22nd of Sept. the prisoner came for a glass of ale—he gave me a half-crown—I returned him 2 s . 4 d . out—he went away—I examined the half-crown in about five minutes afterwards—I am quite certain it was the same I received from him—it was bad, and I threw it on the fire—there was no fire alight—I took it out and gave it to Dumain.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long did the prisoner stay at your house? A.  Five or six minutes—nothing passed between him and me about the half crown—he had a glass of old ale—my suspicion was not at all excited by the half-crown—I took it and put it away—in consequence of something I heard afterwards I went and looked at it—I am a pork-butcher and a beer-shop keeper—I am in the habit of taking a good deal of silver. GEORGE DAY. I keep the public-house at Mortlake. On Wednesday, the 22nd of Sept., I saw the prisoner served with some brandy by Elisabeth Day, my niece—he put her down a half-crown to pay for it—I thought it was good—I bit it, and found it was bad—he wanted me to give it him back—he said, "Let me look at it"—I said I would take care of that and him too—I sent for Mr. Davies—he said the prisoner was the same person who had been offering him a half-crown.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did the prisoner bear Mr. Davies say so? A.  He was quite close to him—he wanted to go to his friends—he was in my place half an hour altogether, being searched and all—after I told him it was bad he said, "Give it to me, or throw it away"—he said he did not know he had such a thing about him—he had 6 d . worth of brandy and water after wards—if I had not heard something about it I should have taken the half crown as good—when the second brandy was supplied him he seemed a little fidgetty—he drank about one-third of it.
   MR. HORRY. Q.  Did he appear drunk? A.  He appeared very sober when he came in, and afterwards seemed to be very fidgety—he said he should lose the train and omnibus—he was very anxious to get away. JAMES DOMAN( policeman, V  120.) On Wednesday, the 22nd of Sept., I received from Mr. Davies these pieces of melted metal, and one half-crown from Mr. Langridge, and this other from Mr. Day. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. These pieces of white metal are the same as these two half-crowns—they are both counterfeit, but of different reigns.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY . Aged 27.— Confined Six Months .
