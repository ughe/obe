WOODROFFE, Horace Samuel; being entrusted with 22 11s., the
moneys of Samuel John MeKeand, in order that he might apply the
same for a certain purpose, fraudulently converting the said sum
to his own use and benefit.
Mr. Austin Metealfe prosecuted; Mr. Forrest Fulton defended.
SAMUEL JoHN MoKEAND. I am a credit draper and now carry on
business at 298, Burdett Road, Limehouse. Previously I carried on
business at 417, East India Dock Road. In September, 1907, I saw
prisoner at the former address and gave him the names of a number
of persons who owed me money. He took them down from my dic-
tation and he produced that statement to me when I paid him the
cheque. He put down the list of plaint fees and hearing fees which
would be required in each particular ease, amounting in all to 83 4s.
I gave him E1 12s. in cash for the plaint fees. He came into my
place on October 12, gave me this document, and I paid him &
cheque for E4 3s., being the amount of hearing fees and the allow-
ance for himself that he would require. I did not see him again
until after the cases had been heard. That would be November 23 or
24. He told me then that all the cases had gone through. I did
not ask for the plaint notes at the time, but he has promised often
enough to let me have them. I got eleven out of eighteen in June
of last year. From first to last he never told me that he had only
