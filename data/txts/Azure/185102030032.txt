501. WILLIAM SMITH and JAMES KIMBER, stealing 1 51.-
note ; the property of William Flowerday Silcock.
WILLIAM FLOWERDAY SILCOCK. On 21st Jan. I was at the Clerken-
well police—court, charged with assault by the prisoner Smith, who was my
potboy—I was discharged, on paying 28. costs—I had no silver, took a
51-note out, and tendered it to the officer for payment of the 2$., but the
offcer paid the 28. to Smith for me, and I put the note into my pocket
again as I thought—I left the Court directly to get change, and the pri-
soners and three or four more who were there, followed me—the prisoners
were close to me in the Court—I crossed the road, and went to a public.
house opposite, and there missed my note—the prisoners did not go into
the house, and I wept out into the road to see if I could see anything of
them, but they Were gone—I went back to the police-court, saw Mary
Jones, and went with two officers, and found the prisoners and three others
at the Golden Lion, Bagnigge Wells-road, which is about a quarter of a'
mile from the police-court—I asked them which of the five had picked my
note up ; they denied it, but not individually—I said one of them must
have got it, and they must go back to the station and be searched—Hardy
then came in, and said some of them must know something about the 51.-
note—Kimber then said it was no use denying it, he had got it, and that
Smith gave it him—Smith was present—Kimber gave the offcer tbe
note; he took it out of a gave them both in cbarge—l
did not hear Smith say how he came by it.
