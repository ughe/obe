COURT. Q. Did you say they had been sold? A. I said she had almost ruined me by giving these prints to her mother, and their being exposed for public sale, as it would show I was not a trustworthy person; I could not ascertain how many I had lost - we have an immense quantity, and it would be impossible to detect one or two dozen - at that time none ought to have been issued to the public - I had delivered some of the Juvenile Forget Me Not to Messrs. Westley and Davis.
 MR. CRESWELL. Q. Had you any right to any of them yourself? A. According to the custom of the trade we consider ourselves entitled to one or two impressions, not more.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. I believe you were suspected of having parted with them? A. Yes, at first: after the examination at the office I asked Simpson how he came by the prints, and he said an elderly woman with a child sold them to him - he asked me if there was any one I suspected about my house; I said I could not possibly suspect any one of my work-people; at my suggestion he went willingly to my house, to see if he could point out the person who sold them - he said it was not my female servant; it struck me it might probably be her mother - we went to her mother's in London-street; he knocked, and I staid outside - her mother came to the door, and he told me that was the woman; I had parted with some of them to Messrs. Westley's binders, to be bound in the usual way, and I think to some others. Simpson was out on bail; I believe there were some impressions exhibited by the resective proprietors.
   HENRY BLOMFIELD. I am in the employ of Thomas Norton Longman and others. I went to Simpson's house, in Tottenham-court-road; he deals in books and prints - this was on the 19th of September; I saw him, and asked for some prints - he produced several portfolios, and I saw some impressions for the Gem and the Literary Souvenir for the coming year; the publishers of those works get them out about the end of October - I bought two of them at 6d. each, and asked if he had any more; he said he had had more, and might possibly have some in a week or two- I marked the two I bought - these are them; these letters at the bottom of the print are not put by the engraver, but by a person he sends them to - we receive them perfect - this one is "Jacob's Dream," published by Longman and Co., and this is
 "Saturday Night," published by Marshall.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. When you went to the shop did you see any other prints of a similar description? A. No; I saw none in the window - I went into the back shop: they were in the port-folios, among others - no doubt persons buy a great many prints, and sell one print for more than they give for the whole; these are in the same state as I bought them, except my marking - one of them was soiled; when they are soiled they are of little value.
 COURT. Q. What would the value of them be if not soiled? A.Perhaps 6d. or 1s., or 1s. 6d.; Simpson sells second-hand books - these could not be the engraver's proofs, because they have the letters to them.
   WILLIAM RUDOLPH. I am in the service of Joseph Hogarth, who lives in Somer's-terrace; I went to Mr. Simpson's, at the corner of Howland-street, Tottenham-court-road: I asked him if he had any engravings for the annuals - he said he had, and I bought five of him; these are them - here is our "Holyday Time," two of "Saturday Night," one of "The Stolen Interview," and one "The Masquerade;" they have the letters to them - he took them from a shelf in the shop.
 Cross-examined. Q. I suppose he sold them in the usual way? A. Yes; I did not look whether any were exhibited about the wall or the window.
   JOSEPH HOGARTH. I am a print colourer. On the 19th of September I went to Mr. Simpson's shop, and bought five prints of annuals and another; these are them - some of them are good prints: there may be one or two damaged or dirty - I gave 2s. for the five; I asked where he got them - he said from a girl who represented that she had been sent by her young mistress, and he supposed they were engravers' proofs.
 Cross-examined Q. Are they precisely in the same state as when you bought them? A. Yes, with the exception of my mark - it frequently happens that prints of considerable value at one time, are sold at another, at a very small price.
   WILLIAM MARSHALL. I am a bookseller, and live in Middle-row. I publish the Gem; I gave the steel plates to Mr. Triggs, to have engravings struck off. On the 21st of September I went to Simpson's house, and bought three or four prints, for which I gave 6d. each - these are them - I did not see Simpson; these prints have been rumpled a little.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you get them? A. This one from the window, and some of them from a portfolio; I was in the back shop - persons passing by might have seen this one in the window. The Gem has twelve prints in it, and we sell the book at 12s. - we charge the trade 7s. for the set.
 MR. BOLLAND. Q. Do the prints vary in value? A. Yes; the plates cost from one hundred to one hundred and eighty guineas a piece - these prints are worth 1s. or 1s. 6d. each.
   THOMAS ANDREWS. I am in the employ of Frederick Westley and Davis - they live in Stationer's-court, and are the publishers of the Juvenile Forget Me Not, and are part proprietors. I went to Mr. Simpson's on the 22nd of September - I saw him, and asked if he had any prints for a scrap-book; he said, "Yes, walk in" and shewed me two,"The Schoolmaster" and "The Masquerade;" I bought"The Schoolmaster" for 7d. - he had asked 8d. for it; I offered him 6d. - he said he could not take less than 8d., unless I took a number of them, as they were prints not yet published, and he had to pay a good price for them; he said some persons bought the books, and cut the plates out, but they got theirs in a different way - these are the prints.
 Cross-examined. Q. From where were they produced? A. From a shelf in the back shop - he did not state that he got them at a public auction; I did not ask where he got them.
   HENRY RICHARD BUCKERIDGE. On the 23rd of September I went with a search-warrant, and took Simpson -I got five prints, which I left with Hewitt.
 Cross-examined. Q.Where were they? A.Three of them in the window, and two on a shelf; he said I might search, and did not attempt to escape.
