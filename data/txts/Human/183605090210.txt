JAMES DAY was indicted for stealing, on the   27th of De cember , 1 watch, value 3 l  .; 1 seal, value 1 l  .; 1 watch-key, value 2 s  .; and 1 watch-ribbon, value 6 d  .; the goods of John Thomas Grottick , from his person; and GEORGE DAY for feloniously receiving the said goods, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Stature, &c. JOHN THOMAS GROTTICK. On Sunday morning, the 27th of December, about one o'clock, I hired a cabriolet at the corner of Hart-street, Long Acre—James Day was the driver—I am sure he is the person—I had, at that time, a silver watch in my pocket, and a seal, key, and ribbon, with a guard-chain—James Day placed himself in the cab, by my side, I made no objection to it, he was in the cab when I got in—I directed him to go to No. 66 wood-street, Cheapside—I had been drinking in the evening, but was sober—I fell asleep, and no awaking, I found myself at Battle-bridge—my watch was gone—just at that time Day stopped the cab, and got out, and went round the back of the cab to the hourse's head—I found my watch was gone and desired him again to drive me to Wood-street—he said he would not, and was very impertient—I would not leave the cab, I called a policeman, and gave him in charge—my guard-chain was cut—Part of it remained round my neck—here is the seal, the key, and the ribbon.
   James Day . He was in liquor when I took him up, and the policeman told me to take him to Wood-street—I took him there—he got out, and then got in with a female, and told me to drive him to Maiden-lane—I took him there, and then said he meant Maiden-lane, New North-road. Witness. No; I was not in liquor, I did not take any female in the cab, not did I get out at all. CHARLES KNOTT( police-sergeant N  11) On the morning of the 27th of December I was at Battle-bridge, about three o'clock—the prosecutor called me—he state he had been robbed, and at the same time, I heard him desire the cab-man to pull up—he was driving very fast towards Pen tonville-hill—I crossed from the pavement, and stopped the horse, and desired the cab-man to get out—I searched him, and afterwards I searched the cab—I found none of this property—I then took him to the station-house—he had 5 s . 6 d . in silver, and a knife—the prosecutor was sober—he appeared to me at first to have been sleeping, and rather stupid—it struck me at first he was the worse for liquor but when inquired of him the case, he became perfectly collected, and quite sober—this case was heard at Hatton Garden, before Mr. Laing, and he was discharged.
