32. from Charles Dobbin.
MR. Datron conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES DOBBIN. I am a soldier discharged from the 60th Rifles on
8th January-we were living at Winchester-on coming to London the
prisoner made a statament about stopping for the night--we left our kits
iu a public-house and went for & walk-we had a quart of ale; he took mo
into a coffee-shop in Spitalfields —we went up stairs, and he put his hand
in my pocket and took out 51.- I was the worse for drink-he aiso took a
silk handkerchief off my neck, and he took my discharge and gave it to
the manager of the shop and said he would come back at 9 o'clock in the
morning-he did not do 80-I afterwards got his address, went to Colches-
ter and saw the prisoner at his house there, and afterwards took a policeman
there and enquired whether he knew anything about my things--he said
that he knew nothing about tbem-I told the policeman to search the
house and we found my things in the prisoner's box-I had given him no
authority to take them.
Cross-examined. We did not take a train after arriving in London
you might have done so.
JOSHUA EMANUEL GULLY (Essex Constabulary)On 12th January I
searched the prisoner's house at Colchester, and found the kit, produced,
in a box in a bed-room--the prisoner was charged with stealing it-he did
not reply then--afterwards he said that he did not know anything about it
- on the way to the station he said "I shall have to thank drink for this."
Cross-examined. I followed you upstairs; you never said " There are
your things."
