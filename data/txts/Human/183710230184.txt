RICHARD KIRBY was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of April , 2 pairs of breeches, value 2 l  ., 3 pairs of trowsers, value 3 l  .; 3 waist coats, value 2 l  .; 2 coats, value 2 l  .; 3 pairs of gaiters, value 18  s  .; 8 hand kerchiefs, value 1 l  . 6 s  .; and 1 pair of gloves, value 2 s  .; the goods of Henry Hilton , in the dwelling-house of Henry Davidson . HENRY HILTON. I live in the house of Mr. Henry Davidson, in Bruton-street, Berkeley-square, in the parish of St. George, Hanover-square. The prisoner came on trial to Mr. Davidson as groom, a few days before the 24th of April—I was the head groom until about ten days ago—all the property stated in the indictment is mine, and was taken out of the room over the stable—the prisoner slept there two or three nights—I went out and returned in half an hour—I missed the things at half-past six o'clock in the evening—the prisoner said he was going to his father, who had a job for him, and if he did not get the job, he should return again—he did not return—this was after the things were missed—I went to his father next morning and made inquiry, and then gave information to the police—the coat he had on when he was apprehended is mine—I never permitted him to take my property at all.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are you quite sure the coat shown to you was the coat you had in April? A.  To the best of my judgment—I never saw the prisoner after the 28th of April—I saw him go out of the room about ten minutes before six o'clock—I had seen my clothes in the room ten minutes before—to the best of my belief the coat is mine—I know it by a mark—I asked the tailor if he knew it—I always supposed it to be mine—it is worth 6 s .—I lost 30 l . worth of property altogether—I lost two coats—this was a blue coat—it is now a kind of mixture—it has been dipped since. JOSEPH LEYBOURN. I am a policeman. I apprehended the prisoner on the 10th of October, in Little Chesterton-street, Grosvenor-place, in a skittle-ground—he had this coat on his back. WILLIAM HART. I am a tailor. I mended this coat for the prosecutor, and turned it—I am certain it is his coat.
   Prisoner's Defence.  On the 23rd of April I engaged to go on trial—I remained there till the 28th—on the night of the robbery the prosecutor was ordered out with his master's cab,  he promised me I should have the situation, but he kept on seeing different people, to see if he could get any body else, and I said to him,"As you seem to do business in this way, I shall not stop longer," and said I did not know whether I should return—I went away before he went out of the place.
