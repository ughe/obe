MR. SLEIGH, on behalf of Cracknell, proposed to call the prisoner Jackson
as a witness. MR. JUSTICE ERLE inquired whether such a course had ever
been taken before. MR. SLEIGH was not aware that one prisoner had ever
been examined on behalf of another, although it was very common to examine
one of the parties charged, on behalf of the prosecution. Mr. JUSTICE Erle
expressed some doubt whether it should be done; the statute not having removed
the so called privilege of prisoners not being liable to have any question prut
to them that could operate either for or against themselves, he had never
known this course adopted, and whenever it had been necessary to excamine a
prisoner, he had always gone through the form of trial and sentence, or trial
and acquittal, so that the entire matter should be at an end before such evidence
was given; he would, however, receive the evidence if it was desired, and com-
sult the Judges on the subject.
