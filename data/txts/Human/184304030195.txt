On the 20th of March, about nine o'clock, I missed a coat and waistcoat, which I had seen safe about seven o'clock the same evening at the foot of the bed—my house was not locked—my wife was out—I believe this coat and waistcoat are what I missed. ANN ALLEN. My husband keeps a clothes-shop in Woolwich. At half-past eight o'clock that evening, the prisoner brought this coat and waistcoat, and I bought them of him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  On the Saturday I was at my uncle's (the prosecutor) and I asked him to lend me 7 s . or 8 s .; he said, "No; but he had a coat and waistcoat at home, that I could go and take when I liked." RICHARD WARWICKSHIRE re-examined.  I told him so a day or two before. NOT GUILTY.
 1385. JAMES CRAWLEY, was indicted for unlawfully uttering coun terfeit coin.
   MESSRS. BODKIN and SCRIVEN conducted the Prosecution. RICHARD JOB FRANCIS. I am the son of Rebecca Francis, who keeps the Bull-inn, Shooters'-hill. On the 17th of March, the prisoner came to me for half-a-pint of beer, I served him—he put down a bad 6 d .—I saw it was bad—I cut it in two, and kept one half, and he took the other half away—I am sure he is the man—when he took one half he knocked the other off the counter—he asked for a candle, my mother would not let him have it, and he went away without it—I picked the other half up the next morning, and put it into a china cup, and at last gave it to the policeman—on the 22nd, the prisoner came again for half-a-pint of beer—he put down another bad 6 d .—I showed it to my mother—I told her it was bad—he laid down another sixpence, which was good—I went for a policeman, and gave him into custody, with the sixpence.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Did not you pick up one-half of the sixpence you cut, and the prisoner the other? A.  No—on the second occa sion the prisoner said, "Is it a bad one?"—I said, "Yes," and he paid another. REBECCA FRANCIS. On the 17th of March the prisoner came—he gave my son a sixpence—it was cut in two, and one half fell down—he wanted a light, and I would not let him have it—on the 22nd he came again—he paid my son a bad sixpence—my son brought it to me—I said it was bad—I cut it with a penknife, sent for a policeman, and gave him the sixpence and the piece. WILLIAM BECK( police-constable R  214.) I was sent for, and took the prisoner—I told him it was for uttering a counterfeit sixpence, and said he had been there the week previous—he denied it, and said he came from Maid-stone that day—I received this sixpence and half a sixpence MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint. This sixpence is a counterfeit, and this is half of another counterfeit sixpence. GUILTY. Aged 47.— Confined Six Months .
