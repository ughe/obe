weight of lead, value 58., the goods of the Commissioners for paving St.
Pancras St. George the Martyr, and St. George, Bloomsbury, and being

WILLIAM PITCHFORD (police-constable E 25.) I was on duty in the
neighbourhood of Mecklenburg-square abont nine o'clock in the morning
of the 28th of June, and saw the prisoner in Cromer-street, about a furlong
from the Commissioners' tool-house-he had a carpet bag in his hand, and
went into a marine store shop-I followed him in, and found him offering
some lead for sale-I asked how he came in possession of that lead-he
said he bought it in the country-here are five pieces rolled up-part of it
was in the scale, and part in the bag-I afterwards went to the tool-house,
where the Commissioners kept their tools, and found the roof had been
stripped- this is part of the lead that had been on the roof-there was
more taken than I have here, but this fitted as far as it went.
Prisoner. He stated that he accidentally came into the shop where I
was-he did not see me with the lead, but they told him at Hatton-
garden, if he did not say so, he would not get his expenses. Witness. I
saw something in the bag, and suspected it was lead- I was not told any
thing of the sort at Hatton-garden.
Thomas COLCROFT. I am porter of the Foundling-hospital. I saw this
lead fitted on the roof of the tool-house-it fitted in every respect, as far
as it would go we have lost more than 1 cwt, of lead-I was in Hatton-
garden office the whole time; I heard no one say any thing to the officer
as to what he was to say.
Prisoner. You were not there the first day. Witness. I was not there
on the Monday.
THOMAS OSBORNE. I am in the employment of the Commissioners of the
Foundling-hospital. Sir William Curtis is one of the governors and a
commissioner - I left the tool-house shut on the Saturday evening the
lead was safe then, and on the Sunday morning I missed i the prisoner
was taken the next morning.
