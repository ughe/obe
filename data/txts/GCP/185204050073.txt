MR. PARRY. Q. Do you attend the trial of cases 4. Yes: cases are
carried on there by Record, just the same as at Westminster-the proceedings
are commenced by writ-the trials all take place there on Thursdays, except
by special appointment (the 15th Oct. was Wednesday)-I believe there was
no special day in Oct.--I never saw the prisoner, to my knowledge, at the
Court; if he had conducted a case there, I must have done 80-none but
barristers, attorneys, or their managing clerks have audience there-after
judgment and verdict, the writs of execution do not issue from our office. lt
Cross-examined. Q. How is the business carried on; is there a record !
1. Yes; a writ of trial, or a record lodged by the attorney, and the day of
trial endorsed- I cannot say whether that would be in consequence of a writ
of summons in the superior Courts-I have been in the office upwards of
twenty years—the writs of trial, or records, are not kept at our office; they
are returned to the Courts at Westminster-there, is an entry made of them
in the books as they come into the office--I searched the books before I went
before the Magistrate, to see if there was any entry of this case, in case I
might be asked the books would show whether or not there was such an
entry.
