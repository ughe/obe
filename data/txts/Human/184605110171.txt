THOMAS BENT( police-constable V  95.) On the 5th of May I was on doty in the Wandsworth-road—I saw the two prisoners talking together a little before two o'clock—the man appeared to give her something—I then saw Hayes leave the man and cross the road, and the man sat down by some rail ings—I saw Hayes afterwards in Mrs. Baughan's shop, and I heard the dispute about the shilling—I received this shilling from Mrs. Baughan, and Hayes was allowed to go away—I saw her go to the end of the lane—she there joined Mayes, and they went on—I followed them to Vauxhall-gate—they stood talking some minutes, and then went towards the Triangle-May es then appeared to give her something, and Hayes went to Mr. Knap-ton's shop—she came out—then she and Mayes walked away together—I went into Knapton's and received a shilling from Weedon—I then followed the prisoners up the Clapham-road—they parted—Hayes went into a shop, and I went up to Mayes—he put something into his mouth, and held his head back—he said, "I have got nothing, I have not uttered any bad money;! have no bad money: all my money is good"—I said, "You have given the woman some"—I found on him a sovereign and a half good, and 7 1/2 d .—I found no more bad money—I found part of a penny roll, and some mould, apparently the same as had been rubbed over the shillings—It is the same colour as they are—it is road dirt, apparently mixed with gunpowder—it wai wet, but it has got quite light by being dry—the shillings given me in the shop were very dark, and appeared as if this stuff had been rubbed into the letters—this is such stuff as would darken shillings.
   COURT . Q.  How near was Mayes to Hayes when she wat in any shop? A.  He was only three doors from the shop where she was apprehended—he wts very nearly close—at the second shop she went into, be walked by while she was in—I should think he was fifty yards from the first shop. ROBERT MOORE( police-sergeant V  12.) On the 4th of May I saw Hayes in Mr. Jennings's shop—I went in—Mr. Jennings gave her in charge, and Mrs. Jennings gave me this shilling—I told her to mark it. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint. These shillings are all counterfeit, and cast in the same mould—they appear to hare had some black preparation rubbed over them.
   Mayes's Defence.  I met this young woman in Wai worth road: I asked her where she was going: she said, "To Kennington-common:" I said I would take a walk with her; I thought she was a servant; I had no bad money.
   HAYES  — GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined One Year .
   MAYES  — NOT GUILTY.
