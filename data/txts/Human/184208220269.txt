I live with Mr. Moore, a pawnbroker, at Woolwich. Whittle pledged this watch at our shop about eight o'clock in the morning of the 3rd of August—I gave him this duplicate—it is pledged in his own name, for 1 l ., and the number of his regiment is put, the "73rd"—this is what he described himself. THOMAS WRIGHT. I am a surgeon, at Woolwich. Early in the morn ing of the 3rd of August, I examined the prosecutor, and found him in a weak state from the loss of blood—I found the mark of a blow on his forehead, which I should say was inflicted with the fist—there was only one mark, but it was of very considerable size—there was blood on his face—the hair of his head was completely matted with blood, and there was a wound on the back part of the head, between the ears—the blood might have come from that wound—it was a wound about two inches in length—it appeared to have been made with a bludgeon, or it might possibly have been a kick—there were no other wounds.
   JURY . Q.  Could that wound arise from a fall backwards? A.  Yes, but it appeared as if it had been from a blow with a stick.
   COURT . Q.  Was it such a cut as might have resulted not from a blow at all, but from a blow in the front knocking him down? A.  Decidedly not.
