867. JOHN OXLEY (31), Breaking and entering the dwelling-house
of Renford Davy, and stealing therein fur capes and trimmings, bis
property
MR. GREENFIELD Prosecuted.
EMMA TOMLIN. I am single, and live at 5, Haberdasher Square, Cripple-
gate-on 23rd August, about 11 p.m., I was going into a shop, and say
a man fall down-he lay there several minutes, and then crawled out of
the court to the first doorway, and I asked him if he would have a drink
of water-I spoke to a policeman-I don't know whether the prisoner is
the man.
GEORGE ALDER (City Policeman 169). On Saturday night, 23rd August,
about 11 o'clock, I was in Milton Street, and saw the prisoner crawling along
on his hands and knees-thinking he was intoxicated, I asked him whether
I should assist him-he said "Don't do that, for I have been assaulted by
two men "-I said "Where?"-he said "In that court," puinting to Sun
Court-he had no boots on-I said "Where are your boots ?" --be said
“They have taken them and some money"-I took him to St. Bartholo-
mew's Hospital on a stretcher, where it was found that both his ankles
were broken I searched him there, and found this knife, a piece of
candle, and some matches.
WILLIAM FLOAT (City Police Inspector). I was called to Milton Street,
and saw the prisoner lying on the footway in Alder's charge—he had no
boots on, and he appeared injured-I sent him to the hospital on.
stretcher, and, in company with Sergeant Byron and East and Egau;
examined the premises, and found all correct-from there we went to
Crown Court, and at the back of 86, Milton Street is an iron gate, under
which I found the pair of men's boots produced) which the prisoner
owned when before the Magistrate -inside the gate, but outside the ware-
house, we found three empty bags and two aprons, one of which contained
38 pieces of black fur, which had evidently been thrown down from the
root-I then went to 80, Milton Street, opposite, up to the roof, and over
the roof of several warehouses till we came to the roof of No. 75, which
is the prosecutor's, and found that the trap-door had been pulled up, and
the rope securing it broken-inside the trap-door on the beam were
several finger marks, and the clear impression in the dust of a man's foot,
without a boot; there were the impression of the toes--we found some
workmen's aprons torn up, and tied together to form a rope to pull the
fur from the warehouse to the roof-we entered the warehouse, and found
a large quantity of fur similar to what we have in Court-the door lead-
ing to the street was locked-we returned to the roof, made a further
search, and on the top of 86, Milton Street, which overlooks the recens,
We found a sack containing 80 dozen of unfinished black fur capes and 10
pieces of long black fur trimming, 30 yards) found several marks on
the water-pipe in Sun Court, down the brickwork 20 and 25 feet from
the ground-that is the water-pipe which the prisoner fell from.
