RICHARD ETHERIDGE (police-constable E 164). About eleven o'clock on
Monday night, the 11th of March, I was in Museum-street, and saw the two
prisoners together--Legg had something in a bag-1 asked what he had gol-
he told me an anvil - I asked whose it was-he said his own, and he was
going to take it to Charles-street, Drury-lane, and he had brought it from Mr.
Legg's, in Hoxton Old-town-I said I would go and see where he was taking
it to-he walked with me, and as he was going he gave it to Wigson-Legy
then said he was going to No. 8, Smith-court, Charles-street, Drury-lane-
went there, but the highest number there is 6-I saw the landlady-she said
Legg lodged there, that he was not a smith, but worked for the parish.
LEGG.-Guilty. Aged 20.-Confined Six Months.
WIGSON-Guilty. Aged 20.--Confined Two Months.

1176. ELLEN CONNELL was indicted for stealing 1 pair of boots, value
158.; the goods of Edward Harris.
EDWARD Harris (police-constable E 50.) The prisoner's mother was
employed at the station in George-street, St. Giles, and the prisoner occa-
sionally assisted her---these are my boots-1 lost them from the room--I saw
them safe on the morning of the 19th of March, about seven o'clock, and
missed them about two.
JAMES SMITH (police-constable E 107.) I saw these boots hanging on a
nail at the station between eleven o'clock and twelve-the prisoner was then
cleaning the room, and I left her there.
EDWARD CAMPION (police-constable E 45.) I saw the prisoner at the
station-she went away with something under her apron-no one went out
but her.
Peter ALLEN, shopman to Mr. Franklin, pawnbroker, Tottenham-court-road.
These boots were pawned on the 9th of March, by a young woman the same
size as the prisoner-I believe she is the person.
Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor has only done it out of spite, because
I would not let him do what he wished to do; I know nothing about his
boots.
Not Guilty.
