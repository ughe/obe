1916. HENRY SPINKS was again indicted, with JAMES BECK.
WITH, for stealing, on the 17th of May, 1 half-crown, the money of
Charles Gurney and another, the masters of Spinks.
Mr. Phillips conducted the Prosecution.
Charles GURNEY. Spinks was in my employ as bar-man-Beckwith lead
been in a great many times during the day in question-on the 17th of May,
among other things, I marked this half-crown, which Mrs. Kershaw gut;
and the officer marked it also-here are two black marks on the top of the
crown-it is marked differently from the others that were put into the til
--Brannan brought Beck with into the counting-house, and this half-cron
was taken out of his pocket, and some halfpence-1 do not recollect any
thing else-the officer said to him, “Do you call these halfpencel"-
Spinks then said, "I gave him change for a 58. piece, for half-a-pinto
stout"-they were both sober-there was more silver on him than the
change for a 58. piece.
Cross-examined by Mr. Bopkin. Q. When was it you began this system
of marking money? A. On the 14th of May-I marked twelve hall
crowns all together, and put the marked money into the till on the Sunday
and Monday, and four half-crowns on the Tuesday- I found a 58. piece in
one of the tills-I cannot say which till it was in- I took it out-I took
another customer into custody- he was searched, and nothing but bal.
pence found on him, and we let him go.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. What wages were due to
Spinks? A. A quarter's Wages- I cannot say how much more-there
may have been more--I paid him his wages at the end of every quarter,
he wanted it, we made no particular agreement-I paid him every quare
ter--if our servants want the money they ask for it.
MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did he ever ask you for any wages ? A. No,
should have paid him if he had-I commenced this system of marking,
cause I suspected I was being robbed.
