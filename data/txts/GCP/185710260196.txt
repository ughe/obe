WILLIAM HARKER. I am waiter and potman at the King's Arms, Kev.
On Tuesday, 22nd Sept., about 8 o'clock in the morning, Weleh came with
a lad, who ordered three basins of coffee or tea-Seers came about a quarter
of an hour afterwards, and they breakfasted together at one table-Seers
had one of the three basins-Welch asked me to cook a mutton chop for
him, which he brought with him, and I did so; he pulled his money out,
and said that he had got half a sovereign more than he thought he had,
besides several half crowns and shillings- I saw the half sovereign-he said
that he had been out on the drink, and he should put it in a pocket by
itself-I did not see that he was any the worse for liquor-Seers seemed a
if he had been drinking over night, but did not seem tipsy then.
Cross-examined. Q. How soon after the 22nd were you spoken to about
this matter? A. About 12 o'clock on the same day—a policeman came
and asked me if they had been there to breakfast I had only those three to
breakfast that morning.
DANIEL LANGFORD (Policeman, N 137). On 22nd Sept., about a quarter
to 11 o'clock, I received from Mr. Huntley a counterfeit sovereign, marked
with a cross on the head—I also received another counterfeit sovereign from
John Golding, in Miss Warden's presence- I went with Fletcher to look
for Welch; he was on the roof of the building, and I did not take him at
first-I afterwards went to the King's Arms and made inquiry-I went
round the building, and Weleh was then on the ground—I asked him what
he had had for breakfast; he said, “Some meat and pudding, the same a
the other men"_I asked him where he had had breakfast, he said, “ At
the King's Arms' -I asked him if he had had a mutton ehop for breakfast
-he said, “No”-when he was at his dinner I took him into custody-I
found Seers at the station at Kensington, and brought him from there on an
omnibus—I searched him at the station and found on him 158. 6d. in silver,
and 4d. in copper-I searched Welch and found on him 8s. 6d. in silver,
and 8d. in copper--when I saw Welch at the building, he had no jacket or
waistcoat on; when I saw him at dinner he had & jacket on, but no waist
coat-I gave him into the custody of another constable.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you say that you took him for passing a coun
terfeit sovereigu? A. Yes; he said, " It is not me, you are mistaken"-
there were four or five workmen at the building; there may have been one
near his height, but nothing like him--the witnesses were not quite certain
at first, and I would not take him for an hour or an hour and a half.
Court. . Did you go with Golding and Smith round the building!
