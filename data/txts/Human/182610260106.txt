 2019.  EDWARD JARVIS was indicted for bigamy.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN GEORGE LEIGH. I am deputy parish clerk of St. George, Hanover-square. I have the marriage-register, by which it appears that on the 3d of July last, Edward Jarvis Tollast, widower, and Elizabeth Whitehurst, spinster, were married - it is witnessed by Hemry and myself - I do not remember the parties.
 JOHN HEMRY. I witnessed this marriage - I remember the prisoner as the person married to Miss Elizabeth Whitehurst; she is now in court.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. Were you a friend of the parties? A. No; I am a publican, and live opposite to the church-gate - Jarvis ordered a breakfast at my house for four or five persons, but only himself and Whiteburst came: after breakfast, he said he was going to be married, and was in a difficulty about nobody coming to be witnesses, and asked if I would go over, which I did; I knew neither of them before - I asked him where the lady lived; he said in Windmill-street. Tottenham-court-road - I have seen her several times since.
   HANNAH GREY. Up to the 10th of August, I was a widow; on that day I married the prisoner at St. Mary, Lambeth, Surry.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you known him before? A. Yes, for two or three months; Miss Finlayson is my niece; He parted from me at Kennington about three days after we were married, and I lodged at a butcher's at Camden-town.
   JAMES LEDGER. I apprehended the prisoner on a warrant for an assault, in Church-passage, St. Giles.
     GUILTY. Aged 54.
 2020.  EDWARD JARVIS was again indicted for a like offence.
   JOHN GEORGE LEIGH. I produce the marriage-register of St. George, Hanover-square, by which it appears that Edward Jarvis, bachelor, was married to Rachael Pearce, spinster, by license, on the 16th of December, 1797 - I was not in office at that time - the register is signed Thomas Ash, curate, and by the clerk and sexton; I knew their writing - the clerk and sexton are dead.
   JOHN STRATTON. I know the prisoner; he came to work for me about six years ago - he came from Horncastle, in Lincolnshire; he sent for his wife, and she came up; I saw her, and have heard her say her maiden name was Rachael Pearce, many times, in his presence; they lodged in my house; she is now living in Clarendon-street, Somer's-town - she is the same person who lived with him, as his wife - they have a son about twenty-four years old, and a daughter older; they always treated them as their children; his wife is about sixty years old.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you know where she is now? A. Yes; I saw her six weeks ago - he called her Rachael, and also wife, and she called him husband. The children called them mother and father.
   ELIZABETH FINLAYSON. I am niece to Mrs. Gray. I obtained an extract of the register which has been produced. I saw Rachael Jarvis at Clarendon-street - I afterwards saw the prisoner behind the counter of a shop in Windmill-street - I cannot say he was serving there; I told him of his marriage to Rachael Pearce, of Clarendon-street - he at first denied it, but afterwards said she was his wife; I said, "How could you think of marrying my aunt, when you knew you were married?" he said he knew he was a scoundrel or villain, and said, "I know I am married, and my wife is a good manager, a good liver, and a good provider, but our tempers did not agree;" I said, "You have taken my aunt's money?" he said, "Yes, I have;" I said, "Now, take my advice, young as I am - take 100l. and fly." but he would not.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it by your advice your aunt prosecuted? A. Not exactly; I thought it my duty to interfere. I live with my mother. The prisoner and my aunt only lived together four or five days - she asked my advice about this; she is sixty-eight years old, and I am nineteen.
   JOHN GEORGE LEIGH. I produce the register of the prisoner's marriage to Whitehurst, as I read before.
   ELIZABETH WHITEHURST. This register is signed by me; the prisoner is the man I married at St. George, Hanover-square, on the 3d of July.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am compelled to state that Pearce never was my wife, nor was I in London till about five years ago; I never acknowledged to Finlayson that Pearce was my wife - all our conversation was about my running away. She was married to a man named Jarvis, but that was not me; I had a cousin named Jarvis - he came to town, and he was Pearce's husband; he went to America - she was visiting in Kent - we became acquainted, and we cohabited - I have a son and daughter, but I am not her husband. Whitehurst was my first wife, and I am proud to own it.
   CHARLES McDUFF. I know the prisoner, and know his hand-writing; I believe the signature to the marriage with Rachael Pearce to be his.
     GUILTY. Aged 54. Transported for Seven Years.
 2021.  JAMES SCORNS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 1 pair of stockings, value 1s., and 1 round-frock, value 3s., the goods of Samuel Clift.
 SAMUEL CLIFT. I lost a pair of stockings and a frock from my boat on the 24th of August, at four o'clock in the morning; the boat was then in the canal of  Aston, near Birmingham. I came to Paddington, and on a second journey to Paddington I apprehended the prisoner at Brentford, on the 22d of October, with the frock and stockings on - he had been my servant, and absconded on the 23d of August - I then missed the property; he said they were mine, and that he had pawned more of my things at Birmingham.
 Prisoner. Q. Did I not pay you 8s., and was to pay 3s. a week for them? A. No - he never did.
