299, WILLIAM DAVIS and HENRY LOVELL were indicted for
stealing, on the 31st of Dec., three pairs of boots, value 16s. 6d., of
Charles Williamson.
Thomas LINWOOD. I live at No. 4, Lockwood's-gardens, Clerkenwell.
On Tuesday afternoon, the 31st of Dec., I was passing along Red Cross-
street, from half-past four to a quarter to five o'clock-I stopped at Mr.
Williamson's to ask the price of a pair of Blucher boots-whilst speaking
to the boy belonging to the shop I saw both the prisoners—it appeared
to me as if both were noticing some boots hanging outside-they took a
great deal of notice of me-after I priced the pair of boots I looked round
and saw them close to my arm-I saw Lovell touch Davis, who imme-
diately went away, and went to the corner of Jewin-street, to the chemist's
shop-they looked at me very hard I had suspicion, and went a little
further, and stood looking in the shop of Mr. Reeve, a pawnbroker-1
saw Davis come by and look into a currier's shop, a door or two beyond
Mr. Williamson's, and immediately go back again-Lovell then went by,
and came back again-they went backwards and forwards several times,
sometimes together, and sometimes alone-five or ten minutes after first
noticing them the prosecutor was engaged taking his shoes in from outside
the shop-at last only one parcel was left hanging on the line-I saw
Davis go by, and, as I thought, untwist them, but when I went up
found part of the string on the nail, and it had been apparently cut-be
placed one hand on the boots, and the other on the top-I immediately
went and said to the prosecutor, "You have lost some boots," and called,
"Stop thief!"-Lovell was about fifteen yards from Davis when he went
up and touched the boots, about three doors off-he could see what Davis
was about-he made motions with his hands to him, and shook his head
--when I gave the alarm they both ran away-they turned down Jewin-
street, and into Jewin-crescent-Davis was stopped in Aldersgate-street
