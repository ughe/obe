BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT.
(Monday, September 13.)
JACKSON, Joseph Edward (57, labourer); between August 26
and 27, 1909, stealing one mare, the goods of Frederick Joseph
Atwell.
Mr. Eustace Fulton prosecuted.
FREDERICK JOSEPH ATWELL, 65, Roberts Road, Walthamstow, van
manager. On August 26, 1909, between 6 and 7 p.m., I saw my
blue roan mare at grass in a field by Folly Lane. The next morning
I found it had gone, and the following day, Saturday, I went to
Crossey's stable, at Holloway, and found the mare. Its value is
£5 10s.
PATRICK CROSSEY, 59, Wellington Road, Barnsbury, horse dealer.
Ou August 27, at 2 p.m., I was in Islington Cattle Market, when
prisoner came to me with the blue roan mare identified by the prose-
cutor and said, “I have got a knacker to sell you." I said, "How
much do you want for it?" He said, “ £4." I said, “Not worth it
she is only worth £3 for to kill." After some discussion he agreed to
sell it for £3. I then said, “I do not know you-who knows you!"
He said, "I am known by everyone." I said, "Before I pay you I
want to know someone who knows you." He then took me to the man
at the gate of the Cattle Market who passes horses in and clips the
tickets, and he stated that he knew the prisoner. I then paid
prisoner the £3. The mare was put in my stable and identified by
Atwell the following day.
Detective THOMAS CASTLETON, N Division, stationed at Waltham
stow. On August 30, at 10 a.m., I saw prisoner and told him I was :
police officer and should arrest him for stealing a blue roan mare on
the previous Friday morning from a field at Folly Lane, which he had
sold to Crossey for £3. He said, “You have made a mistake." I
said, “Well, I shall take you to the station, where you will be put up
for identification." He then said, “Well, look here; I will tell you
