JAMES Cox (City Policeman). On the evening of 30th June I
went with the last witness to Rushton Street and waited sonne distance

him by sight at that time, I kept the boy at the back of me and I said
“There is a man coming, be careful, is that the man?"--he said "It
is "-I followed the prisoner as far as 57, Rushton Street, and called to
him-he turned round and run up the steps and tried to close the door
upon me. I pushed it open and told him I was a police officer and
wanted him for passing a cheque on the City Bank-be said " All right,
can I see my wife?"-I said "Yes"-his wife said "Whatever is the
mutter?"-he said "I have got into trouble"-I took him in custody-
I took him to Bridewell Police-station, searched him, and found on him
221. 108. in gold and silver and 254 penny postage-stamps-in taking him
to the Mansion House next morning he said that the boy was in it.
Cross-examined. I asked the boy "Is that the man?” and be eaid
"Yes"-I didn't hear him say "I think it is "-I am sure he said "I
have got into trouble;" it may have been "Some one has got me into
trouble.”
