The next pay day would be somewhere about May 12. I went up to
2, Tokenhouse Buildings and asked for Martin, and Harris said he
was not in town. I told him I wanted a settlement of my account.
Harris said I might expect a settlement for certain the next pay day.
He said they had been hit very hard and had lost about £2,000 and
some of their clients were pressing them hardly. I waited till July 6,
when I wrote letter 101, to which I got no reply. I waited again a
long time and then called at the office, about three times altogether,
and saw Harris. He said something to the same effect as before,
Then I wrote letter 102 on October 27, and, not getting any reply, I
gave my solicitor instructions in the matter. I never had any part
of the money. My solicitor took proceedings on my behalf. When I
perted with my money, having read the circular, I thought the
Financial Guardian" was a paper; I did not know they were
bucket-shop people. From first to last I was not able to see Mr.
George Martin.
Cross-examined by Mr. Wellesley Orr. I did not understand this
was a speculation or a cover transaction. I did not expect to get the
scrip, but I would much rather have taken up the shares than lose
the money. I was prepared to find the value of the shares. I dis
puted the price at the time of closing the shares, but I was alniming
the difference shown upon the contracts. The amount of the profit
was put down at £118 15s. That was done by my solicitors. I was
not angry when I did not get any satisfactory reply in April, but
I could not think they were treating me fairly. I was a double fool
to invest with them again. I did not know whether Rio Tintos have
gone steadily up or not. It was a bad investment for me. I have
not got the money. If they had given me £40 I would have been de
lighted, but they would not pay anything, and they never have paid
