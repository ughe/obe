2526. DENNIS MCARTHY was indicted for receiving, on the 15th al
Aug., 6 balls of hemp, value 28.; lb. weight of nails, 2d. ; 3 leather backe
for boots, 2s. ; 2 leather fronts for shoes, 18.; and ilb. weight of leather.
8d. ; the goods of Henry Snead, of a certain ill-disposed person, well knowing
them to be stolen.
William FREEMAN. I am shopman to Mr. Henry Snead, and live in 'Tot-
hill-street, Westminster-Took has been in the service-about ten o'clock on
the 15th of August, I found a pair of soles in his trowsers when he was going
home- I went with bim to a house in Bedfordbury-the prisoner lives there
-he opened the door-I went into a room on the first floor-the prisoner
said it was his room-it was searched, and six balls of hemp, five soles, some
nails, three boot-backs, and some other things found-I believe them to be
my master's, but would not swear to them- the policeman continued the
search, and was tying up these things-the prisoner said, “You have no oe-
casion to search any further, the devil a farthing's worth more is there in the
place than what you have got"-I never saw him at my master's.
Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. You only know these from
circumstances ? A. Yes I cannot say when I had seen them safe-8d.is
not much under the value of the soles to sell, but if I went to buy them,
should have to pay 14d. or 16d. for them.
ROBERT Took. I am thirteen years old. I have been in the prosecutor's
service-the prisoner met me one day in New-street, Covent-garden-he said,
"Can you get me a bit of leather to mend my shoes with ?"-I said, "I will
get you a bit if I can"-he said, "Don't let any one see you"-next day !
gave him a pair of soles-he gave me 8d. for them-I then brought him a
half sole—he gave me 4d. for it, and said, “ Try to bring me some more i
you can"-I did so, and he gave me 6d.-he repeatedly told me to get me te
--all these things are Mr. Snead's property.
Cross-examined. Q. Did not he refuse to take goods of you? A. No-
I met him by the Boccius lamp-he kept a stall there-he knew my father
was a shoemaker, and lived up a court there.
William CHAPLEN (police-constable B 118.) I found these things in the
room.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Guilty. Aged 43.-Confined One Year.
