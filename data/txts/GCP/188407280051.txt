The Prisoners' Statement before the Magistrate. "It was given to me by
Standard, and I thought it was a valid document. I believed it was a
cheque. I had nothing to do with writing it. I do not know Lingard."
Prisoner's Defence. I have known Mr. Stannard nearly twenty years.
He appeared to be a very respectable man. He gave me this docu-
ment and asked me to go and buy the business, and promised to do
something for me. I did not know what it was, as I know nothing
about baking business or bills. He said "Put it away, it is as good
as a Bank of England note." I said "Why don't you go with me???
He said "I have got to meet my brother at the station." I went and got
the 158. 1d., but I never had fifteen farthings of it. Stannard arranged
to meet me next day at the corner of Brick Lane, and I waited there four
hours, but he never turned up. I never forged it, and did not know
the meaning of it. I am seventy-seven years old and was twenty-five
years in one shop.
