 1517.  JOSEPH HARWOOD was indicted for feloniously assaulting John Sheehan , in an open place, near the King's highway, on the 7th of July , at Isleworth , putting him in fear, and taking from his person, and against his will, a coat, value 50 s.; a waistcoat, value 6 s.; a handkerchief, value 2 s.; an umbrella, value, 2 s.; a shirt, value 6 s.; a pair of shoes, value 2 s., and twelve shillings, his property.
   JOHN SHEEHAN. I am a journeyman tailor, and live at Hampton-court. On the 7th of July I went to see the review on Hounslow-heath, and staid there till late in the evening. I was in a booth at the time this happened. I had taken a little drink, but swear that I was quite sober. I went into the booth between nine and ten o'clock with a young man who works with me; he is not here - and in a quarter of an hour, as I was coming out of the booth, (it was light,) I put my hand into my pocket, and took out half a crown to pay for some beer; and when I had paid the reckoning, some person struck me behind on the head - it was a severe blow; I could not see who it was - I was then dragged by two persons out of the booth - one of them laid hold of my arm. I had about 12 s. or more in my pocket. About twelve people then came and formed a circle round me, and I was shoved from one to the other for two or three minutes, and then the prisoner came up on my left side, and put his hand into my waistcoat pocket, and then into my trowsers pocket. I had two half-crowns in my waistcoat pocket; I put my hand to it after he had taken his out, and found the money was gone. I could not make the least resistance; there were so many people about me - I was afraid to resist. After he took his hand out of my trowsers pocket, I received a severe blow in the face, which knocked one of my teeth out. I was knocked down on the ground, and received several kicks. I got up, and then my coat was taken off my back. I then made towards the booth to save my life; they got between me and the booth, and prevented my getting in, and hawled me three or four yards from the booth. I was again knocked down on the ground - and one of them came and jumped on my legs, sprained it (I was under the doctor's hand with it) my waistcoat and shirt were then taken off my back, and the handkerchief off my neck, and the shoes off my feet - an umbrella was taken from me at the time; they took my coat - I was left naked all but my trowsers and stockings - my money was all taken.
 Q. The prisoner you say is one of the party who illused you, and took your money? look at him, and say if you can safely swear that he was one of them - A. I can, my Lord; he kept staring me in the face before this ill usage began. I had observed him staring at me, and can safely swear that he is the man - he was a stranger before. Nobody came to my assistance; and after my clothes were gone, I was naked. I was hopping about towards the barracks, calling
 "Murder;" they all left me when they had stripped me. I called
 "Murder" while they attacked me also; I made it known next morning, but did not go before a Magistrate till evening, in consequence of my leg being so bad. I went before Mr. Trimmer at Histon, and was obliged to apply to the overseers of the parish, being so ill, and went into the workhouse to be cured; I was fetched from the workhouse about a week afterwards, and saw the prisoner in custody, and was certain of his person, and stated so to the Magistrate. I am quite sure of it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is not your name O'Sheehan - A. No. I had drank part of three pints of beer, and had some bread and cheese between me and another man that day, and I had part of a pot of beer with another man at six o'clock that evening, and about seven I had about three pints in a booth, and had nothing more all day. I went to the review at twelve o'clock, but did not go to drink till half-past four, when it was over. I drank nothing else. None of my companions were drunk while I was with them. I was a little fresh, but nothing to affect me. This happened about half-past nine o'clock, it was light. I did not see the prisoner till I came out of the booth; he was not one of the three who first came up. They did not shove me about till he came up; they surrounded me, and then he came up to me. I did not observe him before. I was frightened - for I had cause so to be. There were at least twelve people around me: I never saw either of them before.
 JURY Q. Was it a rainy day - A. It was a rainy morning before the review commenced; it cleared up between three and four o'clock, and did not rain again.
