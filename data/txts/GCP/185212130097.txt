152. JOHN KILSBY and JOHN BRIDGE WICKERS were indicted
for a like offence.
Messrs. Scriven and CLERK conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH Wain. I am the wife of John Wain; we live at North-street,
Clapham. On 20th Nov. the prisoner Kilsby came about 25 minutes to 9
o'clock for a half quartern cottage loaf; I had seen him peep in at the window
before he came into the shop-he gave me in payment a bad ball crown-I
saw it was bad : I tried it in the detector and bent it-I told him it was bad;
he said it was not, and he had taken it just above-I took it from him, bent
it again, and took the piece almost off-I went round and took hold of his
coat-I said he was a smasher, and I would give him in charge-bis coat
gave way, and be got away; he walked away quietly-when he went out, a
boy named Brown came across, and I sent him for a policeman-I did not
see the boy again that night-this was on Saturday night, and on Sunday
morning he brought me the same half crown that I had bent-I gave it to
the policeman.
Cross-examined by Mr. Robinson. Q. Let me look at the half crown
1. This is it; here is where I bent it first, and here is where I beat it the
second time.
HENRY ARCHIBALD Brown. I was called by the last witness ; she had
hold of the prisoner, but he got away-she sent me for a policeman-when, I
got to a wall I saw Kilsby chuck something over-the next morning I got up
and went there; I found a half crown, which I took to Mrs. Wain-1 spoke
to the constable-I saw Kilsby join the other prisoner 200 or 300 yards from
Mrs. Wain's; they went together-I think they were talking together,
EMMA GOLDINO. My father keeps a grocer's shop, at Claphamn. On 201h
Nov. I saw Kilsby between 8 and 9 o'clock in the evening-he came for a
quarter of a pound of cocoa and a quarter of a pound of sugar-I served him;
he gave me a sixpence, I gave him the change-1 gave the sixpence to my
father, who was in the shop; the prisoner had just left the shop-I gave the
sixpence to my father because I saw it was bad- my father gave the sixpence
to a boy named Taylor, who ran after Kilsby-Kilsby was brought back by
the constable.
ROBERT GOLDING. I am the father of the last witness-Kilsby gave her
#sixpence, and she gave it me directly; it was bad- I gave it to Taylor,
be left the shop with it, and followed the prisoner-be returned in abont ten
minutes, and Kilsby was brought back by a policeman, and the other prisoner
was brought in with him-the boy brought back the sixpence and gave it to
me: I marked it.
WILLIAM John Taylor. I was in the service of the last witness. I say
Kilsby in my master's shop, and Miss Golding served him-he gave her a
sixpence; I took it from my master and followed Kilsby-I saw him and
the other prisoner together about a minute after I left my master's shop; they
were about fifty yards from the shop-I asked Kilsby to give me the right
money-he said, " I bought a quarter of a pound of cocoa and a quarter of a
pound of sugar "-I said, "I know you have ; and you have given my master
a bad sixpence "-he said, “Oh, no, I bave not"-they then went on further,
and I followed them up, Wickers ran back and said if I followed them he
would smash me I met a constable, and be took the prisoners-I showed
the sixpence to the policeman; he told me to hold it tight, and I gave it to
my master-I saw him mark it-the constable and the prisoners were there.
