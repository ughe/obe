By the JURY . They left me on the parade about eight o'clock on the Tuesday night, and I have no knowledge of their movements after that. FREDERICK JEFFREY. I am a clerk to the prosecutor, learning his business, and now live at Canterbury Road, Croydon—I was sleeping at London Road during the whole of July—I recollect Mr. Furness going away on 9th July, the day of the bean-feast—he came back on the 21st—he did not come back between the 9th and 21st—I looked after the men in his employment—I went to the men working at Flaxman Road and Belsize Road every day—he went away on Saturday, and I paid the men the next week—Mr. Furness had left me £8 or £9—I was at the County court on August 2nd, and heard part of what passed.
   Cross-examined.  I am eighteen years old—it is not true to say that I am the foreman—I had to get up pretty early—on Wednesday morning, 13th July, I went to the shop first—I think I was out of the house by 6.30—I had my breakfast out, but I went back at eight o'clock, because if there were any letters I was to forward them to Hastings—I had a latchkey—I did not know about a judgment summons, and did not hear the labourers talking about it—I did not find any letters when I went back—the six or eight men had a bean-feast—about four of them were constant—I got a cheque from Mr. Smith for about £6; I called on him, and got it after Mr. Furness went away—it was in respect of what he was the agent for—I kept the money till Mr. Furness came home, and handed it to him—I usually posted the letters about seven minutes to eight a.m.—I had to buy materials and pay for them. HERBERT SIDNEY( Not examined in chief ) Cross-examined.  I live at 11, Blackfriars Road—I am in Mr. Furness' service—on Friday, 6th August, the prisoner sent for me, and I saw him the next day—he told me that on the morning of July 13th he tried to see Mr. Furness, but could not, and at a quarter-past eight Mr. Furness came out of his house, and he put his hand on the gate, and prevented his going in, and served the notice, and he screwed it up and put it in his pocket—he asked me whether Mr. Furness was away from home in the week the bean-feast occurred, and I said that he was away from that day till the 23rd—I was engaged on the job in Belinda Road—Jeffrey was left to see how the work was going on, and to report to his employer—I do not. know that there was a rumour current that there was a judgment summons against Mr. Furness, but the prisoner told me some weeks before, and asked me to go and see him, and I heard a little of the circumstances between him and the governor, but with regard to the judgment summons I know nothing.
   By MR. BODKIN . I knew Powell about fifteen months before August—a letter was left at my house, and I went to his house; I had only been there before to work—I was working constantly at Belinda Road day after day—Mr. Furness did not come to see the work till we went for our bean-feast—I told Powell I did not know whether Furness had gone to Hastings or where he had gone—we did not know anything of his whereabouts—he asked me whether I should like to stay to breakfast; I said "No," and left.
