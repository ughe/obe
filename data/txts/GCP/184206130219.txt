RICHARD MORRIS re-examined. The wagon was going to Barclay's
brewhouse for grains-it is six or seven miles from my house--we did not
llow the men to take any bay at all-I should say three large horses
might eat half a truss on their return from London-there was 10lbs.
or 12lbs. of hay in the bundle, but they had considerably more than they
could have eaten in their nose-bags, and corn besides, had they stopped
out till eight or nine next morning--we had been robbed previous to this,
and ever since then have refused the men having bay it is unnecessary
--- carter would sooner take corn than hay for his horses--I do not think
I had spoken to the prisoner personally about it, as he was only two or
three months in our service-my father hired him-he came from Mr.
Egerton's service, with whom he lived five or six years; even if we did
allow hay, this would be an unreasonable quantity--the horses had been
in the stable baiting, three or four hours before they started--they would
return between twelve and one in the night-when I first saw the hay in
the wagon, I said nothing about it, because I was determined to see whe-
ther he made a property of it or not.

from under his waistcoat-I went, and asked the gentleman if he had lost

rush, and I lost him he did not come to the station.
