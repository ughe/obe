RENaLs, Mayor.
564. SARAH HOARE (68), and JOSEPH HOARE (23), Stealing
three wooden cases and thirty-three pieces of nun's veiling and 100
boxes of lace, the property of Gotfried Herman; Second Count, for receiving
the same.
MR. ALDERSON Prosecuted and MR. BURNIE Defended.
JOHN WIse (City Detective Sergeant). On 7th July, in company with
two other officers I went to 31, Costers' Buildings, Bunhill Row, where
I saw Sarab Hoare-I said to her, 44 I am s police-officer; I have received
information that a large quantity of stolen property is in your house, and
I intend to search for it "-the house is let by the London County Council,
and she occupied the premises at 3s. 6d. rent-in the first room I found
thirty pieces of nun's veiling in a pile in a corner of the room, covered by
sacking I produce a sample-I afterwards further searehed the room, and
on a bed found two sacks containing fifty boxes of ladies' hose, and under
the bed nine boxes, which were pushed behind other boxes--there was a
miscellaneous stoek in the place; it is a costermonger's-in the kitchen I
found three more boxes of ladies' hose, under the bed hid behind other
things-in a third room I found six or eight children's hats, new,
which have not been identified - I said to the prisoner, Can you
account for these goods, have you any invoices or papers to show where
they came) from ("she said, A tall German, who I do not know,
brought them here last Friday"- then sent her with Sergeant Scott to
Moore Lane Police-station, and afterwards, in company with Seott and
Detective Plummer, I went to Chapel Street, Islington, and saw the male
prisoner; he came up to his stall-I said, 44 You know me, Joe ; I have
searched your house since you left it and found a quantity of stolen
property" said, 44 If that is so, Mr. Wise, it has been left since I left
home " had seen him leave home a few minutes before I
went into the house-I took him into custody, and took him to Moor
Lane Station, where he was charged with his mother-I then went back
to the house and removed the goods to Moor Lane-I found other goods,
not the subject of the present charge--there was no servant employed
at the house-these are not the sort of things he was selling-two other
brothers lived in the house, they are away.
Cross-eaamined. Joseph lives there with his mother-I could not say
which room he oceupied-there were labels on some of the things some
had been removed, the owners' names were not on them-we rummaged
the place-I explained to the female prisoner that we were police-oflicers-
she knew me-on the way to the station the male prisoner said his
mother knew nothing about it.
JoHN SOOTT (Deteetive Sergeant G). I went with last witness to 31,
Costers' Buildings-I took the female prisoner to the stationor the way
she said, 46 A tall man brought the goods on Friday night, and has not
been there since the station the male prisoner said, 44 On Friday the
goods were left by a foreigner, who asked me to buy them, I admit the
goods were brought to my place for me to buy."
HENRY SNELGROVE. I am superintendent of Costers' Buildings the
female prisoner has been a tenant there since Oetober, 1889 she occupies
three rooms and a stable at 9s. 6d. a week.
Cross-examined. Her son lives with her; he keeps three or four
barrows in the shed.
