THOMAS FORTUNE. I am servant to Mr. Bennett, a butcher. On the
morning of the 25th of August, I saw Hadland and Grout come down 8
George's market, near the Elephant and Castle, with this mare-Grout
was riding it, and Hadland walking alongside of it-as they came dom
the market they saw me-Hadland called me by my name, and said,
“ Tommy, will you buy the pony?"-I knew him before very well-
said, "No; the pony will not suit me "-it was a very small pony, about
the size of a donkey-I said, “No; it will not suit me; it is blind, and
such an object I will not have it"-I called Mr. Bennett, my master, and
said, "Here is a pony perhaps will suit one of your little boys"-my
master turned round and saw it, and asked if I knew the two men-I said,
“Yes; I know them both "-master bid 30s. for it, and Grout took it-i
saw him paid-my master gave them a bit of steak, and they went over
the way and had sixpennyworth of gin-the pony was claimed the next
day, and master gave it up to Price.
Cross-examined. Q. You knew both the men very well? 4. Yes; !
have known Hadland six or seven years- I knew Grout by seeing him in
Smithfield-market-I knew where Hadland lived-Showell came to the
public-house while we were drinking the liquor, and master asked him
where he got the pony-he said he bought it at the Elephant and Castle,
and showed a paper with the name of the man he bought it of-I think it
was somebody at Bromley, but I cannot read-my master read it, and
Showell had the paper back again I think master gave 10s. too much; for
it is blind, and had a great wen at the bottom of its belly-I never say
such an object of a pony for the money-it could walk about-I am not
accustomed to horses much I have one myself-no doubt a blind pony
could find its way out of a gap.
Court. Q. You think if there were a small gap, the size of that box,
a blind pony would find it out? A. Yes-in fact I had one myself, and
if I turned him into a field, and there was the least gap in the world, be
would find it, and get out-he was blind.
