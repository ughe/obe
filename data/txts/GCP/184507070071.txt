Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Did you ever see Harding bring
anything to Cooke's ? A. No--I have not sufficient recollection of the
labels I saw to speak to them-I have heard Cooke find fault with Deane,
and at the time Deane leſt I heard him say he would serve his master out-
I afterwards met Deane one day and asked him for 18., which he owed me
- he told me he had plenty of money, and that he was receiving 58. a day
from Mr. Arnold's solicitor-he said he had been promised a good place in
town-he said, "I have got Cooke now, and I will serve him out" -I
never saw Cooke manufacture oxyde of iron-he never let us see anything
of it-he used to work late of a night.
Georor Trew (City police-constable, No. 26.) I took Harding in cus-
tods-I found some keys on him-one of them is a latch-key-I tried it
to Cooke's door, and it opened it.
George WARDLE (City police-constable, No. 325.5. I searched Cooke's
house on the 17th of June, I found there this copperas or oxyde of iron,
some ink-powders and marking-ink, and other things I have given Deane
about 10s. a week, about 21. in all--that is the utmost I have given him.
MR.THOMAS WONTNER. I authorized Wardle to give Deane just sufficient
to support himself, but no more--there was not the slightest promise of any
place made to him.
SAMUEL JAMES ARNOLD re-examined. There is about 7lbs. of this.
oxyde of iron—it is exactly like what we have in stock, and what we have
had for some time—we make it ourselves--1 do not know where to apply
to purchase it-I have been in the trade thirty years - Cooke did not
manufacture this at all,
Court, Q. Would the appearance or condition of this, vary according
to the intensity of the heat, or the length or shortness of the process ? A.
It would, very materially this has the appearance of what we make from
a given heat of the furnace-we were in the habit of making this article in
the same state, this is when Cooke lived with us-he might have been
aware that we had such a thing, but he had no concern with it-he was
porter, and if he had a little leisure time our warehouseman would employ
him in washing bottles, and, for aught I know, he might bave assisted in
making this stuff.
Cooke. I have made hundred weights of it at your house, and
could I not have made this from common green copperas in an iron pot?
Witness. You might.
Cooke. I have bought pounds of copperas to make it in a common
iron pot, but I do not want my boys to know how I make it.
JURY to SAMUEL JAMES ARNOLD. Q. Have you compared this with
other that you have in your possession ? A. I did not bring a sample
down, but I can keep it in my eye.
