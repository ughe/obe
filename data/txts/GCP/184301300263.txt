JOHN JAMES BERRY. I am a brush maker, living in Walnut-tree-walk,
Lambeth; it is my dwelling-house. The prisoner was lodging at my house
on the 21st of Dec., and on the 1st of Jan. I missed the articles stated in
the indictment, to the value of 10l. from my front-room up stairs, in a tea-
caddy-I asked the prisoner if she had been up in my front-room, and taken
any thing away-she said no she had not--l got an officer, and searched her
apartments, but could find nothing-this is my ring-it is the only article
that is found I am sure it is mine--all the other things were in the place
where this was, except the bandkerchiefs, which were in a drawer very near
to it--the prisoner lived with a man there.
Cross-examined by MR. WILD. Q. Is this a lodging-house? 4. No-
very few persons come to see me, I am very much out.
John POOLE. I am a pawnbroker, in Great Queen-street, Lincoln's Inn-
fields-- I took this ring in of the prisoner, on the 21st of Dec., in the name
of Ann Brooks
Cross-examined. Q. You carry on a good business? 4. Yes, a number
of persons come to my shop.
Guilty of stealing the ring. Aged 24.-Confined Two Months.
828. CHARLES PETERS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of
January, 56lbs. weight of coals, value 6d., the goods of Robert Gammon, in
a vessel on the navigable river Thames.

GEORGE WALKER JUDGE. I am a Thames-police inspector. About eight
o'clock in the morning of the 21st of Jan., I was crossing the water from
Prince's-stairs, and saw the prisoner in the after-room of a barge called the
Bill, belonging to Mr. Gammon-I saw him bring a tub of large coals, and
put into his boat-I shot astern of the barge, and got into the boat I told
him to row me across the river-I showed these coals to Mr. Gammon, and
he recognized them.

me a hand to pull this barge in, then you may have them." I had other coals
in the boat.
