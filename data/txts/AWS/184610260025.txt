JAMES PALMER police-constable B 44.) In consequence of instructions
I received, at half-past two 'clock I began to watch the prisoners in Sloane-
street--I saw them go into a coffee-shop, then into the Swan, and to Slack's
-they got there about a quarter to three-Miller went in first and Tustin
afterwards-] kept my eye on the shop, went up to it afterwards, and the
shopman beckoned to me to come in-I went in, found both prisoners, took
them in charge, and received a counterfeit shilling-they were taken to the
station, and put in a cell-I afterwards went into the cell, and found them
both there-Tustin was sitting down, she rose up, and two shillings fell
from her, which I took up, and produce-she had been searched by the female
searcher.
Cross-eramined Q. What did you go to the cell for ? A. To fetch the
prisoners- will swear it was not dark-there is only a grating-I took a
light afterwards-th prisoners had been stripped by the female searcherth
shillings, apparently, dropped from her clothes-] cannot say where; I did
not see them fall; I heard them fall as she rose-nobody had been in the
cell after it was cleared out that morning-it had been occupied at night
-the floor is of wood-I was just inside the cell door when the money fell.
JOHN FIELD.- am inspector of coin to the Mint. These three shillings
are counterfeit, and have all been cast in one mould.
Cross-ecamined. Q. Are there not a great many shillings of the reign of
George the Third A. There is an extensive circulation of money of that
reign.
