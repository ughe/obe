   JAMES WHITE(police-constable, K 285). I was sent for to Sutton-street, Commercial-road- I found Lane, the prosecutor, and his daughter - he desired me to take charge of her for stealing property to a large amount - I asked what had become of it, and Lane gave me forty duplicates - I took her to the station-house, and searched her, but found nothing else - the next morning I went to the cell, and she gave me seventy more duplicates - I then searched her more closely, and found another pinned up in her bosom - I went the same evening to Red Lion-passage, Spitalfields, where I found Kinsman in a room with her two daughters - I asked her if she had any duplicates relating to Mr. Preston - she denied it three times - I said,"You will have no objection to my searching?" - she then said she would go and get them - I went with her to Elder-street, Shoreditch, and she took a bag in which I found 111 duplicates, out of which Mr. Preston identified 17 - two of them Lane said she gave to her, the rest she knew nothing about.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. I understand she gave you every facility? A. After I had taxed her with it she did - I believe there were seventeen duplicates which related to Mr. Preston's property, and the others to her own property.
   THOMAS HAMES(police-constable K 211). When the prisoner Lane was in the cell, she called to me, and gave me these two spoons, which were in an old handkerchief - she said, "These will make up the complement of Mr. Preston's which are lost."
   THOMAS VESPER. I am a pawnbroker. I have a great variety of articles pawned by the two prisoners at different times - Mr. Preston identified the whole at my house - the prisoners sometimes came singly, and sometimes together.
   WILLIAM HUMPHRYS. I am shopman to Mr. Folkard, a pawnbroker, in the Commercial-road. I produce a great many articles which relate to this indictment - the greater part were pawned by Kinsman, some by Lane, and some by another female.
 MR. PRESTON. These are all my property.
 Cross-examined. Q. Lane is married? A. Yes. for any thing I know - I did not see her husband when I first went, but he made his appearance some time afterwards - he was living there nearly the whole time - he left this country just before this discovery took place - I think on the Sunday before - he came out of his room and took leave of me - he said he was going off with the Coldstream to the East Indies - I have no reason to doubt that they were man and wife - Kinsman used to come and work there- I do not know whether Lane sent her out to pawn things- Lane said before the magistrate, that Kinsman knew nothing of it.
 COURT. Q. How often was Kinsman there for the last four or five months? A. Almost every day - she generally waited on me, and cleaned the room - she must have known the property was mine.
 Lane's Defence. Among those articles there is a gold pin of my husband's, which has been in his possession for years - what I have done, I have owned to - the other prisoner is innocent.
 (  James Roper, broker, of Charlotte-street, Whitechapel, and Mr. Cressal, a cabinet-maker, gave Kinsman a good character.)
 LANE -    GUILTY. Aged 45.
 KINSMAN -    GUILTY. Aged 40. Transported for Seven Years.

 1107.  JOHN KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of June , 1 till, value 1s.; 2 shillings; 1 sixpence; and 2 pence in copper monies, the goods and monies of Benjamin Battsort.
   SARAH BATTSON. I am the wife of Benjamin Battson- we keep a baker's shop at Stoke Newington. On the 25th of June, I was in the sitting-room, and saw the prisoner in the shop behind the counter - I ran to the door - the prisoner came from behind the counter, and dropped the till on the step of the door - this is it - it had 2s. 8d. in it - the prisoner ran off - I called my next door neighbour, and he was pursued and taken.
   DANIEL BLAKELY. On the evening of the 25th of June I was sitting inside my door - I heard a noise and went out- I saw the prisoner proceeding from the prosecutor's - I pursued, and took him. GUILTY. Aged 17. - Recommended to mercy. Confined Six Weeks.
 1108.  MARY KEYS was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , 1 bag, value 1s.; 1 purse, value 6d.; 1 half-crown, 12 shillings, and 3 sixpences, the goods and monies of Maria Susan Quantin , her mistress.
 MARIA SUSAN QUANTIN. I am a  widow. The prisoner was my servant- she came for a month on trial, but finding at the end of five days that she did not suit me, I wished to part with her - I was about to discharge her on the 10th of June - I laid my bag on the table in the back kitchen where she was; it contained my purse and 16s. - I missed the bag, and the prisoner was gone to the water-closet - I went there, and heard the sound of money - I sent for the officer - he went to the water-closet and drew up this purse- the bag has not been found - I suppose she threw that down first.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Did she not suggest your sending for an officer? A. Yes; I had not liked her conduct and she was impertinent - she had no money when she came to me, for I wanted a penny the next day - I keep a lodging-house.
   FRANCIS ATKINS(police-constable S 101). I was sent for - the prosecutrix said she was about to discharge the prisoner, and had missed her bag - the prisoner then went to the water-closet - I did not go there with her, but I got a light and went afterwards - I got the tongs, and found this purse - it had no money in it - I found 16s. on her.
