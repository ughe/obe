   Prisoner. Q.  Was I not at the door of your shop when you accused me? A.  No, I brought you back from where you had got to—you had a piece of timber in your hand, not under your arm.
   JURY . Q.  Are you quite satisfied that you saw him with the fowl under his arm going out? A.  Quite—the door is narrow, and you are obliged to turn rather sideways to get out—it was not wood that he had under his arm, the wood was in his hand. JOSEPH HOLLOWAY( police-constable R  137.) I was called, and found the prisoner in Mr. Nicholson's shop—I afterwards went and found the fowl at the step of the next house—the witness identified it—the prisoner first said he knew nothing about it, but when Mr. Nicholson was called, he said he did it for a lark , and when he was taken to the station-house, he said it was his own.
   Prisoner. Q.  Do you say that I said I took it for a lark? A.  Yes, you had a stick of wood in your hand—I did not say I would help you up  —I said you had better be careful not to say anything to criminate yourself.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The man and I went into the shop; the witness was serving him; I came to the door to go out; I did not go two steps from the door, and was returning again, when he came and accused me of taking a fowl; I said I knew nothing about it; I was not going to run away; if I had taken it it is not likely I would have returned. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined One Month .
