   WILLIAM MAYDWELL. I am clerk to Mr. Henry King, a meat salesman of Newgate-market. I know the prisoner, and had frequently paid him money - it was not usual for him to produce an order. On the 4th of March there was a balance of 19 l. 18 s. 5 d. due to Mr. Holden for pigs delivered between five and six o'clock that morning - it was customary to settle the same day, and the prisoner came about half-past ten o'clock that morning, which was rather earlier than usual - he said he called for Mr. Holden's bill for articles sent by Fielden and Sadler. I thought it was all right, and I paid him nineteen sovereigns, seven half-crowns, and one shilling, and he gave me 1 d. In about a hour and a half Mr. Holden came for the bill; I told him I had paid his man; he said he had not told him to come, but perhaps he had taken it home - I saw the prisoner about a fortnight afterwards at Guildhall.
   WILLIAM THACKER. I saw the prisoner apprehended, and saw him searched; he had no money about him at all - he denied having received this bill.
     GUILTY. Confined Nine Months.
 855.  EDWARD HEMMINGS was indicted for a fraud.
   LEWIS RICHARDSON COLES. I am the servant of John Moreland, an umbrella manufacturer, who lives in the Minories - I know the prisoner very well. On the 31st of March he came to our shop, about half-past three o'clock, and brought a written order, signed by Mr. Barnes, which order lost - it was for four 4 feet 9 inches silk umbrellas - they were invoiced at 4 l. 18 s.; the value of them was about 4 l. 8 s.; they were booked to the credit of Mr. Henry Barnes, No. 13, St. Martin's-court. When I read the order I told the prisoner it was not Mr. Barnes's writing; he said Mr. Barnes was out, and Mrs. Barnes wrote it - I believed that, and gave him the goods he applied for. Mr. Barnes came to our house on the 2d of April, and I asked him how he approved of the articles; I then found I had been imposed upon. About half-past five o'clock the same evening the prisoner brought another order for two more of the same kind, as from Mr. Barnes; we sent for an officer, and gave charge of him. I knew he had formerly worked for Mr. Barnes, and supposed all was right.
 HENRY BARNES. I deal with Mr. Moreland. The prisoner had been in my employ about seven years, but had left me about nine months; he had no authority to apply for any umbrellas, on my account, on the 31st of March - he had no order given him to get them, and I have never seen them.
 Prisoner's Defence. I must leave myself to the mercy of the Court - it was distress that caused me to do it.
     GUILTY. Confined Three Months.
 856.  HENRY HALL was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   UNDERWOOD PRICE. I am an ironmonger; I live at No. 13, Cow-lane, St. Sepulchre's. I have an area to my house. On the 25th of March, I went to bed about eleven o'clock at night, and next morning I found three of the squares in the area window had been broken; a man could have put his arm in and unbolted the window; the prisoner had been in my employ for three years as an errand boy - but I had discharged him on the Saturday before.
 THOMAS PUGH. I am servant to Mr. Price; it is the warehouse window which was broken; it was safe after dusk; I was up stairs and heard the window broken; I came down and called the watch; I took two watchmen inside, and left one outside; we found the prisoner in the area, in a state of concealment; he must have lifted up a heavy grating to have got down; there were three squares broken quite out, and some more cracked; he could then have undone the fastenings.
   JAMES WILLIAMS. I am a watchman. I was on Snow-hill, and was called to Mr. Price's house; I found the prisoner in the area; the window was broken, and the frame of the window had been wrenched four or five inches - he did not appear to be at all in liquor or insensible.
   JOHN SHEPPERD. I was the constable of the night. The prisoner was brought to me; I found a piece of liver, and a phosphorous box on him; and in the area, we found a small iron crow, which they call a jemmy, and a knife.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence; stating that he had met two men, who made him intoxicated, and then gave him a crow-bar, and lifted up the area grating and persuaded him to go down, which he refused, but they forced him down, and in the scuffle the glass was broken.
     GUILTY. Confined One Year and Publicly Whipped.
