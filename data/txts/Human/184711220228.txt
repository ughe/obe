WILLIAM TRACEY. I am a linendraper, at Wandsworth. On 3rd Nov., between four and five o'clock, Stepney came to my place, and another girl, who I have no doubt was Moggeridge—they purchased these articles, and paid about 4 s . for them. JAMES STAPLENTON COLLINGBOURNE. I live in Wandsworth. I know Step-ney and Moggeridge by sight—about three weeks ago they came and bought two pairs of boots of me, at 5 s . 6 d . a pair. JAMES DAVID CHANDLEY. I am shopman to Mr. Hyde, a linendraper at Wandsworth—I believe it was on 4th Nov., Stepney and Moggeridge came together and bought two shawls, at 10 s . 6 d . each, and some lining for dresses—they laid out altogether 24 s . or 25 s .  249. JOHN WHEELER , stealing 40 bushels of oats, value 5 l  . the goods of John Dudin , in a barge on the Thames. JOHN ADCOCK. I am a deputy corn-meter, at John-street, Horsleydown. On Monday, 4th Oct., I went on board the Tennant  —I was employed to deliver 330 quarters of oats, 260 into a barge called the Eclipse , and 70 to another barge—Hopkins acted as lighterman in taking the oats and stacking them in the Eclipse  —he was convicted on this indictment last Sessions—he was in the service of Mr. Dudin—when I came up on deck, I saw a skiff under the bow of the vessel, and noticed seven or eight sacks in the skiff—I addressed myself to Hopkins—the prisoner might be then about three or four yards off—he was within hearing of what I said—I asked Hopkins whether Wheeler worked for Mr. Dudin—he said, Yes, he did, it was all right—Hopkins and Wheeler had some conversation—I saw Wheeler take the skiff away, and row it towards Prince's-stairs—I did not count the quan tity in the skiff—there were seven or eight sacks in it when I came on deck.
