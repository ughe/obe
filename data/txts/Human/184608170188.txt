 1696. JANE GILL and JOHN JONES were indicted for stealing 1 cash-box, value 2 s  .; 3 pair of sugar tongs, 245.; 1 child's coral, 16 s  .; 3 necklaces, 10 l  .; 14 rings, 6 l  . 10.; 1 butter-knife, 12.; 2 veils, 11 s  .; 1 pair of spec tacles and case, 1 s  .; 10 sovereigns, 10 half-sovereigns, 2 half-crowns, and 2 shillings; the property of Elizabeth Barkley , in her dwelling-house; and THOMAS WEBB was indicted as an accessary to the said robbery.
   MR. BALDWIN conducted the Prosecution. ELIZABETH BARKLEY. I am a widow, and keep a house of accommodation in Rupert-street, Whitechapel—the prisoners Gill and Webb lodged in my house for about a fortnight—they left a fortnight before the robbery—I desired them to leave—on the 17th Gill came to my house—she said she had met a friend from the Indies, and she was coming that evening to stay—she came about ten o'clock in the evening with the prisoner Jones—we were in the kitchen —they fetched some gin and half-and-half—they went out again and came back j in about an hour—it was before twelve o'clock—(when Gill came she said she was coming with a friend from India, and told me not to tell Thomas Webb, as she did not wish him to know)—when they returned I fetched some brandy—Gill and Jones made some brandy and water—one made it and the other sweetened it—I drank half a glass of it, and did not like it—the rest wai 1 chucked away—my servant Woodward drank some of it—Gill and Jones went out of the room to go to bed, and took a candle and candlestick with them—their bed-room was on the second floor—I had told Gill the room I she was to go to—she knew all the rooms in the house—it was not the I room she had occupied when she lived there, but she particularly wanted I me to save her the best room—I found afterwards that no room had I been occupied—after I drank the brandy and water I fell asleep—at that tine I the outer door of the house was open—it is not usual to go to sleep and leave I the door open—my servant was in the room—I awoke about six o'clock in I the morning, before the servant—I shook her and awoke her—I then went to I my bed-room, which is on the same floor as the kitchen, to go and lay down I—I found the room disordered, and a great hole in the bed like the mark of a I cash-box—I examined my drawers, missed my cash-box, and 15 l . 10. in I gold, and two lace veils—I had seen the things safe the same evening, the 17th—the cash-box had been locked in a drawer, which had been opened as I if unlocked—the veils laid on the box—the box contained a number of trin kets and jewellery—I found the candlestick which Gill had taken up to go to I bed, in my bed-room—I am sure it was the same—the
