I am a journeyman butcher, in the service of Mr. May, near Harelsdon-green. He supplied goods to the two servants, Grafton and Foster—I gave them some meat and some grocery things—I do not know to what amount, nor for how long—I delivered several joints of meat.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You went to the house? A.  We sent down, and took orders. JAMES POPE. I live at Harlesdon-green, and am a milkman. I know the prisoner Norman—I do not know the other female prisoner's name, but she was the person who was in the house, and I know Foster—they came to live in Mr. Tubbs's house on the 18th of August, I think—I sent to the women in care of the house to say that I should be happy to serve the lady, and then they sent to me, I think, but I was not at home—I sup plied them with cream, and butter, and eggs, up to the 27th of that month, to the amount of 1 l . 17 s ., odd-Norman left the cottage on Mon day night, the 27th—I saw her in the morning of that day, and took my bill—she said I had done quite right to bring my bill, and she would settle with me on the morrow morning—I saw Grafton and Foster there, acting as servants—I asked Foster what his mistress's name was—he told me her name was Norman, that she came from Bath, that she was a widow, that there was a carriage and horses, and daughters, and that he expected them to arrive every day—I went up on the Tuesday morning, and they were all gone—I had taken them a roasting pig on the Sunday morning—I took it up in a dish, and delivered it to Grafton—I asked her for the dish, and she said I might leave it; and one day, when I took some butter, I left a basket there, which is a usual thing-people sometimes say, "You may leave it," and we do.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDSRGAST . Q.  You do not blame them for giving you their custom when you call for it? A.  No—I did not go to them for custom myself, but my mother did—I was not at home when the lady arrived, but my mother was let know that the lady had come, and then she went-Foster did not tell me that the horses were sixteen hands high—he did not say he had seen them—he did not say any thing to show that he had not just been engaged. WILLIAM BILLINGDON. I am a corn-dealer. My account of goods supplied to Mrs. Norman, at Mr. Tubbs's cottage, between the 21st and 25th of August last, was 10 l . 0 s . 4 d .—I never saw Mrs. Norman myself—after they left the cottage I fetched the sacks away—they were corn sacks, marked with my own name—I found them after they left in the kitchen—there did not appear to have been any horse there at all—there was a small quantity of straw in the coach-house place, or stable, which had been packed up with other goods.
