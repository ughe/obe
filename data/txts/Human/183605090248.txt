GEORGE GREEN. I assisted in taking the body of Mrs. Haggerstone out of the well, and took it down to the White Hart. I afterwards were so Haggerstone's—I did not see him—I heard his voice—I knew him before—when we told him his wife was very poorly down at the White Hart, he said he would not come—we then told him his wife was deadhe said he could not help it, he would not come until the morning, to tell Mr. Parsons—Parsons is the landlord of the White Hart—Steel was with me—it was a very dark night—I did not go to the house again. WILLIAM CLEAVES. I found a bonnet and a bit of an old handkerchief, in a ditch about seven yards behind the back of the well, on Sunday morning, after Mrs. Haggerstone's death, there was only one string to the bonnet—I found the other string on the Monday morning in the the Short-three-acre field—the well is forty-nine rods and a half from the Short-three-acre field—it is at the top of the Winding-shot field—I gave the bonnet and string to Hart the constable. JOSEPH JOHNSON. On the Monday after Mrs. Haggerstone was found in the well, I found a cap near the top of Winding-shot field, on the Wyatts side—I found a stick about a foot and a half from the laid up on the bank—there was a place on the bank where somebody had been sitting down—it did not appear as if more than one person had been sitting there.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was it a sloping bank? A.  Yes—it appeared as if somebody had been sitting down—not lying down, the grass was just pressed down—I observed no footmarks there. HENRY HART. I am a constable.
