NEW COURT.-Thursday, September 27th, 1877.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
731. EMILY CHARLTON (33), EDWARD CHARLTON (37), and
HENRY CLAYTON (40), Stealing a chest of drawers and other articles, the
property of Arthur Leonard Seeley, in his dwelling-house.
MR. C. MATHEWs conducted the Prosecution; MR. WARNER SLEIGH appeared
for Emily and Edward Charlton, and MR. FULTON for Clayton.
MARY SEELEY. I am the wife of Arthur Leonard Seeley, and now live st
15, Morton Street, Clerkenwell-in June last, I had words with my husband
and he went away in a bit of temper, I remained at 2, Hornsey Park Road,
where we were then living-Mr. and Mrs. Charlton lived at No. 3-when
my husband left me I became intimate with Mrs. Charlton, who encouraged
me to drink, and that went on till 5th July, when I removed to 86, Euston
Road, by Mrs. Charlton's advice to avoid my husband-Mrs. Charlton
ordered the vans and told me to "shut up" as I had nothing to do with it,
and she would not allow me to say anything-on 4th July, I was very
intoxicated and went to bed all the afternoon, I awoke between 3 and 4
o'clock next morning-1 had not then quite recovered-I heard something
moving about at the back of the house - I went into the back-room, and
saw Clayton with a chest of drawers on his back--he put them over the wall
to Mr. Charlton, and Mr. Charlton senior-they had all been packed full of
goods ready for removal, by myself and a servant-afterwards Mrs. Charlton
came upstairs and said "Make baste, the van is come " I went to look, and
saw a van-I said “How did you get into the house !"-she said "By the
window"-I said "I fastened the window"--she said “Oh, we managed
it," and when I went downstairs the kitchen window was wide open-I had
employed Clayton to remove the things-the removal then took place,
Mrs. Charlton taking part; I was about the house till the vans were loaded,
when I and Mrs. Charlton got on the top of one to go to Euston Road-W0
went in a roundabont away to avoid anyone seeing me who knew me-Mrs.
Charlton directed everything-sbe got down before we got to the Euston
Road, because a man was following the vans, and she said "I don't know
you "--we got there about 7 o'clock; I did not look over the things tills
day or two afterwards, when I missed the things mentioned in this list (pro-
duced); afterwards Mrs. Charlton visited me and I asked her if she had any
of the things-- she said "I have not "--my husband joined me at Euston
Road, and on 10th July, Mrs. Charlton came again-my husband was at
home then-I begged her to give up the things" – I said "I am sure you
had the things"-she said "I had not - I said “My husband will
prosecuie you unless you bring them back"-she said she had nothing
belonging to me at first, and then she said “Yes, I will give them up"-I
gave her a dress, and paid her 308. for her trouble in removing the goods--I
did not pay Mr. Charlton anything- I did not engage him; 1 paid Clayton
78. 6d.at the time of the removal I was possessed of 101.
Cross-examined by Mr. W. Sleigh. This is the first time my husband
left me- I had lived at Hornsey Park Road six weeks-my husband is a
reader to the press--be lived there three out of the six weeks-he did not
say he would not live with me any more-I swear that I did not get
intoxicated before I knew Mrs. Charlton; I did not drink, except a glass of
ale at meals when I felt inclined; not wine or spirits-drinking had nothing
to do with the quarrel with my husband-we were half-quarterly tenants in
