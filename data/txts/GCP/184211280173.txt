ARCHIBALD PENNYCOOK. I am a corch-maker, and live in St. Margaret's-
terrace, Westminster. I was in the company of the prosecutor and his wife, going
along the corner of William-street, about a quarter to one o'clock--there was
a mob-when we got up to the people I heard some words, " Go it, Scotty,"
or something-I made a halt, because I could not pass without interfering
Appleton came and asked what I wanted—I said I wanted nothing.--" Then,"
said he," do you want to fight ?"-I said, "No"-" What do you want ?"
said he-I said, "I want nothing but to pass"--he then left me on the left,
and I received a blow on my hat, and, turning round, I observed Nicholls with
his hand up, and down again on my hat, and so on followed the rest that were
round me; as many as could get round me struck my' hat-Phillips roared
out " Police," and ran off calling " Police”-1 leaped out from among them,
and ran after him-I stopped at the head of the street-a policeman came up
- Phillips told him he had had his watch taken from him-Carrington came
and inquired what was the matter, and was given in charge-Appleton then
came forward and made the same inquiry, and he was taken.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. When you came up there was
a fight going on ? A. Yes, but I did not see it; I heard the words,
"Go it, Scotty"-I had not spoken at all—I was near the centre of the crowd
--they got me in among them the blow Nicholls gave me was not when in
the centre of the crowd - it was the second bloy I received-I know nothing
of his fighting
Cross-examined by Mr. CROUCH. Q. When did you receive the blow?
4. As soon as I got to the edge of the mob-my back was towards them-I
do not know whether they call it bonneting—it broke my hat, but it did not
go over my eyes—Appleton did not strike me-I was not going into the mob
-I did not see Appleton do anything.

ISABELLA HOOPER. I was in company with Phillips and Pennycook-my
sister was there, but, from ill-usage, is not able to be here to-day--Penny.
cook received a knock on his hat behind- I said to the person, " What do
you knock the young man in that way for ? what a shame" -- he said, “What
a shame!" in a jeering way, and went away-I do not know that person-I
only saw one blow struck on his hat at that time-Phillips was just by his side,
and was struck by Appleton, as I understood from my sister-his hat was
knocked off-I went to pick it up, and some fellow tried to push me down-
I took it up, gave it to my sister, and ran after Phillips- I had no bonnet or
shawl on at that time, I had left them in the house-I only came out to see
them to the corner, just through a little passage into the street-my sister
was pushed and knocked about, which has brought on an illness.
