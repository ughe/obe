JOHN LANCELOT ATKINSON, registered medical praetitioner. I have
attended Mrs. Carlton, of Delaware Mansions, from November 5th
the present date, and was present when her deposition was taken.
She was very ill indeed, then; we almost lost her that night. She
is an epileptic. I met prisoner on November 6. I cannot say if he
is affected in that way. Epilepsy is hereditary; if both parents have
suffered from it there is great probability of the children having it
There are two primary forms of epilepsy called haut mal and petit
mal. In haut mal the disease is palpable to anyone connected with
the patient; in petit mal he has slight seizures, in which he loses
himself for & second and then returns back to his consciousness.
In conversation he would cease for a moment and then pick up the
conversation where he left off. Those are the more treacherous cases,
Previous to an attack of petit mal the victim is quite calm; there
would be no warning, and many times during an attack of petit mal
a person previously of good character and disposition has committed
terrible outrages. When he recovers he is quite calm; he may bave
some bazy recollection of what has happened or he may not
Prisoner's conduct in this ense would be quite consistent with a
attack of petit mal. A first attack may come on quite suddenly.
Extreme irritability would be a symptom. If a man with the family
history of the prisoner, whose two parents had suffered from epilepsy,
had two attacks of sunstroke it would exeite epilepsy or increase the
number of attacks; anything that reduced the patient's health would
do so.
