 1595.  RICHARD FARNSBY was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , 7lbs. of lamb, value 5s., and 1 iron hoop, value 2d., the goods of William Dix.
 WILLIAM DIX. I am a  butcher, and live at Limehouse. On the 18th of July I fell asleep in my counting-house; this quarter of lamb, and the hook were taken; I had not missed them till the officer brought the prisoner back.
   THOMAS GRAY. I am an officer. About five o'clock on the evening of the 18th of July I was in the New-road - I met the prisoner with a quarter of lamb under his arm, and a hook hanging to it; I asked where he got it - he said, from one Thomas Day, at Bow; he had some conversation with Marsden, and we went to Mr. Dix's.
   ROBERT MARSDEN. I was with Gray - the prisoner at first said he had it from a butcher, at Bow; I said I would walk till morning, but I would find where it came from; he then said he had it from Limehouse, and he took it through distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Fourteen Days.

 1597.  HANNAH DRISCOLL was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 6 half-crowns, value 15s., the monies of Thomas Platt.
 THOMAS PLATT. I live with my mother,  Ann Platt; she is a victualler, and lives in Covent Garden; the prisoner was her servant. On the 28th of July, about twelve o'clock, I missed six half-crowns, which I had seen safe in a bag three days before - I sent for an officer, and we at last found them between the mattress and the sacking of a bed, in which a lodger used to sleep - the prisoner was making it at that time; they were tied up in a bit of rag; as we went to the office, she said if I would forgive her she would tell the truth; I said I could not.
   WILLIAM MORGAN. I am the officer. I took the prisoner; on the way to Bow-street I heard her say to her master, if he would forgive her, she would tell the whole truth; she afterwards said at the office that she had taken them, and if he would forgive her, she would tell the whole truth - as we were going along she said if I would intercede with Mr. Platt she knew he would forgive her.
 THOMAS PLATT. All the half-crowns were found, but I had lost some more money in shillings - they were all dated 1825 - the drawer was locked, and I kept the key always in my possession.
 Prisoner's Defence. Mr. Morgan found the money under Mr. Platt's brother's bed.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1598.  HANNAH DRISCOLL was again indicted for stealing, on the 14th of July , 1 sheet, value 4s., the goods of Ann Platt , her mistress.
 THOMAS PLATT. I am the son of Ann Platt. I know this sheet to be my mother's - it is marked A. P., and is of the same quality and appearance.
   WILLIAM MORGAN. In searching her box I found this ticket.
   CHARLES NEWMAN. I am shopman to a pawnbroker, in Drury-lane. I produce the sheet, but I did not take it in.
 WILLIAM MORGAN. When I found the duplicate; she said her father had given it to her, and she had pawned it - she said a woman at Lisson-grove gave it to her afterwards.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1599.  MARY DAVIS was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of September , 1 jacket, value 15s., the goods of William Thompson.
 WILLIAM THOMPSON. Between twelve and one o'clock on the morning of the 2d of September, I was coming from Tower-hill; I met a woman, who asked me to go home with her; I said, I would, provided she would behave well to me, being a stranger; I went home with her, and gave her a shilling to get some liquor - she went down stairs, and never returned - that was not the prisoner; I pulled off my jacket and put it beside the bed, and laid down to see if any body came - a little while afterward, the prisoner came, and said, "Young man, you can't stop here, my husband and two children are coming to sleep here;" I got up, and said, "Where is my jacket;" she d - d me, and said what did she know about it, but she would take me to the girl who brought me there; I followed her to many holes and corners - she tried to trip up my heels, and got from me, and ran in doors - I sung out for the watchman; he came, but could not find her not the jacket; I cannot tell how long I laid on the bed; I suppose not more than a quarter of an hour; I laid dozing there; I was a little in liquor; I saw some person come into the room, but did not get up to see who it was.
   JOSEPH HALL. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned this jacket with me on the morning of the 2d of September.
   JAMES LEA. I am the officer. The prosecutor applied to me; I knew the prisoner by his description; I went with him to the pawnbroker's, and found the jacket.(Property produced and sworn to.)
