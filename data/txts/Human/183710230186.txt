MR. PHILLIPS conducted the Prosecution. EDWARD LONGDON M'MURDO. I am a wholesale druggist, and live in Finsbury-circus. The prisoners were both in my service, and I believe are husband and wife—about a quarter-past eleven o'clock on the night of the 1st of October, I went out—a friend had dined with me, and I accompa nied him part of his way home—I took with me a patent latch key—I returned about twelve o'clock, and let myself in—I made the door fast in side, as I always do—it was bolted and chained, and could not be opened from the outside by a skeleton key—I retired to bed—I had a particular wooden snuff-box, which I took out with me that night, and on returning home I put it on the mantel-shelf in my dining room, with two other silver snuff-boxes—I cannot be certain whether I saw the other two when I put it down—they are usually there, and I missed nothing—the male prisoner had the charge of my plate, and the key of my plate chest—next morning, about eight o'clock, I awoke, and the male prisoner came into my room, and said, "The plate is all gone, and the snuff-boxes are all gone"—I went down stairs, and saw the female prisoner, who appeared very agitated, and crying—she said she had picked up a telescope on the stair-case—I went down stairs to the butler's pantry, with the two prisoners, and the male pri soner said the plate drawer had been found open, and the plate was gone—some of the plate had been in use at tea time, the night before—I sent the man for a constable—one came, and two other officers were called in—a search was made, but nothing was discovered—the plate chest was kept in the attic—I went up, and examined it with an inventory, and missed the plate stated, and also the wooden snuff-box, which I had put on the mantel-shelf—I something was afterwards said by the female prisoner—Reynolds, another servant, was present at the time, and I rather think the male prisoner was sufficiently near to overhear her, but I cannot answer to that. LYDIA REYNOLDS. I am servant to Mr. M'Murdo. On the morning the robbery was discovered I saw the female prisoner, when she and my master were together—I think the male prisoner was present at the time, I but I am not certain.
   MR. M'MURDO ( continued. )The female prisoner said, "You may de pend on it, the whole mischief has arisen from your latch key"—and she said the robbery must have been during my absence—I cannot say whether she knew the wooden snuff-box was gone at that time—I have not re covered any of my plate.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are there any balconies outside your house? A.  Yes; the balconies of the houses are near enough to each other for a person to get from one to the other—the prisoners have been in my service two years—the male prisoner went for the policeman at my desire.
   COURT . Q.  With the exception of the wooden snuff-box, is there any thing you could have said was not taken while you were out? A.  No; I have a perfect recollection of putting the snuff-box on the shelf—I had been out about half an hour—the plate-drawer was in the butler's pantry below—nobody slept there—the servants slept in the attics—I lost a great many articles which ought to have been in the plate-chest—there wasa cream ewer, a great many spoons, and a silver waiter; but the prisoner tells me he had them down for cleaning.
