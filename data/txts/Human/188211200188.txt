Received of Mr. Everett the sum of 50 l . in part payment of 100 l . security on taking the ale and wine stores, 208, Crystal Palace Road. The balance to be paid on such date as arranged in Mr. Johnson's agreement. "On 17th May I took possession, and I then paid the remaining 50 l .—this is the agreement—I was to be manager at 1 l . a week; I was allowed to sell mineral waters and any extras I liked, and to have the profit on that, and I was to occupy the house and pay no rent; he would pay the rent, rates, and taxes—I was to do nothing to jeopardise the licence—I was not to take lodgers without his sanction—the alterations were not completed when I went in—a cellar was being excavated for the beer; they did not carry it further after I went in, and it is not finished now—beer was sent in by the prisoner on the Thursday, the day after I took possession—there were no stores for a grocer's business, or anything of that sort—on 27th May I opened the premises for the sale of beer—a beer engine had been fitted like an ordinary beerhouse—I asked him for a licence before I opened—he told me several times I need not fear; it was all right with the police, and no one would interfere with me—on 30th May George John Sayers, agent to Mr. Baxter, the brewer, called on me and made a communication, and on 7th June I saw the prisoner and spoke to him in reference to Mr. Sayers's visit—I said that I had been told that Mr. Baxter held the licence for the security of the stock and fixtures, and I asked him for the licence—he said he would go and get it at once—I did not go with him to Mr. Baxter's; he made all sorts of excuses, and said "I have somewhere else to go"—I went alone to Mr. Baxter's at the stores—I was told something there, and while there the prisoner came—I said "I have been given to understand that the licence is at 12, Furnival's Inn, at the solicitor's there"—I went to the solicitor's with Johnson for the purpose of getting the licence—the solicitor would not give it up, as he said it was there as security for money owed by Johnson—on 21st June I saw Johnson and told him that a gentleman had been for the purpose of serving him with a writ to restrain me from selling beer, wine, or spirits either on or off the premises—Johnson said "That is nothing to you;
