Court. Q. Did you see any part of the child strike the grate? A.
No-I saw him push it I do not know whether it struck the grate or

EDWARD Evans. I examined the body of the child after death-
never saw it alive-the chest and abdomen were very healthy, except a
small portion of the lungs adhering to the chest-that would not cause
death at all-I found the blood vessels of the head very much coagulated,
and a quantity of water in the cavities of the brain, which was the cause
of death many causes would produce that effect, such as a blow on the
head, or a fall on the head, and dentition with young children might cause
it, or when the stomach and bowels are not in good order-it had all the
teeth a child of that age would have-they were all quite cut, and had
been so, I dare say, for a year-dentition could not produce the effects I
saw in that child-a blow on the head, or disease of the stomach, might
produce it—there was no symptom of disease of the stomach at all the
child appeared to have enjoyed very excellent health-if it was thrown
with violence by the throat, and the head went against the fender or stove,
concussion of the brain would be the consequence, and that, jointly with
the terror of the child, would be sufficient to produce death-if the child
was quite well before, I should judge that that violence was the cause
of it.
Cross-examined. Q. Would excessive terror effect this alone? 4. I
believe it would-terror at seeing the mother ill used might cause unusual
sensation in the brain which would bring on reaction, and the same ap-
pearances would ensue.--I should say that trembling violently was one of
the sufficient causes to produce the effect-I must have observed the sto-
mach to be out of order if that had been the case-I saw nothing in the
state of the stomach which should cause the child to complain of pain in it
--if the head is bad for two or three weeks, the stomach generally becomes
weak, and sickness would ensue from compression of the brain-that
would account for violent pain in the stomach-all the symptoms I per-
ceived could be accounted for by terror and anxiety-I saw no external
marks of violence, but it must have got well in three weeks-the parents
did not point out any place where I could discover a mark--they pointed
out a scar on the left part of the throat, which had evidently not been long
there-it might have been five weeks, I cannot say-I have no doubt the
symptoms about the brain and head arose from a recent cause-it could
not have arisen from a blow six months previous-no doubt a previous
accident would produce a predisposition for such a cause, if the brain was
then affected, but if the functions were all right it would not.
Court. Q. If the child fell out of window, and was playing about
next inorning? A. I should say that injury was of no consequence what-
ever, and would not produce predisposition-all the symptoms I perceived
might be produced by fright, but it is most probable that the head fell
against some solid substance, and caused concussion of the brain.
MARY ANN SMALL. I am going on for ten years old-I saw my mother
struggling with the prisoner, and saw my sister Charlotte come up to her
---the prisoner took her by the throat, and threw her right against the fire-
place--the back of her head touched the stove, and her back was on the
fender-I am sure of that.
