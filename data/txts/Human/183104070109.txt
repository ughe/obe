   WILLIAM BROOKS. I was in the yard at Mr. Ridge's- I heard the cry of Stop thief! I went about twenty yards in front of Mr. Ridge's, and saw two coppers laying in a ditch, about sixty or seventy yards from the place they were fitted to; I took them, and went and fitted them in the places - they fitted exactly.
 Prisoner's Defence. A gentleman asked me to carry them across the field, and he was to give me 6d. for it - he ran away; I was frightened, and threw them down - the gentleman then called Stop thief! and I ran away; there was a man with the person who hired me, who called out Mr. Kendall - he was a tall man, with a blue coat and light breeches.
 MR. RIDGE re-examined. Q. How long had Kendall been away? A. Since about Christmas - I have not seen him since; he had the key in his possession.
 Q. How lately before these coppers were missed had there been a sale? A.Shortly after Christmas - I do not know whether the house had been empty; the brokers might have gone into it - it was not in my possession; Kendall was a gentlemanly man - he had the use of the fixtures, which had been left by a former tenant.
   WILLIAM CASTELL. I saw two men with a truck, in a lane twenty or thirty yards from the prisoner - it was a truck that would have carried the coppers; there was a field - the prisoner was going towards the truck; it was going on - I did not notice the men, nor see what was in the truck.
     NOT GUILTY.
 932.  JOHN BECKENHAM and JAMES WEBB were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of March , 1 bushel of oats, value 4s., and 1 sack, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Meredith.
   WILLIAM GREGORY. I am a Police-constable. On the night of the 16th of March I was on duty in Praed-street, Paddington - I saw Beckenham coming down with a sack of corn, and he asked me if I could tell him which was No. 137; I said I thought he knew as well as I did, it was not at that end of the street; I then asked what he had in the sack - he said a bushel of corn which he was going to take from Mr. Meredith's wharf to Mr. Whitmore's, and if I went with him there I should find it was all right; I went with him, and Mr. Whitmore denied all knowledge of it - I then took him to the station, and in going down the street he said, "The whole of it is, it is stolen corn, and I had it from James Webb from Mr. Meredith's stable;" I went and informed Mr. Meredith, and he gave charge of Beckenham - I went and took Webb at Mr. Meredith's stable, No. 9 wharf, Paddington; he said, "I own I gave the corn to Beckenham to take to Mr. Whitmore's, and I hope my master will look over it;" he said he was to bring back 4s.
 Beckenham. I said I was going to Whitmore's, but I am no scholar, and do not know the number; Whitmore said he had not ordered it, and I said I would go back and ask the master if he had ordered Webb to send it any where - if it was not ordered it must be stolen. Witness. No, he did not say that - the number he asked for is where he lives himself.
 THOMAS MEREDITH. I have a stable at No. 9 wharf Paddington - Webb is my  carman; I have seen these oats and sack - they are mine; my name is on the sack I have some of the same oats in my bulk; Beckenham was employed occasionally, and had been that day unloading coals - I believe I have been robbed to a great extent, and my horses have suffered; Webb has received money from time to time, and paid it over to me - I sell corn in small quantities.
 Beckenham's Defence. I was completely out of work; I did not know these were stolen.
 BECKENHAM - GUILTY. Aged 30.
 WEBB - GUILTY. Aged 31.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Three Months.
 933.  WILLIAM ANDERSON was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of March , 2 rings, value 16s., and 1 pair of ear-rings, value 15s., the goods of Elizabeth Rider.
 ELIZABETH RIDER. I am a  widow, and live in Elbow-lane, Shadwell. On the 5th of March the prisoner came to lodge with me, and remained till the Wednesday following - as I was opening one of my boxes he saw some duplicates; in consequence of information I examined the duplicates again on the 19th of March - I missed eight of them; one was for a jacket, another for a pair of trousers, another for a shawl, another for some ear-rings, and some for other things.
   WILLIAM HUDSON. I am an apprentice to Mr. Harrison, a watch-maker, in Upper East Smithfield. I purchased some duplicates of a person named Williams, who is gone to sea - I took the articles out of pawn.
     NOT GUILTY.
 934.  JOHN BRADFORD was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of April , 2 crowns, 1 half-crown, and 1 sixpence, the monies of John Franks , alias John Francis , from his person.
   JOHN FRANKS. I am a seaman, and lodge at the Dundee Arms, Wapping. On Saturday, the 3rd of April, I was at a public-house, and met a young woman, named Jane - I went with her to a house, where I saw the prisoner and two women; I then had 14s. 6d. in my pocket- the young woman was drunk; she invited me to go to the house - the prisoner asked me to play at cards with him, which I did; it was my deal - I took the cards in my hand, gave him six, and took six for myself; he let one of the cards fall on the table, and said it was a misdeal - we were playing at all-fours; the prisoner then said, "You d - d black b - r, you have got no money;" I put my hand into my left-hand waistcoat pocket, took out my money, and laid it on the table - he then gave me a sudden blow in the eye, and when I put my hand to my eye, he took up my money, put his hand into my pocket and tore it, but I had no money there; I went out of the house, and, as I did not know the number, I took out my knife, and cut four marks on the window-shutter- I got an officer, and the prisoner was taken sitting on a chair; I was sober.
