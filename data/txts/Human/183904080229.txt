1440. MARIA FORSTER was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of March , 1 purse, value 6 d  .; 3 sovereigns, and 3 shillings, the goods and monies of Joseph Reading , from his person. JOSEPH READING. I am an engineer. I am fifty-seven years old—I went to Woolwich on Good Friday morning—I had something to eat, and got too much drink—I was coming home, and turned down the lane by New Cross—the prisoner followed me—we went down the lane together—I missed my purse, in which I had three sovereigns and some silver, from my left hand pocket—I had seen the money safe a little before I went down the lane—I was rather disguised in liquor—I told the prisoner she had robbed me—she said she had not touched me, nor touched my pros perty—this is my purse, it has three sovereigns in it.
   Prisoner.  You asked me to go down the lane. Witness.  I did not—we had not been drinking for three hours together—the policeman was not coming after me for passing a bad sixpence. JOHN ROSCOE( police-constable R  119.) I was on duty near New Cross—I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner walk down Five Bell-lane—I fol lowed, and when I got near them, the prosecutor told me she had robbed him of his purse and money—the prisoner said, "I have neither seen purse nor money, so help me God"—I took her to the station-house. MART WALKER. I am searcher at the station-house, I found this purse and money concealed on the prisoner's person.
   Prisoner's Defence,  I was walking down the lane—he fell down, and was very tipsy—he had this purse in his hand—I helped him up, and he dropped it into my hand. GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Three Months.
 1441. THOMAS POPKINS , EDWARD COOK, and THOMAS CASSIDY, were indicted for stealing, on the   2nd of April , 110lbs. weight of iron, value 5 s  ., the goods of the London and Greenwich Railway Com pany . MARY ANN SMITH. I am the wife of Charles Smith, and live in Church-street, Deptford, and deal in marine stores. On the 2nd of April, the pri soners came to my house together—each one brought a bar of iron—they appeared to me to belong to the rail-road—I asked whose iron it was—they said Mr. Cook's—I said if Mr. Cook came himself I would buy it—they told me that Mr. Cook was outside—they went to call him and ran away, leaving the iron in my shop—the policeman came and took it next morn ing.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are you quite sure there was not one outside? A.  Yes, they were all inside. JAMES BARRBLL( police-sergeant R  8.) From information I apprehended Popkins on Friday morning—I asked him who the others were who were with him, when he took the iron to Mrs. Smith's—he said Cook and Cus tody, and he should not have taken it if they had not persuaded him. THOMAS WATSON. I am an officer of the London and Greenwich Rail-road Company. I have the Act of Parliament constituting the Company here—I have seen this iron, it is ours—there is but one other rail-road that has a rail of this description—theirs is a quarter of an inch lower than this.
   The document produced by the witness as the Act of Parliament, not having the name of the Queen's printer affixed, the prisoners were ACQUITTED.
