 I am a gardener at Tottenham. The constable sent me after this man, Smith, to take him, and I took him at the George and Vulture, at Tottenham, and brought him back, and delivered him to the constable, with the goods under his arm, ten pair of shoes and two gown pieces.(Produced.)
 Tanslev. I have had them in my care ever since. I found eleven silk handkerchiefs in the yard between Gillam's house and Miller's.
 Q. To Miller. These are the same handkerchiefs you delivered to the constable? - They are.
 Mrs. Gillam being a quaker declined taking an oath.
 Prisoner Tooke. On Monday, the 14th of this month, I was on my journey from Hertfordshire to London; being late in the evening I got a list as far as Enfield highway; from there I walked to Edmonton, where the Statute was then holden; I went into a booth, where I staid till quite day light; proceeding on towards London I had occasion to retire up a passage, where I had not been above two minutes before a man came in and called out Mrs. Gillam! and the people went out, and I went out, and this man laid hold of me, and said, that I had been in the house. What I stand charged at the bar with I am entirely innocent of, neither do I know any thing at all of the parties that were there. I was coming up to London in hopes of getting a birth on board a man of war.
 Prisoner Smith. As I was going on Lower Edmonton, to my friends, going along in the morning I see this bundle laying in the middle of the road, and picked it up; I did not know where it came from; about two or three minutes before that I met two men running up the road. I was bringing of it back, and there were two men came and catched hold of me, when I resigned directly, and they brought me back; I never said a word. I have had my friends waiting here several days, but they are not here now.
 Q. To Miller. Is Mrs. Gillam the owner of the house? - She is.
 Richard Smith,    GUILTY. Death.(Aged 23.)
   William Tooke, GUILTY. Death.(Aged 24.)

   JOHN BROWN sworn.
 I am a  sailor, a lodger; I was robbed the 5th of July, between the hours of eleven and twelve, I came up from Gravesend; I knocked at the prisoner's door, and the prisoner let me in. I happened to be picked up by her one night about a week or a fortnight before.
 Q. Where did she live? - In  Thomas's-court, I think the name is. I cannot say I was over and above sober, not I was not drunk; I was sensible of what property I had about me.
