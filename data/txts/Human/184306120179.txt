This property is mine; I bought this bottle in Tottenham-court-road; she says she saw me in her house, and I never saw her in my life till I was at the station; I could prove where I was, but the gentleman is out of town. Witness.  I am sure these things are my property—I saw Man ship in my house on the second night, and she had an opportunity of seeing me when I went into her room. JOHN EATON( police-constable S  193.) On Thursday evening, the 4th of May, I was sent for, and found Manship in a pawnbroker's shop, in the Hampstead-road—I told her she was charged with a robbery in Old Caven dish-street—she said, "I never was in Old Cavendish-street in my life"—I took her to the station, and the inspector asked where she lived—she said, "I will not tell anybody"—on the Saturday following, I went to a lodging in George-street, Hampstead-road—I found this water-bottle there, two china plates, this cup and saucer and stopper, and a duplicate for a silver spoon. ELIZABETH WEST. I live in George-street, Hampstead-road. On Sa turday, the 29th of April, the two prisoners came to my house for apartments—I showed them some, and let them to Manship—she brought some things is between eight and nine o'clock the same evening—on the following Wed nesday, I said to Vickers, who went by the name of Smith at my house, "I think I know your face somewhere"—she said, "Oh, dear, no"—I said, "I am sure I do; is not your name Vickers?"—she said, "No"—I said, "Do you know Mrs. Stevens, of Sussex-street?"—she said, "No"—I afterwards went with Vickers to the station, to inquire after Manship—I re member the constable coming, and finding these articles in the room, which I let to the prisoners—I was present when he found them—Manship told me Vickers was her sister. THOMAS BARRETT. I am shopman to Mr. Crouch, a pawnbroker, in Grafton-street East. This silver tea-spoon was pawned on the 29th of April, by Vickers—I am positive it was her—I knew her very well—this is the duplicate of it—I knew Manship.
   Manship. Q.  Have I ever sent any thing to your shop that was not my own? A.  Not to my knowledge—you have a great deal of plate at our shop—that is how I became acquainted with both the prisoners. ELIZABETH MARTIN. I am sister to Mrs. Armour. On the 25th of April, I was at her house in Old Cavendish-street, about ten o'clock in the evening—I saw Vickers in the kitchen, and Manship going up the stairs of the first floor—I am sure of the prisoners' persons.
   Manship.  I never was in the house in my life. Witness.  I will take my oath it was you.
   Manship's Defence.  The plates I received of an aunt of mine, at Turnham-green, named Carter; I had them before I was married; I had five or six; they have been all broken but three; when I moved to Mrs. West's I had the three; I bought the cup and saucer, and the bottle in Tottenham-court-road; the tea-spoon is mine; I had half-a-dozen of them; they are not marked, but I had some that are—I kept a very large and respectable house.
   MANSHIP  — GUILTY. Aged 33.— Confined Twelve Months .
   VICKERS  — GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Three Months .
