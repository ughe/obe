   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  Do you mean there is any particular connexion between Mr. Zetterquist and the prosecutor, which made it necessary to get the oil there? A.  No, but at the beginning of winter we could not get good oil, and on that occasion I told him to go to Zetterquist's—I do not mean to say he was obliged to go there always—he was master's groom and porter—he was not told to buy two gallons—I did not examine how much oil there was in the house when he went—whether there might be another gallon in the house which he bought elsewhere, I cannot tell—it was Mr. Franks's sovereign I gave him—I took it out of the cash-box.
   MR. ESPINASSE . Q.  Did you know whether your master was in the habit of dealing with Zetterquist? A.  Yes, for various things—The pri soner was told to go there, and we thought he was in the habit of going there. ELISHA KNIGHT. I am an oilman, and live in Charlotte-terrace, New Cut. On the morning of the 7th of August, the prisoner came to my shop, and bought one gallon of oil—he gave me a sovereign—I gave him 11 s . change—I think it was a five-shilling piece, two half-crowns, and a shilling—he returned to the shop a little after one o'clock, and said his master and a friend had been inquiring about the oil, and if they came he hoped I should say it was two gallons he bad—he said he had purchased one gallon a short time before, which he paid for out of his own pocket—he said if I did not say so, he should get the cook and housemaid, and all of them into a scrape, that they would get the sack  —he staid some time beg ging hard of me to say it was two gallons—I told him it was a system I did not approve of, and if he had purchased a gallon before, it would be quite satisfactory to Mr. Franks to say so—I urged him to tell the truth, saying it would be a gross falsehood on my part to say it was two when it I was only one—Mr. Franks and Mr. Harvey then came in.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you known the prisoner? A.  About a month—he had not purchased another gallon of oil at my shop, that I am aware of—I have only served him once—he was ten minutes with me desiring me to say he had had two gallons—his master then came in—he spoke to him, and then went away—I live very nearly the same dis tance from the prosecutor's as Mr. Zetterquist does—it is not two minutes difference—when the prisoner came the second time, he bought 1 d . worth of rotten-stone to polish harness.
