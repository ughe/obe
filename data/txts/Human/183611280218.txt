MESSRS. ADOLPHUS and DOANE conducted the Prosecution. JAMES WOOTTEN. I am a pensioner, employed by Colonel Jones The barracks, and guard-rooms, and stables at Plumstead Marsh are the property of his Majesty—there was a great quantity of hay there belonging to his Majesty—on Sunday morning, the 27th of November, I went and found the lock of the stable had been hampered—I missed from ten to fifteen trusses of hay from these stables which are near the barracks—I informed Corporal Ashcroft, and we traced the hay on the ground over the fence, by the stable-door down the manor, from twelve to fifty yards from Bur leys' house—there are four or five houses, besides Burley's—he is a gar dener—his house stands on the manor-way in the first row that leads to Plumstead parish—there is a fence round Burley's premises—there is a ditch between his house and the manor, that is, twelve yards from his house one way, and fifty, perhaps, the other—the track of hay ceased at Burley's about fifty yards from his fence—I came back and saw Blagg—he is a gunner, and frequently on duty at the guard-room—Corporal Ashcroft went into the guard-room—I saw Blagg come out—I said, "This is a pretty business"—then he said. "It was I that did it'—(the corporal was not then present)—I had not said any thing about stealing the hay—he said "I have the key"—he did not show me any key—he said nothing more about the key—I said I must make a report, because the property was all littered about the place, from the door—I reported it to Serjeant-Major Bollin—I had been in this stable two days before—we have got two trusses here—I first saw the hay in Burley's shed in the Marsh—I went there on the Monday with the Sergeant-Major—he asked him if he had any hay there, and then we saw some in the shed, part of which is here—it is part of what we missed from the stable—I bound it up myself, and have no doubt of it—I saw it on Friday—I saw about nine trusses at the prisoner's which I had missed form the stable.
   JURY . Q.  Were there any house adjoining his? A.  Yes, and the track was as near to the other houses as his.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  I suppose a good deal of hay is grown in the marsh? A.  Yes, in some seasons—Burley's is three hundred yards from the stable, and about fifty yards from the high-road—the hay that is carried from the Marsh would not pass by Burley's house—it is conveyed to the stables for the horses'use—there are no other farm-houses handy, there are some in Plumstead parish—those farmers would not carry hay past his house, because it is private ground—all the Marsh belongs to the King—it is not possible for hay to be carried across the Marsh in the way this went—this is, I suppose, two or three hundred yards from the main way—we found the first two trusses in the shed—I recognised them, because I cut and bound them about two months ago—they are not cut and bound like a hay binder does it—I know my own binding—I think I bound this, but I do not know.
   MR. DOANE . Q.  Had you missed some trusses from the stable? A.  Yes—these corresponded exactly with what we had and what we lost—there is no public way across the manor. ALEXANDER ASHCROFT.
