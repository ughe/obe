   THOMAS BUTCHER. I keep an oyster-shop, in Holborn. I know Tinson, a tailor, who lives at No. 131, High Holborn - he is the son of the landlord of the Old Bailey public-house. On the 9th of September I saw the prisoner at the King's Head; we staid there about an hour, and then went to the Adam and Eve - he left my company about a quarter-past eleven o'clock; he was drunk, and no one who saw him could avoid seeing that he was so - Tinson was with us; we had a great deal of brandy and water - it was a wet night; we were all nearly drunk - I went to get a coach, and when I came back the prisoner was gone; I have kept my present shop five years, and lived seven years eight doors farther on - I have known the prisoner fourteen or fifteen years; he is a hackney-master, and has had a very good character.
 THOMAS TINSON. My father is landlord of the Old Bailey public-house. I was with the witness and the prisoner at two or three public-house; we parted from the prisoner a little past eleven o'clock - the house was about shutting up; the prisoner was drunk.
 COURT. Q. Were you drunk? A. I cannot say I was sober - I was not drunk to lose my recollection.
 JOHN LAUDON re-examined. Q. Did the prisoner appear to you drunk? A. No, and if the inspector had been bound over he could prove the same.
 JOHN RODERICK. He did not appear to me to be drunk.

 1800.  JOHN BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of July , 2 pistols, value 2l., the goods of John Thomas ; and that he had been previously convicted of felony.
 JOHN THOMAS. I am an  auctioneer. On the 29th of July I lost a pair of pistols from Eccleston-street, Pimlico- I had not seen them for some time; they were deposited with me, with other property, for sale by auction - I took possession of the house and property three or four days before the sale; I know they were there, from the inventory and catalogue - I do not know the prisoner.
   FREDERICK JACKSON. I am a porter, and live in Phoenix-street, Soho. I have seen the prisoner at different sales; the furniture was for sale on that day - I tied a pair of pistols to each of two blunderbusses; I did not particularly notice them - I had occasion to go down from the second floor, and I asked my fellow-servant whether any body was in the house; he said No; I went up again, and missed one pistol from each blunderbuss - I went out, and took the prisoner a quarter of a mile from the house, with something wrapped up in a blue handkerchief under his arm; I said, "I want the pistols;" I opened the parcel, and they were in it - I gave him to the Policeman; he begged I would let him go - I said I would not, as I had been robbed so many times.
   WILLIAM AGGS. I received the prisoner and the pistols.
   THOMAS TAYLOR. I was employed to lot the goods; I know these are one of the pairs of pistols - there was another pair and two blunderbusses.
   HENRY GODDARD. I have a certificate of the prisoner's conviction on the 15th of April last; I know he is the person - (read).
     GUILTY. Aged 37. - Transported for Life.
 1801.  JOHN RYAN was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of August , 1 watch, value 2l., the goods of Dennis Neal.
   DENNIS NEAL. On the 3rd of August I left my watch in my sister's room, No. 12, Gee's-court, between seven and eight o'clock in the morning - I returned between seven and eight in the evening; I then missed it - I had seen the prisoner at the door when I went out in the morning - I saw the watch afterwards at the office; I am certain this is it - it was my brother's.
 Prisoner. Q. Had not I a right to be at the door? A. I cannot say; I had been but two days there - I saw you and another boy.
   JAMES MOODY. I am a pawnbroker. This watch was pawned with me by the prisoner, on the 3rd of August, between one and two o'clock.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was standing at the door, and a young man came and said, "Will you earn 1s.?" I said Yes; he said, "Pawn this watch;" I said, "Why do you not do it yourself?" he said he did not like to go in.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1802.  ANN HERWICK was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of September , 1 shirt, value 4s., the goods of John Brickell.
   WILLIAM HENRY NORRIS. I am a pawnbroker, in the service of Mr. John Brickell. On the 1st of September the prisoner took a shirt from his shop, and walked away with it - it was very near the door; I went out after her; she hesitated, and said she would not have it - I told her to keep it in her own hands - I sent a young man who had been watching her some time for an officer.
 Prisoner. I was not away from the spot where the shirt was - it hung round in a court. Witness. She had gone some yards before I took her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she had taken hold of the shirt to examine it, and it fell down, but she had not moved from the shop.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
