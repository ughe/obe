EDWARD BAYLEY. I am a constable. About eight o'clock on Wednes day evening, the 16th, I received information that Mr. Oliver had lost some lead—I went to his premises and waited till the prisoner came out of the stable—I then took him into custody—I told him it was on suspicion of stealing lead from Mr. Oliver—he told me no, I was wrong—I had Thomas Bayley with me—I said, "No, I cannot be wrong, as this is the young man that purchased it of you"—he then said it was not the lead that was stolen from Mr. Oliver's it was lead of his own—I told him he must go with me to the station-house, and then he said he dug the lead out of the ground—I asked him what ground, and whether the lead was his own or Mr. Oliver's, and after a little hesitation he said it was Mr. Oliver's—I went afterwards, with two policemen, into Mr. Oliver's garden to match the lead, and we found it corresponded exactly with where the lead was taken from—I believe the lead belonged to the place—it appeared as if it had been sawn with a saw.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Quite fresh? A.
