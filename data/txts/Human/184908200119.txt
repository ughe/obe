 1637. HENRY DASHWOOD , stealing 2 bottles, 2 quarts of ale, and 3 pints of wine, value 9 s  . 4 d  .; 3 sovereigns, 2 half-sovereigns, 10 shillings, 1 sixpence, and 2 pence; the property of Thomas Edward Bushnell , his master.
   MR. METCALFE conducted the Prosecution. MARY ANN VIDLER. I am daughter-in law to Mr. Bushneil. I was acting as bar-maid for him on 7th Aug.—the prisoner was in his service—he came to me that evening, and asked for two bottles of wine, two bottles of old ale, and change for a 5 l .-note—I gave him the articles, and 4 l . 10 s . 9 d . change—he said he wanted to take them to Lowndes-square—he did not return—I never saw the 5 l .-note, nor heard of such a customer.
   Prisoner.  I had no wages; I own I had the things, but I did not steal them. Witness.  He was employed as an odd man—he had no wages, but his dinner and supper. THOMAS EDWARD BUSHNELL. I keep the Turk's Head. The prisoner had no authority from me, or any other person that I know of, to get this cash and wine—he was my servant—I have inquired at every house in Lowndes-square—I have never had the 5 l .-note.
   The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate was read as follows:  —"I am very sorry. I was never locked up before; it would never happen any more. I have no objection to pay 7 s . a week till such time as it is paid." GUILTY. Aged 30.— Confined Four Months .
