   ANGELIOUS BERTRAM. I saw the three prisoners in St. Martin's-court - they followed the prosecutor through Leicester-square, Princes-street, and into Wardour street.  Prisoner BENSON. Q. How long was I with the other prisoners? A. About ten minutes.
 ANDERSON'S Defence. I was going to Mr. Jones, in Wardour-street for work. I met Keith, and a man came and took us.
 BENSON'S Defence. I was going along Wardour-street, and met Keith. I stood and spoke to him a minute or two, and was going away, when I was taken, on the contrary side of the way to the other two.
 KEITH'S Defence. I was walking down Wardour-street - the man took me and threw me on the ground.

 BENSON -    NOT GUILTY.
 1494.  ELIZABETH CARTER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of September , a necklace, value 3 l., the goods of Susan Allen , from the person of Harriet Low.
   SUSAN ALLEN. I sent my niece, Harriet Low, (who is five years of age) out on last Friday morning, about nine o'clock, with a necklace on her neck; she came back in about half an hour without it - here it is - but it has been broken in pieces.
   WILLIAM JONES. I am an officer. I took a part of the necklace from the prisoner's bosom, in Mr. Sewel's shop, at Kingsland, about half-past nine o'clock; the other part was brought by a witness. The prisoner said, they were given her by another girl, who had picked them up in the road.
   JAMES WARBOYS. I am a gardener, and live at Dalston. Last Friday morning I saw the little girl crying - I went up to her; she said the prisoner, and another, had ran away with her beads. I pursued them towards London-field - they got out of my sight for about five minutes; I then got sight of them again - they were still running - I came up with the prisoner, and took her to Mr. Sewel's shop; the moment I touched her, she dropped part of the beads on the ground.
 Prisoner's Defence. I do not know the girl who picked them up - she said she had found them; and gave them to me.
 The prisoner received an excellent character. GUILTY. Aged 14.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1 s., and Discharged.
 1495.  NOEL CHAPMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of September , three scent bottles, value 25 s., the goods of Thomas James Rear; and JAMES CARTER was indicted, for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen.
 THOMAS JAMES REAR. I am a  chemist and druggist, and live in Sloane-street. I was in my shop, about nine o'clock in the evening, on the ninth of September; the prisoners and several other persons were there; they left the shop, and I missed some bottles; Gilbert, and my apprentice, were the only persons there then - I had been shewing them to a lady about eight o'clock.
   GEORGE VAUGHAN. I am apprentice to Mr. Rear. On the 9th of September, I saw Chapman in my master's shop; he asked for a penny-worth of liquorice, with which I served him, and he went away. I did not miss any thing till Mr. Rear told me of it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were not several other persons there? A. Yes; the prisoners were the last persons who left the shop, and five minutes after they were gone, I heard of the loss.
   JAMES GILBERT. I am a musician in the Blues. On the night of the 9th of September, I was in the shop, and saw the prisoner Chapman come in.
   EDWARD PERKINS. I am assistant to Mr. Perkins, a pawnbroker. On the morning of the 10th of September, Carter came to the shop, and offered these two bottles in pawn; he said, he brought them from a man. I detained him.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he not go out to look for the person? A. Yes; he soon returned, and said, he was gone.
   RICHARD MAYBANK. I am the officer - I went to Perkins' shop, and took Carter. He said they were given to him by a short man, and he was waiting for him - he did not know his name; I afterwards took Chapman from information. He said, he had not been at Mr. Rear's shop - but the apprentice and Gilbert, identified him.
 CARTER'S Defence. As I was coming out of the Coach and Horses public-house, I met a man at the corner of George-street, who asked me to take them to pawn, and he would give me a pint of beer.
   JOHN LONG. I am a labourer, and live at Chelsea. I have known Carter seven or eight years. I was at the pawnbroker's shop and saw him there - he asked for his ticket - they sent him to find the man; he returned in about a minute, and could not find him.
     NOT GUILTY.
