HENRY YATES re-examined.  A pair of boots had been brought to my house on the Wednesday, and on Saturday the prisoner came to know if they fitted—I said, "Were they paid for?"—he said, "No"—I said, "What were they?"—he said, "7 s .," which I paid him—he did not ask for it. JAMES JOYCE re-examined.  He had no authority to go about these boots at all—he was at liberty to receive the money if offered, but not on my authority—if he had brought it to me I should have taken it.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did he not come to you under a written agreement? A.  Yes—I received 10 l . with him. NOT GUILTY.
 1777. WILLIAM THATCHER was again  indicted for embezzlement. JAMES JOYCE. I sent the prisoner to receive 6 s . 6 d . from Mr. Potter, about the 12th of May—he came home, and said he had not received it—on the 18th of May I sent him to Mrs. Davis for 5 s . 6 d .—he came back, and said she had not paid him, she would call in a day or two.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What did you say when you sent him? A.  I gave him Potter's bill, which was 9 s . 6 d ., but it seems he tore off the first item, which was 3 s ., and received that first—he has accounted for larger sums on other occasions—he had not to enter payments in any book. ANN GIBBS. I am servant to Mr. Potter, of Wandsworth Common, who deals with Joyce. I know the prisoner as his servant—I saw White, my fel low-servant, pay him 3 s . 6 d ., and on the 23rd of May I paid him 6 s . 6 d . for Mr. Joyce—he wrote "Paid" on each bill, which are here. ELIZA DAVIS. I am servant to  Mr. Colburn. On the 18th of May the prisoner brought me a pair of shoes, for which I paid him 5 s . 6 d . for Mr. Joyce—he wrote "Paid" on the bill.
   MR. JOYCE . He never paid me these sums.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did his father offer to pay you the money? A.  No.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY  . Aged 14.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined Fourteen Days .
