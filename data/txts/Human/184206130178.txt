 1904. ELLEN LYON was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 2 pairs of shoes, value 5 s  ., the goods of James Francis Thompson . JONATHAN POLLARD( police-constable D  125.) In consequence of in formation, I went in search of the prisoner on the 6th of June, and found her walking down Marylebone-lane, about twenty minutes before four o'clock in the afternoon, with this gaiter in her hand, and found this pair of shoes in her apron—I asked where she got them—she said she picked them up—I took her to the station, and she was searched by Eckett—who gave me another pair of shoes—the prisoner was the worse for liquor. MARY ECKETT. I am the wife of William Eckett, a policeman. I live at the station at Marylebone-lane—I asked the prisoner if she had any thing in her pocket—she said she had no pocket, but I found her pocket, and this pair of shoes in it—she said, "For the honour of God, don't produce these shoes; there is plenty besides this against me, keep them for yourself, and say nothing about them." FRANCIS WILLIAMS. I am salesman to James Francis Thompson. These shoes are my employer's—they were at a stall outside his shop—I bad seen them safe about half-past one o'clock—I had not missed then till the policeman brought them at half-past six that afternoon.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I met a person; she treated me; she bad these shoes in her basket; she gave them to me, and told me to wait at the corner, which I did, and the policeman and another passed by me; I saw this gaiter on the pavement, and was following a young man, as I thought it might be his. GUILTY. Aged 24— Confined Three Months.
 1905. GEORGE ALLEN and RICHARD BROOKS were indicted for stealing, on the   4th of June , 1 pair of boots, value 3 s  . 8 d  .; the goods of William Buer ; and that Allen had been before convicted of felony. THOMAS SPARKS. I am a stable-keeper, at Ealing. On the 4th of June, I was in Frederick-street, Regent's-park, about half-past two o'clock in the afternoon, and saw the two prisoners—they had just joined together when I met them, and were about 150 yards from the prosecutor's—Allen pulled a pair of boots out of his pocket, and showed them to Brooks—he then put them into his pocket again, and passed by me—I watched them till they turned into Albany-street—I went on the way I was going—I went to Mr. Buer's shop, and went with his lad after the prisoner—I saw these boots fall from Brooks's apron. EDWARD EDWARDS. I am foreman to Mr. William Buer, a pawnbro ker. Mr. Sparks came to our shop, while I was looking for this pair of boots, which I had missed from a board outside—I went after the prison ers, and saw them in Albany-street—they turned down a court, and when they came out of the court, I laid hold of both of them—Brooks escaped, Mr. Buer was coming up the street, and took him—I did not see Brooks drop any thing, but I saw this pair picked up, which are what I missed.
