Q. By the time you got to the top of the stairs, would there have been
time for a person to bare run down those stairs and got away by the pas-
sage, without your seeing him? A. No-I did not see any one running
down those stairs, or along the passage below--the person could not bave
run down those stairs- I do not think he could have run round the second
corner before I ran round the first.
Prisoner's Defence. I went to the Custom-house, on Saturday, with a
young man I was acquainted with, who had some business to transact
there--he left me waiting while he went into one of the offices--while
standing there (and I believe the last witness stated at the Mansion-house
that he saw me standing there ten minutes) I saw two or three persons go
to this coal-cellar, which I thought was for the sake of convenience, and I
went in there; while I was there the person came and said, “Look, he
has gone in there ;" I have been in a most respectable situation up to this
last fortnight, and I have not made my friends acquainted with this.
GUILTY. Aged 28.--Confined Twelve Months.
2210. MICHAEL MURPHY was indicted for feloniously killing and
slaying Thomas Connor.
JEREMIAH HAGERTY. I am a ballast-getter. I knew the deceased,
Thomas Connor, he was also a ballast-getter-on the 5th of June he was
at the Ballast-office, in Narrow-street, Ratcliff-he was very nearly drunk,
and not in a fit state to go to work-I was in the street--we went down a
passage leading from the street to the water-side, and when half-way down
the passage he was told he was too late--we returned back-I and a per-
son named Power were joking together, and in passing by, I tapped Connor
at the back of his neck with a cinder-he turned round and asked what I
did that for-I begged his pardon, and said I did not do it designedly-
he said I ought to mind where I threw things-he stood for a minute,
then turned round to the prisoner and uttered some words; I could not
understand what they were, but the last word he said was “ Peter"-the
prisoner was standing opposite to me, with his two hands in his pockets-
as soon as the words were spoken, he up with his fists and struck Connor
under the left ear-he fell to the ground, with his head on the stones close
to my feet-his head turned to the right side, and blood flowed from his
ear--he was carried to Mr. Buchanan's, the doctor's, at the corner of
Ratcliff-cross, Ratcliff-highway-I saw no more of bim till I saw him at
the inquest, at the London Hospital—I then saw his dead body-I do not
know whether he and the prisoner were acquainted before-Connor did
not attempt to strike the prisoner before he struck this blow-I could not
tell whether the language Connor used to the prisoner was abusive or not,
for I had my head turned the wrong way-Connor's head came against
the ground first when he fell-he fell on the back part of his head
"Peter" is a nickname of the prisoner's-he is offended at it-he has often
got upbraided with that name at public-houses.
