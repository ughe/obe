times a hard task for a plain-dealing tradesman, who has not enjoyed a libe-

assembly and so to frame his address, as to carry conviction home to the
minds of his bearers; but hard as such a task is, under any circumstances,
it becomes almost insuperable to the guiltless man who, arraigned as a
criminal, has to plead in his own defence, and feels that his liberty, his
honqur, his standing in society, his own welfare, and what must be far
more precious to him, the honour and welfare of his dear family, in short, all
that can render life desirable, is at stake; and must be lost or preserved,
according to the impression he makes on his judges; according to the
more or less plain and lucid manner in which he is able to state the facts
as they really gecurred and demonstrate his innocence. No wonder if he
feels the awful nature of his position. No wonder if he is overwhelmed by
the conseiousness of his own utter inability, and if confused at being
placed in a situation so entirely new to him, and called upon to perform a
duty to which he is altogether unaccustomed; no wonder, I say it isif
under such trying circumstances he should lose his courage, his presence of
mind-nay, every hope, save in an uprighteous and omniscient Judge.
