 2250. JANE GRIFFITHS was indicted for stealing 2 aprons, value 2 s  ., and 1 shirt, 3 s  .; the goods of Stephen Betts , the younger; and 1 sheet, value 1 s  . 6 d  ., and 1 curtain, 1 s  .; the goods of Stephen Betts , the elder, her master.
   MR. CHARNOCK conducted the Prosecution. MARY BETTS. I am the wife of Stephen Betts, a butcher, at Putney. The prisoner was in my service about three months—on the 31st of Aug., in consequence of some circumstances, I required to search her box—the police man was sent for; and on her consenting, I went up to her box in her bed-room, and she unlocked it—I found in it two aprons belonging to my son Stephen, and a sheet cut to pieces—I have put it together to see if it would make a sheet—I can swear to it by a B which I put on it myself, and a little dot—I found a long muslin curtain, which was an apron of my daughter's, and I joined it together myself, and made a curtain—it is my own work—I found a night-shirt of my son's in the kitchen table-drawer, which I can swear to by a B that was on it—the letters have been picked out, but I can see that there has been the cotton there—I asked the prisoner how she came by the aprons—she said she had made half-a-dozen aprons for a young man, and he had given her these two—she said the curtain was her own—I said I could swear to it—she said it was hers, and she had more like it—I told her to show me one like it, and she showed me one which was clear muslin—the sheet, she said, she bought of a housemaid at Streatham, where she lived servant—I had never given her these things—she had no right or authority to have them in her box.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  What is there on the aprons that enables you to know they belong to you? A.  I had a dozen of them, and I am two short out of the dozen—I missed them after I had searched her box—these are exactly like the others, and the person who made them is here—this shirt is torn—it is not an old one—here is a darn that I know it by, down the seam, and here is a B on it. ANN FINN. I live at Roehampton. I made a dozen aprons for Mr. Betts, jun.—I am certain these are two of them—I know my own needle-work. CHARLES CANHAM( policeman. V  58.) I was called in to be present at the searching of the prisoner's box, and these articles were found—she said she had made half-a dozen aprons for a young man named Jones, in Cookapur-street, and he gave her these two—I went to the place she described, but could not find the person—I communicated to her the result of my inquiry, and she made no answer—she said she bought the sheet of a fellow-servant at Streatham—I went there to make inquiries.
 (William Roberts, a linen-draper, in Paul-street, Finsbury, the prisoner's uncle, gave her a good character, and engaged to send for her brother to take her to her friends in the country.) GUILTY.  Aged 28.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury and Prosecutor  .— Judgment Respited.
