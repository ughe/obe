THOMAS WHITEMAN. I. am bailiff to Mr. Green, and pay his labourers
on Saturdays. I sometimes received the money from him, and sometimes
from the footman-on Saturday, 4th Nov., I saw the prisoner in the pantry
-- he gave me 6l. first, and told me he was very busy, and I was to go and
pay the men, and he would give me more presently, after he had laid the
cloth-after that I saw him again, and asked him for the rest of the money
- he counted out 91. in silver on a tray-I saw no note or bag, nor did be
say anything to me about them he did not say that he had been to a Mr.
Scriven's—he said that he had had a job to get change__when I had got
the 151. I asked him what it was master said about him he said he did not
know-I said I had hardly money enough to pay the men-he said he
would lend me 21. or 31., but I was not to mention it to Mr. Green-he lent
me 21.-on the Tuesday morning following the prisoner was dismissed, and I
received a message from Mr. Green to go and make inquiries I went to
the prisoner's lodging, at the Greyhound, about 12 o'clock that night I told
him I wanted to see him—he said, "What for 1" I said, “Concerning the
change "- he asked me what odds was it to me, I had my change all right,
had I not - I asked him what he had done with the bl. he had at Mr.
