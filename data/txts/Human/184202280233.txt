I let out a horse and cart, and live in Vinegar-yard, Bermondsey. On the 7th of February, Mendes engaged my cart for eight o'clock in the morning of the 8th of February—we got three rolls of oil-cloth at the comer of Snow's-fields, where I found Mendes with my cart, and took it to Mr. Levy's in Finsbury-market—all but one little piece, which we left at Mr. Weeks', in Wormwood-street—I drove the cart.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you leave Mendes with the cart? A.  I left it against his door—he drove it himself the first thing in the morning—I saw the oil-cloth delivered—I am not acquainted with any men in Mr. Rolls' employ.
   COURT . Q.  How came you to leave the cart? A.  Mendes hired it of me the over-night—he is not a friend of mine—I only know him by sight—I did not think he would run away with a horse and cart—I found them again. THOMAS HOUGHTON. I am porter to Mr. Levy, of Finsbury-market. On Tuesday morning, the 8th of February, Mendes brought three rolls of floor-cloth in a cart—there were 145 1/2 yards—he cut off four yards, which be sent back—he sold the rest to my master, for 1 s . 8 d . a yard—they came to 12 l . 2 s . 6 d . after we measured them—they were in five pieces. JOHN WOODHOUSE( police-sergeant M  13.) I took the prisoner on Sa turday, the 12th of February, at his master's—I asked if he knew what he was charged with—he said. "Yes," he was aware of it, one of the clerks had mentioned it to him—he said he was innocent—in consequence of information I went with Thomas Rolls to the house of Mr. Levy, in Fins bury-market, and received from Mr. Houghton five pieces of oil-cloth—they are identified by Mr. Humble—I went to Mr. Weeks' house, who is a pork-butcher in Bermondsey-street, and obtained four yards, which had been cat from one of the other pieces—the whole measured 146 yards. WILLIAM PLATT. I am in the employ of Messrs. Pickford, in Wood-street. About nine o'clock on Tuesday morning, the 8th of February, the prisoner brought thirteen packages of floor-cloth in the prosecutor's cart—I saw the name of Rolls on it—it was a tilted cart—I had seen it before—I gave him a receipt in his cart-book—this is it—he did not bring any thing on Monday that I am aware of.
   Cross-examined. Q.  In what part of the premises do you receive the goods? A.  In the yard—I always go down and receive the goods—I am quite clear about that—I went down myself that morning—I had no con versation with the prisoner—I bad known him before—Mr. Rolls' is a large heavy cart, drawn by one horse. NOT GUILTY.
