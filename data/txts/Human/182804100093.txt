 972.  GEORGE BROWN and ISAAC CLAPHAM were indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , 1 handkerchief, value 5s., the goods of William Henry Deedes , from his person.
 CLAPHAM pleaded GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Life.
 MR. WILLIAM HENRY DEEDES. I am a  Government officer at the board of Commissioners. On the 1st of March, at three o'clock I was opposite St. James' Church, Piccadilly; I had a handkerchief in my coat pocket - I did not miss it till the officer produced it; the prisoner was then about two yards from me - there was rather a crowd as the Welch children were going to church.
   THOMAS THOMPSON. I am an officer. On the 1st of March, about half-past two o'clock, I was in Piccadilly, and saw the prisoner attempting several people's pockets; I saw them close on the prisoner - Clapham took something from his pocket; I seized him, and he dropped it immediately - Brown had covered him; I had followed them from Gray's-Inn-lane with the procession.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Do you mean to say you saw Brown touch any pocket? A. No.
   DANIEL REARDON. I am an officer. I was with Thompson and saw the prisoners together - Clapham tried several gentlemen's pockets; the prisoner covered him - they were in company together; as the prosecutor came along they closed upon him - Clapham took the handkerchief and was handing it to the prisoner, we took them both.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had Brown touched it at all? A. No; I have made enquiry about him, he is not known to the officers at all.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 BROWN'S Defence. I was going on an errand; I saw the procession and got up by the church - the constables told the mob to stand back, and they took me.
 BROWN -    NOT GUILTY.
 973.  SIDWELL HALEY was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of March , 1 bag, value 2d.; 1 half-crown, and 2 penny-pieces, the property of Elizabeth Wickmill , from the person of Jane Brown.
 JANE BROWN. I am fourteen years old, and live with my mother. On the 31st of March, at seven o'clock in the evening, I was going along with my cousins; the prisoner and three other girls, came up to us; the prisoner left the others, came up, and asked us for a penny; I had none - my cousin had a halfpenny, and I had one; we put them together and gave them to her. I had never seen her before; she then asked for another penny, I said I had no more of our own: she said she knew we had, and that it was to make up three quarterns of gin, and we should have some. I said we never drank gin; she said she would never let us go clear, if we did not give it to her - she put her hand down my bosom and tore the beads off my neck; she threw them at me, and said, "You may have them again." I had a bag of money down my bosom, I took it out, and held it tight in my hand - she took my hand, forced it open, and pulled it out; my cousin asked her for the money, she took and hit her in the eye, and gave her a dreadful blow - she began to run; I called Stop thief! and she was taken; she gave my bag and money to another girl. I am sure she is the girl who took it - there were two half-crowns and two penny pieces in it; they belonged to Elizabeth Wickmill.
   ELIZABETH BROWN. I am fifteen years old. I was with Jane Brown, and in Duke-street, Manchester-square, I saw the prisoner come and take the purse; I asked her to give it up - she struck me in the eye and ran away; my cousin called Stop thief! nobody stopped her - I then called out and she was stopped. She gave the money to another girl, who got away.
   GEORGE PONDER. I saw the crowd, and took the prisoner in charge.
 Prisoner. I leave it to the mercy of the Court.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Life.
 974.  JAMES MATTHEWS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of March , 1 handkerchief, value 4s., the goods of Robert John Parker , from his person.
 ROBERT JOHN PARKER. I am a  clerk. On the 17th of March, I was in Museum-street, at nine o'clock in the evening, I left a jerk at my pocket, and missed my handkerchief; I saw the prisoner close to me, and accused him of it - he denied it: I called the watchman, and he ran away - I cried Stop thief! and he was taken, without my losing sight of him.
 Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Before he ran away, did he not offer to be searched? A. No, it was dark, the lamps were lighted.
   DANIEL HONE. I live in Castle-street. I was in my shop, and heard a cry of Stop thief! I looked out, and saw the prisoner passing; I went after him, and two men stopped him - he dragged them across the street, to Mr. Yardley's area, and laid down; I then took him to a light, where he was searched - but nothing found on him; he struggled very much - we found the handkerchief in the area.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you lose sight of him? A. No; he was taken five yards from my door - I am a constable, and took him to the watch-house.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been on an errand.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
