   COURT to CAROLINE COX . Q.  Did you ever tell the witness that your sister was a married woman? A.  I did a long time back—I told him he had no right to come there, she was a married woman—I said so in her presence, and I told my brother, but he never could see Mr. Williams—my brother came out every other Sunday—Mr. Williams was in the habit of coming down during the week—he never came on Sundays—the prisoner has an aunt—I never saw her, but I heard the prisoner say she had an aunt. GUILTY  . Aged 17.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury, in consequence of Williams's laxity of conduct. — Confined Six Months .
 1919. MARY RIPPINGHAM was indicted for stealing, on the   6th of June , 1 spoon, value 2 s  ., the goods of Thomas Watson .—2nd COUNT  , stating it to be the goods of Ann Richards . WILLIAM HORNE. I am a silversmith. On the 6th of June the prisoner came and offered me a broken silver spoon for sale—I asked her where she got it—she said, "My mistress gave it me for my day's wages"—I asked who her mistress was—she said, "That is my business, not yours"—I sent my young man for a policeman, and she walked out, but before that she said, "If you won't buy it give it me back"—I said, "No, you wait"—my young man brought the policeman, and he took her. GEORGE MUMFORD( police-constable K  207.) I took the prisoner—she said she took the spoon from her mistress's premises, in Jubilee-place—I went there, and saw her mistress.
   Prisoner.  I said I would show him where it was; the Magistrate dis missed the case. ANN RICHARDS. I am a widow, and live in Jubilee-place. The prisoner worked as a charwoman for my daughter—I have known her nearly four years—she left my house on the 6th of June, and was brought back in about half an hour—this spoon is my daughter's, Margaret Watson—her husband's name is Thomas—they live in the same house with me—I had not given the spoon to the prisoner for wages—it was not broken.
   Prisoner.  I accidentally broke it; I sat down with it in my pocket.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 44.— Confined Fourteen Days .
 1920. WILLIAM RUMBOLD was indicted for stealing, on the   9th of June , 3 bushels of soot, value 1 s  . 2 d  .; and 1 sack, 4 d  .; the goods of Francis Cullender . FRANCIS CULLENDEE. I am a chimney-sweeper, and live at Limehouse. I left a sack of soot in charge of Mrs. Dorrell on the 9th of June, and when I returned in the evening I missed it—I afterwards met the prisoner near Cannon-street—I said, "Have you taken my sack and soot?"—he said, "I have not; where was it?"—I said at the corner of Dorset-street—he said, "I have not been that way to-day, I have been at work at the brew-house all day"—I gave him in charge.
