   Prisoner. Q.  Was the window shut? A.  Open—I am certain it was a watch the man gave you—I followed my father and met him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  On 12th Nov., at half-past one o'clock, I went from the Waterloo station to Kingston; a policeman, in private clothes, was in the same train with me; I cannot get his number; I sent the policeman to look for him, and to bring him here; the other prisoner could prove I was not in his company; the policeman pointed me out to the witnesses. GEORGE FREDERICK LEONARD MOLLINEUX( City-policeman ,293). I was at Guildhall when Mr. Crouch identified the prisoner—there were six or seven persons in the dock by him, prisoners and policemen—I was in plain clothes—the prisoner stood on the stairs with me, ready to go up for examination—there were about four prisoners there—I did not point the prisoner out at all—Mr. Crouch came and made signs to his son, who pointed to the prisoner.
   MR. CROUCH re-examined.  I never saw the prisoner before—there were two persons at the bar, and the prisoner and the policeman stood on the stairs—signs were made to my son to know if he could see the man that took my watch—he looked round, and pointed out the prisoner—I had pointed him out before that to the policeman, but my son could not hear.
