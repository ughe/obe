(The prisoner's statement before the Magistrate was here read as follows: "On
Thursday morning I made my way to King's-cross station and took a
ticket for Barnet; as I got down into the fair-field, I asked a couple of
gipsies if they knew of a good horse for sale; they said they had not one,
but could take me where I could have my choice; I agreed to go with them;
we went over to Harlow-bush in a light cart, and I arrived there about
twenty minutes past 9. After we got there I saw two black horses and two
bay ones; I looked at them, and picked this bay mare, the one now in the
street; I asked the price; they asked six guineas ; I said I would not give
it, I would give five; they would not take less than 5l. 158. ; I said I
would split the difference and give 51. 108.; they said they would take it;
that was all I agreed to pay, and did pay. They took the horse up
to bondon; one of them rode the black horse and led the other; I rode up
to Islington gate in the same cart I rode from Barnet, I and the other gipes,
and the cart passed them on the road; one said we would meet them
through Islington gate; we stopped at a public-house, through the gate, till
they came; the gipsy that brought the horses through the gate had a hand-
kerchief and a ticket; they left me the ticket and handkerchief, by dropping
it; they said I could have it, so I picked up the ticket and handkerchief and
put them into my pocket; they tied the two black horses and the bay
behind the cart and left me with this mare, and said, "Good afternoon,
wish you good luck with the mare, I hope she will do you service; that was
all; I made my way home.'”)
STEPHEN CROUCH (re-examined). The gate at which I saw the prisoner
with the two horses is not on the way to Harlow-bush; it is altogether a
different road-it is on the main high-road to London.
