534. REBECCA FREEMAN stealing 1 purse, and a piece of paper,
value 2s. 6d., 1 sixpence, and 1 halfpenny; the property of Charles Edkins,
from the person of Harriet Edkins
MR. PLATT conducted the Proseeution.
HARRIET EDKINs. I am the wife of Charles Edkins, of 9, Borough-road,
clerk to an attorney. On 3rd May, about 5 o'clock in the evening, I was
passing through Smithfield, and saw the prisoner close by me-I am sure she
is the person- saw a man fall down before me, and he was raising bimself
when I felt a tug at my pocket, and missed my purse-it contained a sixpence,
a halfpenny, a receipt for 5l., and, to the best of my belief, a shilling also-the
prisoner walked away, and afterwards ran, and while she was walking before
me, she pulled the purse out-while I was endeavouring to follow her, ano-
ther man, not the one who had fallen down, attempted to interrupt the
two men ran after the prisoner; she went through a court, and I saw the
man who had fallen down pass something to her hand as they crossed the
path, and the woman passed her hand to him, and I saw something pass
which looked like a purse-I cannot swear it was mine they were about a
dozen yards off me-before the prisoner passed the purse to the man, I saw
her look into each corner of itI followed her for a quarter of a mile into a
public house, where I was told she had gone, and I found her there,
Cross-eramined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You did not know the prisoner or
the men before A. No; they were up the passage when I saw the pri-
soner pass the purse to the man-I was up the passage also-she looked at
the purse before that, when one of the men was obstructing me, before they
turned into the passage--] said at the police station I had 6d. in my purse,
I was not sure about the 18.-I did not, when the 1s. was found, say, That
I think is about the money I had in my purse.'
MICHAEL CANNON. I am a porter, and live at 61, Smithfield. On the

cutrix call out, The young woman has taken my purse I"' and saw a man
in the act of stopping the lady from going after the prisoner-the prisoner
walked up Cloth-fair- ran after her, and when she got round the corner of

Barley-mow-passage, and up Long-lane-the prosecutrix was following
behind me- saw the prisoner go into a publie house, at the comner of Char-
ter-house-street, and the man ran away-I told a policeman.
