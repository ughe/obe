Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  How many loaves did you see deli vered to Brown? A.  Five or six—Brown went towards her home, and another officer took her. FRANCES CHARTRES. I live next door but one to the prosecutor. On the morning of the 11th of Feb. I saw Brown bring some bread from the bake-house—Camplin was walking about in the yard, apparently watching—Brown came and joined Camplin—they both went into the back of the home occupied by Camplin, with the bread.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Where is this yard of Camplin's? A.  It joins the prosecutor's—Camplin was walking backwards and forwards, and she looked round into the bakehouse—I live next door to Camplin's, and was at my back window—the policeman was then gone to the front—the bread I saw was not that which the policeman saw given—it was then about seven o'clock—it was light—Camplin and Brown went into Camplin's house together—there is but one yard for Mr. Thies' and Mrs. Camplin's houses—Mr. Thies' bakehouse is at the end of the wash-house, and the bakehouse door fronts the wash-house of my house, because the wash-house projects—when Camplin was walking in the yard she was very near the bakehouse, and looked in several times—directly the policeman had left my house, and gone to the front, I went to my back window, and saw what I have stated.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Where had the policeman been watching? A.  In my father's wash-house—I saw Brown bring the bread from the bakehouse, and carry it into Camplin.

 1096. WILLIAM BROWN was indicted for uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. CHAMBERS and DOANE conducted the Prosecution. CAROLINE DYER. My husband keeps a shop in Wellington-terrace, Spring-place, Wandsworth-road. On the 11th of Feb. the prisoner came for a 1/4lb. of sugar, he gave me a bad sixpence—I asked him where he got it from—he said his mother gave it him—I asked where his mother lived—he said, "Round there"—I said, "Round where?"—he said, in Frederick-place, South Lambeth, but he did not know the number—that her name was Brown—I sent Gastock to make inquiries—she came back, and said, in the prisoner's hearing, that she could not find any such person—I sent for the policeman—I gave the sixpence to my father. JOHN MORGAN. I received the sixpence from my daughter—I held it in my hand till the policeman came, and I gave it him. JOHN MENNIE( police-constable V  70.) I took the prisoner, and received this sixpence—I asked where he lived, he said, "Nowhere"—at the station I found on him this bad half-crown, wrapped in this bit of rag, and pinned inside the lining of his waistcoat—he made no remark. MR. JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Mint—these are both counterfeit. GUILTY. Aged 14.— Confined Three Months .
