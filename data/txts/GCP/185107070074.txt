NEW COURT.- Friday, July 11th, 1851.
PRESENT-Mr. RECORDER ; Mr. Ald. Hunter; and Mr. Ald. SIDNET.
Before Mr. Recorder, and the Fifth Jury.
1503. GEORGE CHAPLIN, feloniously stabbing Paul Peter Ryan, with
intent to do him some grievous bodily harm.
MR. PARNELL conducted the Prosecution.
Paul Peter Ryan. I am a cab-driver. Last Saturday night, at hall-
past 12 o'clock, I was standing at the corner of Dunning's-alley, Bishopsgate-
street, with Savage--the prisoner and a friend of his came up, and ran against
me and Savage-Savage said, “Halloo, governor, take care of my foot "-be
had a bad foot with the rheumatic gout-I said, “Why don't you run over
people ?”-the prisoner turned, and abused me-he said, "Who are you, you
b y snot," and swore in a strange degree at last he came up to me in
fighting attitude, and said, "What will you do, you Cockney b- ?"-his
fists were clenched- I struck him, and knocked him against the shutters of
Mr. Groves's shop-his friend stepped forward, as if he were going to take it
up, and I struck him--the prisoner partly took off his coat-he stood and
looked at me for a second, and put it on again-be said, "I will give you
something," and walked into the road, as I thought, looking for a policeman
- a young man who was standing by said I should not bave struck him first
-I asked if he knew what provocation I had had -he said he did not--while
I was talking, the prisoner came behind me, and gave me a very heavy blow,
but with what I cannot tellit cut my bat, and stunned me--he ran away
directly-as soon as I recovered myself I ran after him-I saw him standing
against a gas-lamp-I knocked him down that was two or three minutes
after he had given me the blow-he got up, and raised his right arm-1
thought he was going to strike me, and I put up my left arm, and received a
knife through my jacket and into my arm-I went to the hospital.
Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK. Q. Are you a pugilist? A. No; I
fight when it is required, I was sober-the prisoner was not drunk; he had
been drinking--I had not a cab then ; I was out of employ-I struck the
prisoner rather a violent blow he went against the shutters, which were two
or three yards off-he had not struck me-he would have struck me-I only
struck bim one blow on the second occasion-it was a pretty sharp blow-
after I knocked him down he took out his knife- I did not endeavour to
prevent him from rising and run against the knife-I did not run against it
at all-he said at the station that he took out the knife to protect himself,
and that I ran against it-I do not recollect his saying that he was sorry, and
that he did not mean to do me any harm-he said I had struck him first.
