 133.  JAMES ROBINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of November , 1 brass knob and escutcheon, value 1s. 4d., the goods of Thomas Bell , and fixed to a building, &c.
   GEORGE PUTMAN(police-constable G 10). On the 15th of November, I was in Castle-street, and saw the prisoner at the door of No. 12, doing something - I asked what he was doing; he said, "Nothing;" I went on, and he ran off - I pursued and took him - I found on him these two escutcheons, but neither of them belong to the door I saw him at - this knob was found by another officer, and this escutcheon which appearad to fit that door exactly.
   WILLIAM BROWNING(police-constable G 167). I heard the rattle spring about two o'clock in the morning - I ran up and found this knob and escutcheon, about five yards from the door of No. 12, Castle-street - I called up Mr. Bell who lives there, about five o'clock - he missed the knob from the door, and found this little screw inside.
   THOMAS MALIN(police-constable G 156). I saw the prisoner doing something with his hands, at the door of No. 12 - I saw him taken; he said, "I hope you will not make it worse than it is."
   THOMAS BELL. I live at No. 12, Castle-street, Finsbury. I was called by the policeman at five o'clock that morning - I missed the knob and the escutcheon from my door - I cannot swear to these, but this screw fits the knob.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not take it off. I had got as far as Wilson-street, when I was taken.
     NOT GUILTY.

 135.  WILLIAM MILES was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of October , 1 box, value 1s.; 3 dresses, value 30s.; 1 gown-piece, value 2l.; 1 petticoat, value 3s.; 3 night-dresses, value 15s.; 1 shift, value 5s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 3s.; 2 pair of stockings, value 20s.; 3 pair of gloves, value 3s.; 1 collar, value 5s.; and 1 book, value 4s., the goods of Joshua Grimmett.
   MICHAEL BROWN(police-sergeant I 20). On the 23rd of October, I was on duty in the Uxbridge-road, about a quarter-past seven o'clock; I met the prisoner carrying this bundle; I said, "Halloo young one, where are you going with that?" he said, "They are things belonging to a servant maid, and I am taking them to No. 94, Park-lane; I said, "No person would allow you to carry them there in these rags - where did you bring them from?" he said, from Kensington, and if I would go to Park-lane, I should see it was right; I said, no, I would take him to Kensington; he went on with me for ten or fifteen yards, and then turned, and said, "My statement is false, they are my things - I brought them from Notting-hill, near Acton." I then said, "Where does your mother live;" he said,"She did live in Wild-court, Wild-street, but she is removed to near Ealing; I am bringing these things there;" I took him to the station-house, and found this bill of parcels inside the bundle.
   MARY DODDRIDGE. I am a ladies maid, to Mrs. Labouchere. Miss Dupree is her sister; this property belongs to her; I packed it up at Blackheath, in a deal-box; it was brought from there to No. 6, Harley-street, and sent the next day to the Green Man and Still, to go home.
   JOSHUA GRIMMETT. I am a carrier. I received this box at the Green Man and Still; I put it in the back of my cart; I did not miss it till I got to Ealing, on the night of the 22nd of October, or about one o'clock the next morning.
 Prisoner. Q. Is it your property? Witness. A. I had the care of it.
   WILLIAM CHAPMAN KINES. I keep a cart and horse. I found this box on my brother's premises in a shed, about thirty yards from my house; just on this side of Acton; I know nothing of the prisoner; here are some bits of ribbons, one glove, and a bit of a knife in it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was employed to carry it for a person.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Seven Years.
