house - what he said was, that he was going to bring it up stairs.
 COURT to JOHN DUNN. You mentioned that these men were gone into the cellar to fetch coals and coke - but is that usual? A. No; the labourers should do it, but they will do as they like - they do fetch them sometimes; they had the coal basket in their hands.
   ABRAHAM GRIFFITHS. I am a constable. I searched the two prisoners at the watch-house; I found on Dade seven wall-hooks and the sucker - and on Goodhall, a little bit of clack.
 DADE's Defence. I had no intention of stealing the solder; I had it given me by Goodhall - and I was bringing it up when Mr. Dunn stopped me.
 GOODHALL'S Defence. This solder I was ordered to get up: I returned the keys, and in the course of the evening Mr. Dunn told us to go and get coals and coke up; when I had delivered the keys to Mr. Dunn, he came running down, and went up to Dade, and said, "What have you got?" he said, "Two bars of metal to take up stais;" he called to me to get the candle, which I did - and when I came up he told me to go and get a constable, which I did.
     NOT GUILTY.
 SEVENTH DAY. THURSDAY, APRIL 13.
 London Cases, Second Jury.
 Before Mr. Common Sergeant
 891.  JOHN EDWARD OAKLEY, alias    JOHN EDWARDS, was indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Mary Charles , widow , about the hour of eight in the night of the 4th of March , at St. Botolph, Aldersgate, with intent to steal, and stealing therein 6 child's shirts, value 2s.; 3 petticoats, value 1s.; 1 frock, value 1s.; 8 caps, value 1s. 6d.; 4 shirts, value 1l. 2s.; 5 neckerchiefs, value 7s.; 3 pairs of stockings, value 4s. 6d.; 7 handkerchiefs, value 11s.; 3 towels, value 1s.; three gowns, value 7s. 6d.; 9 aprons, value 9s.; 1 cloak, value 10s.; 1 pair of sheets, value 2s. 6d.; 1 table-cloth, value 1s. 6d.; 1 shift, value 1s.; 1 shawl, value 1s., and 1 curtain, value 1s. 6d., her property.
 MARY CHARLES. I am a widow, and live at  No. 5, Cox's-court, Aldersgate-street, in the parish of St. Botolph without, Aldersgate. I rent the bottom part of the house, and have the key of that part - the landlord does not live in the house - it is all let out in tenements. I am a washerwoman. I went out on the 4th of March, at near eight o'clock in the evening; I left the property stated in the indictment there, some on my table, some in my drawer; and some in a basket. I returned in about twenty minutes; I had locked my door when I went out, and on my return I found the articles were all gone; this is the property (examining it) - it is all mine.
   ROBERT LOCK. I am a constable. On Saturday evening, the 4th of March, about twenty minutes past eight o'clock, I was in Whitecross-street, and saw the prisoner coming down, with two bundles, one under each arm; this was about half a mile from Cox's-court - I called an officer, named Bradford, and we stopped him, and asked what he had got - he said he had just come from his mother's, in Hare-court, and he met a man, who sad he would give him a shilling to carry these two bundles; I asked where he was - he said at the Merry Carpenters public-house, in Old-street. I told him I would give him every chance to find the man, and went to the house with him, but he was not to be found. I then locked him up, and in consequence of taking another man afterwards, with some kid skins, who told me I might find some more skins in a house in Gloucester-court, Whitecross-street, I went there about ten o'clock that night, and found some more property belonging to the prosecutrix. The whole are not more than one person could carry.
 Prisoner. Had not he a right to take the woman into custody where the property was found? A. Witness. There was a woman frying some fish there, and I saw a trunk open - I looked at the things in it, and saw some wet mud on them, which excited my suspicion. I took them, but I did not know they were stolen - they are a part of this property.
 Prisoner. The reason he did not is, because Harris, her brother-in-law, is an officer, who was with him. Witness. Yes - I called him to go with me.
   THOMAS BRADFORD. I was with hock, and took this man and the things - when we got to the watch-house it was about half-past eight o'clock.
 COURT to MARY CHARLES. Q. Tell us as nearly as you can the time you left you house? A. It was just upon the stroke of eight o'clock.
 Prisoner's Defence (written). Between eight and nine o'clock at night I met a tall man in Redcross-street, with three bundles under his arm - he said if I would carry two of them to the Merry Carpenters, in Old-street, he would give me 1s.
 Two witnesses gave the prisoner an excellent character.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 10.
 892.  SARAH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of February , 1 fowl, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Loveday.
   JAMES THOMAS SELWOOD. I am a salesman in Newgate-market. I saw the prisoner lurking about my shop for some hours; I then saw her go to Mr. Thomas Loveday's shop, and take a shawl off a hamper - she secreted it under her apron. I went and charged her with it; on her being removed the fowl was found under her; she had been close to the place, but when I spoke to her she had got two or three yards from it.
 Prisoner's Defence. I knocked it down with my shawl.
     GUILTY. Aged 40. Confined Fourteen Days.
 893.  JOHN TIPPENDEN was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of March , 1 rummer glass, value 1s., the goods of John Kingston.
   GEORGE REED. I am an officer. The prisoner was brought to the watch-house on the 28th of March, and Mr. John Kingston gave me this glass which the prisoner confessed he had taken, and begged forgiveness - he said he was in a state of intoxication, and did not know what he had done; there were two other glasses found on him.
     NOT GUILTY.
