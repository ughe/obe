   Re-examined.  Baker was in Mr. Field's employment in the department which is closed—whatever he did or said at Horsemonger Lane Gaol he had no authority whatever from me for—Dennis, Cook, Rumble, and Simons are all witnesses here—Mrs. Cook is Dennis's mother—whatever money I paid applied to the whole family—I paid them nothing except for attend ance at the Police Court—Dennis only worked for the two Castles, according to her evidence, and Rumble also—Mrs. Simons resides in the neighbour hood—I know nothing about giving Mrs. Frances 10 s . for a new bonnet—I did not give her money for her mashed  window after she gave her evidence—her window was smashed  —when I used the expression "pieces," that applies to the 50 lbs. of wax in the bag—they offered me a better oppor tunity of judging than the pieces I have seen to-day.

King was not a customer of mine at Lambeth Marsh—he was a customer of Ogilvie & Co., whose partner I am—he was not a customer for wax, but for candles and night-lights—he produced the receipts from Jewell—he paid 15 d ., 18 d ., and 20 d . a pound—King occupies a good position—Johnson & Co. are unimpeachable; they bought some of this wax in an unsaleable state—the Edwards's are perfectly respectable; they are customers of mine for wax—Williams is undoubtedly a respectable man—he did not buy some of the wax—Addis if in William's employ, but Williams had nothing to do with the wax—the Middletons are highly respectable—they had not dealt with me before—the wax was in a state in which we do not sell it; it was in a state in which I would not send it out—when I first went to Jewell's, I told him it would be the worse for him if he did not tell me of whom he bought the wax, and he told me he got all of it from the Castles—I have endeavoured to find Jewell, but have not been successful—George Castle's is a private house; we did not search it or his mother's house—Mrs. Castle offered to allow us to search.
