   Re-examined.  I never heard anything about the scrip—I heard for the first time last night a suggestion of Cooke having told me that he was angry with Grunell—I met Cooke in the street at Barking last night; he was coming to try and find me; and he said, "Good evening; do you remember my telling you, while you were with me, that Grunell had obtained money for the Financial World , and that I was cross and vexed with him about it, and that I had written him a letter or seen him about it?"—I said I remembered nothing of the sort, and that I had made a "statement, and had said everything I remembered, and had spoken what I knew to be accurate, as far as my memory served me, and I was pre pared to stand by it—that conversation was partly in the street and partly in a public house, where we went for quietness—I have seen 3, 4 or 12 of these cuttings at Queen Victoria Street, and at Bow Lane, in a cupboard or in a desk—I saw on the outside sheet of the Financial World  a list of the prices of the shares of different companies published every week.
