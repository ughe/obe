 1809.  GEORGE THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of July , 1 sheet, value 2s., and 1 loaf, value 4d., the goods of James Macintosh.
   CATHERINE MACINTOSH. I am the wife of James Macintosh- we live in Cartwright-street, Rosemary-lane, on the ground-floor; my husband was not in town - it is a lodging-house for single men. The prisoner came that night, and paid me 3d. for his bed, and I trusted him 1d.; I was awoke at four o'clock in the morning, by hearing the plates moved, and saw him going out at the door - he slept up stairs; I followed him, but lost him - I found him at a public-house about ten o'clock; I heard him say to the Policeman, "Don't let her give charge of me - I have left the things at the public-house, and will give them up;" they were found at the public-house.
   HENRY BROWN. I was on duty in Rosemary-lane. The prosecutrix gave the prisoner in charge; he was in the yard of the public-house, washing himself - I went to the Anchor and Hope, East Smithfield, with him; he took the loaf off the shelf in the tap-room, and told me he had done it to get transported - I found the sheet there; he said he had taken it there.(Shirt produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1810.  WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of July , 2 saws, value 8s., and 1 stock, value 2s., the goods of Richard Mitchener.
   RICHARD MITCHENER. I am a blind-maker, and live in Great Castle-street, Oxford-market. On the 30th of July I went out of my shop for a quarter of an hour, leaving these tools safe; it was about half-past four o'clock - my wife was in the back parlour; on returning I found the prisoner and two others in custody in the shop - the Policeman produced two saws and a stock to me; he was a stranger
   JAMES KENNERLEY. I am a Policeman. I was in Oxford-street, and saw the prisoner and two others; I watched them - they watched me along two or three streets; I then kept out of their sight, but could see them - I followed them into Castle-street, and met Betraun; we followed them to the prosecutor's shop, I saw Smith go in and come out, and speak to the two others, who stood some distance off; he went in again, and Betraun said he had come out - I then saw him about two yards from the shop, running down towards Regent-street; I saw Powell secure him, and take something from under his coat - I showed Powell the shop, then followed, and took one of the other two.
   ANGELIOUS BETRAUN. I was with Kennerley, and watched the prisoner and two others to the prosecutor's shop; I saw him go in and come out - he went in again, and came out with something bulky under both arms, under his coat; he ran towards Regent-street - I afterwards went to the prosecutor's shop with another, who I secured; I saw the property in Powell's hands.
   GEORGE POWELL. I am a Policeman. I saw the prisoner running through Castle-street; I followed and took him, with the two saws under his coat, and the stock in his hand - I took him to the shop, and Mitchener claimed the property; the bill against the other two was thrown out.
   ROBERT ISAACSON. I am a Policeman. Powell and I were together; I saw the prisoner cross the street - I secured one of the others.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1811.  WILLIAM SPIERS and JOHN ROBINSON were indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of August , 1 silver spoon, value 7s., the goods of Edward Pinder Lygon and others.
   THOMAS TURNER. I am under-butler to Colonel Edward Pinder Lygon. This spoon was for the service of the whole regiment, and was in his care; I count the plate once a day - it was all right between one and two o'clock on the 2nd of August; I saw a boy down the area, at the kitchen door, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, but do not know who he was; Mason brought me the spoon next morning.
   JOHN DICKSON. I am a shopman to Mr. Hunter, a silversmith, of Tottenham-court-road. On the 2nd of August, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoners loitering at our window, and heard one say to the other, "Break a piece off;" our shop door was open - Rohinson then brought the bowl of the spoon in to me, and asked if it was silver - I detained him, went out and brought Spiers in, and on him I found the other part of the spoon.
   JOHN MASON. I took the prisoners in charge with the spoon.
 Spiers put in a written Defence, stating that Robinson asked him to go with him to sell something, and gave him part of the spoon to hold while he went to know what was the value of it.
 Robinson. I am sorry for having taken the spoon - I never was accused of any crime before.
 Robinson received a good character.
 ROBINSON -    GUILTY. Aged 13. Whipped, and delivered to his Master.
 SPIERS -    NOT GUILTY.
