   Cross-examined. Q.  Was not another policeman present a great part of this time in the shop? A.  No—a policeman named Turner came up, who was sent in by Clark—Harradin did not tell me that he had not bought the lead—he refused to buy it till the man brought his master to say that it was all right—he told me I was to wait till the master came—that was after I had seen the lead—but he was not aware that I had seen it, because his wife came and covered it while I was talking to him—I said I should put the handcuffs on Harradin—Harradin said, "I will go quietly"—I was present when Knight was brought up—I did not hear him say that he had been sent away by Harradin to fetch the foreman—I did not make Harradin's nose bleed—I will not swear I did not—Wright told me if I would let Harradin alone he would go quietly—that was after he had made his escape, and I had taken him—he ran up to two men, and I took him from them—Clark had gone to the station, and Pinfold was gone on with the biggest part of the lead—it weighed about 801 b. ROBERT CLARK( police-constable M  258.) I was with West in George-street—I saw Knight carrying some lead to Harradin's—I took Knight after he had come out, without the lead, about fifty yards from the house—I asked what he went into Harradin's house with—he said, "Nothing"—I asked what he went in for—he said, "—Some money which he left some iron for the night before"—I asked what he paid him—he said, "2 s . 3 d ."—Isaid I must go back to the shop—in going back he said he would tell the truth if I would not hurt him, that it was lead he took at the station—Harradin said he had not seen Knight before till that night—I took possession of a piece of lead at Harradin's shop—it matches one of the larger pieces.
