Cross-examined by Mr. Muir. I had not been a member of any
public body before 1904, nor am I a member of any other public body
now. Warren has been Chairman the whole time, and I have no
tault to find with the way in which he carried out his duties as Chair
man, except that I should have liked him to have been a bit firmer
At times, that is all. He devoted a great deal of his time to his duties!
- Guardian; you could never go without finding Warren there; he
would be there the whole of Thursday, from half-past two till the
Board rose, also at the meetings of the Seattered Homes, the In-
firmary and Workhouse Committees. Sometimes he visited the Scat-
tered Homes, also the Workhouse itself-in fact, every place we had.
When he was on the London County Council he used to be late on
Thursdays; he would arrive at the end of the business of the after
noon. The names of those attending the Committee were taken by
the Chairman or Clerk as they came in, so that whether they arrived
early or late their names would appear, Warren did not vote on the
question of the indestructible paint, but he stated that he thought
1 6d. was too much to pay and that 1s. was quite enough. On the
Committee there was, in my opinion, a party which was a party of
extravagance, ana there was a minority who were the party of eco-
nomy; I was of the party of economy, and I tried to persuade the
Guardians that they were being extravagant.
