   Cross-examined. Q . Was the prisoner's brother in your service? A.  Yes, for seven or eight years—he left me to set up in business as a brewer, in Leicester-square, about eighteen months ago—I think the prisoner came soon after I commenced business—he was very diligent the former part of the time—I was fined 200 l . for adulteration—I do not know that he laboured to keep my connection together—I recollect his finding a 10 l . note—he did not give it to me—it was advertised, but the parties could not tell the number—it was not given up—he told me one evening he had found a pocket-book with a few hundred pounds in it, but I believe it was an unindorsed acceptance—I do not know that he gave it up, and received 6 l . reward—on one or two occasions I and my clerk have been absent when he came home—he has put his book on the desk, and his money in the inkstand—that has not happened for the last six months—he has paid me 3 s . 6 d . overplus money, saying he did not know where it came from—that has been two or three times in three years—it has never been more than 3 s . or 4 s .—he came to me on the Monday morning after he ran away, and said if it was found out on examination that he was deficient, he would pay me—he came again on the Tuesday—my solicitor asked him where he was to be found—he said at his house, 4, John son-street, Somers Town—I gave him in charge—I always examined his book—I have found upwards of thirty cases of embezzlement against him—I have not indicted him for them.
   MR. METCALFE . Q.  Has your beer always been delivered? A.  No—I have found my strong beer short, and the weak'beer too much, in the same proportion—I have long suspected the prisoner and his brother have been interleagued to rob me—he was sent out with a cask of beer, my clerk was watching him, and he bolted  —the cart was left in the street, and taken to the Greenyard—I did not see him again for three days—he then said he had lost his book and money—it was before he was charged that he came and told me he was deficient, and would pay me. FREDERICK SHEDELL. I am clerk to Mr. Blogg, and occasionally take the account when the prisoner comes home—I or Mr. Blogg enter them from his book into the day-book—there is no other person—I have never received this 6 s ., 5 s ., or 4 s . 6 d .—I do not remember seeing them entered in his book at all—the night before he ran away I took the account from his book, and asked him for his previous book, as it ought to be kept at the counting-house—he said it was at home, and he would bring it next morning—Mr. Blogg directed me to watch him, but he did not make his appearance again.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) NOT GUILTY.
