THIRD COURT.-Thursday, November 23rd, 1871.
Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Eag.
55. GEORGE EDWARDS (22), and JAMES MoGRATH (22), Stealing
six blankets and twenty pairs of leggings, of Joseph Baxendale and others.
Mr. BEsLEy conducted the Prosecution; Mr. STRAIGHT defended Edwards,
and Mr. ST. AUBTN defended McGrath.
HARRY ACKERS. I am a van boy employed by Messrs. Pickford and Co.
-about 6.30 on Wednesday evening, 15th November, I was with a van in
Houndsditeh-I was standing on the pavement, close to the van-MoGrath
spoke to me, and said (That cellar is very dangerous, I nearly slipped
down" had not said anything to himI shut down the cellar-flap, and I
saw the other prisoner jump off the shafts of the van with a truss on his
shoulder--I went to run round to eatch him, and MeGrath caught me by
the throat and stopped me-I struggled away from him, and halloaed out
44 Stop ef !"-I ran after the man with the truss, and he dropped it
against the kerbI saw a gentleman, whom I know now to be Mr. Linnell,
run after Edwards-I went back to the van, and Edwards was brought
back by Mr. Linnell-] have seen both the prisoners together in Hounds-
ditch before-MoGrath had a round hat on-I was taken to the police-
station, and showed a number of men, I think five or six-I was asked
which was the person, and the one that held me, and I picked out MeGrath
he had his cap off then.
Cross-examined by MR. STRAIGHT. I have often seen Edwards but not
regularly-I did not lose sight of him on this night-it was 6.30 last Wed-
nesday evening-I was close to the man when he jumped off the van-I
ran round before the horse's head-he ran up a court, and I lost sight of
him-he dropped the truss a goodish bit from the court-I saw Mr. Linnell
run up the court after him.
Cross-ezamined by MR. ST. AUBYN. The cellar was open about a foot
from where I was standing had to look after that as well as my van-
MeGrath was not on the shaft at all-he was on the footway.
JOHN LINNELL. I am an Excise Officer in Her Majesty's Customs-on
Wednesday evening, 15th November, I was in Houndsditch- heard a
ery of 44 Stop thief! saw Edwards drop the bale and run awayI fol-
lowed him up a court, and he was caught by a gentleman named Leighton
-[ don't think he is here-I only lost sight of him as he went up the
court-I saw him stopped when I got into the court-he is the man I saw
drop the bale.
Cross examined by MR. STRAIGHT. I was five or six yards from him when
he dropped the bale-it was 6.30 in the evening-I lost sight of him as
he turned round into the court.
