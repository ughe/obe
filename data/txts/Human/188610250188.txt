I am a fireman in the employ of the London, Chatham and Dover Railway Company—on Sunday, 19th September, I was in this locomotive depot—I saw the two prisoners come from Queen's Road and cross the turntable, where they pulled the catches out—they then went over the bridge into the road, and we went after them to stop them—Leonard was going to capture Chapman, but he struck him with the spanner, and afterwards Davis struck him with a bottle—they got away. MARY JANE ALLEN. I am the wife of Nicholas Allen—we live at the London Steam Saw Mills, Stewart's Lane—on Sunday afternoon, 9th September, I was sitting outside my door with a baby in my arms—I saw the two prisoners come round the corner; Chapman had a spanner in his hand—he put it on a post, and said if he gave one of them a knock on the head with that it would soon settle them—soon after I saw Leonard come over the fence—he said "It is well you are gone"—they returned with foul language, and he went to Davis and asked who did it—he turned to arrest Chapman, and he hit him with the spanner in the ribs, but he meant it for his head, and when his mate went to arrest him he swung the spanner that no one should arrest him—I called for my husband, but he did not hear me—Leonard went to him, and he up with  the bottle and hit him on the head, and when he found he had knocked him down and stunned him, he up with  the bottle and hit him on the head, and then ran away. FREDERICK REED. I live at 22, Stockdale Road, Battersea and am an errand boy—on this Sunday I was in the Stewart's Lane depot—I saw Sambo  hit Leonard with the spanner across the ribs, and then Leonard struck him; then they went to arrest Davis, and Davis up with  the bottle and knocked him down. CHARLES EVERTT( Police Sergeant W  42). I arrested Davis—I read the warrant to him; he said that he was there at the time, but he did not do anything, it was Sambo  and Carter—I said "Where is the spanner?"—he said " Sambo  had it, and I am not going to suffer for others." CONELIUS MONAHAN( Policeman W  275). I arrested Chapman—I read the warrant to him; he said "I know nothing at all about it."
   Davis in his defence stated that he saw Amos shift the catches, and that he only hit Leonard with the bottle after Leonard had hit him. Chapman stated that Amos had moved the catches, and that he hit Leonard with a piece of wood, not with a spanner. GUILTY.
   CHAPMAN then PLEADED GUILTY to a conviction of felony in March , 1883. They were further indicted for assaulting Thomas Leonard , to which they PLEADED GUILTY. CHAPMAN  — Twelve Months' Hard Labour. DAVIS  — Eight Months' Hard Labour.
