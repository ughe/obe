Bostock as soon as he could waived the conversation, and began to talk about his own apprenticeship—he did not say anything about his attempting to break open the door—Mr. Bostock was in liquor—I went so, or words to that effect—he said he did not mean it if he said it—my son said nothing—many more words ensued on that occasion, in the same strain—I have seen him once since, after my son took him him stop there—I found him at his lodgings—my son then told me that his master would not let him work—that was a month ago—I told my son I was going to the master, and I brought him to the master myself the same evening—Mr. Bostock then told him he was not going to keep his shop open for him, and told me he should not come that night—he said so out of my son's presence and in his presence—my son thought he was ill-treated by Mr. Bostock's refusal, and said that Mr. Bostock would not let him come there with me—I asked Mr. Bostock, when he refused to let my son come to work that evening, if he would let me have an interview with him—he said it was not convenient then, he would in an hour—I went in an hour—I saw my son after seeing Mr. Bostock—I told my son what had passed between me and
