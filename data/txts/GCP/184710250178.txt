BENJAMIN FISHER (policeman, 272 R.) On Wednesday, the 29th of
Sept., I went by the direction of the inspector to the prisoner's house,
35, St. Mary-street, Woolwich-the prisoner showed me the house-I asked
him if he lived there, he said, "Yes"-I asked which were his rooms--he
took me to the parlour, and from the parlour to the bed-room, and in the
bed-room I found a few screws which have the appearance of being nen,
these are them I then went down to the kitchen, and in a cellar I found a
large box with a number of drawers in it, and almost every drawer had some
thing in it-these sixteen keys were hanging up in the cellar--almost every
key is marked the prisoner was by at the time I found in that cellar, or
coal-shed, these brass articles in a drawer by the side of the chest and some
nails in the chest, all marked with the arrow-I found in the chest a latch
and staples which have the broad arrow on them, and this file with the broad
arrow on it-I asked the prisoner if the chest belonged to him-he said,
"Yes"-he afterwards said it was his father's-I asked who occupied that
room, he told me that the woman who was there occupied it-he then said be
occupied it, and she did not, only for the use of cooking- I was a consider-
able time looking over the box of nails-then Mr. Wootton came, and he
made a second search at the time I was gone to the Dock-yard to acquaint
Mr. Field -I came back with Mr. Field, and assisted in bringing the things
away-I took them to the Dock-yard, and they have been in the possession
of Mr. Field-at the time the things were found, the prisoner was walking
about, crying, and saying he was a foolish fellow, and he wanted me to gire
him the things that he might throw them away-his wife was there at the
time, and begged me to do it-I said I could not do anything of the kind,
I had my duty to perform.
Cross-examined. Q. He seemed in great distress? A. Yes-he and his
wife and children live in the bed-room--the screws I found in the bed-room
are not marked-the other articles new and old were found in the chest in
the cellar-I know the prisoner by seeing him go in and out of the yard to
his work, that is all-Mrs. Clayton uses the kitchen-there was a benchi
the kitchen, and he used it as a workshop-I have passed the house, but
never knew the prisoner lived in it-I should say the chest had not been
opened for three or four months-it was entirely a tool chest-it looked very
dusty--there were two doors to the cellar, and no window.
