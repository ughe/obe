   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You cannot positively swear where you lost it? A.  I know I had it that day, and I know I lost it that day—I missed it the next morning—I had it on, the day I was at Mrs. French's, and I was no where else—t always wear it—I know I had it in the evening—I never took it off after I put it on—I took off my rings at Mrs. French's, but I do not remember taking off this particular ring.
   COURT . Q.  Was this the outside ring? A.  Yes.
   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You put on the rings that you took off to the best of your belief? A.  Yes—after sitting there I walked home that night—I live about five minutes' walk from Mrs. French's—Mrs. and Miss French have known me about four years—Miss French taught my children—she knew me perfectly well, and I suppose she knew my ring—I do not know whether Mrs. French knew my ring—I seldom saw her—Miss French came to my house as governess—I visited Mrs. French perhaps two or three times—I had seen her frequently—she had been at my house, I cannot tell how many times—perhaps ten times—she had an opportunity of seeing my ring, but a guard ring is so usual to wear, that I should not suppose she noticed it—it is a common ring.
   Q.  Is there nothing remarkable about it? A.  Yes, it has been taken in—I had never seen the prisoner—he had never seen my ring—he was not there the night I lost it.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Are you at all conscious of having dropped the ring from your finger? A.  I could not, it fitted so very tight—I had not tan to any body's house between the evening when I took it off at Mrs. French's and missing it the following morning—I know I put it on the morning that I went to Mrs. French's—I had not taken my rings off for the purpose of washing my fingers, or any other purpose of that sort before I went to Mrs. French's, but I took them off there—my infant was there, and I fancied she had knocked it down—there was no other place at which I could have dropped it—I took all my rings off in Mrs. French's parlour, with the exception of one diamond ring, and I put all on, as I supposed, that I took off. ELIZA SARAH FRENCH. I am single, and live with my parents, in Bond-street, Vauxhall. I know the prisoner, and I know Mrs. Gowland—I remember, on the 13th of October, she drank tea at my mother's, in the front parlour—on the morning of the 14th I remember seeing the prisoner—I should think about ten o'clock—the parlour bad not been cleaned up at that time—I went into the parlour on being informed the prisoner was there—I found him there—my mother came into the room, and went out, and came back again—the prisoner on that occasion pro duced this ring to me—( looking at it )—he asked me to try it on, and asked if it fitted me—I tried it on, and soon after my mamma entered the said to her, "See what a pretty ring Mr. Stanhope has given me"—after I left the room.
