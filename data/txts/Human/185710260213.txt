EDWARD SATCH. I am a jeweller, of 172; shoreditch On 2nd Sept., Brooks and Peach came to my shop—I think it was in the afternoon—I sold them these watches, and took 38 l . 8 s . 6 d . of Brooks, all in gold—Peach was present when he paid me—the watches were selected out of the window—he said very little—I had most of the say—he bought one for himself and one for the lady who was with him.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Did you make an entry of it? A.  Yes, in my book—I have not got it here—policeman came to me about two days afterwards to make inquiry—he asked if they were bought of me and when—I did not refer to my book then, because he placed the book be fore me, and I saw the date—it is the 2nd—it is my writing—I am sure. that is the correct date. SAMUEL JOHN PIPER. I am foreman to Abraham Limes, a tailor, 168, Shoreditch—on Wednesday, 2nd Sept., Brooks and Peach came there together—I served Brooks with a coat, waistcoat, and trowsers—he paid 2 l . 19 s . for them—he paid in gold—I gave him 1 s . change—here is the bill I gave him.
   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Did you make any entry of it in your books? A.  I did—I afterwards referred to the entry when the officers came, and found it—it corresponds with the bill. JOHN BOND. ( Police sergeant, P  2). On 8th Sept., I was at the Peckham station, when Brooks was brought there—as I was entering the charge, he said he was innocent of it—I said, "If you are innocent, you can account for this property that was found to be in your possession the day after the robbery"—he said that he had purchased it, and the constable had the receipts—I said, "Yes, but how did you come by the money to purchase it with?"—(I did not, give him any caution before putting these questions not, think ing I should be examined as a witness)—he said it was money that be had won on a betting transaction, where he had won 40 l . odd—I asked him when—he said the Saturday week previous—I asked where—he said he could not tell—I asked from whom he had won it—he said he did not know—I asked him if he could refer me to any one who was present at the time—he said no, he could not—in booking the charge, I asked what he was—he said he was a betting, man, and he got his living by betting at public houses, where skittles and card playing was going on—I was present at the Police Court when Peach was pointed out by the witness Gosling—I have measured the distance from where the Laites live to the King's Head—it is about thirty feet;
