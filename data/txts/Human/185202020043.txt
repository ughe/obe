I am in the employ of Messrs. Ellis and Everington, drapers and warehousemen, of St, Paul's Churchyard. I know Lyons—on 25th Sept he came into the warehouse, and was shown a parcel of glace silks by Mr. Churchill—he was also offered a parcel of figured silks—he bought one piece to take home, to try if it was suitable for parasols—there was seventy-nine yards of it, at 21 3/4 d ., which came to 7 l . 3 s . 3 d .—it was sent home to him—it was on a Thursday, and I did not see him again; but on the Saturday I saw some more goods sent to the entering-room, to be sent to him—they were the figured silks which had been shown to him on the Thursday—there were 6,316 1/2 yards, and the piece which we sent before—I made no arrangement as to what account that was to go down in with Lyons—it is the course of our business, when we are sending out goods, to send them first into the entering-room—these goods were so sent from our room.
