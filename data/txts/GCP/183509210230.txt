Joun GYNN (City police-constable No. 18.) I am stationed round the
Bank. I remember when Bray's omnibuses first made their appearance
they appeared to me to be running direct against Trevett' s I saw Mr. Bray
and his men frequently-I have heard the men say to Trevett's men that
they would melt the old cobbler's war-they would say to them, "We will
be with you, you b-8," and use very bad language; and sometimes they
made use of the word snob-I do not know that they talked of any body else
beside Trevett-this violent opposition went on, I should think, about a week
-Bray's omnibuses did not take their time, and take their turn as the others
when they first started--they afterwards had time given them--they did
not keep their time as other omnibuses did they used to come in, and
go round Cornhill, and ply for hire, if they could get passengers; if they
did not, they wanted to come in and take time that occasioned a disturb
ance, because they would want to cut in betwixt the three omnibuses
which were allowed to stand at the Bank-I have found it necessary to
interfere, by their coming in irregularly, wanting to cut in, and throw the
others out, which almost endangered the people's lives, by throwing
omnibuses and horses on the pavement--they have come in at times
and taken their times regularly; at other times they have not--they have
gone out in all kind of ways; at other times taking times they have gone
to the corner of Cornhill, and stood there--I have crossed over to tell
them to go on-one Friday evening, about two months ago, Mr. Bray's
omnibus had been plying round Cornhill; he then came and wanted to go
in between the first and second omnibuses which were standing there
spoke to Mr. Bray, and said, “Why don't you wait till the first is gone
and then come in third ?"-he then said, "I will be d d if I don't get
them all turned away from the Bank, if it costs me five hundred pounds
and I will line the street with omnibuses."
