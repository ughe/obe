2216. WILLIAM THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 6th
of August, 1 pair of boots, value 98., the goods of James Wright.
JAMES WRIGHT. I am a boot and shoemaker, and live in Stonecutter-
street, Farringdon-street. I lost this pair of boots on the 6th of August
they were tied to a nail.
WILLIAM HOWELL. I live in Stonecutter-street, Farringdon-street, next
door to the prosecutor. On the morning of the 6th of August I saw the
prisoner pass my shop-door, endeavouring to conceal something under his
jacket-I went out, and he ran across the road into a house--I over-
took him, and took these boots from his hand-he said, "Take the boots,
and let me go"-I dragged him out-he kicked my shins, and tried to
throw me.
de of
Prisoner. I was going through Farringdon-market; a young man asked
me to buy a pair of boots; I bought them, and went home with them.
GUILTY. Aged 19.-Transported for Seven Years.
2217. MARY WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of
August, 1 purse, value 6d. ; 1 half-crown, 8 shillings, and 4 sixpences;
the property of William Kerr Reed, from his person.
WILLIAM KERR Reed. I am a silversmith, living in Bream's-buildings,
Chancery-lane. About hall-past twelve o'clock at night, on the 9th of
August, I was in Skinner-street-two women met me--I believe the pri-
soner was one of them-they held up their arms, to obstruct my going on
-the other took hold of my arm, and when we got to the bottom of Hol-
born-hill she suddenly left-I felt, and missed my purse-I looked round,
and saw the woman that left me had gone across the road I went
over, and charged her with it-she denied it-I thought I saw something
pass to the prisoner--I said, "She has given the purse to this woman"
--the prisoner walked away-1 followed her to Field-lane, and called
"Stop thief"-the policeman being at hand, he took her within twenty
yards-on our way to the station, something dropped, which turned out
to be my purse.

Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. How far did the other woman walk
with you? A. From the top of Skinner-street nearly to the bottom of
Holborn-hill-the second woman walked behind me-I only saw two
women there I will not swear positively that the prisoner was the woman
that walked behind me-my purse was in my trowsers' pocket.
Court. Q. To the best of your belief, was the prisoner the woman
who was behind you when the other walked with you? A. Yes.
THOMAS HANDLEY SPARKS (City police-constable, No. 292.) The prose.
cutor followed the prisoner, and charged her with stealing his purse and
money-I took her-she had got about twenty yards, when she attempted
to give this purse to some girl-it dropped from her band—I took it up.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you mean to swear it must have dropped from
the prisoner? A. Yes, I saw it come out of her hand--the prosecutor
was on the opposite side.
NOT GUILTY.
