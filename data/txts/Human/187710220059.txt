I had given them, and they had won for me 60 l .—I said "That being so, you should hand to me 25 l ."—they were all speaking together—they said that the money I had lost would have to be paid before the money won would be given—I said it was a very strange proceeding; I was going away, and must make some arrangement, and the bookmaker made arrangements with the two others to meet at the Cannon Street Hotel at, I think, about 7 or 7.30 o'clock—they said they would go with me, which they did, and we went out of the racecourse on to Norwood Junction to Victoria, from Victoria to Kennington Park, and then they stopped at a public-house there till I went to my lodgings—I got my pocket-book, and found I had 27 l . in it in notes and gold—I went back to the public-house, and found them standing outside—the cab was kept—we drove from there to a place where Grant said he stayed, some hotel in Aldersgate—he got out, and said he would be away for a few minutes; he would go to his hotel for Mb money—he was away about two minutes or so, and when he came back he had in his hand what looked like a bank-note—I did not see the face of it; it  was folded up, and what appeared to be some sovereigns on the top—he said he was sorry, he found he had not so much money as he thought; that he would not be able to pay all the money he had lost, but he gave it to Cannon, saying to him "I will give you my  money, and you can arrange with, the bookmaker; "and turning to me he said "You had better give him your money also," and I handed the 27 l . to him—we were driving to Cannon Street—I might mention that on getting into the cab before that Cannon began speaking to me about his kinship—he told me he knew very little about London; that he was only in London occasionally—we then drove near to Cannon Street Hotel—we had to go down some street to get to it—I went with the prisoner and Draper into the station, on the platform, where we found the young man represented to be the bookmaker—Cannon and he went apart and spoke to one another, and Grant remained with me—after they had spoken aside for a time, they came and said to me ""We will go round to the private bar and we will arrange the matter there"—the bookmaker and Draper went first—Grant kept beside me, and we followed a few yards behind—when we got in, Draper and the bookmaker went to the far side of the private bar and had some conversation there;
