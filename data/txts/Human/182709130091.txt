 1733.  JOHN SIMPSON and SAMUEL BROWN were indicted for stealing, on the 8th of September , 1 pair of trousers, value 20s., the goods of Thomas Thwaites.
   WILLIAM WHITTINGHAM. I am an officer. On the 8th of September, about a quarter past seven o'clock, I was in Broad-street, St. Giles, and saw the prisoners go to Mr. Thwaites shop - Simpson made several attempts to get these trousers, which hung inside the door - he got them at last by a strong pull, and gave them to Brown, who was looking in at the window - I had seen them together some time before. I ran over and seized Brown with the property; another person stopped Simpson.
   THOMAS THWAITES. I live in St. Giles; these trousers hung inside my door; they were pinned to a coat; it would require a strong pull to get them down.
 SIMPSON's Defence. I saw a waistcoat piece in the window; I was looking at it; the other prisoner came and asked the time, I felt some one brush by me - I looked at the window again, and a man came up, and said, "I have got you;" he would not tell me what for.
 SIMPSON -    GUILTY. Aged 24.
 BROWN -    GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 THIRD DAY. - SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 15.
 Third Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1734.  RICHARD CRAWFORD ENGLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of September , 1 half-crown, the money of Charles Thomas Monday. The prisoner pleaded GUILTY. Aged 14. Confined One Month.
 1735.  HENRY FORSYTH alias    PEAKE was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 60 lbs. weight of lead, value 10s., the goods of John Featherstone , and affixed to a certain dwelling-house of his, against the statute.
   ELEANOR JONES. I am a widow, and live in East-street, Red-lion-square. I let the house No. 4, Great Ormond-street, which belongs to my brother, ( John Featherstone,) to Mr. Mortimer - I saw a pump in the yard about three days before the 24th of August, on which day, the watchman came and told me it was gone. I went in the evening, and missed it - I have seen one at the office, which appears to me to be the same; I do not see any difference in it.
   JOHN WILLIAM MORTIMER. I took this house. On the evening of the 23d, about nine o'clock, I saw the pump safe in the yard, and at nine the next morning, the watchman came to tell me it was gone, and that the prisoner was in custody - it was then missing.
   HENRY TURNER. I am a watchman. On the 24th of August, a quarter before one o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner in Great Ormond-street, with this pump on his shoulder - I asked him what he had got - he made no answer; I then took charge of him - I have tried this pump to the place, and it appears to me to have belonged to there. There was another man with him, but I never lost sight of the prisoner.
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw this pump laying on a dunghill; it appeared to have no owner; a young man saw me take it up; he went to the watch-house, but they would not hear him.
 GUILTY. Aged 23. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
 1736.  JOHN FRANCIS and CHARLES SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 3d of August , 1 live turkey, price 12s., and 18 apples, value 1s., the property of William Hierons.
   WILLIAM HIERONS. I am a farmer, and live at Perryford, near Harrow. On the 3d of August my servant missed a turkey - I went into the yard, and it was gone - I had seen it safe in the afternoon of the day before - it is what we call a milk-white one, and was moulting at the time - I know nothing of the prisoners - I also missed some apples from some small trees; I took some apples to the office, and what I saw there corresponded exactly.
   WILLIAM GREAVES. I am a patrol of Paddington. On the 3d of August I was going my rounds, and at White's-bridge I saw the two prisoners coming across the country; one had a bundle under his arm, and the other this basket across his shoulder - I took a different direction, and met them in a lane, and asked what they had got - Francis said, "I have got nothing, not much;" I said,"What have you got?" he then said, "A turkey, I bought it of a man in a cart at Harrow, for 5s., for a gentleman in the Borough" - I asked what part of the Borough he lived in - he said, he did not know; I then asked where he was to take it to - he said, to the gentleman's brother in Westminster - I asked where there - he said he did not know; I asked if there was any direction on the turkey, and whether it was alive or dead; he said it was alive, and there was no direction; I then asked Smith what he had got; he said, "Some apples, to make a dumpling;" I asked where he was going to to make it; Francis replied, he was going to his house; I asked where that was - he said, in Westminster; I asked what street, and what number - he said he did not know; I then took them into custody, and have brought the turkey here.
   FRANCIS BRADBURY. The prisoners were brought to me with the turkey and the apples - Francis said he had bought the turkey of a man near Harrow, who had forty or fifty of them; it was in a small basket, doubled up head and heels, to crowd it in.(Turkey produced and sworn to.)
 FRANCIS -    GUILTY. Aged 49.
 SMITH -    GUILTY. Aged 40. Transported for Seven Years.
