Cross-eramined by MR. PARRY. Q. You were examined, I think, on the trial
of Mr. Sayward, with a man named Whicher ? A. Yes; I then stated how this
money had been disposed of-it was disposed of between me, Whicher, and
Winter, the whole of it-lI had part of the six pieces-there were thirty-four
pieces of Paris-de-laine--] had three lengths, of fourteen yards each-I had
no whole piece-Whicher had the majority of the six pieces-there were
four taken down to bis house; in fact, the whole six pieces were taken to his
house on the first oceasion-] cannot say whether Whicher had four of the
pieces or not-I brought the contents of one piece from Whicher's, together
with three lengths for myself; and that which Senior had was one piece, in
two halves-the rest was left at Whicher's house-how many that was I
cannot ay-Winter was to have had some, but I believe he sold bis to
Whicher, from what I understood-Senior had two half pieces--I was sen-
tenced to ten years' transportation for the gold-dust robbery-I am under that
sentence now-I do not know what the Secretary of State has done for me
-I know of nothing yet--if my sentence has been commuted it is more than
I know-it has not been communicated to me-I am not expecting a com-
mutation, that I know of-I cannot say that I have any expectation of
anything of the sort-all I can say is, when I left the prison I found the
ticket over my door, '10 years transportation, the same; and T have heard
nothing to the contrary since I have been here-] cannot say that I shall get
anything by giving evidence ; I do not expeet to-| had a very uneasy mind,
and I was very glad to get these things off my mind-] was uneasy after my
conviction, and so I was before-] would have got off if I could, decidedly
-I pleaded not guilty-1 was innocent of the obarge of which I was found
