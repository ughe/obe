THOMAS FARRANT. I am a policeman. I was in company with Hobbs
-I went after James Slater- I came up to him, and told him he must go
back with me - he asked what for- said I would take him to where the
females were who had the clothes-he said he knew nothing about the
clothes or them, and he would break my head if I touched him-1 took
him to Edward-street, and from there to the station-house- I found a large
key of a street door on him, which I found was the key of his lodging-1
told him the females were stopped with the clothes, and I would take him
to them--he said he knew nothing about any young women, and he had not
been in Edward-street-at the station-house he spoke to the prisoner
Slater, and after that she said the clothes had been given to her by a man
who owed her some money-he had told her to hold her tongue-I then
asked her how she became possessed of the clothes-she said a man owed
her 58., and had given her the clothes till he could pay her, and she gave
the other girl the shirt.
Cross-examined. Q. Of course you considered that material ? 4. I
thought it right to be known-I stated it before the Magistrate on the first
hearing, but I am not certain whether it was down in the deposition-
signed my deposition-I will swear I mentioned it I do not know that it
was not in my deposition.
THOMAS Brooks. At the time of the robbery, 1 lived in King-street,
Soho. I was very late on Saturday night when I came in-the man
belonging to the things was very much addicted to liquor, and I did not
like him to have the key-when I came home, I did not lock the door,
but merely shut it to I went to bed immediately, and the deceased man in
another bed- I went to sleep, and never awoke till the man awoke me
in a state of distraetion-I put on some of my clothes-I looked about and
found my coat was gone - I had no other to put on--I went into the street
in my shirt-sleeves--I saw my coat again at Marlborough-street-I never
saw the prisoners till they were at the office--this is my coat-it happened
on Sunday morning, the 10th of July, before two o'clock in the morning.
Cross-examined. Q. Is the house you live in a common lodging
house? 1. It is a cook's-shop, and they take in lodgers-there is only
one room for lodgers.
