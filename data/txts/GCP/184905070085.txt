DUKE, Mayor.
ELIZABETH DRYSDALE. I am a widow--the prisoner's husband lodged in
my house, and she was in the habit of coming backwards and forwards-1
missed four towels and some other things-these are them (produced)-1
lost them in Feb.
Prisoner. They are my own. Witness. I swear to them; they have my
needlework on them.
David Ovenden (policeman). I found these articles at the prisoner's
lodgings.
Prisoner. I have some fellow-napkins to these; they were made out of
my husband's old shirts.
Nor GUILTY.
1178. JAMES HILL, stealing 9 feet of wood, value 18.; the goods of
John Brogden the younger.
JAMES STELFOX. I am in the service of Mr. John Brogden the younger-
we had some boards on 17th April - I have seen the boards, which are here-
I cannot swear whose they are, we use such; but there is so much timber
about there I believe they are not my master's.
John EDWARDS (policeman, R 176). About two o'clock in the morning,
on 17th April, I saw the prisoner take a board from a heap of others belong
ing to Mr. Brogden, and place it inside his garden-he went back and fetched
a second, and then a third-he came out again; he saw me and slammed the
door in my face.
ROBERT WHITEHEAD. I was in Mount-street that night-the policeman
told me something I went in front of the prisoner's house, and saw a board
in his garden ; where it came from I cannot tell.
Not Guilty.
1179. GEORGE EDGERLY, stealing 1 pair of boots, value 88. 6d.; the
goods of George Beaver.
THOMAS BARHAM. I live at Greenwich. On 20th April, about eight
o'clock, I was passing Mr. Beaver's shop-I saw the prisoner take the boots
off the nail-he put them under bis arm-I told the prosecutor's boy.
MARK DRAKE. I was serving a female-she told me the prisoner had
taken the boots-I went and stopped him with them under his arm-he said
he had bought them at a shop, he did not know where I gave him in charge.
GEORGE BEAVER. These are my boots--I missed them from a nail outside
my shop.
Prisoner's Defence. I bought the boots at a second-hand shop; I did not
take notice of the shop ; I was rather too much gone in liquor,
GUILTY. Aged 22.--Confined Six Months.
1180. STEPHEN KENFIELD and SARAH EDSER, stealing 1 shawl,
value 1s.; and 2 pieces of patchwork, value 9d. ; the goods of Sarah Tighe:
Kenfield having been before convicted.
JAMES Dry. I am in the service of Mrs. Sarah Tighe, of the Broadway,
Deptford. I hung this shawl and patchwork up outside the door in the morn-
ing on the 28th April—the policeman came afterwards, and I missed them.

I saw the prisoners together-I followed them, and after they had gone a
short distance, Edser left the man and went in the passage of Mrs. Tighe's
shop-she came out again, and then went in and came out again, and I saw
she had something-Kenfield was standing about sixty yards from the shop,
but in sight of it-Edser went towards him--they then went towards his
house, but he kept at a distance from her--when they got to his door he
stopped till she came up, and they went in together--they lived together--I
