Mr. Justice Phillimore. It may be summed up in this way. In fact
you made Lloyd a present of £868 during the past seven years through
not accepting the lowest tenderer. What is the explanation of that?
Your answer is, “The explanation so far as I know is, we always had good
milk-we never had a complaint about Lloyd."
Witness : In regard to the houses at Herne Bay I only know from what
I heard from Budd, when he was up there the other day, that they were
all let. I do not live in Herne Bay and I have not been there for four or
five months. My house is on the front. When I am there I stick there. I
never lived in a farmhouse. When the question was put to me at the police
court I did not recollect Mr. and Mrs. Calcutt ever staying there with me,
but Mrs. Hirst has told me that they did. They stayed there from Satur
day to Monday one night in August. That was on account of Mrs. Caloutt
wanting to go down and see houses at Herne Bay. I invited them myself.
I do not know about Calcutt being uneducated. I took a fancy to him, and
I used to put him forward as inuch as I possibly could. I was invariably
kind to him. In regard to the cost of the houses, I showed him all the
bills, with the exception of the small accounts that Budd had said he lit the
fire with and Budd's book. I bad the bills for all the timber and bricks and
all the heavy goods, ironmongery, and so on. The account is roughly in that
book. I made the account out for him. I showed him the amount of
money paid for bricks, showed him the number of cheques I sent Budd for
wages account, what I paid for timber, and so on, and they came to what
I bad charged him. He had them all out on the day of settlement, and
took them away with him. This book was never produced in
any proceedings until now. Calcutt saw it. It was never in
dispute. I only used to keep rough notes of these accounts. It was
really put down in the banking account. It is the whole transaction,
The furniture in the Bank of Friendship" was taken down to Calcutt's
house. My sister wanted it, but I preferred his having it. Bourchier,
the carpenter and builder, knew six or eight weeks before the houses were
finished that they were for Calcutt. I paid bim right up to the finish.
In 1903 there was the whitewashing contract, on which Calcutt claimed
£503. Knight certified for £371 and Calcutt was paid £440. I do not
think Knight said £503 was a "terrible price," but he did accept the
pbrase. I never said to Calcutt, “ If you had stood out for another £89
you would have got it." I agreed with Knight that £373 was right. Mr.
Andrews and myself, on the Select Committee, opposed the payment. We
were out-voted on the £371 or £373. I do not recollect whether I took
any steps at the Board meeting to prevent Calcutt getting the £70 extra.
I don't think I was there, or, if I was, I had very likely left before it
came on. In regard to the indestructible paint, there also I said I
thought Knight's was the proper amount. I supported Mr. Kirwen when
he moved the 9d. at the Board. I was there and voted, but I was
Mayor then, and had to get away to a meeting after. I never had any
communication with Mr. Draper about it. I do not know much of bim.
He bas been on the board eight years, I should think I voted against
Calcutt on many occasions. I cannot name sny, or when I voted for him.
