Prisoner's Defence.  On the night of the robbery I went to Drury-lane Theatre, and fell in company with a young man, a sailor, who said he was steward on board a ship; we went to a public house, and met a friend of his, and went to his house, where we remained till about 3 o'clock in the morning; we then came away, and, coming along Drury-lane, there was a disturbance, and I missed the young man; I thought I could meet with him by the Docks or Blackwall, and, crossing Tower-hill, the policeman stopped me; he says I was out of breath, but it was a severe cold I had. GUILTY. Aged 21.— Confined Twelve Months .
 508. ROBERT WATSON , stealing 10 printed books, value 10 l  .; the goods of William Daniel Henry Ochme .
   MR. DENNY . conducted the Prosecution. JAMES THOMAS. I am clerk to Messrs. Ochme and Lloyd, solicitors No. 124, Bishopsgate-street; they have also a place of business at Calcutta, On 8th April I was in the back office on the second floor, near 2 o'clock in the day, and heard footsteps crossing the landing; I went on to the landing and saw the prisoner descending the stairs, with a large canvass bag on his shoulder, apparently heavy—I went into the front room, and missed ten volumes of Bittlewood's "Conveyancing Precedents" from the shelves—I immediately pursued the prisoner, and overtook him at the bottom of the second flight of stairs—I called out, "Drop them; I have got you"—he immediately dropped it, and ran off—I pursued him about fifty yards, and, after a considerable struggle, brought him back, sent for a policeman, and gave him into custody—the books were lying on the mat; they were the pro perty of Mr. Ochme, which I had missed—when I brought the prisoner back, he said, "You had better let me go, and go after the other man; he is going up the street"—I saw no other man. CHARLES BROWN. ( City policeman , 654). The prisoner was given into my custody; I found on him 5 d . in copper, and 6 d . in silver—on taking him to the lock-up, he said, "I was in a public house in Houndsditch, and met a gentleman there, who said he wanted something moved from his office; he took me to the house in question, in Bishopsgate-street, took me to the second floor, into an office, and, pointing to the books in question, he said, 'Take these, and follow me;' I took the books, and followed the gentleman, and when I got down stairs the last witness ran after me and collared me."
 (  The prisoner, in Ms defence, repeated the statement he had made to the officer. ) GUILTY. Aged 59.— Confined Six Months .

 510. ANN PRICE , stealing 1 shawl; the goods of Sarah Egerton .
   MR. PLATT . conducted the Prosecution. SARAH EGERTON. I am servant at the King's Arms Inn, Uxbridge. On 8th April I had a shawl—I hung it up in the taproom at half past 2 o'clock, and missed it a little before 4—this ( produced ) is it—the prisoner was in the taproom that day for three or four hours, and left about half past 3 o'clock, by the bus.
   Cross-examined by MR. SLEIGH . Q.  Did you know her at all? A.  No; I never saw her before that day—I was not in the habit of lending my shawl to any one.
