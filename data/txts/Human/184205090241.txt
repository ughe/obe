   Q.  How does your son go to bed over the stable? A.  There is a door opens close by the harness-room which leads up stairs, to what we call the outer door—it turns to the left when you get to the top of the stairs, and this door faces him—there are two rooms beyond where he sleeps, and one on the right of him, where the stableman used to sleep—my son's room is part over the back harness-room, where the dirty harness is kept—his window looks into the garden—he would come along the garden into the stable-yard to go to bed, and by the harness-room—it is an open stair case—I have two men and a boy besides my son—they do not sleep on the premises—a fire is necessary in the harness-room in wet weather, to dry the harness.
   COURT . Q.  The two harness-rooms are on the ground-floor? A.  Yes, my son sleeps over the back one—that is not where I saw this great pre paration for a fire—that was in the large harness-room—the prisoner locks the harness-room up, and always keeps the key—you do not go through the stable to it—it is a separate door on the left of the stable, and very near the stable—when in the stable, you do not come into the yard to get to it, but into the colonnade.
   Q.  Could any body in the stable-yard see any thing which is going on in the colonnade? A.  They can, if they were to look into the harness room itself, through the large window, any body who was in the stable-yard could—there is no curtain to the window that I know of, to either of the windows which face the garden—the glass is frosted, that people should not look in out of the garden—no body in the garden can see through, unless they get steps or something, and look through the upper panes, which are not frosted—they are rather higher than a man's head—any body can see into the harness-room from the stable-yard. SAMUEL SMITH DAONAL. I am in the service of James Collingbourn, a pawnbroker, at Wandsworth. On the Wednesday night in question, I was in Mr. Shiell's stables—Gardiner said there was a goose—the prisoner then went out of the stable—I did not see him go out—as I was holding Gardiner's lamp, I heard Houghton say he was gone, and when I looked round, he was gone—the door was locked—I tried to get out—we could not, but did a short time afterwards—Houghton's son was not there at that time.
