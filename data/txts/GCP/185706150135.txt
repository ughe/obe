764. WILLIAM SCAINES was again indicted for burglariously breaking
and entering the dwelling house of Elizabeth Coate, and stealing i flute and
case, value 31.; the goods of Alfred William Ray. do
MR. SLEIGH conducted the Prosecution.com
ELIZABETH GROVES. I am servant to Mrs. Coate, No. 7, Burton Crescent.
On the morning after 3rd April I came down, and found the parlour window
open, and some things had been taken out of the house I had on the pre-
vious night fastened the doors and window-I missed, amongst other things,
a flute and a case, which I had seen safe the night before.
SAMUEL COPPIN (Police sergeant, P 15). On 8th May, I took the prisoner
into custody-I found this flute and case standing on a table in his bar-I
asked him how he accounted for this he said he did not know anything
about it.
Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE. Q. Did he say he did not know it was
there! 4. I believe he said afterwards he did not know how it came there
- this was not in the common her which was accessible to all, only to his
own family; it was shut off from the public I do not know whether this
is an old flute; the gentleman valued it at 101 I think-the key was given
to me some days afterwards by the prosecutrix ; it was not in the publie
bar—there is a little door which separates that from the public bar it was
in a place where the cupboard and the table were, it was entirely a private
part of the establishment-it had nothing to do with the public no per-
son would go there, only the family-the prisoner owned the case I asked
him if he had the key, and he said, "No."
ELIZABETH COATE. I live with my mother at No. 7, Burton Crescent
This flute is the property of Mr. Alfred William Ray, who resides in the
house-l saw it safe on the 3rd April.
