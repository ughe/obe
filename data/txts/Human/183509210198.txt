Trevett's and Bolton's omnibuses in particular, and Mr. Bird's also—I believe there was no other that day—I watched them several other days, and saw them drive close up to he pavement, and drive their poles close up to my omnibns, so that no passenger could get in—on those other days I saw the omnibuses belonging to the other defendants I have named doing so—I do not know that there was one who has not done so—the whole of them have been a continual annoyance—on the 15th, I lengthened one of my omnibuses to Maida—hill, still continuing on the same line of road, through Paddington—on that day, a new omnibus, called "The Emperor," No. 352, with the name of "Edmund Sylvester" on it, started against the one of mine, which ran to Maida—hill; and another, called "The Albion," No. 177, with the name of "Thomas Corbett" on it, started in opposition to the one of mine, which went to Paddington—John Sylvester drove the "Emperor, "and John Smith was the conductor; but whether they did so the first day it started, I cannot tell—Gough drove the "Albion," and one of the Trevett's, (Thomas, I think,) was the conductor—I had observed Cow  deroy driving his omnibus the first week, which he every now and then does drive—he was one who ran his pole up against me, and put me on one side, and was annoying me—I saw the other defendants in the first week; and omnibuses would come down from Paddington to where I set out, wait till I came out of my yard, follow my omnibuses up to Paddington, wait till they turned round to go, and then immediately gallop close before them—one day, one of my horses came home with the wheel run over his foot—sometimes there was a pannel broken—I saw Sylvester's omnibus run against my pannel one day in Oxford-street—I have seen them come fol lowing my buses  home, without plying for hire; and they would stop for an hour, to see whether we attempted to go out again—this was done frequently, every day almost—about three weeks after we started, there came a very rainy day—one of my buses  let water—we took the passengers out, and put them in another bus  —I got into the bus that let water, and made them drive it down to the maker's in Whitechapel; and the "Emperor" followed me all the way, and stopped there four hours—John Sylvester drove the "Emperor" there, and John Smith was the conductor.
   Q.  Did you ever meet any of the defendants any where? Q.  Yes; the first I saw of them was Josiah Parkes—I forget when it was but it was near the beginning of my starting—I found him in my yard in Holborn, when I got there, we went into the public-house, and had a glass of brandy-and-water—he told me I was a madman to start if I did not buy times—that the "Pad dington committee" would run me off the road, and ruin me—I said no, I should try—I alterwards saw Trevett, Hill, and Cowderoy, all three together, I think it was on the 4th of July—I had just come home from the bazaar, and got out of the bus,  when Robert Trevett came, laid hold of my coat, and said I should go and have something to drink—I said I had not had my dinner, and I seldom took any thing before dinner—he said, would I come back—I said I would—I went back, and Trevett, Hill, and Cowderoy were there, drinking—they asked me whether I meant to do what was rights—I said I meant to stop if I could, and keep on my omnibus they said it
