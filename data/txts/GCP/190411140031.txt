Cross-examined by MR. SLATER. On the night of his arrest he had been
drinking but he was not drunk-he understood what was said to him
he might have fallen asleep at the start for a few minutes.
Re-examined. He did not say anything to convict himself in any way,
Rutter, in his defence on oath, said he found out people who had judg
ments against them and then tried to negotiate loans for them, getting a com
mission for himself : that he ascertained that Mrr. Fredericks was in want
af a loan and called on her to negotiate one, and asked her if she could
get security ; that she then mentioned Captain Blanford, that he took his
name and placed it before a Mr. Thomas, of Addaide Street, Strand, a
moneylender ; that he went with Bower to interview Captain Blanford
who wanted a loan quickly : that they left him and discussed the matter
when Bower told him to fetch the Captain down and have a talk with him
that they went to a public house; that Captain Blanford there drew the
Cheque for £50 which he (Rutter) understood was for his trouble in the
matter, as he had introduced the borrower to the person who was arranging
the matter, that he went to Captain Blanford's club to get something on
the cheque that night; that he did not say there that he had paid a bill for
him but was arranging a bill for him, that they gave him £10, and he
received the rest the next day, that he received a letter from Captain Blanford
a few days afterwards, but he had unfortunately spent the money that he
did not answer his letter, as he thought he could get the money and give it
back to Captain Blanford.
Bower, in his defence on oath, stated that he had for a number of years
been connected with financial transactions, that he had at various times
raised loans for various people, that he had known Rutter for three or four
years, but had not seen him very frequently that he met Captain Blanford
who wished to baok Mrs. Frederick's bill, that he said he would arrange the
matter for him, as he (Captain Blanford) was in a good position, and the
loan would be covered by insurance ; that they went to the public house to
discuss the matter further, that the Captain there said he would require the
bill to be renewed that he (Bower) then decided to have nothing to do with
the matter; Lhat Rutter said he would carry the matter through; that the
Captain drew the cheque for £50, but that he (Bower) never had a farthing
