GUILTY.— Confined One Month.
 1915. HENRY SPINKS was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of May , 2 shillings, the monies of Charles Gurney and another, his masters.
   MR. PHILLIPS conducted the Prosecution. CHARLES GURNEY. I have a partner, and keep the Crosby Head, in Pit field-street, Hoxton. The prisoner was my barman—I marked eight half-crowns on the Saturday, and on the Monday four half-crowns, eight shillings, and four sixpences—Inspector Shackell saw me mark them—on Tuesday night, the 17th of May, the prisoner went to his supper—I placed the four half-crowns, eight shillings, and four sixpences, in the till, clear ing the whole of the money that was in the till previously—he finished his supper a few minutes after nine o'clock, and went into the shop—at ten o'clock I cleared the whole money from the till, with the exception of the nine shillings—I examined it, and found a deficiency—I called Inspector Shackell and Brannan, and gave the prisoner in charge—he was searched, and some money found on him—amongst it was two shillings which I had placed in the till on that Monday night—I had marked no shilling on Saturday—Brannan asked where he got it from—he said he had taken these two shillings in change for half-a-crown last week, and then he hesitated, and said on Saturday.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did you owe him any thing for wages? A.  I believe a quarter's wages, about 6 l . 5 s ., but he never asked for them—he was taken a few minutes after ten o'clock, and about a quar ter of an hour after he was taken, his attention was called to the shillings—it was within an hour—he was searched immediately after he was taken—he was not spoken to about the shillings directly he was taken—I did not count the money I left in the till—there was not more than 12 s .—I will swear there was not 15 s .—there are five tills—I never leave more than 2 s . in—all the barmen have access to them—there are three besides the prisoner—a great many persons come in the course of an hour.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Are you sure he said he had these two shillings on the Saturday? A.  Yes. JAMES BRANNAN( police-sergeant G  20.) About ten o'clock, on the 17th of May, I was called to the prosecutor's—Shackell was with me—the prisoner was brought to the counting-house—I told him I was a sergeant of police, that he was suspected of robbing his master's till, and asked how much he had about him—he said 30 s .—I asked what change he had given that evening over the counter—he said no change—I searched him, and found a purse, containing a sovereign, a sixpence, three four penny pieces, and also in the same pocket 3 s . 6 d .—three of the loose shillings were marked—I also found some coppers—the prosecutor looked at the marked shillings, and said they were the marked money he had put into the till that evening, about nine o'clock—I asked the prisoner where he got the marked money from—he said, "I got it in change for a half-crown last week"—he then said, "On Saturday."
