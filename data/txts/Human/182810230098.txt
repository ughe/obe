   WILLIAM STAR. On the 16th of October, I was getting up at six o'clock in the morning; I saw the prisoner take the basket of linen from one end of the yard to the other; he had got a ladder, and got over the wall; I went down stairs into the street, expecting to see him - instead of coming into the street, he went down a mews; I am sure I saw him taking the basket towards the ladder.
   JOHN BENSTEAD. On the 16th of October, I saw the prisoner about a quarter-past six o'clock in the morning; he was just getting over the wall, but had nothing in his hand; I dressed myself, and searched for him, but could not find him; in about a quarter of an hour, I searched for him again, and saw him jump from a wall into an empty house; I looked about, and found him in an archway, concealed under some straw; he said he came there to sleep; I told him he must go with me; I sent for the officer - he said If I would let him go, he would never do so any more.
 Prisoner's Defence. I told him I had slept there, and if he would let me go I would never go there any more.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 2167.  THOMAS CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of September , 1 half-crown, value 2s.; 1 sixpence. and 10d. in copper coin, the monies of Edmund Ryan , from his person.
 EDMUND RYAN. On the 30th of September, about five o'clock in the morning; I was in a public-house, dozing - I had a half-crown, two shillings, a sixpence, and 10d., in copper, and four duplicates, which were in my fob-pocket; I had pawned a coat the day before for 6s.; I pulled off my dress at night, but that morning I was going to my work - while I sat on the bench, the prisoner came and sat next to me, and took my money from my pocket; the watchman came to the house, and took him.
 Prisoner. Q. Was not I drinking with you that morning, and you gave me the duplicates to take care of, and at the watch-house you and the watchman both agreed to let me go for half a crown? A. No - we were not drinking - I never saw him before; I did not give him the duplicates, nor offer to let him go for half a crown.
   STEPHEN RENEN. I am a watchman. I went to the public-house; I heard a noise of loud talking as I was going by; I looked in, and saw Ryan sitting on the bench in the tap-room asleep; the prisoner was on his right-hand with his right-hand in his waistcoat pocket; he then went to his left-hand pocket, and took something out, but I could not tell what, and he put it into his own waistcoat pocket - I stood with my back against the counter, and watched him - when he had got what he thought proper to take, I called my partner, and we took the prisoner up to Ryan, awoke him up, and asked if he had lost any thing, he put his hand into his pocket, and said he had lost half a crown, some silver, and, copper; we took the prisoner to the watch-house, and found half a crown, two shillings, a sixpence, and 10d., and four duplicates; the prosecutor was sober.
 Prisoner. Q. Is not my statement true about his offering to let me go for half a crown? A. I know nothing of that.
   DANIEL HANKS. I am a watchman. I assisted in taking the prisoner - we found the half-crown, two shillings, a sixpence, 10d. in copper, and this knife open in his pocket.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was drinking with him; the Magistrate reprimanded the watchman for agreeing to let me go for 2s. 6d.; the prosecutor came home last night, and said he should speak the truth, and that he was drinking with me.
 EDMUND RYAN. I had been out to the City-road the night before with a friend; the prisoner was not in the house when I went to sleep; when I awoke, he said I was his friend.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined One Year.
 2168.  JAMES COHEN was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of October , 1 shawl, value 9s., the goods of John Reed.
   HANNAH REED. I am the wife of John Reed. On the 10th of October I saw the prisoner in a blacksmith's shop, where our poney was being shoed; I sent my daughter there; she had the shawl on, and I saw the prisoner with it in his hand in the shop - my daughter came home without it; she and my husband went afterwards for it.
   MARY REED. I am the daughter of Hannah Reed. I had the shawl in the shop about seven o'clock that evening - I laid it down while I put some straw down; I left it there, as I forgot it, and on going back in half an hour - it was gone.
   EDWARD SIMPSON. I am a last-maker. On the 10th of October, I saw Mary Reed about half-past eight o'clock, and about half-past nine I saw the prisoner at the corner of Warwick-place, and asked what he had done with she with the woman's shawl? he said "I sold it for 2s., on Saffron-hill;" and next morning he told me he had bought a cup with one shilling of the money; he sometimes blows the bellows at the smiths'.
   THOMAS ELLIOTT. I work at the blacksmith's. The prisoner came there on the 11th of October - master asked if he had seen the shawl in the shop; he said No; master said he would give him 3s. to fetch it back; he said he knew nothing about it; nobody but him had been in the shop while I was there, but I went out.
   CHARLES RICHARDSON. I took the prisoner in charge last Friday week; I searched him, but could find nothing - he had a new cap on; I asked where he got it; he said Johnson, the farrier, gave him 1s., and he brought it; he was let go, but taken again afterwards.
 Prisoner's Defence. If she saw me with it, why did not she stop me.
     NOT GUILTY.
