Eleventh Session, 1852-53.
583
KENT CASES.
Before Mr. Recorder.
1028. JAMES BRANSGROVE stealing 1 coat, 1 cloak, 1 pair of
trowsers, 2 waistcoats, and 1 snuffbox, value 4l. 12s. the goods of Thomas
Lond.
THOMAS LOND. I am a farmer. I am lodging at the Spread Eagle
Tavern, Greenwich. I rent some ground at Westbourne-park, Blackheath-
the prisoner oceupied a lodge there, belonging te the estate-I had a room
there for my use, where I was in the habit of leaving some clothes- left
there two carpet bags and a packing case, containing wearing apparel and a
hamper containing wine and liquoron Sunday, 11th Sept., I went there
about 7 o'clock in the morning- found that my packing ease had been
opened, and re-sewn with a thread-I missed from it a travelling cloak, a
great coat, two waistcoats, and a pair of trowsers, which had been lying
outside the packing ease-there was a snuffbox in the pocket of the great
coat-I had left the things all safe on Saturday evening, at 7 o'clock-the
prisoner was there that Saturday eveninghe came from his employ at the
time I left-he was intoxicated - after missing the things on Sunday
morning, I re-packed the package, and left the lodge--I returned at
3 o'clock, and then found the prisoner standing in the middle of my room,
over the hamper, with a quart bottle of Hollands in his hand-he was
intoxicated-the hamper had contained one quart bottle of Hollands and
five pint bottles of porteI examined the bamper, and missed the five bottles
of port and the Hollands, which he had in his hand-I then asked him what
he was about; he said he did not know what he was abouthe fetched me
two pint bottles to put into the hamper, and asked if I wanted any more-
said, Yes," he knew there was morehe was so very bad I could not be
angry with him-I have never seen the other three bottles-about half of
the bottle of Hollands was left I have never seen the other half-I told the
prisoner I had lost some things, but he seemed in a sort of stupor, as if he
did not know what he was about-the hamper was secured with a proper
hamper cord-I gave information to the police on the Monday evening, and
the prisoner was apprehended on the Tuesday-he went awayhis wife
endeavoured to find the things.
CHARLES NAsH. I am assistant to my father, a pawnbroker, in London-
street, Greenwich. I produce a coat and pair of trowsers, pledged by the
prisoner, on Monday, 12th Sept., about 9 o'clock in the evening, for 108.,
in the name of James Smith.
EDWARD CARTER. I am a pawnbroker, in Bear-lane, Greenwich. I
produce a travelling cloak, pledged by the prisoner, on Monday, 12th Sept.,
for 8s.-] asked him if it was his own property; he said it was, and that he
had pledged it at times for 158.-he was sober.
THOMAS SAxBY (policeman, R 37). I apprehended the prisoner on
Tuesday, 13th Sept., about 5 o'clock in the aftemnoon-he was in a cellar
under the floor of the lodge at Westbourne-parky-he was intoxicated-I told
him what he was charged with he said if the prosecutor would allow him
a few days, he would put all things right again-I have known the prisoner
for about two years is a hard working, industrious man-he is not in
the habit of drinking.
