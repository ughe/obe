 233.  WILLIAM NASH was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of November , 12 sovereigns; five half sovereigns; 1 warrant, for payment of and value 6l. 3s. 8d., and four 5l. Bank notes, the property of Thomas Chickley Tiltman , from his person.
 THOMAS CHICKLEY TILTMAN. I am a  fruiterer, of Covent-garden and Newgate-markets. On Lord Mayor's day, I was at the end of Fleet-market, in Fleet-street; between three and four o'clock in the afternoon; I wanted to get through the crowd, but could not; I was trying to press through, and all at once the crowd left me - I put my hand to my pocket, and missed all my money. I missed four 5l. Bank notes, a cheque for 6l. 3s. 8d., and I believe twelve sovereigns, and five half sovereigns, which had been all in one pocket. I turned sharp round, and saw the prisoner, I collared him, and said "You have robbed me;" he struck at me, and knocked me away from holding him - he struck at me four or five times afterwards, but I received only one blow - I got a black eye. I still held him, and called for assistance; a person caught hold of one of his hands, and he said I was not robbed, for I had got my money in my pocket - he was trying to put his hand into my pocket again - I caught hold of his hand, told him to open it, and I took the notes from his hand - the cheque was wrapped in them; I said, "Now give me my sovereigns" - he said he had not got them - I put my hand to his waistcoat-pocket, found something rattle, and said, "You have;" he then put his hand into his pocket, and gave me eleven sovereigns and five half-sovereigns.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was he searched afterwards? A. Yes, and a sovereign and some silver found on him; I had lost some papers, which were not found - the prisoner had not time to pick the money up - he did not say he had done so.
   SIMEON ODWELL. I am a publican. Tiltman called out, "For God's sake, assist me; I have lost all my money;" I turned round, and saw the prisoner beating him all over his face - the blood ran from his mouth - I caught him by the hand and collar; the money was found on him; he took the gold from his pocket himself.
   HENRY BOLTON. I took the prisoner to the watch-house; I found one sovereign and 14s. 6d. on him - I returned him the silver.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, declaring his innocence, but not accounting for his possession of the money.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Life.
 234.  MARY CONNOLLY was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of December , 1 sovereign, the money of Thomas Carpenter.
   ELIZA CARPENTER. I am the wife of Thomas Carpenter, who belongs to the ship Ramilies. On the 5th of December I came up from Queenborough, to go to Somerset-house, to receive his half-pay; I received three sovereigns, and 5s. 4d. I left Somerset-house about twelve o'clock, and went to Billingsgate, but was too late for the boat; as I returned the prisoner stood on the stairs, and said, if I would give her a glass of any thing to drink she would show me a boat that was going away - I went into a house, and gave her a glass of gin; I did not sit down; we came out; she said, "I can't find any boat;" I said, "Very well, good night." I was going to Fleet-street; I went into the Three Cranes public-house, to get a pint of beer, and there the prisoner came in with some friends; she asked me for drink, and I drank with her; I then said I would pay for some more; she then asked how much I had got, and how much I had laid out; she was at the bar; I went into a side room, took out my money, and put it down on the table - I had two sovereigns, and nearly 1l. in silver; she came in behind me, and took a sovereign off the table; she ran outside the little room, came in again in about five minutes, and put down a sixpence: I said nothing to her - I do not think she left the house - I said, "That is not the money you took;" she abused me very much, and I said,"You had better give me the money back again, or I shall see further into it." I gave her in charge.
   RICHARD GEORGE STATHAM. I am a constable. I took the prisoner in charge, and said, "Mary, I must search you;" she used some dreadful language, and stripped herself quite naked; she flew at a man in the tap-room, and beat him; she was made to put her clothes on again, and in going along, from inside her portering-hat, this sovereign slipped down.
 Prisoner's Defence. This woman came to me, and treated me; we were both drunk; the officer said there was a sixpence in my hat - I said it was not mine - he told the Alderman it was a sovereign - it is likely he put it in.
 GUILTY. Aged 30. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Weeks.
 235.  JAMES HARRIS was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   EDWARD CURETON. I am a wholesale-ironmonger, and live in Bread-street, Cheapside. Mr. Williams, an ironmonger, of the Commercial-road, is a customer of mine. - On the 10th of November, about half-past eight o'clock, the prisoner, who was a stranger, came to me - he produced a written order, which he said he brought from Mr. John Williams; I sent for an officer, and had him placed outside the door, to take the prisoner as he went out. I delivered him two dozens of sixteen-inch rasps - he said they were to go on board a ship which would be in the river the next tide; here is the order - (read.)
 Mr. Curter, please send the following order by barer: - six dozens of fifteen-inch horse rasps, the best you have; if he cannot bring them all, send four dozens, as they are wanted immediately, to complete an order to go on bord of ship, and send the others by the Poplar errand-cart, to-day if you please.
 J. DOYLE, for J. WILLIAMS.
   JOHN WILLIS. I am a constable, and took the prisoner as he came out of the house.
   JOHN DOYLE. I am foreman to Mr. John Williams. - This order is not my writing, nor Mr. Williams'. I know the prisoner, but never sent him with this order; he lived with us as errand-boy two years ago, and knew where we sent for articles.
