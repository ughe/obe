CHARLES BRYER BAKER. I am landlord of Dick's Hotel, Fleet Street-on
24th July Mr. Thompson came in with the two prisoners between 4 o'clock and
4.30 p.m.-they sat down in front of the bar and were supplied with about
30. worth of brandy and water, and port wine-I saw Mr. Thompson showing
some potes to the prisoners and he tore, one note up-part of it fell down
on the bar floor and Ketcher picked the piece up and gave it baok to him
the other pieces were in Thompson's hand-when he tore it up he simply
asked the prisoners whether they wanted any money, they made no reply,
and he put the pieces in his pocket-it is not true that be made either of
them a present of any note-they both told me that he had a large amount
of money in his possession, and asked me to take care of it; I refused; the
amount was not mentioned-before I went upstairs I gave directions to my
barman.
Cross-examined by Mr. H. Avory. I had seen Mr. Thompson at my house
three or four times before, but not with the prisoners—when I last saw him he
was so intoxicated that I thought it necessary to tell my barman not to give
him any more drink-he was kissing Ketcher and putting his arm round their
necks, and calling them his dearly beloved boys, and was patting Ketober on
the face; that was a common occurrence; I have seen him acting in an in-
kane way before ; he was not intoxicated when he did that, be perfeotly
knew what be was about-he did not say anything when he tore the note
up; he tore it in three-- he had the others in his hand at the same time,
loose.

THOMAS SMITH. I was barman at the Horseshoe and Magpie-on 24th
July, at 6.30, Mr. Thomson came in with the two prisoners-half a bottle
of seltzer and three fours of brandy were called for--they stayed three-
quarters of an hour I watched them, thinking something was wrong-Mr.
Thomson was sitting between the two; he kissed them and called them
dear ones," once or twice-I saw him take his ring off and give it to
Eldon, who put it on his finger and then returned it to Thomson and told
him to take care of it-Thomson then pulled out a watoh aud showed it to
them and put it in his pocket again, and then called for three fours of
brandy again-Thomson paid ls. 2d. for the first supply and 18. for the
second-I still watched-Eldon left first and Thomson and Ketober went
out after-I asked my mistress' permission, and followed them to the Faloon
public house, where I saw Day- I did not see Eldon again.
Cross-examined by MR. AVORY. I did not see Thomson flourishing any
notes at the Horseshoe.
