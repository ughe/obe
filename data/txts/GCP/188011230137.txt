THOMAS DARVILL. I am head gardener to Mr. Spicer-on Friday, 22nd
October, Inspeetor Kelly showed me these grapes-I compared them
with the stems carefully, and am prepared to say that they came from Mr.
Spicer's vinery by the peculiar cut; any one can see it; if you out 1,000
bunches you would not get them to fit like this they are cut slanting,
Cross-examined. You are a perfect stranger to me-I swear to them by
their fitting together-they are extensively grown--it took a little mani
pulating to match one stalk to another; I could not do it with a knife
they have been cut, not dragged off.
Ro-examined. I have not manipulated them-these bunches exactly
correspond with every branch left behind, and I have not the least doubt
about them; it is a grape which you cannot grow outside if they had
been cut with scissors I could not have matched them.
WILLIAM TILLEY. I am a pawnbroker, of Forest Gate-on, I think,
20th October, the prisoner brought this hamper of grapes into the shop;
it was half full; he asked me 18. 6d. a pound; I bought a pound, and
he took the rest away-I gave them to the inspector on the Friday
following:
Cross-excamined. I have bought rabbits of you, and once last year I
bought some grapes-I had no reason to believe these grapes were stolen;
you said you had just had them sent by train-I do not know you as a
licensed game dealer-you came last year with a horse and cart-I
thought the grapes were dear, and offered you 18. for them-I thought
they were common grapes.
Prisoner's Defence. I am licensed to deal in game and poultry, and I
deal in anything I can buy. I went to market, and bought 2015. of
these grapes; there were some white ones among them. I gave a
Bovereign for them. Nurserymen come to the market and bring their
goods to sell. If I could have got out on bail I might have produced
the man I bought them of. I do my business openly, and do not thieve.
I stand outside Forest Gate Station, and sell poultry, game, and fish to
the public. You can go and cut grapes for yourself at 1s. 68. & pound.
sold then openly, and if Mr. Spicer had asked me for one or two
pounds I should have sold them to him. I do not see what there is to
connect me with them. Nobody but themselves was there when the
police matched the grapes. Instead of being taken before a Magistrate,
I was taken before one of Mr. Spioer's intimate friends and neighbours,
and not allowed to say & word, and he said that instead of being prose-
cuted I ought to be acquitted. The Magistrate said that it was for a
Jury to decide, and I said I thought so too. As to matching grapes,
people come from all parts of the country with them, and who can say
that they have not been tampered with. I hope you will consider my ex-
planation a truthful one, and that I shall leave this Court without a stain
upon my character. A reward of 201. has been offered ; lots of people
think the police do not get the reward; they do not get it directly, but
they get it indirectly.
Nor GUILTY.
