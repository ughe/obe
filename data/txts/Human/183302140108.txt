 666.  SOPHIA BIRCH was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 13th of February , 90 pieces of timber, value 10l.; 30 slates, value 2s.; 20 lbs. weight of lead, value 3s., and 1000 bricks, value 30s., the goods of Charles Thorogood , well knowing them to have been stolen.
 MESSRS. CLARKSON and ROWE conducted the prosecution.
   CHARLES THOROGOOD. I have been a builder. I have some carcases of houses in the neighbourhood of the Alpha-cottages, Regent's-park; I never gave the prisoner permission to take any thing from them - I do not know her.
   EDWARD THOMAS. I am a carpenter. I live in Earl-street, Lisson-grove, near the Alpha-cottages; I went to work close by the prisoner's - I know the carcases of houses which are close by the prosecutor's garden-wall, within fourteen or fifteen yards; I saw the prisoner's son in the yard of those carcases, and a great many more boys on the tops of the houses, tearing down the pots from the chimneys, then the bricks, and throwing them over the wall into the yard of the prisoner's cottage - the prisoner's son was throwing the bricks from the yard of these carcases into the prisoner's garden.
   ANDREW DELLANEY. I am a shoemaker, and live in Bridport-street, opposite the prisoner. I know these five carcases in front of her house - I have seen boys pulling down the bricks: the greatest depredation was in January; but recently I have seen timber carried by the back entrance into her cottage-garden, by lads - the pieces seemed exactly the same as these which are here.
   ROBERT HARRISON. I am a bricklayer, and live in Dorchester-place, very near the Alpha-cottages, and near the prisoner's house. I have seen boys on the carcases of the houses in question, pulling down the bricks, and other boys were throwing them towards the prisoner's garden, where I saw a bricklayer at work, building a wall.
 COURT. Q. You have seen two or three boys who have gone out of Court? A. Yes, and one of them I can swear to as one of the boys; he is the tallest of them - I do not know his name.
   CHRISTOPHER BUNKELL. I am a builder - I live in Dorchester-place, and am agent to the ground landlord of these carcases. I went over these five carcases one day last week - I suppose they have sustained damage to the amount of 200l. and upwards; joists, bricks, and slates, have been taken away, the chimney-pots destroyed, and the carcases greatly demolished - I know where the prisoner lives; there is a door from her garden to the back part of these carcases - I went to her house with two boys last Wednesday; I saw the prisoner there, and said, "I understood you are encouraging these boys to destroy these houses, and loosen the timbers - I am credibly informed there is a quantity of the materials in your cottage;" she said there was no property in her house belonging to any one but herself - I told her I was confident there was, and I was going to get an officer with a search-warrant, unless the property was delivered up; I left a man to watch the premises - I went away, and returned the same day with the officer; I then told her, I should, as a friend, wish her to give up the timber belonging to these houses - she said, "Come in, Sir;" I walked in with the officer as far as the parlour-door; I then said, "Mrs. Birch, you have got a good deal of the property belonging to these houses, and I am authorised by the proprietor to require you to give it up;" she said, "O deary me, we have nothing belonging to these houses" - I turned and looked into the other parlour on the left hand, and saw, I suppose, fifty or sixty pieces of timber piled up - or probably more; I said, "This is the timber we are looking for" - she said "O no, I gave either 2l. 3s. or 3l. 3s. for them, and a load of bricks;" I said, "Who did you buy it of? you must have had a great burgain" - she said, "I bought it of Mr. Morand;" I said, "What, the broker" - she said,"Yes, he is a broker;" I suppose the value of a load of bricks and the timber I saw would be 10l. or 12l. - the bricklayer came in at the time, and she said, "Here is a man who can prove I had them of Mr. Morand; and I had some words about paying the balance of the money, and he saw it come;" she said to him, "Did not you, Mr. Milwood; did not you see it come" - he said, "I saw a load of bricks and a piece or two of timber;" I said, "Did you see all this?" - he said, No; I asked the prisoner if she had any receipt, and she said it should be given at a proper time; I said we should persevere, and she said we might do as we pleased - I begged of her repeatedly to give it up, but she refused; I said there would certainly be a prosecution follow, as the property belonged to these premises - she said it did not; I then went to my employer, and then went to the prisoner's premises with the officer, and found a large quantity of wood, I believe ninety-four pieces in the whole, also a small piece of lead worth about 2s., and some slates - I compared the timber found at the prisoner's with the remainder of the joists left on the buildings; it matched in length and size, and its being faulty at one end - it tallied in every respect, and I have no doubt whatever that it came from there - here are two pieces I can speak to more particularly; I have matched one piece to the place it came from in the carcases, and it matched to the greatest nicety - the ends of these pieces have been cut off and rubbed with dirt; I have seen boys on the carcases from time to time, (and among the rest, Barnes,) throwing bricks into the prisoner's garden.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. How many times did you go to the prisoner's cottage? A. Three times, and the first time I told her the charge, and wished her to give it up, which she refused to do; I first went on the Wednesday, and then again on the Thursday - I am aware that at auctions property is bought for much less than at shops.
 MR. ROWE. Q. Did the prisoner ever state that she purchased these goods at an auction? A. No, she did not.
