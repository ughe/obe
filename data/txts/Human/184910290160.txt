   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Had you known Collins before? A.  I had; he is a son of Mr. Collins, the grazier, a man of great respecta bility—seeing Collins name to the check, and his giving it me in Collins' pre sence, gave me a degree of confidence in it—I cannot say what I should have done if I had not seen his name to it—I cannot say whether his name induced me to pay it—I had no doubt about it being a real transaction, and a good instrument—when I laid the money on the table, Smith took it, and said to Collins," Here Collins, we halve the money when we get it; you take half, and I will take half"—that opened my eyes, but the money was gone then—I have not applied to Collins for the payment of the check—I have applied to Smith, in Collins' presence—I believe Smith's relations are respectable—he has been accustomed to attend the Carence for some years—he has, unfortu nately, spent all his own, and other people's money; there are other checks the subject of inquiry—his father is dead—I am not quite sure whether he was a Magistrate.
