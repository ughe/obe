Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Is Taylor married? A. No; be
receives 108. a week.
Cross-emmined by MR. PARBY. Q. Does be work all the week for that ?
A. He is employed three days a week to come to London, and he has 18. a
journey for that—I allow him one truss of first-crop hay, which is worth about
28.—this rowen hay of mine is very good; my horses will eat that before
they will eat other bay ; we generally feed 'hem with rowen hay io the rack.
JAMES HARROD (policeman, S 179). On Saturday morning, 17th May, I
was io South Mimms New Road—I saw Stokes driving two horses with a load
of bay, in Mr. Giddins' cart—I followed the eart which Stokes drove to the
Swan with two Necks, close to the stable-door—he then whipped the cloth,
untied it, and Taylor woke up, and put down the small bundle of hay on
Stokes' shoulder, he carried it into the stable, and returned ; and then Taylor
put another bundle on his shoulder, and he carried that in—I saw Reynolds
in the stable at the time the hay was carried in—I went to the stable, but the
door was shut and fastened—I returned to the house, and took Turner and
Stokes into custody—there was another person named Pratt ; the first I saw
of him was under the cloth—sergeant Peopell went up, and brought bim down;
he was not assisting the others; he did not interfere with the bay at all.
