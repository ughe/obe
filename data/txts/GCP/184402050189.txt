stones into the truck, and took it away-Tiediman ordered me to help to
shove the truck up, and I did as far as No. 24, Euston-street--I had not
known Wheeler before we took the stones into the house I did not get any
thing for the job-we had a pint of beer between us.

believe they were some that had been taken from Baker's-row.

GEORGE ANDREWS (police-constable G 66.) On the 24th of Jan. I went
to Easton-street, in consequence of information, and saw Wheeler bring a
paving-stone about two feet square out of the house No. 24, and cross the
road with it on his back-I asked if it was his-he said it was-I asked
where he got it from-he said, “ From over the way," pointing to No. 24–I
asked wbere he bought it-he hesitated a minute or two-Mr. Waugh came
to the door, and said, “You had better tell him where you got it from" he
said, "From the back of the workhouse" - in going to the station, I asked
if he had any more stones-- he said, "No"-I went back to No. 24, and found
nine more stones in the yard -I found Tiediman on the Friday morning in
William-street, Hoxton, about five o'clock-I asked his name-be said Hig-
gins, or Hutchins, and said he was a carpenter.
RHODES MOLEs. On the 27th of Jan. I acted as clerk to the Magistrate
at Clerkenwell Police-court-after the evidence had been given against the
prisoners, Tiediman said, "I gave the flags to Wheeler, I had nothing for
them; I had a right to give them away."-Wheeler had been in custody on
the 25th, and he then said, in reference to the stone, " I bought it of one of
the masons at 2d. a foot; I paid him 6s. ; 38. on Monday night, and 3s, on
Tuesday evening; Keefe saw me pay the money"-on the 29th Wheeler said,
"I went to Tiediman, and asked if he had any old bits of paving for sale; he
said yes; I asked what he wanted a foot; he said 21d.; I gave him 28.
a foot; on Tuesday I had the remainder; I paid him 2s. 10d., and we had a
pint of beer."
