937. THOMAS BERRY, stealing 3 handkerchiefs, value 176. 6d.: the
goods of Mary Ann Harvey.
GEORGE Gray. I am a licensed victualler, of Broadway, Barking. On
25th July the prisoner came in as a customer-my sister, Mrs. Harvey, fol--
lowed him in, and made a communication to me; in consequence of which I
sent to the station--a policeman came, and I gave the prisoner in charge for
stealing some handkerchiefs-he said he had not stolen any; he knew nothing
about them as the policeman took him out, he put his hand under his smook
and drew a bundle of handkerchiefs from it-I gave them to the constable-
these are them (produced)—the prisoner said nothing,
Prisoner. I was intoxicated. Witness. He was not
Mary Ann HARVEY. I am single, and keep a draper's shop in Barking.
On 21st July, about 5 o'clock in the afternoon, the prisoner came, and asked
to look at some cotton pocket handkerchiefs -I showed him some: he did
not buy any-after he had left, I missed some silk handkerchiefs off the
counter--there was no one else in the shop at the time—be stood in front of
the counter-I followed him to my brother's, and a policeman was sent for
- I did not see the handkerchiefs dropped-I have no mark on them, but I
lost such, and believe them to be mine.
Prisoner. I fancy I paid her for one. Witness. No you did not; but you
offered me 1s. for three cotton handkerchiefs, marked 4jd. each, but I would
not take it.
ROBERT WHATLING ( policeman ). The prisoner was given into my charge
at Mr. Gray's-I did not see him with the handkerchiefs-he was sober.
Guilty. Aged 25.- Confined Four Months.
