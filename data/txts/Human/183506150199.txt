 1606. CAROLINE SEYMOUR was indicted for stealing, on the   3rd of June , 1 Petticoat, vale 1 s  ., the goods of Emma White ; 1 napkin, value 6 d  .; the goods of Sarah Rowbottom ; 2 gowns, value 3 s  .; 1 apron, value 4.; and 1 pinafore, value 6 d  ., the goods of William Wyatt , her master. SARAH WYATT. The prisoner was in my service for about a fortnight—I missed these articles ( looking at them )—I had seen them on the second day of her coming into the house, which was on the 12th of May—I missed them a few days afterwards.
   Prisoner. Q . Can you show me the two dresses you have brought against me? A.  These are them—I had them made for myself—they were dresses; but they are torn apart—I left them safe.
   Prisoner . I wrote to master, begging him to take it into his considera tion to come forward, and say that part of it was his sister's, Sarah Row bottoms—that napkin in is one belonging to my children.
   COURT to S. WYATT . Q.  Have you any mark on that piece of gown? A.  Yes—the making of it, and the apron too—I know it by this band—I am sure it is my husband's, William Wyatt's, property—they are not worth more than 6 d . altogether.
   Prisoner . Did you not, (on your oath) when I washed up all my aprons, say, "Ann, tie that apron before you, that you don't take cold?"—you know you are insane; you are not fit to take an oath—while I was in custody, you went to my lodings, and took things of mine out of my box to bring against me—you even took balls of tape and worsted. BENJAMIN SKELTON. I am a pawnbroker. I Produce this gown and a napkin, pawned by the prisoner—the gown, I believe, is her own property. EMMA WHITE. This petticoat is mine—it is marked—it was in my box, which was locked.
   Prisoner . I did pawn them, but White was the bar-maid, and she asked me to go and bring a wreath of flowers for her, which I did, and some white-stain ribbon, which is on her bonnet now—she gave me 2 s . for the wreath, and they cost 2 s . 6 d .—she said, "Never mind, I will pay you"—but she never did. Witness.  Yes, I did.
   Prisoner . When I went to bed at night, you said, "There is a white petticoat you may have." Witness . No; I never did.
   Prisoner . She did so, on my oath, if I might take my oath; and this apron Mrs. Wyatt lent me—White gave me the petticoat, with a pair of stockings.
 (The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that the prosecutrix had given her the apron.) GUILTY. Aged 29.— Recommended to mercy by the Jury.  — Confined One Month.
