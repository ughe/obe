was called for, and the two prisoners shoved the prosecutor against the fire-

turned inside out-Pitcher then went back again to him and took his watch-

never saw them again till they were at the station-the prosecutor stopped till

. Joun Marsh. I am waiter at the Duke on Horseback. On Tuesday, the
4th of Aug., I saw the prosecutor there-Pitcher and two or three more were
there-I do not recollect seeing Graham-I went into the tap-room when a
pot of beer was called for by the prosecutor-I took it in, and was waiting
for the money-Pitcher said the prosecutor would pay me, and if he did not
he would I went out for another party, and when I returned I found the
prosecutor on his back, on the floor against the door-Pitcher was coming out
of the door-I said to him, "Bill, don't go away, pay me for the pot of beer;
you said you would”-he said, " I will pay you for the pot of beer, Jack,"
and he gave me 4d. worth of halfpence-when I went in with the pot of beer
the prosecutor had some seals hanging down in front of him, and a chain round
his neck-when I went in with a pint of beer afterwards I missed the chain
and seals--I told my master.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe you let them all go before you went for the
policeman ? A. Yes--the prosecutor appeared to me to be mad-he did not
draw out his watch, and smash it on the table in my presence.
THOMAS WILLIAM GILLING. I am shopman to a pawnbroker at Green-
wich. On the 4th of Aug., about a quarter past seven o'clock in the evening,
Graham pawned this watch-there was a chain to it-I gave him the chain
back, as it was of no value-about five minutes after I went to the door, and
saw Graham go and join Pitcher, and they went away together-Graham told
me he had had a fight, and the watch bad got damaged, and it was his own.
