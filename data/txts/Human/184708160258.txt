I went with Mr. Maynard to Sewell's room, and heard him claim the bag of bones—I asked Sewell if it was his—he said "No"—I said, "Where did you get them?"—he said, "From down stairs, in Mr. Maynard's warehouse."
   SEWELL'S DEFENCE.  I never said any such thing; I was formerly employed as a porter in the Cut to clear gentlemen's house; there are often odds and ends left, such as rags and bones and bottles, that I have the privilege of having; I have offered such things for sale to Mr. Maynard; he cannot say otherwise. JOSEPH MAYNARD re-examined.  I never heard of his clearing out houses—I have bought a small quantity of things of him to the amount of one penny or three halfpence—I have missed things from time to time.
   The prisoner Sewell called DAVID NUNN. I know this bag to be the prisoner Sewell's—he brought it to put some little odds and ends in, to take to my new residence, when I was moving about seven month's ago
   COURT. Q.  How do you know that bag from one like it? A.  It is very remarkable, it has the number 65 on it, I noticed that, as he put it on his shoulder—I believe that is the number—I have the piece which the bag was cut from, it is a piece of drugget—he had been in the habit of making hearth rugs—he had cut this piece out, and what he had left, he made a bag of.
   COURT to JOSEPH MAYNARD. Q.  How do you know the bag? A.  I have missed a bag similar to this—I have lost it for two months.
   JURY. Q.  Was there any number on your bag? A.  No.
   JURY to DAVID NUNN. Q.  Did you see the number on the bag? A.  Yes, when Sewell moved the things from my place six or seven months ago, I thought it was a very remarkable number to be on a bag, number 65. ( The bag was opened, and the number on it was  59.)
   Witness.  I am positive it is the same bag, though I have been mistaken in the number.
   JURY to JOSEPH MAYNARD. Q.  Had your bag any number on it? A.  I did not notice that it had—I cannot swear to the bones, the prisoners were seen coming through the passage—the warehouse runs down the passage—Sewell could take bones from the street to his own room—it is impossible for me to miss the quantity that he had, out of twelve or fourteen cwt. of bones.
   COURT. Q.  Where did you get the key? A.  I cannot say in the hurry who gave it me.
   Sewell.  It is done through spite; a dog that the prosecutor had bit a large piece out of my boy's leg; I asked him for money to pay the doctor; he said at first that he would give me any recompense; I now and then got a sixpence from him, and that was all; if I could bring witnesses I could show that I got some bones from Mr. Lawrence, and some from Mr. Nunn, that very morning.
   COURT to DAVID NUNN. Q.  Had he any bones that morning of you, before nine o'clock? A.  I cannot swear that I gave him them that morn ing—I had always given him bones on a Monday, and every week I have given him some—some were bones of beef—merely the bones from the domestic consumption, laid by for him—we had not a great deal of beef lately—perhaps we had it twice a-week—we accumulate a few bones—I do not know that I gave him any that morning; I might have done so the week before—I have not given him such a quantity as these.
