ELI CAUNTER (Police Sergeant H). On Saturday night, 22nd April,
about 12.30, I saw Jones meet the prosecutor in High Street, Shoreditch
-I stood in the dark, and saw her feel his pockets-he shouted "Police !
I am being robbed "- Penn, who was standing five yards off on the pave-
ment, ran up and knocked Mercer down-I ran up and throw Penn to the
ground, and held him with my right hand and Jones with my left-Jones
dropped some money-some constables came-I directed them to look
about one of them picked up a shilling-Mercer said he would charge
the prisoners-I took Jones, and other constables took Penn to the
station--they denied the charge-Penn said "I don't know the female
prisoner," and Jones said "I don't know the man"-I found in Penn's
trousers watch-pocket this pawn-ticket. ("Boy's boots, A. Jones, 38., 17,
Pearson Street.") They both said "I refuse my address and occupation”
--this envelope was found on Penn. (“Mr. Penn, 11, York Place, Hoxton
Place, Hoxton Street, Hoxton.") The following morning Penn handed 18.
to the constable on duty at the station, and told him to send for some
coffee for himself and the female who was charged with him-I asked the
prisoners if they could refer to any one who would give them a character
--they said they could not-I have no doubt they are the persons who
interfered with the prosecutor.
Cross-examined by Penn. I did not find the pawn-ticket on Jones-you
were walking from Shoreditch towards the City.
Cross-examined by Jones. I did not say at the other Court that I watched
you through Shoreditch ; I did not watch you-I did not show the pawn-
ticket till the remand ; I did not think of it-I made inquiries during the
remand-there were a few people about after the robbery & crowd
assembled.
Re-examined. Penn was sober-the prosecutor was the worse for
drink.
ESTHER PICKLES. I am a searcher at Commercial Street Station-I
found on Jones 158. (produced) in her bosom.
Cro88-examined by Jones. You took it out of your bosom-I did not say
you were trying to hide it in your hand-you did not give me your ear-
rings-you struck me in the chest, and I called a constable for assistance.
REUBEN YOUNG (Policeman G 440). I found this shilling in the gutter
where the prisoners were apprehended.
JAMES FEARNE. I am a postman at Dalston-I saw Jones and the
prosecutor struggling-I crossed the road and saw Penn knook him down
--the detective came up and knocked Penn down.
Cro88-examined by Penn. I was in uniform when you were charged at
the station.
John RANDS (Policeman 177). I heard cries of "Police," and went
where the sounds appeared to come from-I saw Jones in Caunter's
custody- I took Penn; he went quietly.
Penn in his defence said he had been to the Paragon Music Hall, and was
going home, when he was struck by the detective and taken to the station; that
the prosecutor could not identify him till after some whispering, that he did not
know Jones, and when the pawn-ticket was produced she immediately said " That
18 mine."
