LUCRETIA ELIZABETH FAITHFULL. I live at 5, Ealing Road, Bromley-
by-Bow I was married to the prisoner on the 24th August, 1876, at
St. George's Church, Bethnal Green - I had known him as a child-I
was a widow when I married him—my husband died in 1876_the prisoner
came in 1875 to visit my husband in his illness- afterwards I worked to
support my children and had daily wages-the prisoner married me and
allowed me 158. & week to keep himself, his three boys, and my own five
chil ren-he worked for a few weeks-he said his wife had been dead
some time I afterwards made inquiries and found that his wife went into
the union.
JAMES HOWLETT (Police Constable). I took the prisoner into custody on
30th November, at 112, Usher Road, Bow I said "I shall take you into
custody for marrying Elizabeth Faithfull, your wife Mrs. Southey being
then and now alive"-he turned round—I said "Do you deny being
Robert Southey 1" he said “No, I cannot deny that'he asked me if
I could not let him be till Monday morning, as it was awkward being
taken on a Sunday-I said "No, I cannot do that, I have been looking
after you for a long time”-I had been doing so for three months, and
could not trace his whereaboutshe said "I have not seen my wife for
eight years"-before the Magistrate he said “The last time I saw her was
somewhere about two years ago at Illford "-I produce the certificates of
the first and second marriages,
As there was no evidence that the prisoner knew his first wife was alive for
seven years prior to the second marriage, the Jury returned a verdict of
Not Guilty.
