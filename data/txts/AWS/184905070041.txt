Cross-examined by MR. COOPER. Q. What caused you to go to the dunmy
on this 24th April A. To see if my uncle was there ; sometimes he goes
down when the men are there to see that they moor the draft properly-th
prisoners were about twice the length of this Court from me when I first saw
them in the boat-it was about two or three minutes after that that the police-
galley passed-I could have made myself heard if I had called out-I saw
no one there but the prisoners-] first mentioned this circumstance to my
uncle next morning, between eleven and twelve, on the wharf-he came to
me there-I knew where he lived- was never stopped at Wapping-gate with
any currants-] have been a lighterman about nine years-when I was a boy
I was a paper-stainer-] have never been in the custody of the river-police,
nor ever stopped by them.
Cross-ecamined by MR. WOOLLBTT. Q. How have you been getting your
living? A. By my work as a lighterman on the river- am not always in
the employ of my uncle, but of anybody I can get a day's work for-I had
been standing about a long time when I saw the men at the dummy, expecting
to see my uncle-sometimes he does not come down till eleven o'clock-I
think it was a very dark night.
