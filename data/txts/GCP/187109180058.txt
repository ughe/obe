Cross-examined. I work for Mr. Orop, at Homerton, close by the church
-I have about three miles to go-I have worked there nine months—I

JANE INNOCENT. I am the wife of the last witness—I was walking just
behind him on this night-I could have touched him-someone touched
me behind- I went “Oh!"-my husband turned to see what was the
matter-before he could speak someone put their arm up so, and pulled the
watch, but not without a struggle—I could not swear to the person because
his back was towards me-he was taller than my husband, and seemed
younger-I heard my husband say “ He has got it; I know him, I will
have him."
James Cowley (Policeman H 17). I took the prisoner into custody two
nights after the robbery-I met him in Shoreditch, with another man-I
told him I should take him for stealing & watch from the person in Tyson
Street-he said "Very well; I know nothing about it"- I took him to the
station, and placed him amongst four or five others—I brought the prose-
cutor in, and he identified him as the man who stole his watch-after be
was charged he said "I was at the Loggerheads public-house, drinking, from
6.45 till 12.45, and I never left there" - that is about fifty yards from where
the robbery was committed-a very dark turping runs round by the beer
shop, I have frequently seen the prisoner, but not to speak to him—it was
a light spot where the robbery took place—there was a lamp there, and
the light of the beer-shop.
Prisoner's statement before the Magistrate : " At the time the robbery was
committed I was in the Loggerheads, with a light suit of clothes on, and
when the policeman took me he said 'You have had quite long enough in
London, it is quite time you were somewhere else.”
GUILTY.
He also PLEADED GUILTY** to having been before convicted in June, 1866.
