Cross-examined by MR. RIBION. Q. I suppose, when yon came up to
London to consult Dr. Henery you were, without going into details, labour-
ing under some malady or other? A. Certainly-I was not aware of its
having been for some time when I saw him I described my symptoms to
him-he put certain questions to me which I answered the interview lasted
about five minutes, I think, or somewhere about that time; I can't exactly
state how long, it is so long ago--there was a form of examination gone
through ; I don't think it was worth much--there was an examination-I
don't think there were many questions about my mode of living, as far as I
recollect- I told him I was in the army, and wbat regiment I was in, when
he asked for my address-he entered my name in a book-the medicine
was not made up then ; it was sent to me two or three days afterwards-if
I am not mistaken I wrote to ask the cause of the delay- I don't think I
received any answer to that letter-- I received the medicine, and I suppose
he considered that enough-I did not receive any answer stating that there
was a difficulty or delay in making it up--I paid him for it when I was
there ; it was a box containing twelve bottles--I have not consumed all the
medicine- I don't know whathas beo ome of the rest I gave one bottle up
to the solicitor-Dr. Henery gave me general directions as to the regimen I
should follow; printed directions. I have not got them, I burnt them-I
thought they were of no use-I did not follow those directions further than
I had been following them all my life he recommended cleanliness and
exercise, and so on; all those sort of thiugs I have been in the habit of
attending to all my life there were certain articles that he recommended
me not to eat, and he recommended bathing-he said I ought to follow
those directions; at the same time I was to take the medicine-I derived
certain benefit from it, up to a certain point; there is no doubt of that-as
I received the subsequent boxes of medicine I sent him up a cheque for them
-no one recommended me to consult my own family physician ; I did it of
my own free will.
Cross-examined by MR. KEMP.Q. Upon any of the occasions on which
you went to Dr. Henery's place, did you see Anderson A. I did not-I was
never there but once I did not see Anderson on that occasion, no one but
Dr. Henery and the boy who let me in--all the communications I had with
Dr. Henery after that were in writing-I only saw Anderson once, to the
best of my belief-he did not give me any letter personally-he sent me
letters, but never delivered them to me--the receipt was the only occasion
upon which I saw him write-Wien I saw him he told me that Dr. Henery
had a claim against me-at first, he said 1501.-I did not say, "It cannot be
as much as that; I think a cheque for 201. or 301. would settle his bill"-
nothing of the kind-I said I acknowledged owing Dr. Henery for a box of
medicine, which I was perfectly prepared to pay--that was ten guineas;
but I could not possibly pay anything more, and would not pay it-I told
him I would communicate with Dr. Henery-I did not say that my
regiment would be at Gravesend shortly-I probably said I should be going
to Gravesend shortly, and that in coming back through town I would see
Dr. Henery- I did not see him I wrote to him previously, therefore I did
not think it necessary-I had no written communications with Anderson
he once wrote to me-I never wrote to him.
