HENRY MARLBOROUGH I am a labourer in the employ of Sir Charles
Price and Co.-on 24the June Cooper brought me from there an order for

weights out and saw it put on board the lugboat between 4 and 6 on the
evening of 24th June-I have seen the hogshead taken from Mr. Harrison's
cellar-it is the same.
Cross-eramined by Mr. SERGEANT PARRY. Was it an ordinary turpentine
cask into which it was put 4. Yes ; one which had been used for turpen-
tine, and the marks were on the head.
JOHN NOEL. I am a lighterman in the employ of Charles Price and Co.-
on 24th June I had charge of the John lugboat, and received eleven casks
of oil and nine casks of turpentine into it between 4 and 6 in the evening
I received these two delivery notes with them-] moved the boat out into
the river, and made it fast to a coal bargeit was 7 'clock before I left her
-I gave the two notes later the same evening to Lewis, a lighterman in my
employ- saw Pemberthy that evening on the City Gas Wharf-he spoke
to a man named Allen, but I do not know what he said, as we were some
distance apart-that was forty or fifty yards from the lighter-Alle and I
afterwards walked up the wharf, and had a pint of beer together, and then
he went homehe was at work on a bargeI saw Pemberthy last on the
City Gas Wharf at 7 o'clock, or a few minutes after-it adjoins Me8srs.
Price's Wharf.
WILLIAM ALLEN. On the night of 24th June, I saw Pemberthy at the
City Gas Works-he asked me if I had got anything to do-he lives five
doors from me, and I knew him by passing my door-I said have got no
more than what my own hands can "he said, 44 Where are you going 1"
I said, 46 To shift the chains" came down to the bottom of the wharf,
and got on the old barge, as it was high water, and then he went away-I
was then on the barges which were moored away from the wharf-] always
took you to be a lighterman, but I believe you are not.
ROBERT LEWIs. I am a lighterman, and was employed by Mr. Noel on
the 25th June-] received these papers from him on the night of the 24th
to take to the General Steam Navigation Company's place at Deptford-
got there about 8 look next morning, and it was nearly high water- had
to wait before I could get in started at half-past 8, and took the barge
down to Deptford--I went to the man who takes charge of the goods, and
when I opened the note, and saw the tierces, I found I had not got one of
the casks, and gave information- know the prisoners by sight--I do not
know what they are, but I have seen Smith all over sawdust, passing back-
wards and forward in the Blaokfriars-road.
