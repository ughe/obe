Before Mr. Recorder.
   SURREY CASES .
 1087. JOHN DAVIS was indicted for embezzlement. WILLIAM DANOERFIELD. I keep a public-house in Palace New-road, Lambeth. The prisoner was in my service about nine months—he was em ployed to receive money on my account sometimes, from my regular cuto mers—he usually paid me on a Saturday night, but there was no time in parti cular—there was 3 s . 2 d . due from Mrs. Farrell—I directed the prisoner to recieve it on Saturday night, the 18th of Feb.—he did not return till the Tuesday night following—he was then intoxicated, and I said nothing to him that night—on Wednesday morning I asked if he had got any money for me he said no—there was then due to me 3 l . 10 d . for that week's account, exclusive of this 3 s .—the 3 l . 3 s . 10 d . was due from customers of his own—I allowed him a halfpenny a pot for selling it, but Mrs. Farrell's was a private account
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Have you got your account, out of which you claim this 3 l .? A.  I have not—I desired him to receive this 3 s . about nine o'clock on Saturday night; the time for settling accounts, had then arrived—he lived on the premises—Mrs. Farrell's was a week's account—I did not give him the account—he was told the amount—I gave him 14 s . a-week—I debited him with all the beer that he sold—I believe he sometimes gave credit—he came home about nine or ten o'clock on Tuesday night—he did not serve the customers—he laid down on the seat in the tap-room—he did not go about his work as usual next morning—I did not allow him—in deed, he did not make the attempt—he was up in his own room—I did not see him till I went up at ten that morning—I said, "Have you got any money for me?"—I went to make the best terms could with him, to see what money I could get—when he said he had got no money, I said I would give him through the day to do the best he could, to see if he could get me some money—I did not specify any money in particular—I do not know whether he went out, but instead of his coming to make any terms, he came in front of my bar at night, drinking with some of the customers, thereby adding insult to injury—I do not know that I should have been satisfied if I could have got the 3 l .—I should not have been satisfied without the whole of my money, or security for it—I gave him into custody about ten on Wed nesday night—I had no quarrel with him before I did so—do not know whether he had been in the house all day—I did not inquire—I told the po liceman to take charge of him for embezzlement—for the 3 s . 2 d .—I owe him his last week's wages—I did not tell him on Tuesday night that if he could get me security for the remainder of what was due to me, it would be all very well. nor anything of the sort. ELIZABETH FARRELL. I live in Palace-street, North Lambeth. The pri soner frequently brought beer to my house—he brought it on Saturday, the 18th of Feb., and I gave him 3 s .—the week's account was 3 s . 2 d ., but I had no halfpence, and said I would give him the 2 d . next day, but he did not come again—I paid him the 3 s . for Mr. Dangerfield—the prisoner did not apply for the money, I always paid him on Saturday night.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What time did he come? A.  At nine o'clock—I live about two minutes' walk from the prosecutor—the prisoner appeared sober when I paid him. JESSEE CANNON. I am a policeman. I received the prisoner in custody.
