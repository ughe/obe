Prisoner. On the Saturday before you asked me to go on an erránd for
von I asked you to get me some handkerchiefs, and you got me three dozen
I took them on the Sunday, and on the Monday I brought them back, and paid
you 186. 6d. for half a dozen of them. I said I wanted a dozen very good
ones, and I told you on Monday they were not good enough. I wanted
to go on a week-day, and you said, “Would it not do on Sunday?" I said,
“Yes.” On the Saturday these things came in; I took four, put them in
paper, and put them into my pocket. I believe I told the person to enter
them. You knew that the people were friends, and I have had goods before,
and paid you. Witness. That was a fortnight before, when he took some
handkerchiefs to show to a customer-he had them entered on a memorandum
- he brought nine of them back, and the money for the others.
Court. Q. Could he ever receive goods without their being entered in a
book ? A. Never--we should give any man in charge if he took them out
without-he could not receive them to take out to show a customer without
their being entered, because we do not allow it-those that he says he had to
show, he had on memorandum, and I knew of it-I believe they were en-
tered-I did not see them entered.
Prisoner. Have I not been entrusted to fetch goods from the City, to
take to customers, without any entry ? did I not fetch a lot of carpets, and
take them to Captain Hudson, and other customers? I believe I told them to
enter these handkerchiefs down. Witness. I have asked every young man
in the establishment, and they say you did not; he was never entrusted to go
out with goods from the shop to show, without their being entered- we have
three shops-he was only in one shop-if he had been trusted by any other
person in the shop, it would have come to my knowledge we have only
about five young men there.
John Coox (police-sergeant M 8.) I took the prisoner.
Prisoner. He knew I was going to take these handkerchiefs to a particular
friend--the stock was paid for it was bought for me, by a lady, at their
other concern.
So
JURY to CHARLES J. Brooks. Q. Were the bandkerchiefs in the coat he
wore every day? A. No, it was his best coat--they were folded up just as
they are in the shop—I do not in general send persons with goods on a Sun-
day, only this was a friend of his, and I said if he was going up, he might
take them--the first lot he took with my knowledge was two dozen on the
Saturday-he brought part of them back on the Monday, and the money
- he might have said they wanted to see others, but I had no knowledge
that he meant to take others.
Nor Guilty,
Before Mr. Coramon Sergeant.
