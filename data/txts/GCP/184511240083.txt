65. JOHN SILK was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief, value 23., the
goods of a man unknown, from his person.
Georor Russell, (City police-constable, No. 34.) On the 13th of Nov. I
was in Cheapside--I saw the prisoner following several gentlemen, and sound-
ing their pockets with his hand-he has a way of doing it with his elbow-I
saw him follow one gentleman and take this bandkerchief from him-he ran
off, and I followed-he knows me-I said to him, “You may as well drop
it"-he did so--I have not been able to find the gentleman.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. A gentleman stopped the prisoner!
A. Yes I did not call after the gentleman who lost the handkerchief-he
was about five yards from me when I saw it done-1 was on the same side of
the way I then crossed over near to Queen-street-I never lost sight of the
prisoner.
Court. Q. How far was be from the gentleman when you caught him?
4. About 100 yards.

66. DANIEL AHERON was indicted for stealing 56lbs. weight of plums,
Value 1l.; and 1 box, 4d., the goods of Alexander West.
ALEXANDER WEST. I keep an eating-house in Church-lane, Whitechapel.
On the 25th of Oct, the prisoner came in and asked for some pudding--as he
was going out I observed a box of plums under his arm-I called to him to
drop it, which he did on the threshold of the door-the plums were mine-
they were worth 11.
Prisoner. Q. Was I drunk or sober? 4. I cannot say-you did not
make me any answer.
ELIZABETH WEST. I served the prisoner with the pudding-I heard my
husband say, “Drop it," and he dropped it, I saw the plums under his arm--
it was half-past twelve o'clock at night.
EDWARD BURGESS (police-constable H 198.) I took the prisoner at a
public-house in Wentworth-street-I suppose there were fifty thieves round
