 NEW COURT. Wednesday, September 10, 1834.
 Second Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.

 1463.  ISAAC FERRY was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 28th of July , 3 pairs of shoes, value 19s., the goods of William Langley , he well knowing the same to have been stolen.
   CHARLES BRITTON. I live in the New-road, St. George's. I am foreman to my uncle, Mr. William Langley - he is a shoemaker- I missed three pair of Blucher shoes on the 28th of July, when the officer told me of it - I had seen them safe on the Saturday night before, at seven o'clock - I went with the officer to Hare-street, Brick-lane - I saw the prisoner standing at his door - Smith, who went with me in plain clothes, asked him whether he had any shoes to sell that would fit him - the prisoner pointed to some over the door, and said, "There are some" - the officer said,"They are not good enough, have you any thing better?"- he said, "No, I have nothing better" - Smith then asked whether he had bought any on Saturday night - he said, No, he did not - Smith then said he had information that there were some sold there which had been stolen - the prisoner said, "No, I never do business in that way - I never buy any thing unless I know where it comes from, and that all is right" - Smith then asked if there were any offered for sale on the Saturday night - he said, "No, I believe not; but I will ask my wife," and she said, "No"- the other officer then entered, and said, "We have got information that there is stolen property here, and we are come to search your house" - the prisoner said, "I see you are officers, you are welcome to search" - they went into the back room, picked up one pair of shoes, and asked me if I knew them - I said, "Yes" - Smith then asked the prisoner where he got them - he said he bought them in Petticoat-lane on the Friday, and gave 5s. for them - they kept on searching for about a quarter of an hour - when they came to the front room, where the prisoner's wife was sitting, in the corner, behind her chair, under a table covered with green baize, were these other two pairs - I can swear to them - they have my uncle's mark on them.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is it not the custom of small shoemakers, when in distress, to sell shoes on Saturday nights to shops? A. It may be - Smith asked the prisoner if he had any shoes to sell - he did not offer to sell these - he said he had kept these for his own use, and he had worn one pair - he was liberated on bail, and has surrendered here - he made search for the person he bought them of, and had one person taken up.
 Q. Was not that person's name Smith, and was he not a cousin of this very Smith the officer? A. No, he was not - the magistrate asked him that - he said he was not, but that he always called the policemen cousins and uncles - I do not know what his name was - these shoes hung on our door-post - a person might stand at the door and take them.
   HENRY WEBB(police-constable H 80). I produce the first pair of shoes that was found at the prisoner's - the other officer afterwards found the other two pair - when I got up to the door, Smith and the prosecutor were talking to the prisoner - Smith said, "Were any shoes sold here on Saturday night?" - the prisoner said, "No" - Smith then asked if any were offered him; he said, "No, not to me, but I will ask my old woman" - he turned to his wife, and said, "Were any shoes offered?" she said, "No" - I said,"I have information that some are here which are stolen" - he said, "I have no objection at all to your searching;" and under the bed I found this pair.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was this the first thing that happened? A. When I went up, the prisoner and Smith and the prosecutor were in conversation - we had been waiting at a distance - we thought if we had gone in at first, he would have made away with them, most likely - there were three men in the house, and the prisoner's wife, who would very likely have assisted her husband to get out of trouble.
 Q. Do you know the boy who was taken up by the prisoner after he was bailed? A. I had seen him about four months ago - I had spoken to him - he gave his name as Peacock - I do not know how long Peacock had known Smith - he said he was related to one of the officers - I should not like to state who gave me the information - I did not receive it from the prosecutor - I received it from a person who was afterwards charged.
 Q. Upon your oath, from the same source that you derived intelligence that the shoes were in the prisoner's house, did you not know who the thief was? A. No; he did not tell me who stole them - he said they were sold at that house, and he was along with the party who sold them- it was Peacock told me.
 Q. Was not Peacock charged before the magistrate with stealing them? A. No; he was taken up for being in company with the person who sold them.
 COURT. Q. Do you say he was charged in the presence of the magistrate with being the person who sold the shoes? A. Peacock said, in the presence of the magistrate, that he in company with the person who sold them.
