EDWARD Day. I keep the Royal Oak-I was called to the bar by my
daughter, in consequence of disgusting and obscene language by the prisoners,
and found Cook pulling one of the young women about, and trying to kiss
her-her father was rebuking him-he became very excitable towards the
father, who I believe struck him-I slipped in between and told Cook he
ought to be asbamed of himself for striking a man old enough to be his
father-he became more violent, and I asked Walters to try to get him
out-he made two attempts, but was unsuccessful--one of them, when he
was got outside, jumped on the counter, and swore he would kill me.
Cook. I never jumped on the counter. Witness. Yon did, and broke the
glass; I bolted the door, and you broke it open and made a most foul oath
that you would kill me -1 know nothing about your watch.
MARY ANN DENNIS. I am the prosecutor's daughter-I was with him
on 20th March, and the prisoner followed my brother in out of the tap-
room, and Cook took bold of me and kissed me; their discourse was not fit
for any female to mention, and my father said he would not bave it, and
would not have us pulled about like that Walters tried to get him out; he
took him round the waist and said, “We have been mates all our life
time, why should not we be mates now ?”—Cook had a watch with a
silver Albert to it-he put it in his left trouser's pocket, and went out of
the room directly-he came in again, and said that he had lost his
watch-my mother said to Mr. Day, “You ought to have a search as
we have not been out of the room-that was before he kissed me, and
before my father came -- the landlord induced them to go, and I followed
my father out—he had a quart-pot in his hand with some ale in it,
which he was carrying home; directly he got outside the house he was
knocked down ; I cannot say who by—he got up again, and I saw the
prisoners run off-I did not drink with either of them-I never saw them
before.
