CHARLES PALMER(583 L ). About 1.30 a.m. on 8th April, I was going home from duty—I heard a police whistle, and proceeded in the direction from whence it came—I saw Bushbridge with the prisoner, and Law with the pony and cart—Law said something to me—I went down to the bottom of Tate Road, and there saw a dock constable—I went across a field and found two sacks, each containing twelve hams; further on I found four against the dock fence, and coming back I found a sack containing seven—about 9.40 I saw the prisoner in the cart—the hams were all of the same sort, and the same as the eight other hams produced. GEORGE GLEN. I am superintendent to Messrs. Williams, in the Royal Albert Docks—on April 8th I had a case containing what were supposed to be hams, under my charge—I last saw them between five and six o'clock on the Saturday evening, and they were then in sound condi tion—between eight and nine on Monday morning, I found the case broken, and with only five hams remaining—I compared them with eight or nine hams shown me, and they were of the same description—I should think our shed where the hams were is not more than two hundred yards from the marsh where the hams were found—there is a paling between the marsh and the yard; it would be almost impossible to get over the paling; it is a difficult climb—a man would come in by the gate to get access to our yard. FREDERICK WILLY( Dock Policeman ). On Saturday evening, 7th April, I examined Mr. Williams' premises about 9.30; every case was intact at that time. CHARLES PALMER( Re-examined ). The hams found on the marshes were taken to the Police-station—I believe the Inspector (who is not here) showed them to the prosecutor. GEORGE GLEN( Re-examined ). The hams shown to me at the station were of similar nature and quality to those remaining in the case; I have no doubt they were part of the same case.
   The prisoner in his statement before the Magistrate and his defence stated that the two men had engaged him to drive them, as they were going to fetch bags of chaff, and that they went and fetched the sack which lie believed con  tained chaff. GUILTY of Receiving.— Judgment respited, for the prisoner to have an opportunity of giving information.
