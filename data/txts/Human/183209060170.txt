2160. CATHERINE MANCLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of April , 2 towels, value 2s., and 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of Richard Webb.
   MARGARET WEBB. I am the wife of Richard Webb, and am a laundress - we live on Church-hill, St. Pancras. I have known the prisoner twenty years - for the last few months she was in the habit of washing for me; I missed two towels.
   JAMES BOURNE. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Brewers-street, Somers'-town. I have two towels, pawned for 8d., on the 21st of May - to the best of my belief, by the prisoner; I knew her well by her coming to our shop.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. And yet you have seen her frequently? A. Yes, but my employer took these in.
   CHARLES MERCER. I am in the employ of a pawnbroker. I have a handkerchief - I do not know who pawned it.
   CHARLES MUMFORD(Police-constable E 162). I took the prisoner at No.9, Manchester-street, Gray's Inn-road; I found the duplicates there.
 Cross-examined. Q. I suppose she was only a lodger? A. I believe it was an empty house, and an old person was there to take care of it - the prisoner directed me there; she said the duplicates were in the middle drawer, and that they were hers.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received an excellent character.
 GUILTY. Aged 37. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 2161.  JEREMIAH NEALE and WILLIAM READ were indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , 2 gallons of wine, value 30s., and 2 bottles, value 10d., the goods of Thomas Cotterell.
   THOMAS COTTERELL. I live in Little Queen-street, Holborn. At the next house to me there were some bricklayers' repairs going on at the back of my premises; on the 9th of August Read came and asked me to allow him to go down stairs and remove a large stone at a place which they were going to break up - I went down into my cellar with him, and helped him to remove the stone; I did not see Neale at that time, but between four and five o'clock in the afternoon, I saw Neale, who was the labourer, drunk- I had allowed him to fetch water from the pump in my cellar: I went down in about an hour, found my winecellar door open, and missed between two and three dozen of wine.
   WILLIAM BURNHAM. I am pot-boy to the prosecutor. I went down into his cellar, and found the wine-cellar had been forced open, but I did not know that any wine was gone; the prisoners were working in Mr. Robinson's cellar on the other side of the brick wall - they could get from one cellar to the other; I saw that Neale was tipsy when he came into our kitchen for water - he broke part of the dresser; he said he was not tipsy - I said he was, and I would not go away: their master then came there, and when he was gone I asked them how they got so tipsy - they said they had been drinking some porter; I asked where - they told me, and then I found they had been drinking some finings; I then went to look at our taps, to see if they dripped, and knocked down a bottle of wine which stood there; I asked where they got that from - they said from where the bottled ale stood; I said it was wine, and I gave it to them to smell - they threw it behind them into Mr. Robinson's cellar; I then asked them how much more they had taken - Read said five bottles - he told Neale to give me back what he had, and he gave me back three - one of them was ale, and the cork had been drawn.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You stopped a long time in the cellar? A. Yes; I did not take any myself; they were both tipsy, and hardly knew what they were saying; I saw no bottle of wine but the one that was broken.
   SAMUEL WISE(Police-constable A 86.) I was sent for, and informed the cellar had been broken; I watched to see if any thing was taken away - I saw one of them come up and look, and then I saw Neale come up with a hod on his shoulder - I stopped him, and found in the hod four bottles of wine; I took two of them - he threw the others down and tried to get off.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he not tipsy? A. He had been drinking, but he could walk and run very well; he walked to Bow-street afterwards - he was not tipsy.
   THOMAS COTTERELL. I found ten bottles of wine in all, but I missed more than two dozen.
 Cross-examined. Q. What could become of the two dozen? A. I do not know; four of the ten I found were broken, and six are here - there had been a cork drawn of the ale, but I believe they had only just tasted that; they might have taken some wine at dinner time.
 Neale's Defence. I know nothing about it, only Read brought me the stuff, and told me to drink it.
 The prisoners received a good character.
 NEALE -    GUILTY. Aged 17.
 READ -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Confined One Year.
 2162.  JOSEPH PITT was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 18lbs. weight of ham, value 8s., the goods of Charles Charlton.
   GEORGE RUSSELL. On the 28th of July I stopped the prisoner, in consequence of information; I found this ham on him, and took him - he said he took it from Charlton's, and was ready to pay for it.
   JOSEPH RICHARDSON. I live with Mr. Charles Charlton. It was his ham, and was on a board outside his window.
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been up to Notting-hill; a man had given me four or five glasses of liquor - on my return home I came near this shop, and I suppose I took it, not knowing what I was doing.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Confined Seven Days.
