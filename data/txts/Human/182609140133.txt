 1626.  THOMAS TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 1 pair of boots, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Croft.
 THOMAS CROFT. I am  stable-boy to Mr. Gale, of Phoenix-yard, Oxford-street. I lost a pair of boots from the stable; I went the same evening to Bow-street - I saw the prisoner there about half-past four o'clock - he had a horse to look after in the same yard.
   WILLIAM GUY. I am a patrol. Philpott, (who is not here,) gave the prisoner into my custody, with these boots, which he said he had stopped him with, and he had stolen them from Mr. Gale's yard; the prisoner said he could not swear that.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1627.  FREDERICK WILKINSON and ELIZABETH ADAMS were indicted for stealing, on the 12th of September , 2 pictures, framed, value 12s., the goods of Thomas Smart.
 THOMAS SMART. I am a  carver and gilder, and live in Greek-street, Soho. I lost two pictures on the 12th of September; I went out on business at seven o'clock that evening, and when I returned, in an hour and a half, they were missing - they were safe when I went out.
   SIMON SOLOMAN ZILVER. I was opposite Mr. Smart's shop on the evening of the 12th of September; I saw Adams walking about, and I noticed her; I then saw Wilkinson at the corner of Mr. Smart's door - he took the pictures, and ran with them into Bateman's-buildings, with Adams; they went together. I heard in about two minutes that they were stolen, and ran with others through Greek-street, to Soho-square, and then to Dean-street; I then saw the prisoners opposite, and we went and took hold of them - I said, "Where are the pictures?" Wilkinson said, "I do not know any thing about them;" Adams said, "I did not take them." We then took them to the watch-house; I had not seen them together before; she was standing in the street when he went to the shop - she did not see him take them, but she joined him the moment he had got them; they only walked to the corner of Bateman's-buildings, and then I saw them in Dean-street.
   JOHN PRATER. The prisoners were brought to me at the watch-house.
 WILKINSON'S Defence. I was coming down Dean-street, that gentleman came and took hold of me, and said I had stolen pictures; I said I had not, and would go with him any where. As to this girl I know nothing of her.
 ADAMS' Defence. I never saw this young man before- I am innocent.
 WILKINSON -    GUILTY. Aged 17. Transported for Seven Years.
 ADAMS -    NOT GUILTY.
 1628.  ABRAHAM FOWLER was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of July , 1 watch, value 20s., the goods of John George Emett , from his person.
 JOHN GEORGE EMETT. On the 3d of July, while I was walking in the  Strand, in company with two friends, we met a procession of some Society; we got partly through the crowd when I found myself stopped, and the pressure appeared to me to be more than usual; I gave the person a push with my hand, and he made off; Richardson, who was with me, asked if I had lost any thing; I then felt, and my watch was gone.
 Cross-examined by MR. ANDREWS. Q. You have the misfortune to be blind, I believe? A. Yes, very nearly so.
   JONATHAN RICHARDSON. I was with Mr. Emett, near Somerset House, and saw the prisoner very near us, actively engaged at the head of a mob, which I evidently saw was created for the purpose of making depredations. I cannot say I saw him make an attack on my friend, but he was very near him; I did not see the watch taken, but some time afterwards I asked Mr. Emett, and he said, "I have lost my watch" - I went to the head of the mob, and saw the officer - I told him of it. I did not see the prisoner till he was at Bow-street.
   JONATHAN RICHARDSON, JUN. I was with Mr. Emett, and saw the prisoner in the Strand, in a mob - I saw him look at Mr. Emett, and make a stop, and appear as if he was picking his pocket.
   JOHN VIRGO BUCKLAND. I took up the prisoner in the Strand.
   WILLIAM BEAZLEY. I live at Vauxhall. I was in the Strand, and saw the prisoner near Mr. Emett, but did not see him do any thing.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1629.  CHARLES WALKER was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of August , 2 candlesticks, value 3s., the goods of William Wood.
   MATILDA WOOD. My brother, William Wood, keeps the  Grapes public-house, in Portpool-lane. On the 14th of August I saw the prisoner in our skittle-ground, with this pair of candlesticks in a handkerchief under his arm; I asked what he had got - he said nothing - he then threw them down, and said, "If they are your's you are welcome to them" - my brother sent for an officer, who took him. I saw him come out of the wash-house, where they were kept.
   WILLIAM WAINWRIGHT. I am an officer, and took him.(Property produced and sworn to).
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to the skittle-ground, and saw my handkerchief on the ground - I took it up, and the candlesticks were in it.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Confined Three Months.
