Was that the night of the fire? - I do not know; when I went up it was Mr. Fox, and he said, Molly, I have picked up a box, but what is in it, God knows; I asked him, where he found it; he said, in the street, on the pavement; he went up stairs; Mr. Fox said, take it in the kitchen, perhaps we may find an owner for it; the next day in the afternoon, Mr. Fox came down into the kitchen and looked at the box, and said, Molly, there is no direction on the outside, I wonder if there is any within; he opened the box, and there was no direction, only a parcel of buttons; he said, he did not think they were of much value; he would look in the paper to see if they were advertised; on the day that he went to sell them, he said, they were not advertised, and he might sell them as well as another.
 Mr. Silvester. What is Mr. Cooke? - He keeps an academy.
 How near is Well's-street where you live, to Vine-street where the fire was? - No great way.
 You heard of the fire? - Yes.
 This was the evening of the fire I understand? - I do not know.
 About seven in the evening, whereabouts upon the pavement did he find it? - He did not tell me; he said, to the best of my knowledge in Vine-street; my master never comes into the kitchen; nobody goes into the kitchen but myself.
 Did you look at the outside of this box to see if there was any direction? - Yes; there was no direction.
 No mark upon it? - Not to the best of my remembrance; I saw none upon paper.
 What became of the other part of the box? - I do not know; there was no direction on the box.
 Mr. Garrow. You would have found no difficulty in burning it if you had been desired? did not you advise him to go to Vine-street where the poor people had been burnt out? - No, I had no business with it.
 The prisoner called six witnesses who gave him an exceeding good character.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Tried by the first Middlesex Jury before Mr.  Baron EYRE.
 870.  SUSANNAH REDMAN was indicted for feloniously stealing one quadrant, value 18 s. the property of Hugh Edge Thomlinson.
 HUGHEDGE THOMLINSON sworn.
 I received this as a security for a debt; I  deal in coals; I live in Great Peter-street, Westminster; I lost a quadrant; I took some goods in possession, as he desired me, and locked them up in a house, where I live now, in the Broad-way, Westminster, No. 4; I had the lower part of the house for selling coals; this quadrant was in a room up two pair of stairs; the front room; and the prisoner lodged in the back room up two pair of stairs; it was in a bureau, which were part of the things I had in possession; as soon as I got the things in my possession, I put them in this room, and locked the door; I believe it was the beginning of last January; I did not miss it till some time in March last; I found where it was pawned, and whose name it was pawned in; by seeking for a ticket of Mr. Hill, I found out who pawned it; I knew it again; I saw it at the pawnbrokers; there was no particular mark about it; but I am acquainted a little with instruments; I am in the mercantile line.
 Mr. Fielding, Prisoner's Counsel. You seem to be a very odd old gentleman; we have seen you in all the Courts I am sure: So a part of this house is where your coal-shed is, where you keep your few coals, and your sticks? - Yes, my few coals; I suppose Mr. Cassell might be indebted to me ten or twelve pounds; at the time he was in prison, I was such a blockhead as to let him have money.
