 1730.  JANE SLIM was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of July , 1 piece of lace, value 4s.; 3 pairs of stockings, value 6s.; 1 sleeve. value 1d.; 1 handkerchief, value 1d.; 3 pieces of cotton, value 1d., and 2 ozs. weight of tea, value 6d., the goods of Peter Sandys , her master.
   PETER SANDYS. I am a clerk to Mr. Frederick Robinson - I live in Barossa-terrace, Cambridge-heath. The prisoner was in my service - these articles were taken from her box by the officer; I can swear to these stockings.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was there any charge made against the prisoner about the silk stockings before she left the house? A. Yes; the whole of her wages were not paid - she was taken up some days after she left my service; I had given the officer directions to take her, but he put it into another officer's hands - I gave instructions to the officer that night, but she was sent home on account of her illness; we had another servant coming that night, and did not think it right to keep her in the house - I sent my  servant home with her, because she intended not to go home, but take lodgings in the neighbourhood: she acknowledged that she took things out of my house - it was the neglect of the first officer that she was not taken up sooner; the officer, who searched her box and found the things, is not here.
 COURT. Q. You say you had not paid her wages? A. No; her arrangement was that she should have what she wanted at a neighbour's shop - she got into debt 1l. 5s. and I had undertaken that should he paid, the rest was paid to the prisoner - I stated that I should prosecute before any application was made for her wages; I knew what was due, but she could not give me change.
   ISABELLA SANDYS. I am the prosecutor's wife. The prisoner was four months in my service - she was charged with taking the property, and gave up the key of her box - these stockings, tea, and several other things were found; my husband told her she would be prosecuted - she begged for mercy; I interceded for her, and I was the cause of her being sent home; it was on Monday morning the things were discovered - my husband came home between eight and nine o'clock at night, and then the officer was sent for.
 Cross-examined by MR.  PHILLIPS, Q.  Is that officer here? A. No; I valued the things at about 10s. or 11s.
   FRANCES CLARK. I was at the prosecutor's when the articles were found - this white handkerchief is mine; the prisoner begged forgiveness - I slept with her; no other servant slept in the same room.
   CHARLES LEWIS PHILPOT. I am an officer. I went to her mother's - the prisoner came out; I said, "Jane, you have got into a mess;" she said she took a little tea, but nothing else - I brought her to the office.
 Cross-examined. Q. Who sent you? A. Kennedy, the other officer - he went twice and could not find her; I found her at her sister's, who is in place - her mother went with me, and called her out.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1731.  SUSANNAH SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 shawl, value 1s.; 6d.; 1 pair of trousers, value 2s.; 1 gown, value 1s.; 1 pelisse, value 1s.; 1 frock, value 6d.; 1 shift, value 6d.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 1s., and 1 flat-iron, value 6d., the goods of William Smith , her master.
   WILLIAM SMITH. I am a shoemaker, and live in Baldwin's-place. On the 29th of August I made an application to the parish for my wife, who was sick, and they sent me the prisoner as a nurse - I know these articles to be mine.
   JOSEPH SMITH. I am the prosecutor's son. I saw the prisoner on the Tuesday after the Saturday she left us - I asked her what she had done with my trousers, which I had missed on the Sunday; she said she did not like to see my father - I asked her for the duplicate; she gave me ten duplicates, all of Mr. Nicholls'; I gave them to my uncle, who was coming by.
   SAMUEL HEMPSTEAD. I am servant to Mr. Nicholls, a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned seven of these articles at different dates; 1 lent 5s. 11d. on them.
   ANDREW LLOYD. I am a constable, and took the prisoner; she said she had pawned the things to buy the children bread.
 Prisoner's Defence. He never gave me any money, and I took the things to provide the family with necessaries - from the 20th of July to the 23d of August, I had not common necessaries; I was to have 1s. 6d. per week, and I never received but two eighteen pences.
   WILLIAM SMITH re-examined. She had eighteen pence a week of me; I was at home to every meal - the place was never without food; there were different articles which I took from her lodging; she was with me seven or eight weeks.
     GUILTY. Aged 57. Confined Three Months.
