JOHN MONGAN. I am a cow-keeper, and live in Lovegroverstreet, Kent-
road. On the evening of the last night of Peckham fair, the 23rd of Sept.,
about nine o'clock, I was at the Eel-pie-house-I went in the dancing-room
and came out a little after ten o'clock-I then went before the bar, and had
something to drink--I recollect having my watch then, because I had looked
to see the time a minute before I went to the bar-1 had known the prisoner
perfectly well for four years-he was standing before the bar with some others
-I do not know what he was about I knew him and his family-he lives
in the neighbourhood-be was there at the time I came up to the bar, and
looked at my watch he spoke to me, I do not know whether he saw
me pull it out he was standing behind me I remained at the bar till he and
another person, not in custody, took hold of my arms-three or four others
came behind me and shoved me against the tap-room door, and carried me off
my legs--I fell down, they all fell on me-I should say there were five of
them altogether they were all on top of me the prisoner was one who
shoved me into the tap-room-- I cannot tell whether he was on me I felt a tug
at my pocket while I was on the ground-somebody pulled the men off me
I got up, went to the front of the door, and missed my watch from my waist-
cont pocket--I had a gold guard round my neckI found the gold chain
hanging down, and the ring of it gone except a small part of it which was
broken-I told the landlord the watch was gone- he said he koew nothing of
it-I saw no more of the prisoner till he was in custody I did not see him
when I got off the floor I do not know whether he was in the room then or
not.
