seven—he got up between eight and nine—I was in my sitting room—he came into it, passed through, and went to the wash-house—the wash-house leads to the privy and to the back-yard—he remained in the wash-house, as near as I can tell, from twenty five minutes to half-an-hour—when he came out of his bed-room he had his coat and waistcoat on his arm, and I believe his boots in his hand, but I am not certain whether they were on his feet or in his hand—when he returned, he came through the room where I was sitting at breakfast, and went out at the front door—he went straight from the wash-house to the front door and went out—I observed nothing particular about him when he went out—I believe he had on the same clothes he has now—he had on the same shoes as I had made him, but they were blacked—when he was in a situation he blacked them every morning, but not at other times—how he got the brushes to clean them I do not know, but the brushes were in the back place when he was gone—they were my brushes and blacking—if he used it, it was my blacking.
   Q.  Can you tell whether, on this night, he used any candle or rush-light to go to bed? A.  I never saw Richard have a candle in our house all the time he was in it, unless he asked for such a thing, and that was very seldom—if he wanted one it was merely for a minute to get something out of the room, and he was supplied by our candle which stood on the table—I have not found any candle or rush-light about my premises since that night—he returned about a quarter past seven o'clock on the Tuesday evening, much about that time—I had heard of the murder about half-past ten, or near eleven o'clock—when the prisoner came home I was gone to fetch a pint of por ter and a rasher of bacon for supper—when I returned he was sitting in my room talking to my wife—the conversation when I came into the house was, I believe; on the subject of the murder—the first time, I believe, I spoke, or Richard spoke to me, was, "It is a shocking thing: do you consider he could not have done it himself?"—the reply I made was, "Richard, how could it be possible for persons to do such things themselves, having their hands tied and their eyes blindfolded; is it likely?"—that is all the con versation I remember between Richard and me, till afterwards—he was sitting at the table a few minutes after, then got up and went into the back place—he went through the wash-house, as if he was going to the privy—he said his inside was out of order, and he went out at the door as if
