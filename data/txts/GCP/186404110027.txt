THOMAS SPURGIN. I am a clerk- I was at Mr. Smart's shop, and heard
the prisoner ask for two ounces of canaster tobacco-she threw down &
sovereign, and the change was placed before her, a half-sovereign, three half-
crowns, and 18. 60.-- she said that she had previously paid 41d. an ounce
for it-I heard money move on the glass, but cannot say what she did with
it, because the child was sitting on the counter between us Jones then said
"I know what your little game is; you are ringing the changes "-I then
beard money move again as it was put down on the glass-Mr. Jones then
took up a half-crown, which I noticed was of a different colour to those he
put down-the prisoner was given in custody.
BAMUEL HALYARD (City-policeman, 513). The prisoner was given in my
custody, with this half-crown, by Mr. Jones, who also gave me this sovereign
(produced).
WILLIAM WEBSTER. This is a bad half-crown.
Court to WILLIAM JONES. Q. Before you agreed to give her the change
had she taken up the whole? A. No, she had not removed the coins from
the counter when I agreed to give her the silver.
The Court considered that this did not amount to an uttering.-Nor Gunty.
405. JOHN WILLIAM EMERY (49), was indicted for a like offence.
MR. COLERIDGE conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZA MANSFIELD. I am the wife of Charles Mansfield, of the Blue Boar's
Head, Long Acreon 2d March, about 5 d'olock, the prisoner came in for a
quartern of rum- she gave me 18., which I put on the bar-parlour mantel-
piece where I bad just placed one pound's worth of silver, but this was put
separate—the rum came to sixpenoe, and I gave him two threepenny-pieces
in change-he poured out the rum, and gave the cabman, who was with him,
part of it, but bronght it back from the eab, and said, "Will you be kind
enough to change this rum, my wife cannot drink it, and put some gin into
it?"-he then gave me another shilling, which I put in the detector, and
bent it nearly double-I then looked at the first shilling, and found that was
bad too-the prisoner was given in custody.
CHARLES MANSFIELD. I have heard my wife's evidence it is correct.
ALFRED WELLS (Policeman, T 133). The prisoner was given into my
custody with these two shillings—I found on him two threepenny-pieces,
and 41d. in copper-going to the station he said to Mr. Mansfield, "If you
will give me the two shillings back again I will make it all right to you."
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These shillings are bad, and from the same mould.
The prisoner produced a written defence, stating that he was drunk, and
received the shillings in change for a half crown.
ALFRED WELLS (re-examined). He was sober, but he appeared to get
drunk afterwards.
