Third Jury, before Mr. Justice Vaughan.
1629. EDWARD OUSDEN was indicted for that he, on the 19th of
June, at St. Mary, Stratford, did about the hour of twelve at night, the
dwelling-house of Martha Sturgeon, feloniously and burglariously, break
and enter, with intent to steal, and stealing therein, 5 fowls, price 108.;
and 2 dead fowls, value 4s., the goods and property of the said Martha
Sturgeon.
Charles COLYER. I am a policeman. On Tuesday morning, the 20th of
June, about half-past one o'clock, I was in High-street, Bow, in the parish
of St. Mary, Stratford-I was at the further end, near Bow-bridge, looking
up the town, and saw the prisoner come from about the spot where Mrs.
Sturgeon lives-he appeared to have something in his hand-he was but
a few yards from the house when I saw him-it might be five yards-I
did not see any body with him—there was not a soul moving about the
town-I followed him up the town, and saw him go to his father's door-
his father is a jobbing carpenter, and lives nearly opposite to our station-
house-I saw him go to the door, and keep knocking—I knew who it
was, and did not go to him-I returned a few steps, and waited about
two o'clock I had occasion to go to a shop opposite his father's house, and
he was still there, knocking at the door-two more of our men came up
to me, and said something-the prisoner then walked away-we went
across the road, and saw some fowls lying close against where he had been
standing, at his father's door--there were five live ones and two dead ones,
one with the head pulled right off-the prisoner went to the corner-one of
the policemen, who is not here, went to lay hold of him, and he ran away
-the man sprang his rattle-I went to his assistance, but the prisoner got
right away from us-he ran through Bow Church-yard, and directly he
got away I went back, and picked up a white pillow-case, all over blood,
with white feathers of fowls in it-it was in a narrow passage in the
church-yard, through which he had run- I took him into custody on Wed-
nesday morning, the 21st, which was next morning, in Whitechapel-I am
quite sure he was the man I saw come in a direction from Mrs. Sturgeon's
house, and afterwards run from his father's door-I have known him many
years.
