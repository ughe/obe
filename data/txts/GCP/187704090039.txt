WILLIAM BATHURST WOODMAN, M.D. I am an hospital assistant physi.
cian and examiner at Apothecaries' Hall-on 16th February, about 1 o'clook,
Mr. Finnimore fetched me to 1, Morpeth Road I saw the family, their
sufferings were entirely consistent with poisoning by white arsenio I filled
this bottle (produced) with vomit from the sink in the back soullery-I
have analised this bottle of tea, there is only a slight trace of white arsenio
in it, but sufficient to cause the illness from which Mrs. Barker was suffering
examined the milk and water, there was a very large quantity in that,
some of which can be seen now in solid orystals not dissolved -I analysed
some water from the kettle, there was only about a teaspoonful--it gave
reactions with three tests, showing white arsenio I found a large quantity
of white arsenio in the vomit which I took from the stone sink--that
arsenio could not have been obtained from colouring matter on the tea; I
have never heard of arsenio being used in tea; copper is very frequently - I
should expect to find a sediment in the kettle if a large quantity was put in
--there were no grains of white arsenio in the water prooured from the
kettle, it was a quite clear solution-it is possible that a person could drink
* breakfast oup of the tea without feeling the effects of it before he had
finished the draft, it sometimes takes an hour or two before the symptoms
come on--there is very little taste, some persons say none at all-I think it!
has a slight taste-I tried the water with three tests and found the presence
of poison, there was a very small quantity of water, the rest was accidentally
spilled and I cannot say what the proportion was-there was no green colour
in the water—this could not be accounted for by phosphoric paste.
