Cross-examined. Q. Did you call the defendant's attention to the cost
having been taken from the pawnbroker's ? A. Yes; it was after the pro-
duction of this coat that she gave her evidence I was not present when
she swore to the statement.
COURT. Q. Was this about the coat before her final examination 4
Yes, and the money found in the box was before her final examination.
COURT to Mr. Cook. Q. In what state did you find her pulse? A. There
was considerable power in her pulse on the last occasion-I should have
expected to find her pulse more in a state of collapse-she was not ins
fainting state when I got there-I ordered her brandy and water-I found
no evidence of recent fainting.
HENRY FINNIS (City policeman). In the course of Monday evening, I went
to Harrison's house I saw the prisoner, and she made a statement to me
her husband was there when I got there, and he and I went round the house
-I examined the staircase window—the opening of glass was about a foot
and a half long, and better than a foot wide; it was a sort of angular shape-
adjoining the pane of glass was a wooden panel, which was not moveable ;
it was screwed up with two screws-no one could have got in or out of that
window.
COURT. 2. The shutter covered the hole? A. Yes ; not the glass-the
glass gave light on the staircase-I desired the husband to draw the screws
-he drew one, the other he could not get out; he broke it off.
Court to WILLIAM JARVIS. Q. You were sent to examine the house;
where does it open at the back? A. Nowhere-the window on the stairs
seems to open to hardly anywhere-there is the brick work under it—it is
about a yard square-it is a sort of well or shaft.
JURY. Q. Did she say that she saw a face through that window ! A.
She did—if any one were there they must be in a very small space, and
must stoop down to look through it-I believe Finnis got through after it
was unscrewed, and went on the roof.
Guilty. Aged 20.-Recommended to mercy by the Jury, no one having
received any injury.-Confined Eighteen Months.
