   HENRY VINCENT sworn.
 I am a house-keeper, I live at No. 117 in Swallow-street; I am silver spur-maker to his Royal Highness the Prince of Wales, and several others; I have lived there six years; I have known the prisoner  Fletcher I may say twenty years, but for these last six or seven years in particular, he has done a great deal of business for me; he makes spur-rowels for me; he is of that business; he was apprentice to it; he can work at Smith's work; but that is his real business; he keeps an old iron shop in Newtoner's-lane.
 Did he live there in March last? - Yes.
 You know he keeps that shop? - Yes.
 Is he a married man? - Yes; and has three or four children.
 Does his wife live with him? - Yes.
 Did she live with him in March last? - Yes; he is an honest, hard-working man.
 (The prisoner Fletcher called another witness, who gave him a very good character.)

THOMAS PETERS was indicted for that he, well knowing that Thomas Banks did duly serve our Lord the King, on board the Stirling Castle , from the 4th of December till the 19th of October following, and that afterwards, on the said 19th day of October, he departed this life, and was intitled to certain wages and pay, then due to him from our said Lord the King; and that afterwards, a certain will and restament of the said Thomas Banks , by the name and description of Thomas Banks, of Portsmouth, mariner, was duly proved by John Murphy , the executor therein named, and probate duly granted, did, on the 4th day of April last, unlawfully, wilfully, knowingly, and feloniously, personate the said Thomas Banks, to receive his wages, against the statute.
 A second count, only with the allegation of the death of the said  Thomas Banks.
 (The case opened by Mr. Silvester.)
   HENRY HUNTER WILLIAMS sworn.
 I am clerk in the Navy-office; I have the pay-books of the  Stirling Castle; I have an entry of Thomas Banks, on the 4th of December, 1778; he served as able seaman, and is supposed to be lost in the Stirling Castle, on or about the 19th of October, 1780; there never was any further account from any of those ships; she was lost in the year 1780, in the West Indies, at the time the Thunderer was lost, and divers others.
 Were any wages due to him at that time? - Yes.
 Mr. Garrow. You are a clerk in the Navy-office, and produce that book from the Navy-office? - Yes.
 Do you know any thing more than that book informs you? - No.
 Is it the practice to pay wages up to the time the ship is supposed to be lost? - Yes.
 You know nothing of this man being brought on board the  Stirling Castle subsequent to 1778? - I do not.
   THOMAS FLETCHER sworn.
 I am clerk in the Prerogative-office; I produce this will from the office where they are originally kept.
 Mr. Garrow. Who delivered it to you? - The Record keeper, Mr. Watts.
