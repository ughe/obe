JOHNSON, Mayor.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Mary Roscow was in her husband's
shop? A. Yes—it was old Mr. Roscow's shop I went to—I believe be is the
husband of Mary and the father of Jeremiah Roscow—old Mr. Roscow was
not at home—Mrs. Roscow paid me on the Saturday night—I think it was 68.
for the week's work—I have known old Mr. Roscow and his wife about twelve
months—I worked for them regularly something like six or seven months—I
have left them about six months—it was through old Mr. Roscow's influence
I got the badge.
GEORGE GBEEN. I am a mattress-maker; I live at Stratford. I worked
for Mr. Roscow—l saw two large bales in the passage, by the private door
of his house—I cannot say whether it was in Mareh or April—it is a good
bit ago, because I have left Mr. Roscow about three months—I suppose it was
about a month before I left him—I did not see the bags brought, nor see
them unpacked—I only saw some ticking and cotton that was on the outside
—I eannot say what sort of ticking it was.
Cross-examined by MR. DOA NE. Q. When you say you worked for Mr.
Roscow you mean the old gentleman? A. Yes, and this is his wife.

A. Two bags of cotton, or a bag and a half—it was in two bogs—there was
