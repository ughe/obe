1759. JAMES PEARLE , stealing two gowns, value 2 l  .; the goods of Betsy Danes . JAMES LINE( policeman, N  282.) In consequence of information, and receiving this duplicate ( produced ) from a man named Cole, I went to Mr. Cockburn, pawnbroker, at Richmond—I produced the duplicate, and was shown a gown—I received a description of the person who had pledged it; and in consequence found the prisoner—I asked him if he had lost a duplicate; he said "No"—I showed him this ticket, and said, "Do you know anything about this?"—he said, "No"—I asked him to go to a public-house with me, and he refused—I asked him where he was the day this property was pledged; he said he should not tell me—as we were going to the station, I saw his hand in his pocket—I had a struggle with him, and in his hand I found a duplicate for two blankets, and at the station I found another duplicate. JOSEPH GRIFFIN. I am assistant to Mr. Cockburn, pawnbroker, of Rich mond. I produce a silk gown, pledged on 7th July by the prisoner—I gave him the duplicate produced. BETSY DAVIES. I am a widow. This gown is mine, and has my work on it—I saw it safe within a few days of missing it—the prisoner lodged at my home. GUILTY Aged 40.— Confined, Three Months .

 1760. JOHN PRIOR , embezzling on   10th May , 3 l  . 10 s  .; the moneys of William Shaw , his master.
   MR. ROBINSON conducted the Prosecution , WILLIAM HOOD. I am in the service of Mr. Shaw, a hay-salesman; the prisoner was also in his service. On 10th May, I sent him out with a load of hay to sell—he returned between 7 and 8 o'clock—I asked him whether be had sold it, he said he had—I asked whether he had the money—he said no, he should have it in a fortnight—on 21st June, I was at Smithfield-market, and had three loads of clover there, which I sold to a person we call Joe , for 4 l . 6 s .;—I told the prisoner to go with him, and Joe  said, as soon as the clover was unloaded there would be ready tin  —I said, "Do you mean money?"—he said, "Yes"—I sent the prisoner with the load, and in the evening I met the prisoner and Joe  in the Old Kent-road, and asked him if he had got the money—he said he had got 3 l ., that the old woman had got no more, and the old man was not at home—he gave me the 3 l .—he did not state the person's name to whom the clover was sold—he remained in the service a couple of weeks, and then left of his own accord—three or four days after he was taken into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARRY . Q.  You then saw him in Smithfield? A.  Yes; Mr. Shaw is in the habit of attending there, and selling hay— Long Joe  was not present when the prisoner told me he had only got 8 l .—I had told Long Joe  not to be in that cart, but be off about his business—the prisoner only sold hay on credit, when I or Mr. Shaw gave him orders so to do—I do not know if Joe  is here.
