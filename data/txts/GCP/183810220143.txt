of Edward Burchell.
EDWARD BURCHELL. I live in Exeter-street, Lisson-grove, and am a car-
pier. I left the prisoner in bed, in my house, on the 11th of September, and
my coat and trowsers hanging on the side of the bed - I lost them--he had
lodged with me for a week, but he never came any more after that day-I
SAW him on Thursday last, near Union Hall, and took him-I said, "Where
are my things ?"-he said, " They are all right, I was coming this evening
to you to make it all right, don't lock me up" - I have not seen them.
William BROADHURST. I live in Exeter-street, opposite the pro-
secutor's. I know the prisoner, he generally wore a jacket-on the
morning of the 11th of September, I saw him go out of the prosecutor's
house, with a small bundle under his arm, and the prosecutor's coat on his
back--I knew the coat well.
