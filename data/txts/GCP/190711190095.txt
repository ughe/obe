LEONARD ELSTON MCQUEEN. I for some years carried on business
as tailor; at 32, Rathbone Place, since Christmas last; before then
for twenty years at Dean Street and Berwick Street. I saw Redgrave
first on August 3, but did not speak to him. That would be the
occasion when he said I declined a cheque. Some little time before
that I had obtained an equity of redemption from Campbell and re-
ceived a telephone message from him that morning to see him. 1
was going to Thorp and Saunders in London Wall, solicitors, on this
business when I met Campbell on Ludgate Hill. Ho came up and
shook hands with me, and we chatted a minute or two. He said:
"Could you oblige me with a cheque by doing a cheque for me.
I understood he wanted me to give him a couple of pounds as accom
modation on a cheque. I told him I could not, but would see if I
could do it later on in the day if he came to my business place. Red-
grave came up at the time, but did not speak to me. I think I knd
known Campbell about two and a half years. I have known Cole-
man about five years, having mede his acquaintance when I had
a tailoring business. He is a woollen jobber, and I bought woollen
from him and made clothes for him and his friends. When I
say "When I had a tailoring business " I mean I was in business
on my own account. The next time I saw Redgravy wis on August
13. Campbell called at my place on that day I think he came up!
