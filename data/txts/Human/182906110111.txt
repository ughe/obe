I knew my mistress was very severe, and I know the property is not hers - it belongs to a man she lives with, who has a wife - I wanted to know what I could get one for, as I knew she would make me pay for it; I asked what he would charge for a milk pot the size of that, he said 2l. but he said," You can sell this for old silver, and it will come to 15s. or 16s." - I said it was a good deal of money to pay, but I would go and ask my mistress to come.
 MR. HUNT. This is broken purposely.
 MRS. NUTTALL, re-examined. Q. Did you make her pay for any thing before? A. Yes; for a baking-dish which I lent her - she was very insolent, and I did keep 2s., and told her when she replaced the baking-dish I would give it her back again; I am a little hasty sometimes. GUILTY. Aged 32.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutrix. Confined Three Months.
 1269.  CHARLES HARRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of June , 1 handkerchief, value 2s., the goods of George Weston , from his person.
   JOSEPH DAWS. I saw the prisoner take a yellow silk handkerchief out of Mr. Weston's pocket; I have seen Mr. Weston before several times, and heard him called Weston - I do not know what he is; he was before the Magistrate at Bow-street - I only know that his name is George, by hearing him called so by several gentlemen who have been with him; this happened on the 6th of June, about six o'clock in the morning, at Mr. Belshaw's wine-vaults, at the corner of the Piazza, Covent-garden; I went in there to get a glass of liquor, and I saw the prisoner take the handkerchief from Mr. Weston's pocket; I took it out of his hand immediately as he drew it from the pocket - I gave the handkerchief to Mr. Weston.
 JOHN EAMES. I am a constable. I have had the handkerchief ever since Mr. Weston gave it me; he is not here.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went to get a glass of something to drink - I was very much intoxicated, and knew nothing of the transaction.
     NOT GUILTY.

GEORGE HICKS was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of May , 2 gowns, value 10s.; 2 aprons, value 1s.; 2 window curtains, value 1s.; 1 pinafore, value 3d.; 2 child's flannels, value 3s; 3 caps, value 1s.; 5 shirts, value 1s.; 3 children's rollers, value 6d; 4 bed-gowns, value 3s.; 1 frock, value 1s.; 1 petticoat, value 1s.; 3 handkerchiefs, value 1s.; 1 bed curtain, value 1s., and 1 box, value 6d., the goods of Mary Griffiths.
 MARY GRIFFITHS. I am a  widow, and live at No. 4, White-horse-court. On the 4th of May I was moving from No. 9, Twister's-alley, from one house to another; the property was there between five and six o'clock in the morning - I missed it about twelve; I found it at the pawnbroker's
   THOMAS BROOK. I am a pawnbroker. I produce two gowns, three aprons, and two curtains, pawned by the prisoner on the 4th of May, in the name of George Hicks.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You have known him some time? A. Yes; he gave his own name and address - I had been told the prosecutrix had asked 2l.,
 and I asked how she could ask that for them, as they were not worth so much.
 MARY GRIFFITHS. I lost more property than this.
 Cross-examined. Q.Where is Mr. Griffiths? A. He has been dead these three years; I am not married now; I do not live in the house with the prisoner - I know Hawksby, but I never asked through him for 2l., of the prisoner; I never said, that if the prisoner would give 2l. I would drop the business - I had no conversation with him about these things; I know the prisoner by sight, but I do not think I ever spoke to him.
 DAVID MCCRAY. I took up the prisoner in his father's house - he gave himself up to me; he said two young men came and asked him to pawn the parcel for them, and they would recompence him.
 MR. PHILLIPS called -
 - KING. I am a butcher. I was talking to the prisoner, and two young men came up and called him away; I went home, and did not see him for an hour or an hour and a half afterwards - I did not notice that they had any bundle; I do not remember what day it was - I was at the corner of Adam and Eve-court, and he was called over to Playhouse-yard.
   WILLIAM HAWKSBY. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Whitecross-street; I have known - the prisoner from an infant - the prosecutrix said, in my presence, that 2l. would satisfy her for these things - I am quite sure of that.
