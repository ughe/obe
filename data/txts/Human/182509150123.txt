1554. RICHARD ECCLES was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , seven shillings, the monies of James Walker , his master. The prosecutor did not appear. NOT GUILTY.
 1555.  WILLIAM HODGES was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of August , a printed bound book, value 6 s., the goods of William Simpkin and Richard Marshall, his masters.
   MARK DOCKWOOD. I am foreman to Messrs. William Simpkin and Richard Marshall. The prisoner was in their employ on the 12th of August. I know this book to be their property - it was taken from the prisoner, and was missing from the shop.
   CHARLES PAYNE. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner came with this book to pawn, and having a suspicion of him, I asked him where he got it from; I forget what answer he made, but I said I should stop him - he ran away; I pursued, and took him - he then said he lived with a Mr. Robinson, in Paternoster-row.
   ABRAHAM GRIFFITH. I took the prisoner, and found several duplicates upon him. I did not hear any thing of this book.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1556.  WILLIAM HODGES was again indicted for stealing, on the 18th of March , thirty-two printed bound books, value 5 l., the goods of William Simpkin and Richard Marshall , his masters.
   MARK LOCKWOOD. I know these books to be the property of Messrs. Simpkin and Marshall - we have had a number of them so bound - I do not know how many; I do not know of any of them being missed in March last.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. Have you any private mark upon them? A. No. There are a great number of this description sold.
   CHARLES PAYNE. Two parcels of these books were pawned at our house, by the prisoner - these two were pawned on the 25th of June, for 4 s. I shewed them to Messrs. Simpkin and Marshall, who identified them as their property - they have not been out of my possession. Here are three books pawned, and six more, which I bought of him at different times.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1557.  HENRY SUMNER was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of September , twenty-six yards of floor-cloth, value 4 l. 14 s. 6 d., the goods of James Webb Southgate and Jeremiah Mitchell.
   ROBERT SPARROW. I am in the employ of Messrs. James Webb Southgate and Jeremiah Mitchell, of Fleet-market. Last Thursday night I was down in the cellar with three of our men - we heard some one in the shop; I went up, and found the prisoner, who was a stranger; he had got the roll of floor cloth from the place where it was, and removed it about half a yard - he was endeavouring to put it on his shoulder; I closed the gate which had been left open, and asked what he was going to do; he then begged hard for mercy, and said he had come from Birmingham, and left his parents.
 Cross-examined by MR. LAW. Q. Might he not as well have thought of putting St. Paul's upon his shoulder. A. I should think not quite; he said, before the Magistrate, that he came by the desire of a friend, to make a purchase of some floor cloth; the roll consisted of twenty-seven yards - he was endeavouring very hard to get it on his shoulder; it is a load for any person, it weighs about one hundred pounds - it would have been madness for him to have attempted to carry it.
 Q. How long had you been in the cellar? A. About a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes - it was safe when I went down, and when I came up, he was attempting to put it on his shoulder.
   THOMAS BARKER. I am one of the City police. I have had this cloth in my care.
 JEREMIAH MITCHELL. I am in partnership with Mr. Joseph Webb, Southgate. This is our property - there is eighty-one feet of it; it is worth four guineas and a half.
 Cross-examined. Q. Is it not very heavy? A. Yes, it is not likely, that a lad in a week state of health, could carry it.
 Prisoner. I went into the shop, and when the man asked me what I was going to do, it confused me very much.
 THOMAS BARKER re-examined. Q. Did not the prisoner state, when the charge was made, that he wished to be taken to a friend, for whom he had looked at this oilcloth? A. No, at that shop, he said nothing, but when we got to Mr. Mitchell's, he said he hoped he would forgive him, and he wished to speak to him in private, but Mr. Mitchell said, "What you have to say, say;" he then said, I have been obliged to abscond from my friends at Birmingham, through having a bad disorder, and I wanted to get something that I might go home to them."
     GUILTY. Aged 20. Confined Three Months.
 1558.  JOHN WILKINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , six pieces of printed cotton, containing one hundred and sixty-eight yards, value 10 l., the goods of John Bate.
   CATHERINE BATE. I am the wife of John Bate, and live in Fore-street, Cripplegate- he is a haberdasher and draper. On the 22d of July, about two o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the prisoner with two other men about the house, and sometimes at the corner of Basinghall-street - I continued to watch them till about five o'clock, I was then about to leave the shop to go to the parlour, when I turned my head and saw the prisoner taking the cotton - I lost six pieces; I ran into the street, and saw him endeavouring to cover it with his apron - he turned up Moor-lane; I afterwards saw a gown on the back of the woman he lives with, made of some of the same cotton - he was taken on the 2d of August - when he committed the robbery, he had a fustian coat like a shooting jacket on, and a leather apron with a bib to it. I had observed him about the house previously to that day.
