 1847.  ELIZABETH KLEAVER was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 looking-glass, value 2s.; 1 bolster, value 8s., and 1 blanket, value 2s., the goods of John Moorecraft.
   SARAH MOORECRAFT. I am the wife of John Moorecraft, a carpenter- we live at Somer's-town; these articles were part of the furniture of a room let to the prisoner on the 15th of July - on the 23d of August I missed them, and charged her with it; she said, she would replace them, - I said, if she would in five days, I would forgive her; but before that time, she was taken for another offence.
   WILLIAM CRUSH. I am a pawnbroker of Somer's-town; I produce these articles which were all pawned by the prisoner at different times - she has had a good character for the last twelve months.
   RICHARD COOPER. I am a constable. I took up the prisoner for another charge, and found the duplicates on her.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress, and received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 48. Confined Fourteen Days.
 1848.  ELEANOR MURPHY was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of July , 1 watch, value 20s.; 2 seals, value 7s.; 1 watch key, value 1s.; 2 rings, value 10s., and 2 sovereigns, the property of Robert Wildman , from his person.
 ROBERT WILDMAN. I am a  rope-maker. On the evening of the 15th of July, I went with the prisoner to a house in Bluegate-fields; we went to bed, and I laid till morning - she was gone when I awoke, and I missed my watch and money; I am sure I had them when I went to bed - I put my watch in my hat, and put it into the cupboard; there was a ring on my finger which was taken off - my money was in my trowsers pocket, which I put into the cupboard; this is my watch.
 Prisoner. He gave me the watch in my possession, knowing me for many years; we were brought up children together. Witness. I did not give it her to keep.
   HENRY HINKIN. I am a cordwainer. The prisoner bought a pair of boots of me on the 16th of July - she gave me a sovereign, and I gave her change.
   WILLIAM JEFFERY. I received this property from my father, who is dead.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1849.  RICHARD CLINKAM was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , 1 watch, value 30s.; 1 seal, value 15s., and 1 key, value 10s., the goods of Frederick Miller.
   MARY MILLER. I am the wife of Frederick Miller, and live at Somer's-town. On the afternoon of the 16th of August, I went out to get some flour; the prisoner came into the shop where I was, and from there he crossed and went into mine - I saw him goacross my shop, and into the parlour where I had left my watch; I did not see him take it, but crossed directly - I missed it, and detained him; I accused him of having been into my room - he denied it, but I found the watch in his bosom.
   WILLIAM BATTAN. I am an officer; I was called in to take the prisoner in charge.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 10. Whipped and Discharged.
 1850.  JAMES BARKER& WILLIAN JOHNSON were indicted for stealing, on the 27th of July , 1 coat, value 2l.; 1 waistcoat, value 15s.; 1 handkerchief, value 2s.; 1 pair of spectacles and case, value 15s.; 1 prayer-book, value 6s.; 1 knife, value 6s., 1 comb, value 2s., and 1 ink bottle, value 4d., the goods of Joseph Webb.
 JOSEPH WEBB. I am a  cooper, and live in Clerkenwell. In consequence of information I received, I looked and missed my coat, waistcoat, silk handkerchief, spectacles, and some other little things which had been in my waistcoat pocket - I had seen them all safe in the afternoon before, and before eight o'clock that morning, Dismore told me they were gone - the coat and waistcoat are lost; these spectacles and these little articles are mine - this pocket-book I can swear to; it was in my waistcoat pocket.
   THOMAS DISMOME. I live with my father in Clerkenwell; he is a jeweller. On the 29th of July, I saw a person get out of Mr. Webb's parlour window; he ran off - he appeared to have a black coat and a handkerchief under his arm - I told Mr. Webb; I saw the prisoners at Hatton-garden the same day - Barker had a similar dress on to the person I saw; but I could not see his face.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. It was not Johnson then? A. No. I never saw him till he was at Hatton-garden.
   ELIZABETH WARD. I saw the prisoners between seven and eight o'clock on the morning of the 29th of July, lurking about Mr. Webb's premises, which are only two doors from ours; I did not see them run away.
   THOMAS GROVES. I am a watchman. I heard of the robbery - I went on the 31st of July, and saw the two prisoners together about fifty yards from me; they saw me - Barker ran away, but Johnson did not - I took Johnson; I found these spectacles, pocket-book, knife, and comb, upon him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did Johnson know you were an officer? A. I do not know; but Barker did.
   JAMES ROBINSON. I was with Groves; we went up Aylesbury-street, and saw the prisoners - when we came near them, Barker ran, I pursued him up John-street, and took him; I found 2s. and an old handkerchief on him.
 BARKER'S Defence. I was not up that way at all that morning; I had been for some tea and sugar - as I was coming back these gentlemen met me; one of them put his fist up and was going to hit me - I ran to the top of Aylesbury-street; they called Stop thief! and I stopped directly.
