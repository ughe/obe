   Prisoner . I left the lump with the trowsers—I did not know it was a purse as I was going along the street I felt a lump, but took no notice of it—I left the trowsers at the dyer's, and returned to my master directly; and in the afternoon he said, "You took my trowsers, did you feel any thing in them?"—I said, "Yes, a lump, but I did not take any notice of it—and when I got to the shop I left it there"—he asked if I could take my oath that I left the lump—I said, "Yes"—he said, "Well, go to the dyer's and ask for the trowsers, and examine them," which I did—I said to the young woman, "When I brought them, there was a lump in them"—she said, "No, there was not, "—but she said there were five sovereigns, a double-sovereign, and seven half-sovereigns—I came back to master, and he said, "How the devil should she know what was in the purse?"—he said, "Put your hat on"—I went back with him—he said, "Fetch up your box," which I did, and he searched it, but found nothing—he said, "Go to Rolls, and say I will have a search-warrant"—I went there, and they said, "The sooner the better"—master asked if I could take my oath that I left the lump there, and I said I could. NATHAN DELPH re-examined . It was a slate-coloured leather purse, with two slides—the five sovereigns were at one end—I had told the contents be fore the prisoner went back. HARRIETT MAY. I have been three years at Mr. Rolls' next August—I saw the prisoner when he brought the trowsers, about a quarter to ten o'clock on the 11th of February—he said he had brought his master's trow sers to be cleaned, and asked when they would be done—I said on Satur day; and as soon as they were done we would send them home—he left them—I laid them down behind the counter till a quarter to twelve o'clock, when I entered them in the day-book—I did not feel any thing in the pocket—if there had been the smallest thing possible in the fob I must have felt it—I am positive there was no lump at all—the prisoner said nothing to me about a lump—the trowsers were rolled up when he brought them; but I opened them when I entered them in the day-book—I threw them open on the counter—the flap came completely open—I saw all the pockets and the fob, and am certain there was nothing in them.
   Prisoner Q.  You did not open them till twelve o'clock? A.  Not till a quarter to twelve o'clock—there was nobody in the shop but me—the workmen do not go through that part of the shop—I never stirred from the counter from the time they were brought—there was a young woman, but she never went near the trowsers—her back was towards me—she never touches any thing—she sat there till one o'clock.
