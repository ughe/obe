Q. Does any body in your house know that she had pawned them? - No, not one.
 Prisoner. I lodged with Mrs. Riley six weeks, at three shillings a week; on the 1st of August my week's rent was due, being Saturday. I paid her two shillings on Sunday morning, and promised to pay her one shilling. It so happened, on Saturday night I was out late, and I was committed to Bridewell for twenty-eight days. Mrs. Riley came to me at Bridewell, and I told her what had happened, and told her I would get them out when I came out. When I came out, and would pay her what I owed her, and the week I had had the key, she staid with me some time, and I begged her to have mercy. When my time was out, she came to the door, and said I must go with her; she said, I must pay her twelve shillings for the month's rent, and get the sheets; I told her I would pay the four shillings I owed her, and get the sheets besides; with that she would not let me go any where; she took me to Guildhall, and I was committed to the Compter; she told the Alderman that I had been missing six weeks, where of I had been missing no longer than what I have told you. I could have got them out again, and should have got them out again, if that accident had not happened; I did not do it with an intention to wrong her out of them, far from it. When I had my hearing with the Alderman, she would have made it up with me.
 Jury. The prisoner has said that the prosecutor promised to forgive her on her paying twelve shillings and taking out the sheets.
 Court. That could make no alteration, the prosecutor cannot compound felony.    GUILTY. (Aged 45.) Imprisoned one month in the House of Correction.

 537.  ANN BURN was indicted, for feloniously stealing, on the 11th of September , two cotton table cloths, value 1s. and two linen aprons, value 1s. the goods of John Bazeley.
 Mrs. BAZELEY sworn.
 My husband's name is John Bazeley; I am a housekeeper, I  keep a public house in Dowgate. I employed the prisoner as a servant on the 9th of September, and she took these things away on the Thursday following, in the morning, before I was up, without any wages or any thing else; I missed two cloth aprons and two linen table cloths the day she went away, as soon as I got up. A young woman in our house met her the next day, and she told her she wanted to speak to her, and she brought her back to our house on the Friday following. I see her, and this apron was on; the other things, she told me, that she had sold them herself to an old clothes woman.
 Q. Could you ever find the other things? - No.
 Q. Before she said that she had sold them, did you tell her it would be better for her to confess? - No, not I myself; I believe the officer asked her what she had done with them, and said, perhaps I should favour her if she told the truth.
 Q. Did she confess before or after the constable said that? - Upon my word I don't know, because I did not hear it.
 Q. What was done with the linen apron that was on her? - I took it from her, and the officer has kept it; his name is Mackenzie. I can very well say it is my own, because I had lent it to one of my servants before, and they tore the corner of it, and I put a piece on. When she came first in, she said you accuse me of a thing that I am not guilty of. I am very well assured it is my apron.
