Q.  Did not you say to him, "I should not have cared where he had gone, if he had not gone to that white-livered son of a b----, Wright?" A.  I did not, it is not language I should have used—he came to beg his brother off—I said nothing of the sort.
   Q.  Did you say, "He will get half my connection, keep him till he gets the remainder, and then turn Wicks about his business?" A.  I recollect no thing of the kind—I cannot swear it did not pass, but I think it very unlikely—I forget whether I saw hhn on the 6th of July.
   Q.  Did you say to him at the Southwark police-court," I suppose I shall hear again of what I said about your brother and Wright?" A.  No, I do not recollect seeing the brother there—I certainly did not say so to him—I will not swear he was not there—I will not undertake to swear whether I said so or not
   COURT . Q.  Was it Moore's privilege to be charged 5 s . profit, taking the larger profit himself f A.  Yes—the prisoner in this instance would have rather a less profit.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Is there an account opened for Wicks in your ledger? A.  Yes—my clerk opened it for his more immediate guidance—this entry is in Moore's writing in the ledger—the amount entered to Wicks on the 13th of Jane is exactly the same as the prisoner has accounted for in the cash-book—I had daily opportunities of seeing these entries.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When the prisoner came into your employ, did he claim the advantage of selling coals? A.  He did not—I should not have allowed it—till I got to the Police-court I had no notion of any such claim—he did not receive money from the customers generally, only from the hucksters—I have no opportunity of knowing what customers he intro duced—when Moore came to me I agreed to allow him this privilege, as he had several connexions in the docks—the prisoner entered his dis bursements in a book—I gave him into custody simply on discovering these defalcations—when I had the conversation with Mr. Wright I had not made the dis covery—the entries in the ledger were made from his book—I have never said that he was honest since I found out this—the ledger is in Moore's writing—if there was an error in the prisoner's book it would go through the whole of the books.
