   Cross-examined.  The policeman said I was under the influence of drink, because I stumbled over a carpet—my carman and I had had one or two pints—I never told any one that I thought the prisoner was the man, though I saw a fellow very much like him on the same night, selling boxes in the London Bridge Tavern, and I made a report at the police-station—he said "Don't you know me?"—I said "You look like the man I am looking after"—I did not say "I have just lost my watch and chain, and I think you have it"—he did not say "Do you think I have got it?" nor did I say "I don't know, but you are very much like the party; had I not seen you with those goods you are selling I should have given you in charge." ( A man named Brindsley was here brought into Court ), that man was with him; they were selling boxes together—I have not said that I would not appear if the prisoner's friends gave me 10 s .; but two fellow, strangers to me, came up, and said "If we give you 30 s ., and send your watch back, will you take no notice? we know where it is"—I said "Send the watch back first"—I did not send word to the prisoner's mother, I did not know where she lived, but she came crying to the police-court on the 27th, and offered me money. BENJAMIN POLLEN( Policeman ). I was on duty on London Bridge on 26th Oct., and at 9.45 p.m. Crawley spoke to me, he was called away by another man, he went to the London Bridge Tavern and returned, we then went to a public-house, and the prisoner was there drinking with other men, and before Crawley gave him in custody some of the men said, "That is not him," he was afterwards pointed out to me and I took him in custody—he said, "I am innocent, I can prove where I was; I was at the music hall till twelve;" but before the Magistrate he said, "I did not say so, I said that I was at the Drummond public-house, Snowsfields, from eight till twelve."
   Cross-examined.  I made no note of the conversation—I did not think it necessary.
   Re-examined.  I received the watch from Crawley on the 30th, at a little before nine o'clock. JOHN PENNY( Policeman ). On 25th October, at 7.30 p.m., I was on duty under the dry Arch of London Bridge—I saw the prisoner and Crawley—as they passed the prisoner said, "It is all right, governor"—I made no reply and they passed on; Crawley came back in ten minutes and complained to me, I referred him to the station—on Monday I went to the station and picked out the prisoner from nine other men—I had seen him before, and have no doubt he is the man—Crawley was the worse for drink.
