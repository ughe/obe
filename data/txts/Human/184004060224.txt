   COURT . Q.  Why did you bar the door, I do not understand? A.  Because I had a suspicion he knew something of the charge—barring the door would prevent his getting out.
   Q.  Could he not get out of the window? A.  That he might, but he could not get into my room, and if he had got out of the door I did not mean to go to sleep—I was not particularly afraid of him, but I did not intend that he should have gone out of my house, without he did get up—if he did get up I should have given information—it was for the public interest as well as my own. MARY ANN ALLBN. I am the wife of the last witness. The prisoner lodged at our house, and did so on the 17th of March last—I do not know Mr. Templeman's cottage, I never saw it—I know it is but a short dis tance from our house—I have never been to it, but I know where it is—the prisoner has been lodging with us nearly a twelvemonth, off and on—the last time he was with us for a whole week, the only week he has been with us since he had been at the Barnsbury Castle public-house—he was with us from Saturday night till the Tuesday week, a week and two days—he did not pay me—I never received any money from him for that, because he had none—he told me he had no money many times.
   COURT . Q.  Did he not pay you at any time during the twelvemonth? A.  Yes—the last time he came to us was the Saturday week previous to the murder.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  When did he tell you he had no money? A.  Frequently—he did not take his meals with us generally since he has been out of employ—he did not take his meals with us at all in this week, only after the Sunday—he took his meals with us two Sundays—MRS. Jarvis brought him some bacon and eggs on the Sunday morning, as he came on Saturday night—on the following Sunday, (the Sunday previous to the murder,) he asked me if I had any breakfast, he wanted some breakfast—I did not answer him, because we had none in the house ourselves—that was between eight and nine o'clock—he said he should go over the water to his cousin's, but his handkerchief was so dirty, and I lent him a stock of my husband's—he then went out directly—he returned at one o'clock in the day—I was at home—he said he had been out, and drank too much beer, and it had made him very ill on an empty stomach—he went out, and was very sick—he came in again, and I asked him if he would have some dinner—he said he was too ill—he asked if he might lie down—I said he might, and he did so—my little boy called him up at five o'clock, for me to make the bed, and put the children to bed—he had gone to lie down in his own room—my children slept with him—he got up, and came into the room where I was sitting—he asked me if I had got any tea—I told him I had not—he asked me to let my little boy go and fetch him some, for he had taken 1 s . while he was out—my little boy did fetch him some—he said, "I have got 6 d . left"—he gave my little boy 6 d ., and he fetched some tea and sugar—I made him some toast—he had his tea, and went to bed—on the following morning, (Monday,) before he got up, Mrs. Jarvis came, I think, about eight o'clock, or a little after—she came to my door and asked if Richard was up—I said, "No"—I opened the door, and she called, "Richard"—if the front door is open you can hear any body
