London on account day." Receiving no reply we wrote informing

Cross-examined. There is no statement in prisoner's letter of Feb-
ruary 2 that he has been recommended to us by anybody as a reliable
man. We made no inquiry of his bankers, as we had no doubt that
our firm had been mentioned to him by Mollwraith, and we took it,
rightly or wrongly, that McIlwraith's were recommending him to us.
We knew that Earle was having what is called a "flutter." I am
afraid it very often happens that & man who has a "flutter" is not
able to pay his differences, but that does not by any means show that
he is a fraudulent man. There is nothing wrong in speculating if you
pay your way.
Re-examined. If a man who has no property at all gets an intro-
duction to a firm of brokers in order that he may speculate, that is
very wrong indeed, and if he does it on a wholesale scale it is still
worse. We regarded "Earle's " letter as in the nature of an intro-
duction from McIlwraith's, who we felt sure would not introduce to
us a man who was not a sound financial man.
