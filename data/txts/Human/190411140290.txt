Church Passage, Greenwich—on Monday, October 17th, Mrs. Dannatt called upon me in the usual way, between 11 and 12 am., for my sub  scription—she had a bag on her arm—when she left she  went towards Church Street—I had said good morning, and when she had got about five or six yards from my door I heard a blow—I had not got further indoors than the sill of the door when I heard a scream, and turned my head and saw Mrs. Dannatt lying on the ground—I got her up, and with the assistance of the neighbours brought her into my house, and bathed her face with warm water—I saw a man running by, but could not swear to him; I was looking after Mrs. Dannatt—he ran as hard as he could go—Mrs. Dannatt had a black eye, a cut across her nose, and was bleeding very much. MARY ANN DAWES. I live at Greenwich—on October 17th I was in Church Passage about 11.45 to 11.50 a.m., when I heard someone call "Stop him, stop him"—Brown was running towards me—when he got close to me he looked at me and said, "You do, and I will serve you the same"—he meant if I stopped him—he ran past to Roan Street—he was a stranger—I saw Mrs. Dannatt on the ground—on October 20th I picked out Brown at Greenwich Police Court in the yard from 11 or 12 men in a row—I recognised him at once—I said, "That is the man."
   Cross-examined by Brown.  I was close to you—you had not so much beard as you have now. WILLIAM BENNETT. I live at Roan Street, Greenwich, opposite Church Passage—I was in Roan Street, about midday, near the corner—I saw two men running down Roan Street—Brown was one of them—I recognised him as a chap I had seen standing about—I only know him as Brown—I saw thorn run round the corner—both the prisoners passed me—Brown was last, about twenty yards behind the other—on October 20 I picked out Brown from about a dozen men—I told the police I knew the man Brown. ARTHUR WELLS. I am a carman, and lived at Eaton Street, Deptford, but I have moved now—on October 17th I was in Roan Street, just before twelve—I saw three men running from the direction'of Church Passage—the prisoners were two—they came within a yard of me—Bresnahan was leading, the second man I cannot recognise, and Brown was about a hundred yards behind—I had never seen them before—on October 20th, at the Greenwich Police Court yard, I picked them out from nine or ten men at once.
   Cross-examined by MR. FULTON . The first time I saw Bresnahan was in Roan Street.
