JOHN Moss (City Detective Sergeant). On 16th April I received a
Warrant, and took the prisoner on it at his office in Gresham Street-I read
the warrant to him, and he said that he had a full answer to the charge
- I took him to the station, and found on him 11. 38. 1d., some letters not
referring to this business, and two memoranda of deposits dated 11th and
18th March.
R. F. MULLINS (Re-examined). The purchaser would pay the freight
upon goods consigned in this way—the bills of lading were to be handed
by the prisoner to the purchasers on the receipt of the cash-this
"Mullins, Higura, and Co.” is my writing, and the "J. C. Marsh ” is
the prisoner's,
By Mr. STRAIGHT. The rent of the office was 3307. a year-it was not
through my instrumentality that Mr. Woodhead was sent down to see the
prisoner before he was taken in custody-I instructed nobody to go and
see him our instructions were that the rent was not to exceed 2001., but
it was necessary to have a little more room and we consented to take
another office we were to pay 1151. a year out of 1651., the difference
was on account of certain business which he said he could send us from
England—we were to act for houses in England, and if there was a com-
mission of 51. per cent. we were to divide it equally-he only had
authority to send us any consignments which we authorised.
JAMES WOODHEAD. I am a clerk, and was employed by the prisoner
in January-I remember receiving the advice of the consignment of Mr.
Smith's goods to Glasgow-I knew by the letter that they were restricted,
and received instructions from Mr. Marsh about the beginning of Feb-
ruary not to inform Mullins that they had been restricted—I had an
advice from New York that Mr. Ross's goods were shipped--I think
they arrived after I left Marsh's service on 1st March-he gave me no
instructions about them.
