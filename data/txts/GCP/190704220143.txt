FREDERICK ALBERT WADSWORTH. In February, 1906, I was a candi-
date for a relieving officership. I spoke to several Guardians, among
them Skinner. I asked him to use his influence for me. He said
there would be some expense attached to it. I said I was willing to
pay. He said 1v would cost £20, as there were seven or eight others
to share with him; two he mentioned were Bissell and Crump. I
bgreed to pay him £10 the day after the appointment and the rest
As soon as I could. I obtained the appointment, and on the day fol-
lowing the election I called at Skinner's shop and paid him £10 in
gold. He then said that no part of it was for himself, but that it
was to go for the others of his party. He said, "You must not say
anything about this or you will incriminate yourself as well as me.
In October, 1906, I paid him another £2 10s. I had had to borrow
£20 from my father, and had been paying it back. On December 19.
I met him at a Board meeting. He said, "Has anyone been to see
you yet?" I said, "No." He said, “If anyone comes you must know
nothing about it; there has no transaction taken place between us."
I said, "All right."
Cross-examined by Mr. Oliver, Skinner said he wanted the money,
mot for the party," but for his parrty." I did not ask who they
were. When he mentioned Orump as one of his party I thought it
strange seeing that I had already paid Crump, but I was not at alt
Surprised. I never asked Crump whether Skipaer had paid him. I
knew that I was doing wrong in paying this money. I have a wife
and family to keep, and I was willing to run the risk.
GEORGE FREDERICK WADSWORTH, father of last witness, deposed to
londing his son £20 just before the election.
FRANK AUSTIN HINTON. Early in 1906 I was vandidate for a post
under the superintendent relieving officer. I had some conversation
with Fordham, then a relieving officer, and he spoke to Skinner.
Later on I saw Skinner, He asked me the salary I was reodiving-it
was £100 a year. The new post I was seeking was at £140. I øsid
I understood from Fordham that I was to pay £10 for the appoint-
ment. Skinner said it was worth £40 to me. He said that he was
fighting hard for another candidate, Jacobs, who was in rather dis-
tressed circumstances, but that if Jacobs fell out, as he expected, he
would run me. Finally we agreed that I should pay £40. He said
it was not all for himself; there were others to divide it amongst. He
said it was a criminal offence, and that if I opened my mouth I should
b e implicated, so that I was not to say a word to anyone. At the elec.
tion I was unsuccessful. I did not pay any money.
Cross-examined by Mr. Oliver. Jacobs did not get elected. In
opite of my having promised this £40, I did not get a single vote. It
1s not the fact that Skinner refused to support me. I am certain that
it was arranged between us that he was to have £40 if I got the posi-
tion.
