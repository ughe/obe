—he said he was going in the lug to get some trefoil seed—he said, "If William comes, and asks where the barge was last night, tell him she was here; I don't want to hare any  bother  with him, and when I come back I will give you a penny; but if he don't say any thing to you, don't you tell him"—he went and left me in care of the barge, and went home to breakfast.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Did you get your penny? A.  No, I did not care about it—I do care about a penny, but when I was in the barge I did not think of it—I have never got into any trouble—I was never in prison, or threatened to be tent there—I do not recollect Sunday the 29th of March—I was never charged with having rope of my master's—I know Joe  Williams—his name is not Taffy—I know nothing about Taffy—Williams is no friend of mine—sometimes I am in the barge with him—I sometimes walk with him—I was not with him last Sunday—I am with him some Sunday a on the barge—I do not remember any day when he was in a scrape, but I beard about it—I was never talked to about it by my mistress—I have been in her service about three months—before that I worked at Mr. Dennis's in the rope grounds for about three months—I left because his house was burned down—I went on Wednesday for these tares—I got them on board from the Giraffe  steamer about six in the after noon—I left after the tares were on board, when she got to St. Mary Overy's dock—I then went away in the boat and left Long on board with the there—I came about eleven o'clock the next day—the tares were all safe then, as far as I know—I was on board the barge all the Thursday night, and Long went away—Long came on Friday morning and this took place—he told me he ought to have been there before, because he was rather behind—he got forty one bags of trefoil seed in the lug boat—I did not fasten the boat with the hitcher—I held on with my hands—I could not have forgotten it if I had.
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  You were before the Justice? A.  Yes—this is my mark on, this paper—( looking at his deposition )—I am fifteen years old in about five months—I have never said I was going on for fourteen, not that I am aware of, I might have made a mistake saying I was fourteen—I may have said I was going on for fourteen years—I some-times make mistakes, in telling my age—if ever I said I held on to the barge with a hitcher that may have been a mistake, because I held on with my hands—I never said I held on with the hitcher—it was about ten o'clock on Thursday night, as far as I know, when I got to Vandervorde's barge—I could not see well across the river—it was not raining—it was a nice mild light night, but the moon was not shining—Vandervorde's barge was lying outside the tier—it was the very outside one—people on shore, who wanted to get to his barge, must have crossed the others—there are no cov ings to his barge at all—the deck and the barge sides are all even—there is no irregularity, no rise at all, only at the head and stern—she laid with her head up and her stern down—timber heads are what you make craft fast to—there are covings to the hatchway—they could make an unevenness on the deck—I saw Vandervorde on Friday morning—when he was speaking to Long he was dressed in a brown coat and black trowsers—he seemed to be up and dressed for the day—there was no smock-frock, or any thing of that kind—there was a man in a smock-frock that helped Long to lift up, but Vandervorde was not on deck then—the sack was deposited by the fore-hatchway—Vandervorde's cabin is aft—Vandervorde was not going for any seed that day that I know of.
