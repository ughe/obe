Cross-spamined. The prosecutor said he should know him better if he

the man- I have made enquiries about these men; they bear very good
characters, Frederick works at a Mrs. Waghorn's and the other one works
where his mother lives.
Ro-examined. I believe they have lost their work through this.
WILLIAM BLATCHER. I am the brother of the prosecutor on 27th
February, I was in Lane Street, about 20 yards in front of my brother, I
heard him call out and ran towards him, he was getting up off the kerb,
the two prisoners were in front of him, and the shorter one struck me
and knocked me down; on getting up they ran away down St. George's
Market-I and my brother went there and saw two boys, and in con-
sequence of what they said I went and got a policeman and then went
to 4, St. George's Market, and saw the prisoners there-I am positive
the prisoners are the med.
Cross-examined. It was getting dark-I believe my brother was in
front of the prisoners when I saw them-I did not see the rule (produced)
-I have not seen the watch since I did not see the woman Louise
Smith there it is about four or five minutes' walk from the place where
this robbery took place to the place where we found the prisoners- I am
positive these are the two men, although I had only seen them for two
or three minutes.
LOUISA SMITH. I live at 9, Staple Street, Long Lane, Bermondsey -
on the Saturday night, 27th February, between six and seven there was
a bit of a bother at the corner of Lancaster Street, and I saw the tall
prisoner strike the prosecutor, and then the short one went to take his
watoh I was about four or five yards away-I could not swear that
these are the two men that I saw-I am an unfortunate-I saw a man
and prosecutor's brother, and a woman and a baby there besides the
prosecutor struck his brother with the rule by mistake.
Nor GUILTY.
ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, MAY 3RD, 1876.
