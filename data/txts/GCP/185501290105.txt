ROBERT FLEMING (policeman, A 459.) From information I received on
Thursday, 11th Jan., about 1 o'clock, I went to Bear-lane, and saw the
deceased-I believe she was quite dead then I caused her to be removed to
the workhouse-I inquired of the people there who was the driver; they
pointed out the prisoner-I went to him and asked him if he was the driver
of the carriage, pointing to the timber carriage-he said he wasI asked
him how the accident occurred-he said it was in consequence of the load
being too large to go into the gate ; they had to take off the fastenings, te
cut off some limbs, and that he went on and neglected to put them on
again-I took him into custody- the book carrier was there-he is here
to-day.
Cross-examined by Mr. GIFFARD. Q. Just tell me exactly what it was he
said; how the accident had happened ? A. He said it occurred in conse-
quence of his going on, and they neglected to put the fastenings on before
he removed.
MR THOMPSON. Q. Just try and recollect as accurately as you can, are
you sure it was, " And they neglected ?" A. Yes; perfectly sure.
COURT. Q. Then you were wrong before; because what you first said was,

EDWARD NEWLING. I am a book carrier, and live at No. 3, James-street,
Camden-town. I was book carrier with the trolly-my duty is to bind the
load, and see it delivered, and get the goods signed foron 11th Jan., 1
accompanied the prisoner with a load of timber to Mr. Edes, in Bear-lane;
the prisoner was the driver (the-witness was informed by the Court, that
he was not obliged to answer any questions which he considered might involve
him in trouble)—then I would rather not say anything---it was the pri-
soner's duty to drive the horses straight to the place where we were going
to-he had nothing to do with the binding-—by binding I mean seeuring
the timber together.
