 2170.  GEORGE LUDMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of August , 4 handkerchiefs, value 2s., the goods of Adam Randwell.
   IVAN HOPPEE. I saw the prisoner with another young man; the prisoner took these handkerchiefs from Randwell's door, he held them up, and then put them into his bosom - I ran and stood before him, and he was secured.
   WILLIAM FULLER. I live with Adam Randwell, a linen-draper. These are his handkerchiefs, and were taken from the door.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was running, hearing an alarm, and they took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Six Weeks.
 2171.  THOMAS MELTON was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 1 gross of curtain-rings, value 4s., the goods of Robert Robertson and James Robertson.
   JOHN ALLMET. On the 8th of August I was with Hitchings, and saw the prisoner pass with another young man; Hitchings turned after them, and they both ran away - I saw the prisoner drop a parcel from under his coat, which I took up.
   CHARLES HITCHINGS. I am a Policeman. I was with Allmet; I saw the prisoner drop this parcel of rings, and secured him; he was nearly three-quarters of a mile from I took up.
   CHARLES HITCHINGS. I am a Policeman. I was with Allmet; I saw the prisoner drop this parcel of rings, and secured him; he was nearly three-quarters of a mile from the prosecutors'.
   ROBERT ROBERTSON. I live in Bedford-place, Commercial-road. These rings belong to my brother James and myself; they were in my father's house - a young man had come into the shop, and asked for locks, and taken them.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Six Weeks.
 2172.  MARY OWEN was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , 1 crown, 8 half-crowns, 25 shillings, and 9 sixpences, the monies of Henry Bone , from the person of Mary, his wife.
   MARY BONE. I am the wife of Henry Bone, who lives in Adam and Eve-court, Whitecross-street- he is a private watchman; the prisoner rented a room of us in the next house, and had no business in my room. On Wednesday the 29th of August I had this money in a bag; there was a French shilling in it - I put it into my pocket, and put my apron over my face, and went to sleep between four and five o'clock in the afternoon; I was suddenly awoke, and the prisoner stood in the room; I said, "Oh, dear, why do you come creeping up here?" she stood a moment, then put her hands together, bent herself half double, and said,"Oh, you don't know my trouble; John is gone a walking with Mary Gray, and holding an umbrella over her;" John is her husband - I told her to go away; she went, and between nine and ten o'clock next day a person came to receive some money, and I missed it from my pocket - the prisoner came up about half an hour after I missed it, and seemed to feel for me very much, and wished very bad wishes on the person who took it; she came again about three o'clock, and said, "Oh, Mrs. Bone, my dear woman don't fret, for you will never see it any more;" I received information, and at last sent for an officer - 2l. odd of the money was found between the bricks of a cellar belonging to the prisoner.
   MARY ANN GREEN. I am the wife of Thomas Green. I had seen the Policeman looking about the prisoner's cellar, and shaking the mats, but a person said something which made go and take a light down into the cellar - I there picked up this bag with the money in it.
   SOPHIA GRAY. I live in the same house as Bone. On the 30th of August, at five o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the prisoner come out of her own cellar, with silver in her hand - I told a Policeman, and the cellar was searched; I saw her go to the gin-shop, and have three half-quarterns of gin, which she paid 6d. for.
   JOSEPH GREGORY. I am a Policeman. I received the bag from Green; it contains 2l. 8s. 3d., besides a French shilling - I found nothing on the prisoner.
   MARY BONE. This is my purse.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, declaring that she had never had the money in her possession, and that Green must have put the bag in the cellar.
   MARY BONE. I was not out of doors - other people had access to the cellar.
     GUILTY. Aged 40. - Confined Six Months.
 2173.  JOSEPH LYE was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of August , 2 pairs of shoes, value 5s., the goods of James Corss.
   JAMES CORSS. I am a shoemaker, and live in Shoreditch. I was not at home when this happened.
   JOHN HARPER. I am a Policeman. On the 21st of August I saw the prisoner about two hundred yards from Corss' shop, and suspecting him I took him - I found these shoes on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 15. * - Transported for Seven Years.
