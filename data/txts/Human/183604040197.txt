Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Had you set a man of the name of Seth upon making inquiries? A.  I had not—I knew he had made in quiries—he is a police-constable, who apprehended Channell—the other person was taken on Channell's information—I believe Seth did not-under take that Channell should be admitted as a witness—this was two years ago last January—the prisoner have not been living in the neighbourhood, to my knowledge—I have been three weeks in the place—the man tried all in his power to use the knife—he kicked my brother-officer, and struck me—he said he was guilty, and meant to plead guilty before the magistrate—that was on Wednesday, and Thursday he was taken before the magis trate.
   COURT . Q.  Have you the warrant here that you read to him? A.  No. 1 left it at the magistrate's office—I read the warrant naming Mr. Strachan—no mention was made of what had happened to Mr. Strachan—I cannot recollect the words I used. GEORGE CLARK. I am clerk to the magistrate at Brentford. I know of no promises held out to prisoner Channell—I have no reason to believe there were any.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you not tell Mr. Harmer's clerk that this man would be received as witness against the other, this very morning? A.  No, I told him I thought he was very likely to be a witness—it was not in tended from the beginning that he should be a witness. JAMES MORGAN STRACHAN. I do not remember Channell coming to my house—I was absent from home during Easter week—on return the sergeant of the police called to say some information had come up which would lead to a discovery of the robbery on my premises—I felt great anxiety that it should be discovered, because of the number of depreda tions from the same quarter—the police-constable came before me the day after, and told me this information had come to Mr. May that would lead to discovery—I mentioned the name of Channell as the person from whom it could be obtained—I heard nothing more till I heard that Channell had gone with him to the magistrate, and made depositions upon which the warrant was grated for Cork, and he was taken into custody—I attended the next morning before the magistrate—I believe Channell was set at large. NOT GUILTY.
