Cross-examined by Mr. Phillips. Q. This conversation of yours, I
understand, was on the 25th of March ? A. Yes, after post time the conver-
sation at the Bank of England was about one o'clock, before the post went
out-he himself directed my attention to the letters and signatures of
Messrs. Carne--it would take two days to communicate with Falmouth by
post--if I wrote on the 25th, the letter would reach Falmouth on the
morning of the 27th, and we could have an answer about the 29th-there
was abundance of time to get an answer before the 2nd of April there was,
no doubt, abundance of time for Lewin Caspar to have absconded in that
time I am acquainted with Mr. Carne's handwriting I should say this
letter (No. 4) is similar writing to his-it is like it-I read the letter
casually-the signature of Mr. Carne is different from the body of the
letter—there is no particular observation which sttikes me in looking at
this letter-I say the same with respect to this (No. 3)it is precisely
the same-the signature is similar to Mr. Carne's.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Look at them again, first at No. 3; does the
handwriting of the body, as far as the signature, appear the same hand-
writing as that on the side ? A. I should think not-I certainly think
them different handwritings- I did not understand Mr. Phillips to inquire
whether the writing was similar or not-I did not know what his questions
alluded to

Me. Phillips. Q. The question I ask you is, from the word “Gen-
tlemen" down to “ Obedient Servant," does any thing strike you as re-
markable in that letter? A. Certainly not-there is nothing in the hand-
writing of this (No. 3) which strikes me as remarkable-(looking at
No. 4)-nor is there any thing about the body of this letter which strikes
me as remarkable-I think I perfectly understand your question-I do
not observe any thing remarkable from the word “Gentlemen' down to
"Your obedient Servant"-the signature of Mr. Carne is decidedly dif-
ferent from the body of the letter, and I should say this receipt is different
from the handwriting of the body of the letter.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. Now take the letter No. 3 in your hand, and tell
me, when my friend desired you to read from the word " Gentlemen"
down to "Obedient Servant," you included in your observation the
writing at the side, “ Please to deliver to Marsh and Co.," &c. 4. I did
not-in my opinion, that writing is different from the body of the letter
I should not think the words " Falmouth" and "March" are the same
handwriting-(looking at No. 4)—I should think that the words " March"
and "Falmouth" in this letter were not written by the same hand I can-
not judge whether the word “Falmouth" was written at the same time as
the body of the letter, but I think it is different from the body of the
letter.
