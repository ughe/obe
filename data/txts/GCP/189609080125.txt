were part of our stock which was stolen.
Cross-examined. They are all silver I have been shown another one.
AUGUSTIN HARRIS (Inspector V). On July 23rd I went to Mrs. Munn's.
shop and found entrance had been effected by forcing the bolt of the re-
volving shutter at the bottom, and afterwards forcing the door leading
into the shop, apparently with a jemmy-I found property had been taken
from show-cases in the window; cases were strewn on the floor the electric
wires were separated near the fanlight over the door leading to the shop,
and that cut off communication with the other part of the premises.
EDWARD HILLMAN. I am assistant to William Stoneham, pawnbroker,
of 47 and 49, Pitfield Street, Hoxton--this lady's small oxydised watch
was pledged by a man for 58. on August 1lth.
MARGARET WEBB. I have known the prisoner for about six years-I
pledged about five of these watches, I think ; the prisoner asked me to do
So, and gave them to me.
Cross-examined. The prisoner lost his wife on Whit Tuesday- I was
with her for the seventeen weeks she was ill he has one daughter,
aged fourteen - the bome depends on his earnings-after his
wife's death I and his sister - in - law have taken care of his
place - he gave me these things to pawn as he was short
of money, and his daughter was ill, and he said he wanted the
money to send her into the country she went into the country I have
known him about six years-I have not been going backwards and
forwards there for more than about twelve months-I knew him by sight,
not to speak to-I knew his wife-when the police stopped me, I told
them at once he had given me the things to pawn, and I said where they
could find him, and gave his name and address. Not Guilty.
