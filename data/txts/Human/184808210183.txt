ELIZABETH  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 18.— Confined Four Months . ROBERT COOK. I am a butcher at Sloane-square, Chelsea—the prisoner Elizabeth was in my service. On 13th Aug., about seven o'clock in the morning, she tapped at my bed-room door, and asked Mrs. Cook to let her have as much money as she could for her father—I gave her 6 s .—about a quarter to eight a message was sent to my wife who left the house, and I saw her go over to Mr. Forbes, and come back with a basket containing five pounds three ounces of beef, fourteen ounces of suet, three pounds of potatoes, one quart of scarlet beans, two pieces of soap, and a cloth enclosing the beef, with my initials on it—I can swear to the beef and suet—I cut it myself, and had the other portions in my shop—I found Elizabeth had left the house—I went to her mother's, and found both prisoners—Elizabeth said in Jane's presence that the basket was hers, but not the contents—the beef was safe at ten minutes past twelve the night before—the prisoner had no right to enter the shop. JOHN FORBES. I am a perfumer of 14, Sloane-square. On 13th Ang., about seven o'clock, I was getting up, looked into the street, and saw the prisoner Jane with a basket—she waited in the street while a girl came out of Mr. Cook's, took the basket in, brought it out again, and gave it to Jane—I went across, and asked what she had in the basket—she said "nothing"—I said I should look—she would not let me—I said, "I am a policeman"—she opened the basket, and I saw the meat in it—I said I would take it, and give it to Mr. Cook when he got up—she asked me to go in and see her sister—I would not—I let her go—I gave Mrs. Cook the basket.
   Jane's Defence.  I thought the basket contained my sister's dirty clothes.
