JAMES BAKER (policeman.) On the 8th of March, from half-past eight
to a quarter to nine o'clock, I saw the prisoners together and stopped them
-Abbott had got a bundle-I went up to them, and asked Abbott what
he had got there-Power answered, "I have got something for my
mother," and said, Abbott was carrying it for him-I asked what it was,
Power said he had brought it from Mr. Hawkins's, a confectioner, in Cam-
berwell-road-I asked whereabouts-he said the other side of the green
-I asked where there--he said leading away to Peckham-I asked
where he was going to carry it-he said to the Elephant and Castle-
I asked where there-he said past Horsemonger-lane, the first turning
on the right-I asked whether it was the house before he got to Horse-
monger-lane-he said, “Yes"- I said there was no house, only a coach-
maker's before you come to the lane, and I did not believe his story, I
should take them to the station, and inquire whether it was true or not-
Power said, “Will you go to the place with me?"-I said I did not be-
lieve his statement-he said it was not far, and he was not going to pass
the Elephant and Castle, he was going to turn down the New Kent-road
-I asked whether he worked for Hawkins-he said he did -I asked
for how long-he said for some little time, and it was Mr. Hawkins's
brother he was going to take the clothes to-I asked what Hawkins's
brother was-he said he could not tell me I asked the name of the
place where he lived-he said he did not know-I called Jones, a con-
stable over, and we took them to the station with the bandle-they said
at first they would not go with me--we left them in charge of the in-
spector, and I and Jones went to Camberwell to inquire for Hawkins, but
could not find any such person I saw the bundle opened at the station-it
contained the articles stated the prosecutrix saw and claimed them-
Abbott said he was carrying them for Power.
CHARLES JONES (police-constable P 230.) I assisted in taking the pri-
soners to the station--as we were going I asked Power where he got the
things from-he said he had brought them from Mr. Hawkins, a confec-
tioner at Camberwell, and was going to take them to Mr. Hawkins's bro-
ther in Horsemonger-lane-I said it might be true, but I doubted it-be
directly answered, “Oh, you are a very nice man; but I do not blame
you if you can get a shilling or two by it."
RICHARD DAVIS (police-constable P 55.) I saw Bowman, and in con-
sequence of what he said to me I went to Edmond-street, made inquiry,
and found that Mrs. Harris's house had been robbed-(I had previously
seen the prisoners and the bundle at the station - I saw this boot on the
bed at Mrs. Harris's-I said I had every reason to believe that the fellow
boot was at the station- I took possession of it-this tea-caddy was broken
open.
MRS. HARRIS re-examined. The articles in this bundle are mine, and
were in my box when I went out that night.
Cross-examined. Q. When you got home was your door open ? A.
No, I found it locked as I had left it-I unlocked it-there was no other
mode of getting into the house-I have back windows, but they were all
fastened-I examined every one when I came home.
COURT. Q. What parish is Camberwell in ? A. St. Giles's, Camber-
well, it is called-I always understood it to be St. Giles's, Camberwell.
