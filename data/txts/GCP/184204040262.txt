Q. Did you, between the Thursday night when he was discharged and
six o'clock next morning, pay a visit to his father? A. I did, at five in
the morning-I went to tell him I intended to give the prisoner a second
time into custody, and that he pleaded very much for me not to proceed
with the case, and said he should very likely be transported, or lose his
situation--the inspector and the policeman heard the prisoner say that
to me in the station-after that the prisoner greatly insulted me- I told
his father that, and that there were a great many duplicates in his posses-
sion, that I could find a clue, not only to the coat, but also to other

Q. If he was not about to be discharged from stealing the coat and um-
brella, why have you not indicted him for it? A. The charge was made
for the pencil-case, after going round to the pawnbrokers to see if the
umbrella belonged to me-he was not indicted for the coat and umbrella,
because they were not clearly proved-I saw the duplicate of a pencil-
case-I thought it to be mine-I went to the pawnbroker's and examined
it, and claimed it, at least as the one that was left at my house-it was
not when the Magistrate was about to dismiss the charge about the coat
and umbrella, that I preferred this charge- I swear that I produced the
pawnbroker's man to prove that the prisoner had pawned it-the pawn.
broker said it was the pencil-case that was brought, but he could not
swear to the prisoner as the person that pawned it.
