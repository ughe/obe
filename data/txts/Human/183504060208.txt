   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Are you quite sure the represen tation made by Moore was, that Grobecker was the ground-landlord of the houses in Sidney-square? A.  And a street near there; and I am quite sure that Grobecker said he was—I should not have let my bricks have gone unless he had—it was on Friday, the 25th or 26th of September, that I went to Grobecker's house with Moore—I sent a Bill of Exchange to Grobecker for his acceptance, after sending some bricks—that was the agreement—the bills were to be drawn by Moore, and accepted by Gro becker—I sent three Bills of Exchange to Grobecker for his acceptance—I have no recollection of sending to him for the acceptance of a bill which I had indorsed before I sent it—I recollect a bill coming back in consequence of Moore's writing—I have no recollection of sending him a bill with an indorsement.
   Q.  Was not the representation made, that Grobecker would be secu rity for Moore; and as a counter security, he had got the agreement of his ground-landlord for houses in Mile-end-road? A.  I never heard of an agreement till after Fagan told me I was dealing with swindlers—Mr. Catlin was not employed as my attorney to apply to these parties for pay ment of the Bills of Exchange—I have not seen an agreement between Moore and his ground-landlord, in the possession of Mr. Catlin—I saw it in the possession of the police-sergeant—I was told that Grobecker would be security for Moore for the payment of the bricks, and Grobecker agreed to it—he agreed to pay me, and said it made no difference whether he paid me or Moore.
   MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  When did you employ Mr. Catlin as your attorney? A.  After Inspector Fagan sent me two or three letters, saying I was dealing with swindlers, and begging of me to come to town directly—that was three months after Grobecker agreed to become security.
