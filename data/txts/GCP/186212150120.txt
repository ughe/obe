JOSEPH CAMP (Policeman, L 128). On Saturday afternoon, 220 November,
I saw the prisoner talking to Ham-I was in uniform, and was twenty yards
off— I saw the prisoner take something from his right-hand trousers' poeket
like a brown-paper parcel, and throw it over the house-Ham took hold of
his collar, and not knowing him to be a constable, I went over, but he said.
"I am a constable ; he has thrown some bad money over that house "- I took
the prisoner to the station, and found on him & crown-piece and one
shilling, both good.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am Inspeotor of Coin to Her Majesty's Mint-
these three crowns are all bad, and from the same mould-these three half.
crowns are bad, and from the same mould.
Prisoner's Defence. I am a shoemaker-I had an appointment to meets
man named Bradbury, at the Elephant and Castle, at 3 o'clock; he gave me
this rarcel, and I had not walked fifty yards when the constable came up to
me face to face. I had only bad it three or four minutes. I did not know
he was a constable, and I told him to come to the station and see I
certainly threw it over the house. I asked the policeman if he knew the
man he had seen me with. He said, "No." He says now that he knows
the house where he lived. He admitted to me that he had never seen me
before, but took me for another man. The man has now gone away.
GUILTY.-Confined Twelve Months.
ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, JANUARY 5TH, 1863.
