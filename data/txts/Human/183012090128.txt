 267.  JAMES HARE and JOHN SMITH were indicted for stealing, on the 5th of December , 4 bushels of malt, value 25s., the goods of Edward Jones ; and 1 sack, value 2s., the goods of Henry Hole.
 RICHARD JONES. I am brother of Mr.  Edward Jones, proprietor of the  Hope Brewery, at Edmonton. Last Sunday morning week we found the brewery broken open in the morning, and missed four bushels of malt, and a sack, which were the property of Mr. Henry Hole.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was it not in consequence of a letter from Mr. Jackson that the prisoners were taken up? A. Yes.
   HENRY HOLE. I lent twenty sacks to Mr. Jones - I have seen one of them since, at Mr. Jackson's - I was present when Hare was apprehended; he said he had found it in Islington.
 Cross-examined. Q. How far is that from where it was stolen? A. About seven miles.
   THOMAS JACKSON. I am a corn-dealer, and live in Chichester-place, St. Pancras. On the Monday before the 10th of December, Smith the prisoner, called to inquire the price of malt between eleven and one o'clock; he went away - Hare then came to ask if I was a buyer of malt; Smith then came and brought a sack of malt which they both said they had found by the coach rank at Islington; I saw on the sack "Henry Hole, Edmonton," marked in red letters - I told them they had better go to the person whose name it bore, and tell him they had found it; they said they thought they should not get paid if they went, and asked me to pay their expences; I said, "Then write, or I will write for you, to save all trouble;" I took a piece of paper and wrote in their presence, and gave the letter to a man who came in on business - they saw what I did, and said they thought it was the best thing that could be done; they left the malt with me.
 Cross-examined. Q. You wrote by their desire? A. Yes, and Mr. Jones came to town; the prisoners called frequently after I had written, to know if I had had an answer - and when Mr. Jones did come, I sent to the White Hart and one of the prisoners was there - he came directly, and the other soon after; I know the prisoners to be hackney coachmen - there was nothing to prevent their cutting the name out of the sack
     NOT GUILTY.
 268.  ANN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of December , 1 sovereign, and 1 half-sovereign, the monies of William Abel , from his person.
 WILLIAM ABEL. On the 12th of December I was in Hackney-road,  Shoreditch- I saw the prisoner, who followed me, took my arm, and took me up a dark street; I was not sober, but I knew what passed - she asked me to give her sixpence, which I did; she put her arm round me, and I lost a sovereign and a half. which were in a piece of printed paper in my left-hand breeches pocket - I missed them in three or four minutes, or it might be ten minutes. I cannot say exactly; I followed after the prisoner directly -I told her she had robbed me, and I took her by the arm, called the Policeman, and gave her in charge; she denied it - I told the Policeman, in her presence, that she had taken a sovereign and a half of mine.
   FRANCIS FEATHERSTONE. I am a Police-officer. I took the prisoner on the night in question; the prosecutor stated that he had lost a sovereign and half; I asked her what money she had in her pocket; she took out some halfpence and a sixpence, and said that was all she had about her I took her and searched her, and on unlacing her stays, a piece of paper dropped; I took it up, this is it - it is printed paper, and a sovereign and a half were in it.
 Prisoner. I picked it up at my feet, wrapped up in the paper, I did not know what it was, but being distressed I put it inside my stays.
   WILLIAM ABEL re-examined. Q. You say you were intoxicated; now were your clothes in such a state that this might have fallen from your breeches pocket on the ground? A. No, my Lord, my clothes were not down at all, and that pocket was not unbuttoned; I was not so intoxicated as to be uncertain about it; it was about one o'clock in the morning - I had been at work from the dinner time before; I am aware that this is a very serious charge, but it was not possible for me to have dropped it.
 FRANCIS FEATHERSTONE. The prosecutor was a little in liquor, but he seemed quite collected, and walked pretty well; he was in a state to know what he was about.
 JURY to W. ABEL. Q. Was your sixpence in the same pocket with the sovereign and a half? A. No, it was not, I am sure; I did not open that pocket at all - when I saw her, I put my hand into that pocket, felt the money safe, and buttoned it again.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Transported for Life.
