BEFORE THE COMMON SERJEANT.
(Monday, November 29.)
CROSLAND, Thomas William Hudson, maliciously publishing
certain defamatory libel and defamatory words of and concerning
Henry Frederick Walpole Manners Sutton.
Mr. George Elliott, K.C., and Mr. Starry Deans prosecuted; Mr.
Valetta, Mr. Wing, and Mr. Horace Samuels defended.
Mr. Valetta submitted that the prisoner was entitled to be dis-
charged. On September 8 the recogmsancæ were enlarged by the
Recorder in order that a plea of justification might be filed, which
was done oh October 5. On October 15 a replication was filed, and
on November 16 a demurrer to the plea of justification was filed. The
prosecution were therefore to be assumed to have admitted the facts
stated in the plea of justification. [Archibold, p. 1,126 ; Lord Camp-
bell's Act, 1843, sec. 6; R. v. Brown, 3 Cox, . 127; Bailey v. Baker,
Dowling N.S., 891, 60 Geo. 111. and 1st Geo. c. 4, secs. I and 9.]
Mr. George Elliott stated that the prisoner had not until to-day
pleaded to the indictment or filed his plea of justification. At the
suggestion of the Recorder that there might be a demurrer, alternative
documents were drafted and, for convenience, copies were handed to
the clerk of the court and defendant's solicitors. He was at liberty
now to Ne either the replication or the demurrer.
The Common Sergeant held that the pleas were not really com-
pleted ; the record would stand on the pleas of not guilty and justifica-
tion. Mr. George Elliott then put in the plea Of replication.
On affdavits that a material witness for the defence was in
America the case was further adjourned to February 9, 1910.
