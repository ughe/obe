988. JOHN FREDERICK was indicted for a misdemeanor.
Messrs. BODKIN and ESPINASSE conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE ADAMS. I am a baker, and live in Hill-street, Woolwich. In
the evening of the 8th of February, the prisoner came to my shop, and
asked for a penny loaf-I cut him a pennyworth of bread, and he tendered
me a shilling- I took it up, and said, " This is a bad one"-he said, " Is
it?" I said, “ Yes, it is; and I think you know it" he said, “You had
better link me" -- which means, to handcuff him-I said, " Is that what
you mean? I think you are what I term a smasher"-I had the shilling in
my hand, and said, “ I will mark this; and if you can prove you got it
legally, you shall have it; if not, I will not give it you"-I marked it,
and put it into my left-hand pocket-he wanted to have it-he got to the
shop-door, and abused me in very bad language-I kept it apart from all
other money till the 16th of February, when I gave it to Riordan.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. What mark did you put on it?
A. A cross on the head-he said he got the shilling from a woman at
Greenwich.
DANIEL RIORDAN (police-constable R 207.) I produce the shilling I
received from Mr. Adams.
Eliza COCKLE. I am the wife of Benjamin Cockle, who keeps a beer-
shop in East-street, Greenwich. On the 11th of February, the prisoner
came between six and seven o'clock in the evening-he asked for a half-
penny pipe of tobacco-I served bim-he gave me a sixpence-I gave him
5fd. change, and he went away-I bit the sixpence, and it was bad-I
put it under a tumbler on the shelf, and gave it to the policeman on the
following Monday, when he came for it-he told me to mark it, and I
notched a piece out of the side.
JOHN WALKER (police-constable R 182.) I took the prisoner about
ten o'clock in the evening, on the 15th of February, at Greenwich I found
on him 8d. in copper, a 4d. piece, a biscuit, and a halfpenny loaf, and
on a man who was with him I found 10s. in silver, 6d. worth of copper,
five pipes full of tobacco, and five halfpenny-worths of thread--I have the
sixpence which I got from Mrs. Cockle.
SAMUEL EDWARD FREEMAN (police-sergeant R 3.) I saw the prisoner
searched by Walker-he threw a small bag on the table, which he took
from the prisoner, and it was supposed to be empty-I found in it this
sixpence.
JOHN FIELD. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint. These two
sixpences and this shilling are all counterfeit.
GUILTY. Aged 17.--Confined One Year.
