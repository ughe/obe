GEORGE EDWARD THOMPSON. I am ten years old ; I live with my father
in Craven-street, Strand. I recollect the prisoner coming to our house in Jan.
it was on the day before we were to go to school I do not remember
the day-it was about the middle of Jan. I have seen this paper before
(looking at it), it was sent to my papa after I went to school I came home
from school before Christmas we were to have six weeks holidays we
were to go to school on a Wednesday- the day we were to go to school
the prisoner came he asked if I was going to school, and if not he asked
f he should come and teach me private lessons in the house he said if my
mamma would give him a room he would teach me and my two sisters
that was agreed to by my mamma--he went down to Hoddesden on Tuesday
- he said he was going to get his things, and he would come back on Wednes-
day ST had known him before he was the French master at Hoddesden--he
came on the Wednesday—I saw this bag on the Thursday, the day he began
to teach us I was in the room every day, he used to keep this bag on the
'sofa in the room-it was a bag exactly like this I have handled it, I used
to take the things out of this bag, and put them on the table for him-he
taught me in his room. I saw the big a good while, while he was there,
Cross-examined. Q. Are you able to say positively whether this is the
bag! 1. It is exactly like it-he had & portmanteau with him I did not
see that he had a small bag with straps he brought his things in wat
he came about 9 o'clock.
COURT. Q. How long did he continue to teach you after the Wednes
day! 4. About a fortnight.

day, 21st, arrived in London. Wednesday, 23rd, went to Thompson

MR LILLEY called to
August MABES (through an interpreter). I am a German. The prisoner
is not a German-I have known him since 1849, or 1850-I was in Paris
on 18th, 19th, or 20th Jan. last I saw the prisoner, and spoke to him-it
was through me that he came to London—I had no particular business with
him-I recollect the time at which he left Paris 11 accompanied him to
the railway station at Paris I had heard him say the evening before that
he wanted to buy himself a travelling pocket for his journey; by a travel-
ling pocket I mean & carpet bag—I offered to lend him my travelling pocket
I lent it him I call this looking at the carpet bag) s travelling pocket-
this is not the one I lent him, it was a similar one to this I took it to the
railway station-I saw the prisoner had several of his packages in his dress-
ing gown doubled up, clasped together—I helped him to put them into the
bag—the bag was not quite full; not over full after the things were put into
the carpet bag, be locked it, and the prisoner himself gave it to the employé
who received it at the railway—the prisoner had my bag, and another beside
it, and a courier's bag round his neck-he registered only one bag, the bag.
