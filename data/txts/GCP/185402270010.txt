356. JOHN WINGFIELD, stealing a sovereign, a shilling, and a six-
pence; the moneys of James Eales, his master.
JAMES EALES. I am a hay dealer. The prisoner was in my employ
rather better than twelve months on 1st Feb. I gave him 91. 28. 10d. and
6d.-91. was to pay for six loads of straw, 28. 10d. to pay the tolls, and 6d.
to get refreshment on the road-this was on Wednesday, about 8 o'clock in
the morning—he ought to have been back about 7 or 8 o'clock in the
evening-he did not return till 5 o'clock the next morning-I asked him
where he had been, and he said the foreman was not at home, and he could
not get loaded before 9 o'clock at night.
Thomas GASKIN. I am foreman to Mr. Morton. On 1st Feb. the pri-
soner came for some straw, about 4 o'clock in the afternoon I was at home
- there was no delay in loading his wagon-he paid me 71. 188. 6d, and
said that was all his master gave him, and his master would send the other
the next time he came-I let him have the straw.
Prisoner. I said I had lost a sovereign. Witness. No; he said that was
all his master gave him.
WILLIAM SAWYER (policeman, S 249). I took the prisoner-he said he
had lost a sovereign, and 18, 6d. he had spent-he said a person named
Wiltshire ought to be taken into custody, not him.
GEORGE CHATHAM. I went with the prisoner part of the way to Rick
mansworth-James Wiltshire was with the prisoner-I went with them to
the Bell public-house-I saw the prisoner change a sovereign to pay for
pot of porter.
