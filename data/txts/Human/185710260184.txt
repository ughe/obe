   MR. BODKIN . Q.  If I understand you, the angry feeling was principally between Mr. Gower and Mr. Robertson? A.  It was by both of them; Mr. Gower first stated that he had opposed him in Scotland, and he should oppose him at his meeting in the Queen's Bench, and when he went up to the Insolvent Debtors' Court—I think Robertson's words to De Salvi were "It is no debt of mine, it is Mr. Brown's," and, "Mr. Brown had your money." CHARLES EDWARD WILDE. On 8th July last, I was a prisoner in the Queen's Bench Prison. About 2 o'clock that day, I observed the prisoner, Mr. Gower, and Mr. Thompson, go up stairs towards Mr. Robertson's room—about five or six minutes after they had gone in, I heard a scuffle, and I saw the prisoner run out of the room, and Mr. Gower after him—they ran along the corridor towards the stairs—I went immediately to Mr. Robert son's room and found him wounded and bleeding, supported by Mr. Thomp son—I then followed the direction that the prisoner and Gower had taken, but did not come up with them—I returned towards Mr. Robertson's room—before I got there, I saw this knife lying down on the ground between Mr. Robertson's room and the stairs to which the prisoner and Gower had run; it is in the same state as it was then, except that there were some marks of blood upon it—I then went down stairs and found the prisoner and Mr. Gower detained—I said to the prisoner, "You are the assassin; your name is Salvi"—he made no answer, but started back in voluntarily.
