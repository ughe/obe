 2006.  NATHANIEL GRIFFITHS was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 1 pair of trousers, value 10s., the goods of Thomas Griffiths ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
 THOMAS GRIFFITHS. I am a    carpenter, and live in Whitmore-road, Hoxton. The prisoner is my son; he has been employed a little at my business, but not much- he lodged in my house; I missed a pair of trousers on the 21st of July, from the room he and his brother slept in - these are them.
 Prisoner. I did not steal them - he lent them me to wear; he murdered my mother; when I came out of the House of Correction, I wanted a little money to go to a place, and he told me to go and steal. Witness. No, I never did - I never lent him the trousers; he might have taken them unknown to me.
   JAMES LEVICK. I live at Hoxton, and am a pawnbroker. I have the trousers, which were pawned by the prisoner on the 21st of July, and on the 28th he came and had another shilling on them; he gave his own name and address.
   WILLIAM EPPS(Police-constable N 63). The prosecutor called me to his house on the 1st of August - I found the prisoner and his brother there; I took charge of them; in going along, I said to the prisoner, that it was a pity - he said he knew he had robbed his father, and he would do so again, for he ought to keep him.
   GEORGE DUNCAN(Police-constable N 59). I was called in; I found five duplicates on the brother, but none on the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I came home from work, and there was nobody at home; I went by Mr. Barrows' beershop, and saw my father in the bar - I then went past again, and Mrs. Barrows said to me, "Nathaniel, your father is asleep in our room;" I then went and took a walk, and when I came back, he was asleep on the step of the door; I shook him, but he did not awake; I went and took a walk again till eleven o'clock - my father had just then gone in doors - I went to go in, but he shoved me out, and shut the door in my face; he is not worthy the name of a father.
   THOMAS GRIFFITHS. It is false; I provided him with clothes as well as I could; I work as a carpenter; he was employed at the saw-mills, but was discharged for getting drunk and keeping bad company; he got all my things almost.
   GEORGE KEMP(Police-constable N 82). I have a certificate of the former conviction of the prisoner, which I got from Mr. Clark's office; I attended the trial, and know he is the man - he was imprisoned three months.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 2007.  JOHN DUIG was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of August , 13 shirts, value 2l.; 6 waistcoats, value 1l.; 2 pairs of trousers, value 1l.; 9 cravats, value 1l., and 1 trunk, value 8s., the goods of Frederick Davey Cooper.
   FREDERICK DAVEY COOPER. I live in Guildford-street, and am a medical student; I send my linen to my father, at Bury St. Edmond's, to be washed. On the 3rd of August I employed a man to fetch my box of linen from the Golden Cross, Charing-cross; he did not return with it, but I met him in Guildford-street the same night, and gave him into custody - it was not the prisoner; he said he had lost the box; it contained all the articles stated, and a letter containing some money.
   THOMAS MOORE. (Police-constable E 16.). I produce some articles, which I got at a pawnbroker's shop - the prisoner took them in, but did not pawn them; I took them from his hat, and one shirt was on his back - this was on the 4th of August - I asked him if the two shirts on the counter were his; he said Yes - I asked if they were marked; he said Yes - I asked him his name, and he said Edward Connelly; as I was taking him, he threw a duplicate behind him, and I found 5s. on him.
   WILLIAM HOW. I am a pawnbroker. I have a shirt, pawned by the prisoner on the 4th of August, in the name of David Conner, for 4s.; it is marked F. D. C.
   JOHN MURPHY(Police-constable C 135). On the 3rd of August, Holmes, who had been sent for the box, came to me, and said he had lost it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been out of work some time - I was told to go to London-bridge, where I should get work: on my way home I picked up these things in Holborn, near Southampton-street - I took them home, and the next morning I pawned one of these shirts, and was going to pawn two more when the officer was sent for.
     NOT GUILTY.
