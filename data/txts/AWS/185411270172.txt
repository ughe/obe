JoHn FLINT SOUTH. I am senior surgeon of St. Thomas's Hospital. The
deceased was brought there, between 4 and 5 o'elock, on 30th Oet.-he was
then very drunk- examined him, and found a wound on the left side of
the nape of the neck, about one inch long a wound on the back of the
blade bone, on the left side of the spine, which was the important wound ;
a deep wound in the left loin ; a superficial skin wound on the outside of
the left knee, and two other superficial wounds-he died from wound of
the lung-the wound between the spine and blade bone penetrated the lung
-I found that afterwards, on a post mortem examination-I have no doubt
that was the cause of death-it is very probable that that wound might
have been inflicted by a person lying below him, by striking round him
over the shoulder-this dagger might produce such a wound.
Cross-eamined. Q. The other wounds were not necessarily mortal 7 4.
No-the penetration of the lung, produced collapse of the lung, and caused.
death.
