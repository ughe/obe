I went and met the prisoner at the Docks, either the same day, or the next—I showed him a parcel containing four butts, eight hogsheads, and four quarter casks—he inquired the price—I referred him to Mr. Ramsbotham—he said the quantity was immaterial—he talked very largely, and, I think, said he was a man of property—he made an appointment to see Mr. Ramsbotham.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Did you hear what passed between Mr. Ramsbotham and him? A.  No; I heard him say the deed in question was worth four or five hundred pounds—I do not know which—it was not three thousand pounds.

On the 11th of September last, I went to St. Katharine's Docks in consequence of an appointment made by my clerk—I saw the prisoner there—he was a stranger to me—he said he had been told that I had some wines on the quay, which would suit his purpose, and he wished me to make a bargain with him for them—he wished to offer me a price for them—he had already seen them with my clerk in the morning—he looked at them again, and said they would suit him—he talked about other wines, saying he wanted to purchase very largely of other qualities—atlast it was decided that he would have these four butts, eight hogsheads, and four quarter-casks, which formed one parcel, and he would call on me next day about it—he told me he was a man of property, but was not much known in the market—that he had lately had cash left him, and he in tended to pay cash for every thing he bought—he called on me next morn ing, between ten and eleven o'clock—the price was then named, and fixed at 17 l . per butt, which would be altogether 170 l .—I asked him for a reference—he told me he always wished to satisfy parties he did business with—that he intended to name a party he had been travelling for, but he had had a dispute with the house, and consequently would not wish to refer me there, but he stated he had lately come into possession of property, which he had received as heir to his father—I told him he had said he in tended it to be a cash transaction—he said he intended to pay cash, but that that day he was short, and he would give me the title-deeds of his property, of which he had lately come into possession, as security for the payment of the money in the course of a few days, and he produced to me this deed—( looking at one )—he particularly pointed out to me this part, that it was to be held by Thomas Trevethen, his father, his heirs and assigns, as absolute property, and stated that the property was waste land at the time the document was written, but it had been built on, and was then worth 500 l ., and in accounting for the indorsement on it, he said that a short time before it had been in the hands of some party for an advance of 200 l ., but as this would only be for a few days, it was not worth while indorsing it again—he depo sited the deed with me as security for the payment—he then asked me for the transfers, as he could not get the wines, or transfer them to any other party before I had transferred them to him—I ultimately gave him the wine warrants, in consequence of the representations he made with respect to this instrument—I had refused to do so before—I parted with them be lieving what he said was true—I have never received any cash since—I had occasion to leave town on the Saturday after I had parted with the warrants, the 15th, and Mr.
