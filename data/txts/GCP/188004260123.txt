Cross-examined by MR. FRITH. I did not give a confident opinion in the
case of Reg. v. Mahew, in which I appeared-I pinned my faith too much
upon the City solicitor, and it turned out to be the clerk's writing-I
gave my opinion as to the writing being the same and afterwards found
out that I was wrong and retracted it I had given a hasty opinion-I
opened my ears instead of my eyes, and that has been a lesson to me ever
since; we are all liable to be mistaken-I do not profess to be infallible,
we all make mistakes, but the best men make the fewest--in this case I
have not relied at all on what Sergeant Littlechild told me I gave my
opinion before I saw him I have compared with him the post-office
order-I gave evidence in Fortescue and Bagshaw, and you cross-examined
me-the evidence I gave against Bagshaw was as strong as I
could give it, and the Jury acquitted him-if he had not been
Loquitted I should not have had to give evidence against him
again I gave evidence in the adjoining Court against a gentleman who
was tried for libel, and Mr. Netherlift was called on the same side, and
the Jury gave a verdict against us--that is not the first time Juries have
done 80-after I had passed a confident opinion, a witness came who said
that he wrote part of the post-card in question-in 25 or 30 years I
cannot always be infallible, but I give my opinion after careful exami-
