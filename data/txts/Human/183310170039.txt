and desired the door should be shut in his face - she applied to Mr. Tufnell to know what she should do; he applied to a lawyer, and brought her home word in my presence, that Madame Follet could not hurt her or call on her for the money, she being a married woman; she must sue her husband; and to treat it with indifference - Madame and Mr. Follet frequently applied, but Mrs. Hampton never would be seen - at another time when Madame Follet called for her bill, Mrs. Hampton told me to tell her to do her worst and her best, and to sue her husband for it, and I told her so - Madame Follet again applied and she desired me to tell her to wait till October, and she would pay her; I told her so, and if Mrs. Hampton had the least suspicion of the receipt being forged, she was in the habit of hearing them apply for their money every week three days out of seven in August, and never would be seen by her - she was in the habit of going to Regent-street, to purchase articles, and passing by their door, and going out of an evening with Mr. Tufnell, in August, and passing Madame Follet's house, where she could have inquired about the receipt - I never received money from her to pay to Madame Follet - the money in question I received was for Mr. Barnes, which I paid him, and brought her the bill and receipt - I have gone with different messages which she has given me for Madame Follet, and she has been in the habit of hearing them give loud knocks at the door, and stating they would have their money, and not leave the house till they got it; and she has desired me to show them into my room, and sent her daughter up and down with messages, when he was in the house, declaring he would not leave the house till he had his money - I have not the least hesitation in saying this is all done to put me out of the way and banish me, to crush the facts I am in possession of concerning the criminal intercourse between them, such as his sleeping with her, her clothing him, and feeding him, and making him presents of money, and diamond pins; also of her lending him at different times, large sums of money - when he has received her money from her agent in the city, he has borrowed large sums from her, which made her unable to pay her debts; and I am in possession of her pawning jewellery, clothes, and different things, even her petticoats, and other things, with pawnbrokers who have from her received authority to take them in - the whole of it is a conspiracy between Mrs. Hampton and Mr. Tufnell to banish me and my evidence - Colonel Hampton is daily and hourly expected in England, in consequence of suspicions of their criminal intercourse, for he has heard of her shameful proceedings with Mr. Tufnell, and they daily expect he will arrive - their principal object is to defraud the tradespeople and throwing all the blame on my head, and to banish my evidence against them.

   WILLIAM THOMAS BARNES. I am a jeweller and silversmith, and live at No. 15, Henrietta-street, Cavendish-square. I knew the prisoner as the servant, of Mrs. Hampton; Mrs Hampton was a customer of mine - at present she is a debtor to the extent of many hundred pounds for jewellery and plate - between the 15th and 22nd of July, I remember the prisoner paying me on two or three occasions, money on account of Mrs. Hampton - on two or three occasions, in the month of July - (refering to his book) - I have an entry on the 17th of July, to credit of Mrs. Hampton, "cash 50l.," and on the 20th of July, "Of cash 60l.," - here is "Mrs. H." with the date on one of the notes I received from Mrs. Hampton; the date looks like "20, 7.," which is July.
