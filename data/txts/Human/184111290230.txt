Before Mr. Recorder .
 283. JEREMIAH DELAY was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of November , 52lbs. weight of lead, value 8 s  ., the goods of James Easton and another; and CHARLES MAYHEW for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen. WILLIAM TOOGOOD TIMEWELL. I am a clerk in the service of James Easton and another, engineers, wheelwrights, and lead merchants, in the Grove, Great Guildford-street, Borough. This piece of lead, now produced, weighing 52lbs., resembles the lead on my master's premises—it has no particular mark, but it is cast in a particular way—the prisoner Delay was employed as a labouring bricklayer on the premises—he had access to the lead—he was desired to remove it out of the way where they were building a boiler—I found it at a marine store-shop in Redcross-street—I was after wards with the officer when he took Mayhew in Castle-street—he said he had taken a piece of lead from Delay, and had sold it in Castle-street, that Delay gave it him in the Grove, Great Guildford-street, and that it was handed out at the door of the factory to him.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did Mayhew say he got it from Delay? A.  Yes—I have no doubt I told the Magistrate of it—I meant to do so if I did not. SILAS HARRIS. I am a blacklead merchant, and live at the Grove, Great Guildford-street. On Monday, the 29th of November, I saw a bricklayer's labourer come out of Mr. Easton's gate with something, which he gave to Mayhew, who was waiting at the side—I followed him to Wright's shop in Redcross-street, and saw it weighed—I afterwards fol lowed Mayhew to his house in Castle-street, and then gave information to the police, and to the prosecutor. MANY ANN WRIGHT. I am the wife of James Wright, a marine-store dealer. The prisoner Mayhew came to my shop with this lump of lead—he said it was his property—I gave him 1 d . a pound for it—that is the price given at our shop for lead—I do not know whether it was new lead—I gave 4 s . 4 d . for it. SOLOMON GARNETT. I am a policeman. I went to Redcross-street, and found the lead, and to Castle-street, and found the prisoner Mayhew—I told him I wanted him for stealing a piece of lead out of the Grove—he told me he had a piece from the Grove, that a man had given him, and he had sold it at a shop in Redcross-street.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who was with you? A.  Mr. Timewell—I asked Mayhew the name of the man who had given it him, and he said be did not know—I did not hear him mention Delay's name. JAMES TIDMARSH. I am a policeman. I took Delay into custody—he denied all knowledge of it, but in going to the police-court, I told him to carry it—he said, "It is not the first time I have carried it—it dropped from a cart in the Grove, and I gave it to Mayhew to sell for me—he gave me 18 d . for my share."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Who was present at the time? A.  Several police-men —he told it to me without my asking.
 (Delay received a good character.)
   DELAY— GUILTY. Aged 40.— Recommended to mercy .— Confined One Month.
   MAYHEW— NOT GUILTY.
