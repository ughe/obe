Joseph Mahon. I am a stationer, carrying on business in Blackman-
street, Borough. I have a warehouse in a stable-yard in Great Suffolk-street,
where I mostly deposit waste paper, done up in bundles and bags, a variety
of millboards and rags- in the years 1839, 1840, and 1841, I had deposited
about thirty tons of large bundles of paper, which I purchased of the Excise-
about a week before Wednesday, the 26th of April, I saw that paper in my
warehouse all safe, except a portion that I had removed myself-I am not in
the habit of selling the paper in the state of that produced-that is the state
in which I buy it-in consequence of information from Oliveira, I went to
the warehouse on the morning of the 27th, (I was not at home on the 26th)
-I missed about seven tons of paper, and very near two tons in bags-I
missed my scale-beams and rope, a quantity of linen rags and millboard—I
had the paper in stacks 'according to their size-there was seven large stacks,
and one or two dwarf ones--there was about three tons and a half gone out
of one stack of one particular size, and others out of different stacks-the
value of the whole seven tons I consider to be about 2001., the bags about
301., averaging 1l. a bag, the rags about 31., and the scales and beam about
21.--I have since been shown by the officers some paper, said to be found in
an unoccupied room in Pamp-court-it was part of the paper I lost-my ware-
house is in the parish of St. George-the-Martyr, Southwark-it is called St.
George.
