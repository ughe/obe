1781. ANN JOY, stealing 1 watch and 2 seals, value 21. 108. 3d.:
goods of Jackson Allen, from his person.
JACKSON ALLEN. I live at Chelsea. On Saturday, 1st September, ale
half-past ten in the morning, I saw the prisoner in Knightsbridge, and went
with her to a public-house-I had a watch in my pocket, which I left at the
public-house, and went and got it again and put it in my fob-pocket-te
prisoner was then with me--we had some gin, and I was not very sober-1
then went with her to a house in Queen's-buildings-I did not get home for
an hour and a half after that, and then I missed my watch, and a little silver
from my waistcoat pocket, and this handkerchief- this is my watch produced.)
GEORGE LAWES. I am assistant to Mr. Attenborough, pawnbroker,
Pimlico. On Saturday afternoon, 1st September, the prisoner, who I know
by sight, came and asked 11. on this silver watch-I asked if it belonged to her
-she said it belonged to her husband who was in trouble--she gave her name
"Ann Leary."
JAMES WEBSTER JONES (policeman, B 59). On Tuesday, 4th September.
I met the prosecutor, and in consequence of what he told me I went to Exeter
buildings and took the prisoner-I asked her where the duplicate was-se
said she had pledged it for 1s. with a woman named Fitzgerald, in the court
- I went there, and she gave it up to me, I went with the prosecutor o
Attenborough's and got the watch.
Prisoner's Defence. I keep a stall; he came to me in the street, gave me
two quarterns of gin, and asked me to go to a house ; he gave me the watch
to raise money on, because he had got none; I afterwards told him I had lost
it, and he wanted me to make an affidavit of it.
JACKSON ALLEN re-examined. I did not give her the watch to raise
money on-I did not ask her to make an affidavit-she does keep a stall-I
was sober enough to know what I was doing, I knew nothing about her
before.
Not GUILTY.
1782. MARY DONOVAN, stealing 1 coat, value 238.; the goods o
Laurence Hyam and another, her masters.
WILLIAM KING. I am shopman to Mr. Laurence Hyam-be has one
partner, and lives at 86, Oxford-street-the prisoner was in his employ about
five weeks, as housemaid-she was the only housemaid-there is a housekeeper
besides—this coat (produced) is Mr. Hyam's property, and has our ticket
on it.
JAMES ALDOUS. I am a pawnbroker, of Berwick-street. On 5th Septem-
ber, about half-past six, the prisoner came and offered this coat in pledge-
seeing the mark on it, I asked how that came-she said Mr. Hyam's house
maid told her to pledge it; if I did not like it I might go and see--I went
there and could not get in- I went again next morning, and recognized the
prisoner as the housemaid-I am quite sure she said the housemaid, not the
housekeeper.
WILLIAM GODFREY (policeman, C 147). I took the prisoner at her mas-
ter's, and asked her if she knew anything of the coat- she said "No"-
the way to the station she said she took it from the room up-stairs.
Prisoner's Defence. The servant boy about the house ordered me to
pledge it.
William King re-examined. There is a servant boy in the house--he
is not here or the housekeeper.
GUILTY. Aged 20.-Confined Three Months.
