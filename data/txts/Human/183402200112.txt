 522.  CATHERINE HICKEY was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of February , 1 pair of breeches, value 1s. 6d.; 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d.; 10 shillings, 2 sixpences, and 3 halfpence, the goods and monies of Timothy Flynn.
   TIMOTHY FLYNN. I am a labourer, and live in Cock-alley- the prisoner lives with my son; but she tells me she is not his wife - on the morning of the 5th of February I was in bed - about two o'clock the policeman came into my house and inquired for my son, Patrick Flynn; but he was not there - the prisoner came into the passage; but she did not come into the room - when the policeman was gone, I went to bed again; but I cannot say whether I locked the door, I was in such a flurry, being ill - about an hour afterwards I heard a footstep, and saw the prisoner in my room - she went over to where my breeches were, and took them, and a silk handkerchief, ten shillings, two sixpences, and three halfpence, which were in my breeches pocket - I got out of bed as well I could, and called, "Stop thief" - the prisoner got out of the room, and I went after her; but I could not take her - I have not seen any of them since; I went to the station the next day and stated what I had lost - when I ran after her, I had nothing on but my shirt - I saw her by the gas-light.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Was there any gaslight in your room? A. No; but one is opposite my window - my shutters were not shut - I did not hear her open the door - I heard her foot - I did not say anything to her till she was going out - I then called, "Stop thief" - she did not give me time to speak to her when she was in the room - I get my living by labour - I travel the country; and if a gentleman asks me to get a bottle of gin or rum for him, I do so - I should not know whisky in this country from water - I have quarrelled with the prisoner - she never put me into the station - I have been there for getting drunk, but not for anything else - I have been at the Two Bells, Whitechapel - the prisoner did not give me into custody for beating her - my wife was in bed with me when the prisoner took my breeches - the officer had disturbed her when he came in before - I do not know whether she was awake when the prisoner came in - I found her awake when I went up again - the prisoner robbed me twice before - I charged her with the robbery at Lambeth-street - my wife took her there; but she forgave her - my son lived with me; but I turned him out, for his behaviour - he lived with the prisoner in Essex-street - when I called after the prisoner, my next door neighbour put her head out of her window and asked what brought me out so late in the morning - I told her that Carroty Kit had robbed me.
 COURT. Q. You know you are on your oath to speak the truth - do you mean to swear that this prisoner came in and took your breeches? A. Yes.
   ANN HINTON. I live next door to Flynn; about three o'clock that morning I heard the alarm of "Stop thief" - I got out of bed, opened the window, and saw Flynn and Hickey - she was going across the ruins as if from Flynn's house - he was standing at the door, and he said, "Carroty Kit has been and robbed me of my breeches, and my money and handkerchief" - he was in his shirt and night-cap - the prisoner had a bundle under her arm.
