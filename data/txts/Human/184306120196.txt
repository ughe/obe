To the best of my be lief, it is mine, and I am positive of it—I do not say that because I think it will make the case stronger—my son is not here—I discharged a man named Lazarus, who used to carry things from my barn. EDWARD MENCE. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Sidney-place, Com mercial-road. On the 20th of May, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, the prisoner was on the road with a cart and two horses—I was up stairs, and was called down by my wife—I looked out at the window, and saw the prisoner in the act of unloading the straw, and there was another helping him—I afterwards came down, and the cart was gone—I found three trusses of straw left at my place—they were afterwards delivered to the po lice-constable—they had not been touched, or done anything to, in the mean time—I had purchased straw of the prisoner before. GEORGE MURRAY. I am a policeman. On the morning of the 20th of May, I saw the prisoner drive a cart with two horses from opposite Mr. Mence's door—I watched him—he went as far as the George—there was another cart just before him—he and the driver of that cart went into the George—I took the names that were on the carts—I afterwards found some straw at Mr. Mence's, and took it to the station—I afterwards apprehended the prisoner—he said the straw he sold to the cheeesemonger he had bought on the road, and he could bring twenty people to prove it. NOT GUILTY.
