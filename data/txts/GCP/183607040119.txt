Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
1756. THOMAS WOOL was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of
Jane, 60 musquash skins, value 108.; and 60 other skins, value 108, the
goods of John Bates; and ROBERT TOOMAX and JOHN WAT,
SON for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been
stolen ; against the Statute, &c.; to which Thomas Wool pleaded
GUILTY. Aged 36.-Transported for Seyen Years
THOMAS BENJAMIN Hunt. I am warehouseman to Mr. John Bates, a
furrier, in Long-lane. I gave out one hundred and thirty-eight dozen and
s half of musquash skins, and five dozen of them were missed these are
the skins, to the best of my knowledge-I missed them on Wednesday
week last, the 22nd of June-Wool was in our employ. YTJ
Thomas Smith. On the 23rd the prisoners, Toomay and Watson,
came to my shop, and offered these skins for sale--I supected they had
been stolen, and questioned them-Toomay said they bought them at
Gravesend in exchange for glass- I called the policeman, and they both ran
sway-I followed them, took them, and gave them in charge--when they
got to the station-house, they said they had them of Wool, who was at the
station-house door.
Uud
Watson's Defence. These two young men came up to me, and asked
where was a furrier's, and I told them.
WILLIAM PAYNE. I am a pipe-maker, and work for Mr. Jupp, in
Wood-street. I was with him last Wednesday week-I have been since
working for Mr. Jackson, in Union-street, at making pipes - Thomas
Gold and John Collins work there with me-I was with Watson, on Wed-
nesday week, the 23rd, in the country-he came from Buntingford--we
went there for the good of our health, and to sell ornaments for fire-stoves
-We came to London the same day, and got here between eight and nine
o'clock in the evening-I was with Watson when Toomay came up to us
in Union-street, Southwark, with the skins in his hand, and asked if we
knew where to sell some skins-he said were they hare skins or rabbit
skins, because he understood them-he said no, he did not know what
they were-Watson said he knew one person who might buy them-he
went with him that was on Thursday morning between nine and ten
o'clock-I did not see Wool there I left Watson and Toomay at the
corner, and went to my own work.
Toomay. I did not know they were stolen-I was to get a shilling
for selling them-I received them from Wool.
TOOMAY-GUILTY. Aged 24.--Confined Three Months.
WATSON-NOT GUILTY.

Sixth Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
1757. HENRY TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of
June, 30lbs. weight of lead, value 6s., the goods of John Foord, his
master.
CHARLES HENRY FALCONER. I am a Thames police surveyor. On
the 24th of June, I was near a brewery which is building on the Surrey
side of the river observed the prisoner, and another man leave the
roof of that brewery, where there had been some leaden gutters laid-the
prisoner came down a ladder-I went to the gate, and saw him go on to
Lambeth Palace-I then stopped him, and took him back to Waterloo-
