I found a quantity of extravasated blood which had escaped from a ruptured vessel beneath the left lobe of the brain—that blood had coagulated and caused the pressure, paralysis, and death—in my judgment, that was caused by violence—the clot of blood under the brain was just above the orifice of the eye—I think such a blow and fall as she is described to have received. would be sufficient to produce death—I found the liver considerably harder than natural, and its structure was diseased—I should say that the deceased was evidently a woman of intemperate habits.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were fetched by the prisoner himself? A.  I was—he appeared to be very anxious about his wife, and evinced the kindest manner and most attentive conduct—a fall would have produced the injuries I saw—I should infer that the rupture was very slight—I have seen drink ing produce paralysis; but then it alters very materially the structure of the brain; and this was not so altered.  1130. SARAH SMITH  (23), was indicted for unlawfully uttering coun terfeit coin.
   MESSRS. CLERK . and W. J. PAYNE . conducted the Prosecution. MYRA THORNE. I am servant to Mr. Putley, a butcher, of High Street, Borough. On 2nd Sept the prisoner came about 9 o'clock in the morning for a pound of steak; I did not serve her, but she paid me with a half crown, which I looked at, and gave to Mr. Putley, who sounded it, and gave it back to me; I put it into the bowl with some more silver and one half crown at the bottom—I put this half crown on the top of the silver—I gave her change before I put the half crown in the bowl; she left; and directly she had gone, I took the half crown out of the bowl and gave it to Mr. Putley.
   Prisoner. Q.  Did not he say that it was good? A.  He sounded it, and said, "That will do"—I did not put it into the bowl before I gave you the change—I have seen you once or twice before dealing at the shop. GEORGE PUTLEY. I am a butcher, of High Street, Borough.
