On 8th March, about half past 12 o'clock in the evening, I took the prisoner—she was given in my charge by the prosecutor—a person followed us to the station, brought a phial, and said, in the prisoner's presence, that he saw her drop it—this is it ( produced )—the prisoner said it contained vitriol. NATHANIEL BRADFORD PIERPOINT. I am a surgeon, and live at No. 23, Little Pulteney-street, Golden-square. On Monday, 7th March, the prisoner came to my shop to purchase some oil of vitriol—I asked her if she was aware it was poison, and for what purpose she wanted it—she said she did not know it was poison, but wanted it for the purpose of cleaning a fender she had purchased, to get the rust off—I supplied her with six drachms of diluted sulphuric acid—I said, "I will write on the label, 'Vitriol—poison;' be careful of it, because if it is taken it will injure any person, or if it is thrown on anybody it will injure them;" I did not mean thrown , I meant spilt  —I cannot say that this produced is the phial, but the label is the same that I put on, and this phial would contain that quantity—I examined it at the police court, and there was a very slight portion of liquid remaining in it, which I ascertained to be an acid, and I should imagine it to be diluted sulphuric acid—the burns on these gloves would arise from the sprinkling of diluted sulphuric acid upon them—almost any acid would produce them.
