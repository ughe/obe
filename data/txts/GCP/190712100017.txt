To Mr. Black. I cannot think of all the dates, but my memory
as to times and places is accurate.
EDWARD THOMAS BRIEWACK, agent employed by the Music Pub-
lishers' Association. I have known Kidd about four years. On
Beptember 3 of this year I had some conversation with him. We
were speaking of pirated music and piracy generally, and he onid
that he had given it up and was selling fruit in the market in Chapel
Street instead. He then said he did not think much of the Musical
Copyright Act of 1906, and if he was single he would risk a month
and do it easily if they caught him. He then referred to the con
vietion of Willetts at the Old Bailey in 1906, and said that, consider
ing the money he had made, nine months was nothing. Also, he
said that he did not think the sale of pirated musio would ever be
stopped with the penalties now in foroe, as a month to a pirate did
not mean much, and if caught a second time he could always give
a false name and address and avoid the heavier penalty for second
conviction. The penalty is £5 or one month for a first conviotion, and
wo months for a second conviction, without the option of a fine. So
far as I know, Kidd did not know that I was in the service of the
association. On September -6 I saw Walsh with a sack filled with
parcels in Pentonville Road, about 3.15 p.m. He took the parcels!
into Myers' receiving office and came out with the sack empty. He
then went to the corner of Upper Street and Liverpool Road and
joined Kidd, who was having his boots cleaned. On September 13
