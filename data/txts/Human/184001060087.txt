MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution . JANE WELLS. I am the wife of John Wells, a pawnbroker, in London, and live in Upper Phillimore-place, Kensington. On Monday, the 23rd of December, between two and three o'clock in the afternoon, I was with my husband in Piccadilly—I had received a cheque from him, and I had in exchange for it at the bankers a 30 l . note, and I had 9 l . 10 s .in gold, and eight shillings and two sixpences in silver—the gold consisted of so vereigns and half-sovereigns—I placed the note with the gold in my purse in the front part of my pocket, which hung by my side—it forms a part of my under petticoat—there is a pocket-hole in the pocket, and also in my top petticoat, which is over the petticoat of which the pocket forms a part—I had the same gown on as I have now—it has a pocket-hole in it near the opening of my petticoat, but rather more forward—I had a large shawl on—it was a dirty day—I got into Hardwick's omnibus—I went with my husband from the bankers to Piccadilly—the omnibus overtook us—we required seats, and there was only one—I got in, and I was told my hus band might be accommodated if he would walk on to Bond-street—when I got in the two prisoners were there—there was a vacancy between them, which I occupied—I rode on to Bond-street—two persons there got out, and my husband was taken up at the White Horse Cellar, and also a lad—my husband made an observation, and said, "Am I to sit here?
