Guilty.-Confined Nine Months.
Before Edward Bullock, Esq.
169. JOHN GABRIEL was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of Nov.,
1 Waistcoat, value 128.; and 1 pair of trowsers, 168.; the goods of James
Stevens, in a vessel on the navigable river Thames.
JAMES STEVENS. I am a seaman on board the Dedalus-she is now
gone to sea, but she was lying at Woolwich. I bad a jacket and trowsers
on board the Hebe, which was lying off the Dock-yard-these are them-
being on shore I did not go on board to put my things up, but to the best
of my knowledge I saw these things safe on the Friday, as they were gone
on the Saturday- I know the prisoner well-I took him on suspicion be-
fore I knew he had taken the things—he had been a messmate in the same
berth with me in the Hebe.
TO
DE
Cross-examined by MR. WILDE. Q. How many other messmates were
there? 4. Ten-several persons came on board the vessel occasionally
- there were two came to visit me, and had tea-I kept my bag sometimes
in the mess, and sometimes I tied it to the stanchion, to keep it from the
dust- I took the prisoner on suspicion-I went up before the Magistrate
-- he was dismissed, but sent a prisoner on board the ship-he was under
the sentry's charge-I was not on board the vessel when Myers came on
board- I was in the Dock-yard at work--I did not see him—there were
two females who visited me--they were not in the habit of visiting me
frequently, but they came once or twice, and they came on the Monday
previous, but I lost things out of my bag on the Monday- I lost several
other things--I am married, and have four children-the names of the two
females who visited me are Mrs. White and Mrs. Watts—they are both
married-the husband of one of them is at sea, and the other is a caulker
in the Dock-yard-I gave an account of this before the Magistrate, and
signed my name to some paper-this is my name to this deposition-this
was read over to me before I put my name to it-to the best of my know-
ledge I stated that I had seen these things on the Friday morning or Friday
evening, but there are other things, which have not been found yet-these
are only a part of the things—the prisoner and I have had no quarrel —
we have been the best of friends-I know Sheen, a Thames police-officer
- he went with me to a Jew's house to buy a pair of trowsers- I have
seen Sheen to-day.
Q: Have you ever made use of words to this effect," that if the prisoner
got off you would give him a slick that would send him to Guy's!” A. I said
I would send bim below, or something to that effect-I might have used the
word Guy's I said I should like to have him on board the ship again-
he came on shore with me on Saturday, the 16th, at twenty or twenty-five
minutes after six o'clock-he was in my company-I left him then, and
after that I went to a public-house, wbere he promised to meet me in a
quarter of an hour I went there and he was gone-I then went down to
the Nile, and sat some time-he did not come, and I thought there was a
screw loose somewhere-he had been on board of the vessel at work with
the rope maker, and I had been on shore at work in the Dock-yard-he
