Q. During the time that this opposition was going on, did you see Cow-
deroy drive ? A. Yes, several times-he drove his own bus, and his con-
duct was as much against me as could be to prevent my getting passen-
gers--I have seen Newell conducting his own bus, and his conduct to me
has been the same as the others, trying to get the passengers into his own
bos--when I have come in their time, or before their time, they would
hurry on to catch me, and then keep with me they would run over any
other man's time to keep up with me it was Newell's that followed me
out of the road.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Did Richard Bird, Thomas
Bird, Cowderoy, or Johnson, ever drive out of their road against you !
A. No-they are proprietors, having their own times--when I drove in
their times, they ran with me; and they have come out with me, and
stopped beyond their own time-I never saw either of them conduct or
drive the "Albion" or the "Emperor," but they have cheered them on to
go on against me it was the “ Albion” and the “Emperor" which fol-
lowed me wherever I went-I have played at skittles once or twice with
the men-I have played at skittles at the Royal Oak, with John Sylvester
and Brush, that is Smith-I have played five or ten minutes--I have been
there while the game was going on I will swear I have not played
half-a-dozen games- I have dined with Sylvester and Smith, and drank
together at dinner-I went to Wales' tea-gardens, at Bayswater they
followed me there, and I think I had one game at skittles- there were
seven or eight besides them; that was after business I went there for
pleasure, and to see if they would follow me we played together, and I
think drank together-I do not recollect being at any other places with
them-I have drank wine with Mr. Trevett, close to his stable, at Mr.
Gamble's—it was some time in June - I went down there, and Mr. Trevett
asked me to take a glass of wine with him--he asked me to treat his men
-he treated them with some, and I treated them with some,
Q. That was while all this wickedness was going on A. It was at
the beginning of it-I did not hear Trevett on that occasion complain that
my father had set up an omnibus in opposition, when he knew that the
times were filled up I did not hear any one say so my father has no
partner-he never had one-he never drove an omnibus, but he has one
now, which he bought about a fortnight after he began, at the time he was
so much annoyed by the other two- I do not know exactly who he bought
it of-I believe he had seen Mr. Norton several times before he bought it
"I cannot say who he bought it of-he got the money by earning it--
