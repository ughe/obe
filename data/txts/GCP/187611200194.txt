ELIZA COOK, I am a widow, and live at 1, Robert Street, Brighton-
on 8th July, the prisoner hired my room for two nights, and came into it
about 10 o'clock that evening-I heard her about very early in the morning
which caused me to get up, and I saw her go out about 6 o'clock and cross
the road, and turn up North Road; that is in the direction of Preston-
she turned the corner, and I did not see her again till I saw her at the
Town Hall at 9 o'clock, a policeman came and told me something, in con-
sequence of which I went into the room and examined the bed, and was
fully convinced that a birth had taken place there, as I saw what is usual
on such occasions --I am able to say that from my own knowledge.
Cross-examined. There was no appearance of concealment in the room
more than decency required, I did not notice that the prisoner brought
anything with her when she came.
DAVID RICHARDS. I am a surgeon to the work house infirmany, Brighton
-on the morning of 9th July, the prisoner was admitted to the lying in
ward, at about 8.30-I examined her and found that she had been recently
delivered, but it was not complete; the child had been born, but the after
birth had not been taken away-she said nothing at that time-I examined
the child and found that the string had been cut, about a foot from the body
and there was a mark round the neck, evidently produced by some string or
tape; it was a narrow mark-the tape is not here: it was cut off-the neck
was reddened as if from the pressuro of a piece of string or tape; the skin
was broken, and it took about a fortnight to heal-as far as I could judge
that wound was caused by the pressure of a string.
