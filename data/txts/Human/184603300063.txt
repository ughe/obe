   MR. BALLANTINB . Q.  You were with your brother for the sake of get ting him a situation? A.  O dear, no, he was going to market with me—he came to my house—I have passed by Cock-lane, but I was never in it in my life—my brother is married, and I have understood he has lived there a few weeks—I have never been to see him—he has been out of employ a month Or two.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  He lived on the second floor in Cock-lane? A.  I do not know—I have known him living there a few months—I have seen Fidler and my brother together before that time—when I had spoken to Fidler I quitted the van, and went about my business.

 880. THOMAS PAYNE was indicted for stealing 1 handkerchief, value 3 s  ., the goods of Thomas Whinney , from his person; and that he had been before convicted of felony. THOMAS WHINNEY. I live in Shepherd's-market—on the 28th March, a little before twelve o'clock, I was in Bridges-street, by the theatre—the of ficer spoke to me, and I missed my handkerchief from my pocket—this is it—I had had it a minute or two before.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  You did not see the prisoner? A.  No, I did not see other persons about. WILLIAM WEST( police-constable F  106.) I was in Bridges-street, and saw the prisoner take this handkerchief from Mr. Whinney's pocket—I had watched him a few minutes—I saw him at the corner of the street and fol lowed him—when he took the handkerchief I took hold of him—he threw it on the ground, and I took it up and took it to Mr. Whinney.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was there any other person saw this beside youself? A.  Not that I am aware of—there was one person behind the prisoner. JOHN WHITE( police-constable R  180.) I produce a certificate of the pri soner's former conviction at Clerkcnwell—( read—Convicted 5th Nov., 1844, and confined six months, six weeks solitary )—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY.— Aged 17.— Transported for Ten Years .
