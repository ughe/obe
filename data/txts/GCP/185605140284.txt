THOMAS GOWLAND. I am a silversmith and jeweller, I live in Leaden-
hall-streets on the afternoon of 5th May, the two prisoners came hastily
into my passage, and I went to the door of my shop immediately a crowd
came round—the lady came up with the policeman-the prisoners were
brought further into the passage to the side door of the house - I begged
the policeman to keep the people back, and said he had better bring the
lady farther in-they went in, and I heard the prisoners urging the lady to
take the purse back again I saw Jones put her hand towards the
prosecutrix with something in it-she appeared to me to pass it to the
prosecutrix--the prosecutrix said they had given her the purse back again
-the prisoners could hear that.

Cross-examined by MR. RIETON. Q. Were you in the shop A. No,
in the passage, I went from the shop to the passage--I saw something in
one of the prisoners' hands-I could not tell exactly what it was it was
about the size of the purse-I saw the left hand closed and something in it-
I understood from what was said, that they were urging her to take it
back-I immediately told the policeman-I cannot tell positively what was
said, but I concluded they were urging her to take it back, by their words
and actions before forming that conclusion, I heard the evidence before
the Magistrate-there were a few persons outside, I got the policeman to
keep them back from the passage--it is the passage leading to my side door
--my window goes up the passage, it forms a lobby to my shop.
