 FIFTH DAY. TUESDAY, DECEMBER 11.
 Third London Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 218.  JOHN MEDENS was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of December , 2 pieces of striped cotton, containing 84 yards, value 42s., the goods of James Morrison and others.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN WILLIAMS. I am warehouseman to James Morrison and Co., silk-mercers, of Fore-street, Cripplegate; the prisoner, who is in the employ of Mr. Bateman, a gasworker, came to our premises on the 4th of December, he went into a cellar which we keep as a warehouse; knowing he had no business there at that time, I ran by another staircase into the same cellar; I stood about thirty feet from him, and saw him put something into a basket- he covered it over with paper, looked about him, and went up again, by the same stairs; he went across the warehouse and into the shop; when he got near the door I collared him, and asked what he had got; he threw down the basket and struggled, but I held him and gave him in charge; I then examined the basket, and found this piece of striped cotton, which is my employers'; it is marked, and I know the pattern.
   JAMES LOVELL. I am a Police-officer. I took the prisoner, and produce the property.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Transported for Seven Years.
 219.  WILLIAM LAYBORN, alias    LABAN, was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of November , 21 yards of galloon, value 2s., the goods of James Morrison and others.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
   ROBERT LUSHER. I am in the employ of Messrs. James Morrison and Co.; the prisoner came to our shop on the 16th of November; he had been served by another person, but I saw him put a piece of ribbon under the cuff of his coat; I spoke to Smith, who came and stood by me, while the prisoner paid for what he had bought; he then requested him to turn his cuff up, which he did, and this ribbon fell on the counter.
   HENRY JOHN SMITH. I am the managing person in the shop, I was called, and desired the prisoner to turn up his cuff, and this piece of ribbon fell down.(Property produced and sworn to.)

 120.  SARAH NEWBY and JANE BRACKERS were indicted for stealing, on the 29th of October , 1 purse, value 2s. 6d.; 1 watch, value 10l.; 2 seals, value 2l.; 1 watch-chain, value 2s. 6d.; 1 watch-key, value 1s.; 2 sovereigns, 2 shillings, and 1 promissory note, for payment of and value 30s., the property of Mark Rudkin , from his person.
 MARK RUDKIN. On the 30th of October, about two o'clock in the morning, I was at the entrance of a court in  Fleet-street, and the two prisoners were with me for about a quarter of an hour; in a short time I missed my watch, and accused them of taking it - I did not feel it go before I missed it - (I thought I perceived Bracker's hand near my pocket; put my hand there, and found my watch and purse were safe - I cannot recollect whether I took hold of her hand or not then;) I accused them of taking it, and Newby walked away, but Brackers stopped, and said, "Here is your watch - you have dropped it, I have found it;" she gave it me, and went away out of sight; I then missed my purse; I told the next watchman of it- they were taken soon after, about a quarter of a mile off; I am satisfied, in my own mind, that I had my purse safe when I saw the prisoner's hand on me - I felt it in my pocket.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where had you spent the evening? A. I had dined with a friend between five and six o'clock - I had not been drinking from then till one o'clock; I was tipsy; I did not accuse any one else of this; I recollect I did stop with other females - I cannot say how many, perhaps, two or three - matters might occur that I cannot bring to my recollection - I did not go into any house; the purse was a crimson figured one - I never described it as a long green purse - it was said by the officer, at the watch-house, that I described it so; he was sober; if I did describe it so, it must have been from my state of confusion at the time; but I do not see how I could describe it so.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Were you also accused of having said there were 5s. in the purse? A. I do not recollect; I did not afterwards say there were 10s. in it, to my knowledge; I said there was some small change- I did not say any thing about the loss of my watch - it was a gold watch; I felt Bracker's hand about my person- Newby was away from me at that time.
 COURT. Q. At what time can you swear you had your purse, and knew what was in it? A. About ten o'clock; I was then at my friend's house, and paid a bill; there were then two sovereigns, a 30s. note, and some small change in it - it was a crimson purse; I was not up a court with any body but the prisoners; I had been to an auctionshop, and left it about twelve o'clock.
