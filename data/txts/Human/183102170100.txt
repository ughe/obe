 582.  CHARLES STRIKSON was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of January , 1 coat, value 1l., the goods of Thomas Wilson.
 THOMAS WILSON. I live in Hoxton Old-town. On the 12th of January, I had just driven my gig up to a stable, facing  Shoreditch church, about half-past eleven o'clock at night; I got out of the gig, had just rung the bell, and on turning round I saw the prisoner take the coat from the side of my gig - he pulled it off, hung it on his arm, and was walking off with it, away from me; I went up to him, and said, "What do you do with that coat, you scoundrel?" the Policeman came, and took him.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What are you? A. A publican; I keep the Plough - I did not perceive that the prisoner was tipsy; he said it was a lark, or something of that kind - he had got about two yards from the gig.
   JOHN MASTERS. I was with the prosecutor - I got out of the gig first; I saw the prisoner take the coat - he was walking away with it; he did not appear to be tipsy.
   THOMAS HURRAN. I am the officer. I took the prisoner and the coat.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating that he was intoxicated; and, in the act of falling down, had caught hold of the coat to save himself, when he was taken.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Transported for Seven Years.
 583.  NATHAN SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of January , 1 half-sovereign, 2 half-crowns, 1 pair of gloves, value 1s. 6d., and 1 handkerchief, value 1s. 6d., the property of John Hopkins , his master.
 JOHN HOPKINS. I live in  Shoreditch, and am a linen-draper; the prisoner was my porter- he slept in the shop with another person. On the night of the 21st of January we found we had been robbed of 16s.; and on the Saturday night following I marked a quantity of money, and left it in the desk - on the Sunday morning I went to the desk, and missed one half-sovereign, two half-crowns, and four shillings in silver; I had marked the silver, but not the half-sovereign - I sent for an officer, and had the two porters examined, up stairs; he found on the prisoner this half-sovereign, and the two marked half-crowns.
   THOMAS GARTON. I am an officer. I found on the prisoner the half-sovereign and two half-crowns, which the prosecutor spoke to, and 1l. 2s. 6d. besides.
   JAMES SIMMONDS. I produce these gloves and handkerchief, and the key of the box, which were found in the prisoner's trunks.
 MR. HOPKINS. These gloves and handkerchief were taken from the prisoner's trunk, up stairs, in the dressing-room; the prisoner said they were mine, but I have no mark on them - these half-crowns have my mark on them, and the half-sovereign had a little mark at the end of the A, which I saw - I did not put any mark on it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did not the prisoner say, "This handkerchief is mine?" A. No. he said, "It is yours, Sir;" I had not left any sovereign in the desk - the cashier, generally, takes out all the money at eight o'clock; he did so that night, but I was not there - I put the money into the desk, between eleven and twelve; the prisoner might have access to the desk, but he had no business there - he did not say, in my presence, that he had put a sovereign in, and taken the change out - I had a very good character with him.
 JURY. Q. Was it possible for him to have put a sovereign into the desk? A. I counted all that the desk contained when I marked the money; the sum total was about 2l. 15s., and from that 17s. was abstracted.
 The prisoner received an excellent character.
 GUILTY. Aged 30. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s., and Discharged.
