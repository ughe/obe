 (Witnesses for the Defence.)
   ELIZA WATTS. I am the prisoner's daughter. When the boy came to offer the clock, my mother asked him where he got it - he said he picked it up by the Marshgate; she said if he had stolen it from any body, she would have nothing to do with it - she asked him that several times; she said clocks were plentiful, and cheap enough, but she would not mind giving him 3 s. for it - she was very ill at the time, and could not get up - the other boy did not come in. I saw him at the door, but my mother did not turn him out - I will swear that - it was the boy's uncle who sent for the porter - it was my mother's 6 d. which she put upon the table to pay for some cheese for supper; my mother did not say to them, that if 2 l. would do, she would give it them. Sullivan said he would make it up for 5 l. My mother did not offer a sovereign and 10 s., she had not a sovereign about her. I heard of the man kissing my mother, and wanting to sleep with her, and I was quite shocked at it. I do not know what my mother usually buys. I was at home at the time. I had left my service at a public-house, and was at home, but I do not know what became of the clock.
   ALEXANDER WATTS. I am thirteen years of age. I am the prisoner's son-in-law. The boy came in with the clock, and asked 12 s. for it; my mother asked where he got it; he said he picked it up at the Marsh-gate, at the steps of a door; my mother said clocks were very cheap, and said she would give him 3 s. for it. I then went out, but was at home in the evening, when Sullivan and Welsh came. I did not say that if 2 l. would do to make it up it should be given - I did not see my mother take out her purse, nor hear her say that it would be at home the next morning; she said it would be at home soon; and she did not offer to treat them with porter; Sullivan sent for it, and put the penny into his pocket; I did not hear the man say "I will take no money - I will have my clock;" but Welsh said he would take 5 l. to make it up with Sullivan.
     GUILTY. Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1511.  RICHARD WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , five books, value 5 s., the goods of Alexander Black and others, his partners.
   PETER PICKTON. I am in the employ of Alexander Black; he has two other partners - he is a bookseller, and lives in Tavistock-street, Covent-garden. The prisoner was porter there; I missed some books from time to time, and on the 10th of September, at seven o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner with an octavo volume under his coat, coming down stairs - he then went into another kitchen - I then went into the front kitchen, where he went first, and searched among some shavings, and found this octavo volume.
 ALEXANDER BLACK. I have two partners. This book is one that was found at a house (the Welch Harp) where the prisoner said he had left it; I was present when it was brought back by the clerk; the prisoner told me that he had taken it on the day before.
   WILLIAM FENNER. I am a pawnbroker. I have three books pawned, I believe, by the prisoner, on the 1st of September.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Confined Three Months and Whipped.
 1512.  BRIDGET RODWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of August , two pairs of stockings, value 3 s., the goods of Mary Blackburn, widow.
 MARY BLACKBURN. I am a widow and live at  Clerkenwell. The prisoner had washed for me five weeks - I missed several things, and, on the 17th of August, I missed these two pairs of stockings; I then sent for the officer.
   WILLIAM JORDAN. On the 17th of August, Blackburn sent for me; I went to the prisoner's lodgings, and asked her about them - she denied any knowledge of them - I found them wet in her pocket.
     GUILTY. Aged 33. Confined Two Months.
 1413.  GEORGE BARTON and JOHN RAVENOR were indicted for burglariously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Sophia Isaacson , widow , about one o'clock in the night of the 4th of August at St. Anne , with intent to steal, and stealing therein, a spoon value 8 s., the goods of George Ferriman , six spoons, value 12 s.; a tea-pot, value 2 s; a jug, value 1 s., and an apron, value 6 d., the goods of the said Sophia Isaacson; two shoes, value 2 s., and a desk, value 1 l., the goods of Mary Roat ; two boots, value 15 s., the goods of Nicholas Fairless , and a shirt, value 5 s., the goods of Thomas Matthews.
 SOPHIA ISAACSON. I am a widow. I live in  Canton-place, East India-road, in the parish of St. Anne, Limehouse. I believe I was up last on the night of the 3d of August - I was as late as a quarter before twelve o'clock; the house was then secure every where, and my property was safe. My servant was up first next morning; one of my lodgers came and told me that my house had been broken open in the night. I came down and found the back door leading from the garden broken open - I believe it had been secure when I went to bed. I missed some spoons, some knives and forks, a tea-pot, a jug, and an apron, of my own property; two shoes and a desk, the servant's property, and two boots of Mr. Fairless, and one shirt of Mr. Matthews - that part of the property which was not my own, I do not know the value of. I afterwards saw the spoons shewn to me by the officers. I never saw either of the prisoners till they were before the Magistrate.
 MARY ROAT. I am the prosecutrix's  servant. I got up about six o'clock in the morning on the 4th of August; it was quite light. I cannot say how the back door had been opened - it had been fastened with bolts the night before, when I went to bed; but it was open when I got up - the front kitchen shutters were open, and one of the windows thrown up. I missed the property which my mistress has described. I did not find any phosphorous, but found some matches which were not there when I went to bed - they appeared to have been lighted - some of them I swept up, and others I have taken care of. I am sure I fastened all the doors when I went to bed - the desk which I lost was worth about 1 l.
