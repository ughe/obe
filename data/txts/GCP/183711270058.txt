Thomas Cow LING. I live at Dagenham, in Essex, and am a farmer.
On the 23rd of September the prisoner was employed by me as a porter
and salesman-Richard Lee was then my servant-he is not so now-the
prisoner used to sell for me till I came to the market-I sent the prisoner
that morning 20}cwt. of potatoes, and after we had done selling, "Well,
Bill,” says I, "you had 20fcwt. this morning"_"No, master,” says he,
"I only received 14fcwt."- I said, " This is strange, when I sent 20 cwt.'
the other 6cwt, would weigh about 670lbs.-- he did not pay for that he
accounted for the rest-1 went home and spoke to Lee-I gave Lee 20 cwt.
to give him, and he accounted for the rest.
Cross-examined by MR. PRICE. Q. You are a very large potato mer.
chant? A. Middling- sell perhaps betwixt one and two hundred tons in a
year-I have known the prisoner about four months--my son is not here-
he was before the Magistrate, but he never spoke a word he had trans-
acted business for me till September, but he had nothing to do with this
I was not present when the prisoner received these potatoes--my son often
sent potatoes to the prisoner previous to the 1st of September, but he was
always up the same day-he takes a very active part in the business-he
has not received any profits since September, to my knowledge-Lee was a
carman of mine I have more carmen-West is the man that took them
up out of the ground-the prisoner is not a servant of mine, but an inde-
pendent porter and salesman--when the potatoes are delivered to the pri-
soner I have done with them, but I am to have the money-as soon as I
come I take the sale-I delivered them to him to sell part of them
-when they were not sold we left them in the market, in the prisoner's
charge, to be sold the next market day-when I deliver a quantity of potatoes
to the prisoner I expect they may be all sold-I am generally there before
all sold-I was up by six o'clock in the morning on this day, and they are
about 10cwt. I should think were sold-I settled with him about eleven
o'clock- I lost my weights the same week-I think it was before the po-
tatoes were sent-my son is very busy, and we did not know he was
wanted-he is well acquainted with the prisoner--the prisoner was not a
servant of mine he had the entire control of this property in his care to
dispose of-Irrever could find any difference between the weight of a
bushel of large and small potatoes.
Court. Q. How do you know it was 20 cwt ? A. We took them
by the measure-it could not vary 201bs.-- it is not possible we could be
deficient of 6cwt.-when I send them they are sent to the prisoner to sell
for me; and when he he has sold them they are no longer my property,
but I look for the money.
RICHARD LEE. I am in the prosecutor's employ. On the 23rd of Sep-
tember I went up with a load of potatoes-I had five sacks-each sack
contained 2cwt., and twenty-one sieves, with 1cwt. in each-I delivered
all I received at home to the prisoner, except three sacks which he had
nothing to do with.
Cross-examined. Q. Who do you receive the potatoes from ? A. James
West-I had three sacks beside-I delivered one to Mr. Topps-I cannit
think of the names of the others--I delivered them where my master told
me--I know the house, but I do not recollect the name I do not know
whether the prisoner is in a large way of business--I do not know whether
the stock of potatoes he had about hiin was large - I did not see any other
people's potatocs delivered while I was there.
