117. WILLIAM SULLIVAN was indicted for a like offence.
MESSRs. Ellis and BODKIN conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM WILKINSON. I produce an examined copy of the prisoner's pre-
vious convietion-(Read: William Sullivan, Convicted at this Court, in
Feb., 1856, of uttering counterfeit coin: Confined nine months") I was an
efficer then, and was present the prisoner is the person. I
SARAH CHESSMAN. I at shopwoman to Mr. Mapleden, who keeps a
pie shop in Shoreditch. On 18th Nov. I saw the prisoner in the shop,
about half past 8 o'clock in the evening—he asked for a penny pie, I served
him, he gave me a half crown-I gave him change, and he went out. I put
the half crown in the till there was only one other half crown there, which
I had taken of a female a few minutes before I left them both in the till
till my master came home, and gave them to hit I did not mark them-
on the 21st I saw the prisoner again I knew him at once when he came
into the shop, he asked for a penny pie, and gave me a half crown- I went
to my master, and gave it him, and told him it was the same man that had
come on the Tuesday evening.

WILLIAM WEBSTER. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint. These
two half crowns, passed on the 18th, are bad—they are of the reign of
George III, 1819, and are the same date, and from the same mould the
one passed on the 21st is also bad, it is of William IV., 1836.
Prisoner. I was not in London on the 18th, nor within ten miles of it.
SARAH CHESSMAN re-examined. I am quite sure he is the person that
passed the half crown on the Tuesday.
Prisoner. You could hardly pick me out in the Court till I was pointed
out. Witness. No, I picked you out from others I found no difficulty in
it at all I knew you directly, the others all appeared shorter than you.
GUILTY.-Four Years Penal Servitude.
