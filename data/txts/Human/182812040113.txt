 227.  WILLIAM PERRIN, WILLIAM TURNER, and JAMES JOHNSON, were indicted for feloniously ripping and cutting, on the 2d of November , 2 cwt. of lead, value 2l., the goods of Samuel Brooks , and fixed to an unfinished house.
 THREE OTHER COUNTS, varying the manner of stating the charge.
 MR. SMITH conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN LEE. I was employed as private-watchman on the premises of Mr. Brooks, in St. Paul's-road, Ball's-pond, Islington. On the morning of the 2d of November I was on duty, and about five minutes before two o'clock I heard something - I went up against the houses which are building by Mr. Simmons, I heard a chopping of lead on the top of the house, and something falling at the back of the premises - I got some other watchmen, we surrounded the house; I proceeded to the back, where I saw three or four men standing over this lead, folding it up, some of them were stooping down - I caught Perrin, who was one of them - he staid with me a quarter of an hour, and then made a great resistance to getaway, but another watchman came up and he was secured, all the rest got away - two or three of them went inside the building; I was not able to discern who they were; I afterwards saw Johnson and Turner brought out of the house in custody; I have compared this lead with the roof, and it fitted exactly to the width of the gutter - I saw the end of the gutter the day before, but I did not go on the roof; I did not go up again till the 5th of November, and then I fitted the lead - there are five pieces of it.
 Cross-examined by MR. BODKIN. Q. You compared the width of the lead? A. Yes; these houses were building by Mr. Simmons or Mr. Brooks, I cannot tell which - I believe Turner has been employed in the buildings; I never saw the other two; Perrin said they were taking away the lead by Mr. Simmons' order -I had seen the prisoner at a public-house at eleven o'clock; they asked a boy, who was with me, to go in and drink.
   JONATHAN HICKS. I know the premises in question -I saw these houses on the Saturday before, but was not on the top till the morning the lead was taken.
   HENRY KINGSBURY. I am a watchman. I was called to assist Lee - I went into the back part of the premises, and saw three persons standing over the lead; one man got away, and two got into a house - I followed them into the house, which was unfinished, and when I came back from the watch-house I took Turner into custody.
   DAVID RICHARDS. I went on the top of the premises about three o'clock, I climbed up inside one of the houses - I went on four houses, and on the fifth I saw Turner and Johnson lying on their backs, with their feet in the gutter; I called out "You are prisoners-surrender quietly;" they made no answer: I said I would fire on them - they then jumped up, and said, "You dare fire?" and ran over four houses; I said it was in vain for them to attempt to get away, as there were watchmen there - they then said they would give themselves up; I asked what made them cut the lead up - they said Mr. Simmons would not pay them their wages, and he told them to cut the lead up, and make money of it.
   JOHN MORGAN. I am a solicitor, and am attorney to Mr. Brooks - he holds this land under the Marquis of Northampton; there are unfinished houses on it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Will you undertake to swear they are Mr. Brooks' houses? A. Yes - he makes advances; he cannot take them away himself.
   WILLIAM BLAND. I am a surveyor, and live at Brunswick-terrace, Islington. I was employed at this place by Mr. Brooks, and was to pay in advance on the buildings in question; I have seen the prisoners on the ground - there were other buildings as well as Mr. Simmons: I have not seen the prisoners on these houses.
   SAMUEL BROOKS. I am proprietor of the ground at Ball's-pond. The prisoner Turner worked for Mr. Simmons - Perrin worked for his father, who is building five houses on the same ground, and Johnson for his brother, who is building nine houses there.
 PERRIN'S Defence. I had been at work on the ground from the time the buildings began; I have sometimes worked for my father; I had worked the last fortnight for Mr. Simmons - when I went for my wages. he went to Mr. Brooks, who would not advance him a farthing, and he was in danger of his life among the labourers; we got him away - Mr. Simmons directed us to go and get this lead, and put it into his lock-up house, to pay the men with what money could be got for it.
 JOHNSON and TURNER made the same Defence.
   ROBERT SIMMONS. I have lately been building some houses on Mr. Brooks' ground; there were lead gutters upon two of them - Mr. Hancock supplied the lead, and I paid for it; the prisoners were all in my employ - I remember the Saturday night when this occurred; I have reasons for it: the men applied to me for their wages - I had just paid the money out of my pocket for the lead, and I went to Mr. Brooks for money to pay my men, according to a verbal agreement, but he refused to give it me; I told the prisoners to get down this lead, and I would make money of it on the Monday morning - they went a little too late.
 MR. SMITH. Q. Are you of opinion they went a little too late? A. Yes - they had a right to have gone when I ordered them, and then they might have got the money on the Saturday night; I did not know when they went till now.
   JOHN JOHNSON. I am a bricklayer, and live at No. 8, Queen-street, Islington. I was at the Royal George public-house on this Saturday night; I heard Mr. Simmons come in and say Mr. Brooks would not advance him a farthing - he told them to go and take the lead off, that he might make money of it to pay them.
