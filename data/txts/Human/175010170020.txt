I went into the bar; all the company that had been in that room, but themselves, were gone; then I saw Bourne go out with a pipe in his mouth, about half an hour after nine o'clock; after he was gone, I saw the tankard standing upon the end of the table; then I was call'd into another room to take my reckoning; I staid about five minutes; when I came out again, the prisoner and the tankard were gone; they had had three reckonings, the time they had been there; I search'd directly for the tankard, but could not find it. When the young man came that had drank a dram with him, I ask'd him if he knew the prisoner; he told me a house where he us'd to drink, at the Blue Anchor, in Goodmans-fields ; I went there the next morning, the man said he had not been there for a fortnight, but directed me to the Crown and Thistle in Ailoffe-street. I went and got a warrant, and constable; he was taken there, and brought to the Red Lyon, in Red Lyon street; there I was sent for; he was taken before Justice Rickards, but would not confess any thing.
   William Harding deposed to his being at the taking the prisoner; and that he went to Bridewell some time after, to see a person that owed him same money ; and there said, in the bearing of the prisoner, he supposed Bourne would be hang'd. The prisoner made answer, and said, it would not be through me, for I have been loyal enough in this affair.
 Prisoner's Defence.
 I was going that way about a month ago; a sailor met me, and ask'd me if I would go and have some drink with him ; I said yes; he carried me to this woman's house; I said, I never drank any beer in my life; I must have a quartern of rum; I had it, and paid her for it; he went away, and Mrs. Green has sworn there were no body else in the house. I left five people in the house when I went out; particularly one was a constable. The next day they came to me at the Black Lyon in Ailoffe-street ; Mr. Harding came up first, and wanted me to make it up; and said, if I would not, he'd swear against me; I knew he was no friend of mine. I saw the constable coming towards me, I had been inform'd Mr. Newman at the Anchor and Crown had a warrant against me; so upon my seeing them I ran, and Mr. Harding came, and laid hold of me.
 Prosecutrix. There was no soul in the room when he went away; the rest went away about an hour and half before.
 To his character.
   Thomas Walker. I have known the prisoner seven years and half, he has an extreme good character as ever I heard, I have often left him alone where I have had 6, 7, 800. or 1000 l. and nobody to obstruct or molest him, and he never attempted to meddle with any thing.
   David Kennear. I have known him about seven years, he lived with a cheesemonger in Thames-street, next door to me about four or five months; he always had a very good character for what I know.
   George Darvey. I have trusted the prisoner with a great deal of money, and a number of goods divers times; I always found him the most strict, honest man, in the world.
   Peter Adkins. I have known him about two years; I never heard any thing to his misbehaviour in my life.
     Acquitted.
