 1444.  JOHN BALCH was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of June , 4 tubs, value 5s. 6d.; 1 pail, value 1s.; 1 spoke shave, value 1s. 6d.; 1 cask of paint, value 10s.; and 7 wooden bottles, value 10s.; the goods of Henry Jarman.
   MARY ANN JARMAN. I am the wife of Henry Jarman, and live in Henry-street, Old-street. We have a shop in Featherstone-street, where he carries on the business of a cooper- the prisoner had nothing to do with the business - nobody had any business in the shop but my husband and his servants - the shop was locked up by a witness - I was told something was wrong there on the 30th of June, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, I went to the shop, and found it broken open - I found this property at the station-house - the prisoner had nothing to do with the shop - we only rent the shop - I believe the prisoner worked in a shop over ours.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. You do not live at the shop? A. No; I was there in the morning, as it happened at night - I go frequently every day - there are several workshops under the same roof - my husband was unfortunate in business, and has taken the benefit of the Insolvent Act - he has been in prison eleven weeks, and will be out next Monday - he was in Whitecross-street at the time.
   CHARITY WOOD. I am the wife of William Wood, and have the care of these premises - we live in the lower part, and occupy the parlours. About eleven o'clock at night I was going out to shut up the kitchen door - the prosecutor's shop faces the passage - I saw a light in his shop, and two men in it - I called my husband - we went down the yard and called to them, and asked what they wanted there - they made no answer, but put out the light, and came out very gently - one crept over the dust-hole, and tried to get over the wall - I pulled him away by his coat - he had a hammer in his hand - I looked at him and said, "It is you, William Knowles" - he got out at the street door, and has not been on the premises since, although his wife lives there - I found the prisoner standing behind the door by the things - I had seen him in the shop - the door had been broken open, and he was standing by the side of a great many of the articles - I said, "You have broken the shop open, and gone into the shop" - he said he had not broken it open, for he found it open - we talked to him, and wanted him to go, but he was very abusive, and insulted me, and said he would not go off the premises that night - we screened him too much, for my husband moved the things into the shop again - after that he abused us and struck my husband, I went for a policeman, who came and took him - the prisoner does not live on the premises, but was employed by a man who has a shop there - the other man lived on the premises, and works for his brother down the yard.
 Cross-examined. Q. He told you he found the door open? A. He did - he attempted to strike my husband afterwards, but did not strike him - that was about twenty minutes after I found the shop open - they do not work very late - they do not work by candle light, but he was on the premises, because he associated very much with Knowles - his master's shop was locked up long before that.
 WILLIAM WOOD. I have heard my wife's evidence - it is correct - I found the prisoner standing behind the door, and saw him come out of the shop - he had no right there at all - the other man escaped, and has not been taken - I found a poker which the door had been broken with.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you find the property? A. Outside the shop - the poker was about two feet from the prisoner - he said he would stop there all night - I said he should not - I quarrelled with him, and sent for a policeman, because he was going to strike me.
   MATTHEW PEAK. I am a policeman. I was sent for, and took the prisoner into custody - Wood gave him in charge for stealing a quantity of things out of Jarman's shop - I saw the poker there - it tallied with the marks on the door - I took that out of Knowles's room by the fire-place.
   JAMES JOHNSON. I am a cooper, and work for Mr. Jarman. I locked the shop up on Saturday evening, between nine and ten o'clock, and left every thing safe - the small casks were standing on the shelf, the washing tub on the ground, and the pail hanging on a nail - the prisoner had no right there - I gave the key to Mrs. Jarman - I know this property.
 Prisoner's Defence. I solemnly declare my innocence - it is not an unusual thing for me to be in the yard - I have worked till ten or eleven o'clock on the premises by candle light.
   THOMAS MERRYMAN. I am a furniture japanner, and live on the premises - the prisoner was in my employ - he has worked there as late as ten and eleven o'clock when there was occasion for it - I was in my own apartment on the night in question - Wood called me down - I saw him in the yard, and considered he was something the worse for liquor - he remained there after he was charged with this - he has been in my employ upwards of a year and a half - he was a very honest man.
 COURT. Q. What part do you occupy? A. The shop over the prosecutor's - I have nothing to do with the prosecutor's shop - the prisoner said he had found the door open.
