 Prisoner's statement before the magistrate." I want to see my brother."
 (Defence.)
 Dr.  EAST , medical officer of Brixton Prison, said that prisoner had two delusions: one, that his brother had been strangled in the streets of Antwerp by deceased and another man on the Somali, and that he heard his wife continually crying to him; and another that his wife and his brother's wife were passengers on the ship. The conclu sion he (witness) came to was that the man was insane.
 Mr. Symmons intimated that he did not intend, after the evidence which had been given, to press the charge any further.
 Verdict, Guilty, but insane at the time of committing the act, so as not to be responsible in law. Prisoner was ordered to be detained during His Majesty's pleasure. WETTON  , Frank (27) , indicted for the manslaughter of Alfred Hillier , pleaded guilty of an assault occasioning actual bodily harm. This plea was accepted by the prosecution.
 Prisoner (who bore a good previous character) was released on his own recognisances in £25 to come up for judgment if called upon.
   BEFORE THE RECORDER .
 (Wednesday, July 19.) SIMPSON  , Thomas (47, estates manager), who on April 27 (see page 21) pleaded guilty of perjury, was brought up for judgment.
 In view of the prisoner's state of health and all the circumstances, he was sentenced to be imprisoned for 12 weeks and 1 day, entitling him to be immediately discharged.
