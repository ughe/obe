WILLIAM BAILEY. I am a labourer employed by Messrs. Page, son
& East-on March 18th I assisted to load the dumb barge Sarah at Nine
Elms Wharf with bales of india-rubber-there were three truck loads, 228
bales-I got a loading note- I helped to fasten the barge down with
tarpaulin after it was loaded-it was nailed down-the 228 bales were
all safely on board before it was nailed down,
Cross-examined by Mr. HALL. There were two of us working at the
loading-we started at 2.30 and finished at 5—the bales were stowed
in the centre of the barge and filled her—when the filling was completed
we sheeted it at once-it was done carefully-nothing could have been
got out without disturbing the nails-the bales were of different sizes ;
I should say the average was about 3 feet square; some weighed 3 owts.-
I could not carry one by myself, I dragged them into the barge with
my hands- I did not use clutch irons-the smallest one was about ?
feet square-I could not lift that—the lightest weighed well over lfcwt,
probably 2 cwts., and the heaviest about 3 cwts.- I did not see the barge
go away, I did not go down the river with it.
Re-examined. I did not see any of the bales weighed.
