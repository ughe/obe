WILLIAM ENGHAM. I am a cashier to Messrs. Lacy, Hartland, and Co.,

WILLIAM SMITH (Police Sergeant X). I arrested the prisoner on 5th
November about 9 o'clock in Chippenham Road, Paddington-I told
him that Mr. Hancock charged him with forging and uttering a bill for
251.-ho said "Can I see Mr. Gurney?"-I said " Yes, at the station"
-at the station the charge was read to him, and he said "I did not
intend to defraud; I gave Mr. Gurney 251. to meet the bill, and asked
him not to present the bill."
SAMUEL GURNEY (Re-examined). The prisoner came to me some time
in October and asked me to negotiate this bill for 251.-I said Well,
is the other all right”-he said “Yes, it will be all right"--I said
“Very well, I will try and get this one done for you”-I got it done
for him, and gave him the money-I gave him 201. on account-it was
for three months, and will be due on 3rd January. (This bill was addressed
to William Hancock by George Truscott, jun., endorsed by G. Truscott, jun.)
When the prisoner gave me the 25l., and I found I could not get the
other bill back, he said I had better keep it for this one.
WILLIAM HANCOCK (Re-examined). After I had charged the prisoner
this other bill was shown me-it is drawn and endorsed by the prisoner;
it is a fac-simile of the other one; it is not my signature.
The prisoner in his defence said that he thought he could use Hancock's
name, and that he had no intention to defraud, as was shown by his paying
in the money on the day the bill became due.
Guilty of uttering. - Recommended to mercy by the Jury, because of his
attempt to make reparation.-Three Months' Hard Labour.
