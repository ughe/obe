JoHN EDwARD CHAMPNEY, 27, Hans Place. Chelsea, shipowner,
I received a letter dated 10th June, 1903, signed Stuart D.
Ross, and I called upon Mr. Ross next day at his offioes in
Queen Victoria Street. He spoke about the Anglo-Egyptian
Company, both as to the scheme for amalgamating with other
eompanies and forming new ones. He said the Anglo-Egyptian
Company had worked sueeessfully. He took me across to show
me various aut omatie machines in the warehouse close by in
Upper Thames Street. He also assured me as to the profit I
should have by underwriting shares in the new company, and
after that had been talked about he mentioned the Anglo-Egyp
tian Company as being the promoting company, and the profits
they would make. He explained as to the amount he had re-
eeived in eash and the amount in shares. He said there were
8,000 shares of the Anglo-Egyptian and there would be 212.000,
80 that we should have a return of 308. a share in eash. Then
he eventually said he thought he could prooure some shares
for me from Captain C. A. Osborn. I said if he could prooure
some at the right price I would have some, and he said he had
paid as high as E2 158. for them himself. I then left, and I
afterwards received the letter dated 11th June signed by Stuart
Ross stating, I can secure you abeut 300 shares at 508." 25th
June I reeeived a letter enelosing a transfer in Ross's name. I
then saw him at his office and drew his attention to it and asked
for an explanation. He said Captain Osborn did not want his name
to appear and so had transferred the shares to him, Stuart
Ross, so that Stuart Ross might transfer to me. I saw him
about this on June 16th. I accepted the explanation Stuart
Ross gave to me and was satisfied. It was not an unreasonable
thing, and it removed my objection. As long as it was bona-fide
I had no objection,
