THOMAS GATFIELD. I assist Clarkson occasionally. On the 23rd of March I was at his place, and saw the prisoner come there with some brass and lead—Clarkson bought it of him—I forget what the amount was—it was weighed—Clarkson went out, and while he was gone I said to the pri soner, "You have been shaking the dead, have you not," (I had looked at the brass on a former occasion, and saw what it was)—he said, "It is a perquisite I and my mate have, we belong to the churchyard, and we dug it up"—I afterwards saw the plate unfolded, and saw the name of "Lucy Goddard" on it—I sold that with other brass at Mr. Frost's, a large dealer, who buys it for melting.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How soon after did you take this brass away? A.  The same evening—I give Clarkson 5 d . a lb. for all I have of him—he gives 4 d . for it, and I get 6 1/2 d . from the melter—I saw the bill offering 20 l . reward—that did not open my mouth—I shall not get any part of it I dare say—I should not have come about it if the policeman had not come after me—Clarkson sent the police after me—I never bought coffin-plates before these.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  Why did you put the question to him about "shaking the dead?" A.  I observed that he had brought coffin-plates on former occasions—the plates were broken up when I took them to Frost's—they were only bent when the prisoner brought them, but Clarkson ordered them to be broken up—I took 11 1/2 lb. of brass altogether to Frost's on the 23rd—the 2 1/2 lb. which the prisoner brought was mixed with other metal, and carried away altogether—I have no interest in the reward—if there is anything for me I shall be very thankful. JAMES JOHNSON. I am a gate-keeper in Chelsea pansh. After this dis covery a tool was shown to me—it was the single of the garden line, which I had been in the habit of using in the yard, but it was altered a good deal since I left off using it—it then had a round point, but when it was show to me it had been made flat, like a chisel or screw-driver. JOHN CAPERER. I am an undertaker. I was employed to conduct the funeral of Lucy Goddard—her coffin was placed in No. 2 vault, under Chelsea church—a brass engraved plate was placed on the coffin, with "Mrs. Lucy Goddard" on it—I have looked at the coffin since this dis covery, and the plate is gone, also two more from coffins of the same family.
