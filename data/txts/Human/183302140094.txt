   JOHN WHITE. I am a painter, and was employed by the prisoner at the prosecutor's. The prisoner gave me this picture in May last, and told me I might take and pawn it, or get what money I could on it, till he could get money from Mr. Phillips to pay my wages - I pawned that and two razors for 10s. close by where I lived; I redeemed it on the 13th of December, and took it to Mr. Bardingdon, in Wardour-street, where I offered it for sale for 2l. 10s.; I was taken the next day.
 COURT. Q. Did you know its value? A. No; I should not have thought it worth a quarter of what they say it is.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Where did you receive the picture? A. At Mr. Phillips' door; I had no suspicion that it was stolen - I received it between six and seven o'clock in the morning, when I went to meet the prisoner; I never was in trouble before - I was taken on this charge, and confined forty days; as soon as I understood the picture was stolen, I told what I knew - I admit stating to the officer that I received the duplicate from the prisoner, but I did not know he was an officer; I thought there was something wrong, and I did not wish to bring the prisoner into trouble - I told the truth when I was taken into custody; I had a child by one Lambert; I was not brought up on account of forging a certificate of marriage - I did not produce to Lambert's mistress a printed from of a certificate of marriage; I was charged once with a rape, but I was not guilty.
     NOT GUILTY.
 634.  WILLIAM LAWLER was again indicted for stealing, on the 20th of November , 1 picture, value 1l., the goods of Harry Phillips.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   ROBERT THOMAS. I live at Birmingham. I bought this picture of the prisoner, with some others, about a year and a half ago - I gave him a sum of money for the lot.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. What are you? A. I am a clerk - I know this picture by the mark of Wilson on the back of it; it had not been out of my possession - I had kept it in a cupboard; I could not hang it up - I kept it till about a month ago, and then sent it up to town.
 MR.  HARRY PHILLIPS. This is my own private property; it was not the subject of sale - it was at my house when the prisoner worked for me; I never gave it him - he stated to me that he had sent it to Birmingham; I know it by the size of it - by the subject - by the stock marks, one of which has been attempted to be obliterated, and by the name of Wilson, which was on it at my house.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was it framed? A. I really cannot tell; I have fifteen hundred or two thousand pictures - some of them are taken out of frames to sell; this was at a house of mine in Warwick-street - the prisoner was employed there as well as at Bond-street; the prisoner said his wife had sent this by mistake to Birmingham, with some furniture.
 Prisoner's Defence. I can prove that I have had it for five years, and I have only been in Mr. Phillips' employ for four years.
   ELIZA ATKINS. I live at No. 20, Ann-street, Waterloo-road; my husband died in July last - he sold this painting to the prisoner, between four and five years ago; I have no doubt of its being the same - the canvas on the back corresponds; but I think this looks rather cleaner.
 MR. CLARKSON. Q. What was the subject of your picture? A. Three ships and a small boat, and the middle ship was rather sinking in the water; I am now in the service of Mr. May - there was some chalk on the back; I do not know that it had a scratch on it - I would not venture to swear this is the picture, but it is exactly like it; I did not go before the Magistrate - I was asked to come here last Friday.
 MR. PHILLIPS re-examined. Q. Is not the prisoner a very clever fellow? A. Yes he is; I did not know he was capable of copying pictures, till the other day one was shown to me.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Confined Six Months.

 635.  SOPHIA COOPER was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of February , 5 sovereigns, the monies of George Osborn , in his dwelling-house.
   ANN OSBORN. I am the wife of George Osborn - we live in Cowheel-alley, Golden-lane. On the afternoon of the 13th of February we had six sovereigns in a box under the bed: I took it out, and gave my husband one of them, in the presence of the prisoner - I then locked up the other five sovereigns, put the box under the bed again, and put the key into a glass, in which I had been in the habit of keeping it; I went out between seven and eight o'clock that evening, leaving no one in the room but the prisoner - I had asked her if she would go with me; she said No, she would stop there and lock herself in - I returned in a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes; I found my door open, my three little children laying in bed, and the prisoner was gone - I went to the box, and found it a little way under the bed, not where I had left it; I took it up, it had been unlocked, and was open then, but the key was in the glass, as I had left it - I turned out the contents of the box on the table, but the five sovereigns were gone; I took up my infant, and went out, locking the door of my room - I found the prisoner in an alley, and gave charge of her; I had left my children in the room while I went out - the eldest was five years old.
