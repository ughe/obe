2448. GEORGE FRY and MARIA LANGLEY were indicted for steal-
ing, on the 15th of February, 1 table cloth, value 18. 3d. ; 1 flannel shirt, 1s.;
1 candlestick, 18. 6d. ; 1 flat iron, 4d.; and 1 blanket, 1s. 60.; the goods of
Jeremiah Lyons: and that Langley had been before convicted.
MARY LYONS. I am the wife of Jeremiah Lyons, and live in Long-lane,
Ratcliffe. The prisoners both lodged at my house-when they went away I
missed a blanket, a table-cloth, a flannel shirt, and a flat-iron-these are
them.
Fry. I have a pension from the East India Company-it was due on the
22nd of August - there was 21. 128. due to me. Witness. He owes me
31. 12s.--the key was sent home by another lodger, and I found all the things
gone-there were not any things borrowed of me.
Langley. Every article here has been lent to us. Witness. No, they
have not--I lent her the flat-iron to iron with, not to get a quartern of rum
on-the flannel shirt was not lent to you to pledge with a piece of diaper.
GEORGE MURRAY police-constable 68 K. I took the prisoners. The
duplicate of the flat-iron was given me by Langley, and two duplicates of a
table cloth and a flannel shirt were given me by Mrs. Lyons-Langley said
what she had done was under the direction of Fry that Mrs. Lyons had given
her permission to pledge these articles.
THOMAS ALLEN. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a table cloth and a
flannel shirt, which were pledged by Langley in the name of " Fry, 7, High-
street"-they correspond with the duplicate I have.

A. No-but I recollect Mrs. Lyons saying, when she came to make inquiries,

said that.
Mary Lyons re-examined. I did not tell Allen that I had lent the cloth
to Langley--the policeman was with me.
GEORGE MURRAY re-examined. I went with Mrs. Lyons- I did not hear
