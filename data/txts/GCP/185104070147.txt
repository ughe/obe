THOMAS M'GUIRE. I live in Powis-street, Woolwich. I know the
prisoner-on 6th March I bought a pricker, a jigger, and a drill of him,
and a wheel of William Ellis-these are them produced)-the prosecutor
came and claimed them- I gave 2d. for the jigger, 2d. for the drill, 8d. for
the pricker, and 2d. for the wheel.
GEORGE Botton. I am a shoemaker, and live in Salutation-alley,
Woolwich. On 5th March I bought this pricker of the prisoner, and the
day after this pincer-punch, for 4d. each (produced).
WILLIAM Ellis. I am a furniture-dealer, of Woolwich, I bought
these six articles produced) of a man named Stevenson, for 9d., and
exposed them for sale-on the Friday the prosecutor came, examined the
tools, and claimed them-I delivered them to the policeman, except one I
had sold to M'Guire.
Joun STEVENSON. I am a blacksmith, and live with Botton. On 8th
March the prisoner asked me to sell some tools, and I took them to Mr.
Ellis, and handed the prisoner the money-I did not examine them.
JOHN NEWELL (policeman, R 340). I took the prisoner on 7th March,
searched him, and found this tap, which the prosecutor identified, and the
key of a room-I went with the prosecutor to the prisoner's room- the
key fitted the door, and we there found two brushes, which the prosecutor
said were bis--the other articles were given me by Ellis, Stevenson, and
M'Guire, and I have had them ever since.
CHARLES GAGE re-examined. All these articles are mine-the pincers
have one end broken off, and the brushes are marked this tap is mine, the
officer fitted it to the tub-I never gave or sold any of these articles to the
prisoner, or any one else.

afterwards-1 did not see any tap in the hamper.
