who supplied what she had-she said, “Mr. Boulter, it was part of what
was left the night before"-I asked the prisoner if he was hungry-he said,
"Yes"-I fetched them some bread and bacon--they did not eat it in the
way I have seen poor people eat sometimes-they cooked it first--when I
found he had filled his belly, I said, "I am a constable, and it is necessary
you should give me some information respecting the death of this child, or
I must take you into custody-there is a report that this child has been
very much neglected"-(for, while they were preparing their refreshment,
I had been out to make inquiry in the neighbourhood)- he said, “We have
been short "-I asked what was the cause of it--he said he had not employ-
ment, and no means of getting it for them I said, “ Have you made any ap-
plication for parochial assistance in your extremities, or medical advice for
the child ?"-he said no, he had not, in that instance, and the reason he gave
was, because he had before that time been passed on to his parish, which
was Nessing, in Essex, which he had left, thinking he could do better by
coming to town; and I think the girl said, they left because they had not
victuals enough there I do not think the prisoner made that observation
-I asked him if he had neglected the children in consequence of cohabit-
ing with some woman he said, the children and himself had certainly
been very short, but he could certainly acquit himself of any thing like
neglect-- he said nothing about the woman-he made the same observation
afterwards, on the way to the station-house.
WILLIAM BARNARD BODY. I am a surgeon. I did not see the child
before death--I saw it on Sunday, the 30th of December, but did not
open the body till the following day-it appeared very much emaciated-I
could see nothing to account for death-there was no appearance of exter-
nal marks-on opening the body I found a disease of the lungs, adhesion
of the pleura, and a large abscess, which had broken-it died from con-
sumption, which must have been going on for some time---supposing it to
have had small-pox, then measles and hooping-cough, that might produce
consumption, but not necessarily so all the viscera of the stomach was in s
state of health-the stomach was quite empty, but it appeared that during
the night the child had a very severe diarrhea- I have no means of judg
ing whether disease was produced by want of food-even supposing there
had been neglect, it was impossible to ascertain it decidedly in that state-
I cannot say the child died from neglect of being supplied with food-whe-
ther the emaciation was the result of consumption or want of food I cannot
state--there is hectic fever with consumption--that would produce very
great emaciation there is no distinction between emaciation produced by
disease and shortness of food.
