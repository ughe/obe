 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Will you swear that he had not left the house at half-past six o'clock? A. I will take my oath, that he came in between twenty minutes before seven and seven.
   LEVY SOLOMON. I live in Rosemary-lane, about a mile from Globe-fields. As I stood at my door on the Sunday in question, the prisoner came and offered me a watch, and said he wanted money - I offered him 15s., and he agreed to take 18s., which I gave him; this was between nine and ten o'clock in the evening.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you ever seen him before? A. No. I do not know whether this is a usual time for selling watches, but I keep my Sabbath on a Saturday - I gave the watch to my son-in-law.
   JOHN JACOBS. Mr. Solomon gave me this watch, and I put it into my pocket. I afterwards gave it to the officer.
 Cross-examined. Q. How came you to give it to the officer? A. I was minding another shop for my father-in-law, and I shewed it to a fellow-shopman; we had some dispute about the value of it, and I said "Go to the pawnbroker's next door, and ask what they would lend upon it;" they said 1l.; the pawnbroker then gave information of it, and the officer came and took it.
   ROBERT DAVIS. I am an officer. I produce this watch which I got from Jacobs on the Monday. The prisoner was apprehended on the Sunday.
   JOHN BRADMAN. I am a watchman. I took charge of the prisoner on the Sunday night; I was going by at half-past ten o'clock, and Mr. Payne told me, if I saw the prisoner to take him; while I was there, he came home, and I took him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 MR. PHILLIPS to MR. PAYNE. Q. Was there not a young woman in your house? A. There was a young woman out of place, who slept with my daughter; but from the time she came in, till she went to bed, I kept sight of her; the seals of my watch were in my hat in the room where the prisoner slept; they were covered with this handkerchief; if he chose he might have taken them, but they were only brass.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. Confined Three Months.
 1757.  JOSEPH BAKER was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 3 saddles, value 3l. 10s., the goods of Benjamin Sealy.
   EDWARD HANDS. I am a watchman of Saint Luke's. On the 20th of August I went into Crown-court, between four and five o'clock in the morning; I saw the prisoner coming along the court, and another person with him. The prisoner had three saddles on his right shoulder - I caught hold of him, and he immediately threw them down - I called a porter to assist me, and we took him to the watch-house.
   THOMAS MORGAN. I am foreman to Mr. Sealey; he is a livery stable-keeper. On the 19th of August I locked up these saddles safe in his stables - I went to the Leaping Bar public-house, to sleep, and the next morning, when I went to the stable, I found the door open, and the watchman there. I then looked and found the first padlock broken off; the second stable was on a springlock, that was broken - the third stable had a padlock, and the staple of that was drawn out. I know these saddles to be my master's.
   JAMES MADDEN. I am a porter. I was coming out of my door, and saw the prisoner in custody of the watchman; he told me to take up the saddles. I put them into my basket, and took them to the watch-house.
   JOHN BROWN. I am watch-house keeper. I went to see where they had been taken from, and found two locks broken, and the staple of the other drawn.
   THOMAS FARROW. I am a watchman. My brother watchman called me to assist him, which I did.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was going up Golden-lane, about half-past four, and met a man, who looked like a groom; he asked me to take these saddles to Smithfield. Soon after I took them, he made off.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 1758.  MARGARET BURKE was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of July , 11 yards of silk, value 30s., the goods of William Andrews and Nelson Andrews.
   ROBERT LLOYD RICE. I am an assistant to Messrs. William and Nelson Andrews, silk-mercers, who live in Oxford-street. On the 28th of July the prisoner came and looked at some shawls; while I turned my back, I suppose, she took the silk, which I saw fall from her, under her clothes.
 Prisoner. I met a young woman, who wanted me to go with her to buy a shawl - I went with her: the shopman got over the counter, and in getting over, he knocked a piece of silk down on the floor - which I know was black; this is brown - and at the office he said he did not know whether it fell from me or from the counter. Witness. I saw her doing something which I thought was adjusting her stocking - I thought she appeared different to what she was before; and I said, "You have got something, I am sure." I then took hold of her, and it fell from her - I took hold of the other, but she went away: the prisoner acknowledged afterwards that she had taken it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOSEPH WORMALD. I received charge of the prisoner. I searched her and found two half-crowns and a duplicate - in going along she asked me what I thought she would be done to - I said I could not tell.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
