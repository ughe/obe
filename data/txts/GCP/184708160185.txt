JOHN GUNN policeman G 224.) I went with the prosecutor to Gobby's
- he came home with his van--the prosecutor asked him if he had any of his
sacks on his premises-he hesitated, and said he did not know, or something
to that effect-the gates of the stable were wide open, and we walked in
Gobby took two sacks off the place, and said, “I believe I have a sack or
two of yours here"- the first he took, Mr. Powell said, “This is not mine"
he took down two more, and Mr. Powell said, “These are mine; how did
you come by them ?"-he said, “I do not know, they have been here some
time" - Mr. Powell said, "Have you got any up stairs ?"- he said, “ Up
stairs I have got no place for sacks”-Mr. Powell said, "I am informed you
have; will you allow me to look ?"-he said, " I have no place up stairs" -
I then said, "I am a constable; if you have no sacks, you can have no
objection to our going up"—he said, "I have only got my room up stairs,"
and he looked round the stable, and said, " Here is all the place I have got
for food for my horse"- at last he said, “We will all go up stairs"—there is
a door from the stable to the bottom of the stair-case, and as soon as that
was opened, on the first step, I tumbled against a sack-I showed it to Mr.
Powell -he said, “This is one of my sacks; will you allow me to look at the
corn ?"-Gobby said, “Yes”—Mr. Powell put his band in, and said, “This
is very much like my corn"--Gobby said, "I know it is, I bought it of your
George"-we went up stairs, and in the bedroom found two sacks-one con-
tained whole beans, and one split beans--Mr. Powell put his hand in the latter,
and said, “These are my beans"-Gobby said, “Yes, I know they are ; I
bought them of your George the same time that I bought the oats”_Mr. Powell
said, “How could you rob me in that manner ?"he said, “ George told me
there was an overplus of the vessel, and I paid him a sovereigo, and he spent
a shilling"-he talked about his looking over it, but I will not say what-
my attention was directed to the beans.
(Gobby received a good character.)

1920. THOMAS JOHNSON was indicted for stealing 50 veneers, value
88.; the goods of Charles Watson, his master; and GEORGE JOHNSON and
MARY ANN JOHNSON for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing
them to have been stolen: to which
THOMAS JOHNSON pleaded GUILTY.
MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution.
MARY ANN Watson. I am the wife of Charles Watson, of Arthur-street,
St. Luke's - he makes boxes and writing-desks, and uses veneers on the
morning of the 2nd of July I came down stairs, about seven o'clock in the
morning--I found the parlour-door was pushed against me-on going in, I
found Thomas Johnson behind the door, and another man ran to the back-room
-I found an apron laid on the floor, full of veneers, and Thomas Johnson's
pockets were full of veneers—the strange man pushed me on one side, and
ran away-- I called my husband, and he came down—I have not seen George
Johnson or Mary Ann Johnson there.
