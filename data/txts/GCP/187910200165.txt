The Common SERJBANT expressed a doubt whether the tickets were orders
for the delivery of goods, and they were not charged as requests.
MR. SAFFORD, on the authority of Reg. v. Kay, 1 Croion Cases Reserved,
submitted that the ticket was an authority entitling the bearer to goods, and
that therefore it was an authority voithin the meaning of the statute, and that
as the word authority did not appear in the old stutute, but had been intro-
duced into the next one, it was not affected by the old decisions, and the
decision in Kay's ense was that it was nut necessary that anything should
appear on the ticket stating that the bearer ras entitled to goods, but that it
mous the same as in the citse of a turnpike ticket, where it was sufficient that
persons had been in the hubit of treating similar documents as wurrants for
the payment of money. (See Reg. v. Chambers, 1 Crown Cases Reserved,
257, and Reg. v. Fitch, Russell on Crime', vol. ii., p. 823.) In some cases,
such us a paronbruker's duplicate, it was not necessary for any signature to be
attached, the mere delivery being sufficient to entille the bearer to the article.
MR. Wills contended that all the cases cited applied to the receipt of
money, whereas this did not
The Common SERJEANT considered that there was no doubt that under the
oli statute this would not have been a forgery, the only question was whether
it was one under the nero statute ; he should hold that it was, reserving the
question for the Court above if desired.
