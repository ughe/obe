WILLIAM GLEED (police-constable V 276.) On that Wednesday morning
at a quarter before four, I was near the Swan, and saw the prisoner with
basket, going in the direction of Clapham-he asked me the way to Brixton
and said he had been sleeping in a garden, but he was glad he had not lost
the basket-he afterwards said he had got over the wall at the Greyhound.
and had been asleep-he did not say he had been turned out of the Grey
hound, where he had been drinking—the basket appeared to be full of some-
thing—there was a kind of handkerchief or apron on the top of it-on the
30th of July, in consequence of what I heard, I went to Mitcham, and found
the prisoner reaping in a corn field-he had his white trowsers and shirt on,
and that was all-I charged him with stealing linen in the Clapham-roadbe
said, “ Linen, what linen- I took no linen; so long as you don't take me
for a deserter, I will go with you "-he went about ten yards, and pretended
to be looking for his jacket-he then set off running-I ran after him about
half way across the field-be then turned, and gave me a blow on the bead-
I stopped him till another constable came and assisted me-I am certain that
the prisoner is the same man I had seen in the morning after I took him
he said he had been to the Dorset Arms, and went to sleep there, and had
left his stock there.
RICHARD GOLDING (police-serjeant V 31.) I am stationed at Merton. On
the 30th of July, Gleed told me something, and I took him to a corn field-
I saw the prisoner there--Gleed tried to take him he ran away-he was
pursued and taken-he went home and cleaned himself, and then he said be
would not go to military duties--I said it was not that he was wanted for,
it was for stealing linen--he said, “Linen, I could soon show you what I
had with me"- I went to M'Leish's, and found a basket and these articles.
TROMAS EMMERSON (police-serjeant V 16.) I went with Golding to
M'Leish's, and found these things-on the morning of the 31st of July I saw
the prisoner at the station-I told him we had found the linen he was
charged with, at his sister's in Acre-lane-he said he knew nothing about
any linen, we could bring nothing to his charge.
ANN M'LEISH. I am a widow-the prisoner is my brother. On Wednesday
morning, the 29th of July, he came to my place between four and five o'clock
in the morning, and brought a basket and these articles-he left them, and
said he would call again in a few days-he staid about half an hour or three
quarters, and went away-I did not know what was in the basket before be
went-the policemen came, and I let them have it.
Prisoner's Defence. I came out from barracks, and was drinking at the
Swan; I then went to the Dorset Arms, and had a pint of beer; I went
round the corner, took my stock off, and went to sleep ; I awoke, and went
into the road; I found the basket with these articles: I did not know w
was in it.
