HENRY JONES. I am barman at the Welsh Harp, Aylesbury Street,
Clerkenwell-I knew the prisoner as a customer when I was at the Bull, in
Holborn-I recollect him coming to the Welsh Harp, I don't know the
day-he passed over some money to me in a little bag, and asked me to
take care of it for him-there was 181. 186. 6d.-he said he was afraid of
losing it, and would call in the morning for it, or at night-he then went
sway--I went out to the door about half an hour afterwards and saw the
eart standing there—the boy spoke to me-I then said to the prisoner,
"The boy tells me you have lost the money, you know you gave it to me;
take it away, I won't have anything to do with it" and I gave him the
money back.
Cross-examined. Q. When he gave you the money, did he say he felt stupid
from drink and did not like to carry it about with him. A. Yes, he had
some beer in our house, and before he came there he had had his share.
WILLIAM BROADHEAD. I am a draper, at 48, Central Street, St. Luke's
-on 15th June the prisoner came to me with a cart and some goods-I
paid him a bl. note and 131. 108. in gold, 181. 198. altogether he was to
take that to his master he gave me a receipt for it.
JOHN BRAIN. I am in the employ of Mr. Baker-on 15th June I went
with the prisoner to Central Street, St. Luke's-I stopped in the cart, and
the prisoner took the goods in--coming away from there the prisoner said
he had received some money-we then went to the City Road - the prisoner
then said he had lost his money-he was sober--we went to the Welsh
Harp, and he told me to take the horse and cart back to the Angel,
Inlington-I went there and brought the cart back-I told him I had
brought the cart back, and he said " All right," and went down Jerusalem
Passage—the barman came to the door, and I said something to him.
Crow-eramined. Q. The prisoner was quite sober all the evening! A.
Yes-he was sober when he went to the Welsh Harp-he was rather in-
toxicated when he came away from there.

WILLIAM FAULKES (City Detective). I took the prisoner into oustody on
29th of last month-I told him the charge, and he said " All right”-
bringing him past the Welsh Harp I said "You were seen to go there with
some of the money "---he said "All right, I had it after that."
Crow-examined. Q. He told you that it was lost! 4. He told me
previously that he had lost it, and he had had it after he had been in the
Welsh Harp-he said before, he lost it before 9 o'clock in the evening-he
gave rather a confused statement-he was very much confused and alarmed.
