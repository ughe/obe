Cross-examined by MR. JONES. Q. Where did you get the number?
1. I took it in the first instance, and I went last night to Bow-street, to
at the two numbers, as they were summoned there by me on the 11th of
August-one was Hill's, and the other Bardell's I wish to say, that the
driver of Mr. Hill's was most insolent-his language was most dreadful
Sir Frederick Roe said he would fine him 51. the next time - the
driver of Mr. Bardell's had more sense, and behaved with more propriety
I never saw any thing improper in the conduct of Mr. Bray's omnibus
I live at No. 213, Holborn, nearly facing Day and Martin's, twenty-five
or thirty-five doors from Mr. Bray's-he lives up a yard-I have known
him some years, and when I ride in an omnibus, it is generally by him.
Cross-examined by Mr. KNOWLES. Q. In connexion with what was it
you mentioned the name of Bardell? A. The two omnibuses had blocked
up the way of Bray's, to prevent my getting in, and the police summoned
them-I went to prove the case-I cannot tell which of the Bardells it was
-- there was the name of Bardell on one of the omnibuses-they stood and
blocked up the whole road—I dare say there were thirty or forty carriages
which could not go on--I begged of the first man to go on, and he refused
-Bardell's, I think, was the last.
Q. Then in point of fact, he could not have got forward? 4. He could
have given way to give space, but not quite sufficient for the others to go
past-he could not get out, but still he might have accommodated a little
if he liked-he could not get back, there were twenty or thirty carriages
behind him.
JOSEPH Hudson. I am a tobacconist, and have lived in Oxford-street
upwards of thirty years. I know Mr. Bray's omnibus—I have observed it
interrupted by other omnibuses for the past two or three months I have
seen the "Emperor," No. 352, and the other, No.177, with "Thomas Corbett"
on it, one before and one behind Bray's, and they have driven up against
his steps to prevent the passengers going by him, and half a dozen others
have done the same—there must have been a great deal of danger in pea-
ple getting in or out of his omnibus- I have seen Bray's turn, and the
others have turned about also I have complained to the police repeatedly
-I have complained to Martin, the officer-the other omnibuses have driven
so fast that unless I had stopped them, I could not complain to their con-
ductors about it-I have on one or two occasions stopped them, and said
if they drove so fast I should take their numbers, and go to the police-
office.
Court. Q. Upon those former occasions, have you seen Bray turning
round, following the others, and using the same course that they did to him?
4. No.
