Q. How much money did you take of your own from Cracow, when
you went to Vienna ? A. Why should I have money with me, and put
myself to expense ?-I did not have any-I paid for the cloak, it may be
out of my own money, or it may be out of other money-I took moner

cloak out of my own money or that.
MR. PLATT. Q. Was Koerner absent from Vienna any time? 4. Yes
during his absence his wife resided in my house-after he returned he went
to live with his wife; but not in the same house-he went to reside two ek

Q. After Koerner returned did you and him converse about forged notes!
A. No; I only spoke to him to pay the rent-I never had any con-
nexion with Koerner respecting forged notes- I communicated to the Consul
every thing I heard from Koerner, the day after he left-I was never tried
before a Court-no one told me any thing—when I was discharged, they
merely sent me a passport as a merchant, as usual, and dismissed me.
Q. Why did you go to Vienna ? A. The Consul showed me a letter
that I was to go to England, and told me the government required I should
come to Vienna, and proceed to England-I only received two hundred
guilders at Vienna, to enable me to come over to England-I am a Jew-
Koerner is a Jew, and his wife a Jewess.
Q. When this note was held up to the light could you see anything in
the paper which could not be seen otherwise ? A. The water-mark could
be seen, and could not be seen otherwise—when I paid the 30l, on account
of the notes, he told me they were to be Austrian notes, 1000 pieces, at 3.
-a Bank-note is a piec- the notes were to be 50 florins each-the 301.
was only on account-I should have paid him the difference--they were
Austrian notes-the 30!. was paid on the 24th of August.
