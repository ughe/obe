 565. ELIZABETH ANN CARTER was indicted for a like offence.
   MESSRS. LAWRENCE and POLAND . conducted the Prosecution. MARY GRAHAM. My husband keeps an eating house at No. 27, Stangate-street. On Saturday, 12th April, I was there—the prisoner came at near 10 o'clock at night—she asked for a quarter of a pound of pork and a 1 d . worth of potatoes—they came to 4 1/2 d .—she laid down a 5 s . piece—my little boy said, "Mother, I will go for change"—he went over the way to Mr. Broadhurst and brought the change back—I gave it to her, and she left—soon after Mr. Broadhurst came and said the crown was bad—on Saturday, 3rd May, the pri soner came again about the same time in the evening—I was in the parlour—I heard her talking to my husband—I recognised her voice, and went into the shop—I said to her, "You are the same woman that was here this night three weeks"—she said, "I think you are mistaken, ma'am." GEORGE ALFRED GRAHAM. I am eight years old. On Saturday evening, 12th April, I saw the prisoner in my mother's shop—I was sent for change to Mr. Broadhurst—I got it, and gave it to my mother—on Saturday, 3rd May, about 10 o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came for a quarter of a pound of roast pork and 1 d . worth of potatoes—she asked my father for them, and put down a 5 s . piece, which was given to my mother—I was in the shop when she came in, and heard my mother say something about her having been there before, and then I saw she was the same person. HENRY BROADHURST. I keep the Crown public house at Stangate. On Saturday night, 12th April, this little boy came with a crown—I gave him change, and after he was gone I found the crown piece was bad—I put it away in a drawer by itself—on Saturday, 3rd May, Mrs. Graham came to tell me the woman was there—I went, and found the prisoner in the shop—I fetched a constable—I gave him the crown which I had received on 12th April. GEORGE GRAHAM. I am the husband of the first witness.
