He bought that chain on Whit Sunday for a halfpenny, and a thrush-bird for 4 d .; he gave me the chain to look at; while I was look ing at it the parlour bell rang, and he went away, leaving the chain with me. On the 25th of May I apprehended the prisoner at Newbury, on another charge—in searching him I found in his pocket, among other things, this chain—I said, "Halloo! what do you call this?"—he said, "Oh, it is nothing but an old chain that I found on the road"—he said nothing about Merritt having given it to him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I forgot that at the time. Three men lodged in the third floor besides me. I got up in the morning, and the landlady let me out. I had no bundle, or anything in my hand. GUILTY. Aged 25.— Transported for Seven Years.
 1971. WILLIAM WEBB was again  indicted for stealing, on the   16th of May , 2 chaise-springs, value 1 l  . 15 s  .; the goods of Thomas Gower , his master. THOMAS GOWER. I am a coachmaker, at Greenwich. The prisoner was in my employ for about a week—I had a pair of chaise springs in my shop—the prisoner said he knew a person at Woolwich, where he had been at work, that wanted a pair of springs, and he repeatedly asked me, in the course of the week, if I would dispose of them to his friend—I said they were disposed of to another person, which they were—but as the per sons did not come for them, and thinking that the sale of these would enable me to make a new pair, on Whit Monday, the 16th of May, I trusted the prisoner with them to take to Woolwich and show his friend—he said he did not know the man's name—the prisoner had repeatedly asked, in the coarse of the week, to buy them himself, which I refused, and also refused his taking them to Woolwich, till the Monday morning—they were worth 35 s .—I have a copy of the account which I gave him with the springs—I did not tell him what to do if the man did not pay for them—he took them from me, in the full confidence that the man would take them at that price—it was to be a ready money transaction, of course.
   Prisoner.  I worked for Mr. Gower nine days and three-quarters, but could get no wages, and understanding these springs were for sale, I asked him if he would sell me them; he said a person had been about them; I asked him for money; he gave me 4 d . and 6 d . at a time, in the course of the week; on Saturday night he gave me half-a-crown, and said, if I would call in about an hour, he would give me some more; hearing that he had deceived so many of his men, I said, if he would give me another half-crown I would make shift till Monday; when I went on Monday he said, "I think you shall take these springs, and bring me back a few shillings, as I am very short this morning." Witness.  I deny the whole of it—I had paid him his wages, all but a trifle—he only worked for me four days and a quarter—he was to have 18 s . a-week—he received 6 s . 4 d . in the course of the week, and 5 s . on Saturday night—when he came to work on Monday morning I gave him the springs to take to his friend at Woolwich—he said he should be home between nine and ten o'clock, and I never saw him till he was apprehended—I gave information to the police, and they put it in the "Hue and Cry."
