 1030.  ANN PAVEY was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of January , 1 blanket, value 2s.; 2 towels, value 6d.; 2 pillows, value 5s.; 1 spoon, value 2s.; 2 sheets, value 4s.; 2 candlesticks, value 2s.; 1 table-cloths, value 1s. 6d., and 1 counterpane, value 3s., the goods of Jane Bull.
 JANE BULL. I am a  widow, and live in Tooke's-court. I let part of my house to a person named Tetsoll; the prisoner was an aunt of theirs, and visited them - they ran away in my debt; on the 28th of January I went into their room, and missed the property stated - I made inquiry, and on Monday night they sent me the duplicates, in a letter, by post.
   WILLIAM HUMPHRIES. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Skinner-street. I have two towels, a blanket, a counterpane, two pillows, two candlesticks, and a table-cloth, which were pawned by the prisoner.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. When were they pawned? A. Between the 17th of December, and the 24th of January; I have received part of them before, in the name of Tetsoll - I believe the prisoner is their aunt.
   THOMAS HUETSON. I have a silver spoon, pawned by the prisoner, on the 18th of January.
   GEORGE BARBER. I am an officer. I received the prisoner, and got the duplicates from the prosecutrix.
 MRS. BULL. These are my property; the prisoner had been upwards of three months with her nephew and niece.
 Cross-examined. Q. Were these things never pawned to pay your rent? A. Not to my knowledge; I had seen the prisoner's husband come there, but not after these goods were gone - I said I had no wish to prosecute, if I could get my things, and I was offered the money - this is the letter I received (read.)
 MADAM, - It is with feelings of very great pain I now address you, but I can assure you it was distress and trouble made me leave your house, and induced me to act in the way I have done; but you may rely on it, as I hope to be saved, in a very short time, I will pay you, and make all things right. I cannot say more, but you shall hear from me again very soon.
 Your's, obediently, J. TETSOLL.
 Prisoner's Defence. My niece was very ill, and I went there now and then; they were in great distress, and were in the habit of pawning the things the whole year round - they pawned these things to pay the rent, or Mrs. Bull said she would play the deuce with them; they expected 10l. of a gentleman, who owed them 30l. - I did not know of their going away.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1031.  ELIZABETH POWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 3 table-cloths, value 9s.; 2 petticoats, value 3s.; 3 shirts, value 9s.; 3 shifts, value 9s.; 4 handkerchiefs, value 7s., and 1 apron, value 6d., the goods of Matthew Fletcher.
   SARAH FLETCHER. I am the wife of Matthew Fletcher- he lives in John's-place, Islington-green; I take in washing - the prisoner was in my service; I gave her 7s. a week, and her board, but she did not sleep there. I missed some articles on the 25th of February. and asked her about them - she denied it; I sent her home with a basket of linen, and that she did not carry home.
   GEORGE ELLIS. I am a Police-constable. On the 27th of February the prisoner was given into my custody- I searched her, and saw her throw away the duplicates of this property.
   THOMAS HARVEY. I am a pawnbroker. I have a number of articles pawned by the prisoner.
   GEORGE BARBER. I am a pawnbroker. I have two shifts and three handkerchiefs pawned by the prisoner -I gave her these duplicates.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that she had pledged the articles by the prosecutrix's desire.
 SARAH FLETCHER. I never sent her to pawn any thing.
     GUILTY. Aged 47. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1032.  JOSEPH BLUMSON and MARY THOMPSON were indicted for stealing, on the 14th of March , 1 shawl, value 4l., the goods of Joseph Horner.
 MR. DOWLING conducted the prosecution.
   SARAH ELIZABETH ROBERTSON. I am shop-woman to Mr. Joseph Horner, of Myrtle-street, Hoxton- he is a shawl-dyer. On the 14th of March I missed a shawl off the counter, between five and six o'clock in the evening; this is it - here is a number on it, which I put myself.
 Cross-examined by MR. DONNE. Q. When did you see it last? A. About ten minutes before - I was not in the shop all the time; we do not sell shawls.
   WILLIAM EDWARDS. I am shopman to Mr. Sowerby, a pawnbroker, in Brick-lane. At a quarter-past seven o'clock on the evening of Wednesday, the 14th of March, Thompson came to our shop, offered this shawl, and wanted 10s. on it; I asked whose it was - she said Mrs. Smith's, No. 3, over the way; I said there was no such number there - she then said down King-street, No. 3, or No. 9; I put the shawl under the counter, sent: for the Policeman, and gave her in charge - this is it; Blumson came to our house, and said, "Have you not sent a woman to the station-house?" I said Yes; he said, "I bought the shawl about two years ago;" I said, "The pattern has not been up so long;" he then said it was eighteen months ago, and he bought it of a man at his door - he then asked me the value of it; I said about 2l. 10s. - he said,"That is what I gave for it."
   WILLIAM KING CLARK. I am a Police-constable, and took Thompson. I asked her who the shawl belonged to - she said Mrs. Smith, and she would take me to her; she took me to No. 3, Matthew's-buildings, where I saw Blumson laying on a bed - she said to him, "Joe, get up and fetch Mrs. Smith;" he got up, and went away, but did not return - I took her to the station, and soon after the pawnbroker and Blumson came; Blumson there stated that he had bought the shawl eighteen months ago for 30s. - I have seen the prisoners together very often.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did he not say who he bought it of? A. Yes, of a hawking pedlar, who came to his door- Blumson was out on bail.
