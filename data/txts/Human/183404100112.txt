 736.  WILLIAM CLARK was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of March , 3 cheeses, value 25s.; the goods of John Ballingall Whyte ; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
 JOHN CONERLEY (police-constable H 135.) On the 12th of March, about a quarter past seven o'clock, I was on duty in Essex-street, Whitechapel - I saw the prisoner come out of Whitechapel, with these three cheeses on his head - I asked what he had got there? - he said, he brought them from Ratcliffe Highway, and was to meet a man at Shoreditch church with them, who would give him 1s. - I said that would not do for me, and was taking him to the station-house - he threw down the cheeses, and ran away - I left my brother officer with the cheeses - I pursued, and took the prisoner.
   TIMOTHY TOOLEY(police-constable H 32.) I was with Conerley - I took the cheeses.
 JOHN BALLINGALL WHYTE. I live in  Upper East Smithfield. I lost three cheeses - I have no doubt these are mine - they are different sorts - I had had 111 of this sort in, the day before.
 Prisoner's Defence. The gentleman hesitated to swear to them before the magistrate. I was standing at the London Dock gate for work, and a young man asked me to carry these cheeses in a basket to Shoreditch, and said he would meet me there - I did not know him.
   WILLIAM NORMAN(police-constable H 15.) I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got at Mr. Clark's Office - I know the prisoner is the person.(Read.)
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Transported for Seven Years.
 726.  JOHN TOFTS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April , 1 handkerchief, value 4d.; the goods of John Piper , from his person.
 JOHN PIPER. I live in Wheeler-street. I was walking with my wife at the end of  Church-street, Shoreditch, on the 5th of April, at a quarter before ten o'clock at night - I felt the prisoner's hand in my pocket - I turned round, and seized the prisoner as he was taking his hand away from my pocket - at the same time I kicked against my handkerchief on the ground - I said, "What did you touch my pocket for?" - my wife turned and said, "Here is your handkerchief" - I held the prisoner three or four minutes - he threatened if I did not let him go he would give me a b-y punch of the head - there was another with the prisoner, and he threatened me also - I let the prisoner go, and they both went about twenty yards down the street - they turned down a street, and both ran off - I turned back, and walked with my wife down Shoreditch, and met them both again - I then gave the prisoner in charge - I am positive he is the person.
   SARAH PIPER. I was with my husband. I felt something pulled from the side of me - I turned and saw this handkerchief on the ground - my husband seized the prisoner - I am sure he is the person.
   HENRY CHARLES BARKER(police-constable H 26). I took the prisoner, and have the handkerchief.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming down the street, and this man accused me of stealing his handkerchief - a person had just rushed past me - he let me go; then met me again and gave charge of me.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Confined Six Months
 737.  EDWARD TAPPER was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of April , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s.; and 1 pair of buckles, set with diamonds, value 5l.; the goods of George Warriner , from the person of John Ellis.
 MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
 GEORGE WARRINER. I am a  wine-merchant. On the night of the 4th of April, I was at a party, No. 74, Newman-street - I left at three o'clock in the morning - I took off the shoes which I had worn at the party, and put my boots on - my shoes had a pair of diamond paste buckles in them - I put them and the buckles into my pocket - when I got to Hanway-street, I missed them - I returned to the house to inquire for them - I have not seen them since.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. They might have dropped from your pocket? A. Yes; I saw them last in the hall.
   WILLIAM CULVERHOUSE. I am in the service of Mr. Partington , a confectioner. I was at the house in Newman-street- I came away with Ellis, some of the party had then left - I saw a pair of shoes on the pavement, with a pair of buckles in them - Ellis took them up - this was close to the house - the prisoner, who was the policeman, and dressed like one, came up and said to Ellis, "You had better give me those shoes, I shall probably find out the owner" - and he took them from him - I saw the prisoner again on the Saturday night afterward, (this was on the Thursday) at my master's shop - I had seen him go past, and my master went after him, and brought him back - Ellis and I said he was the man - the prisoner asked us whether we were sure of it - we said, "Yes" - and he said, "You have got that to prove at the bar."
