233. MARGARET DORE , stealing 1 handkerchief, value 4 d  ., the goods of Mary Ann Pitt ; and 1 pair of gloves, 4 d  . the goods of Benjamin Pitt , her master. MARY ANN PITT. I am the daughter of Benjamin Pitt. The Prisoner was our servant—I lost a handkerchief about a mouth or six weeks before she was taken—my handkerchief was found on her at the station—this is it—( produced )—it is mine—these glove are my mother's—they were found on the prisoner. GEORGE BEAK( policeman, V  164.) I took the prisoner to the Section—I found this handkerchief and gloves on her—she said she did not know any thing about them.
   Prisoner.  My mistress made me a present of the gloves, and I said I did not want them; I lived six months with her, and she did not pay me my wages. MARTHA PITT. I did not give her these gloves, but another pair very much like them—she has got them in her bundle now—I owe her some wages, but not six months'—I owe her 1 l . 0 s . 6 d .—the ladies I work for will not tell me the value of all the articles. NOT GUILTY
 234. MARGARET DORE was again  indicted for stealing 1 petticoat, value 1 s  ., the goods of Charlotte Randall . CHARLOTTE RANDALL. I am servant to Mrs. Rann, of Upper Bedford-place. Mrs. Pitt washed for us—sent her this petticoat to wash—it was not returned—it is my property. GEORGE BECK( policeman, V  164.) I found this petticoat in the prisoner's bundle at the station-house.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I made the petticoat, and brought it from Ireland. GUILTY . Aged 17.— Confined Four Months .
 235. GEORGE BROWN  , alias    Dixon stealing 15 sovereigns, 1 half-crown and 10 shillings, the money of Michael Horden Peek .
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. MICHAEL HORDEN PECK. I am a dry-salter, at Southwark-bridge. The prisoner was in my service about four months—on the 16th Oct. I gave him fifteen sovereigns, a half-sovereign, and 10 s . in silver, to take to Spa-road, Bermondsey, to Mrs. Julia Adams, a manufacturing chemist—Mr. Barber manages her business for her—I have done business for them for years—the prisoner did not come back—I did not see him again till the Tuesday fort-night—I charged him with this—he made no reply—it was his duty to come back, and he did not.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALDWIN. Q.  Are you a manufacturing chemist? A.  No, a dry-salter—I supply muriate of lime and oil of vitriol—I had connexion in business about two months ago with a man named Bateman—I know Marshall-street—I have no place of business there, Bateman had—when I did business with him there was an illicit still there—I had nothing to do with it, I had no benefit from it—I used sometimes to get sweet spirits and allum.
