 1611.  THOMAS GINSON was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of August , 1 watch, value 20s., the goods of George Birch , from his person.
 GEORGE BIRCH. I am a  carpenter, and live in Bear-street, Clare-market. On Sunday, the 6th of August, about twelve o'clock in the day, I was turning out of Broad-street, St. Giles', into Drury-lane, and saw four or five persons there, together; one of them, who, to the best of my knowledge, was the prisoner, came and put his hand on my shoulder, and pulled out my watch, above my head- he ran away; I gave an alarm, and cried Stop thief! the prisoner was taken on the Wednesday, and, to the best of my belief, he is the man.
   GEORGE JAMES JENNINGS. I am a Bow-street patrol. From information I received from Griffiths, who has absconded, I took the prisoner.
   JOHN BARBER. I live in Drury-lane, and am a hatter. On the 6th of August, about twelve o'clock, I was in my shop - I heard a voice exclaiming, "Don't take the man's watch;" I went, and saw the prosecutor on the ground, and a man going across the road, up Vine-street, but I do not think it was the prisoner.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1612.  BENJAMIN CHATTERTON was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of August , 4 sovereigns, 3 half-sovereigns, and 15 shillings, the goods of Edward Parr , from his person.
 EDWARD PARR. I am a  broker, and live in Harp-alley, Strand. On the 8th of August, about six o'clock in the evening, I was in a house which may be deemed a lodging-house; it is a private house, and has been mine for upwards of twenty years - I had lived there one night- there had been soldiers quartered there upwards of eleven years; I had recently come out of trouble, as I was convicted illegally; I went to my house, where I found the prisoner; I had known him before; I gave him a bottle, and he got some gin. I begged him to dine with me, which he did - there were four persons in the house. After dinner I laid down on the sofa, and fell asleep; I had four sovereigns, three half-sovereigns, and fifteen or sixteen shillings in my purse, in the left pocket of my small clothes; it appears I could not have slept two minutes before the house-keeper, Mary Jenkins, awoke me; the servant said to her, "Did so and so give you my master's money?" - I felt in my pocket, and my money was gone, and the prisoner also; he sat at my right hand, very near me, before.
 COURT. Q. Are you sober now? A. Yes - I have taken nothing.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PHILLIPS. Q. Where did you dine to day? A. I have had no dinner, upon my solemn oath; I may have drank some porter; I do not recollect taking only part of a pint.
   ELIZABETH CHATTERTON. I lived servant with Mr. Parr. I was hired by Mrs. Jenkins, the house-keeper - I have often seen the prisoner - he was an acquaintance of Parr's, and he knew Mrs. Jenkins; on the 8th of August, tween six and seven o'clock in the evening, Parr came home - the prisoner was there - they shook hands and Parr asked him to dine, which he did; Mr. Parr laid down on the sofa - I went into the kitchen, and when I returned, the prisoner was lying upon Parr, and his hand in his left trouser's pocket, and his waistcoat was unbuttoned; I said"What are you doing, Sir;" Nothing, said he, and he gave me a push with his arm, and put a brown purse into his pocket - he said, "I am in haste, I can't wait any longer;" I called the house-keeper, and said, "I will not move out of this place till you send for an officer;" the officer came, and I was searched; I did not know but what his roguishness might have said (as there were nobody in the room but him and me,) that I had taken it.
 Cross-examined. Q. You would not for the world be in a house with a bad character? A. I am forced to do many things I do not like for a bit of bread; this is a lodging-house; I do not know whether they let lodgings for an hour.
   ISAAC SAYER. I am an officer. The housekeeper was accused of the robbery by Parr - she wished to be searched, and I searched her very strictly - she then informed me that Chatterton had gone off with the money; I went to his house and searched him, but found nothing.
     NOT GUILTY.

   MARTHA FRANCIS. I am the wife of George Francis - he is a musician, and is now at Boulogne - we lived together till he went abroad; he has been gone a year and a half, and I have no expectation of seeing him again - he has engagements in France, which suit him better than being here; I have been married to him eleven years - I live at No. 16, Lisle-street, Leicester-square- the prisoner was my cook eight or nine months; I had been absent from home for ten days or a fortnight previous to the 10th of August; I left her to sleep in my house; I found her there on my return; I received information from a person who works for me, and went with an officer to the corner of Cecil-court, Castle-street, on the 12th of August; I found the prisoner there in company with two or three persons, with whom she had gone to lodge; she had left me on the 11th by my request, but I had not missed any articles till I received information; I found this property there in a box which I knew to be her's; I had left two servants in the house; she said the tea-caddy was her's - I said I had such a one - the towels and the cotton I can positively speak to.
