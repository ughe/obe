However from several expressions, the prosecutor and his friends were convinced that they did know the prisoner, and in a little while after they heard the feet of two people walking, as if in great confusion over their heads, and they were then pretty well satisfied, that the prisoner at the bar was in the house, and was in company with Mrs. Sandon, who has been the unfortunate cause of this prosecution; Gentlemen, the prosecutor and his friends went up stairs, there they met a servant, coming out of a room with a candle in her hand, and one of the friends of the prosecutor took a candle from her, and they were going to enter the room, Mr. Sandon the husband, was going in first, he was willing and desirous to be thoroughly satisfied, whether the prisoner and his wife were there or not, and immediately a pistol was fired by somebody; that it was loaded, was most certain, because the ball passed through a part of his hat, there was no light in the room at the time, but however, he saw by the firing of the pistol, that it was perhaps the prisoner at the bar, he was pretty positive at that time, however, they called fresh assistance and took the prisoner and Mrs. Sandon; the witnesses will inform you, in what situation they found the prisoner at the bar, and how they found Mrs. Sandon, I believe they were both armed at that time, Mrs. Sandon had pistols loaded in her hand, as well as the prisoner; whether or no, the prisoner was the person that fired the pistol at Mr. Sandon, it will be for you to enquire; there is little doubt, I should apprehend, that it will be proved to be him; if you find that he fired the pistol, you will have no difficulty in pronouncing him guilty of shooting, maliciously and feloniously, against Mr. Sandon: Gentlemen, we shall call our witnesses and prove the facts; and if you believe he was the person that fired the pistol at the prosecutor, you will find him guilty.
   MARY JONES sworn.
 Examined by Mr. Chetwood. Where do you live? - At the Royal Hotel, in Long Acre.
 Did you live there in October last? - Yes?
 Did you ever see the prisoner at the bar? - Yes.
 Did you ever see him at that house? - Yes.
 Do you remember him there on the 24th of October, and do you remember Mr. Sandon, the prosecutor, coming to that house? - Yes.
 Was the prisoner in the house at that time? - No Sir, not when they came in first, he was out.
 Did he come in during the time Mr. Sandon was there? - Yes, and he went up stairs into the room.
 Who was with him? - A lady.
 Did you see Mr. Sandon go up stairs afterwards? - No, I was in the room with the Count, at the time they came up stairs.
