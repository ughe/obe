 1839.  WILLIAM PATTISON FLANAGAN was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of September , 1 coat, value 20s.; 2 handkerchiefs, value 2s.; 1 watch, value 30s.; 1 seal. value 5s.; 1 key, value 2s. 6d., and 11 sovereigns, the property of Edward Swiney , in the dwelling-house of Sarah Mountain.
   EDWARD SWINEY. I am a coachman, and lodge at Sarah Mountain's, Short's-garden, St. Giles'. I lost the property stated, from my lodging room, where I slept - the prisoner's wife came to visit me; she is my own sister - I left her in the room, on Wednesday afternoon, the 2nd of September; when I came back the property was gone out of my box - the officer found my watch on the prisoner's person; I believe I lost it all on the 2nd of September; I did not see the prisoner there.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1840.  ROBERT CROSS was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Rebecca Smith , on the 15th of August , at St. George, and stealing therein, 2 frocks, value 2s.; 1 bed-gown, value 6s.; 1 pelisse, value 4s.; 9 yards of printed cotton, value 7s.; 1 scarf, value 3s., and 1 shawl, value 5s., her property.
   REBECCA SMITH. I am a widow, and have three children - I live at No. 2, Church-lane, in the parish of St. George. On Saturday. the 15th of August, I went out at half-past eleven o'clock in the morning - there are no lodgers; it is a small house, with two rooms and a kitchen; I locked my door, and saw two bad boys throwing stones; I turned back, and put my shutters too - my children were at that time at school, and no one was in the house; I locked my door very particularly, and when I came home at a quarter-past twelve o'clock, I met my little boy, who had come home from school; he said to me,"How came you to leave your window open;" I went to the house, and found the window was open, and the curtain torn down; the parlour door was wide open - my drawers were open, and the property stated was gone; the person had got in by pulling open the shutters, and lifting up the window, which was down, and fastened by a latch. I found my property at a pawnbroker's on the Monday - I know the prisoner, and knew his father; they did live three or four doors from my house, but they had moved a short time before; his father is a watchman.
   JOHN GEORGE. I am a pawnbroker, and live in High-street, Shadwell. On the 15th of August the prisoner pawned this gown-piece with me.
   JOSEPH THOMPSON. I am a pawnbroker, in Broad-street, Ratcliff. I have a shawl and a frock, pawned by the prisoner, between twelve and one o'clock on the 15th of August.
   HENRY OSMOND. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Broad-street, Ratcliff. On Saturday, the 15th of August, the prisoner pawned a child's coat, for 2s.
   JOHN NORRIS. I am an officer. I was sent for, and took the prisoner - he took me to the pawnbroker's.
   REBECCA SMITH. These articles are my property; I am quite sure my window was shut down - I thought it would not open without breaking, but it did, and he had broken a knife in opening the drawers.
 Prisoner's Defence (written.) I have never before, on any occasion, been accused of an offence against the laws of my country, but have lived with my parents, and have at all times maintained a good character with those who knew me. This act I was tempted to commit, at the request of  William Budd, who opened the window of the house, and asked me to get in, and told me what to do; when I came out he took the bundle and went away with it; afterwards sharing the 10s. This statement is not made with a view of exeulpating myself, by implicating him, but to shew the Court, that although I am guilty of an offence, the commission of it was not contemplated or planned, but accidental, in consequence of meeting with William Budd.
 Three witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
 GUILTY - DEATH Aged 13. Recommended to Mercy by the Jury, on account of his character and youth, and believing him to be seduced by Budd.
 1841.  GEORGE SICE was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 1 watch, value 1l.; 1 pair of breaches, value 15s.; 1 pair of shoes, value 2s.; 1 shirt, value 5s.; and 1 pair of trousers, value 2s., the goods of James Cox ; and 2 coats, value 3l. 10s., and 1 gold seal, value 5s., the goods of George Cox ; and 2 1/2 yards of woollen cloth, value 30s., the goods of David Callaghan , in the dwelling-house of James Cox.
   DAVID CALLAGHAN. I am a journeyman-tailor, and work at Mr. Cox's, Little Russell-street, Bloomsbury. The prisoner is a shoemaker, and boarded in the house, but - did not lodge there - I missed this piece of cloth on the 26th of July; the prisoner had been there a good deal before - I left the cloth there on the Saturday night, and it was taken.
   JOHN GANT. I am a pawnbroker, and live in London-street, Tottenham-court-road. I took in this coat on the 27th of July, from Kilerby.
   BENJAMIN KILERBY. I am a tailor, and have known the prisoner about six months; I pawned a piece of cloth and a pair of breeches for him, but not this coat.
   GEORGE MACKAY. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Grafton-street, Fitzroy-square. I have a remnant of cloth and a pair of breeches, which were pawned by Kilerby.
   BENJAMIN KILERBY. These are what I pawned, by the desire of the prisoner - I got 25s. for them, and gave it to him.
   DAVID CALLAGHAN. This cloth is my property.
   JAMES COX. This watch and other articles are mine.
   GEORGE COX. These articles are mine.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the property had been stolen by a boy named  George Weston, in whose company he was. GUILTY (of stealing to the value of 25s.) Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
