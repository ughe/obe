EDWARD AGER, builder, High Street, Plaistow. On Saturdar.
August 31, about 11 p.m., I went to see a neighbour a few doon
away, leaving my door ajar. After a short time I returned and met
Charles Smith and a policeman; on going into my house I found
that two vases, which were there when I left, had disappeared their
value was 5s.6d. I have known prisoner several years and have
employed him. At the police court he declared that he took these
vases for wages due to him; I say there were no wages due at all
I do not owe him a penny. I never authorised him to take the
vases.
To Prisoner. You saw me during the week. I paid Ellis, the
fishmonger, 3s. out of wages due to vou; this was by your instrue
tions.
CHARLES Smars, labourer, 6, Albert Road, Plaistow. On August 31,
about 11.20 p.m., I was sitting in my front garden when prisoner
came and asked me if I wanted to buy a pair of vases; I said,
"No"; he said, "Come on, you can have them for a shilling." I
asked him where he had pinched them; he said he got them from
the fair at Wanstead. He showed them to me; they rattled, and I
told him he would break them; so he turned them upside dom
Three or four cards fell out; one had on the name and address of
Mr. Ager, so I took the card to him. I did not take the cases;
prisoner took them away.
Detective-sergeant FREDERICK STEVENS, Plaistow. On Septes
ber 1 I went to prisoner in a public-house and told him that i w
8 police officer and should arrest him for stealing a pair of vases
from Ager's house the previous night. He said, " All right." I
said, "I shall send you to the station while I go to your house.
He said, “It's no use going round there, they're smashed; I would
have smashed his head if I had met him." At the station ba
made no reply to the charge. I have not been able to find the
vares.
PRISONER. What I said was that if I had met him I should have
hit him on the head with the vases.
Prisoner's statement before the magistrate : "I took them for
wages."
PRISONER (not on oath). I met Ager several times during the
week and asked him for my wages; he said, "All right, old boy.
On the Saturday I went to his house 20 times, and he was always
out. I met Ellis, the fishmonger, who told me, "I have seen Ager,
and he has given me 2s. out of your money, and he is going to give
me the other ls. by and bye"; I said, "What, give you 3s, out of
3s. 4d.; how am I going on about dinner to-morrow if I've only go
4d. to take ?" I subpoenaed Ellis for to-day, but he refused to take
the subpoena.
Verdict, Guilty. Prisoner confessed to a previous conviction,
and his record was & very bad one. Sentence, 12 months baru
labour.
