MARY CHEESEMAN. I am the wife of William Robert Cheeseman; he is a printer's warehouseman; we live in Collingwood-street. On the 4th of February I was standing with my child in my arms, looking at the show; I felt the prisoner brush by my side - I turned to look at him, and put my hand outside my clothes and felt my money; I put my hand in my pocket to be more certain, and felt my purse and money - I pulled my gown over it; I then saw the prisoner by my side again - my child being heavy, I put my right hand to its back; I then felt the prisoner touch me again, and missed my money - I turned and looked at him; he walked one or two steps - I caught hold of him; he broke from me, and I cried Stop thief! three or four times; he got a few yards, and was stopped -I had 6s. 9 1/2d. in my purse.
 Cross-examined. Q. Has the purse been found? A. No; there was no person on my right hand but the prisoner; there were a good many on my left.
   THOMAS BARKER. I live in Britannia-street. I was at my window - I went down; the prisoner was brought back, and I took charge of him - I found on him, one crown, one half-crown, some shillings and sixpences, and half pence; in all 14s., but no purse.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Transported for Seven Years.
 750.  THOMAS LODGE was again indicted for stealing, on the 4th of February , 1 crown, 2 shillings, and 2 sixpences, the monies of Henry Jennings , from the person of Diana Jennings.
   THOMAS BARKER. I am a City officer. I heard the alarm on the 4th of February, and took the prisoner about nine o'clock in the evening; I found 14s. on him, and amongst it a crown-piece.
   DIANA JENNINGS. I am the wife of Henry Jennings. On the 4th of February I was near my own door, in Britannia-street, with my child in my arms; I heard the alarm, and felt in my pocket; I missed a crown-piece, two shillings, and two sixpences; I saw the crown-piece found on the prisoner, and I know it was mine, by some spots of wax on it; my husband is a shoemaker - I had it when I was at my own door.
   ELIZA FRANCES JENNINGS. I was standing by my mother, looking at the exhibition; I found the prisoner standing looking over my shoulder, close to my mother.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years more.
 751.  HENRY LEE and GEORGE LEWIS were indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of February , 1 shoulder of mutton, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Thomas Mendey.
   CHARLES WILLIAM SNOOK. I live in Garden-row, Brick-lane. On the 2nd of February I was standing at the corner of Wellington-street - I saw the prisoners playing; I afterwards saw Lewis lift Lee up, and he took a shoulder of mutton from Mr. Mendey's shop - I saw Lee in custody, but Lewis ran away; I am certain of them.
   THOMAS MENDEY. I live in Brick-lane, and am a butcher; I was in my shop - I saw Lee on the back of another boy, and while I was serving a customer a little girl told Snook that a boy had run away with some meat.
   JAMES TAYLOR. I am an officer. I took Lewis in Smithfield - I believe the prisoners were in great distress.
   DANIEL CHAPMAN. I am an officer. I received charge of Lee.
 LEE -    GUILTY. Aged 12.
 LEWIS -    GUILTY. Aged 16. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 752.  WILLIAM LOWE was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of January , 8 handkerchiefs, value 10s., the goods of Peter Pige ; and that he had before been convicted of felony.
   THOMAS STERN. I am shopman to Peter Pige, a pawnbroker, of Church-street, Bethnal-green. On the morning of the 21st of January, I saw the prisoner close to the window - I afterwards saw him running; I went out, stopped him, and took him back to the shop, and found on him these eight silk handkerchiefs, which are my master's.
 PETER PIGE. I had seen these handkerchiefs safe about ten minutes before.
 Prisoner. Q. Where had they been hanging? A. Over the iron bar outside the window - they were tied by a string; a little girl told me you snatched them off - I pursued, and saw you trying to tuck them under your jacket, as you were running.
 Prisoner's Defence. The handkerchiefs were laying under the shop-window; she kicked them with her foot - my wife died three days after I was put in prison, and left four children; I was discharged from the chilvers brig in September.
   EDWARD DARKIN. I am an officer. I produce a certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office - he had one month imprisonment in the House of Correction; I know he is the person.
     GUILTY. Aged 55. - Confined Fourteen Days.
 753.  HENRY MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of February , 3 locks, value 4s.; 8 screws, value 2d., and 8 brass handles, value 2s., the goods of David Browne , and fixed to a building of his.
 SECOND COUNT, stating them to belong to  Thomas Copeman.
   JOHN MATTHEWS. I live in Selby-street, Waterloo-town. On the 6th of February I was in my front room; I saw the prisoner facing my house, opposite the door of an empty house - he went in there, and was there twenty minutes; he went up stairs, came down, took the street door in his hand, and uncrewed the lock - I went out and tried my key to the door; it would not open it - I thought he had gone out, but he had not; he opened the door - I said, "You are a prety rascal, are you not?" I saw some locks in his pocket, and I sent for Humphreys.
   EPHRAIM HUMPHREYS. I live in Agnes-street, Waterloo-town, and am a carpenter. I went to No. 21, Selby-street on the 6th of February - I found the prisoner in custody; I took him to the station - I saw him searched, and three locks, four keys, four handles, two screw-drivers, and a brad-awl found on him.
   THOMAS COPEMAN. I live at No. 24, Selby-street, and am a shoemaker. I have the care of No. 21, which is opposite Mr. Matthews' - I saw the locks and keys all safe three days before; I missed these articles; they all fit the doors exactly.
