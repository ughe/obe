919. GEORGE BROWN (18) and JOHN SMITH (23), Robbery on
David Pitkin, and stealing from his person 21. 158., his monies.
MR. ST. AUBIN conducted the Prosecution; and MR. LILLEY defended Smith
DAVID PITKIN. I am a seaman, stopping at the Sailors Home, Wells
Street-on 26th February, about 1.40a.m., I was in the Commercial Road, and
the prisoner Brown put his hand in my pocket; I pushed him away-I am
a German-Smith then came and took me by the throat, and knocked me
down on the stones--I missed 21, 158, from my pocket.
Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY. I was sober. I have kept sober all the time
I have been a sailor- I had been for a walk-I had no ship at that time
-you can go into the Sailors' Home at any time of night you like; the
door-keeper is there-I had been with my two shipmates to one public-
house, but only one that was at 10 o'clock-I had left the Home about
4 o'clock, after dinner-I had been walking from 4 till 10 o'clock-I had never
soen either of the prisoners before I have a mark here where I was knocked
down-I saw my money in the public-house-1 treated my shipmates—I
treated myself to a drop at the same time.
By THE COURT. I left the Home about 4 o'olock, that was the day before
I walked about till 1.40 the next morning--I was going from one ship to
another, trying to get a ship—the Dooks close in London sometimes at 10
and sometimesat 11 o'clock - I do not know at what time the East India Doeks
close you can sleep whenever you like at the Sailors' Home-if you go to
bed at 4 o'clock, you sleep on as long as you like.
• JOSEPH WHITING (Policeman H 254). On 26th February, I was on duty
in the Commercial Road, and heard a cry of “Murder” and “ Police" – I
ran and saw the two prisoners running away—I followed them, and stopped
Smith first, and then Brown-Smith broke away from me, and I detained
Brown-I asked him what he was running for, he said "Nothing LI took
him to the bottom of the court where we met the prosecutor, who said
"That man has robbed me."
Cross-examined by MR. LILLEY. The cry seemed to proceed from 200 yards
from where I was standing that is the main thoronghfare--all the shop
lights were out; but a public-house there burns lights all night long-I
caught Smith by the collar as he tried to run past me in the court-they
ran out of High Street into the court--we had a struggle and he broke
away-that was the only opportunity I had of seeing him-1 had never seen
him before.

GEORGE WITHEY (Policeman H 213). I was on duty in Commercial Street
at 1.40, and saw Smith running-I stopped him and asked him what he
was running for-he said "Nothing - I got him on the ground and a cab-
man came galloping up the street and said, in the prisoner's presence, that
& sailor had been knocked down in Whitechapel Road ; that another police-
man had taken a man to the station, and Smith was his mate-Smith made
no answer to that, I took him to the station, the prosecutor was there,
and said that is the man who knocked me down.
Cross-examined. The cabman drove away I did not take his name or
number-Smith made a butt at me, and we were both on the ground, and
I was holding Smith by his cravat.
