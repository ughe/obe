532. WILLIAM WATSON (35), Burglary in the dwelling-house of
William Castang, and stealing therein five overcoats and other articles.
MR. WOODGATE Proscouted.
EMMA CASTANG. I am the wife of Edward Castang, of 60, Albion
Road, Dalston-on Sunday, 20th March, I shut up the house myself, and
locked the front street door, but I could not say about the window; it
was quite closed, and the curtains were pinned to and the blind was
down-next morning I came down about half-past 6, and observed that
the curtains had been unpinned, and a pile of things gone which were
lying on the sofa in the breakfast parlour-I know these boots produced);
they were taken from the breakfast parlour with a pair of child's kid
boots.
Cross-ocaminod. Two pots which had been in the window had been
put into the area to enable them to get in.
EDWARD PARNELL (Policeman J 374). I was on duty on 27th March
about 7 o'clock and saw the prisoner with some other men-I watched
them, and in consequence of what I saw I arrested the prisoner, and
found on him a jemmy, a candle and matches, and a small knife-the other
two men ran away when they saw me apprehend the prisoner-he was
trying to hand the jommy to a man, and I looked over his shoulder and
saw it, and shook him, and it dropped on the pavement-at the station
Sergeant Brockwell went and visited the prisoner-at the time I arrested
the prisoner he was wearing these boots (produced).
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrato. "I bought the boots for
two shillings in Petticoat Lane."
He repeated the same statement in his defenoo.
EMMA CASTANG (Re-examined). I missed besides these boots five coats,
a cloth dolman, a child's pair of boots, and eightpence in change, which
was lying on the table.
Not GUILTY.
