247. MARIA SMITH was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of William Shans , at St. Matthew, Bethnal-green, on the 17th of November , and stealing, 2 pelisses, value 8l.; 4 curtains, 4l.; 6 dresses, value 6l.; 1 shawl, value 2l.; 3 table-cloths, value 15s.; 10 yards of carpet, value 10s.; 2 pairs of sheets, value 12s.; 3 pillow-cases, value 4s.; 8 handkerchiefs, value 8s.; 3 caps, value 10s.; 2 rings, value 10s.; 1 pin, value 4s.: 2 brooches, value 10s.; 1 clasp, value 1s.; 1 necklace, value 2s.; 1 time-piece, value 10s., and 10 sovereigns, his property.
   MARY SHANS. I am the wife of William Shans; we live at No. 5, Broughton-place, Hackney-road, in the parish of St. Matthew, Bethnal-green. On the 17th of November I went out, about half-past five o'clock in the morning, to market - I keep a small fruit-shop, and my husband is a gentleman's servant- I locked the door, and had the key in my pocket; all the doors and windows were secure - I returned at a quarter before seven o'clock, and found the outer door closed, but the moment I put the key to it, I found it was open - I went in, and found an inner door quite open, which I had left locked, and had the key in my pocket; both doors must have been opened with picklock keys - I was afraid to go in, but my husband's sister went in first - I stood at the outer door; she ran up stairs, and when I went in, I missed all the articles stated in the indictment, and several other things, which I cannot recollect - I value my pelisses at 8l., and my dresses at 6l. - I lost ten sovereigns from a writing-desk, and a great many other articles - I have recovered none of them - I went to the grocer's-shop, next door, and gave an alarm - I know nothing of the prisoner; she was taken on the following Friday.
   ANN HOLT. I live at Mr. Jones' No. 9, Broughton-place. On Saturday, the 17th of November, at half-past six o'clock in the morning, I was going out for my milk, I saw Shans' street door open, and two women coming out, with their aprons full of something; I saw two other women go in, within three minutes after - the prisoner was one of those I saw come out; she was the outside woman, and had her apron full; I did not know her before - I am quite sure she is one of them. I did not see the other two women come out - I did not give information.
   JOHN HOLLETT. I am a watchman - my beat is in Hackney-road. In consequence of information, and a description of the person, I and Rigg took the prisoner, and put her in the watch-house; we took another woman with her - Mrs. Holt identified the prisoner at the watch-house. The prisoner said to me at the watch-house, when the other woman was gone, "If I had spoke my bl-y mind, you would not have let that girl go;" she also said Mrs. Holt was a false-swearing old b-t-h.
   JOHN RIGG. I am a watchman. I had information of the robbery, and said I thought I should find the person - and on Friday night I and Hollett took the prisoner and another girl.
   JAMES GLIBBERY. I am a constable of Bethnal-green. The prisoner was brought to me on Friday night, about half-past nine o'clock - I found nothing on her but the key of her lodging.
 Prisoner's Defence. On Wednesday night I spoke to Rigg, near the Olive Branch public-house, and he said,"Let me have none of your noise to-night - you are very abusive when in liquor;" and on the Friday night following I went with a young woman to have some drink; he came over, held up his light, and took me to the watch-house - Holt said at first that I had a bundle, which she thought was was broken victuals.
 ANN HOLT re-examined. It did not seem like broken victuals; it was bulky, and more like wearing-apparel than any thing else.
 JAMES GLIBBERY re-examined. The prisoner lives about three quarters of a mile from the prosecutrix's; she gave me a false direction the first night, but next morning she gave me a right direction; I found the place, and searched it, and when I went in I found the bed was warm, though I had the key in my pocket.
   LEAH REYNOLDS. I am a Leghorn trimming weaver, and live at No. 1, Shepherd's-court, Old Nichols-street, in the adjoining room to the prisoner. On the Friday night she came home very much in liquor, between ten and eleven o'clock - she asked me for a light, which I gave her; and at three in the morning, I was taken very ill, and called out,"Maria Smith, are you in bed?" she said Yes, and asked if she should get up; I said No: I laid awake till seven, when my husband went out, and she asked me what time it was - I went into her room at nine, and she asked me to lend her a few halfpence, to get a breakfast. This was last Friday three weeks.
 Q. How long had she lived there? A. She had lived there about a fortnight before the robbery; a young man used to come backwards and forwards to her; he was there that Friday night; they occupied a ready furnished room, up one pair of stairs - I remember the officer searching that room; the young man had slept there the night before the officer came.
 Q. How did he get in? A. I think he drew the staple of the door. The prisoner keeps good hours - I do not know how she gets her living; she comes home about eleven or ten o'clock; I do not know whether the young man is her husband - I do not know what he is called. I go to bed about half-past nine or ten o'clock; my husband is a cabinet-maker, and has worked for Mr. Wright nearly four years, and I work when I can get any.
 Q. What makes you so particularly remember the night in question? A. Because she came to my door for a light - she has done so before. I get up about half-past eight o'clock - I did not get up that morning till near nine; the prisoner was not up when I went into her room; I spoke to her about three o'clock, about seven, and again at nine; I have been taken ill before, and called to her. There is no lock to our street door, only a small latch. I do not know how she or the young man get their bread.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 22.
 SIXTH DAY. WEDNESDAY, DECEMBER 12.
 Fifth Middlesex Jury - Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
