 927.  ROBERT WESTWICK, alias    MITCHELL, was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of March , 2 shoes, value 2s., the goods of William Hayhan.
   JOHN JENKINS. I am in the employ of William Hayhan; he is a shoemaker, and lives in Ratcliff-highway. On the 2nd of March, a person said a man had taken some shoes - I went out, and found the prisoner about twenty doors off I said, "You have stolen my master's shoes" - he said he had not - I told him he had - he then pulled them from under his jacket; they were master's, and had been hanging at our door.
 Prisoner's Defence I was passing the shop - I picked up the shoes a yard and a half from the prosecutor's door; I carried them publicly in my hand to see who would own them, till I got near Old Gravel-lane; I then put them under my jacket - this man asked me for them, and I gave them to him.
     GUILTY. Aged 60. - Confined Three Weeks.
 928.  THOMAS POSTON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of March , 4 pieces of carpet, value 3s., and 40 pieces of candle, value 6d., the goods of James Willis.
   JAMES WILLIS. I keep  Fladong's-hotel, Oxford-street. The prisoner was in my service twenty-two months, as porter, and was so on the 1st of March; many gentlemen complained of losing property - I got a search-warrant, and found on his premises a quantity of carpet, pieces of candle, and a number of duplicates of glass, which I believe to be my property; I have pieces of carpet similar to those found, and the candles are a peculiar make - I have missed candles from time to time.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What is the peculiar make? A. They are made to fit candlesticks which I have - they are not a common length; I had them made short for the use of the bed-room; they are tallow - I had not sold any carpets - I had a man named Hill in my employ; I sold him some carpet once, but I sold none while the prisoner lived with me - when I said I had not sold any carpets, I meant while the prisoner had lived with me - some of these pieces are old, and some are much worn - I know nothing of the prisoner buying any carpet of Hill - I may have sold him six or seven patterns, or there might be twenty; these may be called remnants; they are cut up I took the prisoner on the recommendation of our head porter, Hill - I believe he had lived formerly with my predecessor, for four or five years.
   EDWARD RURRIDGE. I am an officer, of High-street. I went to the prisoner's lodgings on the 14th of March; he was not at home, but his wife was - I found some of these carpets on the floor, and some on a chair - the candles were in a cupboard - I was called down stairs, and when I got up again, I found his wife had removed them into another room - I took the prisoner there between five and six o'clock next morning, and told him it was on suspicion of stealing various things from his master - he said, "My wife has told me all about it; I have not taken any thing" - he did not show the least fear.
     NOT GUILTY.
 929.  JANE TAYLOR was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of March , 2 pewter pots, value 2s., the goods of George White.
   JAMES PAKES. I am servant to George White, who keeps  the Hope and Anchor, in Hereford-street. On the 8th of March, about eight o'clock in the evening, I was telling my brother what a quantity of pots had been stolen, and I observed the prisoner in the tap-room - I was standing at the door; she came out and stood by my side, and then shifted to the lower step - I heard something rattle, saw her stooping, and I went to her, and picked up a pot; where it came from I cannot tell - I took her to my master, and on looking down, saw another pot hanging below her clothes - he sent for an officer.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you take the pot from under her clothes? A. No; it hung below her clothes; I cannot say what it hung to; she was tipsy- she said it was tied to her gown - there were a set of fellows larking in the room - she complained it had been tied to her tail - I do not believe she meant to steal them.
     NOT GUILTY.

 931.  CHARLES ALLINGTON was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 2 fixtures, (i. e.) 2 coppers, belonging to Samuel Ridge , and fixed to a building; against the Statute, & c.
 SAMUEL RIDGE. I live at  Bishop Bonner's-hall, in the parish of Bethnal-green. On the 25th of February I missed two coppers from a house of mine in the neighbourhood, which I had let.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. I believe you had the misfortune to have a tenant named Kendall? A. I had - he ran away; I had my rent, because the goods were sold - the key was delivered up to me the day this bill was found; I do not know where Kendall is.
   WILLIAM CASTELL. I was in a barn at Bonner's-hall on a Friday morning - I heard something; I went out, and saw the prisoner with two coppers on his back - he threw them down when he saw me, and ran off; I have seen them fitted to two places in the kitchen at Bonner's-hall, and they fit exactly; he was twenty or thirty yards from the house when I saw him - there are gates in front of the house, and they were open.
 Cross-examined. Q.Do you know Kendall? A. I know he lived there - he is gone off; the door was open, but had not been broken.
