2329. JAMES GLIBBERY was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of June , 1 clock, value 3 l  ., the goods of Samuel Taylor . SAMUEL TAYLOR. I live at Nos. 23 and 24, Tysoe-street, Clerkenwell. On the 11th of June, about nine o'clock in the evening, I saw my clock safe on a bracket in the back-parlour of No. 24—the door of No. 23 was open, which communicates with that back-parlour—I missed the clock at eleven o'clock—I saw the prisoner at the station-house on the 1st of Au gust, and charged him with stealing it—he said he had bought it of a man whom he knew, but he did not know where be lived, and he had taken it to Mr. Smith's, an auctioneer, in Guildford-place, and obtained 1 l . on it, to pay the man of whom he bought it. WILLIAM SMITH. I am an auctioneer, and live in Guildford-place. The prisoner came to me, on the 11th of June, with this clock—he asked me to lend him 1 l ., which I did—I did not ask where he got it, as he had been in the habit of bringing goods and getting money on them. RICHARD WHITE. I am a broker, and live in Ray-street, Clerkenwell. I bought this clock of Mr. Smith for 1 l . 15 s . JAMES GOODEVE. I bought this clock of Mr. White. WILLIAM BRADLEY.( police-constable G  165.) I traced the clock through the different hands, and went to the prisoner's father, who is a broker—I waited till the prisoner came in—I asked him if he recollected having a clock in June—he said yes, and he had taken it to Mr. Smith's—I said, "Where did you get it?"—he said, "I bought it of a man I know by sight, but I don't know where he lives; he uses a house just by my father's"—I said, "Could you find him now?"—he said he did not know—he looked about for nearly two hours, but could not find him.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I know the young man very well—I bought some chairs of him—I think he said he lived in Fetter-lsne—he asked me if I would buy a clock—I said I could not tell till I saw it—he brought it, and asked me 30 s . for it—I said I would give him 1 l .; and to know whether it was worth that or not, I went to Mr. Smith's, and got 1 l . on it. NOT GUILTY.
 2330. HENRY ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of July , 1 bag, value 1 s  .; and 200lbs. weight of sugar, value 6 l  .; the goods of Thomas Cobley , the younger, his master. THOMAS COBLEY, JUN. I live in Essex-street, Whitechapel, and am a master carman. The prisoner was in my service—on the 23rd of July, I sent him to St. Katharine's Docks for twenty bags of sugar—he was to deliver ten of them at Topping's wharf, and ten at Forster and Whistler's—I re ceived information, and went to Forster and Whistler's—I found he had delivered ten bags there all right—I went to Topping's wharf, and found he had only left nine bags there.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Did you give him the bags? A.  No. JOHN BIRD. I am a delivery foreman at St. Katherine's Docks. On the 23rd of July, I delivered twenty bags of East India sugar, about four o'clock in the afternoon, to Mr. Cobley's wagon, under the direction of the prisoner—ten of them were labelled to go to Topping's wharf—they weighed about 200lbs. each. DANIEL HAGGERTY. I am clerk at Topping's wharf, Tooley-street.
