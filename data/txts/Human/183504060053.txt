 931. JAMES SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the   17th of March , 1 cloak, value 7 l  ., the goods of William Corrie , in the dwelling-house of William Henry Morris , and another. GEORGE BEST( police-constable A  45.) On the morning of the 17th of March, about a quarter to twelve o'clock, I met the prisoner in New-street, Spring-gardens, carrying this cloak—part of it was tied up in a handkerchief, and part hanging out—I asked where he got it—he said a gentleman gave it to him in Spring-gardens to carry into the park, and he was to wait for him at a tree in the park—I took him there, but found no gentleman—I then went to the maker of the cloak, whose name was on it, and found out the prosecutor. WILLIAM CORRIE. I live at No. 43, Bloomsbury-square. This cloak is mine—I gave 8 l . 10 s . for it, in December last—it is lined with silk and velvet, and has a fur collar. On the 17th of March, about a quarter to one o'clock, I hung it up in the dwelling-house of William Henry Mor ris, Craven-street, Strand—I do not know the parish—it is a private house. GEORGE BEST. I am a policeman. Mr. Morris's house is in the parish of St. Martin-in-the-fields—I saw the maker's name on the cloak, "Keen, No. 2, New Bond-street."
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was coming down Charing-cross—a gentleman asked me if I had any thing to do, and asked me to go with him—he took me against a lamp, and told me to take the cloak to the park and wait at the first tree I came to, until he came to me—the policeman went with me—we went round the park—there was nobody there. GUILTY  of larceny only. Aged 17.— Confined Six Months.
