EDWARD Genet. I am a clerk in the Custom-house, and live on one side
of the chapel, and Mr. Bridgman on the other. I have a lodger who keeps a
little dog-it is a very sharp little dog, which barks at strangers and persons
coming in and out-I used sometimes to be bothered with its barking of a
night, and have gone out to see what was going on about a month before this
transaction, I beard from the servant at No. 17, that some persons had been
seen in the burial ground the night before-I mentioned the circumstance to Mr.
Bridgman next evening, and his son was in the yard at the time--Mr. Bridg.
man made use of a scriptural quotation, and said it did not matter so much if
the spirit was at rest, about the bones being disturbed; but if I thought there
was any likelihood of anybody coming into the chapel-yard, he would have a
watch set-I said I thought it might be a fancy of the lady next door-he
said perhaps it was his servant-girl, that she walked in her sleep, and per.
haps she walked into the chapel-yard-he said he had occasion, some years
since, to attend at Union-hall about a transaction of that kind, which took
place in his yard-I saw a fir-tree planted about the 11th or 12th, which
was not there before, to my knowledge.
SARAH GENET. A lodger of ours had a dog, which used to disturb us by
constantly barking-I heard from a lady at No. 17, that some persons had
been seen in the burial-ground, and remember my husband stating that to Me.
Bridgman, who replied, that he did not think any one came after the bodies in
the ground-he made some scriptural allusion, and said if the spirits were at
rest, it did not so much signify about the bodies-I do not recollect the exact
words, but he said if my husband thought so, he would have a watch in the
yard-my husband said, no, he did not think it was required-Mr. Bridg.
man said he did not know that any persons had come there for a very long
time, once there was a case of some persons being taken up, and he had to
appear against them about a fortnight or three weeks before this tomb was
disturbed, young Bridgman borrowed the lodger's dog-he said his father's
horse shyed at dogs, and he wanted to make it used to dogs- I think he had
a little fancy dog of his own at that time—he returned the lodger's dog in
about a week.
