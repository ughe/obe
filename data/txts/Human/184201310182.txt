SAMUEL HARRIS( police-constable L  177.) On Thursday, the 20th of January, about half-past one o'clock in the day, I was walking in the New Cut, which is no great distance from the prosecutor's premises, and saw the prisoner drunk—in consequence of something that had been told me, I went and took him into custody for being drunk and incapable of taking care of himself—I had not received information about the robbery then—I found on him six sovereigns, a half-sovereign, 3 s . in silver, one dozen and a half of spoons, one pepper-castor, and a cream jug, all metal and quite new—about three hours and a half afterwards, when he got better, he was asked in my presence how he came by this money—he said it was an annuity left him by his mother, and he had received at Cockburn's banking-house that day, the sum of 17 l .—he said he lodged at the house of Elizabeth Williams, No. 71, Granby-street—I went there that same night, and found a new blue pilot coat, a new suit of moleskin, some new flannel, a boa, some calico, three pairs of stockings, and a piece of toweling—I afterwards went to Mr. Lewis's shop, showed him the moleskin, and got from him this hat, round frock, and pair of trowsers—I produce all the articles—I was present when the prisoner was examined before the Magistrate—I told the Magistrate I had been to Cockburn's to make in quiry whether the prisoner had an annuity, and whether he received it on that day, and that they had told me no—the prisoner upon that remarked that he was drunk at the time he told me so—this cash-box and book I received from a stranger at Union Hall, on the 20th, who said he had picked them up. ISAAC LEWIS. I live in the New Cut On Wednesday evening, the 19th of January, the prisoner came to my shop, about a quarter to eight o'clock, and bought a suit of white moleskin and a pilot coat, which came 30 s .—he paid me 19 s . 6 d . and a fourpenny-piece in silver, and four half-to and two 5 s . papers of coppers—one packet was halfpence and the other penny-pieces—on Thursday he came again and bought a hat, a pair of trowsers, and a round frock, for which he paid me 26 s . in silver and 3 d .—the silver consisted of shillings and sixpences—he did not take those things away with him—I had not a hat his size, and he said, "I will pay you for the hat, get me one to fit, and I will call for it to-morrow"—I af terwards gave the things up to the officer—the suit of moleskin produced is what I sold the prisoner—he had been drinking when he came on Thursday—he was sober on Wednesday.
