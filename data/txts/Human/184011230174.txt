MARY NEVILLE. was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of No vember , 1 shawl, value 2 s  .; 1 petticoat, value 9 d  .; 1 apron, value 3 d  .; and 1 pair of boots, value 5 s  .; the goods of Timothy Coffee , her master. TIMOTHY COFFEE. I live in Ark-court. The prisoner was my ser vant, and left me on Sunday morning, the 1st of November, without notice—we then missed these things—the shawl my wife had lent her—I cannot swear to the property—I will take her back again. THOMAS DAVI. ( police-sergeant L  5.) The prisoner gave herself into custody to me—she said she had robbed her master—I found on her the duplicate of the boots, the petticoat, and apron. JAMES REARDON. I am a pawnbroker. I took in these things, but not of the prisoner. GUILTY. Aged 35.— Recommended to mercy.  — Confined Five Days.
 238. GEORGE BAKER. was indicted for a misdemeanor.
   MESSRS. CHAMBER . and PAYN . conducted the Prosecution. HENRY POMEROE. I live in Charles-street, Westminster-road. At a quarter to six o'clock in the evening of the 5th of November, the prisoner came to the shop, and asked for some squibs and crackers—I served him—he threw down a crown-piece—I took it up, and said, "I am afraid this won't do"—I went to the lamp, and said, "This is a bad one"—he said, "Give it me, and I will give you another"—I said, "No, I must detain you"—he went off, and having no other person in the shop, I could not pursue him—I gave the crown-piece to Cornelius. SAMUEL CORNELIU. ( police-constable L  83.) I received this crown-piece from Pomeroy. RALPH FEN WICK. Jun. I live in Regent-street, Kennington-cross, about twenty minutes' walk from Pomeroy's.
