220. ROBERT WALKER and THOMAS SHARP were indicted for
stealing 10lbs. weight of solder, value 58.; and 10lbs. weight of lead, 1s. 8d.,
the goods of William Turton Newton, and another, their masters,
MR. BODKIn conducted the Prosecution.
JOSEPH TAYLOR. I am foreman to William Turton Newton, and another,
of Margaret-street, Cavendish-square. The prisoners were employed by him,
Sharp as a journeyman plumber, and Walker as his labourer—it is the custom
for them to work in pairs-my masters were building in Lowndes-st., Lowndes-
square, Chelsea-about five in the afternoon of Tuesday, the 14th of Nov.,
I went there the plumbers work till half-past five-I went into the second
floor front room, and found them both-Walker was in one corner of the room,
with his face to the wall and his back towards me—he appeared to be button-
ing up his clothes-Sharp was standing about six feet from him-he was not
at work, and was quite near enough to see where Walker was--I asked
Walker what he was doing there-Sharp replied that his brace button was
burst-Walker gave no answer-I went up to him, ordered him to turn round
and let me see whether he had got any thing-he turned, and was walking
into another room-I told him to stop and unbutton his waistcoat-he did
not do it-I opened his waistcoat and found a bar of lead, and a bar of solder
rolled up in lead-I said, "This looks very like a brace button being
broken"--he had a belt round him, which was taken from him at the station,
that would enable him to carry these things-it has a strap to expand or con-
tract according to circumstances-Sharp remained in the room during the
whole of this, but did not say any thing-it would take a man a quarter of
an hour to roll the solder in the lead and put it on this belt--the lead would
have to be cut open first-it is pipe, worth about 1s. 8d., and the solder
about 58.
