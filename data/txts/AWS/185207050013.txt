707. HENRY ADAMS, feloniously uttering a forged 51. Bank note, with
intent to defraud.
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIaM HENRY DODDEMEADE. I am assistant to Mr. Lodge, a hosier, of
the Strand. The prisoner came there on 11th June, and asked to look at
some shirts-he selected two at 5s. each, and two neck ties at 38. 6d.; two
pairs cotton socks at 1s., and a merino waisteoat 3s. 6d. they came to
11. 2s. 6d.-he handed me a 5L-note-the colour of the paper attracted my
attention, and then the water mark-I asked him if be had any other money
-he said he had none-I told him I must go and get it changed-] went to
Drummonds', who told me it was a forgery-I then went to Scotland-yard,
and took a policeman back to the shop, having been absent about ten minutes
-the prisoner was given in charge- handed the note to Sandars at Scot-
land-yard; it had not been out of my possession-] told the prisoner it was
a forged note-he said he was quite innocent, and had received it on the
Exchange, in the way of business ; that it was only the second note be had
ever handled, and if it had been ten times its value he should not have known
better-he said at first that he was a stock broker, and then a stock broker's
clerk-when he was about to be given into custody, he said he hoped we
should not press it and offered to make any recompense for the trouble he
had given-when he got to the station he gave the name of Henry Adams-
the inspeetor asked his address, and he would not give it-the clerk of the
Court also asked his address, and he declined to give it--he said be hoped
I should not prosecute him, as he had got a wife and four children.
