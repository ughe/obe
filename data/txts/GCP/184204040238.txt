John MEMBRY. I am a butcher, and live at Royal-hill, Greenwieh.
About nine o'clock on Saturday evening, the 12th of March, the prisoner
came into my shop and told me to send Mr. Baldwin a small leg of mut-
ton-Mr. Baldwin was a customer of mine-I sent my boy with a leg of
mutton to Mr. Baldwin's--the constable came back with a leg of mutton
on the Sunday-it was then cut in two, and part of it cooked-1 could not
swear to it.
Cross-examined by Mr. BALLANTINE. Q. Are you certain about the
prisoner? A. Yes, confident-I was not told by the policeman that the
Magistrate ordered I should prosecute-he did not tell me himself that I
must.
Thomas GEORGE PHYPERS. I was sent with a leg of mutton to Mr.
Baldwin's—as I was going along Upper George-street the prisoner stopped
me and said, " Are you going to Mr. Baldwin's ?" I said, "Yes"-be
said, “Stop a minute, Mr. Baldwin is gone out"- I said, “Very well, I
will take this leg down to Mr. Lloyd, and call as I come back"-he met
me again-he said, “Mr. Baldwin is not at home yet, I don't like to pay
you for it till he sees it"-I said, “Very well, I will take it home"-he
said, “Stop a minute," he took it out of my basket and went towards
the door, and I went home.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you ever seen him before? A. Yes, he is a
French polisher-I did not say it was so dark I could not see his face, and
that I knew him by the smell of the paint-I am certain of that I let him
take the leg of mutton out of my basket.
John MEMBRY re-examined. I believed the prisoner's story to be true,
knowing him to be a neighbour of Mr. Baldwin's I thought he might
have called to order it-the boy had no authority from me to give it to the
prisoner-I should not have delivered it to him myself.
REUBEN BALDWIN. I never got this mutton.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 22.-Confined One Month.
