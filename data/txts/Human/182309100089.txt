 1183.  NICHOLAS THORP was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of July , a suit of boys clothes, value 5 s.; three shirts, value 2 s.; a night gown, value 6 d.; a cap, value 2 d., and a pinafore, value 6 d., the goods of Michael Clokeley , from the person of Amy Abbott.
   AMY ABBOTT. I am servant to Mr. Sayer, of Tower-street. On the 25th of July, about eight o'clock at night, I was going home from Whitechapel with John Clokely, who is seven years old; his father's name is Michael. Opposite Whitechapel church, a bundle was suddenly snatched from my arm by a man, it contained the property stated in the indictment; and he immediately ran up a court to the right. I saw it in half an hour at Spitalfields watch-house.
   THOMAS BROWN. I am a watchman of Whitechapel. On the 25th of August, I saw the prisoner pass the end of Montague-street into Wentworth-street, with a bundle under his right arm; I followed him, and when I came up with him, he was in the hands of an officer; I first saw him about two hundred yards from Whitechapel church. Almond said, he had dropped a bundle, and I found it about thirty yards from where it was taken.
   THOMAS ALMOND, On the 25th of July, about ten o'clock, I stopped the prisoner in Wentworth-street; he dropped the bundle when he saw me, and ran off; he fell, and I took him. The prosecutrix claimed it.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. A man ran by me and knocked me in the mud; then Almond laid hold of me and picked up the bundle; it was never in my possession.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. Transported for Seven Years.
 TWELFTH DAY, TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 22.
 Middlesex Cases, Fourth Jury,
 Before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1184.  BARTHOLOMEW MORAN was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , a pair of half-boots, value 4 s. 6 d., the goods of Jonathan Ward.
   JONATHAN WARD. Elizabeth Ward is my daughter; she is nearly eleven years old - here are a pair of half-boots, which are my property.
   ELIZABETH WARD. On the 29th of August, about the middle of the day, I saw the prisoner by my father's door with two or three more. I was coming to the door, and saw them looking at the shoes which hung at the doorpost - they took them down, and hung them up again, and then I went away.
   ISSAC CLARK. I live opposite Ward, at Highgate. On the 29th of August, about two o'clock, I saw four boys at his door; the prisoner was one of them. I did not see him take the boots; my son said they were stolen. I saw the prisoner with something bulky in his apron; I went over to Ward's, and saw them crossing the road, and took hold of one of the others - the prisoner then ran up a little passage, and came out without the bulk - I went to him and asked what he had done with the shoes - I found them in the passage, behind the door, where he went up.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1185.  HENRY GODFREY, HENRY ARCHBOLD, and AUGUSTUS FREDERICK BOARDS were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , a saw, value 15 s., the goods of John Biggs and William Holmes ; and three wedges, value 3 s.; an axe, value 2 s.; a pealing iron, value 6 d.; a beetle, value 6 d., the goods of the said John Biggs ; and an axe, value 2 s.; a mattock, value 2 s., and a peeling iron, value 6 d., the goods of the said William Holmes ; and a chisel, value 1 s., the goods of Thomas Bolton; and RICHARD JORDAN and ABRAHAM BANKS, were indicted for feloniously receiving the same, knowing them to be stolen.
   JOHN BIGGS. I am a labourer. I was at work on Mr. Bower's premises, felling some timber; Holmes and Porter were also at work there, about the end of May or the beginning of June; the saw was the joint property of Holmes and myself. I lost other tools; I was working at Coneyhatch.
   WILLIAM HOLMES. I know the saw, mattock, one axe, and anger. Biggs and I bought the saw between us - they were stolen out of a shed, but I do not know when.
   THOMAS BOLTON. I missed a chisel, very much like one that is here, but I cannot swear to it - I locked it up about the middle of May, in a shed, with other tools - in Honeyburn's shed.
   THOMAS PEART. On Sunday morning, the 13th of June, I saw Boards and Godfrey, between four and five o'clock; I was a long time with them, and went to Mr. Honeyburn's, in Coneyhatch-lane - Boards pulled a spar away from the shed and got in, and handed out these tools to us - we hid them in Mr. Robinson's hedge, about two fields off. I went there with the constable about a fortnight afterwards, I shewed them the place and they found the beetle.
 Cross-examined by MR. WALFORD. Q. When were you taken up - A. One Monday, a fortnight after - I told Dr. Owen of a good many robberies which I had been concerned in. I have come from the House of Correction now.
   JOHN MORRISON. I am a horse patrol. On the 29th of July, about ten o'clock in the evening, I saw Banks and Jordan in the Swan and Two Necks, public-house, near Finchley Common; they said they found some tools in a field, and wished to give them up to me, which they did; I left them at a public-house, and they went away.
   THOMAS PEART re-examined. I saw Jordan about a fortnight after the robbery. As Boards and I were going towards the Swan we met him. Jordan said,
 "Have you planted them?" He said, No; it was wet that night, and he could not. Jordan told him to plant them near the road, so as to get them handy. I asked Boards what they were worth; he said 1 l. Jordan said he would not give that for them. Boards had said before, that he had got somebody to buy them.
