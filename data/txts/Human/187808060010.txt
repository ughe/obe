CHARLES ALLAN ANGIER. I am a solicitor, and have been in partnership with Mr. Randall twelve years—Mr. Crawley was a client of ours—I never saw the prisoner but once in my life, and I never spoke to him.
   Cross-examined . You called on us last autumn to induce us to compro mise Mr. Crawley's matter—I do not recollect what took place, except that I never spoke—I was called up by my partner to be present, but I never spoke to you—I do not remember taking down the correspondence, and ask ing you if it was your writing—I did not threaten to lock you up if you corresponded again, but I believe Mr. Randall did—I was not in the room when you entered it.
   Re-examined . I have communicated with Mr. Crawley since last Session, and he has come from abroad to be examined if necessary.
   Witnesses for the Defence . THOMAS HALL. I am managing clerk to Mr. Emmett, a solicitor, of Essex Street, Strand—I am aware that Richard Pigeon holds a note of hand for 1,250 l .—I have seen it—I believe Mr. Angier asked to take proceedings upon it, and Mr. Pigeon refused to give it up except on payment of his costs—I know that he has declined to send in a bill of costs—Mr. Emmett is too unwell to attend on your behalf to-day—I am unable to find in his office your correspondence with Mr. Maskelyne.
