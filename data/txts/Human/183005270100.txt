 1246.  SARAH JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of May , 2 half-crowns, 8 shillings, and 4 sixpences, the monies of John Bell , from his person.
   JOHN BELL. I am a lighterman. I met the prisoner last Thursday in the street; I went to the public-house to give her something to drink, and then walked by the side of the bridge - I had 16s. or 17s. in my pocket; just as I was leaving her I put my hand into my pocket, and my money was gone: I had not a farthing - I accused her of taking my money; she said she had not, that what money she had was her own - the night-constable was on the spot, and he took her; when she got to the watch-house she threw some money on the table, and said it was all the money she had - the officer went to where we had been, to see if he could find any there; he could not - he then came back; the prisoner stooped down, and threw the rest of the silver on the floor - there were 15s. found in all; I lost about 17s. - I had it safe at the public-house when I paid for some liquor.
 Prisoner. He took me and two other women, and treated us - he was not sober. Witness. I was not quite sober; there was no other woman with us.
   GEORGE BAYLIS. About one o'clock on Tuesday morning I saw the prosecutor and prisoner cavelling together -I went up; he said she had robbed him of 15s. - I took her to the watch-house: she threw 4s. 6d. on the table, and declared it was all she had - the night officer searched her in my presence, and the rest of the money dropped from some part of her garments; the whole money we found was 15s., which was what the prosecutor stated - he was intoxicated, but not very bad.
   JAMES PEACHEY. I am a constable. I searched the prisoner, and found the money in the bottom of her petticoat; she put her hand down, and threw it on the floor - she had at first thrown down the 4s. 6d., and said it was all she had in the world.
 Prisoner's Defence. I produced my pocket, and said I could give account of the money I had; I had changed a sovereign in the morning, and paid part of it away - if I had had more time I could have sent for the gentleman who gave it me.
   JAMES PEACHEY. She was intoxicated - she did not say she had changed a sovereign; she said positively at first that she had but 4s. 6d.
     GUILTY. Aged 32. - Transported for Fourteen Years.
 1247.  WILLIAM WELLS was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of May , 1 pair of shoes, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Jacob Russell.
   THOMAS EMBERSON. I live with Mr. Jacob Russell, a pawnbroker. On the 28th of May a pair of shoes were taken from about half a foot within the door; a person might get them by getting on the step - there are two steps; I heard the noise, went round, and saw the prisoner with them; I took him directly.
 Prisoner. The shoes were given to me by a boy. Witness. There was no other boy.
   ROBERT HOWARD. I have a pair of woman's shoes, and took the prisoner - he said he took a knife and cut them down; he said he was very hungry at the time.
     GUILTY. Aged 12. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1248.  JOHN TABBY was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of April , 4 half-crowns, the monies of Joseph George , from his person.
   JOSEPH GEORGE. I lost four half-crowns on the 24th of April from my left-hand breeches pocket, between twelve and one o'clock in the morning, as I was passing Smithfield-bars- I took out half a crown to go and have some supper, and my money fell down; I picked it up, and was walking away when the prisoner came and put his hand into my pocket - I caught his hand, but he got from me; the officer took him - I cannot be positive of his person - I was very much in liquor.
 JOHN SNOWSILL. I was in Smithfield - I saw the prosecutor take out half a crown, which he dropped; he stooped to pick it up. and some one shoved him over it -I turned, and seized a tall man; he said he was not the man - the prosecutor then said he was robbed of four half-crowns.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. He was very tipsy? A. Yes - he said he was robbed, and several persons came up; I did not see the prisoner till the officer had hold of him.
   JOHN CARLISLE. I am an officer. I was present and saw the mob - I went up, and heard the prosecutor say he had dropped half a crown, and on stooping to pick it up, some person shoved him down; I stood close at his elbow, and all in a minute the prisoner came and put his hand into his left-hand pocket, and put something into his own pocket; I saw the prosecutor lay hold of his wrist, but he twisted from him - I followed, and took him; I took hold of one of his hands, and told the watchman to take his other hand; I took him to the Compter, and found four half-crowns in his left-hand pocket.
 Cross-examined. Q. What did the prosecutor say? A. He said he dropped one, and had four more in his pocket - I gave this account to the Magistrate; when he had taken the money he ran into the mob, but was not out of my sight a minute - when I took him, the prosecutor said, "He is the man that had his hand in my pocket;" he did express some doubt of him, but he was drunk.
