JOHN EDWARD COX. I am a member of the Oxford University Aqua tic Club, and live at Hyde Park-gate; I am a member of the University. I know this to be my cap, and this jacket is mine, and a comb in the pocket—I lost a comb exactly like it—I had left it in my locker—I went to Searle's room on the Wednesday, and saw a great many of the lockers broken open—the lids were torn up—I did not notice them particularly—I think there were marks of some instrument used to prize them open.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How do you know the cap? A.  By the general wear of it—I have no initials on it, but I once cut the strap shorter on one side than the other, that I might fasten it under my chin—it is not a very common kind of cap—I think mine and my brother's are the only ones in the club—I wore it on the 4th of April—I will not swear to the cut—I have no doubt of the cap, I think I should know it among a thousand that had been worn. THOMAS LUXMORE WILSON. I am a solicitor, and live in Bedford row. I am not a member of the club—I keep things at Mr. Searle's, in a room under the Oxford room—these trowsers are mine, and this jacket I have worn five years—it has my name in it, written by myself, and I know the mittens and blue striped Guernsey frock. PHILIP LYBBE POWIS. I am a member of Baliol College, Oxford, and live at Furnival's Inn; I am a member of the club. These two Guernsey frocks are mine, and this jacket I know by the particular sort of lining in the sleeves—I lost a blue handkerchief like this, but cannot speak positively to it—I believe it is mine—one of the frocks has my name in my own handwriting. CHARLES STEWART re-examined.  I took Petty for being in the room where the things were found—it was on the 5th of April, between ten and eleven o'clock—I found other articles in the room—this drinking-horn, another cap, and a silk handkerchief—this comb was found in a drawer in the room.
   MRS. AUSTIN re-examined.  I first saw Petty in the room when I took the policeman up to show him where I had removed the property to—I do not know how he came there—we both went up to Warren's room, and found Petty with Logden—I had not seen him there before—I saw two men go out at the street-door about an hour before, but cannot say he was one of them—Parker had given me information, which made me notice the noise there had been in the house—that was before I saw Petty in the room. MARIA PARKER re-examined.  After the two male prisoners went out with things, Petty and another man, not Bromell, came back—I saw them as I stood at my room door—the other man ran out—I had never seen him before—he ran away from the policeman—Petty and Bromell went out about ten o'clock—it must have been past ten—I saw Petty afterwards when the policeman took him—Bromell did not come back with him—he was in custody at the time—I had not seen Petty return, as I had gone to fetch a policeman, but he must have returned. CHARLES STEWART re-examined.  I saw Petty and another man, and Logden, in the front-room—I forgot to state that the other man was there—he escaped—he was not Bromell.
