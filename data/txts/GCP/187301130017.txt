THOMAS CASSELLS. I live at 2, Upper Tachbrook Street, Pimlico, and am
& doctor of medicine-on Tuesday, 24th December, I saw the prosecutor
about 11.15 or 11.20 in the morning-he sent for me I found him suffer-
ing from four incised wounds; one on the front of the shoulder bone, one
on the top of the shoulder, one a little lower down, on the upper arm;
the last was lower down, considerably outside the arm-the first-mentioned
wound was the most dangerous, it struck the shoulder bone-I did not
probe it, because I could see the bone ; it cut into the bone--the lowest
wound was the deepest, the other two were merely superficial-there was a
considerable loss of blood-if the first wound had been a little lower down
it would have penetrated the lungs- I should say the wounds were inflicted
by such an instrument as this knife from the position of the wounds, I
should judge they were not self-inflicted, nor likely to have been received
in a struggle to get possession of a knife.
Cross-examined. I saw the prisoner's hand on 27th December-there
was a wide open wound on the back of it-I can't say whether that would
be caused in a struggle I don't believe the wounds the man had could
have been accidentally caused-the blows must have been given direct-
the wound furthest from the front was as far back as you can reach, a little
above the tibia, it was a cut in and downwards.
WILLIAM SANDEROOOK (Policeman B 303). On Wednesday afternoon,
24th December, I took the prisoner into custody at her own place about 4
o'clock in the afternoon-I found this knife in the room on the 27th.
Cross-examined. I found it on the clothes box-I was there on the even-
ing of the 24th, and not again till the 27th--I searched the room on the
24th, but I did not find the knife thon-I did not search that place on the
24th, because I found a penknife on the table, and I thought that was
sufficient, and I did not look any further--the landlady is not here.
JOHN EDMUND PARTRIDGE. Q. by the Prisoner. When you came to me
were you drunk or sober 4. Sober—I did not say I was a drunkard and
a blackguard, and 1 did not care how soon I settled myself-my governor
left me a legacy of 251., and does that show that I was a blackguard !
COURT. Q. Was she in the habit of coming to Windmill Street, where you
were employed, and annoying you? A. She came repeatedly, and asked
me to live with her, which I told her was impossible—it was in consequence
of her violence that I separated from her.
The prisoner addressed the Jury on her own behalf (Mr. STRAIGHT not
disputing the facts). She alleged a series of ill-usage on the part of her
husband, and stated that he had wounded himself whilst in a state of drunken-
nese.

Guilty of unlawfully wounding.--Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
116. CHARLES ASH (20), Unlawfully, maliciously, and feloniously
wounding George William Taylor, with intent to do him grievous bodily
harm.
MR. MONTAGU WILLJAMS conducted the Prosecution; and MR. CHARLES
MATTHEWS the Defence..
The prisoner having stated in the hearing of the Jury that he was guilty of
unlawfully wounding, they found that verdict. * -Five Yeard Penal Servitude.
