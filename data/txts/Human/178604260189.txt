Is he here? - No.
 When did he deliver it to you? - I have had it this fortnight or three weeks; I got it from the register's seat, where it belongs, I know by the probate act, which was wrote by the clerk.
 Do you know of your own knowledge at all where Mr. Watts got it? - From that seat; it comes to that seat from the Proctor's; I did not see it go to that seat, but know it could not go to any other seat.
 Do you at all know from what place, or from what person, Mr. Watts got that paper? - From that seat.
 How do you know it? - I have told you as plain as I can.
 Mr. Baldwin. You belong to the Commons? - Yes.
 And you brought that from the proper office? - Yes.
 Court. You got it from him in the office? - Yes.
   WILLIAM HARRIS sworn.
 Look at the prisoner, do you know him? - Yes; that is the man that brought me the probate; he came to my house in the name of  John Murphy; we board and lodge sailors, and lodge them without boarding; he came to us on the Tuesday night, and on the Wednesday night he staid out late, and said he had been about a piece of business; and he turned out a piece of paper, and a certificate for an administration; he did not say to whose will; he went up to get a probate again on Wednesday following; he came to my house the 29th of March.
 Did he desire you to go? - Yes, for the probate for an administration; we got the administration at Mr. Mackintosh's, and went down to Crutched Friars; he gave it to Peters, and Peters gave it to me.
 (The probate produced.)
 In what name did he go to Mr. Mackintosh? - In the name of Murphy, when he went to the Navy Office, he asked them to look in the book of the  Stirling Castle, and he asked him if it was proved in the Commons, and he said yes, and he asked him if he should put it down for Monday, and we went accordingly to the Pay-Office for the money, and there was a sloop paid, and this was put off till Tuesday, and the Stirling Castle books were called, and he answered and went in, what he said there I cannot say, when he came out he said he was ordered to go to Mr. Dyson, he said there was a person belonging to this will, and it was lodged in the Commons, and he said it was signed by the Mayor of Portsmouth and we went to Mr. Knight the agent to Mr. Dyson; he came to us in the name of John Murphy, his real name is Peters, by his own account.
 You was not called in to the Navy Office when he was called in? - I was at the door.
 Then you do not know what he said then? - No.
 Did he tell you what he went in for? - He said it was twenty-seven pounds of the Stirling-Castle, that he was the sole executor of  Thomas Banks, and that he had been to demand that money, and when he came out he was desired to go to Mr. Dyson.
 Mr. Garrow. Mr. Harris, this man owed you some money? - Yes.
 You watched him pretty close, you kept the probate in your pocket? - Yes.
 You went the first time, and was told the business could not be done, then you went again? - Yes.
 He saw nobody the first time, and the second what passed you cannot tell? - No.
 Did you know  Thomas Banks? - No, never at all.
 Can you read? - Yes.
 When was it the prisoner told you what he was going to demand, and in what right? - On Wednesday night.
