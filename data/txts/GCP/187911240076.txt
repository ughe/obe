MR. BESLEY submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury as to
removing property value 101., as the only property proved to be removed som
a chair and a table, and that if property was removed, the value of beds,
bedeling, and wearing apparel should be deducted, but there was an utter
failure of proof that any property was removed by him, and if he absconded
and did not take 201. with him he would not be liable except for contempt of
Court, and his wife and daughters might have removed the property after ha
went to Rotterdam, for which he would not be responsible unless he now
present, aiding and assisting them. Mr. Gill contended that it was not
necessary to prove that any one was present at the removal of the goods; that
no one had an interest in the removal of the stock but the bankrupt himself
and that the fact of the things having disappeared was a proof of their
removal. The COURT considered that there was a case to go to the Jury.

dwelling-house of Mary Ann Hooper, with intent to steal.
MR. GEOGHEGAN Prosecuted.
FRANK HOOPER. I live with my mother, Mary Ann Hooper, at 21, St
Mary's Road, Islington-on Saturday, 8th November, between 9.30 and 10
p.m., I was in the dining-room, and thought I heard a noise in the garden-
I called up the dog, and it barked at the closet door inside the house—the
back door leading into the garden was closed, bolted, and chained—the closet
door opening into the hall was bolted on the outside—there is a window in
the closet looking into the garden, and large enough for a man to get through
-I opened the closet door and found the prisoner there, lying on the floor,

window was shut or open-his boots were lying at his side-I called
somebody-I took hold of him and he asked me to let him go-I said "I
