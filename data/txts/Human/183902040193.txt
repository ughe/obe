780. JOHN TILLETT was indicted for stealing, on the   18th of Janu ary , 1 cloak, value 20 s  ., the goods of Theodore Tillett : and ALEXANDER EBBS , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing it to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.; to which
   TILLETT  pleaded GUILTY. Aged.— Confined Three Months., THEODORE TILLETT . I am a widow. The prisoner Tillett it my son—I missed a cloth cloak from a box in my room, in Mark-street, Finsbury, a month ago next Friday—my son came that night, and I gave him into cus tody MATTHEW PEAK. I am a policeman. I took Tillett into custody, and in consequence of what he told me I went with Brannan to No. 6, Old Cock-lane, Shoreditch, and found Ebbs—I said, "Do you know any thing about the cloak?"—he said, "No, I know nothing about it"—I went to wards a closet by the fire-place, and Brannan went towards at chest of drawers—Ebbs followed him, and said ht had got the cloak—I then, saw the cloak in Brannan's hands.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did he not say, "Oh, yes, there is a cloak," before Brannan found it? A.  I cannot say, because I was look ing into the cupboard—Brannan was going towards the drawer before he said any thing about having it. JAMES BRANNAN. I am a policeman. On Saturday, the 19th of Janu ary, I went with Peak to Ebbs's lodging—Peak asked him about the cloak—he said, "I know nothing of a cloak; I have no cloak here"—I west towards a chest of drawers—the prisoner followed me, and opened the drawers—I put my hand upon the cloak, and he did so  at the same time, and said, "Oh, ah, here is a cloak"—I said, "How did you get it?"—he said he lent a boy 4 s . on it, to get his things out of pawn.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you understand whether that boy was Tillett? A.  I did not—I have since heard that it was not—I have been looking for one Lenton, but cannot find him—I believe Ebbs is a willow-weaver—I
 found no other stolen property on his premises.
   EBBS  — NOT GUILTY.
