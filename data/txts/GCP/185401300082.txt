ELIZABETH ELLEN HARRIS. My maiden name was Reader. I am
daughter of Mr. Reader, who died at Woolwich-my husband is a poor
man; he is not able to work, I have three children.
Cross-examined. Q. About how old are you? A. Thirty-four or thirty-
five; it is four years ago since I last saw my father-he then came down to
my house at Lewes I have seen him at the lodging house at Woolwich, and
stayed there a fortnight; that was ten years ago—that was not Mrs. Chap-
man's lodging house-I have not seen him since he was at Lewes, four
years ago.
MR. BODKIN. Q. When you saw him at Woolwich, was he keeping
lodging house himself? A. Yes, in High-street; he sent for me in a
case of sickness

CHARLES JOSEPH CARTTAR, Esq. I am one of the Coroners for the
county of Kent. I held an inquest on the body of John Reader, on 15th
Octu, at Woolwich-the prisoner, Mary Chapman, was the first witness
examined-she did not tender herself; she was summoned there by my
order-she was sworn-I took a note of what she stated (reads: She said,
"I knew the deceased by the name of John Reader-his age was about
seventy-two years, a labourer-he had lodged at my house about the last
four years he paid me 28. a week-he was taken ill last Friday week, 7th
Oct.--I sent for the doctor, who attended him, until his death, daily- I did
all the doctor ordered, and every attention was paid to the deceasedhe
died on Thursday last, 13th Oct, about 5 o'clock-he was sensible up to the

the will I have left with Mr. Pidcock, the solicitor-the deceased has no

it way in consequence of information conveyed to me, that I put the
