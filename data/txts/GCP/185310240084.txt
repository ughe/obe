CHALLIS, Mayor.
ESSEX CASE.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
1117. JAMES ALLEN PHIPPS, stealing 1 gelding, price 91.; the
property of William Watson.
ELIZA Watson. I am the wife of William Watson; we live at Ilford,
On the afternoon of 20th May, I went to a field where I had a cow-I drove
the cow home, and milked it-I had a pony in that field-I left the gate
open, and about a quarter of an hour after I saw the pony in the road-I went
afterwards to look for the pony, but could not find it-I saw it next day,
about 1 o'clock, at the Red Lion stables, at Ilford-the policeman came and
told me it was mine-it was worth between 8l. and 91.
John HOCKING (policeman, K 65). I was on duty in Barking-lane on
the evening of 20th May-I saw the prisoner riding a pony with this bit of
cord tied to its mouth, which drew my attention-I asked him how far he was
going-he said, “That is my business, not yours"- I asked him whose pony
it was-he said, "It is my pony, and d- d well I know it, for I gave 71.
for it"-I took him into custody, and the next day I told the last witness-
she went to the Red Lion, and saw the pony, and claimed it.
Cross-examined by MR. GIFFARD. Q. How was it the prisoner was not
tried before? A. He was up here, but it appeared he was insane-I heard
that he had escaped from a lunatic asylum-I was not in Court when he was
called on to be arraigned. (See page 239).
Not GUILTY.
KENT CASES.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
1118. WILLIAM TOOKEY, feloniously uttering a forged order for
108. 6d., with intent to defraud. MR. GIFFARD conducted the Prosecution.
HANNAH COLE. I am the wife of Mr. Thomas Cole. In Dec. last, a
club called the "Slate Club" was instituted for the purpose of providing
funds for sick persons-my husband was appointed treasurer of it, and the
prisoner secretary-the quarterly payment to him in respect of his secretary-
ship would be 10s. 6d.-on 5th Jan., he brought me this paper (read-
" Jan. 5, 1853. Sir, pay Mr. Tookey the sum of 10s. 6d., on account of the
Slate Club. James Howe and Rt. Penny, Stewards;" on the back of which
was written, “Jan. 5, 1853. Received of Mr. Thomas Cole 108. 6d.,
being a quarter's salary for the Slate Club. William Tookey")—that on the
back was already written when he brought it-I paid him the 103.60.-he came
to me three times with those papers, and I paid him the same up to Midsummer
- there were thirty or forty members at the commencement, each of whom
was to pay the prisoner quarterly at the rate of 1s. a week, but some did not
pay up, and were thrust from the books, so that there was not more than 10s. 6d.
quarterly due to the prisoner-at Midsummer there could not be more than 21s.
due to him-it might be less, but it could not be more-he continued living
at Greenwich up to 5th Sept., and then left, in consequence of which inquiry
was made-the stewards looked over the papers, and these were found
among them.
Prisoner. Q. Should you have paid me if those names bad not been on
the paper? A. I should-immediately I paid it it was entered in the books
among other articles of expenditure.
