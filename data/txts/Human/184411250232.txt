 197. EDWARD GARROOD was indicted for obtaining money by false pretences. ANTHONY CHAPMAN( police-constable V  100.) I reside at Tooting, in Surrey. On Sunday, the 3rd of Nov., about twelve or one o'clock, the prisoner brought a parcel to my house—he had been conductor to an om nibus—on the paper parcel he brought there was "2 s . 2 d ." marked, and he demanded 3 s . 2 d . for the carriage—it had come from the New Inn, Old Bailey—I said, "3 s . 2 d .?"—he said, "Yes, here is the bill, 2 s . 2 d . paid out, and 1 s . carriage"—my sister-in-law paid him the 3 s . 2 d . in my presence—unless I had believed that "2 s . 2 d ." had been paid out, and 1 s . was due for carriage, I should not have paid it.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Did you say you thought it was too much? A.  I did—I thought the whole of it was too much—I believe the prisoner was in the service of Mr. Holden, the omnibus pro prietor—after he was gone, it struck me there had been an alteration in the bill, from 1 to 2—the prisoner was not taken for ten days afterwards—he was not in the town. JAMES MILLER. I am in the service of the Merton carrier. On Sun day, the 3rd of Nov., in the morning, my master gave me a parcel, directed to Mr. Chapman—I carried it to Tooting—the prisoner came up to me, and asked where I was going—I said to Mr. Chapman's, and I asked him if he could tell me where it was—he said he would go with me and take it—I said I should be very glad if he could assist me—the money he ought to have received on that parcel was 2 s . 6 d .—there was 1 s . 2 d . marked on the bill, and 1 s . 4 d . was the carriage from London—I gave him the parcel to take—we got to Mr. Chapman's, and he took it in—I gave him the bill—this is it—it has been altered from 1 s . 2 d . to 2 s . 2 d .
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you first saw him, did you not ask where Mr. Chapman lived? A.
