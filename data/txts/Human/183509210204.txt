   Q.  Did you not direct both Lee and Beales to run in oppsition to Bind and Johnson, and nut to leave them till they had your orders, if they did not earn one shilling? A.  Never—I belive they have been in every one's time, but I never gave them order's except not to go in the women's time, (there are some female properties)—that was about a month or five weeks before the prosecution commenced.
   Q.  Have you not sworn to Mr. Phillips that you never gave them any specific directions at all, but that they ran when they pleased, and did as they liked? A.  Yes, they did—I have never, on any occasion, directly or indirectly, by myself or Mr. Nortson, sought or agreed to settle this matter if I could get money paid me—I did not, after the 8th of June, give direc tions to Lee to run my omnibus against Bird and Johnson.
   Cross-examined by MR. KNOWLES . Q.  Do you know Thomas Parker? A.  Yes; he is foreman to Mr. Aldridge—I might see him about the 5th of this month, with the two Bardells, and there might be a conversation about this indictment—I told them I had no animosity against them, but their names must be in the indictment.
   re-examined. Q.  You were a bankrupt the first time, about seven years ago? A.  Yes; I obtained my certificate about two months after wards—I did not leave my premises—they took bills at certain dates, which were afterwards paid—the second time I was a bankrupt, was about ten months ago—I had lost 900 l . in three weeks, by bad debts, which was the case of my being a bankrupt—I got my certificate on the 15th of January—every one of my creditors signed it, who had proved—I do not know where Lee or Mann live—I live nearly oppsite Day and Martin's, within a few doors—I hardly know what the meaning of a time is—I understand it is to start three minitues before another—If my omnibus started from the Bank at ten o'clock in the morning, it would go to Padding ton, and it would stop there for half perhaps—they went just as they could—that would necessarily interfere with different times.
   COURT . Q.  Tell us every thing that was said by you to John Bardell? A.  He asked me if I would go with him to get Mr. Norton to take the bail for his brother, without some certificate, or something; I forget what; and would I go and advise Mr. Norton to do so, without giving any trouble; and I did so—he did not ask me why the Bardells were made defendants—when I went with him he complained of Mr. Norton having refused the bail, and asked whether, if he were there on Monday, prepared with the bail, he would take it; and 1 told Mr. Norton not give them any more trouble, but to take their bail—I did not want to be litigious ducting himself wrong to my buses. ROBERT BRAY. I am the prosecutor's son.
