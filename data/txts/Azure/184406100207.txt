1787. PHILIP CHILD was indicted for stealing 4 sovereigns, 8 half-
sovereigns, 3 half-crowns, 12 Shillings, and I sixpence, the monies of Charles
Ion.
MR. DOANE conducted the Prosecution.
CHARLES ION. I am a milkman, and live in John-street, Vassal-road,
Brixton. The prisoner married my sister---be was out of work some time ago,
and J allowed him, his wife, and children to remain in my house for about
six weeks or two months, without paying any rent until be should be enabled
to get work—I used to keep my money in a little box, locked up in a chest
under our bed—I saw it about nine o'clock in the evening of the 14th, and
there was then 81. in sovereigns and half-sovereigns, and ll. in silver—I had
put it there to pay my rent with—on the 16tb, I wen! out about half-past
four to my work—I came home about eight to breakfast, about twelve to
dinner, and when I came home at night the prisoner was gone—he had given
me no intimation of his leaving—be did not come home that night-—I sus-
pected something might be wrong, and my wife missed the money—I directly
went to the station, and gave information of the robbery—on tire 2nd of
May, I received a letter of which I took no notice—a friend came to me iast
Saturday week, and in consequence of what be said, I met the prisoner that
evening at Kennington church, and gave him into custody—the person who
came to me was with him.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. What is that person's name? A. I
do not know—I took a policeman with me—r never made any effort to find
the prisoner—he has gone away when out of work on former occasions—I
cannot say where to—he might have gone into the country in search of
—I have got a trifling subscription among my friends to repay the money—I
bad no conversation with the prisoner when I met him—I gave him into cuv
tody directly—I had made no arrangement with the person who came to me
—I merely agreed to go to the place.
MARY ION. I am the prosecutor's wife. I saw the money safe on the
15th of Jan., at two o'clock, when I went to the box and took out 28.—.—
I left the 81. in gold, and ll. in silver—locked the box up safe, and put it
under the bed down stairs—I went out, leaving the prisoner and his wife in
the house—I returned at half-past five in the evening, and he was gone—his
wife and children were there—I did not see bim again till he was in custody.
—next day I went to look for my money, and it wag gone—the box was
locked, and in the same place I had left it—the key was in my pocket-•-a
false key must have been used—it is a common lock.
Cross-examined. Q. Are you quite sure the prisoner was not at home that
night? A. I am positive—I went into the room at half-past five o'clock
when I came home, and many times after, but did not see him—their room
was next to mine in which the money was—there were lodgers in the house,
but they did not come home till between nine and ten—they do not have to
Ijass our room—it is not far from the stairs.
MR. DOANE. Q. Did either of the lodgers run away next day ? A.
NOT GUILTY.
