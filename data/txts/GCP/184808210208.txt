Prisoner's Defence (written). “I lived with my prosecutor eight weeks
along with my husband : I went to see my friends at Chatham; I left my
husband in the room ; a week after I was gone, and he then absconded : on
Monday, the prosecutor came to Chatham to me, and told me my husband
was gone, and that they had missed their sheets; I told them that I
coming home in a day or two, and that I would settle everything: they told
me they did not wish to hurt me; we went out together, and when we re-
turned, her husband had got the man to take me in charge; I was taken to
Woolwich, and the Magistrate heard my case; he fined me 108., and in de
fault of payment, I was to go seven days to Maidstone; I was brought back
again in twenty minutes, and told that my prosecutor was not satisfied, they
wanted their expenses; and the man that took me was not a Metropolitan
policeman: the Magistrate said he could not pay tbem their expenses; they
then sent me for trial, to see if they would get them here; I have been in
confinement seven weeks; I hope you will have mercy upon me, as it is my
first time of being in prison, and it will be for ever a warning to me."
Not Guilty.
2034. JAMES CAREY, stealing 1 balf-crown, the money of John Bayley,
his master. MR. MELLER conducted the Prosecution,
RICHARD BAYLEY. I live at Woolwich, with my brother John, who keeps
a grocer's shop-he had lost money--the prisoner was in his service. On
9th Aug. I marked a half-crown and a five-shilling-piece, and six shillings
were marked and put in the till in my presence-after my brother was gone
to London I placed the half-crown in the tillit was the only half-crow
there -- the prisoner came in ten minutes - he went to the part where
the till was-I then went to the till, and the half-crown was gode-
followed the prisoner, who had then left to go to the Arsenal, and charged
him with having stolen the half-crown-he said he had not-I told him to
produce the money he bad in his pocket-he produced a sixpence, some
copper, and this half-crown-my brother returned, and gave him into custody.
Cross-examined by Mr. Cook Evans. Q. Has he been out of custody since
he was taken ? A. Yes we preferred another charge against him, about 3
half-crown-I saw him when he was out of custody close to our house-
he was in our shop about twenty minutes on the day this half-crown
stolen- this is the half-crown-these are the marks I made on it-I did not
mark other money.
WILLIAM GLADWIN (policeman, R 122). I received this half-crown from
the inspector-the policeman who received it is dead.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
