EDWARD HENRY. I live in Bread-street, Cheapside, and am in part-
nership with my brother. I know the witness Moss perfectly well as being
in the employment of Mr. Hyams—I have dealings with Mr. Hyams-
on the 25th of March, he came to our house - I do not exactly remember
the time, but it was in the morning-he came to bring some watches for
his employer, and asked for some money for Mrs. Hyams, as Mr. Hyams
was out of town-I paid him this £50 draft- produced)--this is his band-
writing on the back-he wrote that in my presence-looking at the date of
the cheque I recollect it was on the 25th of March-I gave it him on
account of Mr. Hyams, for Mrs. Hyams.
JANE BRADLEY. On the 25th of March, I was living in the service of
Mr. and Mrs. Moss--since I was before the Magistrate I have been under
the care of Lea, the officer-I had lived with them two years, but used
to sleep at home with my friends. On the 25th of March, he lived at No.
12, New-street, which is by the side of the London Hospital-Mr. Moss
generally went out to work of a morning in a kind of long rough working
dress-he used to go out between eight and nine o'clock, or about nine
o'clock-on the morning of the 25th of March he went out about half-past
eight o'clock-he had not his working dress on then, but a better suit of
clothes-he returned between one and two o'clock-he usually gave a
double knock, but when he returned this day, only one knock came to the
door-my mistress opened the door.
Q. When you were before the Magistrate do you remember what you
said on that subject-did you say your mistress opened the door? 4.

named it to Mr. Humphreys-it said I had opened the door, but I did not
open the door-I went to Mr. Humphreys' office and told him of it-my
mistress opened the door-I heard my master's voice while I was in the
kitchen-Moss did not stay but a very few minutes-he did not stay to din-
ner-I made some observations about it at the time--after my master had
