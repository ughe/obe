Witnesses to character were called on behalf of Mrs. Dobkins and
prisoner Sonkin, and Sonkin made a statement to the jury, not on onth.
Verdict. Sonkin, Shepherd, and Cohen, Guilty ; Dobkins, Not
guilty.
Judge Rentoul, in ordering the female prisoner to be discharged,
said he quite agreed with the verdict; there was not enough evidence
to convict, though he had not felt justified in stopping the onse. No
risk, however, was run, prisoner having been given in charge to the
jury.
Before sentence was passed, the following three prisoners were
tried:
SMITH, Albert (23, porter), LONG, George (23, salesman),
WILSON, Charles (23, waiter); all breaking and entering the ware-
house of Robert Norton and another and stealing therein 2,700 yards
in length of silk and the sum of 8s. 1fd., the goods and moneys of the
said Robert Norton.
THOMAS KINGSTON. I am clerk to Sir Robert Norton, trading as
Anderson and Norton, 11 and 12, Bayer Street, Golden Lane. On
November 19 I locked the premises up about eight o'cloek. Every
thing was then secure. On the following morning about 20 past eight
I found the warehouse in the hands of the police. From the second
floor I missed 2,700 yards of silk valued at £200 and 8s. 1 d. in cash
from a private drawer underneath where the silk had been placed. I
On the first floor there was a packing case about 4ft, bin, by 5ft.
The outer door had been broken open. The silk produced forms
part of the stolen goods.

Aroused my suspicions. I saw Constable Collins at the top of the
