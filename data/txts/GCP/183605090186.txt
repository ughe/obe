1309. WILLIAM HENRY FRY was again indicted for stealing, on
the 19th of December, 1 pair of boots, value 5s. 6d., the goods of Joseph
Peate, his master.
John Hope. I am shopman to Mr. Carpenter, a pawnbroker. On the
19th of December the prisoner pawned this pair of boots at our shop,
for 2s, 6d.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHAMBERS. Q. Was that the first time you
saw him? A. Yes I am quite sure it was him-he might have been
five minutes in the shop-i took notice of him-I cannot describe any
thing very particular in his appearance, but I thought he was a shoe.
maker, for sometimes they pawn their master's work, and take it out again,
which makes us particular in writing their tickets-my impression is that
he is the same-I may be mistaken-when shoe-makers come to pani
shoes, we have thought they might belong to a master, and we take one
pair, but if they bring another pair, we will have nothing to do with them
--that made me observe the prisoner-I did not express a doubt about
him before I said, "I have no doubt of him."
Erasmus CHARLTON. I am a policeman. Mr. Peate spoke to me
respecting the prisoner on the 19th of December, and I went to Carpenter's
the pawnbroker's, and saw a pair of boots--I took one to Mr. Peate, and
he instructed me to go to pawnbrokers to look for property-I apps
hended the prisoner in consequence of what occurred in January,
Cross-examined. Q. It was after January Mr. Peate spoke to you!
4. No, on the 19th of December, and on the 12th of January the prisoner
absconded--I had been round to the pawnbrokers before that..
JOSEPH PEATE. I know these boots-I believe they are my property
- they are not my own manufacture-I bought six pairs on the 9th
December, which had no particular mark except the size on the toe!
have no private mark on these there is nothing by which I can identify
them.
WILLIAM JONES. I was shop-boy to the prosecutor. I know these
boots by my mark at the toe-No. 3.- I wrote that myself-I marked
five pair all at once.
Cross-examined. Q. Where is the other pair, I thought there were siz!
4. One pair has been sold-I marked them all with the same figure -- the
other four pairs have been sold-on the 19th of December I was sent to
watch the prisoner, and watched him into the pawnbroker's shop-he came
out of there, and went home-I came home and told mistress, and she
went with me to Carpenter's, and found him tying the ticket on the boots-
I took them in my hands, and knew my mark on them-Mr. Peate yu
very ill at the time, and I told Mrs. Peate--the prisoner continued to work
at the shop after that, till the 12th of January.
JOSEPH PEATE re-eramined. I was ill at the time this happened, and was
