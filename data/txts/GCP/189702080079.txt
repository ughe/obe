214. JOHN DWYER (40) and HENRY PASH (49), Robbery with
violence on Joseph Halliday, and stealing £7 7s.6d., his money.
JOSEPH HALLIDAY. I am a French polisher, of Alsac Street-on
January 28th I met the two prisoners in Newington Butts-Dwyer said,
"How are you !"-I said, "I am all right”-he said, " Are you going to
stand anything to drink?"-we went to a public-house, and had some
drink, and then three more glasses—I said, "I am going to the urinal”_
one said, “Come and treat us; we have got 7 d. between us"-he called
for drink and tobacco, and I paid for it-he said, “ Are you going to
stand another, Joe?” _I said, "Yes"--we left the Hampton Arms about
4.30, and went to New Cross Road to have a game at skittles for 1s. -
he asked for my purse ; I gave it to another man-we played a game-
I said, "I will have a small lemon"-Pash said, "No, give him a soda"
I said, "No, it is too cold "-I left; and when I had got about
200 yards they called out, and ransacked my pockets, and left me lying
there, and got away, I saw Dwyer again next day, coming home from
work, and gave him in charge-I saw Pash on the Friday morning, but
he gave me the slip, and ran towards the Elephant-I saw him again
last Friday evening, and picked him out from eight or nine others.
Cross-examined by Dwyer. I was knocked down on the other side, by
St. Andrew's Road, but they have altered the name to Rotten Street; I
did not say that it was Rockingham Street when I signed the police-
sheet; that is a mile off.
Cross-examined by MR. BURNIE for Pash. I was in the public-house
about an hour, and came out about six-I have not seen the third man
since-it was dark when I was knocked down; it was about 6.30—it was
Pash who suggested that I should have soda-I do not say that I was
quite sober; I had had a glass or two-I had never seen Pash before, but
I knew Dwyer.
Re-examined. I was sober enough to distinguish the three men who
came out of the public-house with me, and these are two of them.
