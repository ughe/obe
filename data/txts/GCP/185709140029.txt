THOMAS GEORGE PRIGMORE. I was an engineer, employed at Mr. Bauwen's
from June, 1856, to the end of the year. I had charge of the machinery
and piping--several of them told me what a difficult job I should have
when I had to do with the coil of pipes, and I asked Dawson to show me
where the coil of pipes was, so that I might know, if I had anything to do
with them; he lifted up the door of the furnace, and I stooped down, and
saw the coil of pipes from the door of the furnace they were red hot, and
I asked him the reason why they were so; he told me that they could not
work the stuff unless they were so, and it was all right-I said that I was
satisfied, if such was the case-I only looked through the furnace door; I
had heard talk of the sight hole, but I never saw it-I am sure it was the
furnace door I looked through.
Cross-examined by MR. SERJHANT PARRY. Q. Was there anything
between the fire and the pipes ! 4. No- there was no large piece of
Stourbridge tile-I left on 20th Sept, 1856-1 was there while Hastings
was there- I did not see him, or any one, cleaning the pipes I WAS
employed in the engine department the conversation between me and
Dawson occurred soon after I went there I was not examined at the trial
at the Queen's Bench-I have been found out since by Hastings I have
not received any money from him-I am at work at Mr. Marsden's, in
Bishopsgate Street I received 5l with my subpena, to pay my expenses
up from Wells, in Somersetshire, where I was on a job, and I have received
48 for my loss of time in London-I came up once before, but did not
attend I had two subpænas after that, for which I got 58. each--that is
the whole
MR. HUDDLESTON, Q. Had you to come up from Wells, and go back
again! Yes; it is about 160 miles-I helped to manage the job-I lost
about seven days at the last trial--my master told me that I had better
stop, but I am expecting to go back.
