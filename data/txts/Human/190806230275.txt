Of course, I concluded he was joking. He said "I gave it to your wife." I took no further notice as I thought he was joking. We had some drinks and after a little general conversation we parted. If he said anything more I did not hear it as I am rather deaf. Warren got up a subscription for Bryan, who was dying, to which I gave 10s. 6d. I think all the Guardians contributed, most of them much larger sums. I was not at the meeting at Spooner's. I never heard a word about Barker giving £300 to Guardians until it was said at the police court. On one occasion I voted for Barker, junr.; he was highly recommended by Knight and others for the quality of his work on a very large con  tract he had just completed. Barker was much below the highest tender. I understand he paid Trade Union wages. I had no corrupt motive. I never heard Knight complain that he could not get time sheets from Calcutt. Knight used to say, "I have only got these accounts yesterday," or "this morning—I suggest we give Calcutt so much on account"—and his suggestion was accepted. The accounts were afterwards certified by Knight with or without correction. With the exception of the indestructible paint I never voted against Knight's recommendation; I relied on him as a competent architect. As to the indestructible paint Knight suggested 1s.; Calcutt would not take less than 1s. 6d. The question had been discussed at ordinary and full Board meetings, and at public houses; we were very much against Knight having given the order without consulting us for the use of this paint, which caused extra expense in labour and cost. I think Hirst said at the Board" £700 odd is rather a tall order to give without consulting the Guardians." Knight said, "I did not know it was so much." I said, "You, an architect, give an order like that without knowing the quantities—are you acting as an architect or not?" We were greatly annoyed at the order being given at all, and in consequence, passed a resolution, "That the Committee are of opinion that neither the architect, builder. or other person should be allowed to give or execute orders contrary to the contract, specification, or schedule of prices without the express authority and permission of the Guardians." There was discussion at several meet ings. Calcutt wrote that he would not accept less. We did not want to go to law and I formed an honest opinion it was better to par the 1s. 6d. I am not sure that I was present, as my name is not recorded, but I take the responsibility of the vote. I almost invariably left for home at 8 to 8.30 p.m.
 Cross-examined: I should have voted for the 1s. 6d. if I was there—on several occasions I believe I voted for it. Under the circumstances I thought it was worth the money. Knight said it was not worth more than 9d. I assumed he knew the Guardians were dissatisfied with him for giving the order and that he wanted to work it out on Calcutt.
