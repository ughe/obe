On 21st Oct., when Hodgkins was brought there, he was in a perfect state
of insensibility, he died in two or three hours. I was present at a part of
the post mortem examination I believe the cause of death to have been a
blow-the remote cause was effusion of blood into the cavity of the skull,
thereby compressing the brain; the immediate cause I believe to have
been a blow wbilst in a state of intoxication, the vessels thereby being in a
full or congested state.
