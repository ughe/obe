I believe he had; he was not in any kind of partnership with me; I never had any cards with his name on the back of them; he came with a condition that he should recommend his former customers, and I allowed him 15 percent.; I did so with all my shopmen - he was allowed to take shoes out and sell them to his own customers with my leave, or with the shopman's permission in my absence, and to book them afterwards - I should have no objection to take him again.
 COURT. Q. What was it you permitted him to do on speaking to you, or to any body in your shop? A. If any of his friends had a pair of shoes, I allowed him the profit of them, but I never allowed him to take any, except for sale.
 HANNAH ANDREWS. I was in the service of Mr. Bonham. On the 12th of July, I observed a pair of shoes in the prisoner's coat pocket, which hung in the cupboard - he took them out with him; I sent the apprentice to see where he went to - he gave me information, and I found them at the pawnbroker's.
 Cross-examined. Q. You were in the shop when he went out? A. Yes; the cupboard was not locked - any one might have gone there - he put the coat on openly before me - he has borne a good character.
   WILLIAM DEMPSTER. I am a pawnbroker. These shoes were pawned with me, on the evening of the 12th of July, by the prisoner, I believe, in the name of John Dunn.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you a great many people come to your shop? A. Yes; I think I can be positive he is the person who brought them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not take them to defraud my master; I cannot see how it is possible the witness can speak so positive to me, my brother is so much like me, we are continually taken for one another.
   JOHN PROCTOR. I am apprentice to Mr. Bonham. On the 12th of July, I was sent to watch the prisoner; I saw him go to the pawnbroker's and pawn the shoes.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you go into the shop? A. No; I did not see him with the shoes - I watched him in, and watched him out; Mr. Bonham went there afterwards.

 1622.  JAMES HARGER was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , 5 lbs. weight of silk, value 7l.; 120 yards of cotton-braid, value 2s., and 1 bobbin of silk, value 1s., the goods of John Henry Machu , his master.
 MR. PHILLIPS conducted the prosecution.
 JOHN HENRY MACHU. I am a  silk-trimming manufacturer, and live in Twister's-alley, Bunhill-row. The prisoner was my porter; on the 10th of September I received 58 lbs. weight of silk from the dyer's - it ought to have been kept in the silk-room, a place quite detached from the warehouse - on the 11th of September, early in the morning, I was in the warehouse, and my attention was drawn to an iron safe; I saw behind it a quantity of sewing-silk; it was afterwards weighed; when I first saw it it was loose, and was the very same sort as that I received from the dyer's; I had not any more in the warehouse - it was among a lot of waste paper; I took it up to convince myself what it was, and let it fall again; I had been looking there for a cord; about this time the prisoner was coming to the warehouse; I sent him away with some goods, and he returned in about an hour; he had not time to go behind the safe before he went away; there was a boy left in the warehouse - when he returned the boy had left, and I went to the place, and saw the silk still there; no one else had gone into the warehouse; the boy was never in the warehouse afterwards - about half an hour after the prisoner returned, I saw the silk in the cotton-room, which is a building quite detached from the warehouse - I am not aware that the prisoner had any occasion to go into that room, and did not see him there - I found the silk in a handkerchief, which I believe to be his; I have seen him with one of the same pattern; he was with me when I found it; I asked him how to account for it; he said he did not know, the silk was not his - I asked if it was his handkerchief; he said he did not know - I sent for the officer, and when he came I told him how wrong it was for him to do so, and I had been served so by some of his predecessors; he made no reply - he said when he was going out that he was a lost man, and did not care what became of him; I afterwards went to his lodging with the officer.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. You say, when the boy went away the prisoner had returned? A. Yes; and in about half an hour I found the silk in the cotton-room. I had been some few moments in my house, once or twice; a person might have gone into the warehouse without my seeing them, but not without my hearing the door go; my house is about six feet off; I went up stairs into the bed-room which looks into the warehouse; I was there five or ten minutes, I might be a quarter of an hour altogether in the house - I employ forty or fifty other workmen; I believe they were not on the premises; I did not see any one go from the warehouse to the cotton-room; they might have done so, but it is very improbable.
 MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Could any person have gone out of the warehouse without your hearing them? A. I think not, because the door makes a noise; it was not the business of any person to have been employed there but the prisoner.
 COURT. Q. When you came back the prisoner was in the room where the silk was? A.
