ALBERT C. F. POLLEY. I am engaged in the Judicial Department
of the Privy Council Office. I have known defendant from 25 to 27
years. I am 37 and he is 38. He has been in the Post Office for 17
years, I think, and before that he worked as an indoor servant. He
has always borne the character of a thoroughly respectable man. He
never mentioned to me that he was given to betting.
LORD ROBERT CECIL. I first knew defendant towards the end of
1899. I only knew him for the six or eight months he was in my ser-
vice as odd man. He was an additional postman at that time and was
able during part of the day to come into my house and help in the
ordinary domestic work. I believe it is one of the rules of the Post
Office not to employ these extra postmen unless they have some other
employment as well. Defendant left my service early in 1900. While
he was with me he gave complete satisfaction, and, as far as I know
was perfectly honest, straightforward, and truthful. I have made
inquiries and I believe that to be his general reputation. I have been
shown a letter from Lady Cecil, my wife, to the defendant to the same
effect.
JOHN CROMPTON, recalled. After October 15, the date I have spoken
to, the prisoner was seen date-stamping letters when he should not
have been doing so. On October 26 he was actually seen putting &
letter down at 2.30, when two winners were in, which was date-stamped
1.30. As far as I know it has been almost systematically going on for
about three months. I am told £20 or £30 have been paid out.
Verdict, Guilty.
Sentence, Six months' hard labour.

POLAK, Mary, pleaded guilty of maliciously publishing a certain
defamatory libel of and concerning Thomas Mitchinson.
Mr. Curtis Bennett prosecuted.
Prisoner, baving promised not to repeat the offence, was sentenced
to one week's imprisonment, and ordered to pay the costs of the pro-
secution.
WAUGH, Algernon (36, clerk); embezzling and stealing a certain
valuable security, to wit, & cheque for £20 48. 6d. received by him
for and on account of the inhabitants of the united parishes of St.
Margaret, Lothbury, and St. Bartholomew-by-the-Exchange, his em-
ployers.
Mr. Bray, for the prosecution, stated that the prosecutor, Canon
Ingram, bad regarded this as a civil rather than a criminal matter,
but the prisoner had gone straight to the police and made a confession.
The money had been entirely refunded and he did not propose to offer
evidence.
