WILLIAM STAFFORD. I am solieitor to Mr. Thomas Holt. On 81st. Oot..
last year, the prisoner applied to me for an advance of 6001.-he proposed
the security of the household furniture, plate, &c., of the gentleman upon
whose behalf he professed to apply, which he afterwards declared to be himself
--- he had his carriage at the door, and at bis solicitation I went with him to
his house, called Holly-house, in the New Kent-road-I went over the prin
cipal rooms of the house, the drawing-room, dining-room, chief bedrooms,
and so forth-I then told him that my opinion unhesitatingly was that the
property bad cost him more money, but I could not advise any client to lend
5001. upon it without the valuation of an auctioneer as to what it was likely
to realise he had spoken of having a large amount of plate, and I said, "If
you will let me see the plate, I shall be able to judge without any difficulty"
- he said the plate was locked up previous to his wife going out of town, and
she was then at Herne Bay I saw the furniture, linen, china, glass, and
other things, except the wine-I said to him, in rather a familiar manner,
“Why, my dear fellow, you have got into a very splendid position bere; is
this at all settled on your wife?"-he said no; that he had been doing a very
successful business, amounting to 3,0001. a year, for several years past in the
law agency, and that he had got far above the world; but he explained to
me that the alterations then in progress in the law had rather interfered with
the coming off of some negotiations which he had on band-be distinctly
stated that it was bis own property-I asked him; and he stated that after
applying the money as he proposed, which he was seeking to raise through
me, he should not owe 2012 in the world; when I asked him if there was any
settlement upon his wife and family, and when he declared there was notbing
of that kind, I afterwards told him that I must treat his application strictly
as a matter of business, and I must request him to make the usual statutary
declaration that the property was free from any ineumbrance whatever, and
he said, “Oh, my dear Sir! I will make it with the greatest pleasure, without
any hesitation whatever; when I apply the money as I propose, I shall not
owe 201. in the world”-it was in consequence of the representation that be
was possessed of this property, and that it was not subject to any charge or
incumbrance, that I got the money for hin.
COURT. Q. But he had not, at that time, told you that there was no en-
eumbrance upon it ? A. No; he had not in direct terms told me that, but
on my proposing the declaration he distinctly stated that there was not any
encumbrance of any sort or character--the words he used were, "Oh, my
dear Sirl there is not any encumbrance, no man has a claim of a shilling
upon it; and when I have applied the money I am seeking to raise througla
you as I propose, I shall not owe 20%, in the world "-in consequence of that
I advanced him 3201., in one 300l.-note and one for 201., that was the money
of Mr. Holt.
