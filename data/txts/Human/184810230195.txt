EDWARD RAY. I am a surgeon, of Dulwich. I assisted Mr. Chapman in the post-mortem  examination—I attribute death to the combined effects of inflammation of the bladder, the uterus, and the peritoneum—she had certainly advanced into the second month of pregnancy—abortion had taken place, and I should suppose from unnatural causes.
   Cross-examined. Q . It constantly arises from natural causes, does it not? A.  It is not common, unless violence is employed—all the appearances I found might have arisen from natural causes if there was severe inflammation—I have not known severe inflammation supervene on abortion—I have read of such cases, even when produced by what I should call natural causes. I wish that some medical man should examine my hand, to see whether it is in a state to reach the uterus of a person in a rigid state, like this young woman's. I did what I could, but I told her I could prove nothing. I told her to keep her mind easy, and gave her some laxitive medicine. When I tr ied to examine her next time, I had not got my finger more than an inch when I found menstruation coming on again, and I told her to go to bed, and keep her feet warm. I drew her water off for her, as she complained of pain. I gave her an ounce of salts to take home, and I gave her at the same time four pills. I told her to take half an ounce of salts that night, and next day to take two pills, but no salts. I said, 'Take them alternately, till your bowels are relieved.'
