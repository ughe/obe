619. JAMES LLOYD and JOHN DINSDALE were indicted for
stealing, on the 19th of January, 10 wooden staves, value 158.; and
pieces of wood, value 58.; the goods of the Company of Proprietors of the
Grand Surrey Canal, the masters of the said James Lloyd.
Thomas GEORGE Morris. I am in the employ of the Surrey Canal Compo
ny, as a constable. On the 19th of January, I was on the premises between
eleven and twelve o'clock-I saw Dinsdale just inside the gate, coming out
with a horse and cart--the cart appeared loaded with lath wood, but on els-
mination, I found it contained staves and deal ends, which had been taken
from the bonding-yard - I had asked him what he had got beside lath
wood, and he answered, “Nothing"--when I found these other things, I
asked him how he came by them-he said a man gave them to him that he
did not know his name, but he should know the man if he saw him I took
him down to the bonding-yard, where there were four men at work
pointed to one man, and said, "Is that the man?"-- he said, “No-
then pointed to Lloyd, and said, "Is that the man ?"-he said, "Yes"-
Lloyd is a deal porter--he works for the company occasionally-I asked
Lloyd if he had given Dinsdale any staves or deal ends--he said, “No;
he did not know any thing at all about giving it-Dinsdale said, "Yon
did give it me, Jem, you know you did, and I promised you some beer"-
asked Lloyd to go with me to the Superintendent of the Dock, and he
went directly.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. What time did you begin to unload
the cart? d. A little after eleven o'clock--the men go to dinner at twelve
o'clock-Charles Henry Davis, John Brown, and William Roach, were al
work with Lloyd I think it was between eleven and twelve o'clock when
I took Dinsdale to the bonding-yard, and pointed out Lloyd to him-1
think it was before the men went to dinner, but it might be after they re-
turned--I had unloaded the cart first, and it took some time-the men
must have passed by where the cart was, to go to dinner-I will not swear
it was not past one o'clock.
