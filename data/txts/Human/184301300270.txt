RICHARD GOODMAN. I am a salesman, in the employ of Mr. Harvey. On the 5th of Jan., the prisoner showed me a pattern of satinette, and gave me instructions to send thirteen yards to Mrs. Richards, of Greenwich, at 2 s . 6 d . a yard—I cut it off, and gave it to the prisoner—it came to 1 l . 13 s . 0 1/2 d . JOHN HOUGHTON. I am in the upholstery department at Mr. Harvey's. On the 7th of Jan., I had some work to do at Greenwich—the prisoner gave me a brown paper parcel, and told me to deliver it to Mr. Richards, of Greenwich—I gave it to my man—he brought me back 1 l . 13 s ., which I paid to the prisoner, on Monday the 9th, between nine and ten o'clock in the morning—I said I had got some money for him—he said, "What is it for?" I said, "For the parcel you gave me to take to Greenwich"—on the fol lowing day, Tuesday, he asked me if I recollected what money I paid him the day before, as he had put it into his pocket with his own money, and did not recollect it—I told him a sovereign, a half-sovereign, a half-crows, and a sixpence.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you receive money? A.  Occasionally—I some times pay it to the young men that serve, and sometimes to the cashier—I never was directed to pay the cashier—we consider it right either way. SAMUEL BARNARD. I was directed to deliver the brown paper parcel at Greenwich—I delivered it to Mr. Richards, and paid the money to Houghton. ELIZA WILLIAMS re-examined.  This sum has not been accounted for either on the 9th or 10th—it is not in the prisoner's book on either of those days.

   ELIZAWILLIAMS . I am the wife of Samuel Williams, a cow-keeper, in the Old Kent-road. On the 13th of Jan. the prisoner came for a penny-worth of milk—I served him a halfpenny-worth by mistake—he gave me a shilling—I told him it was bad—he said he did not know it, he would go and get me 1 d . for the milk, if I would take the mug of milk and give him the shilling—I said I would not return the shilling, but detain him—I sent for an officer—the prisoner walked out of the yard till he got round the corner, then he ran—he left the shilling in my hand—I called a policeman, gave him the shilling, and he gave it me back again—I put it on a shelf, and the next morning I marked it and gave it to the officer.
