   COURT to MR. DUKE. Q.  Seager represented himself to you not as the owner, but that he had a principal; are such transaction common in trade? A.  Very common—I understood he was the agent—there are jobbers who sell articles which are not shipped—they are called general agents—I have had about thirty tons of rope before under similar circumstances—if a party ordered the rope, and it did not answer, when delivered, that would be a fair reason for returning it—I think this rope was made in the country, and afterwards sent to London for sale—the marks do not indicate foreign ship ments, as a matter of course—I believe they were country goods sent to town, and sold in town—coasters discharge their cargo at that wharf—it is not a place necessarily implying that goods are going outwards—we make a mark on the goods, which we know them by; we do not choose the world to know who our correspondents are—the address is not put on goods at the wharf.
   MR. BALLANTINE to EBENEZER KIPPLEWHITE. Q.  When you took Seager into custody did you tell him it was for removing six packages from the Custom-house, taking then in a cart to Budge-row, getting 21 l ., and signing Mr. Booth's book in his own name? A.  I told him that was the charge—I will not be certain that I said he paid Mr. Booth 50 s . on 4th Aug. for back  ing the goods—it is very likely I may have said, "We have proof that you have taken the gunny-bags having the shipping-mark on them off the rope, and then taken the covers away with you"—I cannot say positively that I said so—I recollect the substance of what passed; very likely that was part of it, I am not certain—he said he took them to West Kent wharf, in a boat, and that it was his intention to have taken them to Paul's-wharf, and that Holliday was to meet him there—I do not recollect his telling me that the waterman would not let him go so far on account of the tide running so strong, and at the suggestion of the waterman, he left them at the West Kent wharf—the waterman told me that—Seager acknowledged it at the Mansion-house—he said, that on the 14th he fetched them in the cart, and took them to Mr. Booth, and got 21 l . on them from Mr. Booth, and told Mr. Booth they were from Gardiner and Holliday—I swear he did not say to me that Mr. Booth said he only knew him, Seager, in the matter—Mr. Booth said so at the Mansion-house.
