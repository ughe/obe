I am private watchman to Messrs. Petty and Hanson, coal—merchants, Bankside. I know the stables of Messrs. Jones and Co.—they have a back door opening to Moss's-alley—it is almost oppo site to Cann's-alley, which leads into White Hind alley—Simpson's stable is at the corner of White Hind alley, and has a door opening to Pye—gardens—I was on duty on the morning of the 29th of Aug., and Pickard called my attention to Simpson's stable door—it was open—I passed the door, and heard some persons in the stable, but I did not see them—there was a light in the stable—I saw one sack taken into that stable by a short man, who looked more like a coal—porter than anything else—I then met a couple of police men, and told them what was transpiring. JAMBS WELSH. I live opposite Simpson's stable; it is in a narrow pas sage one way; the other way is wider. On the 29th of Aug. I was up at a quarter to four o'clock—I looked out at the window, and after a little while I saw the stable door open, and a man brought a sack—he stooped as he brought it—I could not tell the man—he carried it into Simpson's stable, and put H down, then brought another sack from White Hind alley—he pitched that down in the stable, then went back and brought another—he did the same thing with that—the sacks appeared to be full—I then went to another window, where I had a view into Cann's-alley—I saw King—he was in the stable—I saw Simpson about a minute after the first sack was delivered, and before the second was delivered—he was at the door of the loft of the stable—he could see what was going on—he could see both ways—he was not doing anything—he remained there during the time I saw the other two sacks pitched—I do not know whether he remained there after that—I did not take any more account of him—I saw him distinctly—I have no doubt of his being the person—he was able to see what was going on—he could see each way—by taming over the loft door he could see into Cann's-alley—he looked out two or three times after the sacks were come—there is a gas—lamp at the corner, which was alight at the time—after the first sack had been delivered I saw. Simpson come from Pye—gardens, and go into the stable, and afterwards I saw him looking out of the door of the loft.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  Where is this Pye—gardens? A.  Adjoining the gas—works—Simpson came down Pye—gardens, to go to the stable—that was after the first sack had been delivered there—he was not at the stable when the first sack was delivered—I then saw him go into the stable, and go on—it was after the other two sacks had been delivered I saw him up in the loft—the other two sacks were taken in while he was in the stable—I cannot say whether it was as much as five minutes after the last sack had been delivered before I saw him up in the loft—I saw the sacks brought out of White Hind alley into the stable.
