   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Who first applied to you? A.  I applied myself. WILLIAM STILLING. I live at No. 11, New Inn-yard, Shoreditch. I know Norman and Guy—Norman came to me as his wife—they went by the name of Mr. and Mrs. Thompson—I had a house, No. 15, Bridge water-square, that was to let—Thompson and the woman came about a fortnight before last Michaelmas—Thompson said he thought the house would suit him—he asked what rent it was—I said, "58 l . a year"—I wanted 10 l . for fix tures—he said he would not pay for any fixtures, he would give me the rent—he took it for three years—he said his goods were coming from Man chester, and he was in the Manchester trade—upon this arrangement I asked for a reference, and Thompson wrote down this reference for me—this is what he wrote in my presence and hers—( read )—"Mr. Hobbs, builder, Eagle New Tap, Manor-street, Chelsea"—I went to Mr. Hobbs, and saw him at the Eagle Tap, in New Manor-street—I asked him if he knew Mr. Thompson—he said yes, very well, he was a very respectable man, he had a good deal of property, and lived in a house of his brother's at Man chester for ten years, and always paid his rent very well—I asked him if he would be afraid to let him a house of 50 l . or 60 l . a-year—he said not for 100 l . a year, he should be sure of the rent—he said I need not be afraid of letting him the house, I should be sure of the rent—in consequence of that I gave him possession of the house with Norman, who had passed as his wife—I have not got possession of the house now—there are some of the children in it now.
