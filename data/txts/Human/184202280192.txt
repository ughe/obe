PHILIP COLE. I am in the employ of Mr. Tuckwell. On the even ing of the 18th of February I was ordered not to go to work but the prisoner was ordered to work; and then Mr. Tuckwell told me to tell him not to work—this chain and weight were in the place where they were lift between seven and eight o'clock that evening. EMMA SHIRLEY. I keep a marine-store shop at Greenwich. the prisoner brought these things to me that night, between nine and ten o'clock, and I would not buy them.
   Prisoner.  I did not take the chain there, I never had it. Witness.  This is the chain he brought—the policeman came immediately after—the chain was then lying where the prisoner put it—I am sure he brought it—he pitched it down in the shop—I knew the prisoner before—I cannot swear he brought the crow-bar, but he brought the weight, bolts, and the chain. GEORGE BOOTH( police-sergeant R  2.) I received information—I went to Shirley's and asked what she had bought of a person, describing the prisoner—she said he had brought that chain, and left it there for the approval of her husband—Mr. Tuckwell came and identified it—I then found these other things in Shirley's house. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Six Months.
