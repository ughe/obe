   Cross-examined. Q.  Perhaps you will tell me you only had the poles to defend yourselves if attacked? A.  Yes—we did not ask him to come out, we did not threaten to take his life—I would not do such a thing—neither of us called him a b—I am quite sure Carter did not desire him to come out, and say he would take his life, nothing of the kind—I did not hear it—the prosecutor walked out by himself—I think the officer helped him up off the stairs—I went in with Carter to take him out—I was not altogether sober—I do not know whether Carter was—Wiseman was not, Carter was—I cannot answer for his being exactly sober, he appeared to me to be sober—I should think we were out of the house about twenty minutes—we left Wiseman inside all that time—we were standing by the door the greatest part of the time, but we did not offer to break it open, as it was locked—when the officers came they made him open the door, and we went in—the officer was with us when we went in and got Wiseman out—I had not the stick in my hand when the officer came—we put them down when we saw the officer come, not having any fear—we never made use of the sticks. JONATHAN SHEPHERD( police-constable R  105.) I went to Mrs. Ham brook's on the 5th of February, near upon one o'clock—I was called by Carter and Smith—I could not get in for a short time, somebody inside said they had lost the key, and could not open the door—it was afterwards opened—I went in, and saw there had been a good deal of blood on the lower part of the stairs—it had been wiped down, but was very visible—Wise man was gone home then—Carter and Smith had no sticks in their hands that I saw—Wiseman must have been gone, according to what they stated to me some little time—Smith was bleeding from his hand when I went in—I asked him what it was—he said a man who had been inside was cut with the shovel in the eye, and bleeding—I asked where he was—they said they had sent him home—I saw nothing of Wiseman that night—I had heard the wit nesses talking outside, I thought there was too much noise, and returned to see the cause of it—they met me at the end of the passage, told me this, and I got admittance—the prisoner said he was willing to go—he did not say he lived in the house—he gave his address, and said he lived in Bridge-street, Greenwich—( looking at his deposition )—this is my handwriting—it was read over to me after I signed it—I do not recollect that the prisoner said he lived there, Mrs. Hambrook said he did.—( The witness's deposition being read, stated  —"I took the prisoner into custody at Mrs. Hambrook's—he said he lived there, and the others had no business there, that the others had struck him, &c.")—It ought to have been written, "She said he lived there."
   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you quite sure Wiseman was gone before you mule? A.  Yes, there is no mistake about that—it was their loud talking that brought me to the place.
