 Cross-examined. Q. You dined at the York Arms? A. Yes - we had wine for dinner; we got to Red Lion-square between eight and nine o'clock - we had stopped at a public-house on the way; I recollect the music in Red Lion-passage - I did not dance, nor did he; Mr. Arundel might move round with a lady - there were several women about, and two ladies were talking to us; the prisoner was there - we all five went into Holborn; I had some sherry and bitters - we treated the girls with what they chose; the prisoner was not intoxicated - I do not think I drank there; I swear the prisoner was not intoxicated; I was perfectly sober - how the purse got into her bosom - I cannot tell.
   GEORGE CONNER. I am pot-boy at the White Lion, Drury-lane - I saw the prisoner there with two more bad girls - I saw her take the purse out of her bosom, and give it to Pinnel; she was tipsy, but not insensible.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was Arundel rather tipsy? A. No, he was cheerful.
   HENRY BURBIDGE. I am a Policeman. I received the prisoner in charge; Pinnel handed me the purse with four sovereigns in it - the prisoner said she was drunk, and was not aware of having put it into her bosom.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you notice whether she was tipsy? A. She appeared rather so; she fainted away two or three times.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in Red Lion-square - the gentleman came to me; the music was playing - he asked me to go and have a glass with him; we went to Holborn- he asked the music to follow; I went into a gin-shop with him, and got very much intoxicated.
     NOT GUILTY.
 NEW COURT. WEDNESDAY, FEBRUARY 22.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 784.  GEORGE PARKER was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 1 carriage-strap, value 2s., the goods of John Trotter.
   WILLIAM BURNABY. I am footman to John Trotter, Esq., of Park-place, Edgware-road. This strap was taken from the hind part of the carriage, as it was going down Oxford-street, from Covent-garden, at half-past eleven o'clock on the night of the 17th of February; I was inside, and saw the prisoner come from the pavement, get on the step, and take it; I stopped the coach, got out, and took the prisoner with it on the spot: I got into the carriage on purpose, as we had lost so many straps.
   JOSEPH STANNAND. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner, and have the strap, which I took out of his waistcoat pocket - it is the check brace.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Three Months.
 785.  PETER PERKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of February , 1 silk gown, value 9s., and 2 suits of boy's clothes, the goods of John Garrard.
   JOHN GARRARD. I live in Hackney-road, and am a clothes salesman. On the 6th of February, about eight o'clock in the morning, I was opening my shop, and taking the shutters backwards; I was asked by a woman if I had lost any thing - I looked, and said I had: she said a man had come out with something, which appeared to be a silk gown and some boys' clothes, and gone down the next turning; I went, but could not see him.
   ESTHER GILHAM. I was coming across the road, and saw a man come out of the shop, with a bundle; he looked round, and the prisoner came out after him - they hurried down Austin-street; the prisoner is one of them - I described him to Vann, and he took him.
   THOMAS VANN. I took the prisoner from Gilham's description.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Seven Years.
 786.  JAMES PHELPS was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of March, 1831 , 3 bushels of wheat, value 30s., the goods of George Corfe.
   WILLIAM BARRETT. I am an officer of Isleworth and Twickenham. On the 22nd of March, 1831, about two o'clock in the morning, I fell in with the prisoner and another, near Sir Benjamin Hobhouse's gate, three or four hundred yards from the prosecutor's - it was in the high road to London; the prisoner was first - I laid hold of him, and I saw behind him a man carrying a sack - I put the prisoner aside, and seized the sack; the man who had it struggled with me, and we both went down, but I still held the sack; the prisoner begged me to let it pass - I said they might go for that time, but I would retain the property; they went on - a great deal of the wheat came out of the sack; I took it, as well as I could, to the prosecutor's - the next morning I went to the barn; I saw a little track of wheat from there to the high road, and on to the place where I met the men, where it had come out in a body; I compared it with what was in the barn, it corresponded - the other man has since been transported for another robbery - I did not find the prisoner till last Wednesday night; I knew him before - he had lived in the neighbourhood twelve months.
 Prisoner. Q. Can you say I was near the man? A. Yes, you was close to him; I told you I would bring you to justice, but you might go that time.
   JOSEPH FULLER. I am a labourer in the service of Mr. Corfe; his barn was locked up, and all was right - I went the next morning at six o'clock, and found the lock had been removed, and somebody had been in the barn - I missed about three bushels of wheat - the sack was not my master's; there was a track of wheat from the barn to the main road - I had threshed the wheat; I have been all my life acquainted with wheat - I have no doubt it was Mr. Corfe's.
