RCHARD LARKIN. I am in partnership with Mr. Webster, as stationers,
5 6. Piccadilly--I have seen the envelopes marked A E J and they
cime from our shop- they are the ordinary envelopes we sell-Dr. Teggart
customer of ours--I produce a dio marked " 49, Jermyn Street, S.W.
I have looked at the letter marked D, where the top has been torn away
-there still remains the lower parts of the 4, the 9, and the S. and W.--
this appears to be paper that has been stamped with this die, and the
measurements correspond-it is the same quality paper that we have
supplied to Dr. Teggart, with this die on it--letter H is on a different
paper.
Cross-examined. I could not swear positively that we supplied this piece
of paper to Dr. Teggart-I don't think this blue paper came from our shop,
the bottle paper; it is paper that might be used for other purposes
Besides wrapping up bottles; it is a very common kind of paper-the only
piece of paper I can identify as coming from our shop is the one with the
remainder of the figures 4, 9, and the letters S. and W -We supplied
peper similar to this (H), but I cannot identify it--the envelopes bear our
name; we have supplied similar envelopes to Dr. Teggart, and also to a great
many other persons-we only sell retail.

| letter D and envelope C-on the 19th June I had made inquiries at Mesers,

nopived several envelopes and sheets of paper from Dr. Teggart, which I

bui I am shaking so I don't think I can write "-I said "Well, do your
best"—he said "What shall I write ?”—I said " As on this envelope ; " I
produced one which Mr. Teggart supplied me with, and said “Will you
write as on this envelope, His Grace Lord Oranmore and Browne, 28,
Queen's Gate, S.W.'"- this is the envelope (L)-he wrote this in my
presence, as it now appears-I next saw the two female servants separately,
