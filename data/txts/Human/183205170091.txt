JOHN CALLER(Police-constable S 67). On the morning of the 30th of April, about half-past six o'clock, I saw the prisoner in Pancras-road, carrying this copper and pot in a bag - I asked if he had not got a copper; he said Yes, that he had found these articles in a field, and was going to take them to the station-house; I said he had better go to the station with me - he said he had no objection, but when he had got a few steps, he put them down and said I might take them; I took them, but was forced to put them down once or twice to take hold of him, as he was very obstinate; I went to the Adam and Eve, in Pancras-road, and found the wash-house door had been broken open, and this chisel was there, which exactly corresponded with the marks on the door; the copper was missed, but the bricks had been pulled down, and we could not fit it.
   SARAH WATERFIELD. I am a widow, and keep the  Adam and Eve. The officer called me up - I missed a copper and a pot; I am sure these are mine; I had seen them safe on the Saturday night, and this was on the Monday morning.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was in search of work, and saw these articles in a bag; I was going to take them to the station-house.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1291.  ROBERT WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of May , 3 chisels, value 1s.; 1 pair of compasses, value 6d.; 1 gouge, value 6d.; 2 gimblets, value 6d., and 3 files, value 6d., the goods of Henry Bull ; 4 chisels, value 2s., and 2 gimblets, value 8d., the goods of John Syrett.
   THOMAS WILSON(Police-constable T 59). On the night of the 1st of May I was on duty at Hyde-park-terrace, about eight o'clock; I went to the back of the buildings; I then went by a ladder into an attic of an unfurnished house, and found the prisoner there, with these articles on him - I asked how he came there; he made no answer.
   HENRY BULL. I am a carpenter, and was at work at these houses. On the morning of the 2nd of May, when I went to work, I missed some tools, which I had left there; some of these are mine, and some belong to John Syrett.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1292.  THOMAS ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of May , 1 umbrella, value 8s., and 1 hat, value 5s., the goods of Richard Molineux.
   JOHN TODD. I am an ironmonger, and live in Bury-street, Bloomsbury. On the 5th of May I met the prisoner in Bury-street, with an umbrella and a hat -I stopped him - Mr. Molineux came up and claimed them; he was taken into custody - he wanted me to go up the street, and said he would point out the person who gave them to him.
   RICHARD MOLINEUX. I am a coach-wheelwright, and live in Little Russell-street; I came up while Mr. Todd had hold of the prisoner, who was scuffling to get away -I knew the hat and umbrella immediately, and gave him in charge; I had seen them in my passage half an hour before - my house is two hundred yards from where he was taken.
   JOHN CARNEY. (Police-constable E 34). I received the prisoner; he said a man gave these things to him, but did not say where he was to take them.
     GUILTY. Aged 24. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1293.  WILLIAM ANDREWS was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of April , 1 saw, value 3s., and 1 knife, value 8s., the goods of Isaac Clift.
   ISAAC CLIFT. I live in Holywell-lane, Shoreditch, and am a patten-manufacturer. I employed the prisoner on the 10th of April; when the men left work, I missed a saw from the shop - the prisoner came to work on the 11th, and I went into the shop, and asked him and the rest of the men if they knew any thing of it; the prisoner said he had taken it to a man where he lodged, and would go and get it - I missed my knife that day, and from information I found them in pledge; the prisoner absconded the day I missed my knife; he came on the following Friday, and I had him taken - he begged my pardon, and said he would make any arrangement, if I would not prosecute him.
   THOMAS STEVENS. I am a fellow-workman of the prisoner's. On the 10th of April I met him in Shoreditch - he told me had taken the saw, and pawned it for 6d.; on the Wednesday I heard him in the passage - he said,"Larry, bring the knife up, I want to take it to be ground."
   JAMES ASHLEY. I am apprentice to a pawnbroker, in Shoreditch. I have a saw, pawned by the prisoner, for 1s. on the 10th of April.
   JOHN NEALE. I am assistant to a pawnbroker. I have a patten-knife, pawned on the 11th of April, to the best of my belief, by the prisoner - I know I have seen him there.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he could not get his wages, and had pledged the saw to obtain victuals.
   ISAAC CLIFT. I did not owe him any thing.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1294.  HENRY BROUGHTON was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of April , 1 coat, value 7s., the property of John Morgan.
   PHILIP JONES(Police-constable N 217). On the 26th of April I was at my window in Church-street, Hackney - I saw the prisoner coming down the grove with a great coat; two more men were behind him - he was walking very fast, and when he turned into the main road, he ran as fast as he could; I ran after him for about ten minutes, and took him - while he was running he threw the coat down in the dirt; this is it - he said at the station that he bought it of the two men who were with him, for 2s. - the other two had run off; it was a quarter before eight o'clock in the morning, and he was coming from the prosecutor's.
   JOHN MORGAN. I am in the employ of Mr. Barber, of Hackney; I attend his stage-coaches. On the 26th of April I placed my great coat in a chaise, in his coach-house, about seven o'clock in the morning - I missed it at eight, and saw it afterwards in possession of the officer.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that he had bought the coat of two men in Hackney-fields.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. - Transported for Seven Years.
