JOHN CONNOLLY. I am a waterman and lighterman. At half-past ten
Ndlock that night I saw the prisoner in the hold of the Nelson, which I
had locked down before dark-he was a stranger-I said, “Halloo, old
bow. I have caught you at last; I suspect you have been robbing this
horse that I have the care of"-I called for assistance-the steward and
hind mate came to my assistance when I spoke to the prisoner he took
something from his bosom, and threw overboard-it resembled glass-I
heard the splash-I cautioned him not to throw them overboard, as they
were left in my care.

1092. HANNAH PARKER was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of
February, 1 pair of scissors, value 2d.; 1 purse, value 1d.; and 1 shil-
ling; the property of Henry Cronk.
Åns Cronx. I am the wife of Henry Cronk, and live at Deptford. I
had a pair of scissors, a purse, a duplicate in it, a shilling, and several
other little articles, in a little basket hanging by the side of the fire-I
saw them safe on Thursday, the 17th of February, about six o'clock in the
evening, and missed them about nine next morning-I spoke to the police-
man---the articles produced are them.
JOHN STREKGTH (police-constable R 96.) On the 18th of February the
prosecutrix came and told me she had been robbed of a purse and several
other things--I apprehended the prisoner at her mother's, and charged her
with robbing Mr. Cronk-she said, if I would let her go she would tell me
where the things were-I said I could not, she might do as she liked about
telling me-she then went into the yard, and showed me a broken pane
where she had put these things through-I went into the room, and found
the articles in a bird-cage-here are the scissors, the purse and duplicate,
and basket.
