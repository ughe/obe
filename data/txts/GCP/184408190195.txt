ROBERT BUTTERWORTH. I am a carman. On the 15th of July I went to
the Three Compasses, in Little Guildford-street, St. Saviour's, for some
beer for supper-I had a jug with me-Mr. Cole and Mr. Parsons were
there I drank some beer with them-while standing there the prisoner came
in and called for a pint of beer--Mr. Peacock, the landlord, drew it-be put
his hand into his pocket and pulled out some halfpence; it was not sufficient;
he then pulled out an old coin-the landlord said he did not take such money
as that-be drank the beer, and wanted another pint- the landlord refused
he struck the pot on the counter twice-the landlord came round from the
bar, turned him out, and closed the door on him about five minutes after-
wards he came back and rapped at the door-I, being close to it, opened it
I was going home, and before I could get away from him he cut me on the
head with a pair of tongs, and I fell senseless on the ground-when I came to
myself I was at the station-house-I was hurt on my head, my left knee, and
both my legs--the skin of my head was broken-it bled a great deal-a sur
geon's apprentice attended me for about ten minutes- I did not go to him
again-1 had never seen or spoken to the prisoner before.
