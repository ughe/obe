1378. GEORGE STAINER and HENRY JORDAN were indieted
for a misdemeanor.
Messrs. CHAMBERS and SHEA conducted the Prosecution.
ANNA MARIA COLWELL. I am the wife of a tobacconist, and live a
Bridge-house-place, Newington, Surrey. Stainer came to our shop on the
14th of April, for fox. of tobacco-it came to 1d.- he gave me a sixpence
-I had not change enough-he said he would have foz.-I gave him 4...
and took his sixpence, and laid it at the corner of the till-Langley then
came in- I took the till out, and showed him the sixpence- it was a
bad one.
Cross-examined by Mr. Bodkix. Q. How long elapsed from the time of
your taking it and the officer coming in? 1. I shonld think not a minute
-my husband was out at the time-there had been other customers in the
shop, but only Stainer brought a sixpence when my husband went out
there was silver in the till; but I put the silver I take in a corner of the
till, to show my husband what I have taken in his absence-there is no
division in it; but I took the till out, and showed the officer the sixpence
in the corner I had put no mark on it before.
MR. Shea. Q. Did you shut the till hard? 4. I do not remem-
ber-I do not think that one sixpence could jump out, and another
jump in.
MARY Evans. I live with Mr. Budden, who keeps the Rose. Im
Stainer about five o'clock, on the 14th of April-he offered me a sixpence
- I just laid it in the till there were other sixpences there--the officer
came in at one door as the prisoner went out-he asked me for it-I had
just laid the sixpence out of my fingers- I gave it him-I am sure it
was the same sixpence-it was just in one corner of the till-I had not lost
sight of it.
Cross-examined. Q. Is yours a till that draws out? A. Yes; we put
the money through a notch in the counter-when I drew it out I saw a
good deal of silver in the till—I had just laid the sixpence out of my hand
when the officer came, but not out of my sight-all the silver was in my
sight, and that among the rest.
JURY. Q. Had you shut the till? A. No, I had nearly shut it.
MARIA LOUISA JAQUES. I am a tobacconist. and live in Lambeth.
On the 14th of April, Stainer came for a foz. of tobacco-it came to 1d.
he gave me a sixpence- I gave him change-I put the sixpence into the till
-there was no other sixpence in it—there were shillings and half-crowns
-in a short time Langley came in-I showed him the sixpence it was
bad one-I gave it to him.
Cross-examined. Q. Do you always attend to the shop? 4. Yes-
had sold a good many cigars-some are 3d. each–I dare say I had sold
some that afternoon when I gave the officer the sixpence I looked in the
till, and there was no other sixpence there--we must have taken some and
passed them again.
EDWARD LANGLEY (police-constable A 11.) I watched the two prisoners
on the 14th of April - they went to Colwell's shop-Stainer went in, and
Jordan waited ten yards off-he came out-I went in and got a sixpence-
I followed the prisoners to the Rose, in Westminster-road-Stainer went in,
Jordan was about five yards off-Stainer came out, and they joined again-
I went in and got a sixpence there they went a little way on to West-
minster-road, then turned back and went to the New Cat, to Jaques-
