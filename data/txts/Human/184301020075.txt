JOHN ELLIOTT( police-constable K  344.) I was called to Mrs. Westgate's on Tuesday the 27th of Dec., the prisoners were given to me—Pratt said she had pledged some of the things, and her husband had got the tickets—I found twenty duplicates on Dowsett—he said some of them related to the stolen property, and the rest were his own.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did not Dowsett say that he had got the duplicates from Pratt? A.  No—she said she had given up the duplicates to her hus band—she did not say her husband had been speaking to her about it—Dow sett said he had not pawned them, and had nothing to do with it, that she had pawned them, and he got the duplicates from her—Pratt did not contra dict any thing he said—nothing was said to me about their being married, bat at the station be gave his name William Dowsett, and she gave hers Keziah Pratt—he said she was not his wife, and she did not say she was. WILLIAM CARPENTER. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Charles-street. I have two pillows, and a flat-iron pawned by Pratt, on the 30th of Sept., and on the 1st of Oct.—two of those duplicates produced by the officer are what I gave her. JOHN GOODRIDGE. I am a pawnbroker. I produce three pillows—one of them I took in of Pratt for 1 s . 3 d . on the 23rd of Sept.—one of the dupli cates produced by the officer is what I gave.
   MRS. MAY . I cannot identify either of the pillows which the pawnbro kers state were pledged by Pratt. NOT GUILTY.

WILLIAM DOWSETT and KEZIAH PRATT were again  indicted for stealing, on the   12th of Aug ., 1 bolster, value 3 s  .; 1 flat iron, 6 d  .; and 20lbs. weight of feathers, 10 s  .; the goods of Ann Westgate . RUTH MAY. I let these premises to Pratt—Dowsett came and lived with her—we had a flat iron there on Monday morning, the 26th of Dec., and on the Tuesday morning it was pledged—I missed a bolster, and the feathers out of the bed, this is the bolster—I can swear to it—I cannot swear to this flat iron, but it is something like ours—this bolster is Mrs. Ann Westgate's.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  She is not here? No—I know this bolster by a place that I sewed at the top—I only came for Mrs. Westgate as she could not come—this is her bolster.
   Pratt.  I sewed the holes up in that bolster myself. Witness.  No, you did not, I sewed it myself.
   Pratt.  You would have had all the things back if you had been quiet, but you gave us in charge. JOHN GOODRIDGE. This bolster was pawned at our shop, in the name of Ann Dowsett, but I did not take it in—this is the duplicate that was given of it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Then you do not know who pawned it? A.  No, it is not my taking in. JOHN ELLIOTT( police-constable K  344.) I took this duplicate for the bolster out of Dowsett's pocket.
