MR. STRAIGHT submitted that as regards some of the counts the proof was
defective, especially with respect to the obtaining goods upon the payment of
cheques which could not be said to be a false pretence as he actually had
money at his bankerd' though not suficient to meet the cheques when presented.
MR. METOALPE contended that the mere giving a cheque amounted to a
representation that he had funds to meet . MR. JUSTICE Lush was not
aware of any authority which went to that extent; the giving a chegw upon a
bank where he had no account would be a false pretenos, because it was a
representation that he had an account there, but he was not aware that it had
been held that when a man really had an account, although it might not be
suficient to meet the cheque, that that would amount to a false protence. The
substantial question, however, for the Jury, was whether the prisoner was really
carrying on a legitimate business or whether it was a mere pretende to raise
money.
