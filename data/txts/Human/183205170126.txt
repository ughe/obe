   JANE ELIZABETH FARRIS. I am single, and live at No. 27, Frith-street. On the evening of the 17th of May I was at work at the front window, and saw the prisoner at the door of No. 38, which is opposite; he was endeavouring to get the padlock off the door - I watched him, and saw him go to the end of the street, and then return to the door; he was still endeavouring to take the padlock off; he opened it about half-past eight o'clock, then went in, and shut the door after him - I informed Mr. Fenn, my landlord.
   CHARLES FENN. I am a cheesemonger, and live in Frith-street. On the evening of the 17th of May, between eight and nine o'clock, in consequence of what Farris said, I went over to No. 38 with a person who was then in my shop; I found the door ajar; I stood on one side, and the other person on the other, and in a few minutes we saw the prisoner coming out of the shop, with some tools under his arm, and some before him; I said,"My friend, what do you do here? who are you? do you work for Mr. Sawyer?" he said, "Yes, I am Mr. Sawyer's man;" I sent for Mr. Sawyer and the Policeman - the prisoner managed to lay the tools down on the bench, close inside the door - the officer came, and took him.
 JAMES BARRACK. I went to the door with Ferris, and what he has stated is correct; when the prisoner came to the door, and we asked what he was going to do with the tools, he shifted them before him - I said, "Where are you going?" he said, "I am going out;" I said,"Not out of here till somebody comes" - the officer took the same tools which the prisoner had.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you take two head planes to the station-house, and then say they were not them? A. I took none; the officer took two small planes at first, and then I said there were two larger planes, which he had in his hand - I went back, and pointed them out to the officer.
 GREGORY  COLE DUDLEY(Police-constable C 6). I produce the plane, which I got at No. 38, Frith-street - these two bead planes were first pointed out, and I took them; the gentleman said there were two other planes as well; I went back, and they pointed out these.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been out of work a long time, and knowing that Mr. Sawyer would give me work, I went there, and saw the door open; I went in, and thought I should see Goodwin - I had nothing in my possession.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1410.  JOHN GOODGE was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of April , 1 gown, value 5s., the goods of John Matthews and another.
   THOMAS WILLIAM BLUETT. I am shopman to John Matthews and Leonard Clare Matthews; they are pawnbrokers. The prisoners has been a customer there; he came on the 19th of April - I was standing behind the counter, and heard a noise, as if there was something tearing; I went round the counter to the prisoner, and saw him taking a gown away - it had hung, on a nail: he was putting it up in his arm, and was in the act of taking it away; it had caught by a nail in taking it down, and had torn; I followed him into the court - he had come in by the back door; when he saw me coming towards him, he dropped the gown in the passage; he did not carry that into the court - I followed, and asked what business he had to take that gown; he said, "You don't mean to say such a thing of me;" I laid hold of him, and sent for the officer, to whom I gave the gown - I had seen it safe a few minutes before.
 GEORGE OLLEY. I live in Wellington-street, Pentonville, and am a lint-maker. I was in the shop, and saw the prisoner enter; he enatched the gown from a nail - it caught on another nail; I called to the witness, and went for the Policeman; I did not see the prisoner drop it; he appeared in liquor.
 WILLIAM PERRY (Police-constable G 25). I received the prisoner in charge; he struggled very hard, and when he could not get away, he pretended to be drunk, but I really think that he was quite sober.
 Prisoner. I was going to get a handkerchief out of pledge; I had drank rather more than did me good, and stumbled, and caught the gown to save myself - I then fell out again.
   GEORGE OLLEY. I saw him snatch the gown, but I could not see his face; the gown fell on the other nail, and then I saw him give another tug at it.
 JURY. Q. Did you see him take it off the nail? A. Yes; he had a duplicate of a handkerchief in his hand - there is a sort of lobby at the door which he entered by; and in that lobby he dropped the gown, two or three yards from where it had hung; I saw him twisting it up.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1411.  WILLIAM EDWARDS was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of May , 1 plane, value 2s., the goods of Thomas Capps.
 THOMAS CAMROUX (Police-serjeant N 3). On the evening of the 18th of May, between seven and eight o'clock, I was in Pitfield-street, Hoxton, and saw the prisoner, with another boy, coming from Queen-street, Hoxton-market, at a gentle run - they saw me and separated; I followed, seeing something bulky under his coat; I overtook him in Ashford-street; I asked what he had got, he said Nothing - I unbuttoned his coat, and found the plane, which he said belonged to his father.
   WILLIAM WORLEY. I am servant to Mr. Thomas Capps, of Old-street-road, pawnbroker. This plane is his - it was put out facing his door, on a stall - I had seen it safe that morning; I think our house is three hundred yards from Pitfield-street.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Confined Fourteen Days.
