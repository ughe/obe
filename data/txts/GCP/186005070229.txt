ROBERT PACKMAN (City-Policeman). On the 17th, from some information
I received, I went to Stone's-end police-station, and saw Barrett there—I.
told him I was sorry to see him there, and asked him if his friends were
aware that he was there-he said he believed they were, but he bad not seen
them-I said I understood he was there about some notes-he said, “Yes
I am ; I should like to tell you the truth about it"-I have known him
some years-he knows me to be an officer-I then said, “You know I am a
police officer, and whatever you say I shall have to make use of in evidence
against you before the Magistrate"-he then said, “I received them notes
from a person last night in front of the Mansion-house" he told me that
his grandmother had lately left him between 20001 and 30001. and that this
was some of the money to go on with—he meant the grandmother of the
person from whom he had received them; he said, “He gave me these
notes to get cashed for him"-I said, “Is that man a stranger to you ?"-
he said, “No; bis name is Reuben William Streaker, and he lives some-
where near Kennington-park”-I bad, previous to seeing the prisoner, seen
the four notes which the officer had—I said, " I see on the back of some of
the notes there is the name of Sole and Turner, and they are solicitors in
Aldermanbury, close to where you live"-he said, “Yes, I wrote the name
of Sole and Turner on one of the notes, but not on all of them"-I said, “I
saw you with a man in Finsbury-circus two or three days ago, who was a
stranger to me" he said, “That is the man I bad the notes of "--I had
seen him with another man as I told him, I saw the two prisoners together
about three days previous to Barrett's being taken in custody.
Barrett. I wrote the name of Williams on the note. Witness. I did not
have them in my hand—I only saw them in another person's hand; I should
not know them-I saw the name of Sole and Turner on one of them.
HENRY TIGH. (Policeman, M 226). On 16th April, I took Barrett at the
shop of Mr. Lyons, clothier, High-street, Borough-there was a female in
his company—this 101. note was passed to me in Barrett's presence by
Mr. Lyons, I asked Barrett where he got it from-he said he had been
paid bis wages in a 201. note, which he had changed for two 101. notes-he
said his employers were wine merchants-I asked him where the other 101
note was-he said that he had given one 101. note to a friend at the
Elephant and Castle-I asked him if he had the bag containing the notes--
he said he had not-I asked the female, in his presence-she said she had
not the bag-not being satisfied with them I took them to the station--the
male prisoner gave his address Barber's hall, Monkwell-street-Whiffin,
the female, gave her's 54, Wood-street, Cheapside-she was searched by
the female searcher, Mrs. Fenson, from whom I received this bag with the
contents-it contained a 1001. note, and two 101. notes--those are the notes
which Mr. Turner has just seen on the 17th, I called at 54, Wood-street,
the address given by the female, and there I saw Streaker leaving the bouse
-I then recollected having seen him the previous night standing outside
Mr. Lyons' shop during the time I was speaking to Barrett-I did not say
anything to him on this occasion-I afterwards apprehended him-I told
him that I wanted him for being concerned with a man named Barrett, and
a female named Whiffin, for stealing & quantity of notes-he said, “The
notes are not stolen ; I have done wrong, but I was led into it by Barrett"
-I took him to the station, searched him, and found on him this piece of
paper, which I now produce--the sums stated on this piece of paper amount
to 1531. 108. 3d.
