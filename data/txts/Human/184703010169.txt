   Prisoner. Q.  Did not you cross the road and take it off the fence your self? A.  No—I did not follow you with it, and put it on the wall—you were walking when I came to you about ten yards from the wall—it is pos sible I may have played at skittles with you, but I do not recollect being in your company—I never said I would catch you the first opportunity I had because you said you would report me for drinking when on duty—you never said so, and I never said I would have you. HANNAH WILKINSON. My husband keeps the Old Friends public-house, Garrett-lane, Wandsworth. This pot belongs to him—his name is on it.
   Prisoner. Q.  What time did you lose it? A.  I cannot say, for I send beer out at all hours—I did miss it, but not till the policemen spoke to me—I have lost eight quart and thirteen pint pots, between Christmas-day and the 15th of Feb.—I had mentioned my losses to the policeman.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I saw the pot hanging on the rails; I passed it about twenty yards, turned round, saw the policeman crossing the road with the pot in his hand; I walked on about 100 yards; he turned and put the pot down on the low wall; I went on, and he took me; I never touched the pot. WILLIAM LEA re-examined.  The prisoner was 100 yards from the house—there are two or three public-houses and beer-shops in the lane—I did not put the pot on the wall. GUILTY.  836. ELIZABETH GRADY was indicted for stealing one purse, value 6 d  .; 1 half-sovereign, 6 half-crowns, and 1 shilling; the property of Philip Samuel ; and 1 bonnet, 2 s  ., the goods of Ann Whitley . PHILIP SAMUEL. I am shopman to Mr. Cohen; the prisoner was servant in the house. On the 14th of Feb. I went to bed about twelve o'clock—I left my trowsers on a chair in the bed-room, with a purse in the pocket, and one half-sovereign, six half-crowns, and one shilling in it—about a quarter after seven next morning I got up, found the pocket turned inside out, and the purse and money gone—the street-door was open and the prisoner gone—she was in the house the night before—I went to the South Western Railway station at Nine Elms—I got there about a quarter after o'clock, and found the prisoner there, going into the train for Gosport—I had her appre hended she had a bonnet on her head which belonged to the nursery-maid—this purse is mine. GEORGE WILLIAMS( police-constable V  94) The prisoner was given into my charge by Samuel—I told her she was charged with stealing a purse and 2 s . 6 d .—she had a bundle in her right hand—she shifted it, and put her right arm under her cloak—I took this purse out of her hand—there was no money in it—she had the bonnet on. ANN WHITLEY. I live in the house of Solomon Cohen. The prisoner was my fellow-servant—this is the bonnet which I lost.
   Prisoner.  It is my bonnet, and the one you have on belongs to me. Witness.  They are both mine. GUILTY. Aged 23.— Confined Six Months .
