13th April, about 10 o'clock, the prisoner came there-my husband and I
were sitting in the parlour adjoining the shop, having our supper-I went
out attend to the prisoner-h wished to back a horse, named Nancy,"-
think, for the Chester cup, for 10.-I called my husband, who came to the
counter, and went to the desk to write a ticket-the prisoner handed me a
51. note-I took it next door to ask for change, which they had not got, and
while I was there my husband came in, took the note from me, and sent me
some to mind the shop-I found the prisoner waiting there-he said if he
had known the difficulty we had to get change he could have got it himself
by getting a glass of ale, which I advised him to do, as Mr. Massey appeared
8O long gone, and he said something about being so delayed that he had better
have brought his nightcap-my husband returned in about twenty minutes
with an officer.
Cross-examined by MR. GIPPARD. Q. You took the note into your hands
first, I believe A. I did; I did not know that we had not got change but
I did not like to change, it being so late-I went out with it in my hand
momentarily-my husband saw it, before I went out, and handled it-we did
not say anything to each other about it, the prisoner could have heard us-
I said, will go next door and get it changed," and went out-I did not
object to the look of the note, I merely thought it was late, and I did not know
the party passing it--Mr. Massey did not tell me not to change it-I was
not gone a second before he came after me, and sent me back- there were
two or three other customers in the shop, besides the prisoner, but no one
connected with the shop.
