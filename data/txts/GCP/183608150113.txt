1899. JOHN CRIPPEN was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of
July, 2 printed books, value 88., the goods of Edward Yerlett.
BDWARD YERLETT, Jun. I am the son of Edward Yerlett, who keeps
a chandler's shop in Britannia-street, St. Pancras. On the 19th of July, I
met the prisoner in the New-road reading a book, and I saw, when he
shut it up and put it under his arm, that it was my father's book--I spoke
to him, and when I got home I asked my father where his receipt-book was
-- he could not find it, and I mentioned what I had seen--the prisoner
was in the habit of frequenting our house-it was a family receipt-book-
on the evening of the same day the prisoner came to the shop while I was
in the parlour, and waited about twenty minutes, and just as he went out
we missed a Sacred Biography " from a shelf which he could reach-I
followed him and found it under his coat. A

ELIZA WINTER. I live in Britannia-row, St. Pancras. I was at Mr.
Yerlett's shop the evening the prisoner came I saw the book under his
coat, and he left the shop with it.
MICHAEL LANE. I am a bookseller, and have a stand at No. 20, in
the New-road. I bought a receipt-book of the prisoner on the 19th of
July, for 2s., knowing he was a bookseller, and in the habit of selling
books--it was not very cheap, as it was imperfect.
Thomas Hewett (police-constable E 159.) I produce a “Sacred
Biography," which was delivered to me.
EDWARD YERLETT. I know these books to be mine-I never authorized
the prisoner to take them.
Prisoner. Q. Have not I many times moved books from your shelves,
and sold them, and returned you the money three or four days afterwards?
4. Not within this twelvemonth-I have had no quarrel with him about
a license—he never travelled with my license--a person, about two years
ago, who was in the book-way, that I travelled for, he and I together tra-
relled with that person's license, but not with mine-I never had a license
in my own name, to my knowledge, since I have known the prisoner-it is
nine or ten years ago I travelled for a man named Curtis, who had the
license in his own name-I let the prisoner have that he was out with me
some time-when one person employs another, and they take out a license
in their own name, by indorsing it, it entitles them to travel in that name,
and it was indorsed by Curtis.
Prisoner. Several days we travelled together, and many days I travelled
by myself, till a policeman asked me to look at my license-he said, "Is
your naine Crippen?"--I said, “ Yes"—he said, "Show me your license"
it had Curtis' name on it--when I said Curtis was not my employer,
the policeman said, "Don't continue to travel under the license; you are
able to a fine;" and then I gave it up-I was held in degradation by both
