 2242. WILLIAM DOUGLAS was indicted for stealing, on the   16th of August , 2 trusses of hay, value 5 s  ., the goods of Thomas Chapman , his master.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. THOMAS CHAPMAN. I am a cow-keeper, and live in Cheyne-street, Bedford-square—I have some premises in George-street, Euston-square, I keep hay and fodder there. In consequence of what I heard, I counted the trusses of bay, on the night of the 15th of August, and on going on the morning of the 16th I missed two trusses of meadow-hay—the prisoner had been in my service, and had gone out with his cart that morning—I pursued and overtook him near the seventh mile-stone—the policeman stopped him—I went to the cart, and found the two trusses of hay, which are mine—I send food for my horses in the nose-bags, but no hay. GEORGE MOORE. I am a cow-man, in the prosecutor's employ. On Monday morning, the 16th of August, I saw the prisoner go to the hay loft, take two trusses of hay, and put them into his cart—he went on to Edgeware—I told my master and the policeman of it.
   Prisoner.  I put them out of the loft, and he put them up to me. Wit  ness. No, I did not. THOMAS BOYCE( police-constable S  204,) I went after the cart, at half-past five o'clock that morning, and overtook the prisoner at Edge-ware, and after some distance, I asked him where he was going—he said, "After some green clover"—I said, "Perhaps you will give me a lift"—he said, "Yes, jump up"—I saw in the cart two trusses of hay—I went on with him to the Welsh Harp public-house, where I expected he would have parted with them, but he did not—I then got out, and followed him on—the prosecutor came up, and gave him into custody—I asked how he came by the hay—he said he did not know, but he had been riding on them for two miles. GUILTY. Aged 37.— Confined Six Months.
