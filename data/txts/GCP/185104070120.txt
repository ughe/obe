Cross-examined by Mr. O'BRIEN. Q. Can you swear that these are
yours? A. I swear to these skins-I am not positive to the presses or
the books I believe the prisoner was a sort of master manufacturer-I
believe he did make skins-I bought a few skins of him before he went
into my service, to the extent of 5l., as he was in distress-he has been
in my employ since last June prosecuted a man for stealing some skins
in the Old Court yesterday--the prisoner took a few skins home at one
time for his wife to work, but he gave it up, saying, she was too busy to
attend to it.
MR. GIFFORD. Q. Would a person acquainted with skins be able to
know at what factory they had been manufactured ? A. Most likely: I
know my own from others--the prisoner did not manufacture skins; he
was a journeyman gold-beater-the skins are worth about 21.-- the skins
be took home for bis wife to work were perhaps 150--that was about two
months after he came to my service, in June-since then he has had
nothing.
HENRY HEFFERMAN. I live in Gunpowder-alley, and am a master gold-
beater-these gold-books are my make-I sold them to Mr. Law, of
Bethnal-green-I swear to them.
Cross-examined. Q. Used you to make for anybody else ? A. Yes;
for a number of persons in the trade, but not of this sort of paper-we
bave portions of paper, and use them all up sometimes, and these we sup-
plied to Mr. Law-perhaps we made up a hundred of these bundles-they
are all tied in this way-about three years ago I sold some books to the
prisoner-I make all the books myself—what I make depends on my
orders-they are all made of waste-paper--the papers are all different-
if you open these books, you will find there are two rules on the top of
the page, and they are on a religious subject-Mr. Law bad the whole of
that kind of paper; every particle of it-these books are worth 28.
Mr. O'BRIEN to MR. LAW. Q. If a person worked at home, would he
require books of this kind ? A. Yes; but the prisoner was working at
my place.
John HILL. I am a journeyman gold-beater, and work for Mr. Law-
I know this press-it belongs to him-I am certain of that-it was on his
premises.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you ever work with it? A. Yes, within the
last six months--I do not know that there is any mark on it, but there is
something peculiar about it, and it has had a new screw within the last six
months-all the men in the employ used it-I believe the prisoner used
it-a man named Falla worked there-he was convicted yesterday (see
page 884).
DAVID LIVERMORE. I am a gold-beater, and work for Mr. Law-I
know both these iron presses well—they are his-I have seen them on
his premises, and worked with them.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you see them last? A. This one.
six or seven months back, and the other, three or four months back !
