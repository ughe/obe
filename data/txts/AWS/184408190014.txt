MAGNAY, Mayor.
OLD -Wednesday, August 21st, 1844.
Third Jury, before Eduard Bullock, Esg.
1918. WILLIAM THOMAS was indicted for stealing 2 printed books,
value 28. the goods of John Brown ; to which he pleaded
GUILTY. Aged 8.-Confined One Month.
1919. THOMAS ROBERTS was indicted for stealing 1 cash-box, value
128. ; 4 sovereigns, 7 half-crowns, 2 shillings, and 12 pence; the property
of James Parkins, in his dwelling-house and afterwards, about ten o'clock
in the night, burglariously breaking out of the said dwelling-house
MARGARET PARKINS. I live at No. 99, Cheapside, in the bouse of my bro-
ther, James Parkins. On the 21st of July, I saw the shop shut up at nine 'clock
at night-the men shut up the shutters-I saw them leave, and bolted the
door after them-the house and shop are connected-] went up stairs, and
shortly after my attention was attracted by a noise like a bar failing-I went
down stairs, and saw that a bar had fallen across the shop, leading from the
cellar door at the back of the shop-I went up stairs again-about half an
hour after, I heard a noise like the drawing of the latch-] went down again,
and found the street door opening into the shop unbolted-the servant was in
the house-I bolted the door again, and went up stairs-abou ten o'clock
the prisoner came to the door-he had lived with us, but was not doing so
then-he asked if our foreman had left some money for him-I said, he had
not-he asked me if I could give him anything- said I could not-be
then went away-I bolted the door after him, and went up stairs.
JaNe FIELD. I am servant to Mr. Parkins. On the 21st of July, about
seven o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner in the shop-about half-past
nine, I was up stairs with Miss Parkins, and heard a noise in the shop-I
went down, and saw the bar that had fallen down in the shop-] went out
about half-past nine to fetch the beer- fastened the bottom bolt of the door
-on my return a little boy, who was a servant in the house, opened the door
for me when I came back- afterwards heard a noise at the street door,
went down, and found it unfastened.
JAMEs PARKINS. I live at No. 99, Cheapside-i is my house, and is in
the parish of St. Mary-le-Bow-] live in it occasionally-my servants live
in it-on the 21st of June I left the house about five o'clock in the afternoon,
leaving my sister and the servant there-I had three desks in the counting-
house-I had a cash-box in my desk, it was fastened-I returned about nine
next morning, and found the counting-house broken--3l of silver was taken
from my desk, and 21. from Hudson, my foreman's desk-there was no money
in the cash-box-it was locked-th prisoner had been in my employ some
time before-in consequence of receiving a letter from him, I called on him-
he said he was very sorry he had done wrong; he could not account for it,
and if I went down the cellar stairs I should find the cash-box- went and
found it-I think his intention in committing the robbery was to get out of
the country, as he did not spend the money or go away-having lost his situa-
tion, he was destitute, and did not know what to do.
JOHN STOREY (City police-constable, No. 414.) I took the prisoner into
custody.
EDMUND WASHINGTON. I am an officer at Giltspur-street prison-I
searched the prisoner, and found concealed in the bottom of his stocking
about 21. 8s. 6d., and 11s. he gave me.
