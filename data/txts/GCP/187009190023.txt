734. WILLIAM CURTIS (29), Barglariously breaking and entering the
dwelling-house of Thomas Bransom, with intent to steal.
MR. BRINDLEY conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM BRANSOM. I am a furniture dealer, of 69, Cable Street, St.
George's on 6th September I returned home about 9.45-as I went into
the house I heard glass falling- I stood and listened, but heard nothing
more-I unlocked my bed-room door, against which I keep a large bedstead;
that had been moved away, and I saw the shadow of a man outside my
window, with his arm through the glass-I said “Who is there t he
jumped down, and I went to the yard-door and caught him-he said "What
is this ; here is a shilling; here are two shillings. I only fell against the
window ; let me go "-it was the prisoner-I said, “I want to see what
damage you have done "-lodger came down, and I went and looked at
the back window, keeping hold of the prisoner--I found that two squares of
glass were broken, the catch was put back, and two gimlets, which were
put to keep the catch down, had been removed-be said " You see I have
only broken a window "-I shut the door, bolted it, and said " I shall give
you in charge"-I called a neighbour, and my wife fetched a constable-
two gimlets, a sorew, and a bar of iron 2 ft. long. were found outside
the window-he must have got the gimlets from the inside, he could
reach them through the broken glass—I kuew the prisoner before, ooming
to a lodger in the house.
Prisoner. Q. Is there any opening into the back yard! A. Yos-you
did not say you came to see a lodger, Mr. Rose--you frequently did come
to see him, but he has left my place five weeks; that is the reason you
knew the ins and outs of the place so well-you may have lodged in the
house with my mother formerly.
THOMAS BURKs (Policeman H 226). I was called, and the prisoner was
given into my charge-I found a square of glass over the catch broken, and
the catch put up, and a square broken at the bottom-I found this bar, and
marks corresponding with it-I also found this other bar, which is part
broken off the first-there were marks both on the top and bottom sash,
which might be caused by this bar—the prisoner said that he went to
water there.
Prisoner. l. Was I drunk or sober 4. You appeared sober.
T. BRANSOM (re-examined). These iron bars do not belong to me; but
the gimlets do.
Prisoner's Defence. I went there to see a friend ; I have been in the
habit of going to the house. I am innocent. I had just come out of the
back room.
GUILTY.
