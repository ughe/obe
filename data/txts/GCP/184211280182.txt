HUMPRERY, Mayor.
278. WILLIAM GARRATT was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of
Nov., 1 sachrometer and case, value 31.; the goods of John Perry.
Join PERRY. I am a brewer, and live in St. George's-road, Southwark.
This is my sachrometer-I saw it safe on Monday the 21st of Nov., and it
was missed on the Monday following--the prisoner had been in my service,
but was discharged on the 11th of June last.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Have you a great many other
servants ? A. Yes-he was discharged for drunkenness- I had no charge
against him for dishonesty.
BENJAMIN HAZLEBY. I am a pawnbroker. I took in this sachrometer of
the prisoner, on the 25th of Nov., about six o'clock in the evening.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you know him before? A. Not to my know-
ledge--my masters's shop is in the Lower-road, Deptford-that is rather a
populous neighbourhood -- it was candle light-there were other customers in
the shop, but I do not know that I should know any of them again- I was
the only person serving.
Court. Q. Are you sure he is the man that pawned this ? A. Quite sure,
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 40.--Confined Four Months.
279. THOMAS HAYES was indicted for a misdemeanor.
SUSAN WINDSOR. I am the wife of William Windsor, of Aldridge-street,
Bermondsey New-road. Between five and six o'clock in the evening, of the
1st of December, I went down into the front room found the window open,
and the prisoner concealed behind a chair under the window my sister
seized him, and he was given in charge.
Prisoner's Defence. I had been to the hospital to see my sister, and was
going home; two men gave me a push, and something I had went over to
their house; I knocked at the door, no one came, and I went to the window
I did not know whether the house was empty or not; I went in, then the
lodger came home, and they came down and laughed at me.
Witness. He was crouching down concealing himself behind a chair.
GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Nine Months.
280. JOHN CAMPBELL was indicted for stealing, on the 11th of Nov.,
1 coat, value 158.; 1 handkerchief, 6d.; and 1 pair of gloves, 1s; the goods
of William Wright.
WILLIAM WRIGHT. I am in the employ of Messrs. Cormack, of New-cross,
Deptford. On the 11th of Nov., between eleven and twelve o'clock, my coat,
handkerchief, and gloves hung in my master's stable-I saw the prisoner in
the yard between ode and two, with my coat on his arm-I asked what he
did there--he said he wanted the kitchen-door-I asked what he wanted there
- he said, "To get a bit of bread'i"-I asked what he did with that coat-ho
said it was not mine--this is the coat-I am sure it is mine-there was no
one but him to take it--the door of the harness-room was open-he was walk-
ing about the yard - I followed him some distance, he then gave me the cost
and said, “Let me go this once, I will never do it any more," and that he did
it for want.
Prisoner. Q. Did you see me in the stable? 4. No, you were close to
my master's house, and close to the harness-room.
JURY. Q. Did you keep your coat hanging up in the harness-room? 4.
Yes, on a wooden peg-it was possible for other persons to go into the yard.
GUILTY. Aged 27.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined Two Months.
ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, DECEMBER 12TX, 1842.
