   Cross-examined. Q.  Was it not the same stable in which his master's horses were? A.  Yes—I was before the Magistrate—while I was there Little and two other persons were there—I do not know whether Little was charged—he was taken up on what I told Mr. Bennett—I did not tell the police anything about Little—I did not see him with the sack—I saw him at the stable—it was not through what I said that he was taken—I did not at first say that it was Little that was carrying the sack—I am quite certain of that—I told Mr. Bennett Little was in the stable, but I did not say that he carried the sack. MARY ANN BACON. I am the wife of John Bacon, of Chadwell—the prisoner Tinsworth lodged in our house. On Saturday night, the 12th, he was not at home at all—he came home on Sunday morning a few minutes before ten o'clock—he had on a pair of corded smallclothes and asmock-frock—the same things that the policeman has produced. WILLIAM BONNETT. I am a carter at Chadwell-heath. On Saturday night, the 11th of Feb., I placed a cart full of turnips in the shed of the White Horse—Bennett afterwards told me of a sack that was in it—I did not go to the cart then—I went and gave information to the police—the sack did not belong to me—I know nothing about it. JAMES CRAWFORD( police-sergeant K  253.) I am stationed at Chadwell, near Barking. On Monday morning, the 12th of Feb., I received information—I went to a place called Cat's-lane, and found this sheep-skin with the head attached—I examined it—I showed it to the drover—he said it was the skin of the sheep he had lost—I carried it to Mr. Webb'a stable—I then went to a field belonging to Mr. Webb—I had seen some sheep driven into that field the night before by Ringe the drover—I discovered footmarks of three per sons leading over three or four fields to where I found a sheep had been slaughtered, in a field adjoining Cat's-lane—I compared the marks in the field with some boots which I got from the prisoner Tintworth's feet, and they corresponded—I also saw a mark of a knee, where a person had been kneel ing, between where the sheep was taken from, and where it was slaughtered—I was with Tebenham when he compared these marks with a pair of breeches found at Tinsworth's lodgings, and they corresponded—I have kept the boots, and produce them to-day—the left boot was the one I examined most particularly—here are peculiar nails which made the impression in the earth, three nails in the heel, and the tip projects out a little—it correspond ed in every respect with the mark in the field—I afterwards traced the same footmarks from the place where the sheep had been slaughtered towards the back of the White Horse-yard—I afterwards received information from Bon nett, and found the sack and meat in his cart—I apprehended Little, and he was discharged—I found a knife on Tinsworth, with blood on the handle where the blade shuts—it had been apparently fresh cleaned when I found it—the blood was inside where the blade closes.
