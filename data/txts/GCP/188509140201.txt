CHARLES DREW (Dotective E). On 24th August I took the prisoner on
a warrant for defrauding Mr. Benguard, and said that it was by means of
a forged order for the Lyceum Theatre-he said "I never wrote the orders,
another man wrote them and gave them to me; I sold them, and we both
shared the money "-I have a great number of other tickets.
Cross-examined. You gave me the name and address of the man you
said you had them from--you said that you would not put us to any
trouble, you would plead guilty to all of it; that you were hard up, and
wanted some money, and did not know what to do- I asked your wife if
she had any tickets or letters.
LIONEL CARLISLE. I live at 6, Park Road-on 13th or 14th July I
heard that the prisoner had an order for the Lyceum Theatre, and I went
out and asked him how much it was-he said that he usually got 6s. for
adress circle ticket--I said that it was a great deal more than I could
pay, and he came down to 38.-I afterwards gave it to Mr. Stoker, who
said it was a forgery-this is it.
FRANK BUTTERFIELD. I am a clerk, of 82, Old Broad Street-the
prisoner had been there before, and on July 16th he came and produced
& ticket admitting two to the dress circle of the Lyceum Theatre on
Monday, July 20th, which he offered me for 68.-I expressed my surprise
--he assured me that they were genuine, and said that he had a friend at
Court, and on the strength of that I gave him 5s. for the ticket-I pre-
scribed it on the 20th at the theatre, was told that it was worthless, and I
had to pay-next morning I got this letter from the prisoner. (Dated
July 2017, telling the witness not to go, as the seats were all booked for that
night, and to keep the ticket till ho called for it tomorrow. Signed, "0.") I
then went to the Lyceum and saw Mr. Stoker-the prisoner did not call
next day-this is the ticket he sold me.
Cross-examinod. I said a few days before that if you would get me a
couple of tickets for the Lyceum I would buy them.
BRANS TOKER. I am acting manager at the Lyceum Theatre-I think
I have seen the prisoner before, but cannot call to mind where these two
tickets are not genuine--the prisoner had no authority to dispose of tickets
under Mr. Irving's management—there are no complimentary orders
except to Mr. Irving's friends.
Cross-examined. You are not a member of the profession, and would
not be entitled to complimentary tickets.
The prisoner in his defence stated that the tickets toere not written by him,
but that he bought them of Frank Maynard, of Newington Butts, of Cook's
Cirous Company and St. James's Theatre, and sold them, believing them to be
genuine, and that Maynard's landlady and the manager of the Carpenters' Arms
could both prove this; that it was customary to buy and soll complimentary
tickets, and it was as easy for him to buy them as any one else, and that he had
pupil, a friend of Mr. Irving's, who he would loso had ho committed such an
offence.
Not Guilty.
