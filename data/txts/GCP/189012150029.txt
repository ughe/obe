were about £400 or £500, I think, speaking from memory-the mill alone
cost £100-Bevitt and Penning provided it--the liquidators were
appointed at the general meeting- I was present-I did not see Marks
there, nor Cohen-Mr. Snell proposed Klenck or Fry-I did not know

think there were any trade debts left, except perhaps a small printing
account--there were a journal; letter-book, share register, and transfer-
books-you have the share register or ledger.

and Provincial Bank, South Kensington branch-I established that
branch in 1875, but I had been a bank manager twenty years
before that—the directors desired, in consequence of the falling off
of business, to reduce my salary in November, 1886, and I declined to
remain—I did not hear of Higham Benjamin as one of the syndicate-
F. Smith was one of the syndicate, or their nominee-we dealt with the
syndicate as acting for the vendor-W. H. Penning, whose name appears
on the prospectus as a director, and is described as a Fellow of the

who gave a report on the mine-I was associated with him afterwards as
