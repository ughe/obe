MR. ATTORNEY GENERAL . Q.  Are these the two pieces? A.  Yes. JOHN BUSAIN. I am an inspector of the V division of police. I went to Mr. Shiell's stable on the night of the 6th of April—I arrived about a quarter past eleven o'clock—I went into the stable, and saw the trunk on a female, and proceeded to search the harness-room, the door of which was locked—I got the key from the elder Houghton—I unlocked the door—I beg to correct myself, I was called by the superintendent to go into the harness-room, which was then open—it was at a subsequent search I got the key—the superintendent ordered me to break open a flat wooden box, which was there, locked—I found in that a quantity of clean linen belonging to a male, and some books—I searched the drawer of a table there which was shot, but there was no lock on it—I found in it this knife closed as it is now—I examined it, and observed what appeared to me to be stains of blood—I found it had been newly sharpened, and on this part of the handle was what appeared to be a stain of blood, and the knife appeared to be rusty after it had been sharpened—the stain of blood was partly on the handle, and partly on the blade—I showed the knife to Dr. Ridge the next day—I found some keys in the drawer, which sergeant Palmer has possession of—I laid them on the table—on the right-hand side of the fire-place, in a corner, standing by the chimney, I found this axe—I did not examine it that night—I kept possession of the key of the harness-room myself—I looked at it, and could not distinguish whether there were stains on it that night—I locked the room when f left, and took the key with me—I observed next morning what appeared to be stains of blood, one mark on the pole of the axe, and one on each side the edge along here—I do not think they were more visible than they are now, because I have kept it so carefully—there were lines across the room to hang clothes on, and on a line next the fire-place, I found this piece of silk handkerchief, which appears to be marked with blood in several places—I found divers clothes, but not on the line—the articles of female wearing apparel I found in a saddle-rest in the harness-room—here is what appears to me to be part of a flannel petticoat, forcibly torn in two, and one part cut in the seam—these two pieces were together as they are now, and correspond exactly where it appears cut at the seam—they also correspond in quality; they were both together, thrown over the saddle-rest—I found what appears to be part of a calico petticoat, close to the saddle-rest, underneath a box-coat, which was thrown over the chair, and one of these was knotted as if tied together, but it became untied after wards—both are marked with blood, and on one of these there are marks of blood close to where there have been pieces cut out—they are not drops but splashes of blood—not as if dropping from a person's nose—I found also a pint bottle, under the window; there were a few drops of liquid in it—I poured it into the palm of my hand, tasted it, and believe it to be milk—I found the bottle on the 8th, but I had always kept the harness-room locked up, and had the key—on the night of the 6th I assisted in searching the fire-place, and found a quantity of calcined bones in the ashes—the fire was laid with straw, wood, and coal—I removed them, and searched underneath—there was a pair of brand-irons at the fire-place, so as to bring the fire out further from the chimney—I believe it was a Cob bett's register-stove—I found the bones under the fuel, among the cinders and ashes underneath—I showed them to Mr. Allen the same night—
