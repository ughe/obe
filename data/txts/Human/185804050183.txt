EDWARD BURKE( Policeman, M  214). I was on duty in Rotherhithe, on 8th March, about 2 o'clock in the morning I saw the two prisoners; they were followed by a sergeant of police—I stopped them, and asked Brown what he had about him; he said he had nothing—I felt his coat pocket, and found these cigars—I asked him where he got them; he said, "From the Red Cow, at Deptford"—he then said, "It is no use giving the worms  any trouble, go down to the Black Horse"—he was taken to the station—there were twelve dozen cigars found on him, and 2 l . 11 s . 8 d . CHARLES HODGSON( Policeman, S  28). I followed the two prisoners that morning, and called to the last witness to stop them—I searched Hopkins, and found on him a cribbage board, a pack of cards, and a timepiece—these are them—I asked him what he had got; he said, "Nothing"—I opened his coat and found this timepiece—he was further searched, and this cribbage board and pack of cards were found—I went to the prosecutor's house, and found the cellar flap had been opened by a crow bar. JOHN WOODWARD BARRETT. I keep the Black Horse, at Rotherhithe—I was awoke that morning, by the police, about 3 o'clock—I found my house had been entered by the cellar flap—it had been all safe at 12 o'clock on the previous evening—this timepiece is mine, it was on the kitchen mantel piece—this cribbage board and cigars were all right in the bar parlour—I missed 3 l . 8 s . in money altogether.
   Brown's Defence.  We were coming from Deptford, and saw this time piece, and took it up; as to saying, "The Black Horse," we did not; we did not know where it came from; as to calling the policemen worms , it is quite false.
   BROWN  — GUILTY.*— Four Years Penal Servitude.
   HOPKINS  — GUILTY.— Confined Twelve Months .
 481. HENRY WARBURTON  (21), was indicted for burglary in the dwelling house of Henry Griggs , with intent to steal. HENRY GRIGGS. I keep the British Queen, in East Street, Old Kent Road. On 18th March, I went up to my bed room, on the second floor, about a quarter or twenty minutes before 10 o'clock at night—I found the door shut; Iopened it and found the prisoner at my drawers—I said, "What are you doing here?" he said he thought it was the club room—I have a club room, but that is below—I gave the prisoner in custody—he resisted very much, and struck me in the throat—the things in the drawer were disturbed—theroom door was kept locked, and my wife had the key in her pocket—the prisoner was very violent—he threw me back on the bed, but I held him tight—Ileft him in the officer's hands.
   Prisoner. Q.  Where was I standing? A.  At the drawers—there was money in the top and in the bottom drawer—there was money in the drawer which you had got open—the drawers were not locked. ANN GRIGGS. I kept some money in the drawers in the bed room—I had the key, and I had locked the room door about two hours before the robbery—Ihad gone up to put two sovereigns in the drawer—I kept the money there—the drawers were not locked. WILLIAM PLANK. I am a clerk. I was in the prosecutor's house on the night this happened—I heard the noise—I went up stairs, and found Mr. Griggs and the prisoner—I stayed there to assist him.
