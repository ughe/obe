   KENT CASES .
 1073. RICHARD MASHITER was indicted for stealing 1 umbrella, value 4 s  ., the goods of William Nicholls . JOHN TAYLER. I am a corporal in the Royal Marines. On the 13th of Feb. I saw an umbrella in the hands of the prisoner—I put him in the guard-room.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What time of day was this? A.  About nine o'clock in the morning—he belongs to the same room as I do—I saw him bring the umbrella open into the room with him—it was a fine morning—I have not known him above a fortnight—I have not been long in the regiment myself—it was opened in the room to be looked at. ELLEN NICHOLLS. I am the wife of William Nicholls—this umbrella re sembles mine—I had sewn a button on mine, but any person might do that—I cannot swear to my work—I cannot point out any difference between the umbrella I lost and this one—it appears to have had a button sewed on since it was made—it looks like my work as far as I can see—it looks like mine, certainly—there is no mark on it whatever—it is sewn on with silk.
   Cross-examined. Q.  There is a great resemblance between this and yours, you are not able to swear to it? A.  No, mine was very like this—it is the sewing on of this pearl button to which I refer, but it is a thing anybody might do.
   COURT . Q.  Have you always given the same account? A.  Yes—I was before the Magistrate—I had rather not swear to it—I thought it was a thing any person might do—I expressed a doubt at the Police-court. WILLIAM NICHOLLS. I think this umbrella is mine, I have no doubt of it, but I do not know it from anything but the use of it—I have had it about two years.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you reason to pay any particular attention to it? A.  No—umbrellas are made by dozens in the same form—I speak to it from its general appearance—I lost it out of a return-cab, which I left at the railroad at New-cross. THOMAS HAWKINSON.
