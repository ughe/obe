   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How can you say it was Hare? A.  Because I am sure it was him—that was the first time I had seen him—I think it was twice he was knocked down by blows from truncheons, first by Kayser and then by me—I think he was not knocked down by Chantler—I had seen Payne before, and knew him well—when he came up he said, "Don't go with them, go home quietly"—he did not say to. me, "If you will let him go, he will go quietly home with me"—I did not hear the police say, "We must take him to the station"—Kayser told Hare that if he would go quietly to the station he thought the inspector would not take the charge—I think I said to Hare, "If you bite me again I will knock your brains out"—he had got my hand in his mouth then—I put one of my knees on his chest to keep him down after that—he was drunk—I cannot distinguish any thing particular that William Goodright did, only the rescue.
   Cross-examined by MR. ROBINSON Q.  Was this rescue at the latter part of the transaction? A.  Yes—I had received several blows then—one of the Good rights touched Hare, but I cannot tell which, they were all there—I could not say how many were assisting in the rescuing—there might be nearly twenty. NICHOLAS CHANTLER( police-constable P  224). I went with Wigzell and Kayser to Nelson-street—I saw the officers and Hare there—I saw Flinn, and while I was assisting Wigzell to take Hare, Flino took hold of my belt and struck me with his oilier hand—no one else struck me to my knowledge—I have known him about a year and a half, and am sure it was him—I think he had hold of my belt for a minute and a half—I had got hold of Hare at the time, who was kicking and fighting, and very riotous—Payne was there, he took hold of my truncheon, and said "You b—r, you mean to kill the man"—that was because I struck Hare on the leg with my truncheon, because he kicked Wigzell—a person named May came to assist me, and at that very time I was separated from Wigsell—I should think Wigzell was alone four or five minutes.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . When you went to Nelson-street, you found Wigzell aid Kayser had got Hare in custody? A.  They said they were going to take him into custody, and he resisted—they had got him, one on each side, and I went behind and pushed him—the people were peaceable then, but when we got some distance the people said, "Don't go"—I swear that Hare kicked Wigzell above his knee, and then I struck Hare on the legs.
   COURT . Q.  Was it necessary for your brother officer's defence for you to strike him? A.  It was necessary—I did forbear as long as I could.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  What time was this? A.  I should think it wanted twenty minutes to nine o'clock—it was getting a little dark—I should think there were 250 people—I had seen Flinn about Camberwell before, the same as other people—there were two or three females about—what Flinn did was to take hold of my belt and pull me away—I am sure it was him—he had taken no part in the transaction be fore, only pushing—all the people were pushing one another.
