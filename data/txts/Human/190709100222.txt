JOHN STEWART, 200, Silver Street, Edmonton, carman to Mr. Henderson, spoke to securing the pony and cart at half past eight at night, on March 21, with two padlocks, and to going with Mr. Henderson to identify the things on August 7. JOHN DUNN HALLIDAY, draper, 214, Burdett Road, Bow. On March 31, I had a Stratford cart and some harness which was loss. A pony was lost at the same time from the stable. I next saw the trap at the Dorking Police Station on August 11. It was not in the same condition as when it was stolem. When I had it it was of a dark green colour, picked out with black, and the cushions were dark green. When I saw it at Dorking it was a dark blue with cushions to match. The name of the maker had been erased from the axle boxes and the splashboards altered. I knew it to be my trap by the tyres and one or two marks I could swear to.
 Cross-examined. I valued the trap at £20. I did not see say of Mr. Luck's other carts, and could not say whether my cart was painted the same. ALBERT WYATT SHERLOCK, Cypress Villas, Dorking, carriage builder. I have had business dealings with Thomas Luck. On May 10 his son brought me a Stratford cart, with instructions that it was to be thoroughly done up. The cart was taken to pieces, thoroughly cleaned, and painted blue and yellow, the shafts being yellow picked out in black, and the straight wings replaced with bent. It looked a totally different cart when I finished with it. The caps had some grit marks on them and these were removed with a file, and the name of the maker was also filed off. I do not remem ber what the name was. The cushions were re-covered with blue cloth. My charge was £5 10s., which Mr. Luck paid yesterday morn ing. He runs an account with me. The work was done in my shop in West Street, and a passer by could easily see the trap. After it was finished it was put in the show-room facing the street.
 Cross-examined. Luck is a respectable and well-known tradesman in the town. I have done work for him for a number of years. I had three carts of his to overhaul in May and June. It is the usual practice to clean the caps of the axles with a file. the name is never very deeply indented, and any cleaning of the caps would obliterate it. Luck's trade colours are blue, yellow and, black. With yellow we always have blue as it suite the yellow. There is no truth in the statement that the cart was sent to me in order that I might disguise it. I put the value of the cart at about £8.
 Superintendent GEORGE BOON, Surrey County Constabulary, stationed at Dorking.
