Q. Have you seen Mr. Abrahams's son about this matter? A. Only
this morning--that was not when my father was under examination I
did not see him afterwards, till I saw him here in court-I cannot say
whether he is taking notes--my brother Selim has been walking about,
sometimes alone, and sometimes in the passage I have not seen any of
the Abrahams with him-my sister has not been walking about she is
waiting outside- I have not seen Mr. Abrahams, or any one else, to tell me
what was going on in Court to-day.
Mr. Bodkin. Q. Is that the 3001. note you took to the Bank?
Yes--that is my writing on it-I wrote that on it when I got it changed
I have written on it, “H. Solomon, Foreign Money Exchange Office, 53,
Strand" -(this was No. 15.)
Q. You have been asked how many times you went out that day, and
how long you were gone each time ; did you look to any clock or watch,
or are you speaking to the best of your memory! A. To the best of my
memory-when Mrs. Abrahams came in, she went straight on to the back
shop ---a person coming in in the way she did, could see before them the room
in which the furnaces were I did not know her name then, but I noticed
her person I was told her name at Lambeth-street-persons pointed her
out-I knew her person when I saw her there I did not see any thing
she brought I saw nothing in her hands-Moses looked at the summons
when I showed it to him-he did not tell me he could not read or under-
stand it-Mr. Abrahams told me there was a mistake in my deposition
about the sovereigns for the cheque-he told me that he had ascertained at
the bankers' that it was part sovereigns and part notes.
SELIM SOLOMON. I am the eldest son of Henry Solomon, who has been
examined to-day-I manage my father's business in Coventry-street-I used
to sleep in the Strand- I remember Easter Wednesday, I believe it was the
3rd of April-my brother Isaac came for me that day, and I went to my
father's, in the Strand- I found my father there he gave me two bars of gold,
and gave me directions where to take them- I took them to Messrs. Bult,
the gold refiners in Cheapside-I saw Messrs. Bult when I got there the
bars were weighed—the weight was upwards of 400 ounces, to the best of
my recollection-it was to be 778. 9d. per oz. standard - I received 12001.
in the whole on those two bars--I had a cheque on Messrs. Robarts-I
went there with the cheque, and received eleven £100 notes and 100 so-
Vereigns returned to the Strand and found my father there-I gave
him that money to the best of my belief my father was in the shop when
I went back-I went into the parlour-while I was there the prisoner
Alice Abrahams came there I was sitting in the parlour when she came up
the stairs--the parlour is up stairs, and is a back room--Mrs. Abrahams
went into the drawing-room, which is the front room on the same story as
the parlour-my father followed her into the room, and I saw my father
give her six £100 notes and 100 sovereigns--they were the same notes I
had given my father--to the best of my recollection it was about ten
minutes after I gave them to him that he gave them to Mrs. Abrahams-
