ELIZABETH HILLYER. I am an assistant at the post-office in High
Street, Islington-I produce a declaration of trust made on the deposit
of 101. by the prisoner; he was accompanied by a female, I do not
remember her-this paper bears my signature—the depositor signs a book
at the time of the deposit - the female did not sign a book- I do not
remember whether any statement was made by her in the prisoner's
presence as to whom the money belonged-the documents were forwarded
to the General Post Office the same night in the usual course of business
-I made an entry, this is it-I saw the prisoner again when he withdrew
the money-I paid it to him-he did not produce the depositor's book; he
gaid he had lost it-I asked him for it-he signed this receipt in my pre-
sence-he went away to get Elizabeth Gale's signature, and when he
returned he produced this paper, with the signature, “Elizabeth Gale,"
on it.
Cross-examined. Q. I suppose if he had signed the name of Elizabeth
Gale in your presence you would not have acted upon the document,
whether he had authority or not? A. Oh ! no; I required her signature,
and he went to get it-I refused to pay the money without it.
