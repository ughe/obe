JANE JONES I am the wife of Jesse Jones, of 52, Woodchester
Street, Harrow Road—the prisoner lodged in our first floor front room
with his wife; they came on 19th November-I knew him as Hyett, &
carpenter- I did not go into the room while he was lodging there.
LOUISA MOMORROW. My husband is a tobacoonist, of 72, Farring.
don Road-on December 13th I served the prisoner with half an ounce

gave him change-I had no other shilling there about two minutes
afterwards a detective came in, and I gave it to him-he marked it
this is it.
Cross-examined. I picked out you and another man very much like
you-I can hardly discern you now, but here is my printed tobacco
paper. (This had the name of McMorrow on it.)
FREDERICK CLEVELAND (Detective G). I was with the other officers,
and saw the prisoner go into a shop-when he came out I went in, and
Mrs. McMorrow gave me this shilling-I marked it.
WILLIAM JOEN WEBSTER. I am Inspector of Coin to the Mint-none
of these coins came from these moulds—all these articles form: part of
the stock in trade of a coiner--these forty shillings are bad, and so is
this shilling found in the bed and the one uttered—the three shillings
found on Parker are counterfeit, and two of them are from the same
mould as some of the forty..
Prisoner's defence. They were given to me by a man to take care of, but
the one found in my possession I bought when I went to the man's
place. He said he did not want the things, he would call for them.
Parker knew nothing about them, nor did my wife. I have been put
away through a man.
GUILTY.
