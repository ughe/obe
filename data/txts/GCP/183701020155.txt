488. THOMAS WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of
December, 1 shilling, 2 sixpences, 2 pence, and 4 halfpence the monies of
Thomas Elliott.
Anx MARIA ELLIOTT. I am the wife of Thomas Elliott, and live in
Catherine-street, Vauxhall. On the 6th of December the prisoner came to
my house about a quarter past one o'clock, (at dinner time,) and said he
had been a very unfortunate young man, he had been transported, and now had
returned from Hobart Town-(I haveason there)- he told me he had brought
aletter for me, which was 2s.4d., and it was left at the dead letter office, Somer-
set-house, if I would go with him, he would get it--I said I could not
go then, I was going to get my children's dinner-I said I would go
on Wednesday-he said he could not go on Wednesday, as he was going
to Mr. Capper's office to receive his penny a day, as a convict-but would I
go that evening? I said, I did not know how to go, but I would go in the
evening-he came at ten minutes before five o'clock, and I gave him tea,
I left my children, took my baby, and went with him-as we were going
along, he said my son was quite well, and that he was drinking with him on
the 20th of June at a public-house at the top of Waterloo-street; we went
on to Somerset-house right under where the man saved his life by hanging by
the brickwork--there are a great quantity of steps--I had the baby in my
arm-I said it was rather awkward to go down with the baby-he said, “I
will hold the baby"-I said "No" he said, "Give me your money, I will go
down and get the letter"-I gave him one shilling, two sixpences, two penny-
pieces, and four halfpence-I waited round the yard till a quarter before
eight o'clock, and then the sentry asked me what I was waiting for-I said I
was waiting for a young man who was gone to get a letter-he said there was
no such place there-I never saw the prisoner again till I was fetched by
the officer, to Union Hall, and the turnkey asked if I could swear to him
