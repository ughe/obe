Prisoner's Defence. In 1854 I was married to Ellen Henderson, and was
foroed to leave her through her drunkenness. I was despatched to the
Cape in 1855, and the night I passed through London, she was in prison
for drunkenness, and I could not see her. I did not see her till 1863, when
she was living with Mr. Stafford. I have seen him in her room at Horsely-
down. I went away to Manchester, and came back in 1864, and then she
was living in New Compton Street, still kept by this man. I took her
away from him, took her to Manchester, and lived with her there, but she
was locked up there two or three times for drunkenness. I took her to
London, and in one of her drunken fits she told me that since she was
married to me she had got married to John White. I then left her, went
to Somerset House, found that she was married to John White, and have
never lived with her since. I have no children by her, but I have one by
my second wife.
GUILTY.
