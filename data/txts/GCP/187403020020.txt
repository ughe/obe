Cross-examined by the Prisoner. When you handed me over money I
gave you a little ticket for the amount, and you initialled it, and when I
balanced with you that was considered a voucher for the cash-you had
your money-box to keep those tickets in, and the key was in your own
possession---I do not recollect omitting to give you a ticket on any occasion,
or making a mistake about 51. which I gave you to pay petty accounts, and
deducting it from the “overs," making you 51. short-I see here is an entry
of 111. 198. 11d., on 22nd November, altered to 61. 198. 11d. ; I cannot say
at this distance of time whether that refers to the 51. you speak of we had
such trust and confidence in you that, before we discovered this, we were
discussing the propriuty of taking a shop at the West End, and putting you
in sole charge.
Re-examined. The term “overs" relates to the small accounts-every-
thing above a shilling would have to be entered each day-the total amount
of "overs" allowed at the expiration of the six or ten days would be the
amounts he had received and not entered they should not amount to as
much as 7l: or 81., but he was in the habit of neglecting to enter sums
considerably larger than a shilling.
JURY. Q. Do you keep a cashier? A. No, we had a confidential clerk,
Mr. Warren, one could scarcely call him a cashier--Mr. Meiter is com-
paratively ignorant of the financial department-it was not the prisoner's
duty to hand over money to Warren, only to me- I do not think he had
so much to do in serving customers that he might forget to make entries-
he might forget a matter of 38. or 48.---there might be a little pressure
sometimes for three quarters of an hour or so if several customers come in
at once, but then he would have an hour with scarcely anything to do-
the total amount of “overs" on 15th October was ill. 158. 5d. ; on 27th,
11. 78. 3d.; on 4th November, 61. 58. 2d. ; on 22nd, 61. 198. 11d.; on 1st
December, 71. 135. 3d.; on 17th, 81. 78.; and on 31st, 111. 15s. 6d.; making
a total of 541. 38. 72.
