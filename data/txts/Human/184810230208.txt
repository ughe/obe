   MR. PARRY . Q.  Look at this other property, which do you identify? A.  This is a plate that is partly melted—it is a stereotype plate of "genuine Court-plaister"—these leads have a particular mark on them—here is a little round mark, slightly indented, which arises from a little defect in the mould—almost every one of these has the mark—this one I cannot identify, but ray foundryman does.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  I believe you stated it to be yours from the remain ing impression of the plug mark? A.  Yes, and no other—on this piece of lead which has been partly melted", there is the stamp of Court-plaister—if he had taken any impression of one of my leads for the purpose of enabling him to make more by it, it would not have the same impression that mine has—it is too fine a mould—I did not employ a reporter to take down the evidence before the Magistrate, and afterwards publish it—I had a communi cation with a gentleman who sits below me—the second day we were at the office he came there—I had seen and known him some years—my communi cation to him was that I had been robbed, and that it was coming on at the police-court, and he was to come down—he was not to take a report—there was a reporter there—I did not employ him to report the case—there was another reporter at the office, but 1 was not acquainted with matters of this kind—a reporter reporting at a police-court must know these things—I never was in a police court—I knew there was a regular reporter there, and I told this gentleman, and he came—I will not say that I did, or did not tell him to come—I do not believe that I did—I have no recollection that I did—I had continual communication with him in the police-court while the case was going on—that person did not report the case—the case was reported by another reporter—I think it was not reported by him on the second occasion—I do not think he would have made the mistakes that were made—I did not speak to him till the case had been before the Magistrate.
