 148.  PAULO OFFRACIUS was indicted for feloniously stealing, on the 4th of January , a silver watch, value 40s. and one metal chain, value 6d. the property of Juan Chevrier.(The prosecutor and prisoner being foreigners, an interpreter and a Jury of half foreigners were sworn.
 JURY.
   Martin Robinson,
   Henry Sace,
   Solomon Erwood,
   Jacob Richcart,
   Francis Feather,
   Godfrey Shore,
   Joseph Simpson,
   Peter Prestiot,
   John Wilmot,
   Guiseppe Shum,
   Corneliud Paas,
   Peter Poland.
   JONATHAN MURRAY sworn.
 I am a pawnbroker, in East Smithfield; the prisoner pledged a watch with me. Q. Did he speak English when he pledged it? - A. No.
   JUAN CHEVRIER sworn.
 I am a foreigner; I lodged on the 4th of January in the house of Joseph Gomez; it is No. 4; I don't know the name of the street.
 Q. Do you know where the prisoner lived? - A. Yes; in the same house; I lost my watch on the 4th of January; I had left it locked up in my chest about twelve o'clock at noon; when I returned at seven in the evening, I missed it.
 Q. Do you know who took it out of your chest? - A. Yes; the prisoner at the bar; it was a silver watch, and the chain a metal one; the prisoner with his own key opened it.
 Q. Are you sure you did not give or lend him the watch? - A. No; I left it locked up in the chest.
 Murray. On Monday the 24th of January, about four or five o'clock in the afternoon,  Paulo Offracius, the prisoner, pawned a silver watch, for two guineas, which the prosecutor has got.
 Q. How did you make him understand? - A. A few days before, he came with a countryman of his to pawn a pair of knee buckles; he understood English; and when he came to take out the knee-buckles, be pledged this watch; he asked three guineas and an half for it; he pledged it in the name of John Fracius; he could understand money in English; and he said, if I would give him the paper, he would come again for it; by order of the Magistrates, I gave it to the prosecutor; the maker of it lives but a few doors from us, Mr. Jackson, a man that frequently serves foreigners with watches.(The watch was produced in Court.)
 Q. To Chevrier. Are you sure that is your property? - A. Yes.
 Murray. This is the watch I took in pledge from the prisoner.
   ELIZABETH GOMEZ sworn.
 I am the wife of  Joseph Gomez; the prosecutor and prisoner both lodged with me: On Monday the 4th of January, the prosecutor came home about seven in the evening; he went up to his chest, and came down, and said he had lost his watch; my mother and I went to a great many pawnbrokers, and found the watch at Mr. Murray's.
 Q. Had you no conversation with the prisoner? A. None at all.
 Q. What is your mother's name? - A. Ann Evans.
 Murray. Mr. Beare came to enquire after the watch about seven in the evening; and about eight o'clock this young woman and her mother came upon the same business.
   JOSEPH BEARE sworn.
 I apprehended the prisoner; the prosecutor came to my house, and said he had been robbed of his watch; that is all I know of it.
 Prisoner's defence. I pawned the watch with the prosecutor's leave; I had lent him two guineas; I asked him for the two guineas; he said he had not two guineas; I said, if he had not got it, I was in want of money, and he said pawn the watch; the prosecutor's chest was open, and he told me I might take it out.
 Q. (To Chevrier.) Did you owe the prisoner two guineas? - A. I never knew him in my life.
 Q. Did you owe him two guineas? - A. Not a farthing.
 Q. Did you give him leave to pawn your watch? - A. No.
 Q. (To Mrs. Gomez.) How long have you known the prisoner? - A. About three weeks.
 Q. Do you know any thing of his character at all? - A. No; I have known the prosecutor about three months.
 Q. Do you know of any transaction between these two people about money? - A. No.
 Q. You never heard of his lending the prosecutor any money? - A. No.
 Q. Were they upon any terms together? - A. No further than living in the house together.
 Q. (To the prisoner.) Is there any body here that knows you? - A. No.
 Q. Where is your captain? - A. At Jamaica. GUILTY. (Aged 18.)(The prisoner was recommended to mercy by the Jury and the prosecutor.)
 Tried before Mr. COMMON SERJEANT.
