Lambeth. On 22d Sept, I was going into the gallery entrance of the Victoria Theatre—the prisoner was standing outside—she asked me for a drop of gin—I refused—she called two girls, and they dragged me to the Victoria stairs—one of them put her hand over my mouth, and half a crown and a penny fell out of my pocket—I saw the prisoner pick it up—a con  stable then came—I did not lose sight of her—I saw her go right up the road—I did not follow her—the constable took her into custody. GEORGE LOCKETT. I am a shoemaker, living at No. 4, John's place. I was At the Victoria Theatre on the night in question—I saw the prisoner pick up half a crown—I saw the last witness dragged into the theatre—I know nothing of the persons who did it—there were two or three girls—the prisoner was standing up against the side of the gallery—I saw her take np the money—she vent towards the Victoria Tavern—I saw her after wards taken into custody. JAMES BRADLEY. ( policeman, L  93). I took the prisoner into custody—she was not sober—she had been about the Victoria Theatre for an hour and a half previous to that. Witness for the Defence. CATHERINE CONWAY. I was at the Victoria Theatre on the night in question, with my basket—I saw Mrs. Bassett come, and she asked me, did I see her old man—she was running up stairs, hallooing out for her hus band—a policeman went up stairs, and she came down with him, and he told her to go outside the door—two girls came up to this young girl ( the prosecutrix ), and asked her if she would stand a quartern of gin, she said no, she had no money—she was fumbling with the money, and she dropped the half crown down on the floor—sbe made a reach to pick it up, and she could not; one of the girls, with a cloak on, came and shoved her, and this young man ( Lockett ) came along with her, and picked it up—I said, "Why do you not give her in charge?"—the prisoner went outside the door—she was given in charge—the young man made a grab  at the mo ney, and said, "If I was her I should give you in charge"—he said, "I thought I could get it to give it to the owner"—the prisoner was out side the gallery door when the other two girls shoved the prosecutrix—she did not take any part in pulling her into the gallery door—it was the girl that had the cloak on that picked up the half crown. GEORGE LOCKETT. re-examined.  I saw the half crown down—I did not try to pick it up—I was there accidentally—I know none of the parties who were there—I am quite sure the prisoner is the woman who picked it up—she went away towards the Victoria Tavern—a policeman came up, and she was given in charge—that is what I saw. SUSAN ABRAHAM. re-examined.  I saw the prisoner pick up the half crown—it was knocked out of my hand—she was one of them that did it—she said "Give me a penny, I have got a penny, to get a half quartern of gin"—it is quite false what the witness Con way has said, because it is quite round the corner whdre she was—I dropped the money inside the stairs. GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Two Months .
