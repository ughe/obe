   COURT. Q.  Did you hear the children hooting at the prisoner? A.  No, they were kicking up a great noise before this happened—I did not hear the girl say anything, she was only looking on—I am quite sure she did not say a word, nor was she making any noise—she was not with the children who were making a noise—she was going past, and was going to turn to look at the woman on the ground.
   Prisoner. Q.  Where were you? A.  Against my own door—I lodge there—that is the statement I made to Mr. Cottingham—I said I was op posite Mr. Shea's house, not that lived there—I did not wish to say where I lived, because I knew I should be annoyed by the people that lived in the house—I said it was immaterial where I lived, I lived just by there. GEORGE ELISHA NEWTH. I am a surgeon, and live in Great Suffolk-street. On the 20th of July I was called to attend the prosecutrix—I found her lying on the bed, very faint—I examined her person, and found that the bottom of her belly, in the * * *, it was a wound about an inch and a half long, and was bleeding very much—there was a wound and a bruise—there was a good deal of blood on her person—in my opinion, it was a dangerous wound—she is suffering from it now very much—the bladder was very much affected, and is now—as soon as she came to, she complained of very great pain at the bottom of the belly, great pain on pressure—I administered proper remedies—she is has complained more or less since of that pain—she at first suffered from not passing her water, but now she has great difficulty in holding it, and has great pain when she passes it—she feels much easier when she is in a recumbent posture—she has been confined to her bed up to this time—in my opinion, such a contusion and wound might have been caused by the kick of a powerful man—if it had been a little higher, it might have ruptured the bladder—she is certainly recovering now, but I expect she will feel the effect of it almost ever after.
   COURT. Q.  When you say there was a wound, do you mean to say that the skin was broken? A.  Yes—I am quite sure it amounted to a wound—it bled—it could not have bled without the skin breaking—it must have been a very violent kick.
   Prisoner. Q.  You told Mr. Cottingham it was not much of a wound, and not near the bottom of the belly, and that very trifling blood ran from it? A.  I never said anything of the kind—the Magistrate said if there was any danger to her life I was to get further opinion, that the prisoner might have the benefit of all the medical advice that could be obtained, because, if the girl died, he perhaps might be committed for murder; but as the girl was getting better the mother did not wish anybody else called in. WILLIAM GRIST( policeman, M  223.) I took the prisoner, and produce one of his boots—it has large nails in it—he said he was going into the country to work, and knew nothing about it. SUSAN M'CASLAND. I am the prosecutrix's mother. On the 25th of July I sent her for a mug of pump water—she was quite well when she went—she was brought home senseless—she has been confined to her bed ever since, and hardly able to sit up more than a few minutes in the course of the day.
   Prisoner. Q.  Was not she at Mr. Sutton's funeral? A.  No, she was in bed.
