DAVID FITZGERALD was indicted for feloniously breaking and entering the dwelling-house of William Speller , on the   10th of March , at   Christ Church, Surrey , and stealing therein, 1 tea-caddy, value 12 s  .; 1 work-box, value 8 s  .; 1 table-cloth, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; 1 table-cover, value 10 s  .; and 5lbs. weight of beef, value 3 s  ., his property. WILLIAM SPELLER. I am an iron-plate worker, and live in York place, Christ Church, Surrey—I rent the whole house. About a quarter to eight o'clock on the night of the 10th of March, in consequence of information, I went into Thorough-street, which is about one hundred and fifty yards from my house, and saw the prisoner and a lad about fourteen yean old—the prisoner passed me with some articles—I found nobody further on and returned, and then found the prisoner kneeling down—I nearly stepped on him—I looked over his shoulder—he asked me what I was looking at—I said, "Nothing particular"—he said, "You can go on"—I said, "I know that," and as he rose up I collared him, and told him I thought he had something belonging to me—I brought him towards the gas-light, and saw the tea-caddy and work-box shine through the hand kerchief—I afterwards found the table cover in William-street, in the road—I gave him in charge. HARRIET SPELLER. I am the prosecutor's wife. On the night of the 10th of March, I heard a noise in the front-parlour, like the rattling of plates' and dishes—I had noticed the window between two and three o'clock in the afternoon, it was then down—I went into the parlour on bearing. the noise, and found the window and one blind open, and missed from the table a piece of beef, a tea-caddy, a work-box, a table-cloth, and a table-cover—I had fastened the blinds and shut the window down that day, and they remained so till I heard the noise—it would require a light to see into the parlour at eight o'clock—I took a candle with me—there was the appearance of a candle having been on the table, and a lucifer match, and a smell of sulphur. RICHARD SENIOR. I am a plumber, and live in Lesson Grove. I was spending the evening at Speller's—he brought the prisoner to the house and gave him into my charge, with the work-box and tea-caddy tied in ft handkerchief—he threw them down at my feet—I took them up and gave them to the constable—I had gone to the house about five o'clock, and the window was down then. ROBERT TURTLB( police-constable M  56.) On the night of the 10th of March, I was called to the house, and received charge of the prisoner with the things—I found a box of lucifers in his pocket, and Mrs. Speller gave me one partly burnt, off the table.
 (  Property produced and sworn to. )
   Prisoner's Defence.  I had only come out of the country on Saturday evening—I had spent the Sunday with a friend—as I came home I saw the smallest box lying down in the middle of Thurlow-street, and about six yards from it was the work-box—I tied them in my handkerchief, and the gentleman came up and took me. GUILTY. * Aged 18.— Confined Twelve Months.
