 225.  WILLIAM BRIAN was indicted for stealing, on the 30th of November , 1 pocket-book, value 2s., and I bill of exchange, value 15l., the property of Charles James , from his person.
 CHARLES JAMES. I am a  camp-equipage maker, and live in Coventry-street. On the 30th of November, about twelve o'clock at night, I was going home, and in King-street, Covent-garden, I missed from my pocket a pocketbook, containing a bill for 15l.; I was close to the houses - a person tried to pass between me and the houses; there was not room, and during that time I felt a motion at my pocket - I looked round, and saw a person walking away from me; I put my hand to my pocket, and my book was gone - I went after the person, and the Policeman laid hold of him; it was the prisoner - I saw him drop the book while the Policeman held his collar.
   JOHN JOHNSON. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner, as Mr. James said he had taken his pocket-book; both my hands were engaged - I looked down, and saw the pocket-book slip down at his feet; he seemed surprised that he should be apprehended; there were two youngsters there, who ran off, but I could not follow them.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been to Chelsea, looking for employ, and then went to see my mother - I met two young men, and had a glass or two with them, and in King-street I was charged with this.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Life.
 226.  HENRY HOLMES was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November , 3lbs. of soap, value 18d., the goods of William Fisher.
   MARY FISHER. I am the wife of William Fisher- we keep a little shop in Noble-street. On the 24th of November, about seven o'clock in the evening, the prisoner, came for a halfpenny worth of tobacco; I served him and he went out - this soap was inside the counter - I did not miss it till about half-past seven o'clock, when Mrs. Perry came to know if I had lost any; it was a whole cake - I saw the prisoner in custody with it at eleven o'clock that night - he lived next door to me.
   NATHANIEL NICHOLS. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner about half-past nine o'clock at night, charged with offering the soap for sale to Mrs. Perry; Mrs. Fisher saw it at the station, and said it was impossible to swear to it, but hers was soap of that description.
   CHARLOTTE PERRY. I live at St. Helena-place. On the 24th of October a little boy came to sell me this soap, and asked 1s. for it - I asked where he got it; he said his father sent him out to sell it - I said I suspected it was stolen, and should detain it till his father came; I went to the door after him, - the prisoner came up to the door, and said, "Give me that soap, Mrs. Perry - it is mine;" I refused, and went round to the neighbours, to inquire if they had lost any; Fisher claimed it.
   JOHN STANLEY. I am thirteen years old, and have known the prisoner three years. On the 24th of November, about a quarter-past seven o'clock, I saw him in Noble-street - he gave me this soap, and told me to go and sell it at Perry's, and say my father sent me with it; I went to her with it - I am sure I have told the truth.
   MARY FISHER. I lost the same kind of soap as this; the prisoner's parents are unable to support him, but can send him to sea now.
 GUILTY. Aged 13. -    Judgment Respited.
