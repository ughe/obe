WILLIAM SERJEANT. I have been head warehouseman to Chapman four
years--the pumice soap was sold 11d. a tablet, never at id., and the per-
fumes at 4fd. a bottle, never at 31.; the perfumes were labelled in a
sbow case against the door of 402, Edgware Road- these are the tickets;
they are in Mr. Bevis's writing, who is going to Canada--when the police
came I was instructed to go into the shop and remove them from the
case--Mr. Walters can swear that I sold the essences at 412., we never
sold or marked them at 31d. I remember Sergeant Williamson coming,
and after I had had my tea he came into the essence room with Thorpe
and looked at the essences--they did not go into the cellar on that
visit, but they did the next time, and I followed them--Mr. Thorpe said
"Is that all soap?"--I said “No, there are four boxes over the road, go
and fetch them,” which he did Sergeant Williamson said " Have you
any more soap ?"-I said " Yes, we have plenty of much better quality;
here is a piece, what do you say to that?"-he said "We don't want
that, where are the currants and the sauces you have bought at the door?"
