1912
CENTRAL CRIMINAL COURT
CROSBY, MAYOR.
ELEVENTH SESSION.
LONDON AND MIDDLESEX CASES.
BEFORE THE RECORDER.
(Monday, July 22.)
HULBERT, Charles (32, postman), who pleaded guilty last Session
(see page 341) of laroeny, was brought up for judgment.
Evidence was called by the Post Office to show that prisoner's allega
tions of unfair treatment in the matter of overtime and boot allow-
ance were groundless.
Bentence: Six months' hard labour.
WATERS, William Frederick (19, stevedore), who was convioted
last session (see page 357) of common assault, was brought up for
judgment.
Prisoner, having expressed his contrition for the offence, was re-
leased on his own recognisances and those of his father (William
Frederick Waters) in £100 each to come up for judgment if called
upon. The Recorder remarked that it was monstrous the violence
to which inoffensive men engaged in their employment were sub-
jected.
SCHOLFIELD, Edwin Weston (49, porter), pleaded guilty of steal-
ing a postal packet containing a purse, 13s. 2d., and three halfpenny
postage stamps, the property of His Majesty's Postmaster-General,
he being an officer of the Post Office.
Prisoner, an Army reservist, had been in the service of the Post
Office for 17 years. Evidence was called as to his previous good
character.
Sentence: Nine months' hard labour.
COOKE, George Edmund (33, postman), pleaded guilty of stealing
3 postal packet containing a postal order for 5s., the property of
His Majesty's Postmaster-General, he being an officer of the Post
Office; stealing a postal packet containing a postal-order for 28. 6d.,
the property of His Majesty's Postmaster-General, he being an officer
of the Post Office.
