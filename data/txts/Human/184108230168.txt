 2226. THOMAS M'DONNELL was indicted for stealing, on the   10th of July , 1 coat, value 18 s  ., the goods of Isaac Joscelyne . ISAAC JOSCELYNE. I am a tailor, and live in Shoreditch. On the morn ing of the 10th of July I put a summer coat at my door—I missed it in about an hour—it was brought back by my shopman, and this now produced is it. ROBERT SCALES. I am a letter-sorter in the Post-office. I was near the prosecutor's door that morning—I saw the prisoner and another lad together—they went to Mr. Joscelyne's shop, and sat down a little while—the prisoner then changed his apron with the other—the other then took the coat off the horse, and the prisoner ran away with it.
   Prisoner  I was at work at Woolwich that day, seven miles from here. GUILTY.** Aged 18.— Transported for Seven Years.
 2227. MARTHA CORBETT was indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of September, 1840 , 10 printed books, value 2 l  .; 9 blankets, value 2 l  . 5 s  .; 2 pairs of pillow-cases, value 6 s  .; 1 table-cloth, value 5 s  .; 5 sheets, value 2 l  . 10 s  .; 1 carpet, value 3 s  .; 2 handkerchiefs, value 4 s  .; 1 sheet, value 12 s  .; 2 towels, value 1 s  . 6 d  .; 2 pillows, value 10 s  .; 2 quilts, value 10 s  .; 1 pair of boots, value 9 s  .; 1 carpet-cover, value 10 s  .; and 4 dishes, value 18 s  .; the goods of William Thurnell , her master. MARY THURNELL. I am the wife of William Thurnell, of Regent-place, City-road. The prisoner was my servant—I searched her boxes, and found nothing—I then searched her person, and found four duplicates of a blanket, a bolster, a pillow, pawned at Mr. Somers's—they are mine—my husband afterwards found about thirty-eight more duplicates in the kitchen. WILLIAM HUMPHREYS. I live at Mr. Somers's, a pawnbroker. I have a bolster, pillow, and two blankets, pawned by the prisoner.
   Prisoner.  Mary Ann, their niece, asked me to pawn these things—I did not refuse it, but I gave all the money I received for them to Mary Ann, and she gave it to her sister, who was discharged. On Satur day night, the 14th, my mistress accused me of taking these things, and turned me out of doors at twelve o'clock at night—I told her her niece gave me them, but she would not hear what I had to say, because they were in my name. She owes me a quarter's wages, which is 2 l . 1 s .
   MRS. THURNELL re-examined.  I have a niece, named Mary Ann Smith—she positively denies every thing of the kind—she was before the Magistrate, but her evidence was not wanted—she is nineteen years old, and has a good character—she has a sister, who is married. GUILTY. Aged 49.— Confined One Year.
