   Cross-examined.  I first looked over 9, Lordship Lane, I never intended to take it, and then over 228, Crystal Palace Road—he wanted 75 l . for the Lordship Lane one and 100 l . for the Crystal Palace Road—I chose the latter after looking over both—there was a shed erected in the back yard, and a cellar was being excavated and a beer engine, counter, and fittings being erected when I looked in on the 17th—I signed the agree ment and went in—I knew I was only allowed to sell beer off the pre mises—I have heard that the British Empire Company had given a lease of the house seven years before to Mr. Bendall, but not to sell beer—I only sold beer to be drunk on the premises to Mr. Johnson and his sons; they led me to do it—two clerks from the British Empire Company were not served with two glasses of ale; I will swear it—Mr. Johnson was served with the injunction—I know the British Empire Company allowed beer to be served off but not on the premises—I cannot say if the British Empire Company allowed Bendall to serve beer off the premises for seven years, but forbade Bendall or Twiddy serving it on the premises—I was not allowed to sell it on the premises—I did it to Mr. Johnson and his son; he said he had no doubt some of his pals  would call on the Sunday morning for wets  —1 did not agree to that—I think the British Empire Company served me with the injunction because they would not allow beer to be sold there—I know nothing about Bendall—I have sold nothing in the house since I shut it up; I am living there—the beer engine and counter are there, and what is left of the beer—Johnson did not send in more than 50 l . worth of beer—when I said at the police-court I did not know the value of the stock on the premises I had not been over it then—I have used some myself since—my pals  did not come for a wet  —I am a stranger in the place—it is only the injunction prevents me selling—when I asked Johnson to pay me all he said was he was at law—he did not tell me he was at law with Messrs.
