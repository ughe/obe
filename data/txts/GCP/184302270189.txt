SAMUEL GILBERT. I am a publishing bookseller, and live in Paternoster-
row. The prisoner was in my service, as a porter and collector for about eleven
months-in consequence of something that occurred, I gave him into custody,
on the 9th of Feb.- he was taken to the station-I saw a duplicate found on
him-I proceeded with it to the shop of Mr. Lamb, a pawnbroker, in Stan-
hope-street, Clare-market, and there I found this copy of the Mirror, and
twenty-three numbers of Knight's London-I afterwards went to Mr. Winton,
a publican, in Wych-street, Strand-I found there these six numbers of
Froissart's Chronicle-these are not works which I have published, but which
I have sold and dealt in--the prisoner was my porter, but he had nothing to
do with selling property across the counter, or dealing with it in any way--he
would have access to the books-he came to me at eight o'clock in the morn-
ing, and remained till nine at night I had a right to all his time during those
bours-I paid him his wages every Saturday-I missed this volume of the
Mirror.
Cross-examined by Mr. Payne. Q. You cannot tell whether any of these
others have been sold ? A. I cannot-they are sold by all the other book-
sellers in the trade-Froissart's Chronicle has been out nine months, and
Knight's London commenced in March, 1841; but some are numbers, and
some are parts--one of these is for March, 1841-tbe duplicate found on the
prisoner relates only to this volume of the Mirror --whether this book ever
was in my shop I cannot say-other persons beside myself sell in my shop. .
the volume of the Mirror that we missed, I saw last in the prisoner's hand, but
whether it was sold I cannot tell.
MR. DOANE. Q. What is the last date of the numbers of Knight's London
that you found at the pawnbroker's? A. September, 1842.
