   Prisoner. Q.  Did your daughter bring home an invoice? A.  No; the customer takes the invoice for every amount received, from 6 d . upwards—it was your duty not to let a customer go from the shop without a bill. WILLIAM SOUTH. ( Policeman ). On Saturday night, 31st Oct., I was called into Mr. Whitter's shop—the prisoner was standing against the window, looking in—he seemed confused when Mr. Whitter asked him something—Mr. Whitter said, "I give him into your custody for robbing me"—I said, "We had better go in at the private door, as there are people passing"—we did so, and Mr. Whitter said, "Search him now"—I put my hand in his waistcoat pocket, and he pulled several pieces of silver out of his trowsers pocket, and this marked shilling and sixpence with them—he said, "Pray forgive me, for the sake of my friends," or "Will you forgive me, for the sake of my friends?"—I took him to the station.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was it not, "Do not lock me up for the sake of my friends?" A.  I am sure he said, "Forgive"—he did not give me an answer immediately—he seemed confused, and as though he could not speak for a minute. ( MR.
