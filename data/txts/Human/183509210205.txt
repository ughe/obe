   COURT . Q.  Tell us any street into which you turned and they followed you? A.  we went at nine or ten o'clock, one Sunday evening, to Mr. Savory's, on the other side Camden-town, near the Regent's-park, on pur pose to see if they would follow us—we stopped there till very nearly two o'clock in the morning—the "Emperor" followed us, and remained there all the time—John Sylvester drove it, and Smith was the conductor.
   MR. BALL . Q.  Did you go to Whitechapel? A.  No, I went to Marylechone police-office, which is out of my beat, and the "Emperor" ac compained me there, and stopped all the time I stopped—I broke down twice, and they stopped at the same time—I once broke dewn in the New-road, and once in the Edgware-road, both out of our road, and they stopped with us, and followed us home—we lengthened one of our omnibuses, No. 349, to Maida-hill—I think on the 6th of July—I drove that at time—the "Emperor" followed me that very day, and pursued the same kind of conduct us before, in running before me—In going to Maida-hill I went through Paddington—If in driving I came within Bolton's times, I had his five omnibuses with me also—he had three minute's time—I think his first time was a quarter before ten o'clock, and he had two three minutes after that—If I came within his first time he would hang back, and take a minute or two of the other man's time, and the other one would hurry to make his time up; so that I had two with me, and very likely three or four—If I came within Bardell's time, they would hang back upon me in the same manner—Richard Bird did the same—Thomas Bird the same—Carpenter, Cowderoy, Hill, Johnson, and Newell did the same—It was he followed me out of my road a day or two after the sale, up Hinde-street, and two or three other streets which I turned up to get out of their way, as they were hunting us so much—Pearce did the same—Parker and Sellick the same—l never saw Robert Trevett drive, but I have seen him on the box of the "Emperor"—I have heard him halloo to them to "Keep at him," and cheering them on—I believe the "Albion" used to put up at night at Trevett's stable, but I did not see it—they used to follow me to
