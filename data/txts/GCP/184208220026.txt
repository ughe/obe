JOSEPH BULKELEY JOHNSON. I am a tea-dealer, and live in Nicholas.
lane. I have more than one partner--this box of tea is mine.
THOMAS KNIGHT. I am porter to the prosecutors. At a quarter past two
o'clock on the 5th of July I was returning from dinner, -on coming in at
the door I saw the prisoner coming out at the door with the box of tea
under his arm-I said, " Where are you going with that?"-he made no
answer, but dropped his arm, sprang off the steps, and ran off about a bun-
dred yards-he was caught by two men-I never lost sight of him--this is
the box of tea.
Prisoner's Defence. I and another lad were sky-larking; he knocked
my hat off, and threw it into the warehouse; I went to get it, and knocked
down the tea-chest; I took it up, and was going to put it in its place,
when this man was coming in, and I was stopped on the steps. Witness.
We do not keep the chests of tea at the door-it was about eleven feet in-
side the shop-I saw no other man there.
WILLIAM BUTCHER (City police-constable, No. 540.) I produce a
certificate of the prisoner's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's
office-(read)-the prisoner is the person.
GUILTY. Aged 20.- Transported for Ten Years.
2219. WILLIAM COWLINGE was indicted for stealing, on the 16th
of July, 1 sovereign and 1 shilling, the monies of Samuel Jennings, his
master.
SAMUEL WILLIAM FRANCIS. I am servant to the prosecutor. On the
16th of July the prisoner sent me to Foster and Smith, in Bishopsgate-
street, with a picture which my master had to be framed-I got a sovereign
and a shilling for it-it was my master's money, and I gave it to the pri-
soner as his servant-he ought to have given it to my master.
SAMUEL JENNINGS. I live in Goswell-street; the prisoner was my
servant. I ought to have received a sovereign and a shilling from the pri-
soner-he never paid it me-he ought to have paid it the same evening.
GUILTY. Aged 21.-Confined Nine Months.
2220. THOMAS KITCHEN was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of
July, 1 handkerchief, value 2s.6d., the goods of William Snoder, from
his person.
WILLIAM SNODER. I am a tallow-chandler, and live in Red Lion-street,
Holborn. I was in Newgate-street at a quarter past three o'clock on the
15th of July-I felt something at my coat, turned round, missed my
handkerchief, and saw the prisoner with it under his coat-I said, "You
have got my handkerchief"-he ran and dropped it-I picked it up and
gave it to the policeman.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. How near were you to him when
you saw it? A. About three yards-I saw him stopped-I never lost
sight of him.
EDWARD Cort (City police-constable, No. 285.) I took the prisoner,
and have the handkerchief.
GUILTY. Aged 23.-Confined Three Months.
