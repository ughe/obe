EDWARD LATTER (City Policeman). On 29th September I took the
prisoner into custody at Mr. Attenborough's shop I asked him where he
got the cheque from, and he said a man of the name of Mott gave it to him
I asked him if he knew where Mr. Mott lived, and he said I should find
him down at the East or West India Docks-as I was taking him down to
the station he said that he filled the cheque up and that Mott signed it-
at the police-station when the charge was entered and read over he said he
was guilty-he gave the name of Harry Graham-in consequence of infor-
mation I went to 34, Northumberland Street, Marylebone, and found a bag
there ; it was locked-I opened it with a key which I took from the prisoner
st the police-station-in that bag I found the cheque-book produced and
the invoice of Goy & Co., and purse, and in the purse I found a cheque
for 52, purporting to be signed by Mr. Williams-when he was taken into
custody he was wearing a midshipman's cap-I found the gold lace from it in
his pocket-those things were given up at the Mansion House to Mr. Griffin.
WILLIAN FREDERICK WILLIAMS. I am a jewel-case maker, and carry on
business at 88, Broad Street, Golden Square-the prisoner was formerly a
clerk in the counting-house, for some few weeks, in the name of Greenwood
-be left me about six months ago—I kept an account at the Holborn
Branch of the London and County Bank--this order for the cheque-book,
dated the 23rd September, is not written by me or by my authority-the
body of the order is in Greenwood's writing--these three cheques for 201.,
B1, and 51., were not written by me or by my authority-I know nothing
about them—they are all signed “W. J. Williams & Co.”-that is the way
I signed my cheques--the prisoner used to fill up the body of cheques for
me-be used to practice my signature on pieces of paper that were found
at my place.
Prisoner. 0. Did you find me guilty of any misconduct while I was
with you! 4. I found you attempting to rob me of a 101. note, and that
was the reason you left.
Prisoner. There was no attempt to rob, the note was found between two
sheets of a newspaper I had on the desk.
The Prisoner's Statement before the Magistrate : "An I have to say is, all
the cheques were written by a man named Burton. I had nothing to do
with getting the cheque-book. I neither wrote the order for it nor did I go
for it."
DAVID BURTON. I live at 16, College Place, Camden Town-I am olerk
in the Middlesex Registry Office, and have been there four years I know
the prisoner slightly-I knew him as Greenwood, or Greenfield, but it is so
long ago since I saw him that I almost forget-I did not know him by the
name of Graham-I know nothing whatever about these cheques, and
never saw them in my life I have not seen the prisoner since last March
I did not know he was in existence.
The prisoner, in his defence, stated that he had seen Burton committing an
