JOHN GOLDING. I am foreman to Fletcher and Warden, butchers, at
Kew on 22nd Sept., about a quarter to 8 o'clock, Welch came for a
mutton chop, and gave me a sovereign ; I gave him 198. 7d. change, and
took it in doors to Miss Warden, who acts as cashier-Seers had been to my
house that morning for some beef steak, which came to 7d. or 9d., and paid
me with a half crown-he did not appear to be drunk-I got change from
Miss Warden-he said, “Well, I did not know you would have to go after
change, or else I would have let it be till by-and-by"-I did not know what
he meant-I bave seen Welch at my house several times, purchasing things
-Miss Warden afterwards gave me the sovereign, and I marked it, and
xave it to the policeman--in the course of that day I went to Ridley's
buildings to look for Welch, and found him doing something on the roof, in
his shirt sleeves—he was in his jacket when he came to my house-I have
no doubt at all that he is the person-Henry Smith was in the shop.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you any doubt how? 4. Not much, but I
bad at the time; so much so, that I left him to look for somebody else-T
said that I could not see any person like the man-it was after that that I
made up my mind that it must be Welch the prisoner said something
about not taking him into custody if I could not make up my mind I had &
strong suspicion, but there was a difference in his dress he was not taken
for an hour and a half or two hours after that I have seen him come to my
house, but I never saw him with anybody else; he has come and bought
articles several times.
MR CLERK. Q. You told the prosecutor that you had a strong suspicion
that Welch was the man: how near were you to him? 4. He was upon &
high place and I was on the ground; he continued at his work while I went
round the building-he was about eight feet above me, he had no conversa-
tion with me, or with the policeman-when he came away from the building
with the policeman he had his jacket on, and then I could swear to him.
