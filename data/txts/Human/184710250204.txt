2501. JAMES LEE was indicted for stealing 45lb. weight of lead pipe, value 11 s  .; 1 basket, 10 d  .; the goods of George Robinson , his master. GEORGE DAY. I am foreman to Mr. George Robinson—the prisoner was in his employ. I was at work on the 4th of Oct. at Richmond, and about four o'clock in the afternoon I saw the prisoner coming out of a shed where there was lead kept—he was bringing part of a coil of lead—he placed it at the end of the shed, near a wall, where there was a path on the other side—he went back to the shed—he had no business whatever with the lead—it was my master's—he moved it about twenty yards. THOMAS PIRIES. I went into the shed on that day and saw the prisoner bending a piece of lead backwards and forwards—I told the foreman and went back to my work—in half an hour afterwards I saw the prisoner take a roll of lead and put it behind a place on my master's premises—I am certain it is my master's lead. GEORGE WOODS( policeman, V  209.) I took the prisoner and have the lead—I found this basket at the prisoner's lodging. GEORGE DAY re-examined.  This basket is my master's property. GUILTY . Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .
 2502. ALEXANDER STORAR was indicted for stealing 1 barometer, value 10 s  ., the property of Robert Bastow and others . BENJAMIN WILLMOT WHEATLAND. I am an officer of the Southwark Literary Institution. On the 18th of Sept. I missed a barometer—this is it—it is the property of Robert Bastow and others. JAMES WALTER. I am a pawnbroker—I have a barometer I took in—I cannot say of whom—this is the duplicate I gave of it. RICHARD MERRONY( policeman, R  102.) I took the prisoner—I found this duplicate on him of the barometer pawned on the 18th of Sept.
   Prisoner's Defence.  A man named Hardy owed me some money; I asked him several times for it, and he told me to call on Saturday afternoon, and he would give me part and security for the rest; I called, he gave me a shilling, and said he had a barometer out of repair, I might pawn it; I did so; I sent my wife to the man afterwards, and he was gone away—I told the officer where I lived and where I worked. RICHARD MERRONY re-examined.  Yes, he did—I went to his masters, who said he knew nothing dishonest of him. GUILTY . Aged 18.— Confined Six Months .
 (There were two other indictments against the prisoner.)
 2503. SARAH GROOM was indicted for stealing 1 shawl, value 2 s  ., the property of Phoebe Pearce ; having been before convicted of felony. PHOEBE PEARCE. I am single, and live at Mortlake. On the 18th of Oct. I saw the prisoner at Richmond with my shawl on—I had seen it safe at home at four o'clock that afternoon—I went home, it was gone from the table where I had left it—I went back after the prisoner and gave her in charge—the policeman took the shawl from her—this is my shawl. SOPHIA PEARCE. I know this shawl—it belongs to Phoebe Pearce. JAMES STRUDWICK( policeman, V  205.) I found the prisoner lying in a privy with this shawl. JOHN CANNON( police-sergeant, V  6.) I produce a certificate of the pri soner's former conviction—( read—Convicted Sept. , 1845, and confined four  teen day solitary)—the prisoner is the person. GUILTY . Aged 20.— Confined One Year .
