 2246. GEORGE STREAK was indicted for stealing 1 ass, price 1 l  . 5 s  .; the property of William Edwards . WILLIAM EDWARDS. I live in Little Manor-street, Clapham. On Friday, the 5th of Aug., I had a donkey turned out on Clapham-common—I saw it safe at nine o'clock in the morning, and missed it about half-past nine—I saw it again about four the same afternoon, in Smithfield—a man named Timson had it there for sale. EDWARD LAWRENCE. I live at Walworth. On the 6th of Aug., about twelve o'clock in the day, I was in Blackfriars-road—I saw a donkey—a little boy was riding it, and the prisoner was walking by the side of it—the prisoner asked me to buy the donkey—he asked 12 s . for it—I said I had only 8 s .—they went on about forty yards, and then the little boy came run ing back, and said I should have it—I bought it for 8 s .—I am sure it was the prisoner who offered me the donkey for sale—I took it to my own home, and put it in harness to see if it would be of any use to me, and it would not, it laid down—I sent Timson to sell it for half a guinea—I afterwards heard something, and the prisoner was given in charge. WALTER CHARLES DODD. I live at No. 2, Union-street, Walworth. I was with Lawrence when he bought the donkey—I saw the prisoner—he had a boy with him—I did not hear the dealing, but I saw the money paid by Lawrence to the prisoner. WILLIAM GIBBS( policeman, V  77.) I apprehended the prisoner—I told him he was charge with stealing a donkey—he told me he did not take the donkey off the common; but if he had been present when the other boy was tried, he would have given the young b—r more than he had got.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I met the boy on Kennington-common; he asked me to go with him; I went with him to Blackfriars-road, and he sold it there; he said, "It is one of Mr. Edwards's donkeys, but don't say anything about it;" he gave me half the money, and had half himself. NOT GUILTY .
