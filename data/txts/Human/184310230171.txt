MR. HORRY conducted the Prosecution. JAMES SCOTT. I keep the Uxbridge Arms, at Notting-hill. I deal with Messrs. Strong and Larchin for beer—on the 20th of Sept., I paid the pri soner 19 l . 19 s . 11 d . on account of the firm—he gave me this receipt.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  How long have you dealt with the firm? A.  From the 8th of last March—I knew Mr. Larchin for some years—the prisoner was a collecting clerk—I made his acquaintance through Larebin and Co. WILLIAM GLASS. I keep the King's Arms beer-shop at Pentonville. I deal with Strong and Larchin—on the 21st of Sept. I paid the prisoner 2 l ., and on the 23rd of Sept. 3 l ., on account of the firm—this is the prisoner's signature in my book.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you been a customer of the house, introduced by the prisoner? A.  Yes—I only knew I was dealing with that house through him—I believe the prisoner kept a beer-shop at Wai worth—the mo nies I paid him were on account of the goods I had had. SAMUEL STRONG. I am a brewer, and am in partnership with Henry Lar chin and Henry Smith, at Ratcliff-cross—the prisoner was in our service—it was his duty to receive money, collect orders, and to account to me for the money he received—he had an allowance of ten per cent, and twopence in the pound for expenses—there is no commission remaining due to him on any monies that he has paid—he has never accounted to me for 19 l . 19 s . 11 d . from Mr. Scott, on the 20th of Sept.—on the following day I learned he had received 20 l ., and I asked him the reasons he had not paid it, with other money he had paid on the Wednesday—he said he had not enough by 1 l ., and he would bring it on Saturday—he promised to call with it—I did not see him afterwards—he has never accounted to me for 2 l . and 3 l ., received from Mr. Glass on the 21st and 23rd of Sept.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you make any inquiry whether he had received these two sums? A.  I had not an opportunity of doing so—I did not see him afterwards—he solicited us to employ him—we did not go to him—there was no writing executed between us—I told him he ought to keep a book to enter the money, and, in fact, he had a book when he brought the first money—he was to pay me once or twice a week—not less than once a week cer tainly—there was an understanding about that.
   Q.  Have you not been in correspondence with his attorney, and found, on application, that 60 l . was claimed for balance due from you to him? A.  No; no claim of 60 l . has been made on us by Salmon and Long, on his account—we received a letter on the 8th or 9th of Oct. from them, in which they say that there was a large sum due, but they do not state the amount—that was before I gave the prisoner into custody—his attorney made a demand on us for money, in answer to a letter we wrote to the prisoner, addressed to his own house—I rather think we gave him into custody the day after we re ceived the letter—I will not swear when we received the letter—I believe it was not nearly a week after we received it that we gave him in charge, but I cannot tell—he was not to receive a commission upon all the custom he got, but upon all the monies he paid in, because there were a great many bad debts—I believe he went about to the publicans, and drank with them to get custom—I settled with him last on the 20th of Sept.—here is the book—I never had a general account—I always paid him as he went on—I received 5 l . 19 s . 10 d . on the 20th of Sept, as a balance of an ac count of Scott and Glass—I do not know when I had received any money before that had any reference to Scott—I have received from the prisoner, in
