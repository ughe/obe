ROBERT L. HARRIS. My mother keeps the Nag's Head. The prisoner has been about two years and a half in our establishment—he had notice to quit before this, and was to have left the following day.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Has not a gentleman, sleeping in this room since, complained of something? A.  Not to me—we had three or four gentlemen sleeping in the room over this, and I heard that one of them went into this room by mistake—two carriers slept in the house on the night of the rob bery—they regularly sleep there twice a week—I understand the chimneys were swept that morning.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Did you take the prosecutor's money? A.  Cer tainly not—nobody else has complained of losing money in the house—the prosecutor frequented our house. CHARLOTTE FENNER. I am chambermaid at the house. I went into the prosecutor's room between seven and eight o'clock in the morning, as the pri soner told me there Was a mess in the corner of the room—I went up with Jewson, and found there had been a cat in the corner—we looked into the bed, and under the bolster Jewson found a bag, with three notes in it—I took them to Mr. Coulter—there was no gold in it—the bag could not have got by accident where I found it, for it was tucked under the bolster with the sheet.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Was it wrapped up in the sheet? A.  No, tucked up with it—I have heard something of a complaint from a person sleeping in the room since—the prisoner told me Mr. Coulter told him there was a mess in the room, and that he had lost some notes.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  Was there any complaint of any thing being lost from the room? A.  Nothing of the kind. SARAH JEWSON. I am chambermaid at the inn. I went into the room between seven and eight o'clock in the morning, and found the bag and notes under the bolster, tucked in with the bottom sheet.
   cross-examined. Q.  Did you hear a complaint made by a Mr. Minny sleeping in the room afterwards? A.  He complained, three weeks after this, of somebody coming into the room, and he asked what they wanted—they said they mistook the room—I never found out who it was—the sweeps had been in the house on both occasions. WILLIAM TILLIARD. I am waiter at the house. I got up on the morning in question about a quarter past eight o'clock. THOMAS BENSON. I am a carrier, and put up at the Nagg's Head. I slept there on this night—I got up about five o'clock, and left at half-past eight—Constable. who is too ill to attend, slept in another part of the house.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You saw the prisoner that morning? A.  Yes—he was cleaning his boots when I came down—the sweeps had gone before I came down. JOHN YATES( police-inspector. ) I took the prisoner, searched him and his box, but found nothing—he said he had never been in Mr. Coulter's room that morning. NOT GUILTY.
