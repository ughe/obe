 Q to Harding. Was your wife with you all the time?
 Harding. She was, excepting the time she fetched a pot or two of beer
 Q Who fetched the rest of the beer ?
 Harding. No body fetched any but my wife.
   Jane Harding. I am wife to the last evidence. The prisoner came to our house with an acquaintance I wash for on the 28th of March.
 Q. Had you seen him before?
 Jane Harding. Not to my knowledge; he came between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, and staid 'till seven in the morning; there were also three other acquaintances I wash for.
 Cross-Examination.
 Q. Had you any liquor?
   Jane Harding. We had three or four pots of beer.
 Q. Who did the prisoner come with?
   Jane Harding. He came there with a woman.
 Q. How came they to stay all night?
   Jane Harding. When they had staid pretty late then they begged to stay all night.
 Q. Who came first, the other company or the prisoner and young woman?
   Jane Harding. The others came after the prisoner came in.
 Q. Were they quite sober?
   Jane Harding. They were very sober.
 Q. Who fetched the beer?
   Jane Harding. I did. I went for two pots of beer to the coach-and-horses, at the corner of the Old-Bailey.
 Q. Was that all you had?
   Jane Harding. Yes; that lasted them 'till morning.
 Q. Had you no more?
   Jane Harding. We had no more than two full pots before the others went away.
 Q. How much beer might you have in all?
   Jane Harding. Four or five full pots were all we had.
 Q. Did no body go to bed in the room?
   Jane Harding. No; we all set up. The prisoner slept in a chair 'till seven o'clock, and the young woman went away as soon as it was light, but I could not awake the prisoner then.
 Q. At what hour did the young woman go away ?
   Jane Harding. At about a quarter after six.
 Q. How many were there of you in company?
   Jane Harding. There were four besides my husband and I; some of them were going into the country, so they went away in time to the inn to go by the waggon.
 Q. About what time did they go away?
   Jane Harding. About five, or between four and five o'clock.
 Q. What is the name of that woman that staid ?
   Jane Harding. I cannot tell her name no farther than Betty.
 Q. How long had you washed for her?
   Jane Harding. I had not washed for her above three weeks.
 Q. How often might you have washed for her in that time?
   Jane Harding. She brought caps and aprons every day, and then took them away with her.
 Q Were you all sober ?
   Jane Harding. We were all very sober?
 Q. What cloaths had the prisoner on that night?
   Jane Harding. I cannot tell, I did not take much notice of that.
 Q. How often have you seen him since?
   Jane Harding. I never saw him before that time nor since, 'till I saw him in Bridewell.
 Q. Are you sure it was a woman that came with the prisoner?
   Jane Harding. I am sure it was.
 Q. Was it not a man?
   Jane Harding. No; it was not.
 Q. Who did you say fetched the beer?
   Jane Harding. It was a young woman, one of the company, who fetched that; I fetched none after the prisoner came in.
 Q. In what was it fetched?
   Jane Harding. In single quarts.
 To his Character.
   James Baynham. I am a turner; the prisoner was a turn-over to me, and has lived with me two years and a half; he behaved very well during that time.
 Q. Is he out of his apprenticeship?
 Baynham. No; he is not.
 Q. Has he continued with you all the time?
 Baynham. No; he left me one Monday night, and gave me no notice of it.
