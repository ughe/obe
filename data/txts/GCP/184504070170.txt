951. JAMES DILLON was again indicted for feloniously breaking
and entering the dwelling-house of James Martin, on the 26th of Feb., at
St. Mary, Islington, and stealing therein, 1 printed book, value 178.: 1
trinket-case, 12; 4 cigar-cases, 3s. ; and 1 smoking-pipe, 108.; his pro-
perty.
JAMES MARTIN. I live in a cottage in Albion-gardens, Maiden-lane,
in the parish of St. Mary, Islington. On the 26th of Feb. I left home
about half-past five o'clock the door which is in two halves was fastened
and bolted, and the windows safe--they do not open-I returned at ten
at night, found the top part of the door broken open, the bolt wrenched
completely off, and only hanging by part of the screws--I missed a book,
a trinket case, a cigar case, brass tube and pipe, which were safe when I
went out-I have seen nothing but the book since-this now produced is
it-it is the “Builder," and is my own binding.
WILLIAM Wilson. I am in the service of Mr. Gill, a pawnbroker, in
Wilmot-street, Brunswick-square. On the morning of the 27th of Feb.
the prisoner pledged this book--I knew him before, and am sure he is the
person.
THOMAS WITHERS (policeman.) I apprehended the prisoner- I found
on him this duplicate for a book.
WILLIAM WILSON re-examined. This is the duplicate I gave him.
Prisoner. This book was pawned by a young chap, at Mr. Blackburn's,
in Skinner-street, Somers-town, on the 27th of Feb., being in the trade (a
plasterer,) I thought it would be useful to me, and bought the ticket of
him and redeemed it the same day; I took it to Mr. Gill's; had I known
it was stolen I should not have kept the ticket.
CHARLES BLACKBURN. I am assistant to my father, who is a pawn-
broker. I was applied to to search our books- I looked at the dates of
26th and 27th of Feb., and found so book of this kind pledged on those
days-only one book was pledged, and that was a Bible which we have now.
Prisoner. I did not take the book or anything else out of the prose-
cutor's place; if I had stolen it I should not have pawned it where I was
well known.
