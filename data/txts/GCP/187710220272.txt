ROBERT SHARPE. I am in Messrs. Penn's service-I know Clarke's
writing, I have seen him write several times to the best of my belief this
is his writing-I have seen him write more than twice-when I have sent
for the book he has entered things in it-I have seen him making the
entries over and over again-I find here on the 18th and 19th in Clarke's
writing in reference to Porter "11cwt. 2qr. 3lb. of iron borings”-I do
not see the gate book. (The entries were here read.)
HENRY WILLIAM NEVILLE (re-examined). These are the entries in the
gate book--they correspond with those on the same date in the day book:
11 cart-loads of iron turnings 11ton lcwt. blb., and four loads of heavy
scrap 5tons 8cwt. 81b., four loads light ditto 4tons 4cwt. Iqr. 2016. on
the 17th, and on the 18th nine loads of cast iron borings 11tons 8cwt. 2qr.
10lb.; adding those together the heavy iron makes 5tons 8cwt. 816.,
and the scrap iron 4tons 10qr. 20lb., and the rest weighs 11tons 19qr.
10lb -the entries correspond with those of the day book.
Cross-examined by MR. BESLEY. I cannot account for these irregularities
in the gate book, all I know is that the slate was sent to me-the gate
book is copied by the gatekeeper-I suppose he keeps the slate the iron
turnings would not be spread all over the factory that I am aware of-I
have nothing to do with the outside department.
JOHN JAMES OVERHILL. I am a carman in my mother's service-in
April last I was employed by the man named Brown for Mr. Penn at
Evelyn Wharf-we were there I believe three days, I helped to load-two
of Mr. Porter's men were there on two days, and one man on one day-
Fennell was not there—this is my signature to my deposition. (This
stated : "I was employed there three days, I saw Fennell present when
it was loaded.") I did say that it is true, but I can't remember seeing
him-I recollect that the iron was weighed I saw Clarke there then with
a slate in his hand-we took the iron down to Evelyn Wharf and shot it
into a barge there-I recollect it because one of the horses fell into the barge
-two carts were employed—I was employed by Mr. Porter.
Cross-examined by Fennell. I saw you about the premises when the
loading was going on, but I did not see you watching the loading.
Cro88-examined by Mr. Srms. I had been to Mr. Penn's place before, but
not with an order-I was the carman—there was another, his name was
Brown-he was with me on those three occasions—there was nothing to
strike my attention as to what was going on the detective asked me at
the Greenwich Police-court whether I saw anybody with a slate?
