WILLIAM BROWN. I am a bookseller, and live in Russell-court, Drury-lane. I know the prisoner well—I was at his shop when Holland came there; and by the conduct of Mr. Jolly, I should day, that Holland was a person whom he had not before known, as he seemed to speak of him as a person not likely to buy—Holland came in, and looked about at the books in the shop, and Mr. Jolly kept indicating to me, that he was not a person who would buy, he thought—Holland said, "Have you such a book on botany?" and Jolly said, "I have not"—Holland then took a book from his pocket and said, "Is this of any use to you?"—Jolly looked at it, and said, "What is the price?"—he said 7 s .—I think he said, "Is it your own property?"—hr said, "Certainly"—he took the money from his pocket and gave it him, and I left the shop—I have frequently bought books in my shop—If I had a medical book to sell, I should take it to a medical bookseller; and if I had a law-book, I should take it to a law-bookseller—I have known the prisoner twelve months—he has been a very honest, sober, industrious character.
