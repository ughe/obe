our station, I thought I had done something wrong as regards the signalling.
I immediately sent for Mr. Chapman, the station-master, to tell him of what I
had done. I did not at that moment know of the accident. He came down
on the platform from his house, after half of the ballast-train had passed the
station; he came to me, and I think I told him what had taken place as to
my signalling. All would have gone well if I had been left alone, and not
have received the telegraph message, which seemed wholly to confuse me. I
touk my eyes off the signals for a moment, and believed at the time I gave
the signal line clear' that it was the right thing for me to do."
