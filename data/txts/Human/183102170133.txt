662. ANN PAYNE was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of February , 7 lbs. weight of pork, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Zachariah Richard Catchpole.
 ZACHARIAH RICHARD CATCHPOLE. I am a  cheesemonger, and live in High-street, Shadwell. On the morning of the 17th of February I saw the prisoner near my shop window - I missed a hand of pork, which I had seen five minutes before; I went after the prisoner, and took her with it under her shawl.
 Cross-examined by MR. HEATON. Q. Have you always told the same story? A. Yes; I never said I found it several yards from her - it had not been on the ground when I took it from her, but it dropped from my hand; it had been within my window - I did not hear the Magistrate refuse to commit her.
 COURT. Q. Are you sure she had it under her shawl? A. Yes, she had a child in her arms - another woman had been asking the price of some pork.
   WILLIAM SHARMAN. I am an officer. I took the prisoner, and have the pork.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded poverty, and received an excellent character.
 GUILTY. Aged 38. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Sergeant Arabin.
 663.  JOHN BARNES was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of January , 1 pair of trousers, value 2s., the goods of Hymon Levy.
 HYMON LEVY. I am a  slop-seller, in Broad-street, Ratcliff. I lost these trousers on the 27th of January from my door-post, but I was not at home.
   CHARLES LEVY. I am eleven years old. I was at home, and the trousers were at our door - a great crowd came - a man came, took down the trousers, and ran into the crowd again; I lost sight of him - I saw the prisoner at the office on Saturday, and I know he is the man who took them - I am quite sure he is the person; I ran, and called Stop thief! but he got into the crowd - I saw his face.
   SAMUEL WAKELING. I am a Police-constable. The prisoner is one of the sailors who was in a mob, in the highway - I took him at Ratcliff-cross, and found these trousers on him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. We went to go to the Admiralty - many of us were without clothes; I picked up the trousers in the crowd.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Transported for Seven Years.
 664.  WILLIAM PATTISON was indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of February , 1 watch, value 31s.; 2 seals, value 30s., and 2 keys, value 1s., the goods of Ann Stubbs , his mistress.
   ANN STUBBS. I live in Peerless-row, City-road. The prisoner was my errand-boy for two days - he went out with me about ten o'clock in the morning; I sent him back for something, and when I returned, the watch which I had left hanging up, was gone, with the seals and keys, from the back parlour.
   EDITH WARWICK. I am servant to Mrs. Stubbs. I let the prisoner in when he returned, but did not see him take the watch - he went into the back parlour to warm his hands.
   JOSEPH WILLIAM CLARK. I am servant to Mr. Fleming, a pawnbroker, of Farringdon-street. On the 3rd of February this watch and seals were pawned, but I do not know who by.
   JOHN BROWN. I took the prisoner on Friday evening, two days after the robbery, and found the duplicate on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never had the watch.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Seven Years.
 There was another indictment against the prisoner.
 665.  GEORGE NORMAN and JOSEPH PORTER were indicted for stealing, on the 16th of February , 4 bushels of malt, value 35s., the goods of Francis Nash.
 MR. HEATON conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN ROVE. I am clerk to Francis Nash, of Pickle Herring-wharf, Tooley-street. Norman was his carter, and on the 16th of February I directed him to load forty sacks of malt to go to Mr. West's - I saw them loaded and counted them in the waggon; I saw Norman leave the premises with them - Porter was not with him then: thirty-nine of the sacks were marked, "J. Collins," and one" F. Collins."
   JOHN AYRES. I am occasionally employed by Mr. West. On the 16th of February I was at his brewery in John-street; the prisoners came with a waggon, and said they had brought twenty quarters of malt - they left twelve sacks there; Porter brought them all in - I saw him count them, and counted them myself; the empty sacks were put on the waggon, but not tied.
   THOMAS YATES. I am servant to Mr. West, at his brewery, Turner-square, Hoxton. The prisoners came there with a waggon, and said they had brought fourteen quarters of malt - I attended to the delivery; after Porter had brought in six quarters I saw that he had an empty sack doubled up on his shoulder, and a full one on it - when he had shot that I took the empty one, and asked if he had brought two sacks at once - he said his mate had thrown it on his shoulder; after delivering four or five more sacks he took the empty sack, and said he would throw it into the waggon - master came up, and I told him what had occurred; he counted the empty sacks - there were twenty-eight, which was right; I should think an empty sack on his shoulder would be an hindrance to him.
 JAMES WILLIAM WEST. I was at Turner-square - I did not measure what was delivered.
     NOT GUILTY.
 666.  RICHARD MOXAM was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of October , 84 dozens of fire-works, called crackers, value 28s., the goods of Charles Cockett.
 CHARLES COCKETT. I am a  bedstead-maker- my wife makes fire-works, at No. 10, Willow-gardens, but we do not live there - I have known the prisoner ten years, and employed him; I lost these fire-works - he left, and I had him taken.
   GEORGE KEMP. I am a Policeman. I could not find the prisoner till the 14th of February - he had absconded; I got these fire-works from Shoreditch workhouse, and some from Gloucester-court.
   FRANCIS DAVIS. I am cook at Shoreditch workhouse.
