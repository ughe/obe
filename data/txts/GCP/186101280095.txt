HENRY NAYLOR (Policeman, M 118). On Monday, 7th January, from in-
formation I received, I went to Paradise-street, Rotherhithe, at 5 or 6
d'clock, and saw a van with Jelf, Percival, and Preece in it, going on towards
London-I asked Jelf what was the matter-he said, “Nothing"-I said,
"I received information at the station that some men had been stealing
copper out of the waggon "-he said, "No, there is no copper gone out of
the waggon "-Preece came up at that time, round to the back of the
wagon-he heard what passed, and said, "No, there cannot be any gone,
for I was at my horses' heads all the time”-I returned to the station, and
shortly afterwards Gaylor came in-be and I went together to McGregor's
premises-he rang at the bell three or four times-MoGregor opened the
little wicket-gate-Gaylor asked him if he had any metal-he said, "No,"
he bad had no metal in that day-Gaylor said, “I know better, for I have
seen one or two lots come in," and asked him where he kept his metal-he
said, “Upstairs"-he got a candle and went upstairs, and in a little room
on the right, on the first-floor, we found a quantity of metal–Gaylor asked
him how he came into possession of it-he told bis daughter to bring the
invoices, which she did, and I saw one dated November 14—Gaylor said,
"That cannot be it, for this metal is all over ice and frost "-I believe this
to be the invoice-Gaylor said, “I shall take charge of this metal"-McGregor
said that his man had been washing the metal ---Gaylor said that he must
trouble bim to be at the police-court-ye brought away the metal, which
was all over ice, and rime, and frost, from the top of some more that which
was underneath was quite dry-we could not get in at the wicket-gate with-
out ringing the bell; there is no handle to turn to open it.
Cross-examined by MR. ROWDEN. Q. What were the exact words you
used to Jelf when you saw the wagon come up! A. I said, "What is the
matter ?"-I asked him if there had not been some men stealing some metal
out of his van–I did not say "Have you lost anything out of your
wagon ?"
Cross-examined by Mr. H. GIFFARD. . Did you see Percival when you
came up? A. Yes; he was about four yards behind Jelf, when I first saw
bim-when vessels sheathing is being unloaded, there are frequently clusters
of persons loitering in groups round those parts of the docks where
sheathing is being unshipped ; but I believe there is no difficulty in keeping
them off.
