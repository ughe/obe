EDWARD SAMUEL VAUGHAN. I am a tobaeconist of Rendezvous Street,
Folkestone-in May last, in consequence of an advertisement which I saw, I
applied to Buer by letter for a loan of 3001., and in reply received this letter,
requesting a personal interview-I attended there accordingly and saw Kelly
-- he went to a desk and picked up & letter, which I recognised as mine !
said I had called to see Mr. Buer, and he said Mr. Buer had been obliged to
go into the country, and was unable to see me according to appointment, and
would be back in the course of the evening-I said I could not stay in
London later than the afternoon-he said no doubt things might be arranged,
and the fee would be two guineas-I said I wanted a letter from Mr. Buer
in answer to mine to know whether business might be done, as it would be
useless to run me into any expense unless they entertained the security I
offered--I returned to Folkestone, and the next morning I received this
letter. (Read: “19, London Bridge Station Approach, etc., 21st May,
1879. Sir,--I have no doubt that business can be done, and upon your
forwarding P. O. order for 428., I will pay the matter immediate attention.
Waiting your reply, I am yours faithfully, H. W. Buer.") My wife then
wrote this letter enclosing the money. (Read: “1, Rendezvous Street,
Folkestone, 22nd May, 1879. Dear Sirs, I enclose you P. O. order for
21. 28., and trust you will kindly attend to my application by return, as I
must send an answer to the trustee.") I was in liquidation, which I had ex-
plained in my first letter, and I was endeavoring to obtain a loan to offer a
dividend -I afterwards receivod this letter. (This stated that it would be
necessary for a representative to wait upon the toitness, and us ho resided a
considerable distance from the office, 21. 188. 3d. must be forwarded for
travelling expensos.) I wrote back.declining any further business with them,
and demanded the 21. 28. back, which I never received ; nor did I have the
loan.
Cross-examined by Mr. Wright. I paid the 21. 28. as the necessary
inquiry fee--my furniture was in the possession of my trustee if the
arrangement for the security was satisfactory to the defendants I was willing
to introduce the trustee to them they did not tell me that without seeing
the trustee nothing could be concluded--the 21. 28. was quite enough to go
to Folkestone.
Cross-examined by Mr. JARVIS. I did not represent to Kelly that the
trustee was willing to be surety for me—the security I offered was a bill of
sale and possession of my lease as collateral security-the trustee was willing
to receive a dividend under the circumstances--there was no misrepresentation
on my part at all.
