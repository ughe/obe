There was a man with a whip in his hand, and a pair of boots on, said, They are gone into Smithfield after you. I rode into Smithfield as hard as I could, and came back again. There were two or three men said, they believed I had lit of a parcel of sharpers, and said they were gone the other way. There were several people came out and said it was a house of bad fame, and that bad people used it, but they said they would endeavour to remedy me. They told me to go my Lord Mayor, and acquaint him about it. (Since that the man of the house has left it.) I have been after the prisoner several times, but never could find him. I happened to be at Blackheath fair, and there I saw the prisoner standing close by me, dressed very grand, with ruffles on. I took hold of him, and charged him. The people said, I was going to rob a gentleman. I said, If I do wrong I must suffer; but I will tell no body till I come to a justice of peace. I got him by the collar, when two men came up and offered to strike at me with sticks, upon which the prisoner got from me, and ran in at the Green-Man. I ran in, and caught him by the collar again. Some people were for me, and some against me; but with assistance I took him before Justice Russel. The man that rescued him walked by me several times. When the prisoner owned before the justice, that he parted with my watch, and had had my money, one of the gentlemen that seemed to take my part, and the officer, were sent in pursuit of the men that cleared him from me, but they could not find them. As Mr. Nightingale's servant had found false money upon his man, I desired the justice to give me leave to search him. I pulled off his shoes, but found nothing upon him, but a pocket-book. Then the justice told me, as the fact was done in the city, I must take him before my Lord Mayor. I sent my horse and cart home, and brought the prisoner up by water. I brought him to this Session-house, the day the Sessions ended, the Sessions before last; he was committed to the Compter.
 Q. from the Prisoner. Did you not ask me to play?
 Prosecutor. No, I did not.
 Prisoner's Defence.
 As I was sitting in the room, there came in the prosecutor and another man, they asked for a person; the landlord said he had not been there yet, but he expected him. I pulled out a guinea to pay my reckoning. The landlord returned it, and said it was too light. I pulled out seven or eight, and said he might take which he liked. These men were for playing a game at whist; the prosecutor said he did not mind three pence; he went out and called for brandy and water, and a pack of cards. The landlord said he allowed no cards: then it was agreed to toss up. The prosecutor said, That was all one, the landlord would hear us, and come in and prevent us. He was for hiding under his hat. They were to guess the best two in three. I won six-pence of the prosecutor, and when I went out at the door, he came after me and said, The gentleman has got a good ten or twenty pounds. I said, I never would play for ten or twenty pounds. Said he, Do not be afraid, you are winning. I said, What shall I do? I shall be ruined for ever. Said he, Do not be afraid, if you lose, I will make it up to you. Then I said, If you will be as good as your word, I will. He did not play with me at all, the other man and I played for seven guineas.
 Q. from the prisoner. Whether you did not come out of the room to persuade me to play?
 Prosecutor. I never went out of the room till I had lost all my money.
 Prisoner. His partner struck me.
 Prosecutor. My partner was quite complaisant with him.
 Prisoner. They both talked together, as if they had been acquainted seven years.
 Prosecutor. I never saw the man before in my life.
 Prisoner. I was just come out of the country: I never saw one of them before. I lived with my mother. She gave me twenty-nine guineas, when I came up from her.
 Prosecutor. I saw him shake the bag, a little money came out, not all. I imagine the false money was fastened in.
     Guilty. Imprisoned.
