SAMUEL JOHN THOMAS. I am a surgeon, in the London-road. I was seat for, and first visited the deceased on 21st Sept.—he complained of great pain in his head and behind his right ear—I examined it, but perceived nothing, and considered it was a disorder within the head that caused him to complain of pain there—there was no discharge—I went again on the Sunday follow ing, and he then complained of having received a blow—the aunt told me first, and the boy himself afterwards—in consequence of that I examined the parts behind the ear, and on shaving the hair, found a small tumour on the projecting part of the bone—feeling there was matter or some fluid there, I made an incision, and there flowed a small quantity of pus , or matter, very foetid—I saw him daily after that—on the Tuesday following, not considering the incision free enough, I made a larger one, and a small quantity more matter escaped, still very foetid—he gradually got worse—I attended him till he died, which was about twelve, or a little after twelve, on the night of 6th Oct., so 1 was informed—I afterwards made a post mortem  examination—on dividing the scalp, I found that portion situated over where the abscess was, in a gangrenous condition, and the pericranium was separated from the skull—it did not adhere as it ought to do—that was precisely over the tumour—the skull beneath the bone, beneath the section of the tumour, was in a state of necrosis  or mortification—on examining the inner portion of the skull more particularly, which contains the passage to the internal ear, it was in a very much more advanced state of mortification—the dura mater  had a large patch of ulceration upon it immediately beneath the affected portion of the bone and scalp, and on removing the dura mater  I found a portion of the bone in a slightly inflamed state—there was a small quantity of reddish white matter on the dura mater , not like that which had escaped from the tumour—that was evidence of decomposition, arising from extended inflammation or fever—in my opinion death was caused by irritative fever, the result of this inflamma tion and destruction of the parts—there was a small quantity of fluid in the ventricles of the brain, rather more than
