 1651.  THOMAS WHITTARD was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Ann Nichols , on the 26th of May , and stealing 1 shirt, value 15d., her property.
   ANN NICHOLS. I live in Goldsmith's-row, in the parish of Shoreditch. On Sunday morning (I cannot say whether it was one or two Sundays before Whitsuntide) I missed this shirt, which I had put into a box on Thursday morning - the prisoner lives next door to me; he was in custody early in June at his mother's house, and I heard him say, voluntarily, that he had pawned the shirt at Mr. Harris', Hackney-road - he said he got in at my back window; I cannot say whether that was open or shut.
   JOSEPH HEWETSON. I am shopman to Mr. Harris, a pawnbroker, Hackney-road. This shirt was pawned on Saturday, the 26th of May; I believe the prisoner to be the boy.(Property produced and sworn to.) GUILTY of stealing only. Aged 12.
 1652.  THOMAS WHITTARD was again indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Ann Nicholls , on the 2nd of June , and stealing 1 gown, value 5s., and 1 pinafore, value 1d., her property.
   LUCY NICHOLS. I live with Ann Nichols, my mother. I put this gown on a basket one day between ten and eleven o'clock, and missed it between nine and ten at night- I had brought it from the pawnbrokers.
   THOMAS EAGLES. I apprehended the prisoner on Sunday morning, the 3rd of June, and told him it was for stealing something from Mrs. Nichols, his next door neighbour; he said, voluntarily, that he had pawned the gown at Miller's, in Kingsland-road, for 1s., and that he got into the house, by the back window - I examined the house; the casement seems as if it had been left open, and there is nothing to prevent a person getting in.
   EDMUND SUTTON. I am servant to Mr. Miller, of Kingsland-road; I have a gown, pawned by the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY of stealing only. Aged 12. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.

 1654.  MARTHA KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of July , 2 gowns, value 30s.; 1 shawl, value 1l., and 1 pencil-case, value 1s., the goods of Harriet Hawkes.
   HARRIET HAWKES. I am servant at the British and Foreign Club-house, Piccadilly. On the 2nd of July I went out, about half-past three o'clock, and left these articles in my bed-room - I was sent for at half-past five, and went to Queen-square.
   EMMA BUNCE. I live in the same house; my husband is a groom. On the 2nd of July I was in the kitchen, and saw the prisoner go up the area-steps - I followed and overtook her, about two hundred yards off, with a bundle - I asked her to return with me, to No. 105, Piccadilly - she said she was going a little further, and would return bye-and-bye- I said I wished her to return immediately; she said she could not think what the housekeeper wanted with her -I said she would tell her when she got there; when we got to the corner of Dover-street, she said, "Take the bundle, let me go," but did not offer to give it to me - she went and towards Hyde-park - I pulled her back, and got a Policeman, who took her with the bundle - she said she had come and there for a person named Bennet, who she washed for, who owed her some money - nobody of that name lives there - the bundle contained these articles.
   DAVID JONES. I am a Policeman. I saw the prisoner detained by Bunce, she said "Take the bundle, and let me go" - I took her into custody.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 42 - Transported for Seven Years.
 1655.  BARNETT BENJAMIN was indicted for feloniously receiving, of an evil-disposed person, 4 shirts, value 1l., the goods of James Gibbs , well knowing them to have been stolen.
   JAMES GIBBS. I live in Holywell-lane, Shoreditch. On the 16th of March my house was broken open - I lost a set of black silk handkerchiefs, four shirts, and other articles.
   FRANCES GIBBS. I am the prosecutor's wife. The four shirts were produced by the officer; I know them to be my husband's - the house was entered through the brickwall; my initials are on the shirts now.
   ALEXANDER MITCHELL. I am a surveyor of the Thames Police. On the 17th of May I searched the prisoner's house, in Playhouse-yard, Golden-lane - he keeps a clothes shop; he was not at home; I found on the shelf, in the shop, a bundle, containing some shirts, four of which Gibbs claims; I did not see the prisoner till the 22nd of May, when he came to the office; I asked if he kept the house; he said Yes, and had done so for thirty years.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     NOT GUILTY.
