   Cross-examined. Q.  I believe you married a son of Mrs. Trott? A.  No, Mr. Benjamin Trott is my husband's brother—I have known the pri soner pretty well all her life—I do not know that she was left an orphan—I knew her by being there with Mrs. Parfitt, a relation of mine, who brought her up from her infancy, and treated her as her own child—she has been with the family ever since—she and her husband were placed in this house by Mrs. Parfitt—it was part of her duty to attend to the lodgers, in consequence of which they lived rent free—they married very young—it was her own choice, and her friends were all agreeable, I believe—he was a man of a gloomy disposition, and of very retired habits—he fre quently passed my house, but we merely passed the compliments of the day, "Good morning, a fine day," and so on—we never associated fur ther—I was not in his company of late, and do not know whether that manner had increased of late—I do not know of my own knowledge of any attempt he has made to destroy himself—I have heard so.
   MR. PAYNE Q.  How long ago did you hear of any thing of the sort? A.  last week—I only heard of it then. THOMAS LEADBEATER. I am a chair-maker, and live in the Borough-road. The deceased was in my employment—he worked for me last on the Wednesday before he died, the 17th of September—he did not come to work after that—I saw him on Wednesday morning—he had been in my employ eighteen or twenty months this time—I have known him much longer, he was apprenticed to me seventeen years ago—on one or two oc casions he appeared rather dull, that was all—I did not see him after Wednesday. HARRIET JONES. I am the wife of Essex Jones, a chair-maker, and live in the Borough-road—I knew the deceased. On Wednesday afternoon the prisoner told me he was taken very ill, with a violent vomiting, after eating some apple-pudding—I sent my son to inquire after him from day to day—I saw him on Wednesday when he went to his dinner, and saw him no more till he was dead—I was present when he was taken off the floor—I saw his forehead and nose bruised—the prisoner said, in the course of the Saturday night, that she wished him to be opened to satisfy the world, as he died so suddenly—I was there till Sunday morning—I washed out a cup of castor-oil and peppermint-water—there was nothing in that—I saw no other cup in the room.
