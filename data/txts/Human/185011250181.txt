MARY SHERIDAN. As I was searching the prisoner at the Lambeth station I heard something fall from her dress—I saw a sixpence on the floor—I said, "You have dropped this sixpence from your hand"—she said, "I did not drop it from my hand; do not let them see it, I will be good to you when I go out; they do not know that I had it; they asked me whether I had any more, and it will be all the worse for me having that"—I gave the sixpence to the Inspector—he marked it in my presence, and gave it to the officer.  167. ROBERT GISSING , unlawfully having counterfeit coin in his possession.
   MESSRS. CLERK and W. J PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM HANCKE. I live in Victoria-place, Clapham-road. On 15th Oct. I saw the prisoner and another lad at the bottom of the Lansdowne-road; they appeared in conversation—I saw the other boy in about five minutes come to the Landsdowne Arms for a quartern of gin—he tendered what appeared to be a shilling—the landlady refused it, and said it was bad—she took it in her hand and put it in the detector—the boy said he took it of a woman at the corner, with a black bonnet and a blue shawl—the potman and the boy went out to look for that woman, and I went with them—I saw the prisoner on the opposite side—I followed him till I lost sight of him at some new buildings—I turned up Larkhall-lane and over-took him—he came out from some building—I went and told him he must come with me to the Lansdowne Arms; he wanted to know what for—I told him I would tell him when I got him there—this was about twenty minutes before three o'clock—I do not know what became of the other boy—I believe the potman brought him back with the policeman.
   Prisoner. Q.  How long was I standing with the lad? A.  You were standing talking with him on the right-hand side—the man who sweeps the crossing was there; I did not notice anybody else there—when you went alone you were trying to conceal yourself. SAMUEL SIMPSON. On the afternoon of 15th Oct.
