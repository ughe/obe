158. CHARLES NORRIS, stealing 48 knives, value 41.; the goods
of Frederick Barnes.
WILLIAM ALLUNT Law. I am assistant to Mr. Frederick Barnes; he is
an ironmonger. On 21st Dec., about 2 o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the
prisoner at the counter, purchasing some percussion caps- I was not serving
him, I was about sixteen feet from him-while I was away from the counter,
I saw him put some parcels of knives into his pocket-they were in parcels,
on an iron chest, behind him I went to the invoice to see how many were
missing- I went to the prisoner, and told him he had some of our knives-
he took one parcel out of his pocket, put it on the counter, and said, "Here
are your knives -he then ran off-I followed him, and as he ran be dropped
one parcel in the road—the policeman took him-I took the parcel which he
dropped in the road to the station-I did not lose sight of him.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Had you known him before?
A. Yes, I had seen him in the shop about a week before, and I believe he
purchased a gun about twelve months before-he follows fairs, and keeps a
target for persons to shoot at the value of the knives was about 41. 11s.-
this is the parcel I picked up in the road-it is the property of my master-
this is worth about 365.—there was one more parcel found in his pocket.
MR. BALLANTINE here stated that he could not resist the evidence. U

159. JAMES BROOKS, burglariously breaking into the dwelling house
of Sir Charles Merrick Burrell, Bart., and stealing 1 coat, value 80s.; bis
property : and 5 whips, value 10s. ; the goods of Daniel Davies.
EDWARD OLIYER ( policeman, A 337). On 18th Dec., at twenty minutes
before 6 o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner come out of Richmond-
mews into Parliament-street-he had this coachman's box coat with him, and
these five whips-he came towards me; and asked me what time it was-I
told him it wanted twenty minutes to 6 o'clock-he said, "I am afraid
I shall be too late for the rail," and he hastened off-I went into the Mews,
and tried the doors- I found the coach house door of Sir Charles Merriek
Burrell open-I had seen it about a quarter of an hour previous—it had the
padlock on then, and it was locked-I had had the padlock in my hand-
when I saw the door open the padlock was gone-I called the coachman, and
after speaking to him I went and overtook the prisoner at Charing-cross,
walking very fast-I asked him where he got the coat and wbips--he said
he came from over the water, and they belonged to his master-I told him
he must come back with me-he let the coat drop-I told him not to drop it
-he threw it down, and said, “B r the coat ; carry it yourself"-I took
him to the station, and found on him a black linen bag, a knife, a small
looking glass, and 14d.-he threw the whips down, and I took them up.
Prisoner. I walked civilly with you; but you held my collar so tightly I
could scarcely breathe ; then I threw them down. Witness. I had hold of
your collar, not your handkerchief.
