MR. CARTEEN called
WILLIAM LEONARD (police-constable D 50.) I was on duty on the
25th of June, in York-place, Baker-street, near the Globe tavern--I saw
the prisoner there on a four-wheeled cab, and the prosecutrix with him,
sitting by his side on the box, I am sure of that the prisoner got down,
went and knocked at the Globe door, and asked for a quartern of gin--he
spoke to me, and said, “I am only going to get a quarter of gin"-I said,
"A cab is not allowed to be here, make haste" he said, “I am afraid my
horse will bolt," and I stood by his horse's head while he fetched the gin-
the prosecutrix drank a glass on the box, and I saw her give him two
halfpence and a penny piece, and the prisoner said, “Here is two half-
pence and a penny" -- the prosecutrix did not speak, so I could not tell in
what state she was-she wanted to have another quartern of gin-I said
I would not allow it, I should take the number of the cab, which I did,
and she said to the cab-man, "If we have another quartern of gin we will
make it all right”--they drove away into the New-road, towards the York.
shire Stingo, she sitting by his side on the box.
COURT. Q. You did not see her get inside at all? A. She did not
while in my sight-I am positive she is the same woman I did not see ber
off the box-Ross is in my division-I have known him about two years
