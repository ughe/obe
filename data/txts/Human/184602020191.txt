JAMES BRUTON. On the 27th of Jan. I was reading in the coffee-room, about half-past ten o'clock—I was knocked for from the room, by Mrs. Bruton—in consequence of what she said, I went to Joiner-street immedi ately I had arranged my dress—it was then within a minute or two of half-past ten o'clock—I went to No. 30, Joiner-street—I went into the kitchen, and saw the prisoner by the copper-fire—I said to her, "Ellen, have you seen my watch?"—she said, "No"—I went home late the next day, and my watch was lying on the table in the house where I live. ANN BRUTON re-examined.  This is the watch—it was brought back on the Wednesday night at past eleven o'clock, by Mordy—it was not going then—it was going when it went away. JAMES BRUTON re-examined.  This is the watch—my name is engraved on the back of it—I saw it in the bedroom before I quitted it to go into the coffee-room—I am not aware that any injury had happened to it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How many minutes elapsed before you got to Joiner-street? A.  I should say I was not two minutes going from one house to the other—I ran very fast—in three minutes you could walk the distance easily—the prisoner appeared to me as if she was stooping down—I did not accuse her of taking the watch—I did not believe it—I said if she would tell me the honest truth, I would be merciful to her—she said she would confess if she was guilty—I told her more than once that I would be merciful, Which I should have been.
   COURT . Q.  Did she come back to your house? A.  I brought her back with me from Joiner-street—she never left my house again, and had no op portunity of taking the watch to Joiner-street, except she took it before quitted the house. WILLIAM MORDY. I live at No. 30, Joiner-street. I remember seeing some woman in the kitchen of that house, on Tuesday, the 27th of Jan.—I will not be positive about the time—I think it must be when I came home to breakfast—my regular time for coming home is half-past eight—some times it is nine—I had not breakfasted that morning—it was when I came in and opened the door—I do not think it was as late as ten—I saw the woman where the copper is—on the Wednesday after I left my work, my mistress opened the door and told me something—in consequence of what I heard, I went to the copper in the kitchen, and found the watch up the chimney flue—there is a flue from the kitchen chimney to the other—somebody had put it up there, I suppose—it was placed in the interme diate passage, on a brick that lies flat between the copper flue and the chimney, about eighteen inches from the hob of the kitchen chimney—I had been searching everything in the kitchen—I then placed the light on the hob, looked up the chimney, and saw a dirty rag—I put my arm up, pulled it down, and found the watch in it—I took it to Mrs. Bruton.
   Cross-examined. Q.  I think you say it was about nine o'clock you saw this woman in the kitchen? A.  I would not be positive to a few minutes—I am certain it was before ten o'clock—I found the watch on the Wednesday evening, in the kitchen where I had seen the woman. JAMES PERRY( police-sergeant L  20.) I took the prisoner.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You have known her from infancy? A.  Yes, and never knew any charge against her.
   COURT to ANN BRUTON . Q.  Did any person leave your house to go to Joiner-street, between the time you missed the watch, and the evening of the next day? A.  No—I did not send any one there, and no one quitted the house for the purpose of going there. NOT GUILTY.
