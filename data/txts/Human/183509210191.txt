SOLOMON BENNETT re-examined.  I produced nobody but Grout—there were four men before the Magistrate—they gave evidence, but the fouth man was never in custody. HENRY BANKS. He was produced by you as a prisoner.
   Witness.  No such thing; Hadland came as a witness for Grout—there might have been a fourth man before the Magistrate, but I did not produce him—I believe it was the prisoners produced the fourth man—I think his name was Chetham, but I had never seen him—the Magistrate did not detain him.
   MR. MAHON . Q.  Did you hear the Magistrate say they were all guilty alike? A.  Yes; the three prisoners, and the man not here—Hadland came as a witness, and Showell came afterwards, voluntarily—Showell and Grout have been out on bali ever since. THOMAS FORTUNE. I have known the prisoners four or five years—Showell and Grout's parents keep cows, and they are cow-keepers—I have seen them about the Elephant and Castle buying horses, and any thing of that sort—old cart-horses come by in waggons.
   COURT . Q.  Do you mean the Elephant and Castle, where coaches stop? A.  Yes; I have seen horses sold at Davis's door, who keeps a knacker-shop in the neighbourhood—Showell's father is a cow-keeper at Walworth—Hadland's brother keeps cows—I knew where to find Showell and Grout—It is down the first street on the left hand, in the Kent-road, close against the Elephant and Castle—I have seen them buy a horse, and sell it at Smithfield—I do not know any other way that they get their living—I am at Smithfield every day of my life, and have seen Showell there buying a horse—he produced a paper to me and my master. HENRY BANKS. I had the direction in my room three days, to ascer tain if I could find the man—Taylor the policeman has got it—he is not here.
   Prisoner Showell . I gave the paper to Taylor—I have bought two and three horses a day, and sold them at Smithfield—a man may often buy three horses a day, without a halfpenny in his pocket, if he sells one as fast as he buys it—I have used every exertion to find the man. SOLOMON BENNETT. They came to me between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning. I took the policeman to the Elephant and Castle, to take Grout—I said to Grout, "You know who you bought the pony of"—he said "Yes," and took me to Showell's house—Grisby's is about five or six hundred yards from my house—I brought it at very near eleven o'clock—It could not be later. MARIA GRISBY. The pony was brought to my husband's about half-past ten or eleven o'clock. BENJAMIN GRISBY.
