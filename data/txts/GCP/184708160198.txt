CARROLL, Mayor.
WILLIAM EDWARD Drew. I come from the office of the Clerk of the
Peace for Middlesex-I produce an indictment, presented at the adjourned
Session at Westminster, on the 16th of July—the names on the back of the
bill are " John Pheasant, of Bow.common, in the county of Middlesex,
labourer," and "John Wormington"-I attended at that Session-I cannot
tell whether Humphreys paid the fees on that occasion-I have no doubt
of having seen him there-a Session seldom passes without my seeing him
- I do not know Pheasant-the bill is against John Wooldridge, Frederick
Annesley, Rose Joseph, Mary Smith, and Sarah Gale-there are two counts:
one is for keeping a common bawdy-house-warrants have not been issued
upon that, I believe some application was made to the Court-the course
pursued by us is that the prosecutor shall enter into recognizance in 1001,
before the warrant is allowed to go out of the office, and he has not entered
into that recognizance.
MARY LANE. I am the daughter of John Lane, and live at No. 6,
Lazenbury-court. On the 24th of April, between eight and nine o'clock in
the evening, Humphreys called at our house and asked if Mr. or Mrs. Lane
were at home--they were not, and I saw him-he said he had called to in-
form my father and mother that they were under indictment, and that if we
called on him at Mr. Delamere's office, 43, Lincoln's Inn-fields, we should
receive every information, and that we had nothing to fear-he said we were
under indictment with two other houses of Twig's and Wilson's, and that he
had been there and left his paper-I asked why he did not bring one to me,
for I could not recollect all he had got to say, and I asked if he would be so
kind as to put it down in writing-he said he would, and while I was getting
him the pen, ink, and paper, I said, " I suppose you are aware that our
house is not in the same parish;" I thought it was a parish concern-he
turned round, looked at me, and told me not to ask him any more d-d
questions ; if I wanted further information I was to go to Mr. Delamere's
office between eleven and twelve o'clock, or else go to the other houses and
get information - I asked by whom we were indicted, and for what; and said
we did not keep a disorderly house, or anything of the kind-he said it was
Mr. Pheasant, of Bow, who had indicted us, but he could not tell us for
what.
Humphreys. Q. Will you swear that your's is not a disorderly house of
the very worst description, where there are children of eleven or twelve years
of age ? 4. I will it is in the parish of St. Paul, Covent-garden-I am not
to answer whether it is a common brothel-it is not an open house - we
never let children in, neither do we keep any females.
COURT. Q. Did you ever see Pheasant at your house? 4. Never, and
I know every creature that enters it.
SARAH WILSON. I am the wife of John Wilson, and live at 29, Rose-
street, Long-acre. On Saturday evening, the 24th of April, Humphreys
called and left this paper with me," W. H. Humphreys, at Mr. Delamere's,
43, Lincoln's Inn-fields"-- he said the house was under indictment, but if my
husband would call on him on Monday morning, from eleven till twelve
o'clock, things might be amicably arranged-he then said, "Good night," and
that was all--my husband does not keep any house jointly with Twig or
Lane-he has nothing at all to do with it-they live at different places.
COURT. Q. Do you know them? A. Only as neighbours-I am not
aware of any ground for considering our house a brothel-he wished to extort
money, and we did not think fit to give it him-ours is not a house that we
were apprehensive of having indicted.
