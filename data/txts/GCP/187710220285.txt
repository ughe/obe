Walter WILLIAM MILLS. I am a farmer at Farningham--I remember,
on the evening of the 14th July, perfectly well, seeing some men on don-
keys pass my farm at eight minutes before nine I was in the garden
close to the road-it was light enough for me to see the faces of the men
--there were two, at first the younger prisoner Reynolds and his brother,
and there were five donkeys—then another man followed with three don-
keys–I cannot recognise him—they joined company together shortly
after that I was standing in the main road, just off the farm, when I
observed, about 200 yards off, a scuffle between the men, and, as I sup-
posed, the donkeys- I went the other way to The Bull, and saw no more
-while I was there Schofield came in-I examined his head, and found
a bruise as large as an orange-I went to the exact spot the next morning,
where I had seen the souffle, and there I found the prosecutor's hat
secreted in the hedge--it is between half and three-quarters of a mile
from there to The Bull.
Cross-examined by Mr. Rieton. I know the London and Maidstone
Road—there is a pathway before you come to the turning to the station
I was standing here when I saw the men pass (referring to the plan)--the
way they were going wonld lead them to the Lullington Castle Hotel
I don't know much about the donkey-drivers—I don't like them, nor
would you if you lived where I do- I crossed before the orchard, and
crossed the heath and out on to the road, and I saw the senffle when I was
on the brow of the hill-it was eleven minutes past nine by my watch when
I started to go to The Bull—I don't know whether the men came into
The Bull—I know it has been proved that they turned round to the right
towards the beerhouse called The Alma-I know The Alma perfectly well
-it was a mizzling night-not darkish when I saw the prisoners, as I
thought, dividing their donkeys, but, as it turned out, they were knocking
a man about there is a house called Beenham's Farm, a little lower down,
and a public-house called The Woodman a little lower down on the other
side of the way-the road under your thumb would be the nearest way
to Swanley Junction (referring to the plan)—there is another road-I
know nothing of Pateman-there is a house-cart there belonging to Pate-
man-I did not hear of the row between the excursionists and the donkey-
drivers, because I was in London.
CHARLES FREAD (Superintendent of the Kentish Constabulary). On Sunday
morning, the 15th July, the prosecutor was brought to me by Mr. Hutton
- his face bore various black marks on it, and there were several bumps
about his head he made a complaint.
Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. He did not tell me who the man was
- he said he had seen a man on Dartford Heath who very much resembled
him-he did not say he could swear to him-I did not take down what he
said he did not tell me the man's name I believe Hutton mentioned a
man's name whom they stated they passed on the heath-I know Gordon
well—I did not take him up—the two prisoners were taken up on the
following day, the Monday--if the prosecutor had positively identified
Gordon I should have thought it my duty to have him arrested -he told me
the circumstances under which he met him, and that he very much resem-
bled the man-I ultimately gave directions to a constable and sergeant
to investigate the whole matter, and left it to them.
