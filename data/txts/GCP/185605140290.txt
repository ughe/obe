CHARLOTTE KING re-examined. I am in the hospital now, I was not when
this happened--this ( produced) is my certificate.
ROBERT BARR (policeman, N 207). About 12 o'clock on this night I was
in the Caledonian-road, and saw Wren and the prosecutrix outside Smith's
coffee house talking-I saw them again about a quarter past 12 o'clock.
Hamilton was then there, and they all went into the coffee shop-about &
quarter past 1 o'clock I was called to take Raymond into custody; I had
seen him by the coffee shop about half past 12 o'clock, and about a quarter
of an hour afterwards saw him cross the road from the coffee shop when I
took him he was standing on the opposite side, about forty yards from the
coffee shop- they both denied the charge.
Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON. Q. How long was it from the time you
first saw these women to the time you apprehended Raymond ? A. About
an hour and a half-there was a man with them when I saw them first, at a
quarter past 12 o'clock, and there might be two or three others, but I cannot
be certain whether there were two or three others besides Wren-I did not
see Raymond at that time they were outside the coffee shop then-there were
two others, I believe—when I saw Wren the first time, he and the prose-
cutrix were by themselves the second time I saw Wren, the prosecutrix,
Hamilton, and, I think, two men, I cannot be certain whether there were
one or two-Wren was one-they were strangers to me-Raymond was not
there—I do not know when the assault was committed—I cannot tell you
whether it was before or after she was knocked down that I am speaking of
- they were talking together, and I walked on-my beat is by there-I
went on as far as North-street, which is about 150 yards- I went up the
Caledonian-road, but at the distance I went did not command a view of
the coffee house-I should not have heard a scream, because there are
80 many carriages—I go down as far as King's-cross- I was at the end of
the Caledonian-road by King's-cross when King spoke to me.
Cross-examined by MR. MAC ENTEER. Q. When you accused Wren, what
did he say? A. He denied all knowledge of it_ the prosecutrix said that
she had told Mrs. Smith-when she gave him ir charge Mr. Smith was
present, but I do not know whether Mrs. Smith was-Wren has been long
at the coffee house-I saw Raymond at half past 12 o'clock that evening,
and again at a quarter to 1 o'clock, standing by himself just by the door
in the Caledonian-road-no complaint was made at that time, I have often
seen him standing there, but not lately.
COURT to CHARLOTTE KING. Q. Did you leave the door to find the young
woman? A. No, I had not time to do so—as soon as I got to the door I
was robbed-I did not repeat this story about Wren in the presence of
Mrs. Smith-I was talking to Wren in the shop, and Mrs. Smith was in
the barshe did not hear what I said that I know of, she was in the
parlour-my head was very sore, but there was no bruise-1 am not in the
hospital in consequence of any hurt that I received on that night.
COURT to ROBERT BARR, Q. Did King appear hurt, did she complain?
A. She did not complain of being hurt at all she made the statement that
she had been knocked down-(Wren's statement before the Magistrate was
here read as follows: «The girl did send me to ask the prosecutrix to come
out and see her, but when we went to the door the girl was not there: the
prosecutrix ran down the road after her.")
