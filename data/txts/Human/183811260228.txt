   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . A.  Was the prisoner a stranger to you? A.  No, he has been for some years the Linfield carrier—I drive for Mr. Coombes, whose van is an opposition van to the prisoner's—he drives for his father—we never had any words before—his brother broke his leg in my master's employ, and they were angry with me because I took his place—I heard him swearing something about his brother before he struck me—I did not, on that, come out of my van, and ask what he meant by it—I will swear that—he called me out, and I asked what he wanted—he spat in my face, and began to call me all manner of names—I did not call him a s—y-nosed b—or a d—rogue, or any thing of the kind—I did not say any thing about his having been in gaol, or about his having been on one side of the wall, and I would try to get him on the other—I never lifted my hand against him—I applied to the Magistrate at Linfield for a warrant, and he gave it to me, and I got one from Union-hall—Mr. Bent did not refuse to give me one—the prisoner came down to Linfield with his wagon after this—I did not see him at the Quarter Ses sions—I understood he was there—I went there, and they said it would be tried here—I never said I did not believe he took my watch—I said one of them must have taken it, I could not say which—I was quite sober.
   Q.  Did you ever say to any person that you never accused him of stealing your watch, but you were determined, by some means or other, to keep him off the road, the rest of his family would be of no use? A.  No, never—I never said so to Peacock, a broker in the Blackfriars-road—I will swear that—Peacock came with Ellis, and wanted to make it up—I took him to be the gentleman, but he would not tell me his name—they sent for me over to a public-house—I told Peacock I could not think of making it up after being so dreadfully used—my watch was in my fob under my smock-frock—I had looked at it at the gate not three minute before—I stopped at Beckett's, and had a pint of beer among three of us—that was all I had—I did not stay there five minutes at the outside—Ellis was taking something up there—we generally call to see if there is any thing to take—he had his van there—we did not both stop there—he told my mate to stop, and said "Where is old Dick? I want to see him"—I do not know why he should spit in my face—I never had a mis  —word from him in my life—I never raised my hand against him—I cannot say how long he has been in business—I have not lived at Linfield long myself—I lived at Brighton a good while—the last service I had was at the Catherine-wheel, in the Borough—I kept flys at Brixton, and kept a beer-shop in Kingsland-road, which I left because it did not answer.
   MR. RYLAND . Q.  How long had you known Ellis? A.  Many years—I saw nobody but the three persons I have described, when I came out of the wagon, I thought one of them must have taken my watch—I am sure it did not drop—I never knew Peacock till he came with Ellis to make it up, and I refused, as I was bound over in 30 l . to appear.
