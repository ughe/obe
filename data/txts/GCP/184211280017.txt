GREGORY WILLIAM PAGE. I am servant to Mr. Kay, a chemist, in Upton-
place, Commercial-road, about two or three dozen yards from Mr. Binstead's.
On the 29th of Oct., shortly after one o'clock in the day, the prisoner came
and asked for a penny-worth of sugar of lead-I served bim-he gave me a
half-crown, which appeared to be a good one-I gave him four sixpences, and
was about to give him fivepenny-worth of halfpence change, when he asked
me if I had not two shillings instead of the four sixpences-I said, "No"-
he said, "Give me the half-crown, and I will give you a shilling"- I did so,
and he gave me the sixpences-he then gave me a bad shilling-1 saw it was
bad, but I gave him the change, without saying anything to him-I let him
go out of the shop, then called the man out of the yard to attend to the shop,
and followed him-when he got a few doors past the shop, a woman came up
to him, and walked with him, till they got a few doors past the George-he
then parted from the woman, and went into Mr. Binstead's shop-I waited
outside, and saw the policeman go in, and come out with him in custody-1
went to the station - I kept the shilling which the prisoner gave me, in my
hand, apart from all other money-I gave it to Mumford.
