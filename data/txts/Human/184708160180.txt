Yes—my brother had forced his way through the gate—I could not get through—I am sure I kept the person whose hand I found in my guard—his hand was entangled in the guard—a few minutes before I approached the gate my brother had cautioned me, and I felt my watch safe—the snatch at my pocket was made by the same person whose hand was in my guard—I felt a dash at my pocket—I was not in the crowd above half a minute—I seized both the hand and the collar of the prisoner—I heard him say I was never more mistaken, and that he was not the person—I called the policeman—he came through the gate to me—I then went to Bow-street station-house—I was surrounded on all sides—there was a greater crowd before me than there was behind me—my watch was on a swivel—the swivel was not broken—the watch must have been broken off it.  1914. WILLIAM BROWN CHATWIN was indicted for bigamy. HARRIET ANDERSON. I live in Darling-street, Bethnal-green. I was married to the prisoner at Greenwich church about fifteen months ago—the ceremony was performed in the vestry-room—I had lived with him three or four months before, and have lived with him ever since. JOSEPH LUCAS. I live in Suffolk-street, Bethnal-green. I was at Hackney church when the prisoner was married to my sister Rebecca—he lived with her after the marriage—I saw her about nine days ago.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE Q.  Was she married in her right name? A.  I do not know—I cannot answer whether Rebecca Lucas was her right name—I know her mother was not my mother, as I am told—I do not know that she was treated as an illegitimate child—she went by the name of Rebecca Lucas—I never heard her called anything else. JAMES WEST( policeman K  113.) I produce a certificate of the marriage of William Brown Chatwin, bachelor, and Rebecca Lucas, spinster, at Hack ney church, in the presence of Joseph Lucas and Mary Ann Lucas—I have compared this with the register—it is correct. JOHN RIVERS FOUNTAIN. I am parish clerk at Greenwich—I produce a certificate of marriage from the parish Church of St. Alphage—I am not clerk of that Church—persons are never married in the vestry in that Church that I know of—William Brown Chatwin and Harriet Anderson were married at St. Alphage on the 16th of June, 1846. GUILTY . Aged 30.— Confined Six Months .
