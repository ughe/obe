THOMAS TAURWAY. I am a journeyman printer, and live at Shaftesbury-
terrace, Pimlico. I was going across Buckingham-gate, and saw the prose-
cutor in a mob-he had hold of the two prisoners-he said they had robbed
him of 101.--I heard Needham say, "We have not robbed you of 101."-(that
was when the 21. was found)-I observed no one speak to the prisoners-I
came up when the policeman came up-after the mob cleared away, a person
said, "There is the money lying there"-I looked down, and saw the two
sovereigns lying near where the prosecutor had been standing—I saw it picked
up-I called the prosecutor back, and the money was given to the policeman.
Cross-examined. Q. You heard them say they had not taken it, that it
had dropped out of his purse ? A. Yes.
WILLIAM MITCHELL (police-constable A 128.) I found the prosecutor
holding the prisoners—he charged them with having robbed him of 101.-I
did not see anybody speak to the prisoners-one prisoner had five shillings,
and 6d. in copper, upon her, and the other one shilling, a penny, and a half-
penny-there was no gold.
(The prisoners received good characters.)

1675. JOHN SMITH was indicted for embezzlement.

DAVID Davis. I am a grocer, and live in Crown-street, Finsbury. I was
indebted to Mr. Manning 61. 10s. 2d.-on the 2nd of March I paid 678. 2d. to
the prisoner, deducting something for a cask the prisoner gave me this
receipt.
Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. It was through the defendant that
you became a customer of the prosecutor's? A. Yes.
ROBERT BURBIDGE. I am a grocer, and live in Whitecross-street, St. Luke's.
I was indebted to Mr. Manning in the sum of 77.--I paid it on March the
12th to the prisoner.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe you became a customer of Mr. Manning
through the introduction of the prisoner? A. Yes.
