617. CHARLES ADAMS was indicted for embezzlement.
Messrs. CLARKSON and Payne conducted the Prosecution.
ELIZABETH HARDY. I keep a nursery-ground at Edmonton-I succeeded
my father in the business. The prisoner was in my father's employ many
years-he after that continued in my employ--he was authorised to sell
trees, and receive the money for them, on my account- there was a book kept,
in which the entries of things sold, and the sums received, was made-it is
in the prisoner's handwriting-it was his duty to pay me every evening, and
to enter it in the book-here is an entry, on the 13th of Nov., to Mr. Fryer,
" three apples, three cherries, 6s; two pears, 2s. ;" 8s. in all-I received 8s. on
the faith of that entry-it 12s. had been paid, I never received the difference
-I never received any garden pots purchased of Mr. Fryer on that occasion
-I find an entry on the 28th of November, to Bartholomew Humphery, of
one standard plum, 2s.6d.; three standard apples, 38. ; one standard cherry,
18. 6d. ; making altogether 73.- I received the money upon the faith of that
entry, as so many sold, and so much received-if he received 88., I never re-
ceived the other shilling.
Cross-examined by NR. WILDE. Q. The persons send their servants, and
it was the prisoner's duty to go and take these things from the ground ? A.
It was- I have generally seen him every day, I have occasionally seen per-
sons assisting the prisoner in extracting the trees from the ground-I never
saw any money transactions with those persons-there were three other per-
sons on the premises- it was the prisoner's duty to call them to help him if
he wanted them--the prisoner was to superintend for me- I placed implicit
confidence in him-he has been living near my premises - he has a garded,
not a nursery-I am not aware that he had any of my things on his premises
-I cannot tell whether my pots were there—there have been no misunder-
standings as to the way in which bis ground was employed-I bad reason to
complain of his laxity of duty on my premises—I was not aware that be sold
any things off his ground—I have not been informed that there was a com.
mission allowed to persons who helped to get up trees- there is a gratuity al.
lowed to a gentleman's servant, but in the trade there is a greater gratuity.
MR. CLARKSON. Q. How long is it ago since you found him out in acts
of this sort ? A. About two years and a half-I then forgave him-he then
absconded, but at the intercession of his sister, I consented to take him again-
in his book a lesser number was entered, and in one instance the 6 was turned
into a 5- I never authorized a commission of 33 per cent. on the sale of fruit-
Henry FRYER, publican, Tanner's-end, Edmonton. In Nov. I bought
eight trees at the prosecutrix's-I asked the price, and the prisoner said the
same price as the others, which was 18. 6d. each-they were brought home
for me by Colton-the same day I bought three casts of pots for the pri-
soner--they came to 58. 3d. I received a bill frum Cotton, and have lost it
-it was made out in Miss Hardy's name, and amounted to 128.—I did not
buy the pots on account of Miss Hardy-I sent the prisoner the balance,
68. 9d., deducting the price 1 paid for the pots.
BARTHOLOMEW HUMPHREYS, coachmaker, Tottenham. At the latter end
of Nov. I went to Miss Hardy's, and bought one plum-tree, 2s.6d.; four
apple-trees, 1s. each, and one cherry, 18, 6d. ; 8s. in all the prisoner
trees.
