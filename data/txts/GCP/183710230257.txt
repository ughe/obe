JOSEPH BIGGS (police-constable V 26.) On Monday last I received in-
formation that something was going on wrong at a house in the Clapham-
road-I met the prisoner Mary Ann in James-street, near her mother's
house, with a parcel in her hand, I asked her what she had got there she
said, "What do you mean?"- I said, "Where did you bring that from ?"
-she said her mother gave it her-I then said we would go back to Mrs.
Bainbridge's, and see if it was all right—we went back to the house, saw
Miss Bainbridge, and asked if she gave her these things-she said, "Yes,
all but the apron "—they were little muslin things—I do not know that
any of those things are here the prisoner Mary said Miss Bainbridge had
given her a great many things I then said, “If Miss Bainbridge had
given a great many things, perhaps you have no objection to let Miss
Bainbridge go to your house, and see what she has given you"-we went
back, and found a large box-I asked for the key, and opened it and took
out a great many articles which Mr. Bainbridge said were his mother's
the stockings were in that box-I then went into a front room--there was
another box-I asked who it belonged to—the lodgers said to Mrs.
Cooper-I asked for the key, and opened it--there were silk stockings
and various things, which I took out, and Mr. Bainbridge said they were his
mother's I found these articles in the boxes, which Mary said were hers,
but the lace veil was down-stairs, on the top of a bed which was turned
up-I found a shirt or two, and some other little articles there were
some things in the coal-hole, but I believe they are not mentioned in the
Indictment—they made frequent observations that the things were all given
to them..
