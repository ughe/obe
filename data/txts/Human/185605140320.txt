   Cross-examined by MR. RIBTON . Q.  Where did you get your purse? A.  From a man that picked it up in a gentleman's garden close by—he brought it into the shop, and gave it me—I do not know him—he is not here—this piece of paper was shown to me at the station house by one of the policemen—I had told him that the half sovereign was in a piece of dirty sort of newspaper, and that it had got a small hole in it from wear—I did not tell him it was torn in a triangular shape—the bill I paid was for butter and bacon, to a young man who called for it—no one serves in the shop but myself—my husband interferes very little in the business—I did not see Ponman at all till I went to the station—nobody came into the shop after the butterman left, except the prisoners.
   MR. LILLEY . Q.  Had you your purse safe after that man left? A.  Yes; I am sure of that.
   COURT . Q.  Had you ever seen any of these boys at your shop before? A.  No, nor in the neighbourhood—none of my money was marked. THOMAS INGRAM. ( policeman, R  474). I was on duty at Sydenham, about half past 6 o'clock, on 14th April, and, in consequence of information, I apprehended William Reynolds and Gore—as I was taking them to the station, some one called out, "Policeman, mind one of these has got some thing in his hand"—I found that William Reynolds had two half crowns and three shillings in his left hand—I afterwards searched him at the station, and found 5 d . in copper, and a penny on Gore—when I took the money from Willian Reynolds, I said, "What have you there?"—I laid hold of his hand, and he dropped it into my hand—he did not say anything.
   Cross  examined. Q. Did not he put his hand into his pocket and take out the money? A.  I cannot say positively whether he did or not—Mrs. East had told me she had been robbed by three boys—I afterwards went to fetch her to the station—I asked her what money there was—she said, "A half sovereign, wrapped up in a piece of paper"—I asked her if she would know the piece of paper; she said,. "Yes"—that was all she said—she said it was a piece of old newspaper—that was all the descrip tion she gave of it—when she was at the station this piece of paper was lying on jthe desk, with several others, and she looked down, and said, "That is the piece of paper the half sovereign was wrapped up in; I have had it in my possession three or four weeks"—the other pieces were not like this, quite different—I believe all the prisoners live in one street, somewhere in Shoreditch.
