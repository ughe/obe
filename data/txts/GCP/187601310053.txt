Cross-examined. He was courting me, but we were not engaged to be
married-I did once wish to marry him I do not do anything for my living,
I look after my father's house - I used to meet the prisoner nearly
every night by appointment and walk with him-on this night the 14th, he
called for me at 7 o'clock, but I was not at home I was not engaged
mending a bedtick on that occasion, or doing anything for my father that I
recolleet, never had a pair of, soizzors tied to my waist by a string in my
life-I met the prisoner at Jeffrey's publio-house, about 10 o'clook--he
looked in at the door and was called in-I was in the public house from 8
to 10 o'clock, with Amelia Foster, drinking all the evening-she is not here
-I am prepared to say that I did not see the prisoner before 10 o'clock
that night-I met him first at the Grey Coat public-house, Mr. Jeffrey's,
where I had some ale with him, and everything was very comfortable and
pleasant-we left there and went to Douney's publio-house, in Peter Street
--the other young woman and her mother were with us--we had some
drink there, and still everything was pleasant, there was no quarrel-the
prisoner was a good deal the worse for drink-when he first came into my
company he was intoxicated-we left Douney's about 10,15, to the best of
my recollection-he did not kiss me as we came out, nor bad he his left arm
round my neck, but he had twenty minutes afterwards, when he struok me
in the side-we had been standing in the corner in the interval —I do not
recollect that we were chatting, we stood twenty minutes without speaking
-I had not fallen down before I was struok-I do not reeollect anything
after I received the wound—I do not recollect picking up his cap and
putting it on his head, and telling him that I was not hurt, but the
policeman told me that I did—I told the policeman when I recovered, at the
hospital, that I did not recollect his coming I do not recollect the policeman
coming up and shaking the prisoner, directly I received the stab--the police
man tells me that I said, “Don't lock him up, he has not hurted me," but I
have no recollection of saying it. 2012
Re-examined. I was not much the worse for liquor, but I cannot say
that I was in my right senses, as I ought to be I was in liquor, but I
knew what was going on and what I was talking about.

the prisoner standing side by side by Westminster Buildings-he had his
arm round her neck and gnve her a fearful blow on the left side with his
right hand, and she fell to the ground and said " Oh"_I caught him by the
collar, and said "You scoundrel how dare you strike a woman like that"
--she got up and said "I am not hurt, don't lock him up she put his
hat on his head and she went in doors-she was directly opposite her
father's door-the prisoner went away down Old Pye Street, saying " It is

Street-he said "Is this the man you saw strike & woman in Old Pye
