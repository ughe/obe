Cross-examined. Q. How long was she in the shop? A. A very few
minutes- I did not know her before- saw her afterwards in company with
some other women-I said I knew her by the child—she had not the child in her
arms at the time-I looked round for the child-I said I was sure she was the
woman-before that, the policeman pointed to her and said, " This is her," or
"Is this her," I am not sure which he did not say, " This is the woman"-
there were four women in the cell-he pointed to the prisoner and said, "That
is the woman;" and I said, "I knew her without."
Mr. Bopkin. Q. Did you know her without his pointing? A. Yes, whet
I went to the cell -- if I had gone alone, without the policeman, I should have
known her, of my own knowledge—she had a child when she caine to my
house-she had not one in the cell - I have no doubt about her being the
person-my house is about half a mile from the King of Prussia-it lies be-
tween there and the Coach and Horses.
ANN MIDDLEDITCH. I am the wife of James Middleditch, who keeps the

the till on some shillings, while I gave her the halfpence; I immediately per.

her it was bad-she said I did not know a bad sixpence-Benton, the police
man, was there I gave him the sixpence-he laid his hand on her shoulder,
and said she was the person he wanted--the sixpence was bent.
