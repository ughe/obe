PHILIP HUTTON. On the 26th of August I was clerk to Mr. Wright-I
had been in his service about a week. I received directions from him for two
reams of paper, one to go to Adland's, and one to Clark and Bedford's-
prisoner was in the room-Mr. Wright said they were to be taken home by the
porter-after Mr. Wright left, the prisoner tied up the paper, and said it was
to go home-I said there were no porters, he must wait til the porters came
in-he said the paper was wanted that afternoon, and he would take it-I
then told him he had better not; Mr. Wright had desired the porter to take
it, and he would not like him to take it-he made no answer, but took the
paper,
Prisoner. Q. Did I not say, I did not think Mr. Wright intended me to
be commission traveller and porter? A. Yes-I did not say that I did not
perfectly understand Mr. Wright, but I thought he said you were to take it
you went and inquired, but I knew the porters were not at home you did not
ask the boy to take it.
