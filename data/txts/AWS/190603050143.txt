JAMes MURPHY, detective-inspector, City Police. On Decem-
ber 14 I went to Brewer-street and saw S. Ross. I told him
I was a police offer and held two warrants for his arrest,
respecting two cheques he had received from Mr. Prentice and
Mr. Champney, and asked him to accompany me to the police
office He said that if his brother George was wanted he
would surrender. At the office I read the warrants to him and
he said, "I am quite innocent of fraud of any deseription, or
any misrepresentation. No two brothers had worked harder or
more honourably than we did in the interests of the share-
holders. We ourselves have lost a lot of money." That was
11.40 a.m. G. Ross surrendered about one o'olock. I read the
warrant to him. He made no reply.. They were formally
charged, and made no reply.
