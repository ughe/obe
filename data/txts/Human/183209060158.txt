 2116.  THOMAS HAMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of August , 3 lbs. weight of ham, value 3s., the goods of John Loveridge.
   ROBERT DARBY. I am servant to John Loveridge, cheesemonger, Crawford-street, Marylebone. I saw the prisoner take this ham from the window, and run away - he was stopped by the Policeman.
   MARY MORGAN. I was opposite the shop, and saw the prisoner walking up and down for a quarter of an hour - he then took the ham.
   THOMAS HARRIS. I am a Policeman. I stopped the prisoner, who Darby was pursuing, and found the ham in a bag, under his arm.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Six Months.

 2119.  ROBERT WILLIAM HUTTON was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of August , 1 bag, value 1s., and 1 coat, value 3l., the goods of Richard Davies.
   JAMES WILLIAM LYMN. I am ten years old, and am apprentice to Charles Stocking, Regent-street. On Thursday, the 2nd of August, I was sent by Richard Davies, the foreman, with a coat in a bag, to Finsbury-square - when I got near Waterloo-bridge, Strand, the prisoner and another came up to me; the other said,"Will you go on an errand for me?" I said Yes - he said he would give me 3d., and that man took the bag to hold while I went; the prisoner could hear and see what passed - the man sent me to some street, and said the name of Painter was over the door; the prisoner came with me to the house, and then ran away - the name of Painter was over the house; when I came back the other man, who was to wait at the court, was gone; I saw the prisoner on the Monday following, in Air-street - both of them were together, one a little way from the other - I informed the officer, who took the prisoner; the other ran away - when I saw them the prisoner was laying down, and the other standing a little way from him; the other spoke to me, and asked me to go to Mr. Bloomfield's - I said I would not; I went home to my master, told him I had seen the man, and then returned with an officer, and pointed the prisoner out, who was still there.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. What time of the day was this? A. I started about eleven o'clock in the morning, on Thursday, the 2nd of August; I was about half an hour going to where I lost the bag; when I saw the prisoner some days after he was asleep on the step - I had no conversation with him that day; they were about ten minutes with me when the bag was taken, I think - I observed him particularly; the one who took my bag had a black body coat on - I did not notice his neckerchief; I noticed his face.
 COURT. Q. Have you any doubt the prisoner is the man who went with you to Painter's on the 2nd of August, and who you saw in Air-street on Monday? A. He is the man; he had the same coat he has on now, I think- I am confident of his person.
 RICHARD DAVIES. On the 2nd of August I delivered Lymn a coat to carry to Mr. Freer, King-street, Finsbury - it was my property; I sent him out a little after eleven o'clock from No. 65, Regent-street; he returned in about two hours, without the bag, with a Policeman, and was crying - on the Monday following he came and took me to Air-street, and pointed out the prisoner; I felt doubtful about him, and desired him to be particular - he persisted, and I gave the prisoner in charge.
 Cross-examined. Q. When the boy came home the first day was he not much alarmed and disturbed? A. He was crying, but did not seem particularly frightened; he was certainly confused.
   ROBERT CULLEY. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner in Air-street, sitting on the step of a door; he denied the charge, and said before the Magistrate that he should be able to prove the boy had sold the coat some where.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he asleep? A. I cannot say- he was hanging his head down; he appeared asleep - it was about half-past nine o'clock.
 Prisoner's Defence. I can prove my innocence by witnesses.
