This was between the hours of three and four o'clock in the afternoon; one Mr. Phillips, a founder, came in with her; they had something to drink together, but I can't tell particularly what. He parted with her at the bar, and went away. She called for a pint of half and half. The kitchen and parlour join: the door was open; she came to me in the parlour, and said, Landlord, give me change for a guinea. I said, I will, What have you to pay? She said, I have only this pint to pay. I put my hand in my pocket to give her change. Said she directly, Will you lend me a guinea? I don't want for money, and pulled out a box of gold, I believe there might be eight or ten guineas in it, and said to me, I'll lend you five guineas, I'll be d - nd if I don't. Before she went out of the room, she there pulled out another box of gold; besides money, there were several gold rings, above twenty, I believe. Said I, your shewing me your gold, is nothing at all. What do you want to borrow a guinea for, when you have so much about you? I pulled out my money, three guineas and a half, and a shilling. She took a guinea and a shilling out of my hand, and said she would keep it. I said, What do you take my guinea for? you want change for a guinea. Said she, I'll keep it. I said, What should you keep it for? I never saw you before in my life: I shall punish you, if you don't let me have it again, without any further trouble. Then Mr. Cook said, Give the man his guinea again, he is only a servant here; he can't afford to lose his guinea in this manner; if you don't he will send for a constable. Said she, I don't care for that; Where will you send me? I am well known there, if he does: I'll keep it for a trick that I know. By that time, my wife came into the room, and said; What is all this noise here? There were eight or ten farmers by. She swore she would be d - nd if she gave it me again; and if I had her before a justice, she would swear I wanted to be bad with her; and made use of many bad, indecent words: this was before the farmers. But when she took my money, there was no body in the room but she and I, and Mr. Cook.
 Q. Did you ever get your guinea and shilling again?
 Gilbert. No, I never did. She was there three or four hours. I left Mr. Cook with her, to see if he could persuade her to let me have my money again. She told Mr. Cook, if he would do so and so to her, (meaning in her indecent language) she would give him a crown of it. Finding I could do nothing with her, I charged a constable with her.
 It not being a felony, but a trespass, for which he might, and was advised to, bring an action against her, she was    acquitted.
 She was detained to be tried for assaulting and beating the prosecutor's wife.
 See her tried No. 375, and 517, in Mr. Alderman Stephenson's Mayoralty.
 97. (L.)  Isaac Lamotte was indicted for stealing a linen handkerchief, value 10 d. the property of John Kirby, Dec. 11. ||
   John Kirby. On the 11th of December, going out of this court into this yard, I felt a hand in my pocket: I turned about and saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand. I took him with it in his hand, and brought it into court. ( Produced in court and deposed to.)
 Mr. Montague. I was at the court-door at the time, and saw the prisoner in court at the time sentence was passing on the convicts last sessions; then he went out, and in a few minutes time, Mr. Kirby brought him in again with the handkerchief.
 The Prisoner said nothing in his defence.
     Guilty. T.
 98, 99. (L.)  James Harris, and Daniel Trigg were indicted for stealing a linen handkerchief, value 6 d. the property of Adam Wright, Jan. 8. +
   William Pain. The little boy that had his pocket picked, is named Adam Wright; he is gone to the boarding-school. On the 8th instant, at the time the fire was in Pater-noster-row, I saw, I believe, a dozen of these little pilfering boys, among the people. I kept my eye upon them. They went from the Pater-noster-row to Newgate-street, and back again. They met this little boy, named Wright; one of them, seemingly out of kindness, took him by the hand to lead him, to shew him the fire, while the rest of the gang picked his pocket, just at the end of Pater-noster-row. The little boy was foremost, pushed on by the others; the flap of his pocket was in so. that his handkerchief was visible.
