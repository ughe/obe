Sophia MONTGOMERY re-examined. I have four children in the workhouse,
all very well treated, as well as the house will allow.
Court. Q. Are they allowed enough for them? A. Yes--they thrive
upon it-my children get as much as they would wish--they have milk and
water, and bread and butter in the morning and evening-three days in the
week they have meat, and on other days soup or rice-milk.
CAROLINE GIBBs re-examined. I have three children in the workbouse, one
is four years old, one three, and one twelve months-the treatment of those
young children, and the other children in the work house, and the pri-
soner's child, was such as they ought to have-her child had four ounces
of bread and half a pint of milk and water for its breakfast; and on meat days
for its dinner, four ounces of meat, and three potatoes; and in the evening
again, bread and butter, and half a pint of milk and water---young children
do not have soup--they have rice-milk-the prisoner herself gave her child
its food-it was a fine fat child.
Tuomas New. I am a surgeon, and live in Mile End-road. I was called
on Sunday evening, the 24th of March, to this child-it appeared to have
come by its death by drowning--I have no doubt at all about that the
usual means were resorted to to restore it, but in vain-I inspected the body
after it was opened-it was a perfectly healthy child in every respect, and a
remarkably fine child as ever I saw in my life-every part of the viscera was
perfectly healthy, and every part was firm and strong-I am not a parisb
doctor, nor connected with any parish Union.
COURT. Q. Was there any breaking out on the child ? A. There were
two or three eruptive spots about it-there seemed a dislocation of the
tether-that might have been for some time-the child would have limped.
Prisoner's Defence. I went to Whitechapel workhouse in great distress;
I was there upwards of four months, and had the best of treatment; from
there I went to Bethnal-green; I had not been there longer than a month,
before my little boy, four years old, had a very bad head; I complained to
the doctor, and it went to the Infirmary, but it never got, to say, properly well;
it was shaved five times, and the third time the barber was rather intoxicated,
and cut some of the flesh of the child's head, and that troubled me; it was ill
a long time, but it got well; but its eyes got bad, and it was nearly blind ;
after that the body was in a dreadful state; I spoke to the doctor about it,
and thought it proper the child should have a little mutton, instead of the hard
meat; and on soup days I wanted it to have a little meat, which was not al-
lowed, and then it was up in the Infirmary again ; and there it was all the
time I was there, and it came out with breakings out all over its body, and
I brought it out so; the other little cbild I put too soon on its feet, and its
knee-pan gave way; I spoke to the doctor, whether it should have an iron;
he said it would do more hurt than good, and he told me to bandage the leg
up, and to use ointment; they talked about an over-haul, and many of the
inmates said they knew of mothers and their children being turned out at a
