MR. PRENDERGAST. Q. Just point out what was done to these shou!
-(handing him those found on the prisoner.) A. These are not the shoe
- these are the shoes that were mended (looking at the others)--the po
soner told the shoemaker to put a piece under the right shoe, and the
left was to be soled and heeled-I heard all that was directed to be
done, at the Waterloo Arms, on the Saturday week before this Sundar
-I do not know the shoemaker's name, I know the man-I saw him i
the police-office--it was George Powell-I am not a shoemaker.
Witnesses for the Defence.
GEORGE Powell. I am sometimes a navigator, and sometimes make
shoes. On Saturday, the 26th of April, I received a pair of shoes from
the prisoner to mend, at the Waterloo Arms--I have practised shoemaking
occasionally for the last ten years-these are not the shoes-(looking a
those found in the prosecutor's house) - I should say they would very nearly
fit the prisoner-I know they are not his--these are the shoes I repaired
for him--they are the shoes I received at the public-house, when
Wells was present—those taken from the prisoner's feet)-I know theu
by the work, and by the pair of plain tips that I put on them with fire
holes in them, and the back lining of each shoe was sewn with a needle
and thread, by my wife--I received no other shoes but these at the
Waterloo Arms-they are different to the others- I had them on the
Saturday, and I took them to the prisoner's on the Sunday morning, about
half-past eleven o'clock-these other shoes I never saw before I was at the
police-office-they are a similar cut shoe to the prisoner's—those I mended
are oxonians- I should not say the others would fit the prisoner-I never
saw any of his shoes like these-- he had on an old pair of blucher
boots when I had these-he had no others to my knowledge-I bare
known him quite well, and seen him frequently--this cap is not his!
never saw him wear a cap like it-he generally wears a blue cloth cap
with a peak to it--(looking at one produced by the prisoner)--this is the
cap he has generally worn-I never knew of his having one like the
other.
MR. PAYNE. Q. How old are you? A. Twenty-five-I have known the
prisoner pretty well all my life, I have mended shoes for him a great many
times- I never mended a pair of oxonians for him before these-I will not
swear the other shoes will not fit him-I should not say they would ft
bim as the others do—I never saw them tried on—I live at No. 6, Byatt's-
buildings, George-street, Camberwell, not far from the George-it was
between five and six o'clock on the Saturday that the prisoner gave me
the shoes to mend-a fellow-servant, named Field, who works with the
water-casks, was there, and Wells- I took them home to my own house
about six o'clock-I mended them on Sunday morning from nine o'clock
till about half-past eleven, when I took them home-there have been
times when I have not seen the prisoner, when I have been at work, at
no other time I swear that-three months, I believe, is the longest time
I have lost sight of him--he lived in Cork-street-for the last six months
he has slept in a stable-I did not see him there, visit him at bis house,
or work with him, but I have worked for him-I have been to his father-in-
law's, in Cork-street, where he did live-he has not lived there for the last
six months--I have seen him at the Waterloo Arms, where he mostly
had his meals.
SARAM POWELL. I am the wife of last witness. I assisted him in
