JoHN SATH. I am a member of the Salvage Corps-at 8 a.m. on
12th February I took charge of the first floor front room at 250, Tufnell
Park Road-as soon as I entered. noticed the smell of paraffin-] saw
marks of fire on the bed, behind the door, close to the cupboard, and
against the window-the sofa was severely damaged by fire, and the
pictures burnt out id the middle--they had not been burnt in that fire,
because the paper behind them was clean; and not burnt-the wains-
coting was quite clean-I saw H. Brown," who represented Culmer
at the Police-court-the prisoner is the man-he said he thought the fire
occurred through a pipe being in his pooket behind the door; he
had a coat hanging up-he said there Was a lamp-I took up a piece
of a lamp off the floor-I saw two pieces of carpet saturated with
paraffin-) found traces of paraffin in other parts of the room.
Cross-acamined Some parts of the paper were burnt- made no report;
when Mr. King, the surveyor, came I showed him round-I do not
think the things were moved; there was not room-the paper was
burnt under the window, where the fire was, against the bedstead, and
the wainscoting under the paper was burnt-I have been aequainted
with fires going on for four yearsI had been to fires before I came to
England.
ELLEN SAUNDERS. My husband's name is William-I live at 7,
Dalton Street, Peckham--about 1.30 a.m. on the 1st June I was
awakened by great knocking at the door, and holloaing at the back-I
looked out at my window-I saw Mrs. Oulmer sitting on the cistern on
a tin trunk-she had a dress on, and a cloak, hat, and umbrella in her
hand-I called my husband and children, to get them out of it, beeause
she told me her house was on fire-No. 5 was next door-I went in front
and saw flames rushing out of the street doorI saw George Culmer in
the front, and Whitehead with him-Whitehead told me his name was
English-] knew Culmer as Culmer--they occupied No. 5 next door.
