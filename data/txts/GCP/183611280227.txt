Q. Is it not very easy, when a bulwark is unshipped, for the mere
motion of a man's body to cause him to slip off a vessel? 4. Yes- this
was a frosty night-the decks were frosty and slippery—the vessel was
lying quite still and quiet-there was no wind- I cannot undertake to say
whether the man fell in consequence of a blow or push or not-I went to
look for the body, and two or three more came after me in a boat-I cannot
say who they were I saw the body after it was found it was the body of
John Lott.
JOHN MACHIN. I am Captain of the brig Barnsley. I was in bed
I heard an alarm, and came on deck--I saw the deceased swimming in
the water, and made an attempt to save him he had got hold of our
cable, and he pulled himself partly out of the water-I got into a boat, but
he was lost--the prisoner was in our boat trying to pick him up.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he appear very eager to save the deceased ?
4. Yes-the decks were very slippery-the ship was down about eighteen
inches, slant with her head-she was afloat, but the cargo laid forwards
--the prisoner was very much affected at this I have known him from a
boy-I never saw him out of temper with any one-I never saw the de-
ceased swim-the tide was very strong, and drew him under the yeasel's
bottom.
JOHN West. I am a fisherman. I found the body two tiers beyond
where he was lost, and took him ashore.
(The prisoner received an excellent character.)

works Company, his masters: and JOHN CURLEY, for feloniously re-
