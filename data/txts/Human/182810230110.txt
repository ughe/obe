The prisoner then added, "I make no remarks on the extraordinary conduct of my prosecutors, in taking papers from my private desk, during my absence; they have deprived me of several of them which are material to my defence. I would further remark that several of them were not finished, nor were they intended to be sent to any body, but merely written for my own amusement, and would probably, in a few minutes, have been destroyed."
 COURT, Q. Would you wish to call any witnesses? Prisoner. No, I adhere to my determination.
 Q. Do you wish to call witnesses to character? A. I beg leave respectfully to state that I adhere to my former determination. GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 58.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Jury on account of his large family and former respectability.

 Before Mr. Justice Park.
 2203.  JOSEPH HUNTON was again indicted, for that he, on the 1st of September, at St. Mary-le-Bow, having in his custody and possession a certain bill of exchange which is as follows: -
 No. 307, £94.13s, London, 23 August, 1828,
 Four Months after date pay to our Order - Ninety-four Pounds, Thirteen Shillings, value received.
   JOHN DICKSON and Co.
 To  Richard Luck, East Peckham, Kent. feloniously did falsely make, forge, and counterfeit, on the said bill of exchange, an acceptance of the same, which is as follows: -
 Accepted and payable at the Spur Inn, Borough. R. LUCK. with intent to defraud Sir William Curtis, Bart, and others; against the Statute, &c.
 2d COUNT, that he, on the same day, at the same parish, having in his custody and possession a certain bill of exchange which is as follows (Bill set out as above) - upon which said last-mentioned bill of exchange was written a certain false, forged, and counterfeited acceptance of the same, which is as follows (Acceptance set out as above) - he, the said Joseph Hunton, afterwards(to wit) on the same day, at the same parish, feloniously did utter, and publish as true, the said last-mentioned false, forged, and counterfeited acceptance of the said last-mentioned bill of exchange, he well knowing the same to be false, forged, and counterfeited, with intent to defraud the said Sir William Curtis , Bart. and others; against the Statute, &c.
 3d COUNT, the same as the second; only for offering, disposing of, and putting away the said forged acceptance, instead of for uttering and publishing as true.
 4th, 5th, and 6th COUNTS, the same as the three former, only charging the prisoner's intention to be to defraud  John Dickson.
 7th, 8th and 9th COUNTS, the same, only with intent to defraud  Richard Luck, the younger.
 MESSRS. LAW and CLAKKSON conducted the prosecution.
   ABRAHAM WILDEY ROBARTS, ESQ. I am a partner in the house of Sir William Curtis and Co., bankers, of Lombard-street, City; the firm consists of Sir William Curtis, his son, and myself. In the course of last year and the present, the house of Dickson and Co. were customers of ours- I believe they resided in Ironmonger-lane; the firm consisted of the prisoner and John Dickson - the firm stood in our books in the name of John Dickson and Co.; we were in the habit of frequently receiving bills from them, for the purpose of being discounted; the prisoner exclusively carried on the financial department of the firm, at least as far as we were concerned; he frequently came to our banking-house with bills. On the 1st of September, he brought several bills, and brought this list with them - (looking at it) - there is some cash entered at the bottom of the list, but that did not come into my hands - this is a list of bills which were offered for discount; it is in the prisoner's handwriting - I saw him myself that day; I looked at the total amount at the bottom of the list, and ran over the bills - they corresponded.
 Q. You have a bill of exchange in your hand, was that among the number of bills brought by the prisoner on the 1st of September? A. It was, and it corresponds with the entry on the list - it agrees in the sum, the names of the drawer and acceptor, and where payable.
 Q. Did you say any thing to the prisoner on that occasion? A. I can only recollect observing to the prisoner on that occasion, as well as on every other, when he brought bills to be discounted; I always expressed my very great objection to discount them - I perfectly recollect objecting to discount them on this particular occasion; he invariably made a reply, and on this occasion he told me, distinctly, that they were all bona fide legitimate commercial transactions, and upon that representation I discounted them; after having received them from him, I wrapped them up in this list, and took them into the discount-office, and put them into a box there - they would then be taken from that box, and entered by Mr. Thomas Brackett, the discount clerk, in a book kept exclusively for that purpose; it was then the practice for Mr. Brackett to put them under a lead on his desk - they would then be taken from there by Mr. Clunie, another clerk, who keeps the bill-book.
