   Q.  How soon were you applied to about this after you came to town? A.  I was applied to by a letter, which appeared to have miscarried, about a week ago—I do not know the contents of it—I came forward spontaneously, on seeing the accounts in the papers—I was not before the Magistrate.
   COURT . Q.  Were you on the pier on Thursday, when the packet arrived? A.  Yes—there were two arrived, one about four o'clock, and the other about five—I do not know whether any packets arrived before—I do not know at what time all the packets arrive. CHARLES TOWNLEY. I reside in West-street, Camberwell—I hold, an ap pointment in the Trinity-house, and have done so thirty-seven years—I have known Mr. Bridgman twelve or thirteen years, ever since he has been there—I was at Ramsgate previous to Sept., and remained there up to the 14th or 15th of Sept.—I met Mr. Bridgman there on Wednesday morning, the 6th of Sept., about a quarter or twenty minutes past nine, in Ramsgate harbour—I spoke to him, and was in company with him on and off for an hour and a half, witnessing the landing of some troops and horses from a transport—I saw him again, about a quarter past five that afternoon, coming from Camden square, between there and High-street, on my way to Broad-street—I merely passed the compliments of the day to him then, as a lady was with him—I saw him again either on Thursday or Friday morning, but I cannot say which, at Ramsgate.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  What enables you to fix on Wednesday the 6th of Sept.? A.  I am satisfied it was that day, because that very morning the China  transport came into the harbour with troops, and a number of persons went down to see them disembark—I went to see it, and met Mr. Bridgman there—it is stated in Lloyd's list, that the China  arrived on the 6th, and a part of my family went to Dover that day, and in their absence I went over to Broadstairs—on my way there, met Mr. Bridgman coming from Camden square—I have Lloyd's list in my hand, which states that the China  arrived at Ramsgate on the 6th—I am sure the vessel I saw was the China  —I saw her name—I have very seldom been to Mr. Bridgman's chapel—I have lived in West-street twenty-six or twenty-seven years—I was at Ramsgate three weeks—( referring to a memorandum )—I went on the 26th of August—I have no memorandum of meeting Mr. Bridgman on the 6th of Sept.
   MR. BODKIN . Q.  What made you go to Broadstairs? A.  I had engaged with a part of my family that went to Dover on the Wednesday, that we would go to Broadstairs in the afternoon, and meet them on their return—I am quite satisfied that it was on that very same day I saw Mr. Bridgman—the China  had arrived from Quebec.
   COURT . Q.  Did you meet Mr. Bridgman on the pier? A.  Yes; I did not see him land—he was walking at the end of the pier to view the troops dis embarking—the usual hour of the packets arriving from London, was three or four, and sometimes five, it depended on the wind and tide—two came every day.
