51. THOMAS POOLE was indicted for bigamy.
WILLIAM BALLARD. I am clerk of the church of St. John the Baptist,
Hoxton. I produce the register of marriages at that church—on the 16th
of December, 1838, the prisoner Thomas Poole and Priscilla Sparks were
married there—I could not recollect the woman—-l have known the prisoner
from a child.
Cross-examined by M PHILLIPS. Q. What age do you suppose be
was in 1838 ? A. About eighteen or nineteen—it stands in the book that
they were both of full age—the Minister did that, as the prisoner told him
—I did not undeceive the Minister—I could not do it—it is against the
law—I could not help it.
HENRY PARKER. I am a carver. I was at this wedding—the prisoner
had worked for me some time—I did not know the woman till I saw her
on the morning of the wedding—I believe they lived together afterwards,
—l saw her at the police-offee, but I never visited them.
Cross-examined. Q. How long had you known the prisoner?
About two months—he appeared quite a young man.
MART TBVMPER. I became acquainted with the prisona between
three and four months ago—I vas then a milliner—I knew him two or
three months,and we were married at Aldgate-cburcb, on the 11 th Of October
—be represented himself to me as single—we lived together a fortnwt,

was married—I went to his father's the next morning, and found the pi,

Cross-examined. Q. He tells me he got acquainted with you on the
31st of August? A. I cannot say the day—he got no money from me,

about him.
COURT. Q. Before you married him you say he represented himself
