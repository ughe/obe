I took out ten sovereigns; I kept the one sovereign, and put the bag into the box again, and locked it; when the broker was gone I and the prisoner were going into the school-room again, and as we came out of the bedroom door, she told me her mother was calling me - I did not hear her mother call me; I looked over the bannisters, and told her I could neither hear nor see her mother call me - she insisted that her mother was calling me; I went down one flight of stairs, as far as the boy's school-room, and then returned, as I did not think her mother was calling me - she looked over the bannisters, and told me her mother was in the kitchen, and that she was calling me; I went down stairs, and saw her mother in the parlour - she did not want me; I went up stairs - she was then standing just inside the school-room door - I told her her mother had not called me, that I had been down, and spoken to her - she made no reply to that, but said, in a very hurried manner, that she had thought of a key, which would fit my room door, and that the key of the school-room fitted it, (we had enquired for a key to the bed-room door) she put the key into the key-hole herself, and locked the door - she was trying to see if the key fitted it; she left the key in the door, and I tried it myself, to see if it was locked - the key was taken out of the door; I do not recollect which of us took it out, but if she took it out, she gave it to me, for it was in my hand directly; Mr. and Mrs. Smith, the prisoner, my husband, and myself were in the house at this time; Mr. Smith was engaged down stairs in the boys' school-room - nobody was up stairs besides the prisoner and the man who brought the furniture in, and I was there the whole time with him - I should think this took place about eleven o'clock, as near as I can guess; the school hours are from nine till twelve - it was her duty to remain in the school the whole of the day; the moment she gave me the key, she went down stairs; she did not tell me she was going - she returned at ten minutes after twelve o'clock - I asked where she had been - she said she had been out with a friend; I had sent down stairs to inquire for her, but she could not be found - she then told me to dismiss the children, and they all went home - they ought to have gone at twelve o'clock, but I did not know the time; I remained in the school-room with her a few minutes; there was a desk in the room, which I told her was not the one we had bought with the fixtures - she said it was; I heard my husband coming up stairs, and I took the key she had given me off the school-room table, and unlocked the bed-room door - the school-room commands a view of the bed-room - when I sat in the room I could see any body who went into the bedroom - nobody had gone to it from the time she locked it, till I opened it for my husband; the school-room door was open the whole day; when I went into the bedroom, my husband sat down, and my attention was called to the trunk - I found it was shut, the bolt of the lock was turned, but it had not caught - it was like a desk lock; it was my husband called my attention to the box when he sat down - he said I had not locked it, and found the bolt was shot, but it did not go into the lid; I examined the bag, and found that it was open - I had twisted a string round it when I paid the broker, but did not tie it - I found the mouth of the bag open; we counted the sovereigns, and twenty-five were deficient - I found forty-seven in the bag - the prisoner was in the school-room; we went into the school-room, and told her we had been robbed, and that we should like to know where she had been while she was out - she said she would not tell us; we told her we would have a Policeman, and then she ran away down stairs - my husband ran after her, and I went down and found her at the front gate - she still said she would not tell us where she had been; my husband said, if she would, he would take her there himself, and not have a Policeman - she would not tell us, and we sent for a Policeman - she then ran into the kitchen, and fastened the door - we could not get in, we tried to get in repeatedly; I went to the front window of the kitchen, but could not see her; a Policeman came, but she would not let him in - he tried the door and window - she had fastened them both; he at last got in at the window, and let us in at the door - the prisoner slept in the kitchen - her bed was opposite the window, on chairs; she was examined at the office between one and two o'clock, I think, and after that the Magistrate ordered the house to he searched - I went with the Policeman; we searched the kitchen - Mrs. Smith was there, with the Policeman, and myself; both the Policeman and her mother searched the bedding, and I saw the mother find two purses under the bed, either between two beds, or between the bed and mattress - one was a brown purse, and the other a bead purse; I had seen the bead purse frequently in the prisoner's hands - she was in the habit of using it - neither of the purses were mine; there was about 4d. in the bead purse, and in the brown purse there were fifteen sovereigns, and 12s. 6d. in silver - the brown purse has two ends to it - the gold and silver were separate.
