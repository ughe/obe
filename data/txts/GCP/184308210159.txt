Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST. D. Did not the prisoner generally
have two or three months' credit for the goods he sold ? . No, he allowed
the customer, when he received ready money, two and a half per cent.-on
the 29th of April be brought me ready money for some goods, that was the
day they were sold-he was with me fourteen months-he had perhaps
brought me ready money ten times during those fourteen months--my
husband is a journeyman engineer, and I carry on this business I mann-
facture in my own house in tambour work--I and Mr. Jay had no transac
tions in business before the prisoner came-I did not know him-I gave the
prisoner Mr. Jay's bill every month to deliver-it is usual to send in a state-
ment of goods--I found the last bill at his house this is my book-I told
the prisoner Mr. Jay's was a very extensive mourning warehouse--I thought
very likely he might do business there if he would call--I had no acquainta
ance with the prisoner-I had employed his sister about two months--that
was his introduction to me--I paid him about 141.- he told me he had other
commissions as well, but not in the same line-that he was doing business
as a fancy stationer, and collecting debts for another person he had no
weekly salary—I have no acquaintance with Mr. Leslie-my husband has
seen the prisoner many times—he never concerns bimself in the business.
MR. DOANE. Q. Did he know the prisoner was engaged by you ? 4.
Yes, I consulted with him before I engaged him.
PHILIP JAY. I am in the service of William Jay, of Regent-street-We
had bugle goods from the prisoner from time to time-I never heard of Mrs.
Anderson-on the 12th of January I paid the prisoner 91. 158. for these goods
by a check-on the 9th of February I paid him 131., on the 10th, 51. 168.
-- he did not in June make any application to me for money-I did not tell
him I was going into the country where I had some houses, and I would
endeavour to get some money-he always had his money-at first we used
to pay him every time, and then we took more, and took a monthly account
he had his money every time he called for it.
Cross-examined. Q. Did you deal with him entirely in his own name?
4. Entirely in his own name I always paid the money to him for himself
the 10th of February was the last of these sums.

MR. DOANE. Q. Did he deliver you the invoices made out by Mrs.
Anderson? A. The invoices were put on the file and brought to me-I
never heard of Mrs. Anderson in the matter.
MR. PRENDERGAST to AMELIA ANDERSON. Q. The goods, the prisoner
had of you were a few at a time? 4. He would have from 301. to 401. worth
at a time-I sent a stock round to all the customers that they might take
what they pleased—I sent Mr. Jay as much as he ordered, 21. or 31. worth
at a time--the goods were taken in two boxes-he had a boy to carry them
who I employed—the prisoner got money for me from a loan society-Mr.
Anderson was responsible for it, and has paid it since the prisoner did not
lend me 108., nor get a coat out of pawn, nor advance me small sums--I have
sold collarets--some of them are made of lace, and some of crape, embroi-
dered with bugles—Mr. Jay had some of them I sold the prisoner two
bugle collarets for mourning.
