 1350. FREDERICK READING and AMELIA READING were indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of March , 1 sheet, value 8 s  .; and 1 blanket, value 8 s  .; the goods of Samuel Mayhew . MART MAYHEW. I am the wife of Samuel Mayhew. We keep some furnished lodgings at No. 15, Harrison-street, Gray's Inn-road. The pri soners came to lodge there as man and wife—I cannot say whether they are married—they left on the 7th of March—I went into the room, and missed the blanket and sheet now produced—the female prisoner gave me the duplicate on the Monday, when one of our lodgers and the policeman brought her to me—I had asked her on Sunday for the sheet—she said she could not give it me then, she would give it me in half-an-hour, but she went out, and left. WILLIAM SMITH. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. I have a sheet pawned by the female prisoner. THOMAS BURDICK. I am a pawnbroker. I have a blanket pawned by the female prisoner.
   Amelia Reading's Defence.  We were in distress, and my husband out of work; I pledged it on Saturday, and told her on Monday I would re deem it if she would wait; she said she would.
   MARYMAYHEW re-examined.  She did not abscond from the lodging—I believe they were distressed. AMELIA READING — GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined One Month.
   FREDERICK READING— NOT GUILTY.
   NEW COURT .— Monday, April  11 th,  1842.
