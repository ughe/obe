ROBERT EMERSON (police inspector P). I examined Mr. Stiff's premises—
there are a pair of large folding coach-house doors, and at the top of one of them
there was a hole cut; apparently it had been done some time—any person, by
laying hold of that hole, and putting their feet against the door, could open it
suffciently for the bolt to fly away from the staple—two inches below that
the door was split, as if there had been some violence—there is a door in the
coach-house into the washhouse, the latch of which was slightly bent, and it
could be pushed open—the next door, leading from the washhouse to the
kitchen, had the beading broken off, and there were marks such as such an
instrument as this chisel would make was delivered to me,
having been found in the front parlour—a brick was out at the side of the
washhouse and kitchen-door; and a person, by putting their hand through
there, could reach the bolt and open it—the door leading from the kitchen to
the rest of the house had the lower panel out, so that a person could put
their hand through and undo the the bolt—it appeared to have been done
with such an instrument as this—I did not notice that any locks had been
picked—I took Taylor next day, and told him what he was charged with—
be made no reply—in consequence of information I took Rie and Haines
Newgate, introduced them to a party of twenty prisoners or more, and
Haines picked Costello out from among them.
DANIEL MARTIN. I live at 6, Somer's-place, Brixton, and am Mr. Stiffs
foreman. A month previous to the robbery at Mr. Stiff's my house was
attempted ; they did not succeed ; I believe they were prevented by a black
cat who had a kitten—my wife drew my attention to the eat-entailing, and in
the morning I found the house had been attempted—on 24th Sept. Taylorleft
at five o'clock—I remained till a quarter to six—Costello worked for me about
three months ago—on the day after the robbery I asked Taylor where
Costello was, as I would give him a job—he said he had not the slightet
knowledge; he had not seen him a length of time; he did hear be wu
somewhere about Somers-town—it was not my intention to give him any job.
Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was that, in point of fact, where
he did live? A. I understand he lived in Parker-street, Drury-lane—he has
not, that I am aware of, lived at Somers Town—it had not been arranged that
Taylor should leave earlier than the other men, but we were out of paper,
and I gave orders to leave off at five o'clock—there was nothing irregular in
his leaving at five—his father is a very respectable man.
COURT. Q. Was the attempt On your house known in the offce? A. Yes ; I
had mentioned it to the men, but not in the boys' room, which I have charge
of—I mentioned how they were disturbed—all the boys left work at five—
there was nothing to keep any of them till a quarterto six.
