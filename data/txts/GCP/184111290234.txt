Cross-examined by Mr. RYLAND. Q. How many persons have you ac-
cused of stealing this money? A. No one but the prisoner and her mother
--we did not accuse them, we gave them in charge-we laid an information
of our loss at the police office, and they advised us not to take either of
them till we made inquiry-I gave evidence against them both before the
Magistrate--the mother was discharged on the third hearing-they were
out on bail between the examinations--I mean to swear that I never ac-
cused any third person of having improperly had this money-I was mar.
ried on the 28th of February last, at St. Sepulchre's church-my husband
and I have not lived on very bad terms with respect to this money-it was
money which came to me in my own right-I always had it in my own
possession-I never charged my husband with endeavouring to get hold
of it against my will-I swear that I had counted my money on the 31st
of October-I had eleven 51. notes altogether in this bag, and one 20l. note
-I have a memorandum of the numbers of the notes, which I wrote when
I took them from the banker's I wrote it on the 29th of October-I received
them on the 17th of September-I counted my money on the 29th of October,
and on the 31st it was safe in the bag- I counted it all over on the 31st, and
it was all safe--the 29th was the day after we went into the prisoner's mo-
ther's house - I had no particular reason for counting it then I went to
my drawer, and saw it safe in the bag- I have the numbers and dates of all
the 5l. notes; at least my husband has- I had not paid any of the Sl.
notes away before I discovered my loss-from the 17th of September till
the 31st of October I had not paid any away-I was never out of the
house--we had taken the Axe public-house in Milton-street, and all the
money was paid away then except the note the prisoner stole.
Q. You said this moment you had not paid away any ? A. Not of that
money-there was none of the eleven 51. notes or the 201. note paid
away.
