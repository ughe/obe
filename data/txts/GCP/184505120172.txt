RICHARD MORGAN. I am a grocer, and live at East Ham. I was at the
Rabbits public-house on the afternoon in question I saw the prisoner
come there on a horse-I think Bates came afterwards--the prisoner was
riding without a saddle, and appeared as if he had been drinking-he said
his horse would trot any b— mare from Romford to London-I called
for a pint of ale, and sat down by the table--the prisoner and Bates were
sitting together--the first conversation I heard was about Ireland - I do
not know who began the conversation-I thought from the prisoner's
appearance that he was an Irishman- I had never seen him before, or
Bates either, that I know of- I heard the prisoner say something about
Ireland, and Bates said he would lay him a sovereign he never was in
Ireland in his life-the prisoner said he would lay him 51. that he was-
he said, "Because I have got an old coat on you think I have no money,"

JAMES Othen. I keep the Rabbits--the moment I heard the crash I
went out of the bar and took the prisoner by the collar--the deceased was
sitting in front of the bar-window, and the yard was full--the prisoner
dared me to hold him and was very violent--the deceased was bleeding
profusely from the mouth and nose, some one said his leg was broken, and
I immediately sent off my son to Ilford for the police.
