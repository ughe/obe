GEORGE COX. I lived at 6, Brunswick Place, Parson's Green—I have known Williams about 12 months—about the beginning of June he said that he had got two perambulators, one which cost five guineas and another seven, and a filter and some Irish linen, all in pledge—he gave me a list of them, stating what he wanted for the pawn-tickets; he also said he had a Bible in pledge for 30 s ., which cost three guineas; that the gentleman who had it would get it out and let me have it—I gave him 5 s . for the tickets for some table and bed linen, for which I paid 16 s . to Mr. Attenborough, of Newington Butts—I saw Dundas on the morning of the summons at Westminster and I met Williams and Grey.
 (  Grey here stated that he would PLEAD GUILTY . )
   Cross-examined by Williams.  I bought a bed of you for 6 s .—I don't know whose ticket it was, I have pledged it since—I know you sell things on commission, pawn-tickets or anything else—you wanted to borrow my van to move some things from Wandsworth—I understood that Mr. Grey had some things which he wanted to move to Richmond—you went to Willis and were very nasty because they would not let you have a van—one was afterwards got from Bollinger, of Notting Hill. GEORGE ROBERTS. In November, 1884, I had the letting of 33, Mark ham Street—Cramer took it at a rent of 75 l ., and referred to R. Sidney, from whom I received this letter. ( Stating that Cramer would make a re  spectable tenant) Cramer then got possession—no rent was paid. RAMSEY MORRIS. I am clerk to the Newcastle Colliery owners—about 6th January, 1885, I got this order. ( Signed E. Carter, for Wallsend coals . ) We delivered the coals to 86, Kennington Road, and this signa ture, "C. Carter," was brought back—we never got the money.
   Cross-examined by Sidney.  I applied for it a few weeks afterwards—we do not give credit. GEORGE JAMES AYRES. I am a clerk, and live at 86, Kennington Road—I rented four rooms of Carter, that is Sidney, and I live there still—Mr. and Mrs. Williams lived there as man and wife, as Mr. and Mrs. Dundas.
   Sidney's Defence.  The filter and the perambulator were bought on the credit system, payable by instalments. I should have met the engage ments; I was receiving money at the time. I am a mariner, and since that I have expended 4,000 l . as a dealer in property; no one can say that I owe a shilling.
   Thomas Williams's Defence.  I have had nothing but a half-hundred weight of coal. I have been in prison three months, and I was never in prison before. I could have given more references. GUILTY of conspiracy only.
