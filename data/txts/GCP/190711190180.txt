Witness. I gave evidence of what I saw. What I did not see 1
know nothing about. The gate of Paddington goods station through
which I came the Stafford Gate-is not far from this place, about
370 or 380 yards. No. 1, Hampden Street is on the way to Cirencester
Street. I ocupy all my spare time in making shoes. I do nine
hours a day for the railway company; if on night duty, ten hours. I
do the shoemaking for money. I did not tell the magistrate I wa
with the Great Western Railway Company, because my senior officer
does not like us to leave the station while we are on night duty, and
I did not want them to know. I went out on this night because the
canteen was shut and I had not taken any refreshment in, as my wife
was away. I cannot tell you what Jenkin said when I jumped beside
him; he had no chance to say much. He must have heard what
I wid, I should think. I was severely handled at the time pushed
and mauled about. Sometimes I take prisoners into a station
my own. When I went into the station the inspector asked me what
I saw. I believe he spoke to the constables first. I told him I had
seen Sexton strike Jenkin and I jumped in and assisted him like
all respectable Englishmen should do. I suppose the constables said
something then. I positively swear I had never seen Jenkin of
Adams before. I was on the scene by mere accident. I did no
know Howard. (To the Recorder.) I have been with the Great
Western as a detective for two years. Before that I was a polit
constable, having been an appointed servant for six years. Before
that I was a shoemaker; that is my trade. I am not aware of any
complaint against me by the railway company. I did not see me
policemen drunk. I had nothing to drink.
Re-examined. I am 35. Until I was 29 I was shoemaking,
33, Malvern Road, West Kilburn. You cannot get a job unless you
