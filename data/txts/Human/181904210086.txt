739. JOHN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of February , one musket, value 7 s., the goods of William Haggett.
   WILLIAM HAGGETT. I am a labourer, and live in Essex-court, Park-street, Borough. On the 24th of February, between ten and eleven o'clock in the morning, I missed my musket out of the passage. I went after the prisoner, and took him at Ratcliff-cross, between twelve and one o'clock that day, with the musket on his arm. I was informed he had gone that way, pursued him, and took him. I am certain he is the man.
   JOSEPH ADAMS. I am an officer. I took the prisoner in charge on the 24th of February, with the musket. He made his escape from the office, and was taken again. I am sure he is the man.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 - I am an officer. I was bringing the prisoner to Newgate; he broke from me and ran down Newgate-market - I lost him, but retook him again about fifteen days after, and am sure he is the man. He had the marks of the handcuffs on his wrist that he ran away with.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 740.  THOMAS JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of April , two coats, value 5 l., the property of George Smith.
   SUSAN SMITH. I am the daughter of George Smith, who is a labourer, and lives at Hackney. I was crossing the road, and saw the prisoner with two coats under his arm. I ran in, and missed my father's coats from behind the door; I pursued him, calling Stop thief! and a gentleman stopped him. I am certain he is the man.
   SUSAN POWELL. The prisoner ran by me with the coats under his arm; the last witness called Stop thief! he jumped into the hedge of a garden, left the coats there, and returned without them. The constable stopped him. I never lost sight of him.
   THOMAS GOODWIN. I am a constable. I stopped the prisoner, who was running in Grove-street. He said there was nothing amiss, it was only a joke; I took him back and found the coats in the hedge.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I saw a man run into the garden with a bundle, and pursued him, but lost him.
     GUILTY. Aged 19. Transported for Seven Years.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 741.  MICHAEL GARATTY was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of April , three yards and a half of velveteen, value 9 s., the goods of George William Rainbow , from his person.
   GEORGE WILLIAM RAINBOW. I am a tailor. On the 22d of April, about half-past two o'clock in the day, I was behind a coach, in the Commercial-road. In about a minute the prisoner and another man came up - the prisoner sat by my side. In a little time he took my bundle from under my arm; I told him to let it alone - he revised to give it to me, and said it was a joke. He struck me several blows about the head, and gave the bundle to his companion, who ran away with it. I got down and pursued him; he then turned back, and gave it to the prisoner on the coach; they knocked me down, and struck me several blows. I took it from them, and took the prisoner into custody.
 Cross-examined by MR. LOVET. Q. There were a great many people in the road at the time - A. A great many. The prisoner continued sitting on the coach. He did not ask me to make room for his companion.
   WILLIAM PARSONS. I was passing, and saw a man give the bundle to the prisoner, who was riding behind the coach - the prisoner knocked the prosecutor down. I stopped to look at them, and took the prisoner to the office. I believe he was the worse for liquor.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I had been drinking; the prosecutor grumbled at my coming behind the coach, and knocked my hat off; I took his parcel and threw it after my hat, another man came, and took both hat and bundle.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 742.  JOHN MORGAN was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of March , one coat, value 25 s.; one pair of gloves, value 6 d., and one umbrella, value 4 s. 6 d., the goods of Samuell Waller.
   SAMUEL WALLER. I am a cooper, and live at Wapping. I left my coat and umbrella in the counting-house of Messrs. Osman and Co. - it was stolen.
   WILLIAM MORBEY. I am a carman. On the 26th of March, about eleven o'clock in the morning, I saw the prisoner come out of the counting-house, with the coat and umbrella; he said Mr. Donegal had sent him for them. I collared him, and took him back. He offered me his own coat to let him go.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner. I was in distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 24.
     Judgment Respited.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 743.  DANIEL MAHONEY was indicted for stealing, on the 23d of April , one handkerchief, value 15 d., the goods of Henry Beverly Wakeling , from his person.
   HENRY BEVERLY WAKELING. On the 23d of April, about eight o'clock in the evening, I was in Drury-lane with Mr. Young; the prisoner and another were following us very close. I felt a hand in my pocket, turned round and collared the prisoner - his companion ran away. My handkerchief was not found; I had it a moment before. I am certain I caught the prisoner's hand in my pocket.
   JOHN YOUNG. I was with Wakeling; he said he had lost his handkerchief, and seized the prisoner - the other ran away. Nobody else had been near us.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Six Months.
 Third Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
