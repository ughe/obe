Before Mr. Recorder.
 1992. HENRY JOSEPH KILLERBY was indicted for feloniously sending to Elizabeth Challenor a certain letter, threatening to kill and murder Elizabeth Spriggs , the daughter of the said Elizabeth Challenor; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   MR. PAYNE conducted the Prosecution. ELIZABETH CHALLENOR. I am the wife of John Challenor, and live at No. 45, White-street, Borough. I have a daughter named Elizabeth Spriggs, by a former husband—she is thirteen years old—I know the prisoner—I had occasion to make a complaint of him some time ago, with reference to my daughter—he was imprisoned for a year—he came out of prison on the 26th of Feb., I think—I received the letter in question by post, on the 13th of Aug.—this now produced is it—I had received one previously, on the 20th of July, and one before that—I do not know whose writing they are, but I had many letters before which were proved to be in the prisoner's writing, and I took these to be the same writing—the letters that were the subject of the previous complaint were in the same handwriting. EDWARD KENDALL( police-sergeant, A  30.) I am acquainted with the prisoner's handwriting—I have seen him write on one occasion—I believe these letters of the 20th of July and the 12th of Aug. to be the prisoner's writing.
   COURT. Q.  What was it that you saw him write? A.  The direction of an envelope and his signature—it was at his father's house, before I appre hended him on the first occasion—it was done for the purpose of ascertain ing his handwriting—I did not prove his writing on the first occasion—there was another witness who had seen him write on more occasions, a foreman in an establishment where he worked—he is not here to-day—the Magistrate said it was not necessary, and that I was sufficient—I believe these letters to be in the handwriting of the prisoner.
   Cross-examined by MR. BRIALY. Q.  You have never seen him write but once? A.  Never, that was merely a direction and his name—it was in Sept., 1845—I requested him to write it, I have got it here—that is all I know of his writing, but I have plenty of writing here which was aworn to as being his, on the former trial.
   MR. PAYNE Q.  Was it when you went to apprehend him in Sept., 1845, that you got him to write? A.  No, some time before, when I was making inquiry to detect the person who had sent the letters—he wrote the direction, "Miss Elizabeth Spriggs" and his own name, in my presence—having seen him write that, I have not the shadow of a doubt of these letters being his—the character of the writing is particular—I paid particular attention to his writing—I went for that purpose. ELIZABETH CHALLENOR( re-examined. )—The prisoner was apprehended on this charge on the 15th, I think—I have had conversations with him about it—I never spoke to him on the subject of the letter of the 12th of Aug.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Have you known him for any length of time? A.  I never knew him to be the son of Mr. Killerby, till I saw him a year and a half ago—I think he is about nineteen years of ago—I have lived neighbour with his parents for twenty-two years, but never knew him—I do not know of any acquaintance that he had with my daughter—he never spoke to her, not at any period—I have not been in the habit of having any communication
