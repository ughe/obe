I have no ticed Bray's ominbus going from Paddington to the Bank repeatedly—I have noticed other omnibuses near Mr. Bray's; in my opinion, they were racing against him—I have seen one behind him, and when Bray's omni bus stopped, the other endeavoured to drive against him, in order to get his passengers—I should suppose if any timind female wanted to get into Bray's omnibus, she might have been alarmed by it; but I never saw that—I have seen one omnibus close before him as well as behind—when Bray's stopped, the others would stop—I have seen that—their conduct was such as to obstruct him in his regular course from Paddington to the Bank—I never saw any improper conduct of Bray's to the others. JOHN MORRIS. I live at No. 7, Chapel-place, Oxford-street, and am an accountant. I have seen Bray's omnibuses, and other omnibuses fol lowing them, and with them frequently—I cannot tell the names, but I think I have seen the "Emperor"—In general one went before Bray's, and the other behind—I have been them draw up when people were getting in very frequently—I have seen more than two omnibuses with Bray's oc casionally—they were acting in such a way as to obstruct Bray's—I thought it was a little rivalry. JOHN BATTERBEE. I live at No. 133, Oxford-street, and am a shoe maker. I have observed the omnibus with the name "Aaron Bray" on it, gernerally accompanied by there others, one before, one behind, and one by the side of it—that was a very common occurrence, almost every day—generally when he stopped they put their poll against him, so as to obstruct people getting in.
   COURT . Q.  Did you see the parties conducting Bray's omnibus acting so? A.  No.
