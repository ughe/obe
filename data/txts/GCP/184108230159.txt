SOREN SAMUEL PALM. I live in High-street, Shadwell. On Saturday
night, the 21st of August, about a quarter before ten o'clock, this cloth
was at my door-I saw it move, ran immediately to the door, and saw the
prisoner with it on his shoulder-I followed him across the road, laid hold
of bim, and brought him back into the shop with it-it is worth 51.
Prisoner. As I was going across the road from his door, a man gave
it me to carry; I went along; the prosecutor came and knocked me
down and collared me; he would not go after the man. Witness. He had
got across the road when I took him-I first saw him about five yards
from the door-this is the cloth.
GEORGE ROBINSON police-constable K 370.) I took the prisoner in
charge-he said he was carrying it for a man behind—I saw no man behind
-I came up about ten minutes after it happened.

2212. JOHN HOPKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of Au-
gust, 1 pair of boots, value 38. 6d., the goods of Henry Warrener.
HENRY WARRENER. I am an apprentice to a shoemaker, and live in
Strutton-ground-the prisoner lived in the same house. I left a pair of
boots in the room he slept in-I missed them afterwards—these produced
are them I had placed them there on the 20th of August.
Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. What was the prisoner in the
house? A. A workman-he had been there six or seven weeks--the
boots are worth 38. 60.- people coming into the employment have ls.
footing to pay.
JOHN JAMES LLOYD. I am a pawnbroker, and live at No. 18, Strutton-
ground. I have a pair of boots, pawned by the prisoner for 13. 6d., on
the 20th of August, in the after part of the day.
Cross-examined. Q. Had you seen him before? A. Frequently.
SAMUEL BRYAN. I am a policeman. I took him in charge-he said
he knew nothing of it-I found the duplicate in the cell he was locked
up in.
JOHN JAMES LLOYD re-examined. This is the duplicate of them.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
GUILTY. Aged 30.-Confined Three Months.
2213. ALEXANDER WETHERLEY was indicted for stealing, on
the 13th of August, 1 plane, value 48.; 1 square, value 28.; and 1 auger,
value 18.; the goods of George Cock; and that he had been before con-
victed of felony.
GEORGE Cock. I am a carpenter, and live at Putney. On Friday, the
13th of August, I was at work on the top of a building at Putney-I went
to dinner at twelve o'clock, leaving some tools on the building--I missed
them about a quarter after one, when I came back--they were worth 78.-
these are them- produced.)
WILLIAM Parker (police-constable T 47.) I was on duty at Chiswick
on the 13th of August. I stopped the prisoner in Devonshire-row, with
one plane, one square, and an auger-I asked where he got them--he said
they were his own property, he had brought them from his own home;
that he took them to Hammersmith to pawn, but they refused to take
them-I stopped him five miles from Cock's premises, at a quarter to seven
o'clock, the same night as the robbery.
