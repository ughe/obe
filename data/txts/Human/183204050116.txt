THOMAS WARD. I am a labourer, and live in Beaures-street, St. George's, Middlesex - the prisoner lodged seven weeks with me, in the name of Harris; a man came occasionally, after some time, who was described as her husband. The prisoner left on Monday, the 19th of March, and never returned - she took the key with her; I got into the room, and missed these articles; I afterwards found her, locked in a room, at a house in Duke-street.
   WILLIAM OSMOND(Police-constable K 61.) I took the prisoner, and asked what she had done with the property; she said she had pawned them, but refused to tell where -I found thirty duplicates on her.
   DANIEL KENNEDY. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Shadwell. I have a sheet, pawned with me by a woman on the 17th of March; this is the duplicate I gave for it.
   JOHN ROSS. I am servant to Mr. Reynolds, a pawnbroker. I have a blanket and three sheets, pawned on different days - these are the duplicates given for them.
   MARY WARD. I am the prosecutor's daughter. These articles were furnished to the prisoner, when she took the lodging.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went for some money to redeem them, and should have redeemed them on Saturday -I gave the duplicates to the Policeman as soon as he took me.
     GUILTY. Aged 29. - Confined Six Months.
 1089.  ELEANOR MILLER was indicted for stealing, on the 25th of February , 1 sheet, value 2s., the goods of John Foy.
   THOMAS KEARNEY(Police-constable G 171.) On the night of the 25th of February the prisoner was given into my custody by the prosecutor - she owned she had taken the sheet to pawn, and given the duplicate to her husband.
 Prisoner. I said I had wrapped the bit of a sheet round my shawl, which was in pawn - it was of no value itself. Witness. She said she had wrapped it round her own shawl.
   JOHN FOY. I am a shoemaker, and have a house in Portpool-lane. The prisoner came with a man named Fitzgerald, and took a ready furnished room - she represented him as her husband; they lived together - I went into the room, and missed the sheet - I followed the man into the room, and gave him in charge; she acknowledged that she took it, and said the man was innocent, but the duplicate was found on him - I do not know that he was not her husband.
   JOSEPH HUDSON. I am a pawnbroker. I have a sheet, pawned with this shawl, on the 21st of February.
 Prisoner's Defence. The prosecutor was quite tipsy: he brought me down stairs to look for my husband; he gave me a glass of gin, and gave me in charge - I left all my own things there.
   JOHN FOY. She did not leave any thing in the room worth moving.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1091.  TUERESA REED was indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of March , 1 table-cloth, value 1s. 6d.; 1 shift, value 1s.; 1 apron, value 6d.; 1 shawl, value 1s. 6d.; 1 handkerchief, value 6d.; 1 pair of stockings, value 6d.; 2 caps, value 2s.; 1 shirt, value 1s. 6d., and 1 basket, value 1s., the goods of Philip Fruchard.
   MARY FRUCHARD. I am the wife of Philip Fruchard; he lives in Great New-street, Fetter-lane. On the 22nd of March I left the prisoner, who I knew before, at my house, about nine o'clock in the morning, when I went to my mother's; I returned at seven in the evening - she was then gone: I missed some Irish linen that night, and the next day I missed the shawl and these other articles.
 Prisoner. Her daughter has my shawl, and wears it now - she had her own washed in the hospital, and I lent her mine to put on. Witness. The prisoner had been in Bartholomew-hospital, where my daughter is now dying; this shawl is my daughter's, but was at my place.
   THOMAS GOLDING(Police-constable L 84.) I took the prisoner - she was wearing this shawl at the time, and had this basket in her hand, containing the tablecloth and other articles - she was then in Blackfriars, more than a mile from the prosecutrix's.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. She asked me to go and help her wash the next day - I saw her the night before, drinking with a man in a public-house, and that morning she said she had been out all night, drinking, and was not able to go to washing - I took the things to get washed by Sunday.
 MRS. FRUCHARD. I did not ask her to help me - I was going to wash on the day I lost these things; she came to me that morning, and I asked her to have a cup of tea, because she was in distress - I had asked her the night before to call on me: I left her taking breakfast, while I went to my mother's, in Compton-street.
 COURT. Q. Did you know where she lived? A. No; I had no conversation with her about washing - my daughter had not desired her to get them washed; they were in a pan where I keep dirty linen - I had been out to try to get my mother into the poorhouse; my husband was at home, and at work.
     NOT GUILTY.
