   NEW COURT .— Tuesday, April  12 th,  1842.
   Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1382. DAVID RENTON was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of April 1 watch value 3 l  .; I watch-key, value 10.; and 1 watch-guard, value 10 s  .; the goods of Archibald Mackinnon , from his person. ARCHIBALD MACKINNON. I am private in the 72nd Hinhlanders. On the 4th of April I was at the George and Dragon public-house in Hollabnd-street—I was drunk, and went up to bed—the prisoner went up stairs with me—I had a watch, key, and chain with me—the key now produced is mine—the watch has not been found—the key was fastened to the silver guard—the prisoner did not sleep with me.
   prisoner. Q.  Had you the watch on you when you went to bed. A.  I cannot say that I had. RICHARD PHELPS. I am shopman to Mr. Aldous, a pawnbroker, in Greek-street, Soho. This watch-key was pawned, by the prisoner on Tuesday morning, the 5th of April, between eight o'clock and half-past, for 1 s .
   prisoner. Q.  About how long would it take me to come from Holland-street and return again? A.  Hardly two minutes.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I know nothing of it. I was at the foot of the stairs with the sergeant of his party, there were seven or eight persons with him, that evening, and it might be some of them that did it. I saw no watch in his possession that night; there are a good many keys like that. ARCHIBALD MACKINNON re-examined.  I can swear to the key—it has two stones in it, one red and the other blue, and there is a little hole or scratch on the red stone, by which I know it—the prisoner has two stripes on his arm, which shows that he cannot be a man of bad character. GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Three Months.
 1383. ISAAC SAMUEL HALL was indicted for unlawfully assaulting Eleanor Willett , with intent, &c.; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 16.— Confined Six Months.
 1384. JOSEPH MOORE was indicted for a misdemeanor. JAMES POTTER. I live in Tyson-street, Bethnal-green—I was in the employ of Samuel Parker Noble, of Charles-street Fitzroy-square. On the 7th of April last year the prisoner came and said, Will you send a date, of stout to Mr. Tulk, of Duke-street, Westminster?—I said, "Yes"—before he left he said, "Will you lend me 10 s ., it will be re turned when the stout is sent to Mr. Tulk's"—I spoke to Mr. Noble, and he lent him the 10 s .—he is not here—I had never seen the prisoner before—he said he had come out without any money, and he had to buy-some thing for the young ladies—I supposed he meant Mr. Tulk's daughters—he did not say so. WILLIAM PRICE. I received the stout at Mr. Tulk's from Mr. Noble's man—it was brought in a cart.
