he marked "P" in a diamond, and numbered 1, 2, and 3--they were to be
delivered on the Thursday-he told me to call at at their counting-house in
Paneras-lane, on the following Saturday, for the money-be gave me a refer
ence to a Mr. Woolcombe, St. Michael's-alley, Cornhill I went there and
made inquiries of bim, and when I returned home I received a letter from
Gardiner and Holliday-I delivered the goods on 30th at Custom-house-
quay-on 31st July I went to their office, saw them, and asked for my money
they said they did not know that the goods had been delivered at Custom-
house-quay, as I was rather later with them than they expected, and I had
rather led them into a dilemma, by not letting them know they were there, and
therefore they could not pay me on that occasion-I called several times
afterwards for my money on some of those occasions I saw Seager-it
might bave been four or five days after I had delivered the goods that Seager
told me they had been shipped-Gardiner and Holliday were not present
then, I tried for some time to get my money, and was ultimately obliged to
get the assistance of my solicitor to go to them, I could not get my money
or my goods-I afterwards ascertained that the goods had been removed
from Custom-house-quay-I traced them to Johnson and Day's, the packers,
in Eldon-street, Finsbury-I afterwards went with Mr. Crauch, my soli
eitor, to Gardiner and Holliday's office, and then started to go to Cusell's
office in Lime-street, my father-in-law, Mr. Tucker, having received an order
in Cusell's name to inspect some tools-as we were going, we met Ousell-
Mr. Crauch told him that we were going to his office about some tools which
be was offering to sell --Cusell said, “What tools ? what tools do you mean,
I don't understand you !"-Mr. Crauch said, "Oh, you must know what I
mean, it is about those tools which Gardiner and Holliday have got from
this chest of mine Mr. Bond for shipping, and which they are now offering
for sale"-Cusell stammered a bit, and then said, “What shall I do for my
money?"-Mr. Crauch said, “We had nothing to do with his money," and
asked what money he meant-he said money he had advanced on them
Mr. Crauch asked what he had advanced on them-he said he could
not tell exactly he admitted that he and his friend Mr. Loup had
advanced 401.--as near as I can recollect he gave us to understand
that he had advanced 211., and Loup the other 211-Mr. Crauch, Cusell,
and I, then went together to Gardiner and Holliday's office we saw Holli-
day, who said Gardiner had gone to Ramsgate, to purchase & vessel- Ousell
said he must be paid, and he went away to fetch his friend Loup we were to
wait till he came back with him we waited about twenty minutes or half
an hour, and finding he did not come back we went in search of him to
Loup's office, in Aldermanbury--they were not there we went back and
found Loup and Cusell at Holliday's office, with Holliday-Gardiner was
not there-Seager was in the opter office-Loup said that if the 211, that
he advanced was paid he had no objection to the goods being given up-Mr.
Crauch had not mentioned to Loup the circumstances under which the goods
had been obtained from me a good deal was said-Mr. Crauch said in
Loup's presence, that the goods had been obtained for shipping, and they
were evidently trying to sell them for anything they would fetch, and if they
were not given up, or the money paid, we should go before the Lord
Mayor immediately this was about 16th Aug - it was then agreed that I
should receive my tools back upon Gardiner and Holliday's paying Loup
the 211.-1 subsequently got my goods back from Johnson and Day- I had
