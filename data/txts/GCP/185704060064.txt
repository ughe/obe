499. WILLIAM EDE, stealing 1 bank note for payment of 51., the
property of Louis Noverraz. MR SHARPE conducted the Prosecution.
Louis NOVERRAZ. I am a Swiss, and live at No. 38, James Street, Man-
chester Square. On 27th February I was looking for a situation the
prisoner came to me while I was in Oxford Street, looking at some photo
graphic portraits-he said to what perfection they brought them, and be
asked what I was doing; I said I was looking for a situation-he told me
he was acquainted with a butler who was going to leave his situation
because the family was going out of town, and he thought I should do for
the place, and he would introduce me to the butler about 6 o'clock-he
asked me if I would take a glass of ale, and if I would go with him
should have a chance to see the butler-we went into a public house near
where we were ; we had a glass of beer at the bar-he told me he was in
business as a poulterer-we then went out to see this butler, and he took
me to the parlour of the Coach and Horses-while we were in the parlour
another man came in, and he asked if that was a general room or a privata
room-I said, “I suppose it is public"-the prisoner and he began to bet
about the stone that they would throw at the skittles, and we went down
stairs to the skittle-ground, and the prisoner betted with the other man, and
he advised me to bet-I said, “You may bet yourself," but at lást I did
bet, and we played altogether-after they had betted about the stone we all
began to play- I lost 21. on the first game, and I would not play again-
the prisoner advised me to play again, and said I would be likely to win-
I put down 21. again, and lost the second game-we were playing all three
together when I lost the second time I would not play again, and the
prisoner advised me to play for 5l., and said I would be sure to win- I put
the 5l. on the third game, and I saw the prisoner and the other party
speaking privately together, and I thonght it must be wrong, that they were
robbing me, and I said I would go up stairs, and the other man gave me
back 21.- I had at that time given 51. to the other man by the prisoner's
advice-the game was not over-I went up stairs and spoke to the landlady
-the prisoner followed me the others remained down stairs-there were
two others besides the prisoner, but one did not play-when I came out of
the room from speaking to the landlady, the prisoner was in the house
went down to the skittle ground with the landlady, and the two men who had
been there were gonewe came up stairs again, and the prisoner was gone
-I saw the prisoner again a fortnight or twelve days afterwards in Berners
Street, Oxford Street; I went to him, and asked him if he recollected me
he said, “No”- I went to look for a constable to give him in charge, I
could not find one-I passed by the prisoner again, and he said, “Yes, I
recollect you ; how do you do?" -I came with him, and gave him in charge
to a constable ; I said I charged him with robbing me in skittle playing by
false pretences—the prisoner offered me 21. or 31., and then 41.; he made
me the offer more than once the policeman was present when he made the
last offer-he offered me 21.
