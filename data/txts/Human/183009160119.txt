 1731.  MARY WATERS was indicted for stealing, on the 19th of July , 1 counterpane, value 2s., and 2 sheets, value 2s., the goods of James Clark.
   LYDIA CLARK. I am the wife of James Clark. The prisoner lodged at our house from October till the beginning of July, when she was taken for robbing us; she had not left the house, but she had not slept at home the night before.
   DANIEL BROWN. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on the prosecutor's complaint; she denied having taken the property - I found the quilt in Laystall-street, not at a pawnbroker's.
   GEORGE UNDERWOOD. I am a pawnbroker. The prisoner pawned a sheet on the 17th of May, and another on the 28th of June.
 The prisoner delivered in a written Defence, stating that poverty had caused her to make use of the property, which the prosecutrix had allowed her to do on a former occasion.
 LYDIA CLARK. I never gave her leave to pawn anything at any time.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1732.  JOHN AGGUS was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 6th of September , of a certain evil disposed person, 12 boxes, value 12s., and 1.800lbs. of tinplates, value 30l., the goods of John Worrall Grazebrook , and George Munro , well knowing them to have been stolen, against the Statute, &c.
 MR. DOWLING conducted the prosecution.
 JAMES BROWN. I am an officer. I saw the prisoner; he said he wished me to go to Spicer-street, where he had some boxes of tin, as he had seen in the paper that some had been stolen, and he thought they might be them - he said he had received them from a man in  Bethnal-green, who drew them to the Flower-pot; the man left him there, and he drew them to his own house.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Then he came of his own accord, told you he had the property, and the worthy Magistrate committed him? A. Yes, I have been searching for the other man, but cannot find him.
     NOT GUILTY
 1733.  JOHN SAMUEL BLAIN, was indicted for feloniously receiving, on the 6th of September , of a certain evil-disposed person, 9 boxes, value 9s., and 600lbs. of tin plates, value 20l., the goods of John Worrall Grazebrook , and George Munro , well knowing them to have been stolen.
 MR. RYLAND conducted the prosecution.
   JOHN WORRALL GRAZEBROOK. I am in partnership with George Munro. In the beginning of this month, we lost a number of tin plates, and on the 7th of September the prisoner called, and said he had a quantity of tin plates, and asked if we would exchange some of them - we said if they were our mark and make, we should have no objection; Mr. Munro asked him if he had any objection to tell who he got them of - he said he had not, but he should not like his name to be mentioned, and he would point out the house of the person he had them from - Mr. Munro said we had lost a great many, and he was suffered to depart; we saw him in custody two days afterwards - we saw the boxes of tin plates.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. There is no doubt they are your property? A. No, the boxes have our marks on them; he took the officer to Aggus' house to show us the man - he was bailed.
   JAMES CRANSTONE. I am clerk to the prosecutors. On the day after he had been to our house, I went with Brown and Attfield to the prisoner's - we said, "We've come about some plates;" he said he had no objection to change plates with us, if we would give him others for them - he said he had bought them cheap, and they were too large; I took them to our place - Attfield asked him if the whole of the premises belonged to him; he said that was not a fair question - Attfield said, "I am an officer, and shall take you if you prevaricate so;" I asked if he had any receipt - he said No; a woman then came, and said she had an entry in a book, but it has no date to it; it states, "Bought nine boxes of tin plates, of Mr. Aggus" - I said if he would go and show us the person he bought them of, we would exchange them; we went to Mr. Aggus', but found nothing there - there was a cart in the place, with the name of J.  Aggus, Pott-street, Bethnal-green, on it; a girl said it was her uncle's, and he was gone into the country to buy some pigs - the prisoner said the plates had been paid for in a public-house opposite, that he did not take a receipt, for he was rather tipsey at the time, but there was a person who could prove it - he named the person, but I forget the name.   NOT GUILTY.
 1734.  WILLIAM COLLIS& JOHN FARRELL were indicted for stealing, on the 13th of July , 2 pieces of woollen cloth, containing in length forty yards, value 40l., the goods of Thomas William Williamson.
   JAMES GILL. I am warehouseman to Thomas William Williamson. On the 13th of July we gave Collis two cloths to deliver, and an order for two to be brought back; he did not return that day, which caused suspicion - they were to be brought from Mr. Rush's, Bridge-street, Westminster; he went out about ten o'clock in the morning - I think it was on Tuesday, and I saw him on the Saturday; his father called to say he had got his son, William, back - Collis had been in the prosecutor's employ about two years; I went, and saw him - he said he had obtained the cloths from Mr. Rush, and had by appointment met a boy, named Young; they took a coach, drove to Whitecross-street, and took them to the house of a man named Nathan, where they sold them for 10l. - they were worth upwards of 40l.; I have never seen them since.
