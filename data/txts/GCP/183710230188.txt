2403. BRYAN HENDREY and THOMAS SULLIVAN were indicted
for stealing, on the 29th of September, 1 iron chest, value 43. 6d. and
5 account-books, value 58.; the goods of Mary Bray.
MARY BRAY. I am single, and live in King-street, Holborn, in the
house of Mr. Guthrie. Between twelve and one o'clock on the 29th of
September I was in bed-there was a knock and ringing at the door, and
I saw two men-I spoke to the policeman, who came into the house
went to the street door and let the men in-I afterwards went into the
street, and saw an iron chest, containing some books, belonging to me
they had been taken from the front cellar of the house--there was a receipt
of the Bank of England for 4001. in it-Sullivan came next morning, at
half-past six o'clock, and I let him in to work.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Then he was there after you missed
the chest ? A. Yes.
WILLIAM GARLAND EDWARDS. I live with my father, in King-street,
Holborn. On Friday evening, the 29th of September, between tire and
six o'clock, I was going to No. 15, King-street, and saw the two prisoners
carrying an iron box-I knew it belonged to the prosecutrix - I have
seen it in the cellar.
CHARLES JONES. I lodge in Eagle-street, Holborn. On Friday even-
ing, the 29th of September, I was standing at the corner of Leigh-street,
and saw the two prisoners go by with the chest-I afterwards saw the
chest about half-past eight o'clock the same evening, at Denham's house,
in Eagle-street-Denham was behind the counter-I opened it, and found
some account books and other papers in it-I went to Miss Bray, and
knocked at the door, but could not make her hear-I at last gave her no-
tice of this.
WILLIAM DENHAM. I keep a marine-store shop in Eagle-street, Red
Lion-square-between five and six o'clock, on the 29th of September, the
chest was brought to my shop by the prisoners—they said it was their own
--that their master had given them leave to sell rubbish, and one thing or
another about the empty house, before they began to repair it, and that
Mr. Guthrie was their master- I gave them 2s. 9d. for it-I bought it as
old iron, at 28. 4d. per cwt.
GEORGE COLLIER (police-constable E 38.) I assisted in taking the
chest to Miss Bray's, and took Denham into custody.
John LEE. I am a policeman. I took Sullivan into custody at Den.
ham's.
ALEXANDER GUTHRIE. The house Miss Bray lives in is mine. Hen-
drey had been employed there as a labourer-I saw Sullivan there on
Friday-I saw the chest in the cellar on Thursday- I called Sullivan into
the cellar on Friday, and gave him directions respecting some stones-
said nothing about the chest-I gave him no authority to take it away, but
I spoke to the other prisoner, about some loose iron which was in the attic,
that he might take it and sell it, to get him some beer-1 said nothing
about the chest, but that he might take away what was of no use-the
chest was not in a part of the premises where I had control--I told Hend-
rey, up in the attic, that he might take away any old iron he found about
the house, and blacking bottles in the shop, and clear away the rubbish
I saw the chest the day before-it appeared to me as if it had been lying
there a long time, and was decayed - I mentioned it to Miss Bray, and she
said she kept her books there--the prisoners did not know of that con
versation—it had the appearance of going to decay, and was rusty, and in
a very bad state.
NOT GUILTY.
