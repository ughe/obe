Henry MILSTEAD (police-sergeant, K 12). On 29th Oct. I was
called to Mr. Vain--he gave Fitzgerald in charge for stealing a 500.-
note--she said she knew nothing of it—at the station the female searcher
brought her some tea and said, "What a bad woman you must be to
rob your master in that way"-she said, “I am sure I did not take
it, the woman who assists me in charring has got the run of the house, she
must have taken it and dropped it in the street, and my brother must have
picked it up "-I had cautioned her-next morning going to the office she
said her brother, Fitzgerald, her sister, Donovan, and herself, had been
to her sister's at Barking, and when she returned her mistress accused her
of drinking.
WILLIAM FRENCH. I am a pawnbroker, of Poplar, which is four miles
from Barking. On 14th Oct. Donovan came, and took two articles out of
pledge, amounting to 178. 4d.-I believe they had been pledged by her
sister Hawkins-she tendered me a new 501.-note--I asked how she got
it-she said some person from Barking had sent her with it to take the
articles out- I told her to take it back, and tell the parties to come them-
selves-next day Fitzgerald's sister-in-law came.
Cross-examined. Q. You saw nothing of Fitzgerald? 4. No.
WILLIAM SKINNER. I keep the Ship, Poplar. On 14th Oct., about
six o'clock in the evening, Donovan came, and called for three gallons of
beer and half a gallon of rum-she presented this 501.-note (produced),
and said she had it from her cousin, who had just come from sea-I said
I had got no change-I let her have 101.-she left the note with me, and
was to come next day for the balance-she came and had it, leaving 51.
in my hands, which I have now I wrote my name and address on it.
