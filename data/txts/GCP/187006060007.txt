GEORGE SOLOMON SYMONS, I was a wine and spirit merchant, in 1868,
carrying on business at St. Dunstan's Hill-on 18th June, 1868, I supplied
the defendant with wine amounting to 1001. 138. 9d. -I am a creditor for
that amount--the defendant gave me two acceptances, one for three months
and one for four months, dated let July--they have not been paid - I had
no idea when supplying these goods that the prisoner was selling through
Mr. Burton, by auction- I had no other transaction with the prisoner.
Cross-examined. Q. Have you your invoices ? 4. No-I left them at
home--this was not very cheap wine--it was about 32.. and 42s. a dozen
I think the lowest was 328.--the prisoner complained of it--I have no
recollection of his offering me 101. to take it back I can't say he did not
-Mr. Arnold came in in a friendly manner, to arrange between us-I
proposed to take off 8l. or 91-I don't remember the prisoner threatening
to sell it by auction--there are plenty of wine sales in the market-I am
not aware that it is the custom to send them to auction.
Cross-examined by Mr. SRRJEANT BALLANTINE. Q. Were the bills given
for the reduced amount, after the arrangement ? A. Yes.
JOHN ARNOLD (re-examined). I am a wine and spirit broker, of 23.
Great Tower Street-I am & creditor of the prisoner for 471. 168 ild-on
13th June, 1868, goods were supplied to the amount of 981. 18. 8d.; on 11th
July, 511. 18. 6d. and 371. 108. Ild. for duty--we received cash on account
25th July, 441.; on 18th July 521. ls. 4d., leaving 471. and 31. 4. 6d, for a
little matter of champagne--I was not at all aware that the prisoner was
selling goods through Mr. Burton-after the bankruptcy I met the defend-
ant in the street, and expressed my indigpation at bis second failure.be
said he was forced to do it, as his creditors pressed him so much.
Cross-examined, Q. Did he tell you he had a creditor from whom he had
borrowed money! A. I don't think so-I don't remember it-I had done
business with him for some years--I was a creditor under the first
bankruptcy-I dare say I commenced doing business with him in 1858 or
1859– I remember Ward becoming his partner--he was only & partner a
short time, and then they dissolved I heard that Ward took the stock,
agreeing to pay the debts, and pay Page his share ; but I have no knowledge
of it-I valued the stock when they first went into partnership-I was
employed to do so by Ward, if I recollect right-I did not value at the dis
solution--Page became bankrupt shortly after, and Ward before him, so I
heard; but I have no knowledge of the fact I was a creditor under Page's
bankraptoy-that was not for goods supplied to Page and Ward, but
supplied to Page only-I think before the partnership; but I am not sure
-after I received the dividend under the prisoner's first bankruptcy I con-
tinued to do some business with him, on an express understanding that the
cash should be paid every Saturday-it was a cash transaction merely, from
the day upon which the goods were supplied until the Saturday-that went
on for some time I never took bills from him-I never had any but cash
transactions with him after the first bankruptoy-I heard there was a dis-
pute between him and Symons, and I was asked to say what should be
done--I suggested that the wine should be returned, and the whole thing
concluded.
