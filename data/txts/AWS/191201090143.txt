DONALD THOMAs WALDRON, 3, Courtnay Road, Walthamstow. I got
into communication with prisoner by answering an advertisement in
the Daily Chronicle" of September 17, 1908. I saw him at Sugar
house Lane, Stratford. He said he wanted a young man to take con-
trol during his absence, to do all the tin cutting, look after the girls,
to do the buying, and if anyone came for any stuff I was to sell it.
There were three girls eleaning old beef and other tins. He showed
me round the place. He had a motor saw-bench, a machine for rolling
out the tins flat, and a pony and cart. He told me he had 8 ninety:
nine years' lease and was going to start making tin toys, lamps, and
that kind of thing. We entered into an agreement. My wages were
to be 30s. a week, and I paid him 220 as security. He paid me 30s. 8
week for six weeks, then he told me he could only give me 25s. I
agreed to that on the understanding that I had two half-days off for
the other 5s. He said he would return my E20 if I would wait till
he sold his house in Farrant Street.
Verdict, Not guilty.
