1024. WILLIAM WILSON, stealing 1 sack, value 2s. 6d.; the goods of
Edwin Mollett and another.
RICHARD Amos (policeman, K 390). On the night of 6th April, about
half-past eleven, I was on duty on Chadwell-heath, and saw a wagon going
towards London after it had passed I saw the prisoner come from behind a
shed, go to the back of the wagon, and take something off; I do not know
what-he crossed the road towards a hedge, and was ont of my sight about
two minutes-- he then crossed the road again and went towards his home-I
knew him before, and knew where he lived-just before he got home he threw
something over some timber, and went in doors immediately I went to the
place and found this sack ( produced)-I then met Crawford, and we went to
the prisoner's house and took him into custody. 65
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Have you not had a quarrel with the
prisoner? 4. No-he was a little the worse for liquor I never had a
dispute with him, and said I would do for bim some day-when I found the
sack, the wagon was gone on towards London, and I went after the prisoner
he passed us just at his own door, and wished me good night--this was
about eleven miles from London-- the prosecutor's is about twenty miles
from London.
Joun DANCEY. I am Mr. Mollett's horse-keeper. On this night I was
with the wagon, and had care of it-we were going towards London--there
was a sack of potatoes on the tail-it was safe at a quarter past eleven, when
we were about 100 yards the other side of Chadwell-heath-when I got off the
wagon at Ilford turnpike I missed it-this produced is it-it is Mr. Mollett's.
Cross-examined. Q. How do you know it? A. By these particular places,
and this hole is where I tied a string round to keep the potatoes in-I cannot
say how many sacks of the same sort my master has got I have been in his
service fifteen or, sixteen months.
JAMES CRAWFORD (policeman, K 258). I met Amos about half past eleven,
and in consequence of what he said I went to Wilson's house and took him into
custody-Amos produced this sack, the bottom of which was very dirty, as
if it had been in some ditch-the front of the prisoner's smock was all over
dirt the same as the sack.
CHARLES MOLLETT. I am in partnership with my brother, Edwin Mollett
-this sack is our property.

1025. JOSEPH HARRIS, stealing 41 yards of doeskin, valus 208.; the
goods of Horatio Talbot.
JOSEPH BENTON (policeman, K 381). On the evening of 1st March, I
saw the prisoner and another lad near Mr. Talbot's, the tailor's shop, at Strat-
ford Church-I watched them about the shop for three quarters of an hour
the other lad went into the shop and took something out, crossed the road, and
gave it to the prisoner, who was about three yards off when he went into the
shop-I ran to them, and tried to take the prisoner-be dropped this cloth as
I was grabbing him, and ran away-I kept close to him-a lad stopped him,
and I took him.
