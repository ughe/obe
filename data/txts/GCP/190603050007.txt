PRISONER (not on oath). About a fortnight before my arrest
I was living in a lodging house in the City. There a man, who
said he was agent for sixteen firms, told me he would find me
work. He said he had smashed his hand in a railway car.
riage and could not write. He sent me to various places to
order different things. One day he said that one of his young
governors in Salisbury Square was going to get married, and
the clerks and others had subscribed for a present to him;
afterwards he said a dinner was to be given, and asked me to
suggest a place. I had been to a club dinner in the Old Bailey,
and suggested that place. Later, this man said he had to get
some cigars for Mr. Boavan, of the Blind Institution; that Mr.
Beavan wished the order to go in his own name. At the
man's request I signed it in the name of Beavan. I met the
man outside St. Paul's Station; he told me to get the cigars
and take them to Mr. Beavan, and he would meet me in half
an hour. I went for the oigars, and the story of prosecutor
and Isaacs as to what happened is correct. The man I refer
to is not here. I gave the deteotive the best information I
could to find him, but the detective has not found him. I did
not obtain these goods by false pretences. When I said "I'm
done,” I was referring to being arrested; I have never been
in that position before.
Verdict, Guilty. Fifteen months' hard labour.

WILLIAMS, Alfred (35, tailor), and LEEKE, Ernest (61,
tailor). Both stealing four tes cloths, property of Alfred
Crampton and others, and feloniously receiving same. Leeke
pleaded guilty; Williams pleaded not guilty.
Mr. Bodkin prosecuted.
JAMES BROWN, Detective, City Police, I was in Cheapside
with Detective Anderton at 20 minutes to one on 16th Feb-
ruary; saw prisoners together. They were in conversation,
walking towards the west end of Cheapside, We followed
them to the corner of St. Paul's Churchyard. They went down
Paternoster Row through Canon Alley into St. Paul's Church
yard, walked up St. Martin's-le-Grand together, then turned sud-
denly back, walked down Paternoster Row through into St.
Paul's Churchyard, where they got again into deep converse-
tion. Leeke then walked off, followed shortly behind by
Williams. They walked round St. Paul's Churchyard into
