THOMAS LEADBEATER. I am a chair-maker, and live in the Borough-
road. The deceased was in my employment-he worked for me last on
the Wednesday before he died, the 17th of September-he did not come to
work after that, I saw him on Wednesday morning-he had been in my
employ eighteen or twenty months this time I have known bim much
longer, he was apprenticed to me seventeen years ago on one or two 00-
casions he appeared rather dull, that was all--I did not see him after
Wednesday.
HARRIET JONES. I am the wife of Essex Jones, a chair-maker, and live
in the Borough-road-I knew the deceased. On Wednesday afternoon
the prisoner told me he was taken very ill, with a violent vomiting, after
eating some apple-pudding-I sent my son to inquire after him from day
to day-I saw him on Wednesday when he went to his dinner, and saw
him no more till he was dead-I was present when he was taken off the
floor-I saw his forehead and nose bruised--the prisoner said, in the
course of the Saturday night, that she wished him to be opened to satisfy
the world, as he died 80 suddenly I was there till Sunday morning-I
washed out a cup of castor-oil and peppermint-water-there was nothing
in that I saw no other cup in the room.
Cross-examined. Q. There was an immediate talk about a Coroner's
inquest on him? A. Yes, they said they expected there would be an
inquest.
