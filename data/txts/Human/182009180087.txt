1012. SAMUEL NICHOLS was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of September , one pair of upper leathers, value 2 s. 6 d., the goods of John Gibbons.
   JOHN GIBBONS. I am a shoemaker. The prisoner was my journeyman. I missed a pair of upper leathers, and found them at Wenston's. He said he had them from Robinson, and, in consequence of what Robinson said, I took the prisoner and told him. Robinson said he had the pair of upper leathers from Sadler - he said nothing. Sadler was convicted of receiving them.
   ROBERT WENSTON. I am a shoemaker, and live in Goddard's-rents, Webb-square. Gibbons came to me, and saw a pair of shoes which I was making. I had the upper leathers from Robinson.
   JOHN ROBINSON. I am a shoemaker. I had the pair of upper leathers from a man named Sadler, who keeps a grinder's shop.
   ELIZA KING. I live in Angel-passage, Long-alley. I saw the prisoner sell the pair of upper leathers to Sadler, and saw Sadler sell them to Robinson.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. They were not his.
     GUILTY. Aged 40. Confined Three Months, and Whipped.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1013.  HENRY ALLWAY was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of July , one gown, value 7 s.; two shifts, value 10 s.; two pair of stockings, value 3 s.; one handkerchief, value 1 s.; one apron, value 1 s., and one frill, value 1 s., the goods of Mary Anderson.
   MARY ANDERSON. I lodged in West-street. On the 12th of July I went out about eight o'clock at night, and returned at two o'clock in the morning, when I missed these things.
   THOMAS CARLOS. I am a watchman. On the 12th of July, about one o'clock in the morning, I found the prisoner in Greyhound-yard laying down very tipsy, without his hat. His head laid on a bundle containing these things, he afterwards went home with a woman who left him in the dark.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I met her in Smithfield very drunk. She took me home with her. She went away and took my hat with her.
 - FREEMAN. I found his hat in the prosecutrix's room.
   ELIZA GAMBLE. I live at No. 19, West-street. The prosecutrix is a common prostitute.
     NOT GUILTY.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1014.  JAMES WADE was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of August , two spoons, value 5 s., the goods of William Sadgrove , and a watch, value 30 s., the goods of Esther Weston, spinster.
   WILLIAM SADGROVE. I have a warehouse in Moor-fields. I lost two spoons.
   ESTHER WESTON. I am a servant to Mr. Sadgrove. On the 3d of August the prisoner came to mend a square of glass about eight o'clock in the morning. I missed my watch and two spoons from the kitchen.
   THOMAS VANN. I am an officer. I apprehended the prisoner in his master's parlour. Mr. Sadgrove told him he suspected he had stolen these things. I found the spoons broken up in his waistcoat pocket, and the watch in his breeches pocket.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 21. Whipped and Discharged.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1015.  JOSEPH WILSON was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of July , thirty-nine yards of floor-cloth, value 20 s., the goods of James Baber.
   JAMES BABER. I keep a floor-cloth manufactury, at Knightsbridge. On the 9th of August, I found the prisoner in custody with this floorcloth, which is mine,
   WILLIAM PEARSON. I am a broker, and live in Old-street. On the 8th of July, between ten and eleven o'clock, the prisoner brought this floorcloth for sale, and asked 6 s. for it-it is worth 21 s., which made me suspect him. I asked him where he got it? he said a man sent him to sell it; I told him to send the man. He left the shop, went a step or two up the road, and loitered about for ten minutes. I kept my eye upon him. He returned and said the man was not at home, and demanded the floorcloth, or if I would buy it, I should have it for 5 s. I detained him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 1016.  JOHN THOMPSON was indicted for stealing, on the 29th of August , one spoon, value 7 s., the goods of the   Earl of Morton.
 SECOND COUNT. Stating it to belong to the    Baron Douglas.
   GEORGE PARKER. I am butler to the Earl of Morton. On the 29th of August, I was in the pantry, and laid the spoon in the window, which looks into the area. Between four and five o'clock in the afternoon, I saw the prisoner come down the area, and as he did not ring the bell I went to watch him, and saw him earnestly looking in at the window. Before I got to the hall door, I heard the pantry window open - ran back and saw his hand reaching in and take the spoon; I ran out to seize him - he threw the spoon down under the window and escaped - he had moved it about two feet. I followed him calling Stop thief! he knocked a man down, who tried to stop him. He was secured before I lost sight of him. He dropped it into the sink.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I heard the alarm, and ran to see what it was. I could not overtake him, and was stopped myself. He said I was the man, and had the spoon about me.
   GEORGE PARKER. re-examined. I said so, as I saw it in his hand, and did not find it until I returned.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant.
