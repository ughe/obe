JOHN HARVEY( Police Serjeant,  67 W ). On February 20th, about 4 p.m., I went to the prisoner's room and saw the child's body—it was taken to the mortuary—it had a number of bruises—I asked the prisoner how she accounted for them—she said that it was done by its head against the bedstead, and the sores by picking itself; that it was all right when it went to bed the night before, and had two slices of bread and an orange and that she was awoke about 3 a.m. by the shaking of the bed-stead and found the child in a fit and took it in her lap and went off asleep—she did not point out the place where she said that the child struck its head—I made this plan ( produced )—I formed an opinion that the child's head could not go through this triangular place. ARTHUR SEWELL( Police Inspector, W ). I made this plan of the prisoner's bed—the diameter of this triangular place is 6 inches—there was no place larger than 6 inches—I said to the prisoner at the inquest, "I shall arrest you for the death of your son"—she said, "I know, all right; they are all d—d liars."
   DR. LUCAS ( Re-examined ). I saw no indication on the sores as if they had been picked—I cannot say the width of the child's head.
   Prisoner's defence; "I am not guilty of the crime. The child had the rickets and was very bad and could not properly get about. I have never been locked up in my life. I had a bad husband, and picked up with another man and had children. I thought the child was getting better. I was never a bad mother to him, and I think it very hard that I should be blamed for it." GUILTY.— Seven Years' Penal Servitude.
