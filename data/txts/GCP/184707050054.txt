Mr. Phinn. Q. Is your ironmongery business carried on under the same
roof as your brother's curriery business was ? A. Yes; it was carried on
separately- I was not accused of helping to remove his goods, to conceal them
from the assignees- I did not assist my brother, or Wiltshire, to conceal his
goods from the assignees- I was examined privately at the Bankruptcy.
court; but not regarding the 921., or my interview with Mr. Bickmore--my
brother never brought me to the Bankruptcy-court when he was examined
about the 921.- I believe I was present once when my brother was ex-
amined; but I was not examined- I heard Mr. Bick more there state that no
application had been made to him for the money by my brother-my brother
did not then ask me to be a witness for him--that was in the year 1843-it was
in the autumn, I think I was not there when Mr. Duncan was my brother's
counsel, nor when my brother was examined about the 921. on a previous
occasion-I was there in Nov, or Dec., I think-he was then examined about the
921.- I think Mr. Bickmore was examined the same day-I was not examined
as a witness then when I saw Mr. Bickmore, while my brother was in gaol, I
commenced the conversation by telling him I was going to Chelmsford after
my brother-I did not ask him anything about money, before he spoke about
money to me-he told me had received a large sum of money from my
brother, and when my brother returned, he would see and settle with him-
he did not say what the sum of money was- I did not ask him to give my
brother some money to help him to come out of prison-I called on bim
again in the evening with my brother, but did not see him-I think I next
saw him in Nov. ; that was before my brother became bankrupt-I think it
was in 1843; the same year in which he went to prison-I then had the
conversation with him that I have stated-Mr. Payne was my brother's at:
torney in the bankruptcy-I saw him in Court about the time the discussion
was going on as to the 921.- I did not state to him the conversation I had
bad with Mr. Bickmore-I never had any conversation with Mr. Payne-I
told my brother about it when he came home-I don't know that I said any.
thing particular about it to any one but my brother.
MARY WILLET. I am a widow, and am the mother of the defendant and of
last witness. In March, 1844, my husband was living-but my son Jobn carried
on the business as an ironmonger, at Cogglesball-I know Mr. Bickmore-
about March, 1844, he came to our shop for some goods-he looked some out,
and I think he had them-my son John was there-Mr. Bickmore asked me if
Joseph was at home I said “No ;" and I asked him for my husband's ac
count, my son Joseph's account, and the account of the money he had re-
ceived: and I asked him, was it 901. or 921., or what was it-he said, "Your
son Joseph and I shall not fall out, but we shall make it right one day"--that
was all the answer he gave-I said nothing more to him.
Mr. Puinn. Q. I believe you were examined at the Court of Bank-
ruptcy. A. Yes-I don't recollect the Commissioner threatening to commit
me for not answering properly-I was not present on either occasion when
my son was examined about the 921.-I knew on both occasions that
he was going to London to be examined about it; but I did not come
forward.
GUILTY.-Confined One Month, and then Transported for Seven Years.
