I am the husband of the last witness, and keep a tripe-shop in Walworth-road—I buy the horses to slaughter—on the 25th of August, I went to the Waggon and Horses, public-house, in consequence of what my wife said—when I came home I found the pony tied to the back of my house—I asked her what sort of man it was—she said an elderly man in a fustian coat—I went and saw nobody in the public-house but him—It might be between ten and eleven o'clock—It was neither of the prisoners—he was fifty years old, and I think lame—I said, "Have you got a horse to sell?"—he said, "Yes"—I drank with him, as he had apint of porter—I went and saw the pony—he said it was worth 30 s .—I said, "No"—he said, "Will you give 25 s .?"—I said, "No, it did about the place for half an hour after that, I dare say, and the pony was still tied up—In about a quarter of an hour I saw him taking it away towards the Rockingham Arms, New Kent-road—I knew nothing of it being sold till the policeman came and asked if I was not about buying a pony on Tuesday; I went to Union Hall, I there saw the same pony—I have not seen the man since—I should know him if I did—I never saw either of the prisoners near him—the Elephant and Castle is a place where ponies are often exposed for sale by men out of the country—I have bought many myself and seen hundreds bought.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I had 1 s . in my pocket when I bought it—I could only pawn for ten more, and when I came back I could only give him 11 s .—I said, "I had not enough, will it make any difference if I give you the rest to-morrow?"—he said, "Not at all, but you must give me my cloth and saddle back"—we had sixpenny-worth more gin—In about half an hour he brought me a halter—I was to give him the money and halter the next day—I told him any body about the elephant and Castle would tell him where I was if he inquired—I went over the water, and when I came back, I heard of the disturbance—I went to the policeman, and said, "I would meet him next morning, as I understood I was wanted—I went according to promise, but he did not come—a man was apprehended last Saturday, but it did not prove to be the man—Brown, the policeman, was with me two days looking for the man.
