we went to search if we could find any of the property she had lost but found none - she pointed to a house, we went in, and while looking below (there was a man and his wife in bed) we heard a window pulled up, and footsteps up stairs; we immediately went up stairs and met Daley coming from towards the window - the prosecutrix then came up stairs and said that was one of them, that was Daley, and on looking round there were some more men asleep in a bed; she did not point out any of them, but on the third bed, on coming towards the door, we saw a heap on the bed, and on pulling the coverlid down we found O'Donnell underneath, with his clothes on; we tried to awake him, to get sense into him, but he appeared quite speechless, we could not get a word from him - the man and woman who were below came up stairs, and said he had not been out of bed since nine o'clock that morning; on taking hold of his hand to look at it, the sleeve of his jacket was all over wet mud - we took Daley into custody, and brought him out at the door, and on coming out of the door of the house the watchman pointed to the things that laid in the court; (there were three beds in the room, the prosecutrix did not recognise any body on the second bed); the watchman picked the things up and took them to the watch-house with Daley, and as we turned to come on I saw a handkerchief lying down, which I picked up, and told the girl to tie it round her neck, which was quite bare; and when we came just to the bottom of the court I met Cooper, the inspector; I delivered the girl to him, and remained close in sight of the premises till he returned again with the watchman - they went into the house and brought O'Donnell down, and took him to the watch-house - I remained behind; I know nothing more.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you tell the prosecutrix she had better go home? A. I did; she stood with O'Donnell close at the corner; the others were at a distance from them, at the turn of the court - she said one of the men had brought her there.
 Q. Then of course she gave you to understand there were other men there? A. No.
 COURT. Q. What were her words? A She said she had been brought there by one of the men: those were her words; that was not the first time, but when we went to her assistance - when I first went to her she said nothing about any men.
 MR. BARRY. Q. She pointed out O'Donnell in bed? A. We turned the clothes down, and she said she could swear that was one of the men; we could not make him understand, speak, or move - she said Daley was one of the men, that was before we saw O'Donnell - the man and woman who came up were both undressed; there were seven inmates altogether - I do not know their names; I knew Sheridan by sight, as I had his brother in custody a little while ago; I did not know him by name till then - I have ascertained that he keeps the house; I have been a patrol these five years - I believe there are twelve houses in the court, several of them had their windows up and people looking out; I saw none of the inhabitants of the court down below except the people of that house; a window next door but one to the house was open, and a person looking out at No. 10 - the next house to that is empty; there is a small gutter in the court - I found the handkerchief close to the end of the gutter, it was a pocket handkerchief - I found one patten lying by the side of it.
 COURT. Q. She pointed out Daley as one of the men? A. Yes; he was coming from the window, with his coat off - he was the first we saw on going into the room.
   ROBERT WOODS. I am a watchman. On the morning of the 30th of March, about a quarter before two o'clock, the patrol called me for a light - I was opposite Playhouse-yard; I went up to Lion and Lamb-court with him, and saw O'Donnell, Daley and Burke, all three standing there together - I knew Burke before, but not the other two; I am certain of the prisoners - the patrol told the prosecutrix to go home; she went away -O'Donnell went after her in a few minutes, and I saw him come back with her; Burke came down Playhouse-yard over to me, and spoke to me - then Daley and two or three more, who were standing there, went up Playhouse-yard; I had spoken to them before that - I heard a scream of Watch! called out; I went up Lion and Lamb-court, where the scream came from, and the prosecutrix was just getting up; she pointed to No. 8 - I went in there with the patrol and another watchman; I saw Daley, who had just got his coat off, going to bed - several more were in bed there; I saw O'Donnell in bed with the clothes covered all over him - Coombs said Daley was one of them; we took him to the watch-house with her; as we went along Daley said it was not him, and said, "Here is a shilling for you to drink, it was not me, and I nudged you when you were there; it was O'Donnell;" O'Donnell was in bed covered with the clothes, and appeared to be very much in liquor; Coombs said at the watch-house that she could swear to him by his voice - she had pointed him out when he was in bed; when we came down, Finch picked up a shawl, bonnet, pair of pattens, and a handkerchief, and gave it to me to take to the watch-house - they were all rolled up together.
 Cross-examined. Q. All rolled up together? A. There was a shawl, patten, and bonnet rolled up together when it was given to me; I did not see them picked up- I do not think  Brown  the watch-house keeper is here; he was examined at Worship-street, and sworn.
 Q. Where were you standing at the commencement of this? A. Opposite Playhouse-yard; I had been walking about two or three minutes - I did not see the prosecutrix come up to the court the first time; I might be in another part of the lane - she did say she had been brought there by one of the men.
 COURT. Q. When did she say so? A. I forget now; it was when I was up the court - I do not know whether it was after she was told to go home; I went up the court when I heard the screams.
