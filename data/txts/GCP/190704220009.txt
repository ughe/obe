CHARLES Ellis, off-license beerhouse keeper, 387, Mile End Rond.
On the evening of March 18, about quarter past 10, prisoner came
into my beerhouse and called for half a pint of "old" in & oan, the
price being 3d. and id, on the can. He tendered in payment a two-
shilling piece, which turned out to be a bad one. I served him
myself and gave him 1s. 8d. change. Afterwards I had doubts
about the coin, and, on putting it to my teeth, found it to be gritty.
I rushed out at once and found him half-way down Churton Road
talking with another man. They walked along till they came to
Grove Road, where I saw him have a drink of beer and hand the
can to another man. I caught him by the coat ouff and told him to
come along. He said, "What for!" and I said, "You will see when
you get back." I sent for a constable and told him prisoner had
tendered to me a bad two-shilling piece. Prisoner said he had never
seen me before, but I am certain he was the man. I gave the coin
to the constable.
Police-constable 620 K. I was called to Mr. Ellis's beerhoune
about quarter past 11 on March 18, where Ellis gave prisoner in
charge on a charge of uttering a counterfeit two-shilling piece. Pri-
soner made no reply. I searebed him there and then and found on
him 2s. and 3d. in bronze. I then took him to the station. I also
found upon him some tobacco and a copy of "Answers."
