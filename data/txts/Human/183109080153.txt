 1910.  JAMES BLAND was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of August , 1 coat, value 18d., the goods of William Curtis.
   WILLIAM CURTIS. I am a hackney-coachman. On the 31st of August my coach was on the stand at Whitechapel; I left it for about five minutes, about a quarter before ten o'clock at night, and when I returned my box-coat was gone from the seat - I went to the station-house, and saw it; the prisoner was in custody.
   HENRY COTTON. I am a Policeman. I was on duty, and saw the prisoner going down Wentworth-street, about two or three hundred yards from the coach-stand, carrying this coat; I asked him where he got it - he said a man had given him 2d. to carry it down the street; I asked him what man, and he said he did not know where to find him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 15. - Confined Three Months.
 1911.  MARY SMITH, JOSEPH BURLEY, JAMES SMART, and CHARLES WILLIAMS were indicted for stealing, on the 18th of July , 1 watch, value 4l.; 2 seals, value 10s.; 1 chain, value 6d., and 1 watch-key, value 2d., the goods of Thomas Sawyer , from his person.
   THOMAS SAWYER. I am a revenue-officer. On the 18th of July I had been at the Pitt's Head, Bath-street, Bethnal-green; I went at half-past four o'clock in the afternoon - there was a meeting of the weavers' society; I had not been playing at skittles; about twelve o'clock at night, as I was walking up to the skittle-ground door to come out. I saw the prisoner Williams' head above all the rest; I noticed him - when I came up, he was standing with his back against one side of the door-way; he took a step, and came in front of me, as close as he could to me - and at that moment I felt my watch going out of my pocket; I wanted to get my hands down, but I could not, as some other parties confined them: there were several persons about, but I cannot tell how many - I collared Williams and said he had got my watch, I was immediately hustled by some persons who were about; they shoved me away from him, and he immediately went off and I saw no more of him - I cannot say I noticed either of the other prisoners.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. You do not mean to say, that this meeting of the trade lasted till twelve o'clock at night? A. They had a dinner there; several of them were gone, but not all - there had been dancing; I had not danced; there was 18d. to pay for a ticket for two persons to go into the skittle ground - I had dined there, I had not seen Williams in the skittle ground; there might be half a dozen or more persons about - no woman asked me what I wanted with her husband; I was as sober as I am now; I will not swear I was not any the worse for liquor - I might be a little, but no great deal, I swear I was sober.
   JOHN THOMAS. I am a Policeman. On the 19th of July I was in Ratcliff-Highway, at seven o'clock in the evening: I saw Smith, Burley, and Smart - Smith was five or six yards before the others, and she appeared to be quarrelling; I noticed them, and passed on till I came to King David's-lane, then returned, and saw them still in the highway; I saw Burley deliver something into Smith's hand - she raised her hand up, and I saw it was a watch and appendages - she went on to Mr. Hemming's shop, went in there, and stopped about two minutes - I went on to New Gravel-lane, and saw two officers - I told them my suspicious, and showed them Burley and Smart, who were standing in an uninhabited house, next door to the shop where Smith was; Smith came out of there, and went into a Jew's shop - he is a sort of general dealer; she came out in about half a minute - my brother officer said, "Where's the watch?" - I said, "Can't you see it is concealed under her apron, in her right hand" - we followed them all till they came to Bluegate-fields; Smart then parted from them, and was going up Bluegate-fields - one of my brother officers took him, and the other took Smith.
   JOHN HENNAN. I am an officer. I secured Clark; I said, "I want you" - he said, "What for?" - I said,"Two of your party are stopped for stealing a watch, and I think you know something about it" - he said, "I know nothing of it, I have only been with them half an hour;" Smith said at the watch-house that she had had the watch of a young man, named Lacy, whom she had been living with for eight days - I fetched Lacy, and he denied having had a watch for some years.
   HENRY LEE. I am an officer. I have heard what the two last witnesses have said - it is correct; I took Smith; I asked what she was going to do with the watch which I found on her - she said a sailor, named Stacy, or Lacy, gave it her; I fetched him, and he denied it.

   JOHN CHUMLEY. I am a labouring man. I was there, and saw Burley inside the skittle-ground - Williams was outside; about twelve o'clock I heard that Sawyer had lost his watch - I did not see Smart nor Smith.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.Were you acting as waiter there? A. Yes; the prosecutor was there from four o'clock till twelve - he was nearly outside of the door when we heard of the loss of his watch.
