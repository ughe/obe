   Prisoner's Defence.  I was there three weeks; the last twelve days I was in rather distressing circumstances; I was going out on the Saturday morn ing, and, when in the street, she called me and told me as I was going to re ceive money I might make use of a blanket or sheet; I told her I would not do so if it was not requisite, but if I was disappointed I should feel obliged if she would allow me to do so. GUILTY. Aged 31.— Recommended to mercy.— Confined Three Months .

 1248. WILLIAM BROWN was indicted for feloniously receiving 7 knives, value 3 s  .; 13 forks, 6 s  .; and 2 printed books, value 15 s  .; the goods of Joseph Singleton , clerk; well knowing them to have been stolen.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. REV. JOSEPH SINGLETON. I live at Bay Braham, in Cambridgeshire. On the 14th of Dec. my house was broken into—the persons got in through a larder window—I lost, among other property, a considerable quantity of plate, two dozen knives, two dozen forks, and a number of books—since the 26th of Feb., two books, seven knives, and thirteen forks have been produced by the officer—they formed part of the property stolen from my house that night—the books are a copy of "Milton," and the "Life of Christ."
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  I believe the parties you sus pect have been prosecuted? A.  No—these are London knives, and have "Alderman, Barbican," on them—they are not of a very recent purchase—here is one of another description, and of another maker—I lost two knives of this sort—there was some writing in this "Life of Christ," but the leaf has been taken out.
   MR. CLARKSON . Q.  I believe the persons have been prosecuted, but not for this charge? A.  I am not certain whether they were in this robbery; it was proved that they had my property in their possession—their names were Cuttriss, Hall, and Bennett—I am firmly persuaded that these knives and forks are mine—I had some of the same description and the same maker. JAMES BRANNAN( police-sergeant G  20.) I know the prisoner—I was a witness against Cuttriss and Bennett at the last Chelmsford assizes—I have never seen them with the prisoner—on the 26th of February I went to the prisoner's house, Nelson-street, Hackney-road, with Mr. Clark, the superin tendent of the Essex constabulary—the prisoner opened the door—I said, "I want you"—he said, "What for?"—I said, "I will tell you"—I went in—I saw a female take a key off the mantel-piece and go up stairs in a hurried manner—I follower her—she came down, and when I came down after her the prisoner was gone—Clark went in pursuit of him—I waited there some time—when Clark came back we searched, and I found these knives and forks
