   Cross-examined by MR. MAHON . Q.  Did you tell him you were going to prosecute him? A.  I did not—I did not tell him I would not prose cute him if he made a statement—I used no inducement to get a con fession—I had been up stairs in his room an hour before I found the goods in his box—on my wife's telling me he intended to leave, I went to look under his bed, thinking he might conceal something there, but found nothing—I did not particularly look at the boxes—I saw them—I should know them again—I cannot say whether they had common locks or not—when they were brought down, he said, "Somebody has been at my trunks"—I had been up stairs a quarter of an hour before—Mrs. Good ings lodges at my house—she said that I had been to his box—she said she saw me at the boy's box—I certainly acknowledge that I went into hit room, to search his bed, and I saw one of the boxes—there was a bundle of dirty clothes there—I have known the prisoner many years—I do not think he was in my service in 1831—I will not swear he was not—I did not follow him to two or three shops, to bring him back to my employ—I know Mr. Decrow—he is the same trade as me—I never went there to him, nor did I speak to him in the shop—I have been in the shop, but never while he was there—I know Mr. Hale, in Pratt-street, Lambeth—the prisoner was there—I did not go to him there—I have been to Hale's shop, he was there at the time I went—I went on business—I did not speak to him about coming back to my service—he has called on me once or twice when I have, been standing at my door—I was never at Mr. Poulter's shop in my life—I do not know him—I know he was in Mr. Poulter's service—he came to me, and wished to return to me—I did not ask him—he wanted too high wages for me to take him again—I do not believe he ever lived with me but once before the last time—I will swear it.
   Q.  Were you at Epsom-races, this year? A.  I drove my cart there—I had no words with the prisoner on that day—I did not a busehim, or swear at him—I had no quarrel with him whatever—the officer has the boots—they are children's boots and shoes—there is no mark besides "No. 7" on them, and I can swear to the manufacture, by the workmanship—I know the workmanship, and have the manufacturer here—there is no stamp on them or number, but I can swear to the make of them.
   COURT . Q , What workmen do you keep? A.  Several; I have a Wholesale manufacturer, who supplies me—they are not made in my house—he supplies other people as well as me—these are the boots—they have no private shop-mark of my own—I bought the shoes of an other person, who makes goods, and travels from shop to shop to sell them—I missed a great number of boots and shoes of all sizes—when the pri soner produced the boxes they were locked—I had never seen them before he Drought them down—I never saw the inside of his box.
