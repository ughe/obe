   SARAH ROBERTS. I am the wife of Edward Roberts- we live in Nottingham-court, on the second floor, and have but one room; the landlord does not live in the house. On the 14th of April I went out about half-past nine o'clock in the morning, and locked the door; I returned about half-past ten, and found it broken open - every thing was in confusion, and I missed these things; I had left the scissors on the table - I do not know the prisoner.
   MARY KENNEDY. I live in the kitchen of this house. I met the prisoner coming down stairs that morning, rather after half-past nine o'clock - she bade me good morning; I never saw her before, but am sure she is the woman- she went out, and I took no farther notice till Mrs. Roberts came in and missed her things; I then told her, and at half-past four o'clock I met the prisoner in Short's-gardens, and detained her.
 SARAH ROBERTS. I went out with Kennedy to look for the prisoner, and at half-past four o'clock saw her, and gave her in charge to Wadey, who found my scissors on her, but nothing else - she said she had bought them for 1 1/2d., but I swear to them.
   JAMES WADEY. Roberts gave the prisoner into my charge in Short's-gardens, for breaking into her room and robbing her - she denied it; I found a pair of scissors on her, which she said she gave 1 1/2d. for - she did not say when.
 SARAH ROBERTS. I had these scissors ground and new rivetted about a month ago, and had a point made to cut button-holes, as I do slop work; I have had them above two years.
 Prisoner's Defence. I am sorry I bought them if they were stolen - if I had liberty I could bring the woman forward who I bought them of; I do not know her name, but know her by sight.
     NOT GUILTY.

 948.  THOMAS SKUCE was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling-house of Jane Hills , on the 31st of March, and stealing 1 box, value 1d., and 6lbs. of iron, value 4d., her property.
 JANE HILLS. I rent two rooms in  Half Nichol-street, Bethnal-green, and also hire a cellar - the landlord does not live in the house. On the 28th of March I saw this box of old iron in my cellar - the cellar is divided into two parts; I have the back part, the other is not used - my part was locked, but there is a hole from the front cellar which a boy could creep through from the yard; he could then open a door in my cellar, which is neither locked nor bolted; it is merely boards which form part of the partition.
 GEORGE ALLCOCK. I was an officer when this occurred. I now live at Bethnal-green. On the 31st of March I was in Half Nichol-street about half-past eight o'clock, and saw a boy coming along with something under his arm; I went over and found it was this box covered over - in consequence of what he told me, I went to the next house to the prosecutrix, where the prisoner's mother lives, and found the prisoner there; I asked if he gave his brother that box to sell - he voluntarily said that he did; I asked where he got it - he said out of the prosecutrix's house; he is older than his brother who I had met with the box; it contains old iron, and is worth 5d.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I found it in my mother's cellar.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY (of stealing only.) Aged 16. Whipped and Discharged.

 949.  JOHN CARLISLE was indicted for unlawfully procuring 12 counterfeit shillings, with intent to utter the same.
 MESSRS. SCARLETT and ELLIS conducted the prosecution.
 GEORGE STARR. I keep the Cross Keys public-house, Wood-street, Cheapside. On the 6th of March, about seven o'clock in the evening, the prisoner came in and asked for a glass of spruce and the newspaper - I gave him the spruce, and directed him to the coffee-room to read the paper; he gave me a shilling in payment, I gave him a sixpence and 4d. - I afterwards found it was a bad shilling; he was then gone - I thought he was in the coffee-room, but he had not gone there at all; I threw the shilling, into the fire, and it melted down like lead almost immediately - on Saturday, the 13th of March, he came about the same hour, and asked for a glass of spruce and the newspaper; I left my man to serve him - I recognized him and said, "You are the very man who gave me the bad shilling last week;" he then offered me a good one for it - he had not paid me any thing that night; I called in the constable who took him - he paid a good half-crown for what he had, and as the constable took him into the room he dropped a shilling, which I marked and gave to Earl, observing that it was a bad one; I am certain it dropped from him.
   FRANCIS EARL. I am a constable of Cripplegate. I received the prisoner in charge about seven o'clock in the evening - as I took him from the bar to the parlour, to search him more conveniently, a shilling dropped on the ground; I have every reason to believe it fell from him -I found in his waistcoat pocket twelve counterfeit shillings and four good sixpences, wrapped in a piece of rag and then in paper.
   JOHN FIELD. I am an inspector of the Mint. The shilling produced is counterfeit, it is Britannia metal, not worth a farthing; the twelve others are also counterfeit, and cast in the same mould - Britannia metal weighs rather more than one-fourth less than silver.
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Confined Six Months.
