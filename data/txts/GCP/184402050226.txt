Mr. Clarkson conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES MEAD. I am beadle and engineer of the parish of St. Olave, Southwark
-I have charge of the engine-after the fire at St. Olave's it was kept in the arch
No. 9, at the bottom of King's Head-yard, Tooley-street-I kept the key of
the arch. On the 23rd of Jan. I had my attention called to the archway--the
evening before, the carpenter, whom I brought, secured the arch-door with a
padlock-the stand-pipe and branch were there safe at eleven o'clock that
night-my attention was called to it in the morning by a policeman, saying
the place was broken open-I went about half-past seven o'clock-I then
missed the stand cock, pipe, and branch-the prisoner keeps a marine-store
and rag shop in Bermondsey-street-on Friday, the 26th, I saw the stand cock,
and branch, which I missed, in the possession of the policeman who brought it
from Mr. Paine's, in the lane-the prisoner's shop is about two or three hun-
dred yards from Tooley-street.
Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. Was that all the property which
was produced ? A. No, here is more-it was entire when I saw it the night
before, and was worth between 31. and 41. when new-the prisoner's shop is
in a very bad neighbourhood-there are plenty of bad persons about where it
was stolen from.
JAMES MILLERY. I am in the employ of Mr. Paine, brass-founder, Shoe-
lane. I know the prisoner-between ten and eleven in the morning of the
24th of Jan. I purchased these thirty-nine pieces of metal of him--they would
make a stand-cock and stand-branch of a fire-engine if they were entire-it
was broken when I bought it- I gave him 5d. a pound for the brass, and 8d,
for the copper-I paid him 78. 5d, altogether-there was a short man with
him about half an hour after, I delivered the metal to the officer.
Cross-examined. Q. There was nothing to attract your attention as being
suspicious ? A. Nothing-I only took about a minute to buy it-1 had
often bought of the prisoner before I went with the policeman to his shop on
the Thursday.
GEORGE WARDLE (City police-constable, No. 325.) I took the prisoner on
Thursday, the 25th of Jan., at his own house, in Bermondsey-street- I told
him I came respecting some metal he had sold to Mr. Paine, in Shoe-lane, on
the Wednesday-he said, "I have sold some metal to Mr. Paine"--I said I
was a policeman, and the metal be sold belonged to a fire-engine--be said he
had sold no such metal as that to Mr. Paine-I said I must take him.
THOMAS BARNES (City police-constable, No. 334.) From information I
received, I went to Mr. Paine's, on Wednesday, the 24th-I there found this
metal, with the name of the parish on it-it has been indented, but the name
is very distinct-it was by Mr. Paine's request that I looked at it.
RICHARD FULLER (police-constable M 120.) I was on duty at seven
o'clock in the morning of the 24th of Jan., and noticed the arch in which the
engine was kept-at a quarter past seven I found the arcbway was broken
open, and part of the engine gone--I found the padlock in the mud.
John HIGHWOOD (police-constable M 102.) I was on duty by this arcb-
way on the night of the 23rd, and morning of the 24th--the lock was on safe
at twenty minutes past six o'clock.
JAMES Mead re-examined. These are the pieces which, when entire, would
form the stand-cock and stand-branch of our engine.
Nor GUILTY.
Before Mr. Recorder.
