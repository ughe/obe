Detective-sergeant ALBERT Hawkins, Stoke Newington. I wm
present when the prisoner Friend and two other men were discharged
by the Justices at Dorking Police Court on August 9. I told Friend
I was going to arrest him and take him back to London for stealing
two horses from Chingford on July 12. He said, "I never stole
from Chingford. I was at my house getting a cup of tea for my
missis at eight o'clock on the morning of July 12. She was expecting
to go to bed (to be confined). I live at 542, Old Ford Road, and
went out to get some butter, and when I came back with it I met
a man named Wado. He had two horses with him, and I asked him
if he was going to the Barbican. He asked me if I knew anyone
who would buy them, and I said I knew several people. I asked
him how much he wanted for them, and he said £30 for the two. I
told him if he liked he could go with me and put them on the rail
at London Bridge. I knew a man named Luck. who would buy them.
I took the butter home and then went with Wado. I sent a wire to
Luck to meet me at Dorking Station, and Mr. Luck was there and
met us. Wado led the horses to Mr. Luck's place, and I jumped up
with Mr. Luck in his trap. The horses were put in the van and tried,
and Mr. Luck gave me £8 10s. in gold and two ponies, which were
put on the rail for London Bridge, and Wado took them away. I
got a sovereign out of it for my trouble; Wado gave it to me." Prom
Old Ford Road to London Bridge Station is about four miles. I
