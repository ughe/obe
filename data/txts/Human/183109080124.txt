I was locked up on Sunday, the 21st of August, about a misunderstanding with my master: the female prisoner was there - I heard her say she had lost her money; she then said she had found it - she counted fifteen sovereigns, and put them into something she had taken them out of before; I did not see what it was.
   HENRY LENNON. I am an officer. I took charge of the prisoners.
   GEORGE PHILLIPS. I am an officer. I received information - I searched Lethulier's sister, and found six sovereigns on her.

   FREDERICK SMITH. I am a shoemaker. The prisoners each ordered a pair of shoes of me on the Monday - I was to get them done that night; they paid me 10s. 6d. on them.

 1801.  JAMES BLACKFORD was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of August , 60 yards of calico, value 25s., the goods of Robert Philps and another; and that he had been before convicted of felony.
   ROBERT PHILPS. I live in High-street, Poplar, and have one partner; we are linen-drapers. On the 7th of August I lost this calico from my shop, but I knew nothing of it till the officer came - it had been safe at nine o'clock that evening, and the officer came at eleven.
   AARON GERMAIN. I am an officer. I came down my heat, and stood against the toll-bar; a man crossed into the road; he saw me, and turned round - I followed him, and saw the prisoner with this parcel in his apron; I took him, he said he had it from a pawnbroker.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, stating that the goods were given to him to carry by a man named Scott.
   WILLIAM MATTHEW. I am a Police-officer. I have a certificate of the former conviction of the prisoner; I know he is the person - (read.)
     GUILTY. Aged 17. - Transported for Life.

 1802.    ALEXANDER BRAND and MARY ANN, (HIS WIFE) were indicted for stealing, 1 carpet, value 10s.; 2 sheets, value 8s.; 1 set of bed-furniture, value 12s.; 2 window-curtains, value 7s.; 2 pillows, value 7s.; 1 candlestick, value 2s.; 1 looking-glass, value 9d.; 1 bolster, value 6d.; 1 blanket, value 6d.; 1 poker, value 1s.; 1 shovel, value 1s., and 1 pair of tongs, value 1s., the goods of George Miles.
   LOUISA MILES. I am the wife of George Miles, a carpenter, in Regent-street, Chelsea. I let the prisoners a furnished room on my first floor, about Christmas, at 5s. a week; they paid regularly till within the last five weeks- the woman said her husband was a cabinet-maker; we had given them warning several times, not liking them, and they left on Tuesday, the 14th of July, without saying a word - I was at home; they had nothing of their own, and walked out of the house - I did not expect they were going for good; I got into their room with the key of another door, which we found would open it, and missed all the articles stated in the indictment; the man returned by the house, between eleven and twelve o'clock at night - I sent for an officer, and gave him in charge; they had no right whatever to pawn my property - they owed me five weeks' rent; the lodging was let in the usual way - I had no security for the furniture; we asked for the rent - he sent us down a few duplicates of wearing-apparel, as security for rent, and I found they belonged to another person.
   Alexander Brand. Q. How long did you know the goods to be in pawn before I was taken? A. The same afternoon; I did not know before - I had been continually asking them for a pair of sheets, which I had lent them, and as they did not return them, I suspected they might be pawned; we did not find them in linen - my husband, on the Friday before, had threatened to send for an constable to turn him out, because he would not leave; we did not like him in the house, because he came home at all hours - they sent down twelve tickets on the Sunday, and said they would pay the rent on the Tuesday; they were not as security for the property - I did not know it was pawned.
   HENRY BOND. I am servant to Thorp and Co., pawnbrokers, of King's-road, Chelsea. I have a blanket, bolster, pillows, curtains, book, handkerchief, sheet, carpet, two pillows, looking-glass, and other things, pawned at different times, by a girl, in the name of Walker; she said she pawned them for her mother - she had been in the habit of pawning at our house for twelve months; the female prisoner had authorized me to take in whatever pledges the girl brought - I know she was their daughter- they were pledged at different times, from January to May.
 MRS. MILES. The girl lived with them - she was about thirteen years old.
