 1115.  MARGARET HUDDART PALMER was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of February , 1 silver spoon, value 4s., the goods of James Fogg.
   JAMES FOGG. I am a Police-surveyor, and live in Martha-street, St. George's; I have employed the prisoner to make clothes for me. On Sunday, the 26th of February, she brought home a pair of trousers: I told her to take them back to alter - I had two silver tea-spoons on the sideboard, and when she was gone I missed one; I found it at a pawnbroker's - I met her on the Wednesday following, and asked what she had done with the spoon; she made no answer; I told her I found it was at the pawnbroker's, and asked for the duplicate; she said she had destroyed it.
   WILLIAM COOMBE. I was shopman to Mr. Watts, of Hereford-place, Commercial-road. I have a spoon, pawned on the 27th of February, by the prisoner, for 2s.(Property produced and sworn to)
 The prisoner pleaded poverty, and received a good character.
 GUILTY. Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy. - Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1116.  JAMES CHARLES PEARCE was indicted for stealing, on the 5th of April , 1 pair of boots, value 22s.; 2 coats, value 20s.; 2 jackets, value 6s.; 1 smockfrock, value 6d., and 1 waistcoat, value 1s., the goods of John Towersey.
 JOHN TOWERSEY. I am a  post-boy, and live in Oxfordshire. On the 4th of April I was at my master's stable at Tetsworth; I left the articles stated in the stable - I missed them the next morning; I mentioned my loss to John Berry, a post-boy, on his way to Uxbridge; I saw a waistcoat taken off the prisoner's back on the Friday following, before the Magistrate at Uxbridge - that was one that I had lost.
   JOHN BERRY. In consequence of hearing of the loss I made inquiry at Uxbridge; I found the prisoner, and had him taken into custody - I found on him a pair of boots and a frock; these are them.
   GEORGE JEWELL. I saw the prisoner at Uxbridge - he offered me this jacket; I bought it of him for 12s., with this waistcoat.
   JOHN BROWN. I have a blue jacket which I bought on the 5th, of the prisoner, at Uxbridge, which is twenty-seven miles from where the prosecutor lives.
   THOMAS ALMAN. I bought a coat of the prisoner for 16s. and a pot of beer, at Uxbridge.
   WILLIAM INGRAM. I am an officer. I took the prisoner; he had a smock-frock and a pair of boots on - I found a stall-man, to whom the prisoner had sold a great coat; I said I would take it - the stallman said, "Let him give me my money, 4s. 6d.," and he gave it him out of his purse - I saw a piece of gold in his purse; I took him to the White Hart, and searched him, but I could not find any gold - I asked him to open his month, and I found this sovereign in it.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 JOHN TOWERSEY. I found these things on the Friday- they had been sold on the Thursday - I left them safe on the Wednesday night, at half-past eleven o'clock; we found 1l. 7s. 3d. on him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I was coming through Brentford on Wednesday morning; I met a man, and bought the things of him.
     GUILTY. Aged 21. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1116.  WILLIAM DEAN was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of March , 10 spoons, value 30s., the goods of Benjamin Best , his master.
   BENJAMIN BEST. I am a coal-merchant- the prisoner was my errand-boy. I missed some spoons, and asked him about them; he denied it; I told him it would be better to tell the truth - I saw a spoon in Old-street, but could not swear to it.
   HUGH FISHER STANGER. I bought a spoon of two men, but the prosecutor gave it back to me, and I melted it.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1117.  WILLIAM SMITH was indicted for stealing, on the 8th of March , 1 cloak, value 12s., and 1 shawl, value 3s., the goods of Timothy Kalahan.
   ANN LYON. I lodge in Short's-gardens, Drury-lane. On the 4th of April I met the prisoner in Long-acre, and in consequence of information, I asked him for some duplicates; he said he was the wrong person, and he went on; I followed him, and said he had robbed Timothy Kalahan, and if he did not give me the duplicates, I would have him taken up - he gave me the duplicates, and I gave him into custody.
   MARY KALAHAN. I am the wife of Timothy Kalahan; we live in Short's-gardens. The prisoner lodged with us; I found one of my boxes broken open, and missed a shawl and a cloak.
   EDWARD NEWSHEAD. I am in the service of a pawnbroker, in Tothill-street. I have a cloak, pawned on the 14th of March, in the name of William Smith, by a person like the prisoner, but I cannot swear to him; this is the duplicate.
   WILLIAM GEORGE FRY. I am in the service of Mr. Lorton, a pawnbroker. I have a shawl, but did not take it in; this is the duplicate of it.
   JOSEPH MAYS(Police-constable E 63.) I took the prisoner, and got these duplicates from Lyon.
 Prisoner's Defence. I worked as a carpenter for the prosecutor; he owed me a little money, and would not pay me - I pawned the things, and gave up the duplicates.
   TIMOTHY KALAHAN. I had employed him, but I do not owe him a farthing.
     GUILTY. Aged 26. - Confined Six Months.
