H. A. STACEY (Re-examined). I produce the file in the liquidation of J.
J. Keighley of 4, Priest's Court, London, residing at Clyde Villa, Croydon,
made on January 1st, 1879, and dated 3rd January the first meeting of
creditors was on 28th January, when & resolution was passed for a liquidation
by arrangement, and not in bankruptcy--Mr. Lovering was the public
Recountant, and the committee of inspection was Mr. Pentiman, Mr. Smithson,
Mr. Hertz, Mr. Hughes, and Mr. Dear-I find here a statement of affairs;
the creditors unsecured are 7,0371. 148. 10d. ;creditors partly secured,
2712. 58. 5d. ; other liabilities, 3001.; creditors for rent and taxes, 311.148. 2d.:
liabilities on bills discounted, 2,6291. 198. 10d. : total, 10,2701. 148, 3d.- the
assets are, stock in trade, 1,1631. 18. 9d.; book debts estimated to produce
2851. 68. 94.; furniture, pictures, and fittings, 702. ; surplus from creditors,
621. 08. 2d. : total, 1,5307. 88. 8d.
RICHARD WADDINGTON. I am a stuff manufacturer of Bradford-in 1878
Keighley owed me between 100%. and 2001, and in December, 1878, I saw
him in my office; Mr. Sutcliff was present-Michell said "If you will be
quiet and not push your account, you will be paid 20s in the pound; I am
going into partnership with Mr. Keighley, and am going to bring in from
3,0001. to 4,0001. " 23 far as my memory serves me--we said that we would
be quiet and not push our account-he saw our stock, and asked me to send
patterns, and said that if he sold them he would pay cash if we would let
him have them-we sent samples, and he paid me 308. for one sample piece in
Keighley's place I told him we should not deliver any goods until the whole
account was paid- we afterwards took out a writ-I have never said that if
he would pay 108. in the pound we would not prosecute, but if he did not
we would I introduced Michell to Mr. Robertshaw in the Liberal Club
as well as I remember, Mr. Robertshaw said "Shall we get our account ?"
he suid “Yes, it will be all right."
Cross-examined. I attendel Keighley's meeting of creditors in London
-I did not meet Michell in Cheapside and say "I mean to have 108. in the
pound out of you or Keighley " I said just the contrary, I said that I had
minde up my mind to prosecute every case, as it wils an outrage to commercial
morality-. I said nothing about 108. in the pound—this was the same week
as Michell's first meeting of creditors-I don't remember going to Keighley's
meeting at all I went to Michell's meeting, and I was very angry; most
people are when they lose their money--I do not remember saying that if we
did not get 10s in the pound the Bradford creditors had resolved to prosecute,
or that some gentleman stopped me and said that it was too soon to talk of that
-mysolicitors are Wood and Killick of Bradford, and I think a Mr. Wood of
St. Paul's Churchyard is their London agent-I think he was at the meeting of
creditors on 13th March-I did not go about among the creditors at Bradford
trying to get up & resolution fora prosecution-I talked to one or two persons,
and we did ultimately decide to prosecute, or there would have been no
prosecution.
Re-examined. I do not believe I said anything about 108. in the pound
being paid- I said many times, “ Whenever & case of this kind occurs I am
determined I will prosecute," and I am of the same opinion still-it was
after I was at the meeting of his creditors on 13th March that I saw Michell
in Cheapside.
