JAMES WHISKARD. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Bishopsgate-street. On the afternoon of the 21st of April, the prisoner brought this watch to pledge—I asked him whose it was—he said his own—I asked him where he got it from—he said his father gave it to him—I said, "Where does he live?"—he said, "No. 16, Worship-street"—I could see by his manner he had not got possession of the watch as he should have done, and I called in a policeman, and sent him to where he said his father lived—he brought him back, and said it was all wrong. JAMES HALL( City police-constable  14.) I took the prisoner into cus tody at Mr. Whiskard's shop—he said he wanted to go to No. 16, Worship-street, where his father lived—I took him there, and showed him the house—I said, "Shall I knock at the door, is this the house?"—he said, "No"—I looked at him, and he began to cry—I said, "Is it all wrong?"—he said, "Yes, sir"—I took him back to Mr. Whiskard's, and at the station-house I found on him a cheque for 21 l ., a silver watch, and a metal watch, which I produce, and a few shillings. WILLIAM HOUGH. I am a watchmaker in Mrs. Bennett's employ. This gold watch is hers—it was in her care to repair—the prisoner was our er rand boy, and was left in the workshop at different times while we were absent—he was intrusted with this silver watch to bring up to town—I know the metal watch to be Mrs. Bennett's property, but when it was taken I cannot say. JOHN BENNETT re-examined.  He was one of two errand boys—the other is in prison now for robbing us—they had both been in the house some time together.

 1373. HENRY KNIGHT was indicted for stealing, on the   23rd of April , 1 half-crown, 7 shillings, 6 sixpences, and 3 fourpenny pieces, the monies of William Covell , his master. JAMES COVELL. I am the son of William Covell, a butcher, in Broad way, Deptford. The prisoner was in his employ—in consequence of sus picions I entertained on the 17th of April, I marked one half-crown, seven shillings, six sixpences, and three fourpenny pieces, and put them into the till—I examined the till the following morning, and found the marked money still there—I then put it into a purse into a desk in the shop, and af terwards missed the money from the desk, but not the purse—I sent for a policeman—the prisoner was searched—one marked shilling was found on him, and another the policeman got from the maid servant—I had left the key of the desk on the mantel-shelf in the parlour, and the desk was in the shop.
