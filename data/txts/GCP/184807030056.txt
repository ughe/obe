persons to walk ? A. I think there is.
MR. BODKIN. Q. Was he upon that? A. I would not undertake to me
- I know he was just at the corner--a woman at the time said they en

WILLIAM BROOM Cross (policeman, G 217.) I was on Clerkenwell-emo
on the evening of this meeting-the meeting appeared to be composed
working men, costermongers, and some very low sort of characters there
were a great many Irishmen-I know Williams perfectly well-I saw him
there, in the van- I did not see Vernon there; I have known him for some
months past-I was seven or eight yards from the van when Williams sole
- he said when he gave the signal they were to fall in four deep, and
would then take them where they would meet ten times that number
-I went away after I heard that, to report to my superintendent
when I returned Fussell was standing up and speaking-when he had
finished, Williams said, “Now, my friends, fall in four deep"-I did not
observe whether Vernon was there then-I subsequently, on the same
evening, about half-past eleven, saw the crowd in Redcross-street, and son
Vernon then at the window of Cartwright's coffee-shop-I believe he was a
the window in the room-he was addressing the crowd-I was not be
enough to bear what he saidI believe I saw Williams at the same window-
I did not hear him speak; he had spoken previously, when I was not there-
the City and Metropolitan Police then came to disperse the crowd-they did
not disperse quietly-there was a very great uproar indeed, shouting, and
pelting the police with stones--that was both in Redcross-street and Golden
lane, where the police had driven them—they collected together there-1
of the police under my observation were injured--there was a policema
injured there-I think the uproar took place almost immediately after Vernon
had spoken from the window-I did not observe whether he came down from
the house or not-I should think it was about an hour before the crowd
finally dispersed--the uproar was going on during all that time-a great
many stones were thrown-the police were obliged to use their truncheons.
Cross-examined. Q. You say you were so far off that you could not hear
Vernon ; how far off were you? A. I do not know the distance particularly
but I should say between twenty and thirty yards--there was an uproar and
noise-1 was not standing there perhaps more than five minutes there wu
a great crowd-I was not taking notes—the crowd was flowing backwards
and forwards--I had been pusbed a good many times, but not at the time
am speaking of I was not in uniform-I was not with Hardy; I saw him
afterwards.
COURT. Q. Had you instructions yourself? A. Yes: I had been fol-
lowing special duty for a long time previous, and had not been in uniform for
some time previous to this assembly; it was not connected with that particu-
lar meeting.

to say about the 29th I was not called before the Magistrate-I do no
know whether I was in the middle of the crowd: I was round about it,
