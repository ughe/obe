CUBITT, Mayor.
18, Palace-chambers, Pall-mall--the dog which has been produced here to
day is my property-I had it safe about 28th or 29th of August-she had
been stolen four times before I had occasion to go to Liverpool on the
Wednesday, and upon returning I found the dog was not where it ought to
have been-I put out bills, offering 11. reward-I had some communication
with Mr. Hutchinson, and on 24th September, about 9 o'clock in the
morning, the housekeeper came up with the prisoner, and told me that Me
Kendillon was there. I asked him to come in, and said to him, “Where i
my dog!"-he said, "In Shoreditch”-I said, "Do you really want me to
pay 31. to recover my dog again? I really think a sovereign ought to do this
reward that I have offered, and which I am quite willing to give;" in fact,
I authorised the housekeeper to give 21. if the dog was brought backhe
said he could not get the dog for less than 31.--I then asked him to godon
with me to Shoreditch where the dog was, as I wanted to find out about him
myself; I would give him something for his trouble, and prosecute the man
who bad it-he said he could not go down with me to the place; he had no
objection to go with the little page, a boy about fourteen or fifteen-
I would give him the 31. he would bring me the dog back afterwards-I did
not do anything more then-I told him I must consider this first; I did not
know whether I could trust him with the 31., and if he called at my office
at 1 o'clock I should then decide-he came to my office at 1 o'clock-I then
repeated to him that a sovereign, or two, ought to have been the sum which
he should have taken and no more, and he again said he could not take lom
than 31.-I then gave him the 31. and asked him when he would bring back
my dog-he said, “In an hour and a half, at half-past 2 I shall be here with
the dog"-I would not have given him more than 17. if I could have
helped it-I saw the prisoner next within two minutes of half-past 2 at my
office with the dog, which was tied to one of the desks-one of my clerks
had received it-I then said to the prisoner he was either the thief himself
or an accomplice; that he had had six months before, and he would not get
rid of me so soon as that, and the quicker he walked out of my office the
better-he was rather confused, and said, “I am not the party who stole the
dog or an accomplice," and he gave me a paper with the name of the man
who stole the dog, as he said, but I did not take any notice of it-he went
out of my office and the next morning I went before the Magistrate,
warrant was granted, and the prisoner was taken into custody.
Cross-examined. Q. How often did this man show himself to you! 4
Twice; he came first to my chambers and then twice to my office, the last
time with the dog-I never went to his place-I gave 17. before to get the
dog back-she is worth about 51.—it has cost me about 201.- I gave two
guineas for her at first, at a sale.
