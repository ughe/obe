Old-baily, and but 70 of them were executed : Whereupon I muſt now
obſerve, That there were 18 of this Number who had een (ſome of them
twice) Condemn'd and Pardon'd before, but abus'd that Mercy to their
great Shame, and the Hurt of honeſt People. And here, for the Satis-
fa&tion of thoſe who ſeem to wonder and be uneafie at the preſent En-
creaſe of common Malefactors, I muſt likewife take notice, That this
Calamity proceeds chiefly from the late Rebellion, which (as it plainty
appears) has had this dilwal Effect, That it has debauch'd many in theſe
Nations, and made thoſe that were already of looſe Principles, and in
clin'd to Vice, more wicked than they were before, and much more au-
dacieus and impious : So that their Clamours are very Unreaſonable and
Unjuft, who complain of the Severity of this preſent Government, the
many Inftances of Mercy ſhewn by His moſt Gracious Majeſty, (even to
his worft Enemies) which far furpaſs all we caa find of this nature in
former Reigns, ſpeaking aloud his Unparallel'd Goodneſs and Clemency:
inſomuch that if there were any Reafon to complain, 'twere rather on
the other ſide, viz. That Juftice has given way too much to Mercy, and
that many who have receiv'd it have taken too little Care to improve it
as they ought to have done. Now let them conſider, who malicioufly
and wickedly aſperſe this our happy Government, repreſenting it as Cruel
and Tyrannicah, how few of the many Rebels in Great Britain have ſuffer'd
the Puniſhment which the Laws of their Country had juſtly inflicted on
them; and then let them admire, and be thankful for, that Tranſcendene
Mercy by which they ſtill live; and let them fhew their True Gratitude
by their future Obedience and Loyalty to the Beſt of Kings, who now
reigns over us; and I pray God may long continue a Bleſſing to us. Ansen.
