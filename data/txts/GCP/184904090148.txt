Dennis CALLAGHAN. I am a carrier-at four o'clock on 24th March, I
pat two baskets of linen, from the Reform Club, into my cart, to take to my
mistress, who is the wife of John Walton-1 went into the Junior Club with
another basket of linen, and, while there, somebody took away cart, horse and
all that was at a quarter-past four o'clock.
Mary French, I am housekeeper to the Reform Club-these articles
are their property-I counted the articles myself, and put them into the
basket-I saw Callaghan take them away.
Prisoner's Defence. I saw them publicly exposed in Petticoat-lane, and
bought them to turn a penny.
Not Guilty.
997. EDWARD MOORE and CAROLINE BICKERS, stealing 2
capes, 1 shawl, and 1 victorine, value 3s. 6d.; the goods of Helen Mary
Stocker: 1 shirt, 1 waistcoat, and other articles, value 16s. 6d. ; the goods
of Thomas Bliss Stocker: to which
BICKERS pleaded GUILTY.
Aged 20.-Recommended to mercy.-Confined Six Months.
THOMAS Bliss STOCKER. I live at Kentish-town ; Bickers was my ap-
prentice. On 24th Jan. I went to Church, leaving her at home I came back
about eight o'clock in the evening, and she was not there-I missed the
articles stated-I had had the scarf safe within a week,and had spoken to Bickers
about it-I said, "How is it that there is no one but you in the house, and
this is the second scarf that is missing"-she said she knew nothing about it,
where could she take it to-on 29th March I went with a policeman to 9,
Tottenham-place, and found her lodging there, and in a box, which I knew
to be hers, I found these things (produced)—she acknowledged it-Moore
was not there.
HELEN MARY STOCKER. I am the daughter of the last witness-this
dress is my mother's, this cardinal and victorine are mine, and this shirt my
father's-these blinds were safe in a drawer in my bed-room a day or two
before Bickers left--the dress she has on is stolen.
William RANSLEY (policeman, 8 61). I went with Mr. Stoeker to 9,
Tottenham-place, and saw Bickers—I found these things, some in her boxes,
and some in Moore's I went again about a quarter-past four o'clock, and
found Moore in the back kitchen-he said, "I suppose you are the man who
has taken my wife away"-I said I had taken a female away, and must trouble
him to go with me to the station-he said, "I will go ; did you take anything
out of that box ?"- I said, “No”—he said, " Did you take anything out of
that one ?" I said, “ Yes"-he said, “That is my box"-going to the station
I said I thought it was very foolish for him to have a young girl like her
there-he said she was his wife, and then that she was not-he said he
assisted in bringing the boxes away, but he did not know that any of the
contents were stolen.
Moore's Defence. I am a widower; I fell into company with Bickers,
and she said she had got a bad master and mistress, and no father or mother;
and if I were willing she would come and live with me as my wife, and act as
a mother to my child, to which I agreed ; two or three weeks afterwards she
asked me if I was in the same mind; I said, “Yes ;" she said she would tell
me when her month, or quarter, would be up, and if I would come at seven
o'clock in the evening and fetch her things, she would go home with me;
I brought her home on 24th Feb., I gave everything into her possession, and
