   Prisoner.  I gave Daggett-court, where I did live. Witness.  I could not find his mother. WILLIAM HILL re-examined.  I found his mother at No. 7, Daggett-court, Finsbury, by the name of Conolly—he gave that name at the Compter, but gave the name of Conway to the officer.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I was in a public-house in Kent-street, at the time the man says the butter was stolen—I have sent several times to the wit nesses, but they cannot leave—I ought to have Mr. Henderson, of No. 2, Old Bailey, here as a witness to character—I am not the man—the witness had another man at the Town Hall, who wore much the same dress as I had, but he was discharged. RICHARD SLADE re-examined.  It was taken about five o'clock, or a little after five—another man was taken up on suspicion by a policeman, N 51, and when they took me to the Town Hall I found it was not the right man.
   Prisoner.  The morning I was taken I went to look for work, and saw a man near a wagon—he said, "Do you want a trouser?  will you have a drop of beer?"—I said, "I don't know"—he said, "Drink it"—he said, "Go to Humphrey's tap"—I went there, and while I was drinking gin, the man came in and gave me in charge. WILLIAM HILL re-examined.  I gave him into custody while he was drinking gin—he was in the tap of the hotel, which is the width of Lon don-bridge from the place, and about half as far again—he was on a different side of the bridge.
   Q.  Then he was close on the spot the following morning? A.  Yes—I was talking to a cooper when he came by, and from the description Slade had given of him, I suspected he was the man—I went and got a police man, and when he saw me look at him, he avoided me, and got into the tap—the reason the other man was taken was, he was dressed like the pri soner—I never saw the prisoner before—the butter has never been found. RICHARD SLADE re-examined.  I have no doubt whatever that the pri soner is the man who took the butter—I am quite certain of it—I have hot the least doubt on the subject—it was not dark at the time.
   Q.  Why not interfere? A.  I did not know but what it was right, as they had been delivering a great many goods from the wharf that day; but not seeing Hill about the place, as he was generally there when they are loading, I went and inquired—I am quite certain the prisoner is the man—I am not mistaken in him. NOT GUILTY.
