Q. What was the object of your calling Salmon to face the prisoner?
1. To tell him he had done wrong in opening the bin-each man has a bin
for the food for his horses for the week-I did not tell Salmon that he had
gone into the stable at night, and opened the prisoner's bin with a false
key-the prisoner had charged Salmon with going to his bin in the night:
but he did not repeat it before Salmon's face-I heard of it from a great
many quarters--the prisoner never told it to me to my recollection- I will
not swear he did not-it was stated, on that occasion, that Salmon opened
the bin with a key; and I suppose it was a false key.
Q. Did I not ask you whether the prisoner complained that Salmon had
opened his bin with a false key ? A. I believe it was 80-Salmon did not
deny that he had done so I told him I had a good mind to send him
about his business--I might tell him to go-I believe I did tell him to go.
Q. You say you saw the mark of a sack having been thrown down; was
'it not hard frosty weather? A. No, it was not a frost that night; it was
a thaw--the white oats would weigh about 40 lbs. a bushel, and the black
about 38 lbs.
Francis Salmon. I am a carter in the employ of Mr. Holcombe. On
the morning of the 12th of February I saw two sacks in the cart-one of
them was three parts full, and the other about half full-it was Mr. Hol-
combe's cart, and the prisoner was the driver-I examined the sacks, and
found there were oats in them-I took some black oats out of one sack,
and some white ones out of the other, and took them to Mr. Black--I saw
the prisoner drive the cart away- I do not know whether the sacks were
in it then-I did not look into the cart any more.
Cross-examined. Q. What time did you go to the stable that morning!
4. About ten minutes past five o'clock-the prisoner was then inside, just
opening the great gates into the yard-he went away with the cart about a
quarter past six, which was about a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes
after I had seen the sacks in it-I was in the stable with him all the morn-
ing-I did not say any thing to him about the sacks, as I did not know but
that Mr. Black might have ordered them the cart was about a dozen
yards from the stable door--when I took the oats out of the sacks, the
prisoner had gone with his horses to the pond to water-I took them to
Mr. Black after the prisoner left-I could not take them before, as Mr.
Black was not moving- I could have awoke him-his house stands in the
garden; but I thought he would soon be out, and I did not like to disturb
him-I did not examine the sacks in which the oats were-I never had any
quarrel with the prisoner-we might have had a few words in contradic-
tion - I swear I have not had words with him in anger half a dozen times.
Q. Do you remember going to his stable one night, when all the men
were gone? A. Yes I went to do my work. I suppose-I unlocked the
