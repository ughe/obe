   Cross-examined.  They were in the house about two months while I was there, as far as I know they lived comfortably together—I heard some scuffling before I went down. CHARLES DANIEL FOTHERBY. I live at 55, Gravel Lane—on 13th December about 12.20 I heard a little bit of scuffling downstairs in the parlour underneath—I got out of bed and listened, and after two or three minutes I heard "murder and help" screamed—I ran downstairs and saw Mrs. Davies lying in the passage, and the prisoner close to her—I pulled her up and put her on a chair, she said she was stabbed—I said, "Where?" she said, "Here, but I can't tell you"—I said, "If you are stabbed, where is the knife?"—I looked about but could not see one—the prisoner said he had done nothing—I went for the doctor.
   Cross-examined.  I have known the prisoner some time—as far as I know he has always behaved in a proper manner—I have not spoken to him above three or four times—he was very drunk on this night, and did not know what he was doing; he was like mad with drink, and when he saw what he had done he turned all manner of colours. CHARLES GRAHAM( Police Inspector M ). On the morning of the 14th a little after 2 I went to 55, Gravel Lane—I saw the prisoner in the passage—I asked if he was the landlord, he said, "Yes"—we went into the back parlour—I found Mrs. Davies there, and Dr. Wilson and his assistant—I asked Mrs. Davies who had committed the assault upon her, she replied, "My husband"—he made no reply at that time—in conse quence of what the doctor said I thought it right to take a statement from her—the prisoner was present—it was made in answer to ques tions that I and Dr. Wilson put to her—I took it down and read it over to her, and she put her mark to it: this is it. ( Read : "I believe I am in a dying state, and make the following statement voluntarily. My husband and I have not for the last two days been on good terms. He has been drinking. He struck me first with his fist. I defended myself as well as I could. He had then no knife in his hand when he was putting up the shutters. He found some wooden boxes in his way. He got angry at this, and struck me with the shop knife. When I received the blow I rushed to the door and screamed 'Murder.' Mrs. Fotherby came. I showed the wound to her. I am positive my husband done it; there was no other person in the room. I don't know the cause of the quarrel. I was perfectly sober.
