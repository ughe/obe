296. JOHN SMITH, burglariously breaking and entering• the dwelling
house of John Riley, with intent to steal.
JAMES ARMSTRONG (policeman H 87). On 18th Feb., at 8 0'clock in
morning, I found the prisoner in Mr. Riley'S cellar, under the house—there
are bars about an inch and a half asunder under the shop window, on the flags
—I found this iron bar ( produced) broken off, by which means anybody could
get into the cellar—I tumbled in, turned my light on, and found the priwner
—(1 had called to him before I went in, and asked what be was doing there;
be made no laid hold of him; I said nothing, but he said, Bad
company has led me into it; I am innocent, two men broke the dap, and
unlocked the padlock, and put me in, and I was to go up stairs, open the
front door for them, and let them in; and hearing the noise when you came
to the flap, I took you to be searched him, and found in his peket
this wax taper (produced), and two lucifer matches; there were wreral
matches thrown about the ground—while I was searching him, he said, " I
have got no tools about me, the men have got the tools, I am innocent, I
have been led into bad passed the cellar flap about twenty minute
before, it was safe then, it locks inside—he said the men lived in Brick-lane,
but next morning he denied knowing anything about them.
Prisoner. I had been at my aunt's, and had been drinking. Witness.
appeared a little the worse for liquor; he could walk very well, he appeared
to know what he was saying—going to the station, he said one Of the men
vas named Burton—a man named Burton is known to one of our sergeant',
Jackson—he repeated next morning that bad company had led him into it;
he knew nothing about the men—he did not say he bad been drinking
his aunt.
JOHN RILEY. I am a cheesemonger, of 75,Whitechapel-road, in the parish Of
St. Mary's, Whitecbapel. On the nigbtof Wednesday, 18th Feb., I wascalled
up by a policeman, I went down, and found the prisoner in custody—I had
seen the cellar shut up at 10 0'clock the night before—I found the dap
forced when I came down—I lost no property, there was not time—there
cheese in the cellar, and butter in the shop.
Prisoner's Defence. My mother and myself were returning from the play,
she told me to run home and light a fire against she came home; I met two
gentlemen standing by the cellar. who said if I went down and opened the
door they would give me a trifle of money; I went down, I did not
Ybat I was about, or I should not have done it.
