GEORGE JAMES NEWMAN. I am a member of the firm of Newman and
Houseton, of Ward's Wharf, Commercial Road, Blackfriars-about Dee.,
1885, Serné asked our firn to take some tins of capuala for sale; there
was no specified number-I can't say the exact quantity that came, but I
think about a couple of gross, 12 dozen being a gross-the value was
supposed to be 28. 6d. a tin; he was to supply us at 18.-we tried to sell
them-we advanced him about 151.-We were not able to sell any, except
& very few tins, not any quantity-they were returned to him we received
this letter from him: "19th Dec., 1885. Gentlemen,-As you have taken
a further stock of capuala before having started any advertisement, should
you not sell these, I hereby agree to take it all back and refund the amount
already advanced, and pay you the same by weekly instalments of 108."-
he did not repay it; I don't think he paid any; he may possibly have paid
one weekly instalment, or perhaps two-he said he was unable to pay, that
he had not the money-we returned the things to him--we never ordered
any more.
Cross-examined by Mr. GEOGHEGAN. I don't know that we undertook to
advertise the capuala-we were to have it at a certain price, and adver.
tise it ourselves- I did not agree that he was to supply us with 1,000,
tins; the agreement was with the firm-I saw Serné personally, and
personally agreed with him about it.
JOHN CONQUEST (recalled). On the night of 12th October, about 11, I
was in the Strand-I there saw Goldfinch-I said to him "I am a police
officer; I shall arrest you for being concerned with Leon Serné in setting
fire to his house, 274, Strand, and causing the death of his two little
boys"-he said “Yes, all right”-I took him to the station at Bow
Street; he was detained, and I went back to 279, Strand, with P.C.
Colins--I there saw Serné-I said to him exactly what I had said to
Goldfinch-he replied " Thank you, sir, thank you, sir"-I took him to
Bow Street Station-he and Goldfinch were there together charged with
the offence I had already stated to them-Serné made no answer-Gold-
finch said "I spoke the truth at the inquest; I can't say any more."
Cross-examined by Mr. GEOGHEGAN. I was present at the examinations
at the police-court; they were very lengthy examinations, beginning at
two and ran sometimes to half-past five-at the close of the case for the
prosecution the Magistrate said he had made up his mind to send the case
for trial-after that the prisoner's counsel said under those circumstances
any witnesses they had to call they would call at the trial-I know Mr.
and Mrs. Burton - I have seen Mis. Burton, not Mr.--I subpænaed Mrs.
Burton-she was examined before the Coroner-she was not called before
the Magistrate-the distance from the window from which the Sernés
were rescued was within 10 or 12 feet of the nearest window-I saw Mrs.
Bishop afterwards and took her statement about the matter-I submitted
her statement with others, and I was directed to subpoona her-I called
on Mrs. Lazarus at the second-hand boot shop in Holywell Street--her
house is almost immediately behind the fire, about the same distance 88
Mrs. Stanley's house--I saw Mr. Callagan and his daughter.
