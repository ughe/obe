Cross-examined by Mr. PAYNE. Q. Did not he say, "If you had been a

Johnson's Defence. I am entirely innocent, though appearances are against
me; I was going up Wilstead-street, when I was stopped and charged by
the prosecutrix with robbing her of half-a-crown; she said, if I gave it her
back she would not prosecute me; I denied the charge, but being excited at
the time I said I would give her one rather than be locked up, as I was going
to work next morning; I never saw the half-crown, neither was I in company
with either of the prisoners.

1937. JOHN PENNY was indicted for stealing 12 feet of lead-pipe, value
10.; the property of William Witley Coorze, his master; and RICHARD
GARRETT for feloniously receiving the same, knowing it to have been
stolen.
John RICKETTS. I am foreman, in the employ of William Witley Coorze,
of 20, Frith-street, Soho-Penny was in his employ for four years-he was
at work there on the 16th of Aug.-he generally had on his working dress,
a light jacket and cap-there was a piece of lead-pipe, weighing about fifty-
four pounds, on the premises-it was safe on Saturday night.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You did not see it since the Saturday
previous ? A. It was a piece resembling this (produced) in all respects-1
know the weight of the piece I lost, this answers in point of weight-I have
not weighed it since-the policeman has—it has not been in my custody
--we had no reason to believe otherwise than that Penny bore a good cha-
racter up to this time we did not miss the lead till the constable came and
made enquiries.
