Before Mr. Recorder.
1390. THOMAS BROWN was indicted for stealing, on the 21st d
May, 29lbs. weight of potatoes, value 18., the goods of Joseph Choat and
another.
Mr. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution.
JAMES HALL (police-constable K 336.) On Wednesday, the 21st of
May, about half-past nine o'clock in the evening, I was on duty at Dage.
ham, in Essex, and met the prisoner-he had a bag on his back, with
something in it-I passed him, without challenging him-I afterwarde
turned round, looked at him, and followed him-directly be found in
doing so, he ran off, and ran about 200 yards, or more I caught him just
as he had got one foot within his cottage-I went in after him, and asked
what he had in the bag-it was still on his back-he said, a few potato
that he had had given to him-I told him I must take him into custody-
I took him to the station, searched him, and found sixteen potatoes round
his body, in his coat pocket, and in his smock frock, which was tucked up
-the sack contained sixteen pounds of potatoes-he afterwards said it wa
no use telling a lie about it, he found them on his road home-I afterwards
showed them to Mr. Choat.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Were you acquainted with him!.
A. I had seen him before, working about the neighbourhood.
Philip Choat. I am a farmer, residing at Barking, in partnership with
my brother Joseph. The prisoner was in our service three or four years-
he is a single man, and had 13. a week-in May I had some potato clamps
on my farm, which was wholly under the prisoner's care and management-
the policeman brought some potatoes to me on the Thursday morning!
am certain they were part of my property--they are a Shaw potato--they
were grown in the marsh-the prisoner had no authority to carry any
away for himself-the same day he was apprehended I found a quantity
of the same sort of potatoes in a sack about the premises.
Cross-examined. Q. What is the worth of the potatoes found on the
prisoner? A. 1s.--I know these potatoes by the size and look-other
persons have Shaw potatoes a great many are grown in the marshes, but
they are some distance from me--my farm is at Barking, which I believe
is not within the jurisdiction of this Court-the prisoner was taken at
Dagenham, about a mile and a half from Barking-there are marsbes
between Barking and Dagenham.
Mr. RYLAND. Q. Where were these clamps of potatoes ? A. Close to
the buildings, in the stack-yard, in the parish of Barking.
(The prisoner received a good character.)
Guilty. Aged 27.-Confined One Month.
