HENRY TUNE. I am a shoemaker, and live in Blackfriars-road. The prisoner was in my employ for nearly twelve months, and worked on the premises as a clipper—he had no authority to take any work or anything off the premises—on Friday evening, the 4th of April, he left my house about five minutes past eight o'clock, at his usual time—in consequence of suspicion I sent for him to come back, and told him I suspected he had some property on him belonging to me—he at first denied it—I sent for a policeman, and while he was gone for he pulled a piece of leather out of his pocket, with a piece of something else, and said that was all he had got, and he hoped I should forgive him—he placed it upon the table—it was my property—it had my mark upon it—I afterwards saw him searched at the station, and some lasting found on him which he had no authority to take—these are the things produced—I afterwards went to his lodgings and found a great quantity of materials of every description belonging to me—he had not the slightest authority to take them—he had 16 s . a week. ALEXANDER HAUGHEY( policeman ,) I took the prisoner into custody on the 4th of April—I searched him at the station, and found this lasting on him.
   The prisoner put in a petition confessing his guilt, pleading poverty, and imploring mercy for the sake of his wife and children. GUILTY. Aged 35.— Confined Six Months .

 1011. WILLIAM BENBOW was indicted for stealing 1 handker chief, value 3 s  ., the goods of William Harding Birchall , from his person. WILLIAM HARDING BIRCHALL. I have an office in Newgate-street. On Sunday evening, the 22nd of March, between six and seven o'clock, I was walking in the London-road—the witness Goldsmid came and touched me on the shoulder—in consequence of what he said, I felt in my outside coat pocket, and missed a silk handkerchief, which I had safe not a minute before—it has never been found. JOSEPH GOLDSMID. I live at Newington-causeway. On Sunday even ing, the 22nd of March, I was in the London-road, and saw two boys—I saw one of them pick the prosecutor's pocket of a handkerchief—I cannot swear it was the prisoner, or that he was one of the two—they ran away—I was on the opposite side of the way—I was examined before the Magis trate—the signature to this deposition is mine—( looking at it. )
   Q.  Listen to this, "I saw the prisoner take from the prosecutor's hind pocket a blue handkerchief with white spots; I know him by sight quite well; I have been in the habit of seeing the prisoner for the last twelve month, and I am quite certain he is the person that took the prosecutor's hand kerchief." Witness.  My not swearing to him now is in consequence of passing through a field, and observing, among other boys assembled there for the purpose of gambling, a person resembling the prisoner so much, that I can not swear to him now—when before the Magistrate I laboured under the delusion that it was him; but seeing another lad so like him, I cannot conscientiously swear to him. NOT GUILTY.
