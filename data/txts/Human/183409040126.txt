On the 29th of August, I went to Mr. Clack, No. 77, Manchester-street, and found the lead here produced- I then went to the hospital, and looked at the cistern, and the other pieces of lead - I put the other pieces of lead with these, and they completely from the bottom of the cistern - I have no doubt these are part of it - I found this belt under the prisoner's waistcoat - it has the appearance of having carried lead.
   JOHN SOWARD. I am the superintendant of the works- the prisoner was in my brother's employ, who is a plumber- this lead and what was left formed the bottom of the cistern.
   CHARLES CLACK. I am brother of Mr. Clack, who lives at No. 77, Manchester-street - I was at home on the 27th of August, and he was out - the prisoner brought this lead about six or seven o'clock - I did not know him then - he asked me if Mr. Clack was at home - I told him no, but he could leave the lead till Mr. Clack came home if he liked - I weighed it - there was half a hundered weight and seven pound - he came the next morning, and brought two or three bits more, which he said he had forgotten - Mr. Clack was at home then, but he was not up, and the prisoner was to come again in the evening, but did not.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you ever say you did not know who the man was? A. I could not describe him to Mr. Clack - the prisoner rang the bell, and I went up stairs - he was in the shop then - I was up in half a minute - I do not know whether a man could have unrolled this lead from his body before I got up, but it was on the bench when I got up - it was rolled up - my brother was taken up - I cannot tell whether I stated that the prisoner was the man before my brother was taken up - I had not known the prisoner before - I had no suspicion of any thing being wrong - the lead was taken into our vault - I parted it into two lots, and wrapped it up in some new bits of our own.
 JURY. Q. You found the lead on the bench, and the prisoner in the shop, when you went up stairs - where did he then go? A. Down stairs to our lower shop, and when he left, he went up the area steps.
 Witnesses for the Defence.
   SUSANNAH SPRINGETT. I am a widow, and I live at the hospital - I saw the prisoner and Harvey both in the cistern together on the day stated, and I gave them a pint of beer to drink in the cistern.
 COURT. Q. Did they ask you to give them beer? A. No; I asked if they would like another pint - Harvey came out of the cistern, and took it of me - they were both down inside the cistern.
   JOHN PILLING. I am a carpenter. I was at work at the hospital - I saw the prisoner going away - I did not observe any thing particular about him - I saw Harvey come back again, and carry away a bag or a parcel between his arms.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. Do not you know that plumbers have a bag to carry tools in? A. Yes; it was something of that kind.
   GEORGE PITKIN. I am a carpenter, and was at work there. I saw the prisoner go away - I saw nothing more than ordinary about him - within eight or ten minutes of his leaving, Mr. Wheeler had ordered us some beer, and the prisoner drank some of it.
 MR. BODKIN. Q. Had he not a bag? A. I believe he had some tools - I did not notice whether he had a bag.

MARY VARLEY was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of July , 19 handkerchiefs, value 4l. 15s.; 9 pair of stockings, value 3l.; 24 pair of cotton stockings, value 1l. 16s.; 16 shirts, value 5l.; 12 towels, value 4s.; 6 night shirts, value 12s.; 1 dressing coat, value 18s.; and 1 night cap, value 9s.; the goods of Ann Porter.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the Prosecution.
 ANN PORTER. I am a  widow, and keep an outfitting warehouse, at Charing-cross. In July last, the prisoner came and said she had recommended my house to old Mr. and Mrs. Varley, her husband's father and mother - that they wanted a great many things; and first, they would have some silk handkerchiefs and stockings to choose from- I had my bonnet on, and was going out - I told my young woman to show the handkerchiefs - she said they were to be shown to Mr. and Mrs. Varley, and to be returned if not approved - she came several times, and at last I began to have some suspicion.
 Cross-examined by MR. DOANE. Q. Has not she been in the habit of dealing at your house for some years? A. Yes; since October, 1831 - either she or her husband paid for the goods she had - she asked for the running credit of twelve months, which she had, and paid soon after Christmas, but it was of small amount - we entered the goods she had in the waste book, which is here - I entered these goods to Mrs. Varley, but supposing it was for old Mrs. Varley - this entry was similar to the previous entries - I made out a bill of parcels to Mrs. Varley - I did not write"Old Mrs. Varley's."
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Would you have let her have these things for herself? A. No, not in any way; her entries before this were not more than 8l. in a year - I knew old Mr. and Mrs. Varley by report, and one of the parcels was directed to Pangbourn, near Reading.
   ELIZA GUY. I was in the prosecutrix's shop when the prisoner came for these things - I heard her say she had recommended Mrs. Porter to Mr. Varley, of Pangbourn, and she wished to have some handkerchiefs and other things - Mrs. Porter was going out, and I made up the order - I offered to send it, but she took it away herself - the goods were to be taken back or exchanged, if not approved by old Mr. and Mrs. Varley - the next day, she came again - she said two of the silk handkerchiefs were not approved of, and she looked out seven others - she wanted some more of the stockings, but we had none - she came again, and went on dealing, till the bill came to 25l.
 Cross-examined. Q. You entered the goods to Mrs. Varley? A. Yes, as we had before; and I gave her a memorandum of them, as she said they would want to know the price - I knew they were not for her.
