 1947.  HENRY HOWELL was indicted for stealing, on the 23rd of July , 1 pair of trousers, value 5s., and 1 handkerchief, value 3s., the goods of Daniel Miller.
   DANIEL MILLER. I live in Bridge-row, Paddington, and am a brick-maker; the prisoner lodged with me and worked for me. On the 23rd of July I got up early and went to work - he did not come to work; I went to look after him - I found him coming out of the house; I went into my room, and missed these articles: I went and found him - he had my trousers on under his own; I asked what he was going to do with them - he said to pawn them, to get a shilling or two to go to Wormwood-scrubs; I found the handkerchief in his hat.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Had you been accustomed to use each others things? A. No; I had lent him a pair of shoes to pawn, to pay me some money I had advanced for him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Three Months.
 1948.  JAMES HEALEY was indicted for stealing, on the 26th of July , 1 coat, value 20s., the goods of William Crawshaw.
   CHARLES HILL. I live with my father at the Southampton Arms, Tottenham-court-road. On the morning of the 26th of July I was looking out at the window - I saw the prisoner go up into a cab, and take a coat up and put it on his back; he walked into Southampton-court, and came back without it - I told the driver of it.
 Prisoner. Q. Why did not you give the alarm directly I took it? A. I knew you was about among the cabs - you had been feeding the horses with bay; I have had no quarrel with you.
   WILLIAM CRAWSHAW. I am driver of the cab No. 1057. I lost my coat on the 26th of July; I saw it again the same morning, in the hands of the Policeman; the prisoner came back to the stand, and began to feed the horses - I do not know whether he had been in the habit of doing so.
 Prisoner. Q. When I was taken you asked the witness what sort of a coat it was, and what did he reply? A. I asked him whether he saw the coat taken from the cab, with the grey horse - he said Yes; that it was a coat with three or four capes, and it has three - he said he saw you get up, take it up, and put it on, look round and then go off with it on your back.
   DAVID LAING(Police-constable E 58). I took the prisoner on the stand in Tottenham-court-road, on the 26th of July; a lodging was pointed out to me at No. 5, Southampton-court - the prisoner had lodged there, but left the Saturday before and was gone to No. 7; I went there, and found this coat in the back room first floor.
   ELIZABETH HADLEY. I am the wife of George Hadley; we live at No. 7, Southampton-court. The prisoner hired our first floor back room on the 21st of July, and slept there till the Thursday; the coat was found there - the street door was on the spring-lock, but could be opened outside; no one had occupied that room after he slept there - he had not given it up.
 Prisoner. Q. Was there not another man in the room? A. There was while you were there, but not afterwards.
 The prisoner put in a long written Defence, stating that he had not been at Hadley's lodgings after the night of the 22nd, on account of two other men sleeping there: and declaring his innocence.
   CHARLES HILL re-examined. Q. When did you tell the prosecutor you had seen the coat taken? A. When he was making inquiry for it, which was about five minutes before six o'clock - it had been taken about a quarter of an hour before.
   DAVID LAING. I found the coat about seven o'clock.
     GUILTY. Aged 18. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1949.  WILLIAM FRASER was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of July , 1 half-crown, 2 shillings, and 1 sixpence, the monies of John Mackewen , from the person of Sarah Mackewen , his wife.
 SARAH MACKEWEN. I am the wife of  John Mackewen; he is a horse-doctor; I sell fruit in the market. On the morning of the 31st of July, the prisoner came to me in Spitalfields-market, a little before seven o'clock; he asked for a pottle of raspberries, and offered 3d. for them, which I said I could not take - he went away and came back, and told me to tie him up a pottle; while I did that, I felt his hand in my pocket - I held his hand, and said,"You little rogue, you have got your hand in my pocket:" I took it out - he had some silver in it, and some copper: I saw one half-crown - I gave him into custody; he said it was his parents' money not mine, but what his mother had given him: I had a good deal of silver in my pocket, and some half-crowns.
 Cross-examined by MR. LEE. Q. Did you see his hand in your pocket? A. No, but I felt it - it was outside my pocket when I saw the half-crown in it; I cannot swear that the half-crown was not in his hand before: I did not see him with any newspapers.
   THOMAS STONEHOUSE(Police-constable H 81). I took the prisoner when the prosecutrix had hold of his hand; I found one half-crown and 1 1/2d. in his hand, and 6d. worth of half pence, and 2s. 6d. in his pocket.
 The prisoner put in a written Defence, denying that he had touched the prosecutrix's pocket; and stating that the money in his hand he had received for newspapers which he had sold in the street.
