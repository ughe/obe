ALFRED Spice (police-constable V 41.) On the morning of the 14th, about
half-past five or twenty minutes to six o'clock, I saw a wagon in the Clap-
ham-road-when it stopped, a basket of apples fell, and the prisoner stepped
off the path and picked them up-it was going towards town, in a direction
from the prosecutor's house, and about a quarter of a mile from it-I saw a
washing-tub on the rods of the wagon, but none of the rest of the property-
I have not seen the washing-tub which the officer saw-it is not here--I
asked the prisoner whether she was moving-she said from No. 50, Lark-
hall-lane I said there was no such number there she said there was
about half-way up the lane-I asked what the name of the place was-she
said, "No particular name, Larkball-lane."

262. MARY MACAULIFFE was again indicted for stealing, on the 3rd
of Sept., 1 gown, value 11. ; 6 knives, ls. ; 6 forks, 18.; and 20 paintings
and frames, the goods of Mary Ann Pitman, her mistress.

MARY ANN PITMAN. I am a widow, and live at No. 60, Walcot-place,
Lambeth. The prisoner was in my service for three weeks-she left on the
6th of Oct.-I missed a quantity of pictures from a closet in the kitchen
after she was gone-the policeman brought them afterwards the closet
was opened one day for the carpenter to ease the door, while the prisoner was
there-I locked it afterwards- I cannot swear to the knives and forks- I lost
some, and these look like mine--this dress is my sister's, and was kept in a
wardrobe in the room in which the prisoner slept. oktobra tt

tures at Mr. Dempster's, the pawnbroker's-he is not here--he gave the pro-
perty up before the Magistrate-I afterwards went to the different pawn-
