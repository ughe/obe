 1347. WILLIAM GUNN, was indicted for stealing, on the   18th of March , 135 newspapers, value 2 l  . 13 s  . the goods of Herbert Ingram and another, his masters.
   MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM DANIEL STEVENS. I am warehouseman to Palmer and Clayton, printers of the "Illustrated London News," Crane-court, Fleet-street. On Saturday, the 18th of March, about eleven o'clock in the day, the prisoner came to me for nine quires—he was then in the service of the owners of the paper—about an hour after he came for five more, or 135 copies—I gave him a delivery ticket for them—in the due course of business they should have been delivered to his employers—he did not bring me a ticket—he was to get one part of it signed by the publisher, and bring that part signed back to me—he did not bring the ticket of the five quires back—he did the nine.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Is it the practice for the person who takes the things to bring back the ticket? A.  Yes. WILLIAM LITTLE. I am publisher of the "Illustrated London News," and live in the Strand. The prisoner was in the proprietors' employ—on Saturday, the 18th of March, the prisoner was employed to bring copies from the printer's—at half-past eleven o'clock that morning he brought me a quantity—I do not know how many—he brought a delivery-ticket which I signed, and delivered to him—he did not bring me any more to my recol lection, there were so many deliveries—if he brought five quires more I do not remember seeing him more than once that day—he had no authority to sell any copies—135 copies are worth 2 l . 13 s . 9 d .—he did not come back after that Saturday.
