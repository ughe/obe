Carroll, Mayor.
Witness for the Defence.
FRANCIS JENNERY. I am a carman, and live at No. 55, Bermondsey.
street-Cockling drove my van for about four months, during my illness
on that morning I was unloading a load of goods at Bankside, and I was en
gaged by an unknown man to fetch some iron from Camberwell-he said the
carman was to meet him against St. George's church, to fetch some iron from
Camberwell, to bring it to Plimbey's-he did not agree about any price-1
left that to the man after he had done the work-I sent Cockling - he came
back about four o'clock in the afternoon, and gave me 68.
Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Where were you unloading the goods ? A. The
load came from Russell-street to Bankside--it was ordered at half-past six
o'clock-they were unloading about half-past nine in the morning-I do not
know what morning it was- I did not put it down-it was on a Saturday,
person came up almost against Mr. Plimbey's-he hardly spoke two words to
me-I do not know the name of the person from whom I took the goods-
was from a private house--the ladies had gone into the country for their
health--they were not unloaded at any house or wharf, but on the shore at
Bankside-Id o not know whose house they were fetched from in Russell-
street-my carman went-I was ill in bed at the time-I am ill now, I am
not fit to be here- I got up about eight o'clock-I had my breakfast, and
walked to Bankside-I went by myself-they were then unloading these
goods-my carman's name is Cockling down here--I do not know whether
his name is Conley-I only know him by driving for 'me-I only know his
name is “Cockling" down on the subpoena-I cannot swear whether Haines
is the man who came up while we were unloading I cannot swear to any
man-I went up to the Magistrate, but I was too late-I knew my man was
an innocent man- I was too ill to go before the Magistrate again-1 hare
been ill these ten months-I never saw the man before or since who came to
my carman-- I did not notice whether he was tall or short, nor how he was
dressed- I did not inquire where he lived, or what he was about-I have no
impression whether he might be a gentleman or a common working man-1
had but two words with him, and did not notice bim-I had no character
with Cockling-he behaved very well to me,
JAMES JENNINGS. I am a master mariner-I was in the police- produce
a certificate of Haines's former conviction at this Court-(read-Convicted
23rd of Oct., 1837, and confined six months)-he is the person.
HAINES-Guilty.
CONLEY and COCKLING-Not Guilty.
