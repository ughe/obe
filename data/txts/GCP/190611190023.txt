CLAUDE VANZANDT, manager to Mr. Hooydonk. Prisoner never
paid to me the amounts which are the subjeot of this indictment,
Whether prisoner paid me or Mr. Hooydonk depended upon which of
us was in the place. Sometimes Mrs. Hooydonk would receive money.
When accounts were paid they were marked " Paid" on the slips pro-
duced. As prisoner received the orders he wrote them down. I do
not know what prisoner paid Mrs. Hooydonk. I can only speak to
those slips which I marked myself. I cannot speak to Hooydonk's.
The slips were not produced before the magistrate; they were not
asked for.
The Common Serjeant could not understand how prisoner over came
to be committed without this evidence. Under Finlay's Aot, dealing
with the fraudulent conversion of money received by a person who
is not a servant, the prosecution must prove all the elements of lar
oeny or embezzlement, except that the accused was & servant, what
his position was, that he was not merely a debtor having a general
account, that he received certain súms which it was his duty to hand
over, and that he fraudulently converted not a general balance, but
particular sums. On these depositions that could not be proyed. The
whole of the evidence really consisted in the one statement proved by
the police-constable, that the prisoner admitted that he received the
money, but said as he was not a servant they could do nothing.

would have been taken up by a solicitor and thoroughly worked out.

allowed to the prosecutor for coming forward in the interests of the
public, but this had been so often pointed out that it was not worth
