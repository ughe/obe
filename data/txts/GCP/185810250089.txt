1009. EPHRAIM MANNING (21), (a soldier), Feloniously forging and
uttering a request for the delivery of 1 pair of boots, with intent to defraud.
MR. BODKIN conducted the Prosecution. --- ore 10%
William KEEN. I am a boot-maker, of Artillery-place, Woolwich, and
am shopman to Mr. Phillips there, who makes for the companies and detach-
ments of the Royal Artillery, On 17th September, the prisoner came to
the shop with this order (Read : “Woolwich, 17th September, 1858.
Messrs. Phillips, please to give gunner E. M. Manning, & pair of boots.
12th Battalion, Royal Artillery. H. Collins, Pay sergeant.") I knew Cola
lins to be the pay-sergeant, and, believing it to be a genuine document, I
let him have the boots--they were worth 198.—these are them (produced);
I could swear to them from a thousand—I did not know him previonsly-
I saw him again a week or ten days after I went before his Colonel, and
pointed him out from several others all dressed alike.
HENRY COLLINS. I am pay-sergeant of the 7th company of the 12th
battalion of the Royal Artillery at Woolwich-the prisoner was one of my
company-I did not give him any order to obtain boots in my name—the
paper is not my writing, or written by my authority-I have seen the
prisoner write, but cannot say whether this is his writing.
* John NEWALL (Police-sergeant, R). On 29th September, the prisoner was
brought to the station, and I told him the charge--he said that he had not
done anything of the kind, and did not know anything about it.
THOMAS WILD. I am assistant to & pawirbroker of Woolwich these
boots were pawned there on the afternoon of 17th September, by a femalo.
Nor Guiry.

1010. WILLIAM THOMAS (40), Unlawfully obtaining from Augustus
North, by fraud at play, 1 watch, value 152., and 1 chain, value 31. 108.,
his property. MR. RIETON conducted the Prosecution.
AUGUSTUS NORTH. I am a brewer, of Cardiff. In July last, I was in London,
and on the 30th, I went to Woolwich- a gentleman addressed me on board the
boat - some conversation took place, and when I got on shore at Woolwich
he asked me to go and have a glass of ale with him before we parted-after
some persuasion, I went with him to Mr. Murray's, the Gun Hotel, into a
room upstairs, in which there was nobody else I had one glass of ale, and
after I had drank it, the prisoner came in alone, and after that, another man
caune in and introduced a ring for sale to the whole three no one would
buy it, and he immediately introduced three cards—the prisoner and the
one who was in the boat with me got betting, and wanted me to bet-I
said no, as I had left my purse in London-they got from 5l. to 101.- I saw
which was the black card when he laid it down, and saw some money and
a 101, note pass between them-I said, “I know which is the card”- they
said, "Why do not you bet if you know the card ?" both the prisoner and
