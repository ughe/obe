 NEW COURT. WEDNESDAY, DECEMBER 15.
 Third Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 243.  JAMES BOWDEN was indicted for stealing, on the 6th of December , 11 pairs of stockings, value 10s., the goods of Samuel Everingham.
 WILLIAM JACKSON. I am shopman to Mr. Samuel Everingham: he is a  hosier, and lives in Oxford-street. I was at his door about twenty-five minutes past ten o'clock in the morning of the 6th of December: I saw the prisoner looking at the things - I went behind the counter, and in a few minutes I saw something fall at the door; I ran out, and saw the prisoner in about a minute in Noel-street - he had an apron on with something in it; I brought him back to the shop, and called an officer, who opened his apron, and found these eleven pairs of stockings, which are my master's, and had been at the door.
   JOHN MC.GILL. On the 6th of December I was called, and took the prisoner in the shop; I found these eleven pairs of stockings in his apron - I took him to the watch-house, and asked what he meant to do with them; he said some boys in Regent-street said they would give him 6d. a pair, if he could get some.
     GUILTY. Aged 13. - Transported for Seven Years
 244.  ALICE DAVIS and ANN DAVIS were indicted for stealing, on the 30th of October , 35 yards of ribbon, value 20s., the goods of William Davies.
 GEORGE HOWELL. I am shopman to Mr. William Davies, a  haberdasher of Chiswell-street. On the 30th of October I saw both the prisoners in my master's shop, about half-past five o'clock; one of them asked to look at some ribbon - I showed them a drawer and a basket of ribbons; they bought two lengths of 1 1/2 yard each, which came to 2s. 2 1/4d., and they paid for them - I saw Alice Davis had a handkerchief in her hand, which she put on a piece of ribbon on the counter; she took her handkerchief away, and I missed the ribbon, but I did not see what was done with it - she put her handkerchief on the counter several times, but no more ribbon was missed; I did not see Ann do any thing but talk to the other - I did not hear what was said; I told Mr. John Davies, and he went to the door - I showed them some crape handkerchiefs, but they said they did not want any; they were then going out - Mr. John Davies stopped them when they had got about two yards from the counter - he took them into the back shop, and Ann delivered up two half-pieces of ribbon; I did not see them delivered up, but when I went in a young man was measuring them over.
   JOHN DAVIES. I am brother to William Davies. In consequence of information from Howell I came into the shop - I waited about the door till the prisoners were going out, and then detained them; I took them to the back shop - they went quietly, but one of them appeared confused when I spoke to them; I did not see Ann do any thing when she came from the counter, I am quite sure of that; I spoke to them, and asked how many pieces of ribbon they had got; Ann held herself down, and I saw she had a handkerchief and a bag in her hand - she gave me this half-piece of ribbon; I then said to Alice, "How many have you got?" she said, "We have got no more, Sir;" I asked them to walk up higher in the warehouse - I again asked Alice, and told her I had seen her several times, and always suspected that she stole ribbons; I felt their pockets, and found nothing, but in about ten minutes I lifted up Ann's cloak, and found this other half-piece under her arm; they were shown to the shopman.
   GEORGE HOWELL. I saw Alice put her handkerchief on one of these, but I cannot swear which - it was one of the same pattern as one of these; I believe I have stated that I could not swear that either of the ribbons produced were what I saw Alice draw off the counter - it was the same pattern. but I cannot swear it is the same price; here is the private mark of the shop on them - when we sell these we sell them with the mark on; I had sold them some ribbon - this is it.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. You went from the prisoners to Mr. Davis? A. Yes - I do not believe they had then paid for what they had bought, but I cannot say to a certainty; I did leave them when I went to Mr. Davies, and they were within four yards of the door: Mr. Davies was about twenty-five yards further in - I found the prisoners in the same place when I came back; the door was open, and they might have gone out.
 COURT. Q. Do you speak to these ribbons by the marks on them? A. Yes.
 JURY. Q. Are you sure you had not sold them these? A. Yes.
   JOHN DAVIES re-examined. Q. Did you say any thing before the Magistrate about Ann stooping down? A. I said they did not stoop at the time they went out.
   DAVID WILSON. I am an officer. I was sent for, and took the prisoner and these two pieces of ribbon - as I went up to them I saw one piece on the counter, and Mr. Davies was in the act of taking the other from Ann: Ann said she had picked them up from the shop floor - I took them to the station.
