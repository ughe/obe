WILLIAM WYBURD. I am an inspector of bank notes at the Bank of
England. This 102. note is a forgery—there is a date plate to it. I have
no doubt the date has been let in by another process, not engraved on the
original plate-it is not engraved on the plate in genuine notes the signature
of the supposed cashier is a forgery—the two bl. notes are also forged-the
dates of all the three notes are the same, and I have no doubt the same date
plate has been used for all three—the name of the cashier appears to have
been partly impressed, and filled up with ink I have no doubt the signature
to all the three notes has been effected by the same process.
Cross-examined. Q. Is it a tolerably good imitation 4. I think the
engraving is a very fair specimen--the process of the manufacture differs from
ours; the paper is very inferior to ours--the water mark is not stamped,
but impressed all three apparently come from the same plate
COURT. Q. Is the value," 10" and "51." engraved on the original plate,
or put in by a different plate 4. I think it is engraved in the original
plate.
