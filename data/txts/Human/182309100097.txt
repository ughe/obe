I went to his garden, and there was not six potatoes dug up. There is not room in his garden to grow these. I am certain of the corn.
 Cross-examined. Q. You do not mean to swear to corn - A. Yes; it is grown in marsh land; there is no corn like it in England. I have a sample of the bulk.
   RICHARD BARNETT. I thrash for the prosecutor, and believe these oats to he his, but cannot swear to them.
 Cross-examined. Q. No man can swear to oats - A. No.
     GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Six Months.

 1218.  MARGARET KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of August , a purse, value 6 d.; a sovereign, a crown, a shilling, and two sixpences, the property of James Smith , from his person.
   JAMES SMITH. I live in High-street, St. Giles's, and am a hair-dresser. On the 27th of August, about half past eleven o'clock at night, I was going home down Oxford-street - between Charles-street and High-street I was accosted by the prisoner - she asked me to go home with her - I desired her to leave me; she insisted upon walking with me; I stopped when I got to Bainbridge-street; I stopped there that she might not know where I lived, being just at home. I desired her to leave me - she went off in a very abrupt manner, which rose my suspicion. I put my hand to my right hand trowsers pocket, and my purse, which contained a sovereign, a crown, and two shillings, was gone; it must have been taken while I stood endeavouring to get rid of her - I did not feel her hand in pocket - I stopped with her nowhere else. I followed her immediately - she was not above four or five paces off - she ran into a private house, the door of which stood open; I followed her up four or five stairs - did not like to go further, but returned to the door and called the watchman - two came in, and found her concealed at the top of the garret stairs; she said she supposed I only wanted my money, and it was down stairs; she had concealed it under the stairs at the bottom; we went down, and she shewed the watchman where it was; my money was still in it. I swear that I had no intention to have any thing to do with her.
   EDWARD YARRANTON. I am a watchman. I heard a cry of Watch! and found the prosecutor standing at the door, of No. 6, Bainbridge-street, which is not a bad house, but a lodging-house for labouring people. I went up and found the prisoner at the top of the stairs; he said that was the person who robbed him of his purse. She said he only wanted his property, and she would give it him. I found it under the stairs.
   JOSEPH LOCKETT. I am a watchman. I saw the purse found.
   JAMES SMITH. It is my purse, and contains the money.
 Prisoner's Defence I asked him for some drink, and parted with him; and at the corner of the street, I stopped down, and picked up the purse.
   JAMES SMITH. It could not have fallen out of my pocket.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Transported for Seven Years.

 1220.  RICHARD MADDISON was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , two pair of boots, value 8 s., the goods of John Powell.
   JOHN POWELL. I live in Play House-yard, Golden-lane, and am a boot-maker; the prisoner lodged with me; I worked in his room. On the 1st of July, about six o'clock in the evening, I placed these boots in a box by my side, and missed them in the morning; he slept there next night, and appeared an industrious man.
   SIMON PARSONS. I lodge in the same room. The prisoner came home in the evening, went out and returned between ten and eleven o'clock, and left in the morning before the boots were missed - we all worked in the room.
   WILLIAM SHARP. I am a pawnbroker. On the 1st of July, between seven and eight o'clock in the evening, the prisoner pawned these boots for 4 s.; he said he had them to make for a lady, who was not at home; and he wished to pawn them till morning.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I found the duplicate of them among the tools, but did not pawn them.
     GUILTY. Aged 27. Confined One Month.
