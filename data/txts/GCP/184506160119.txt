1874. MATILDA WODWORTH was indicted for stealing 7 shirts,
valge 168.; 2 flat-irons, 1s. ; and 2 blankets, 6s. ; the goods of Samuel
Emmerson, her master.
SOPHIA EMMERSON. I am wife of Samuel Emmerson. The prisoner
was onr servant- I lost these articles-the shirts belonged to a lodger, but
I had the care of them, and have to make them good the other things are
mine.
GEORGE YOUNG. I am a pawnbroker-I have five shirts and two flat
irons-the prisoner pawned one shirt on the 22nd of April, and she pawned
some of the others.
Prisoner. I own to pawning the shirts ; I told her the property was
pawned, and she promised to forgive me.
Sophia EMMERSON re-examined. She embezzled some money which I
promised to forgive her for, but not for this she told me the night before
she left that she had pawned the things, but she did not say where.
Guilty. Aged 33.-Confined Two Months.

1375. THOMAS GEORGE KETTLE was indicted for stealing 1
parse, value 1s. ; 1 metal top of a shaving-glass, 1s. ; 3 half-crowns, 14
shillings, 4 sixpences, and 1 groat; the property of William Ottway, his
master.
Mary Hows. I am the wife of Robert Howe. On Saturday morning,
the 6th of June, the prisoner brought me a parcel to take care of till a young
man named Hutchings called for it-I did not open it-my husband said
to the prisoner, “What is this?" and he said "Money."
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. How long have you known him?
4. Not a great while-he never lent us money-he once brought 2s, which
were to be left at my place I never made use of them-he called again for
them in about two hours-he never left money with me before.
WILLIAM OTTWAY. The prisoner was in any service-on the evening
of the 6th of June I went to my bed-room, and missed 11. 3s. 100.-I sus-
pected the prisoner-I went up stairs to his bed-room, looked in his box,
and found 78.--the next morning I asked if he had paid his tailor, whom
he had agreed to pay his week's wages to every week-he said, no, he
could not pay it without money-I said, "Have you got no money ?"-he
said, "No"--I said, “I think you have"-he said, "I have got 38. up
stairs in my box"-I said, "Have you not got more"-he said, "I think
there is 4s. or 58.; you have been looking into my box ; I thought some-
body had been there"-I said, “Yes, I have" -he went up stairs and
brought down 68.-(there were 7s. there the night before)—his mother and
I went to Howe's-she gave me 178. 10d., which, with the 6s. the prisoner
gave me, tallied with what I had lost.
Cross-examined. Q. I believe you have got all the money? 4. The
policeman has it—the prisoner lived with me eighteen months before-up
to this time I had no reason to be dissatisfied with him.
JAMES Hands (police-sergeant K 21.) I took the prisoner into custody.
Cross-examined. Q. Did he say anything? 4. He told the inspector
that Howe had brought him into all this trouble, that he had induced him
to rob a good and a kind master-he appeared in a great deal of trouble.
MARY Howe re-examined. It is a great falsity-my husband is a green-
grocer, and has been so for six years.
Guilty. Aged 19.-Confined Three Months.
