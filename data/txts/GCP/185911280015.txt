Cross-examined by MR. METOALFE (with MR. LEWIS). Q. What do you
mean by receiving letters every two or three days! A. During the time I
was absent he wrote to me, until I came over-it is one day's post-I do not
think I received any letter between the 21st and 25th-(The following
letter was here put in and read : “To Mr. Logan. London, October 20th,
1859. Dear Sir, on the 23d of last month we handed your clerk 667.
towards your acceptance of 1301. due on that day, for which we shonld have
sent the whole amount as arranged with you but through the amount having
been entered by our cashier to the smaller amount in his diary. The bill
was returned on the 24th unpaid. When we gave our cheque for the full
amount, and made application to your clerk for the 651. to be returned to
us, his reply was that the bill had not been presented at the office. This
was in reply to our telling him that he should have brought it to us and
made some inquiry about it, as he acknowledged he knew he had to receive
the whole amount from us ; and he gave us a cheque for it on the Bank of
England; upon presenting which it was refused payment, the bank not
having his signature. We then requested him to send a cheque to you for
your signature, which he said he did at once, and has been daily expecting
to hear from you or to see you back. We cannot think that you are treating
this matter in the manner you ought to have done, in as much as it was done
for your accommodation, and you shonld have at once returned the cheque.
signed, to reimburse us for our cash ; we therefere beg you will immediately
on receipt of the present send us the amount. We are, sir, yours obediently,
Blogg and Martin.")—the prisoner told me he would repay me the 307. before
he told me he was retaining it for the 651.—he said that, either on the 28th or

-I had not seen the pass-book at that time; I don't remember Mr. Wil.
liams being present; he was present on one occasion, I think about the 28th,
29th, or 30th-it was a day or two after I had seen the prisoner-I can't
say the exact day, perhaps it was the 29th-I first saw the prisoner alone
I am sure that I spoke to him about the 301. before I saw the pass-book-
I think I spoke to him before I saw Mr. Williams-I did not know that the
651. had been advanced until I received Blogg and Martin's letter at Boulogne,
dated the 20th, and which the prisoner sent to me in his own letter the
first time I saw the prisoner be told me he had paid 651. to Mr. Stevenson
and he held his receipt-I asked him where he got the 651.-he told me
that he had borrowed 1007. of Mr. Williams-Mr. Williams was present
when that was stated and he confirmed it-I asked if he had any
objection to show me the receipt for it-he said he would, but he never
did-Mr. Williams is now bail for him in 4001.--I said at first that
I should let Blogg and Martin bring the action for the 651. and I would
defend it--I afterwards said I should not defend it unless the prisoner's
friends would guarantee the costs in any event, win or lose-I do not
remember Mr. Williams saying that that was unreasonable, and he
should not do it-he said he would see about it; he would inquire about it.
