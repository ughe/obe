THOMAS CORNEY, Esq. I am Deputy of Broad-street ward. The Bank
is situated there I can hardly say that it is left to me to settle the times
of the omnibuses there, but I generally do—the officers of the ward seem
to be under my direction-I wrote a circular letter to the omnibus pro-
prietors in 1833, in consequence of the great confusion at the Bank, and the
Governor of the Bank having written to me that he should put the Bank
Act in force ; I saw him, and told him I would enter upon it, and make
some arrangement, which I did I was instructed by the police committee
to make some arrangements, that times might be set by the proprietors, and
kept by the time-keepers-I invited the proprietors to meet me, to make
some arrangements which I prescribed; one was, that no longer should
any cabs be allowed there, as we found a great many robberies topk place,
and there were thieves taking advantage and robbing the public--the officers
were ordered to remove any persons of that description- few of the par-
ties met me, but by no means half their number_Mr. Shillibeer was then
