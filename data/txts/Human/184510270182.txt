   MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  Did your mother take out any summons that day? A.  She did not tell me—she went to Kingsgate-street and took a summons out, but she went to High-street for a warrant—she did not tell me whether she got one or not—she told me she had been talking to Mr. Whip, but she did not tell me any more—I was with her—I did not hear her ask the Magistrate for any warrant.
   Q.  You have been giving evidence here in marriage cases before, have you not? A.  Yes—I do not know which was the last time—it was a charge of bigamy—I was a witness, and gave evidence of a marriage of my mother to a man on the Easter Monday in 1844, I think—the man set up a story that he was not the man, but that my mother had married some body else in his name, but she was married to him, for I saw her—I was present at the church—there was another witness, a woman—I have known my mother married once, and I believe she was married the second time—I have heard of a man named Sheehan, but I do not know him—I cannot say whether I have seen him or not—I do not recollect ever seeing him—no man of that name ever lived along with my mother—she was married to M'Cann on Easter Monday, 1844—I believe she married again after that. THOS. HYETT re-examined.  I did not know Mr. or Mrs. Smith before—when the prisoner gave me the parcel she said, "Take that to Mrs. Smith," telling me she lived at No. 3, Pier's Cooperage—I knew Pier's Cooperage, and No. 3—I am not able to read the numbers—I found out No. 3 by asking a little girl down there if she knew a person named Smith at No. 3—I asked the neighbours, and they told me, and I went to the third house down—the policeman came to take me to the police-office. THOS. HICKMAN re-examined.  I found out Hyett through the prose cutor—I went with him to the boy's house. HENRY SMITH re-examined.  I inquired of the neighbours if they knew the little boy that had brought the parcel, and it happened that some of the children in the Cooperage knew him, and by that means took me to his place. THOS. HYETT re-examined.  A little girl in the Cooperage, named Logan, knew where I lived—I did not ask her where Mrs. Smith lived—I went to the third house down and asked, and it was not there—they said it was at the other third door—I went to the other third door, and that was Mrs. Smith's. GUILTY. Aged 32.— Confined Twelve Months .
