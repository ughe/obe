   George Day . What he says is false—he came here only to get a few shillings for this prosecution. GEORGE ADNUM. I live in High Holborn, in the service of Mr. Benton, a pawnbroker. I produce this gold seal—it was pledged with me on the 9th of January last, by George Day, I believe.
   George Day. Q.  Are You positive? A.  I do not say that I am positive, I believe you are the person—the address given was No. 6, Denton-street, Somers-town and the name of James Smith—that was where you lived at that time, as your brother Charles tells me.
   George Day . I did not live there at that time—the seal has been pledged in the name of Smith which is James Day's mother's maiden name, that is how it came to be pledged in that name—I never saw any of the property—I heard of this seal being pledged about a fortnight ago, but not before.
   James Day . I was the one that pawned it. GEORGE ADMAN re-examined . Is might he James Day that pawned it, but I believe it was George Day.
   James Day . I came and asked you if you would take it in—you asked me how I got it—I said I got it in a raffle, and was going to raffle it again—I asked you if you would be a member. Witness, I still believe it was George Day that pawned it. JAMES DAVIS( police-constable C  90.) The prisoner George Day was put into my charge—I took to the station-house and searched him, and then searched his cab—his sister went with me, who had lost some money, which she gave charge of him for—his sister went to the stable with me, which is in Lancaster-street—I found this box there—I brought it to the station-house, and a female who was given into custody with George on the other charge gave me the key—opened it and found this ribbon and key, which the prosecutor claims, and the license for a cabriolet—I referred to the police reports, and found one of them had been charged with stealing a gentleman's watch—I went and told George Day that I had found them, and asked if I had to take James Day, who was then with the cab, who was I to leave the cab with—he said it was a bad job, and that James Day put the key and ribbon there—he did not say whose box it was.
