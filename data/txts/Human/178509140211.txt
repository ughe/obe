Gentlemen, upon the evidence, I may say of these two men, the prosecutor and the defendant, both the prisoners were convicted: it being afterwards heard by a variety of people, who knew the prisoners, what evidence had been given at the Old Bailey; they insisted upon it that the evidence of both the prosecutor and Harvey in the most material parts were false: an investigation took place, I believe some affidavits were procured, and, if I am not misinstructed, laid before the Recorder, he inquired into the circumstances, and his humanity induced him to procure a respite for these two convicts the day before the day appointed for their execution; the matter afterwards underwent an investigation before the King in Council, and in a fortnight a pardon was issued for the two men. Gentlemen, as this is a prosecution instituted merely for public justice, I shall not make any observations to inflame your passions, or to mislead your judgments, I shall content myself in submitting to you simply, and in as plain a manner as I can, the evidence I mean to produce to you on this occasion. I understand the defendant means to call witnesses, if he does, and I think it necessary and proper, I may trouble you with a few observations on that evidence. Gentlemen, I have no wish on this occasion but that justice should take place; this poor man,  Peter Newberry, is a hard working labouring man, that lived by cutting wood, and lodged in the house of one Peter Goodman; Goodman got his living that way; he had been at work all day, and just before the present defendant, and the said soldier, who alledged he had been robbed were in search of Newberry, Newberry had been out, and was returning home, and a person met him, that I shall call as a witness, and told him there were the old soldier and the defendant Harvey, whom they knew to be a kind of constable, pretty active in taking thieves, and they advised him not to go home; his answer was, I have done no harm, I am not afraid of any one, I certainly will go home: the witness went with him to his lodgings, and saw him go in, and when the prisoner entered the lodgings, the old soldier and Harvey were up stairs searching for Newberry; on his coming in, a woman called to him, and told him here is Newberry; Newberry is come in; upon which the old soldier and Harvey came down stairs, and in the lodgings within the house took Newberry into custody. I am instructed, and shall call another witness, who advised the man not to go home, and his answer will be proved by four or five, if not more, witnesses, who were all present at the time of the caption of Newberry, and they will, upon their oaths, tell you, that Newberry had been taken in the lodgings, and consequently had never been guilty of these two great signs of guilt, fear and flight. Gentlemen, if upon the evidence you should be satisfied, that what the defendant has sworn is false; if you should be convinced that what my witnesses say is true, it will then become your duty to convict the defendant of the crime imputed to him; but if you have any doubt, in that case you will acquit him: I shall press it in no manner, I shall make no observations without I find it absolutely necessary.
   JAMES BATTISCOMBE sworn.
