FREDERICK Le GRos CLARK. I am assistant surgeon at St. Thomas's
Hospital, and lecturer on anatomy--on the 14th of Oct. I was informed
a man had been brought in with a severe wound in his neck-I went
across to the surgery and saw the prosecutor, and one of my colleagues
was on the spot endeavouring to staunch the blood from the woundhe
had suceeeded temporarily in doing 80, by putting his fingers in the wound
-we conferred together, and thought the only means of saving his life
would be to perform an operation, which consisted in making a wound
lower down in the neck, and passing a ligature round the trunk of the
carotid artery--as his fingers were bloody it was agreed I should perform
the operation, which I did-there was not more than a spoonful of blood
lost in the operation, and that caused the cessation of the blood-rin my
opinion, if he had been left to himself, he would unquestionably have been
dead in two or three minutes- putting instrument, more or less pointed,
would have inflicted the wound- table-knife would very probably have
done it--there is no question but such a knife as his-(loohing at one)-would
have inflicted the wound.
JOaN DavIs (City police-constable 551.) I heard of this affair on the 14th
of Oct. about half-past one o'clock-in consequence of hearing of it I went to

true that one of our officers had been taken to the hospital dangerously
wounded-the prisoner said if I came up she would tell me all about it-she
stated that they had a quarrel at dinner, and she threw a knife at him-she
did not know she had cut him till she saw him stooping down, and she saw
the blood running from his neck-while I was there an order came from Mr.
Smith, the doetor, that she should be taken, and I took her into custody-
there were two knives on the table-I enquired which was the knife she did
it with-she said this one-(producing it.)
