643. GEORGE HILLIARD was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of February , 1 pair of scales, value 2s. 6d., the goods of Isabella Jones, widow.
   JAMES KENNERLY. On the evening of the 1st of February I was in High-street, St. Giles', and saw the prisoner, in company with another person - I followed the prisoner, who was going my way; I met Betraun - we followed the prisoner and the other on to Holborn, and from there on to Great Warner-street, Clerkenwell; I there saw his companion push open the door of the shop No. 9 - he came out again immediately, and joined the prisoner, who was close by him when he went in - they then both ran off. I followed them, and laid hold of both of them; but the other got away; I kept the prisoner till Betraun came up.
   ANGELIOUS BETRAUN. On the evening of the 1st of February I joined Kennerly in High-street, St. Giles' - we followed them to Great Warner-street; I received the prisoner from Kennerly, and in his jacket pocket I found these scales.
   ANN HUNT. I am the daughter of Isabella Jones, a widow, who keeps a shop at No. 9, Great Warner-street. These scales are hers - I used them about three minutes before they were missed.
 Prisoner's Defence. The other  boy took them, and I did not know of it - he saw them coming, and tried to shove them into my pocket.
   JAMES KENNERLY. He said so at the time, but I did not see it.
 The prisoner received a good character.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. - Confined Six Months.
 644.  JOSIAH RANDALL was indicted for stealing, on the 31st of January , 1 saw, value 8s., the goods of Emanuel Coward.
   EMANUEL COWARD. I am a carpenter, and live in Gray's Inn-lane. I was at work at Messrs. Child and Vickers, the distillers, in Stone-street, Borough. On the 31st of January I left my tools in a basket in the stable there - I went again about a quarter before one o'clock, and missed a saw out of my tools; I made inquiry, but could not find it.
   THOMAS COWARD. I am the prosecutor's father - I have known the prisoner twenty years or nearly so. In consequence of information, I went after him, and found him near the Crown, in Holywell-lane - I told him he had been taking a saw from Child and Vicker's distillery - he denied it; I said, "You have pawned it, give me the duplicate;" I went with him to his lodging, and after some conversation, he gave me the duplicate.
   WILLIAM CLARK. I am shopman to Mr. Attenborough, a pawnbroker, in Crown-street. On the 31st of January, about dinner time, this saw was pawned by the prisoner; I lent him 2s. 6d. on it, and gave this duplicate.
   THOMAS WHITTLE. I am a Police-constable. I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded poverty.
     GUILTY. Aged 46. - Confined One Year.
 645.  JOHN HART was indicted for stealing, on the 12th of February , 1 pair of boots, value 8s. 6d., the goods of Ephraim Wigginton.
 MARY WIGGINTON. I am the wife of  Ephraim Wigginton- we live in Old-street, and keep a shoe warehouse. On the 12th of February I saw the prisoner at our shop; he looked at a pair of boots and a pair of shoes also - he desired me to put up the shoes in the window; while I was doing that he ran out - I looked, and missed the boots immediately; in consequence of information I went on the following morning to Ironmonger-row, where I saw him at work; I knew him to be the man, and I asked him for the money for the boots which he took away on the Saturday; he said he was not looking at boots, he was looking at shoes - I said he had taken away the boots which he had on: I knew they were the boots which had been in my window - he said he had bought them in Gravel-lane; he first said at a house, and then he said of a man - I got an officer, and he was taken - I know the boots by a mark inside them.
 Prisoner. She said I wore fustian trousers. Witness. No, it was dark - I said I was not sure whether he had the same trousers on then which he had before.
   WILLIAM BELSHAM. I am in the prosecutor's service. I know these boots - I had been in the habit of cleaning them; I saw the prisoner in the shop when I was sent out, and when I came back he was gone - I saw him when he was taken, and knew him to be the same man.
   HENRY PERRY. I am a Police-constable. The prisoner was brought to our station with these boots on his feet - he said he had bought them of a man of the name of Callaghan, who worked at Curtis' bacon yard over the water, for 8s. 6d. on the Saturday evening.
   JOHN CALLAGHAN. I am a labourer at Mr. Curtis' bacon yard, in Holland-street. I never sold a pair of boots to the prisoner, or lent him or gave him any - I have known him some time; he brought a pair of boots to my house on the Saturday before he was apprehended, and said he bought them in Ironmonger-row - I do not know whether these are them.
 Prisoner's Defence. I did not buy them nor steal them - I went in to buy a pair of boots; I was drunk, and did not know what I did.
     GUILTY. Aged 25. - Confined Three Months.
 646.  JOSEPH MOORE and BENJAMIN JENNINGS were indicted for stealing, on the 22nd of January , 18 bushels of soot, value 15s., the goods of Thomas Spearing.
   THOMAS SPEARING. I live in Broomyard-alley, Longalley. I was called out of my bed between three and four o'clock - I went to my shed, found the door open, and the lock and staple had been drawn; I missed a quantity of soot which had been loose about the shed - I stated it as ten sacks at first, but that was not correct; I suppose six sacks were gone - I went to No. 10, Cock-alley, and found the prisoners there; some soot was on the floor of the room they were in - I called for a basin of water to try whether it was my property or not, and it was; because there was saw dust mixed with it as there had been with the soot at my shed - we cannot help taking up the ashes of whatever there is mixed with the soot, and this was saw dust of mahogany - I compared it with that at my shed, and found it was the same - it is very common in the trade to mix saw-dust with soot.
