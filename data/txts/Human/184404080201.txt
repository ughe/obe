THOMAS PRESS HEALEY, Esq., barrister-at-law, King's-bench-walk, Temple.  I am part proprietor, with Mr. Withers, of "The Medical Times"—The pri soner was oar clerk until the 16th of March, 1844—he has not accounted to me for 1 l . from John Steghall, or 8 s . from Mr. James Cook—I discharged him on the 16th of March this year—I had not then discovered these sums—there were some other matters that I spoke to him about, and I distinctly asked if he had received any other monies that he had not-entered in the book, and he distinctly said, "No"—He explained the non entry of the 8 s . in the book by stating that it had been paid in wages by him, and debited in the book, it does not appear so debited—I discovered about the 8 s . after he was in custody, before the Magistrate—in the event of his having paid 8 s . in wages, he should have entered 8 s . less in his account—but I find this has not been the case—he has entered the full amount of wages paid.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What day was the money received? A.  The 17th of February—on that day he made up his payments, and 2 l . 15 s . 11 d . was the sum total of the day's expenses, but there are no particular items to make up that aggre gate—it is an aggregate of the two or three days preceding, as well as that day—if the 2 l . 15 s . 11 d . is cast up correctly, the 8 s . cannot be in cluded, because each item in the book is marked, and this 8 s . would have been marked as having been received—1 l . 7 s . was paid on Saturday night to the prisoner and the boys for wages.
   MR. PARRY . Q.  He paid 1 l . 7 s . every week out of the till? A.  Yes. This receipt book ought to contain an entry of this 8 s .—it does not contain it—if he had paid it in wages it oughtto have appeared 8 s . less in this book—he ought to have taken. 19 s . out of the till, and debited himself 1 l ., 7 s .
