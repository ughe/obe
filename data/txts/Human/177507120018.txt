We then went to the Hoop and Horse Shoe, and there they began to play at shaking a halfpenny, and putting it under the candlestick; then M'Oris said, he hoped Gibbons would do by him, as he did by the gentleman the night before; that was, give him a shilling for every guinea he could show; then M'Oris pulled out two guineas and an half, and he gave him two shillings; then M'Oris said, Gibbons was a captain, and he gave countrymen a shilling for every guinea they could shew. I then pulled out two guineas and an half, and he was to give me half a crown, when the landlord came in; then they invited me to play with them, I told them, I did not choose to play at all; they played again, and M'Oris said to me, let me have your money; no, I said, I did not chuse that at all; upon that, he took hold of my left hand, and took the money out of my hand; he held the money in his hand, and shewed it to Gibbons, and said, see this young man has two guineas and a half, I hope you will do by him as you did by me; one of them asked me what o'clock it was, I pulled out my watch to see, and put it in again? as soon as I had put it in, M'Oris took hold of the chain and pulled it out, and said, it was a good watch, and threw the watch and money into Gibbons's hat; then they walked together up and down the room with the money and watch; I said, they had taken my money and watch, and I did not chuse to loose it in that manner; then M'Oris bid me come to the door; when we came to the door, there was the landlord and a constable waiting to take them for swindling somebody else. I told the landlord they had taken my watch and money. Gibbons was taken, but M'Oris got off. When Gibbons was taken to the watch-house, he dropped a parcel of counterfeit guineas in the coal tub, I never saw my money nor watch after they walked up and down the room, together with it. On Friday following, as I was going to work at the cooper's in Barnaby-street, about five o'clock in the morning, three fellows overtook me on London bridge, and asked me if my name was not so and so, and if I had not lost my watch; I said, yes; they said, if I would go with them they would let me have my watch again; I told them, I did not chuse to go with them; they said, it was but a little way, and if I would release Gibbons out of prison, I should have my watch and money again. I then observed the chain, seals and key of my watch, hang out of one of their pockets; the key is a very remarkable one; it being very early in the morning, I was afraid to attempt to stop them, lest they should knock my brains out, or use me ill.

   Morris Linnakan. I keep the Hoop and Horse Shoe, Tower-hill. On the 6th of June, the prisoner and M'Oris came to my house; I have often seen them there; I suspected them to be sharpers; I got a constable to take them up, when they came out. M'Oris made his escape; we took Gibbons, and carried him to the watch-house; he was not searched in my house, but when we came to the watch-house, he dropped a purse of counters into the coal basket, and he was searched, and a draught found upon him.
   Edward Hutchinson. I am a constable, upon the 6th of June, towards the evening, Mr. Linnakan, the publican, came for me; I went to his house, he said, there was a young man he believed had been robbed; we staid at the door to take the men as they came out, but one of them got away; we took the prisoner to the watch-house, there he dropped a purse of counterfeit guineas, and before the justice, there was a draught upon a person in Fleet-street, found upon him; we went to enquire for the person but there was no such person to be found.
   Jacob Cockin. I was in the watch-house; I saw the prisoner drop something; I got a candle, and in the coal basket found a purse of counterfeit guineas.
 The counterfeits and the draught were produced in court.
