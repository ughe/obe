   MR. HAKE . Q.  Was there any appearance of hysteria  on the 4th, when you saw Mrs. Brenton? A.  At first, perhaps, one might feel inclined to think so; but in a very short time my opinion was formed that it was not hysteria , decidedly not, from what I saw subsequently.

ELIZABETH BAKER. The deceased and the prisoner were my lodgers saw Mrs. Brenton on the 3rd of Oct., and on the 4th—she mentioned to me her intention of getting up the next morning—she appeared in better health and spirits than she had for some time—she did not complain of auy sore bones, or any sore upon her person—on the Saturday I was changing the linen with the nurse—Mrs. Houston, the deceased's mother, was there at the time (she often came to see her), and she pointed our at tention to the bruise or wound—that was the first time it had been heard of—it appeared to me like a blow—Mrs. Brenton was not at all given to drink, as far as I know—I saw her every day—on the Thursday previous to her death she was sensible—she called me three times by my name, and asked me for some tea—I made her some—she said, "Oh, my back"—I said, "Is your back bad, dear?"—she said, "Yes, Mrs. Baker, it is; Henry kicked me"—she asked me to call for Mrs. Fisher, and she said, "O Mrs. Baker, take care of my baby"—I saw her that day when Mr. Baker was present. THOMAS FISHER. I am a surgeon. I attended this case the greater part of the time from the 7th Oct., and was present at the post mortem  examination the last day—I first saw the bruise on Mrs. Brenton's back on the second day of my seeing her, on the 8th—in my opinion it might have existed about three or four days, or it might have existed longer—I am of opinion that it was caused by violence—I have seen many wounds—I form that opinion from the symptoms combined, from the appearances previous to death and afterwards—I recollect the Saturday on which she died—she stated to me on that day that she believed she would not recover, I was questioning her—I wished to ascertain the state of her mind—her father was present—we were to meet with Mr. Laver, who did not keep his appointment—I was alone with her part of the time, and her father was with me a portion of the time—at the time she made a statement to me I was alone with her—I wished to ascertain her state with respect to her sanity, and to know whether she was sensible enough to answer my ques tions, to ascertain in what state her health was—I asked her questions about her state of health—I asked her where she was in pain—she said, "In my back"—her father put the next question.
