1756. CHARLES JOHN COOPER and WILLIAM CROMER, stealing
I handkerchief, value Is.; the goods of Parkins Jones, from his persons.
2nd Count, charging Cromer with receiving.
Parkins Jones. I am relieving-officer of Fulham Union. On 3rd July,
between 7 and 8 o'clock, I was at Putney fair-a policeman spoke to me, and
I missed my handkerchief, which I had seen two minutes before I saw the
prisoners run away, jump over a ditch, and run into the osiers-I saw &
handkerchief pass between them-the policeman pursued, and took them.
LIONEL HODDER ( policeman, V 52). produce a certificate of the death
of the policeman, Edward Cock-I saw him dead I was present when he
was examined before the Magistrate-bis deposition was read over to him, and
the prisoners had an opportunity of cross-examining bim-(Edward Cock's
deposition was here read as follows: "I am a constable, No. 245 V; on the
evening of 3rd July I saw the prisoners together in Finch's-field, Putney,
where a fair was held; seeing them attempt to pick several pockets,
I watched them two hours, and then saw them behind Mr. Jones; I spoke to
Mr. Jones, and with him followed the prisoners; they ran away into the
osier-beds, among the mud and dirt, and tried to make their escape; I
followed, and saw the handkerchief close to Cromer's feet, in the osiers.")
I was with Cock that day watching the prisoners--I saw each of them make
several attempts at gentlemen's pockets-they both closed behind Mr. Jones
--Cooper appeared to be tugging at his pocket-he appeared to pass some-
thing to Cromer-they then moved away in a great hurry, and I spoke to
Mr. Jones--we followed the prisoners into the osier-bed- just as I got by the
side of Cromer, he threw this handkerchief (produced ) in between the osiers
-I took Cooper, and my brother constable took Cromer and the hand-
terchief.
OR
PARKINS JONES re-examined. This is my handkerchief.

1757. PETER DAVIS, stealing 2 tame rabbits, 1 basket, and 2 knives, f
value 5s. 4d.; the goods of Thomas Craft.
SIMEON WAGHORN policeman, L 101). On 8th Aug., about 3 o'clock in
the morning, I was on duty in Jonathan-street, Vauxhall, and saw the prisoner,
about forty yards from the prosecutor's, standing, with a basket in one hand,
and a rabbit in the other-he was stroking it-I was in my uniform he could
See me I went up to him, put my hand into his basket, and said, "What
Have you got here?"-he said, "Another rabbit"-I said, “What are you
going to do with them?"-he said the one he was stroking was a buck, and
the other he was going to take to the buck, to 17, Vauxhall-street-it was
dark-I asked where he lived- he said, “Two or three doors below a rag-
shop in Jonathan-street"-I asked him bis name-he said, “Brown," and
that he was waiting for his brother-I said I very much doubted what he
said, and he threw the rabbits down, and ran away-I pursued bim, and he
was stopped by another constable-I never lost sight of him-I afterwards saw
te basket at the station, and found two knives in it-I found some matches
and several pieces of candle in his pocket.
GUILTY.
