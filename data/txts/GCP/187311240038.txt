Prisoner's Defence. Mr. Free found in my last year's wages-book dis-
crepancies amounting to 1501. This 861, which is brought forward in this
book is supposed to be part of what I have stolen, but if last year's wages-
book is produced, it will show that the 861. was brought forward by me, I
having paid it out of my own pocket to make the accounts up. There
was not enough money, and I used money belonging to myself to make up
the payments, and that is brought forward here. When I wrote, I ad-
mitted that I owed 1561. I had received money in 1871 and 1872 amounting
to 1001., and as I had paid the money out of my own pocket I should have
pocketed the 1001., but instead of doing so I wrote it into the account, and
kept the 801. out, so I was cheating myself of 201. When the deficiency
occurred, I tried to account for it, but could not till Mr. Wontner produced
the receipts. If last year's balance sheet and wages book are produced, I
think they will prove that what I say is correct. With regard to Bloom-
field saying that the men are not of opinion that I should be let off, it is
entirely false. One man mentioned something favourable to me, and he
was suspended for a month. He is now present.

Witnessess for the Defence.
WILLIAM JAMES YOUNG. I have been connected with the gang nine
years, and as far as I am aware the prisoner conducted the business in a
very straightforward manner-I never had occasion to suspect anything-
I have been suspended for four weeks by the committee because I suggested
that the thing should be expressed as a debt instead of involving a prose-
cution--my suspension is a loss of from 81. to 101. to me.
Cross-examined. As a member of the gang, I saw the wages-book-I
was a member of the committee twelve months ago--the wages-book was
in the prisoner's possession--I have not seen it since he left.

-I have known you to use your own money to pay our acoounts with-
