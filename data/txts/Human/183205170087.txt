 Prisoner. Q.Look at me; I never saw you till I was at the office? A. I am positive you are the man, you were taken two days after; I gave you seven sovereigns, and the rest in silver.
 COURT. Q. How far is your shop from where the prosecutor lives? A.About a quarter of a mile; it was pawned between eight and and nine o'clock in the evening - he was in the shop nearly a quarter of an hour, I had several people there; I asked if he was a lodger, and put L. for lodger on the duplicate.
 JURY. Q. Was he in the shop? A. Yes, and close to a gas - he had not the same dress on then; I had not seen him before, but I am certain of him - I described him to the prosecutor.
 MR. HECKSHER. I took him myself; there was no officer employed.
 COURT. Q. Are there any other lodgers in the house? A. No, there is one female servant; it is Miss Benjamin's house - her brother and sister live with her - the female servant attended to my room - the prisoner might have seen I had a watch in my room as he went up stairs; I am a watch-maker, and have a great many watches there - there were four together - I missed one; I took the prisoner four days afterwards; I missed the watch at eleven o'clock at night - it was from Mr. Delbridge's description I took the prisoner; he had gone away that night and we saw no more of him till he was taken.
 Prisoner. Q. Had not I given notice to go away? A. I believe he had; I missed the watch on the 18th, and can be on my oath I had it in my hand at one o'clock - he left about two in the afternoon.
 JOSEPH BENJAMIN. I live with my sister. The prisoner lived there, and left about two o'clock on the 18th of April; he said he would go, and asked for three days wages, which I paid him - he had lived with us seventeen years off and on, and behaved honest; we had some dispute that day about his being gone two or three hours to Billingsgate; he did not return till near two o'clock, and then left; he took some aprons and other things with him - I did not see him go to his room to fetch them, the maid is not here; the prisoner went up and got his clothes; the prosecutor's door is generally closed - he works at home in general.
 MR. HECKSHER. I had been called out in a hurry; I shut the door but did not lock it.
 JURY. Q. Is there no one else in the house? A. There is a boy, but he had no business up stairs; he could go up.

 1280.  MARY CONNELLY was indicted for stealing, on the 14th of April , 1 blanket, value 10s., the goods of Edmund Bird.
   JOHANNA BIRD. I am the wife of Edmund Bird, who keeps the  George, in West-street, Smithfield. On the 14th of April I saw the prisoner leave our house with a large blanket in an apron - I sent one of the waiters after her; the blanket had been hanging in the passage to dry, and I saw it safe when she came in five minutes before.
 THOMAS PROTHERO. I am an officer. I took the prisoner on Monday the 16th of April - I have not found the blanket.
 JAMES BROWN. I was called by my mistress to go after the prisoner - I had been in the tap-room, when she came in; she had no blanket then - I gave her a light; I was up stairs, and my mistress called to me that she had gone out with a blanket; I went out, but could not find her.
 Prisoner's Defence. A friend told me to call on them there, between seven and eight o'clock - I went, but could not see them; I then went out, and on the Monday morning the officer came and asked if I had been to the house, and if I had taken a blanket - I said I had been there, but I had taken nothing out; I saw no blanket, and if I had been seen to come out with it they should have stopped me.
 THOMAS PROTHERO. I never asked whether she took the blanket, or whether she had been at the house - I took her there, and Mrs. Bird indentified her.
 JURY to MRS. BIRD. Q. Was the prisoner a customer? A. Yes; she was well known in the place; I called after her, but she turned up a court and got away before the waiter could get after her - she had come in empty handed.
     GUILTY. Aged 43. - Transported for Seven Years.
 1281.  MARY McBRIDE was indicted for stealing, on the 21st of April , 2 shifts, value 6s., the goods of Samuel Michael.
 JAMES TAGUS SHOUT. I am shopman to a pawnbroker. On the 21st of April I took in these articles of the prisoner- I advanced 1s. 6d. on them.
 SAMUEL MICHAEL. I live in  Fetter-lane. I employed the prisoner to clean the house- she lived in the kitchen; I know these two shifts - I bought them myself because my wife was in the hospital, and she would not use them because they had marks on them - they were clean in the box; I did not employ the prisoner to pawn them - I missed them on the 23rd of April; I bought them myself in the fair in Petticoat-lane, six or seven weeks ago - I know one was marked, but I do not know what it was; I cannot read - I know them by these stripes.
 Prisoner's Defence. I worked for him six weeks - one of these shifts is mine; I said to him, on the Saturday night, "Mr. Michael, I will be obliged to you to let me have the price of a pair of shoes;" he said he had no change, and I took one of my own shifts, and the other one which might belong to him, but I found it in the room -I did not mean to defraud him of it; I told him I must have something to pawn.
 SAMUEL MICHAEL. She did not tell me so, nor ask for any money; I owed her nothing - she had drawn more than was due to her; she did not tell me she had pawned any thing.
 Prisoner. He is single - the girl he keeps is an English girl. Witness. I was married at Hull, about three years ago; I do not know at what church, nor who was present - I had known my wife before; I travel with goods as a furrier.
 COURT. Q. What did the prisoner do for you? A. She cleaned the room and lighted the fire; she lives in the kitchen with another woman, and I live on the first floor.
