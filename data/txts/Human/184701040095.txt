WILLIAM DICKER. I am a pawnbroker—I produce this shirt—it was pawned at our shop—I do not know by whom—this is the duplicate that was given for it.
   Prisoner's Defence.  The duplicate I had given me by a boy named Williams, who is gone to sea; the braces I bought of a woman in the street, a street hawker.
   JURY to JAMES BROWN. Q.  How do you know the shirt? A.  I bought two of them near the London Docks—I did not mark them—when I saw the prisoner going away, I knew the handkerchief that the bundle was tied in that he had in his hand—he had been on board painting for three days. GUILTY . Aged 18.— Confined Six Months .
   ADJOURNED TO MONDAY, THE>  1 ST OF FEBRUARY , 1847.
