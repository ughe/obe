It muſt certainly give great Satisfaction to every Man, who has a due Concern for the
Peace of the Kingdom, and che Honour of His Majeſty's Government, to ſee fo
numerous an Appearance of Perſons of great Rank and Fortune attending the preſent
Service. For without a vigorous, ſteady, and impartial Adminiſtration of Juſtice, the
Ends of Government will be totally defeated..
And what are the Ends of Government? They undoubtedly are, the Welfare of the
whole Community, and the Happinefs of every ſingle Man in it: as far as the Happineſs
of Individuals is confitent with the welfare of the whole.
Theſe are the great Ends of Government. And it is very certain, they cannot be
obtain'd without a due Execution of the Law upon Offenders of all kinds.
And it is no inconſiderable Inſtance of the Wiſdom of our Law, that the Oppor-
tunities of bringing Offenders to Juſtice are as frequent, as the Nature of the Cale, in
the ordinary courſe of Things, ſeems to require. Juſtice is, as it were, brought
Home to cvery Man's Door (wice in the Year, at the ſtated Returns of the Circuits.
By this meaſure, two very wiſe and falucary Purpoſes are at once ferved. The Pro-
fecution is ordinarily commenc'd and fioifh'd while Things are recent, while Facts and
Circumftances are freſh in Memory, and while Witneſſes may be prefum'd to remain
under a proper Impreffion. And, on the other hand, the Perſon who is the Obicct of
the Proſecution, hath an carly Opportunity given to him of clearing up his Innccence,
if it ſhall happen to be his Cafe.
It were to be with'd, that theſe ſtated Scalons had been at all Times found fufficient
to ſatisfy the demands of publick Juſtice. But the Hiſtory of former Times informs
us that they have not; and our own Experience convinces us, that they are not at pre-
fent ſufficient for that purpoſe.
For what has been the caſe of this, and of ſome of the neighbouring Councies for
many, too many, Years part? Daogerous Confederacies have been form'd, for very
unwarrantable, very wicked Purpoſes; for robbing the Publick of that Revenue which
is abſolutely neceffary to it's Support; and for defeating the fair Trader in his
juſt Expectations of Proft. Theſc, to mention no more, are the neceffary un-
avoidable confequinces of the Practice, which now goes under the general Name of
Smuggling
And however Perfons may palliate the matter to themſelves or others, I wiſh every
Man who hath been directly or indirectiy concern'd in this Practice, would lay his Hand
on his Heart and put this Queſtion to himſelf, Wherein lies the real difference; (I ſpeak
now of a difference in Foro Concientie) wherein lies the real difference, between this fort
of Robbery, and the Crime which uſually goes under that Demomination ?
But this is not all, This wicked Practice hath been ſupported by an armed Force. By
numbers of diſſolute People aſſembled together, and acting in open Day-light; in
Defiance of all the Law, and all the Juſtice of their Country, and to the Terror of
