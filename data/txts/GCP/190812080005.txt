(Tuesday, December 8.)
HOMEWOOD, Dorothy (27, bookkeeper), who last Session (see
page 5) pleaded guilty of forging and uttering a banker's cheque for
the sum of £250, and attempting to obtain from Samuel Smith by false
pretences certain articles of jewellery, value £250, with intent to
defraud, and who had been since detained in ousady, was now
sentenced to one month's imprisonment, entitling her to be at once
discharged.
GORHAM, Albert William (25, carpenter), pleaded guilty of forg-
ing and uttering, knowing the same to be forged, a certain receipt for
money to wit, for the sum of £26, and a certain request for the pay-
ment of money--to wit, & notice of withdrawal from the Post Office
Savings Bank for the sum of £26, purporting to be signed by John
Halerow.
Bentence, Nine months' hard labour.
ALDERMAN, James (34, cook), pleaded guilty of forging and
uttering a certain order for the payment of money to wit, a seaman's
advance note for the payment of £3 5s., with intent to defraud.
Sentence, Six months' hard labour.
PRINCE, John (20, servant); stealing one telegraph money order
of the value and for the payment of £10, the property of Thomas
Frederick Bayfield, his master.
Mr. Morris prosecuted.
THOMAS FREDERICK BAYFIELD, M.D., Russell Priory, Russell Square,
Prisoner has been my servant for three or four years. On October 14
I expected to receive a telegraphic money order for £10. As it did
not arrive, I made inquiries at the West Central Post Office, New
Oxford Street, and as the result of what I heard returned home to
get some papers so that I could be identified. I was shown a tele-
gram at the post office Prisoner oan neither read nor write. I have
no other servant except prisoner. I have not received the money.
To Prisoner. I did not give you the paper to go to the post office
and cash it.
