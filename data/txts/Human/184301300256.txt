   Q.  Was there not a question about his mother paying the amount of the sums you charge him with; and did he not refuse because he was to have 4 l . 10 s .?— A.  No—he proposed before the Magistrate to pay, but I refused it—his mother came to me to make proposals, but I said it must take its course—this book which is here is the prisoner's hand-writing—the books in which the entries were made were not kept at my other shop—they used to come to me every night, and go back in the morning—there were not the same books for both shops—I do not know that the prisoner had a private memorandum-book—I believe the policeman took a small pocket-book from him, but I never knew he had one till it was taken from him—the books were always fetched to the shop where the prisoner was, or I took them myself—they might have been away for half a day, and then he used to make his entries on a small slip of paper, and copy them in the book—my order-book and cash-book were not left at my other shop on the 5th of Dec.—I took them on that Monday morning, about half-past nine or ten o'clock to have a settlement with him, but I was called out—my other shop is in the London-road—I do not know that there has been any talk about an action being brought against me for giving the prisoner into custody—I was never threatened with an action, and I did not prefer this charge in conse quence of any threat.
   Q.  Are these entries in this part of this book in the prisoner's writing?
   A.  Yes, they are something which he has been pencilling which are nothing regarding the trade—Mr. Capron's name is not here—I cannot tell what this entry of 4 l . 10 s . is—it is not in the regular way of the book—I never saw this entry before—I have had possession of this book from the time I made the charge—the prisoner has not had an opportunity of making an entry since—this is not the entry of his commission—there is his commission-paper just dropped out of the book—( pointing to it )—I have not torn any leaf out of this book—I had the prisoner before the Magistrate on the Thursday even ing—I did not then state that I did not wish to prosecute him—I said I wished to be lenient with him—there was no arrangement between us as to how much he owed me—there was on a former transaction long before this, when he was deficient 2 l . 10 s ., but I forgave him that—he was to pay me 10 s . a week—the Magistrate did not say he could not see any case against the prisoner—he began to take depositions against him—the Court was not sitting here at that time—I cannot say how soon it sat afterwards—the Magistrate said, we must take our witnesses before the Grand Jury—he did not say he thought there was no case against him—he said he thought the case was very black against him—we could not come to this Court the first time, because the prisoner was remanded—he was remanded a second time,
