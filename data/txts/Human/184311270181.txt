263. JOHN WILLIAMS was indicted for stealing 1 1/4 lbs. weight of bacon, value 7 d  .; the goods of Catherine Clark . WILLIAM CLARK. I am in the service of my mother, Catherine Clark, and live at Woolwich—about twelve o'clock on the 27th of Oct. I missed a piece of bacon—Mr. Drew gave me a description of the man who took it—I saw the prisoner go into a public-house—I ran to the policeman and told him—I found the bacon in the public-house—it was cooked. JOHN DREW. I am assistant to Mr. Stewart, a druggist—I saw the pri soner take a piece of bacon from Mr. Clark's shop, and put it under his coat. THOMAS BARNS THOMPSON( police-constable R  12.) I went into the public-house and saw the prisoner eating the bacon—I said, "What is this?"—he said, "Some I brought from London with me"—I said, some had been taken from Mr. Clark's shop—this is Mr. Groves the Magutrate's handwriting—( read )—"The prisoner says, 'I stole the bacon because I was hungry.'"
   Prisoner's defence.  I did not take it; while I was waiting in the room, an old lady opposite said, if you say you took it because you were hungry they will let you off, that is the reason I said so. JOHN DREW re-examined.  There were greasy marks on his coat, exactly on the spot where I saw him put the bacon—I was not above three yards from him when he took it.
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Two Months .
 264. GEORGE HARRIS was indicted for unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Six Months .
 265. MICHAEL SMITH was indicted for uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. BODKIN  and ESPINASSE conducted the Prosecution. JOHN COOPER, licensed victualler, Deptford.  On Sunday afternoon, the 12th of Nov., the prisoner came to my house for a glass of gin, which came to 2 d .—he gave me a half-crown—I gave him change, and he left—I put the half-crown in the till—I am sure there was no other there—in less than five minutes I looked at it again—no one had been to the till in the mean time—I found it was bad—I marked it and put it on the shelf—no one knew where it was—it remained there till Lovell came—on the 20th I gave it him from the same place—I am sure the prisoner is the man.
   Prisoner.  I got a little drink and was not aware of it, Witness.  He was sober. BENJAMIN LOVELL( police-sergeant R  15.) On the 20th of Nov. I received this half-crown from Cooper—I took the prisoner in High-street, Deptford—he said he knew nothing about it. THOMAS JOSEPH HUNT. I keep the Fox, King-street, Deptford. On the 18th of Nov., a little after nine at night, the prisoner came for a glass of gin—he gave me half-a-crown—I saw it was bad—I said, "This is a bad one"—he said, "If so, I took it of my master in part of wages"—he then gave me a good one—I gave him change—I retained the bad one and sent for a policeman, marked it in his presence, and gave it him—he took the prisoner. JAMES DEAN( police-constable R  319.) I was called to Hunt's, and took the prisoner in charge—I received a half-crown from Mr. Hunt, which he marked—I afterwards gave it to the inspector—the charge was not pressed, and the prisoner was let go. SAMUEL EDWARD FREEMAN( police-sergeant R  5.) On the 18th of Nov., the prisoner was brought to the station—I received this half-crow from Dean.
