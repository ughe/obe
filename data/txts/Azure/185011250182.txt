JAMES LOUGHTON. I am landlord of the Rose, in Tower-street,
Waterloo-road, Lambeth. On 7th Nov. I met the prisoner going out at
my back-door as I was coming in—I said there was no one there—he said
he wanted to go backwards, and I suffered him to go—I bad observed that
my skittle-ball was safe when I came out of the yard—I had laid it don
myself, and about eleven that night I missed it—I went after the prisoner
and found him next door to a coffee-shop in the Waterloo-road—he
he knew nothing about it—I gave him in charge, and from some informa-
tion I went the next day, between one and two, to Mr. Butcher's, at the
Great Britain—I went into his skittle-ground and saw two skittle-balls—
one of them I was confident was mine—this is it ( produced )—here is
mark on one side and one on the other—I told Mr. Butcher it was mine,
and he told me where he bought it—I went to Mr. Lockett's, at the Crown
and Anchor, and asked bim how he became possessed of it, and he told me.
Prisoner. Q. When I asked to go backwards did I not go? A. I do
not know—I did not see you come out of the house—I was away on my
business—I am sure no one went into my yard besides you—there are
plenty of ways to go into my yard.
CHARLES LOCKETT. I was waiter at the Crown and Anchor in the
Waterloo-road. On 7th Nov. I saw the prisoner there—I said I had been
trying to sell a skittle-ball to Mr. Butcher, but it was not large enough—
the prisoner said he had a ball about the size that Mr. Butcher wanted—
he went and fetched it—I believe this is it—I asked bim what he wanted
for it—he said two shillings, and all I could get over that I might keep
myself—I took it to Mr. Butcher's—he gave me 28. 3d. and a pint of ale
forit—l gave the prisoner 28. and kept the 3d.
