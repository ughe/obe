 Prisoner's Defence. I came home from work at half-past eight o'clock - I said to my father, "Shall I go and enter myself into that benefit society to night?" he said,"You are getting a young family, I think it would be advisable;" I said my master would not give me any money - he said, "You shall not be lost for that;" and he went out, changed a sovereign, and lent me two halfcrowns and one shilling - I went to the Sugar Loaf to be entered, but the man, who was to propose me, was not there; I stopped, and had a pint of porter, a small glass of gin, and a short pipe of tobacco, which cost 4 1/2d. -I remained there to see if this man was there when the society broke up, but he did not come; that made me late - as I turned into Grey Eagle-street to go home this young man came and said, "I will have you about my sovereign;" I said, "Go about your business, you drunken fellow" - he laid hold of me, the watchman came up, and took me; the watchman said, "Come to the watch-hosue, you can see the man is drunk."
 Five witnesses gave the prisoner a good character.
     GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 24.

 1829.  HENRY KING was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of June , at St. Anne, 3 watches, value 46l., and 1 seal, value 4l., the goods of Charles Wieland , in his dwelling-house.
   CHARLES WIELAND. I am a watch-maker, and live at Limehouse, in the parish of St. Anne, and rent the house. I became acquainted with the prisoner's friends at Boulogne; his father keeps an hotel there, and is a very respectable person - in consequence of this I became acquainted with the prisoner, and he lodged in my house as a friend; he had been there about eight days on the 10th of June, when my son missed some watches - in consequence of what he said, I looked and missed three from a drawer, and a gold watch, which was locked in a dressing-case in my bed-room; he had access to any part of the house - he left the house when I was out; I expected to see him again in the evening - he did not return; this was on the 10th of June - I did not see him again till the 11th of July.
   THOMAS WALKER. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a watch, pawned by the prisoner on the 10th of June.
   CHARLES FREEMAN. I am an officer. I saw this robbery mentioned in the reports; I apprehended the prisoner on the 11th of July, on suspicion, and found a watch pawned at Walker's; the prisoner told me he had sold the gold watch to a watch-maker at Chatham, and the other to a shoemaker at Gravesend; I went and found them both.
   JOHN IGGLESDEN. I am a watch-maker, and live at Chatham. I bought this watch of a person; it was not the prisoner.
   SAMUEL SAUNDERS. I live at Gravesend. I bought this watch of the prisoner.
   CHARLES WIELAND. These are my three watches - here is one silver repeater, worth 30l., with the seal attached to it, which is worth 5l.; I should charge 35l. for the watch and all - this other watch is worth 22l.; he broke open two sets of drawers and a dressing-case. He attended his father's billiard-rooms at Boulogne.
 GUILTY - DEATH. Aged 20. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor, on account of his family, and believing it to be his first offence.
 1830.  THOMAS EDWARDS and HENRY DUFFY were indicted for stealing, on the 3rd of September , at Hornsey, 2 cows, price 40l., the property of John Bacon.
 EDWARDS pleaded GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 18.
   JOHN BACON. I live at Grenville-mews, Somer's-town. On the 3rd of August I had six cows in Southwood-lane, Hornsey, which I should think is at least thirteen miles from Dulwich - my man left them in Southwood-lane, on the 3rd of August - he was examined at the office, but the Magistrate did not bind him over, on account of the expence: I saw them in the field about three days before, and on the day before, I think; I will swear I saw them safe three days before they were stolen - on the 3rd of August, about two o'clock, I found two of them were gone; I went to Smithfield, and inquired about them among the drovers and Police - I got information, and went back to my fields to be certain they had not broken out - I found them both in Kennington-pound on Tuesday morning, the 4th of August, about ten o'clock; they appeared jaded, and very thin indeed - their bags were ready to burst with milk - I took Edwards in custody on Tuesday morning, in an open shed, at Dulwich; he had lived with me, and left me on the 17th of February - the prisoners are own brothers; Duffy came twice to see him while he lived with me - Edwards gave the name of Edwards when he lived with me - the other goes by the name of Henry Duffy; I know him by his visiting his brother - I took him in Bunhill-row, on the 6th of August, in consequence of information.
   JAMES PAGE. I live at Muswell-hill. On Monday morning, the 3rd of August, between four and five o'clock, I was going to work, and saw the two prisoners in Mr. Bacon's field; I saw them drive the two cows out of the field - Edwards opened the gate, the other drove them out, and Edwards fastened the gate up after him - I live adjoining the field, and knew the cows; I have seen them since, and know them to be the same - I knew Thomas Edwards before, but not Duffy; I rather think he is the other man by his clothes - I looked at him, and believe he is the man, but I am not exactly positive; I said I thought him the lad, when he was taken up, and think so still.
 JURY. Q. Have you any other means of being certain of him except by his clothes? A. No; I thought they were going to take them home - I knew Edwards had been in Bacon's service.
