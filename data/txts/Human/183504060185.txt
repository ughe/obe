WILLIAM CHITTENDEN. I am a policeman. I went to the prisoner's house and examined the hay; and found two pieces of paper in it, which Warden has—I saw a ladder in the yard, which I had seen on the prosecu tor's premises, just against the hay-rick.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Where were you when you saw the ladder against the hay rick? A.  Against the enclosure—it was on Friday morning, the 20th of March—it was the same ladder, by the appearance of iter—it has about seven or eight rounds—it was decidedly the same—I examined it—I was looking at the hay-binder for a quarter of an hour—the top and bottom round was plain; it is rather uncommon to have them so at both top and bottom—I found the first piece of paper in part of a truss—I took care of it, and produced it before the Magistrate—I went with Warden to the stable—I was outside when he first went into the gates, but followed him afterwards—I heard him calling for Taylor, but he was then out of my sight for about half a minute at the outside—the hay binder has ackow ledged that he borrowed the ladder of the prisoner—it is not the prosecu tor's.
   C. S. WARDEN re-examined . My writing is on both sides of the paper—C. S. W., my initials was written after finding it—the writing on the other side was written before—it alludes to the conduct of two watchmen which I noticed—I found these papers in the hay, and I had put them into the hay.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I am innocent. HENRY TAYLOR. I am the prisoner's son. I had purchased a quarter of a load of hay in Smithfield-market, on Tuesday, the 17th of March; that was nine trusses—I took it home—my sister opened the gate, and let me in with it—I then put it into the stable—when my father came home, I told him the hay was a little dusty—I paid 1 l . 5 s . for it—he told me to take the bands off, shake the hay up, and throw a little salt in it—I did so—we had one horse—it was out from Tuesday till Saturday—some of the hay was gone—I was not at home on Thursday aad Friday, and did not see the stable till Saturday—there was then no hay in the stable, but the hay I had bought—when the officers came on the Saturday I was in-doors lying on the bed—my sister called me up—I told the officers whew I got the hay.
