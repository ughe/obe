JOSEPH GRANT (4 R 331). I was on duty at Hounslow on 15th No-
vember-I went to Mrs. Ray's about 6 o'clock-I met the prisoner coming
out of the door and I took & bad florin from his hand took him into
custody and searched him-I found 68. 6d. in silver, good money, and

WILLIAM WEBSTER. These four florins are all bad, and from the same

106. WILLIAM HEY (41), STEPHEN WILLIAM PUCH (63), Un.
lawfully conspiring with others to defraud Isaac Lewis of his moneys,
Second Count—with intent to hinder and pervert the due course of law and
justice.
MESSRS. BESLEY and STRAIGHT conducted the Prosecution ; Mr. OOOPS
defended Hey, and Mr. GRIFFITHS defended Pugh.
ISAAO LEWIB. I live at Richmond Terrace, Waterford Road, Fulham,
and am a builder-I was plaintiff in an action of "Lewis against Clark"
Mr. Lake was employed by me to swear personalty-his charges were 254
- I gave him an I 0 U for that amount before the taxing of the posts I
was only allowed three guineas at the taxing for Mr. Lake's servioeshe
took out a plaint in the Sheriff's Court-I employed Mr. Johnson-I showed
the summons to him and went with him to the Court-I saw Mr. Wood, the
barrister, there he had a brief before him-Mr. Lake was also there-
saw Hey after the adjournment- I don't recollect seeing Pugh-I saw Hey
in conversation with Mr. Wood-Hey had some papers with him-Mr. Lake
went away, and, in his absence, there was an adjournment-I had seen Hoy
at Mr. Lake's office before the hearing- I had agreed to settle with Mr. Lake
for 151., and the second time I went to his office Hey was there I said I
was come to settle, and Hey said Pooh, pooh, he would not for 151.-he
said, "You can pay 161. down, and 101. a month afterwards" - Mr. Lake
and me bad agreed to settle before that for 151.-I said “Well, if it is like
that, I hope Mr. Lake will be as lenient as he can to me," and Lake said
"You and me can settle that" --nothing more was said then I went away
I did not leave the 154 with them-I saw Pugh at the taring of the bill-
I did not attend on the adjournment day-I did not go into the office at the
Sheriffs Court at any time for the purpose of consenting to the arrangement
- before I got the bill of oosts I signed a consent paper, and, after that, I
signed this bill of costs-I saw Pugh at the taxing-I had never seen him
before, to my recollection—I did not know who he was at all—he asked me
if my name was Lewis, and I said "Yes"- and he said, "Are you come
about the bill of costs, and I said “Yes"—he said "You will find it all
right"--I said I never heard of such a thing as 201. costs for a 154. debt, and
he said “You know more of bricks and mortar than you do of a bill of costs;
I have been in the law line these twenty years, and I know how to draw &
bill of costs"-we then went into the taxing room-Hoy was there, and
Mr. Johnson, and Mr. Biggenden-I believe they went through the question
about the charges-Biggenden came to me some time after I had paid the
money into Court-I had not seen him before that I went to Mr. Maynard,
& solicitor, after that, and placed the matter in his hands-I saw the defend-
ants at the Sheriffs Court one day after the taxing-I did not speak to
them—they appeared to be acting in a similar way to what they did on my
occasion, only saw them again on that occasion before they were at
Guildhall-I did not know that no solicitor had been employed in my case.
