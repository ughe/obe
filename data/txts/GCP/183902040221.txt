John TRIPP. I am warehouseman to the prosecutor. On Friday, the
7th of December, Sutcliffe made a communication to me, and I procured
Shelswell and Taylor-I went in to Sutcliffe's while Shelswell and Taylor
remained outside - I went into the warehouse at the back of the shop, and
saw a cask of butter covered over with a sack there was no bead on it,
nor any top hoops--I saw the place where the dairy mark ought to have
been was cut out-while I was there Christall came in and spoke to Sut-
cliffe-Hopkins also came in, took the cask in his arms, took it out, and
put it into the truck--that was while Christall was talking to Mr. Sut-
cliffe-Hopkins had drawn up with the truck as Christall came in-I came
out, and gave notice to Shelswell, and he and Taylor went down Red Lion-
street, following Hopkins-1 afterwards went to Toy's, after borrowing a
great coat of Mr. Sutcliffe, that I might not be known-Toy was not at
home-he came in in seven or eight minutes with a butter-knife in his
hand-it was a regular knife for drawing samples of butter-I asked him
what he wanted of a butter-knife-he said he had borrowed it of Mr.
Jarvis -I knew Toy before, and that he had one child-I asked him if he
was aware my employers had been defrauded of twenty casks of butter,
and asked him if he knew any thing respecting these butters-he said
he knew nothing at all about them I then told him, I had received
information from Mr. Sutcliffe that there were some more casks in or
near his premises-be said he knew nothing at all about them I told
him there had been two men stopped, offering a cask of butter for sale,
and if he did know any thing I begged of him to tell me he then said he
did know, but he would not tell me, for if he did he should get other people
into trouble--I never said, “Never mind other people, look to yourself,
you have got a wife and family" he then said he did know, but be would
not tell me, I told him he must take his chance then.
Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you not say to him, “ For
God's sake tell me all you know aboutit ?" A. Yes I said, "If you tell
me what you know about it I will make it all right"-I do not know that
there is a heavy penalty for having possession of smuggled butter-there
is a penalty I do not know the amount of it.
THOMAS SHELSWELL. I am an officer of Lambeth-street office. Tripp
applied to me on the 7th of December, and I went with Taylor within a
few doors of Sutcliffe's-I did not see any thing brought out of the shop-I
saw a cask of butter in a truck, covered with a sack-the sack was thrown
carelessly over the top of the cask-we followed the truck to opposite the
door of Potter, a green-grocer, in Rosemary-lane-Hopkins was drawing it-
