purse, value 2s., and 15 sovereigns, the property of Ann Grimmett, in the
dwelling-house of Elizabeth Beatrice Alsager.
Mr. Bopkin conducted the Prosecution.
ANN GRIMMETT. I was in the service of Mrs. Elizabeth Beatrice Alsager,
the widow of Captain Alsagar; she lives at Balham-hill, in the parish of
Streatham. On the 8th of February the family left town, and I left on the
19th-I left fifteen sovereigns in a small drawer in a chest, in my bed-
room, at the top of the house-I locked the drawer and took the key with
me-the prisoner had been left in charge of the house, and had Willis, her
niece, with her-I returned on the 12th of October, and went to my drawer
next morning-I found it still locked, but had a difficulty in getting it
open-I found the things in it all turned about, and my money gone-I
called the prisoner up, and told her I had been robbed-she said she did not
know any thing about it-(she was merely a charwoman left in charge of
the house)- I told her the drawer must have been undone, I thought by a
key-I asked if there could be any key in the house which would undo
the drawer-she then fetched a key, wbich I tried to the drawer-it un-
locked three of them, one of which my money had been in--next morning
I noticed marks of a chisel on the drawers all over the house, and on my
drawer also-- they could be seen without opening the drawer-iwo chisels
were found in the house-the police were called in.
Court. Q. Before you noticed the marks, had you been all over the
house to examine whether they must have been made after you ascertained
your own loss? A. No, I did not see the marks till the police found them
out-my sight is not good, and I might not observe them if they had been
there before.
Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. How long has the prisoner's
husband lived in the neighbourhood with her? 4. Some years-he
worked on Mr. Borradale's farm-- I do not know whether he was his bailiff
--the prisoner was discharged on bail- I think the marks on the drawers
were mentioned before she was discharged-we also indicted Willis, and the
Grand Jury ignored the bill-I do not know any thing of the prisoner and
her husband being known as people of property-I have known her seven
or eight years in the neighbourhood - I have not spoken to Willis this
morning-the prisoner only brought me one key-she had not two in her
hand-I have not brought the drawer here, or the chisels-she went for the
key directly I told her to fetch what she had- I lost a purse, which I have
not found there was no other charge against her.
SAMUEL LONGBOTTOM (police-constable V 10.) I took the prisoner in
charge on Monday, the 18th of November, and went to her lodgings the
same day, in the Balham-road, rather better than a quarter of a mile from Mrs.
Alsager's—I found her husband there, and in a box under their bed I
found from 4001. to 500l. in one bag, and about 1001. in another bag, in
gold and silver-the 1001. was in a black stocking- I found under the
same bed a tea-caddy, which I produce, with two purses in it, one con-
tained about 91. or lol. in gold, silver, and copper--there were two or three
farthings, and separate from the purses, not wrapped in any thing, I found
filteen sovereigns loose in the bottom, where the sugar basin would be-a
purse, with money in it, laid over it-her husband was present when the
money and caddy was found, but not when the caddy was broken open-
