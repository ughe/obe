1922. WILLIAM BASTERFIELD and WILLIAM DAVEY were in dicted for stealing 4 bushels of mixed oats and chaff, value 7 s  .; and 1 sack, 6 d  .; the goods of Samuel Pocock , and another, the master of Basterfield; to which
   BASTERFIELD  pleaded GUILTY . Aged 49.— Confined Six Months . EDWARD HENRY CHAMBERS. I am horse-keeper to Messrs. Samuel and Lewis Pocock, of Phoenix-wharf, White Friars. Basterfield was their carman—he went out with a team on the 20th of July—I delivered him two sacks, one contained about four bushels of horse-feed, consisting of clover, chaff; and oats, which he was to give to his horses—the property produced is my masters', and is worth 6 s . or 7 s . CHARLES RANDELL( policeman N  172.) I saw Basterfield come out of the field, and I watched him to Davey's shop—Davey came out and spoke to him, he then went on, and Davey ran after him, and overtook him—Basterfield then lifted a sack out of the cart—Davey took it on his back, and went to wards his own stable with it—I stopped him.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  Davey went away with it? A.  Yes, he hurried away—I told the clerk twice that he hurried away—I do not know whether is is written down that he walked—he went in the direction of his own stable—I asked him where he got it—he said he bought it of a man—I asked him which man—he pointed to Basterfield, and my brother con stable took him into custody.
   COURT. Q.  What shop does Davey keep? A.  A greengrocer's—the names, "Samuel Pocock and Lewis Pocock, Caledonian-fields," were on the cart.
   DAVEY  — NOT GUILTY .
 1923. JANE CLINE was indicted for stealing 2 sheets, value 5 s  .; 2 pet tocoats, 3 s  .; 2 handkerchiefs, 3 s  .; 2 bed-gowns, 3 s  .; and 1 sheet, 5 s  .; the goods of Henry Willaim Byrn , her master; and that she had been before con victed of felony WILLIAM AYRES. I am a pawnbroker. I produce a sheet pawned by a woman—the duplicates which are produced by the officer correspond with the property produced, and with the duplicates which I have. WILLIAM THOMAS( policeman T  60.) I went to the prisoner's house—I found there a towel, a handkerchief, several pieces of lace, and twenty-six duplicates, some of which Mr. Ayres has identifies. MARY BYRN. I am the wife of Henry William Byrn, and am a laundress. The prisoner washed for me—I lost all this property—some of it is mine, and some is my employers'.
   Cross-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q.  Are you able to identify these articles? A.  Yes, many of them—the names have been taken out of some—the prisoner is married, and has six children—I have heard that her husband does not allow her much, and I felt for her. JOHN JONES( police-sergeant T  31.) I produce a certificate of the pri soner's former conviction—( read—Convicted 5th of April , 1841, and confined four months. )—she is the person. GUILTY .* Aged 32.— Confined One Year .
