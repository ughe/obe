I went to Sutcliffe's shop with Shelswell—I saw the truck standing opposite the shop—I followed it to Potter's—I heard Shelswell ask Christall whose property it was—I afterwards went and apprehended Northcote, and took him to a pub lic-house—he sat down before the fire, and I saw him with a pocket-book and a letter in his hand—Childs snatched the pocket-book out of his hand—on the 10th of December I was looking out for Vincent—Sullivan was with me—we met Vincent in Whitechapel-road—he put out his hand to shake hands with Sullivan, but Sullivan drew his hand back and said to me, "Here is Vincent, this is the man that gave me the order"—I took hold of him by the arm, and told him I wanted him for giving Sullivan a forged order, by which he obtained the butter from Barber's wharf—Vin cent turned round and looked at Sullivan and said, "Oh, the vagabond has come  that, has he?"—he stated something in a muttering manner, which I could not understand, and I took him away to the station-house—after he had a walked a few yards with me, he said, "I know nothing about it."
   Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS . Q.  You are quite sure he said nothing more, not what he muttered, but did not he say, "I know nothing about it then?" A.  I cannot tell, it might be that—Vincent had been in prison a week at that time on this charge.
   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  When Christall was asked in the presence of Gregson who the property belonged to, he said Gregson, did he not? A.  He said he bought it of Gregson—he did not say it belonged to Gregson.
