 Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Had not there been some degree of intimacy between you? A. Nothing further than his being a customer. I saw he had plenty of money, and I once asked him to lend me a 5l. note - he showed me an acceptance for 50l., and asked if I could make use of that - I kept it an hour, and then returned it; I never talked about money on any other occasion - he owed me money for eating and drinking at my house; I knew his address, and wrote him two letters, which he answered - the Lord Mayor has them. The letters found him in Bride-lane, Fleet-street; I called there twice, and could not see him; I had not given orders for his apprehension - he was taken in about two months - if I had given instructions for his being taken before there can be no doubt he would have been taken.
 COURT. Q. How long was it before he took the watch that you had talked about the 5l.? A. It might be about a fortnight. I did not see him take the watch, and he did not ask my leave - he never denied having taken it.
   JOSEPH MARTIN. I apprehended the prisoner on the 5th of April - he gave me a key, which he said belonged to his box, at his lodgings, at the Talbot Inn, in the Borough, and if I went there I should find a pocket-book, with the duplicate of the watch in it.
 Cross-examined. Q. Where did you see him? A. At the corner of Swithin's-lane - I told him what I took him for, and he said if I would go with him to the prosecutor he would soon make it all right.
   ROBERT HESKETH. The last witness gave me a key of a box, in which I found a pocket-book, and this duplicate in it.
   WILLIAM STAPLE. I am a pawnbroker. The watch was pawned with me, by the prisoner, on the 31st of January.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never took it with any felonious intention, which my prosecutor is, I believe, well aware.
     NOT GUILTY.
 376.  JOSEPH NIGHTINGALE was again indicted for stealing, on the 24th of November , 1 silver snuff-box, value 30s., the goods of Thomas Lake.
 THOMAS LAKE. On the 24th of November I was at the  King's Arms public-house, in Philip-lane; the prisoner was there; we had dined at one table - the other persons were gone; and he I were alone in the room; I called the waiter to get me some snuff; I took out my box and offered it to her, and I think the prisoner reached out his hand, and handed it across the table to her - she returned with the box and some snuff in it, and gave it to the prisoner to pass on to me, I believe, but I am not quite certain whether he took it or not, but it came into his hands; he took some snuff and, in a short time, went out with the box; I did not see him again till last Saturday; I saw the box at the Mansion House - this is it; I did not know where the prisoner lived, but he knew where I lived.
 Prisoner. Q. Did not you give me the snuff-box? A. Yes; I think I did, and you gave it to the waiter, and she returned it; I did not fall asleep in the interim - I got a little drowsy - I was not asleep when you left the room; I saw you go - I made no remark to the waiter about your leaving the room; I waited some time expecting you to return; I never said to the waiter, it would be all right if you had got it; she did not tell me you had it; I went two or three days afterwards to know if you had returned the box, and the answer was you had not called.
 Cross-examined by Mr. PRENDERGAST. Q. Did not you put your box into the prisoner's hands? A. Yes; he took it for the purpose of handing it to the waiter, and she went out; I did not afterwards express myself satisfied that it had not been stolen; I had seen him five or six times before, but I did not see him again till Saturday last - I was very angry about it.
   JANE CRAGHILL. I live at the King's-arms, Phillip-lane. I saw the prosecutor and the prisoner there; I was called, and the snuff-box was given me to get some snuff; I did not like to go, as it was wet, and I gave it to our young man to go; when I came back, the prisoner took the box, as I supposed, to pass it to Mr. Lake; the prisoner came a day or two afterwards, and I asked him if he took the box, he said he did; I said, it was not a gentlemanly sort of action; he said I might make myself comfortable, as he would return it; he staid at the house about three hours, and had something to drink to the amount of 3s.; but he went away and did not pay - the money belonged to me.
 Cross-examined. Q. On your return, did you see the prosecutor? A. Yes; he was not asleep - he told me to give the twopence to the man for fetching the snuff; he was sitting down, but I could not get at him, and I gave the box to the prisoner, but I did not see him put it into his pocket; I ran up as far as Wood-street after the prisoner, and asked him for the money; he said he would not pay.
   JOSEPH MARTIN. I received the key of the box of the prisoner, which I gave to Hesketh, who found the duplicate.
   ROBERT HESKETH. I went and found the duplicate in the box.
   ROBERT ESSEX. I am a pawnbroker, and live in Aldersgate-street. I received this box in pawn on the 24th of November, for 1l.; the ticket is in the name of Joseph Nightingale; I gave the person this duplicate.
 Prisoner to JANE CRAGHILL. Q. Did not I return the same day? A. No; it was late before you went away; I did not say the gentleman had been gone more than an hour.    GUILTY. Aged 35. Transported for Seven Years.
