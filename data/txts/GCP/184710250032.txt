MELVILLE ROBERTSON. I am a baker at Featherstone-street. On Satur
day evening, 2nd of Oct., my mother came to me- I took the crown which
I received from my mother and went into the shop-I found the prisoner
there, and said, "This is a rank bad crown-piece-I asked her where she
got it-she said her husband obtained it in payment for his wages, he being
a bricklayer-I said I was not justified in giving it up, but if her husband
liked to come with a policeman I would give it up--the prisoner left the shop,
and I did not see her again-I marked the crown, and gave it to the police-
man on duty-I saw the prisoner four or five days afterwards at the police-
court, and pointed her out.
EDMUND WHITE (police-sergeant, G 9.) On the 2nd of Oct. I was called
into Mr. Robertson's shop, and received this crown.
MARTHA THOMPSON. I am daughter of Clark Thompson, a publican, of

shilling piece--I showed it to my mother-it was a bad one-I marked il
and gave it to the policeman-this is it-she said she had got it from a man-
she was taken into custody.

ROBERT HARRIS. I keep the Crooked Billet in Ratcliff-highway. On the
1st of Oct., about half-past six o'clock in the evening, I saw the prisoner--he
asked me for threepenny-worth of brandy, I served him in a teacup-- he gave
me a five-shilling piece- I gave him 48. 9d. change, and put the five-shilling
piece in a little private till, apart from all other money-he then had half
pint of beer, and paid me with one of my own pence-he went away, and
directly be was gone I looked at the crown, and found it was bad- I went to
the door, but the prisoner was out of sight--a policeman went past, and !
gave him the crown-he marked it, and gave it me again-1 kept it--on the
15th of Oct., about half-past six o'clock in the evening, the prisoner Care
again-he called for half a pint of beer-I served him-he gave me a bad som
ling-I put it in my mouth, and went round to the fore door-the prisoner said,
"Master, don't you like it?"-I said no, it was a bad one; closed the door,
and sent for an officer--the prisoner pulled off his handkerchief, and wanted
to leave it for the beer, I would not take it-1 did not mention about the
crown till I got to the station-I knew he was the man that passed the crown
