AMELIA ANN HARVEY. I live in Vine-terrace, York-road, Lambeth. On 25th Aug, I took one ticket for myself, and one for my sister, to go to Putney—I went by the train from the Waterloo-station, at thirty-five minutes past four o'clock—I went on to Richmond, and gave up the tickets to the prisoner—my sister asked him if there was anything to pay, and he said, "Yes, 8 d . each"—I gave him a half-crown, and he gave me 1 s . 2 d ., out.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you go down to Putney at the instance of Bent, the policeman? A , Yes; I have known him about nine months—I never lived with him, nor did my sister—I live with my mother—I get my living by needlework—I am not in the habit of keeping company with Bent; he sometimes comes backwards and forwards, and buys tobacco of my mother.
   MR. BALLANTINE . Q.  Is the house your mother lives in belonging to the Railway? A.  Yes; it is perfectly respectable. ELIZABETH ANN HARVEY. I am sister of the last witness—I live with my mother—I went down with my sister to Richmond—I saw the 1 s . 4 d . paid to the prisoner.
   Cross-examined, Q.  Did your sister tell you that the policeman asked her to go down? A.  No; my sister paid my fare and her own—I have known Mr. Bent about eight months. PATRICK MEHAN. I am a reporter. On 2d Sept. I went in a second-class carriage to Richmond—I did not take any ticket—when 1 got to Rich mond the prisoner was taking tickets there—I paid him 1 s . 2 d .—I afterwards gave information—when I was going down the officer spoke to me, and asked me to do this.
   Cross-examined, Q.  Were you accidentally on the platform? A.  Yes; I paid my own fare out of my own pocket—I went alone—I think Bent was in the same train; not in the same carriage—I have known him as a police man for some years—he asked me where I was going, and 1 told him, and he said there were some officers that he suspected—I gave evidence before the Magistrate, and I think I reported the case on that occasion—I was a witness, by accident—I was not invited by the officer to do this; he asked me to do it—I was never in any difficulty of any kind—there was never any charge made against me—I think I was charged at the Mansion-house with knocking a man down—I was never charged with a more grievous offence; certainly not—I was never charged with violating the person of a child; it is abomi nable that I should be asked such a question; it is false; I know nothing of such a charge; I deny it most indignantly—I do not think I have ever travelled with Bent before; certainly never in company with him; he might be in the same train—I very likely might have taken a glass of spirits with him—I think I had some refreshment at Richmond with him and the Miss Harveys—he is no personal friend of mine; I know him from bis frequently bringing charges to the courts—I have not frequently drank with him; I dare say 1 may have drank with him; I have treated him; I paid for it this time—I have not received anything for this, nor do I expect it.
