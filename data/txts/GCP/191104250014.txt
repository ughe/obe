NELLIE SAUNDERS (called at prisoner's request). On this night I
was walking with prisoner and a man named Tear. Fleming and
another man walked behind us and they were jeering and insulting
us. Tear said to prisoner, “There's two men behind us, shall I lash
out?” I said, "Don't take any notice,” but Tear turned round and
hit Fleming and ran away. Then the police came and arrested prisoner.
Inspector John HOCKINGS, L Division, said that on prisoner being
charged at the station and cautioned he said, “I don't know any-
thing about it; I never done it; I don't know the man; I have not
touched anybody; I don't know what they mean." He did not
mention Tear's name.

(Defence.)
ARTHUR HOBSON (prisoner, on oath) swore" before God " that he
never struck the blow ; Tear knocked Fleming down and ran away.
GEORGE HUMPHREYS said that shortly after this occurrence he was
at a coffee-stall with Tear, who said that he had just hit a man, and
that when he did that he never made any bloomer.
WILLIAM Cook said that on the night of the affair he was with
Tear and a man named Oakley. In Tear's presence Oakley told
witness that prisoner had been arrested for hitting a man, but that
he (Tear) was the man who struck the blow.
BEATRICE BELL said that she was with Tear on this night. Tear
told her he had struck a fellow down and that the police had taken
Hobson for it.
Verdict, Not Guilty ; a similar verdict was entered on an indictment
for assault oceasioning grievous bodily harm.
ALBERT, Kate Bobington (28, servant), pleaded guilty to four
indictments charging her in respect of three women with using certain
instruments with intent to procure miscarriage.
Mr. Leycester and Mr. Shearman prosecuted; Mr. Werninck
appeared for prisoner.
Sentence, 12 months' hard labour.
