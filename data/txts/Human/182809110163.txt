 1880.  JOHN COLEMAN and HENRY REYNOLDS were indicted for stealing, on the 7th of July , 3 salmon, value 7s., the goods of John Wells.
   JOHN JONES. I am errand-boy to Mr. John Wells - he is a fishmonger, of Norton-street. The two prisoners are dustmen; they came on the 7th of July, about a quarterpast five o'clock in the evening, for the fish guts, which they used to take away - I said they could not have them then: Coleman said he had met our man going to tea, who said I was to give him the key to get them - I let him have it, and they went down; Reynolds afterwards went away with the cart - Coleman came through the shop soon after: when Gibbard returned, the salmon were missing from the ice-box; three were taken out of eleven - they had just gone away; we went to look for them, and saw Coleman and another man with the cart - we asked where Reynolds was; he said over the way; just as he said that, Reynolds came out of the French Ambassador's house - we asked if they had any salmon; they both said No - that we might search them and the baskets, and all; the cart went on - I staid with Coleman at the corner of Harley-street; he wanted me to go home - I said I would not, and would follow him: I did so - we overtook the cart at the Yorkshire Stingo public-house, in the New-road; I there saw them take the heads of the salmon and some money, and divided them - I then saw something under Coleman's smock-frock, and asked him to let me look at it; he would not, but said he would wring my ears off if I attempted it - I followed him down Stingo-lane, into his house; I could not get in there.
   THOMAS GIBBARD. I am shopman to Mr. Wells. I went to tea at five o'clock, leaving eleven salmon on the ice; I met the prisoners, and told them the boy would show them what to take; when I returned, I missed three: Jones and I followed the prisoners as far as Weymouth-street; I searched Reynolds, but found nothing; I looked in the cart and nose-bag.
   JOHN LEPAIR. I am lampist to the French Ambassador. I trim the lamps; the men came to take our dust away - I said it was too late to do it; I saw a dirty rag under the hall window, covered over a salmon - I had not seen the prisoners there.
 REYNOLDS' Defence. I have been to the house for this two years.
 COLEMAN's Defence. I never was in the cellar at all, nor near it; there are a parcel of lodgers in the house.
   JOHN JONES. There are two lodgers.
 COLEMAN -    GUILTY. Aged 24. Confined Six Months.
 REYNOLDS -    GUILTY. Aged 30. Confined Four Months.
 1881.  ELIZA BURKINSHAW was indicted for stealing, on the 1st of July , 6 looking-glasses, value 4l.; 20 sheets, value 4l.; 3 pillows, value 5s.; 3 bed-curtains, value 3l.; 30 towels, value 1l.; 13 table-cloths, value 2l.; 9 drinking-glasses, value 5s.; 1 blanket, value 1s.; 2 pictures, value 2s.; 4 pillow-cases, value 2s.; 16 knives, value 2s., and 15 forks, value 1s., the goods of Catherine Barker, spinster, and Amelia Barker, spinster.
 CATHERINE BARKER. I am single, and live in Longacre - these articles belong to me and my sister Amelia, and were in  Hart-street, Covent-garden; the prisoner's mother had the care of the apartments, and lived there with her.
   FRANCIS FAGAN. I am an officer. On the morning of the 15th of August, I went with two others to No. 15, Carlisle-lane, and apprehended the prisoner, and in a box in her room found eighty duplicates; I then went to Hart-street, and took up the prisoner's mother - the prisoner had told me the duplicates were in a box; I found a bunch of keys in her room, and another in her mother's room - here they are; I tried them - one unlocks the drawers and another the padlock of the door.
   ELIZABETH FEATHERSTONE. I am the wife of William Featherstone - we live in Covent-garden. In the early part of last winter, the prisoner brought some sheets, looking-glasses, and bed-furniture, which I pawned for her at different times.
   SARAH FEATHERSTONE. I pawned some sheets, towels, and other things for the prisoner at Mr. Bird's.
   GEORGE SMALLBONES. I am a pawnbroker. I have some towels pawned by the prisoner, in December - here is also a table-cloth and some pillow-cases, pawned at different times - here are the duplicates I gave for them.
   SAMUEL HAVLOCK. I am a pawnbroker. I produce some sheets and other things, pawned by Featherstone and her daughter at different times.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I never put a key to the padlock - the door pushed open itself.
 GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Six Months.
 1882.  ELIZABETH BOLDERIDGE was indicted for stealing, on the 3d of September , 1 coat, value 10s., and 1 pair of trousers, value 10s., the goods of John Maidment.
 JOHN MAIDMENT. I am a  bricklayer. I lost these things on the 3d of September; I had not seen them very lately - I lodge in the prisoner's mother's house.
   WILLIAM BOLDERIDGE. I am the prisoner's brother - she pressed me to take these things a week before I did; she said she would give me 6d. to take them, and I did so - she was not present; she does not live at my mother's - I took them to her, at her lodgings.
     NOT GUILTY.
