 1187.  CHARLOTTE THOMAS was indicted for stealing, on the 22d of July , a pillow, value 2 s.; a pillow-case, value 6 d.; five knives, value 1 s.; six forks, value 1 s.; a blanket, value 6 d.; two pair of stockings, value 1 s., and two half-handkerchiefs, value 1 s., the goods of John Gifford, her master.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 MR.  JOHN GIFFORD. I live on Muswellhill. The prisoner was in my service, as cook, up to the 22d of July - she had been with us more than a year - after she was gone we missed the articles stated in the indictment; among others, I found she lodged with one Glascott, in the neighbourhood - I went there with an officer, and found her at home in a room up one pair of stairs - she said she knew nothing about it; search was made in her presence; a pillow and case found on her bed, and inside the pillow, among the feathers, were five knives and six forks - they were parlour knives, and had never been used - we missed more - the initials were nearly picked out of the pillowcase; but the thread holes were visible. I found a blanket and two pair of stockings, with the marks on; I believe I had never worn them; I never gave her any stockings; they were tied up in a pair of stockings belonging to a man who had formerly lived with me. A cravat of mine had been cut in two; I found half of it on her neck with my name on it at full length.
 Cross-examined by Mr. ALLEY. Q. You took her with a good character from a friend of your's - A. No, she came from Wales; she broke her collar-bone some time ago.
   ELIZA GLASSCOTT. I live on Muswell-hill; the prisoner lodged on my first floor in July or August; and lodged there when the prosecutor came - she lodged there alone.
   JOHN CONWAY. I assisted in the search, and found the property.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. About four months before I left her service, I wanted a flannel for a house-cloth; she told me to take the ironing blanket - I kept the blanket, and cut up some old petticoats of my own. I broke my collar-bone, and had a pillow of my own, and took the wrong one away. The prosecutor said before I left, that he would do me all the injury he could on account of my leaving his service to be married.
 MR. GIFFORD. I never used any such language.
   ANN HORTON. I am servant to Mr. Gifford. I never knew that the prisoner had a pillow of her own.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Judgment Respited.
 1188.  WILLIAM MOLE was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of August , in the dwelling-house of James Bennett , a hat, value 10 s.; four sovereigns; and 20 s., his property.
   JAMES BENNETT. I am a hatter, and live at No. 28, Oxford-street- the prisoner was shopman at another shop of mine, No. 31. On the 4th of August, about six o'clock in the evening, I returned from the city, and was told he had gone out with a new hat on, and left his own behind. I found the till empty, which contained four sovereigns and 20 s. in the morning. He was the only person there except the errand boy. I went to his lodings - he was not there - I went there again about two o'clock in the morning, and found him; the hat was there; the constable charged him with stealing the hat and money; he said it was all very true. Four sovereigns and some silver were found in his pocket.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. In the morning you left 5 l. in case change should be wanted - A. Yes; he managed the shop. I saw his wife when I first went to his lodgings. She fetched him, and next time I found him at home. I have every reason to suppose he was intoxicated, and in that state he might take the money, meaning to take care of it.
   WILLIAM BARKER. I am errand-boy, and am thirteen years old. The prisoner went out about five in the afternoon with a new hat on, and left his own behind. I said,
 "Why, you have got one of master's new hats on." He said, Yes; he was going to shew a gentleman the shape of it. I saw him at the desk about a quarter of an hour before he left, and when my master came home he missed the money. I saw he appeared the worse for liquor. He told me to get the tea ready, and he should return in ten minutes, but never returned. We shut up at nine o'clock.
   ROBERT NEEDHAM. I am a constable. I went with the prosecutor to the prisoner's lodgings, and found him in bed. The hat was on the drawer, which Bennet claimed. I said he must go to the watch-house. I said I took him for robbing his master. I did not mention money or any thing. He was very much intoxicated, and said he very well knew all about it. I found four sovereigns and six shillings in his pocket.
     NOT GUILTY.
 1189.  RICHARD NORRIS was indicted for stealing, on the 10th of September , five pieces of horse-hides, value 10 s., the goods of Noah Slee and Jonah Slee.
   JOHN SHIELDS. I am a Thames Police officer. On the 10th of September, about half-past seven o'clock in the evening, I met the prisoner at the top of King-street, Tower-hill, with a basket of skins. I asked where he bought them from. He said from Mr. Slee, of Bermondsey. I asked where he was going to take them. He said at Mr. Connel's, near Shadwell. I asked if he had a note, or bill. He said, No; that Mr. Connel was just gone on, and he supposed he had got it. I said I should go with him to Connell's. He went, and shewed me the house. I went in and asked Connell, a shoe-maker, if he knew the prisoner, or any thing about the skins. He said, No; but a woman came in, and asked what was the matter. I asked if she knew anything of it. She said, Yes. Connell said he knew nothing of them; and then she said so too. He looked at Connell, and said,
 "You know I was to bring them here." I went over to the prosecutor's, leaving him in custody.
