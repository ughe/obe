Cross-examined by MR. DOANE . Q.  You say this woman came to your shop? A.  Yes; on Saturday, about one o'clock—I described her dress as nearly as I could—I said she had a white bonnet—I believe she had—I only know her by the shawl and the gown she had on—she spoke as if she had lost her front teeth—I think I told Mrs. Mather so before the pri soner's face—I think I said she lisped—I did not think of that till you re minded me of it—I know the prisoner was the woman—I did not say she was lame—I am quite confident of that—I did not say the prisoner was not the woman—Mrs. Mather did not say she would make me swear to her—I never heard her make use of that expression—she did not say so in the presence of the prisoner—I served in my shop the whole of that Saturday—there were not many customers—we chiefly send our bread out—I am positive there was no other half-crown in the till then—there might have been one taken in the day—my husband was at home at the time I took this—he was in the bakehouse setting a batch—he came into the shop in a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes, and saw this—I am quite sure there was only that one—when he went to the till, he said, "Who have you been taking this of?"—we had no words about it—I know a person named Alberry—she was not at my house that day—I will swear the pri soner is the person who came to my shop and gave me this half-crown. JAMES SMITH. I am the husband of this witness. I recollect going to the till on Saturday, the 7th of February—there was this one half-crown in it—I saw it was bad—I took it, and threw it out to my wife—I said, "Who have you had here? you hare had a smasher ," and my wife took it. ANN MATHER. I live at North Brixton, and keep a general shop. On the 10th of February the prisoner came in about ten minutes to six o'clock—she asked for half an ounce of tobacco—it came to 2 d . all but a farthing—the laid down a shilling, but kept her hand on it, and entered into some conversation—when she took up her hand, I saw the shilling was bad—I said, "My good woman, this is a bad shilling"—she said, "Is it?"—I called my nephew, and asked him if it was bad—he said, "Yes, very bad"—she said, "I just took it there," pointing towards London—Mrs. Smith had told me that she had taken a half-crown of a tall, thin woman—I sent for her, and said, "Is this the woman you took the half-crown of?"—she said, "Yes"—I sent for the officer, and he took the prisoner and the shilling.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did Mrs. Smith describe the woman at all? A.  She said she was a tall, thin woman, and she should know her again—I sent for her, and she said she was not so certain about her bonnet, but she was the same woman. WILLIAM WOODWARD. I am a police-constable. I took the prisoner, and received the half-crown and the shilling. JOHN FIELD. I am Inspector of counterfeit coin to His Majesty's Mint. These are both counterfeit.
