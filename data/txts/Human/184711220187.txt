   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q.  How much did you understand was the value of the rope? A.  I have since understood it is not worth 26 s . a cwt.
   MR. BODKIN. Q.  That is, to sell at public auction? A.  Yes—I never had any intimation from Seager of any person being the owner of the rope but himself—when Holliday came, on 3rd Aug., he never intimated that he had anything to do with it.
   COURT. Q.  Where was the rope put? A.  In my second-floor warehouse—Holliday came there the day the tools were fetched away, and the rope was then lying in the warehouse in his view, but he never referred to it. JAMES BROWN re-examined.  On 15th June I received a receiving order with ten packages of rope from Mr. Pearce's man, at Custom House Quay—I subsequently received this order, signed "Gardiner and Co.," to deliver the packages to Cusell and Co.—on 23rd June this delivery order, signed Cusell and Co., was received at the quay, but not by me, I think it was by Mr. Williams. GEORGE WILLIAMS re-examined.  These ten packages were delivered to Wellock on 23rd June. ARTHUR WELLOCK. On 23rd June Cusell employed me to lighter ten packages or rope to Frost and Schroeder's, at Wapping—I afterwards called at Cusell's office, in Lime-street, and received the money for the job—Rolt was the waterman I engaged. JOHN ROLT. On 23rd June I lightered ten packages of rope to Frost and Schroeder's, at Wapping, and landed them there. WILLIAM NORMAN FROST. I am a ship-chandler, at Wapping. On 23rd June I saw Loup on St. Andrew's wharf, which is next door to our ware-house—he asked if I was a buyer of rope—I asked what rope it was—he said he could not tell me, but it was lying at Custom House Quay; and I might see it there—I accordingly went, and met him there—he said it was not his own, it belonged to some party in the City, he did not say who—he asked what I would give for it—he said he did not know the value of it—I said 36 s . a cwt., which he agreed to—there were ten packages; they were in wrappers, and I believe were marked in a diamond—I asked if it was ware-house make—he said he did not know—before he accepted my offer, he went away to consult some one, and then returned back to me, and said I should have it—he was not gone a minute, he just went outside the dock—I did not see him speak to anybody—after purchasing the rope, it was arranged to be lightered to my place, which was done immediately, on account of the tide serving—he followed the rope to my place, saw it landed and weighed, and received the money, 43 l . 2 s . 4 d ., which I paid him—he wrote this receipt in my presence—( read  —"June 23rd; settled by cash, 43 l . 2 s . 4 d . for H. Cusell and Co., J. LOUP ")—I had never seen Cusell at that time, nor had I ever seen Loup before.
