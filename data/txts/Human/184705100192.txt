HENRY WADLOW( police-constable R  267.) About eleven o'clock at night on the 9th of April, I was called to the Crown and Anchor—I heard there were disorderly persons inside, but I did not think it my duty, accord ing to our instructions, to go inside—I then saw Mr. Thorn attempt to put the prisoner out—the prisoner took him by the collar and threw him against the wall—he then followed him, took hold of him again, and threw him on the table—I said I should take him into custody—he was very obstinate, but I succeeded in getting him outside—the moment I got him outside he hit me with his clenched fist in the eye, and knocked me down backwards, and commenced kicking me while I was down—he thrust his fingers into my throat and pressed my head to the ground—he very much hurt my throat—I had two black eyes—he fell on me with his knees on my cheat while I was down—I got on my knees and struck him with my staff—he knocked me down a second time and hit me in the eye—Dybell then came and kicked me in the side twice, while I was down—he then caught me by the thumb and pulled it back and very much injured it—I called for assistance while I was on my knees—I lost my senses, and when I came to my senses again the prisoner and Dybell were on me—I still had them by the collar—two civilians  came and sprang my rattle—the prisoner was taken on the spot-Dybell made his escape, and was taken the next morning—I was unable to attend to my duty for five days, in consequence of the injuries I received—I was obliged to have medical attendance—I have been since put on lighter duty.
   Prisoner.  You were standing at the bar drinking—you had a pint of half-and-half. Witness.  No, it is quite false.
   Prisoner.  It is true—I went in the tap-room and a man asked me to drink—the officer came and told me to go out—I said "I shan't till I have drank this pint and had another pint," and he struck me with his staff. JAMES SULLIVAN. I am in the service of an eating-house keeper near the Crown and Anchor—about eleven o'clock that evening I heard the police man crying out for assistance—I went out and saw the prisoner hit him in the eye with his hand—I ran up to lay hold of the prisoner, and he hit me between the two eyes—I got up again, and saw Dybell run up and kick the officer in the side, or in the back, while he was down—assistance came, and the prisoner was taken—Dybell got away. HENRY EDWARD DRINKWATER. I am a surgeon—I act during the ab sence of Mr. Stewart, the surgeon to the Police-force—I was called to attend Henry Wadlow—he had two contusions on his forehead—his right hand was considerably bruised and lacerated—the skin of the knuckles was torn off—he appeared like a man who had been severely beaten—he suffered in con sequence of it for several days.
   Prisoner.  I never kicked him. GUILTY. Aged 25.— Confined One Year .
