NEW COURT .— Saturday, March  4 th , 1843.
   Sixth Jury, before Mr. Recorder.
 1003. GEORGE NICHOLSON was indicted for stealing, on the   28th of Feb ., 84lbs. weight of copper, value 3 l  ., the goods of George Green and others; to which he pleaded GUILTY.— Transported for Seven Years .
 1004. JOSEPH HOYE was indicted for stealing, on the   25th of Feb ., 100lbs. weight of beef, value 1 l  . 17 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of Thomas Bonsor , his master; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined One Month .
 1005. DENNIS SULLIVAN was indicted for stealing, on the   24th of Feb ., 1 lb. weight of tobacco, value 3 s  . 10 d  . and 1 jar, 2 s  . 2 d  . the goods of Charles William Schnitzler ; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 11.— Confined Three Months .
 1006. WILLIAM DEAN was indicted for stealing, on the   27th of Feb ., 2 half-crowns, the monies of Robert Eales ; to which he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 15.— Confined Three Months .
 1007. CHARLES MOOR was indicted for stealing, on tie   25th of Feb ., 1 coat, value 3 l  . 3 s  ., the goods of Edward Banks ; to winch he pleaded GUILTY. Aged 24.— Confined Three Months .
 1008. THOMAS BROOME was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of Feb ., 7 quarts of brandy, value 2 l  . 17 s  .; 12 bottles, 3 s  .; and 1 box, 2 s  .; the goods of the London Dock Company .
   MR. BALLANTINE conducted the Prosecution. WILLIAM HODDER EVANS. I am foreman of the case-wine warehouse, No. 4, London Docks. I was occupying a box on the floor, No. 10, which is the second floor of the warehouse—there is an entrance into the warehouse by a staircase—between that staircase and the box which I occupy there is a pile of spirits, which prevents my seeing any persons passing up the staircase—there is a staircase leading to No. 11, and a person on that staircase could see a person on the staircase leading to No. 9—there is a trap-door through No. 10, from which goods are let down to No. 9, and from that out of a loop-hole—on the 7th of Feb., between three and four o'clock, the prisoner came to me at my warehouse—he handed me orders for five cases of wine—they were such orders as authorised me to deliver them to him—the smallest of those cases contained-three dozen, and the largest six dozen—I had known the prisoner many years—he was in the habit of coming with orders, and he knew the mode of business—the proper way of delivering those cases was down the trap-door, and through, the loop-hole—when he delivered the orders to me, I told him to make haste down, he should have them directly—one of those cases was in No. 10, and four were in the upper floor—in order to get out of No. 10, the prisoner had to go round those cases of brandy which were piled up—Watkins was at that time going up the stairs of No. 11 floor—he called out to me, and in consequence of that, I sent Smith round—the prisoner, in going out, would have to pass the cases of brandy, and the case now in question was one that was in that pilr—Watkind went after the prisoner—Smith went after him, and I followef—ehen I got down I found the prisoner standing by this case of brandy, directly under the tail of his cart—Watkins and Smith had got to him beforr—I heard the prisoner say he believed he had got the wrong case—I said I thought he had—he was then taken into custody
