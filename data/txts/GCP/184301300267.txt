JAMES NICOL. I am clerk to Mr. Joseph Harvey. The prisoner was in
his service on the 10th of Jan. as a salesman-he had been there from two to
three months-Mrs. Clark was a customer of ours-it was the prisoner's duty
to receive sums of money, and to pay them directly to the cashier, and then
to tell me of it, that it might be placed paid in the book-he did not tell me,
on the 10th of Jan., that he had received 31. 188. 6d. from Mrs. Clark-oh
the 16th he came to me, and said, "I have made a mistake, I wish you would
put it to rights for me; I received the money from the customer in Surrey-
street, Strand ; and though I ought to have made it paid in the book, I
omitted to do so; I wish you would do it for me now"-I said, as I had no
doubt he had paid the money to the cashier, I would do so, and asked the
amount-he said 31. 188. 108., but he had only received 31. 18s. 60.; that he
had received it on the 11th, and paid it to the cashier on the 12th he did not
say which cashier-I then made inquiries of the cashier--the prisoner keeps
a book in which he writes the various amounts he receives during the day-I
referred to it on the morning of the 16tb, after he had spoken to me, and
found that no such sum had been entered--in the afternoon of the same day
I found it had been entered it had been interlided betwixt the two days.
