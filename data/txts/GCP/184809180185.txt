2218. WILLIAM JOHNSON, unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
Mr. CLERK conducted the Prosecution.
SARAH SWINDELL. I am the wife of James Swindell, a general-dealer in
Wellington-street. On 14th Sept., the prisoner came for a quarter of an
ounce of tobacco, and gave me a shilling-I gave him 114d. change, and he
left- I looked at the shilling, it was one of the Queen's reign-I tried to bend
1-I then put it into the till there were three other shillings there--an officer
came in in a few minutes I had not taken any other money-I then took
the money from the till-there were four shillings altogether, one a Queen's,
and the other three King's.
Ann Dixon. I assist Mr. Turner, a general-dealer, of Broad Wall. On
14th Sept., the prisoner came for half an ounce of tobacco-he gave me a
shilling -I tried it with my teeth-it was hard, and I did not think it was
bad-an officer came in, and I gave him the shilling.
Thomas West (policeman, N 249). On 14th Sept., I was with Whit-
lamb-I saw the prisoner in Friar-street with another man-We followed them,
and saw the prisoner go into Mrs. Swindell's shop-after he came out, I went
in, and received this shilling from Mrs. Swindell-I came out, and saw the
prisoner again, in company with the same man-they went on to Broad Wall,
and the prisoner left the other man, and went into Mr. Turner's-I went in
as soon as I heard the money go on the counter, took the prisoner by the
collar, and told him I wanted him for passing bad money-I saw Whitlamb
take the shilling
JOHN WHITLAMB (policeman, M 89). I took the shilling--the prisoner
became violent--the other man got away.
CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am inspector of coin to the Royal Mint-
these shillings are both counterfeit.
Guilty. Aged 20.-Confired Six Months.
2219. WILLIAM WESSON, stealing 1 sack, value 1s.; and 1f bushel of
beans, value 8s.; the goods of James Page, his master.
JANES PAGE. I am a farmer at Merton. The prisoner was my carterit
was his duty to feed the horses--I went to his house, and found these beans
--they are mine, and the sack also be said it was his custom to take the
horses food to his house be had no business to do so, there is a proper bin
in the stable for it.
Cross-examined by ME. CHARNOCK. Q. How far is his house from the
Stable? 1. About fifty yards; I think I found these beans under the bed
they are inferior-he has been with me five or six years-be said he bad
taken them to feed his horses--he treated his horses well, and liked to see
them fat--they work the better for it.
