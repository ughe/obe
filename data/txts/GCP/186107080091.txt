Prisoner's Defence. I never saw the money; the two notes I had were my
own; she is my sister's husband's aunt ; I should not have gone there for
Mrs. Thorpe's wages, only more for the sake of a comfortable home. I had
money before ever I went to Mrs. Thorpe ; the two 51. notes I had were
money paid to me for three marble chimney-pieces that my husband left
when he died; that he had made; the ear rings were my own.
COURT to ANNA THORPE. Q. Had she been backwards and forwards from
your place before! A. She left once, saying that she was very ill, and then
came back again on the Tuesday-she was away for two or three days.
Prisoner. Q. How was it you sent everywhere to inquire about me
I sent no one to inquire where you were.
COURT. Q. What time was it when you went up stairs and saw this
money! A. About half-past 6-I saw that all the money was quite safe
then I had no particular object for looking to see, only I went into the
room to fetch a tea-caddy and I saw the draw a little open, and I looked to
see that it was safe, and it was perfectly safe, and within half an hour after
that there was not a vestige of it left.
