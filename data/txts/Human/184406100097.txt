 1603. JOHN WALLER and JOHN DAVIS were indicted for stealing 1 coat, value 1 l  ., 1 s  . the goods of William Fisher ; and that Waller had been before convicted of felony. CHARLES KENNEDY. I am in the employ of William Fisher, a tailor, in King William-street. At a quarter past ten o'clock at night, on the 23th of May, I saw Waller take this coat—I ran after him, and then Davis had it—I took them back—this is my master's coat—I did not lose sight of them. HENRY JOHN M'DONALD( City police-constable, No , 517.) I received the prisoners, and this coat in charge. WILLIAM NORTON( City police-constable, No.  286.) I produce a certificate of Waller's former conviction, which I got from Mr. Clark's office—( read )—he is the person.
   WALLER  — GUILTY. Aged 19.— Confined Twelve Months .
   DAVIS  — GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months .

 1607. JAMES SMITH was indicted for stealing 1 coat, value 2 l  ., the goods of Aaron Chapman . HENRY TOWNSEND. I am coachman to Aaron Chapman, of Highbury-park. I was standing with his carriage at the London Tavern on the 22nd of May—I turned, and saw my coat move on the box—I then saw the pri soner walk deliberately away with it—I caught him with it under his arm—it is my master's coat. ROBERT COUSINS( City police-constable, No.  2.) I received the coat and the prisoner in charge.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I saw a coat lying on the footpath, and not knowing what it was I stooped to pick it up; I saw the carriage standing close by; I was making my way up to the carriage, as there was a man sitting on the seat, to ask who the coat belonged to, but I saw a person coming towards me before I got up to the carriage; he accused me of stealing it; I said I had picked it up. HENRY TOWNSEND re-examined.  He was going from the carriage when I saw him, and it was about one minute from the time I saw the coat move till he had the coat.
