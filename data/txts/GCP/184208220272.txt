THOMAS SMITH. I keep the White Hart at Battersea--the prisoner
my pot-boy. On the 27th of July, about half-past ten o'clock in the
morning, I missed fourteen sovereigns from my till-the key of the kitchen
door will unlock the bar-door, but I did not know it then I saw me
money safe at half-past six that morning-the till was locked-there are tw
tills, one inside the other-the inner one goes within four inches of the
bottom--this money was between the two tills-when we want the mone
to change, the inner till is taken out, and then we get to the gold and silve
--there was 91. 38. in silver in a bag, and 271. 10s. in gold-I lostno
silver-there was a shilling dropped in the bar--I have not found any dl
the gold-I found the till still locked-if we go out, the till is locked, and
the key put between two liquor-tubs-it was found there I keep the key
of the bar in my pocket-1 charged the prisoner as soon as the money
was missed.
MARY COOPER. I am in the service of Mr. Smith. On the morning
of the 27th of July, about a quarter to seven o'clock, the prisoner came
into the kitchen-he asked where master was- I said he was outside-he
said no more, but went and took the key of the parlour door, and tried the
bar door-he said that was no use-then he went to the kitchen-door-
he said, “This is the one"-be then went into the bar-I saw him stand
with his hands close to the till—when he came out, he put his hand into
his right-hand pocket, and said, "All I have taken is two cigars"-master
was in the yard at the time, at work-mistress had not come out of her
room.
Prisoner. She came and asked me to come and sit down by the side of
her, and asked me if I had unlocked the bar. Witness. No, I never spoke
to him-I did not ask him why he wanted the key to open the bar-door-
I was alarmed when I saw it--when mistress came down and missed the
money, she asked me, and I said I had not seen any money-I was taken
up on suspicion-I did not tell my master of it.
John PAYNE (police-constable V 11.) The prisoner and Cooper were
both brought to the Wandsworth station-Cooper said several times to the
prisoner, "John, you have got the money, why don't you give it up
- I told her not to say anything-she said, “He has got the money,
saw him go in"-be made no answer.
JOSEPH TIGHE (police-constable V 199.) I received charge of the pri-
soners-on the way to the station, Cooper said in the prisoner's presence,
"I have not got the money, John has got it, I saw him at the till" she
said, "John, tell me where the money is, and I will go back and get it,
and spare all further trouble" he made no reply-I found 21. 48. 6d. In
silver, and 10 d. in copper on the prisoner, and 1s. 4d. on Cooper about
an hour after the robbery was discovered.
Prisoner. The money that was taken from me, was money I had by me
for setting up skittles, and 38. a week for wages- I was two months and
three weeks with him, and 18s. I had for a watch that was raffled at the
Waterman's Arms, Mr. Prior's.
