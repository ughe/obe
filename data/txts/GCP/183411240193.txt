WILLIAM DOUGLAS. I am a journeyman harness-maker, living in
Crown-street, King-street, Westminster. "I have been out of work lately
-I went to Richmond on Monday, the 27th of October, with the two pri-
soners--we set out from London at half-past eight o'clock, and arrived at
Richmond at twelve o'clock at night—Gray said he was going to see his
friends there we went to the corner of Richmond-green both the pri-
soners told me to stand at the corner of the green, and they parted from
me-I staid half an hour, I suppose; they returned both together, and Gray
said they had not seen their friends—they went to the corner of a place
where there was a field, at the corner of Mr. Long's house, and they said
they were to lie down a bit—instead of laying down, they went to a wall
close by Mr. Long's- I went with them as far as the wall-Gray said
that was his uncle's—they both got over the wall-I got on the top of the
wall, and sat on it-they got over another wall close by, that wall parted
Mr. Long's wall from the house—they then went to the left, towards the
house-I then lost sight of them-after they had been gone a little while,
I saw a light in the back apartment of Mr. Long's house_the house ad-
joins others, I believe—I heard as if it were the report of a gun-the
prisoners came back again in about half an hour, or three quarters, to
the place where I was—it was near two o'clock— I was not sitting on
the wall all the time--1 was at the wall when they came back-they had
two boxes, one containing the tea, and two or three square pieces of wood
in the other-Bryan had the box containing the tea-Gray had the small
box-I saw the spoons, but I cannot say how many-I did not see them
