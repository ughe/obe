   JURY. Q.  What part of the house do you do you live in? A.  The kitchen, which is up stairs—the lady and gentleman live in the room above—they could not go to the room above without my knowing it, because they did not know the way of the house, or who I had got in it—they were hardly out of the room during the day I went for the change. ROBERT CORLETT re-examined.  I came home on the Saturday evening, be tween eight and nine—I remained down stairs with Mr. and Mrs. Gale, not in the room where I kept my money—I had left that room soon after four—I locked it, had the key with me, and the key of my box—I did not go up to the room till I went to bed—the box was not opened then—the money could not have been taken till Sunday—I slept there that night, and left the trunk safe at a quarter-past ten on Sunday morning—I tried it.
   JURY. Q.  Had you the key of the room with you on Sunday morning? A.  No, I left it in the door for Mrs. Gale to make up the room—the lid of the trunk was not broken, it was unlocked—it did not seem to have been forced—I tried the key of my room to a box in the prisoners' room, in the prisoner's presence, and it fitted it; but I have not seen the key of the pri soners' box. WILLIAM GREY( policeman. ) I took the prisoners, and found the money in their box—the house is in the parish of Christchurch.
   JURY to SARAM GALE. Q.  Was the hole in the shilling a drilled hole, or a punched hole? A.  It was a little raised on one side—it was at the top of the head—I had received a fortnight's rent from them—14 s . altogether, 4 s . of which was for attendance during her confinement—it was all in shillings—I did not see them with any gold.

   PHŒBE JOLLY  — NOT GUILTY.
 2242. WILLIAM JOLLY and PHŒBE JOLLY were again  indicted for stealing 1 crown, 2 half-crowns, 1 shilling, and 1 sixpence; the monies of Mary Ann Carnes . MARY ANN CARNES. I am a widow, and live at 9, Princes-street, Union-street. On the 11th of July the prisoner Phoebe came to me, told me she was a cap-maker, and wanted part of a bed—I took her to sleep with me—I took her for a single woman—on the Tuesday before she left on the Wed nesday, the prisoner William came—they had an interview—they did not say what relationship there was between them, they both break fasted with me that morning—I went out between twelve and one o'clock, leaving them in my room, and nobody in the house but a little girl twelve years old—I was away three quarters of an hour or an hour, came back, and found the prisoners had just dined—they left between seven and eight o'clock, leaving no one in the house but me—he said to her, "Now, my dear, shall I take your box;" he went away taking her box—two hours afterwards I went to my drawers, I found them locked as I had left them, and missed my money which had been in a little brown bag—nobody else had been in the house—I did not see the prisoners again till they were in custody—I had given her warning—I never saw bag or money again—there was nearly 3 l . in it.
   William Jolly. Q.  Did not a young man lodge in the house? A.  Not that night—there was only an old man lodged in the house, he house, he was out all day—I had seen my money in the drawers the night before—they had not been opened since: I had the key in my pocket.
