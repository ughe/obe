 1875.  GEORGE BARHAM was indicted for stealing, on the 24th of August , 2 bottles, value 6d.; 1 quart of brandy, value 3s.; 1 quart of wine, value 3s., and 60 penny-pieces, the property of Henry Brett, his master.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   HENRY BRETT. I am a wine and brandy-merchant, and live in Drury-lane - the prisoner was my cellarman. On the evening of the 24th of August, in consequence of a communication from Kirby, I went in pursuit of the prisoner, and caught him in Whitechapel-road; a 5s. paper of copper was found on him, which I can speak to - it was tied in part of a newspaper, and I had the other parts of the same paper, which exactly corresponded, and which likewise contained penny-pieces; it was part of the Morning Advertiser, of the 4th of August.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q.When had he left your house? A.About half-past eleven o'clock at night.
   ANN KIRBY. I am the niece of the prosecutor, and live in his house. On the evening of the 24th of August, we had seven 5s. papers of copper in his bed-room, tied up in newspapers - I went into the room, and saw them all safe at nine o'clock; my uncle was then at home - he went out just afterwards, and the prisoner left about half-past eleven; he had no business in that room, but could get there- as soon as he was gone I went into the room, and missed one paper; my uncle came home soon afterwards, and I informed him - he went after the prisoner; I have since seen the paper of copper found on him - it is in the same paper, tied in the same way, and to the best of my belief, is the paper which was missing.
 Cross-examined. Q. Had you placed it in the bedroom? A. No.
   WILLIAM WHITAKER. I live in Drury-lane. I produce a perfect copy of the Morning Advertiser, of the 4th of August; I have examined these parts - they exactly match, and they are all of the paper of the 4th of August.
   JOHN PERKINS. I am a Policeman. I went in pursuit of the prisoner with the prosecutor, we overtook him in Whitechapel-road, and found on him a 5s. paper of copper, tied up in this newspaper - the paper broke, but I have preserved it, and produce it; I went to the prisoner's lodging, No. 20, Alfred-street, White Horse-lane, Stepney, and found there, a bottle of wine, a bottle of brandy, and some other bottles, with Mr. Brett's seal on them - before we went befor the Magistrate, I said to the prisoner, "Mr. Brett has lost several papers of coppers;" he said he had never meddled with any before, and he did not know what induced him to do it then, except that he had been drinking.
 MR. BARRY to MR. BRETT. Q. Had you not given or sold spirits and wine to the prisoner? A. I certainly had both given and sold him some about Christmas, in consequence of his wife being confined, but I am quite confident I never gave him this brandy.
 COURT. Q. How long had he been with you? A.About two years; I always considered him a very good character.
 The prisoner received a good character. GUILTY. Aged 30.
 Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor. Confined Three Months.
 1876.  THOMAS GREEN was indicted for stealing, on the 2nd of August , 1 jacket, value 6s., the goods of Thomas Frenchum.
 THOMAS FRENCHUM. I am a  labourer, and live at Ponder's-end, near Enfield, Middlesex. I was cutting wheat in a field on the 2nd of August, and saw the prisoner there, in company with some other men - my jacket was in the field, and a knife was in the pocket; I missed it.
 JAMES KING. I am a pawnbroker, and live at Edmonton. This jacket was pawned with me on the 2nd of August, about twelve o'clock, by the prisoner, in the name of  Joseph Moore, of Hoddesdon.(Property produced and sworn to.)
     GUILTY. Aged 23. - Confined Three Months.
 1877.  JOSEPH ATKINS was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of August , 1 saw, value 3s., the goods of Robert Darley.
 ROBERT DARLEY. I am a  carpenter, and live at Hoxton. I left a saw on my work-bench, in Salisbury-street, on Saturday the 20th of August, and when I returned at two o'clock it was gone.
   WILLIAM EGLINTON. I saw the prisoner at my house on the 20th of August, where Darley has a work-shop - the prisoner asked me if I had any work for him; this was about a quarter after one o'clock.
   ROBERT PIERCEY. I am in the employ of a pawnbroker, in Tabernacle-row. I have a saw, pawned by the prisoner, about two o'clock on the 20th of August.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 Prisoner's Defence. I went in, and said I had no money - Eglinton told me to go and pawn this saw, which I did - I was waiting for him, and saw the prosecutor, who accused me of stealing the saw; I denied it - he said I had better own to it, and he would forgive me; I then gave him the duplicate, and he gave charge of me.
   THOMAS DORLEY. I am a Policeman. I took the prisoner - he owned pawning the saw.
     GUILTY. Aged 20. - Confined Three Months.
