JOHN HOLLINGSWORTH I am carman to Mr. Laurence Dorgan, a glass dealer, in Sheppy-yard, Little Trinity, Minories. I cannot say whether it is Holy or Little Trinity-parish—on the 31st of March I went there in the afternoon to look for the foreman—my master has a warehouse there, on the second floor—I found the prisoner on the top of the stairs, with a basket containing fourteen decanters, which had been taken from a shelf in an inner warehouse—there was another man with him, he was not secured he ran down stairs, and left a hat and decanter by the side of the basket—the prisoner lifted a basket from his shoulder and ran up two or three stairs—in two or three minutes he come down, went down on his knees, and begged pardon—he begged I would him go, and said he had been into it by the other—I left him in charge of a man on the first floor, and fetched Forrester—i found the warehouse door open—the lock was shot, as it would be when it caught and there were marks on the door, as if a small instrument had been applied to wrench it open—there was a hay fork near, and the marks are such as that would make.
   Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON . Q.  The prisoner said the others one had brought him? A.  Yes—I had not been in the warehouse from nine o'clock in the morning—I found the prisoner there at two o'clock—the other person was a man dressed in black—this boy did not attempt to run away—he ran two or three stairs towards the third floor—he had a cap on—the other one ran down and left the hat behind him, which belonged to the prisoner who said the other man had lent him a cap to carry the basket with—the things were packed in the basket—the foreman had not been out half an hour—I saw only the back of the other man, he was not better dressed than this man—he was about the same age. GEORGE CULLUM I am foreman to the prosecutor. I was there nearly the whole of that morning, till nearly two o'clock—I locked the warehouse door when I went—i took the key to the counting-house—I returned in about twenty minutes, from what I heard—the door had been wrenched open—I had seen the prisoner in Sheppy-yard is the morning, a little after ten o'clock, I think—i saw one or two others, but not in company with him—sixteen decanters were missing from the inner warehouse—they were safe when I left, at a quarter before two o'clock.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Do you know the name of the parish?— A.  It's called the Holy Trinity but I don't know—the doors were forced by some pressure—there are bolts at the top bottom, and they are folding doors and one lock's into the other—it is not a very strong door—it is not likely that it had been opened by the lock being only on the spring, without being turned—I locked it full two or three inches—it is a single lock—I am quite sure both bolts were locked. JOHN FORRESTER. I took the prisoner—i know the warehouse—they call it sheppy-yard in the Holy trinity Minories.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How do you know it is called the Holy Trinity? A.  I have seen it on paper many times—i never saw it printed the Little Trinity—I have seen "The Holy Minories, in the Tower Liberty, in the country of Middlesex"—I have been constable there forty years—the City separates at the corner of Harris-yard—I am constable of Portsoken ward—this is the property. GEORGE CULLUM These are Mr. Dorgan's property.
   Prisoner  I went to work for a man in Bristol-street at half-past nine o'clock—I received 1 s . 6 d  —I was walking along the Minories, and a man asked me if I wanted a job—I said, "Yes"—he took me down a yard, and lifted these things a job—I said I had better take his cap, as I could carry them better—and in my agitation I might take these things and throw them off my shoulder, but I don's recollect it. JOHN HOLLINGSWORTH re-examined  The other man saw me—he put down a hat and the decanter and ran away—i came in at the top of the stairs and saw them. GEORGE CULLUM, I am certain I saw the prisoner that morning between ten and eleven o'clock—I have ascertained that he had been employed between that time and time of the time of the robbery—I left the door locked, and I found the lock shot when I returned—it shot into an from fastening—the door was broken, but the lock was not pushed back.
