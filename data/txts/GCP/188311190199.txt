HENRY DAROY POWER. I am a licentiate of the College of Surgeons, 66,
Old Kent Rond-I was called by the prisoner's husband on the night of
23rd October to see the dead body of the child—it was very greatly
emaciated-he said he wished a post-mortem the subject of & certificate
was broached by him or the prisoner, I could not say which, and I declined
to give one.
FREDERIOK HALL. I am foreman to Mr. Burridge, an undertaker, of 72,
Southwark Bridge Road-on 26th October I fetched the body of a child
from No. 4, Milton House--it was then in a blue coffin--I took it to the
mortuary at St. George's in the Borough, and left it there there was no
other body of a child there.
WILLIAM THOMAS. I am the mortuary keeper of St. George's on the
morning of 27th October I found the body of a child there, which had been
brought by Hall-it was in a blue coffin.
ROBERT GIBSON BROWN. I am a surgeon, of 144, Brixton Road-by direction
of the Coroner I made a post-mortem examination of the boily of a child at
the mortuary of St. George's on 27th October-it was a child about three
months old, in a blue coffin--there were no marks of violence on the body
-it was very emaciated, there was a total absence of fat all the organs
were perfectly healthy—the stomach had a small quantity of milk in it, but
the intestines were quite empty-it was very thin indeed, almost transparent
--the omentum had no fat in it-it weighed 5 lb., it ought to have weighed
11 lb, or 12 lb.-I aseribed the death to starvation, there was nothing else
to account for it--there was nothing in the condition of the heart to suggest
cyanosis as the cause of death-extreme debility might simulate it-if the
child had had improper food there would have been irritation in the
intestines, and there was none.
Cross-examined. If improper food had been administered before the
17th and not subsequently I should not expect to find irritation of the
intestines.
By the Court. The child died from want of nourishment; what I saw
indicated that there had been no food at all, proper or improper.
CATHERINE NEVIN. I am the wife of a porter in the old Kent Road —
I have known the prisoner a short time I went with her with the child to
Dr. Mason-he asked her what she fed it on-she said on nursery biscuits
and corn flour he asked if she gave it the breast she said she did he
said he thought the breast sufficient for it; that was all.
Cross-examined. I had known her between five and six months-the
baby was a day old when I first saw it--I have seen her suckle it once or
twice and give it corn flour in a bottle.
