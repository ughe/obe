   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  How long was it before the offi cer came? A.  About ten minutes—I held him all the time—he did not tell me the handkerchief was falling, and he caught it to prevent its falling—a person at the door said, "What has he done?" and then he said, "The handkerchief fell down, and I picked it up." I had hold of him at the time the officer came—he did not attempt to escape—there was only one person in the shop, but four or five in the boxes—the person in the shop was a stranger—he could not see what he was doing, without turning round to look at him.
 (  Property produced and sworn to. )
   Prisoner's Defence.  I saw some yellow handkerchiefs hanging up, and went in and looked at than—I saw some more hanging near the counter—I went to look at those, and they came down in my hand—I said, "Here is one of your handkerchiefs come down"—he said, "I know all about it, you wanted to steal it"—I said, "I have not stolen it, and am not going to run away"—he said, "I will take good care you shall not," and he gave me in charge.
   JURY to WILLIAM POTTER . Q.  Was the handkerchief out of sight till it was given to you? A.  Yes, his jacket was buttoned—I saw his hand withdrawn from his pocket, it had nothing in it—when I laid bold of him he gave me the handkerchief from his pocket. JOHN STORY. I am an officer. I found the prisoner in the shop—Pot ter had the handkerchief in his hand—the prisoner was standing quietly there—he certainly denied it.

GEORGE PUTLEY. I live in High-street, Borough. Peacock was in my employ for about three months as foreman—in consequence of information I received I spoke to Hack and Wilkinson, and the prisoners were appre hended—Peacock was not authorized to sell on a Sunday—I do not open shop at all on a Sunday, at any time—if a regular customer sends, I may accommodate him, but then I expect to be applied to, before I allow him to serve—when I engaged him, he asked too much wages, and I said, "Remember, there is no Sunday work."
