 2352. HENRY ATTFIELD was indicted for stealing, on the   4th of August , 1 flannel shirt, value 2 s  .; 1 shirt, value 2 s  .; and 2 shifts, value 3 s  .; the goods of Edward Patmore . SARAH PATMORE. I am the wife of Edward Patmore, a labourer, and live in Great Mitchell-street. On Saturday morning last, I hung some linen out to dry in the back yard—I went to take it in about half-past ten o'clock, and found the prisoner in the yard taking the linen off the line—I never saw him before—he had these things in his arms, and threw then on the dust-hole—I asked what he did there—he said he had been to the privy—he had pulled his boots off to go through the passage, and had them in his arms, he rushed by me, and ran through into the street, and the policeman stopped him without my losing sight of him. RICHARD MADDOX. I was on duty last Saturday night in Brick-lane—I heard the cry of "Stop thief," and saw the prisoner running, and the prosecutrix after him—I stopped him. GUILTY. * Aged 18.— Transported for Seven Years.

I am the brother of Henry Woolcott. I re ceived this order from Adcock—the prisoner came to the shop again the same day, and I saw him—I knew him as John Davis, who had lived a in Frith-street, Soho, and afterwards in Orange-street, Bloomsbury—I gave the order to my brother, and told him he was John Davis. HENRY WOOLCOTT. On the 8th of July, I saw the prisoner at my shop—I went in with the order in my hand, and asked him if he knew Mr. Cooper well—he said, "Yes, very well"—I said, "Do you live in his service?"—he said, "Yes"—I asked him how long he had lived with him—he paused, and said, "Eight months"—I said, "I understand your name is John Davis"—he looked at me very coolly, and said, "It is John Davis"—I said, "I thought your name was John Davis; you can go and tell you master, Mr. Cooper, if he wants my goods, to send the money for them." WILLIAM ROPER PEARSE. I am high-constable of Dartford.
