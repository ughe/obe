JAMES JONES. I am foreman to Messrs. Emanuel and Thomas Silva,
wharfingers. I know the warehouse D No. 1-there were thirty-five
casks of oil in it, some was brown seal, and some pale seal oil-on Wed-
nesday, the 23rd of March, I had been at the warehouse-I then had
this key with the label now produced when I left I locked the door,
and hung the key in the counting-house, in its proper place I had no
occasion to go to it after that before the discovery, nor had anybody else to
my knowledge if the key had remained in its proper place, it should
have been hanging up in the counting-house--Rice was a weekly ser-
vant of Messrs. Silva- I did not know him before he came into their
service--I have known Lodan many years--hé was occasionally em-
ployed by master, but seldom-he knew all our warehouses very well. I

JAMES BERRY. I am cellarman to Pilcher and Co., oil-merchants, in
Morgan's-lane. I have dipped two barrels of oil at the warehouse of the
prosecutors, D No. 1, to see what was gone-they appeared to be deficient
- took a sample from the two barrels, which I produce-I dipped seve-
tal-they differed in colour-these two were what I call straw-oil, and
corresponded with that found in the cans--the ground was wet with oil
bere are two samples from the cans-in my judgment they are the same
quality as these samples taken from the cans--they are all straw-oil, but
one barrel is paler than the other the oil in one barrel corresponds with
one can, and the other with the other.
