MARY RUMBLE. I was married to the prisoner on the 20th of March, 1815; he said he was married before, but he had writings drawn up of separation, and gave me a letter, which he said was his wife's hand-writing.
 This letter was here read, it was dated the 26th of February, 1815, and signed  Mary Hare, stating, that if the prisoner would send her a divorcement she would sign it.
   JOHN HARE. I do not believe this to be my daughter's writing.
 The prisoner, in a written defence, complained of his first wife's leaving him and cohabiting with the lieutenant of his regiment, and that she finally married again, and that his ignorance of the law led him to suppose he might do the same.
     GUILTY. Aged 36. Confined Three Months.
 717.  JAMES SHEAN was indicted for stealing, on the 27th of March , 60 lbs. of bacon, value 20 s., the goods of John Loveridge.
 JOHN LOVERIDGE. I live in  Crawford-street, Mary-le-bone. On the 27th of March, I was serving behind the counter, and saw the prisoner and another man come up and take a side of bacon from the door. I ran out and called Stop thief! The prisoner threw it down - I took it up, and two watchmen and I secured him. I am positive he is the person.
   SUSANNAH KING. I was in the shop; a woman said something, I looked out, and saw the prisoner with the bacon on his shoulder - he dropped it at my feet, and the watchman took him.
   JOHN M'ADAMS. I heard the alarm, and saw the prisoner running - I followed and secured him. The prosecutor said he was the person.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Confined Two Months.
 718.  WILLIAM JONES was indicted for stealing, on the 20th of March , a boy's dress, value 20 s., the goods of William Sharpe.
   WILLIAM SHARPE. I am a tailor, and live in Shoreditch. On the evening of the 20th of March, I saw the prisoner snatch at a dress which hung on a nail inside my door. I followed, and caught him about forty yards off - he had not pulled it quite down.
     NOT GUILTY.
 719.  WILLIAM OSBORNE was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of March , a handkerchief, value 3 s., the goods of John Hartwell Bournel Williams.
 JOHN HARTWELL BOURNELL WILLIAMS. On the evening of the 7th of March, I was in  Aylesbury-street, and felt somebody fumbling at my pocket, and on turning round, I saw the prisoner with my handkerchief in his hand, it was safe a few minutes before - he dropped it; I picked it up and secured him.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   JOHN HAYFORD. I took the prisoner in charge.
     GUILTY. Aged 16. Transported for Seven Years.
 720.  THOMAS PHILLIPS was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of March , a handkerchief, value 2 s., the goods of a person unknown, from his person.
   WILLIAM JORDAN. On Sunday evening, the 2d of March, I was in Berwick-street, Soho, at a fire, I saw the prisoner with three or four others very busy at several gentlemen's pockets - he put his hand into one person's pocket and took out this handkerchief, and put it into his jacket pocket - he joined his companions and shewed it them; they went into the crowd again, and soon after they ran off out of the crowd towards a coffee-shop. I seized him at the door, and charged him with robbing a gentleman of his handkerchief; he said he had none about him - I found it in his pocket. He said he bought it of a boy for 6 d.; then fell on his knees and begged forgiveness. I could not find the owner; he resisted a great deal.
     GUILTY. Aged 17. Fined One Shilling and Discharged.
 721.  SUSAN OSWALL was indicted for stealing, on the 7th of April , eight sovereigns, value 8 l., and four half crowns, value 10 s., the monies of John Holmes , from his person. The Prosecutor did not appear.
 NOT GUILTY.
 722.  MARY CONNELLY and JEREMIAH CRAWLEY were indicted for stealing, on the 6th of April , a watch, value 30 s., and a watch key value 6 d. the goods of William Beal , from his person.
   WILLIAM BEAL. I am a ship owner, and live at Lime-house. On the 6th of April, at seven o'clock, I went into the Bell public-house Ratcliffe-highway. The female prisoner was in the tap-room; she drank with me - we were together about an hour. She then asked me to go home with her, and took me to a private piece of ground near the fields; we stopped there about six minutes, and then a man passed me very close, (this was about eight o'clock at night). The man pushed before me, and in an instant I missed my watch from my fob. I instantly charged her with stealing it - she denied it, and said she did not know that I had got one. I said I would not let her go till she gave it me - she asked if I saw a man pass; I said Yes.
 "Well," said she,
 "he has got your watch, and I will take you to his home." She took me to a house in Seven Star-alley, to the male prisoner's mother - I took her to the watch-house. I only know that one of the two must have taken it.
   THOMAS BAXTER. I am a watchman. On Sunday night, the 6th of April, the male prisoner's mother came to me - I went to her house and searched it, but could not find the prisoner. I found the watch in a chest of drawers.
   FRANCIS JACKSON. I am the watch-house keeper; I saw the prisoner Connelly brought in - while I was searching her, she said,
 "I have not got it,  Jerry Crawley has it." I could not find him for three nights.
 (Property produced and sworn to.)
   WILLIAM SUMMERS. I am a head borough, I was informed of the robbery and took Crawley - he said the other prisoner gave him the watch at the top of West's-gardens to take care of; that is about two hundred yards from where the robbery happened.
 CONNELLY'S Defence. I never saw it till it was at the watch-house. I did not leave him.
 CONNELLY -    GUILTY. Aged 18.
 CRAWLEY -    GUILTY. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
