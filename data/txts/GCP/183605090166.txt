GEORGE ARCUER. I am a satin-dresser, and live in Raven-row, Spital
fields. On Monday, the 2nd of May, the prisoners called on me with :
pattern of silk-Bonsor asked me if I could dress figured silk after the
French fashion-I said I could and had dressed thousands of yards-pat-
terns were produced to me-I think Lilley produced some patterns-
think they had been dressed-I asked him if they were not French silks
he said he did not know-I think it was Bonsor produced the pattern of
the silk in question-I observed that it was undressed-Bonsor said "INI
send you a piece how soon can I have it back-can I have it the next day"
I said, “ Perhaps the next evening or the following morning"-after
showing me the patterns they both left together-I think I returned the
patterns which Lilley showed me to him the pattern of this silk I returned
to Bonsor was given to understand a piece would be sent, and it came
shortly afterwards, in three pieces a length runs about sixty to seventy
or one hundred yards—this was forty-three yards in three pieces-on open-
ing the parcel I found it was the pattern Bonser had shown me the sample
of-this yard and a quarter is the piece which Bonsor brought, and there
were two others besides the three came together packed in a brown paper
parcel I took the smallest piece to Eastman and Co. and left it with
Hughes the foreman-I took the other two lengths to the police-office-I
have dressed silk for Bonsor before, but never for Lilley-I should say it
is not in a state fit for sale there is a bar at one end and it is cut
in a jagged sort of manner-it is very unusual to find a piece of silk in
that jagged state- I did not notice the ends till I opened the parcel--the
piece shown to me has a bar at one end-there was nothing extraordinary
in that piece-nothing except the other end being cut in an untradesmat-
like manner.
