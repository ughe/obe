   Q.  Pray what are you now? A.  I am not doing anything now, and have not done anything for about four months—I have been a shopman—I have not been in a retail shop—I have not been a shopman for the last four years—I joined Mr. Hill in Sept., 1839, and I have not been a shopman since, and never in the employ of any person—I left off being a shopman on join ing Mr. Hill—I am now living at No. 13, Wood-street—the house is let to a Mr. Leith—it belongs to a Mr. Melville, a patent thread-manufacturer in Wood-street—I do not lodge there—I have the upper part of the house—the warehouses are let to Mr. Leith—I am a tenant until March—I am not there to take care of the house—I paid 150 l . a year rent—I have now let the house to Mr. Leith, from the 25th of Dec. last, but he occupied it six weeks or two months previously, on condition that I remained in the house until I met with a private residence—Mr. Leith paid no rent until the 25th of Dec.—I carried on business there as a Manchester warehouseman—I never assigned my stock to my father-in-law—I did not cart it away in the night—I dis posed of my stock to Messrs. Sewell and Cross—I sold the whole of it at certain prices, with a discount off—I did not-dispose of the whole by the lump, but by stating certain quantities of goods at cost price, which I dis posed of to them, with a discount off—I had had some of those goods for nearly eighteen months, I should say—the whole of the stock amounted to I should say 800 l .—perhaps 200 l . or 150 l . worth I might not have had more than a month—I will swear that I had not more than 150 l . worth a month before—I did not sell any of the stock to any one besides Sewell and Cross—I sold none in what is called the slaughter-house  —I never sold my stock in that way—I swear that positively, not to anybody—I did not dispose of my stock the last time I compounded with my creditors—I had the manage ment of that stock—my father-in-law did not have it—I swear that—I went on with my regular trading, my father-in-law being security—I disposed of it in the general way of business, to various houses round London.
   Q.  On your solemn oath, were you not in partnership with the prisoner? A.  Never, to my knowledge—I have asked my solicitor, and he distinctly tells me that the agreement then entered into, never was of any effect, and is not binding—I intended to go into partnership with him—I do not know whether it was before or after this debt which I got the deeds to cover—the agreement between the prisoner and myself, as regards the partnership, was this: I had been speaking to my father-in-law that I wanted money; Mr. M'Gooch did not bring the money which he agreed to do, into the establish ment, therefore, Mr. M'Gooch withdrew from it—the prisoner proposed to me that he would bring in 4000 l . to the establishment, and join me, providing I would produce the same—I agreed with the prisoner, on this condition, that if I paid Mr. M'Gooch out of the establishment, he would not enter into any agreement with him, unless he would have it binding to forfeit 1000 l . if he did not fulfil the engagement—I do not say that I have ever had 4000 l .
