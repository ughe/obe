Fifth Session, 1872–73.
ELIZABETH CATO. I am the wife of Thomas Cato, of 105, Newington
Causeway-on the night of the 27th I was in my bedroom, and saw this
property safe—the door leading to the leads was closed as I passed down
no one went up stairs, to my knowledge, till the police arrived—the door
that goes on to the leads leads to the water-closet, and we do not look it
till we go to bed-nobody has a right on those leads but our own family,
Prisoner. I had been drinking all day. There was another party who
has escaped. A man came to the station, and said that he saw another
man. Witness. A boy came to the station, and said that he saw & man in
the passage the leads are 8 feet high.

260. PETER MOEWEN (31), Unlawfully obtaining eight sacks of four
from George Hart, by false pretences; also two other sacks, on 18th January,
from Joseph Smith.
MR. GRIPPITHS conducted the Prosecution, and Mr. Rigton the Defence.
JOSEPH SMITH. I am a baker of 144, Wyndham Road, Camberwell-
on 18th January « man named Woollett called on me and bought two sacks
of flour, in payment for which he handed me this cheque—(This was dated
18th January, 1873, drawn by Peter McEwen on the National Bank, Old
Cavendish Street, West, for 41. 163., payable to Mr. Smith or bearer
believing it to be a good and genuine cheque, I let the man have the flour
-I should not have done so had it not been for the cheque-I afterwards
paid it away to my miller, and it was returned to me, marked "No offeota"
--some time in the next month I saw the prisoner, I don't know the date-
I went to his office, 5, Billiter Street, City, he is a wine-merchant, I think;
it was about three or four days after I received the cheque baek dis-
honoured-I asked him for the money for the cheque, as it was returned to
me-he said he was very sorry, and he would come down in the evening
and pay me; he did not come that evening—he gave me another cheque
two days afterwards ; it was on 30th January-this is it, it is for 41. 164.
-I did not present that-he paid me 21. on it on the Monday before he
was taken-I did not see him again until he was in custody
GBORGS HART. I am a baker at Cold Harbour Lano, Brixton-on 23rd
January I was carrying on business in East Street, Walworth-I advertised
that business for sale, the prisoner came and offered to buy it, he agreed to
give me 901. for it-there was an agreement in writing-Mr. Hagan, the pri-
soner's solicitor has it-he acted for us both-at the time it was written out
the prisoner also agreed to give me something for the flour-he paid me
this 51. cheque as a deposit on the 25th, and I gave him this receipt-he
also gave me this cheque for 171. 108. at the same time, that was for the
four-(The cheques were both on the National Bank, Oxford Street Branch
The agreement was produced)-I let him have the flour, it was in eight sacks
on the following Tuesday, I paid both the cheques to Mr. Barrett, the
miller, and they were returned marked "No effects "-I gave the prisoner
28. change at the time he gave me the obeques,
Cross-examined. It was on Saturday, 25th January, that he gave me
the chequos--they are dated the 27th - I did notice that-I know the bl.
cheque was dated the 27th ; I did not look at the other-I did not know it
was post-dated.
