JOSEPH NORWOOD BROWN, M.R.C.S. I am house physician at Univer sity College Hospital—on April 24th the prosecutrix was brought in; I found her lying on a sofa bleeding from many cuts on her hands, arms, chin, forehead and eyes; there were three clean cuts on her chin, the longest was one and a half or two inches long, not very deep—there was a small cut on the left side of her lip, a cut near her nose, and another on the left eyelid—the main injuries were on her hands and arms—there were five or six cuts on each arm; one was two inches deep, going down to the bone, and on one hand a large cut two and a half inches long—they had been inflicted with a sharp instrument—she was in a state of collapse; she had lost a good deal of blood.
   The prisoner produced a written defence, stating that the prosecutrix had lived with him five or six years as his wife while he was employed, but when he was unemployed she left him; that she pawned his medals, and brought home a sailor from Shadwell, and he found them in bed together, and that when this happened he had the razor in his pocket, as he was taking it to be ground. GUILTY.— Ten Tears' Penal Servitude.
