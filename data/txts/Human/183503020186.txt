Fifth Jury, before Mr. Common Sergeant .
 875. WILLIAM COTTON and ELIZABETH COTTON were in dicted for stealing, on the   20th of October , 1 table-cloth, value 5 s  ., of Elizabeth Thomas . ELIZA STOUT. I am in the employ of Mrs. Elizabeth Thomas, a laundress, at Gloucester-cottage, Camberwell. We work for Mr. Webster, in St. Mi chael-alley. The prisoner was in my mistress's employ—I recollect him going to fetch Mr. Webster's linen—there was a bill sent with them—I missed one table-cloth—that was five or six months since, and other ar ticles have been missed since then—I cannot swear that I washed this table-cloth.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Is Mrs. Thomas here? A.  No—she does not know much about it—I receive the clothes, but Mrs. Thomas has on a few occasions, when I was out of the way, done it—I knew by the list five or six months ago, that this table-cloth was missing—Mr. Webster had the list back—I did not go before the Magistrate till last week—this prisoner used to wheel the linen home sometimes, and sometimes bring it on his head—I did not say this was the table-cloth that was lost five or six weeks ago—I said there was one lost. JOHN GIBBS. I am waiter at Mr. Webster's. There was a table-cloth sent to Mrs. Thomas, about the 20th of October—the prisoner was em ployed to fetch the linen—I remember the list coming back, and one cloth was short. I have not seen the list since—I believe this is the cloth that was sent, but I cannot swear it. NOT GUILTY.
 876. WILLIAM COTTON was again  indicted for stealing, on the   18th of February , 1 pair of sheets, value 20 s  ., the goods of Elizabeth Thomas . LETTY DOUBLE. I am servant to Mr. Taylor. I know the prisoner—on the 18th of February he came to our house for the linen, and I gave him a pair of sheets to take to Mrs. Thomas, who washed for us—he brought back the other articles, but not the sheets.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHAMBERS . Q.  Did you put the sheets into the basket? A.  No—my mistress did in my presence—I saw them on the basket, and gave them to the prisoner—there was only that one pair of sheets, which were rolled up, and there were no other large things—they were tied up tight—they could not have fallen off—the basket was too large. ELIZA STOUT. On the 18th of February the prisoner brought the basket to me, but there were no sheets in it—there was a pair down in the bill.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You told me you did not always receive the thing? A.  No, I do not; but I received these because mistress was ill—I missed the sheets about half an hour afterwards—the basket was by my side that time—there have been blankets and other things lost—the prisoner only brought one basket in that day—I think it was Wednesday—perhaps I had a dozen bundles and baskets to examine that day.
   Prisoner . I always brought the things as I had them.
   JURY to ELIZA STOUT . Q.  Did you tell him this was missing? A.  No—I told my mistress—he brought this basket home on his shoulder, to the best of my belief—he was not accused of this till last Saturday week. NOT GUILTY.
