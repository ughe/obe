WILLIAM GARDINER (police-constable V 279.) On the 6th of April I
was at Wandsworth, and in consequence of some charge by a tradesman I
went to the house of Mr. Shiell, in Putney, Park-lane, inquired for some-
body, and was directed to the stable in consequence of my inquiry-the
stable is about 200 yards or more from the dwelling-house-when I came
there I saw the prisoner-he answered the ring. I was accompanied by
two lads, Speed and Dagnal-on his answering the bell one of them asked
him if the coachman was come home-he said, "I am the coach-
man"-(he is the coachman of that family I now know)-I was standing
a little on one side-I then stepped up to him, and told him I had come to
take him into custody on a charge of stealing a pair of black trowsers from
Mr. Collingbourn-he said, “Yes, I bought a pair of breeches of Mr.
Collingbourn, and I did not pay him for them;" and pulling out his purse,
he said, “You can take the money to him"-I said, "No, that has nothing
to do with the charge I bave come upon" he said, "Very well, then I
will go with you to Wandsworth, and settle with Mr. Collingbourn"-I asked
