to assist, and I heard some one say, "There is the b-y cork-cutter gone
to help the Peelers"-James and Henry Goodright were edging in against
the officer, and Shae was inciting Hare on, and saying, "Go it, Tom-give
it him, Tom."
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. Are you a master or journeyman!
A. A master-my shop is at No. 16, Windmill-lane- I went to see that my
brother was safe-I was sober-I have never been in the police-I never
was a witness but once before, which was at Horsemonger-lane-Payne
took hold of Hare by the braces and endeavoured to get him away-I
believe he said, “Don't go, go along with me.”
Cross-examined by Mr. Robinson. Q. Did you know the Goodrights? A.
Yes-Passfield was standing on the raised foot-path by the side of the road
- there was a number of people there there was not a depth of two peo-
ple between Passfield and the officers-sometimes there was a large space
of twenty yards round the officers, and sometimes not three yards.
Cross-examined by Mr. CHARNOCK. Q. Was Wigzell close by you
when Shae used the words ? A. No, he was at a distance from me-
Chantler was in the same row with him-I do not know whether Kayser
was gone for the other officers- I did not see any female hold of Shae's
arm-there were several females about-I did not see them talking to him
- there were three persons taken beside the prisoners-Jarvis was one, and
two other Goodrights-I thought Jarvis was one, and when I saw his
brother I thought otherwise the prisoner, Mannix's brother, Cornelius,
was taken up, I believe, on my representation - he was discharged-to the
best of my belief Michael Mannix was in the crowd-I am certain of it-I
was standing in Windmill-lane, close to Mr. Wills's floor-cloth factory-I
might be ten or twelve yards from Shae—the riot lasted from half-past
seven till half-past eight o'clock-it was about a quarter past eight o'clock
when Shae said what he did -I had dined at home I had not a cup extra
-I was near falling into a ditch in supporting my brother who was intox.
icated.
