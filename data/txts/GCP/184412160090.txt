at St. Marylebone, 1 watch, value 77. 108. ; 1 watch-chain, 78. ; 1 cret
41. 168. ; I hat, 18s.; 1 cap, 6s. ; 5 waistcoats, 31. 178.; 3 neckerchiels
17.8s.; 1 jacket, 11. 8s.; 2 pairs of pantaloons, 68.; 1 purse, 68.; 14 sheets
21. 16s; 14 handkerchiefs, 7s.; 3 pairs of stockings, 1s. ; 3 boots, 16.
4 shirt-fronts, 53. ; 4 curtains, 12s.; 2 shirts, 68.; 5 towels, 4s.; Isole
eover, 28.; and I pillow-case, 1s. ; the goods of Louis Jean Auguste Re-
lange, his master, in his dwelling-house.
Louis JEAN AUGUSTE BELANGE (through an interpreter.) I am a cabi
net-maker and upholsterer, and live in Charles-street, Middlesex Hospital;
I occupy a shop at the bottom of the house, and have a room to live in at the
top; the prisoner was in my employ as a journeyman. About eight o'clock
on Friday erening last, I told him to fetch some beer and some milk-
were both in the shop at the time-- he went out with two jugs, and did
not return-about a quarter of an hour afterwards I missed from my waist-
coat pocket the key of my room up stairs- I went up and found the door
open, and the key in it--on going into the room, I found my trunk broken
open--there were three locks broken- I went down, called a friend, te
turned with him, afterwards had a policeman in, and then missed my gold
watch from the top of the trunk, and a mosaic chain attached to it-1
missed all the articles stated from the trunk-I had seen the watch sale al
nine that morning, and my trunk was then locked-I afterwards went
with a friend to Mr. Brown, in the City, and gave information of the
robbery.
John Dixon (City police-constable, No. 619.) On Saturday morning,
about a quarter-past ten o'clock, the prisoner was given into my custody
by Mr. Brown, in Gravel-lane-he said something in French, which I did
not understand-Mr. Brown said to me that the watch and chain was on his
neck-he was then about to make off-Mr. Brown spoke to him in French
-I saw the guard on his neck-it looked like gold--on our way to the
station he managed to take the guard from his neck-I searched him at the
station, and found on him a red silk purse containing 128. 5d., a duplicate
for four waistcoats, one jacket, a scarf, and a neckerchief-not finding the
watch on him, I immediately returned to see whether he had thrown it
away-he effected his escape from me at the station, and ran down Bishops
gate-street and Liverpool-street-1 pursued him-he ran into a place where
there is an infant-school, and into an out-place, which was dark- I lost
sight of him for a moment, and then saw him in a room, lying on his back
- I took him again--this chain, which was found with the watch by the
sergeant, is exactly like that which I had seen round his neck.
