GEORGE HAVILL. I keep the Old King's Head, Green-bank, in the parish
of St. Olave's. On the night of the 24th of Oct. the prisoner came in about
eight o'clock, went into the tap-room, and called for a pint of ale-I afterwards
saw some women come to him-1 beard the mother-in-law call him a Green-
acre and a murderer-I saw the wife hit him right and left as hard as she
could, about the face-the child was on his lap at that time I turned the
women out-the mother-in-law called him a Greenacre, & murderer, a man
eater, a woman-eater, and another expression almost too bad to mention-every
thing she could say against him she did-after I had put the women out, the
prisoner remained in the tap-room with the child I did not see the women
again till after the child was killed-abont a quarter of an hour after they were
gone I said to the prisoner, “You had better leave my place"--ho said," Master,
I won't hurt you nor any one else," and went out very quietly indeed with the
child for about five minutes, then came back very quietly, went into the tap
room, remained about five or ten minutes, and the people would not let him go
out-he applied to me--1 was called, and said, " It is all right, you had better
let him go out"-he came out of the tap-room in the front of the bar with
the child in his arms-I was inside the bar -1 had occasion to leave the front
of the bar for not a minute, and went behind a curtain which is across the bar
and when I had been there about a minute, I heard a woman seream out that
the child was murdered I went in front of the bar, and the prisoner said, I
told you I should do it; I am always as good as my word; I shall be hung at
the Old Bailey: I hope every one will come and see me hung--he went into
the tap-room, laid himself down on the bench, and began to cry I went for
a policeman-I remained at the tap-room door till the policeman came, and
gave him into custody--the counter in front of my bar is a kind of shelf,
about eighteen inches wide, made of pewter-I considered him sober when
he came into my house-I think I had seen him two days before, he and his
wife came together for a pint of ale--I have known him five or six years.
Cross-examined. Q. When did you first repeat the expression which you
say the prisoner used about being hung at the Old Bailey, &o. . I mer
tioned it at Union-hall-I think you will find it in my deposition-I will
swear I recollected it when I was at Union-ball.
Q. Did not be say, “I have done it, I have done what I said I would,
will go in and sit down, and I will give myself up?" 4. No, not those
words exactly I am positive he said what I have stated I will swear it
was sober-I should say a dozen people heard him say this, customers
Donovan, Spelling, and Stead are not regular customers.
George TOMPKINS (police-constable M 166.) I went to Mr. Havill's on
the 24th of Oct., about half-past nine o'еlock at night--I was not sent for
It is on my beat-I went in, seeing a mob round the door---the prisoner was
sitting down, leaning on the table-he was given into my custody by Havill
took him to the station-in going along he said to me, I shall go to the
Old Bailey for what I have done" he was sober, but appeared very much
agitated—I took him part of the way, but he got so very violent, I was
obliged to call the assistance of another constable -I had hold of him.
Cross-examined. Q. He talked extremely loud, did not he? 4. I do
not know about talking loud-be plunged himself about, and kicked about,
and flung his arms about he did not kick me, nor tear his hair. W
