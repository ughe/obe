EBER WATKINSON (748 K). I am stationed just outside Manor Park
station-I was called inside the railway station by the officials, and from
information received I went on to the down platform- saw the prisoner
and watched him until a down train ran into the station- attempted
to leave and I stopped him and asked him to come to the booking office-
a lady was there, and I said, 44 This lady charges you with stealing her
purse"-he said, 64 You have made a mistake, lady, I bought a box of
chocolate, and I will show you where I threw the box ; I will give you
28. not to lock me up, and I will show you where the purse is "I went
with him into the closet of the ladies' waiting-room and found the purse
in the bottom of the pan-I took it back to the lady, and it only
contained a half return ticket; the money was not there took him
back to the lady, and he said, 44 Don't lock me up, for the sake of my old
mother."
Prisoner's Defence. On December 23rd I was sent by my master to
Manor Park. I got out at the station before, by mistake, and had not got
time to get back to my carriage again as I am a cripple, 80 I got into a
second-class carriage and on the seat I saw the purse which I picked up.
I went to the box and got some chocolate. The lady charged me with
stealing her purse. I said, I have not, I was not in the carriage." If
she had said she had lost the purse I would have given it to her, but she
said I had stolen it.

KENT CASES.
183. HERBERT ROBERT FAUTLEY (36), Feloniously marrying
Marie Louise Dupertius, his wife being then alive.
Mr. GRAZEBROOK Prosecuted, and Mr. PURCELL Defended.
EMMA ELIzA BRIGHT. I am the wife of Thomas Bright of 63, Coleman
Road, Camberwell-Sarah Elizabeth Fautley, formerly Lee, is my sister--]
was present when she was married to the prisoner on February 2nd, 1880-
they separated, she had misbehaved herself before that, not before she
married him, but after she left him-she went off with somebody else-she
had always been a trouble to him-they lived together ten yearsI did
not know of their coming together again.
Cross-eamined. I lost sight of her-she has been a trouble to her
family, and a man named Harrison was mixed up with it-she was not in
good health when she left her husband, she had epileptic fits-it may
have been the beginning of the year that she left him, after that my sisters
heard she was dead, and I heard the same and believed it-I never saw
her again until I saw her outside the police-court-I never saw her
between 1890 and 1897.
Re-examined. I did not see the prisoner after his wife left him-I
never enquired of him whether she was dead-he was a soldier in 1880,
but he is a painter by trade.
