CHRISTOPHER THOMAS GREAVES. I am a pawnbroker's assistant. This pencil-case was pawned at our house for 2 s . on the 11th of February, in the name of John Wilson—I cannot say who by.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You were at the Police-office? A.  Yes—I have no recollection of the prisoner saying he was the person that pawned it—I heard him say he had bought it of Newsome. THOMAS TOWNSEND( police-constable M  223.) I took the prisoner into custody on a charge of stealing a coat and umbrella—that charge was dismissed—he was charged again at six o'clock next morning, on suspi cion that it might lead to some other missing property, which had been pledged—the property was not named at that time—no property was named in the second charge—he had a quantity of duplicates in his pos session, among which was one for a pencil-case, pledged for 2 s .—that was not named at the time I took him.
   Cross-examined. Q.  How long have you been a policeman? A.  Eighteen months—the first charge was the coat and umbrella—I am sure the umbrella was named in the first charge—I cannot be mistaken in that—he was set at liberty from that by the inspector—Mr. Newsome gave him in charge the second time, at six o'clock next morning, on suspicion of stealing some more property, not named—he was given in charge on suspicion of felony—that was what Mr. Newsome said—I did not ask what felony—he said he would tell the inspector when he got to the station—I took him to the station—I did not then know what he was charged with—I am positive the umbrella was named in the charge on Thursday night—it was not from the addition of the umbrella to the coat, that he was taken on Friday morning—the inspector is not here—he was only the acting in spector on duty—it was Sergeant Logan—he read over the duplicates in the station on Thursday night—I looked at them, and saw them in his hand—I did not hear him read them out, not to take any notice—I counted them over, and there was thirteen, after the inspector gave them into my hand—I did not read them—he read them out loud—he read them to satisfy himself—he read them over to Mr. Newsome, I believe, and among them was the coat and umbrella—I believe he read them over to Mr. New some that he might attend, and claim anything named in them, which belonged to him—I did not pay particular attention to them—I cannot say that Mr. Newsome heard every one more than I did—Mr. Newsome and I were standing outside, and the inspector was inside—I cannot say that he read them to Mr. Newsome.
