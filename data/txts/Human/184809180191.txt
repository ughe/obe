 2232. THOMAS JONES , stealing 1 handkerchief, value 2 s  . 6 d  .; the goods of John Barton , from his person. JOHN BARTON. I live in Westminster-road, and am an auctioneer. On 8th Sept. I was in Oakley-street, Westminster-road—I felt an attack on my pocket—I turned and saw a person turn up a street, who I have no doubt was the prisoner, by his brown coat which he wore—I went into the main street—there was a crowd with some music—I stopped for a moment, and found a second attack on my pocket—I turned and saw the prisoner dose to me—he went away, and I pursued him—he was stopped, and I went up—I put my band into his pocket, and found a handkerchief—he said, "That is not your handkerchief," and I think he said, "That is cotton"—mine was a silk handkerchief. WILLIAM COCKERELL( policeman, L  108). I heard a cry of "Stop thief!"—I saw the prisoner stopped opposite Allen-street—I went up, and Mr. Barton accused him of stealing his handkerchief—the prisoner said, "I have not got it; yours was a silk one"—he took a cotton handkerchief out of his pocket, and said, "That is not yours; that is a cotton one." JOHN BARTON re-examined.  I saw nobody with the prisoner—there might have been, as there was a crowd of people—he was close behind me, and nobody else near me.
   Prisoner.  He said, "That lad stole my silk handkerchief;" that was why I said mine was cotton. Witness.  I did not say anything about a silk hand kerchief. NOT GUILTY.
   Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
