In regard to the question put to me at the Inquiry as to Calcutt having paid me £124 for wine, spirits, &c., in the course of 12 months, I withdraw my answer to that, because the next day we went through the accounts and we found it worked out at £30 a year. Whatever the amount was it was in addition to anything he bought and paid for in the house. I used to give turkeys to different friends at Christmas time. I have a lot of relations. I suppose I sent away 15 or 16 to them. Out of 40 turkeys Calcutt bought about 13 and the balance I sold. For the last two or three years I have not bothered with them at all. I remember Grimes coming on the Board. I did not advise Calcutt to split his customers between me and Grimes. I did not know that Grimes was supplying Calcutt with turkeys. I heard some one say so in evidence, and I said he could not have done because I had supplied him that year with 20. That is what I said, but the biggest number he ever bad from me was 13. I only got that information from the Inquiry. I think I explained last night that I told Calcutt, to whom I was giving these turkeys, that I was going to send one to my private house and one to the "Eagle" at Snaresbrook, and Calcutt said "I am going that way—to Leytonstone. If you do not mind I will take them." So I (said "With pleasure." So he took them. I knew that Calcutt had a piece of land at Herne Bay. I do not know whether there were three or four plots. When Calcutt first spoke to me about building the houses at Herne Bay I mentioned his own plots to him. He said he did not care to build the houses there for himself, but he would ultimately build for someone else on those plots. He did not like the position. He chose the plots not built on. I was going to build him one house and he chose the corner plot in the Blackburn' Road. I took him down there after he had said he would not build on his own plots, and I showed him what I had got and he selected this corner plot. I had got a piece of thirty-seven plots. I had a farmhouse there. The place where the houses are built might be a mile, at the outside, from the sea. There was not a single house there other than the ones I built for the contrac  tors. There are many houses round about there. The whole lot of ground was sold in plots. I bought one piece of twenty-seven plots. There had been no other houses built in that road. I never tried to tell any of it. The land is still vacant. The only plots sold were the ones to Calcutt, the one to Cordy, and one to Lloyd. Cordy was a tradesman—not a contrac tor to the Mile End Board. Lloyd is a contractor for milk. I do not remember admitting at the Inquiry that Lloyd from the year 1902 onward had had £800 more than would have been paid to the lowest tenderer. I think he has been the milk contractor all the years that I have been on the Board of Guardians. I do not know that in each of those years other people had tendered at lower sums. I know Lloyd always used to get the contract year after year. We only used to go by the figures; it would be 10d. or 10 1/2 d. per gallon. We should not know how much would be con sumed. I do not think different people tendered at lower rates. I have not read the report of the Inspector after the Inquiry that I recollect. I did not know that there had been tenders every year from 1902 without a break to 1908 of £120 to £150 lower than Lloyd. We used not to get it in that way but at per gallon. It might make a farthing difference, or something like that.
