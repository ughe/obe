I did not speak to you—I did not say you had kicked your mother—I did not know you had done so—I am quite sure I did not speak to you or interfere. CHARLES SOMERVILLE. I am a porter, in the Borough-market, and live in Mint-street. On Sunday evening, the 25th of July, about half-past eight o'clock, I was in Mint-street, right opposite out door, and saw the prisoner assault the prosecutrix—she fell into my arms—the prisoner was leading his mother in his arms along the road, about five yards from me, and the children sat up a hooting, seeing her drunk—the prosecutrix was coming along with an empty jug in her hand—the prisoner was coming one way, and she the other—the prisoner stood still, and she looked him in the face, but never said a word—in about a minute, he said, "If you don't get out of the way, you young b----, I will kick your guts  out of you," and he no sooner said that than he dropped his mother on the pavement; ran towards the girl directly, and kicked her at the same moment—I went to assist her—she was about five yards from me—I caught her in my arms, if I had not, she would have dropped on the kerb-stone, and cut her head open—she was in a fainting state—I got a man to assist me, and took her home.
   COURT. Q.  Did you say there were other children there? A.  Yes, several—they were hooting at him before the girl came up—they were laughing and hallooing at seeing a drunken woman—she was so drunk that it was a sight to see her—she could not stand—the children were not all together—this girl was quite by herself, with the jug in her hand—she was on this side, against a shop, more in the gutter—the other children were more across the road, making a noise—she did not say a word, or do anything, that I observed—I should have seen or heard her if she had—she was about five yards from me, and four yards from the prisoner—when he had kicked her, he ran away directly, and left his mother to herself.
   Prisoner.  My mother was solid and sober; it was me that was tipsy. Witness.  His mother was quite drunk—he had had a little drop, but was perfectly conscious of what he did—he ran away directly—it happened right opposite my door—I did not see him come from the public-house, or where he came from—it was not done opposite the public-house, but opposite the chandler's shop, on the left-hand side of the public-house, three or four doors from it.
   Prisoner.  If you stood there, you must have heard the words the girl made use of; but you were not there, you were at your door, and I was at the public-house, and there are five or six doors between. Witness.  No, only three—it was right facing my door—I am quite sure she did not speak a word to you. CHRISTIANA EDENEY. I am wife of Edward Edeney, of Mint-street.
