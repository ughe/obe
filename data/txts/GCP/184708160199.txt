Humphreys. Q. Is it not a common brothel? 4. No, it is a house of
Bocommodation, but we do not let in children, as you wish to insinuate.
Court. Q. Did you ever see Pheasant at your house? A. No I do
not know him, nor can I say what means he can have of knowing how our
house is conducted.
Pheasant. Q. The last time I was at your place, was I not robbed there?
A. I never saw your face.
Thomas Twig. I am a brush-maker, and keep a coffee-house, at No. 1,
Lower Serle's-place, Temple-bar. On the 11th of June last, I was at West-
minster-ball, and saw Humphreys there-I understood that he was going to
get some fresh bills of indictment-he asked me if I was coming to settle with
him about the indictments, that if he had 10l. from me, and 51. each from
Wilson and Lane, he would squash the indictment, and if I did not come into
those terms, he would ruin me- I told him I would not give him anything,
for I did not keep a house in the parish-I did not keep or have a house in the
parish at that time, nor had I done so for these four years-1 keep no house
jointly with Wilson and Lane, and never did, or ever partook in the profits
of any house in which they were interested.
Humphreys. Q. How long is it since you were indicted by the parish of
St. Martin's for No. 23, Rose-street, where you now live, and where you
slept last night? 4. Four years ago-my wife does not sit and take the
money there-- it is not a common brothel.
John WORMINGTON. I know Humphreys, and bave had conversations with
bim on this subject-he said there was a conspiracy, or an indictment, or some-
thing, against bim-it was Pheasant that told me there was an indictment
against these parties in Covent-garden-he said he was in trouble about an
indictment against him, that he could scarcely show his face anywhere for fear
of being taken up, and he said "Humphreys is in this trouble at the present
time, he is confined at the Sessions-house by Serjeant Adams"- I said, "You
are a stranger to me, I am let into something which I am not aware of, can
you give me any explanation about this affair ?"-he said, “Well, I don't
know, it is a very strange thing altogether; will you go into the Sessions-
house, and see what is the reason that Humphreys is not come out?"- that
had reference to the indictment at the Session about a week after that, I saw
him by the Victoria, he asked me if I had seen Humphreys-I said I had not
-- he said "He bas called on me"-I said, “Have you got paid for anything
you have done"-he said, “No, I have not"-I said, "I think he has led me
and you into a shocking trouble" -- he said, if I could arrange things in this
matter, there was a party at Covent-garden who was indicted (he did not
mention any name), and if I could go to that party, and get them to meet him,
very likely he could remunerate me something - I did not do so---before I
went before the Grand Jury at the Westminster Sessions, I saw Humphreys
in the street, near the York Hotel, in the Waterloo-road--that was the first
time I ever saw him--we came into communication, and he asked me some
questions-he said, “Well, you know Davis ?" I said, "I have known the
man as a neighbour for years"-after a little conversation he asked me if I
knew Shepherd-street-I said, "Shepherd-street, where ?"-he said, "Oxford-
street" - I said, " I have known it for forty years, I lived near there"-he said,
"Well, there is a chance of getting some money in Shepherd-street"-I said,
"In what way?"-he said, “There is a man there who has offended me very
much, named Wooldridge, and I mean to indict him"- said, "I would
