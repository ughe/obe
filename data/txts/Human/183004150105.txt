 892.  HENRY JEFFERIES was indicted for breaking and entering the dwelling house of Henry Watson , on the 8th of March , and stealing, 6 caps, value 16s.; 2 frocks, value 1s., and 1 petticoat, value 3d., his property.
   BENJAMIN WATSON. I live in Playhouse-yard, Whitecross-street; I rent the shop and parlour of Mr. Fisher, who lives in the house. On the 8th of March I went out about ten o'clock in the evening, and returned about ten minutes after ten; my little girl, who I had left, at home, brought me the key - as I went home I saw the prisoner come out of the house with a bundle under his arm; I took hold of him, and asked what he had got there - he made no answer; I called an officer, gave him in charge, and found the articles stated in the indictment upon him; I had seen them safe that evening - I found my back parlour shutters had been opened and the sash thrown up.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Have you made inquiries about him? A. A gentleman called and stated that he bore a good character; he appeared in great distress, and I understand had just returned from the East Indies.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 GUILTY (of stealing.) Aged 19. -    Judgment Respited.

 894.  GEORGE GARVER was indicted for stealing, on the 4th of March , 1 silver mug, value 5l., and 1 cruet-stand, value 2l., the goods of Jane Louisa Shee , in her dwelling-house.
   ANN WALTON. I am a servant to Jane Louisa Shee; who lives in Southampton-row, Russell-square. On the 4th of March, about half-past nine o'clock in the morning, I went out, and left the street door ajar - I returned in less than five minutes, and saw the prisoner open the street door wider, and run out with the silver pint mug and cruet-stand in his hand - I knew them to be my mistress', and took them from him; I had left them on the sideboard in the front parlour - I do not know whether the parlour door was shut; he struggled, and struck me before I could get them from him - when I had got them I let go of him; he got away, but recollecting there was more plate where this was, I called Stop thief! I went in, shut the door, and did not see him taken - he was brought back by the witness in two or three minutes; I know him again quite well.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Did you know him before? A. No.
   STEPHEN FREWER. I am a harness-maker, and live in Marchmont-place, Little Coram-street. I heard Walton scream, and saw her struggling with the prisoner on the step - I saw him get from her, and followed him: I lost sight of him for a moment only, and saw him stopped by a man - I took him back to the house, and am sure he is the man.
 Cross-examined. Q. He had turned the corner? A. Yes.
   JANE LOUISA SHEE. I am a widow. This is my property; I valued them together at 5l. at Hatton-garden.
 The prisoner received a good character.
 GUILTY (of stealing to the value of 99s. only.) Aged. 13.
 Recommended to Mercy. - Judgment Respited.
 Before Mr. Justice Park.
