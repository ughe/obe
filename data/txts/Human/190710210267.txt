Bleach naturally thought it right to ask me whether I had any objection to the name going up, and I said "No." I did not in any way suggest that the name of "Herbert Smith" should be put up in Sun Street; it did not origi  nate from me. This was following on the interview with Mrs. Smith. The reason why assented to the name of "Herbert Smith" going up was that, although the business was shut down as from June 30, a good many accounts had to be cleared up—it took six months or more to clear it up. There were bargains open which had to be shut, and accounts had to be finished with. The exhibits in the letter-book which has bean exhibited show that only two letters were ever written from Sun Street. Dealing with the Joint Stock Bank account from the dates that have been given, out of a total of £13,039 paid in, according to Mr. Cash's figures, a sum of £11,519 was paid out to clearing bankers, and over the counter only £1,506 15a. 0d. Out of that amount £150 came to my account by way of notes. This is Herbert Smith's "No. 2" account. I have looked through Herbert Smith's account, and I find that £4,565 was said into his account in notes, and £199 0s. 11d. paid in in coin. Out of a total of £4,764 paid in in notes and cash, I provided quite £4,000. I was financing Harris, if necessary. I had to occasionally, because he had not got enough money to pay his accounts. Harris was carrying on business in exactly the same way as Smith did—he was protecting himself. He had an account for that purpose in the same way. For the pur pose of assisting Harris I let him take some of my Stock Exchange accounts with one broker. Referring to Mr. Cash's figures, I, find payments out to stockbrokers between October 5, 1903, and June, 1904, totaling to £5,879 17s. 10d. After Harris had closed these transactions of Herbert Smith's I never did any business on my own in the name of Herbert Smith. I never had any direct association or responsibility with the firm of Herbert Smith, and never controlled it it is not true to suggest, as has been suggested by the detective in his information, that I was practically "Herbert Smith." In 1904 a bankruptcy issue was directed to be tried as to whether I was "Herbert Smith." That came on before Mr. Registrar Gifford, and lasted about a fortnight. I was represented by counsel and examined and cross-examined for days. The result of that was that Mr. Re gistrar Gifford delivered a considered judgment in my favour.
