Cross-examined by Mr. CLARKSON. Q. Did you have the prisoner from
Lewis and Allenby's? A. Yes, they gave him a good character he left
me on the Thursday evening-he received his wages on the Saturday fol-
lowing about 101. was then due to him the three guineas was left, as he
said he could be sure to account for it-I paid him 91. odd-I heard,
about two hours after, that Mrs. Stapleton had paid him the 101-I sent a
clerk with the policeman, and he was found near the Elephant and Castle.
HARRIET Hurst. I live in Horseferry-road. I bought a shawl at the
prosecutor's, I am not quite certain on what day it was it came home in
the evening-I saw the person who brought it-I think it was the pri-
soner-I paid him 31. 38.-I did not look at the bill and receipt, but kept
it, and sent it to the person I bought the shawl for.
MARGARET CHETWYNDE STAPLETON. I live in Portman-place. On
the 25th of April I had some shawls to look at, from Mr. Holmes's ware-
house- a young man brought them from there—I cannot recollect him-I
chose one shawl, and paid 101. for it to the best of my belief this is the
bill and receipt which was given me-(looking at it.)
ELIZABETH ALLWRIGHT. I am servant to Mrs. Stapleton. The pri-
soner is the person who brought the shawls, and signed the receipt
WILLIAM OLIVER JAMESON. I am clerk to Mr. Holmes. I recollect
the prisoner coming to me the day after he was discharged (on the Friday)
- he wanted me to make a shawl returned in the book which had been
entered in the day-book, as left in Portland-place on approbation.
ANDREW Vallance (police-sergeant C11.) I took the prisoner. I
said I had a charge against him, respecting some shawls of Mr. Holmes's
-he said he was sorry for it.
Prisoner's Defence. I intended to pay the money-I went to my
brother's to get it, and send it by his servant.
