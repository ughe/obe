311. JANE SMITH (19), Unlawfully uttering counterfeit ooin.
MR. Cooke conducted the Prosecution.
WILLIAM WADSWORTH. I am in the employ of Messrs. Grant, linen.
drapers, in Oxford-street-on the last day of January, the prisoner came
there in the afternoon and bought some binding, which came to about 7d.
-she gave me a five shilling piece - I handed it to Mr. Wallace, the
counting-house clerk, and it was found to be bad the prisoner produced
three balfperice good money.
handed me the five shilling piece
it was found to be counterfeit, and the
five shilling piece.
JAMES OUTING (Policeman, E 75). The prisoner was given into my custody
with this five shilling piece (produced)-she was taken to Marlborough
street, remanded till 5th February, and then discharged-she gave the
name of Emma Burkes.
MARY VISE. I keep a poulterer's shop in Newgate-market on the even
ing of Saturday, 15th February, the prisoner came for six pennyworth of
eggs, at about a quarter past 10 at night-she gave me a half crown I saw
it was bad—I went for a policeman and gave her in custody I had seen
her at my shop before I gave the bad balf-crown to the constable.
JOSEPH MODONALD (City-policeman, 256). The prisoner was given into
custody on the night of 15th February-I received this bad half-crown
(produced) from the last witness the prisoner was searched at the station-
house-I found a penny on her she gave me an address — I could find no
account of her at the place she was not known there.
Prisoner. The address that I gave was right.
COURT. Q. What address did she give ? A. 36, Surrey-street, Blackfriars'
road-I inquired there for the name of Jane Smith, and could not haar
anything of her--they did not seem to know such a person.
WILLIAM WEBSTER. These coins are counterfeit.
GUILTY.-Confined Siz Months.
312. WILLIAM HILL (50), was indicted for a like offence.
HENRY PENEL I keep an eating-bouse in King William-street, City-on
24th February, at half-past 11 at night, the prisoner came there and asked
for a glass of ale, which was three halfpence-he gave me a bad half-crown
-I broke it in two, and told him it was bad-upon that he offered to give
nie three halfpence, but I refused to take it-I gave him in oustody, and
gave the officer the broken half-crown.
ROBERT HAWKRIDGE (City-policeman, 444). The prisoner was given into
my custody on the morning of 24th February-I got this broken half-
crown produced) from the last witness--when the prisoner was searched,
there was found on him 48. 4d. in coppers, and a bad shilling--he said he
had no home in London.
