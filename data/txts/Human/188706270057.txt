FREDERICK ACKHURST( City Policeman  571). About 11 o'clock on 31st May I was called into Cannon Street station—I went to the station master's room, where the prisoner was given into my custody for uttering a gold coin—he handed the station-master this ticket, which was after-given to me—I took him to the police-station and searched him and found on him a watch and chain, a ring, and a red handkerchief in his coat pocket—when charged the prisoner made no reply.
   Cross-examined.  The porter had told him all about it before I saw him—I was outside the station at the bottom of Walbrook—the prisoner was wearing his water and chain in the usual way. JOHN HOWLETT( South Eastern Railway Constable ). On 31st May, at 10.30, I was clipping tickets for the Wye special train—Pool made a communication to me, and in consequence I carefully examined all the tickets passing through my hands—the tickets 0040, 0041, 0042, 0043 did not pass through my hands.
   Cross-examined.  The train went from No. 7 platform, on the right hand side of the bookstall as you go in—Cox, another collector, was with me on that day, he is here—I was first spoken to about 10.30—the barriers were not opened till after I had seen Pool—I examined every ticket that went through including 0015, which I clipped.
