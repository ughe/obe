   By the Prisoner.  When you gave me the account you said something about having marked the items alphabetically, referring to the lists of items on the backs of the papers, and that I should find all the items by referring to the letters—I cannot say whether you wrote the items in pencil at the time—you have had no opportunity of writing them since I have had it, and they were upon it when I received it—when I advanced the first 20 l . you had money of mine amounting to 40 l ., and the 15 l . made about 60 l .—the other sums were a matter of account—they were received by you, to be accounted for to me as a matter of account—the one set of sums were received from clients on account, but this was money received from me—the money was only half mine—you did not ask me if I was perfectly satisfied, and I did not say, "I suppose I must be"—I think I said, "How dare you hand me such an account as this?"—you said before the Magis trate, "If you cannot treat me like a gentleman I cannot manage any further business for you"—you made some remark about calling on Monday or Tuesday, and I told you never to come near my house again. HENRY WILLIAM LOCKYER. I am clerk to Mr. Besley, Mr. Besley was retained for the prosecution in the Queen v. Lytton by May, and I received 5 l . 15 s . Od. fees—that is all I have received from May, I had no other consultations from him.
   Cross-examined.  I have known you some time, and this is not the first brief you have handed me—you paid me the 5 l . 15 s . Od. on the adjourn ment at the Court, and I presume you knew there would bean adjournment and further fees to pay—I hardly think you have paid similar fees before—you have paid larger fees—it was 3 guineas and a consultation—I saw you afterwards with Mr. Hambro outside Mr. Besley's chambers, but do not know whether you saw him—I believe you did, and had a conversation—I have the fees, but the other clerk books the attendances—the fees booked were inclusive of the 5 l . 15 s . Od,—the other consultations were booked to Mr. Fullagar—I believe you had only the consultation on the brief; the others were by Mr. Fullagar and Mr. Hambro—the special consultation you had was subsequent to the adjournment.
   By MR. WILLIAMS . He never paid me more than the one consultation marked on the brief.
   By the COURT . I entered it as "May's matter "as though he were the Solicitor, and afterwards in Mr. Fullagar's name—they were entered in the day-book when I returned to the office as "Mr. W. May paid Mr. Besley's fee "so and so, and then afterwards "Mr. Fullagar." RICHARD NORTH. I am Mr. Metcalfe's clerk—on the 9th April at the Middlesex Sessions, May handed me a brief—I asked him what he was going to mark—he said three guineas—I said, "You mean three and one," and he said, "Mr. Fullagar said I was not to give you more than three"—I said, "Then, being two counsel in the case, we must have consulta tion," and he said "Put down two and one"—he paid me, and I initialled the brief and dated it—I had no other fee at all.
