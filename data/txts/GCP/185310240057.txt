Mr. Payne, as Amicus Curiæ, suggested that as the two counts in the
indictment were antagonistic, the prisoner, Edward Smith, by pleading Guilty
to the second count, had exhausted the whole indictment, and was not and could
not be charged to the Jury on the first count for burglary. MR. COMMON
SERJEANT (after having consulted with the learned Judges) said there was very
great difficulty in the case; not so much in the antagonism betubeen the two
counts, but because the prisoner had never specifically pleaded : he had pleaded
Guilty to the second count, and the first was only got at by implication, as he
had not pleaded Not Guilty to it, he could not be charged the whole case must
therefore be tried over again, or a verdict of Not Guilty must be taken on the
first count. MR. RIBTON elected the latter.
