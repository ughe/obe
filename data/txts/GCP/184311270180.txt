JANE CHRISTIE SWINYARD. I am the wife of Edward Swinyard, a baker,
of New-row, Deptford. On Friday, the 24th of Nov., about half-past eleven
o'clock, I caught a glimpse of some one from my yard- I went into the shop,
and there found Myers on the counter, with his hand in the till-I said,
“What do you do at my till?"-he jumped up off the counter, and said, "I
was not at your till"-I had put four penny pieces in it just before, and they
were gone, and I saw the money in his hand.
Myers. It was the money I went in with. I did not put my hand into
the till.
TIMOTHY DARLEY. I followed the prisoners from Rotherhithe, which is
upwards of a mile from the prosecutor's-I saw them looking in at the pro-
secutor's shop--Myers then went in--I afterwards saw Griffiths leave the
shop-window, and in the meantime Mrs. Swinyard came in and took Myers,
who was in the act of taking the money out of the till-I took Griffiths back
to the shop, and gave him in charge.
JAMES MAHONY (policeman.) I took Myers, and found on him a knife,
a fourpenny-bit, and four penny pieces in his hand-he slipped them into
his pocket-they dropped down through his trowsers, and I picked them up.
DANIEL M'PHERSON (policeman.) I produce a certificate of Myers' for-
mer conviction-read)-be is the person.

HENRY TEMPLE. I am in the employ of Mr. Holmes, at Deptford. On
the 21st of Oct. I heard Ward say to Mr. Holmes, that Mr, Stanley would
be obliged to him to let him have 301. till the following Tuesday, which
he was to take bim when he went for his orders—I delivered him a parcel of
silver, which Mr. Holmes gave me.
SAMUEL HOLMES. I delivered the parcel, containing twenty crowns, 120
half crowns, 150 shillings, eighty sixpences, and thirty groats, to Temple-
Ward was in my service, and was to deliver it to Mr. Stanley.
ABSALOM JOHN STANLEY. Ward did not bring the 301. of silver to me-
I have not received any part of it.
Maria Ann Bax. 1 keep the Star at Canterbury-between six and seven
o'clock in the evening of the 25th of Oct., the prisoners and two men arrived
at my house with a horse and cart-the prisoners asked for beds, and slept
there in the morning they got up, and Rafferty asked if I could give him
gold for silver-he produced 151. in silver, and I gave him fifteen sovereigns
for it.
JAMES Epps (police-constable C 5.) I apprehended the prisoners at Can.
terbury, and charged them with absconding with 301. in silver from Deptford
-when we got to the station, Rafferty said that they were the boys, and they
might as well speak the truth--Ward said, “I am the boy that took the
money"-1 found on Rafferty eight sovereigns, five half crowns, a silver
scent-box, a gold ring, and a knife.
Rafferty's Defence. He met me. I did not know about the money when
it was taken; he asked me to go with him.
Guilty. Aged 17.--Confined Three Months.
