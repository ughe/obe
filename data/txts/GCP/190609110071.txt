ALBERT Yxo, Detetive-Bergeant, New Scotland Yard. On
September 1 I went with Inspector Sexton, Inspector Parsons,
Sergeant Story, and P.C. Gillard to 5, Warren Street. We
entered with keys taken from Robert. On the left-hand side of
the workshop we found a large bench fixed with a number of
shelves on the side and on the wall. Behind the bench I found
six zino patterns for moulds, showing signs of recent use,
box containing 36 two-frano pieces, a small cardboard box con-
taining 36 two-frano pieces, and 56 five-frano pieces, false Belgian
money. Some were wrapped in packets of five with paper be-
tween the coins; and 40 five-frano pieces, false French money.
On the top of the bench a tin mustard box, containing 70 coun-
terfeit shillings, and a small iron saucepan. On the bench were
17 counterfeit florins, 12 bottles containing various fluids and
acids used for coining, a large glasg chemical gauge, a plating
rack used for putting the coins into the plating bath, a ladle
for pouring metal into moulds with metal inside, and a quantity
of tin, copper, and insulating wire, two wooden troughs used for
pouring the plaster of Paris in and making the mould shape,
and & wooden hand clamp used for holding the mould shape,
a large three-burner paraffin stove full of oil, and having the
appearance of having been recently used with soot on it. Under
the window was another bench with two vices fixed on it, five
files, much used, for opening the moulds, three pairs of pliers,
a small hand vice, two metal spoons, with plaster in them, and
an enamelled bowl with plaster on it, three ordinary chisels, a
screw hammer, & 'scraper, three tin saucepans, an oil stove,
three scouring pans for mixing emery in to scour the coins.
