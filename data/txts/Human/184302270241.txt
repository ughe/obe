There were two doors to this house, a side door, and a door to the street; no other—I was guarding the front door and a window very near it, that no person might make their escape—Kay was standing at the side door—we were so stationed round the house that no one could escape—the windows were all fast down with the catch and not dis turbed—I examined every part of the house, and am sure no one could have escaped without our notice.

 1090. MARY ANN LANDERS was indicted for knowingly uttering a counterfeit shilling, having been previously convicted of uttering counterfeit coin.
   MESSRS. DOANE and SCRIVEN conducted the Prosecution. CALEB EDWARD POWELL. I am solicitor to the Mint. I produced a copy of the record of the conviction of Mary Ann Landers on the 9th of May, 1842, in this Court—I have examined it with the original in Mr. Clark's office—it is a true copy—( read. ) JOHN CHAPMAN WOREDRAPER( police-constable F  122.) I was present at the trial of Mary Ann Landers on the 9th of May, 1842—it was the prisoner, I am quite confident. ELLIZABETH BURROWS. I live at No. 20, Upper Stamford-street. On the 24th of Feb., in the forenoon, I saw the prisoner looking at the window, holding up her hare-skins—I took a hare-skin to her—she was to give 2 d . for it, and 4 d . for kitchen stuff—she tendered me a shilling—I told her I had no change—she asked if I had anything else to make up the shilling—I produced four wine bottles which made up the amount, and she went away—I put the shilling into my pocket—I had no money of any kind in my pocket—a few minutes afterwards I gave it to the constable, John Meek.
   COURT . Q.  Were the whole of the things worth 1 s .? A.  Yes—she would not let me have the shilling till I gave her the bottles to make it up—I am certain she is the woman—I recollect her countenance so well—I had never seen her before—she was with me not quite ten minutes.
