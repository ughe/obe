EDWARD JAMES New (Re-examined). On February 1st the prisoner
was brought before the Magistrate, charged, on his own confession, with

MR. KAPADIA submitted that there was not sufficient publication in law,
avid, secondly, that the alleged libel was an absolutely privileged communi-
cation, as it was a statement made in the course of a judicial proceeding to
a judicial officer, who wrote it doron, and requested the prisoner to sign it.
(Johnson v. Evans, Espinasse's Reports; Ran v. Lumby, Hutton's Reports.)
Mr. Avory contended that a statement made to an inspector at the station
was not made in the course of a judicial proceeding, as no judicial pro-
cording was then commenced, and that a man could not create a privilege
by inv nting a fictitious proceeding. With regard to publication, he argued
that if this were not a real charge, and the prisoner's object in going to the
station was to get the libel published through the inspector as his agent, there
soas publication in law. The Common SERJEANT held that he should direct
the Jury that, if the prisoner believed the charge to be true, they must acquit,
but that if they believed it was altogether an invention, then they must convict
the prisoner.
Guilty.—Three Months' Imprisonment.
