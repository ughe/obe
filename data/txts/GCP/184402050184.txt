750. WILLIAM ALDERSON was indicted for stealing 7lbs. weight of
bacon, value 3s. 9d., the goods of Daniel Arthur Sykes.
DANIEL ARTHUR Sykes. I live in New-end, Hampstead-lane. On the
7th of Feb. I saw the prisoner in my shop-he took a piece of bacon off the
counter, and threw it to a person at the door-I came into the shop-he asked
for a pennyworth of tobacco-asked what he had done with the bacon
- he denied taking it.
Guilty. Aged 18.-Confined Three Months.
751. SAMUEL MULLETT was indicted for stealing 6 jugs, value 38.;
14 cups, 3s. ; 17 saucers, 3s. ; 6 cruets and tops, 108. ; 4 chimney ornaments,
158.; 5 basins, 1s. ; 2 milk-pots, ls. 6d. ; 1 tea-pot, 2s.; 6 plates, 3s.;
1 basket 18.; and 2 twopenny pieces; the property of Samuel Smith
MR. CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution.
SAMUEL SMITH. I am an omnibus proprietor, and lodge at the King's
Arms, Folham ; I formerly kept it. I first knew the prisoner as a waterman
- I know the members of the Association at Fulham, and recommended him
to them as a conductor-on the 10th of Nov. he was at my house-Mr. Webb
came down from the meeting-a quarrel took place between the prisoner and
Webb-he used very bad language, and I said it did not become bim, he was
getting a living under us--my wife was in the bar; she interfered in the
quarrel; I told her it was no business whatever of hers, gave her a brush
with my band, and said, “Go along"-a summons was taken out against
me on Monday, and I appeared at the office-my wife did not appear on the
Saturday I received information ; on Sunday morning I went to my drawers,
and found they had been stripped of their contents-I made my wife put on
her things, and leave the house-she went across to the prisoner's father's
I went to my drawers, and found the whole of their contents gone--in the
room where my wife was I found a large portmanteau belonging to the pri-
soner, nailed and corded, in readiness to be removed - I did not allow them
to be moved away-my wife never returned home-on the Tuesday I went
with the constable to Mr. Gray's, in Drummond-street, at five in the mord-
ing, and in the back room, first floor, we found the prisoner and my wife in
bed together-I gave them into custody, and they were taken to the station
in Albany-street-they were discharged at the station-I afterwards followed
them to Ireland, and gave them into custody- I went to where they lived in
Ireland, and found six jugs, fourteen cups, seventeen saucers, six cruets and
tops, four chimney ornaments, and other things, in a basket, and two
twopenny-pieces in the prisoner's box-they are all my property, and the
prisoner had seen them used at my house.
