By the JURY . None of my children hawk in the street; one has sold watercresses, but not now, they are all over—he would not earn 1 s . a week he only goes out on Sundays—the dispensary is a quarter of an hour's walk from our place—Dr. Nicholson would charge 2 s . for a bottle of medicine—he had always attended the child—my husband was dis charge from his place not through drink, they were slack; he has gone back to the same place. JOHN WRIGHT. Up to 25th July I had been out of work—my late employer started me again on Monday, 27th—I was to have a guines a week, and 1 s . extra if I worked on Sunday—I had been at work every day that week—on Saturday morning, 1st August, I left home for work at Wimbledon at 4 o'clock—I knew the child was ill, and told my wife to go to the relieving officer for a doctor—I got home between 7 and 8 o'clock—the child was much better—it became worse at midnight, and it died at 3 o'clock on 8unday morning—I brought home my wages, a guines; we were in debt—I had to pay my rent and keep my wife and family for the next week.
   Cross-examined.  Between 12 and 2 o'clock the child was very ill with diarrhoea; it stopped again—I had not the 25 s . then, nor yet 1 s ., it was all laid out except 1 s . or 2 s .—I gave it all to my wife and she laid it out—I did not go for a doctor because I did not think there was any danger—the child was teething—I was discharged in consequence of slackness of work, nothing else; I was taken on again and hare been there ever since. RICHARD LAMBERT LAMPREY. I am a registered medical practitioner it Wandsworth—in August I was acting as substitute for Dr. Nicholson, the parish medical officer—if an order had been given by the relieving officer it would have been my duty to attend to this child—I had attended it in September, 1884, for a short time—on 2nd August I saw its dead body—I made a post-mortem examination—there was no mark of violence, and no particular disease—the organs were healthy, except that they were anaemic—the cause of death was exhaustion from diarrhoea—a child of that age suffering from diarrhoea ought to be medically attended at once—if this child had been seen during Saturday it might have had a fair chance of living
   Cross-examined.  If medical aid had been obtained at midnight there is a probability that it might hare lived—I cannot say with confidence that it would—diarrhoea is amenable to treatment as a rule—I am not sure whether in an urgent case I should be entitled under the Poor Law orders to give relief—I have told them to go and get an order—I can't say that I have given relief first.
   MR. FULTON submitted that there was no case to go to the Jury; the only offence, if any, which the prisoner had committed was an error of judgment; that the evidence left it in doubt what the actual duty of the prisoner was, or whether in such a case as this he was not allowed a certain amount of dis  cretion. MR. POLAND referred to the case of Clark v. Jocelyn, 27th New Series, p. 762, and contended that there was a sufficient evidence of neglect by the prisoner for the Jury to act upon. MR. JUSTICE HAWKINS left the case to the Jury. NOT GUILTY. There was another Indictment against the prisoner for the manslaughter of the said child, upon which no evidence was offered. NOT GUILTY.
