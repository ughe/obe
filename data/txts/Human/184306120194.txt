 1959. MURTY DUGGAN was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of June , 2 ounces of tobacco, value 8 d  .; 2 shillings; 3 sixpences; and 1 groat; the property of George Wellman . GEORGE WELLMAN. I keep an oil-shop in Coram-street, Bloomsbury. On the 7th of June, about four o'clock in the afternoon, I went over the way for three or four minutes—a boy called me back, and I saw the prisoner coming out of my shop—I asked him what he wanted—he said, "Half an ounce of tobacco"—I served him, and he paid for it—as he was going out I looked in the till, and found all the silver was gone but a sixpence—I had had 4 s . 4 d . in it before I left—I went and caught him just as he was going into his own house, which is two doors from mine—he walked back with me to the shop—he asked me how much silver he had taken from the till—I told him—he produced this tobacco first, and then the silver, and said that was all he had of mine—he hoped I should do nothing with him—I gave him into custody.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  You had let him go at first? A.  Yes; I took the money and tobacco from him, and then took him in the room—I had seen him go past—he gave me two shillings, three sixpences, and a fourpenny-piece, and the tobacco—that was all that I am aware that I had lost. JAMES COBBINS( police-constable E  79.) I took the prisoner—he said he had given him back what he had taken, and he did not think he could hurt him then—he was the worse for liquor. GUILTY.  1960. GEORGE CAMPION was indicted for stealing, on the   29th of May , 1 coat, value 10 s  .; the goods of Thomas Duggan . THOMAS DUGGAN. I am the master of a schooner. On the 29th of May I came to London on business—I saw the prisoner that afternoon near Lon don-bridge, with a boat, and I engaged him as a waterman to take me on board my schooner—I got in his boat to look for the vessel—we could not find it, and he rowed me back—I asked what he would take me to Greenwich for—he said 3 s .—we then went to have a pint of beer, and I thought I would stop till my vessel passed—he said he would wait no longer, and I paid him the 3 s .—he then said I might as well give him the preference to put me on board my vessel when it came—the vessel did come, and he put me on it—I told him to come up, and I would settle with him—he would not, but he pulled off with my coat in his boat—on the Friday following I saw him in a public-house—I asked what he had done with my coat—he said he had pawned it—I said he had put himself to a great deal of trouble, when he might have had his money at first—I gave him in charge the next morning—this is my coat.
