ROBERT Owst. I am a hoop-merchant, and have one partner. We
carry on business at Bermondsey. On the 20th of May we discovered
781bs. weight of nails, but we had previously lost many other parcels-
they were a peculiar sort, but there are other nails of the same sort-I
went, from information, to the prisoner Lawrence's marine-store shop, in
Mill-street--when I got there she and her husband were at home I waited
till the officer came-I had one of these nails in my pocket--I asked her
husband if he had any nails of that kind-he said he had I said I wished
to see them--he went into the back-room, but could not find them there-
he then said they were up stairs-he went up stairs, and brought down a
sack containing the nails, in a bag--they were the same sort I had had
stolen from me, and the bag must have been mine, as it had my initials on
it-I think the prisoner Lawrence was silent, but her husband said she
had bought them--there was a good deal of fretting and crying from her,
but I believe she said but little--this is the bag and nails.
JOHN TAYLOR (police-constable I 73.) I went to the house re-
specting the nails--the prisoner's husband said they had bought a quantity
of nails of that description, and they were up stairs--I went up with him,
and brought down the sack--the bag of nails was in it-I then took the
husband to the station-house, the woman begged I would not take her
down, but allow a person to remain with her-she was not in a fit state to
go, and I left a man with her-her husband said the nails were purchased
on the Saturday night, but he did not buy them.
BRIDGET GORMAN. I am the mother of Anthony Gorman. I know
the prisoner Lawrence by sight-I went and cautioned her three or four
days to buy nothing of my boy--and the last time I went her husband
told me he would not- I went to the prisoner, because she so often bought
of boys- I thought my boy had got into bad company, and I told her not
to buy of him-I did not take my son with me, but she knew him well
-she used to take him in, and give him some meat, and he used to go in
and out there--I have cautioned her not to buy of my son, when he was
there himself.
Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE. Q. You told her your son was a
thief? A. Yes, I cannot keep my son from bad company--I have a hus-
band, but he is a very drunken bad man-I spoke to the prisoner above
three weeks before my son was taken-her husband followed me out, and
gave me his hand and word that he would not buy any thing of him-
there were two or three others concerned in this.
(Lawrence received a good character.)
LAWRENCE-GUILTY. Aged 38.-Recommended to mercy.
