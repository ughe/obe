JOHN EASTWICK was indicted for stealing 1 peck of oats, value 1 s  .; the goods of Thomas Guiver , his master; and WILLIAM SHEARMAN , for feloniously receiving the same, knowing them to be stolen. THOMAS GUIVER. I am a coach-proprietor, at Cheshunt. I keep my horses at the Ship-inn, Tottenham—Eastwick was my horse-keeper there—I thought it necessary to put some marked papers amongst my oats—I have some oats here which have the papers amongst them.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNWELL. Q.  Does Eastwick feed his horses at Tottenham? A.  Yes—I bring up the oats, and he cuts his own chaff—he had been about five months in my service. RICHARD SINCLAIR( policeman N  321.) I saw Mr. Guiver on the 14th of Aug., and I gave him some pieces of paper—on the morning of the 17th of Aug. I saw Shearman about the street—I watched, and saw him stop at the Ship, at Tottenham, about two o'clock in the morning—he remained there till about four o'clock—soon after he left I stooped his cart, and found these oats and this chaff—the papers were in the oats—I went and took Eastwick, on a charge of robbing his matter—he said he had not, he had only given Old Billy  some sweepings—there were five pieces of paper found in the oats—I saw one piece picked out.
   Cross-examined by MR. PARNELL. Q.  Is the Ship a regular coaching-house? A.  Only Mr. Guiver's horses feed there—other horses stop there—there is one ostler employed by the publican.
   Cross-examined by MR. METCALFE. Q.  Did you understand who he meant by Old Billy ? A.  I know Shearman goes by that name—I have known him some years—he said he was seventy-eight years old—I never heard anything particular against him—I had him in custody once about a macintosh—he was in company with a woman that had it—he was discharged, and examined as a witness.
 (Eastwick received a good character.)
   EASTWICK  — GUILTY . — Confined Six months .
   SHEARMAN  — GUILTY.  — Recommended to mercy by the Jury. — Confined Three Months .
