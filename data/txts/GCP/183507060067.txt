WILLIAM COLE. I am cashier in the banking-house of Messrs, Stone.
This cheque, for 121. 10s. was presented at our counter on the 11th of
March, and paid by myself in gold. I do not recollect who presented it.
Johs KEMPSTER. I am a clerk in the gold pay office, in the Bank of
England. On the 12th of March I paid this £200 note, No. 9,515, and
gave for it fifty sovereigns, and a ticket on the pay-clerk for 1501. in notes.
John HAWKE. I am a pay-clerk in the Bank of England. On the
12th of March I received a pay-ticket, for which I gave two £50 notes and
ten £5 notes--the fives were Nos. 13,563 to 13,572 inclusive, dated
Feb. 4, 1835.
WILLIAM OSMOND. I keep the Waterloo Hotel at Ostend. On the
14th of March the prisoner arrived there, and resided at my househe
gave his name as Charles Houghton, and remained with me until the 8th
of April-be paid me two £5 notes-these are them; Nos. 13,564 and
13,565-he wrote nothing on them I wrote his name myself, which en-
ables me to speak to them they are dated Feb. 4, 1835.
REBECCA BROAD. I live at Gravesend. On Whit-Monday the prisoner
took my apartment, and came there the next day. He gave me no name
-I made out his bill in the name of Long for him, as he said it was very
curious, that there should be Little, Long, and Broad in the househe
paid the bill.
DANIEL FORRESTER. I am one of the City police. In consequence of
information, I took the prisoner into custody on the 19th of June at Graves-
end-I did not tell him the charge I searched him, and found two keys
on him--one was the key of a carpet-bag, and the other of some drawers
I was searching the drawers, and said, “I do not find what I want"-he
said, “You will come to it presently;" and at the bottom of the drawer I
found a carpet-bag-the key opened it, and I found 31 sovereigns, three
£5 notes, and a 1000 franc note-I found a pair of mustachios in a pocket-
book in the pocket of a coat which hung up in the room, and he had a
dark wig on at the time I apprehended him; and I found in the drawer some
dye for the hair (cheque read.)
Prisoner's Defence. I have been allowed to plead Not Guilty," to afford
myself a chance of escaping the rigour of the law. I am encouraged
to hope the Court will listen to a brief narrative of circumstances which led
me to the commission of the crime-I not only acknowledge, but I deeply
feel the enormity of the crime; but some circumstances connected with my
melancholy case you may not deem unworthy of your notioe very early
in life I lost my father, I was placed in a situation, when quite a child,
and lived with Mr. Atkinson, a respectable bill-broker, about two years
I gave him such satisfaction that he obtained a situation with the prosecutors
for me I was their junior clerk upwards of seven years, and endeavoured
to merit their approbation--this my long stay in their service will suffi-
ciently prove I had earned their confidence-my name was entered in the
books of the Stock Exchange, and I became acquainted with many persons,
which led me into extravagant habits, which so oppressed my mind,
sought to obtain another situation, but without effect, in order to avoid the
temptations I was continually exposed to--my resolution was so weak
that all my attempts to disengage myself from my gay acquaintances were
fruitless-with a view to remedy this evil I got married, (but rashly and
unadvisedly,) and having but 501. a year, I could not maintain a wife
