1265. AUGUSTA LAY was indicted for stealing, on the 15th of March,
1 spoon, value 4s. 6d. ; part of a brooch, value 1s. 6d. ; the goods of Ed-
ward Dutton, her master.
EDWARD DUTTON. The prisoner was my servant- she came to me on
the 2nd of March-I saw this spoon safe on the 28th of February, the day
before she came-I missed it on the last day of February, or the 1st of
March-- I supposed the other girl had lost it-I saw nothing of the spoon
till the 15th of March, when the policeman brought it--this brooch is
also mine-it was safe seven or eight days before-it was found on her
I swear it is my property.
ELIZABETH DUCKETT. I am the wife of Joseph Ducket, n policeman.
I searched the prisoner one Monday-I do not know when I found 9s. 6d.
on her, and the brooch in her bosom.
JAMES UPJOHN. The prisoner came to my shop on the 15th of Mareb.
about three o'clock in the afternoon-she offered this spoon for sale !
detained it and her.
CHARLES JAcks (police-sergeant T 20.) I took the prisoner in charge.
NOT GUILTY.
1266. AUGUSTA LAY was again indicted for stealing, on the 1st of
March, 1 handkerchief, value 2s.; 5 pieces of silk, value Is. 6d.; I
pocket-book, value 1s. ; 1 printed-book, value 1s. 6d.; 1 fork, value 6d.;
1 brooch, value 6d.; the goods of Job George, her master.
JOHN GEORGE. I live at Richmond. The prisoner was in my service
for about seven weeks—she left me on the 1st of March on the 15th I
was told something, and missed the things stated these now produced
are mine.
CHARLES Jacks (police-sergeant F 20.) I searched the prisoner's box,
and found these articles in it.
GUILTY. Aged 16.-Recommended to mercy by the Prosecutor.--
Confined Nine Days.
1267. ROBERT LOOSELY was indicted for receiving, on the 31st of
March, 2lbs. weight of marjoram seed, value 14s., the goods of James
Richard Tyler, well knowing the same to have been stolen ; against the
Statute, &c.
JAMES RICHARD TYLER. I am a seedsman, living at New Brixton. I
bought 3lbs. of marjoram seed at Mr. Sangston's—I sold llb. of it, and
lost the other from the seed room-it was in a paper bag- I believe this
now produced to be mine.
ALEXANDER ANDERSON LANGSTON. Here is my writing on this bag,
the same as there was on the bag I sold to Mr. Tyler, and the name of its
seed "knotted marjoram."
EDWARD DENYER, I am a seedsman at Brixton. On the 31st of
March, the prisoner came with this seed to sell I asked him what he
wanted for it-he said 4s. or 58. a pound--I had known him as being in
the habit of buying seed of me and others-I have always considered him
very honest, I had the best opinion of him- I told him the quantity was
of no use to me.
