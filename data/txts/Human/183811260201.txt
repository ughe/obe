   Cross-examined by MR. PRENDERGAST . Q.  You did not want furniture? A.  No, I was glad to get my house and fixtures, but I should rather have had a good tenant. JOHN SEARLES LADE. I am clerk to Mr. Pontifex. In consequence of directions from him, I went to the prisoner Hobbs, at Stamford Villa, in the Fulham-road—I saw him, and asked him if he knew a lady by the name of Norman—he said he did—I asked him if she was likely to pay the rent of a house of 40 l . or 50 l . a-year—he said yes, she was a very respect able' person—that he had a very good reference from his brother, who lived at Southampton—he said that I had no cause to be afraid of the rent, as that would be always certain, and I had no cause to go twice for it, that she kept a phaeton and pair—he said she was very close in her dealing—I communicated to Mr. Pontifex what he told me.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Where was it you saw Hobbs?— A.  At Stamford Villa, Fulham-road—it was an unfinished house—I did not count how many unfinished houses there were—I should think they would let for about 40 l . a-year—there were workmen engaged upon them—the conversation lasted about ten minutes perhaps—he was not doing any thing—I met him at the door. THOMAS OSLIFFE. I am in partnership with John Udall of Winchmore-hill. We are grocers and general-dealers—On the 29th of September, the prisoner Norman came to our shop—she gave the name of Norman, and ordered several articles, which were sent down—here is our bill, amounting to 5 l . 10 s . 1 d .—she said she had taken a house of Mr. Pontifex, and she would pay for the articles as we sent them down if we chose, but that she generally paid weekly—she had a book, and we entered several things—the prisoner Foster fetched several articles—the book was cast up on Satur day night, and they were to be paid for on the Monday—Foster came, I believe, every day—I made up the book by the direction of Foster—he said he only knew the family since they came there—I was to go down on the Monday with the bill, but Mr. Pontifex's servant came to tell us they were gone—there was 1lb. weight of tea, 3lbs. weight of sugar, blacking, brooms, &c., and such like things.
