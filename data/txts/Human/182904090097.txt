   WILLIAM SPENCER. I am a patrol. On the 28th of March, the prisoner was given into my charge, between six and seven o'clock. charged with stealing her mistress' gown; I saw it on her back - she said it was her own; she was in the act of taking off her gown which was over it when I entered the room; I went into her bed-room, and saw her in the act of putting the apron and handkerchief into her pocket; I took them from her - she said they were her own - and when I shewed her her mistress' name in full she did not claim them any more; the handkerchief has the name at length - there is W. R. on the apron; a piece of linen was brought in by Sarah Medd from the window; Rumsey said it had been cut off some in her chest; I gave them to the constable.
   SARAH MEDD. I am Mrs. Rumsey's niece. I found the linen in the area under the prisoner's bed-room window - I did not see it fall from the window; it might have dropped from any of the back windows; I saw it compared with the linen in the chest - it tallied with it; the prisoner said nothing about that, there were seven yards of it.
   WILLIAM HENRY HOOPER. I am constable of the night. I saw the prisoner in the watch-house; a gown, apron, and handkerchief, were delivered to me, claimed by Mrs. Rumsey; the linen was afterwards delivered to me; I said to the prisoner, "Do you persist in saying the gown is yours;" she said, "Yes, and I can bring a person forward who made it for me;" this was an hour after she was brought to the watch-house; but when a person said she had altered the gown for the prosecutrix, then the prisoner begged her mistress' pardon.
 MRS. RUMSEY. I had had the gown for twelve months; I never gave her permission to take it, but told her never to go into my room; my name is on the handkerchief at full length, and my husband's initials on the apron.
 Prisoner's Defence. I went up stairs and took the gown out to try it on, not intending to steal it - but hearing her coming up, I pulled my own over it till I could get it off in my own room; I came down to take it off; she charged me with stealing it, and got a man to hold me that I should not take it off till the constable came; I thought the apron was mine till I saw the mark.
     GUILTY. Aged 22. Transported for Seven Years.
 NEW COURT, Fifth Day.
 Fourth Middlesex Jury - before Mr. Common Sergeant.
 862.  WILLIAM BISHOP was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of February , 2 quarts of beer, value 10d., and 1 cann, value 2s., the goods of James Webb.
   ELIZABETH WEBB. I am the wife of James Webb- he keeps the  Alfred's Head, public-house, Silver-street, Stepney. On the 9th of February, about half-past eight o'clock in the evening, while my pot-boy, Robert Hawkins, was out serving his beer, the prisoner, whom I had seen before, came in and said that Robert had sent him for two pots of beer - I asked where Robert was; he said at the end of Princes-street, that he was busy, and in a great hurry; I was going to draw it in two pots, but he said Robert wished it to be in a cann - I drew it in a cann and gave it him.
   CAROLINE WEBB. I was present at the time, but I have nothing to add.
   ROBERT HAWKINS. I did not send the prisoner for any beer on the 9th of February; the cann has not been found.
 Prisoner's Defence. I never was near the place.
     GUILTY. Aged 15. Confined One Month.
 863.  MARY ANN EVETTS was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , 2 blankets, value 5s., the goods of Francis Gray.
   FRANCIS GRAY. I am a mason. The prisoner lodged with me; the blankets were lost on the 17th of January.
   CHARLOTTE GRAY. I am the prosecutor's wife. I know the blankets - I never authorised the prisoner to pawn them, but I knew of it afterwards.
 Prisoner. Q. Did you not go with me to pawn them? A. I did go with a bundle, but I did not know my blankets were in it.
   FRANCIS PARKER. The prisoner pawned a blanket and pillow on the 17th of January, and another blanket on the 13th of February; the prosecutor's wife used to come with her sometimes, and I believe she was with her on the 17th of January; I had not time to say this before the Magistrate.
     NOT GUILTY.
 864.  THOMAS HEDGES was indicted for stealing, on the 28th of February , I bee-hive, value 2s., the goods of John Fauch.
   JOHN FAUCH. I live at Harrow on the Hill. I lost a bee-hive on the 28th of February from my yard - it was found on the prisoner's premises on the 13th of March; he lives about three miles from me; this is my hive - it was a hive of bees when I lost it - it was not so bad as it is now.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Is not one hive exceedingly like another? A. Yes; but I know this by its form, a chalk line round it, and this mark on the top; this line I cut off one I work with; the prisoner said he bought it in the December preceding of his son-in-law,  John West.
   RICHARD WALTER. I am an officer. I found this hive between the cieling and the roof of the prisoner's kitchen, on the 9th of March - there were two hives there; he said he bought one about a month before, and the other before Christmas, of his son-in-law, and the mice had destroyed the bees.
   JOSEPH DREW. I apprehended the prisoner - he said he bought a hive o bees of his son-in-law, West, about a month before, and the other before Christmas; West has absconded - he boarded with the prisoner, but I do not think he lived with him.
 Prisoner's Defence. I gave 12s. for them.
     NOT GUILTY.
