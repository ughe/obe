JOHN NAPIER. (Re-examined) I saw this boiler when I first went to the

Thomas JOSEPH TULLY. I am a builder-I have known Parram over
seven years-he is a smith-I have had dealings with him—this invoice,
dated April 18th, is made out by Parram for goods sold to me—they came
from his premises, and were paid for-they were fitted up in four houses in
the Marlborough-road-I told Mr. Napier where they were to be seen-the
amount of this bill is 191. 98. 8 d.-it includes ranges, register stoves, and
things of that description-here is another bill of 16th May, 111. 88. 10d.-
I bought these goods of Parram--they were delivered and paid for some
were fixed, not all—I told Mr. Napier where he could see them—there is
another invoice of 23 May for 138. 8d. for repairs, which contains the receipt
for the other two bills.
Cross-examined. Q. What character has Parram borne in yonr estimation;
is he a straightforward honest man! A. Yes, as I told the officer when he
came-I gave him orders for stoves in the usual way-he fitted them up for
me-I might have seen them at his place first-living so near, I was often
in there.
HENRY LINDSAY. I am a clerk to Mr. Allan—I recollect Hardy being
taken on 4th June-I came at 7 o'clock in the morning to open the premises
-I am not aware of any goods being sold to Parram on 2d June-if he had
come there and bought any goods of Hardy it onght to have been reported
to me.
COURT to J. NAPIER. Q. Just give me a list of the goods taken possession
of ? A. Metal for two kitchen stoves, five stoves, seven pieces of metal, and
twelve copper doors and frames.
FREDERICK BIRD. I am a gaoler at Newgate-I received this letter from
Hardy, and it was sent in the ordinary way to the party to whom it is
addressed. (Read, see page 387).
Hardy's Defence. If the witnesses who have been examined bad been
there at any other time in the day they would have seen plenty of other
carts loading in Smith's-ride. I had no authority to load there. I used my
own judgment, and had done so long before Mr. Napier or Mr. Hutchinson
came there, to avoid Messrs. Smith's waggons which were in front. On this
2d of June, I think it was, I took a boiler; it might have been that one.
It was not whole, and I handed three pieces of metal into Mr. Parram
cart that morning. It was Adam's place to account for it ; it was in his
department. I merely handed the metal in, and never before but once in
my life did I ever load anything into Mr. Parram's cart. I will swear that,
only twice. As to the copper doors and other things, I know nothing
about them.
PARRAM received a good character.-Guilty.-Seven Years' Penal
Servitude. HARDY.-GUILTY.
