   Cross-examined. Q.  Are you related to the prosecutor? A.  No—I have known him about three months—I was helping him all that day—I have no sister—I am not in any way connected with the family.
   Prosecutor.  My name is Patrick Henry—I am called Patrick Henry, since the day I was at the office—I went by that name, because they said it was necessary—before that I used generally to be called Henry—I was confirmed in 1827—before that I was called Patrick only, and after that I was generally called Henry—my mother used always to call me Patrick Henry—I generally call myself Patrick Henry, but before I went to the Police-office, Henry, generally—my mother always called me Patrick Henry after my confirmation—I was confirmed by the name of Henry only—I did not give up the name of Patrick—the bishop called me Henry, by my desire.
   JURY . Q.  At the time of your marriage what name did you sign? A.  I was married to my first wife—she is now dead—I signed Henry, not Patrick—I was married by banns, and called Henry—I never had any other wife—when the gentleman spoke, I thought he might allude to this party, whom I know nothing about—she has no claim to meat all—on any solemn occasion, such as being sworn, I call myself Patrick Henry—( the signature to the certificate found on the prisoner was Patrick Henry Smith. ) NOT GUILTY.
 2154. BRIDGET SMITH was again  indicted for feloniously attempt ing to administer to Catherine Smith a large quantity of oxalic acid, with intent to murder her. THOMAS HYETT. On Friday, the 26th of Sept., I saw the prisoner in Church-street, Cornwall-road—she gave me a brown paper parcel, and told me to take it to No. 3, Pier's Cooperage, to tell Mrs. Smith to get tea ready, and she would come—I took the parcel, and delivered it to Mrs. Smith—I am sure the prisoner is the person. CATHERINE SMITH. On Friday, the 26th of Sept., I received a parcel from Hyett—it contained lump and moist sugar, and a little tea in another paper—I took it and showed it afterwards to my son, and to Mrs. Pem berton—I gave it to my son in the same state as Hyett had given it to me.
   Cross-examined by MR. DOANB . Q.  Did you expect that evening, after what Hyett told you, that some woman would come and drink tea with you? A.  No—he told me a woman was coming to drink tea, but he did not mention her—I expected a woman to come to tea, from what he said, but not the prisoner—I could not expect anybody to come till I heard what the boy said—he said a woman was coming to tea, and to have the kettle boiling.
   COURT . Q.  When he said that, did you think anybody would come? A.  No—I said to myself, "I don't know who sent it;" nor did I expect the tea from anybody. ELIZABETH PEMBERTON. On the 26th of Sept., I went to Mrs. Smith's—she gave her son a parcel in my presence—I saw him take it to Mr. Handley's in the state it was in.
