To the Recorder. A shed is about 100 yards long and there was one
man there to watch. Prisoner had not time to draw out the rum
while I was watching him and it must have been all ready beforehand,
Detective-constable WALTER LILLEY, Victoria Docks. On
August 10, at 8 a.m., I went to "D" jetty. There were some casks
of rum stored in "D" shed, about 200 puncheons. I examined the
casks and found that one had had the tin removed from the shive and
the shive had been taken out and so replaced that you could take it
out with your finger and thumb. The shive is a kind of wooden
bung; we have to use wood on account of the spirit. With the shive
in that position the rum could easily be removed from the cask and
transferred to a bottle. Behind the cask there is a gangway. I
there found two empty bottles, which had contained beer. A good
number of people have access to that shed and anybody knowing the
condition of the casks and wishing to abstract the spirit would have the
opportunity of filling up those bottles. I pointed out the puncheon
to the Custom house officer on the station, and in my presence he
drew & sample from the cask.
SAMUEL HECK, Custom House officer. I examined the cask men
tioned by last witness. Its full contents should have been 101 gallons
I found a deficiency of six and a half gallons, but do not assume that
six and a half gallons were taken out, it being customary to find even
in & cask which has not been tampered with a vacuity representing
deficiency of three gallons or four gallons. The deficiency in this case
was an unusual one, and I estimate that 24 gallons had been removed.
The strength of the sample I took was 40.4 deg. over proof. The
spirit is from Demerara, and the average strength would be 36 deg.
or 40 deg. over proof. I found two of the bottles of rum produced
by the police to be 39 deg. over proof and have no doubt the spirit
came from the cask I examined. The value of the spirit in the seven
bottles is 10s. 10d., and the duty upon it is £1 2s. 4d. - £1 12s. 5d.
HARRY BOWXAN, assistant superintendent, Royal Mail Steam Packet
Company. On August 4 we were discharging rum from the Catalonia
at "D" jetty. Prisoner was not working for the company and had no
right to enter the premises or take rum. The rum in these bottles 15
the property of the company, who are the only people that discharge
rum in the dock. The rum on being discharged would remain on the
jetty a short time before it goes into bond.
Police-constable WILLIAM WALLER, 218, K. On August 9, about
7.30 p.m., I was on duty at the Victoria Dock and saw prisoner. IN-
formation had previously been received that a man named Compton
was wanted for being in unlawful possession of bottles of rum. I took
him into custody; he said, " All right." When charged at the station
he made no reply.
