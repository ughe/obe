Inspector Jones to the prisoner Jones's house—he gave the inspector these 14 watches—I put Lepine into the cab, and said, "You under  stand what you are taken in custody for?" he said, "No"—I said, "You are going to be charged with Jones with committing a burglary at 8, Falcon Road, Battersea, on the night of the 5th;" he said, "I did not go to work on the Tuesday, and when I went on Wednesday Jones told me had bought some watches from a man over the counter on Tuesday, I know nothing about the robbery"—Jones was not present.
   Cross-examined.  Lepine was at work in the shop—some watches were lying on a glass shelf, and others were taken out of the cases, and the weres were under a glass, they were undergoing repair. JAMES HENRY GIBBS. ( not examined in chief ).
   Cross-examined.  I am Mr. Reeve's manager—I know Lepine as a working watch repairer for Jones—I understood him to be Jones's servant—he actually brought one of these watches to our shop after my master had been robbed of it, and asked me to look at it—I did so—he asked me what I would lend him on it—I said, "5 s ."—he said, "I bought it this morning, and I should lose 4 s . by it"—I gave it back to him, and he went away—our shop is about 100 yards from one of the prosecutor's.
   Re-examined.  The shop where the burglary was committed is at Clap ham, four miles away—the name of Gibson Reeve is up at the shop in Union Street—I cannot say whether it is also at Battersea—I identify the watch as one of the stolen watches—I told Mr. Reeve, and was directed to give it back to Lepine, saying nothing. GIBSON REEVE. ( Re-examined ). I also have a shop at Clapham Junc tion, Battersea—my name is up there, "Gilson Reeve"—nothing is said about business being carried on anywhere else—my other shop is in the Borough—it there says "And at Whitecross Street"—I lost a great many other things, and have put a mark against them on the printed list, also a great many things which are not in the list—there were five or six dozen rings; none of them were found at the prisoner's. EMILY FROST. I am housekeeper to the prosecutor at 8, Falcon Market—on the night in question I went round the house about 11.45—it was quite safe—I went to bed at 11.50—I was disturbed about 3.15 by noises like the chopping of wood and a bell ringing, and there were various noises—I sat up in bed—when I went down at 7.15 I found the street door and the windows open, which had been closed over night and the place in the great confusion—some one had got in through the top staircase window, which was wide open—they could get there from the back; there was a scaffold there; that window was closed when I went to bed—they could get down the staircase, and then get through the lift leading to the shop. LAWRANCE LEWIS. I am in Mr. Reeves's employ at his fourth shop, 96, Park Street, Southwark—on 14th March I went to Jones's shop to buy a watch—he shewed me one—I bought it, and asked him to show me another, pointing it out—I gave the one I bought to Mr. Reeve; it is one of those produced—I was instructed to go and buy a watch—he did not show me the second one; he assigned no reason for not doing so.
   Cross-examined.  There were two other watches like it lying in the window—I know he is a watch-cleaner.
   Jones received a good character. NOT GUILTY.
