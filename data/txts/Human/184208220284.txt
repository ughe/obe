JAMES ILLMAN re-examined . My wife is subject to fits, which affect her mind at times—I am a blacksmith by trade, which took me out a good deal, and the public-house was left to her—she had the last fit two or three months before she Jeff me—I cannot tell when before that—she has frequently had fits, I cannot say the time—Mr. Hampton, of Reigate, at tended her for it—he did not attend her three months before she went away—he has attended her in illness from fits—he did not attend her at the fits, but she has been ill afterwards—he attended her some time in the beginning of the summer, I do not know what for, farther than for fits—she had had a fit just before—I have no doubt he knew that—I cannot tell when that was, I never set it down—I never knew her deranged, only from fits—she is subject to fits. JAMES HENSLEY. I am in the service of Mr. Harrison, who keeps the Cock, at Sutton. On the 3rd of August I went with fly , and met Mrs. Illman and Pratt—they got into the fly  I drove them to London, and put them down at the Bull, in Devonshire-street, Bishopsgate, between one and two o'clock in the morning—the house had closed. WILLIAM COCKRELL( police-constable L  108.) I was at the Elephant and Castle on the 5th of August—I saw Nye there in a cab , and a large box—I took her into custody, and took possession of the box—it con tained the articles stated—I afterwards went to the West-end, and took Pratt into custody—I found on the table before him two umbrellas, a band-box containing a silk bonnet and several pieces of patchwork, and a basket containing a night-gown, a night-cap, a dirty sheet, and a gown—I asked him at the station what property he had about him—he said none but what belonged to him—I found some money on him—the only articles of male apparel in the box were two shirts and five silk handkerchiefs. NOT GUILTY.
