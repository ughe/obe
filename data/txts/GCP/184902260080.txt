715. CHARLES WHITCHER, stealing 1 counterpane, and other goods,
value 31. 108.; the goods of Henry Wood and another, his masters.
Messrs. BALLANTINE and PARNELL conducted the Prosecution.
GEORGE HOWARD (City police-inspector). Bradley took the prisoner into
custody on another charge-after he was in custody I went to his house, in
Church-passage, Aldgate--I searched, and found this counterpane and other
things- I found his wife there, and I had seen bim there just before!
found this table-cover, this toilet-cover, and blanket, and some pieces of
carpet on the floor, about two or three yards square-Mr. Wood was with
me- found two pairs of blankets, and two quilts more, Mrs. Whitcher
begged for one of each to be left behind-she said the children would
be without any.
Cross-examined by Mr. Parry. Q. When did you search the house!
1. About an hour after the prisoner had been taken-I have since heard
that his house was searebed in Nov.- I took the table-covers off a shut-up
bedstead, which looked like a chest of drawers, and the blanket and counter-
panes off the bed-the carpets are not here, they were lying on the floor--this
table-cover is worn a little, not a great deal--the counterpane and blanket
are worn-I have not examined whether there are any boles in them; they
appear nearly new-this table-cover on the bedstead had glasses, mugs, and
ornaments on it-I do not know whether Mr. Wood saw it in Nov. 1
expected the carpets to have been here; they are at Smithfield-there was one
piece of carpet taken from the first floor; it was about as old as this table-
cover--the prisoner's wife was present-she said, "For God's sake, don't
take all the things : I shall have nothing to cover my children"--Mr. Wood
gave her back a counterpane and the fellow blanket to this one-- I probably
might have said if she bad one she would want all; I do not recollect it--I
probably might have said to Mr. Wood, "Don't give them back, as they are
yours"-Mr. Wood claimed them there-be said he sold things like these,
and be believed them to be his.
HENRY Wood. I am in partnership with my brother; the prisoner was
in our employ twelve or thirteen years--we dismissed him on 4th Nov.-bis
premises were searched on 18th Oct.--we did not take away anything at
that time I saw the premises searcbed after the prisoner was in custody,
and saw these things found-his wages were 208. a week-these blankets are
worth about two guineas a pair-they are three yards and a balf long, and
three yards wide--we keep a great stock of them some of them have been
missed, we hardly know how many- this counterpane is worth 148. or 154.
-We have missed a large quantity of them--this toilet-cover is worth about
43.-we missed articles of this kind and of the same size-I have no mark op
them--we had table-covers similar to this-we have missed about 200-W
had a large stock of them---they were not in the department where the
prisoner was; they were in the same warehouse-one of the carpets found
was a square carpet; the other was painted drugget--the carpet was
worth about 328.
Cross-eramined. Q. Was not the drugget very old ? A. Perhaps two or
three years--the prisoner was dismissed in Nov. last, in consequence of the
way in which I found we had been plundered-all the porters were dismissed
--when I searched the prisoner's house in Oct. I had two policemen with me
--I do not recollect seeing this table-cover then; I saw this blanket and this
counterpane-this toilet-cover is a cot-quilt-it is a very good one, and
worth about 45.- we have blankets from 38, a pair to 80s.- I do not identify
