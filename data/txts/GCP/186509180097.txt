George Ross. I am a gunner in the Royal Artillery-ou the morning

SAMUEL LING. On Wednesday morning, 9th August, from some infor-
mation that I received, I went to a house in Prince's-rund, Plumstead, and
in a bedroom on the top-floor I found Murphy and Smith partly dressed-
I said, “ Thomas Murphy and Emily Smith, you are charged with breaking
into a shop at the corner of Nightingale-vale, on Tuesday morving"-
Murphy said, “I don't understand you "-I said, " It was Tuesday morn-
ing, between 2 and 3 o'clock, following that of last Mouday night"-Morphy
said, " I was not out at all that evening"-Smith said," " I was in by hulle
past 12"-I said, “Dress yourselves ; you must go to the station "-I
allowed them to walk together on the way, Murphy said to Smith, “If
they come to you, don't split one word about it;" a little further on he
said, " If they come to pump you in the cells, don't let out one word
about it."
Murphy. Q. How far were you from me when I said that 4. Within
half a yard-you spoke in a low tone, but quite loud enough for me to
hear—the other constable walked on the right of me-he heard some of
what you said.
The prisoners' statements before the Magistrate : Buckland says, “I am
innocent." Murphy says, I was in bed on that night at half past 12."
Smith says, "I am innocent ; I was in bed at half-past 12, and Mrs. Welsh
and her danghter can prove it."
Buckland's Defence. At the time the policeman says he saw me with
Murphy I was at work. I was coming down by the shop at the time the
robbery was committed, and the constable came and took me.
Murphy's Defence. On 8th August, I was indoors by half-past 12 with
this female. At the time I was charged my mother and Mrs. Welsh told
the constable they would prove I was in bed. Mrs. Welsh did not come, as
her son-in-law was going to India ; the Magistrate sent us for trial, because
the woman was not there.
