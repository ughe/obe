WILLAM BURBIDGE. I live in Bridge-street, Southwark, and conduct the
business of my late father, for his executors-Thomas Keene is one, and there
are others-it is a wholesale hat and cap manufaetory-about the 9th of
Nov. the prisoner Gerhard came to the shop-he said he was going to open
a shop at the Triangle, Kennington Cross, and wanted a few hats and other
things-he selected about 401. worth-I asked him for a reference, and he
gave me one to a Mr. Maymott, No. 13, Charles-street, Northampton-squan
-I either wrote or caused a letter to be written to Mr. Maymott, abd
received an answer-I took the answer to Mr. Maymott, to know if it was
his hand-writing-he said it was-after having received this letter, and seen
Maymott myself on the subjeet, I permitted Gerhard to have ten or twelve
dozens of hats, on the 14th of Nov., amounting to 401. some odd "shillings,
and on the 16th of Nov. I sent him goods to the amount of 111. 4s. 9d.-+|
went to the shop about a month afterwards, and saw the goods-| went to
the shop again shortly after Christmas, found it shut up, and no one on the
premises-when Gerhard came to me he had whiskers and no mustachios
-I have never been paid a farthing.
COURT. Q. In what state was the shop when you went there withina

saw it shut up- was aware some goods had been seized for rent- tookt
