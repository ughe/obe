   MESSRS. SCRIVEN and CLARKSON conducted the Prosecution. MARY LEARY. I am a fruit-seller, and live in New-court, White-street, Southwark. On Friday evening, the 10th of July, between nine and ten o'clock, I was selling fruit in the Dover-road, and saw the prisoner Wilson and another man, who I believe to be Lloyd—Wilson wore a cap, and the other a hat-Wilson said, "What have you got there, old lady?"—I said, "Gooseberries"—he said, "Will you give me a pennyworth?" which I did, and he gave me a shilling—I said, "Is that a good shilling?"—he said, "Oh yes, it is"—I said, "If it is not, why do youk'give it to an old woman like me?"—Lloyd wu standing by the stall near him at the time—I gave them lie?, and they went away—I put the shilling between my teeth, took it out, and had it in my hand when West, the policeman, came up—I gave it him, and he took it away.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Did not you say at first that Wilson was not the man? A.  No—I am sure of that—I was asked if I knew the man—I looked at him, and said, "Why, he wore a cap"—I swore to bin when he had a cap on—I did not when he had a hat on—he wore a cap at the stall—when I saw him again he had nothing on—the policeman put a cap of him, and I said, "That is the man"—the shilling had not been out of hand till the policeman had it.
