 425. THOMAS KING  (26) , Unlawfully uttering counterfeit coin.
   MR. MEAD Prosecuted HENRY BATE. I am a chemist, of 44, Thorn Road, South Lambeth—on 15th March, about 3.80, I served the prisoner with a seidlitz-powder—he gave me 1 s .—I put it into the till, where there were no other shil lings, and gave him the change—in about half an hour Hubbard came with Mr. Read, and from what they said I looked in the till, found the shilling, and gave it to Hubbard—no one else had served in the shop since the prisoner came—this is the seidlitz-powder; the label has been torn off, but sufficient remains for me to identify it.
   Cross-examined by the Prisoner.  I did not positively say at the station taat you were the man, but I had no doubt whatever—I also said at the police-court that I believed you were the man, and I say so now; I am not doubtful. CHARLES REED. I am am a baker of South Lambeth Road—on 15th March, about 4.30, my wife served the prisoner with two halfpenny biscuits—he put down 1 s .—she gave him the change—I looked at the coin before he left, and saw that it was bad—I went after him, stopped him, and told him he had given me a bad shilling—he said, "I was not aware of it. Some man gave it to me to come and get some biscuits"—I said, "You had better come back to the shop with me"—he called to some man who was walking across to the other side of the road, but he took no notice—I took the prisoner back to the shop, and while waiting for a constable he took the seidlitz-powder out of his pocket, and began to tear off the label—I gave him in charge with the coin.
   Cross-examined.  I did not see a man running away—you offered me all the money yon had, and said that you would call and pay the rest. SAMUEL HUBBARD. ( Policeman W  310). I was fetched to Mr. Read's shop, and he gave the prisoner in custody for giving him a bad shilling—I searched him, and found on him sixpence in silver, three halfpence, and this seidlitz-powder—I saw the name of Bate on the label, and I went there with Mr. Read—I produce the two shillings I received from Mr. Read and Mr. Bate—I asked the prisoner his address—he refused to give any. WILLIAM WEBSTER. These coins are both bad—not from the same mould.
   The prisoner in his defence stated that he received the coins and seidlitz-powder from another man. GUILTY — Nine Months' Imprisonment.
