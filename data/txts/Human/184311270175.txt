JOHN NEWMAN. I am apprentice to the prosecutor. On the 21st of March last, by direction of my employer, I called on the prisoner, and said I had come from Mr. Dunkin for the amount of his bill—I produced a bill from my pocket, which I showed him—his words were to this effect, "I cannot settle it now, but I will when I pay my Christmas bills"—he said nothing about a receipt, or having paid it.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Did you know the prisoner's person before you went? A.  I did not—I will swear he was the man who I saw—when at Woolwich, I said I thought I could swear to him—that was on the 6th of Nov.—I did not sign my deposition then—I said I was doubtful whether I could swear to him or not, but when I saw him I said it was him.
   MR. WILKINS . Q.  Was he present when you said you was doubtful whether you could swear to him? A.  I had no doubt of him when I looked at him—I had not looked at him when I expressed a doubt.
   MR. LONGER examined by MR. CLARKSON . I am a Commissioner of the Court of Requests. When this case was decided, I applied to Mr. Parker, our legal adviser, as Mr. Dunkin wanted to impound the receipt, whether we had authority to do so, and after taking advice, I said I did not think I could—I insisted on its being given up to the defendant—one Commissioner said if he was an honest man, he would leave it in the bands of the chairman, and then he handed it to me—I requested the clerk to seal it up—on the 6th of Oct. it was said that the debt was debarred by the statute of limitation—the prisoner said he did not wish to take the benefit of that, and would pro duce the receipt—we examined the prosecutor's books very minutely before we gave our decision—I believe nine Commissioners were present when the case was decided. NOT GUILTY.
