ALFRED CROUCH. I am a builder at Worthing, Sussex I advertised
in the Exchange and Mart on 29th March for a tricycle-I received this
reply (From A. Fitzgerald, of 49, King Henry's Walk, of March 30th,
offering a tricycle for a ten days' P. 0. 0. for 51. 58.)-I sent the post-
office order, believing the statements in the letter-I never got the
tricycle—I stopped the payment of the order.
THOMAS WIGLEY. I am assistant to George Crump, postmaster of the
Ball's Pond Post-office, Islington-I cashed this post-office order in April
for 57. 58. in error for the elder prisoner, the order purporting to come
from Mr. Crouch-I found out afterwards it was post-dated-I was very
busy
Cross-examined by James. I remember the date I cashed it-I picked
you out from others at Clerkenwell; they were rather rough.
CHARLES FREDERIOK Barr. I live at Totten, Hampshire-I am ad
outfitter-on 15th Maroh I advertised in the Ecohange and Mart for a
good Salvo tricycle-I received this letter from 188, Boleyne Road,
Kingsland, March 15th, from George Eagleton (Offering a Balvo tricyols
for a 10 days' post-office order for 5 guineas) -I sent this post office ordor,
dated 15th March, believing George Eagleton at that address had just the
Salvo I wanted-I got no tricycle-I stopped the order.
MARY ANN Wood. I am a widow, of 162, St. John's Street Road,
Clerkenwell—I know Arthur Ayden as George Eagleton-he brought me
this post-office order in March to cash-he said he received it in payment
for a tricycle I advanced him the full value, 51, 58.-he fore the signature
off the letter and gave it to me with the order.
MARY LOON. I am a widow and a newsagent at 73, Newington Green
Read-I received about a dozen letters and post-cards addressed to
Eagleton at the request of a young man I cannot identify-I only delivered
them on one oecasion.
Arthur Ayden's Defence. I am guilty of obtaining money under false
pretenees but not of conspiracy with James Ayden. I throw myself on
the mercy of the Court.
James Ayden's Defence. I was requested by Arthur to receive letters for
him addressed under an assumed name, and I did so, and handed them
to him not knowing their contents or anything connected with them until
about a week before my arrest, when he told me to open them, and
anything appeared to require an answer I was to take them to him, if not
I was to see him in the evening. I opened about a dozen letters, and
some he returned to me to take care of, and those were the letters found
in my possession. I was handed one or two post-office orders, for which
I obtained cash in a fair and legitimate manner. There was nothing in
the letters I opened to induce me to come to the conclusion that they
related to any fraudulent transactions. I understood my friend was
engaged in selling tricycles on commission, and I supposed the correspon-
dence related to the business. I deny that Brasher cashed the order for
me. He is mistaken in my identity. I was placed with men in labourers'
und bricklayers' clothes, so that it was impossible to properly identify me.
GUILTY*. ARTHUR AYDEN then PLEADED Guilty to a conviction of
felony in July, 1880, at the Mansion House in the name of John Aydon.
