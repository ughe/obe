1782. JAMES WRAY was indicted for stealing 6 muffs, value 25l.; the
goods of Jacob Davis, his master.
JACOB DAVIS. I am a furrier, and live in Bull's-head-court, Newgate-
street-I have lost six fur muffs--they were in our possession in Sept.
or Nov., I believe-they were worth 251.- I have only found this one,
I can swear it is one I lost-the prisoner was in my service-this muff
has my name on it.
Cro88-examined by MR. BALLANTINE. Q. When did the prisoner leave
your employ. A. He finally quitted on the 31st of Oct. I did not miss
the muffs till the end of June-I believe the prisoner bad gone into partner-
ship, or started for himself as a fur-seller-he bought one parcel of things of
me, amounting to 121.--I have heard that he was in partnership with Mears,
a furrier, who purchased things of me three years ago this is the muff that I
speak of—it has "I D and Co." on it-this mark is not on all my property,
but my instructions are to mark them all-I do not sell; my warehouseman
does-the prisoner was my warehouseman; no one else sells, but if two or
three customers come in, we all assist-Bone never sells-Arrowsmith sells
very seldom--this is Marten sable fur, it is known by no other name.
CHARLES ARROWSMITH. I keep the books for Mr. Davis-this muff
has not been sold.
Cross-examined. Q. How do you know it has not been sold ? A.
Because when the prisoner was accused he never once attempted to say it
had been sold, but said that it had been left in his stock by his late partner,
John Mears.
COURT. Have you examined your books ? A. Yes, I cannot trace that
it has been sold-the books would enable me to trace it pretty accurately.
