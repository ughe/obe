   WILLIAM LUMLEY. I was coming from Covent-garden driving a horse and chaise - I was in Brownlow-street at ten minutes before three; I heard the scream of a female; I looked and saw the black bonnet of a woman - the cart was going on, but I turned my mare round and stopped it; the prisoner was given to the policeman.
   THOMAS PERRING(police-sergeant F 1). I took the prisoner, but did not see the accident - there is a little descent of the paving there towards that part, which the wheel of the cart might rather slip into.
   JOSEPH TUMMON. I was standing behind my bar at the corner of Brownlow-street; the first I saw was when the old lady was squeezed between the post and the cartwheel; some person took her - the prisoner was going on, but I ran and caught hold of him.
   JOHN GILL. I am a surgeon - this woman was given into my charge on the 25th of May, about half-past three o'clock - I examined her side and found a fracture of two or three ribs; she was taken to the Middlesex-hospital, and died on the 8th of June, about eight in the morning - the remote cause of her death was the fracture of the ribs, but she had diseased bones, and the slightest thing would cause a fracture - the immediate cause of her death was a chronic cough, and the secretion of mucus in the lungs, but the fracture of the ribs increased the cough.
 Cross-examined. Q. Might not this have caused her death, independent of the fracture? A. Yes, certainly; but that no doubt, increased the cough.
 COURT to LUMLEY. Q. Had you any difficulty in stopping the cart? A. No; the prisoner was full of contrition, he never said a word.
   THOMAS BANKS. I saw the cart come out of Brownlow-street; I passed the prisoner's horse in my cab, and he made a halt just opposite this stall - the good woman trying to save her stall, put herself right in the way - if she had sat still, or stood up, it certainly would not have happened - the prisoner did not attempt to run away or escape.
     NOT GUILTY.
