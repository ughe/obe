   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  When Payne came up did not he say, "Don't go with them, Tom ; go home quietly?" A. "Come along with me" was what he said—he might say, "Go home quietly"—Wigzell said, "You had better let him alone, or you will get locked up yourself"—I am positive Hare is the person who was there, I took such particular notice of him—he was down on the ground four or five times—he might have been on one occasion knocked down by a blow with a truncheon, but I did not see it—I did not strike him on the shin with my truncheon—I struck him on the head—I do not recollect hearing Payne say, when he had hold of Hare's braces, "Are you going to kill the man?"—we took Hare for being drunk, throwing up his hat and jacket, and wanting to fight.
   MR. CLARK . Q.  Do you mean that Hare was knocked down four or five times? A.  I did not say he was knocked down, only when I struck him on the head—the other times he threw himself down. THOMAS WIGZELL( police-constable P  137.) I went with Kayser to Nelson-street—I saw Hare there—I never saw him before—he was throw-ing up his jacket, and was stripped to his shirt, wanting to fight somebody—Kayser told him to go quietly—he said he would not—then Kaysef took him, and I assisted him—Hare asked us both several times why we were taking him—Kayser said, "For being drunk and disorderly"—he said he would not go, and he kicked me first in Nelson-street—I drew my staff, and told him if he kicked me again I would break his legs—when he got round the corner out of Nelson-street he struck me in the face with his fist—he struck me five or six times in the face, and kicked me several times on my right leg—Kayser struck him—neither of us had struck him before he had struck both of us in the face—I should say there were up wards of 200 persons present—I saw Payne there—he came up to Hare, and said, "Tom, go home; don't go with them"—I said he had better mind his own business, or he would get locked up himself—he then took hold of Hare by the braces, and said he would be d—d if he should go, and struck me in the fate—soon after this Kayser went away, and when he was gone Hare struck me in the face with his clenched fist—when I took my staff out I had kept it in my hand, but I had not used it up to this time—Chantler was at this time with me—the people got in between Hare and us to rescue him—I am certain Henry, James, and William Goodright were amongst the persons who assisted at the rescue—when Hare struck me in the face, I struck him across the shoulders with my truncheon, and knocked him down; and I struck him on the feet when he was on the ground kicking, to keep his kicks from me, when he was trying to kick me in the privates—he did not kick me there, but he was aiming at that part—I had known the three Goodrights before, and they got between Hare and me—one of them got hold of me, but I could not say which—Hare knocked me down, and I was kicked and jumped upon—I lay insensible on the ground several minutes—I was kicked in my side and the back of my head—there were many of the mob besides the prisoners interfering at that time—Hare got away from me—I got up and tried to follow him, and I was knocked down by a man who is not here—I got up, and went to
