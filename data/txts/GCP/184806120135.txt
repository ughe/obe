1617. THEODORE WICKS and EDWARD MARSHALL were indicted
for a robbery upon Sarah Sayer, and stealing 1 purse, value 6d., 2 balfpence,
half-farthing, and 1 coin, 1d:; her property.
MR. PRENDERGAST conducted the Prosecution.
SARAR SAYER. I was ten years old last Feb., and live with my father and
mother. One Friday, between twelve and one o'clock in the day, I was sent
by my father to take home some paper he had sold, and in crossing a field
near Peckham Rye, a number of boys came up to me-Marshall was with
them-one of them, named Tilbrook, pulled up my petticoats and threw me
dows-another boy told him to let me be, and then they left me I walked
on for two or three minutes-Wicks then came up to me and said, "I mean to
- you"-he threw me down and threw me over a ditch, pulled up my
dothes, and pinched me, and knelt upon me-I hallooed he told me to hold
my tongue, or he would cut my head off; and said, if I would let him do it, bie
would give me a bird's-nest which he had in his band-I kept hallooing-he
undid bis clothes and called Marshall to hold my mouth and hands-
Marshall came up and held my mouth and hands-as Wicks was pull-
ing up my petticoats, he saw my pocket-he turned me round, felt in
my pocket, and got my purse out-I saw it in his band there was 2s.
and 14d. in it, a half-farthing, and a new copper penny with a bit of silver in
it-Marshall could see what he did they ran away together-I got up and
went straight across the field went to a policeman, and told him what bad
heppened--I saw Wicks again in about a fortnight at the station-I am sure
he is the boy, and I am sure of Marshall too.
Cross-examined by Mr. O'BRIEN. Q. The policeman took you to see
him? 4. Yes-there was no other boy with him-the policeman told me I
was going to see if it was the boy that had thrown me down and robbed me
I had never seen either of the boys before I had seen Wicks before in the
Walworth-road I did not recollect him when I saw him in the field, but it
came to my mind afterwards.
