 1253.  JAMES EASTMAN was indicted for stealing, on the 18th of May , 2 books, value 7s., the goods of George Collier , his master.
 GEORGE COLLIER. The prisoner was in my employ. I am a  bookbinder to the British and Foreign Bible Society, in Earl-street. These two Bibles were mine, as being in their employ - these are them.

 1256.  JOHN ASHBY was indicted for embezzlement.
 SECOND COUNT, for stealing two half-crowns, the monies of  William Henry Gardiner.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 WILLIAM HENRY GARDINER. I am an  ironmonger, and live in Norton-falgate. The prisoner was my shopman- he was to put what money he received into the till. In consequence of suspicion I marked 28s. in half-crowns and shillings - it was marked on the tail; there was no more in the till. I went to Mr. Hems, and gave him four half-crowns and two shillings, marked money, to send a person to my shop, to lay out, on the morning of the 1st of May - there was then 16s. in the till. I went out, leaving the prisoner in care of the shop - I examined the till about ten minutes past nine o'clock, and found 34s. in it; I had seen other customers at the shop; there was but 23s. of the marked money. I went to Hems - he returned with me; I called the prisoner into the counting-house, and told him I missed money out of the till; he appeared confused, and denied having taken any - I called Hems in, who said to him, "I know all about it - come pull out what money you have in your pocket;" he then pulled out two of the half-crowns which I had marked; Hems said, "I can swear to these;" he had other money, not marked in his pocket.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Had you put any marked money into the till the day before? A. Yes, and spoken to Hems that day, but I found all right that day. I cannot say what other customers had paid.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Was there any gold or notes in the till? A. No; he could not have given change.
   JOHN HEMS. I am a friend of Gardiner's. I received from him some marked money on the 1st of May; I gave Pitches some of it, and afterwards saw two half-crowns found on the prisoner; they were what I gave Pitches.
 Cross-examined. Q. Do you know a young man who lives in the house? A. I believe there is one.
   THOMAS PITCHES. I went, by desire of Hems, between eight and nine o'clock in the morning, to Gardiner's shop; I paid the prisoner four half-crowns and two shillings marked, as Hems gave me, and one shilling of my own money - I made a purchase.
   BENJAMIN BEAVIS. I am a headborough. I received this money, in the prisoner's presence, from Hems; it has been in my possession ever since; the prisoner said he had put some other money into the till instead of the two half-crowns.
 MR. GARDINER. It was his duty to put the money into the till immediately. I know these half-crowns by the marks.
 MR. BARRY. Q. Did not you find some small silver in your till? A. Yes.
     NOT GUILTY.
