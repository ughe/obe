1759. JAMES SMITH was indicted for that Henry Harland, the younger,
being in the dwelling-house of John Harland, did steal 1 watch, value 46.; and
1 coat, 21. 101.; the goods of Richard Harland : 1 mantle, 1l. ; 1 shawl, 11.; the
goods of John Harland : 1 waistcoat, 58. ; 1 belt, 28. ; 3 scarfs, 6s. ; 1 shawl,
28. 6d.; and 3 shirt-collars, 6d. : and having committed the said felony,
did feloniously and burglariously break out of the same dwelling-house: that
the prisoner did incite him the felony aforesaid to do and commit, and felo-
niously received the same goods, well knowing them to have been stolen.
Mr. Ryland conducted the Prosecution.
RICHARD HARLAND. My father's name is John Harland-I have a brother
named John, and another named Henry-we all live with my father, in
Union-row, Old Kent-road. My brother Henry went to bed on the night of
the 5th of July-in the morning I was awoke, and he was gonebe had slept
in the same bed with me -I missed a watch and a coat- I took a deal of paine
to find Henry--I saw him again about ten days after, about the Wednesday
week--he was then in custody--the policeman produced the articles to me
which I had missed.
COURT. Q. What time did you go to bed that night? A. Between
eleven and twelve o'clock-all was then shut up safe-our house is in the
parish of St. Mary, Newington--the watch was worth 41., and the coat 12. 108.
-there were other things gone belonging to my father and mother, to the
amount of 71. or 81.
PRISCILLA HARLAND. I am the wife of John Harland. On the morning
of the 6th of July I missed a great-coat of my busband's, a mantle and shawl
of my own, and various articles belonging to my son John.
John HARLAND. I am a son of the last witness. On the morning of the 6th
of July I missed three coats, four or five pairs of trowsers, five waistcoat,
and some other things I saw a handkerchief produced by Kerney-hal
