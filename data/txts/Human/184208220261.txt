   MRS. BURROWS re-examined . I always spell my name Margaret  —I never signed my name at the bottom of a blank piece of paper with a stamp on it—I never signed my name to any paper for the prisoner, the corner of which was burnt off, and then signed it again—I never gave him a receipt of any kind—I never received 12 l . from him, nor any money whatever, not a farthing.
   MR. PHILLIPS called HENRY DUNFORD. I am an auctioneer and house-agent, and live at Greenwich. I know the prosecutrix from a very slight acquaintance since the 9th of July—I was employed in the first distress at that time, as the appraiser—about the 10th or 11th of this month I saw her at her own house—she said to me, "If Hughes will give up the receipt and pay me the difference, I won't hurt him; if Hughes behaves well to me, and I have 10 l ., I will go into the country and squash  the concern"—I cannot say that she said, "You know, Mr. Dunford, a word before the Grand Jury will do it"—this was at the second distress—she never told me that the prisoner owed her 12 l .—I was present when the agreement was made to redeem the goods—that was the time this occurred.
   MR. PAYNE . Q.  When was this conversation between you and Mrs. Bur rows? A.  On the 9th, 10th, or 11th of August—I did not take particular notice—I had occasion to go there—I had a conversation with her respect ing the moving a sippy-table, and in the conversation this came out.
