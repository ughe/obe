OLD COURT.-Friday, Feb. 4th., 1853.
PRESENT-Mr. BARON ALDERSON ; MR. BARON MARTIN; and Sir Joux
MUSGROVE, Bart., Ala.
Before Mr. Baron Alderson and the First Jury.
308. EDWARD JOHN LAMUDE was indicted for unlawfully, know.
ingly, and by false pretences, obtaining from William Henry Ward an order
for the payment of 31. 153. ; with intent to defraud.
MR. PARRY in opening the case stated, that the proseoutor being desirous of
purchasing a gold chain, and observing one marked "fine gold, 31. 158.," in
the window of Mr. Benson, jeweller, of Cornhill, went in and purchased it of the
defendant, who was shopman to Mr. Benson ; at the same time inquiring if &
was all gold; to this the defendant answered it was not gold of the finest quality,
but that it was all gold, and upon that the purchase was concluded. Upon ita
being afterwards examined by an assayer the proportion of gold was found to be
so small that the chain was said to be worth no more than 298., workmanship
included.
MR. BARON ALDERSON inquired whether there was any evidence of know-
ledge on the part of the defendant. MR. PARRY replied that the position de
held as shopman was primd facie evidence of that; conpled with the fact that
on being taken into custody he refused to give his name. MR. BARON ALDERSON
did not consider that sufficient ; if any one was to blame it was the master; #
had been held in Rex v. Bower, that er posure for sale where the article taas
sold by the shopman was exposure by the master; and that seemed precisely
this case ; besides, here there was no active pretence by the defendant, the
prosecutor was the person seeking the purchase, and this was in his opinion the
distinction between this case and others, which were referred to by Mr. Parry
in argument.
SIR FREDERICK THESIGER (who with Mr. Clarkson appeared for the
defendant, called the attention of the COURT to a marginal note in Rex
Bower, which stated, not that the party was indictable, but that the statue
12 Geo. 2 applied, which statute made the master liable to a penalty.
MR. BARON ALDERSON expressed considerable doubt whether this could be
maintained as a false pretence; it might amount to a fraudulent bargain, but
it was not of necessity an indietable offence, unless it affected the public at large
ultra the individual with whom the bargair was made; he would, however, hear
the evidence if it was desired. MR. PARRy believed he had stated all the
facts. MR. BARON ALDERSON put it to the JURY whether upon these facts they
could be satisfied, that the defendant must necessarily have known the real
nature of the article sold, so as to make him eriminally liable. The JURT
replied that it was too doubtful, and found a verdict of Not GUILTY.
