On the 9th of January I saw the prisoner take a tea-pot from the window ledge in the tap-room, and go out of the house with it—I told Mr. Aris. ROBERT ARIS. I keep this house. The tea-pot was on a shelf in the tap-room, on the 9th of January I missed it—I have known the prisoner as a gipsy.
   Prisoner's Defence.  I went to have something to drink—this woman was intoxicated, and they were all righting—she accused me of taking the tea-pot, but I never saw it. GUILTY. Aged 20.— Confined Three Months.
 609. MARIA LEGGETT was indicted for stealing, on the   7th of January , 1 hat, value 7 s  .; and 1 handkerchief, value 3 s  .; the goods of Edward Butcher , from his person. EDWARD BUTCHER. I am a plasterer. On the 7th of January I was very tipsy, and lost my hat and handkerchief, but have no recollection of the circumstance at all. JAMES BRADLEY. I am a policeman. On the 7th of January, between one and two o'clock in the morning, I found the prisoner in George-street with something in her apron—I asked what she had got—she refused to tell me, and was walking on—I asked again, and she said it was her old man's hat, who was at home in bed drunk—she then said it was her cousin's hat—I took her to the station-house. WILLIAM PACK. I am a policeman. I found the prosecutor very drunk this night, and took him to the station-house—I found the handker chief which he identified. NOT GUILTY.
 610. MICHAEL KELLY was indicted for stealing, on the   1st of Ja nuary , 3 gowns, value 3 l  . 10 s  .; and 1 shirt, value 2 s  .; the goods of Daniel Kelly : and JEMIMA KEMBLE , for feloniously receiving the same, well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.; to which
   KELLY  pleaded GUILTY. Aged 17. MARY KELLY. I am the wife of Daniel Kelly, and live in Wentworth-street—the prisoner Kelly is my son, and lived at home with us. On the 1st of January I missed three gowns out of his bed-room—these are them—( looking at them. ) JAMES GOLDER. I am a pawnbroker. One of these gowns was pledged with me by Kemble in the name of Ann Williams, No. 5, Crown-street—I am sure she is the person. JAMES CLARK. I am a policeman. On the 2nd of January I took the Male prisoner into custody—next day I went to a house in Clarence-lane, St. Giles's, and found the female prisoner in a room in bed—this gown was hanging on a chair in the room—I asked her whose it was—she said her own—that she brought it from her sister's at Peckham—I took her to the station-house, and the prosecutrix came and owned the gown.
   Kemble's Defence.  I met this young man—he followed me up and down for a quarter of an hour, and said, "Where are you going?"—I said, "What have you got in your bag?"—he said, "If you will take me up stairs I will show you"—he showed me the gowns—he gave them to me on the Wed nesday night—I pawned one next morning for 4 s ., and gave him the money, and he gave me this silk frock as a present.
   KEMBLE  — GUILTY. Aged 17.—Both Confined Six Months.
