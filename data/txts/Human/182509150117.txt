   JOSEPH COOPER. I am an officer of Queen-square. I apprehended Croft on Saturday night, the 3d of September, in Peter-street - he was then dressed in a blue coat and dark trousers. I found this bit of crape in the crown of his hat, folded up, and pinned with black pins. I have seen him wear black crape on his hat of the same width as this. I asked him where he had been all day, and he said at his mother's. I asked where that was - he said at No. 52, Duck-lane. I went there, but could not learn that he had been there. I afterwards asked if he knew Sullivan - he said he did not. About one o'clock on Sunday I apprehended Sullivan, secreting himself in Mr. Sullivan's premises, the boat-builder, at Millbank - he had got over the garden pales, and was behind some articles on the premises. I asked if he knew George Croft - he said No, he did not. I asked him again, and he admitted that he did know him, but had not seen him for three months. Sullivan had the same coat on then as he has now, and a black hat. I knew both the prisoners before. I found at Croft's lodgings a letter signed "George Croft." A girl of the name of Mary Alexander lives there.
 Prisoner SULLIVAN. I was walking up and down the field - I was not concealed. Witness. Yes, he was concealed - there is no way to get into that field but over a paling.
 CROFT - GUILTY. Aged 17.
 SULLIVAN - GUILTY. Aged 14.

 1538.  WILLIAM RUSSELL was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of August , sixteen pieces of deal boards, value 10 s., the goods of Thomas Trott.
 MR. ADOLPHUS conducted the prosecution.
 THOMAS TROTT. I am a  builder. The prisoner was employed by me as a carpenter, in some buildings in the New North Road, Hoxton. I found all the materials, and gave him charge of them. I told him not to let any thing go off the premises on any account, as I would prosecute the first person I found taking any thing, having been a loser before. I had occasion to take the prisoner before a Justice, for leaving his work (I think on the 1st of September) and after that some discoveries were made. I had had no suspicion of him before. I had had things hinted to me, but I did not know the person.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had not the young man been some time in your employ? A. Yes - he employed a person under him, whom he turned away, but I did not oblige him to do so; I believe he did so by keeping part of his money back - he told me he turned him away because he was not able to earn the money he charged me for him. I had not requested two men to take the prisoner's place, and said I would turn him away before I had him before a Magistrate - I there charged him with leaving his work; the case was heard by the Magistrate - I believe it was not dismissed. Mr. Twyford asked me, when I went again, whether I would go into the higher charge. I swear I never heard the Magistrate say "I don't like your turning round in this manner, and preferring another charge when you find the first won't do." I saw Mr. Harmer there. The prisoner went to Clerkenwell for some days, and was then admitted to bail. When I went to make the first charge against him I did not know of this. I think he left my service on the 29th of August, and I think I made the charge on the 2d of September, but I cannot be certain, unless I had my book here. John Provan had left my service about three weeks before Russell did. I did not state at the office that Provan had told me Russell had robbed me - I said he had hinted that some one was robbing me, and I thought it was Russell.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you employ Provan, or did Russell? A. He did; I had nothing to do with him at all, but to pay him his bills. I had no distinct information as to carry conviction home to the prisoner, before I took him to the Magistrate. I gave directions to have my property better watched, and it was after that I received this information. Kearney is the person I set to watch.
   JOSEPH CLIMPTON. I was employed at Mr. Trott's building by the prisoner, to do task work for him, and when my master and he fell out, master set me to work after he left him. I saw the prisoner and the apprentice take away fifteen or sixteen of the mitre strings of the stair-case, at breakfast time, one day in August last, but I cannot recollect when. I sat on the bench, eating my breakfast at the time, but I did not say any thing, as I thought they were fire wood. On the next day, which was a Wednesday, Mr. Trott sent me to work in Aldermanbury. I did not tell what I saw till the prisoner was in custody.
 JURY. Q. You are a carpenter - did you ever know an instance in which the carpenter was prevented from taking such pieces away as chips? A. No - I have done it myself hundreds of times, and never was denied. I come here to tell the truth.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you ever do so when you had received an express order not to do it? A. No, of course I did not.
