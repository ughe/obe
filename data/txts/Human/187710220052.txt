WILLIAM HUTT( City Detective ). On 18th October I went to this billiard-room to see a gentleman—I saw the prisoner take a cue up and attempt to strike a ball—the marker saw that the other gentleman did not intend to play, and took the cue out of the prisoner's hand, and said "I will have no nonsense"—the prisoner walked up to a young man, and said "Where is my half sovereign?"—the young man seemed frightened, and said "I have no money of yours"—the marker stepped between them, grasped him by the throat, and he was taken downstairs—afterwards they said that he had a revolver, and I went down behind Mr. Brown—he attempted to stop Mr. Collins, who pushed him out on the pavement—some coppers fell out of his pocket which were picked up—I heard Black man say "He has got a revolver"—Brown and the potman seized him, and Brown took the revolver from his hand—I seized his right hand, twisted him round, and said "I am a police officer; I shall take you in custody"—he said "D——you, I will shoot you"—I said "I will bet you don't"—I did not see whether the revolver was cocked or not—the charge was drawn at the station—there were seven chambers loaded with bullets—he said that he was very sorry he had not settled some of us, and he would do it, and that he would shoot me—he was perfectly sober, but no doubt he had been drinking. JOHN EGAN( Police Inspector ). The prisoner was brought to Seething Lane station—I read the charge to him, and he said "I wish I had shot somebody"—I drew the charges; these are the bullets ( produced ).
   The prisoner in his Defence stated that he had just landed from the United States, where he was in the habit of carrying a pistol, and thought it safer to keep it in his pocket than to leave it on a table, and having taken two or three glasses of stout, which he was not accustomed to, he did not know what occurred, and that the pistol must have got cocked in the struggle. GUILTY — Twelve Months' Imprisonment.
