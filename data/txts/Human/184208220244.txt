 2524. EDWARD GIFFORD and SARAH ANN HUNT were in dicted for feloniously receiving, on the   6th of January , 124 yards of silk, value 11 l  .; 12 yards of merino, value 12 s  .; 33 yards of ribbon, value 10 s  .; and 120 boot-laces, value 2 s  .; the goods of John Palk Griffin , well knowing them to have been stolen; against the Statute, &c.
   MR. LUCAS conducted the Prosecution . CHARLES JAMES COVERLY GRIFFIN. I am the son of John Palk Griffin. On the 5th or 6th of January last, when I left ray father's pre mises in Ironmonger-lane, Cheapside, I locked the door, and left it secure—I came the next morning, and found the premises had been broken open, the policeman there, the property in confusion, and property to the amount of 600 l . or 700 l . taken away—amongst it were two pieces of silk, twelve yards of merino, thirty-three yards of ribbon, and 120 boot-laces—a por tion of each are here now, and I can swear to them as ours.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  How do you know them? A.  I know this silk by our mark being on it in my own writing—I know it had not been sold, because it was in the warehouse on the night of the robbery. JOHN GRAY( police-sergeant C  14.) On the 4th of July, from infor mation, I went to No. 5, Warwick-street, Charing-cross, and found Hunt there—I asked her if Mr. Gifford was at home—she said he was not, Mr. and Mrs. Gifford were gone to Gravesend—I said I had a warrant to search the house, and produced it—I asked her if she had any keys of the upper part of the house—she said she had not, Mr. and Mrs. Gifford had taken them with them to Gravesend—I then went up stairs—shortly after I let Gifford in—he had a brown paper parcel, which I opened—it contained these two pieces of silk—I afterwards found some keys lying on the side board in the front parlour—I said, "What keys are these?"—one of them opened a cupboard in the front parlour, where I found some ribbon, and the other the attic, where I found an immense mass of property—these are the boot-laces and the ribbon—I found some of them in the attic, and some in the front parlour, and in a writing-desk in the back parlour I found a gold chain, two brooches, two diamond rings, a watch-hook, a seal, a pearl ring, a diamond pin, and in the front parlour I found ten eye-glasses, a locket, a brooch, and a pair of earrings—this blood-stone ring I found on Handley's person (see p. 907)—there were some other
