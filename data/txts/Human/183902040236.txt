KENT CASES
   Before Mr. Recorder.
 826. GEORGE BAILEY was indicted for stealing, on the   22nd of January , 11/2 yard of carpet, value 5 s  ., the goods of Richard Etty ; to which he pleaded GUILTY — Confined Three Months.
 827. JOHN SAWYER was indicted for stealing, on the   15th of January , 1 pair of boots, value 3 s  . 6 d  . the goods of John Esterford , his master. JOHN ESTERFORD. I am a shoemaker, and live at Royal Hill, Green wich. The prisoner was in my employ in the beginning of January—I missed boots and shoes; and one day I missed a pair while he was waiting for his work, and found them in his basket, as he was going out of the shop with them—I found another pair of my boots at the pawnbroker's.
   Cross-examined by MR. HORRY . Q.  Was any thing said about your overlooking it if he confessed? A.  Yes, I said I would overlook about the shoes—the boots were taken first—I have given him work for a year and a half—he calls at the shop for it, and goes away with it—I am quite certain I did not give him these boots to make—I never gave him more than two pairs to make at once—these boots were all ready for sale—they did not want any thing done to them—I did not give them to him as a pattern to make others by—I may have done such a thing, but I never gave him these—I saw them hanging on the door-post before he came into the shop, and when he was gone I missed them—he promised to give me the duplicates of these boots—he gave me one, but I could not find the other. JOHN LEVICK. I am shopman to Mr. Nash, a pawnbroker, at Green wich. On the 15th of January the prisoner pawned these boots for 1 s .—he looked like a shoemaker, and they often pledge work.
 (  Property produced and sworn to. )
 (The prisoner received a good character.) GUILTY. Aged 22.— Confined Two Months.
 (There were two other indictments against him.)
 828. ELIZABETH JONES  , alias    Taylor, was indicted for stealing, on the   13th of January , 2 candlesticks, value 2 s  .; 1 towel, value 2 d  .; 1 tablecloth, value 2 d  .;1 fork, value 1 d  .; the goods of Joseph Wilson : and 1 handkerchief, value 1 s  . 6 d  ., the goods of George Henry Wilson . GEORGE HENRY WILSON. I am a labourer, and live at Woolwich with my parents. On Saturday night, the 12th of January, the prisoner asked me to give her a night's lodging—I took her home, and she slept with me down stairs—my father and mother slept up stairs—when I awoke the next morning the prisoner was gone, and I missed my handkerchief—I had not given it to her. DANIEL RIORDAN( police-constable  R 207.) I met the prisoner in Green wich market-place, on Sunday morning, the 13th of January, about nine o'clock—I observed a bundle under her arm, and asked if what she had belonged to herself—she said yes, it had belonged to her some time—I found she had two candlesticks, a tablecloth, a towel, and frock. JANE WILSON. I am the wife of Joseph Wilson, a pensioner. These articles are his, except the handkerchief, which belongs to my son—the things were safe on Saturday night, at eleven o'clock.
