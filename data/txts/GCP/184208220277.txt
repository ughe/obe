John Brown. I am a pensioner from the army, and live in Upper
Crown-street, Westminster. I am not of any trade or occupation last
Monday evening, I was on the road that leads by the side of Ken-
nington Common, passing in the rear of those who were standing on the
footpath, looking over the palings enclosing the Common I saw the pri-
soner, not in the dress he is now in, but in a smock-frock, draw his hand
with a stone, and throw it into the Common at the police did not see
what sort of a stone it was a policeman immediately jumped over the
palings like a shot-the prisoner went a little to his left to conceal himself
behind the mob- the policeman immediately seized him by the collar with
his left hand-the prisoner resisted violently the mob journeyed about,
shoved both the policeman and the prisoner about, and shoved them, I be-
lieve, into the middle of the road, where I saw the prisoner and the police-
man down on the road, the policeman uppermost, and the prisoner covered
over, with a considerable quantity of blood on him, which I thought flowed
from himself, but which I found afterwards was from the policeman's head
-I told the policeman that that was the man that threw the stone I was
junted about with the mobI drew a cane, which I had in my hand, in a
menacing manner, at the mob, which kept them back till two or three po-
