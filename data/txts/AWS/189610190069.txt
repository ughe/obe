THIRD COURT. OURT.Monday, -Monday, October 26th, 1896.
Before Mr. Common Serjeant.
793. GEORGE JAOKSON (27), and NORAH JAOKSON (28),
Stealing a mare, van, and other articles, the goods of Charles Clements.
Second CountReceiving the same.
Mr. SELis Prosecuted.
CHaRLEs CLEMENT3. I deal in greengrocery at 4, Prince's Yard,
Prince's Road, Notting Hill-on the morning of September 12th I went
to Covent Garden with my horse and van-I went into the market to
make a purchase, leaving my horse and cart in charge of a woman, and
when I returned about seven o'clock they gone, and the woman too-
IBaw her afterwards-the van was laden with potatoes, beans, scarlet run-
ners, and other vegetables-th cart and horse were mine, and worth about
E15: altogether the value of the things stolen was about E18 10s.-two
hundredweight of potatous cost me 5s. the beans were worth 1s. 6d. per
basketI got my horse and cart back again, but I lost the contents, and
the cart was all broken, and the horse's leg was cut behind-when I
found the things gone, I made a statement to the policenext morning
a policeman came to me and took me to Rotherhithe, where I found the
horse and van in Bashan's Yard-next day I went to Bow Street, and
charged the prisoners with stealing it-I had not seen them before.
HENRy HowE. I am a greengrocer, of 336, Southwark Bridge Road-
on Saturday, September 12th, I was at Spitalfields Market-whe I got
home at 8.20 I found two one-hundredweight bags of potatoes standing
against my door-in the afternoon the prisoners called with a horse and
van-George came in my shop, and my missus said, This is the gentle
man that fetched the two bags of potatoes in "I said, 4 How much do
you want for them bags of potatoes mhe said, 3s. each said,
You take them out; don't want them "he forced me to have them
eventually I gave him 1s. 9d. a bag, and when I paid him, he said, "I
have another bag outside in my van I have had a break-down "I
went and looked at the van, and it had broken down, and I took com-
passion on him and bought the other bag-Norah said they had had an
accident; she trusted her little brother to harness the horse, and he had
harnessed it wrong, which caused it to kickthe horse's hocks were all
cut to pieces, and bleeding-they came to my shop at eight 'clock at night,
and asked if I would stable the horse and dvan-I said No; I could not put it
in with my pony he would kick it-their coming back raised my suspicion for
that it was stolen, and I went to Mr. Bashan, and got him to stable it
the night, and I saw the name on the cart, and went to the Police-station
-on the Sunday morning Clements came, and I took him to the stable
where it was.
