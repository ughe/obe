Yes - I do not know whether my evidence was read over to me; I believe it was; I did not see what was in the sack - it was given out to us as corn; I abused Kent, because the corn was not given to the horses - I had no money; they offered me beer several times, but I refused it - I had one pint with them; I did not like to make a disturbance about it, as I had only been there a few days - I had no quarrel with them about a jacket, it was with another servant; I did not mention it till after that - we had the nose-bag full for the horses to eat.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Had you any quarrel with them? A. No; the horses had nothing more than was in the corn-bin - Colopy was with me.
   JOHN COLOPY. I was employed to drive a team for Mr. McIntosh. I saw Hale and Kent give Haynes two sacks of corn, at the George, at Brompton; he took it into the stable, and brought the sacks out again.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you see the sacks filled? A. Yes - 5s. 6d each would be a fair price for them; I knew it was for the horses - I did not like to tell master, but I did three days after; I only see the foreman once a week - I had part of the beer which was bought with the money - I cannot say how much I drank; they were taken up about a week after.
   THOMAS POWELL. I am ostler at Mr. McIntosh's stable. I gave out two sacks, each containing three bushels, every day for the horses; and did so on the 8th of May - it was chaff and corn mixed; Mr. McIntosh's name was on the cart.
 Cross-examined. Q. How long did the men remain in the employ? A. I do not know - I knew nothing of it till master told me.
   RICHARD STARKEY. I know this corn - this mixture is worth 4s. or 5s. each sack.
 KENT'S Defence. The witness is a false man - he lost a jacket, and said if we did not give it up he would go and tell of the robbery which he never saw.
     NOT GUILTY.

 1322.  DOMINICK FEELEY was indicted for bigamy.
 MR. CLARKSON conducted the prosecution.
   SARAH SOUTH. I am a widow, and live on Saffron-hill. The prisoner lived with me - he was married to Jane Cowley; they lived together at my house - they went out to be married and slept at my house after that; she went by his name, and they lived together for five or six months - the prisoner asked me to drink with him and his wife; I am sure he called her his wife.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. Was it not a Mrs. Hyde you saw? A. No - her name was Cowley; she is outside the Court now; they left me to keep a public-house, but I have not seen her for ten or twelve years till lately.
   JOSEPH MOULD. I am parish-clerk of St. Andrew, Holborn. I have the register of the marriage of Jane Cowley and Thomas Feeley, by licence, on the 22d of February, 1812.
   CHARLES FISHER SAVIGNEY. I am beadle of Holborn, and know Jane Feeley. In November, 1812, I apprehended the prisoner for deserting his wife; she applied for relief to the parish - I relieved her; she is now at the door - the prisoner acknowledged her as his wife, and was committed to prison.
 Cross-examined. Q. Have you seen much of his wife since? A. No - not till latterly.
   SARAH GRIFFITHS. I am a widow- the prisoner was clerk to my late husband, who kept the Cooper's Arms, public-house, Crown-street, Finsbury. I was married to the prisoner at Shoreditch Church on Shrove Tuesday; he passed as a single man for some time before we were married.
 Cross-examined. Q. I believe you and your husband did not live on the best of terms? A. Why I found he had another wife, but I would not appear against him; he died six years ago - I made him support me, for he had 2000l. of money; I have not been reluctant to prosecute the prisoner - I never lived with a Mr. Chippendale.
 Q. How long did you live with the prisoner before you married him? A. That is not a fair question; I did not live with him; he paid me a sovereign a week.
 Q. Did you not cohabit with him? A. Why as many of the first ladies in the land have done I did do it; I knew he had a wife; when I found that out we parted beds, but lived together for about a month - I discovered it six weeks ago; I have no children - he had no money with me. I have been arrested - the prisoner owes me nearly 200l.; I  keep the Coopers' Arms now; the license is not in the prisoner's name.
   GEORGE YARROW. I am clerk of Shoreditch; I have a register of the prisoner's marriage to S. Griffiths on the 19th of February, 1828. - (read.)
 Prisoner's Defence. This is a complete conspiracy against me - this woman will swear though a nine inch board - she owes me 100l. now.
     GUILTY. Aged 34. Confined Three Months.
