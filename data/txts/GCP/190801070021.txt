JOHN HARRIS, secretary to Clayton and Shuttleworth, Lincoln.
On April 4, 1905, I received this letter addressed from 104, Stern-
held Avenue, Streatham. "I should feel much obliged if you would
recommend a form of stockbrokers, London or Provincial. Awaiting!
with interest your early and favourable reply, yours faithfully, John
· Earle." I had never heard of "John Earle." In reply I wrote, "I
have received your letter of yesterday, from which I understand it
is your wish to have the names of brokers who are dealing freely in
tha shares of the company. If you address your inquiries to Mesers.
Vickers and Son, Lincoln, Mr. R. N. Whaley, Lincoln, or Messrs.
Blake and Wheeler, Leicester, I have no doubt you will be able to
transact business satisfactorily." A day or two afterwards I had a
letter from Messrs. Vickers thanking me for having mentioned their
name,
Cross-examined. I frequently receive letters like that addressed to
me by Earle. It is quite a usual letter and it is quite a usual thing
for the secretary of a company to give the names of brokere dealing
in his company's shares. In my letter I did not recommend at all.
I simply mentioned the names. I thought the writer wanted to deal
genuinely in the shares of the company. I did not recommend the
writer as a reliable person to Messrs. Vickers. My letter does not
infer that he was. I knew nothing about him.
