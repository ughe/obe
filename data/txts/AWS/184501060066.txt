SAMUEL LEE. I am potman at the Fountain. I remember the morn-
ing of the 23rd of Dec. the prisoner came to the house-I saw him put a
half-crown down to my master- thought he had rum or shrub-it was a
coloured liquor-Mr. Farnham doubled up the half-crown-I saw it was
a bad one, and it was put on the shelf behind the bar-the liquor was paid
for in halfpence-th half-crown remained on the shelf till it was given to
the policeman.
FRANCEs ANN FOsTER. I am the wife of John Foster we keep a
lodging-house in Neweastle-court, Strand. On the 23rd of Dec., at near
three o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came, with a female in his com-
pany-h asked where I could get some gin, and whether he could have a
bed for the night-I said I did not know whether the night-house Was
open, but I would take his order-he said he would have a quartern of gin
-I said he had better have sixpenny worth, as there were two of them-
he said no, he would have a quartern-h dropped a half-crown on the
floor by his foot-I took the decanter in my hand and the half-crown, and
went to the Fountain-th half-crown was refused, and I took it back-I
am certain it was the one I took from the prisoner-] had no other money
-somebody went back with me from the Fountain, and the prisoner was
taken into custody-] bit the half-crown, and gave it to the officer.
Cross-eramined. Q. You said you did not know whether you could get
the gin, but you would go to the night-house, and every body knew that
that was the Fountain A. Yes.
