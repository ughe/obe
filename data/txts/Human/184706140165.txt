1497. MARY ANN HOY was jndicted for stealing 1 shawl, value 30 s  .; 1 bonnet, 10 s  .; 2 petticoats, 2 s  .; 2 pairs of drawers, 1 s  . 6 d  .; and 1 pair of boots, 2 s  .; the goods of Esther Cohen : 1 shawl, 30 s  .; 1 gown, 4 s  .; and 1 handkerchief, 6 d  .; the goods of Charlotte Cohen , her mistress. CHARLOTTE CHHEN. I am single, and live in New Gravel-lane. The prisoner was my servant—on the 28th of May I told her to get up and open the shop—in about half an hour my sister came down, and told me some thing—I found that the prisoner was gone—I had not told her to go—my boxes were broken open—they had been shut—one was locked the night before—all the things were lying about the floor—I missed two shawls, a dress, two petticoats, and a silk handkerchief—one shawl belonged to Esther Cohen, my sister—she lived with me—this shawl produced is hers—the other things are mine—I have had this dress three years—I had only bought the shawls on Monday—I made these two petticoats—I had not had them a week in the house—they had been safe in the boxes—we went after the pri soner about eight o'clock in the morning, and caught her at Barking about five o'clock in the evening. THOMAS JOHN BLAKE( police-constable K  137.) I went with the pro secutrix to Barking, took the prisoner, and told her I wanted her, for stealing things at Cohen's, in New Gravel-lane—she said, "Yes, I stole the things; I will take you where they are"—she took me to her house, in Barking—I found some there—she put her hand into her pocket, and gave this duplicate for some of the things.
   Prisoner.  I have lived with those girls seven months, and kept them by my prostitution. CHARLOTTE COHAN re-examined.  It is not so, on my oath. GUILTY. Aged 17.— Confined Six Months .
 1498. SELINA ALLEN was indicted for stealing 2 coats, value 1 l  . 5 s  .; 1 set of curtains, 8 s  .; 3 yards of silk, 7 s  .; seven Knives, 18 d  .; 7 forks, 1 s  .; 2 pairs of shoes, 1 l  . 8 s  .; 1 waistcoat, 2 s  .; 4 blinds, 16 s  .; and 1 brush; 1 s  .; the goods of George Macnalley , her master. GEORGE MACNALLEY. I live in Park-street, Camden-town. The prisoner was in my employ six years—about a month ago I missed a pair of boots—they were my son's, George Robert Macnalley's—I asked the prisoner where my son's boots were—she said they were sent over to the shoemaker's to be repaired—I also missed my own boots—she told me she knew nothing of them—I told her if she did not tell me where they were I would call a police man and take her up. BENJAMIN BADDELEY( police-constable S  248.). On the 4th of June I took the prisoner in charge at Mr. Macnalley's—I told her what it was for—she wished to go to the water-closet—I would not allow her—I followed her into a back room—the prosecutor said he should press the charge—I took the prisoner to the station—she was there searched by Mr. Doss, who gave me these duplicates. ANN AMELIA DOSS. I live at the Police-station, in Albany-street—I search female prisoners. I searched the prisoner, and found forty-five duplicates in a bag, between her petticoat and her dress—she said she had pledged some, and another woman some—I gave them to Baddeley. JAMES MARLOW. I live with Mr. Castle, a pawnbroker, in High-street, Camden-town. I produce a brush, pawned in Dec. last—this is the ticket of it—I produce a coat and some white window-curtains—I find duplicates of them.
