JOHN EATON and CAROLINE EATON , breaking and entering the dwelling-house of John Purslow , and stealing therein 86 yards of cloth, 2 coats, and 16 waistcoat-pieces, value 28 l . 16 s ., his goods. MR. COOPER conducted the Prosecution. SARAH PURSLOW. I am the wife of John Purslow, a tailor, of 6, Upper King-street, Bloomsbury. On the night of 22d Feb., I fastened up the shop—I saw the shop-door last at eight o'clock next morning—about twenty-five minutes past eight my daughter called me—I found the shop open and everything taken from the counter.
   Cross-examined by MR. PAYNE . Q.  Had not you opened the shop door that morning? A.  No, I found it open about ten minutes after I came down—the shutters were not taken down—my husband was up-stairs—he is not here—the property has not been traced. SARAH PURSLOW. I am the daughter of the last witness. On 23d Feb., about half-past eight, I opened the passage-door and saw the female prisoner—site asked if my father Wanted a waistcoat-maker—I went up to ask him, came down, and told her "No"—she said, "Thank you," and shut the door—she looked very stout, and part of her gown was spread over the handle of the door—in about five minutes I found the door wide open, and called my mother down.
   Cross-examined. Q.  When you saw a stout woman at Marylebone, did you say you knew her by her bonnet and breast? A.  Yes; I had a good view of her face, and am quite sure she is the party. WILLIAM HARRIES. I am a sawyer. On 23d Feb., about half-past eight, I was taking down my shutters, and saw the male prisoner coming out of Mr. Purslow's shop with a bundle of clothes, and give it to a woman under an archway—I could see two rolls of cloth, and part of a coat—I followed them as far as Orange-street.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you ever seen the man before? A.  No, but I cannot be mistaken in him—I did not tell Mr. Purslow—I thought they were parties who worked for him—I did not notice his eye—the right side of his lace was to me—( prisoner's left eye was injured ).
