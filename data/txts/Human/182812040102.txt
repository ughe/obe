   GEORGE HOGBURN. I am a labourer, and lodged at Pavey's with Humphreys. I went to bed about ten o'clock on the night of the 15th of November - I expected Humphreys home that night; I heard a noise in the course of the night, and thought it was Humphreys coming up to bed quietly, that he should not disturb me- I had pulled my waistcoat off when I went to bed, and tucked my watch inside the sleeve of my waistcoat; I heard the steel seal gingle against an old coin which was fastened to my watch - I then suspected something was going on wrong; I jumped out of bed, and called, "Will, Will," three times - Humphreys did not answer; I suspected it was him; he ran down stairs without saying a word - I ran down stairs after him, and saw a person going out of the window on his belly; two persons outside were pulling him out: I caught hold of his legs, but they pulled him out - he had no shoes on when I caught hold of his legs; there was another man inside the room, standing on the top of an old bedstead while I was pulling his legs, and he hit me on the forehead-they got him clear away; then the other man and I had a tustle together - he caught hold of my throat, and pretty nearly strangled me; it was dark, and I did not know who it was, as not a word was said: after the struggle he jumped out of the window frame, and was pulled out; I lost my watch, two shirts, and a night-cap - my watch was worth about 50s.; it was silver: I examined the premises directly we got a light, and found that they had got in at the front window, they had gone to the back of the house, and took two squares out of the window-frame - I suppose they could not got in that way, but then went round and broke a glass, and opened the front-window by unhasping it; I found Humphreys' hat and handkerchief in the room where they entered, and I found his shoes outside the window - I know them to be his; he did not come home that night to sleep: I was present next morning when he was taken, and he did not deny the hat, shoes and handkerchief being his - he was asked if they were his, and did not say they were or were not; I think he was asked if they were his: he said he was there, and three others whom he named, were with him and told the constable where to take them: he said he came up stairs first, took my watch and gave it to Peat, who was in with him - I have not found it since; I have got one shirt and a night-cap from the watchman.
   JOSEPH GREENER. I am a watchman of Staines which is about a quarter of a mile from Knowle's-green. Early on the morning of the 16th of November I met two men, but on the night of the 15th, between eleven and twelve o'clock, I met four men, who were quite strangers to me, and in the morning, between twelve and one o'clock, I met two persons in Staines - they said, "good night, watchman;" I said good night, and shortly after two others came up - one of them had a bundle under his left arm; I caught hold of it, and asked what he had got - he said his own property; he let go of it, and ran away: it was quite dark, and I do not know who they were - the prosecutor claimed the property in the bundle - I produce a shirt, which Hogburn swore to; I took all the prisoners in custody on the Sunday morning - I was sent for, and took Humphreys near Pavey's house, about eleven o'clock in the morning; he gave him in charge for breaking into his house - he asked me if I thought it would be of any service if he told who the others were, and from his information I took the the other prisoners - he said he was captain of the gang.
   NATHANIEL JONES. I employed Mrs. Pavey as a laundress. I find in this bundle five neck handkerchiefs, two pocket-handkerchiefs, a cotton gown, a table-cloth, and a cotton print - my wife had sent them to Pavey; I cannot swear that from my own knowledge, but I know them to be mine.
   GEORGE HOGBURN. There is a shirt and night-cap of mine in the bundle: they were in the house that night, down in the room where they got in. (Looking at the hat, shoes, and handkerchief) these are Humphreys' hat and handkerchief - they were found in the cottage, and these shoes are his; he wore the hat and handkerchief that day at dinner time - I knew it well before; I used to see him three times a day - I have no doubt of their being his hat and shoes; I know them, because the toes have flat pieces of iron to them; I found them outside the window myself.
   HENRY NORRIS. I am in the employ of William Elston, who rents a stable. I know Peat - he is in the employ of Mr. Harding, a horse-dealer; Elston rents part of the stable, and Peat sleeps there - I locked him in on the night of the 5th of November; Mr. Elston came home after that, about twenty minutes after eleven o'clock, and Peat was gone away: I took Elston's horse to the stable, and he was not there - I saw him next morning, about ten minutes to seven o'clock; I saw his bed - it had not been slept in that night, and he looked as if he had been up all night, I said, "Bill, why, where have you been to?" he said he had been out, but he had soon returned after I had been to the stable and not found him.
   GEORGE LAYTON. From Humphreys' information I assisted in apprehending the other prisoners.
 HUMPHREYS -    GUILTY- DEATH. Aged 17.
 EDMEADS -    NOT GUILTY.
 PEAT -    NOT GUILTY.
 MANNING -    NOT GUILTY.
