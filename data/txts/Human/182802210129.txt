 725.  WILLIAM CLARK, EDWARD WAGHORN, and THOMAS FOWLER were indicted for stealing, on the 26th of January , 1 book, value 3s., the goods of Thomas Batemen.
 THOMAS BATEMAN. I am a  bookseller, and live in Goodge-street, Tottenham-court-road. I lost a book on the 26th of January, but did not miss it till the officers told me of it; it had been inside my window, among some others, for several weeks; I am not certain when I had seen it, but I know it by the label.
   WILLIAM JORDAN. I am an officer. I saw the three prisoners together, in Leather-lane, that Saturday afternoon, about two o'clock; I watched them through several streets, to Gray's Inn-lane, where I met Cadby and Colton - we all followed them for five or six miles, through Camden-town and other places; when they got to Goodge-street, I saw Fowler take this book - the other two were close at his elbow; I told Colton and Cadby, who immediately ran across, and before I crossed they had got the three prisoners into a shop.
   JOSEPH CADBY. I was with Jordan, and followed the prisoners through a number of streets; I saw Fowler take the book, and the other two were close to him; I ran over and took Clark and Fowler into a linen-draper's shop.
   WILLIAM COLTON. I was following the prisoners - I saw Fowler take the book, and give it to Waghorn, who ran off with it, at the end of Goodge-street - I pursued and took him.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 CLARK'S Defence. I was looking for a situation - I saw Fowler, and asked if he knew whether it had gone five o'clock; before he could speak, the officers came, and pushed us into a shop.
 CLARK -    GUILTY. Aged 20.
 WAGHORN -    GUILTY. Aged 21.
 FOWLER -    GUILTY. Aged 24. Transported for Seven Years.
 726.  JOHN ABRAHAMS was indicted for stealing, on the 2d of February , 9 seal-skins, value 27s., the goods of Solomon Phillips.
 SOLOMON PHILLIPS. I am a  seal-skin manufacturer, and live in Fishmonger-alley, Borough, my factory is in Redcross-yard, Borough. On Friday, the 1st of February, I counted my seal-skins, and had seventy - I went home, and did not return till the Sunday morning - I then missed nine skins, but I had before heard that some were missing; I have seen three of them since, which I know to be mine, from the marks on them - each man puts his own particular mark upon his skins.
 Cross-examined by MR. CLARKSON. Q. Was not the prisoner your servant? A. Yes, and up to this time he had a good character - I will take him again.
   MICHAEL HUGHES. I am a watchman. On the morning of the 2d of February, I was in Paternoster-row, Spitalfields, and saw the prisoner with a bundle under his arm; I asked what he had got; he said his work, which he had brought from the Borough, and that he was going to take it to a house in Lamb-street, next door to a dry-salters; I followed him, and as he did not seem to go into any house, I asked him again, where he was going; he said, "Stop, I will tell you - the skins are my father's;" I took hold of them, and he ran away - I pursued, and took him.
   THOMAS HART. I was beadle of the night. On the morning of the 2d of February, the prisoner was brought into the watch-house with these skins, which he said were his father's.
 GUILTY. Aged 17. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Three Months.
 727.  THOMAS FITT was indicted for stealing, on the 17th of January , 12 ozs. weight of tobacco, value 3s., the goods of William Henry Isaac.
 WILLIAM HENRY ISAAC. I am a  tobacconist, and live in Upper York-street. On the 17th of January, between six and seven o'clock in the evening, I received information from some little girls, and pursued the prisoner; I saw him opposite the Yorkshire Stingo public-house, with two or three other persons; I seized him, put my hand to his pocket, and felt this parcel of tobacco; he begged I would not give him in charge; but wished me to go with him to Stingo-lane, and he would hand me over another parcel of tobacco, which he had given to another boy; I refused, but took him into a shop; he gave me the name of the other boy, which was Collier - he wished to go away, and offered to leave his coat; I left him in the shop, and went with my brother to Stingo-lane, but could find no such person; when I returned, he told the officers the name of Collier, and they took him; he said the prisoner had thrown away one packet of tobacco; when I first took him he said he took it from my window, and begged I would not give him in charge - they are packets of Dutch C'naster, and I have brought another to shew the similarity; I had three packets of this sort, and all in blue papers; I have not opened them, but they feel like tobacco - I had them from a very respectable house; I have not any dummies in my shop.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Had you any mark of your own on this parcel, before it was taken? A. No; I did not say I would not hurt him, if he would tell me where any of the other boys lived; nor anything of the kind; I will not swear that this parcel was not mixed with others before I marked it - I marked it in my shop, before it went to the office - it was then laying on my shop-counter - I can only swear I had packets similar to this, and lost packets similar to this.
   RICHARD HARTLEY WALL. I was not the officer who was sent for, but I received this tobacco from Buckbridge, who is now dangerously ill.
 Cross-examined. Q. Before you got it, where had it been kept? A. In the cupboard at the office.
 GUILTY - Aged 16. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined One Month.
