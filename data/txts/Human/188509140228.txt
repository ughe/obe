 917. GEORGE H1CKMAN  (20), Robbery with violence on Philip Humphries , and stealing a watch, his property.
   MR. GREENFIELD Prosecuted; MR. FIRMINGER Defended. PHILIP HUMPHRIES. I am a marble mason, of 142, Kennington Road—on Saturday night, 22nd August, a little after 12, I was in the Lamb✗th Road going home, when the prisoner met me, struck me in my face with his fist, and knocked me down—I fell on my back on the pavement stupefied for a moment—when I got up my watch was gone, and the prisoner had run away—he was brought back by a constable in less than two minutes—I charged him; he was taken to the station—I am sure my watch was safe in my pocket a few minutes before—my coat was open.
   Cross-examined.  I had had something to drink in the Ship, Kenning ton Road, and the City Arms in West Square; no others—I did not have a good deal—I was in bad health, and what I had affected me—I don't remember everything that occurred—I have no recollection of going to a coffee-house in Joyner's Street with a woman that evening—I would not swear I did not—I did not know there was a coffee-house in the street till the following Monday—I had looked at my watch a quarter of an hour before—I walked straight; I did not roll about—I left the public-house at 12—there were clocks in the public-house—I was with nobody in the public-house—here is the mark where the prisoner struck me, and the back of my head was sore afterwards—I know he is the man by the parties that saw it, and I saw him under a lamp—he had a billycock hat on—I did not strike him with my stick—I was not sober.
