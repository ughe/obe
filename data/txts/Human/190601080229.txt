HEAD submitted on behalf of' Willetts that there was no case to go to the Jury, firstly because the proprietorship of the copyrights had not been sufficiently proved by the mere production of the cer  tificates and quoted the Copyright Act 1842, 5 and 6, Vic. chap.  45, sec  tions 11 and  24, which, he submitted, excluded the form of proof of pro  prietorship that had been adopted in this case, a criminal trial; and that the publishers and proprietors of the copyright not having produced the assignments in writing to them of those copyrights, it must be taken that they did not exist; Law v. Routledge  1865 Law Reports, Chancery Appeals, Leyland  v. Stewart , 4, Chancery Division Weldon  v. Dicks  1878, 10 Chancery Division. The learned judge held that the production of the certificate was sufficient evidence of proprietorship. MR.
