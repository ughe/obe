   THOMAS LAING. I am a hair-dresser - the prisoner came to me last Friday morning, to my house in St. Ann's-court, and asked if I wanted any weaving silk, I said, No - he asked if I could tell him where he would sell it - I said, the hair merchants were the most likely people; he said he knew none of them, and if I would endeavour to dispose of it, he should feel obliged. I took it to Mr. Hudson's, and the witness came in and asked me the price: I said 22s. - he asked if I could not take less; I said, No, that it was not mine; he said he should like his father to see it, and then claimed it; I took him to Russell.
 Cross-examined. Q. You knew him? A. Yes; he is a neighbour - he brought it openly, and did not desire me to keep it secret; he hears an honest character, and has lived there four years.
   FRANCIS THOMPSON. I made some silk in June for Mr. Danford; I have no doubt this is our make, but I will not swear it is what I made to that order.
 Cross-examined. Q. Did you make it for him only? A. We have made no more for the last three years.

 1974.  THOMAS PIKE was indicted for stealing, on the 13th of September , 1 plank, value 3s., the goods of Catherine Harding.
 CATHERINE HARDING. I am a  timber merchant, and live at Somers's-town; I saw the prisoner walking from my premises with this plank on his shoulder; I followed and took him - he was formerly in my employ.
   SAMUEL CLARK. I took him in charge; he said he bought it at a sale, but afterwards said this lady's foreman gave it him.
 The prisoner put in a written defence, stating the plank was given to him to saw by a man named Gilham.
     GUILTY. Aged 70. Confined Two Months.
 1975.  THOMAS HILBERT was indicted for embezzlement.
 MR. BODKIN conducted the prosecution.
   GEORGE ILES. I am a baker, and live in Carey-street, Lincoln's Inn-fields. Previous to the 10th of September, I had levied a distress on Mr. Hipworth, of No. 25, New-road, St. George's in the East, for 18l.; I took the prisoner to the house on the 10th of September, and left him in charge with the warrant, and ordered him not to go out or be turned out till the money was paid, which was then 9l.; I called next morning, and saw him at breakfast, and said I understood he had received the money; he said he had; I said, "Let me have it:" he said, "I'll be back in a few minutes;" he went out, and did not return; I saw him next day at the Ship and Shears public-house; he then said he had lost the money, and I must take it by instalments; he has not paid any of it - I took him two days ago.
 Cross-examined by MR. BARRY. Q. You rejected his offer, and took proceedings? A. I made no reply; he lived in a tenement of mine - he is a coal-whipper - he did not say he had lost it when I went to the house.
   BENJAMIN HOPWOOD. I am tenant of this house; I found the prisoner there on the 10th of September; Mr. Spring, a friend of mine, paid him 9l., in my presence - he gave me a receipt, and left between nine and ten o'clock; here is his receipt.
   CORNELIUS CRAYCRAFT. I was in possession of the house with the prisoner, and remained there till the money was paid; we then went and had a pot of beer; we then went to another house, and had a pot of beer, and three half-quarterns of gin.
 Cross-examined. Q. Was he not very tipsy? A. No; he was sober; he told me he should take the money up next day.
 Prisoner's Defence. I throw myself on the mercy of the Court - I was intoxicated.
 GUILTY. Aged 25. Recommended to Mercy. - Confined Fourteen Days.
