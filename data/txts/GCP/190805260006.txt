"Mitre Tavern," Blackfriars, during which he had an account at the

About this time they made the acquaintance of another man, upon
whose advice they made up their minds to use the blank cheques.
They appeared to have got rid of four of them at least. Their
method was to watch houses where tradesmen served, and after the
tradesmen had left the house they would go and say that a mistake
had been made in his book, and ask the servant for it, so getting his
name. They would then write a cheque to the tradesmen, and take
it to him with the book, in payment of his account, getting change

EVANS, Harry (28, porter); breaking and entering the shop of
the Crown Emporium Company, Limited, and stealing therein four
rings, the goods of the said company.
Mr. Sheridan prosecuted.
Police-constable FREDERICK EDMONDS, 597 City. On April 24,
about five to four a.m., I was in Lawrence Lane when I heard
smash of glass and ran into Cheapside, where I saw prisoner stand-
ing with his face close to a broken window at No. 105, the Crow
Emporium Company, Limited, jewellers. On being observed prisoner
ran away. I gave chase aud caught him in Cannon Street. He
said, "It's just my luck; all right, I will go quietly." I took hin
to Cloak Lane Station and charged him; four rings were found
him (produced); they were in his trousers pocket. Prisoner said
they were not the property of the Crown Emporium.
To Prisoner: I did not see you steal the rings from the shop.
Police-constable JEREMIAH PLATT, 542 City. On April 24, at 3.55,
I was on duty in Wood Street, Cheapside, when I heard a smash of
glass, and ran down Cheapside, where I saw the jeweller's shop!
window broken, inside which I saw the stone produced, and four ring
missing from a card which was in the window. All the other cards
were full. I did not see prisoner till I went to the station.
George WALTER PELHAN, manager of the Crown Emporium Com;
pany, Limited, Cheapside. On the morning of April 24, when 1
arrived at the shop, between eight and quarter past, I found a police
man on duty. I had left it all safe on the night before. One of the
trays was slightly eased on one side as though it had been pressed
from the front. There was a hole in the window and rings missia
from a card directly opposite. These are the rings produced ; the
are made of 9-carat gold. The damage to the window would be
about £4 15s, ; it was insured. We sell the rings at 5s, each.
