MARY FRANCES GANNON. I am a widow and live at 12, Pembroke
Square. Kensington-on 12th or 15th August I advertised to let my house
and saw the prisoner with his father-the elder person said in the prisoner's
presence that he had lost money and desired to let his house at Dulwich
for 51. 108., and take mine for 21., thus saving himself 31. 108. & week-I
gave him possession on 21st August-I left the house leaving some boxes in
a back room, and everything was taken out of them--they were not related
to me—they never made me & present.
Cross-examined. The arrangement was made with your father-he wrote
afterwards agreeing to take the house.
JOHN PRESTON. I am assistant to Mr. Harrison, a pawnbroker, of
Aldersgate Street-I have given up three pieces of silk which were pawned
by the prisoner--we received half the money.-this is the memorandum
produced "151. advanced on four pieces of silk for one year; 85, Newgate
Street" the other is for 77. 108.
JOSEPH KEYWORTH. I am clerk to Mr. Beall, house agent, Regent Street
-in September last we had the letting of Windsor House, Slough, on our
books-an application was made to us in the name of Smith for it-the
prisoner afterwards called to learn the terms of letting, and proposed to
take it in his brother's name, Henry Smith, for seven years, at 401. or 40
guineas & year, and to pay 101. for the fixtures-he referred us to Mr.
Gannon, of 12, Pembroke Square-I applied there and received in reply
the letter marked "H" produced--the prisoner afterwards called and I
told him I had received it-he called four or five times, and on one
occasion took the agreement away with him-he brought it back signed
“Henry Smith," and said that he would pay the 101. for the fixtures and
take the agreement back when signed on behalf of the landlord-he paid
for the fixtures and one guinea for the agreement.
Cross-examined. We advertised this shop at Windsor-my impression is
that I only saw you-we never received any rent.
