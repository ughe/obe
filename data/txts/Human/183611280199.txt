But in case the said Mr. Mutch shall fail or neglect to procure the said appointment by the time specified, then and in that case the said Mr. Mutch agrees to refund the said deposit when thereto required; but in case the said Mr. Baker shall refuse to accept the said situation when appointed thereto, then and in that case the said deposit shall become forfeited; and it is further agreed upon by the said parties, that the balance of the said sum of 100 l . shall be paid to the said Mr. Mutch by quarterly instalments of 22 l . 10 s . until the whole shall be liquidated. In witness whereof, the parties herein mentioned hereunto place their signatures, the day and year first above written. G.S. MUTCH .
   Witness.  I certainly thought the prisoner could do what he undertook under this agreement—I was deceived—I paid him the first 5 l . on the 13th of September, and in all 10 l ., including the two payments of 50 s . each—I have not got the situation—I told him that I thought I was unqualified for it—he replied, "There are greater fools than you in the Treasury"—I signed a paper on the 13th of September, and delivered it to the prisoner—I believe it to be a copy of the one which he gave me on that occasion, except that my signature was to the one and his to the other—he was to keep the 10 l . if I got the situation, and have 90 l . more.
   Prisoner . On my second examination there was a letter written by you, forwarded to me, which I handed to the Magistrate, wherein you state you had a prior situation offered to you by as artist, a Mr. Morrant. Witness.  This is the letter, it is my writing—( read )—
 "Oct. 4, 1836.
 "Sir,—I beg to inform you I have received an officer of an engagement for five years from Mr. Morrant, painter of Bond-street, as the enclosed letter will show; and as I feel at full liberty to close the negotiation with you, according to the time specified in the agreement, namely, fourteen days I have accepted Mr. Morrant's offer conditionally, that is to say, if I do not receive the appointment before five o'clock on Saturday evening next, I shall return you the agreement; but in case you can procure the appoint  ment before that time, I will thank you, if you can so manage matters that I shall not be called upon to enter on the officer before the next quarter, namely, the 25th of December, 1836, as I then shall be better qualified with my pen. My not entering before December will not in any way inter fere in the money matters between us. If you can procure my uncle's appointment dated from last quarter-day, namely, the 29th of September, you will confer an additional favour on, Sir, your obedient servant, WILLIAM BAKER ."
   COURT . Q.  Did the uncle get any thing? Witness.  No.
