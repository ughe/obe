Prisoner. Q. How old are you? A. In my thirty-first year I was con-
fined on the 5th of May-the child is living with my parents-it has been in
the workhouse-Dr. Hill attended me you paid him his fee that evening,
and pawned your coat for the money; and the day I was confined you
pawned a coat for 258.-the coat you have on was not then in the state it is
now-you were obliged to have the sleeve ripped up, as you had a bad
finger-you had no other coat when you went to register the child you
wore my father's old trowsers, which were much worn-I do not think they
were torn-you were in a deplorable state with regard to clothes - you had no
means of obtaining them, but of course what you had were in better order
than they are now saw no holes in them at that time it was about a
quarter to eleven o'clock in the morning when you left the house to have the
child registered person was measured for some clothes at Mr. Jukes's
ho gave his name as James Seymour, 15, King-street, Bath-street, City-
road-he wore a frock coat, and was genteelly dressed-Dr. Hill met me
walking with you, not with that person-I know Mr. Hilliard, of George-
yard, Lombard-street-he has known me eleven years have not some-
times seen him for four years together he carried on a correspondence with
me prior to his marriage-when he told me he was married, it ceased he.
has been married about eight years, I had an appointment to meet you there
on the 7th of July.
COURT. Q. Did you go by the prisoner's name before the child was regis-
tered? A. Yes, for nearly ten months, and passed to the world as his wife
-the people of the house spoke to me as his wife, and I represented myself
$0--the prisoner went to register the child with my full consent under
stood it was to be registered in his name_1 asked relative to the name-he
said he would make it all right.
Prisoner. Q. Did you represent yourself as my wife? 4. Yes, always
When I left my father's house, you had a prospect of a situation of 1502. a
year-you left me on the 1st of November.
WILLIAM HOLLAND (police-constable N 146.) On the 2nd of July I took
the prisoner in charge-I read the warrant-he said he would not go with
me unless it was backed by the City authorities-he resisted-1 was obliged
to get two City officers before I could get him to the station-he was calling
to the people to see how a citizen was carried away—he afterwards said he
would walk quietly, and did so-all he said about the charge was, "They
cannot prove I am not married" _1 am quite sure he said that.
Prisoner. Q. Was you ever suspended? A. Yes-twelve years ago it
Was alleged I had said I would not take a thief, because there was no re-
Ward-it was not proved- I was reinstated, with pay-I was not on the case
of Davis for obtaining 5001.- I was not reported to the commissioners for
any thing but what I have named I do not know Broad, a swindler.
Prisoner's Defence. She passed by my name ever since she left her
father's house. I am not the person who did this.
GUILTY.
The defendant entered into his own recognizances to appear for judgment
when called upon.
