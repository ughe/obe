Before Mr. Justice Talfourd.
402. JOSEPH JOLLY, feloniously killing and slaying William Reed
he was also charged on the Coroner's Inquisition with the like offence.
Mr. RYLAND conducled the Proseculion.
JOHN SNAZELL. I am a labouring man, at Wanstead. knew William
Reed when he was alive-he was a labourer at Wanstead- know the
prisoner-on day last month, he and Reed and I, my father; and a man
named Connor were engaged in pulling down a shed belonging to Mr.
Kennerton- were removing the remaining part of the shed inis cartiai
I and Connor were on the shafts, and Reed and Jolly were shoving behind
when we got between Mr. Finnis's gate, and Mr. Brown's lodge, Reed
said that Jolly did not shove enough, and struck him somewhere about the
body-it was no particular blow-they might have four or five rounds--it
might last ten minutes, tumbling and falling about-the falling was the
worst-there was no particular blow, but Reed was striking Jolly a round
or two afterwards, and Reed overreached himself and fell, the road being
a bit slippy, and he cut a bit of skin out of his forehead and down his
nose - E persuaded them to leave off, and I thought Reed would have
struck me-I and my father parted them-Reed did not like being parted
at all - Jolly was agreeable to leave off-Reed seemed to walk very well
after that-we then came on for 100 or 120 rods, and I then left them-
be walked that distance-h seemed to be a little bit freshy like, from a
drop of beer-neither of them were quite sober-we had been allowed
two pints a piece, and they had had antextra seven pots unknown to use
they both seemed a little excited by drink.

COURT. Q. There was no regular fight A. No; they were tume
bling and falling about-I will not be sure whether Jolly had bis coat off,
but Reed had not bis waistcoat off-Jolly would have left off before, but
Reed would not, his spirit was regular savage like.
Cross-examined by Mr. BIRNIE. Q. When you parted them, did you
bear anything said by either of them A. No they seemed to be civil
and quiet enough then-the wound on the forehead and nose was made
by his falling on the gravel-it was owing to the ground being wet and
greasy-hi hands seemed to be out, as if striking at Jolly.
