First Session, 1844-45.
MR. RYLAND conducted the Prosecution. BTS
GEORGE WILLIAM HENRY YOUNG. I am a hawker and pedlar, I live
the Old King Harry, in the Mile-end-road-I generally travel the
Conntry with a van and Sheffield cutlery-I sleep in the van when I am
velling. On the 9th of Oct. I was at Croydon in the morning, and at
Mile-end in the evening-I slept in the Mile-end-road all night on that
day I placed the whole of a drinking-booth in a loft belonging to Mr.
Wilton, who keeps the Old King Harry--it consisted of the canvass and
other things stated -I valued them at about 301.-on the 30th of Oet. I
received information, and came to town- I went to Mr. Walton's, and
found my goods were gone-I know the prisoner Taylor-I had given him
an odd job now and then, but not regularly-I gave a description of him,
and after some hours' search I found him near Rateliff-bighway--he took
me to Mr. Wren's, in Rosemary-lane--I saw Rayner there on the top of
the stairs-he was in his night-gown, just got out of bed-he came down
stairs to me--I said to Taylor, "Is this the man you sold the goods to ?"
he said, Yes, it was-Rayner said it was a bad job, he cquld not get them
back that night, he might the next day, or he could be next day, or
some such words--a friend called in some policemen--they searched the
house, and some goods were found, some bits of green-baize, some
crockery, some glass, a piece of canvass, and a couple of small flags--they
were shown to me, and I knew most of them as my own property-it was
rather late in the evening-I should say it was after eleven-I saw no
pewter measures then I have seen some since, and a copper boiler, &
gravy-dish, and other things—I believe them to be mine-most of them I
can swear to-they were some of the articles I had left in the loft.1
