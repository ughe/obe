ROBERT THOMPSON. On the 18th of July I went into the Jolly Sailors public-house, Ratcliffe-highway, about five minutes after twelve o'clock at night; I met the prisoner on leaving the bar; she asked me to go with her; I said I had no money - she said never mind; I went home with her; I had my watch then - she pulled off her bonnet and shawl, with an intention of going to bed; and as I was taking off my clothes, she took my watch, which I was going to put under the pillow, and looked at it at the candle, then put it in her bosom, and said, "I will take care of it;" she asked me to give her 6d. to get a pot of beer at the public-house next door; she went out, and in a minute a young man came in; he pretended to be drunk, asked where his wife was, and turned me out - I asked him to excuse the light - I went to look at the number, and it was covered with soot and grease - I went to the Policeman, who saw a young man go over to the prisoner, and he called out that was the girl; I crossed to her, but she covered her face with her gown, and ran away - I saw her, and took her.
 Prisoner. He was very much in liquor - I persuaded him to leave his watch with the publican, but he would not; he hesitated some time, then went down to Shadwell church, and came to me in a quarter of an hour - he then went with me, and said he had no money.
   GEORGE SEAMAN. On the 18th of July, about one o'clock in the morning, the prosecutor came to me, and said he had been robbed of a silver watch and two gold keys; he took me to the door of the house - after wiping the soot and grease off. I found it was No. 22 - he described the person, who I believed was the prisoner; I went in pursuit of her - I told him to stop at the door, and I went to the bottom of the court; a man, who is always with her, came up North-east-passage - he went up the court and saw the prosecutor; he returned, and went and spoke to the prisoner - I called the prosecutor and told him I had suspicion that that was the person: he came to me and went across - the man ran away, but we came up to the prisoner; she pulled the tail of her gown over her head - the prosecutor said, "This is the woman;" she said, "I have not got it" - I said, "No doubt you know who has got it;" in going along, she said, "O my God! this man will swear my life away, I never saw him before," but before the Magistrate, she said she had been with him.
 Prisoner. The prosecutor came to me yesterday, and said if he had known it, I should not have been imprisoned, as I was falsely accused.
 ROBERT THOMPSON. No, I did not. GUILTY of stealing, but not from the person. Aged 20. Transported for Seven Years.
 1686.  SARAH BOND was indicted for stealing, on the 9th of July , 1 pillow, value 2s., and 3 smoothing irons, value 1s., the goods of Mary Barnes.
 MARY BARNES. I am a  widow. The prisoner hired a furnished room of me about four months ago, and remained nearly a month; I accused her of taking these things - she said she had pawned them.
   JAMES BOURNE. I am a pawnbroker. I have a pillow, pawned for 1s. by the prisoner in the name of Bond.
   FRANCIS PARKER. I have some irons, pawned with me by the prisoner, I believe.
   ISAAC GREADBOROUGH. On the 9th of July, I took the prisoner.(Property produced and sworn to.)
 The prisoner pleaded distress.
 GUILTY. Aged 36. Recommended to Mercy by the Prosecutor and Jury. Fined 1s. and Discharged.
 1687.  HENRY BAKER was indicted for stealing, on the 16th of August , 7 spoons, value 30s.; 1 snuff-box, value 30s., and 1 ring, value 10s., the goods of Charles Trenholm.
   MARY TRENHOLM. I am the wife of Charles Trenholm. I have known the prisoner twenty-one years. On the 16th of August, he came into my shop, which is a toy-shop; I was packing up a trunk, and I put the articles stated into it - he asked if I had my former husband's snuff-box; I said, Yes - he said, "Let me look at it;" he looked at it, gave it me back again, and I put it into the trunk - a person then came in, and I went away on business; I returned in about ten minutes - the prisoner still stood there; he said,"Your trunk is too full," and he pressed his hands upon it - it was then locked and taken to the Commercial-road, where I was then moving; on the 18th I missed this property from my trunk - I went to the prisoner; he said he had not taken it nor seen it - I sent for Mr. Bird, and for the Policeman; he still denied it, but in going out he said he had robbed me and pawned the things.
 Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS. Q. Where is your husband? A. I do not know - I have not seen him for seven months; I never lived in the house with the prisoner in my life - we were not very good friends; he has said some very disrespectful things of me - I was going to Mr. Shelley's, in the Commercial-road; I did not make any arrangement for the prisoner to come there to see me- I went there on the 16th, and had the prisoner taken on the 19th; he had never pawned any thing of mine by my permission - no one was present when he said he had robbed me; he called me into the parlour, and told me he had robbed me - he then told me again in the presence of Mr. Bird and the Policeman; I did not tell him to say he had taken these things as it would save my character, and I would not hurt him - my husband has not parted with me from jealousy of this man; his affairs were embarrassed - the families were unhappy, and we parted; there was no jealousy - we never thought of such a thing.
   HENRY BIRD. I am a carpenter.
