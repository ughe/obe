No. 40, Crispin-street - I did not know where Crispin-street was at that time - he said he wanted to send two casks of bristles to Birmingham; he wanted four casks - the lot consisted of four; he proposed to deal for cash - being a stranger, I should not have dealt with him on any other terms; a contract was entered into and signed by him - I have it here (reads)"2nd of January, sold Mr. Pratt four casks of bristles, in the Elizabeth, at 11l. per cwt., ready money, allowing 2 1/2 percent discount, draft and tare as customary. S. Pratt" - The 2nd of January was on a Saturday; the bristles were lying at Botolph wharf; an invoice was made out on Saturday respecting the delivery-order; an order was given for weighing the goods off on Saturday - I gave that order to his clerk; he had said he would send some body for the order to have the goods weighed.
 Q. Was any arrangement made between you and the prisoner respecting the delivery-order and the payment? A. The cash was to be paid directly, because he wanted the goods immediately, as he wished two casks to send directly to Birmingham; when his clerk called for the order, he had not got the money - I said I could not give him the order for the delivery without the money, but I said I would send somebody with him to see them weighed, and would send our clerk on Monday with the delivery-order, and to receive the money - I sent Brown on Monday; he returned about twelve oapos;clock with the delivery-order, and without the money, and about half an hour after he returned the prisoner came to the counting-house, and said, " You have sent the delivery-order for the money" - I said Yes; he said "Well, I will pay you directly" - the clerk had brought the delivery-order back, and it was on the desk in the counting-house; he said, "I have not got a cheque now in my pocket, but I will pay you directly, may I not have the delivery-order?" I said, "If you wish to pay me directly, I have no objection to give you the delivery-order" - I gave it to him; he went away with it - I went to Botolph-wharf directly after; I followed him immediately; and on getting there he had arrived, and was busy in removing the bristles away, putting them in a cart, loading them - I said, "You cannot have the bristles without first paying for them;" he said Why - I told him I did not know him and the reference he had given me was one which I could not trust; he said he would pay for them as soon as he had got them to his house - the cart was at this time standing on the wharf, and the carter with him; he said nothing about my accompanying the cart - he said he would pay as soon as they were at his house - as I did not trust him; he had no business to trust me, and he would not pay before the bristles were at his house, he mentioned, that he would pay for them at two oapos;clock, which was about the time they would be at his house.
 Q. Was the hour of two mentioned? A. Yes; it was mentioned by Mr. Pratt - this was about one oapos;clock; I consented to their being carted to his house.
 Q. Were they to be delivered when they got there without his paying for them? A. Why, I expected he would pay for them; I had no authority to deliver them without being paid for, but I sold him the bristles, but my intention was not to let them go without the money.
 Q. Would you have delivered them to him unless he was to pay for them on their arrival in Crispin-street? A. No, I would not have delivered them; Brown, the clerk of Botolph-wharf was present at part of the conversation - I then went home, intending to send another clerk to Crispin-street which I did.
 Cross-examined by MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Your master sever appeared at all in the transaction? A. No, I did what I thought proper; there is a dealing in our trade called prompt - by that I understand cash down; so many days prompt means so many days credit - prompt without naming any days, I should think does not mean fourteen days; I should not understand it so; I gave the weighing order on Saturday - I sent the delivery order first on Monday; I afterwards gave it to the prisoner, and went soon after to the wharf - he had not got the bristles in the cart; he was busy when I came, but I stopped them - we then had a conversation, and he was to pay when they got to his house, which would be about two oapos;clock; I let him go on that, and hearing Brown, the warehouseman at the wharf, say he had known him several times receiving goods in a regular way before.
 Q. On those considerations you let the goods go, to be paid for when they got to his house, which you understood to be at two oapos;clock? A. Yes; I did not send anybody with the cart - I intended to send somebody.
 COURT. Q. In consequence of what he said, did you let them go on condition that they should be paid for when they got to his house? A. Yes.
 MR. ADOLPHUS. Q. Did you go to the prisoner's house afterwards? A. Not myself; I sent Brown, our clerk, to his house a little before two o'clock to receive the money, and I called on the 5th but not for the money - I called to get the money, or to see Mr. Pratt about the bristles - this invoice (looking at it) was written in our counting-house by one of our clerks; it is headed "Plaw and Co." which is the firm, but I do not know of any partner - the prisoner had written down references; I went to them, but was not satisfied with them at all - I did not state so before the Alderman I am sure.
 Q. What was the use of the reference, if he was to pay ready money? A. Because he might have brought more; we wish to know with whom we deal, and always have a reference.
 MR. ALLEY. Q. Does prompt on delivery. mean fourteen days after? A. No; the four casks weighed about 20 cwt., and were worth 198l. odd.
 HUTCHINSON BROWN. I am clerk to Mr. Plaw. On the 4th of January, the last witness sent me to the prisoner's residence, No. 40, Crispin-street, to obtain the money for the bristles, before they were delivered out of the cart - I arrived there a little before two o'clock; the prisoner was not at home - the cart and the bristles did not come there; I remained there till five minutes past three, and left in consequence of being informed it was not likely he would return - there was abundance of time, from one till three o'clock, to carry the goods from the wharf, to that place again and again; I returned and told my employer what had occurred, and Henry Crump was sent there - he returned almost directly; I saw the bristles in the same casks as they were weighed in, at Mr. Kentapos;s in Falcon-square, on Tuesday, the 5th, about half-past three oapos;clock - he is a respectable gentleman, and deals largely.
