Gedroe Hines. I am potman at the Clay Hall public-house at Old
Ford. On the 3rd of May, about seven o'clock in the evening I went to my
bed-room, which is over the bar-I saw my box was safe and locked up-it
contained 71. 10s. in gold and silver, a coat, a pair of trowsers, and a waist-
cort-I went out, and returned about twenty-five minutes past eight o'clock
--I then saw my bedroom door was open-I went up and saw my box was
open and the money and clothes were gone-I know the prisoner he for
merly lived fellow-servant in the house with me-he was in the habit of
coming to the house afterwards-he knew where I kept my money as well as
I did I suspected him, and went in search of him I did not find him, but he
came to me on the Wednesday following, between nine and ten o'clock, and
asked if I wanted him-I said, “Yes," and he walked up to the station-
house with me-I had been making such a search for him on Monday and
Tuesday that he would be likely to hear of it-he was discharged on the next
day.

ZEPHANIAH TAYLOR, (police-constable K 230.) I am stationed at Bow.
On Friday, the 7th of May, I saw the prisoner in Mr. Bird's brick-field at
Old Ford - I went to the edge of the pit where he was at work-he saw me,
and asked if I wanted him-I said, "Yes"-he put on his clothes and came
away-he asked if there was any further evidence against him-I told him I
did not know-when we got to the station, I found the female Hunt had been
apprehended-she was before the Magistrate-I have since tried in every
place to find her, but I cannot-when I told the prisoner at the station that
there was a woman apprehended, he said, “If there is a woman come to
swear against me I shall get seven years, and if I get that, I will ask for
life"--Hunt was afterwards discharged, and gave her evidence.
