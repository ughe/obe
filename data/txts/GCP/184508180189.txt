1785, EMMA WEBB was indicted for stealing 100lbs, weight of lea-
ther, value 51., the goods of Henry Warren Knox Coxhead, and EDMUND
WEBB for feloniously receiving tbe same, well knowing it to have
been stolen.
HENRY WARREN KNOX CoxHEAD. I am a leather-cutter, and live at
Newington. The prisoners dealt at my shop-I am told they are not
related-I missed some leather, and in particular some which was marked
for springs- this is that piece, and I believe the whole of the leather that is
here is mine.
Jury. Q. How do you know this ? A. Here are the marks on it-
the leather between each mark is wide enough to make a pair of women's
soles—I described this to the officer before I inspected it I never sell it
marked in this manner-here are some pieces with a brand mark, which I
can swear to.
MATILDA TENNANT. I am the niece of Mr. Coxbead. On the 31st of
July he went out-Emma Webb came to purchase something-I had set
a person to watch her-(I had seen her take leather previously, but I did
not stop her on the 6th of Aug. Emma Webb came again-I showed her
some black leather-I had served her with a quarter of a yard of union;
and from what the girl told me, I went to the gate after her, and took this
piece of leather from under her cloak-I had sold her a small piece of
leather, but not this.
.
Emma Webb. I gave her some eggs for this piece of leather. Witness,
No, not for this--she gave me two eggs for a small piece a month before.
MARIA MARTIN. I was set to watch the prisoner-I saw her take this
leather from the board, and put it under her cloak-she went out-
brought her back, and Miss Tennant took it from under her cloak. BT
Emma Webb. I did not take it; I bought it in the street of a young
man.
JAMES BURTON (police-constable M 277.) I went to Emma Webb's
lodgings, at No. 1, Henry-street, Kent-street-I knew her before-I found
Edmund Webb there, and a large quantity of leather he said, be had
bought it of different tradesmen ; one of them was Mr. Arnold, and he
gave 22s. for some-I went to Mr. Arnold, and he denied it I found
some of this leather concealed under the bed, and some on a shelf.
Cross-examined by MR. HORRY. Q. Is not this the first time you ever
named a single place as stated by him? 4. No-I stated before the
Magistrate that he said he bought it at particular places- I found him at
work as a shoemaker, and there was leather about.
EMMA WEBB-GUILTY. Aged 26.
