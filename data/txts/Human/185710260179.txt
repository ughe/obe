JOHN TUFFERY. ( Policeman, R  155). On 5th Oct. I was on duty at Lewisham—Hammond came to me—I accompanied him to Lewisham Bridge—we there met the prisoners—they turned round, and ran down Hanover Street—I overtook them—when I caught them, James asked what I wanted with him—I had them both, one in each hand—I told them they were charged with stealing a shawl at Lewisham—James said he had not been there—Sheffard stooped down, caught hold of my legs, and threw me down—James pushed my shoulders—I let Sheffard go—he went off, hat returned just as I was in the act of getting up, and seized James—his coat was torn, and he slipped away—when I charged them, they both said they had not been at Lewisham that morning—I afterwards went to the field, and Hammond pointed out where the scarf was found—there is no thoroughfare through the fields. ALFRED JOHN CROUCH. ( Policeman, R  92). On 13th Oct I was on duty at Greenwich police station—the prisoner James came to the door of the station, and said very abruptly, "What is it you want me for?"—I asked him to be kind enough to step in, and I would show him—he came in, and I produced the printed information—I asked him if he could read—he could not, and I read it to him—I told him I should take him into custody, as he was charged with stealing, in the company of George Sheflard, a scarf shawl—he said that he knew nothing about it, he had not been there at all—I asked him why he ran away from the constable when he was taken into custody—he said that he did not go away from him, but he let him go—I asked him where he had been in the interval—he said at Croydon Pair—I then locked him up—about 3 o'clock on the same day I went to Lewisham, to apprise the parties that James was in custody—when I came back, Sheffard was waiting at the station—he asked me what I wanted him for—I produced to him the printed information, and he read it—he said that he knew nothing at all about it: he was not at Lewisham—I asked him why he ran away from the constable, and he also said he did not run away, but that he let him go—he said he had been at work at different places—I then locked him up.
   James's Defence.  I was going up the Grove to look after my father's mules; I had a nose bag under my arm; I saw Sheffard, and I asked him to come with me, and look after the mules; I had found them there before; Sheffard sat down to do something to his shoe; I walked straight up the fields; I then went to Green, to go down to Lewisham; I never had or saw the shawl before.
   Sheffard's Defence.  I was at work all the week for Mr. Coleman, in the brickfields; but, being wet that day, I could do no work; James came up, and I went with him; I never saw the shawl.
   JAMES  — GUILTY. **— Confined Two Years .
   SHEFFARD  — GUILTY.— Confined One Month .
