   FOURTH COURT .— Friday, September  21 st , 1866.
   Before Robert Malcolm Kerr, Esq .
 833. FREDERICK SCHLETER  (30) was indicted for a burglary in the dwellinghouse of Gerard Koesters , and stealing therein a lamp.
   The prisoner, being called upon to plead, stood mute. The jury were there-fore directed to try whether he was mute of malice or by the visitation of God . JOSEPH NEWMAN(Policeman H 146). The prisoner was tried, con victed, and sentenced to six months' imprisonment on 21st February last—I had him in custody at that time—he pleaded then, and was tried—he addressed the jury through an interpreter. CHARLES ALBERT. I was in court on 21st February last; at the Mid dlesex Sessions—I translated for the prisoner—he spoke to me, and made a long address afterwards. JOHN ROWLAND GIBSON(Surgeon of Newgate). The prisoner has been under my surveillance since 4th August—I have no reason to believe he is incapable of speaking from either physical or mental reasons—he perfectly understands everything that is said to him—he has obeyed the instructions given him by the governor.
   The Jury found that the prisoner was mute of malice, and not by the visita  tion of God, and a plea of NOT GUILLTY  was entered on the Record.
   MR. HARRIS conducted the Prosecution . GERARD KOESTERS.
