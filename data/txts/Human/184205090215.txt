   Prisoner's Defence.  The money was given to me by a gentleman—on Monday night I was going home, and met the prosecutor coming out of a public-house; he asked me if I would take a walk with him; I did; he took me down a place called Europe-walk; going down this walk, he said he had no more money than 6 d .; instead of 6 d . it was a shilling; he gave it me, and wanted to take indecent liberties with me, and I ran away, with the shilling in my hand. BENJAMIN HUBBLE re-examined.  I took no liberties with her—I had been supping that evening at the Red Lion—I left there about ten o'clock—I had received the 15 s . in change for a sovereign—the policeman might have seen her escape from my person—I went to catch bold of her. GUILTY. Aged 27.— Confined Three Months.
 1682. ELLEN WOOD was indicted for stealing, on the   11th of April , 1 coat, value 20 s  ., the goods of Morris Deedy . MORRIS DEEDY. I am foreman in the Victualling-yard at Deptford, and live in New-row. I missed a coat on the 11th of April—it was safe at one o'clock in the day—this is it. MARY DEEDY. I missed the coat on Tuesday morning—it was safe on Monday, the 11th—the prisoner lived in the front chamber.
   Cross-examined by MR. PHILLIPS . Q.  Have you a servant? A.  No—I missed the coat on the Monday—I had seen it last on my husband's back on Sunday. JOHN BROWN. I am shopman to Mr. Marriot, a pawnbroker. I pro duce this coat—I took it in pledge of a woman that answers the description of the prisoner—to the best of my belief it was her—it was pledged in the name of Ann M'Donald.
   Cross-examined. Q.  You will not venture to swear to her? A.  will not—she has never pawned any thing at our shop that I am aware of—I say, to the best of my belief I think she is the person, but I do not swear positively—I cannot say whether she had a child with her as the box was full. JOHN EVANS. I am a policeman. In consequence of information, I went to the prisoner's lodgings—I told her she was suspected of stealing Mr. Deedy's coat—she said she knew nothing about it, and requested me to go with her up stairs to her apartment, to search—I said I should—I called Mrs. Deedy up stairs, and the prisoner took several things out of her box—among them was a shawl, a silk handkerchief, and a pair of new stockings—Mrs. Deedy saw these articles, and said they were in pledge at the time the coat was stolen—the prisoner said they were not, they were taken out a week before the coat was stolen—I asked if she would go with me to the pawnbroker's where she took them out from—she said she would—I went there with her, and asked the pawnbroker if she had taken out articles on the Monday or Tuesday previous—the pawnbroker examined the tickets, and found she had not—I referred him to the 11th and 12th, and he found she had taken out these articles on Tuesday, the 12th—I found she had laid out several sums of money.
   Cross-examined. Q.  What do you mean by "finding out?" was she with you? A.  She was—she was not long at the pawnbroker's—she went with me willingly. NOT GUILTY
