case of any valuable document, if it was security for money, it should be suffi-

exception in the statute where the offence related to any chattel, which a piece
of paper clearly was. The Court would not stop the case, but would consult

JAMES WOOD. I am clerk to Edward Pontifex and another, of Shoe-lane.
The prisoner came into their employment in December last, I think, as
book-keeper-it was his duty to receive money, if paid to him in-doors; but
it was not his duty to collect money out of doors—it was against his duty
to receive money away from the premises, under any circumstances.
(Mr. SLEIGH suggested that the prisoner did not receive the money by virtue
of his employment, it being contrary to his duty to receive it. MR. BALLANTINE
withdrew from the prosecution.)

747. JAMES CHAMPLOVIER, feloniously killing and slaying William
Horrocks.
MR. SHARPE conducted the Prosecution.
THOMAS Rawson. I am a physician, and live at Harberry-terrace, Ken-
sington. On 8th May, about 11 o'clock at night, I attended Mr. Horrocks,
at Notting-dale I found him bleeding profusely from the nose, with a con-
tused and lacerated wound in the centre of the forehead.

nose? A. Yes; I suppose concussion was the cause of the bleeding---no

time of receiving the blow-a druggist had been attending him for some
