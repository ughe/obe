ST. JOHN WONTNER. I am conducting this case on behalf of the Trea sury—I have received several letters signed by Gudgeon, and he has spoken to me with respect to them afterwards—I have some letters before me produced by Mr. Collie—they are all three in Gudgeon's writing, in fact he said at the police-court that he had written them, but as Shuttle worth's clerk—these letters written to Antonio Cannon are undoubtedly Gudgeon's writing, but I believe the signature to be Gudgeon's; it is the same as is on the cheques produced—the last two lines in this pass-book on the left hand and the figures and writing on the last three lines on the right hand side I believe to be Gudgeon's—it is Shuttleworth's pass-book—the last payment is to Leo, 6 l ., which is in Gudgeon's writings—I find other payments to Leo and one payment to Gudgeon of 10 l . on 13th February—the great mass of letters in this bundle marked R are in Gudgeon's writing, addressed to Shuttleworth.
   Cross-examined by Gudgeon . I did not say there was an entry of Gudgeon Brothers, 10 l .; I said Gudgeon—that is on 13th February, 1877—1 do not know the writing, it is no doubt that of the clerk in the bank. CHARLES LLOYD( Re-examined ). These letters signed Shuttleworth I recognise as having Shuttleworth's signature ( Addressed to Antonio Cannon )—some of the signatures to these bills vary a little, but they are in Shuttle worth's writing ( Referring to the sewing machine )—I should say that the endorsement to this cheque is Shuttleworth's.
