 Before Mr. Recorder.
 1573.  MICHAEL BURKE was indicted for a misdemeanour.
 MESSRS. BOLLAND and LAW conducted the prosecution.
   ROBERT ABLETON. I keep a chandler's-shop in Fenchurch-street. On the 22d of August, about twelve o'clock in the morning, the prisoner came in and asked for a two-penny loaf - my wife served him - I was called from the parlour - he tendered a half crown, which I told him was bad, and he must know it - he said he would give me a good one - I said No - I beat it up with a hammer, and threw it into the street - he went out to pick it up, and threw it down the grating - he then returned and said he would have a good one for it, which I refused.
   THOMAS FARNELL. I keep the  Horns public-house, Upper Thames-street. On the 22d of August, about ten or eleven in the morning, the prisoner came and had a glass of gin - he did not drink it, but took out half-a-crown, and laid it on the bar - I said it was bad - he said his eyes were very bad, and he could not see, but if I gave it him again he would give me another for it - I said No - I took out my knife, cut the letter T on it, and nailed it on the tap-room table - he wanted me to throw it down the kennel - Dady, the constable, called that evening, and I gave it to him.
   SARAH CORNISH. I live at the Bull's Head public-house, Crescent, Jewin-street, with my aunt - I am eleven years old. On the 23d of August, between three and four o'clock, the prisoner called for a pint of beer, and gave me half-a-crown, which I rung on the table, and called my aunt, as I thought it bad.
   JOHN DINES. I live in Charter-house-lane. I was in the bar when the prisoner came in and paid this girl half-a-crown - she immediately sounded it on the table. I told her to call her aunt, saying I did not think it good. I afterwards cut it with the shears, and gave it to the officer - it is base metal - he wanted to go into the yard, but I would not let him.
   SARAH TAYLOR. I keep the Bull's Head public-house. I found the prisoner at the bar-door - the bad half crown was given to me - the prisoner said he would give me another, and did so; I said he should not have the other till Dines cut it.
   RICHARD DADY. I am a constable. On the 22d of August Farnell gave me a half crown, which he had nailed to his counter.
   WILLIAM PAYNE. I am street-keeper of Barbican. I was fetched to Taylor's, and took the prisoner. I found a good half crown upon him in a bag, and 2 s. 7 d. in copper; Dines gave me a cut half crown.
 MR.  JAMES FIELD. I am inspector of counterfeit coins; those half crowns are both bad, and from the same die, merely washed.
 Prisoner's Defence. My eyes are very bad. I took them in the street for my oysters.
     GUILTY. Confined One Year, and to find Sureties.
 1574.  SAMUEL PHILLIPS was indicted for a like offence.
   WILLIAM DAVIS. I am servant to Messrs. Wilson and Co. of King's Arms-yard, Coleman-street. On the 1st of August, the prisoner came and asked if I had any old clothes to sell; I agreed to sell him some for 1 l. 9 s. 6 d., he gave me 9 s. 6 d., and said, he would owe me 1 l. - he then gave me two half crowns, then took them back again, and said, he would bring me 1 l. - he returned in half an hour, and gave me eight half-crowns; I looked at them slightly, and he went away; I examined them again, they seemed to sound odd - I left three with Hicks, and went out, and overtook him in Sweeting's-alley; he was running slowly with clothes on his back, and three hats - I stopped him, and took him back; he said, "Are you going to get me into trouble for nothing - do not send for an officer;" I had told him, I would expose him, for it was scandalous; I gave him in charge with the eight half-crowns.
   RICHARD HICKS. I am servant to Messrs. Wilson and Co. I examined these eight half-crowns; I saw that two were bad; Davis left three with me - he returned in about five minutes with the prisoner - we found in all that four of the half-crowns were bad.
 Cross-examined by MR. BRODRICK. Q. Did you put the three into your pocket? A. No. I did not examine them very minutely.
   WILLIAM MACKAY. I am a cellarman. I saw the prisoner running out of King's Arms-yard, at a very rapid rate - I pointed out to Davis, which way he ran.
   JOHN ELLIS. I am a constable, and took the prisoner in charge. Davis gave me eight half-crowns; the prisoner said, "I will defy you to find any bad ones;" I said, there was; he said, "You may find one, but not more;" I said four were bad; he said, he took them in change for a sovereign, of a stranger in the street, of one of our people.
 Cross-examined. He meant Christian? A. Yes; I found a few half-pence in his pocket, and in his left hand, four half-crowns, and two shillings, all good.
   MR. FIELD . Four of these are counterfeits, and of one die.
     GUILTY. Aged 23. Confined Six Months, and to find security for his good behaviour for One Year.
