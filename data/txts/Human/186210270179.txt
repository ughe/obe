   Cross-examined. Q.  Had you an idea it was the company's rope, because of the bad quality? A.  I knew it was the company's rope—it was below what it usually is, and I allowed 2 l ., for that—I thought at the time that the greater part of it was worth about 38 s .,—the lead line was small in point of quantity—I was more particular in looking at the larger size, as that was what I was wanting—there was a quantity of amber line; it was a mixed assortment of rope; but I was seeking ship rope, and not the other.
   MR. METCALFE . Q.  Is the amber line more expensive? A.  Almost double on an average, 36 s ., would be very low indeed for it. JOHN TIMOTHY HUGHES( Policeman , 249). On 20th February last, I went with a search-warrant to Kempster's premises, 20, Charles-street, Bermondsey—it was a cart-shed, close to a stable, in a court—it was not near a shop—there was no appearance of a rope business being carried on there—I found two coils of rope there, which were afterwards identified by Mr. Hounsell—I did not show them to Mr. Urling Smith—they were also shown to Mr. Spratt, who was with me—it was I who took Kempster in custody—when I saw the rope, I took the person in whose stables I found it, in custody; I took charge of the rope also—after the conviction of Kempster and the others, it was given up to the London Ropery Company—I after wards apprehended Bassett, and another officer, who was with me, appre hended Stokes at the same time—I have been seeking the prisoner ever since—I have not a warrant for his apprehension—I apprehended him last Wednesday evening, the 22d—I obtained a certificate of a true bill against him at this Court, and presented it to the Magistrate—I knew where he lived, and went there after him—I employed persons, and had his house watched.
   Cross-examined. Q.  Were Stokes and Bassett in the employment of the company? A.  Yes—Stokes was the foreman, and Bassett under-foreman—I have known ever since this occurred where the prisoner lived; Mr. Vare first told me some information connected with, them, and I found out where they lived—I took him at his own house—when I told him the charge, he said, "I will go with you;" that was all—I did not say I had been looking for him—I merely told him what the charge was against him, and that I was going to take him in custody—I think, on going to the station, I told him I had been looking for him, and he said he had been in the country carrying on business as a commission agent, selling manure.
   MR. METCALFE . Q.  Besides going to his place of residence, did you go to the place at Bow-common, at which he had been employed previously? A.  Yes; several times, and to several other places—I heard that he was known there, but could gain no information which would enable me to get him. HENRY TRIMBLEY GRAY.
