 Re-examined. I had seen prisoner on the afternoon of November 15, 1905. When I went to the police court I was standing in the yard when the van drove up. I saw two or three men get out. I had not been asked before that to see if I could identify any of the men. It was a pure accident that I saw him. I think he was the first who came out of the van. When the men were put up for iden  tification I was about 50 or 100 yards away from Mrs. Smith. I did not speak to her before I identified prisoner. No suggestion was made to me as to the man I should pick out. I was present when my daughter identified the same man. EMILY ELIZABETH READ, wife of last witness. In November, 1905, I lived at 29, Milford Road. On November 15 I went out with my husband and a lodger (Brown). We were in the "Peacock" between three and four, and remained there about half an hour or an hour, or it might have been longer. While we were in there two men came in. I recognise prisoner as one of them. When we left the "Pea cock" Brown, my husband and I went on a tram to Barking Road and went into the "Swan" public-house. The two men followed us on the tram and into the "Swan." They had drinks there and an other shilling was given to them. When we left the "Swan" we went to our own house. The same two men followed us, and Brown invited them to topper. We all had supper together. After supper the two men and Brown went to the door to say "Good-night." I was standing just inside the kitchen and could see what passed in the passage. Maxwell shook hands first and Chasey afterwards. Chasey said, "Who is going to start the game?" Brown said, "We do not want any more game to-night. Finish up to-night—you can call again to-morrow night, if you like." He said he did not know that he meant by saying that. The next I saw was Mr. Brown on the stars and Chasey had him by the throat, the other man was fumbling round Mr. Brown's pockets. I heard the jingling of money. Directly they picked the money up they ran off. I never saw Chasey again till last Saturday week at Stratford Police Court, when I saw him in the dock. He is the same man that I saw at the "Peacock" and at the "Swan," and in my kitchen.
 Cross-examined. I was the last witness called at the Stratford Police Court. I never saw prisoner from the time he was in my house until I saw him in the dock at Stratford Police Court. I was not taken out to identify him because my daughter had done so. No attempt was made to show me this man. I identified him directly I got into Court. I had known for two years that somebody had been knocked about in my house. I was told that the man had been caught.
