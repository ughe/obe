THOMAS BURFOOT, chimney-sweeper, William-street, Borough.  About six o'clock on Saturday evening, the 28th of Oct., I was in William-street—I saw four men about twenty yards from Charlotte-street, about forty yards from the Queen's Head, coming towards me—two were carrying a large black feather trunk with beats nails, and a rope across and across—I waited till they got close to my door—one was then a-head, and another behind by the two that carried tie trunk—when he came up with the others he made an oberservation which I could not hear—I saw one running run up York-street in the direction of the cab -stand, leaving the other three—two were carrying the trunk, and the other was close by—they went on to the end of William-street, crossed George-street to the corner of Robert-street—the trunk was put down under the wall by the beer-shop at the Corner—two men good by the trunk, and one man was standing opposite the other two—the cab  came up William-street to the corner of Robert-streets—the two men and the man who went after the cab  came together, and the two that carried the trunk brought it to the cab  —they all four assisted to put it on the top of the cab  where the cab -man sits—I went behind, and took the number of the cab , 1360—two got inside, and one got on the top—the other was be hind—they drove on to the end of Robert-street—I followed it—I saw if stop—they were geing to turn down Edward-street, but. there being no thorough fare, they turned, round in Edward-street, and two men got down about six yards from the coal-shed—they got up again, and drove up Edward-street to Blackfriars-road—I saw Thomas Jones, belonging to the coal-shed—I looked for a policeman, but could not find one—I gave information to the polioeman, and described the parties—Field and Carroll were two of the four men—I have only little belief as to the other two—one of the others was dressed in a dark frock-coat and one in a kind of fustian coat with buttons—I am not positive about their dress, it being night.
   Cross-examined by MR. CHARNOCK . Q.  Were you always positive about Carrolt and Field? A.  Yes—I saw them for about ten minutes—I never saw them before—it was not very dark, because there was a very fine gasr-light theft—they passed nearly opposite me—Carroll had a cap on—I did not give information that night because I could not see a polioeman—I walked about for half an hour and looked for one—I did not go over the bridge—I first made a communication to the policeman about mid-day on Sunday to Branford—I saw him in Gravel-lane—I heard of the reward of 10 l . at the station—I do not know what is to be my share—I first saw Field and Carroll after this again about one o'clock on Thursday morning at the station—Branford fetched me to them—I had to get out of my bed—the prisoners were sitting by a fire in the station with a policeman—there were five or six policemen there all in uniform—Branford told them to stand up, and said, "Are these the men?"—I said I believed Field and Carroll were two of the men, and I waa not so certain about the others—I entertained the same opinion then as to Field and Carroll that I do now—I did not say at the station that I was certain as to Field—I became certain after—I have not said before that Carroll had a hat on.
