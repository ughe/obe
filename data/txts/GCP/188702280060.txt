GEORGE MILLER (Policeman). I was on duty in Euston Road, about
500 yards from this coffee-stall, and saw the prisoners hustling Morley-
they struggled in the road together, Morley fell into the road; they dis-
engaged themselves and were about to make off-I stopped them-
Morley came up and complained of being assaulted, and that Emery had
put his hand in his pocket-they both said Morley was drunk, but he
was not-I did not see a third man. .
Cross-examined by Smith. The three were quarrelling-a cabman
stopped, and I asked him if he would attend as a witness-he said that
he could not, but I took his number and he is here- I did not refuse to
take the addresses of a lot of people who offered to come as witnesses
for you.
JOHN COLE. I am a cabman, of 27, Fitzroy Street-I was passing with

200 yards off-the third man escaped-I can't say which one struck him,

Cro88-examined by Emory. I pulled up to the kerb-a constable asked

Re-examined. I was on the opposite side of the road-it was too dark
to see their faces when they were struggling, but I never lost sight of
them till the constable brought them up to me, and I am certain they are
